## Problem

Existing token merging methods for accelerating Vision Transformers, particularly those based on Bipartite Soft Matching (BSM), are sensitive to how tokens are split into matching sets and risk merging informative foreground tokens in deeper layers where object correlations become latent. Moreover, these heuristic approaches lack theoretical guarantees about what structural properties of the token representation are preserved during compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PITOME operates on the intermediate token representations (N × h matrices) within each Transformer block, using the key vectors (X^l W_K) as node features to build a fully connected weighted graph. At each layer, it reduces the token count by a fixed ratio r (e.g., 0.9–0.975), so after L layers the ViT encoder outputs r^L·N tokens to downstream components (MLP, LLM, or cross-attention). The method is applied off-the-shelf to pre-trained ViT, BERT, CLIP, BLIP, and LLaVA models without retraining, and also supports a retraining mode where the merging acts as a learnable pooling function.

## Method

PITOME introduces an energy score inspired by graph energy in spectral graph theory to distinguish mergeable tokens (large clusters of similar tokens, e.g., background) from informative tokens (small, isolated clusters, e.g., foreground objects). The score for each token aggregates cosine similarities with neighbors above a dynamic margin m that increases with layer depth, using an ELU-like function to smoothly down-weight dissimilar tokens. Tokens are sorted by energy in descending order; the top 2k are designated as mergeable candidates while the remainder are protected. Crucially, the mergeable tokens are split into sets A and B by alternating indices in the sorted energy order (rather than random spatial indices as in ToMe), so tokens from the same object cluster are likely placed in opposite sets, increasing the probability of correct matches. The actual merging then uses standard BSM with proportional attention weighting. Theoretically, under mild assumptions about within-class and between-class cosine similarity margins, PITOME's coarsened token graph has spectral distance (measured via normalized Laplacian eigenvalues) converging to zero relative to the original graph, whereas ToMe's coarsening retains a non-vanishing spectral gap.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, PITOME achieves an average off-the-shelf accuracy drop of only 0.5% while reducing up to 44% FLOPs (e.g., 86.4% on MAE-H vs. 86.9% baseline; 84.6% on MAE-L vs. 85.7% baseline). On Flickr30k image-text retrieval with CLIP, PITOME retains 291.50/270.94 (Rt/Ri) recall sum versus 291.80/275.52 baseline (0.3% drop), outperforming all BSM-based baselines. On LLaVA-1.5-7B VQA (r=0.9), PITOME scores 75.4 on VQA-v2 and 43.0 on TextVQA, compared to 76.6/46.0 baseline, while DCT drops to 67.8/26.3. Overall, PITOME saves 40–60% FLOPs of base models with minimal accuracy degradation across all four task domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PITOME consistently outperforms all four BSM-based merging baselines (ToMe, ToFu, DCT, DiffRate) across every task and backbone tested. On ImageNet-1k, the gap is most pronounced on larger models: PITOME achieves 84.6% OTS on MAE-L vs. 82.9% (ToMe) and 83.2% (DiffRate), a 1.4–1.7 point margin; on MAE-H it reaches 86.4% vs. 85.6–85.9% for others. On LLaVA-1.5-7B VQA, PITOME leads by 1.9 points on VQA-v2 (75.4 vs. 73.5 for DiffRate) and by a dramatic 12.1 points on TextVQA (43.0 vs. 30.9 for DiffRate), while DCT suffers the largest overall degradation (e.g., 67.8 on VQA-v2 vs. 76.6 baseline). On Flickr30k retrieval with CLIP, PITOME (291.50/270.94) beats ToMe (287.30/270.52) by 4.2 points on Rt and DiffRate (289.33/266.45) by 2.2 points on Ri. On text classification (IMDb), PITOME achieves 93.6% matching the BERT baseline (94.0%) within 0.4%, while DCT and DiffRate drop to 92.4%. The only case where PITOME does not lead is SST-2 text classification, where ToMe (91.2%) slightly edges out PITOME (91.0%), though both are close to the BERT baseline of 91.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PITOME builds directly on ToMe's Bipartite Soft Matching (BSM) as its core merging mechanism, but departs from the random/parity-based token splitting by introducing an energy score inspired by spectral graph theory to identify which tokens to protect versus merge. It positions itself against other BSM variants (ToFu, DiffRate, DCT) that rely on attention scores or cross-modal guidance, arguing these are sensitive to token-space distribution and architecture-specific attention patterns. The theoretical contribution connects token merging to graph coarsening, showing that PITOME's energy-guided selection preserves normalized Laplacian eigenvalues under mild assumptions—a guarantee absent from prior BSM-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free token merging method that achieves 40–60% FLOPs reduction with only 0.3–0.5% accuracy drop across image classification, retrieval, VQA, and text classification, making it immediately applicable to deployed ViT/LLaVA/CLIP models. The spectral preservation theorem offers the first theoretical justification for why energy-guided merging outperforms random BSM, though the assumptions (discriminative embeddings, ordered cluster sizes) are strong and may not always hold in practice. Key open questions include extending to generative tasks (requiring an unmerge mechanism in decoders), scaling to larger inputs via sparse graphs instead of the current O(N²) fully-connected construction, and learning the compression ratio r differentiably per task.
