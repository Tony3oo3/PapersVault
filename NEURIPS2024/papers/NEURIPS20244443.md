## Problem

Large-scale 3D human pose and shape estimation requires training on heterogeneous data sources with incompatible annotation formats (parametric meshes, 3D skeletons from different vendors, 2D keypoints, DensePose), but existing methods are locked to a fixed output format, forcing costly and error-prone re-annotation or consistency losses that don't scale. The paper asks whether a single model can learn to localize any arbitrary point of the human body in 3D from a single RGB image, enabling seamless mixed-dataset training and flexible test-time output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244443/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image crop and a continuous 3D query point in the canonical human volume as input, and produces the corresponding 3D location in camera space along with a per-point uncertainty scalar. The query point is not a fixed set but any arbitrary location on or inside the body, meaning the same network weights can be re-parameterized at inference to output SMPL joints, SMPL-X vertices, COCO keypoints, or any user-defined landmark set. An optional post-processing step fits SMPL/SMPL-X pose and shape parameters to the nonparametric predictions via a fast iterative solver.

## Method

The core contribution is a Neural Localizer Field: a hypernetwork (MLP with learnable Fourier features initialized from Laplacian-eigenfunction-based Global Point Signatures) that, given a canonical 3D query point, predicts the weights of a 1×1 convolutional output layer on-the-fly. This modulated conv layer operates on backbone features (EfficientNetV2) to produce a volumetric 3D heatmap, a 2D heatmap, and an uncertainty map, which are decoded via soft-argmax into a camera-space 3D point following the MeTRAbs architecture. Training uses mixed batches where each example is supervised only on the points annotated for its source dataset (Monte Carlo sampling for parametric meshes, direct joint supervision for skeletons, pixel-space loss for 2D/DensePose), eliminating the need for format conversion. For parametric output, a separate efficient fitting algorithm alternates between per-body-part Kabsch rotations and L2-regularized linear least-squares shape solving, converging in ~3 iterations and running in ~28 ms on GPU.

## Results

On EMDB, NLF-L+fit achieves 68.4 mm MPJPE, a 29.6 mm improvement over the previous best (HMR 2.0 and BEDLAM-CLIFF at 98.0 mm), and outperforms the temporal method WHAM (79.7 mm) despite operating frame-by-frame. On 3DPW (trained without 3DPW-train), NLF-L+fit reaches 59.0 mm MPJPE and 48.8 mm P-MVE, surpassing Multi-HMR (69.5 / 88.8) and HybrIK (71.6 / 82.3). On the shape-focused SSP-3D benchmark, NLF-S+fit attains 10.0 mm PVE-T-SC and 0.85 mIoU, outperforming the dedicated shape method ShapeBoost (11.4 mm). On AGORA (SMPL-X), NLF-L achieves the best NMVE of 98.6 mm (all) and 62.1 mm (body), and on EHF it reaches 36.4 mm full-body MVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most dramatic gain is on EMDB, where NLF-L+fit (68.4 mm MPJPE) beats HMR 2.0 and BEDLAM-CLIFF (both 98.0 mm) by ~30 mm, and also surpasses the temporal WHAM method (79.7 mm) by 11.3 mm while operating frame-by-frame. On 3DPW, NLF-L+fit (59.0 mm MPJPE) outperforms all non-temporal baselines including Multi-HMR (69.5 mm), HMR 2.0a (70.0 mm), and HybrIK (71.6 mm) by roughly 10 mm. On SSP-3D shape estimation, NLF-S+fit (10.0 mm PVE-T-SC, 0.85 mIoU) beats the dedicated shape method ShapeBoost (11.4 mm) and multi-image Sengupta et al. (13.3 mm), while also achieving the best mIoU. On AGORA, NLF-L (98.6 NMVE) edges out Multi-HMR (102.0) and SMPLer-X (107.2), and on EHF it leads with 36.4 mm full-body MVE versus Multi-HMR's 42.0 mm. On skeleton benchmarks, NLF-L matches or slightly trails MeTRAbs-ACAE-L on Human3.6M (39.7 vs 36.5 mm MPJPE) but wins on MPI-INF-3DHP (61.0 vs 60.1 AUC) and MuPoTS-3D (54.9 vs 55.4 mm MPJPE). The paper attributes its broad superiority to the ability to natively train on heterogeneous annotation formats (parametric meshes, 3D/2D skeletons, DensePose) without error-prone format conversions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MeTRAbs volumetric heatmap architecture (Sárándi et al., 2021) and the multi-dataset training paradigm (Sárándi et al., WACV 2023) by replacing the fixed set of output convolution channels with a continuous neural field that predicts the 1×1 conv weights on-the-fly via a hypernetwork. Conceptually, it repurposes the neural field representation paradigm (NeRF, SDF, occupancy networks) in an unusual way: instead of predicting a physical property at a spatial location, it predicts a *function* (a point localizer) parameterized by the canonical body point. This departs from both the fixed-joint heatmap tradition and from DensePose's image-to-canonical mapping, instead performing a direct canonical-to-observation 3D-to-3D mapping for an infinite continuum of points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: by eliminating the need to re-annotate heterogeneous datasets into a single format, NLF removes a major bottleneck in scaling 3D human modeling, and the ability to query arbitrary points at test time makes the model a generalist estimator rather than a format-specific one. The efficient Kabsch-based fitting algorithm (28 ms vs. 33 minutes for the official SMPL-to-SMPL-X transfer) is a practical tool for downstream applications. Open questions include extending to temporal/multi-person settings, handling self-intersections, and whether the continuous formulation provides genuine benefits for truly novel point sets never seen during training. The frame-by-frame limitation and depth/scale ambiguity remain unresolved.
