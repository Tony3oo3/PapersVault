## Problem

OCR-free document understanding with MLLMs faces two coupled challenges: (1) document images contain text at widely varying scales, but pretrained visual encoders are limited to fixed 224×224 inputs, causing loss of fine-grained detail; and (2) naively feeding multi-scale visual features into an LLM inflates the number of visual tokens, and because self-attention is quadratic in sequence length, the computational cost escalates dramatically (e.g., 5× more sub-images → 25× LLM cost). Additionally, prior instruction-tuning approaches that require the model to read entire document texts suffer from truncation when the text exceeds the LLM's context window.

## Input / Output

The model takes a document image and a natural-language instruction as input. The image is first decomposed via shape-adaptive cropping into a grid of sub-images at two spatial scales (n_h×n_w and 2n_h×2n_w), each resized to the 224×224 resolution expected by the frozen vision encoder. The output is an autoregressive text response generated by the LLM. The maximum number of sub-images is capped at 9 (BLIP-2 variant) or 20 (mPLUG-Owl variant) to bound memory, and the LLM sequence length is set to 2048 tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hierarchical Visual Feature Aggregation (HVFA) module, which fuses multi-scale resampler outputs through a feature pyramid: 2×2 max-pooling compresses the finer-scale features to match the coarser scale's spatial grid, and a lightweight cross-attention layer (with the pooled features as queries and the original fine-grained features as keys/values) recovers detail lost by pooling, yielding a single compact feature set whose token count equals the original single-scale count. A training-only reconstruction decoder with MSE loss (and stop-gradient on the target) encourages the pooled features to retain reconstructable information. On the instruction-tuning side, the paper replaces full-text reading with two complementary tasks—Reading Partial Text (RPT), where the model reads a randomly sampled percentage range of the document text, and Predicting Text Position (PTP), where it predicts the relative position of a given text segment—thereby avoiding truncation while teaching layout awareness. The entire system is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8) on BLIP-2-OPT-2.7B or mPLUG-Owl-7B, keeping the vision encoder frozen.

## Results

With the mPLUG-Owl-7B backbone, the proposed method achieves 72.7 ANLS on DocVQA, 45.9 ANLS on InfoVQA, 53.0 F1 on DeepForm, 36.7 F1 on KLC, 34.5% accuracy on WTQ, 68.2% accuracy on TabFact, 63.3 relaxed accuracy on ChartQA, 226.4 CIDEr on VisualMRC, 59.2% accuracy on TextVQA, and 123.1 CIDEr on TextCaps. With the smaller BLIP-2-OPT-2.7B backbone, it reaches 51.4 ANLS on DocVQA and 29.6 ANLS on InfoVQA. The method consistently outperforms UReader (the closest comparable baseline) by 7.3 ANLS on DocVQA and 3.7 ANLS on InfoVQA with mPLUG-Owl, and by 12.7 ANLS on DocVQA with BLIP-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UReader (the most directly comparable baseline using the same backbones and training data), the proposed method wins on all 10 benchmarks with both BLIP-2 and mPLUG-Owl, with the largest gains on DocVQA (+12.7 ANLS for BLIP-2, +7.3 for mPLUG-Owl) and DeepForm (+9.0 F1 for BLIP-2, +3.5 for mPLUG-Owl). Compared to Qwen-VL (9.6B params) and Monkey (9.8B params), the mPLUG-Owl-based model (7.2B params) outperforms them on DocVQA (72.7 vs. 65.1/66.5), InfoVQA (45.9 vs. 35.4/36.1), DeepForm (53.0 vs. 4.1/40.6), KLC (36.7 vs. 15.9/32.8), and WTQ (34.5 vs. 21.6/25.3), despite using substantially less pretraining data (1.1B vs. 1.4B+76.8M). Against document-specific pretraining methods (Donut, Pix2Struct), the proposed method surpasses them on most benchmarks where they report results, e.g., 72.7 vs. 76.6 on DocVQA for Pix2Struct_Large (which uses 1.3B params and 80M pretraining data), but trails on DeepForm (53.0 vs. 61.6 for Donut). Against task-specific SoTA methods (Table 9), the proposed method trails significantly on most benchmarks (e.g., 72.7 vs. 92.3 on DocVQA for InternVL-1.5-Plus with 26B params), which the authors attribute to the much larger model sizes and pretraining corpora of those systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OCR-free document understanding line initiated by UReader, which introduced shape-adaptive cropping (SAC) and full-text reading instruction tuning on resampler-based MLLMs (BLIP-2, mPLUG-Owl). It departs from UReader in two key ways: (1) it augments SAC with a second upscaling level and introduces the HVFA module—a cross-attentive pooling feature pyramid—that compresses multi-scale visual tokens back to the original spatial resolution before the LLM, thereby preserving the LLM's quadratic complexity; and (2) it replaces UReader's 'read all text' task with partial-text reading (RPT) and inverse position-prediction (PTP) tasks to mitigate truncation. The cross-attentive pooling design is conceptually related to MViT's pooled-feature attention but repurposed as a spatial resampler rather than an attention efficiency mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HVFA module offers a practical recipe for incorporating multi-scale visual detail into resampler-based MLLMs without incurring the quadratic token blowup that would otherwise make multi-scale document processing infeasible on standard GPUs. The RPT/PTP training tasks address a real and underappreciated failure mode—text truncation in LLMs during instruction tuning—that silently degrades model quality on long documents. Limitations include English-only training, reliance on resampler-based backbones (preventing full exploitation of ViT's native multi-scale inductive bias), and inheritance of LLM biases; the authors note the approach should transfer to resampler-free models but do not validate this. Open questions include whether the hierarchical aggregation generalizes to three or more scales with diminishing returns, and whether the reconstruction loss can be replaced by more principled information-theoretic objectives.
