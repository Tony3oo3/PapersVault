## Problem

Most deep learning surrogates for spatiotemporal physical systems are trained on a single PDE or narrow parameter family, making them impractical for low-data regimes where simulation is expensive or unreliable. The paper asks whether a single transformer can learn the dynamics of multiple heterogeneous physical systems simultaneously and whether such multi-physics pretraining enables transfer to previously unseen physics or higher-dimensional settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243790/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a uniformly spaced sequence of T=16 spatiotemporal field snapshots (e.g., velocity components, pressure, density) at varying spatial resolutions (128×128 to 512×512) from any of multiple PDE systems, and predicts the next single time-step of the same fields. Fields are per-sample normalized via RevIN before embedding, and the model must infer all physical parameters (viscosity, Mach number, boundary conditions) implicitly from the provided history rather than receiving them as explicit conditioning inputs.

## Method

MPP trains a single Axial ViT (AViT) backbone—using fully axial attention that decomposes joint space-time attention into sequential per-axis operations for O(H²+W²+T²) cost—on multiple heterogeneous PDE systems simultaneously via autoregressive next-step prediction. Three key mechanisms enable multi-physics learning: (1) Reversible Instance Normalization unifies field magnitudes across systems with different scales; (2) a shared embedding space is constructed via 1×1 convolutions whose input channels are sub-selected per system, allowing new fields to be added by expanding the filter; (3) a Normalized MSE loss balances gradients across systems with vastly different state-variable magnitudes. Training uses gradient accumulation with random per-micro-batch task sampling to handle mixed resolutions efficiently, and T5-style relative position encodings are modified to account for periodic boundary conditions.

## Results

On the PDEBench multi-system benchmark, the MPP-AViT-B (116M) pretrained model achieves NRMSE of 0.00240 (SWE), 0.0106 (DiffRe2D), 0.0281 (CNS M=1.0), and 0.0172 (CNS M=0.1) without any task-specific finetuning, matching or exceeding the scaled FNO-B (115M) on all four systems. After task-specific finetuning, MPP-AViT-B reaches 0.00043, 0.0087, 0.0187, and 0.0079 respectively, outperforming the identically-sized task-specific AViT-B on every task. In the low-data transfer setting (100–800 samples), MPP-AViT-B achieves 0.66→0.19 NRMSE (T+1) on the 'Near' compressible Navier-Stokes task, compared to 1.26→0.33 for VideoMAE-K400 and 0.95→0.25 for VideoMAE-SSV2. For 2D-to-3D inflation, the inflated MPP model achieves 0.094 NRMSE on turbulent 3D CNS versus 0.098 for training from scratch and 0.240 for FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PDEBench task-specific baselines, the smallest MPP model (7.6M) outperforms UNet (7.7M) by 10–50× on all four systems (e.g., CNS M=0.1: 0.0312 vs. 1.6650) and beats FNO (927K) on every task except SWE (0.0066 vs. 0.0044). At the 116M scale, MPP without finetuning already surpasses the 115M FNO-B on all four systems (e.g., CNS M=1.0: 0.0281 vs. 0.1451), and after finetuning it outperforms the identically-sized task-specific AViT-B on every task (e.g., CNS M=0.1: 0.0079 vs. 0.0261). In the low-data transfer experiment, MPP-AViT-B consistently outperforms both VideoMAE-K400 and VideoMAE-SSV2 across all training data sizes (100–800 samples) on both 'Near' and 'Far' compressible Navier-Stokes settings, with the gap widening for the more turbulent 'Far' regime. For the 2D-to-3D inflation task, the inflated MPP model beats training from scratch (0.094 vs. 0.098 NRMSE on turbulent 3D CNS) and substantially outperforms FNO (0.094 vs. 0.240) and UNet (0.094 vs. 0.230). On the inverse-problem benchmark, MPP reduces forcing-identification error by ~53% over training from scratch (0.20 vs. 0.43 RMSE) but is slightly behind the Lie-symmetry-based method of Mialon et al. on buoyancy estimation (0.078 vs. 0.062 RMSE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foundation-model pretraining paradigm from NLP and vision (autoregressive next-token prediction, masked reconstruction) to spatiotemporal physical systems, building on the axial-attention transformer architecture from video models (ViViT, VideoMAE) and the hMLP patching strategy from ViT. It departs from prior multi-physics transfer work (Subramanian et al., 2023) which was restricted to steady-state linear PDEs with periodic boundaries, by handling heterogeneous nonlinear time-dependent systems at varying resolutions with mixed boundary conditions. The field-embedding and RevIN normalization strategy is the key architectural departure from single-system neural operators (FNO, DeepONet, U-Net), enabling a shared backbone to process fundamentally different state variables and scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale demonstration that a single transformer can learn multiple nonlinear PDE dynamics simultaneously and transfer to previously unseen physics, providing a concrete recipe (RevIN + field embeddings + axial attention + NMSE loss) for building physics foundation models. The 2D→3D kernel inflation result suggests a practical path to higher-dimensional surrogates without prohibitive 3D training costs. Key open questions remain: whether benefits persist far from fluid-like training distributions, how to enforce conservation laws across heterogeneous systems, and how to handle non-uniform geometries and noisy/incomplete observations that are common in real simulation and observation settings.
