## Problem

Existing open-set 3D object retrieval methods assume single-level category labels and strict disjointness between training and testing sets, which is unrealistic in practice where objects carry hierarchical labels. In real scenarios, training and testing sets share coarse categories (e.g., geometric shape types) while being completely disjoint at fine categories, creating contradictory optimization pressures that existing methods cannot resolve. This paper introduces the Semi-Open Environment setting to formalize this partial-overlap regime and addresses the resulting distribution disequilibrium and class confusion for unseen fine categories.

## Input / Output

Each 3D object is represented by three modalities: 12 rendered multi-view images, a 1024-point point cloud, and a 32×32×32 voxel grid, from which basic features are extracted by pre-trained MVCNN, PointNet, and 3D ShapeNets backbones respectively. At retrieval time, the model additionally receives the coarse category label as a conditional constraint, producing a single semantic embedding z_i ∈ R^d per object. The retrieval objective minimizes a contrastive risk over query-target pairs conditioned on coarse labels, with fine labels used only for evaluation.

## Method

The HERT framework operates in two stages. First, the Hierarchical Retrace Embedding (HRE) module uses a multi-modal auto-encoder to fuse modality-specific features into a unified embedding, then a retrace auto-encoder that injects the coarse label encoding into the unified embedding to produce a retrace embedding, capturing multi-level category semantics while balancing seen and unseen category representations. Second, the Structured Equilibrium Tuning (SET) module constructs a superposed hypergraph whose vertices are weighted combinations of unified and retrace embeddings, with two types of hyperedges: coherent hyperedges grouping vertices sharing the same coarse label (local structure) and entangled hyperedges connecting K-nearest neighbors across the full feature space (global structure). Hypergraph convolution (HGNNConv) propagates information over this superposed structure, and a learnable memory bank distills the resulting conditional embeddings into invariant anchors for unbiased generalization to unseen fine categories. The two modules are trained sequentially with separate loss functions: HRE uses homology, bi-reconstruction, and retrace cross-entropy losses; SET uses memory reconstruction and cross-entropy losses.

## Results

On the four semi-open 3DOR datasets, HERT achieves mAP of 0.5756 (SO-ESB), 0.5678 (SO-NTU), 0.6336 (SO-MN40), and 0.6339 (SO-ABO), outperforming all baselines across mAP, Recall, NDCG, and ANMRR. The most notable gains are on SO-NTU and SO-MN40, where HERT improves mAP by approximately 17.7% and 9.6% over the second-best method (HGM2R), and Recall by 10.2% and 7.9%, respectively. On SO-ESB, HERT reaches a Recall of 0.9346, substantially ahead of the next-best TranGCN (0.9011).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERT outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and every metric. The strongest competitor is HGM2R [7], the only prior method designed for open-set 3DOR; HERT surpasses it by 17.7% mAP on SO-NTU (0.5678 vs. 0.4821) and 9.6% mAP on SO-MN40 (0.6336 vs. 0.5779), with similar margins on Recall. Among closed-set retrieval baselines, TranGCN [15] is the closest on SO-ESB (mAP 0.5063 vs. HERT's 0.5756, a 13.7% gap), while SDML [11], CMCL [14], and MMSAE [34] cluster tightly in the 0.49–0.52 mAP range on SO-ESB and 0.44–0.45 on SO-NTU. C2AE [21], the open-set recognition method, consistently performs worst across all four datasets (e.g., mAP 0.4303 on SO-NTU, 0.4669 on SO-ABO), confirming that open-set recognition strategies do not transfer well to the semi-open retrieval setting. The gap between HERT and the second-best method widens on datasets with more unseen fine categories (SO-NTU has 54 unseen vs. 17 on SO-ABO), suggesting the hierarchical equilibrium mechanism is most beneficial when the unseen category space is larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypergraph-based open-set 3D object retrieval framework of Feng et al. (HGM²R, TPAMI 2023) by replacing the strict disjoint-distribution assumption with a semi-open hierarchical setting where training and testing sets share coarse labels but are disjoint at the fine level. The core hypergraph convolution operation is inherited from Gao et al. (HGNN+, TPAMI 2022), but the superposed hypergraph introduces two distinct hyperedge types (coherent for local same-coarse-class grouping, entangled for global KNN-based cross-class links) that go beyond the single isomorphic vertex model of prior open-set 3DOR. The Hierarchical Retrace Embedding module, built on cascaded autoencoders, is a novel mechanism for injecting coarse-label semantics into the embedding space, departing from the single-level label assumption that underpins all prior open-set 3D retrieval methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semi-open environment framing is a practical and well-motivated refinement of the open-set 3DOR task, acknowledging that real-world category taxonomies are hierarchical and that training/testing splits rarely produce fully disjoint label spaces. The four released datasets (SO-ESB, SO-NTU, SO-MN40, SO-ABO) provide the first benchmarks for this setting, though they are relatively small and limited to three coarse categories. Key open questions include whether the hierarchical equilibrium approach scales beyond three label levels (acknowledged as a limitation), whether the superposed hypergraph structure generalizes to deeper hierarchies, and whether the gains persist when compared against methods natively designed for multi-level retrieval rather than adapted closed-set baselines. Code and datasets are not yet publicly available, limiting immediate reproducibility.
