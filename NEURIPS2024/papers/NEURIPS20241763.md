## Problem

While substantial progress has been made in learning primal optimization proxies, there is no principled framework for learning dual solutions that certify the (sub)optimality of those primal predictions. Existing dual-related work either uses dual variables only as warm-starts for solvers, handles only specific problem classes, or lacks dual feasibility guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first self-supervised learning methodology that combines conic duality theory with neural networks to produce dual-feasible solutions with valid Lagrangian dual bounds for general conic optimization problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes flattened conic problem data (A, b, H, h, c) as input to a fully-connected neural network, which outputs a partial dual variable y ∈ R^m. This y is then projected onto the dual cone K* via a differentiable conic projection layer (Euclidean or radial), and completed into a full dual-feasible solution (ŷ, ẑ) using a closed-form or implicit dual completion procedure. The final output is a dual-feasible pair (ŷ, ẑ) whose Lagrangian dual bound L = b^T ŷ + h^T ẑ certifies a lower bound on the primal optimal value. Notably, the user only needs to identify primal constraints satisfying Theorem 1's conditions (e.g., bounded variables) rather than explicitly formulating the dual problem.

## Method

DLL combines three building blocks: (1) a dual conic completion procedure (Theorems 1–2) that, given a projected ŷ ∈ K*, recovers ẑ ∈ C* such that (ŷ, ẑ) is dual-feasible and provides the strongest possible dual bound for that fixed ŷ, with closed-form solutions available for bounded variables, trust regions, and convex quadratic objectives; (2) differentiable conic projection layers supporting both Euclidean and radial projections onto standard cones (non-negative orthant, SOC, RSOC, PSD, exponential, power), where radial projections are cheaper and avoid gradient vanishing; and (3) a self-supervised training objective that maximizes the expected Lagrangian dual bound over the instance distribution, requiring no labeled dual-optimal solutions. The key distinction from prior methods like DC3 is that DLL exploits dual optimality conditions in the completion step rather than merely enforcing feasibility, yielding stronger theoretical guarantees, smaller network output dimensions, and no correction steps.

## Results

On linear programming (multi-dimensional knapsack) instances, DLL achieves average optimality gaps of 0.07%–1.93%, compared to 19.58%–274.78% for DC3, an improvement of roughly two orders of magnitude. On nonlinear production planning instances, DLL achieves average gaps of 0.23%–1.03% versus 70.76%–87.01% for DC3. Inference time is approximately 1000× faster than Gurobi (LP) and Mosek (nonlinear) interior-point solvers, and 3–10× faster than DC3, with all models trained in under one hour on a single V100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary learning-based baseline is DC3 [Donti et al., ICLR 2021], applied to the dual problem with equality completion and inequality correction. DLL outperforms DC3 by approximately two orders of magnitude on average optimality gap across all tested problem sizes: 0.07%–1.93% (DLL) vs. 19.58%–274.78% (DC3) on LP instances, and 0.23%–1.03% (DLL) vs. 70.76%–87.01% (DC3) on nonlinear production planning. DLL is also 3–10× faster at inference than DC3, owing to its smaller output dimension (m vs. m+n for LP; 1 vs. n+1 for nonlinear) and the absence of iterative correction steps. Against commercial interior-point solvers (Gurobi for LP, Mosek for nonlinear conic), DLL achieves roughly 1000× speedups (e.g., 0.3 GPU.ms vs. 2.8 CPU.s for m=5, n=100 LP; 0.2 GPU.ms vs. 73.5 CPU.s for n=10 production planning), while maintaining sub-0.5% average optimality gaps. The paper notes that DC3 frequently fails to produce dual-feasible solutions, requiring post-hoc correction via DLL's own completion procedure to report valid bounds, further underscoring the robustness advantage of the proposed methodology.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes two narrow prior efforts on dual proxy learning—Qiu et al. [21] for a second-order cone relaxation of AC Optimal Power Flow, and Klamkin et al. [22] for bounded linear programming—into a unified framework for arbitrary conic optimization problems. It departs from the dominant primal-focused optimization learning literature (DC3, LOOP-LC, RAYEN) by introducing a self-supervised Lagrangian loss that directly maximizes the dual bound rather than merely enforcing feasibility, and by exploiting dual optimality conditions (not just feasibility constraints) in its completion procedure. The radial projection operators and closed-form dual completion formulae for standard cones represent new technical contributions that eliminate the need for costly implicit layers in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLL provides the first principled, self-supervised framework that yields valid Lagrangian dual bounds for general conic problems, enabling quality certificates for learned primal solutions without requiring labeled dual-optimal data. The 1000x speedups over commercial interior-point solvers with sub-0.5% average optimality gaps make it practically relevant for real-time or large-scale optimization. Key limitations include restriction to convex conic problems (no discrete or non-convex constraints), reliance on FCNNs requiring separate models per input dimension, and the need for implicit layers when no closed-form completion exists. Open questions include extending to GNN architectures for variable-size problems, integrating DLL as a warm-start into existing optimization algorithms, and handling the MINLP setting where strong duality may fail.
