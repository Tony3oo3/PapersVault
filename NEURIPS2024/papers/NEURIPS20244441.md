## Problem

Return-conditioned diffusion models for offline RL require hand-crafted reward functions that are impractical to design across multiple tasks, and their classifier-free guidance paradigm fails to ensure consistency between the specified condition and the generated trajectory. This paper addresses both gaps by replacing scalar rewards with multi-task preference representations and introducing a mutual information regularization that enforces alignment between conditions and generated trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244441/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes the current state s_t and a learned optimal preference representation w_i* (a 16-dimensional multivariate Gaussian with diagonal covariance, parameterized by mean μ_i* and covariance Σ_i*) as the conditioning signal for a temporal U-Net diffusion model with 200 denoising steps. The output is a state-sequence trajectory segment of horizon h (20 for most tasks, 100 for Hopper), from which actions are decoded step-by-step via a separately trained inverse dynamics model g_ω(s_t, s_{t+1}). The preference representations themselves are learned from trajectory segments τ = [s_i, a_i]_{i∈[1,h]} using a transformer-based encoder that outputs distributional (Gaussian) embeddings rather than point estimates.

## Method

CAMP learns a versatile preference representation space via a transformer encoder f_ψ that maps trajectory segments to Gaussian distributions, trained with a combined KL divergence loss (pulling preferred trajectories toward a per-task optimal representation w_i* while pushing unpreferred and cross-task trajectories away) and a triplet loss (stabilizing the unbounded KL term). The optimal representations {w_i*} are learned iteratively with stop-gradient to avoid trivial solutions. The conditional diffusion model is then augmented with a mutual information regularization term I(τ_k; w), approximated via a variational predictor q_φ(w|τ_k) that predicts the condition from the noisy trajectory at step k, making the objective tractable without full K-step denoising during training. At inference, conditioning on w_i* guides the diffusion model to generate trajectories aligned with task i's preferences, and the inverse dynamics model decodes actions from consecutive states in the generated trajectory.

## Results

On D4RL Mujoco tasks, CAMP achieves an average return of 71.6, outperforming BC (50.4), PT (65.7), OPRL (61.4), and the re-implemented OPPO (59.2), while approaching IQL with ground-truth rewards (81.6). On Meta-World MT-10 with near-optimal data, CAMP reaches 68.9% average success rate, surpassing MT-IQL (65.7%) and MT-BC (62.2%) with only a small gap to MTDiff (76.7%); with sub-optimal data, CAMP (56.2%) matches MT-IQL (56.7%) and substantially outperforms MTDiff (44.0%). On five unseen tasks, CAMP generalizes to 35.8% average success rate, nearly doubling the best baseline (MT-IQL at 19.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IQL (which has access to ground-truth reward functions), CAMP is competitive on medium-expert tasks (e.g., 95.0 vs. 94.7 on halfcheetah-medium-expert, 108.9 vs. 107.4 on hopper-medium-expert) but trails on medium and medium-replay datasets. Against MTDiff on Meta-World MT-10, CAMP achieves 68.9% vs. 76.7% success rate on near-optimal data (a ~8-point gap) but reverses to 56.2% vs. 44.0% on sub-optimal data, showing greater robustness to dataset quality. Against OPPO, CAMP substantially outperforms both the reported (79.7 avg) and re-implemented (59.2 avg) versions on D4RL, and on Meta-World the MT-OPPO variants collapse to 14–15% success rate versus CAMP's 56–69%. Against PT and OPRL, CAMP shows consistent improvements across all D4RL tasks, with the largest gains on hopper tasks (e.g., 108.9 vs. 44.0 for PT on hopper-medium-expert). On unseen-task generalization, CAMP (35.8% avg) nearly doubles MT-IQL (19.9%) and MTDiff (17.8%), with the largest margin on handle-press-v2 (76.8% vs. 60.0% and 36.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion model framework for decision-making (Decision Diffuser, Ajay et al. 2023) by replacing scalar return conditions with multi-dimensional preference representations learned from pairwise trajectory comparisons. It departs from the Bradley-Terry reward-modeling paradigm used in prior preference-based RL (PT, OPRL) by directly learning trajectory representations aligned with preference labels via triplet and KL losses, and from OPPO (Kang et al. 2023) by introducing mutual information regularization to fix the well-known condition-generation misalignment in classifier-free guidance. The multi-task preference formulation generalizes single-task preference learning by incorporating both intra-task (return-based) and inter-task (task-relevance) preferences into a unified representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in offline RL where multi-task preference learning has been largely unexplored, and provides a clean mechanistic fix (MI regularization) for the condition-generation misalignment that plagues return-conditioned diffusion planners. However, the approach still requires preference data (scripted teachers in experiments), the diffusion sampling remains computationally expensive (~30-59 hours training), and generalization to truly novel tasks remains limited (several unseen tasks still yield near-zero success). Open questions include whether the MI regularization transfers to other conditional generative settings, how the method scales beyond 10 tasks, and whether faster samplers (DPM-solver) can close the quality gap without sacrificing alignment.
