## Problem

Prior work on stochastic zeroth-order optimization established O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity under convexity with minimal smoothness, and O(ε^{-(2+d/k)}) under smoothness without convexity, but the combined regime of strong convexity plus Lipschitz Hessian (k=3) remained unresolved. This paper provides the first tight minimax characterization for this setting, showing the optimal rate is O(ε^{-1.5}) with linear dimension dependence, and proves matching upper and lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives T adaptive queries to an unknown f: R^d → R, each returning a noisy scalar observation y_t = f(x_t) + w_t where w_t is subgaussian with unit variance. The function is assumed strongly convex (minimum Hessian eigenvalue ≥ M) with Lipschitz Hessian in Frobenius norm (parameter ρ), and the global minimum lies within a bounded hyperball of radius R. The output is a single point x_T whose expected simple regret E[f(x_T) − f(x*)] is bounded by O(ρ^{2/3} d T^{-2/3} / M).

## Method

The algorithm operates in two stages. The bootstrapping stage runs ⌊T^{0.1}⌋ iterations of a modified Newton step using coordinate-wise gradient and Hessian estimators, with a projection that clips the step size to M/ρ to handle unbounded Hessians under stochastic noise. The final stage performs a single mirror-descent-like step: it estimates the Hessian to construct a non-isotropic hyperellipsoid sampling distribution (shaped by Z_H where Z_H² = Ĥ^{-1}), then uses this distribution in a spherical-sampling gradient estimator whose bias is sharply characterized as O(r²ρ√d/(d+2)) under Lipschitz Hessian. This non-isotropic sampling optimally balances the bias-variance tradeoff in the small-gradient regime, and the final Newton step is projected to the L2 ball of radius M/ρ. The lower bound is established via a KL-divergence argument constructing hard-instance function pairs that are indistinguishable without Ω(T) samples.

## Results

The paper establishes the first tight minimax simple regret bound for stochastic zeroth-order optimization of strongly convex functions with Lipschitz Hessian: Θ(dT^{-2/3}M^{-1}), where T is the number of function evaluations, d is the dimension, and M is the strong convexity parameter. The upper bound is achieved by a two-stage algorithm combining a bootstrapping stage (modified Newton iterations) and a mirror-descent final stage using hyperellipsoid-sampling gradient estimation. The lower bound is proven via a KL-divergence-based construction of hard-instance function pairs. The key improvement over prior work is the linear dimension dependency d, matching the lower bound exactly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method achieves the optimal linear dimension dependency O(d), improving upon Akhavan et al. (2020) which has O(d²) and Novitskii & Gasnikov (2021) which has O(d^{5/3}), while matching their T^{-2/3} and M^{-1} dependencies. Compared to Bach & Perchet (2016), the paper improves both the T-dependency (T^{-2/3} vs T^{-1/2}) and the M-dependency (M^{-1} vs M^{-1/2}), though Bach & Perchet's setting requires an additional Lipschitz gradient (k=2) assumption that the paper does not. The improvement over Akhavan et al. and Novitskii & Gasnikov is achieved by using non-isotropic (hyperellipsoid) sampling in the final stage and a sharper bias characterization for the spherical-sampling gradient estimator (bias of r²ρ√d/(2(d+2)) vs prior O(L√d·r²) bounds). The paper also removes the need for the k=2 Lipschitz gradient assumption required by all three prior works, relying only on the k=3 Lipschitz Hessian condition in Frobenius norm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of stochastic zeroth-order optimization from Flaxman et al. (2005) and Agarwal et al. (2010) by exploiting higher-order smoothness (Lipschitz Hessian in Frobenius norm) combined with strong convexity. It departs from the prior works of Bach & Perchet (2016), Akhavan et al. (2020), and Novitskii & Gasnikov (2021) in two key ways: it removes the additional Lipschitz gradient (k=2) requirement that those works needed, and it replaces isotropic sampling with non-isotropic hyperellipsoid sampling to achieve the optimal O(d) dimension dependency (vs. O(d²) or O(d^{5/3}) in prior work). The two-stage bootstrapping-plus-mirror-descent framework and the sharp bias characterization for the spherical-sampling gradient estimator under k=3 smoothness are the main technical departures from the projected gradient-descent-style algorithms used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tight (matching upper and lower bound) characterization of minimax simple regret for strongly convex functions with Lipschitz Hessian, settling a fundamental open question in bandit optimization. The O(ε^{-1.5}) rate is a meaningful improvement over the O(ε^{-2}) rate achievable with only convexity or Lipschitz gradient, and the optimal linear dimension dependency is important for high-dimensional applications. Open questions include extending the analysis to the online (average regret) setting and understanding the fundamental tradeoff between simple and average regret, which the authors identify as natural follow-up directions. The non-isotropic sampling insight and the bootstrapping framework may also transfer to other derivative-free or bandit optimization settings.
