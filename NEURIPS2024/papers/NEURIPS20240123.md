## Problem

In centralized distributed optimization with compressed communication, no prior method achieves optimal time complexity under arbitrary heterogeneity of both computation and communication times across workers. Existing methods like Minibatch SGD and QSGD are non-robust to slow workers, while Rennala SGD and Asynchronous SGD only handle computation heterogeneity and ignore communication costs. This paper establishes the fundamental time-complexity limit for the full heterogeneous setting and provides a matching algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-worker computation times h_i and communication times τ_i (for a fixed unbiased compressor), a starting point x^0 ∈ R^d, and an error tolerance ε, and produces a random point x̂ satisfying E[‖∇f(x̂)‖²] ≤ ε. A key design choice is that the algorithm must know h_i and τ_i upfront to compute the equilibrium time t* and set per-worker minibatch sizes b_i and compression counts m_i; an adaptive variant (Alg. 7) removes this requirement at the cost of a slightly worse guarantee depending on local time-fluctuation ratios r_i.

## Method

Shadowheart SGD introduces an implicit 'equilibrium time' t*(ω, σ²/ε, [h_i, τ_i]) defined as the solution to a fixed-point equation that balances the harmonic-mean-like contributions of computation and communication across the fastest subset of workers. Given t*, each worker i computes b_i = ⌊t*/h_i⌋ stochastic gradients, sums them, and sends m_i = ⌊t*/τ_i⌋ independently compressed copies to the server, which aggregates all messages using variance-minimizing weights w_i. The paper proves that the resulting time complexity T* = O(LΔ/ε · t*) matches a lower bound for all centralized methods with compressed communication, making it optimal. A bidirectional extension incorporates the EF21-P error-feedback mechanism with a biased TopK server compressor to handle non-negligible broadcast cost, and a per-iteration variant (Alg. 4) accommodates time-varying h_i^k and τ_i^k.

## Results

Shadowheart SGD achieves a time complexity of T_* = (32LΔ/ε) × t*(ω, σ²/ε, [h_i, τ_i]₁ⁿ), where t* is the equilibrium time, and this is proven to be optimal (matching a lower bound) among all centralized methods with compressed communication. In numerical comparisons (d=10⁶, n=10³, uniform i.i.d. times), Shadowheart SGD is up to 10⁴× faster than Minibatch SGD and QSGD, and up to 10²× faster than Rennala SGD/Asynchronous SGD, depending on the noise regime σ²/ε. The method is robust to arbitrarily slow workers (finite time complexity even when one worker has infinite computation/communication time), whereas Minibatch SGD and QSGD become non-robust (infinite time complexity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Shadowheart SGD dominates all four baselines in time complexity across all regimes. Against Minibatch SGD, it is 10³–10⁴× faster in the numerical comparison and remains finite when a worker is infinitely slow (whereas Minibatch SGD diverges). Against QSGD, the improvement is 3× at low noise (σ²/ε=1) growing to 10⁴× at high noise, and again Shadowheart SGD is robust to slow workers while QSGD is not. Against Rennala SGD and Asynchronous SGD (which share the same lower bound when communication is non-negligible), Shadowheart SGD is 1.5–10²× faster, with the gap widening as dimension d or communication time τ̇ grows. The paper proves via a matching lower bound (Theorem O.5) that no centralized method with compressed communication can beat Shadowheart SGD's time complexity up to a constant factor, establishing it as the optimal strategy in this family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rennala SGD framework (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023c), which achieved optimal time complexity for distributed SGD under arbitrary computation heterogeneity but assumed negligible communication, to the more general setting where both computation and communication times are arbitrarily heterogeneous. It draws on QSGD's unbiased compression mechanism for the communication protocol and on EF21-P error feedback for the bidirectional variant. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the 'equilibrium time' t*, an implicit mapping that jointly balances per-worker computation and communication budgets, which the authors show is not merely an algorithmic artifact but appears in a matching lower bound, establishing it as a fundamental quantity for the problem class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing the optimal time complexity for centralized distributed optimization with compressed communication under arbitrary heterogeneity, closing a gap that prior methods (Minibatch SGD, QSGD, Asynchronous SGD, Rennala SGD) left open. The d-times improvement over all baselines in large-n regimes and the robustness to slow or absent workers have direct implications for federated and cross-device learning where device heterogeneity is the norm. Open questions include extending the analysis to statistical heterogeneity (non-IID data), finding an explicit closed-form for the equilibrium time, and validating the theory on large-scale deep learning workloads beyond the logistic regression experiments on MNIST.
