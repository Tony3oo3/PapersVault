## Problem

While class-balancing and model scaling are known to affect worst-group accuracy (WGA) under spurious correlations, their interaction remains poorly understood, with conflicting conclusions in the literature about whether overparameterization helps or hurts robustness. This paper reveals that common class-balancing techniques (upsampling, upweighting) can induce catastrophic WGA collapse during finetuning, and that subsetting's effectiveness depends on group structure — nuances previously overlooked in the group-robustness literature.

## Input / Output

The paper evaluates finetuned pretrained models (ConvNeXt-V2, BERT, ResNet, Swin Transformer) on four group-robustness benchmarks: Waterbirds and CelebA (RGB images) and CivilComments and MultiNLI (text). Each dataset partitions examples into groups defined by the Cartesian product of target classes and spurious features (e.g., bird species × background type), and the primary evaluation metric is worst-group accuracy — the minimum test accuracy across all groups — rather than standard average accuracy.

## Method

The paper's core technical contribution is a mixture balancing method that interpolates between subsetting (removing data to equalize class sizes) and upsampling (adjusting sampling probabilities for balanced mini-batches) via a tunable class-imbalance ratio, increasing exposure to majority class data without over-sampling the minority class. Through comprehensive experiments across model scales (3.4M–109M parameters) and four benchmarks, the authors show that model scaling improves WGA only in conjunction with appropriate class-balancing, while scaling on imbalanced datasets or with the wrong balancing technique can harm robustness. They further identify a spectral imbalance in finetuned features: minority group covariance matrices consistently exhibit larger spectral norm than majority groups when conditioned on the class, quantified via a new intra-class spectral norm ratio metric that correlates with intra-class accuracy disparities.

## Results

On Waterbirds, the proposed mixture balancing (2:1 ratio) achieves 81.1% worst-group accuracy (WGA), outperforming upsampling (79.9%) and subsetting. On CelebA, subsetting achieves the best WGA of 74.1%, while on CivilComments, mixture balancing (3:1 ratio) achieves 77.6% WGA. Model scaling with appropriate class-balancing yields WGA improvements of up to 12% for interpolating models (e.g., Waterbirds, CelebA) and up to 40% for non-interpolating models (e.g., MultiNLI with BERT). Class-balanced upsampling and upweighting both suffer catastrophic WGA collapse over training epochs on the more class-imbalanced datasets (CelebA, CivilComments), performing no better than the unbalanced baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Idrissi et al. (CLeaR 2022), the paper demonstrates that their recommended upsampling technique (called 'reweighting' in their work) suffers catastrophic WGA collapse with standard hyperparameters on CelebA and CivilComments, contradicting their positive findings which relied on extensive hyperparameter tuning using group labels. The proposed mixture method outperforms both subsetting and upsampling on Waterbirds (81.1% vs. lower values) and CivilComments (77.6%), while on CelebA it does not beat subsetting (74.1%) but significantly alleviates the collapse seen with upsampling. Against Pham et al. (NeurIPS Workshop 2021), the paper replicates their ResNet scaling experiments and shows that class-balancing fundamentally changes the scaling story: on Waterbirds, class-balancing enables a much more beneficial WGA trend during scaling regardless of whether a linear probe or full model is trained, whereas Pham et al. reported a neutral or negative trend without class-balancing. On CelebA, class-balancing increases baseline WGA but does not alter the scaling behavior, and the ConvNeXt-V2 results in Figure 3 show a more positive WGA trend than the ResNet results of Pham et al. suggest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the class-balancing line of work initiated by Idrissi et al. (CLeaR 2022), who studied subsetting and upsampling for worst-group accuracy, and on Pham et al. (NeurIPS Workshop 2021), who examined model scaling effects on group robustness without class-balancing. It departs from both by showing that the choice among subsetting, upsampling, and upweighting produces dramatically different WGA dynamics (including catastrophic collapse for the latter two), and by demonstrating that model scaling is beneficial for robustness only when paired with appropriate class-balancing. The spectral imbalance analysis extends Kaushik et al. (ICML 2024) from class-level to group-level covariance matrices, revealing a more nuanced intra-class disparity pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard class-balancing techniques (upsampling, upweighting) can induce catastrophic WGA collapse with standard hyperparameters is a critical practical warning for practitioners who rely on these methods without extensive tuning. The proposed mixture balancing method offers a simple, efficient fix that outperforms both subsetting and upsampling on two of three imbalanced datasets, and the scaling results provide clear guidance that overparameterization helps robustness when combined with proper balancing. Open questions include why subsetting behaves differently from upsampling/upweighting in the empirical (finite-sample) setting despite theoretical equivalence in the population limit, and why interpolation behavior differs across datasets (Waterbirds/CelebA vs. CivilComments). The spectral imbalance finding suggests a new diagnostic tool for understanding group disparities but its causal role in WGA degradation remains unestablished.
