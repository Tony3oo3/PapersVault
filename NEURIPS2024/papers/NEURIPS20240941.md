## Problem

Existing model fusion methods like SWA and Model Soups rely on the assumption that flat loss minima correspond to flat metric minima, an assumption that holds in vision but breaks down for pre-trained language models where loss and metric landscapes are substantially misaligned. This causes uniform averaging to degrade metric performance, and loss-based weighted averaging to overfit to the validation set. Additionally, finding optimal fine-tuning hyperparameters for PLMs is computationally expensive, and no prior work has applied Bayesian optimization to the model fusion problem itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained PLM (RoBERTa-base, T5-base, LLaMA2-7B, or LLaMA3-8B), a downstream training set, and a validation set. It produces a single fused model whose weights are a convex combination of 15 checkpoints sampled from one training trajectory, along with the optimal hyperparameter set λ*. The fusion coefficients δ* are constrained to the simplex (δ_i ≥ 0, Σδ_i = 1), and the hyperparameter search operates on a lightweight proxy model (half-frozen layers or rank-4 LoRA) rather than the full model to reduce compute by up to 66%.

## Method

BOMF is a two-stage Bayesian optimization procedure. In Stage 1, standard BO with a GP surrogate and Log-Expected Improvement acquisition function searches for optimal fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) on a lightweight model, exploiting the empirical finding that optimal hyperparameters align across frozen-layer counts and LoRA ranks. In Stage 2, Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization using the qNEHVI algorithm (via BoTorch) searches for averaging coefficients δ* that simultaneously minimize the validation loss and K task-specific metrics, identifying a Pareto-optimal point rather than optimizing a single scalarized objective. The key insight is that in NLP, the loss surface and metric surface are misaligned (Spearman correlation ~0.14–0.23 vs. ~0.62–0.66 in vision), so optimizing δ on loss alone (as Learned SWA does) gets trapped in local minima; incorporating both loss and metrics as competing MOBO objectives enables the optimizer to navigate the complex, sharp metric landscape while the loss term provides a macroscopic regularizer against overfitting to the validation set.

## Results

On ROBERTa-base GLUE tasks, BOMF achieves 81.40% on RTE, 93.90 F1 on MRPC, 95.54% on SST-2, 93.50% on QNLI, 88.68 F1 on QQP, and 87.86% on MNLI, outperforming all baselines across every dataset. On T5-base SQuAD 2.0, BOMF reaches 81.82 F1 / 76.21 EM, surpassing the best baseline (Greedy SWA at 80.63/75.44). On LLaMA2-7B summarization (SAMSum), BOMF achieves 42.03 average ROUGE, and on Korean medical QA (KorMedMCQA) it reaches 51.16 average accuracy, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOMF consistently outperforms SWA (Izmailov et al., 2018) across all seven datasets, with the largest gap on MRPC (+1.66 F1) and SQuAD 2.0 (+1.51 F1), confirming that uniform averaging fails on PLMs due to loss-metric misalignment. Against OTFusion (Imfeld et al., 2023), BOMF wins on every metric by a wide margin (e.g., +4.32 on RTE, +1.07 F1 on SQuAD 2.0), showing that optimal-transport alignment does not address the core issue. Compared to Greedy SWA and Learned SWA (Wortsman et al., 2022), BOMF improves MRPC by +1.07 and +1.09 F1 respectively, and SQuAD 2.0 EM by +0.77 and +1.98, demonstrating that MOBO-based coefficient optimization surpasses both greedy selection and loss-only learned averaging. TWA (Li et al., 2023) is the closest competitor on SST-2 (tied at 95.54) but BOMF leads on all other tasks, particularly MRPC (+2.32 F1) and QQP (+0.83 F1). On KorMedMCQA, BOMF (51.16 avg.) outperforms the ICL baseline (Dong et al., 2023) by +5.15 points, reinforcing that fine-tuning with model fusion remains essential even for LLMs with strong in-context capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model fusion line of work initiated by SWA (Izmailov et al., 2018) and Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022), which demonstrated that weight-averaging multiple fine-tuned checkpoints improves generalization. The key departure is the identification of a fundamental loss-metric landscape misalignment in PLMs that undermines the flat-minimum rationale underpinning uniform averaging methods, which work well in vision but fail in NLP. To address this, the paper repurposes Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization (specifically qNEHVI from Daulton et al., 2021) as the first application of BO to model fusion, optimizing averaging coefficients over both loss and task metrics simultaneously rather than relying on gradient-based loss minimization alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The loss-metric misalignment finding (Spearman correlations of 0.14–0.23 in NLP vs. 0.62–0.66 in CV) provides a principled explanation for why simple averaging methods underperform in PLM fine-tuning, and the hyperparameter alignment observation offers a practical route to reducing BO search costs by up to 66% using lightweight proxy models. Open questions include a theoretical account of why hyperparameters transfer across frozen-layer and LoRA-rank configurations, and how to extend fusion to quantized low-rank methods (QLoRA) where discrete weight values disrupt continuous averaging. The approach is broadly applicable to any PLM fine-tuning pipeline but requires 75 MOBO evaluations, which may be prohibitive for very large model families.
