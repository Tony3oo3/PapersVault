## Problem

Machine learning methods for charge density prediction face a fundamental accuracy-efficiency trade-off: probe-based methods achieve high accuracy but require neural message passing over millions of grid points, while orbital-based methods are efficient but suffer from limited representational power of atom-centered basis functions. This paper identifies a recipe combining virtual orbitals, expressive learnable basis sets, and a high-capacity equivariant network to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy at 30× greater inference speed than prior methods.

## Input / Output

The model takes atomic element types and 3D coordinates as input and produces a charge density on a volumetric grid (~0.1 Å resolution, ~666K grid points per QM9 molecule). Rather than predicting scalar density values directly at each grid point, the model predicts coefficients and exponent scaling factors for a Gaussian-type orbital basis expansion; the final density is obtained by evaluating this expansion at all grid coordinates. Virtual nodes at bond midpoints are inserted as additional centers for the orbital expansion, and the entire pipeline preserves SE(3) equivariance.

## Method

The SCDP recipe combines three ingredients: (1) virtual orbitals placed at bond midpoints to capture interatomic electronic structure while maintaining SE(3) equivariance; (2) an even-tempered Gaussian basis derived from def2-QZVPPD with a tunable parameter β controlling basis set size, plus learnable positive scaling factors on orbital exponents to adapt to local environments; (3) an eSCN backbone (8 layers, L=6 tensor order) that reduces SO(3) convolution complexity from O(L⁶) to O(L³). The prediction head uses a fully connected tensor product layer to produce equivariant basis coefficients and invariant scaling factors (bounded to (0.5, 2) via a sigmoid parameterization). Training is two-stage: pretraining with fixed exponents followed by fine-tuning with a small learning rate to stabilize the scaling factor learning, using MAE loss over the charge density voxel.

## Results

On the QM9 charge density benchmark, the best SCDP model (eSCN+VO, K=8, L=6, β=1.3, with scaling factor fine-tuning) achieves a normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) of 0.178%, outperforming the state-of-the-art probe-based method ChargE3Net (0.196%) while being 31.7× faster (125.29 vs. 3.95 molecules/min on a single A100 GPU). The method offers flexible accuracy-efficiency trade-offs: a lighter configuration (eSCN, K=4, L=3, β=2.0) reaches 0.432% NMAE at 644 mol/min, while the most expressive model achieves 0.178% at 125 mol/min. On the MD and Cubic datasets, SCDP also significantly outperforms GPWNO and InfGCN (e.g., 2.59% vs. 7.69% NMAE on Cubic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDP outperforms all four baseline families on the QM9 benchmark. Against the state-of-the-art probe-based method ChargE3Net [15], SCDP achieves a lower NMAE (0.178% vs. 0.196%) while running 31.7× faster (125.29 vs. 3.95 mol/min). Against the other probe-based baselines e-DeepDFT and i-DeepDFT [7], SCDP reduces NMAE by 37% and 50% respectively. Against the orbital-based InfGCN [12], SCDP is both more accurate (0.178% vs. 0.869% NMAE) and faster (125 vs. 72 mol/min). Against GPWNO [22], SCDP achieves substantially lower error (0.178% vs. 0.73% on Split 2). On the MD and Cubic datasets, SCDP similarly dominates: on Cubic it reaches 2.59% NMAE versus 7.69% (GPWNO) and 8.98% (InfGCN), and on MD-ethanol it achieves 2.34% versus 4.00% and 8.43% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two previously separate lines of charge density prediction: orbital-based methods (GTO coefficient regression [9–13]) and probe-based methods (voxel message passing [7, 14–16]). It departs from prior orbital-based work by introducing virtual orbitals at bond midpoints, an even-tempered Gaussian basis with controllable expressivity, and learnable exponent scaling factors—addressing the limited expressivity that capped accuracy in earlier GTO approaches. For the prediction backbone, it adopts the eSCN architecture [17], which reduces SO(3) convolution complexity from O(L⁶) to O(L³), enabling higher tensor-order representations than prior equivariant models (PaiNN, Tensor Field Networks, MACE) used for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup over the prior SOTA (ChargE3Net) while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes ML-accelerated charge density prediction practically viable for larger systems and longer simulations, potentially bypassing the O(Nₑ³) Kohn-Sham self-consistent field loop. The flexible accuracy-efficiency Pareto front (up to 171× speedup with minor accuracy loss) is valuable for diverse applications with varying precision needs. Key open questions remain: effectiveness on crystalline materials is still limited, the training cost (12 GPU-days on 4×A100) is substantial, and practical validation via SCF convergence reduction or recovery of physical observables (energy, forces) has not yet been demonstrated.
