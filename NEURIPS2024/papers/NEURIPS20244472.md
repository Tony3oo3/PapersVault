## Problem

Situated understanding in 3D scenes remains underexplored due to the absence of scalable methods for collecting diverse multi-modal situational data. Existing benchmarks rely on single-modality (text-only) situation descriptions that introduce ambiguity in object referral, and prior datasets are limited in scale, question scope, and lack embodied navigation tasks. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a large-scale multi-modal situated reasoning dataset and benchmarks that require models to handle interleaved text, image, and point cloud inputs.

## Input / Output

The model receives an interleaved multi-modal sequence combining a text situation description (location, orientation, surrounding objects), optional ego-view images embedded in both the situation and question, a 3D scene point cloud (object-centric, 1024 points per object), and a text question that may also contain object images. The point cloud is transformed into the agent's egocentric frame (rotated and translated so the agent is at the origin facing +x). Outputs are open-ended text answers for MSQA (scored 1–5 by a GPT-based evaluator) or a single-step navigation action (move forward/backward, turn left/right) for MSNN.

## Method

The data pipeline generates 251K situated QA pairs by sampling viewpoints in real-world scenes (ScanNet, 3RScan, ARKitScenes), constructing situated scene graphs with viewpoint-adjusted spatial relations, prompting GPT-4V for object attributes and GPT-3.5 for QA generation, then applying refinement and answer-distribution balancing. The MSR3D model extends LEO with interleaved multi-modal input support: a frozen PointNet++ encodes object point clouds, a Spatial Transformer models pairwise spatial relations, a pretrained OpenCLIP ConvNeXt encodes 2D images, and a Vicuna-7B LLM (tuned via LoRA, rank 16) generates responses in a GPT-style auto-regressive prefix LM fashion. The key architectural insight is situation modeling via geometric transformation—rotating and translating all scene objects so the agent occupies a canonical pose—which outperforms alternative encodings (as object, as embedding, as cross-attention) in ablation studies. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on MSQA followed by task-specific fine-tuning, with the point cloud encoder frozen throughout.

## Results

On MSQA, MSR3D fine-tuned with text-only input and point cloud scene representation achieves the best overall correctness score of 56.48, slightly outperforming LEO fine-tuned (55.86) and substantially exceeding zero-shot LLMs (GPT-3.5: 50.65, GPT-4o: 49.68, Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 49.73). On MSNN, MSR3D with MSQA pre-training and interleaved multi-modal input reaches 48.4% accuracy, surpassing LEO with MSQA pre-training (37.05%) and LEO with its original pre-training data (31.44%). Removing the situation component causes a significant drop: MSR3D falls from 56.48 to 51.20 on MSQA and from 48.4 to 31.66 on MSNN, confirming the critical role of situation modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSR3D (FT, text-only input, point cloud) achieves 56.48 overall on MSQA, narrowly beating LEO fine-tuned (55.86) by 0.62 points, with the largest margin in spatial relationships (48.91 vs. 46.88) and navigation (61.89 vs. 56.82). All three zero-shot LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) cluster around 49–51 overall but show a pronounced weakness in spatial reasoning (27–32) compared to fine-tuned models (43–49), indicating that general-purpose LLMs lack grounded 3D spatial understanding. On MSNN, MSR3D with MSQA pre-training (48.4%) substantially outperforms LEO with the same MSQA pre-training (37.05%) by 11.35 points, and LEO with its original LEO-align pre-training (31.44%) by 16.96 points, demonstrating both the value of MSQA as a pre-training source and the effectiveness of MSR3D's situation modeling. The ablation removing the situation component reveals the largest performance gap on MSNN (48.4 → 31.66, a 16.74-point drop), confirming that situation awareness is the dominant factor for navigation rather than scene representation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the situated question answering line initiated by SQA3D [41], which introduced location- and orientation-conditioned QA in real-world 3D scenes but relied on expensive human annotation and text-only situation descriptions. Architecturally, it builds on LEO [28] (an embodied generalist agent for 3D scenes) by adding interleaved text-image input support and a situation modeling mechanism that normalizes the point cloud to the agent's egocentric frame. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D VL work is the scalable LLM-assisted data generation pipeline using situated scene graphs, which replaces both simulated environments and manual annotation, and the introduction of an interleaved multi-modal input format (text + image + point cloud) to resolve object-referral ambiguity that plagues text-only situation descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSQA (251K pairs across 1,734 scenes) and MSNN (34K samples) provide the first large-scale, multi-modal situated reasoning benchmarks, filling a critical gap between 3D VL understanding and embodied AI evaluation. The finding that interleaved images in questions actually degrade performance (−12.83 pts) reveals that current models lack the grounding ability to exploit visual cues in queries, pointing to a concrete capability gap. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated data that may not fully align with human preference, coverage of only three scene datasets, and evaluation restricted to QA and single-step navigation. Open questions include whether human-in-the-loop data generation can close the quality gap, whether the interleaved format generalizes to video or multi-step navigation, and whether the scaling trends observed here hold at 10× data scale.
