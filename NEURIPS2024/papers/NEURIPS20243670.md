## Problem

Standard LLM alignment via SFT and RL/DPO inadvertently increases hallucination: SFT on human-labeled responses introduces knowledge unfamiliar to the pre-trained model, while RL reward functions favor longer, more detailed outputs that contain more false claims. No prior work addresses how to make the alignment process itself more factual while preserving instruction-following capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes natural language instructions (from the Open Assistant seed data and 20K augmented instructions) and, for factuality reward computation, retrieved Wikipedia passages (top-20 via DRAGON+ re-ranked by a cross-encoder). It first classifies each instruction as fact-based or not using the SFT model, then produces text responses. The factuality reward model decomposes responses into atomic facts and verifies each against retrieved supports, yielding a 0–1 proportion-of-correct-facts score used to construct preference pairs.

## Method

FLAME comprises two stages. In factuality-aware SFT (SFT*), for fact-based instructions the training responses are sampled from the pre-trained LLM itself (10 samples with 5-shot demonstration retrieved via DRAGON+), avoiding the introduction of external knowledge; non-fact-based instructions retain human-created responses. In factuality-aware DPO (DPO*), a separate retrieval-augmented factuality reward model (RM^fact) — built from atomic fact decomposition with a fine-tuned Llama-2 7B and claim verification with Instruct Llama 7B over 10 retrieved supports — selects positive/negative response pairs for fact-based instructions, which are jointly trained with standard instruction-following preference pairs. The key insight from the pilot study is that fine-tuning on the LLM's own generations (rather than RAG-augmented or human-written responses) reduces hallucination, and that identifying fact-based instructions is essential to avoid degrading instruction-following on creative or non-factual tasks.

## Results

FLAME (SFT* + DPO*) achieves a 51.2% win rate on Alpaca Eval (comparable to the standard SFT + DPO baseline at 50.0%) while substantially improving factuality: +5.6 FACTSCORE points on Biography (47.9 vs 42.3), +7.1 on Alpaca Fact (48.7 vs 41.6), and +6.0 on FAVA (58.9 vs 52.9). The factuality-aware SFT stage alone (SFT*) already improves Biography FACTSCORE from 44.7 to 49.5 over standard SFT, and the full FLAME pipeline further reduces erroneous facts (e.g., 28.5 vs 35.0 on Bio). On TruthfulQA, FLAME improves BLEU from 0.03 (SFT + DPO) to 0.20 and ROUGE from 0.54 to 0.96, confirming gains in truthfulness for long-form generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama-2-Chat 70B (the only external baseline), FLAME trades instruction-following win rate (51.2% vs 66.2% on Alpaca Eval) for substantially better factuality: Biography FACTSCORE improves from 33.2 to 47.9 (+14.7), Alpaca Fact from 39.3 to 48.7 (+9.4), and FAVA from 47.5 to 58.9 (+11.4), while also reducing erroneous facts on Bio from 43.6 to 28.5. Internally, FLAME (SFT* + DPO*) matches the standard SFT + DPO alignment on Alpaca Eval win rate (51.2% vs 50.0%) while gaining +5.6 FACTSCORE on Bio, +7.1 on Alpaca Fact, and +6.0 on FAVA, demonstrating that factuality-aware alignment does not sacrifice helpfulness. The factuality-only variant (SFT + DPO^fact) achieves comparable factuality gains but drops Alpaca Eval win rate to 40.8%, confirming that jointly optimizing both instruction-following and factuality preference data is necessary to avoid the trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard two-stage alignment pipeline (SFT + DPO) established by Ouyang et al. and the self-rewarding framework of Yuan et al., using DPO as the preference optimization mechanism and FACTSCORE as the factuality signal. It departs from prior factuality-focused alignment work (e.g., Tian et al., 2024) by simultaneously preserving instruction-following capability rather than optimizing factuality in isolation, and from RAG-based approaches by showing that external knowledge injection during alignment is counterproductive. The key conceptual departure is the finding that fine-tuning on more factual but unfamiliar data (RAG-generated responses) actively degrades a model's factuality, inverting the standard assumption that higher-quality supervision always improves the student model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that conventional alignment (SFT on human-labeled data + RL with length-biased rewards) systematically encourages hallucination reframes how the community should think about the alignment process itself, not just post-hoc hallucination mitigation. The practical implication—that one should elicit the model's own parametric knowledge rather than inject external facts during fine-tuning—offers a concrete, low-cost strategy for alignment pipelines. Open questions include whether the factuality–helpfulness trade-off persists at larger scale, how to extend the two-skill-set framework (instruction following + factuality) to the full alignment skill taxonomy, and whether the Wikipedia-only factuality reward generalizes to open-ended or debatable claims.
