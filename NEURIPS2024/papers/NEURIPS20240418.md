## Problem

Reconstruction-based model-based RL world models waste finite capacity on highly predictable but policy-irrelevant distractors, degrading the modeling of the dynamics that actually matter for action selection. Existing distraction-avoidance methods (structural regularizations, contrastive prototypes, value-equivalent modeling) encode assumptions that distractors are uncorrelated with actions or reward, and fail when distractors are perfectly learnable yet useless. The paper introduces the Reafferent Deepmind Control benchmark, where backgrounds are deterministic functions of the previous action and elapsed time, to stress-test whether any method can suppress learnable-but-irrelevant content.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 64×64×3 RGB observations and a sequence of past continuous actions, and outputs a continuous action vector. All modifications are confined to the world-model training loop of an otherwise unaltered DreamerV3 agent; at inference the standard DreamerV3 rollout and policy are used unchanged. During data collection, each image is pre-segmented by SAM (prompted with a 256-point grid, filtered by IoU and stability score, then non-maximum suppressed) and the resulting object masks are stored in the replay buffer for use during training.

## Method

PSP modifies DreamerV3's world-model training with three synergistic components. First, the scalar sum of the policy's action output is differentiated with respect to every input image pixel, producing a per-pixel salience map that upweights the image reconstruction loss where the policy is sensitive (extending VaGraM's value-gradient idea to the image domain and to policy gradients). Second, to combat the well-known noisiness of raw gradient saliency, the per-pixel weights are averaged within each SAM segmentation mask, yielding an object-level importance score that is then linearly interpolated (α=0.9) with a uniform weight to preserve baseline reconstruction. Third, a small MLP head is trained to predict the previous action from the stop-gradient image embedding; its gradient is subtracted (scaled by ε=1e3) from the encoder's update, implementing an efference-copy-inspired adversarial objective that forces the latent state to source action information solely from the RSSM's explicit action input rather than redundantly from the image. Together these components let the world model focus reconstruction capacity on policy-relevant objects while suppressing both external distractors and self-generated sensory consequences.

## Results

On the novel Reafferent Deepmind Control benchmark (where distracting backgrounds are complex but fully predictable from the agent's previous action), PSP achieves 383.1±23.8 on Cheetah Run and 128.5±215.7 on Hopper Stand, dramatically outperforming all MBRL baselines (DreamerV3: 158.4/4.6; DreamerPro: 39.7/3.8; TIA: 200.4/0.9; dMDP: 6.7/1.7). On the standard unmodified DeepMind Control Suite, PSP maintains competitive performance (712.3±32.3 Cheetah Run, 865.6±53.6 Hopper Stand), confirming no trade-off between distraction robustness and standard performance. On the Distracting Control Suite, PSP (408.6/417.7) outperforms DreamerV3 (243.8/173.7) on both tasks, though the model-free DrQv2 baseline remains competitive or superior on several settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3 (the base architecture), PSP achieves roughly 2.4× improvement on Reafferent Cheetah Run (383.1 vs 158.4) and ~28× on Reafferent Hopper Stand (128.5 vs 4.6), while matching or slightly exceeding DreamerV3 on unmodified DMC (712.3 vs 521.1 Cheetah; 865.6 vs 867.4 Hopper). Against DreamerPro, PSP wins decisively on Reafferent Control (383.1 vs 39.7 Cheetah; 128.5 vs 3.8 Hopper) and on Distracting Cheetah Run (408.6 vs 179.1), but DreamerPro retains an edge on Distracting Hopper Stand (561.8 vs 417.7) and unmodified Cheetah Run (908.4 vs 712.3). Against TIA, PSP outperforms on Reafferent Cheetah (383.1 vs 200.4, with TIA showing extreme variance ±203.9) and both Distracting tasks, while TIA edges ahead on Distracting Cheetah Run (548.5 vs 408.6) with high variance. Against dMDP, PSP dominates on Reafferent Control (383.1 vs 6.7 Cheetah; 128.5 vs 1.7 Hopper) and Distracting Hopper (417.7 vs 13.2), with dMDP competitive only on Distracting Cheetah (397.4 vs 408.6). The model-free DrQv2 baseline remains the strongest overall performer on Reafferent Cheetah Run (565.1 vs 383.1) and Distracting Hopper (781.1 vs 417.7), but PSP is the best MBRL method across all six task-environment combinations, particularly on the novel Reafferent benchmark where all MBRL baselines collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSP extends the DreamerV3 world-model architecture by modifying only its training loop, drawing on three distinct lineages: (1) VaGraM's gradient-weighted reconstruction loss, generalized here from value gradients on low-dimensional states to policy gradients on high-dimensional images; (2) model-explainability/saliency-map techniques, repurposed as a training signal and denoised via SAM segmentation; and (3) domain-adversarial training (Ganin et al.), adapted into an efference-copy-inspired head that strips self-action information from the image embedding. The paper positions itself against structural-regularization methods (DreamerPro, TIA, Denoised MDP) that encode priors about distractor form, arguing instead for a learning-based approach that identifies task-relevant content from the policy's own gradient signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that learnable-but-useless distractions are more damaging to MBRL than unlearnable noise (because the world model successfully models them, exhausting capacity) reframes the distraction problem and exposes a blind spot in all existing regularization-based methods. The Reafferent DMC benchmark provides a useful stress test for future MBRL methods. Key open questions include whether PSP's object-centric assumption and SAM dependency generalize to continuous video or high-resolution settings, how it adapts when reward structure or salient features shift over time, and whether the biological efference-copy framing can yield further algorithmic insights. The high variance on harder tasks (Hopper Stand) and limited task diversity (only Cheetah Run and Hopper Stand) leave the method's robustness to more complex control problems uncertain.
