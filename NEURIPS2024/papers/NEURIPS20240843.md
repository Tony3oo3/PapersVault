## Problem

Reconstruction-based unsupervised OOD detection faces a fundamental dilemma: generative models must simultaneously compress the ID latent space to capture exclusive in-distribution characteristics while retaining enough reconstruction power for diverse ID categories. Existing pixel-level diffusion-based OOD methods (DDPM, DiffGuard, LMD) address this by modeling the full image distribution, but they incur high training and inference costs and reconstruct low-level pixel details irrelevant to semantic understanding, making ID/OOD separation less effective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (3×w×h) and passes it through a frozen pre-trained encoder (EfficientNet-b4 or ResNet50) to extract multi-layer feature maps, which are globally average-pooled, z-score normalized, and concatenated into a single 720-dimensional (EfficientNet-b4) or 1792-dimensional (ResNet50) vector. The output is a scalar OOD score computed via one of three metrics—MSE reconstruction error, Likelihood Regret (difference in MSE between initial and final training epochs), or Multi-layer Feature Similarity (negative mean cosine similarity between original and reconstructed per-layer features)—which is thresholded to yield an ID/OOD decision. Inference runs at approximately 999 images/second on a single GPU, roughly 100× faster than pixel-level DDPM baselines.

## Method

The core contribution is a Latent Feature Diffusion Network (LFDN) that performs denoising diffusion entirely in the multi-layer semantic feature space rather than pixel space. During training, Gaussian noise is added to the concatenated multi-layer feature vector z_0 at a random timestep t, and the LFDN (16 residual blocks with GroupNorm, SiLU activations, and an MLP for time-step embedding) is trained with MSE loss to predict the clean features from the noisy input. At test time, DDIM sampling with a skipping-step stride efficiently reconstructs the features from a single noisy observation, and the reconstruction quality serves as the OOD signal. The multi-layer feature extraction strategy (stages 1–5 of the encoder) is key: it forces the ID latent representation to be compact across complementary semantic levels, so the diffusion model reconstructs ID features well while failing on OOD features, producing a clear separation in reconstruction error or per-layer cosine similarity.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 as ID data, the proposed method with MFsim achieves an average AUROC of 99.34% across six OOD datasets (SVHN, LSUN-c, LSUN-r, iSUN, Textures, Places365), outperforming the best classification-based method DICE (97.44%) by 1.90%. On CIFAR-100 as ID, the method reaches 97.20% average AUROC, surpassing DICE (83.72%) by 13.48%. Inference speed is approximately 960.6 images/s for MSE and MFsim metrics, comparable to classification-based methods and significantly faster than pixel-level diffusion (DDPM at ~11.4 img/s, roughly 100× slower). On CelebA as ID, the method achieves 99.98% average AUROC with MFsim, exceeding VAE (95.18%) and DDPM (80.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-level generative baselines, the proposed method dominates: on CIFAR-10, MFsim achieves 99.7% average AUROC versus VAE (90.6%), DDPM (78.2%), GLOW (64.9%), and PixelCNN++ (84.2%), while running ~100× faster than DDPM (999.3 vs 11.4 img/s). Against classification-based methods (MSP, EBO, DICE, ASH-S), the method wins on both CIFAR-10 (99.34% vs best 97.44% for DICE) and CIFAR-100 (97.20% vs best 83.72% for DICE), with the gap widening substantially for the harder CIFAR-100 setting. Against distance-based methods (SimCLR+Mahalanobis, SimCLR+KNN), the advantage is even more pronounced, e.g., 97.20% vs 62.44% (SimCLR+KNN) on CIFAR-100. Against the diffusion-based DiffGuard [Gao et al., 2023], the method achieves 1.55 higher average AUROC (92.74% vs 91.19%) and 21.77 lower FPR95 (27.01% vs 48.78%) on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100/TinyImageNet. Against LMD [Liu et al., 2023], the method achieves 6.94 higher average AUROC (85.74% vs 78.80%) across CIFAR-10/100 ID settings. The method does not lose to any baseline on any reported metric, though the margin over classification-based methods is smallest on CIFAR-10 (≈2%) and largest on CIFAR-100 (≈13–35%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion model framework (DDPM/DDIM) from pixel-level image reconstruction to multi-layer semantic feature reconstruction for OOD detection, drawing conceptual inspiration from Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., 2022). It departs from prior diffusion-based OOD methods (Graham et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) that operate on raw pixels, and from earlier generative-based approaches (VAE, GAN, AE) that struggle to balance reconstruction fidelity with compact ID representation. The key architectural novelty is the LFDN—a 16-block residual network operating on concatenated multi-layer encoder features—combined with a layer-wise cosine similarity metric (MFsim) that exploits the structured nature of the feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~100× inference speedup over pixel-level DDPM while achieving substantially higher AUROC, making diffusion-based OOD detection viable for real-time deployment. The finding that feature-level reconstruction is markedly more discriminative than pixel-level reconstruction for separating ID from OOD samples provides a clear design principle for future generative OOD detectors. However, the method's performance is tightly coupled to the quality of the pretrained encoder, and the near-perfect results on CIFAR-10 benchmarks (AUROC > 99%) may not generalize to more challenging real-world distributions where ID and OOD categories overlap semantically, as the failure case analysis acknowledges.
