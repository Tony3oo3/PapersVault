## Problem

Existing deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for dynamic scene rendering suffer from two critical issues: (1) canonical 3D Gaussians synthesized from multi-frame dynamic videos contain significant coordinate noise that propagates into the deformable network, degrading deformation accuracy; and (2) there is no adequate mechanism for aggregating 4D spatial-temporal information, and direct spatial aggregation on noisy canonical coordinates would further amplify the noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video frames with known camera poses and timesteps, initializing canonical 3D Gaussians from COLMAP point clouds (sparse SFM or dense MVS). It outputs per-frame deformed 3D Gaussian parameters (position, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients) rendered via differentiable rasterization at real-time speed (15 FPS on PlenopticVideo at 1352×1014). The two-stage deformation produces intermediate denoised coordinates that serve as input to the second deformation network, making the pipeline inherently sequential in its coordinate refinement.

## Method

DN-4DGS introduces a Noise Suppression Strategy (NSS) that performs two sequential deformations: the first maps canonical (x,y,z,t) to offsets (Δx,Δy,Δz), and the second operates on the modified coordinates (x+Δx, y+Δy, z+Δz), effectively altering the coordinate distribution to suppress noise before spatial feature aggregation. A Decoupled Temporal-Spatial Aggregation Module (DTS) splits aggregation across stages: temporal aggregation (merging features from t−1, t, t+1 via max-pooling, inspired by PointNet++) is embedded in the first deformation, while KNN-based spatial aggregation is deferred to the second deformation where coordinates are already denoised. Training follows a two-stage protocol—only the first deformation is supervised initially, and the second is activated once the first produces sufficiently accurate coordinates—using a combined L1 + D-SSIM + grid-based TV loss. A learnable per-Gaussian embedding Y_i provides coordinate-independent information to complement the aggregated features.

## Results

On PlenopticVideo, DN-4DGS achieves the best PSNR (32.02 dB) and competitive LPIPS (0.043), outperforming 4DGaussian (31.15 dB) by 0.87 dB while maintaining real-time rendering at 15 FPS. On HyperNeRF with Dense Init, the method reaches 25.59 dB PSNR, surpassing 4DGaussian (25.20 dB) and D3DGS (23.43 dB). On NeRF-DS, DN-4DGS attains 24.36 dB PSNR and 0.865 SSIM, leading all baselines including D3DGS (23.92 dB) and NeRF-DS (23.60 dB). Training time is 50 minutes on PlenopticVideo with 112 MB storage, slightly higher than 4DGaussian (40 min, 90 MB) due to the two-stage deformation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DN-4DGS outperforms all Gaussian-based baselines on PlenopticVideo: it beats 4DGaussian [11] by +0.87 dB PSNR (32.02 vs 31.15), E-D3DGS [43] by +0.71 dB, and 4DGS [51] by +0.83 dB, while also improving LPIPS over 4DGaussian (0.043 vs 0.049). Against NeRF-based methods, it surpasses HexPlane-all [15] (+0.32 dB), KPlanes [34] (+0.39 dB), HyperReel [36] (+0.92 dB), NeRFPlayer [35] (+1.33 dB), and DyNeRF [1] (+2.44 dB) in PSNR, though HexPlane achieves higher SSIM (0.984 vs 0.944). On HyperNeRF, the advantage is most pronounced under Sparse Init where DN-4DGS gains +1.51 dB over 4DGaussian (23.31 vs 21.80) and +1.81 dB over D3DGS [13] (23.31 vs 21.50); under Dense Init it still leads 4DGaussian by +0.39 dB and D3DGS by +2.16 dB. On NeRF-DS, DN-4DGS leads D3DGS by +0.44 dB PSNR and NeRF-DS [47] by +0.76 dB. On D-NeRF, the method improves 4DGaussian by +0.48 dB and D3DGS by +0.36 dB while using fewer parameters than D3DGS (0.39M vs 0.52M). The trade-off is a modest FPS reduction (15 vs 30 for 4DGaussian) and slightly higher storage (112 vs 90 MB) due to the additional learnable embedding Y_i and second deformation network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical 3D Gaussians + deformable network paradigm established by 4DGaussian (HexPlane-based feature encoding) and D3DGS (MLP-based feature encoding) for dynamic scene rendering. Its key conceptual departure is identifying that canonical Gaussians synthesized from multi-frame dynamic scenes carry significant coordinate noise that propagates into the deformation field, and addressing this through a two-stage deformation (Noise Suppression Strategy) that first corrects the coordinate distribution before performing spatial aggregation. The Decoupled Temporal-Spatial Aggregation Module draws on PointNet++-style set abstraction for temporal neighbor pooling and KNN-based spatial aggregation, but deliberately separates these operations across the two deformation stages to avoid amplifying noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-in-canonical-Gaussians problem is a shared weakness across the entire canonical+deformable Gaussian splatting family, so the two-stage denoising strategy offers a practical fix that could be adopted by subsequent methods in this line. The paper achieves consistent SOTA or near-SOTA results across four datasets while maintaining real-time rendering (15 FPS), though at roughly half the speed of 4DGaussian due to the extra deformation stage. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the lack of simultaneous supervision across both deformation stages, which makes the second-stage coordinate direction partially uncontrollable; exploring joint or end-to-end supervision is a natural next step.
