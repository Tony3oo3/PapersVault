## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient continual learning methods for LLMs, such as O-LoRA, focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting through orthogonal subspace learning but largely neglect forward knowledge transfer from previously learned tasks to new tasks. The paper addresses the question of how to effectively inject parametric knowledge from prior tasks into new tasks to improve generalization while simultaneously maintaining orthogonality of each task's low-rank subspaces to minimize forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained T5 encoder-decoder model and processes a sequential stream of text classification tasks, consuming text sentences/paragraphs as input and producing a class label as output. A small set of seed samples (8 by default) from the new task is used to compute sensitivity scores that weight the knowledge transfer. Unlike standard LoRA, updates are parameterized as SVD-based low-rank triplets (singular values with paired left and right singular vectors) that accumulate additively across tasks, so the final model is the pre-trained weights plus the sum of all task-specific SVD adapters.

## Method

LB-CL operates in two stages. First, it computes sensitivity scores for each SVD triplet of previous tasks using seed samples from the new task, then initializes the new task's SVD parameters as a sensitivity-weighted aggregation of prior tasks' triplets, enabling targeted parametric knowledge transfer where higher-scoring (more relevant) triplets contribute more. Second, during training, it projects the new task's gradients onto the subspaces spanned by previously learned triplets and subtracts these projections, forcing the new task to learn in directions orthogonal to prior tasks and thereby minimizing interference. The SVD-based parameterization with an orthogonality regularizer on U and V matrices (following AdaLoRA) distinguishes this from LoRA-based approaches, and the combination of sensitivity-weighted initialization for forward transfer with orthogonal gradient projection for forgetting mitigation is the core architectural contribution.

## Results

On the standard 5-task CL benchmark (T5-large), LB-CL achieves 76.7% average accuracy across three task orders, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art O-LoRA (75.4%) by 1.3 points and approaching the multi-task learning upper bound of 80.0%. On the more challenging 15-task benchmark, LB-CL reaches 69.2% average accuracy, slightly surpassing O-LoRA (68.8%) while remaining below ProgPrompt (78.4%), which relies on task IDs at inference. LB-CL also outperforms LFPT5 (71.3%), L2P (59.8%), EWC (47.9%), and LwF (51.3%) on the standard benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LB-CL consistently outperforms O-LoRA (the prior state-of-the-art for PET-based CL) on the standard 5-task benchmark across all three task orders (76.9/76.5/76.8 vs. 74.9/75.3/75.9), with a 1.3-point average improvement. It also surpasses LFPT5 by 5.4 points (76.7 vs. 71.3) and L2P by 16.9 points (76.7 vs. 59.8) on the same benchmark. On the 15-task benchmark, LB-CL (69.2 avg) marginally beats O-LoRA (68.8 avg) but falls well short of ProgPrompt (78.4 avg); however, ProgPrompt requires task IDs at inference, limiting its generalization to unseen tasks, whereas LB-CL does not. LB-CL's performance on the standard benchmark (76.7) is within 3.3 points of the MTL upper bound (80.0) and exceeds PerTaskFT (70.0), indicating effective knowledge transfer across tasks. Among regularization-based baselines, EWC (47.9) and LwF (51.3) are substantially outperformed, as are replay-based methods like Replay (53.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

LB-CL unifies two previously separate threads in parameter-efficient continual learning: the orthogonal subspace learning of O-LoRA [43] (which mitigates forgetting by training new tasks in subspaces orthogonal to prior ones) and the sensitivity-based parametric knowledge transfer of Zhong et al. [53] (which extracts task-specific parameters from a teacher and injects them into a student). The SVD-based low-rank parameterization is inherited from AdaLoRA [50], and the gradient projection mechanism follows Farajtabar et al. [7]. The key departure from O-LoRA is the addition of a sensitivity-weighted initialization step that explicitly promotes forward transfer, while the departure from [53] is the embedding of that transfer within a CL loop with orthogonality constraints rather than a one-shot teacher-student setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful three-term decomposition of per-task generalization error (forgetting, fine-tuning improvement, initialization quality) that clarifies where different CL components act, and demonstrates that sensitivity-guided initialization plus orthogonal gradient projection can simultaneously improve both forward transfer and forgetting in a single SVD-based adapter. However, the evaluation is limited to T5 encoder-decoder models on text classification, no code is released, and the margins over O-LoRA are modest (~1–2 points). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to decoder-only LLMs, generation tasks, or longer task sequences where the accumulated SVD adapters may become unwieldy, and whether the sensitivity scores remain informative under large domain shifts.
