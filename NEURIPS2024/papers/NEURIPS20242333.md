## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models for human preference alignment is limited by two bottlenecks: standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) does not directly optimize for preference and only supports a single training round, while RL-based methods (e.g., Diffusion-DPO) require paired winner/loser images per prompt that are often unavailable in practice. This paper asks whether a diffusion model can iteratively self-improve using only single image-text pairs, without external reward models or preference pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-quality SFT dataset of single image-text pairs (e.g., winner images from Pick-a-Pic) at 512×512 resolution, along with a pretrained diffusion model (SD-1.5). It produces a fine-tuned diffusion model that, given a text prompt, generates images with improved human-preference alignment and aesthetic quality. Unlike Diffusion-DPO, no 'loser' images or reward model are required; the only additional input is the previous iteration's model checkpoint, which serves as the self-play opponent.

## Method

SPIN-Diffusion formulates fine-tuning as a general-sum minimax game over full diffusion trajectories: a main player (test function) learns to distinguish real forward-process trajectories from those generated by the opponent (the previous iteration's model), while the opponent's closed-form optimal response is simply the current model parameters. The key technical challenge is that the SPIN objective requires the marginal probability p_θ(x₀|c), which is intractable for diffusion models; the authors resolve this by working with the joint trajectory distribution p_θ(x₀:T|c) and decomposing the log-likelihood via DDIM's backward process into a sum over per-step denoising losses. An exact objective (Eq. 4.8) sums over all T intermediate steps, while a computationally efficient approximate objective (Eq. 4.9) applies Jensen's inequality to reduce the computation to two consecutive steps (t−1, t), eliminating the need to store the full trajectory. The resulting gradient decomposes into a 'Matching' term (analogous to standard denoising score matching on real data) and a 'Pushing' term (moving the model away from its previous iteration's outputs), modulated by a reweighting factor from the convex loss. Theoretically, the algorithm converges when the model matches the target data distribution, and no further SFT improvement is possible at the stationary point.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic test set, SPIN-Diffusion at iteration 3 achieves the best scores across all four metrics: 22.00 PickScore, 0.2759 HPS, 1.1239 ImageReward, and 6.2481 Aesthetic, yielding an average of 7.4126. Already at iteration 1, SPIN-Diffusion surpasses SFT in Aesthetic score (6.12 vs. 5.95) and PickScore (21.67 vs. 21.45), and by iteration 2 it outperforms Diffusion-DPO (reproduced) on every metric. The winning rate over the SD-1.5 base model reaches 91.6% at iteration 3, and over Diffusion-DPO (reproduced) reaches 70.2% overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SD-1.5 base model, SPIN-Diffusion-Iter3 improves PickScore by 0.80 (22.00 vs. 21.20), Aesthetic by 0.48 (6.25 vs. 5.77), and achieves a 91.6% winning rate, far exceeding SFT's 73.2% and Diffusion-DPO (reproduced)'s 84.8%. Against Diffusion-DPO (reproduced), which requires paired winner/loser images per prompt, SPIN-Diffusion-Iter3 wins on all four metrics: PickScore 22.00 vs. 21.89, Aesthetic 6.25 vs. 5.89, HPS 0.2759 vs. 0.2753, and ImageReward 1.1239 vs. 1.0495, with a 70.2% overall winning rate. Notably, SPIN-Diffusion achieves these gains using only single image-text pairs (no loser images), making it applicable to datasets where paired preference data is unavailable. On the broader PartiPrompts and HPSv2 benchmarks, SPIN-Diffusion-Iter2 and Iter3 similarly outperform both SFT and Diffusion-DPO, confirming generalization beyond the Pick-a-Pic distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the self-play fine-tuning (SPIN) mechanism originally developed for LLMs (Chen et al., 2024) to diffusion models, formulating a general-sum minimax game over full diffusion trajectories rather than marginal image distributions. It departs from RL-based fine-tuning methods (DDPO, DPOK, Diffusion-DPO) that require paired winner/loser images per prompt, and from standard SFT which performs only a single training round. The key technical contribution is decomposing the trajectory-level log-likelihood into per-step score-matching terms via the DDIM parameterization, yielding a computationally tractable objective that only requires two consecutive sampling steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPIN-Diffusion makes preference-aligned fine-tuning accessible in the common setting where only one image per prompt is available, eliminating the data bottleneck that limits RL-based methods. The theoretical result that SFT cannot improve beyond SPIN-Diffusion's stationary point provides a principled stopping criterion. However, the method is fundamentally a distribution-matching algorithm and cannot exceed the quality of the underlying SFT data, and the additional sampling overhead for generating opponent trajectories remains a practical cost. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger models (SDXL, Flux), whether LoRA-compatible variants can match full fine-tuning, and whether sampling acceleration techniques can reduce the iterative cost.
