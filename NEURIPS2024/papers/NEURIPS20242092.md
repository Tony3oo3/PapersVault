## Problem

In communication-constrained federated learning with non-IID data, only a small fraction of clients can participate each round, making client selection critical. Existing clustering-based sampling methods (e.g., Clustered Sampling, DivFL) treat all clients equally regardless of whether their local data is class-balanced or imbalanced, and either incur high computational cost from processing full-dimensional gradients or fail to adapt to heterogeneous heterogeneity profiles across clients.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the C-dimensional bias update vector of the fully-connected output layer (not the full model or full gradient), the current global model parameters, and the target number of clients K to sample. It produces a selected client set S^t of size K for each round and an updated global model via uniform averaging of selected clients' local models. The heterogeneity estimate is computed as the Shannon entropy of a temperature-scaled softmax of the bias update, requiring no access to local data or label distributions.

## Method

HiCS-FL estimates each client's data heterogeneity from the expected local update of the output-layer bias, which the paper shows is linearly related to the client's label distribution (Eq. 6). A novel distance metric combines cosine similarity of bias updates with a weighted absolute difference in estimated heterogeneity (Eq. 9), enabling clusters that group clients with similar imbalance levels. Clients are then sampled via a two-level hierarchical procedure: clusters are selected with probability proportional to exp(γ·H̄_m) where H̄_m is the cluster's average heterogeneity, and within a cluster, clients are sampled proportionally to their data-size weights. An annealing schedule on γ starts high (prioritizing balanced-data clusters to accelerate early convergence) and decays to zero (reducing to uniform cluster sampling to avoid overfitting to a small subset). The paper provides a convergence bound (Theorem 3.4) showing that the sampling-induced variance term is governed by the weighted heterogeneity of selected clients, and empirically validates the gradient-dissimilarity assumption (Assumption 3.1) via scatter plots on FMNIST and CIFAR-10.

## Results

HiCS-FL achieves the fastest convergence across all four datasets, requiring 60 rounds to reach 75% accuracy on FMNIST (2.5× speedup over random sampling), 123 rounds for 60% on CIFAR-10 (7.3× speedup), 86 rounds for 50% on Mini-ImageNet (2.2× speedup), and 27 rounds for 80% on THUC News (3.1× speedup). On THUC News, HiCS-FL reaches 83.2% test accuracy in setting (1), outperforming the best baseline FedCor (81.2%) by 2 percentage points. The method also exhibits the lowest training loss variance, with notably smoother loss trajectories compared to all competing schemes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCS-FL outperforms all four baselines (pow-d, Clustered Sampling, DivFL, and FedCor) on every dataset and every heterogeneity setting tested. The most dramatic advantage is on CIFAR-10, where HiCS-FL achieves a 7.3× speedup over random sampling while the next-best method (FedCor) manages only 1.3×; DivFL is actually slower than random (0.6×) on CIFAR-10. On FMNIST, HiCS-FL's 2.5× speedup exceeds pow-d (1.8×) and FedCor (1.7×), while DivFL is 3× slower than random. On THUC News, HiCS-FL reaches 83.2% accuracy in setting (1) versus 81.2% for FedCor and 80.6% for CS, and maintains a 3.1× speedup in convergence rounds. DivFL consistently underperforms across all datasets (0.3×–0.6× speedup), likely due to its computationally expensive submodular selection over full gradient vectors. The paper notes that HiCS-FL's advantage is most pronounced when a subset of clients has balanced data (settings 1 and 2), consistent with its heterogeneity-guided sampling design.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCS-FL extends the clustered sampling paradigm (CS [11], DivFL [2]) by replacing full-gradient cosine similarity with a lightweight distance metric computed solely from output-layer bias updates, augmented with an entropy-based heterogeneity term. It departs from prior heterogeneity-aware selection methods (Fed-CBS [36], FedCor [28]) that require validation losses or Gaussian process modeling by deriving data-imbalance estimates directly from the structural properties of cross-entropy gradients. The annealed importance-weighted cluster sampling generalizes the uniform per-cluster sampling of CS, reducing to it when heterogeneity levels are homogeneous across clusters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that heterogeneity estimation requires only C-dimensional bias vectors rather than full model updates, making the method scalable to large networks without proportional communication overhead. However, the theoretical convergence analysis is restricted to FedAvg with SGD, and the extension to Adam-based optimizers (used in most experiments) is argued but not formally proven. Open questions include behavior under system heterogeneity (variable compute/bandwidth), interaction with personalization objectives, and whether the Dirichlet-based non-IID generation adequately captures real-world data skew at scale.
