## Problem

Existing LLM routing methods like ZOOTER train the router by minimizing KL divergence between the router's output and softmax-normalized LLM scores. This fails when multiple LLMs perform well for a query, because softmax normalization compresses the top scores into small, near-uniform values that provide a weak supervision signal. The paper seeks a training objective that can handle the common case where several LLMs are equally suitable for a given query.

## Input / Output

The router receives a single text query (e.g., a math word problem or multiple-choice question) and outputs a selection probability over T candidate LLMs via cosine similarity between the encoded query and learnable LLM embedding vectors (768-dim). During training, LLM scores are computed by beam sampling (M=10, temperature 0.2) for open-ended questions or by normalizing option probabilities for multiple-choice questions. At inference, only the single selected LLM is called, making the pipeline ~6× faster than majority-voting ensembling.

## Method

RouterDC pairs a small encoder (mDeBERTaV3-base, 86M parameters) with T learnable LLM embedding vectors, and is trained with two contrastive losses. The sample-LLM contrastive loss (Eq. 4) selects the top-K+ scoring LLMs as positives and bottom-K- scoring LLMs (with score < 0.5) as negatives, then applies a softmax-based contrastive objective to pull the query embedding toward positive LLM embeddings and away from negative ones—allowing multiple LLMs to be valid targets rather than forcing a single-peak distribution. The sample-sample contrastive loss (Eq. 5) first clusters all training queries into N groups via t-SNE + k-means, then at each iteration pulls the embedding of a query toward a randomly sampled in-group query and away from H out-group queries, stabilizing training by ensuring similar queries receive similar embeddings. The final objective is a weighted sum of both losses (λ=1), and training requires no backpropagation through the LLMs themselves.

## Results

On five in-distribution tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, CMMLU, ARC-C, HumanEval), RouterDC achieves an average accuracy of 58.54%, surpassing the best individual LLM (dolphin-2.9-llama3-8b, 54.56%) by +3.98% and the best routing baseline (LoraRetriever, 55.77%) by +2.77%. On three out-of-distribution tasks (PreAlgebra, MBPP, C-EVAL), RouterDC achieves 45.85% average accuracy, exceeding the best individual LLM by +1.90% and the best routing baseline (LoraRetriever, 43.50%) by +2.35%. RouterDC is also ~6× faster than Voting in inference (7.97 min vs. 46.59 min on in-distribution tasks) while using fewer than 100M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Voting [Li et al., 2024], RouterDC improves average accuracy by +4.17% on in-distribution tasks (58.54% vs. 54.37%) and +2.81% on out-of-distribution tasks (45.85% vs. 43.04%), while being approximately 6× faster in inference (7.97 min vs. 46.59 min) since it calls only one LLM per query rather than all seven. Against ZOOTER [Lu et al., 2024], the prior state-of-the-art routing method, RouterDC achieves a substantial +4.57% gain on in-distribution tasks (58.54% vs. 53.97%) and +5.69% on out-of-distribution tasks (45.85% vs. 40.16%), which the authors attribute to ZOOTER's KL-divergence loss being a weak supervision signal when multiple LLMs perform well on the same query. Against LoraRetriever [Zhao et al., 2024], RouterDC leads by +2.77% on in-distribution (58.54% vs. 55.77%) and +2.35% on out-of-distribution (45.85% vs. 43.50%), with the gap driven primarily by the sample-LLM contrastive loss that routes based on per-query LLM performance rather than task identity. RouterDC also outperforms the best individual LLM (dolphin-2.9-llama3-8b) by +3.98% on in-distribution and +1.90% on out-of-distribution, confirming that the router successfully exploits complementary strengths across the seven candidate models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from ZOOTER's KL-divergence-based router training, which the authors argue fails when multiple LLMs score similarly on a query (softmax-normalized scores become near-uniform, yielding weak supervision). Instead, RouterDC repurposes contrastive learning—previously applied to visual and sentence representation learning—into the LLM routing setting via two novel losses: a sample-LLM loss that pulls query embeddings toward top-performing LLM embeddings and away from bottom ones, and a sample-sample loss that enforces embedding consistency among similar queries. The architecture (small mDeBERTaV3-base encoder + learnable LLM embedding vectors) is deliberately lightweight, contrasting with heavier routing approaches like LoraRetriever that rely on task identity or LoRA adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RouterDC offers a practical, parameter-efficient (<100M params) and inference-efficient (6× faster than voting) alternative for assembling heterogeneous LLMs without backpropagating through them, making it deployable in cost-sensitive production settings. The dual contrastive formulation is general and the authors note it could transfer to other routing problems. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7–8B parameter LLMs (no 70B-scale models), single-turn queries (no multi-turn chat context), and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, which limits confidence in the reported margins. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger or more architecturally diverse LLM pools and whether the unsupervised clustering step remains effective when task boundaries are ambiguous.
