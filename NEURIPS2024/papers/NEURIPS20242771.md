## Problem

Existing 3D GAN inversion encoders are built on EG3D, which is limited to near-frontal view synthesis and cannot produce comprehensive 360-degree head reconstructions. When extending these methods to PanoHead's full-head latent space, a fundamental trade-off emerges: encoders that faithfully reconstruct the input view produce unrealistic outputs from other viewpoints, and vice versa. No prior encoder pipeline targets full 360-degree head reconstruction from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242771/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face portrait image (with background removed) as input and produces a PanoHead tri-grid triplane representation (3× the channel count of standard EG3D triplanes) that can be volumetrically rendered from any camera pose across the full 360-degree range. The triplane can also be extracted as a 3D mesh. Inference takes approximately 0.37 seconds per image on a single GPU, compared to 49–87 seconds for optimization-based baselines.

## Method

The framework trains two specialized encoders in the W+ space with high-rate residual features: Encoder 1 is optimized purely with reconstruction losses (LPIPS, L2, ArcFace identity) for high-fidelity same-view reconstruction, while Encoder 2 adds an adversarial loss from a novel occlusion-aware triplane discriminator that operates directly in the triplane domain rather than the 2D image domain. The discriminator is trained exclusively on features corresponding to occluded pixels (computed via inverse rendering from depth maps), using WGAN loss with R1 regularization, so that visible regions do not bias the realism signal. At inference, an occlusion mask stitches the visible-region triplane features from Encoder 1 with the occluded-region features from Encoder 2, combining the best of both encoders into a single coherent 3D representation. This dual-encoder-plus-stitching design resolves the fidelity-versus-realism trade-off that a single encoder cannot overcome.

## Results

On FFHQ+LPFF, the proposed dual-encoder method achieves L2=0.017, LPIPS=0.10, ID=0.87 for same-view reconstruction and FID=65.44 for novel-view quality, matching the best same-view fidelity of GOAE (L2=0.015, LPIPS=0.09) while dramatically outperforming it in FID (65.44 vs. 168.96). On CelebA-HQ, the method scores L2=0.021, LPIPS=0.12, ID=0.84, FID=62.58. On the multi-view MEAD dataset, the method achieves the best LPIPS (0.223 at ±60°) and ID (0.706 at ±60°) among all methods, with FID of 47.207 at ±60°. Inference takes 0.37 seconds per image, roughly 250× faster than PTI (86.52 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two optimization-based methods (W⁺ optimization, PTI) and four encoder-based methods (pSp, e4e, TriplaneNetv2, GOAE). GOAE achieves the best same-view reconstruction (L2=0.015, LPIPS=0.09 on FFHQ+LPFF) but produces severely unrealistic novel views (FID=168.96), roughly 2.6× worse than the proposed method (65.44). TriplaneNetv2 matches the proposed method on same-view fidelity (L2=0.017, LPIPS=0.10) but has a much worse FID (98.97 vs. 65.44), indicating poor 3D consistency. PTI achieves a slightly better FID (59.40 vs. 65.44) and comparable same-view scores, but requires 86.52 s per image versus 0.37 s for the proposed method—a 250× speedup. pSp and e4e, designed for 2D StyleGAN inversion, underperform across all metrics (e.g., e4e: LPIPS=0.24, FID=82.99). On the MEAD multi-view benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best LPIPS (0.223 at ±60°) and ID (0.706 at ±60°), outperforming the next-best W⁺ optimization (LPIPS=0.249, ID=0.570) and GOAE (LPIPS=0.296, ID=0.654), while also attaining the lowest FID (47.207 at ±60°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D GAN inversion line of work (GOAE, TriplaneNet, e4e) from the EG3D framework—which synthesizes only near-frontal views—to PanoHead, which renders full 360-degree head views. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the fidelity-vs-realism trade-off becomes far more severe when a large fraction of the head is occluded in the input, motivating a dual-encoder architecture where one encoder specializes in visible-region reconstruction and the other in occluded-region realism, combined via triplane-domain stitching. The occlusion-aware triplane discriminator, which applies WGAN adversarial loss exclusively to occluded triplane features, is a novel loss design that departs from prior image-domain or full-triplane adversarial objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens the door to single-image 3D head reconstruction with full 360-degree coverage, which is directly applicable to AR/VR avatar creation, film production, and interactive 3D character generation. The dual-encoder stitching paradigm and occlusion-aware discriminator offer a reusable recipe for any 3D generative model where a significant portion of the scene is unobserved. However, the method remains limited to in-domain PanoHead samples (struggling with hats, accessories, and high-frequency details), produces visible artifacts in the back-of-head region, and the mesh smoothness still lags behind optimization-based methods like PTI—areas that remain open for future work.
