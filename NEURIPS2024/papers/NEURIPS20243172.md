## Problem

Deep learning models for precipitation nowcasting trained with pixel-wise losses (MSE) produce blurry predictions that provide limited operational utility to weather forecasters, because MSE entangles probability into the output and cannot handle the intrinsic stochasticity of atmospheric dynamics. While generative models (GANs, diffusion) can sharpen predictions, they introduce non-determinism, poor inference speed, and often worse meteorological skill scores. The paper asks whether a deterministic, non-generative loss function can achieve sharp, skillful forecasts without these drawbacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243172/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a loss-function replacement applied to any spatiotemporal prediction model (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, SimVP, Earthformer) operating on grayscale radar echo or satellite image sequences. Input sequences range from 4 to 13 frames depending on the dataset (e.g., 13 frames at 384×384 for SEVIR, 5 frames at 480×480 for HKO-7), and the model predicts 12–20 future frames. A sigmoid layer is appended to constrain outputs to [0,1], and the loss operates on the 2D DFT of each predicted frame, requiring no architectural changes or additional parameters.

## Method

FACL replaces the standard MSE reconstruction loss with a random selection between two Fourier-domain terms: the Fourier Amplitude Loss (FAL), which compares only the amplitude |F| of the DFT and is invariant to global translation, thereby encouraging high-frequency sharpness without spatial anchoring; and the Fourier Correlation Loss (FCL), which computes a normalized complex correlation between the full Fourier vectors of prediction and ground truth, capturing global image structure while being insensitive to overall brightness. A pre-defined threshold P(t) decreases from 1 to 0 over training, so the model first learns structure via FCL and progressively shifts toward FAL for sharpening. The paper proves via Parseval's theorem that naive L2 loss in the Fourier domain is equivalent to spatial MSE, motivating the amplitude/correlation decomposition, and shows via gradient analysis that FAL's gradient lacks the ground-truth phase (making it insufficient alone) while FCL's gradient is invariant to uniform intensity scaling (making it complementary to FAL). A new evaluation metric, Regional Histogram Divergence (RHD), is also proposed to measure patch-wise distributional similarity with tolerance to local translation and deformation.

## Results

On the Stochastic Moving-MNIST synthetic benchmark, FACL reduces LPIPS by up to 57% (0.2538→0.1092) and RHD by up to 71% (1.1504→0.3391) for ConvLSTM compared to MSE, while MAE and SSIM remain comparable. On real radar datasets, FACL consistently improves perceptual and skill metrics: on SEVIR with SimVP, FVD drops from 555.9 to 243.8 and RHD from 1.5244 to 1.1719; on HKO-7 with SimVP, FSS improves from 0.2617 to 0.3973 and RHD from 1.2623 to 0.8660. The trade-off is a small increase in MAE (e.g., 26.26→27.55 on SEVIR SimVP) and slight SSIM degradation, reflecting the shift from pixel-wise accuracy to structural sharpness. FACL adds no parameters and negligible computational overhead (training time within ~2% of MSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four prediction architectures (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, SimVP, Earthformer) trained with MSE, FACL consistently improves LPIPS, FVD, FSS, RHD, and pooled CSI (CSI4-m, CSI16-m) across all three radar datasets, at the cost of a small MAE increase (~5–10%) and marginal SSIM drop. For example, on HKO-7 with SimVP, FACL boosts FSS from 0.2617 to 0.3973 (+52%) and cuts RHD from 1.2623 to 0.8660 (−31%). Against generative baselines, FACL-trained SimVP on SEVIR achieves FSS 0.5656 vs. LDCast's 0.3971 and MCVD's 0.5230, and RHD 1.1719 vs. 1.5988 and 1.3687 respectively, while MCVD still leads on LPIPS (0.3239) and FVD (99.6). On Stochastic Moving-MNIST, FACL on ConvLSTM (LPIPS 0.1092, RHD 0.3391) outperforms LDCast (0.1541, 0.4343) and MCVD (0.1033, 0.3941) on RHD and is competitive on LPIPS, while PreDiff achieves the best LPIPS (0.0709) but worse FSS (0.7975 vs. 0.8172). Against alternative loss functions (SSL, MSE+SSIM, BMSE), FACL dominates on LPIPS, FVD, FSS, and RHD; SSL improves over MSE but still produces blurry outputs, and BMSE over-predicts heavy rain at the expense of all other metrics. FACL is also applicable to generative models (SVGLP, STRPM), where replacing MSE with FACL further reduces FVD and RHD, though gains in MCVD are minimal since its MSE term fits Gaussian noise rather than image structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in precipitation nowcasting of either improving model architectures or adopting generative models (GANs, diffusion) to address blurry predictions. It draws on Fourier-based losses from super-resolution (Focal Frequency Loss, Fourier Space Loss) and the Fourier Ring/Shell Correlation from cryo-electron microscopy, but recombines them into a novel amplitude-only loss (FAL) and a global correlation loss (FCL) with a random-selection training schedule. A key theoretical departure is the proof via Parseval's theorem that naive L2 in Fourier space is exactly equivalent to spatial MSE, motivating the amplitude-only formulation that is translation-invariant. The paper also introduces Stochastic Moving-MNIST as a synthetic benchmark specifically designed to expose the failure mode of pixel-wise losses under random motion, complementing existing deterministic benchmarks like N-body-MNIST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FACL offers a parameter-free, model-agnostic drop-in replacement for MSE that produces sharper, more skillful forecasts without the inference cost or non-determinism of generative models, making it practically attractive for operational weather forecasting. The RHD metric addresses a real gap in evaluation by providing multi-bin, patch-wise distributional comparison that penalizes blur while tolerating small translations—something binary FSS and pixel-wise MSE both fail to do. Limitations include the assumption of monotonic (single-channel) radar data, the absence of temporal consistency regularization, and the observation that pixel-wise metrics (MAE, SSIM) degrade slightly, which may matter for applications requiring exact intensity calibration. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multimodal medium-range forecasts and whether the random FAL/FCL scheduling can be replaced by a more principled curriculum.
