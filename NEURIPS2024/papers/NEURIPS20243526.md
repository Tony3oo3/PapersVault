## Problem

Generator-reranker systems (generate N hypotheses, then select the best) are widely used to reduce LLM hallucinations and errors, but there is no principled theoretical account of when and how quickly the error rate decays with N, particularly when the reranker is imperfect. This paper asks: under what conditions on the generator's channel noise and the reranker's ranking model is the combined protocol asymptotically error-free, and what is the exact convergence rate?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a query q (a question, source sentence, or code prompt) and an acceptance set X(q) defining valid answers. The generator G produces N candidate strings y_1,...,y_N from Σ*, and the reranker R returns a single top-ranked hypothesis as the final output. The error probability P_err(N;q) is defined as the probability that the selected output falls outside X(q). In experiments, 'acceptable' is operationalized as passing all test cases (code generation) or exceeding a COMET-22 threshold of 0.85 (translation), and hypotheses are sampled at temperature 1 from a single LLM.

## Method

The paper models the generator-reranker system as a multiple-description source coding problem: the generator is a sender transmitting N acceptable descriptions through parallel noisy channels, and the reranker is a decoder that selects the most reliable (potentially corrupted) description. For independent hypotheses, the paper derives closed-form error probabilities for Mallows rerankers (exponential decay at rate e^{-λ}(1-ε)+ε) and Zipf-Mandelbrot rerankers (derived via γ-entmax with γ<1, proven error-free via the dominated convergence theorem on the Hurwitz zeta integral representation). For dependent (exchangeable) hypotheses coupled by a Beta(α,β) prior, the error decays as a power law O(N^{-β}) under a perfect reranker, and Proposition 4 shows that any reranker yielding an error-free protocol in the independent case remains error-free under exchangeability for any finite density p(τ). The resulting 'reranking laws' are validated by fitting α, β, γ, and e^{-λ} to empirical failure-rate curves on code generation (DeepSeek-Coder 7B, MBPP) and medical translation (TowerInstruct 13B, TICO-19).

## Results

The paper's main empirical contribution is fitting its theoretical reranking laws (Zipf-Mandelbrot model with Beta-coupled exchangeable hypotheses) to empirical failure-rate curves across four tasks. For text-to-code generation on MBPP (DeepSeek-Coder 7B, 200 hypotheses), the fitted parameters are α=0.1, β=0.309, γ=0.001, e^{-λ}=0.003, yielding a close match to the MBR-exec failure rate. For machine translation on TICO-19 (TowerInstruct 13B, 50 hypotheses), the fitted parameters are α=0.1, β=0.46, with MBR decoding (γ=0.182, e^{-λ}=0.001) achieving lower failure rates than QE reranking (γ=0.001, e^{-λ}=0.005). The theoretical core establishes that the protocol is asymptotically error-free: error decays exponentially as O((e^{-λ}(1−ε)+ε)^N) for independent hypotheses with a Mallows reranker (Prop. 1), and as a power law O(N^{-β}) for exchangeable hypotheses with a Beta prior and perfect reranker (Prop. 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new reranker to outperform existing ones; rather, it provides a theoretical framework that predicts the failure-rate scaling of existing reranking strategies. MBR-exec (Shi et al., 2022), which performs majority voting over execution results, is the reranker in the code generation experiments; the paper's Zipf-Mandelbrot model with γ=0.001 (near-sparse) fits its empirical curve well, confirming that execution-based voting behaves like a near-perfect reranker. In machine translation, MBR decoding with COMET-22 (Eikema & Aziz, 2020) consistently achieves lower failure rates than reference-free QE reranking with CometKiwi (Fernandes et al., 2022) across all three language pairs (EN→PT, EN→ES, EN→RU), with the gap visible in both dev and test sets. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) on SVAMP and StrategyQA also follows the predicted power-law decay, validating the framework's generality beyond code and translation. The key practical takeaway is that the fitted e^{-λ} values (0.001–0.005) indicate that real rerankers operate very close to the perfect-reranker limit, explaining why even modest N (50–200) yields substantial error reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multiple description source coding framework from communication theory (Ozarow, 1980; Gamal and Cover, 1982) to analyze generator-reranker LLM systems, drawing a direct analogy between parallel noisy channels and hypothesis generation. It builds on the statistical ranking literature (Mallows models, Zipf-Mandelbrot distributions) and the γ-entmax family (Peters et al., 2019) to characterize imperfect rerankers, while its empirical validation applies existing reranking strategies—MBR-exec (Shi et al., 2022), MBR decoding with COMET-22 (Eikema and Aziz, 2020), quality estimation with CometKiwi (Fernandes et al., 2022), and self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023)—as the 'decoders' in the communication-theoretic picture. The key departure from prior LLM reranking work is the provision of asymptotic error-free guarantees and closed-form 'reranking laws' rather than purely empirical scaling observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reranking laws give practitioners a principled way to predict how many hypotheses are needed to achieve a target error rate, complementing the neural scaling laws literature that focuses on model size and compute. The communication-theoretic lens opens concrete avenues for future work: more powerful error-correcting codes (Hamming, Reed-Solomon, LDPC, turbo codes) could inspire more efficient generation protocols than naive repetition, and feedback channels could model interactive LLM protocols with reward models. Key limitations include the asymptotic-only guarantees (large N may be needed in practice), the restriction to binary acceptable/unacceptable decisions, and the imperfect empirical fit, particularly for large N where errors become rare events. Extending the framework to continuous quality metrics and to more realistic dependency structures remains open.
