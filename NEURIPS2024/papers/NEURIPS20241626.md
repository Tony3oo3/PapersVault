## Problem

Existing long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL) methods rely heavily on high-quality pseudo-labels but neglect the quality of learned representations, and they typically assume the unlabeled data distribution matches the labeled data or require predefined anchor distributions. This paper identifies that representation learning is underexplored in LTSSL and that real-world unlabeled data often follows a different (unknown) label distribution than the labeled data, making standard pseudo-labeling strategies suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241626/cropped_page16_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images with a long-tailed class distribution (e.g., 10% of training samples on ImageNet-127, or N₁=500 head-class samples on CIFAR10-LT) alongside a larger pool of unlabeled images whose class distribution may be consistent, uniform, or reversed relative to the labeled data. Images are processed at 32×32 (CIFAR/STL) or 64×64 (ImageNet-127) resolution through a shared CNN backbone (WRN-28-2 or ResNet-50). The output is a single class label per image, produced by fusing predictions from a balanced classifier branch (with logit adjustment) and a standard classifier branch.

## Method

CCL derives a unified probabilistic framework from the variational information bottleneck, showing that logit adjustment, balanced softmax, and class-balanced contrastive learning are special cases obtained by different density approximations (linear classifier, Gaussian kernel, or explicit priors). For LTSSL, the framework is extended by replacing discrete pseudo-labels with continuous pseudo-labels: a dual-branch classifier (balanced + standard) produces a fused posterior distribution over all classes for each unlabeled sample, which is then used as soft targets in a contrastive loss computed via Gaussian kernel density estimation over a mini-batch. Two complementary contrastive losses are introduced: a reliable pseudo-labels loss that merges energy-score-filtered unlabeled samples with labeled data to ensure calibration, and a smoothed pseudo-labels loss that propagates labels through a similarity matrix in the contrastive feature space (solving (I − βG)⁻¹) to enforce weak-strong view consistency. The unlabeled class prior is progressively estimated via exponential moving average over energy-score-selected samples, enabling logit adjustment without requiring a predefined anchor distribution.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (consistent, γ=100, N₁=500, M₁=4000), CCL achieves 84.5% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous best ACR (81.6%) by 2.9%. On CIFAR-100-LT (consistent, γ=10, N₁=50, M₁=400), CCL reaches 53.5% vs. ACR's 51.1%. On the naturally long-tailed ImageNet-127 dataset, CCL achieves 61.5% (32×32) and 67.8% (64×64), surpassing ACR by 4.3% and 4.2% respectively. Under inconsistent label distributions (γ_l ≠ γ_u), CCL maintains a 1.6% average advantage over ACR without requiring predefined anchor distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and distribution setting. Against the strongest competitor ACR [64], CCL gains +2.9% on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100, N₁=500), +2.4% on CIFAR-100-LT (γ=10, N₁=50), +1.0% on STL-10-LT (γ_l=10), and +4.3%/+4.2% on ImageNet-127 at 32×32/64×64. CPE [43] is the second-strongest baseline but trails CCL by 2.8–3.8% on CIFAR-10-LT and 3.2–3.7% on CIFAR-100-LT. DASO [48] and ABC [38] are competitive on CIFAR-10-LT (78.9–79.1%) but fall 5–7% behind CCL. On the naturally imbalanced STL-10-LT (where unlabeled distribution is unknown), CCL achieves 79.1% (γ_l=10) and 77.1% (γ_l=20), beating ACR by 2.0% and 2.0% respectively, while CPE lags by 6.0% and 7.5%. On ImageNet-127, CCL's 61.5%/67.8% substantially exceeds CoSSL (43.7%/53.9%) and TRAS (46.2%/54.1%), confirming the benefit of the continuous contrastive representation learning component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FixMatch semi-supervised pipeline and logit adjustment for long-tail correction, but its central conceptual contribution is a probabilistic framework derived from the variational information bottleneck that unifies diverse long-tail learning methods (BALMS, LA, BCL, GML, PaCo, Proco, T-vMF, WCDAS) as special cases of a single density-approximation objective. It departs from prior LTSSL work (ACR, CPE, DASO) by (i) not assuming the unlabeled distribution matches the labeled one, instead progressively estimating it via EMA over energy-score-filtered samples, and (ii) shifting focus from classifier correction to representation learning through continuous contrastive losses with both reliable and smoothed pseudo-labels. The dual-branch architecture follows ABC and ACR, but the fusion mechanism for pseudo-label generation and the Gaussian-kernel-based label propagation (Eq. 21) are novel extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified probabilistic framework gives practitioners a principled lens for understanding why various long-tail contrastive methods work and where they fail, potentially guiding future design choices beyond ad-hoc combinations. The progressive, calibration-aware estimation of the unlabeled class prior (via energy score rather than confidence) addresses a practical gap in real-world LTSSL where unlabeled data distributions are genuinely unknown and mismatched. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of a convergence proof for the learned features and the reliance on the Gaussian kernel assumption; extending the framework to non-Gaussian densities or providing neural-collapse-style guarantees remains open. The 4%+ gain on ImageNet-127 and consistent improvements across CIFAR/STL settings suggest practical relevance, but the method's added complexity (dual branches, two contrastive losses, energy filtering, EMA tracking) may limit adoption in resource-constrained settings.
