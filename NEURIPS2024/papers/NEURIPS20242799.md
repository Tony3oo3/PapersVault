## Problem

Prior work on memorization in diffusion models has focused on detecting memorized prompts and mitigating at the input level (adjusting text embeddings, rescaling attention logits), but no method has localized memorization to specific neurons within the model. This gap is critical because input-level mitigations are easily circumvented by adversaries and become ineffective once a model is publicly released. The paper asks: which exact neurons in a diffusion model's architecture are responsible for memorizing a specific training sample?

## Input / Output

The method takes a memorized text prompt and a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4) as input, along with a holdout set of non-memorized prompts used to calibrate per-neuron activation statistics (mean and standard deviation). It outputs a small set of specific neurons in the cross-attention value layers of the U-Net's down- and mid-blocks (median 4 for verbatim, 21 for template memorization) whose deactivation prevents verbatim replication of training images while preserving prompt-image alignment. The detection process requires only 10 single-step forward passes per prompt and runs in approximately 14 seconds for verbatim and 44 seconds for template memorized prompts, with no gradient computation needed.

## Method

NEMO employs a two-step neuron selection process targeting the value layers of cross-attention in the U-Net's down- and mid-blocks, chosen because value mappings directly process text embeddings without the softmax interdependence that complicates key-layer analysis. The initial selection computes a memorization strength score via SSIM between noise differences (δ = ε_θ(x_T, T, y) − x_T) across 10 seeds, then identifies candidate neurons whose activations on the memorized prompt are out-of-distribution (|z-score| > threshold) relative to holdout non-memorized prompts, supplemented by top-k highest-activation neurons to capture high-variance cases. The refinement step iteratively re-activates neurons layer-by-layer and then individually, removing any neuron whose re-activation does not raise the memorization score above the threshold, drastically reducing the candidate set (e.g., from 674 to 21 neurons for template memorization). The key insight is that a small number of value-layer neurons exhibit OOD activation patterns that trigger data replication, and setting their outputs to zero permanently interrupts the memorization chain without retraining, gradient computation, or any inference-time overhead.

## Results

NEMO identifies a median of 4 memorization neurons for verbatim-memorized (VM) prompts and 21 for template-memorized (TM) prompts in Stable Diffusion v1.4. Deactivating these neurons reduces the memorization metric SSCD_Orig from 0.83 to 0.09 (VM) and SSCD_Gen from 1.0 to 0.10 (VM), while increasing image diversity (D_SSCD drops from 0.99 to 0.16) and maintaining prompt alignment (A_CLIP = 0.31, unchanged from the 0.32 default). Randomly deactivating the same number of neurons has no measurable effect (SSCD_Orig remains 0.80 for VM), confirming that only a specific subset of neurons drives memorization. FID and KID on COCO show no significant quality degradation even with up to 750 blocked neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wen et al. [46] (gradient-based prompt embedding adjustment), NEMO achieves comparable memorization mitigation (SSCD_Orig 0.09 vs. 0.04, SSCD_Gen 0.10 vs. 0.08 for VM) but is entirely gradient-free, adding zero inference overhead once neurons are identified, whereas Wen et al. requires a gradient computation per seed and prompt. Against Ren et al. [31] (attention logit rescaling), NEMO outperforms on SSCD_Gen for both VM (0.10 vs. 0.14) and TM (0.05 vs. 0.19), indicating stronger suppression of repeated generation, while matching on SSCD_Orig. Against Somepalli et al. [36] (random token addition), NEMO substantially outperforms the 1-token and 4-token variants (SSCD_Orig 0.09 vs. 0.59 and 0.09 for VM) and matches the 10-token variant on memorization while preserving better CLIP alignment (0.31 vs. 0.28). A critical control experiment shows that randomly deactivating the same number of neurons (4 for VM, 21 for TM) has virtually no effect on memorization (SSCD_Orig remains 0.80), confirming that NEMO identifies a specific, non-redundant subset of neurons rather than a generic capacity reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEMO extends the memorization-localization line of work (Maini et al. 2023, which deactivates units until a label flip occurs in supervised models) to diffusion models, where no labels are available and the generative objective makes label-flip detection inapplicable. It departs from prior DM memorization mitigations (Wen et al. 2024's gradient-based prompt adjustment, Ren et al. 2024's attention scaling, Somepalli et al. 2023's random token insertion) by operating on the model's internal structure rather than its inputs, yielding a permanent, gradient-free fix. The method repurposes out-of-distribution activation detection and SSIM-based trajectory consistency—tools borrowed from anomaly detection and image quality assessment—as a proxy for neuron-level memorization attribution in cross-attention value layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEMO offers model providers a practical, one-time pruning procedure that permanently removes memorization from publicly released DMs without retraining, addressing a gap that inference-time safeguards cannot fill once a model is in the wild. The finding that a handful of neurons (sometimes just one) encode an entire high-resolution training image opens the door to targeted knowledge editing and concept removal in generative models. Key limitations remain: template memorization is harder to fully eliminate (often requiring 20+ neurons), some prompts distribute memorization across 100+ neurons, and the results are validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to LLMs, whether it can identify concept neurons for broader knowledge editing, and whether it generalizes to newer architectures trained on deduplicated data.
