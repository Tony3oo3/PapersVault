## Problem

The fully dynamic k-center clustering problem with z outliers in general metric spaces previously had only a (14+ε)-approximation algorithm (Chan et al., 2024) with update time depending on the number of outliers z. This paper closes the gap by providing a (4+ε)-approximation with amortized update time independent of both z and the current number of points, making the algorithm practical for real-world scenarios where the outlier fraction can be arbitrarily large.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242832/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm processes a sequence of insertion and deletion operations on a point set in a general metric space (M,d), with parameters k (number of centers), z (outlier budget), and ε (accuracy parameter). The spread ratio Δ = d_max/d_min of all points ever inserted must be bounded. At any time, the data structure directly outputs a set of at most k centers and an outlier set of at most (1+ε)z points; no separate query step is needed. In the non-discrete version, centers may be any point in the metric space (even if no longer active); a discrete variant restricting centers to currently present points yields a 6-approximation at the same update time.

## Method

The algorithm maintains a hierarchical data structure of at most k levels, each containing one cluster of radius 4r around a center, with the remaining points forming an outlier set. The key technical contribution is combining the sampling-based level structure of Chan et al. with the greedy ball-selection strategy of Charikar et al.: at each level, a random sample of O(ε^{-1}k^2 log k) points is drawn, and a point whose 2r-ball covers a threshold number of points is selected as the center, with the cluster defined as the 4r-ball. A 'dense invariant' (requiring the 2r-ball to cover at least min(z+1, (n_i−z)/(k−i+1) − εz/(αk)) points) determines when a level must be reclustered, and reclustering only triggers when this invariant is violated rather than on every center deletion. The approximation analysis adapts the charging argument from Charikar et al. by introducing artificial outliers that track credit points across levels, proving that the union of 4r-balls covers all but (1+ε)z points when r < (1+ε)·r_OPT. The amortized update time of O(ε^{-3}k^6 log k log Δ) follows from showing each cluster remains dense for Ω(εz/k) or Ω((n_i−z)/(k)) operations before needing reconstruction.

## Results

The paper achieves a (4+ε)-approximation for the (k,z)-center clustering problem in general metric spaces, a significant improvement over the previous (14+ε)-approximation by Chan et al. [5]. The expected amortized update time is O(ε^{-3} k^6 log(k) log(Δ)), which is independent of z and the number of points n currently in the set. The solution allows up to (1+ε)z outliers and holds with probability 1 - k/e^{Ω(log k)}. For the discrete variant (centers must be from the current point set), a 6-approximation is achieved with the same update time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only prior fully dynamic algorithm for (k,z)-center clustering in general metric spaces is by Chan, Lattanzi, Sozio, and Wang [5], which achieves a (14+ε)-approximation. This paper improves the approximation ratio from 14+ε to 4+ε — a factor of more than 3× improvement — while also achieving an update time that is independent of z (the number of outliers), which is critical since the fraction of outliers in real-world data can be arbitrarily large. The key technical difference is that this paper maintains at most k levels (vs. t = k·⌈log_{1+ε}(k/δ)⌉ clusters in [5]) and uses a novel combination of the sampling-based level data structure from [5] with the greedy strategy from Charikar et al. [8], enabling a tighter charging argument. The update time of O(ε^{-3} k^6 log(k) log(Δ)) is independent of both z and n (the current number of points), making it applicable to real-world scenarios where the outlier fraction is large. The paper also notes that Chan et al. [5] proved a lower bound of Ω(z) on the amortized update time for any O(1)-approximation algorithm that strictly allows only z outliers, which motivates the (1+ε)z bicriteria relaxation used here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the fully dynamic k-center clustering with outliers framework of Chan et al. [5], which maintained a (14+ε)-approximation in general metric spaces. The key technical departure is combining Chan et al.'s sampling-based hierarchical level data structure with the original greedy charging argument from Charikar et al. [8] for the static problem, rather than the looser ball-covering argument used in [5]. The level structure is simplified to at most k levels (versus t = k·⌈log_{1+ε}(k/δ)⌉ in [5]), and the dense invariant is calibrated to the static greedy threshold, enabling a tighter (4+ε) guarantee. The update time is also made independent of z, addressing a lower bound shown by Chan et al. that any algorithm with strictly z outliers requires Ω(z) amortized time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from (14+ε) to (4+ε) brings the dynamic approximation close to the static 3-approximation of Charikar et al., suggesting that the dynamic overhead for k-center with outliers in general metrics is fundamentally small. The z-independent update time makes the algorithm applicable to real-world scenarios where the outlier fraction can be large, a setting where prior algorithms become impractical. Open questions include whether the 4+ε can be tightened further (the static optimum is 2), whether the adaptive adversary model can be strengthened, and whether similar charging-argument techniques can transfer to other dynamic clustering variants (e.g., capacitated or fair clustering). The bicriteria nature (allowing (1+ε)z outliers rather than exactly z) remains a limitation for applications requiring strict outlier budgets.
