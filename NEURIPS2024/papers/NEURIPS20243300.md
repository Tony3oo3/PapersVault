## Problem

Existing neural OT solvers for single-cell genomics are restricted to deterministic Monge maps, squared Euclidean costs, balanced mass conservation, and linear (same-space) transport, making them inadequate for the stochastic, non-Euclidean, unbalanced, and cross-space problems that dominate single-cell applications such as trajectory inference, drug response modeling, and multi-omics alignment. GENOT is the first framework to simultaneously satisfy all four requirements: stochastic transport plans, arbitrary cost functions, unbalanced mass, and quadratic/fused OT across incomparable spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes source and target cell measurements that may live in different-dimensional spaces (e.g., ATAC-seq in R^{d1} and RNA-seq in R^{d2}), plus optional shared cell features for the fused setting. It produces a conditional transport distribution π_θ(·|x) for each source cell x, from which one can sample transported cells, compute uncertainty via conditional variance, and (in the unbalanced case) obtain learned re-weighting functions η_θ, ξ_θ that model cell creation/destruction. Notably, the flow operates entirely in the target space, so source and target dimensions need not match, and cost functions can be non-differentiable (e.g., geodesic distances on a k-NN graph).

## Method

GENOT parameterizes the EOT coupling via its conditional distributions: for each source point x, a conditional vector field v_{t,θ}(·|x) defines a flow in the target space that maps Gaussian noise ρ to the conditional π_ε*(·|x). Training minimizes a flow-matching loss over mini-batch samples (x,y) drawn from a discrete EOT coupling computed by a Sinkhorn-based solver (linear, quadratic, unbalanced, or fused variant), making the method simulation-free and cost-agnostic. The unbalanced extension (U-GENOT) re-balances the problem by showing the unbalanced coupling is a balanced EOT solution between re-weighted marginals, then jointly learns the coupling and the re-weighting functions via an additional regression loss on marginal weights. For the fused setting, the vector field is conditioned on both structural and feature modalities, and the discrete solver handles the combined linear-quadratic cost, yielding the first neural solver for the Fused Gromov-Wasserstein problem.

## Results

On the Schrödinger bridge benchmark (Gushchin et al. 2023), GENOT-L outperforms all baselines considered, which are specifically tailored to the linear EOT problem with ℓ₂² cost in the balanced setting. For drug perturbation prediction, U-GENOT-L achieves perfect calibration (error-uncertainty correlation) on 117 out of 163 cancer drugs, and allowing mass variation (unbalancedness) increases accuracy for nine strongly-acting drugs. On the bone marrow modality translation task, GENOT-Q and GENOT-F outperform the discrete GW-LR baseline by orders of magnitude on Sinkhorn divergence, while GW-LR remains competitive on FOSCTTM only in low dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three OT-based flow matching methods: Multisample Flow Matching (Pooladian et al. [64]), Simulation-free Schrödinger Bridges (Tong et al. [84]), and Conditional Flow Matching (Tong et al. [85]). GENOT outperforms all three on the Schrödinger bridge benchmark (Tab. 1), with the key advantage being that the baselines are restricted to the ℓ₂² cost, the balanced setting, and the linear OT formulation, whereas GENOT handles any cost, unbalancedness, and quadratic/fused OT. On the modality translation task, GENOT-Q with geodesic costs substantially outperforms the discrete GW-LR baseline on FOSCTTM in high dimensions, while GW-LR is competitive only in low dimensions. On distributional fitting (Sinkhorn divergence), GENOT models outperform GW-LR by orders of magnitude. The paper also demonstrates that GENOT preserves the signal from mini-batch couplings (Fig. 5), a property that the compared OT-based FM approaches [64, 84, 85] do not guarantee, since they only preserve straight couplings or the specific ℓ₂² EOT coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENOT departs from prior neural OT methods (Tong et al. 2023, Pooladian et al. 2023) that learn a single flow directly transporting μ to ν, which restricts them to linear OT with squared Euclidean cost within the same space. Instead, GENOT learns conditional flows from a fixed noise distribution to each conditional π*(·|x), using flow matching as a black-box training procedure. This architectural choice decouples the cost function from the flow geometry, enabling any cost (including geodesic distances), mapping across incomparable spaces (quadratic/fused GW), and unbalanced formulations. The key theoretical departure is the re-balancing proposition (Prop. 3.1), which shows unbalanced EOT reduces to balanced EOT between re-weighted marginals, extending earlier Monge-map re-balancing ideas (Eyring et al., Lubeck et al., Yang & Uhler) to the entropic and quadratic settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first unified neural framework addressing all four requirements (stochastic maps, flexible costs, unbalancedness, cross-space mapping) that single-cell genomics demands, and is the first neural solver for the fused Gromov-Wasserstein problem. The re-balancing result is practically significant: switching from GENOT to U-GENOT requires only swapping the discrete solver and adding two regression heads, with no architectural changes. Open questions include the non-uniqueness of GW solutions (mitigated but not resolved by the fused term), the empirical nature of unbalancedness hyperparameter selection, and whether the framework scales to the millions-of-cells regime of large atlases. The authors also note the fundamental caveat that there is no biological evidence cells evolve exactly according to an entropic OT plan.
