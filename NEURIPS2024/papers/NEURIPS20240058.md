## Problem

Personalized talking face generation (TFG) requires high identity similarity in both appearance and talking style, but existing person-dependent methods (e.g., RAD-NeRF, GeneFace, ER-NeRF) train a separate NeRF model from scratch for each identity, requiring hours of training, over 1 minute of data, and suffering from poor generalizability to out-of-domain driving conditions. Person-agnostic one-shot methods generalize well but cannot exploit the rich reference video to capture personalized attributes. No prior work bridges these two paradigms by efficiently adapting a pre-trained generic 3D model to a specific identity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short reference video (as few as 10 seconds) of the target identity, a source image, and arbitrary driving audio. The reference video serves dual purposes: its first frame initializes the tri-plane for static appearance, and its audio-motion pair acts as an in-context style prompt for the motion generator. The output is a high-resolution personalized talking face video rendered via NeRF volume rendering plus super-resolution, running at 15 FPS on a single A100 GPU. Adaptation to a new identity requires only 2,000 iterations (~15 minutes, 8 GB VRAM) on one A100.

## Method

MimicTalk builds on the pre-trained Real3D-Portrait NeRF-based one-shot renderer and adapts it via a static-dynamic-hybrid (SD-Hybrid) pipeline: tri-plane inversion optimizes a learnable 3D face representation to capture static texture/geometry details, while LoRA matrices (rank 4) injected into the motion adapter, volume renderer, and super-resolution modules learn personalized dynamic motion-to-image mappings without catastrophic forgetting. For expressive motion generation, the ICS-A2M model uses conditional flow matching with a transformer backbone, trained via an audio-guided motion-infilling paradigm where the model reconstructs randomly masked motion segments conditioned on surrounding unmasked motion and the full audio track, thereby learning to mimic the implicit talking style from context. At inference, a reference audio-motion pair is concatenated as a style prompt, and classifier-free guidance (w=2) on the style prompt enhances style fidelity. A SyncNet-based lip-sync loss is added to the flow-matching objective to ensure accurate audio-expression synchronization.

## Results

MimicTalk achieves the best scores across all quantitative metrics in Table 1: CSIM 0.837, PSNR 31.72, FID 29.94, AED 0.098, and SyncNet confidence 8.072. The SD-Hybrid adaptation requires only 0.26 hours (15 minutes) and 8.24 GB GPU memory on a single A100, making it 47× faster than RAD-NeRF (13.22 h) and 14× faster than ER-NeRF (3.77 h). In the MOS user study (Table 2), MimicTalk also attains the highest subjective scores for identity similarity (4.15/5), visual quality (4.22/5), and lip-sync (4.13/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimicTalk outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the three person-dependent NeRF methods (RAD-NeRF, GeneFace, ER-NeRF), it improves CSIM by 0.002–0.018, PSNR by 0.27–1.28 dB, FID by 0.44–3.62, AED by 0.015–0.024, and SyncNet confidence by 2.44–3.44 points, while reducing adaptation time from 3.77–16.57 h to 0.26 h. Against the one-shot style-controllable method StyleTalk, MimicTalk gains substantially on identity similarity (CSIM 0.837 vs 0.671), image quality (PSNR 31.72 vs 27.39, FID 29.94 vs 45.25), and lip-sync (AED 0.098 vs 0.106, Sync 8.072 vs 7.173). In the CMOS user study (Table 3), MimicTalk is preferred over StyleTalk for both talking-style controllability (+0.549) and identity similarity (+1.735) on a −3 to +3 scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

MimicTalk builds directly on Real3D-Portrait (Ye et al., 2024) as its person-agnostic NeRF-based renderer, but departs fundamentally from the per-identity-per-training paradigm of RAD-NeRF, GeneFace, and ER-NeRF by instead adapting a pre-trained generic model via a static-dynamic-hybrid pipeline. The method transplants LoRA (originally from NLP) into TFG for dynamic identity adaptation and borrows tri-plane inversion from GAN-inversion literature for static texture fidelity. For motion generation, it introduces flow matching and in-context learning (both from LLM/speech synthesis) into audio-to-motion, replacing the explicit style-vector approaches of StyleTalk and Wu et al. with zero-shot in-context style mimicking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47× speedup (15 minutes vs. hours) makes personalized talking face generation practically deployable in real-world applications like video conferencing and virtual avatars, where per-identity training was previously prohibitive. The in-context style control without explicit style compression is a transferable idea that could benefit other conditional generation tasks. However, the method is limited to the face region (hair and torso produce artifacts), runs at 15 FPS, and requires a reference video of the target identity, leaving open questions about full-body extension, real-time inference via Gaussian Splatting, and whether the in-context approach generalizes to longer or more diverse style prompts.
