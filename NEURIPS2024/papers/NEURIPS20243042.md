## Problem

Standard self-supervised learning objectives produce representations that are overly invariant to input transformations, whereas primate visual cortex (area IT) encodes significant structured variability related to object pose and viewing conditions. This paper asks whether explicitly encouraging equivariant structure in SSL representations—without supervised access to transformation parameters—can improve alignment with biological neural responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243042/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural RGB images (ImageNet-1k or ImageNet-100) and applies a paired-augmentation procedure: the dataset is split into two non-overlapping halves, and corresponding images in each half receive the *same* random transformation (crop, color jitter, blur, solarization, flip), halving the total number of random augmentation samples relative to standard iSSL. The ResNet-50 backbone outputs are passed through two separate projector networks (g_inv and g_equi) to produce an invariant embedding and an equivariant embedding; the equivariant embedding is used for linear prediction of macaque IT single-unit firing rates across four electrophysiology datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is a contrastive-equivariant (CE-SSL) loss that converts any standard invariant SSL objective into an equivariant version by applying the same invariance loss to *difference vectors* between similarly-transformed pairs from the two dataset halves. Concretely, if z_i^A and z_i^B are the equivariant-space embeddings of the same base image under two different augmentations, the CE-SSL loss is L(z_1^A − z_1^B, z_2^A − z_2^B), which encourages similar transformations to induce similar displacements in output space (implicitly specifying T_ρ(z) = z + z_ρ). The overall objective is a weighted sum L = (1−λ)L_iSSL + λL_CE-SSL, where λ controls the invariance–equivariance tradeoff. The approach requires no extra forward passes beyond standard iSSL, no explicit transformation-parameter labels, and is compatible with any base SSL framework (Barlow Twins, SimCLR, MMCR). Empirically, CE-SSL with Barlow Twins at λ=0.2 achieves the top IT predictivity score on the BrainScore leaderboard (0.5355 mean fraction of explained variance), with gains roughly 4× larger than those from switching between different invariant base objectives.

## Results

The best CE-SSL model (Barlow Twins base, λ=0.2, trained 1000 epochs) achieves 0.5355 mean noise-ceiled fraction of explained variance on macaque IT, making it the top IT prediction model on the BrainScore leaderboard (~250 models). Across all four IT electrophysiology datasets, equivariant models (λ>0) outperform their invariant counterparts (λ=0) for every base objective. The spread in neural predictivity across the λ sweep is approximately 4× larger than the spread across the three invariant base objectives, indicating that the equivariance term is the dominant factor driving improved cortical alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CE-SSL against three invariant SSL baselines: Barlow Twins, SimCLR, and MMCR. On neural predictivity (the paper's primary metric), CE-SSL consistently outperforms all three invariant baselines across all four macaque IT datasets; the best CE-Barlow model (λ=0.2) reaches 0.5355 mean FVE versus lower scores for the λ=0 invariant versions. On ImageNet-100 transfer learning, CE-Barlow improves over Barlow Twins on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., Cifar-10: 88.0% vs 87.7%, Food-101: 65.5% vs 63.4%, Flowers-102: 89.5% vs 88.3%), while CE-MMCR and CE-SimCLR show more mixed results. On ImageNet-1k transfer learning, gains are marginal and inconsistent: CE-Barlow slightly improves Flowers-102 (94.2% vs 93.8%) but drops on Pets (85.7% vs 86.5%), and CE-SimCLR underperforms SimCLR on most tasks (e.g., Pets: 82.3% vs 85.1%). The authors note that the equivariance benefit for out-of-distribution generalization is more pronounced with smaller pretraining datasets, suggesting the ImageNet-1k task may be near saturation for frozen-linear evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant self-supervised learning line initiated by CARE (Gupta et al., 2023), which first proposed learning equivariance from data pairs, by introducing a 'contrastive-equivariant' framework that applies a standard iSSL loss to difference vectors between similarly-transformed image pairs in a separate equivariant embedding space. Unlike prior equivariant SSL methods (EquiMod, SIE, Amortised Invariance) that require supervised access to transformation parameters or hypernetwork architectures, CE-SSL uses a paired-augmentation data generation procedure that avoids both, while remaining compatible with any base iSSL objective (Barlow Twins, SimCLR, MMCR). The work is also positioned within the neural modeling literature, where prior studies (Feather et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2016) established that biological visual representations retain structured transformation-related variability that invariant SSL objectives fail to capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that explicitly optimizing for structured transformation variability (rather than invariance) improves alignment with primate IT cortical responses, with the spatial-photometric factorization metric emerging as the strongest correlate of neural predictivity across all four electrophysiology datasets. The practical impact is significant: the best CE-SSL model (Barlow Twins, λ=0.2, 1000 epochs) achieved the top IT predictivity score on BrainScore, and the technique adds only ~10% training overhead. Open questions include whether the learned equivariances generalize to unseen transformation types, whether output transformations can be explicitly recovered from the representation, and whether temporal equivariance (applied to video frame derivatives) can capture the 'representational straightness' observed in biological ventral stream responses. The limited transfer learning gains on ImageNet-1k suggest the benefits of structured variability may be most relevant for neural alignment rather than downstream classification.
