## Problem

High-order ODE solvers (e.g., Runge-Kutta) applied to Transformer residual connections fail to deliver consistent gains at scale, and the gated fusion coefficient learning used in prior ODE Transformers cannot be extended beyond 2nd-order methods. Additionally, implicit ODE methods—which iteratively refine solutions using newly available information—had not been explored for Transformer architecture design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240643/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tokenized text sequences (sub-word units via BPE/SentencePiece) and produces the same output formats as vanilla Transformers: translated sentences, summary text, next-token distributions, or classification logits. The architectural modification is internal to each Transformer layer's residual connection, so the external I/O interface is unchanged; the difference lies in how intermediate representations are computed within a single layer (multiple forward passes through the same attention/FFN block with EMA-weighted aggregation).

## Method

PCformer replaces the single Euler-step residual update with a two-stage predictor-corrector loop within each layer. The predictor is a high-order Runge-Kutta method (2nd or 4th order) whose intermediate approximations are combined using learnable EMA coefficients γ·(1−γ)^(n−i), making the scheme extensible to arbitrary order without the combinatorial coefficient explosion of gated fusion. The corrector is a lightweight implicit method (Adams-Moulton multistep or Backward Euler) that refines the predictor's estimate by evaluating the layer function at the predicted state and blending it with previous-layer derivatives using EMA-initialized weights. Training stability is ensured by RK-Norm (layer-normalizing each intermediate approximation before accumulation) and sublayer dropping (rate 0.1), while the EMA coefficient γ is initialized at 0.5 and learned end-to-end.

## Results

On WMT'14 En-De, PCformer (2-order, 12-layer, 286M) achieves 30.95 BLEU / 29.8 SacreBLEU, and on WMT'14 En-Fr it reaches 44.27 BLEU / 42.4 SacreBLEU, setting new state-of-the-art among non-data-augmented models. On OPUS-100 multilingual translation, the 1.2B PCformer attains 35.0 average SacreBLEU, surpassing the 3.8B DeepNet (32.1) by 2.9 points with only 1/3 the parameters. On the LM Evaluation Harness, PCformer (1.3B, 100B tokens) achieves 62.9 average accuracy, outperforming the equivalent Transformer++ (Llama-like) baseline by 5.7 points. On GLUE, PCformer reaches 86.1 average score versus BERT-large's 84.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCformer consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the ODE Transformer family (RK2-block gated and RK4-block [29]), PCformer gains 0.18–0.40 BLEU on WMT En-De/En-Fr and 0.21–0.52 BLEU on En-Ro, with the EMA coefficient learning providing a clear advantage over the gated fusion method. On OPUS-100, the 1.2B PCformer (35.0 SacreBLEU) beats the 3.8B DeepNet [61] (32.1) and BranchNorm [33] (32.3) by 2.7–2.9 points using only 1/3 the parameters, and even the 466M PCformer (32.6) exceeds both 3.8B models. On LM Harness, PCformer surpasses the Llama-like Transformer++ [58] by 1.7 points at 340M and 5.7 points at 1.3B parameters. On GLUE, PCformer improves over BERT-large [10] by 2.1 points on average (86.1 vs. 84.0). On Wikitext-103, PCformer (2-order) reduces PPL to 18.55, beating Shortformer [48] (19.78) and Adaptive Input [3] (21.00). On CNN/DailyMail, PCformer (4-order) achieves ROUGE-L 38.87, surpassing Surface Connection [32] (37.90) and RK4-block [29] (38.68). On time-series forecasting, PCformer beats Flowformer [65] by 2.0 points average accuracy across 10 UEA datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ODE Transformer line of work (Li et al., ACL 2022), which replaced the first-order Euler residual connection with high-order Runge-Kutta methods, by introducing a predictor-corrector paradigm that combines a high-order explicit predictor with an implicit multistep corrector. The key departure is the replacement of the gated fusion coefficient learning (which fails to generalize beyond 2nd order) with an exponential moving average (EMA) strategy that naturally assigns decreasing weights to earlier intermediate approximations and scales to arbitrary orders. The predictor-corrector structure itself is borrowed from classical numerical analysis (Adams-Bashforth-Moulton methods), repurposed here as a two-stage layer computation within the Transformer encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.2B PCformer surpasses the 3.8B DeepNet by 2.9 SacreBLEU on OPUS-100, demonstrating that architectural precision (reducing truncation error per layer) can substitute for raw parameter count. The EMA coefficient learning is a simple, general-purpose technique that any high-order ODE-based architecture could adopt. However, the main open question is inference efficiency: in encoder-only or decoder-only settings, the iterative inner-step computations make PCformer roughly 2× slower than vanilla Transformers, and the authors acknowledge that accelerating this (e.g., via latent-space high-order computation or distillation) is critical for LLM-scale deployment. Whether the predictor-corrector paradigm continues to yield gains at truly large scale (tens of billions of parameters) remains untested.
