## Problem

Black-box LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5) cannot be fine-tuned or aligned via RLHF due to inaccessible model parameters, forcing personalization to rely solely on prompt engineering. Existing prompt-based methods (ICL, RAG, PAG) encode each user independently, failing to capture shared global knowledge across the user group, and conflate retrieval relevance with actual usefulness for answering a new query. The paper asks: how can one perform learning-based personalization on black-box LLMs that simultaneously leverages user-specific preferences and cross-user shared knowledge?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's current query (text) and their full behavioral history (a set of past query–answer pairs), first retrieving top-N relevant records via BM25 or a dense retriever, then reranking to select top-k most useful items. The black-box LLM (gpt-3.5-turbo) generates b=8 candidate responses conditioned on the query plus reranked history; the HYDRA-Adapter then scores each candidate and returns the single best one as the final personalized output (a classification label, a 1–5 rating, or a generated headline/title). Both the reranker and adapter are lightweight LongFormer-Base (110M) models, keeping the personalization stack separate from the frozen black-box LLM.

## Method

HYDRA introduces a model factorization where each user's personalized model θ^(u) is decomposed into a shared base model σ (a LongFormer encoder) and a user-specific head τ^(u) (a single-layer FFN appended to the final hidden states), yielding a hydra-like architecture with one trunk and many heads. Two components are trained under this factorization: (1) a HYDRA-Reranker that scores retrieved history by usefulness (binary labels generated by checking whether the LLM's generation with that history matches ground truth), and (2) a HYDRA-Adapter that acts as an evaluator in a best-of-b rejection-sampling loop, selecting the candidate generation most aligned with the user's preference. During training, both the base model and all user heads are updated jointly via cross-entropy loss; for unseen test users, the base model is frozen and only the new user's head is fitted on their history, enabling rapid adaptation with minimal compute. This design lets the shared base encode global patterns across all users while the small per-user heads capture individual idiosyncrasies, outperforming prompt-only baselines by 9.01% on average across five LaMP tasks.

## Results

On the LaMP benchmark, HYDRA achieves an average relative improvement of 9.01% over the best-performing baselines across five personalization tasks. Specifically, HYDRA reaches 78.0% accuracy and 40.1% F-1 on LaMP-2N (vs. 68.0%/30.8% for the best baseline PAG), 54.0% accuracy and 45.8% F-1 on LaMP-2M, 0.400 MAE and 0.747 RMSE on LaMP-3, 0.178 ROUGE-1 and 2.396 BLEU on LaMP-4, and 0.434 ROUGE-1 and 7.531 BLEU on LaMP-5. Scale-up experiments with 1000 training users confirm that HYDRA maintains its advantage, achieving 74.8% accuracy on LaMP-2N and 0.328 MAE on LaMP-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYDRA outperforms all prompt-based baselines across all five LaMP tasks. Against zero-shot gpt-3.5-turbo, HYDRA improves accuracy by 12.0 points on LaMP-2N (78.0% vs. 66.0%) and reduces MAE by 16.7% on LaMP-3 (0.400 vs. 0.480). Against the strongest RAG baseline (k=4), HYDRA gains 14.0 points in accuracy on LaMP-2N (78.0% vs. 64.0%), 8.0 points on LaMP-2M (54.0% vs. 46.0%), and 31.0% in MAE reduction on LaMP-3 (0.400 vs. 0.580). Against PAG (k=1), HYDRA achieves 10.0 points higher accuracy on LaMP-2N (78.0% vs. 68.0%) and 14.6% lower MAE on LaMP-3 (0.400 vs. 0.560). In the adapter comparison (Table 8), HYDRA-Adapter beats BBox-Adapter on LaMP-3 (MAE 0.420 vs. 0.540) and LaMP-5 (ROUGE-1 0.409 vs. 0.394), but underperforms on LaMP-4 (ROUGE-1 0.145 vs. 0.151), suggesting the adapter's best-of-b rejection sampling is less effective for short headline generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYDRA extends the retrieval-augmented personalization line (LaMP, AuthorPred, Pearl) by replacing the single-stage relevance-based retrieval with a two-stage retrieve-then-rerank pipeline where the reranker is trained to assess *usefulness* rather than mere relevance. It departs from prompt-based personalization (ICL-Random, RAG, PAG) by introducing a learning-based paradigm for black-box LLMs, and from white-box personalization (fine-tuning, RLHF, LoRA/PEFT) by requiring no access to model parameters. The model factorization design—shared base model plus per-user heads—draws conceptually from multi-task learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but applies it to a lightweight adapter (LongFormer-Base) that scores LLM outputs via rejection sampling, building on the BBox-Adapter and CombLM lineage of black-box adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HYDRA is the first learning-based framework that personalizes black-box LLMs without requiring parameter access or fine-tuning APIs, making it practical for proprietary models like GPT-3.5-turbo where data privacy and cost are concerns. The model factorization (shared base + user-specific heads) offers a principled way to balance generalization across users with individual adaptation, and the empirical evidence that head-only updates outperform full-model updates for new users is practically useful. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly large user populations (beyond 1000), how it handles multi-modal personalization, and whether the reliance on LLM-as-labeler for training data generation introduces systematic biases. The framework's dependence on a specific black-box LLM (gpt-3.5-turbo) and its inability to support on-device inference limit immediate deployment in resource-constrained settings.
