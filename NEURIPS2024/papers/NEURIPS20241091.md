## Problem

Second-order optimizers like energy natural gradient descent (ENGD) achieve high accuracy on PINNs but require inverting a full D×D Gauss-Newton matrix at cubic per-iteration cost, limiting them to networks with a few thousand parameters. Existing KFAC approximations handle standard supervised losses but cannot capture the contributions from a PDE's differential operator (e.g., Laplacian terms) that appear in the PINN loss, leaving a gap in scalable second-order optimization for PINNs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE specification (differential operator, right-hand side, boundary data), a tanh-activated MLP architecture with scalar output, and sampled interior/boundary training points, then produces trained network parameters approximating the PDE solution. Unlike standard regression, the loss involves first- and second-order partial derivatives of the network evaluated at training points, so the curvature computation must account for the derivative computation graph rather than just the forward pass. The method is demonstrated on PDEs with up to 100 spatial dimensions and networks with over one million parameters, where exact ENGD is infeasible.

## Method

The key insight is interpreting the differential operator's computation graph via Taylor-mode automatic differentiation as a forward pass through a larger network with shared weights: for a linear layer, all partial derivatives (z, ∂_{x_i}z, ∂_{x_i,x_j}²z, …) are propagated by the same weight matrix W. This weight-sharing view enables applying the KFAC-expand approximation from Eschenhagen et al. (2023), which drops cross-terms between different shared vectors and factorizes the per-layer Gramian block as A⊗B, where A accumulates outer products of the stacked derivative inputs and B accumulates outer products of backpropagated residual gradients. For the Laplacian specifically, summing over i=j recovers the forward Laplacian framework, reducing the number of shared vectors from d(d+1)/2 to d+2. The resulting optimizer (KFAC or KFAC*) uses exponential moving averages of the Kronecker factors, constant damping, and either a logarithmic line search or an automatic learning-rate/momentum heuristic, with per-layer inversion cost O(h³) instead of O(h⁶) for the exact block.

## Results

The paper evaluates KFAC and KFAC* on five PDE problems (2D Poisson, (4+1)d heat equation, 5d/10d/100d Poisson, and (9+1)d Fokker-Planck) using relative L₂ error against wall-clock time. On the 2D Poisson equation with a shallow network (D=257), KFAC is competitive with full ENGD for a long phase before ENGD's cubic-cost inversion becomes prohibitive. For larger networks (D≈116k–1.3M), where ENGD runs out of memory, KFAC consistently outperforms SGD, Adam, and LBFGS while offering competitive performance with Hessian-free optimization across all tested PDE dimensions up to 100d. The key scalability result is that KFAC handles networks with up to ~1.3M parameters (100d Poisson) where exact second-order methods are infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ENGD (Müller & Zeinhofer, ICML 2023), KFAC is competitive on small problems (D=257, D≈10k) where the full Gramian can be stored and inverted, but ENGD runs out of memory for networks exceeding ~10k parameters, whereas KFAC scales to D≈1.3M. Against Hessian-free optimization (Martens, ICML 2010), KFAC achieves comparable final L₂ error on the (4+1)d heat equation, 10d Poisson, and (9+1)d Fokker-Planck equation, though Hessian-free sometimes converges slightly faster in wall-clock time due to its adaptive damping and CG backtracking heuristics. KFAC consistently and substantially outperforms all first-order baselines (SGD, Adam) and the quasi-Newton method LBFGS across every problem and network size tested. The paper notes that KFAC benefits from reusing the same mini-batch for ~100 iterations (rather than resampling each step), which was verified not to provide an unfair advantage through a separate Bayesian search experiment that also tuned batch sampling frequency for all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (KFAC) from supervised regression losses to PINN losses that involve derivatives of the network rather than function evaluations. The key conceptual bridge is interpreting the differential operator's computation graph via Taylor-mode automatic differentiation as a forward network with shared weights, which then allows direct application of the recently developed KFAC formulation for linear weight-sharing layers (Eschenhagen et al., 2023). For the Laplace operator specifically, this recovers and generalizes the forward Laplacian framework (Li et al., 2023), a connection the authors note has not been previously pointed out. The optimizer design reuses components from both original KFAC (Martens & Grosse, 2015) and energy natural gradient descent (Müller & Zeinhofer, 2023), positioning the work as a scalable approximation of the latter's exact Gramian inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINNs are notoriously difficult to optimize with first-order methods, and while second-order approaches like ENGD achieve high accuracy, they require cubic-cost matrix inversions that prohibit scaling beyond a few thousand parameters. This work provides a principled, computationally tractable path to second-order optimization for much larger networks and higher-dimensional PDEs, with the Taylor-mode-to-weight-sharing reformulation being broadly applicable to arbitrary architectures and PDE orders. Limitations include current implementation restricted to MLPs and Poisson/heat equations, the observation that KFAC benefits from less frequent batch resampling (suggesting the approximation needs more iterations per batch to converge), and unexplored improvements like adaptive damping, structured Kronecker factors, and merging interior/boundary approximations. Open questions include whether the KFAC-expand approximation remains tight for strongly coupled or nonlinear PDE systems and whether the approach transfers to convolutional or attention-based architectures.
