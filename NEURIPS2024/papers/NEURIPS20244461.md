## Problem

Current RL-based macro placement methods formulate the task as placing all macros from scratch, which limits the state information available to the policy (only previously placed macros are visible) and produces sparse or inaccurate reward signals. Additionally, these methods largely ignore regularity—a critical industry metric that prevents macro blockage in the chip core—leading to poor PPA outcomes despite good proxy wirelength scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244461/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing macro placement (typically from DREAMPlace) along with the netlist and chip canvas, and produces a refined placement by sequentially adjusting one macro at a time. The chip canvas is discretized into a 224×224 grid, and the state is represented as pixel-level image masks: the current canvas, a PositionMask (which only blocks already-adjusted macros, allowing broader repositioning), a WireMask normalized to [-1,1] (unlike MaskPlace's [0,1]), and a RegularMask encoding per-position regularity change. The output is a sequence of discrete grid-coordinate actions (N² possible positions per step) that collectively yield the final refined layout.

## Method

MaskRegulate reformulates macro placement as an RL regulation problem: instead of placing macros from scratch, the policy refines an existing layout by adjusting one macro per step, giving it access to all macro positions simultaneously and enabling denser, more accurate reward computation. The architecture mirrors MaskPlace's visual CNN pipeline (ResNet-18 encoder, deconvolution decoder over 224×224 masks) but introduces a RegularMask that quantifies how each candidate position changes the macro's distance-to-edge regularity score, and a composite reward r = α·r_wire + (1−α)·r_reg (α=0.7) that balances HPWL reduction against regularity improvement. Both reward components are normalized to [0,1] to prevent scale dominance. The policy is trained with PPO for 1000 episodes (half the 2000 used by MaskPlace), and the regulator can be applied to refine placements from any upstream method, not just DREAMPlace.

## Results

On the ICCAD 2015 benchmark (8 chips), MaskRegulate achieves the best average rank across all proxy and PPA metrics, with average ranks of 1.25 (Global HPWL), 1.0 (Regularity), 1.125 (routed wirelength), and 1.5 (violation points). On superblue1, MaskRegulate reduces global HPWL to 5.77×10⁸ (vs. 7.93 for MaskPlace and 8.96 for DREAMPlace) and achieves the lowest routed wirelength of 116.11 m. Compared to the closest RL baseline MaskPlace, MaskRegulate improves routing wirelength by 17.08%, horizontal congestion overflow by 73.08%, vertical congestion overflow by 38.81%, worst negative slack by 18.35%, total negative slack by 37.89%, and violation points by 46.17%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskRegulate outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across all 8 ICCAD 2015 chips. Against the analytical placer DREAMPlace, MaskRegulate achieves substantially lower global HPWL (e.g., 5.77 vs. 8.96 on superblue1) and dramatically better congestion (rO-H: 1.26% vs. 17.15%) and timing (TNS: −1.33 vs. −2.91). Against the BBO method WireMask-EA, MaskRegulate wins on global HPWL, regularity, and most PPA metrics, though WireMask-EA occasionally matches on congestion (e.g., superblue10 rO-V: 0.09 vs. 0.55). Against the closest RL baseline MaskPlace (which shares the same CNN architecture and HPWL reward), MaskRegulate consistently wins on every metric, with the largest gains in congestion overflow (73.08% horizontal, 38.81% vertical reduction) and violation points (46.17% reduction). Against AutoDMP, MaskRegulate dominates on regularity and most PPA metrics, though AutoDMP occasionally achieves lower HPWL on individual chips (e.g., superblue4: 4.57 vs. 4.15). In the appendix comparison with ChiPFormer (a transformer-based offline RL placer), MaskRegulate outperforms on all 8 chips in both global HPWL and regularity, with the largest gap on superblue7 (7.90 vs. 16.81 HPWL). Notably, MaskRegulate also demonstrates superior generalization: when pre-trained on 4 chips and tested on 4 unseen chips, it consistently outperforms MaskPlace and even achieves better HPWL on some unseen chips than on training chips.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MaskPlace (Lai et al., NeurIPS 2022), reusing its visual representation architecture (ResNet-18 encoder, deconvolution decoder, PPO) and WireMask state encoding, but fundamentally redefines the RL problem from placing macros from scratch to refining an existing placement. It departs from the broader RL placement lineage (GraphPlace, DeepPR, PRNet, ChiPFormer) by arguing that the placer formulation inherently suffers from sparse/inaccurate rewards and incomplete state information, whereas the regulator formulation provides dense, precise signals because all macros are already positioned. The introduction of regularity as both a state channel (RegularMask) and a reward component is a novel addition not present in any prior RL placement method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that reformulating RL as a refinement/regulation task rather than a generation task yields substantially better PPA outcomes (73% congestion overflow reduction, 38% TNS improvement over MaskPlace) with the same architecture—opens a broader paradigm where learned policies can act as post-hoc optimizers for any initial solution, not just from-scratch generators. Limitations include the absence of aspect-ratio and area constraints, no timing-driven training signal, and reliance on a single benchmark (ICCAD 2015) for PPA evaluation, leaving open whether the regulator formulation transfers to other design styles or scales. The finding that the regulator generalizes better to unseen chips than the placer suggests a path toward more transferable placement policies, though the paper does not yet explore transformer-based architectures for this purpose.
