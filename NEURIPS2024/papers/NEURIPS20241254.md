## Problem

Federated Q-learning algorithms face a fundamental tension between sample complexity and communication complexity, but no prior work had established lower bounds on the communication required to achieve any statistical benefit of collaboration, nor had any algorithm simultaneously achieved order-optimal sample and communication complexities. This paper provides a complete characterization of the sample-communication trade-off by proving converse results and constructing an algorithm that operates on the optimal frontier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241254/cropped_page45_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M agents draws N i.i.d. samples from a generative model for every (s,a) pair in a finite MDP with state space S and action space A, and the algorithm is parameterized by a target error ε and failure probability δ. The output is a single ε-optimal Q-function estimate in the ℓ∞ norm over the tabular state-action space, obtained after K = O(log(1/ε)) epochs of collaborative refinement. Communication occurs intermittently through a central server using stochastic quantization, so the bit-level cost per coordinate is explicitly controlled by a resolution parameter J rather than assuming infinite-precision real-number transmission.

## Method

Fed-DVR-Q proceeds in epochs, each reducing the suboptimality gap ‖Q^(k) − Q⋆‖∞ by a factor of 2 via a REFINEESTIMATE subroutine. Within each epoch, agents first collaboratively build a compressed estimate of the Bellman operator applied to the current iterate (the first variance-reduction step), then perform I local iterations using doubly variance-reduced updates that subtract the stale operator estimate and add the fresh one (the second variance-reduction step). The key design choice replacing the standard multiple-small-local-updates scheme is a single minibatched update per iteration, which avoids the positive over-estimation bias (client drift) that accumulates from sequential noisy local steps and thereby permits far fewer communication rounds. A stochastic quantizer with J bits per coordinate controls the bit-level communication cost, and the recentering sample size L_k follows a two-stage schedule (growing then shrinking) to achieve the optimal (1−γ)⁻³ dependence. Together with the lower bound showing Ω(1/((1−γ)log²N)) rounds are necessary for any linear speedup, Fed-DVR-Q's Õ(1/(1−γ)) rounds and Õ(|S||A|/(1−γ)) bits per agent close the gap and establish the tight sample-communication frontier.

## Results

Fed-DVR-Q achieves a sample complexity of Õ(|S||A|/(M(1−γ)³ε²)) i.i.d. samples per agent and a communication complexity of Õ(1/(1−γ)) rounds and Õ(|S||A|/(1−γ)) bits per agent, matching the established lower bound on both metrics. This represents the first Federated Q-learning algorithm to simultaneously achieve order-optimal sample complexity (with linear speedup in M) and minimal communication complexity. The lower bound (Theorem 1) shows that any intermittent-communication Federated Q-learning algorithm achieving linear speedup in sample complexity must incur at least Ω(1/((1−γ)log²N)) communication rounds and Ω(|S||A|/((1−γ)log²N)) bits per agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-agent Q-learning baseline of Li et al. (2023), Fed-DVR-Q improves the dependence on the discount factor from (1−γ)⁻⁴ to (1−γ)⁻³ and introduces a linear speedup in the number of agents M. Compared to the single-agent Variance Reduced Q-learning of Wainwright (2019b), Fed-DVR-Q matches the optimal (1−γ)⁻³ sample complexity while adding the linear speedup in M that the single-agent method cannot provide. Most significantly, against the prior federated baseline Fed-SynQ (Woo et al., 2023), Fed-DVR-Q improves the sample complexity from (1−γ)⁻⁵ to (1−γ)⁻³ (a two-power improvement in the effective horizon) and reduces the per-agent communication cost from M/(1−γ) to 1/(1−γ) rounds (an M-fold reduction). Fed-DVR-Q is the first algorithm to simultaneously match both the sample complexity lower bound of Azar et al. (2013) and the communication complexity lower bound established in this work, thereby achieving the optimal operating point on the sample-communication trade-off frontier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent variance-reduced Q-learning framework of Wainwright (2019b) and the minimax-optimal Q-learning analysis of Li et al. (2023) to the federated setting with intermittent communication. Prior federated Q-learning algorithms (Woo et al. 2023, Khodadadian et al. 2022) achieved suboptimal (1-γ)^5 sample complexity and communication costs scaling with the number of agents M, leaving a gap between upper and lower bounds. The key methodological departure is replacing multiple small local updates between communication rounds with a single minibatched variance-reduced update, which eliminates the 'client drift' bias that forces more frequent averaging in prior approaches, combined with stochastic quantization to control bit-level costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete (tight) characterization of the sample-communication trade-off in federated Q-learning, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds for the first time. The lower bound revealing that Ω(1/(1-γ)) communication rounds are necessary for any speedup — in contrast to policy evaluation where one round suffices — highlights a fundamental consequence of the Bellman optimality operator's nonlinearity that the field had not previously isolated. Open questions include extending these results to function approximation, finite-horizon MDPs, and algorithm classes beyond intermittent communication, all of which would be needed for practical deployment in high-dimensional settings.
