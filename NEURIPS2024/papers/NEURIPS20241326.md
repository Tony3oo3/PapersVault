## Problem

Estimating the full spatial distribution of glioblastoma tumor cells—including undetectable low-concentration infiltrating cells—remains a critical gap in personalized radiotherapy planning. Hard-constrained PDE simulations are too rigid to capture biological complexity and struggle with unknown initial conditions, while purely data-driven models lack the biological priors needed when clinical datasets contain only a few dozen patients. No prior hybrid approach has successfully coupled tumor growth dynamics with tissue biomechanics (elasticity) in a soft-constrained, data-assimilated framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preprocessed multi-parametric MRI (T1Gd, T1, FLAIR) segmentations, a FET-PET metabolic map, and brain tissue probability maps (WM/GM/CSF) obtained via non-rigid atlas registration with tumor masking. It produces a continuous 3D spatiotemporal tumor cell density field on a 72×72×72×96 grid (152M unknowns), an estimated pre-deformation healthy brain anatomy, patient-specific system identification parameters (diffusivities, growth rate, tumor-tissue coupling γ), and a radiotherapy target volume isosurface matched in total volume to the standard 1.5 cm margin plan.

## Method

The core contribution is a physics-regularized discrete loss optimized directly on a dynamic Lagrangian mesh, where particles carry tissue intensities and deform with the brain, naturally absorbing advection into particle motion. The tumor growth equation (reaction-diffusion-advection) is discretized via implicit Euler with cell-centered finite volumes on the deforming mesh, while the Neo-Hookean elasticity equation is discretized via central finite differences on the initial reference grid; both residuals serve as soft regularization terms rather than hard constraints. A trilinear shape-function projection bridges the Lagrangian particle frame and a static Eulerian grid for aligning predictions with imaging observations. The total loss combines physics residuals, initial-condition assumptions (Gaussian tumor blob, multi-resolution brain symmetry), and imaging data reconstruction terms (core, edema, FET-PET correlation, tissue matching), all optimized jointly with system identification parameters using Adam on a 4-level multi-resolution grid for convergence in ~3 hours on a single GPU.

## Results

On a cohort of 58 glioblastoma patients with multi-modal MRI and FET-PET imaging, the proposed method achieves 74.7±3.1% Recurrence Coverage (Any) and 89.9±2.7% Recurrence Coverage (Enhancing Core), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Static Grid Discretization (72.9% and 89.0%) and the clinical Standard Plan (70.0% and 87.3%). The method also outperforms unconstrained nnU-Net (59.0% and 66.8%), physics-constrained CNNs (70.4% and 84.3%), and hard-constrained numerical physics simulations (67.1% and 86.2%). Statistical significance is confirmed via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p=0.014 vs. Standard Plan, p=0.043 vs. Static Grid for Any recurrence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on both recurrence definitions. Against the closest competitor, Static Grid Discretization (GliODIL, the previous SOTA), it gains +1.8% on Any recurrence (74.7% vs. 72.9%) and +0.9% on Enhancing Core (89.9% vs. 89.0%), with the advantage attributed to dynamic Lagrangian tissue deformation that the static grid cannot model. Versus the clinical Standard Plan (1.5 cm uniform margin), the improvement is more substantial: +4.7% (Any) and +2.6% (Enhancing Core). The unconstrained nnU-Net baseline lags far behind at 59.0% (Any) and 66.8% (Enhancing Core), confirming that purely data-driven approaches struggle with the small 58-patient cohort. The physics-constrained CNN (Learn-Morph-Infer) achieves 70.4% (Any) and 84.3% (Enhancing Core), while hard-constrained numerical physics simulations reach 67.1% and 86.2%, both underperforming the proposed soft-constrained hybrid. The ablation variant without inferable anatomy (73.4% Any) shows that learning the initial healthy brain geometry contributes an additional +1.3% over the static-grid baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ODIL (Optimizing a Discrete Loss) framework [Karnakov et al., 2024] and its glioma-specific instantiation GliODIL [Balcerak et al., 2023] by replacing the static Eulerian grid with a dynamic Lagrangian mesh of particles that deform with the tissue. The key conceptual departure is the first successful soft-constrained (as opposed to hard PDE-solved) coupling of a reaction-diffusion-advection tumor growth equation with a Neo-Hookean tissue elasticity equation, using a Lagrangian-Eulerian projection to bridge the moving particle frame with the fixed imaging grid. Unlike PINNs, which parametrize solutions via MLPs, the method directly optimizes discretized field values on the deforming mesh, inheriting ODIL's local-dependency advantage while adding the biomechanical dimension absent from all prior hybrid approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art recurrence coverage (74.7% vs. 72.9% for the prior SOTA) and, as a byproduct, recovers the pre-deformation healthy anatomy, which has independent clinical value for assessing surgical resection extent. The finding that hard-constrained numerical simulations (67.1%) underperform soft-constrained discrete optimization (74.7%) challenges the assumption that stricter PDE enforcement always yields better personalization. Open questions include scalability to larger deformations causing material rupture, applicability to PDEs with narrow-phase interfaces (where multi-resolution acceleration fails), and whether the 58-patient cohort is sufficient to validate the approach before clinical translation.
