## Problem

Diffusion model inference is bottlenecked by sequential noise prediction steps, causing prohibitive latency for interactive applications. Existing acceleration methods (step reduction, architecture optimization, parallel inference) apply a fixed acceleration mode to all prompts, yet different prompts exhibit varying redundancy in their denoising paths. No prior training-free method adaptively decides which noise prediction steps to skip on a per-prompt basis while guaranteeing near-lossless output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play wrapper around any pretrained diffusion model (SDXL, SD-1.5, I2VGen-XL, ModelScopeT2V, LDM-4) and works with arbitrary sampling schedulers (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, Euler, SDE-DPM). It consumes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts, reference images, or class labels—and produces the same outputs (images at 512×512 or 1024×1024, or video sequences), with the key property that the generated result is nearly identical to the full-step denoising output. The only additional runtime inputs are two hyperparameters: a relative threshold δ and a maximum continuous skipping step C_max.

## Method

AdaptiveDiffusion skips noise prediction model inference at selected timesteps while still performing the latent update at every step, reusing the cached noise estimate from the last computed step. The skip decision is governed by a third-order latent difference criterion: at each timestep, the norm of the third-order difference Δ³x_{i-1} is compared against a scaled first-order difference δ‖Δx_i‖; if the ratio falls below threshold, the noise prediction is reused. The paper proves that the one-step skipping error is upper-bounded by O(t_i − t_{i+1}) + O(x_i − x_{i+1}), and derives that the difference between neighboring noise predictions is asymptotically O(Δ³x_{i-1}), providing theoretical justification for the third-order estimator. A maximum continuous skipping cap C_max prevents unbounded error accumulation during long skip runs. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no architectural modification, and is validated to closely match a greedy-searched optimal skipping path via χ² statistical tests.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017 (T2I), AdaptiveDiffusion achieves up to 2.01× speedup with SDXL+Euler (PSNR 24.33, LPIPS 0.168, FID 6.11) while maintaining near-lossless quality versus full-step generation. On ImageNet 256×256 (class-conditional), the method reaches 5.56× speedup with LDM-4 (PSNR 25.79, LPIPS 0.090, FID 4.91). For video generation, I2VGen-XL achieves 1.93× speedup with PSNR 27.20 and FVD 274.5, and ModelScopeT2V achieves 1.46× speedup with PSNR 27.70 and FVD 470.6, both substantially outperforming DeepCache on per-frame quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCache, AdaptiveDiffusion consistently wins on all quality metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID) across every model–scheduler combination while achieving comparable speedup ratios (e.g., 2.01× vs 2.16× on SDXL+Euler, 1.93× vs 2.00× on I2VGen-XL). The quality gap is substantial: on I2VGen-XL video generation, AdaptiveDiffusion achieves +6.38 dB PSNR (27.20 vs 20.82) and a 3.3× lower FVD (274.5 vs 916.0) over DeepCache. Against Adaptive DPM-Solver (compared only on SD-1-5+DPM++), AdaptiveDiffusion dominates on every metric: PSNR 23.2 vs 19.0, LPIPS 0.092 vs 0.195, FID 4.06 vs 6.38, and speedup 1.57× vs 1.25×. The only setting where DeepCache edges out AdaptiveDiffusion on speed is SDXL+Euler (2.16× vs 2.01×), but at a clear quality cost (LPIPS 0.223 vs 0.168). On ImageNet with LDM-4, DeepCache achieves a slightly higher speedup (6.35× vs 5.56×) but worse FID (5.22 vs 4.91) and LPIPS (0.098 vs 0.090).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the three established paradigms of diffusion acceleration—reducing sampling steps (DDIM, DPM-Solver, distillation), optimizing model architecture (DeepCache, Diff-pruning), and parallelizing inference (ParaDiGMS, DistriFusion)—by introducing a fourth axis: prompt-adaptive step skipping. Unlike DeepCache, which caches intermediate UNet features at fixed intervals, AdaptiveDiffusion skips entire noise prediction steps guided by a third-order latent difference criterion, and unlike DPM-Solver variants that use a fixed solver schedule, it produces a different skipping path per prompt. The theoretical error bounds (via Lipschitz continuity of the noise predictor) connect to the ODE solver literature, while the specific use of third-order (rather than first- or second-order) differences as the skipping signal is a novel repurposing of numerical differentiation concepts for inference-time computation scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of the method makes it immediately deployable across existing diffusion pipelines (SDXL, I2VGen-XL, ModelScopeT2V, LDM-4) with 2–5× speedup and near-lossless quality, which is practically significant for interactive and real-time applications. The prompt-adaptive framing opens a broader question: how much of the denoising trajectory is truly prompt-dependent versus model-dependent, and whether similar adaptive criteria could govern other inference-time decisions (e.g., early exit, dynamic resolution). Limitations include restricted evaluation to image/video modalities, degraded performance at extreme few-step settings (≤10 steps), and the need for an additional estimator for SDE solvers—suggesting the third-order criterion, while well-motivated, is not yet a universal signal.
