## Problem

Existing confidence sequences for generalized linear models either target specific distributions (Gaussian, Bernoulli) and carry poly(S) factors in their radius, or are generic but scale with the globally worst-case curvature of the inverse link function, which grows exponentially in S for Bernoulli. This paper provides a unified, poly(S)-free likelihood-ratio confidence sequence for all self-concordant GLMs and shows that a purely optimistic UCB algorithm can achieve state-of-the-art regret without explicit warmup phases.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a context vector x_t from an adversarially chosen arm set X_t ⊆ B^d(1), observes a reward r_t drawn from a GLM with unknown parameter θ* ∈ B^d(S), and maintains a norm-constrained MLE θ̂_t. The output is a time-uniform confidence set C_t(δ) centered at θ̂_t with radius β_t(δ)² = log(1/δ) + inf_{c_t} {d log(1/c_t) + 2SL_t c_t}, where L_t is a data-dependent Lipschitz constant of the negative log-likelihood. For self-concordant GLMs, an ellipsoidal relaxation E_t(δ,λ) is available, enabling closed-form bonus-based arm selection without solving a convex program per arm.

## Method

The confidence sequence is derived via a time-uniform PAC-Bayesian bound (martingale + Donsker-Varadhan KL representation + Ville's inequality) with the unusual choice of a uniform prior Q = Unif(Θ) and a time-dependent uniform posterior P_t = Unif((1−c_t)θ̂_t + c_t Θ), where c_t is optimized to balance the KL term d log(1/c_t) against a Lipschitz-based loss bound 2SL_t c_t. For self-concordant GLMs, an ellipsoidal relaxation is obtained with radius γ_t(δ) = 2(1+SR_s)(4S²λ + β_t(δ)²). The OFUGLB algorithm applies the standard optimistic principle using this CS, and its regret analysis introduces a new Cauchy-Schwartz decomposition w.r.t. a matrix Ẑ_t(θ̂_t) derived from the integral remainder of a first-order Taylor expansion, completely bypassing the self-concordant control lemma that previously forced an extra S factor in the leading regret term.

## Results

OFUGLB achieves state-of-the-art regret bounds across all self-concordant GLBs. For logistic bandits, the leading term is poly(S)-free: Õ(d√(T/κ⋆(T)) + d²κ(T)), removing the S² factor present in prior work (Sawarni et al., 2024). For bounded GLBs, the transient term scales as d²RR_μκ(T) versus d²R⁵S²κ_X(T) previously. In experiments on logistic bandits (T=10000, d=2, time-varying arm-sets of size 20, S∈{4,6,8,10}), OFUGLB achieves the best or near-best regret for S∈{8,10}, outperforming all five baselines including the heuristic-tuned EVILL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFUGLB outperforms all five baselines in the logistic bandit experiments. Against EMK (Emmenegger et al., 2023), OFUGLB achieves at-par or better regret while using exact theoretical hyperparameters without tuning; both outperform the remaining three baselines. Against EVILL (Janz et al., 2024), OFUGLB matches or slightly exceeds regret performance despite EVILL using a single heuristic hyperparameter, highlighting the practical strength of the theoretical CS. Against RS-GLinCB (Sawarni et al., 2024), OFUGLB is substantially better in practice: RS-GLinCB with its exact theoretical hyperparameters performs the worst of all algorithms, likely due to excessive exploration from its switching criterion always being triggered. Against OFULog+ (Lee et al., 2024), OFUGLB achieves better regret despite OFULog+ producing a tighter confidence set at the final time, suggesting the interplay between CS tightness and arm-set geometry matters. Against ada-OFU-ECOLog (Faury et al., 2022), OFUGLB clearly dominates in both regret and confidence set tightness. Theoretically, OFUGLB's key advantage over all prior methods is the poly(S)-free leading term for logistic bandits without requiring an explicit warmup phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the time-uniform PAC-Bayesian framework of Chugg et al. (2023) (martingale + Donsker-Varadhan + Ville's inequality) but departs from the standard practice of using Gaussian or data-dependent priors by employing a uniform prior/posterior, an idea borrowed from universal portfolios (Blum and Kalai, 1999) and fast rates in online learning (Foster et al., 2018; Hazan et al., 2007). For the bandit application, it extends the UCB paradigm for GLBs (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011; Faury et al., 2020; Abeille et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2024) by introducing a new regret analysis that completely bypasses the self-concordant control lemma (Faury et al., 2020, Lemma 9), which was the key source of poly(S) dependencies in all prior optimistic algorithms for logistic bandits. The unified CS subsumes and improves upon prior CSs for Gaussian (Flynn et al., 2023), Bernoulli (Lee et al., 2024), and generic GLMs (Emmenegger et al., 2023; Jun et al., 2017; Li et al., 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open problem from Lee et al. (2024) by eliminating the poly(S) factor from the Bernoulli CS radius and, as a consequence, provides the first purely optimistic (no warmup) UCB algorithm achieving a poly(S)-free leading regret term for logistic bandits. It also delivers the first regret bound for contextual Poisson bandits without reward boundedness. Key open questions include: extending the CS to RKHS (blocked by the non-existence of uniform measures in infinite dimensions), whether the CS radius is information-theoretically optimal, whether an arm-set-geometry-adaptive transient term can be combined with the poly(S)-free leading term, and whether computational efficiency can be improved while maintaining the same statistical guarantees. The experimental validation is limited to d=2 logistic bandits with T=10000, leaving open questions about scalability.
