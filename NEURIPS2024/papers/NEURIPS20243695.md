## Problem

Rácz and Sridhar (NeurIPS 2021) determined the information-theoretic threshold for exact graph matching in correlated stochastic block models (SBMs) but relied on an inefficient (non-polynomial-time) algorithm, leaving open whether an efficient algorithm exists whenever exact matching is information-theoretically possible. This paper resolves that open problem by giving the first polynomial-time algorithm for graph matching in correlated SBMs with two balanced communities, achieving almost exact matching whenever the squared edge correlation s² exceeds Otter's tree-counting constant α ≈ 0.338, and exact matching whenever s²(a+b)/2 > 1.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two n×n adjacency matrices A and B from correlated SBMs with logarithmic average degree (p = a·log n/n, q = b·log n/n) and edge correlation parameter s, but critically does NOT receive the true community labels as input. It outputs a vertex matching permutation π̂ (exact or almost exact, matching (1−o(1))n vertices correctly) and, as a byproduct, community label estimates σ̂_A and σ̂_B used internally for adjacency matrix centralization. The community labels must be estimated from each graph individually, and the resulting estimation errors (polynomial in n when s·D₊(a,b) < 1) are the central technical obstacle the algorithm must overcome.

## Method

The algorithm generalizes the chandelier counting framework of Mao et al. (STOC 2023) from correlated Erdős–Rényi graphs to correlated SBMs in three stages: (1) run the Mossel–Neeman–Sly spectral community recovery to obtain approximate community labels, (2) compute signed subgraph counts over a family of (L,M,K,R,D)-chandeliers using approximately centralized adjacency matrices (subtracting p or q based on estimated rather than true community labels), and (3) boost almost-exact matching to exact matching via a seeded common-neighbor algorithm. The key technical challenge is that when exact community recovery is impossible (s·D₊(a,b) < 1), the community label errors cause some edges to be incorrectly centralized, making cross-moment calculations substantially more complex. The authors show that the probability of all z single-occurring edges in a union graph being simultaneously mis-centralized is at most n^{−z(sD₊(a,b)−ε)/D}, which is negligible for the moment bounds needed. A color-coding scheme (N+1 random colors, counting only colorful subgraphs) converts the quasi-polynomial-time exact chandelier count into a polynomial-time unbiased estimator with only a constant-factor variance increase.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that for correlated SBMs with logarithmic average degree (p = a·log n/n, q = b·log n/n), a polynomial-time algorithm achieves almost exact graph matching (matching (1−o(1))n vertices correctly with high probability) whenever the edge correlation satisfies s² ≥ α + ε, where α ≈ 0.338 is Otter's tree-counting constant. Exact graph matching is achieved whenever the additional information-theoretic condition s²(a+b)/2 > 1 also holds, matching the fundamental limit of Rácz and Sridhar [57]. The algorithm runs in O(n^C) time for a constant C depending only on ε, via a color-coding approximation of signed chandelier subgraph counts. As a corollary (Theorem 2), exact community recovery is possible from two correlated SBMs in regimes where it is information-theoretically impossible from a single graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical results. Rácz and Sridhar [57] (NeurIPS 2021) determined the information-theoretic threshold for exact graph matching in correlated SBMs (s²(a+b)/2 = 1) but only via an exponential-time algorithm, leaving the efficient algorithm as an open problem that this paper resolves. Mao, Wu, Xu, and Yu [41] (STOC 2023) developed the chandelier counting algorithm for correlated Erdős–Rényi graphs (the a = b special case); this paper extends it to general two-community SBMs with a ≠ b, overcoming the additional challenge of imperfect community label estimation. Yang, Shin, and Chung [67] (ICML 2023) also studied SBM graph matching but required known community labels, average degree at least (log n)^1.1, at least 3 communities, and correlation s > 1 − ε₀; this paper removes all four of these assumptions, working in the most natural two-community setting at logarithmic average degree with the weaker condition s² > α. Gaudio, Rácz, and Sridhar [25] (COLT 2022) characterized the information-theoretic threshold for community recovery from correlated SBMs; this paper provides the first efficient algorithm achieving that threshold in the regime where exact graph matching is possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chandelier counting algorithm of Mao, Wu, Xu, and Yu (STOC 2023) from correlated Erdős-Rényi graphs to correlated stochastic block models, resolving an open problem posed by Rácz and Sridhar (NeurIPS 2021) who had determined the information-theoretic threshold but only via an inefficient algorithm. The key technical departure is handling the fact that in SBMs, the adjacency matrix cannot be exactly centered without knowing community labels, and in the relevant logarithmic-degree regime, community labels cannot be exactly recovered from a single graph. The paper also builds on the community recovery algorithm of Mossel, Neeman, and Sly and the information-theoretic analysis of Gaudio, Rácz, and Sridhar (COLT 2022) for correlated SBMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient (polynomial-time) algorithm for graph matching in correlated SBMs, and it achieves the conjectured computational threshold s² > α ≈ 0.338 (Otter's constant), matching the Erdős-Rényi case. As a direct application, it enables exact community recovery from multiple correlated graphs in parameter regimes where this is information-theoretically impossible from a single graph. Open questions include whether the s² > α threshold is truly necessary (a conjectured information-computation gap exists below it), whether efficient exact community recovery is possible in regimes where exact graph matching is not, and how to extend the results to k-community SBMs and sparser/denser degree regimes.
