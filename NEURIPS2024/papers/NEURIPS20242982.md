## Problem

While vision foundation models (VFMs) inherit strong generalization from large-scale pre-training, their style-invariant properties remain unexplored for domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS). The domain gap in DGSS is primarily driven by cross-domain style variations (urban landscape, weather, lighting) rather than content differences, yet existing low-rank adaptation methods for fine-tuning VFMs do not explicitly decouple style from content, leaving the model vulnerable to domain-specific appearance shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242982/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving scene images resized to 512×512 pixels, which are processed through a frozen VFM backbone (DINOv2-Large by default, also tested with CLIP, SAM, and EVA02). At each frozen transformer layer, the intermediate features are decomposed into frequency components, adapted via learnable tokens, and recombined before passing to the next layer. The final output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 19 common scene categories, produced by a Mask2Former decoder that operates on the adapted VFM features.

## Method

FADA applies a Haar wavelet transform at each frozen VFM layer to decompose features into a low-frequency component (LL, capturing scene content) and three high-frequency components (LH, HL, HH, capturing style details such as edges and textures). The low-frequency branch uses learnable tokens with low-rank decomposition (rank r=16) to attend to and reinforce content-relevant features via a softmax similarity map and MLP projection. The high-frequency branch similarly uses tokens but applies instance normalization to the token-feature similarity map, suppressing domain-specific style responses while preserving structural information. The orthogonal property of the Haar basis provides stronger decorrelation than FFT, enabling cleaner separation of style and content. After both branches process their respective components, an inverse Haar transform fuses them back into spatial features that feed the next frozen VFM layer, with only the frequency tokens (≈11.65M parameters) being trained.

## Results

FADA achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all three standard DGSS evaluation settings (G→{C,B,M,S}, S→{C,B,M,G}, C→{B,M,G,S}), outperforming the closest VFM-based baseline REIN by 1.19–1.99% mIoU on every target domain. Against the best Mask2Former-based method (CMFormer), FADA gains an average of ~10% mIoU, and against the best ResNet-based methods (e.g., BlindNet), the average improvement exceeds 20%. On the ACDC adverse-condition benchmark (C→{Fog, Night, Rain, Snow}), FADA reaches a mean mIoU of 71.5%, surpassing REIN (69.6%) by up to 2.9% on the Snow domain. FADA also generalizes to other VFMs (CLIP, SAM, EVA02, DINOv2), consistently outperforming REIN by 1.3–2.1% average mIoU with only ~11–17M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADA consistently outperforms REIN [69], the strongest VFM-based DGSS method, by 1.19–1.99% mIoU across all 12 source-to-target domain pairs in the three standard settings, with the largest gains on G→M (+1.99%) and C→M (+1.83%). Against the best Mask2Former-based method CMFormer [8], FADA achieves an average improvement of roughly 10% mIoU (e.g., 68.23 vs 55.31 on G→C), and against the best ResNet-based methods (BlindNet [2], SAW [58]), the gap exceeds 20% mIoU on average. On the ACDC adverse-condition benchmark, FADA beats REIN by 0.7–2.9% per condition (mean 71.5% vs 69.6%), with the largest margin on Snow (+2.9%). FADA also surpasses all PEFT baselines in Table 7 (LoRA, AdaptFormer, VPT, FourierFT, WHT) on the G→{C,B,M} setting, achieving 66.1% average mIoU with DINOv2 versus 63.7% for FourierFT and 63.3% for VPT. The method is versatile across VFMs: it improves over REIN by 1.5% (CLIP), 2.1% (SAM), 1.3% (EVA02), and 1.8% (DINOv2) in average mIoU, confirming that the frequency-adapted low-rank scheme is not tied to a single backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the REIN paradigm of token-based low-rank adaptation for VFM fine-tuning in DGSS by relocating the adaptation from the spatial domain to the Haar wavelet frequency domain. Prior DGSS methods either decouple style from content in the spatial feature space (IBN, ISW, SAW), enhance content via masked attention (HGFormer, CMFormer), or augment style diversity during training (SHADE, WildNet, AdvStyle), but none exploit the frequency decomposition of frozen VFM features. The key conceptual departure is treating the low-rank adaptation as a frequency-adapted operation: the low-frequency branch stabilizes scene content while the high-frequency branch, augmented with instance normalization on the token-feature similarity map, suppresses domain-specific style responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frequency-space low-rank adaptation is a versatile and effective paradigm for VFM fine-tuning in DGSS, with consistent gains across DINOv2, CLIP, SAM, and EVA02 backbones. The use of Haar wavelet (rather than the more common FFT) is motivated by its orthogonality providing stronger decorrelation between content and style, though the practical advantage over FFT-based frequency methods in this specific VFM setting remains to be further validated. The ~6M additional trainable parameters over REIN is a modest cost, but the approach's transferability to other VFM downstream tasks sensitive to style-content separation (e.g., domain-generalized detection or depth estimation) is an open question the authors flag but do not explore.
