## Problem

Deep learning-based image stitching pipelines cascade registration, fusion, and rectangling into separate stages, causing error propagation and making the system fragile to registration inaccuracies. Existing fusion methods (reconstruction-based and seam-based) cannot correct misaligned content, and rectangling methods require supervised training on curated datasets. The paper asks whether fusion and rectangling can be unified into a single training-free inpainting task that is inherently robust to upstream registration errors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two overlapping RGB images that have already been warped into a common stitching domain by a pre-trained homography network (UDIS++), producing aligned images with binary masks. These are combined into a coarse fusion image (Eq. 4) where the less-distorted left image is overlaid with the right image only in non-overlapping regions. The output is a single rectangular stitched image produced in one diffusion inference, with no text prompt required. The method operates at the resolution of the warped stitching domain (H*×W*) and runs at ~27 it/s on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Method

SRStitcher reformulates both fusion and rectangling as a unified inpainting problem: the seam region (defined by dilation/erosion of the aligned-image masks) is inpainted to correct registration-induced incoherence, while the missing boundary region (the content mask) is inpainted to fill the irregular shape into a rectangle. Both are combined into a single inpainting mask and solved by one reverse pass of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2 Inpainting model. The key mechanism is a pair of weighted masks — a weighted initial mask (built via distance transform with per-region fidelity coefficients ε₁, ε₂) that controls how much original image information is retained in the masked-image input, and a weighted inpainting mask (inspired by the suffix principle) that dynamically shrinks the modifiable region at each denoising step, allowing the seam area to be progressively and gently modified while the empty rectangling area is fully repainted throughout. A Telea-based coarse rectangling step pre-fills the boundary with a blurry weak prior to suppress anomalous content generation. The entire pipeline is zero-shot: no fine-tuning, no task-specific data, and no prompt guidance are required.

## Results

On the UDIS-D test set (1,106 pairs), SRStitcher achieves the best scores across all three metrics: HIQA 47.82±0.55, CLIPIQA 33.25±0.57, and CCS 91.15±0.52%. On the UDIS-D train set (10,440 pairs), it reaches HIQA 54.74±0.63, CLIPIQA 37.52±0.68, and CCS 93.29±0.45%. Notably, SRStitcher exhibits substantially lower variance (±0.55–0.68) than all diffusion-based baselines (±0.89–2.84), indicating superior stability across random seeds. The method requires no training or fine-tuning, operating entirely in a zero-shot manner on top of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2 Inpainting model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRStitcher outperforms all eight baseline combinations on every metric across both test and train splits. The closest competitor is UDIS++ +SD2, which SRStitcher surpasses by +0.84 HIQA, +2.02 CLIPIQA, and +1.78 CCS on the test set, and by +3.25 HIQA, +3.26 CLIPIQA, and +2.11 CCS on the train set. Among the non-diffusion baselines, UDIS++ +Lama (HIQA 46.57) and UDIS++ +DR (HIQA 45.98) are the strongest, but both trail SRStitcher by over 1.2 HIQA points on test. A key differentiator is stability: SRStitcher's standard deviations (±0.55–0.68) are roughly 2–5× smaller than those of the raw diffusion baselines (e.g., UDIS+SD1.5 at ±2.24 HIQA, ±2.84 CLIPIQA), confirming that the weighted-mask guided reverse process substantially reduces seed sensitivity. The SRStitcher-C variant (ControlNet-based) comes closest to the main model on CCS (89.47 vs. 91.15) but lags on HIQA, while SRStitcher-U (UnCLIP-based) underperforms all other variants, likely due to compromises from its fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRStitcher builds on the established three-stage stitching pipeline (UDIS/UDIS++ for registration and fusion, DeepRectangling for rectangling) but fundamentally departs from the cascading paradigm by reformulating both fusion and rectangling as a single unified inpainting problem solvable by one pre-trained Stable Diffusion Inpainting inference. The weighted mask mechanism draws on the Soft-Inpainting concept from Differential Diffusion [24] and the suffix principle, while the coarse rectangling step repurposes the classical Telea algorithm as a weak-prior initializer. Unlike RecDiffusion, which also uses diffusion for rectangling but requires dedicated training, SRStitcher operates entirely zero-shot without fine-tuning or task-specific supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing two separate network stages into a single training-free inference, SRStitcher directly addresses the error-propagation problem that plagues cascaded stitching pipelines, offering a more robust and simpler system for practitioners. The zero-shot generalization for rectangling is particularly notable, as prior deep-learning rectangling methods all required supervised training on the DIR-D dataset. Open questions remain around visible seams under significant color differences, local blurring in challenging scenes, and whether the registration stage itself can be integrated into the unified diffusion framework (the authors point to DIFT as a promising direction).
