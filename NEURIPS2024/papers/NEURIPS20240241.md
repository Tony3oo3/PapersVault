## Problem

Kleinberg et al. (2023) introduced U-calibration error as a measure of simultaneous regret against all bounded proper losses, achieving O(K√T) pseudo U-calibration error for K classes but leaving the minimax optimal bound open. This paper resolves that question by proving the optimal rate is Θ(√(KT)), and further identifies broad subclasses of proper losses (Lipschitz, decomposable) where the rate improves to O(log T).

## Input / Output

At each round t, the forecaster observes the sequence of past one-hot outcomes y_1, …, y_{t-1} ∈ {e_1, …, e_K} and must output a probability vector p_t ∈ Δ_K. The adversary selects from only K finite one-hot outcomes (not arbitrary points in the simplex), and the performance metric is the supremum of regret over all bounded proper losses ℓ: Δ_K × E → [-1,1] simultaneously. The key structural constraint is that the benchmark is always the empirical mean of outcomes, which is the ERM for every proper loss by definition.

## Method

The upper bound uses Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader with geometric noise (parameter √(K/T)) on each class count, which coincides with the algorithm of Daskalakis and Syrgkanis (2016); the critical insight is that the mean forecaster over true plus hallucinated outcomes is the empirical risk minimizer for *any* proper loss, so a single algorithm simultaneously minimizes regret across all of them. The matching lower bound constructs a V-shaped proper loss ℓ(p) = -½Σ|p_i - 1/K| and uses a reverse Chernoff (anti-concentration) inequality on multinomial counts to show the benchmark incurs Ω(√(KT)) expected loss under a uniform adversary. For improved rates, the paper shows that the deterministic Follow-the-Leader algorithm (predicting the empirical mean) achieves O(G log T) regret for G-Lipschitz proper losses via the Be-the-Leader lemma and a telescoping sum over prediction changes, and O(log T) for decomposable losses whose univariate form is a sum of twice-differentiable concave functions, by exploiting a controlled growth rate of the second derivative. A lower bound via squared loss (K=2) and a recursive minimax value analysis confirms Ω(log T) is tight for the Lipschitz class, while a V-shaped loss example shows FTL suffers linear regret on general proper losses, necessitating FTPL in the worst case.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax optimal pseudo U-calibration error (PUCal) is Θ(√(KT)), resolving an open question from Kleinberg et al. (2023). The upper bound of O(√(KT)) is achieved by an FTPL algorithm with geometric noise (improving Kleinberg et al.'s O(K√T) bound), while the matching lower bound Ω(√(KT)) is proven via a V-shaped proper loss. For important sub-classes, the simple deterministic FTL algorithm achieves O(G log T) U-calibration error for G-Lipschitz proper losses (e.g., squared loss, spherical loss) and O(log T) for decomposable losses (e.g., Tsallis entropy with α∈(1,2)), an exponential improvement over the worst-case √(KT) rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's FTPL algorithm (Algorithm 1) improves upon Kleinberg et al. (2023), who achieved PUCal = O(K√T) for general K, by reducing the bound to O(√(KT)) — a factor of √K improvement — through a simple change from uniform to geometric noise distribution. The algorithm coincides with the FTPL of Daskalakis and Syrgkanis (2016), and as a side result, the paper's lower bound proves that this algorithm is minimax optimal in their original setting (online learning with finite adversary choices), which was previously unknown. For Lipschitz proper losses, FTL achieves O(G log T) regret, which is minimax optimal (matching an Ω(log T) lower bound via squared loss with K=2), substantially outperforming the √(KT) worst-case rate. However, FTL is shown to be fundamentally inadequate for general proper losses, suffering Ω(T) linear regret on a V-shaped loss, whereas Algorithm 1 maintains O(√(KT)) — highlighting the necessity of randomized perturbation for the full class of proper losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly resolves the open question posed by Kleinberg et al. (2023) about the minimax optimal multiclass U-calibration error, improving their O(K√T) upper bound to the tight Θ(√(KT)) by adopting the FTPL algorithm with geometric noise from Daskalakis and Syrgkanis (2016). The key conceptual bridge is the observation that the empirical mean is always the ERM for any proper loss, which makes the Daskalakis–Syrgkanis algorithm (designed for a fixed loss with finite adversary choices) simultaneously optimal for all proper losses. The paper also situates U-calibration within the broader omniprediction/simultaneous loss minimization framework of Gopalan et al. (2022) and contrasts it with classical calibration error, which faces known barriers (Ω(T^0.528) lower bounds).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean Θ(√(KT)) resolution provides a definitive answer to a fundamental question in online prediction theory and establishes that U-calibration is strictly easier than calibration, offering a practical decision-theoretic justification for the U-calibration framework. The logarithmic bounds for Lipschitz and decomposable proper losses (including squared loss, spherical loss, and Tsallis-entropy losses) show that the worst-case √(KT) rate is far from tight for the losses most commonly used in practice. Open questions remain: the optimal bound for the stronger UCal (as opposed to PUCal) is unresolved, the contextual and non-proper-loss settings are unexplored, and the connection to the broader omniprediction literature (Garg et al., 2024) could yield further algorithmic improvements.
