## Problem

Existing MIPL algorithms overlook the margins between attention scores of positive and negative instances and between predicted probabilities of candidate and non-candidate labels, leading to 'margin violations' where negative instances receive higher attention than positives or non-candidate labels receive higher predicted probabilities than candidate ones. These violations degrade generalization, particularly in safety-critical applications like medical image classification where misranking candidate labels can have serious consequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-instance bag of variable size (e.g., 3–48 image patches, audio segments, or preprocessed feature vectors) together with a candidate label set of 2–4 labels (one true, the rest false positives) and outputs a single bag-level class label. Instance dimensions vary widely across datasets (784 for MNIST/FMNIST patches, 38 for Birdsong, 30 for SIVAL, up to 1000 for ResNet-34 patch features), and the number of instances per bag is not fixed, requiring permutation-invariant aggregation. The candidate label set is a proper subset of the full label space, and the non-candidate complement is explicitly exploited by the margin distribution loss.

## Method

MIPLMA introduces two complementary margin-adjustment mechanisms. In the instance space, a margin-aware attention mechanism computes attention scores via a temperature-scaled softmax (τ^(t) anneals from 5 down to a minimum via ×0.95 per epoch), which smooths attention early in training and sharpens it later, widening the gap between positive and negative instance scores; attention scores are then normalized per-bag to handle varying positive-instance counts before weighted aggregation into a bag-level feature. In the label space, a margin distribution loss simultaneously maximizes the mean and minimizes the variance of the margin between the highest predicted probability on the candidate set and the highest on the non-candidate set, going beyond simple mean-margin maximization. The full training objective combines a dynamic disambiguation loss (with momentum-based weight updates over candidate labels) and the margin distribution loss weighted by λ, all trained end-to-end with SGD and cosine annealing.

## Results

On the four benchmark MIPL datasets (r=1), MIPLMA achieves 98.5% on MNIST-MIPL, 91.5% on FMNIST-MIPL, 77.6% on Birdsong-MIPL, and 70.3% on SIVAL-MIPL, outperforming the strongest MIPL baseline ELIMIPL in 21 of 24 cases across all r values. On the real-world CRC-MIPL dataset, MIPLMA reaches 44.4% (C-Row), 52.6% (C-SBN), 55.7% (C-KMeans), and 55.3% (C-SIFT), surpassing all MIPL and PLL baselines in every configuration. With ResNet-34 deep features, MIPLMA achieves 68.5% on C-R34-25, a 2.2-point gain over ELIMIPL (66.3%). Overall, MIPLMA wins in 96.4% of all pairwise comparisons (477 wins, 13 ties, 5 losses) against 16 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIPLMA outperforms the three MIPL baselines in 33 of 36 cases across all r values and datasets, with the sole losses being ELIMIPL's narrow edge on MNIST-MIPL at r=1 (.992 vs .985) and r=2 (.987 vs .979). Against MIPLGP (instance-space paradigm), MIPLMA wins in all 12 cases, with the largest gap on SIVAL-MIPL (70.3% vs 66.9% at r=1). On the CRC-MIPL real-world dataset, MIPLMA dominates all 11 comparisons against ELIMIPL, DEMIPL, and MIPLGP, with the most substantial margin on C-SBN (52.6% vs 50.9% for ELIMIPL). All seven PLL baselines (PRODEN, RC, LWS, CAVL, POP, PL-AGGD, and margin-based M3PL/PL-SVM) are significantly outperformed by MIPLMA in every setting, with gaps exceeding 20 percentage points on complex datasets like Birdsong-MIPL and SIVAL-MIPL. The margin-aware attention mechanism (MAAM) also outperforms ATTEN and ATTEN-GATE on MIL tasks (99.1% vs 96.2% on MNIST-MIPL-MIL), and the margin distribution loss improves PRODEN by up to 4.4 points on Kuzushiji-MNIST at q=0.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIPL framework established by Tang et al. (SCIS 2024, NeurIPS 2023, IJCAI 2024), building directly on the attention-based bag aggregation pipeline of DEMIPL and the conjugate label exploitation of ELIMIPL. The margin-aware attention mechanism is a direct modification of the Ilse et al. (ICML 2018) attention formula, introducing a temperature-annealed softmax to dynamically widen the gap between positive and negative instance scores. The margin distribution loss generalizes the maximum-margin criterion from PL-SVM and M3PL by jointly optimizing margin mean and variance, a technique borrowed from margin distribution clustering literature. The key conceptual departure is the explicit identification of 'margin violations' as a failure mode in both instance and label spaces, which prior MIPL algorithms did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIPL is a very young framework with only three prior algorithms, so establishing a principled margin-adjustment strategy provides a useful design principle for future MIPL methods. The demonstration that the margin-aware attention and margin distribution loss transfer independently to standard MIL and PLL algorithms suggests these components have broader applicability beyond the MIPL setting. However, the task remains niche (dual inexact supervision in both instance and label spaces), and the paper acknowledges limitations: inability to process multiple bags in parallel, slight overfitting on simple datasets, and inapplicability to instance-level classification. Open questions include whether margin adjustment strategies generalize to other weakly supervised settings and how to extend MIPL to instance-level prediction.
