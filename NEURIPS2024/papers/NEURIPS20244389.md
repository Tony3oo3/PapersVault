## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of in-context learning via linear estimation assume IID task and feature vectors and fully parameterized attention weights, leaving open whether alternative architectures (e.g., state-space models) can implement the same gradient-descent algorithms, how distributional alignment between in-context examples and queries affects sample complexity, and what happens under low-rank parameterization or LoRA adaptation. This paper addresses all three gaps by developing a unified optimization and risk landscape for ICL under correlated designs and architectural constraints.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single in-context prompt Z ∈ R^{(n+1)×(d+1)} where each of the first n rows is [x_i; y_i] (a d-dimensional feature vector paired with a scalar label y_i = x_i^T β + ξ_i) and the final row is [x; 0] (the query feature with a zero label slot). The model outputs a scalar prediction ŷ_{n+1} for the query. Training is conducted in a meta-learning regime where the task vector β is resampled for each prompt, and the model is trained via gradient descent on the population squared loss with a causal mask preventing self-attention to the query's own label.

## Method

The paper proves (Proposition 1) that under a general correlated-design assumption (zero odd cross-moments), optimizing a 1-layer linear attention model or a 1-layer H3 state-space model is exactly equivalent to solving a 1-step preconditioned gradient descent (PGD) objective, with H3 additionally implementing sample-weighted PGD through its convolution filter. Leveraging this equivalence, the authors derive closed-form optimal risks for three data distributions: independent (Theorem 1), RAG-inspired α-correlated demonstrations (Theorem 2), and task-feature α-correlated features (Theorem 3), showing that alignment amplifies the effective in-context sample size by a factor of α²d + 1. For low-rank parameterization (rank(W) ≤ r), they characterize the optimal risk in terms of the top-r eigenvalues of the fused task-feature covariance (Lemma 3), and derive an upper bound for LoRA adaptation under distribution shift (Eq. 14) that captures how the low-rank update compensates for the spectral shift between old and new task covariances.

## Results

The paper proves that 1-layer linear attention and 1-layer H3 (a gated state-space model) both implement exactly 1-step preconditioned gradient descent (PGD) under correlated designs (Proposition 1), with the all-ones convolution filter in H3 recovering the linear attention solution. For independent isotropic noiseless data (Σ_x = Σ_β = I_d, σ = 0), the optimal risk is L* = d − nd/(n+d+1) (Corollary 1). Under RAG or task-feature alignment with correlation α, the effective in-context sample size is amplified by a factor of κ = α²d + 1, so the risk becomes approximately d + σ² − κnd/(κn + d + σ²) (Theorems 2–3). For low-rank parameterization with rank r, the optimal risk is M − Σ_{i=1}^r nλ_i²/((n+1)λ_i + M), where λ_i are eigenvalues of the fused covariance Σ = Σ_x^{1/2}Σ_βΣ_x^{1/2} (Lemma 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between model architectures and data regimes rather than competing methods on a benchmark. Linear attention and H3 achieve identical optimal risk (L*_ATT = L*_SSM = L*_PGD) under all three data settings tested (independent, RAG, task-feature alignment), as confirmed by Figure 2 where solid (attention) and dashed (H3) curves overlap the dotted theoretical curve. The key 'baseline' is the independent data setting (α = 0); introducing α-correlation via RAG or task-feature alignment amplifies the effective sample size by κ = α²d + 1, yielding substantially lower risk for the same n (e.g., at α = 0.6, d = 20, κ ≈ 8.4, nearly 9× amplification). For low-rank parameterization, the paper shows that restricting rank to r captures the top-r eigenvalues of the fused covariance, and the empirical curves (Figure 3c) match the theoretical formula (13) exactly. For LoRA adaptation under distribution shift (Figure 3d), the upper bound in Eq. (14) tightly predicts the empirical risk, confirming that LoRA effectively captures the spectral shift between old and new task covariances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that 1-layer linear attention implements 1-step preconditioned gradient descent (PGD) for in-context learning (Ahn et al., 2023; Mahankali et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024) in three directions: (1) it adds H3, a gated state-space model, as an alternative architecture and proves it achieves the same optimal PGD loss, providing the first theoretical treatment of ICL with SSMs; (2) it moves beyond the IID assumption to correlated designs modeling RAG and task-feature alignment; and (3) it analyzes low-rank parameterization and LoRA adaptation under distribution shift. The linear attention architecture from Katharopoulos et al. (2020) and the H3 architecture from Fu et al. (2023) serve as the two model families whose optimization landscapes are jointly characterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean α²d+1 amplification factor for RAG and task-feature alignment provides a quantitative explanation for why retrieval-augmented and domain-aligned prompts dramatically reduce the few-shot sample complexity of ICL, offering principled guidance for prompt design. The H3–attention equivalence and the LoRA covariance-shift analysis are practically relevant for understanding when SSMs can replace attention and how parameter-efficient fine-tuning adapts to new task distributions. Key limitations include the restriction to 1-layer models, population-risk analysis, and linear regression tasks; extending to multi-layer architectures, iterative GD, and realistic NLP tasks (e.g., question answering requiring pretraining knowledge) remains open.
