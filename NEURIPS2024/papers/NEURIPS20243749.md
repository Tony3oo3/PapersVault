## Problem

In data marketplaces, buyers must select the most valuable subset of seller training data for a specific unlabeled test query under a budget constraint. Existing data valuation methods (Data Shapley, LOO, Influence) rely on labeled validation data to score training points, which the paper proves leads to fundamental overfitting: any validation-based selection can perform as poorly as discarding all training data and training on the validation set alone (Theorem 1). These methods also fail to adapt to the buyer's specific test distribution, do not natively handle heterogeneous per-datapoint costs, and do not scale to seller corpora exceeding 100K points.

## Input / Output

The method takes the buyer's unlabeled test query X^test (m points), a budget B, seller training data embeddings X^train (n points in R^d, obtained via CLIP for images or GPT-2 for text), and per-datapoint costs c_j. Crucially, the proxy objective requires only X^train and X^test—no training labels are needed for the selection step. The output is a discrete selection vector w ∈ {0,1}^n identifying which seller points to acquire (sampled from the final weight distribution w_T ∈ Δ^n until budget B is exhausted), followed by a linear regression prediction on the buyer's test query. The entire selection procedure is federated: each seller computes only O(d) communication per round using their local data point.

## Method

DAVED reformulates data acquisition as a V-optimal experimental design problem in a kernelized linear regression feature space (using eNTK or pre-trained model embeddings as φ). The key proxy loss is L^ED(w) = (1/m) Σ_i (x_i^test)^T I(w)^† (x_i^test), where I(w) = Σ_j w_j x_j x_j^T is the Fisher information matrix—this directly measures how well selected training points inform prediction on the specific test query without any validation data. The NP-hard discrete selection is relaxed to a continuous convex problem over w ≥ 0 with a budget constraint, then solved via a herding variant of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm that iteratively shrinks all weights and upweights the single most informative datapoint (selected by maximizing negative gradient divided by cost). The inverse information matrix is maintained in O(d^2) time per step using the Sherman-Morrison rank-one update, enabling a fully federated implementation where each seller computes their local gradient and the platform only exchanges O(d) information per round. The paper proves an O(log t₀ / t₀) approximation guarantee relative to the optimal discrete solution (Theorem 2), and a single-step variant that selects top-k points by (x_i^test)^T P₀ x_j scores provides a fast alternative with minimal performance loss.

## Results

DAVED (multi-step) achieves the lowest test MSE across most datasets: 0.37 on Gaussian (1K) vs. 0.87 for the best baseline (Data Shapley), 171.4 on MIMIC (35K) vs. 215.6 for Data OOB, 785.2 on RSNA vs. 1020.3 for Data OOB, and 0.67 on Fitzpatrick vs. 1.35 for Data OOB. On the 100K-point Gaussian benchmark, DAVED multi-step reaches 0.16 MSE while the best baseline (Influence) achieves only 0.97. The single-step variant is competitive (e.g., 0.27 on Gaussian 100K, 9.0 on DrugLib) while being orders of magnitude faster, and Data Shapley and Leave-One-Out are infeasible (N/A) on all datasets exceeding 1K points due to prohibitive runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVED (multi-step) outperforms all seven baselines on Gaussian (1K and 100K), MIMIC (35K), RSNA, and Fitzpatrick, with the largest margins on synthetic data (0.37 vs. 0.87 for Data Shapley on 1K; 0.16 vs. 0.97 for Influence on 100K). Data OOB is the strongest baseline overall and the only other validation-free method, but DAVED still beats it by 30–50% on most datasets (e.g., 171.4 vs. 215.6 on MIMIC 35K; 785.2 vs. 1020.3 on RSNA). Data Shapley and Leave-One-Out are infeasible beyond 1K datapoints due to repeated model retraining, while Influence, DVRL, LAVA, and KNN Shapley scale but consistently underperform DAVED. On DrugLib, DAVED single-step (9.0) edges out Data OOB (10.0), while DAVED multi-step (9.2) is marginally behind. On MIMIC 1K, DAVED multi-step (206.7) trails Data OOB (169.7) and KNN Shapley (175.7), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced at larger market sizes. With heterogeneous costs (Table 2), DAVED multi-step achieves 0.04 MSE on Gaussian (√c cost) versus 1.54 for the best baseline (DVRL), confirming its budget-efficiency advantage extends to non-uniform pricing.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAVED departs fundamentally from the validation-based data valuation paradigm (Data Shapley, Influence, KNN Shapley, etc.) by arguing these methods commit an 'inference after selection' error: using labeled validation data to score training points implicitly overfits to the validation set. Instead, it repurposes V-optimal experimental design (Pukelsheim, Silvey) to construct a proxy loss that directly measures how useful each training point is for predicting the buyer's specific unlabeled test query, requiring no validation data. The optimization leverages the Frank-Wolfe/herding algorithm (Wynn, Bach et al.) with a Sherman-Morrison rank-one update for the Fisher information matrix, enabling a federated implementation with O(d) communication per round. The linear approximation via eNTK or pre-trained embeddings (CLIP, GPT-2) connects to recent work showing kernelized linear regression closely approximates fine-tuning dynamics of pre-trained models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax lower bound (Theorem 1) showing that any validation-based selection method can perform as poorly as discarding all training data and training on validation points alone is a strong negative result that challenges the foundational assumption behind the most widely-used data valuation techniques. Practically, DAVED offers a scalable, federated, budget-aware data acquisition procedure suitable for decentralized marketplaces where sellers resist centralizing data. Key limitations include reliance on a good feature extractor and the linear (eNTK) approximation, which may degrade for models far from the NTK regime; open questions include integrating differential privacy, reducing communication via local steps (FedAvg-style), and extending beyond the linear model assumption to general deep learning dynamics.
