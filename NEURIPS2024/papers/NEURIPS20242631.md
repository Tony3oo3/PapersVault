## Problem

Existing online batch selection methods for data downsampling (e.g., RHO-Loss, Train Loss) are not robust to class imbalance and distributional shifts between training and test time, leading to degraded worst-class generalization. No prior method explicitly optimizes the maximin objective of selecting a small training subset that preserves the performance of the worst-performing class, which is critical when test-time class distributions differ from training-time distributions.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, REDUCR receives a large candidate batch B_t of labeled datapoints (images or text) from a streaming data pool, along with a fixed holdout dataset split by class and C pre-trained amortised class-irreducible loss models (one per class, trained via importance-weighted cross-entropy with up-weighting factor γ). It outputs a small selected batch b_t (typically 10% of B_t) for the target model to take gradient steps on, updated class priority weights w_t over the C-dimensional simplex, and ultimately a trained classifier. The selection score for each candidate point is a weighted sum across all C classes of the excess loss (model loss minus class-irreducible loss), making the per-point computation scale linearly with the number of classes.

## Method

REDUCR formalizes robust data downsampling as a maximin set-selection problem (Eq. 3) and solves it online via a multiplicative weights update over class priority weights. At each step, the selection score for a datapoint (x,y) is decomposed into three terms (Eq. 8): the model loss under the target model θ_t, the class-irreducible loss under an amortised expert model φ_c trained with importance weighting (up-weighting class-c points by γ), and a class-holdout loss that measures the target model's current performance on class c's holdout set. The top-k points by weighted excess loss are selected, then class weights are updated multiplicatively so that poorly performing classes receive higher priority in subsequent steps. The amortised class-irreducible loss models are trained once before online selection begins and remain frozen, avoiding the cost of retraining C reference models at every timestep. Clipping the excess loss to non-negative values stabilizes training by preventing a single class's expert model from disproportionately down-weighting points in the weighted sum.

## Results

REDUCR achieves the best worst-class test accuracy across all six datasets, improving over the strongest baseline (RHO-LOSS) by 4.5–26 percentage points. On the noisy, imbalanced Clothing1M dataset, REDUCR reaches 53.91% worst-class accuracy versus 40.37% for TRAIN LOSS and 27.77% for RHO-LOSS, a ~15% absolute gain over the next-best method. On CIFAR-10, REDUCR attains 83.29% worst-class accuracy (vs. 78.80% for RHO-LOSS) while matching RHO-LOSS's average accuracy (90.02% vs. 90.00%). On text tasks, REDUCR leads on QQP (86.61% worst-class) and MNLI (79.45% worst-class), outperforming all baselines by 2.7–6.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REDUCR is compared against two main baselines: RHO-LOSS (Mindermann et al., ICML 2022), which uses a single reference model to score reducible holdout loss, and TRAIN LOSS (Loshchilov & Hutter, ICLR Workshop 2016), which selects points with the highest training loss. REDUCR wins on worst-class accuracy across all six datasets, with the largest margin on Clothing1M where it surpasses RHO-LOSS by 26.1 points (53.91% vs. 27.77%) and TRAIN LOSS by 13.5 points. On CIFAR-100, REDUCR leads by 8.4 points over TRAIN LOSS and 10.0 points over RHO-LOSS. On the text datasets, REDUCR outperforms RHO-LOSS by 2.7 points on MNLI and 8.4 points on QQP in worst-class accuracy. In terms of average test accuracy, REDUCR matches or slightly exceeds RHO-LOSS on most datasets (e.g., 90.02% vs. 90.00% on CIFAR-10; 72.69% vs. 71.07% on Clothing1M), despite not explicitly optimizing for average performance. The paper also shows that even when baselines are given worst-class checkpointing (Tables 3–4), REDUCR still outperforms or matches them, and achieves comparable results ~100k training steps earlier on MNLI and QQP.

## Relation to Prior Work

REDUCR extends the online batch selection framework of RHO-Loss (Mindermann et al., 2022) and Train Loss (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2016) by replacing their average-performance objective with a worst-class maximin formulation. The key methodological departure is the use of multiplicative weights update (from no-regret online learning) to dynamically reweight classes during selection, combined with amortised class-irreducible loss models that approximate per-class expert models without per-step retraining. This positions the work at the intersection of robust optimization (Sagawa et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2017) and data-efficient training, but unlike group-robust training methods, REDUCR operates in a streaming setting with class labels available and focuses on data selection rather than loss reweighting during full-data training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that REDUCR achieves 15–26% worst-class accuracy gains over the best baseline on imbalanced/noisy datasets while matching or exceeding average accuracy, offering a drop-in replacement for RHO-Loss in settings where worst-case generalization matters. A key limitation is linear scaling with the number of classes (mitigated by superclass grouping), restricting applicability to settings with fewer than ~1000 classes. Open questions include whether the multiplicative weights approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or hierarchical classification, whether smaller class-irreducible models can maintain signal quality, and how the method behaves under more extreme distributional shifts than those tested.
