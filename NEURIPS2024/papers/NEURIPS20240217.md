## Problem

While adversarial training (AT) is known to improve robustness but worsen privacy in general deep learning models, it remains unclear whether this negative trade-off holds for Label Mapping Visual Prompting (LM-VP) models, where a frozen pre-trained model is reprogrammed via trainable prompts and a label mapping layer. This paper conducts the first joint evaluation of (transferred) adversarial robustness and membership inference attack (MIA) susceptibility for LM-VP models, revealing that intuitions from general models do not always transfer.

## Input / Output

The LM-VP model takes a target domain RGB image, rescales it to a size smaller than the 224×224 source domain, and surrounds it with trainable prompt noise to restore the source-domain dimensions. The augmented image is passed through a frozen pre-trained model (ResNet50/152, WideResNet, ViT, Swin Transformer, ConvNext, or EVA), and a trainable fully-connected label mapping layer converts the source-domain logits into target-domain class labels. Only the prompt parameters and label mapping weights are updated during training; all pre-trained model parameters remain fixed.

## Method

The paper treats the LM-VP pipeline (prompt + frozen pre-trained model + label mapping) as a unified black-box model and proposes transfer adversarial training (transfer AT) to enhance its black-box robustness: a fixed threat model (ResNet18) generates PGD-10 adversarial examples, and the LM-VP model is trained with adversarial loss on these perturbed inputs using the SAM optimizer. A key finding is that standard white-box AT is ineffective for LM-VP due to domain shift between the target dataset and the frozen source-domain pre-trained model, whereas transfer AT consistently improves transferred adversarial robustness by 20–35% for small/medium pre-trained models while simultaneously reducing MIA success rates to near the 50% random-guess baseline. The authors further show that LM-VP models exhibit minimal generalization error and low training-data sensitivity (enabling effective training on subsets as small as 100 samples), which explains their inherent resistance to MIAs and allows transferred robustness and privacy to be improved simultaneously.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Transfer AT-trained LM-VP models achieve transferred adversarial robustness (PGD-20) of 63.0% (ResNet50), 79.1% (Swin), and 91.6% (ConvNext), while maintaining natural test accuracy of 70.4%, 89.7%, and 98.0% respectively. Standard-trained LM-VP models achieve higher natural accuracy (86.3%–98.5%) but substantially lower adversarial robustness (35.6%–89.1%). For privacy, Transfer AT reduces MIA success rate to approximately 50% (random-guess level) across most pre-trained models, compared to 50%–80% for standard training. On Tiny-ImageNet, Transfer AT improves adversarial robustness by 3%–24% and reduces MIA success rate by 3%–12% over standard training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external defense methods; instead, it contrasts Standard Training (ST) with the proposed Transfer Adversarial Training (Transfer AT) within the LM-VP framework, using different pre-trained models as the variable. Transfer AT consistently improves transferred adversarial robustness (PGD-20) over ST: by ~20–35% for small models (e.g., ResNet50: 35.6%→63.0% on CIFAR-10), ~20–25% for medium models (e.g., Swin: 57.8%→79.1%), and ~3–6% for large models (e.g., ConvNext: 89.1%→91.6%), at the cost of 10–20% natural accuracy for small/medium models but only ~0.3% for large models. On privacy, Transfer AT reduces MIA success rate to near 50% (chance level) for most pre-trained models, whereas ST yields MIA rates of 50%–80% (e.g., ConvNext ST: 80.14% vs. TAT: 50.32%). Standard AT (white-box) is shown to be entirely ineffective for LM-VP, dropping adversarial accuracy to 0% across all pre-trained models. The choice of pre-trained model is the dominant factor for white-box robustness (ranging from 0% to 57% PGD-20 under ST), while transferred adversarial robustness scales more predictably with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Prompting (VP) framework of Bahng et al. by incorporating trainable label mapping (following Li et al. and Arif et al.) and treats the resulting LM-VP pipeline as a unified model entity for security evaluation. It draws on the adversarial training formulation of Madry et al. and the membership inference attack literature (Shokri et al., Song et al.) to jointly assess robustness and privacy, but departs from prior work by showing that the well-established negative robustness-privacy trade-off in general DL models does not transfer to LM-VP. The paper also introduces transfer adversarial training for LM-VP, where a fixed threat model generates adversarial examples during training, distinguishing it from standard white-box AT applied to the frozen pre-trained backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic security evaluation of LM-VP models, providing practitioners with concrete guidance: standard AT is harmful (reducing adversarial accuracy to 0%) while transfer AT simultaneously improves transferred robustness and privacy across seven pre-trained architectures. The finding that the robustness-privacy trade-off breaks down for LM-VP challenges a widely held assumption in AI security and suggests that model reprogramming techniques may offer a path to decoupling these objectives. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet), a single threshold-based MIA method, and no theoretical explanation for why the trade-off does not hold, leaving open whether the result generalizes to other MR techniques or more sophisticated MIA formulations.
