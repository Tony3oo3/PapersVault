## Problem

Prior work established that first-price auctions achieve full efficiency under uniform bid-scaling but degrade to a PoA of 1/2 under fully non-uniform bidding, yet no fine-grained analysis connected the *degree* of non-uniformity to efficiency. Empirical evidence from Deng et al. (2024) showed that increasing the granularity of non-uniform bid-scaling monotonically worsens welfare, but this phenomenon lacked a theoretical explanation. This paper provides a parameterized PoA bound that formally links autobidders' non-uniform bidding capability to auction efficiency.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a market instance consisting of n bidders, m auctions partitioned into s slices, and per-auction valuations, along with per-slice bid multipliers chosen by each bidder. The key intermediate object is the balancedness quantile function F(t), which maps a cumulative market-share fraction to the minimum balancedness (smallest bidder's share fraction) among slices covering that fraction. The output is a PoA bound of 1 − t/2 where t ∈ [2/5, 1] is the unbalancedness parameter derived from F, with t = 1/2 recovering full efficiency (single slice) and t = 1 recovering the known PoA of 1/2 (each auction is its own slice).

## Method

The paper introduces a slice-based model where autobidders choose one bid multiplier per slice but must bid uniformly within each slice, then defines balancedness of a slice as the minimum bidder's market share fraction and aggregates across slices via a balancedness quantile function. The proof of the upper bound (Lemma 1) partitions each slice's auctions into three sets (rightful winner underbids, rightful winner overbids, rightful winner loses), derives a per-slice tradeoff between efficiency loss and overpayment via a quadratic inequality, and reduces the global bound to an optimization problem over slice-level variables. Solving this optimization via KKT conditions yields a closed-form expression involving the balancedness quantile function, which is then identified as the unbalancedness parameter. The tightness construction (Lemma 2) uses a 3-bidder, 6-auction, 3-slice instance where two bidders 'steal' the third bidder's share on two slices while the third slice is uncontested, achieving exactly the bound for any target unbalancedness t.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes a fine-grained PoA bound of 1 − t/2, where t ∈ [2/5, 1] is the unbalancedness parameter of the market instance. In the extreme case of fully non-uniform bidding (each auction is its own slice, t = 1), the PoA recovers the known worst-case bound of 1/2. When all slices are perfectly balanced (t = 2/5, the minimum for s ≥ 2), the PoA is at most 4/5, showing that even perfectly balanced multi-slice markets suffer irreducible efficiency loss. In contrast, uniform bidding over the entire market (s = 1) achieves full efficiency (PoA = 1), indicating a phase transition when moving to any non-uniform strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The contribution is a parameterized PoA bound that interpolates between two previously known extremes: (1) uniform bid-scaling achieves PoA = 1 (full efficiency, Balseiro et al. 2021a), and (2) fully non-uniform bidding achieves PoA = 1/2 (Deng et al. 2022, Liaw et al. 2023). The paper's bound is strictly tighter than the blanket PoA = 1/2 for any intermediate level of non-uniformity (t < 1), quantifying exactly how much efficiency is lost as autobidders gain more bidding freedom. The results also contrast with second-price auctions, where the PoA is 2 regardless of the level of non-uniformity, suggesting first-price auctions are inherently more efficient in the autobidding setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on price-of-anarchy analysis for first-price auctions with value-maximizing autobidders. Prior results established that uniform bid-scaling achieves full efficiency (Balseiro et al., 2021a) while fully non-uniform bidding yields PoA = 1/2 (Deng et al., 2022; Liaw et al., 2023), and a recent empirical study (Deng et al., 2024) observed that intermediate levels of non-uniformity produce a continuum of efficiency losses. This paper provides the missing theoretical bridge: a fine-grained, parameterized PoA bound that interpolates between these extremes by introducing a 'balancedness quantile function' over slice partitions, thereby formally explaining the empirical phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a principled, theoretically grounded explanation for why deploying more sophisticated autobidders in first-price marketplaces can paradoxically reduce aggregate welfare — a 'prisoner's dilemma' effect that had only been observed empirically. It also yields actionable design guidance: marketplaces should aim for balanced slices/channels to mitigate efficiency loss, and the phase transition from full efficiency (one slice) to at most 4/5 efficiency (two or more slices, even perfectly balanced) suggests that any departure from uniform bidding carries an irreducible efficiency cost. Open questions include whether similar fine-grained bounds exist for second-price or GSP auctions, whether the regularity assumption (no underbidding unless winning all rightful auctions) can be relaxed, and how these results extend to settings with budget constraints or strategic budget reporting.
