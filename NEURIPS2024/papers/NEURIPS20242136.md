## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating LLM critique ability are limited in both comprehensiveness (covering only 1-2 critique dimensions, unclassified response qualities, and narrow task types) and reliability (GPT-4's ability to evaluate textual critiques has not been verified across dimensions and tasks). This gap impedes in-depth analysis of how critique ability relates to task types, response quality, and critique dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242136/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes task inputs paired with LLM-generated responses of annotated quality levels (low, medium, high, correct), response pairs for comparison, and existing critiques for meta-feedback, spanning 9 tasks (translation, chat, QA, summarization, harmlessness, math CoT/PoT, code with/without execution). It produces both scalar-valued critiques (Likert scores, preference labels) and fine-grained textual critiques. Evaluation outputs include Spearman correlations (normalized to -100 to 100) for scalar consistency, pass rates for corrections, preference accuracy with positional-bias mitigation for comparisons, and GPT-4 subjective scores (1-10) anchored to human-annotated references at score 8 for textual critique quality.

## Method

CRITICEVAL evaluates critique ability across four dimensions—feedback (textual analysis + quality score), correction (revision of responses), comparison (preference judgment on response pairs), and meta-feedback (rating of existing critiques)—under nine diverse task scenarios. The key reliability mechanism is a human-in-the-loop pipeline where GPT-4-turbo generates draft reference critiques that are then rigorously reviewed and refined by human experts (average revision rates of 25-48% across dimensions), producing 3,608 annotated textual critiques and 2,892 scalar samples. These human-annotated references serve as scoring pivots for GPT-4-based subjective evaluation, which the paper proves achieves correlations close to human-level agreement (e.g., 82.10 vs. 87.04 for correction) and degrades significantly (average -13.36) when references are removed. Objective evaluation uses Spearman correlation for feedback/meta-feedback, pass rate for corrections on verifiable tasks, and a positional-bias-mitigated accuracy metric for comparisons that requires consistent preference across both response orderings.

## Results

GPT-4-turbo achieves the highest overall scores across all 35 evaluated LLMs, with 7.81/10 subjective and 72.55 objective (Spearman correlation / pass rate) on the CRITICEVAL test set. Notably, open-source InternLM2-20B-Chat (6.20 subjective) and Qwen-72B-Chat (6.01 subjective) surpass GPT-3.5-turbo (5.89 subjective) on overall critique quality. In the feedback dimension, critique-tuned Auto-J-13B (4.21 sub, 36.05 obj) outperforms the much larger Llama-2-70B-Chat (4.12 sub, 32.79 obj), and reward model UltraRM-13B (52.33 obj) beats GPT-3.5-turbo (51.44 obj) on scalar scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4-turbo dominates across nearly all dimensions, achieving 7.81/10 subjective and 72.55 objective overall, though it slightly underperforms the human-annotated reference (8.0). Among open-source models, InternLM2-20B-Chat (6.20 sub) and Qwen-72B-Chat (6.01 sub) both surpass GPT-3.5-turbo (5.89 sub) on overall subjective critique quality, while InternLM2-20B also leads on objective feedback correlation (58.61 vs. 51.44). In the feedback dimension specifically, critique-tuned Auto-J-13B (4.21 sub, 36.05 obj) outperforms the 5x-larger Llama-2-70B-Chat (4.12 sub, 32.79 obj), validating the effectiveness of GPT-4-generated critique datasets. Reward model UltraRM-13B (52.33 obj on F_s) edges out GPT-3.5-turbo (51.44), showing specialized reward models can match general-purpose LLMs on scalar scoring. However, GPT-4-turbo maintains a clear lead on meta-feedback (62.90 vs. 38.89 for Claude-instant-1), and removing human-annotated reference critiques causes an average 13.36-point drop in meta-feedback scores, confirming their critical role in reliable evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-judge evaluation paradigm (MT-Bench, GPTScore, G-Eval) and the critique-tuning line (Auto-J, UltraFeedback, TigerScore) by introducing a unified benchmark that covers four critique dimensions—feedback, comparison, correction, and meta-feedback—across nine diverse task scenarios, whereas prior benchmarks (CriticBench, Shepherd, MetaCritique) each addressed only one or two dimensions on a narrow task set. A key methodological departure is the use of human-annotated reference critiques to anchor GPT-4's subjective scoring, addressing the well-documented unreliability of GPT-4 as a standalone judge for textual critiques. The paper also builds on the scalable oversight and self-improvement literature by explicitly including correction and meta-feedback dimensions that prior evaluation work had overlooked despite their centrality to iterative self-refinement pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRITICEVAL provides the first comprehensive and reliability-verified benchmark for LLM critique ability, enabling systematic comparison across 35 models and revealing that open-source models (InternLM2-20B, Qwen-72B) are approaching closed-source performance in critique tasks. The finding that self-generated feedback can actually degrade correction performance on reasoning tasks (worse than no feedback at all) challenges the assumption that LLMs can reliably self-improve on hard problems, with direct implications for self-refinement and RFT pipelines. Open questions include whether the benchmark generalizes to newer architectures (Llama-3, Claude-3), whether the GPT-4-dependent subjective evaluation can be replaced by more diverse judges, and whether the four-level response quality taxonomy and error-pattern analysis transfer to multilingual or tool-use settings.
