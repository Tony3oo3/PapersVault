## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning is driven by destructive gradient interference between tasks, but the specific factors causing this interference have not been fully characterized. The paper identifies two key factors—correlations between hidden representations across tasks and correlations between class vectors—and notes that prior alignment methods only consider observed tasks, lacking a global view that generalizes to unseen future tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text sequence (n tokens) as input, appends a [CLS] token, and produces a class label via softmax over learned class vectors. The data representation is the [CLS] token's hidden state, constructed as a task-specific interpolation of frozen pre-trained BERT-base token representations rather than through full fine-tuning. Evaluation covers both task-incremental (model knows which task's classes to predict) and class-incremental (model predicts from all classes seen so far) inference settings.

## Method

The core idea is to learn each task's data representation as a task-specific composition (interpolation) of pre-trained token representations, so that cross-task representation correlations are grounded by the stable, general correlations of the pre-trained features. Three transformer-based models implement this: Wire-Fixed learns task-specific attention for [CLS] using a new low-rank key matrix while keeping all other token representations at their pre-trained values; Wire-Neigh expands the search space by incorporating k nearest-neighbor token representations (selected by cosine similarity) with a small mixing ratio s; and C-LoRA adapts all token representations via low-rank query/value matrices scaled by a small factor s to limit deviation from pre-trained features. A probing-then-fine-tuning (PF) strategy is applied at task switches—first training only the classifier on frozen representations, then fine-tuning the full model—to initialize class vectors that focus on distinct features and reduce interference from overlapping representations.

## Results

On the 'All' task sequence (18 tasks across Yahoo, DBPedia, and News Series), the best alignment model (Wire-Fixed+PF) achieves 90.18% average accuracy with only 1.79% forgetting, outperforming the strongest CL baselines IDBR (88.89%, 2.21% FGT) and CODA (87.95%, 2.76% FGT) without using experience replay. On the harder News Series sequence, C-LoRA+PF reaches 78.59% ACC with 2.42% FGT, surpassing IDBR (76.90%, 3.24%) and L2P (73.99%, 3.43%). On DBPedia, all alignment models achieve near-perfect performance (≥99.79% ACC, ≤0.11% FGT). The MTL upper bound (joint training on all tasks) is 90.75% ACC, showing alignment models approach the non-CL ceiling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The alignment models outperform all replay-based CL baselines (ER, A-GEM, MBPA++) by large margins on the 'All' sequence: Wire-Fixed+PF achieves 90.18% ACC vs. ER's 66.42% and A-GEM's 71.60%, while also reducing forgetting from 24.91% (ER) and 19.40% (A-GEM) to 1.79%. Against the strongest rehearsal-free CL baselines, Wire-Fixed+PF beats IDBR (88.89%, 2.21% FGT) by 1.29% ACC and CODA (87.95%, 2.76% FGT) by 2.23% ACC on 'All'. On the challenging News Series, C-LoRA+PF (78.59%) surpasses IDBR (76.90%) and L2P (73.99%) by 1.69% and 4.60% respectively. Among adaptation models, the alignment models clearly outperform uncontrolled LoRA (77.37% ACC, 14.89% FGT) and Adapter (76.31%, 15.97%) on 'All', while being competitive with Prefix tuning (87.53%, 3.80%) but achieving lower forgetting (1.79% vs. 3.80%). On Yahoo specifically, C-LoRA+PF achieves the best ACC (91.38%) among all CL models, edging out L2P (90.82%) and IDBR (90.48%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of continual learning (CL) and parameter-efficient adaptation. It builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by introducing a 'Controlled LoRA' variant with a scaling factor that constrains how far adapted representations deviate from pre-trained ones, and on Kumar et al. (2022) by adopting the probing-then-fine-tuning strategy to initialize class vectors. Conceptually, it departs from prior CL alignment work (Riemer et al., Guo et al.) that aligns gradients or representations between *observed* tasks, instead grounding all tasks' data representations in a shared pre-trained semantic space so that inter-task correlations are inherited from pre-trained token semantics rather than learned post-hoc. The authors explicitly distinguish their approach from adaptation models, arguing that the design motivation is representation alignment rather than parameter efficiency, and from replay-based CL methods by requiring no stored previous data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the method achieves competitive CL performance without experience replay, removing the need to store or generate previous-task data — a meaningful constraint in privacy-sensitive or memory-limited settings. The conceptual insight that pre-trained token representations serve as a natural 'global anchor' preventing destructive gradient interference across tasks is a useful reframing that could inform future CL architectures. However, the paper is limited to BERT-base on relatively small NLP classification tasks, and the authors acknowledge an unresolved 'shifting attention' problem where the model's attention on previous-task tokens can drift after learning new tasks. Whether the global alignment principle generalizes to larger models, non-NLP modalities, or more complex task sequences remains open.
