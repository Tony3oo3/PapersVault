## Problem

Deep MARL incurs substantial computational overhead due to large neural networks, and while dynamic sparse training (DST) has proven effective in supervised learning, direct adoption fails in MARL because bootstrapping learning targets, path-dependent data distributions, and partial observability collectively destabilize value learning in sparse models. The paper asks whether MARL agents can be trained effectively using ultra-sparse networks (85–95% sparsity) throughout the entire training process, rather than only at inference time.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives local observations and maintains an action-observation history τ_i, following the standard Dec-POMDP/CTDE formulation. The method produces sparse agent Q-function networks and a sparse mixing network with a fixed sparsity mask maintained throughout training, outputting individual agent actions at execution time. Training data is stored in episode form (full trajectories) rather than individual transitions, which constrains the replay buffer design and motivates the dual-buffer approach over per-sample prioritization.

## Method

MAST combines three co-designed components to enable stable value learning under extreme sparsity. First, a hybrid TD(λ) target with a burn-in phase (1-step TD for the first T₀ steps, then TD(λ) with exponentially weighted multi-step returns) reduces network fitting error, which Theorem 3.1 shows is discounted by γⁿ in the multi-step TD error bound. Second, the Soft Mellowmax operator replaces the max in the Bellman target, reducing overestimation bias by averaging over each agent's individual action space (not the exponentially growing joint space), at negligible extra cost. Third, a dual replay buffer (large off-policy B₁ + small on-policy B₂, e.g., 50:1 capacity ratio) shifts the training sample distribution toward the target policy, reducing the policy inconsistency error term in Theorem 3.1. Topology evolution follows the RigL drop-and-grow schedule, applied jointly across all agent networks treated as a single network with a global sparsity budget, allowing MAST to automatically allocate different sparsity levels to different agent types.

## Results

On the SMAC benchmark, MAST achieves average win rates of 98.9% (QMIX, 92% sparsity), 98.1% (WQMIX, 90% sparsity), and 100.6% (RES, 89% sparsity), with less than 3% degradation relative to dense counterparts. MAST achieves up to 20× reduction in both training and inference FLOPs (e.g., 0.050× dense FLOPs for QMIX at 95% sparsity on 3m), with model size compressed to as low as 0.062× the dense model. The framework extends the critical sparsity threshold beyond which performance collapses, enabling 85–95% sparsity while maintaining near-dense performance across all tested algorithms and environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAST decisively outperforms all three sparse training baselines across every environment and algorithm. Against SET (Mocanu et al., 2018), MAST leads by 25.7 points on average for QMIX (98.9% vs. 73.2%) and by 19.5 points for WQMIX (98.1% vs. 70.9%), with the gap widening on harder maps like 2c_vs_64zg (97.6% vs. 67.1% for QMIX). Against RigL (Evci et al., 2020), the advantage is even more pronounced: MAST exceeds RigL by 34.3 points on average for QMIX (98.9% vs. 64.6%) and by 29.6 points for WQMIX (98.1% vs. 69.0%), indicating that standard gradient-based topology evolution alone is insufficient for sparse MARL. Against RLx2 (Tan et al., 2022), the single-agent sparse RL framework, MAST achieves a 69.1-point margin on average for QMIX (98.9% vs. 29.8%), with RLx2 collapsing to 9.9–12.1% on harder maps, confirming that single-agent sparse training strategies do not transfer to multi-agent settings due to non-stationarity and episode-form data incompatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dynamic sparse training (DST) from supervised learning (RigL, SET) and single-agent DRL (RLx2) to the multi-agent reinforcement learning setting, where it had previously failed. The key departure is recognizing that naive DST application breaks value learning in MARL due to two compounding issues: network fitting error amplifying TD target bias, and policy inconsistency from off-policy sample distributions. MAST addresses both through a co-design of sparse training with value learning—hybrid TD(λ) targets with Soft Mellowmax for reliable targets, and dual replay buffers for rational sample distribution—rather than treating sparsity as an orthogonal compression step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAST is the first framework to train ultra-sparse (90%+) MARL agents from scratch throughout training, achieving up to 20× FLOPs reduction for both training and inference with <3% performance degradation across QMIX, WQMIX, RES, and FACMAC. This is practically significant for deploying MARL agents in compute-constrained settings (robotics, real-time strategy). Key limitations include the use of unstructured sparsity, which does not directly translate to wall-clock speedup on current hardware, and sensitivity to multiple hyperparameters (λ, burn-in time, buffer ratios). Open questions include whether structured sparsity patterns can be discovered, whether the framework extends to policy-based or model-based MARL, and whether the dual-buffer mechanism generalizes to non-episode-form data.
