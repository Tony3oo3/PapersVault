## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods fail to consistently outperform the simple Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) baseline across all evaluated datasets, with no prior method achieving improvements in every domain. The paper seeks a simple, universally beneficial strategy that can reliably boost ERM performance without requiring complex aggregation mechanisms or test-time adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from M source domains (with domain labels available during training) and produces either class labels (classification) or pixel-wise segmentation masks (semantic segmentation). A key constraint is that domain labels must be known during training to assign data to the corresponding expert, but at inference only the single target model is deployed—no experts or aggregation modules are needed. The framework is applied to standard benchmarks: DomainBed classification datasets (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraInc, DomainNet) and synthetic-to-real segmentation (GTAV/Synthia → Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary).

## Method

LFME simultaneously trains M domain-specific experts (each trained with standard cross-entropy on its own domain) and a universal target model on all domains. The target model's loss combines standard cross-entropy with a logit regularization term ‖z − q^E‖², where z is the target model's logits and q^E is the softmax probability from the corresponding domain expert—effectively a new MSE-based knowledge distillation form that regresses logits toward expert probabilities rather than matching probabilities via cross-entropy. This single regularization term produces two beneficial effects: (1) it implicitly constrains the target model's output probabilities to a smaller range, encouraging the model to learn shared information beyond dominant discriminative features; and (2) it implicitly re-weights training toward hard samples identified by the experts, which contain more cross-domain ambiguous information than hard samples from the target model itself. The approach adds only one hyperparameter (α) to ERM, requires no test-time adaptation, and uses only the target model at inference, avoiding the aggregation design and deployment overhead of naive multi-expert ensembles.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark with ResNet18, LFME achieves 60.8% average accuracy across PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraInc, and DomainNet, outperforming the ERM baseline (58.1%) by 2.7pp and the strongest competitor RIDG (60.3%) by 0.5pp. Notably, LFME is the only method to beat ERM in all five datasets (Score=5), with a particularly large 7.5pp gain on the challenging TerraInc dataset (46.3% vs. 38.8%). With ResNet50, LFME reaches 64.6% average, surpassing ERM† (62.8%) by 1.8pp and SD† (63.9%) by 0.7pp. On the semantic segmentation task (synthetic-to-real), LFME achieves 38.37% average mIOU, improving over the baseline (32.38%) by 6pp and outperforming SD (31.39%), though PinMem leads on Cityscapes (41.86% vs. 38.38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFME consistently outperforms the ERM baseline across all five DomainBed datasets (Score=5), a feat no other method achieves; the largest margin is on TerraInc where LFME gains 7.5pp over ERM (46.3% vs. 38.8%). Among the strongest competitors, LFME edges out RIDG (60.3% avg) by 0.5pp and SD (59.7% avg) by 1.1pp in average accuracy, while also surpassing them on TerraInc (46.3% vs. 43.7% and 42.0% respectively). Against MoE-based methods, LFME outperforms Meta-DMoE (59.0% avg) by 1.8pp and DAELDG (83.4% PACS avg in Table 8) by 0.3pp, confirming that the logit regularization design is superior to feature-similarity or output-averaging strategies. In the ResNet50 setting, LFME leads SD† (63.9%) by 0.7pp and ERM† (62.8%) by 1.8pp. For semantic segmentation, LFME improves over the baseline by 6.0pp in average mIOU (38.37% vs. 32.38%) and outperforms SD (31.39%), but trails PinMem (38.74%) by 0.4pp in average mIOU, with PinMem leading specifically on Cityscapes (41.86% vs. 38.38%). When combined with SWAD, LFME achieves 68.5% average, surpassing CORAL w/ SWAD (67.3%) by 1.2pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFME positions itself at the intersection of knowledge distillation and domain generalization, extending the observation that MSE loss performs comparably to cross-entropy in classification (Hui & Belkin, 2021) into the KD setting by using expert probabilities as regression targets for the target model's logits. It departs from prior expert-based DG methods (DAELDG, Meta-DMoE) that aggregate expert outputs at test time or enforce feature-level similarity, instead distilling all expert knowledge into a single model via a simple logit regularization term during training. The paper also explicitly contrasts with label smoothing, showing that the probability-range regularization effect is achieved inherently without hand-crafted calibration, and with naive aggregation strategies (averaging, model soup, confidence-based selection), which it demonstrates to be ineffective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' finding—that simply adding an MSE term with one-hot labels to cross-entropy (ERM+) consistently improves DG without any experts or extra architecture—offers a practically valuable, zero-cost baseline improvement for the field. The insight that hard samples from domain-specialized experts reveal more out-of-domain information than hard samples from the multi-domain model itself provides a concrete mechanism for why expert-based guidance helps generalization, with implications for future hard-sample mining strategies. Key limitations remain: LFME requires domain labels during training (ruling out internet-collected data), cannot handle single-source DG, and the theoretical analyses are built primarily for classification tasks, leaving extension to regression and label-free settings open.
