## Problem

Goldwasser et al. (2022) proved that undetectable backdoors exist for arbitrary ML models under black-box access, but left open whether white-box undetectability is possible for general models. Their white-box results were restricted to highly structured training procedures like Random Fourier Features. This paper closes that gap by showing that any training pipeline followed by an obfuscation step (the 'honest obfuscated pipeline') admits white-box undetectable and non-replicable backdoors under standard cryptographic assumptions.

## Input / Output

The Backdoor procedure takes a trained L-Lipschitz ANN f: [0,1]^n → [0,1] (or a language model f: T^k → Δ(T)) and outputs an obfuscated backdoored model f̃ along with a backdoor key bk consisting of a PRG seed s* ∈ {0,1}^{λ₁} and a digital-signature secret key sk. The Activate procedure takes a feature vector x and bk, producing a perturbed x′ with ‖x − x′‖∞ ≤ ε that forces the backdoored model to output a chosen target value c. For language models, the input is a discrete token sequence and the trigger is embedded via a steganographic function σ that encodes the seed into the prompt without altering its apparent meaning.

## Method

The construction proceeds at the Boolean circuit level: the trained ANN is converted to a circuit C, a backdoor is planted by partitioning the input into x_C (most significant bits) and x_BD (least significant bits), and the backdoored circuit C̃ routes to a malicious circuit Ĉ only when PRG(x_PRG) = PRG(s*) and a digital signature Verify(pk, x_C, x_S) succeeds. Indistinguishability obfuscation (iO) is then applied to C̃, making it computationally indistinguishable from iO(C) even under full white-box inspection, and the result is converted back to an ANN. The PRG provides average-case hardness for undetectability (resolving the quantifier-order issue that makes naive SAT-based triggers detectable), while the digital signature ensures non-replicability: an adversary who observes backdoored examples cannot forge new valid signatures without the secret key. For language models, where discrete text precludes least-significant-bit manipulation, a steganographic function σ embeds the activation seed into the prompt, and the same iO-based circuit construction hides the dual honest/malicious model architecture.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results tables. The main result (Theorem 12) proves that under the existence of one-way functions and indistinguishability obfuscation (iO), for every honest obfuscated pipeline there exists a backdoor attack on ANNs that is simultaneously white-box undetectable and non-replicable. The backdoor runs in poly(n, sz(C), λ, λ₁, λ₂, log(L), log(1/ε)) time and guarantees that for any activated input x′, ‖x − x′‖∞ ≤ ε and |f′(x′) − c| ≤ ε for any desired output c. A second main result (Theorem 29) extends the construction to language models using steganographic functions, showing white-box undetectable backdoors for LMs under the same cryptographic assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no experimental baselines or quantitative comparisons, as it is a theoretical existence paper. Its primary comparison is with Goldwasser et al. (FOCS 2022), who proved black-box undetectable backdoors for arbitrary models and white-box undetectable backdoors only for the specific Random Fourier Features (RFF) model under the CLWE assumption. This paper strictly generalizes both results: it achieves white-box undetectability (a stronger notion than black-box) for any training procedure followed by an iO-based obfuscation step, rather than being restricted to RFF. The non-replicability guarantee is also extended to the white-box setting via digital signatures embedded in the obfuscated circuit. The LLM extension (Theorem 29) is novel relative to all prior work, as no prior theoretical construction provided white-box undetectable backdoors for language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the undetectable backdoor framework of Goldwasser et al. [2022], who proved black-box undetectability for arbitrary models and white-box undetectability only for the restricted RFF setting. The key departure is showing that white-box undetectability holds for *general* neural networks when the model passes through an indistinguishability obfuscation (iO) step, leveraging the honest obfuscated pipeline as a realistic deployment assumption. The LLM extension repurposes steganographic functions (from the watermarking literature of Christ et al., Zamir) as the trigger mechanism, since the least-significant-bit manipulation used for continuous ANN inputs is inapplicable to discrete token sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that full white-box access to weights and architecture is provably insufficient to detect a backdoor when iO-based obfuscation is applied—challenges the natural defense strategy of model inspection and has direct implications for ML supply-chain security (e.g., banks auditing third-party models). The construction is purely theoretical under strong cryptographic assumptions (iO, one-way functions), and the authors explicitly note that practical instantiations with heuristic obfuscation remain open, as does whether weaker cryptographic primitives suffice. The LLM backdoor formulation (Definition 27) is a first theoretical treatment of the problem, though non-replicability for discrete inputs is left as future work.
