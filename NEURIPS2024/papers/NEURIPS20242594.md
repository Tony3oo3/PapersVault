## Problem

Existing human evaluation protocols for text-to-video (T2V) models suffer from three critical deficiencies: poor reproducibility (inconsistent metrics, methods, and annotator sources across studies), low reliability (untrained annotators with no quality checks), and impractical cost (pairwise comparisons scale as O(N²) with the number of models). The paper identifies that nearly 70% of surveyed papers use comparison-based evaluation but rely on simple win ratios, and most omit annotator training entirely, making results difficult to compare or reproduce.

## Input / Output

The protocol consumes text prompts sampled from the VBench Prompt Suite per Category (8 categories, one quarter selected per category) and videos generated by multiple T2V models under default settings, with all videos height-standardized in the annotation interface. Annotators perform pairwise comparisons across six metrics, and the dynamic module additionally ingests automatic metric scores (subject consistency, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, imaging quality, overall consistency) computed by a pre-trained VBench scorer on an A100 GPU. The output is a set of model score estimates and rankings per metric, derived by maximizing the Rao-Kupper log-likelihood, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals computed per annotator.

## Method

T2VHE defines six evaluation metrics (four objective: video quality, temporal quality, motion quality, text alignment; two subjective: ethical robustness, human preference), each with explicit definitions and 2–3 reference perspectives, and uses comparison-based scoring rather than absolute Likert scales to reduce annotation noise. Instead of win ratios, it quantifies pairwise results via the Rao-Kupper probabilistic model, which estimates per-model strength parameters and a tie-tolerance parameter θ by maximizing a log-likelihood with L-BFGS-B optimization, enabling reliable rankings even under incomplete comparison graphs. A two-part annotator training scheme (instruction-based guidelines plus worked examples per reference perspective) is shown to raise post-training LRA inter-annotator agreement to near-AMT levels (e.g., Krippendorff's α for video quality rises from 0.185 to 0.411). The dynamic evaluation module applies a video quality proximity rule (sorting prompt groups by automatic-score difference via an exponential decay function so that hard-to-distinguish pairs are annotated first) and a model strength rule (discarding subsequent pairs with probability proportional to the exponential decay of the current model-score gap), updating Rao-Kupper estimates every 5 batches and terminating when rankings stabilize for 5 consecutive batches, reducing annotation cost to ~53% of the full protocol.

## Results

The T2VHE protocol evaluates five T2V models (Gen2, Pika, TF-T2V, Latte, Videocrafter2) across six metrics using 2,000 video pairs. Gen2 ranks first on all six metrics for both AMT annotators and post-training LRAs (e.g., Video Quality score 2.25 for AMT, 2.75 for post-training LRAs with dynamic module). The dynamic evaluation module reduces annotation volume from 2,000 to ~1,068 video pairs per annotator (~53% cost reduction) while preserving model rankings. Post-training LRAs achieve inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff's α) approaching AMT levels (e.g., Video Quality: 0.411 vs. 0.451), confirming that structured training enables reliable lab-recruited annotation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a protocol paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key comparisons are internal. (1) Annotator quality: Pre-training LRAs show significantly lower agreement with AMT annotators (e.g., Krippendorff's α = 0.185 for Video Quality) compared to post-training LRAs (0.411), which closely match AMT's internal consensus (0.451). (2) Dynamic vs. static evaluation: The dynamic module annotates only ~1,068 of 2,000 video pairs yet produces identical model rankings to full annotation, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals confirming Gen2's superiority even at the lower bound. (3) Closed-source vs. open-source: Gen2 and Pika consistently outperform all open-source models on video quality, temporal quality, and motion quality; however, Latte surpasses Pika on text alignment (1.40 vs. 1.00 for AMT) and ethical robustness (1.29 vs. 0.82), yielding a higher human preference rank among open-source models. (4) The protocol demonstrates nearly linear annotation cost growth with model count versus the O(N²) growth of exhaustive pairwise comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant human evaluation practices in video generation (surveyed across 89 papers since 2016), which typically rely on win ratios, untrained laboratory-recruited annotators, and inconsistent metric definitions. It builds on the Rao and Kupper probabilistic model for pairwise comparisons to replace the biased and information-poor win-ratio quantification, and draws on VBench's curated prompt suite for evaluation stimuli. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a full standardized protocol—comprising explicit metric definitions with reference perspectives, structured annotator training, and a dynamic evaluation module—that no prior T2V evaluation paper had systematically provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By open-sourcing a complete, reproducible human evaluation protocol, this paper addresses a critical infrastructure gap in the rapidly evolving T2V field where models like Sora, Gen2, and Pika are advancing faster than evaluation standards. The practical finding that trained LRAs can match crowdsourced annotators (Krippendorff's α approaching AMT internal agreement) and that the dynamic module cuts annotation costs by ~50% while preserving rankings makes rigorous human evaluation accessible to smaller labs. Open questions include how the protocol scales beyond five models, whether the dynamic module's initial strength priors remain effective as model quality gaps narrow, and whether the observed open-source vs. closed-source gap will persist as open models improve.
