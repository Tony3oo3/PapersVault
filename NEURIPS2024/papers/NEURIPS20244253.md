## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off between visual fidelity and computational cost: smaller patch sizes capture fine details but inflate token length (and thus quadratic attention cost), while larger patches are efficient but produce distorted images. Even with carefully tuned patch sizes (e.g., DiT's patch size of 2), existing models like DiT and U-ViT still exhibit discernible image distortions, and the standard upsampling module (a single linear layer plus pixel shuffle) is too simplistic to recover fine-grained structure from low-resolution features.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and Gaussian noise as input and produces a generated RGB image. For 64×64 generation, DiMR operates directly in pixel space; for 256×256 and 512×512, it operates in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE (32×32×4 and 64×64×4 respectively), with the VAE decoder reconstructing the final image. The multi-resolution network internally produces noise predictions at R different spatial resolutions, but only the full-resolution branch's prediction is used in the iterative denoising loop; intermediate-scale predictions serve as auxiliary training targets.

## Method

DiMR introduces a Multi-Resolution Network with R branches that progressively refine features from low to high resolution via a 'feature cascade': each branch patchifies the input at a different scale (kernel/stride = 2^(R−r)), adds the upsampled output of the previous branch, and processes the result through Transformer blocks (branch 1, lowest resolution) or ConvNeXt blocks (remaining branches, higher resolution). To inject time information into both block types without the parameter-heavy MLP of adaLN-Zero, the paper proposes Time-Dependent Layer Normalization (TD-LN), which formulates the LN affine parameters γ(t) and β(t) as sigmoid-gated linear interpolations of two learnable vectors, motivated by PCA showing that adaLN-Zero's learned parameters are largely captured by two principal components. Training uses a multi-scale loss that sums MSE at each branch's resolution with weights inversely proportional to the squared downsampling factor, and replaces standard MLPs with GeGLU for further parameter efficiency. The architecture achieves FID 1.63 on ImageNet 256×256 (DiMR-G, 1.06B params) and 2.89 on ImageNet 512×512 (DiMR-XL/3R, 525M params), outperforming DiT and U-ViT with fewer or comparable parameters and fewer training epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DiMR-G/2R (1.06B params) achieves a state-of-the-art FID-50K of 1.63 with classifier-free guidance (CFG) and 3.56 without CFG after 800 epochs. DiMR-XL/2R (505M params) achieves FID 1.70 with CFG and 4.50 without CFG at the same training budget. On ImageNet 512×512, DiMR-XL/3R (525M params) reaches FID 2.89 with CFG after 800 epochs, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (675M) which achieves 3.04 with 525 GFLOPs vs. DiMR's 206 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMR-XL/2R (505M, 800 epochs) outperforms DiT-XL/2 (675M, 1399 epochs) by 0.57 FID with CFG (1.70 vs. 2.27) and 5.12 FID without CFG (4.50 vs. 9.62), using fewer parameters and fewer training epochs. Against U-ViT-H/2 (501M, 400 epochs) at matched training budget, DiMR-XL/2R improves FID from 2.29 to 1.77 with CFG and from 6.58 to 4.87 without CFG. On ImageNet 512×512, DiMR-XL/3R (525M) beats DiT-XL/2 (675M) by 0.15 FID (2.89 vs. 3.04) while using only 39.2% of the GFLOPs (206 vs. 525). Against U-ViT-H/4 (501M, 400 epochs) on 512×512, DiMR-XL/3R improves FID from 4.05 to 3.23. The paper also compares against GAN (BigGAN, GigaGAN), masked-prediction (MaskGIT, MDT, MaskBit), autoregressive (VQGAN, VAR), and other diffusion baselines (DiffiT, SiT, DiffuSSM) in extended tables, where DiMR-G/2R achieves the best FID of 1.63 among all diffusion-based methods on 256×256, though MaskBit (1.52) and MDTv2-G (1.58) from the masked-prediction family report slightly lower FID scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMR extends the Transformer-based diffusion model lineage established by DiT and U-ViT, which both rely on single-resolution patchified inputs processed by homogeneous Transformer blocks. The key architectural departure is the multi-resolution feature cascade, inspired by cascaded diffusion models (CDM), which progressively upsamples features across branches using a hybrid of Transformer blocks (lowest resolution) and ConvNeXt blocks (higher resolutions). The Time-Dependent Layer Normalization (TD-LN) is a direct simplification of DiT's adaLN-Zero, motivated by a PCA analysis showing that the learned scale/shift parameters are effectively 2-dimensional, replacing a parameter-heavy MLP with a lightweight sigmoid-based linear interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiMR addresses a fundamental trade-off in Transformer-based diffusion models—patch size versus visual fidelity—by processing features at multiple resolutions within a single network, achieving SOTA FID scores (1.63 on ImageNet 256×256, 2.89 on 512×512) with fewer GFLOPs than DiT. The TD-LN mechanism is a practical, parameter-efficient time-conditioning alternative that generalizes to non-Transformer blocks, making it broadly applicable. However, the work is limited to class-conditional generation (not text-to-image), and scaling exploration stops at ~525M parameters, leaving open whether the multi-resolution design maintains its advantages at the multi-billion-parameter scale where recent methods operate.
