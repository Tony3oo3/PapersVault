## Problem

Real-world image restoration is hampered by two intertwined gaps: existing IR datasets are small (a few thousand images), labor-intensive to curate via web scraping, and riddled with copyright and privacy concerns; and current diffusion-based restoration models neglect the degradation priors present in low-quality inputs, limiting their adaptability to the diverse and complex degradations encountered in the wild.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241760/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single low-quality RGB image (256×256 or 1024×1024, at ×4 scaling factor) and produces a photorealistic high-quality restoration. A lightweight SwinIR network first generates a smoother reference image from the LQ input, and an MLLM (LLaVA) produces a detailed text caption that conditions the diffusion process. The GenIR data pipeline separately generates 1 million high-resolution (2040×1356) synthetic HQ images with no identifiable individuals, used for training.

## Method

DreamClear is built on PixArt-α (a DiT-based T2I model) with a dual-branch architecture: an LQ branch and a Reference branch (SwinIR output) are fused via a novel Mixture of Adaptive Modulator (MoAM), which computes a token-wise degradation map through cross-attention between LQ and reference features, then dynamically routes each token across K=3 restoration experts (AdaLN-based modulators) via a two-layer MLP router, enabling per-token adaptation to varying degradation severities. A ControlFormer module—ControlNet's trainable copy blocks and zero-initialized layers re-architected for the DiT backbone—provides spatial conditioning from both LQ and reference features. The GenIR pipeline, built on SDXL, fine-tunes the T2I model with learned positive/negative prompt tokens (dual-prompt learning) and uses Gemini-1.5-Pro to generate diverse scene descriptions and filter outputs for quality and privacy, yielding a 1M-image training set. Training uses the standard latent diffusion loss at 1024×1024 resolution; inference employs iDDPM with 50 steps and CFG guidance scale ω=4.5.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks, DreamClear achieves the best FID (20.61 on DIV2K-Val, 22.06 on LSDIR-Val) and DISTS (0.1637, 0.1656), outperforming the closest competitor SeeSR (FID 24.98, 25.91) by a wide margin. On real-world benchmarks, DreamClear attains the best FID (65.37 on RealSR, 74.78 on DRealSR) and lowest NIQE (4.4381, 4.6295), while also leading on CLIPIQA (0.6895, 0.6620) and MANIQA. On the no-GT RealLQ250 benchmark, DreamClear achieves the best NIQE (3.5556) and CLIPIQA (0.7116). On downstream tasks, DreamClear achieves the highest object detection AP (19.3), instance segmentation AP (16.7), and semantic segmentation mIoU (31.9) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamClear's closest competitor is SeeSR, another diffusion-based method with semantic guidance. DreamClear decisively outperforms SeeSR on FID (20.61 vs 24.98 on DIV2K-Val; 65.37 vs 71.92 on RealSR) and DISTS (0.1637 vs 0.1886), and on NIQE across all real-world benchmarks (e.g., 4.4381 vs 4.9102 on RealSR). However, SeeSR edges out DreamClear on MUSIQ (72.04 vs 68.44 on DIV2K-Val; 70.57 vs 66.76 on RealLQ250) and CLIPIQA on synthetic sets (0.7181 vs 0.6963 on DIV2K-Val). Against SUPIR, the other major diffusion-based baseline, DreamClear wins on FID, DISTS, LPIPS, NIQE, and CLIPIQA across all five benchmarks, with a particularly large FID gap on DRealSR (74.78 vs 90.78). All GAN-based methods (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, DASR) are substantially outperformed by DreamClear on perceptual and no-reference metrics, though SwinIR-GAN retains an advantage on reference-based LPIPS/DISTS for real-world benchmarks (0.2680 vs 0.3684 on RealSR), reflecting the trade-off between pixel fidelity and perceptual realism. On downstream tasks (Table 2), DreamClear achieves the best object detection AP (19.3), instance segmentation AP (16.7), and semantic segmentation mIoU (31.9), surpassing SeeSR (18.2, 15.9, 26.9) and SUPIR (16.6, 14.1, 27.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamClear extends the line of diffusion-based image restoration (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, SUPIR) by migrating from U-Net-based Stable Diffusion to a DiT backbone (PixArt-α), and introduces ControlFormer as a DiT-adapted analogue of ControlNet for spatial conditioning. The Mixture of Adaptive Modulator (MoAM) repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm for token-wise degradation-aware routing, a departure from prior diffusion IR methods that largely ignore degradation priors in the LQ input. The GenIR pipeline is the first to fine-tune a T2I model (SDXL) with dual-prompt learning specifically for privacy-safe, copyright-free IR dataset curation, contrasting with the web-scraping approach used by DIV2K, Flickr2K, and SUPIR's 20M-image collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-image GenIR dataset addresses a critical data bottleneck in real-world IR, where existing open-source collections contain only a few thousand images and large-scale alternatives (e.g., SUPIR's 20M) raise copyright and privacy concerns. The MoAM mechanism offers a principled way to handle the heterogeneous degradation landscape of real-world images without requiring explicit degradation labels. Key limitations remain: diffusion-based inference (50 iDDPM steps) precludes real-time deployment, synthesized textures may diverge from ground truth under severe degradation, and PSNR/SSIM scores are lower than GAN-based baselines, reflecting the known perceptual-fidelity trade-off. Open questions include whether model distillation can close the speed gap and whether synthetic data at this scale truly captures the full diversity of real-world camera degradations.
