## Problem

In continual learning, balancing sensitivity to new tasks against stability of old knowledge is critical to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Zeroth-order sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) has been adopted in CL to improve generalization, but it can converge to sharper suboptimal minima rather than the flatter global optima needed for the joint old-and-new knowledge space. The paper identifies that no existing CL optimizer simultaneously enforces both low loss and neighborhood smoothness, leaving a gap between what zeroth-order sharpness guarantees and what true flatness requires.

## Input / Output

C-Flat operates as a drop-in optimizer wrapper around any existing CL method, consuming the same RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet-100) and producing the same class-label predictions. The training data at each phase T consists of the current task's samples plus a replay buffer of exemplars from prior tasks (for memory-based methods) or a gradient projection memory (for regularization-based methods). The only additional computational requirement is 2 forward and 4 backward passes per iteration (versus 1+1 for vanilla SGD), with Hessian-vector products replacing full Hessian computation to keep space complexity at O(n).

## Method

C-Flat augments the standard CL loss with two regularization terms: a zeroth-order sharpness term R_ρ^0 (maximal neighborhood loss difference, inherited from SAM) that drives the model toward low-loss minima, and a first-order flatness term R_ρ^1 (maximal neighborhood gradient norm, inherited from GAM) weighted by λ that constrains the smoothness of the loss surface around those minima. The combined objective min_θ max_{θ'∈B(θ,ρ)} [ℓ(θ') + λρ·‖∇ℓ(θ')‖₂] is optimized via alternating perturbations: a gradient-direction perturbation for the zeroth-order term and a Hessian-vector-product perturbation for the first-order term, yielding 2 forward + 4 backward passes per step. Theoretically, the paper proves that R_ρ^0 is upper-bounded by R_ρ^1 (via the mean value theorem), that C-Flat converges under standard smoothness assumptions with decaying ρ_i = ρ/i^{1/4}, and that the total regularization at a local minimum is bounded by (1+λ)ρ²·λ_max(∇²ℓ), directly linking C-Flat to Hessian curvature. The method is plug-and-play: a single line of code (C_Flat(params, base_optimizer, model, args)) wraps any CL optimizer, and a dedicated C-Flat-GPM variant (Algorithm 2) integrates the flatness perturbation into the orthogonal gradient projection step of GPM-family methods.

## Results

C-Flat is plugged into 7 state-of-the-art CL methods spanning Memory-based, Regularization-based, and Expansion-based categories, evaluated on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and Tiny-ImageNet across six incremental settings. The average accuracy boost across all methods and settings ranges from +0.62% (ImageNet-100 B50_Inc10) to +1.34% (CIFAR-100 B0_Inc20), with maximum per-method gains up to +3.03% (WA on ImageNet-100 B50_Inc25: 80.81→83.84). C-Flat improves nearly all baselines, with only two minor drops (Replay on B50_Inc10: −0.29%, iCaRL on B50_Inc25: −1.03%) and one exact tie (FOSTER on B50_Inc25). Hessian eigenvalue analysis confirms C-Flat induces significantly flatter minima (e.g., MEMO max eigenvalue drops from 67.48 to 28.11 at epoch 50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-Flat consistently outperforms all 7 baselines across nearly every dataset and incremental setting. The largest gains appear on regularization-based methods: WA improves by +2.34% on CIFAR-100 B0_Inc5 (63.36→65.70) and +3.03% on ImageNet-100 B50_Inc25 (80.81→83.84), while PODNet gains +1.65% on CIFAR-100 B0_Inc5 (48.05→49.70). Expansion-based methods also benefit substantially: DER improves by +1.51% on CIFAR-100 B0_Inc5 (69.99→71.11) and +1.51% on Tiny-ImageNet (58.63→60.14), and MEMO gains up to +1.88% on CIFAR-100 B0_Inc20 (69.91→71.79). Memory-based methods show more modest but consistent gains (Replay +1.15% on B0_Inc5, iCaRL +1.80% on B0_Inc20). In the FS-DGPM series (Table 2), C-Flat replaces the zeroth-order FS term and achieves +0.35% to +0.79% over all GPM variants, confirming that first-order flatness surpasses zeroth-order sharpness. The only losses are minor: Replay drops 0.29% on ImageNet-100 B50_Inc10 and iCaRL drops 1.03% on B50_Inc25, while FOSTER ties exactly on B50_Inc25.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-Flat unifies two previously separate lines of optimization research: zeroth-order sharpness-aware minimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) and first-order gradient-norm flatness (GAM, Zhang et al. 2023), combining them into a single loss for continual learning. It departs from prior CL-specific sharpness work (FS-DGPM, Deng et al. 2021; SAM-CL, Tran et al. 2023) by arguing that zeroth-order sharpness alone can converge to sharper suboptimal minima, and that the first-order flatness term is necessary to guarantee genuinely flatter solutions. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play optimizer wrapper rather than a new CL architecture, distinguishing it from the memory-, regularization-, and expansion-based method families it is applied to.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a one-line-of-code optimizer that consistently improves 7 diverse CL methods across 3 datasets and 6 incremental settings without requiring method-specific redesign. The theoretical contribution (convergence guarantee, upper bound linking C-Flat regularization to Hessian eigenvalues) provides grounding that prior CL sharpness work lacked. However, the gains are modest (typically 0.5–2%), no error bars are reported, and the evaluation is confined to class-incremental image classification with ResNet backbones. Open questions include whether the zeroth-plus-first-order combination is necessary at larger scale, whether it transfers to task- or domain-incremental settings, and whether the Hessian-vector-product overhead (2 forward + 4 backward per step) remains acceptable for very large models.
