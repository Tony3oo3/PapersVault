## Problem

Randomized smoothing certification requires estimating class probabilities from binomial samples, and the standard practice of drawing a fixed 100,000 samples per point is both computationally expensive and statistically suboptimal. The Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals used in practice are conservative, unnecessarily shrinking certified radii, and the fixed-sample-size protocol cannot adapt to the difficulty of individual points—wasting samples on easy cases and being unable to request more when needed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Bernoulli samples generated by repeatedly evaluating the smoothed classifier F(x+δ) at random noise δ, along with a target radius r and confidence level α (typically 0.001). Unlike standard randomized smoothing which requires a predetermined sample count n, the proposed confidence sequences adaptively determine when sufficient evidence has been gathered, halting as soon as the point can be certified or rejected at the target radius. The output is a binary robustness decision with guaranteed type-1 error at most α, plus the confidence interval for the class probability at each stopping time.

## Method

The paper makes two core contributions. First, it introduces randomized Clopper-Pearson intervals that add a uniform random variable to interpolate between deterministic interval boundaries, achieving exact 1−α coverage and provable optimality (shortest possible among all confidence intervals for binomials). Second, it constructs confidence sequences for sequential estimation: a union-bound variant that recomputes randomized Clopper-Pearson intervals at geometrically spaced sample sizes (achieving the optimal √((log(1/α) + log log t)/t) width via the doubling trick), and a betting-based variant that uses a martingale wealth process with the Krichevsky-Trofimov estimator and Ville's inequality to produce time-uniform bounds. The betting approach yields a slightly wider √((log(1/α) + log t)/t) rate but is simpler to implement, and both methods are shown to have matching lower bounds, confirming optimal sample complexity for the sequential decision task.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WideResNet-40, ℓ₂, σ=0.12), the proposed Betting Confidence Sequence requires 531±157 samples to certify radius r=0.5, compared to 1976±41 for the Horváth et al. (2022) adaptive method — a 3.7× reduction. At r=1.25 and r=2, the Betting CS needs 2169±257 and 2130±339 samples respectively, versus 3593±574 and 4623±47 for the baseline. In the fixed-width estimation task (Table 2, σ=1), the Union-Bound CS requires 21,513 samples to achieve width ε=0.03, while Horváth et al. needs 81,080 — a 3.8× improvement. The randomized Clopper-Pearson intervals also eliminate the sharp drop at the end of ℓ₂ robustness curves observed with standard deterministic intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the adaptive certification method of Horváth et al. (2022), which sequentially tries predetermined sample sizes {10², 10³, 10⁴, 1.2×10⁵} with Bonferroni correction. The proposed Betting Confidence Sequence (Algorithm 2) consistently outperforms this baseline across all tested radii: it requires 3.7× fewer samples at r=0.5 (531 vs. 1976), 1.7× fewer at r=1.25 (2169 vs. 3593), and 2.2× fewer at r=2 (2130 vs. 4623). The Union-Bound Confidence Sequence (Algorithm 1) also beats the baseline at every radius but is slightly worse than the Betting CS. In the fixed-width estimation task (Table 2), both proposed methods require roughly 3–4× fewer samples than Horváth et al. across all target widths ε ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.03}. The paper also notes that Horváth et al. always abstains on hard examples (those requiring more than n_s samples), while the confidence sequence methods can continue drawing samples adaptively without exhausting the failure budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical estimation toolkit underlying randomized smoothing by building on two distinct lineages: (1) the classical Clopper-Pearson exact binomial confidence intervals (Clopper & Pearson, 1934), which the paper shows are conservative and improves via a randomized variant achieving exact coverage and optimality; and (2) the betting/martingale framework for confidence sequences (Orabona & Jun, 2023; Krichevsky & Trofimov, 1981), which the paper specializes to Bernoulli random variables to obtain adaptive certification procedures. It departs from the standard fixed-sample-size practice (n=100,000) and from the closest prior adaptive method (Horvath et al., 2022), which uses a finite grid of sample sizes with Bonferroni correction, by providing a principled sequential procedure with matching lower bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The computational cost of randomized smoothing (typically 10^5 forward passes per certified point) is a major barrier to real-world deployment, and this paper demonstrates that the standard estimation protocol is both theoretically suboptimal and practically wasteful, with the implicit type-2 error being exponentially small in n. The proposed confidence sequences reduce sample counts by 2–4× in experiments while maintaining formal guarantees, and the randomized Clopper-Pearson interval eliminates the sharp drops at the end of robustness curves. Open questions include whether the betting-based sequence (which has a log(t) rather than log log(t) term) can be made asymptotically optimal in practice, whether these techniques transfer to de-randomized smoothing or non-additive threat models, and whether the speedups persist at ImageNet scale with larger σ values where certification is inherently harder.
