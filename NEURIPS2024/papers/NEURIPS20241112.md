## Problem

Classical zeroth-order methods for finding (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary points of Lipschitz non-smooth non-convex objectives face an irreducible query complexity bottleneck of O(δ^{-1}ε^{-3}), matching known lower bounds. No prior work demonstrated quantum speedups for this most general function class, leaving open whether quantum oracles can break the classical ε^{-3} barrier.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a quantum stochastic function value oracle U_F that maps |x⟩|ξ⟩|b⟩ → |x⟩|ξ⟩|b+F(x,ξ)⟩ in superposition, along with an initial point x₀ ∈ ℝ^d and problem parameters (δ, ε, L). The output is a point x_out satisfying dist(0, ∂_δ f(x_out)) ≤ ε, i.e., a (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary point. A key structural detail is that the quantum gradient estimator O_{g_δ} produces a superposition over the joint distribution of (ξ, w) rather than a single classical sample, enabling quantum mean estimation to reduce variance with fewer oracle calls.

## Method

The paper constructs a quantum δ-estimated stochastic gradient oracle that produces a superposition over the random variable g_δ(x;w,ξ) = (d/2δ)(F(x+δw;ξ)−F(x−δw;ξ))·w using only two queries to U_F, then applies quantum mean estimation (Cornelissen et al.) to build mini-batch estimators with variance Õ(dLσ^{-1}) queries—improving over the classical Õ(dL²σ^{-2}) by a factor of Lσ^{-1}. QGFM performs standard gradient descent on the smoothed surrogate f_δ with these quantum estimators, achieving Õ(d^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-3}). QGFM+ incorporates the PAGE variance-reduction framework in a single loop: with probability p_t = ε^{2/3}/L^{2/3} it computes a fresh full gradient estimator, otherwise it computes a difference estimator Δg_{t+1} whose variance is set adaptively proportional to ‖x_{t+1}−x_t‖², yielding Õ(d^{3/2}δ^{-1}ε^{-7/3})—a factor of ε^{-2/3} better than the best classical method. The adaptive variance scheduling is the key mechanism that distinguishes QGFM+ from both classical GFM+ and the fixed-variance Q-SPIDER.

## Results

The paper establishes query complexity bounds for quantum zeroth-order methods finding (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary points of Lipschitz non-smooth non-convex objectives. QGFM achieves Õ(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-3}) queries of the quantum stochastic function value oracle (Theorem 4.1), matching the best classical rate in ε-dependence. QGFM+ with variance reduction improves this to Õ(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-7/3}) (Theorem 4.3), surpassing the optimal classical bound of O(δ^{-1} ε^{-3}) by a factor of ε^{-2/3}. For smooth non-convex optimization, QGM+ achieves Õ(√d ε^{-7/3}) quantum stochastic gradient oracle queries (Theorem G.1), improving upon Q-SPIDER's Õ(√d ε^{-5/2}) by a factor of ε^{-1/6}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QGFM+ (Theorem 4.3) is the headline result, achieving Õ(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-7/3}) and beating the best classical zeroth-order method GFM+ [6] (O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-3})) by a factor of ε^{-2/3} in the ε-dependence. It also outperforms OptimalZO [32] (O(d δ^{-1} ε^{-3})) by the same ε^{-2/3} factor, though OptimalZO has a better d-dependence (d vs. d^{3/2}). QGFM (Theorem 4.1) matches GFM+ in ε-scaling but requires a quantum oracle, and speedups GFM [35] (O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-4})) by a factor of ε^{-1}. For the smooth non-convex setting, QGM+ (Theorem G.1) improves upon Q-SPIDER [48] (Õ(√d ε^{-5/2})) by a factor of ε^{-1/6}, and also surpasses the classical SPIDER/PAGE [18, 34] (O(ε^{-3})) in ε-dependence. The paper notes that the d^{3/2} dimension dependence remains a limitation compared to OptimalZO's linear d-dependence, and that the quantum speedup is specifically in the ε-exponent rather than the dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: classical zeroth-order methods for non-smooth non-convex optimization (GFM [35], GFM+ [6], OptimalZO [32]) and quantum speedups for stochastic optimization (Q-SPIDER [48], quantum mean estimation [12]). The key conceptual move is to construct quantum gradient estimators for the δ-smoothed surrogate of a Lipschitz objective using only O(1) queries to a quantum stochastic function value oracle, then plug these into a PAGE-style variance reduction loop. This extends the quantum speedup paradigm from smooth non-convex settings [48] to the fundamentally harder non-smooth non-convex regime, where prior negative results [19, 60] had suggested quantum methods might offer no advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that quantum zeroth-order methods can break the classical lower bound of δ^{-1}ε^{-3} for non-smooth non-convex optimization, achieving ε^{-7/3} dependence via QGFM+. The result also yields a modest improvement (ε^{-1/6}) over Q-SPIDER for the smooth case. However, the quantum advantage comes at the cost of a d^{3/2} dimension dependence (vs. d for the best classical method OptimalZO), and the entire framework relies on idealized quantum oracles and state preparation that remain far from NISQ-era hardware. Open questions include whether the dimension dependence can be reduced, whether similar speedups exist for deterministic or constrained settings, and whether the approach extends to quantum online optimization.
