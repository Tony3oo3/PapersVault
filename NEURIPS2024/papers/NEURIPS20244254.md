## Problem

While AIPW/doubly-robust estimators are known to be semiparametrically efficient under i.i.d. data, their non-asymptotic (finite-sample) behavior under adaptively collected data—where the behavioral policy at each step depends on the full history of prior observations—remains largely uncharacterized. Prior adaptive-experiment work established only asymptotic normality, leaving open what finite-sample guarantees are achievable and how the history-dependent data collection mechanism affects estimation error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n sequentially collected context-action-outcome triples (X_i, A_i, Y_i) generated by history-dependent behavioral policies, together with the revealed propensity scores π_i*(X_i, O_{i-1}; A_i) and a user-specified evaluation function g: X×A→R. It produces a scalar off-policy value estimate τ̂_n^AIPW and, as an intermediate artifact, a sequence of n treatment-effect estimates {μ̂_i(O_{i-1})} that must be formed online (before observing the i-th outcome). Unlike i.i.d. settings where cross-fitting splits the data, here each estimate μ̂_i is computed from the full trajectory O_{i-1} up to step i−1, reflecting the sequential nature of adaptive collection.

## Method

The paper's central reduction shows that the MSE of the AIPW estimator decomposes into an irreducible variance v_*²/n plus a sequentially weighted ℓ₂-estimation error of the treatment effect, where the weights g²(X_i,A_i)/π_i*²(X_i,O_{i-1};A_i) encode the history-dependent overlap. This error is then minimized by casting treatment-effect estimation as an n-round online non-parametric regression game (Algorithm 2) and applying no-regret online convex optimization algorithms. Three concrete instantiations are provided: OGD for finite (tabular) state-action spaces, OGD for linear function approximation with bounded feature maps, and a general function-class reduction via standard online non-parametric regression theory. The resulting oracle inequality (Theorem 3.2) bounds the MSE by (1/n)(v_*² + Regret/n + inf_{μ∈F}‖μ−μ*‖²_(n)), and a local minimax lower bound (Theorem 4.1) confirms that the v_*²/n term is instance-dependent optimal, establishing that no-regret online learning recovers the semiparametric efficiency limit even under adaptive data collection.

## Results

The paper establishes a non-asymptotic MSE upper bound (Theorem 3.1) for the AIPW estimator: MSE ≤ (1/n){v_*² + sequentially weighted estimation error}, where v_*² is the optimal oracle variance. An oracle inequality (Theorem 3.2) further decomposes the error into an O(Regret/n) term plus an approximation error inf{‖μ−μ*‖²_(n) : μ∈F}. For the tabular and linear function approximation settings, OGD achieves O(√n) regret (Theorems 3.3–3.4), yielding an MSE rate of O(v_*²/n + 1/√n + approximation error). A local minimax lower bound (Theorem 4.1) confirms that v_*²/n is the fundamental estimation limit, establishing instance-dependent optimality of the AIPW estimator with no-regret online learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so there are no baseline methods compared against on datasets. The paper's 'comparison' is theoretical: it shows that the AIPW estimator combined with no-regret online learning (e.g., OGD) achieves the local minimax rate v_*²/n, matching the lower bound in Theorem 4.1. The key contrast is with prior work on adaptively collected data (e.g., [16, 67, 7]) that only provides asymptotic normality results; this paper is the first to deliver finite-sample (non-asymptotic) guarantees. The paper also contrasts its adaptive-data framework with the i.i.d. setting of Mou et al. [42], noting that cross-fitting (used in [42]) is inapplicable under adaptive collection, motivating the sequential online-learning approach instead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-asymptotic off-policy estimation framework of Mou, Wainwright, and Bartlett [42] from i.i.d. data to adaptively collected data, where the cross-fitting strategy used in [42] is no longer applicable due to the history-dependent nature of the sampling mechanism. The key methodological departure is the reduction of the AIPW estimation problem to an online non-parametric regression game (borrowing from Rakhlin and Sridharan [45]), where the learner sequentially produces treatment effect estimates to minimize a sequentially weighted ℓ₂-error. Prior work on adaptive data collection [16, 67, 31, 8, 7] established asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency but left the finite-sample theory largely unexplored; this paper fills that gap by providing explicit MSE bounds and a local minimax lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic guarantees for AIPW estimators under adaptive data collection, which is increasingly relevant as contextual bandit algorithms are used in experimental design. The sequentially weighted ℓ₂-norm that emerges as the key quantity captures how drifting overlap (deteriorating propensity score coverage over time) affects estimation precision, offering practitioners a concrete diagnostic. The local minimax lower bound confirms that no-regret online learning algorithms achieve instance-dependent optimality, validating the reduction scheme. Open questions include relaxing the strict overlap assumption (Assumption 1), extending the general function approximation results beyond the deferred appendix treatment, and understanding how these bounds behave under more realistic adaptive policies that are not eventually Markov.
