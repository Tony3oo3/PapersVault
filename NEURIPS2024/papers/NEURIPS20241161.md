## Problem

Piecewise-linear RNNs (PLRNNs) achieve state-of-the-art dynamical systems reconstruction but produce models with an exponentially large number of linear subregions (2^M), making them difficult to analyze mathematically. Hand-crafting topologically minimal PWL representations is tedious and restricted to very low-dimensional systems, while existing data-driven inference methods for switching linear systems are complex and poorly suited to high-dimensional or noisy empirical time series. The paper addresses the gap of automatically learning parsimonious PWL representations that are both faithful to the data and amenable to symbolic and topological analysis.

## Input / Output

The model consumes N-dimensional multivariate time series observations (ranging from 3D Lorenz-63 to 64D EEG) and produces a piecewise-linear dynamical system with only 2^P linear subregions (where P << M total units), from which symbolic coding sequences, topological transition graphs, and geometrical graphs are directly derived. The first N latent units serve as readout neurons with an identity observation model, while the remaining M−N hidden units comprise M−P−N linear units and P ReLU units; during training, readout states are replaced by observations every τ steps (identity teacher forcing), but at test time the model generates trajectories fully autonomously.

## Method

The AL-RNN restricts ReLU nonlinearities to only P << M of the M total units, reducing the number of linear subregions from 2^M to 2^P while retaining enough linear units to expand the latent space and disentangle trajectories. Training uses identity teacher forcing (a sparse teacher forcing variant) with MSE loss and the RADAM optimizer, requiring minimal hyperparameter tuning. The key theoretical contribution proves that for hyperbolic, non-diverging AL-RNNs, the partition into 2^P linear subregions forms a topological partition whose induced symbolic dynamics preserves topological properties: fixed points, periodic orbits, and aperiodic (chaotic) orbits in the state space correspond exactly to their symbolic counterparts (Theorems 1–3). Empirically, the AL-RNN automatically and robustly discovers the known topologically minimal PWL representations of the Lorenz-63 (3 subregions) and Rössler (2 subregions) attractors without any prior knowledge, and extends to biologically interpretable reconstructions of ECG and fMRI data where linear subregions align with distinct physiological or cognitive task stages.

## Results

On the Lorenz-63 system, AL-RNN achieves D_stsp = 0.34 ± 0.05 and D_H = 0.081 ± 0.012, competitive with SINDy (D_stsp = 0.24) and shPLRNN+GTF (D_stsp = 0.26) on geometry but matching or beating them on temporal agreement. On the empirical ECG dataset, AL-RNN substantially outperforms all baselines with D_stsp = 3.0 ± 0.7 and D_H = 0.29 ± 0.04, while SINDy diverges entirely and LSTM-TBPTT reaches D_stsp = 15.2. On the high-dimensional EEG (64d) data, AL-RNN achieves D_stsp = 2.6 ± 0.3 and D_H = 0.14 ± 0.03, again outperforming all neural baselines (Reservoir Computer: 14 ± 7; LSTM: 30 ± 21; Neural-ODE: 20 ± 0.5). The AL-RNN also discovers topologically minimal PWL representations using only 2–3 linear subregions for Lorenz-63 and Rössler, matching hand-crafted minimal forms from Amaral et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Lorenz-63 benchmark, AL-RNN (D_stsp = 0.34) trails SINDy (0.24) and shPLRNN+GTF (0.26) on attractor geometry but matches or beats them on temporal agreement (D_H = 0.081 vs. 0.090/0.091). On the empirical ECG dataset, AL-RNN decisively outperforms all baselines: D_stsp = 3.0 vs. 4.3 (shPLRNN), 5.8 (dendPLRNN), 15.2 (LSTM), 12.2 (Neural-ODE), while SINDy diverges entirely. On the 64-channel EEG data, AL-RNN (D_stsp = 2.6, D_H = 0.14) is competitive with shPLRNN+GTF (2.1, 0.11) and dendPLRNN (3, 0.13) but dramatically outperforms Reservoir Computers (14, 0.54), LSTM (30, 0.2), and Neural-ODE (20, 0.47); SINDy again diverges. On Lorenz-96, AL-RNN (1.64, 0.089) is within noise of shPLRNN (1.68, 0.072) and SINDy (1.59, 0.06), while clearly beating Reservoir Computers (2.40), LSTM (5), and Long Expressive Memory (7.2). Overall, AL-RNN's advantage is most pronounced on empirical, noisy, high-dimensional data where SINDy fails and black-box neural methods degrade, while on clean low-dimensional benchmarks it is roughly on par with the best SOTA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PLRNN framework (Durstewitz 2017) by restricting ReLU nonlinearities to a small subset P << M of the M latent units, reducing the number of linear subregions from 2^M to 2^P while retaining sufficient capacity for DSR. It builds on the training infrastructure of sparse/identity teacher forcing (Mikhaeil et al., Hess et al.) and departs from switching linear dynamical systems (Ghahramani & Hinton, Linderman et al.) by using continuous ReLU-based switching rather than discrete state variables, enabling standard BPTT training. The symbolic dynamics formalism (Lind & Marcus) is applied to the AL-RNN's subregion partition in a way that prior PLRNN work did not, yielding provable topological correspondences between the learned map and its symbolic coding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automatic recovery of hand-crafted topologically minimal PWL representations (3 subregions for Lorenz-63, 2 for Rössler, matching Amaral et al.) without any prior knowledge is a strong result for interpretable DSR, and the biological interpretability of ECG/fMRI reconstructions (Q-wave subregion, task-stage alignment) suggests broader applicability to neuroscience and physiology. Key open questions remain: whether a principled method exists to infer the optimal number P of PWL units from data, and whether topologically minimal representations always exist for arbitrary empirical systems. The approach also opens a pathway to applying symbolic dynamics tools (topological entropy, periodic orbit enumeration) to data-driven models, which was previously intractable due to the combinatorial explosion of subregions in standard PLRNNs.
