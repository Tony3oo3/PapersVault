## Problem

Existing shape assembly datasets capture only fragments of the assembly problem: manuals provide task planning without visual grounding, 3D datasets lack action trajectories, and internet video datasets lack 3D correspondence and manual alignment. No prior dataset provides dense spatio-temporal alignments between 3D part models, step-by-step instruction manuals, and real-world assembly videos, which is essential for holistic 4D understanding of assembly in 3D space over time.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 34,441 video frames (sampled at 1 FPS from 98 internet videos) annotated with 2D segmentation masks and 6-DoF poses in camera coordinates, alongside estimated camera intrinsics kept consistent within each video segment. Each frame is temporally aligned to both coarse manual steps (137 total) and fine-grained substeps (1,120 total), where a substep corresponds to the construction of a single sub-assembly. The five benchmark tasks produce hierarchical assembly plans as DAGs, pixel-wise part masks, 6-DoF poses, tracked video object masks, and final assembled furniture configurations evaluated via Chamfer Distance.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-stage annotation pipeline that aligns 3D IKEA furniture models (from IKEA-Manual) with internet assembly videos (from IAW) through dense spatio-temporal grounding. Temporal segmentation decomposes videos into substeps where new sub-assemblies form; SAM-assisted mask annotation with manual brush/eraser refinement handles occlusions and similar-texture boundaries; and 2D-3D keypoint correspondences are used with PnP and RANSAC to estimate per-segment camera intrinsics, followed by interactive 3D pose refinement with cross-frame temporal smoothing. For evaluation, the paper benchmarks five pretrained models (SAM-6D, MegaPose, CNOS, SAM2, Cutie) without fine-tuning across five tasks, plus a modular video-based assembly pipeline that uses GPT-4o for keyframe detection and iterative pose estimation to reconstruct furniture from instruction videos.

## Results

On the IKEA Video Manuals benchmark, all evaluated methods show significant performance degradation compared to controlled settings. For part-conditioned 6D pose estimation, MegaPose achieves the best ADD score of 1.36 (ADD-S: 0.89), while SAM-6D scores 2.34 (ADD-S: 1.85) and differentiable rendering baselines score 3.33/3.29. For part-conditioned segmentation, SAM-6D achieves IoU 0.16 (Top-5 IoU: 0.40), substantially outperforming CNOS (IoU 0.09, Top-5 IoU: 0.21). For video object segmentation, SAM2 Hiera-L scores J&F 73.6 on the new dataset versus 91.6 on DAVIS2017, and Cutie-base drops from 87.9 to 54.7, highlighting the difficulty of real-world assembly videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six existing methods across five assembly tasks, demonstrating that all struggle on real-world internet videos. For assembly plan generation, the GeoCluster baseline from IKEA-Manual [2] drops from F1 43.53 (Simple) on IKEA-Manual to 29.74 on the new dataset, while SingleStep falls from 48.64 to 26.88, confirming that video-derived plans are longer and more diverse. For part-conditioned segmentation, SAM-6D [38] (IoU 0.16) outperforms CNOS [37] (IoU 0.09) by leveraging geometric features, but both fail on occluded and textureless parts. For 6D pose estimation, MegaPose [39] (ADD 1.36) is the strongest method, outperforming SAM-6D [38] (ADD 2.34) and both differentiable rendering variants (ADD ~3.3), though all methods struggle with symmetric parts and challenging viewpoints. For video object segmentation, SAM2 [43] (J&F 73.6) degrades moderately from DAVIS2017 (91.6), while Cutie [48] (J&F 54.7) shows a much larger drop from 87.9, indicating greater sensitivity to camera movement and similar-looking parts. The shape assembly pipeline achieves Chamfer Distance 0.33 with ground-truth poses but 0.55 when using GPT-4o for keyframe detection, with GPT-4o failing to identify the final assembly step in 5 of 15 videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary prior datasets: IKEA-Manual, which provides 3D furniture models with 6-DoF poses aligned to instruction manual images but no video demonstrations, and IAW (IKEA in the Wild), which provides internet assembly videos temporally aligned to manual steps but lacks 3D part model correspondence and pose annotations. The key departure is the introduction of dense 4D grounding—combining 3D part poses, 2D segmentation masks, and temporal substep alignment—directly on uncontrolled internet videos, a combination absent from all prior assembly datasets. The paper also extends the assembly plan generation task from static manual images (as in IKEA-Manual) to physically realistic video-derived plans, and introduces a novel video-based shape assembly pipeline that leverages GPT-4o for keyframe detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dataset to provide 6-DoF pose annotations on real-world internet assembly videos, filling a critical gap between controlled laboratory benchmarks and the unstructured settings where assembly actually occurs. The consistent poor performance of strong baselines (SAM-6D, MegaPose, SAM2, Cutie) across all five tasks—e.g., SAM2's J&F dropping from 91.6 on DAVIS2017 to 73.6 here—quantifies a substantial domain gap that the field has not previously addressed. Key limitations include the dataset's modest scale (98 videos, 36 furniture types), which precludes large-scale training, and the reliance on manual annotation that constrains further expansion. Open questions include whether models developed on this furniture-specific benchmark transfer to other assembly domains, and whether the annotation pipeline can be automated to scale the dataset.
