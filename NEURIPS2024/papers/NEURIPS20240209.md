## Problem

Adaptive learning rate strategies (AdaGrad-Norm, line search, Polyak stepsize) are widely used in stochastic optimization, yet existing theory provides only minimax-optimal convergence rates that cannot distinguish between algorithms. The exact learning rates to which these strategies converge, their dependence on the geometry of the data covariance, and their performance relative to well-tuned fixed learning rates remain unknown. This paper addresses these gaps by deriving exact deterministic dynamics for both the risk and the adaptive learning rate in the high-dimensional limit.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a high-dimensional linear composite objective defined by a data covariance matrix K (bounded in operator norm), a pseudo-Lipschitz loss function f, adaptive learning rate parameters (e.g., η, b for AdaGrad-Norm), and an initial point X_0, and produces a coupled system of ODEs whose solution gives the exact risk curve R(t) and learning rate curve γ_t. The ODEs are indexed by the eigenvalues of K, so the output naturally encodes how the covariance spectrum shapes convergence. For specific learning rates, the analysis yields closed-form limiting learning rates and convergence rates as functions of spectral quantities such as Tr(K)/d, Tr(K²)/d, and λ_min.

## Method

The key insight is that by projecting the SGD iterates onto the eigenvectors of the data covariance K, the otherwise non-autonomous dynamics of the covariance matrix B(W) become a system of autonomous ODEs for per-eigenvalue statistics V_i(t). The paper establishes (Theorem 2.1) that the stochastic risk R(X_{⌊td⌋}) and learning rate g_{⌊td⌋} concentrate around the deterministic ODE solution with overwhelming probability as d→∞, using a resolvent-based statistic S(W,z)=W^T(K−zI)^{-1}W and a Doob decomposition that handles the non-deterministic stepsize. The ODE system is then analyzed for two adaptive strategies on least squares: exact line search, which is shown to converge to a stepsize of order λ_min/(Tr(K²)/d) and can be arbitrarily slower than the Polyak stepsize (1/(Tr(K)/d)) under strong anisotropy; and AdaGrad-Norm, whose learning rate converges to a constant inversely proportional to Tr(K)/d·‖X_0−X⋆‖² in the noiseless case, and exhibits a phase transition in its decay rate when the covariance spectrum follows a power law with parameter β+δ crossing 1.

## Results

The paper establishes that for least squares with λ_min(K) > C > 0, exact line search converges to a limiting learning rate of order λ_min/(Tr(K²)/d), while Polyak stepsize uses 1/(Tr(K)/d) — the inverse average eigenvalue. When the covariance spectrum is strongly anisotropic (Tr(K)/d ≪ Tr(K²)/d), line search can be arbitrarily slower than the optimal fixed learning rate. For AdaGrad-Norm in the noiseless case, the learning rate converges to a deterministic constant η²/(b/η + (1/4d)Tr(K)||X₀−X*||²), matching the Polyak-optimal rate up to a penalty factor. Under power law covariance ρ(λ)=(1−β)λ^{−β}1_{(0,1)} with residual scaling D_i²(0)~λ_i^{−δ}, a phase transition occurs at β+δ=1: for β+δ<1 the learning rate is constant and risk decays as t^{β+δ−2}; for β+δ>1 the learning rate decays as t^{−1+1/(β+δ)} and risk decays sublinearly as t^{−2/(β+δ)+1}.

| AdaGrad-Norm, Power law | β+δ < 1 | constant (δ,β) | R(t) ∝ t^{β+δ−2} |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three adaptive learning rate strategies — exact line search, AdaGrad-Norm, and Polyak stepsize — on the least squares problem. Exact line search, despite being the greedy choice that maximizes per-step risk decrease, can be arbitrarily slower than Polyak stepsize when the covariance spectrum is anisotropic: its limiting rate λ_min/(Tr(K²)/d) can be much smaller than Polyak's 1/(Tr(K)/d). AdaGrad-Norm matches the Polyak-optimal fixed stepsize in terms of the covariance dependence (both scale as 1/(Tr(K)/d)) but pays a constant penalty of ||X₀−X*||², meaning it automatically selects the correct rate without knowing Tr(K) but cannot match Polyak's exact constant without a warm start. Under power law spectra, AdaGrad-Norm exhibits a phase transition at β+δ=1 that has no analogue in constant-stepsize SGD, which always decays as t^{β+δ−2} regardless of the spectral parameters. The CIFAR-5m simulation (Fig. 6, supplementary) confirms that the ODE-predicted risk matches empirical multi-pass SGD risk beyond the Gaussian data assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional ODE framework for SGD dynamics established by Collins-Woodfin et al. [15] (which handled only deterministic learning rate schedules) and the one-pass least-squares Volterra equation of Collins-Woodfin and Paquette [14] to the setting of stochastic adaptive learning rates. The key conceptual departure is that prior deterministic-dynamics results could not accommodate learning rates that depend on the stochastic gradient history (e.g., AdaGrad-Norm, line search), whereas here the authors prove concentration of both the risk and the adaptive stepsize to a coupled ODE system. The analysis of specific adaptive strategies (AdaGrad-Norm, exact line search, Polyak stepsize) builds on convergence-guarantee literature [59, 35, 48] but goes beyond worst-case rates to give exact limiting learning rates and risk curves as functions of the covariance spectrum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first exact deterministic characterization of how adaptive learning rates evolve in high dimensions, revealing that the covariance spectrum (not just its condition number) fundamentally shapes optimization. The finding that exact line search can be arbitrarily worse than the simple Polyak stepsize under anisotropy challenges a core intuition from deterministic optimization and has direct implications for algorithm design. Limitations include restriction to Gaussian data, one-pass streaming, and trace(K) ~ d scaling; open questions include extending to multi-pass SGD, non-Gaussian data, and other adaptive methods (DoG, D-Adaptation, RMSprop) that the framework already accommodates formally.
