## Problem

Existing LLM evaluation benchmarks (e.g., SELF-INSTRUCT, WizardLM) produce prompts that are too easy and undiscriminative, yielding average scores above 67 with variance below 3.2 across SOTA models, making it difficult to distinguish between models of varying capability. As LLMs rapidly improve, static evaluation sets lose discriminative power, and there is no principled mechanism to adaptively generate questions that maintain meaningful performance separation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes handcrafted seed questions (6,000 instances in Chinese and English, split into general text and mathematical categories) and LLM-generated responses used as knowledge sources for the 'response gradient' generalization step. It produces 3,000+ generalized evaluation prompts with reference answers, plus two fine-tuned Baichuan2-13B models that predict a 4-level discrimination index (Low/Relatively Low/Relatively High/High) and a 3-level difficulty score (easy/medium/hard) for any given prompt. The discrimination index is computed via a top-50%/bottom-50% model-score split formula adapted from educational item analysis, and the difficulty score is the gap between the maximum possible score and the mean score across all evaluated models.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-axis generalization pipeline: an 'instruction gradient' applies 12 constraint-based strategies for general text (e.g., restricting vocabulary, limiting sentence count, cross-domain integration) and 8 strategies for math (e.g., introducing variables, combining domains), while a 'response gradient' discards the original question and generates novel questions from LLM responses to an 'Information Inducer' prompt, breaking topic confinement. A self-correct loop uses Chain-of-Thought verification (run by both Hunyuan-standard and Hunyuan-pro) to detect and fix logical contradictions, missing conditions, or common-sense violations in generated math problems, iterating up to two correction cycles before discarding unusable items. Reference answers are obtained via a 5-model ensemble scored by Hunyuan on seven weighted criteria for general text, and a 3-model majority-vote mechanism with expert review for math. Two Baichuan2-13B models are supervised-finetuned on 12-model evaluation data to predict discrimination level and difficulty level, enabling automated filtering of generated prompts during synthesis.

## Results

On the five SOTA models (GLM-4, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4, Claude 3, Qwen), the IDGen hard-seed dataset achieves a mean score of 51.92 with a variance of 10.06, substantially outperforming all baselines in both challenge level and discriminative power. The discrimination index reaches 0.204 and difficulty score 1.941, compared to WizardLM (0.140 / 1.235), SELF-INSTRUCT (0.109 / 1.319), and Instruction Tuning with GPT-4 (0.098 / 1.215). Generalization questions from the framework maintain high usability (94.0% for general text, 96.4% for math) while boosting discrimination from 0.08→0.17 and difficulty from 0.52→1.08 over seed data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDGen (hard seed data) outperforms all three baselines on both discriminative power and challenge level. Against WizardLM, IDGen reduces the mean model score from 69.12 to 51.92 (a 17.2-point drop) while increasing variance from 3.08 to 10.06 (3.3×), and raises the discrimination index from 0.140 to 0.204. Against Instruction Tuning with GPT-4, the variance gap is even more pronounced (10.06 vs. 1.49, ~6.8×), and the discrimination index nearly doubles (0.204 vs. 0.098). Against SELF-INSTRUCT, IDGen achieves a 15.1-point lower mean score and 3.1× higher variance (10.06 vs. 3.20), with discrimination index 0.204 vs. 0.109. Even the SELF-INSTRUCT-Ours variant (same 175 seed questions, IDGen generalization) already doubles the variance of vanilla SELF-INSTRUCT (7.12 vs. 3.20), confirming that the generalization framework itself—regardless of seed difficulty—substantially enhances discriminability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Item Discrimination (ID) theory from educational psychometrics as the explicit optimization objective for LLM evaluation data generation, building on the instruction-data synthesis lineage of SELF-INSTRUCT, WizardLM (Evol-Instruct), and Alpaca. Where prior methods (e.g., Evol-Instruct) increase difficulty and diversity through evolutionary rules, IDGen introduces 'instruction gradient' and 'response gradient' as structured generalization mechanisms and trains dedicated Baichuan2-13B models to predict discrimination and difficulty, closing the loop between data generation and quality assessment. The CoT-based verification for mathematical questions extends the chain-of-thought paradigm from inference-time reasoning to data-quality control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs rapidly improve, static benchmarks lose discriminative power; this paper provides a principled, iterative framework for keeping evaluation sets adaptive, and releases 3,000+ prompts plus two open-source estimation models that lower the barrier for constructing discriminative benchmarks. The finding that a fine-tuned model predicts question difficulty more consistently (0.70) than human annotators (0.52) challenges the assumption that subjective difficulty judgment is a reliable gold standard. Key limitations remain: the framework depends on the capability of the generating LLM, and it does not yet handle mathematically rigorous logical-reasoning problems well, leaving open the question of how to maintain discrimination as frontier models continue to close the gap.
