## Problem

Neural fields for PDE modeling struggle to capture local spatial dynamics, while transformer-based operators operating in the original physical space incur prohibitive computational cost that scales with the number of spatial points. The paper addresses how to model spatio-temporal PDE dynamics on arbitrary geometries (irregular grids, point clouds, meshes) with an architecture that is both spatially aware and computationally efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240431/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder accepts a variable number N of coordinate-value pairs (x_i, u(x_i)) sampled from any geometry—regular grid, irregular mesh, or point set—and compresses them into a fixed-size sequence of M latent tokens (M << N, e.g., 32–256 tokens for 1000–4000+ observations). The decoder is a continuous local neural field that can be queried at any spatial coordinate x ∈ Ω, producing the predicted field value at that location, enabling evaluation on grids different from the input sampling. Dynamics are unrolled autoregressively in latent space for arbitrary time horizons without projecting back to physical space at each step.

## Method

AROMA is a three-stage encode–process–decode framework. The encoder is a VAE that first embeds positions via Fourier features and values via a linear layer, then uses two cross-attention stages (geometry encoding, then observation encoding) to aggregate N observations onto M learnable query tokens, followed by a bottleneck and Gaussian sampling. The dynamics refiner is a conditional diffusion transformer (DiT-style with AdaLN modulation) that operates on the M-token latent sequence, using self-attention for global spatial relations and a v-predict diffusion formulation (K=3 steps) between successive time steps for stable long rollouts. The decoder lifts tokens to higher dimension, applies self-attention, then uses multi-band cross-attention with Fourier-embedded query coordinates to produce a local feature vector decoded by a small MLP. Training is two-stage: the encoder-decoder is first trained as a VAE (reconstruction + KL loss), then the diffusion transformer is trained with frozen encoder/decoder on pairs of successive latent tokens. The key design insight is that the compact latent space (one to two orders of magnitude smaller than the input) preserves local spatial structure—validated by token perturbation experiments showing each token controls a localized region—while enabling transformer-based dynamics at O(M²) cost rather than O(N²).

## Results

On regular-grid PDE benchmarks (Table 1), AROMA achieves the best or tied-best Relative L2 error across all three datasets: 3.65×10⁻² on Burgers, 1.05×10⁻¹ on Navier-Stokes 1×10⁻⁴, and 1.24×10⁻¹ on Navier-Stokes 1×10⁻⁵ (tied with FNO). On irregular-grid and geometry-varying benchmarks (Tables 2–3), AROMA consistently achieves the lowest MSE at all sparsity levels (π = 100%, 25%, 5%) and on both CylinderFlow and AirfoilFlow, with the largest margins over transformer baselines (GNOT, OFormer) in the sparse-observation regime. The diffusion-based latent refiner provides a clear stability advantage over the deterministic variant for long rollouts (200+ steps on Burgers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AROMA outperforms all six baselines on the regular-grid benchmarks (Table 1). Against the strongest baseline FNO, AROMA improves Relative L₂ by 27% on Burgers (3.65×10⁻² vs 5.00×10⁻²) and by 31% on Navier-Stokes 1×10⁻⁴ (1.05×10⁻¹ vs 1.53×10⁻¹), while tying on Navier-Stokes 1×10⁻⁵ (both 1.24×10⁻¹). The transformer baselines GNOT and OFormer are consistently outperformed: AROMA achieves 3.5× lower error than GNOT on Burgers and 2.3× lower on NS 1×10⁻⁵. The neural-field baselines DINO and CORAL struggle most with turbulent dynamics, with DINO showing errors 12× higher than AROMA on Burgers (4.57×10⁻¹ vs 3.65×10⁻²) and 6.9× higher on NS 1×10⁻⁴. On irregular grids (Table 2), AROMA dominates at π=25% and π=5% sparsity, achieving 2–3× lower MSE than the next-best method (CORAL or GNOT), while CORAL edges out AROMA slightly in the fully-observed Shallow-Water case and at π=5% on NS 1×10⁻³. On geometry-varying flows (Table 3), AROMA achieves the lowest MSE on both CylinderFlow (1.48×10⁻² In-t, 2.78×10⁻² Out-t) and AirfoilFlow (5.72×10⁻² In-t, 1.94×10⁻¹ Out-t), outperforming CORAL by 3× on CylinderFlow In-t and DINO by 9× on AirfoilFlow In-t.

## Relation to Prior Work

AROMA synthesizes three previously separate threads: Perceiver-style cross-attention encoders (mapping variable-size point sets to fixed-size tokens), latent diffusion transformers (DiT-style denoising for dynamics), and local neural field decoders (queryable at arbitrary spatial coordinates). It departs from GNOT and OFormer, which unroll dynamics directly in the physical/point space at quadratic cost, by compressing spatial information into a compact latent token sequence (M ≪ N) where self-attention models global spatial relations at reduced complexity. Unlike DINO and CORAL, which rely on global neural fields and meta-learning, AROMA's encoder explicitly separates geometry encoding from observation encoding via two sequential cross-attention stages, and its decoder uses a *local* INR (multi-band Fourier features with cross-attention) rather than a global modulation network. The two-stage training protocol (VAE encoder-decoder first, then frozen-encoder diffusion refiner) contrasts with the end-to-end optimization used by most prior neural operator and transformer PDE solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact, spatially-structured latent representation can support both accurate short-horizon prediction and stable long-horizon rollouts across regular grids, irregular meshes, and non-convex domains, while maintaining linear complexity in the number of observations. The perturbation analysis validating local spatial structure in tokens, and the honest failure case on the chaotic Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation, delineate the current boundary of what reduced-order latent models can capture. Open questions include whether the decoder bottleneck can be alleviated (e.g., via progressive refinement or dedicated chaotic-component modules), whether the diffusion-based uncertainty estimates are calibrated and useful for downstream decision-making, and whether the approach scales to 3D industrial-scale simulations where the current small-dataset validation is insufficient.
