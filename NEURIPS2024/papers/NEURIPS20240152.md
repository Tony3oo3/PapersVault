## Problem

Most graph anomaly detection methods are either fully unsupervised (ignoring available normal labels) or semi-supervised requiring both normal and anomaly labels, which are costly to annotate. This paper tackles the practical yet under-explored setting where only a small subset of normal nodes is labeled, and no anomaly labels are available, establishing a benchmark and showing that existing unsupervised methods cannot fully exploit these normal labels when adapted to this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240152/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G=(V,E,X) and a small set of labeled normal nodes V_l (typically 10–25% of normal nodes, or 0.05–0.5% for the very large DGraph dataset) as input, with no access to any anomaly labels. It produces a scalar anomaly score per node via score(v_j) = 1 − η(h_j; Θ*), where η is a one-class classifier head on top of a 2-layer GCN; higher scores indicate greater anomaly likelihood. The generated outlier nodes are internal to training and are not part of the output.

## Method

GGAD generates learnable pseudo-anomaly (outlier) nodes in the GNN representation space to serve as negative samples for a discriminative one-class classifier. Outliers are initialized from the ego network of labeled normal nodes via a learnable linear mapping, then optimized under two anomaly priors: (1) an asymmetric local affinity loss ℓ_ala (a margin loss) that enforces the average local neighbor-similarity of outliers to be lower than that of normal nodes, aligning their structural distribution with real anomalies; and (2) an egocentric closeness loss ℓ_ec that pulls each outlier's representation toward its corresponding normal node plus a small Gaussian perturbation, capturing the subtle-abnormality/camouflage prior. A fully connected one-class classifier is trained end-to-end with a BCE loss treating normal nodes as positive and generated outliers as negative, with the total loss ℓ_total = ℓ_bce + βℓ_ala + λℓ_ec. This joint optimization yields outlier nodes that are close to yet separable from normal nodes in representation space, acting as hard negatives at the boundary of the normal class.

## Results

On six real-world GAD datasets, GGAD (using 15% labeled normal nodes) achieves the best AUROC on all six datasets, with headline values of 0.9443 (Amazon), 0.8228 (T-Finance), 0.6354 (Reddit), 0.7290 (Elliptic), 0.6476 (Photo), and 0.5943 (DGraph). On AUPRC, GGAD leads on five of six datasets (0.7922, 0.1825, 0.0610, 0.2425, 0.0082), narrowly losing to OCGNN on Photo (0.1442 vs. 0.1501). Averaged across datasets, GGAD outperforms the best semi-supervised baseline (AnomalyDAE) by 11% in AUROC and 5% in AUPRC, and achieves up to 21% AUROC and 39% AUPRC improvement over the best unsupervised method on individual datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGAD substantially outperforms all six baselines in their semi-supervised adaptations across nearly all dataset–metric combinations. AnomalyDAE (reconstruction-based) is the strongest competitor, achieving the best AUROC on Amazon (0.9171) and DGraph (0.5866) among baselines, yet GGAD still leads by ~2.7% AUROC on Amazon and ~0.8% on DGraph. TAM (one-class homophily) is competitive on Reddit (AUROC 0.5829) and Photo (AUROC 0.6013) but trails GGAD by 5.3% and 4.6% AUROC respectively. AEGIS (generative) performs best on T-Finance among baselines (AUROC 0.6728) but is surpassed by GGAD by a large 15% margin. OCGNN (one-class GNN) is the only method that beats GGAD on any metric, achieving a slightly higher AUPRC on Photo (0.1501 vs. 0.1442), though GGAD still leads on Photo AUROC (0.6476 vs. 0.6461). GAAN (generative) is consistently the weakest baseline, with AUROC below 0.65 on all datasets. Overall, GGAD's generative outlier approach with asymmetric local affinity and egocentric closeness priors yields the most consistent and largest gains, particularly on T-Finance (+15% AUROC over best baseline) and Elliptic (+19% AUROC over best baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

GGAD carves new ground at the intersection of generative anomaly detection and graph neural networks. It departs from prior generative GAD methods (AEGIS, GAAN) that generate outliers by adding Gaussian noise to GNN representations without considering graph structure, and from semi-supervised GAD methods that require both normal and anomaly labels. The paper repurposes the one-class classification paradigm well-established in visual and tabular anomaly detection to the graph node-level setting, introducing two graph-specific priors (asymmetric local affinity and egocentric closeness) to guide outlier generation in a way that prior non-graph generative methods (ALAD, Fence GAN, OCAN) cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first evaluation benchmark for semi-supervised node-level GAD where only normal labels are available—a practical setting that reflects real-world annotation costs. The two-prior framework provides principled, interpretable guidelines for generating structurally and feature-wise aligned outliers, and the GGAD-enabled filtering approach demonstrates that the generated outliers can also improve existing unsupervised methods. A key open question is whether the two priors are exhaustive; the authors acknowledge that anomalies with characteristics outside these priors may not be well captured, and extending the framework to handle such cases remains future work.
