## Problem

Optimism-based exploration, the dominant strategy in RL, fails when rewards are partially observable (as in Mon-MDPs): the agent may need to take known-suboptimal actions (e.g., paying a cost to observe a reward) to discover the truly optimal policy, but optimistic algorithms never select such actions, creating a vicious cycle of unvalidated estimates and premature convergence to suboptimal policies. No existing exploration strategy with convergence guarantees handles this setting.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives the current joint state (environment state plus monitor state) and consults three tabular structures: a visitation count table N(s,a), a per-goal S-function table Ŝ_{s^G a^G}(s,a) for the selected goal, and the Q-function table Q̂(s,a). The output is a single action: either a goal-directed action from the ε-greedy S-function policy (exploration) or the argmax of Q̂ (exploitation), decided by the ratio β_t = log(t)/N_t(s^G,a^G) against a fixed threshold β̄. Because the S-function is updated with indicator rewards (1 if the goal pair is visited, 0 otherwise), its learning signal is always observable regardless of the monitor state, unlike the Q-function which relies on a learned reward model to substitute for unobserved environment rewards.

## Method

The core mechanism is a step-wise directed exploration-exploitation algorithm (Algorithm 1) that explicitly decouples exploration from exploitation. At each step, the agent selects the least-visited state-action pair as a goal and computes β_t = log(t)/N_t(goal); if β_t exceeds a threshold β̄, it follows a goal-conditioned policy ρ (ε-greedy over the S-function Ŝ_{goal}), otherwise it acts greedily over Q̂. The S-function is a general value function representing the cumulative discounted occurrence of a target state-action pair, learned via standard Q-learning with indicator rewards that are always observable. This design ensures exploration is driven entirely by the S-function (independent of reward observability or Q-function accuracy), while exploitation uses the Q-function updated with a reward model that fills in unobserved rewards. The paper proves the policy is GLIE (greedy in the limit with infinite exploration) under a bounded goal-relative diameter assumption, guaranteeing Q̂ converges to Q* and the agent eventually acts optimally.

## Results

The proposed S-function-based directed exploration is the only method that converges to the optimal policy in the vast majority of seeds across all Mon-MDPs (partially observable reward settings), while all five baselines fail to learn optimal policies. In the fully observable deterministic MDP setting, pure Optimism is optimal (as expected), and the proposed method matches it. In the stochastic River Swim environment, even Optimism fails in the MDP version, but the proposed method still performs at least as well as every baseline. The proposed agent also observes significantly more rewards during training in all Mon-MDPs, confirming that it explores suboptimal-but-informative actions that baselines avoid.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five exploration baselines: (1) Optimism (greedy with optimistic Q̂ initialization, Even-Dar & Mansour [17]), (2) Naive ε-greedy (Sutton & Barto [75]), (3) ε-greedy with count-based intrinsic reward (Bellemare et al. [9]), (4) ε-greedy with UCB bonus (Auer et al. [4]), and (5) ε-greedy with Q-Counts (Parisi et al. [54]). In the fully observable deterministic MDP setting, Optimism is the best baseline and the proposed method matches it. However, in every Mon-MDP variant (Random, Ask, Button, Random Experts, Level Up monitors), all five baselines converge to suboptimal policies in most seeds because they avoid the suboptimal actions needed to observe rewards, creating a vicious cycle where inaccurate reward models mislead Q̂-based exploration. The proposed method wins decisively in all Mon-MDPs by decoupling exploration (driven by the always-observable S-function indicator reward) from exploitation (driven by Q̂), ensuring uniform visitation of all state-action pairs regardless of reward observability. In the stochastic River Swim environment, Optimism fails even with fully observable rewards due to the stochastic transitions, but the proposed method still matches or exceeds all baselines across all monitor variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Dayan's successor representation (1993) and Sutton et al.'s general value function framework (2011) to define a state-action successor-function (S-function) that drives exploration independently of rewards. It operates within the Monitored MDP (Mon-MDP) framework introduced by the same authors at AAMAS 2024, which generalizes active RL and partial monitoring into a unified setting where a separate monitor process governs reward observability. The key conceptual departure is from optimism-based exploration (UCB, OFU, PSRL) and intrinsic motivation, which the paper shows fundamentally fail when the agent must take known-suboptimal actions to observe rewards — a limitation previously identified theoretically by Lattimore & Szepesvari (2017) and Krueger et al. (2020) but not addressed with a practical, convergent algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first GLIE-guaranteed exploration strategy for Mon-MDPs, filling a gap where all prior approaches (optimism, posterior sampling, intrinsic motivation) either lack convergence guarantees or provably fail under partial reward observability. The explicit decoupling of exploration (S-function) from exploitation (Q-function) is a clean architectural principle that could inform future work in reward-free or reward-sparse settings. However, the results are limited to tabular MDPs with bounded goal-relative diameter (communicating MDPs), and extending to continuous spaces, non-communicating MDPs, and model-based settings remains open. The Mon-MDP benchmark suite (8 environments × 6 monitor types) provides a useful testbed for future exploration research.
