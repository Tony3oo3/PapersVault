## Problem

Existing online continual learning (OCL) methods focus almost exclusively on mitigating catastrophic forgetting, overlooking two more fundamental challenges: (i) model's ignorance, where the single-pass nature of data streams prevents models from acquiring sufficient discriminative features within limited training time, creating a trade-off with model throughput; and (ii) model's myopia, where local task-specific learning drives the classifier toward excessive parameter sparsity, causing abrupt class confusion rather than gradual forgetting. The paper provides the first Pac-Bayes theoretical bound explicitly incorporating model throughput as a term in the generalization risk, formalizing this overlooked trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/EuroSat, 224×224 for CLEAR/ImageNet) arriving at a fixed flow rate, along with pre-trained model weights (MAE or supervised ImageNet pre-training). A critical constraint distinguishing this work from prior OCL is that the memory buffer is only accessible at limited frequencies (every 10, 50, 100, or 500 training iterations) rather than at every step, reflecting realistic off-site storage. The model outputs class labels via a softmax classifier and maintains dynamically updated class mean prototypes (Eq. 2) used for the maximum separation loss and targeted replay.

## Method

NsCE (Non-sparse Classifier Evolution) combines three mechanisms on top of a pre-trained ViT or ResNet backbone: (1) a non-sparse regularization term L_s that penalizes the ratio of mean absolute weight to l2 norm in the final FC layer, preventing the classifier from collapsing onto a few discriminative features; (2) a maximum separation loss L_p inspired by Neural Collapse that enforces an equiangular tight frame structure on class representations, using class mean prototypes for absent classes; and (3) a targeted experience replay that computes a confusion matrix over the memory buffer, identifies class pairs exceeding a confusion threshold τ, and applies a binary classification loss only on those confused pairs—reducing buffer access frequency while focusing replay on the most harmful confusions. The total loss is L_ce + γ(L_p + L_s), and a lightweight NsCE Lite variant detaches the feature extractor once current-task accuracy exceeds a threshold, further boosting throughput without sacrificing performance.

## Results

NsCE achieves 89.9% A_AUC on CIFAR-10 (M=0.1k, Freq=1/100), 74.1% on CIFAR-100 (M=0.5k, Freq=1/100), and 75.7% on EuroSat (M=0.1k, Freq=1/100), outperforming the strongest baselines by 6–14 points. On real-world domain-incremental streams, NsCE reaches 89.2% on CLEAR-10, 84.3% on CLEAR-100, and 61.6% on ImageNet (M=10k, Freq=1/500), surpassing the best competitor (iCaRL at 57.1%) by 4.5 points. The gains are most pronounced under tight memory and low replay-frequency constraints, where NsCE's targeted replay and non-sparse regularization provide the largest margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NsCE outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and memory/frequency setting. On CIFAR-10 (M=0.1k, F=1/100), NsCE reaches 89.9% A_AUC, beating the strongest replay-based method SCR (83.8%) by 6.1 points and the best regularization-based method EWC (81.7%) by 8.2 points. The margin widens substantially on CIFAR-100, where NsCE (74.1%) surpasses OnPro (62.9%) by 11.2 points and ER (61.3%) by 12.8 points, and on EuroSat, where NsCE (75.7%) leads MC-SGD w/SAM (61.3%) by 14.4 points. On the large-scale ImageNet stream (M=10k, F=1/500), NsCE achieves 61.6% versus iCaRL's 57.1% and ER's 55.6%. The advantage is most pronounced under the tightest constraints (smallest buffer, lowest replay frequency), confirming that targeted replay and non-sparse regularization are particularly effective when memory access is limited. Notably, methods relying on heavy augmentation or distillation (OCM, DVC) show the largest gaps, consistent with the paper's argument that increased training time reduces effective throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant OCL framing that centers on catastrophic forgetting, instead identifying two underappreciated challenges: model's ignorance (single-pass learning limits) and model's myopia (progressively sparse, task-specific classifiers). Methodologically, it builds on the Maximum Class Separation criterion (Kasarla et al., NeurIPS 2022) and Neural Collapse theory for its L_p loss, and adapts standard experience replay (Chaudhry et al., 2019) into a targeted binary-classification replay on confused classes. The Pac-Bayes theoretical analysis extends the online PAC-Bayes framework (Haddouche & Guedj, NeurIPS 2022) to explicitly incorporate model throughput as a first-class term in the generalization bound, which is the first such treatment in the OCL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The throughput perspective is a practically critical but largely ignored dimension in OCL evaluation, and the finding that complex strategies (contrastive learning, distillation) may be no better than simply training longer (CE++) challenges the field's default assumption that more elaborate objectives are necessary. The myopia reframing—attributing abrupt class confusion to classifier sparsity rather than gradual forgetting—opens a new diagnostic axis for OCL methods. Limitations include the per-task (not global) nature of the theoretical bound, the reliance on pre-trained initialization for the strongest results, and the fact that key empirical findings (CE++ equivalence, myopia visualization) are demonstrated in a simplified single-task setting rather than full multi-task OCL. Open questions include whether the non-sparse regularization generalizes to non-softmax classifiers and whether tighter global bounds can be established.
