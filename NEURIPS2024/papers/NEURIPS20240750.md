## Problem

While adversarial robustness defenses for GNNs have focused on input pre-processing, edge pruning, and message-passing adaptations, the role of weight initialization and training hyperparameters (notably the number of epochs) in determining a model's vulnerability to adversarial perturbations has remained unexplored. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis linking initialization strategies and training dynamics to adversarial robustness, revealing that these 'free' choices can shift robustness by up to 50%.

## Input / Output

For GNNs, the input is a graph specified by an adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}; for DNNs, a standard input vector x ∈ R^{n₀}. The model outputs a node class label (or class score vector). The theoretical contribution additionally produces a closed-form upper bound γ on adversarial risk, parameterized by the initial weight norms ‖W₀⁽ⁱ⁾‖, the number of training epochs t, the final weight norms ‖W*⁽ⁱ⁾‖, and the perturbation budget ε. Adversarial perturbations are constrained to an ℓ₂ ball of radius ε around either the node features or the adjacency matrix.

## Method

The paper derives upper bounds on adversarial risk by combining two ingredients: (1) a gradient-descent weight-evolution bound showing ‖W_t⁽ⁱ⁾‖ ≤ 2^t ‖W₀⁽ⁱ⁾‖ + 2^{t+1} ‖W*⁽ⁱ⁾‖ under L-smooth loss, and (2) Lipschitz-based robustness bounds from prior work that express prediction deviation as a product of layer weight norms times the perturbation budget. Multiplying these yields a robustness bound γ that grows with both initial weight norm and training epochs, establishing a direct trade-off between clean accuracy (favored by more epochs) and adversarial robustness (favored by fewer epochs and smaller initial norms). For Gaussian initialization N(μ,Σ), the expected bound depends on √(μ² + tr(Σ)), making the variance parameter a direct robustness knob. The framework is instantiated for GCNs (feature and structural attacks), GINs, and general T-layer DNNs, and further sharpened under strong convexity where the initialization effect decays as (1−μ/L)^t. Experiments on Cora, CiteSeer, and ACM with Mettack, PGD, and DICE attacks confirm that lower-variance or scaled-orthogonal initializations consistently improve attacked accuracy without sacrificing clean performance.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that initialization strategy significantly impacts adversarial robustness, with observed gaps of up to 50% between initialization approaches on GNNs. For DNNs on MNIST, the accuracy gap between the best and worst initialization methods at ε=0.1 reaches approximately 60%. For Gaussian initialization on GCNs (Cora/CiteSeer), varying the variance σ produces a 5–15% difference in Success Rate at 30–40% attack budgets. The experiments also confirm a trade-off with training epochs: attacked accuracy rises to an inflection point then declines, while clean accuracy continues to increase toward a plateau.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare a proposed method against external defense baselines; instead, it compares initialization strategies (Gaussian with varying σ, Uniform with varying β, Orthogonal with varying β, Kaiming, and Xavier) under identical architecture and hyperparameters. Across all settings (GCN, GIN, DNN), lower-norm initializations consistently yield higher attacked accuracy and lower Success Rate, confirming the theoretical upper bound. For example, on MNIST with PGD at ε=0.1, the best initialization (smaller norm) outperforms the worst by ~60% in attacked accuracy while all methods achieve similar clean accuracy (ε=0). On Cora and CiteSeer with Mettack and PGD, increasing the Gaussian variance σ or the Uniform/Orthogonal scaling β monotonically increases the Success Rate, with the effect growing at larger perturbation budgets. The findings extend to defense methods (RGCN, GCN-Jaccard), where initialization choice still produces measurable robustness differences, particularly on CiteSeer and ACM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz-based robustness bounds for GNNs established by Abbahaddou et al. (ICLR 2024) by incorporating the dynamics of weight initialization and training epochs into the adversarial risk upper bound. It departs from the dominant adversarial defense literature (input pre-processing, edge pruning, message-passing adaptations) by identifying a previously unexplored axis of robustness: the initial weight distribution. Simultaneously, it repurposes the weight initialization literature—which has focused on convergence, stability, and generalization—toward the adversarial robustness setting, and generalizes the analysis from GCNs/GINs to arbitrary deep neural networks, connecting to Parseval networks and related work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that initialization choice can yield 'approximately-free' robustness gains (up to 50% in some settings) without degrading clean accuracy, offering a new, low-cost lever for practitioners. However, the work is diagnostic rather than prescriptive: it identifies the problem but does not propose a new initialization scheme, training procedure, or robustness-tracking mechanism to exploit this insight. Open questions include how to jointly optimize initialization for clean accuracy and robustness, whether the epoch-robustness trade-off persists at scale, and how to tighten the currently loose bounds (which use 2^t rather than the sharper (1+ηL)^t). Empirical validation is limited to small citation networks and a 2-layer architecture, leaving scalability and depth-dependence unexplored.
