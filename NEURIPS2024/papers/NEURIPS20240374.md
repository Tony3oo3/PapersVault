## Problem

Flow matching provides an efficient, simulation-free training procedure for continuous normalizing flows, but it has not been naturally extended to categorical data such as graphs, where the data live on a discrete set rather than a continuous space. The paper identifies that flow matching can be recast as a variational inference problem over the posterior probability path, which provides a principled and tractable route to adapting flow matching for discrete/categorical data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random noise sample from a standard Gaussian prior and produces a generated graph represented as a set of categorical variables: each node is assigned one of K_v node types and each edge one of K_e+1 edge types (the extra class encoding edge absence). Graphs are treated as purely categorical objects with no geometric embedding; the backbone is a graph transformer that processes node features, edge features, and global spectral/structural features. Generation proceeds by integrating the learned vector field via an ODE from t=0 to t=1, with the final point projected onto the probability simplex to yield discrete assignments.

## Method

The core contribution is Variational Flow Matching (VFM), which reparameterizes the learned vector field as an expectation under a variational approximation q_t^θ(x₁|x) of the posterior probability path p_t(x₁|x), turning the flow matching objective into a KL-divergence minimization. Under the standard linearity assumption on the conditional vector field, a fully-factorized (mean-field) variational distribution suffices to recover the exact marginal vector field, reducing the problem to per-component low-dimensional subproblems. For categorical data, this yields CatFlow: the variational distribution is a product of categoricals, the training objective becomes a standard cross-entropy loss over predicted endpoint classes, and the vector field is computed in closed form as (μ_t(x) − x)/(1−t), where μ_t is the predicted probability vector. This introduces an inductive bias that all generative paths point into the probability simplex, improving gradient behavior and convergence over the mean-squared-error objective of standard flow matching. The model is trained with a graph transformer backbone using structural and spectral conditioning, and generation requires only a single ODE integration without iterative denoising.

## Results

On molecular generation, CatFlow achieves near-perfect performance: 99.81% validity, 99.95% uniqueness, and 0.441 FCD on QM9, and 99.21% validity, 100.00% uniqueness, and 13.211 FCD on ZINC250k. On abstract graph generation, CatFlow attains the lowest MMD scores on Ego-small (0.013 Degree, 0.024 Clustering, 0.008 Orbit) and Community-small (0.018 Degree, 0.086 Clustering, 0.007 Orbit), matching or exceeding all baselines. The method also converges faster than standard flow matching and is not computationally more expensive than any baseline during training or generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CatFlow outperforms all baselines on both molecular benchmarks. On QM9, it surpasses the closest competitor Dirichlet FM [56] by 0.71% in validity (99.81 vs. 99.10), 1.80% in uniqueness (99.95 vs. 98.15), and by a factor of ~2 in FCD (0.441 vs. 0.888); it also beats GDSS [20] by 4.09% validity and 2.46 FCD, and standard Flow Matching [27] by 5.71% validity and 4.71 FCD. On ZINC250k, CatFlow leads Dirichlet FM by 1.69% validity and 1.01 FCD, GDSS by 2.20% validity and 1.45 FCD, and Flow Matching by 5.20% validity and 5.55 FCD. On abstract graph generation (Table 1), CatFlow achieves the best Degree MMD on both Ego-small (0.013 vs. GDSS 0.021) and Community-small (0.018 vs. GDSS 0.045), ties GDSS on Clustering for Community-small (0.086), and is competitive on Orbit (0.008 vs. GNF 0.001 on Ego-small, where GNF is slightly better). The ablation in Fig. 3 further shows CatFlow is significantly more robust to reduced model size and training data than standard flow matching, maintaining higher valid-and-unique scores across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) through a variational inference lens, showing that the learned vector field can be parameterized as an expectation over a variational distribution of trajectory endpoints. Under the linearity condition satisfied by standard interpolants, a mean-field (fully factorized) approximation is provably exact (Theorem 1), reducing the high-dimensional variational problem to per-component classification. For categorical data, this yields CatFlow, whose objective is a simple cross-entropy loss, connecting it conceptually to DiGress's classification-over-endpoints perspective while remaining a continuous ODE-based model. The framework also unifies flow matching and score-based diffusion (Song et al., 2020) by showing that the same variational distribution parameterizes both the deterministic vector field and the score function, and recovers standard FM as a special case when the variational distribution is Gaussian (Theorem 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VFM framework provides a clean theoretical bridge between deterministic flow matching, stochastic score-based dynamics, and discrete data generation, suggesting a path toward models that learn both ODE and SDE dynamics from a single objective. Practically, CatFlow's cross-entropy objective and simplex-constrained trajectories offer a simpler, faster-converging alternative to MSE-based flow matching for categorical graph generation, with near-perfect validity on QM9 and ZINC250k. Key limitations include quadratic scaling in the number of nodes (preventing application to large proteins) and the current restriction to purely categorical data; open questions include extending VFM to mixed discrete-continuous modalities, text, and source code, and exploiting the score-based connection for stochastic sampling.
