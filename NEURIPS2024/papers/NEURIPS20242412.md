## Problem

Relative entropy coding (REC) algorithms encode continuous latent variables in neural compression with codelength near the mutual information bound, but their runtime scales as 2^{D_KL[Q||P]}, making them impractical for blocks larger than 16–20 bits. This forces practitioners to use small blocks where the logarithmic and constant overhead constitutes 40–50% of the total mutual information, yielding sub-optimal compression. Existing acceleration methods are restricted to 1D distributions or very specific geometric settings, leaving a gap for general multi-dimensional neural compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target posterior Q, a shared prior P, and a shared PRNG seed S, and additionally requires the sender and receiver to agree on the dimension-wise mutual information I[Z_i; X] (for allocating partition intervals per axis) and the integer value ⌊D_KL[Q||P]⌋ (to set the number of bins J = 2^{⌊D_KL⌋}). The output is a two-part code consisting of a bin index j* and a local sample index ñ*, which the receiver decodes by replaying the PRNG restricted to bin B_{j*} with seed S_{j*}. In the VAE/MNIST setting, the KL value varies per image and must itself be entropy-coded; in the INR/CIFAR-10 setting, the KL is enforced to a fixed budget during training, eliminating this overhead.

## Method

The core idea is to partition P's support into J equal-probability bins and reweight them with a categorical distribution π, producing an adjusted 'search heuristic' P' that is a mixture of bin-restricted priors. This is equivalent to running standard PFR or ORC with P' as the prior, so the encoded sample remains exactly Q-distributed (or approximately so under ORC's fixed candidate budget). For exact sampling, π(j) ∝ sup_{z∈B_j} q(z)/p(z) minimizes the expected runtime 2^{D_∞[Q||P']}; for approximate ORC sampling, π(j) = Q(B_j) minimizes D_KL[Q||P'] and thus the TV bias for a given sample size. The space is partitioned with axis-aligned grids, allocating intervals to each dimension d proportionally to its mutual information I_d, avoiding the degenerate case where P' ≡ P. A theoretical codelength bound (Theorem 3.1) shows the two-part code incurs only an extra ε-cost over the standard REC bound, which is zero for uniform distributions and O(√D_KL) for factorized Gaussians—negligible in practice.

## Results

On MNIST lossless compression (VAE-based), the proposed method with 2 blocks and 2^(κ+2) samples achieves 1.3898 bits/pixel, compared to 1.4810 for GPRS and 1.3618 for the theoretical optimum — a ~6% improvement over GPRS and only ~2% above the theoretical bound. On CIFAR-10 lossy compression (INR-based), increasing the block size from 16 to 48 bits (enabled by space partitioning) reduces the codelength by approximately 10% with fine-tuning and 15% without, while remaining within 2% of the theoretical RD curve. In toy 5D Gaussian experiments, the method reduces PFR runtime by up to three orders of magnitude while maintaining codelength, and requires far fewer samples to achieve a given bias under ORC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard PFR (Li and El Gamal, 2018) and standard ORC (Theis and Yosri, 2022), the space partitioning approach reduces runtime by up to three orders of magnitude on 5D toy Gaussians while preserving codelength, and enables handling KL blocks ~3× larger (48 bits vs. 16 bits) in practical neural compression. Against GPRS (Flamich, 2024), the proposed method achieves a ~6% lower bitrate on MNIST (1.3898 vs. 1.4810 bits/pixel), primarily because GPRS incurs dimension-wise coding overhead that accumulates in high-dimensional settings. On CIFAR-10 with RECOMBINER (He et al., 2023), the method reduces codelength by ~10% (with fine-tuning) or ~15% (without) relative to the original block-size-16 ORC pipeline, while staying within 2% of the theoretical RD curve. The method does not outperform the theoretical optimum (which assumes exact single-block encoding), but narrows the gap from ~8% (GPRS) to ~2%, making it the closest practical REC scheme to the information-theoretic bound in these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Poisson Functional Representation (PFR) and Ordered Random Coding (ORC) frameworks for relative entropy coding by introducing a space partitioning mechanism that acts as a search heuristic over the prior distribution. Prior acceleration efforts (Flamich et al., 2022, 2024; Flamich and Theis, 2023) achieved fast runtimes only in 1D or under restrictive distributional assumptions (e.g., aligned hypercubes for BBQ), while hybrid coding (Theis and Yosri, 2022) required Q's support to be contained in a hypercube. The key conceptual departure is generalizing partitioning to multi-dimensional spaces via axis-aligned grids, and showing that the 'parallel threads' approach of Flamich (2024) is a special case of the proposed framework. The paper also leverages dimension-wise mutual information estimates to allocate partition intervals, a practical detail absent from prior REC acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling REC to handle blocks with ~3× larger KL divergence (48 vs. 16 bits) while maintaining manageable runtime, this work removes a major practical bottleneck for neural compression pipelines that rely on REC (VAE-based lossless and INR-based lossy codecs). The theoretical guarantee that partitioning strategy and the reweighting π affect only runtime—not the codelength upper bound—gives practitioners freedom to choose heuristics without sacrificing compression efficiency. Limitations remain: the method is largely restricted to axis-aligned grids and factorized distributions, and requires shared dimension-wise mutual information estimates, which limits applicability to non-factorized or one-shot REC settings. Future work on mixture-based 'soft' partitions and convolution-based infinite partitions could address these constraints.
