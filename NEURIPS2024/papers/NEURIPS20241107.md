## Problem

Rendered fashion images from 3D simulation pipelines lack the photorealistic quality of camera-captured photos, yet existing rendered-to-real methods (GAN-based translation, patch retrieval, general I2I) either lack sufficient generative power for fine detail enhancement or fail to preserve intricate clothing textures during translation. The core challenge is simultaneously enhancing realism (wrinkles, lighting, skin) while maintaining fine-grained garment texture fidelity, without requiring paired rendered-real training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241107/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single computer-rendered fashion image (768×1024, produced by Style3D Studio or similar rasterization-based renderers) and produces a photorealistic counterpart at the same resolution. The rendered image is first encoded into the SD latent space and inverted via DDIM to obtain a noise map; no paired real image or text prompt is required at inference. The output preserves the garment's texture pattern, design, and spatial layout while replacing rendered-domain artifacts (flat shading, synthetic skin, artificial lighting) with realistic photographic appearance.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In Domain Knowledge Injection (DKI), a pretrained SD 1.5 model is first fine-tuned on 2,500 real fashion photos (positive domain), then a 75-token negative domain embedding is trained via Textual Inversion-style optimization on 2,500 rendered images while freezing the fine-tuned weights, encoding the 'rendered look' as a concept to avoid. In Realistic Image Generation (RIG), the rendered image is DDIM-inverted to a latent noise map, and denoising proceeds with a CFG-like guidance that uses the negative domain embedding (rather than a positive text prompt) to steer generation away from the rendered manifold. The key texture-preservation mechanism, Texture-preserving Attention Control (TAC), injects self-attention Q and K vectors from the inversion pipeline into the generation pipeline, but restricts this injection to only the first two shallow UNet layers and the first 90% of denoising steps (γ=0.9), exploiting the observation that shallow layers encode spatial texture structure while deeper layers encode domain-level appearance, thereby decoupling texture fidelity from realism enhancement.

## Results

On the SynFashion dataset, FashionR2R achieves the best KID (54.72) and LPIPS (0.067) among all methods, indicating superior realism and texture fidelity, while UNSB achieves a marginally higher SSIM (0.891 vs. 0.881). On Face Synthetics, FashionR2R attains the best KID (73.87) and SSIM (0.831), though VCT achieves a lower LPIPS (0.096 vs. 0.121). In user studies, FashionR2R is preferred over all baselines on overall realism and image quality, with the strongest margin against CUT (only 0.53% of votes favoring CUT on realism for Face Synthetics).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FashionR2R outperforms all four baselines on KID (realism) across both datasets, with the largest margin on SynFashion (54.72 vs. 59.24 for CUT, a ~7.7% relative improvement). On SynFashion LPIPS, FashionR2R (0.067) substantially outperforms UNSB (0.130), CUT (0.170), and VCT (0.178), confirming superior texture preservation. On Face Synthetics, VCT achieves a lower LPIPS (0.096) than FashionR2R (0.121), suggesting VCT preserves some structural content better in the face domain, though it lags significantly on KID (74.45 vs. 73.87) and SSIM (0.807 vs. 0.831). UNSB is the closest competitor on SynFashion SSIM (0.891 vs. 0.881) but trails by a wide margin on both KID and LPIPS. In user studies, FashionR2R is preferred over all baselines on realism and image quality, with UNSB receiving the highest preference rate on consistency (26.6% on SynFashion), indicating a slight trade-off in structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone and repurposes Textual Inversion into a 'Domain Inversion' mechanism, training a 75-token negative embedding on rendered images to steer generation away from the rendered manifold. It extends the self-attention control paradigm from MasaCtrl and related works by restricting injection to shallow UNet layers only, arguing this decouples garment texture from general rendered-domain characteristics. The approach departs from the GAN-based rendered-to-real lineage (CG2Real, Deep CG2Real) and general unpaired I2I methods (CUT, UNSB) by leveraging large-scale pretrained diffusion priors rather than training from scratch on limited paired or unpaired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in fashion e-commerce where rendered garments cannot yet replace studio photography, and introduces the SynFashion dataset (10k images, 20 categories) to support future research in this area. The two-stage DKI + RIG framework provides a reusable recipe for domain-adapting diffusion models via positive fine-tuning plus negative domain embedding, potentially applicable beyond fashion. Key open questions include whether inversion-free methods can eliminate the ~1-minute per-image latency, whether the approach transfers to SDXL or newer architectures, and how to automate the per-image tuning of TAC ratio and denoising strength without manual intervention.
