## Problem

Wasserstein-based Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) is ill-suited for contaminated datasets: outliers inflate the Wasserstein distance, forcing an overly large ambiguity set radius that admits unrealistic distributions and yields conservative solutions. Existing outlier-robust alternatives either abandon geometric structure (using KL divergence) or depend on a sensitive contamination fraction ε and computationally expensive CVaR-based solvers whose constraints scale linearly with sample size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a contaminated empirical distribution (n samples with a known fraction ε of geometrically distant outliers) and a prior mean estimate of the clean distribution, from which a penalty function h(ξ) (e.g., ‖ξ − ξ̄‖) is constructed. It outputs model parameters θ for regression or classification tasks. The key structural detail is that the Lagrangian penalty formulation reweights each data point by an exponential factor exp(−h(ξ)/(λβ)), so outliers receive near-zero weight without requiring an explicit ε threshold or CVaR computation.

## Method

The paper defines a new unbalanced Wasserstein distance UW(P‖P̂) that relaxes the hard marginal constraints of classical optimal transport into a soft KL-divergence penalty, allowing the transport plan to 'discard' outlier mass at a bounded cost. This distance defines an ambiguity set that naturally includes the clean distribution without requiring an inflated radius. Strong duality is established for the resulting DRO problem under smoothness assumptions, and a Lagrangian penalty variant is shown to admit a closed-form dual requiring only Assumption 1 (regular conditional distributions). The tractable dual takes the form of minimizing an expectation of exp(sup_ξ{L(θ,ξ) − λc(ξ,ζ)}/(λβ)) over the empirical distribution, which is solved by a stochastic sub-gradient algorithm with O(1/√T + ε) convergence, where ε is the inner maximization tolerance. This avoids the linear-in-n constraint blowup of CVaR-based solvers and runs in seconds on 20,000-sample datasets where competing methods exceed 200 minutes.

## Results

On linear regression with 10% contamination (ε=0.1, C=8), UOT-DRO achieves an excess risk of 0.031 at n=20,000 samples in 22.2 seconds, compared to 0.380 for Standard DRO (110.8s) and OR-WDRO which exceeds 200 minutes and is infeasible at that scale. On linear classification with contamination factor C=10, UOT-DRO reaches 93% accuracy (excess risk 0.295) versus 66% for Standard DRO and 79% for OR-WDRO. On logistic regression, UOT-DRO maintains ~92% accuracy across all contamination levels (C=4 to 30), while Standard DRO drops to 56% accuracy at C=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOT-DRO consistently outperforms both Standard DRO [7] and OR-WDRO [20] on excess risk across all three tasks (linear regression, linear classification, logistic regression). Against Standard DRO, the advantage is dramatic: on linear regression at n=20,000, UOT-DRO achieves 0.031 excess risk versus 0.380 (a 12× improvement), and on logistic regression at C=16, UOT-DRO maintains 92% accuracy while Standard DRO drops to 56%. Against OR-WDRO [20], UOT-DRO matches or slightly beats excess risk (e.g., 0.033 vs 0.033 at n=10,000 in regression) while being 60–100× faster computationally (11.9s vs 1438.7s at n=10,000; 22.2s vs >12,000s at n=20,000). On linear classification, UOT-DRO achieves 93% accuracy at C=10 versus 79% for OR-WDRO, and maintains 95–96% accuracy at C=30–100 where OR-WDRO peaks at 97% only at C=100. The key differentiator is that UOT-DRO's stochastic sub-gradient solver scales linearly with sample size, whereas OR-WDRO's CVaR-based formulation requires off-the-shelf solvers whose constraint count grows linearly, making it infeasible beyond ~10,000 samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein DRO framework (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018) by replacing the standard Wasserstein distance with an Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) distance that softens the hard marginal constraints via a KL divergence penalty. It departs from the outlier-robust WDRO of Nietert et al. (2024), which relies on CVaR and requires a priori knowledge of the contamination fraction ε, by instead using an exponential reweighting mechanism derived from the UOT dual that is a smoothed analogue of CVaR. The Lagrangian penalty formulation builds on Sinha et al. (2017) for standard Wasserstein DRO but extends it to the unbalanced setting, where strong duality holds under fewer smoothness assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the stochastic sub-gradient solver scales to 20,000 samples in ~20 seconds where the CVaR-based OR-WDRO exceeds 200 minutes, making outlier-robust DRO feasible for large-scale regression and classification. The reduced sensitivity to the contamination estimate ε (needed only in a cheap preprocessing step rather than in the optimization itself) addresses a key practical limitation of prior outlier-robust DRO methods. Open questions include whether the UOT-based approach extends naturally to high-dimensional non-convex losses (e.g., deep networks), how the KL penalty parameter β should be selected in high-dimensional settings, and whether the strong duality results can be relaxed further to cover non-differentiable losses in the original (non-Lagrangian) formulation.
