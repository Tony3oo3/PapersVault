## Problem

Existing graph retrieval models for subgraph isomorphism face a trade-off: late interaction models like IsoNet use explicit injective alignments but compute graph embeddings independently, while early interaction models like GMN enable cross-graph message passing but rely on non-injective, layer-varying attention weights that suffer from oversmoothing. No prior method combines early interaction with explicit, consistent, injective alignment structures that are iteratively refined across multiple rounds of GNN propagation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query graph G_q and a corpus graph G_c (both padded to the same node count n) as input, where graphs are small molecular or scene graphs (7–20 nodes, 6–20 edges). It produces an asymmetric relevance distance Δ(G_c|G_q) via a ReLU-based order embedding between final-layer node or edge embeddings, which is used to rank all corpus graphs for a given query. Intermediate outputs include doubly stochastic node alignment P_t and edge alignment S_t matrices that are refined across T rounds and are row/column-equivariant to node shuffling.

## Method

IsoNet++ is an early interaction GNN that maintains a chain of explicit, iteratively refined, injective alignments (doubly stochastic matrices) between query and corpus graphs. Within each of T rounds, K layers of GNN message passing run with the alignment frozen; at round boundaries, a Gumbel-Sinkhorn soft permutation generator refines the alignment from the last-layer embeddings. The key architectural innovation is node-pair partner interaction: when computing a node's updated embedding, the message-passing network receives not only the node's own partner embedding but also the partner embeddings of its neighbors, so that edge existence/non-existence across the two graphs provides alignment-refining signals. Training uses a standard hinge loss over triplets ⟨G_q, G_c+, G_c−⟩ with a margin, backpropagating through both the GNN weights and the Gumbel-Sinkhorn aligner without any direct supervision on the true alignment.

## Results

IsoNet++ (Edge) achieves the best Mean Average Precision (MAP) across all six datasets, ranging from 0.847 (AIDS) to 0.902 (FM, MM, MR), and HITS@20 from 0.705 to 0.813. IsoNet++ (Node) follows closely with MAP 0.825–0.874 and HITS@20 0.672–0.797. The best baseline, IsoNet (Edge), reaches MAP 0.690–0.783, meaning IsoNet++ (Edge) improves MAP by 10–20 percentage points over the strongest competitor on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IsoNet++ (Edge) outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both MAP and HITS@20. Against the strongest baseline, IsoNet (Edge) [35], the MAP gap is 10–20 points (e.g., 0.847 vs. 0.690 on AIDS, 0.902 vs. 0.783 on FM), and the HITS@20 gap is 19–22 points (e.g., 0.705 vs. 0.479 on AIDS). Against the early-interaction baseline GMN [22], IsoNet++ (Edge) gains 15–25 MAP points (e.g., 0.847 vs. 0.622 on AIDS, 0.902 vs. 0.708 on MR), attributed to GMN's non-injective attention-based alignment and oversmoothing. Against late-interaction subgraph-matching baselines Neuromatch [23] and GREED [32], the advantage is even larger (25–35 MAP points), confirming that explicit injective alignment with iterative refinement is critical. Among the remaining baselines (GraphSim, GOTSim, SimGNN, EGSC, H2MN, GEN), IsoNet++ (Edge) leads by 30–60 MAP points, with the smallest gap against EGSC (0.902 vs. 0.609 on FM) and the largest against H2MN (0.902 vs. 0.243 on MR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms for subgraph-matching-based graph retrieval: the late-interaction, explicit-alignment approach of IsoNet (Roy et al., AAAI 2022) and the early-interaction, attention-mediated cross-graph message passing of GMN (Li et al., ICML 2019). IsoNet++ departs from IsoNet by making the GNNs interact across graphs at every layer (early interaction) while retaining IsoNet's explicit injective alignment as a doubly stochastic matrix, and departs from GMN by replacing its non-injective, layer-varying attention with a fixed-per-round, iteratively refined alignment. The formulation of subgraph isomorphism as a Gromov-Wasserstein / quadratic assignment problem, and the use of Gumbel-Sinkhorn for differentiable alignment updates, are inherited from the optimal transport and differentiable combinatorial optimization literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that progressively refining an explicit injective alignment over multiple GNN rounds yields substantially better retrieval than either computing the alignment once (IsoNet) or using implicit attention-based alignment (GMN), with MAP improvements of roughly 10–15 points over the strongest baselines. Two open questions remain: (1) designing a hashable similarity function for early-interaction networks to enable production-scale retrieval, and (2) incorporating hard constraints (e.g., node/edge class compatibility) that the current soft alignment cannot enforce. The node-pair partner interaction mechanism is a general design principle that could transfer to other multi-graph GNN settings beyond subgraph isomorphism.
