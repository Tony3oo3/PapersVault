## Problem

The online consistency of the nearest neighbor rule was previously established only under strong assumptions: either i.i.d. instances (Cover and Hart, 1967) or uniformly separated label classes (Kulkarni and Posner, 1995). This paper asks how pathological the worst-case sequences on which the rule actually fails are, and shows that under mild stochastic and geometric constraints, such adversarial sequences are almost never generated, yielding online consistency for all measurable label functions in doubling metric spaces.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a stream of instances X_n from a metric measure space (X, ρ, ν) where ρ is a separable metric and ν is a finite Borel measure. At each step it predicts a class label using the nearest neighbor's label from memory, then observes the true label. The analysis characterizes the asymptotic mistake rate (1/N)Σ 1{η(X_n) ≠ η(X̃_n)} and requires the generating process to be ergodically dominated (for F0-consistency) or uniformly dominated (for universal consistency) by ν, with the latter additionally requiring the space to be upper doubling.

## Method

For functions with negligible boundary, the space is covered by mutually-labeling sets (where the nearest neighbor rule makes at most one mistake per set), reducing the problem to bounding the rate at which instances land in a small-mass remainder region via ergodic domination. For universal consistency, the key technical tool is a cover tree decomposition of nearest neighbor events that trades off a packing (metric entropy) bound against a measure bound: the cover tree is adaptively pruned so that the combined ν-mass of remaining 'tail' balls stays below δ, while removed balls are charged to the packing number. This yields Theorem 14, showing that a uniformly dominated process induces an ergodically dominated nearest neighbor process, which then limits the influence of small disagreement regions between η and its F0-approximation. The upper doubling condition (ν(B(x,r)) ≤ cr^d) is what converts small metric entropy into small measure, enabling the trade-off.

## Results

The paper proves two main consistency theorems: (1) Theorem 7 shows the 1-nearest neighbor rule is online consistent for all functions with ν-negligible boundary (F₀) under ergodically dominated processes, and (2) Theorem 12 establishes universal consistency (for any measurable η) on upper doubling metric measure spaces under uniformly dominated processes. The key technical ingredient is Theorem 17, a long-term influence bound showing that the asymptotic rate at which indicated instances appear as nearest neighbors is bounded by γ·(c₁ + c₂ log(1/δ)) + ε(δ). For σ-smoothed processes in doubling length spaces with boundary of box-counting dimension b > 1 and Minkowski content m, Theorem F.5 yields a concrete convergence rate of O((mN/σ)^(b/(b+1))) mistakes by time N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or datasets. The paper positions its contributions by relaxing assumptions of prior theoretical results: Cover and Hart (1967) required i.i.d. instances, while Theorem 7 extends F₀-consistency to the much broader class of ergodically dominated processes. Kulkarni and Posner (1995) required uniform class separation in a totally bounded space, while Theorem 7 relaxes this to instance-dependent margins (negligible boundary). Blanchard (2022) showed a negative result for C₁ processes on the unit interval, and the paper explains why: ergodic domination (retrospective) is insufficient when hard regions evolve over time, necessitating the stronger uniform domination (generative) condition of Theorem 12. The uniformly dominated class strictly generalizes the σ-smoothed processes of Haghtalab et al. (2020), and the paper provides the first convergence rates for nearest neighbor under smoothed processes in doubling length spaces (Theorem F.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical online consistency analysis of the 1-nearest neighbor rule, which was previously established only under i.i.d. sampling (Cover and Hart, 1967) or uniform class separation in totally bounded spaces (Kulkarni and Posner, 1995). It introduces two new process classes—ergodically dominated and uniformly dominated—that sit between i.i.d. and arbitrary sequences in a hierarchy of stochastic constraints, and proves consistency under these much weaker assumptions. The key technical tool is a cover-tree decomposition (building on Beygelzimer et al., 2006) that trades off metric packing bounds against measure-based bounds to control the long-term influence of 'bad' points in the nearest neighbor memory. The work also connects to the smoothed online learning literature (Haghtalab et al., 2020) by showing that smoothed processes are a special case of uniformly dominated processes, and addresses negative results from Blanchard (2022) and Hanneke (2021) by identifying precisely which process constraints are sufficient to avoid the failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental question about when the simplest nonparametric learner is reliable in non-i.i.d. online settings, showing that the adversarial sequences on which 1-NN fails are pathologically rare under mild absolute-continuity conditions. The upper doubling + uniform domination framework is broad enough to capture Euclidean spaces with Lebesgue measure and many natural metric spaces, making the universal consistency result (Theorem 12) practically meaningful. Open questions include whether the doubling/upper-doubling geometric assumption can be further relaxed, whether the logarithmic factor in the ergodic continuity of nearest neighbor processes (Theorem 14) is tight, and whether analogous results extend to k-NN or other nonparametric rules. The convergence rates for smoothed processes in length spaces (Theorem F.5) provide a template for quantitative analysis in other non-worst-case online learning settings.
