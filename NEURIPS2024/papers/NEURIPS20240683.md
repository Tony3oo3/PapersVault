## Problem

PDE surrogate modeling (predicting continuous fields from parameters) and parameter inference (estimating discrete parameters from measurements) have been addressed separately in the literature, with operator learning surrogates for the former and simulation-based inference for the latter. This separation risks nonlinear error amplification when the two models are concatenated at evaluation time, and existing joint approaches (VAE-based, Gaussian-process-based) are restricted to Gaussian latent distributions or lack discretization invariance. FUSE proposes a unified framework that solves both problems simultaneously with a single objective, enabling consistent gains in accuracy and robustness for both the forward and inverse tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes infinite-dimensional functional measurements u (e.g., pressure/velocity/PPG time series at 13 arterial locations for PWP, or horizontal/vertical velocity time series at 10 sensor locations for ACB) and produces two outputs: a posterior distribution over finite-dimensional parameters ξ (32 cardiovascular or 6 atmospheric parameters) and continuous time series predictions s at potentially different locations than the input. The input and output function spaces U and S can differ (as in PWP, where wrist measurements predict cerebral pressure), and the model handles varying levels of missing information through random masking of input channels during training. At evaluation, M parameter samples are drawn from the posterior and passed through the forward surrogate to yield an ensemble of s predictions with mean and standard deviation representing propagated parametric uncertainty.

## Method

FUSE derives a unified objective from the triangle inequality on push-forward measures: d(G̃_#μ^φ, G̃_#μ) ≤ measure matching + operator learning, decomposing the joint problem into a forward loss (L1, supervised L1 regression on parameter-to-output maps) and an inverse loss (L2, FMPE flow-matching loss on the posterior). The forward model is an FNO with a custom band-limited lifting h(ξ) that maps finite-dimensional parameters into infinite-dimensional space via an inverse Fourier transform before FNO layers and a projection Q. The inverse model adapts FMPE with an FNO-based encoder T(u) that applies a band-limited forward Fourier transform to create a fixed-size latent representation of the functional input regardless of resolution, followed by FNO layers and projection to the parameter dimension m. The two objectives are trained with decoupled backpropagation per batch (avoiding hyperparameter tuning of a combined loss), but are unified at evaluation by sampling parameters from the FMPE posterior and passing them through the FNO forward surrogate to propagate uncertainty onto the continuous output.

## Results

On the Pulse Wave Propagation (PWP) task, FUSE achieves a relative L1 error of 0.13±0.05% with true parameters and 0.86±0.93% with estimated parameters (Level 1, full information), with a CRPS of 1.31×10⁻². On the Atmospheric Cold Bubble (ACB) task, FUSE achieves 0.41±0.45% relative L1 error with true parameters and 0.58±0.59% with estimated parameters (CRPS 1.84×10⁻²). In the unified uncertainty propagation setting, FUSE's ensemble mean predictions remain within one standard deviation of ground truth for the median PWP sample, and the L1 error from propagated uncertainty is comparable to the forward-only error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE outperforms all three baselines (cVANO, InVAErt, GAROM) on the forward problem in both test cases: on PWP, FUSE achieves 0.13% relative L1 error vs. 7.49% (cVANO), 3.44% (InVAErt), and 0.70% (GAROM); on ACB, FUSE achieves 0.41% vs. 5.32% (cVANO), 1.11% (InVAErt), and 3.99% (GAROM). On the inverse problem, FUSE dominates: on ACB, FUSE's CRPS of 1.84×10⁻² is an order of magnitude better than cVANO (19.84) and InVAErt (6.65); on PWP Level 1, FUSE (1.31) beats cVANO (5.61) and InVAErt (11.62). The sole exception is PWP Level 3 (minimal information, PPG at fingertip only), where cVANO achieves a lower CRPS (3.66 vs. 8.48), likely due to its Gaussian latent assumption being better suited to highly uncertain settings. GAROM cannot perform the inverse task at all (no parameter estimation), limiting its comparison to forward-only evaluation. The UNet ablation (replacing FNO layers) consistently underperforms FUSE on both forward and inverse metrics, confirming the benefit of the Fourier-based operator architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE unifies two previously separate research threads—neural operator surrogates (FNO and its variants) for forward PDE emulation and simulation-based inference (FMPE, NPE) for parameter estimation—under a single measure-matching objective derived from the triangle inequality on push-forward measures. It departs from VAE-based joint approaches (cVANO, InVAErt) by replacing Gaussian latent restrictions with fully probabilistic flow-matching posteriors, and from GAN-based ROMs (GAROM) by retaining discretization invariance through Fourier-based operator layers. The key architectural extensions—band-limited lifting for finite-to-infinite-dimensional mapping in FNO, and FNO-based conditional encoding in FMPE—are novel combinations not previously explored in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled way to propagate parametric uncertainty through a fast surrogate, yielding interpretable ensemble predictions without requiring expensive repeated solver calls—directly relevant to calibration workflows in cardiovascular modeling and atmospheric simulation. Key limitations include the assumption of a perfect (noise-free) data-generating model, the restriction to finite-dimensional parameters, and the lack of measurement-error modeling, all of which constrain applicability to real-world data. Open questions include extending FUSE to function-valued (infinite-dimensional) parameters, incorporating structural model uncertainty, and validating on real patient or weather data where the PDE parameterization is only approximate.
