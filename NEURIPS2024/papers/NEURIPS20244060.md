## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) methods like SNPE improve sample efficiency by sequentially updating proposal distributions, but they draw parameter candidates independently from the proposal without explicitly evaluating which candidates would most reduce uncertainty in the neural density estimator. This leads to simulation runs with high information overlap and wasted computation, particularly problematic when the simulator is computationally expensive (e.g., traffic simulators with thousands of parameters). The paper asks: can we design a principled acquisition function that selects the most informative simulation parameters for the NDE without requiring access to the intractable likelihood?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prior p(θ) over simulation parameters, a target observation x_o, and a black-box simulator p(x|θ) that can only be sampled (not evaluated in closed form). At each round, it draws N candidate parameters from the current proposal, scores them via the acquisition function, and selects the top-B to simulate. The output is an approximate posterior q_φ(θ|x_o) represented as a masked autoregressive flow, which in the OD calibration application yields a full posterior over 5329-dimensional OD matrices rather than a single point estimate. The NDE is trained with MC-dropout (rate 0.25) to maintain a Bayesian parameter posterior p(φ|D) needed for the acquisition computation.

## Method

ASNPE integrates an active learning acquisition function into the SNPE-C (APT) loop. The key insight is replacing the intractable expected information gain (which requires p(x|θ,D)) with a distributional uncertainty measure: the expected squared deviation between the NDE's marginal posterior p(θ|x,D) and individual component posteriors p(θ|x,φ) across MC-dropout samples φ~p(φ|D). The final acquisition function (Eq. 4) multiplies the proposal likelihood p̃(θ) by this expected squared deviation, balancing exploration of high-uncertainty regions against exploitation of high-likelihood regions. The NDE is a masked autoregressive flow (5 transform layers, 50 hidden units) trained via APT's corrected-likelihood objective, with 100 MC-dropout weight samples used to evaluate the acquisition over the N-sized candidate pool. This avoids any surrogate model or likelihood approximation, keeping the pipeline compatible with powerful flow-based NDEs while adding only a batched inference step for acquisition scoring.

## Results

On the Munich traffic network (5,329 OD pairs, 507 detectors), ASNPE achieves the best RMSNE in 6 of 8 synthetic calibration scenarios, with scores ranging from 0.130 to 0.165. ASNPE consistently outperforms SNPE-C by 0.01–0.05 RMSNE and MC-ABC by 0.04–0.14 RMSNE across all scenarios. On the SBI benchmark tasks (Bernoulli GLM, SLCP Distractors, Gaussian Mixture), ASNPE outperforms SNPE-C on C2ST and MMD in all three settings, and on posterior mean error in 2 of 3 settings, using 1,024 total simulation samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASNPE outperforms SPSA in all 8 Munich scenarios, with margins of 0.05–0.42 RMSNE, confirming that the active acquisition strategy substantially improves over gradient-based stochastic optimization. Against PC-SPSA (the strongest non-SBI baseline), ASNPE wins in 6 of 8 scenarios (e.g., 0.147 vs. 0.193 in Prior I, 5–6h, Cong A) but is slightly outperformed in the two Prior I, 8–9h scenarios (0.165 vs. 0.159 and 0.161 vs. 0.155), which the authors attribute to PC-SPSA's per-iteration parameter updates versus ASNPE's batch-wise NDE retraining. ASNPE beats MC-ABC in all 8 scenarios by 0.04–0.14 RMSNE, and beats SNPE-C in all 8 Munich scenarios by 0.01–0.05 RMSNE, demonstrating the benefit of active parameter selection over uniform proposal sampling. On the three SBI benchmark tasks, ASNPE outperforms SNPE-C on C2ST and MMD across all settings (e.g., MMD 0.146 vs. 0.210 on Bernoulli GLM) and on posterior mean error in 2 of 3 settings, while showing comparable or slightly worse median distance in one case.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASNPE extends the Sequential Neural Posterior Estimation (SNPE) framework of Papamakarios & Murray (2016) by inserting an explicit active-learning acquisition step between proposal sampling and simulator evaluation, building on the APT/SNPE-C training objective of Greenberg et al. (2019) as its NDE backbone. Prior SNPE variants update the proposal distribution heuristically (using the current posterior as the next proposal) without considering which specific parameter candidates within that proposal would most reduce epistemic uncertainty in the NDE; ASNPE makes this selection explicit via an acquisition function measuring expected divergence between marginal and component posteriors under MC-dropout. The approach also draws on Bayesian active learning (EIG-style acquisition) and Bayesian optimization for likelihood-free inference (Gutmann & Corander, Järvenpää et al.), but deliberately avoids surrogate likelihood models, relying solely on the NDE itself for uncertainty quantification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sample efficiency is a critical bottleneck when simulators are computationally expensive (e.g., traffic simulators requiring hours per run), and ASNPE's plug-and-play acquisition step offers a low-overhead way to reduce wasted simulation evaluations without architectural changes to the NDE. The demonstration on a 5329-dimensional Munich traffic network with 507 detector locations provides strong practical validation, though the method is outperformed by PC-SPSA in two of eight scenarios, revealing that batch-based NDE updates can lag behind per-iteration optimization in early stages. Open questions include how the acquisition function behaves at much larger sample budgets, whether parallel or batched acquisition strategies could further reduce wall-clock time, and whether the MC-dropout approximation of p(φ|D) remains reliable as NDE complexity grows.
