## Problem

Traditional LLM evaluation for question answering focuses on single-turn response quality, failing to capture the dynamic, multi-turn nature of real human-AI interactions where users iteratively seek clarification. While human evaluators can assess these interactions, the process is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to scale. This paper addresses the need for a fully automatic evaluation framework that can both generate and assess interactive QA conversations at scale.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a question (multi-choice with options, or open-domain) and an IQA model (LLM) as input. The LEA simulates a human by generating sub-questions to the IQA model, which only receives the immediately preceding prompt without the full original question context, mimicking a true interactive setting. Outputs include helpfulness and fluency scores on a 5-point Likert scale, the number of interaction turns, final answer accuracy, and optional free-form textual feedback explaining the scores.

## Method

IQA-EVAL uses a two-stage LLM-based Evaluation Agent (LEA) operating in zero-shot mode. In Stage 1, the LEA is prompted with a role description, task description, and discussion instructions to simulate a human asking iterative sub-questions to the IQA model until it determines a final answer. In Stage 2, the LEA evaluates the generated interaction against defined metrics (helpfulness, fluency) using a structured prompt with metric definitions and evaluation instructions. A key innovation is assigning personas (Expert, Critical-Thinker, Adaptability-Seeker, Clarity-Seeker) to LEAs, which modifies both the interaction-generation and evaluation prompts to simulate different user groups, significantly improving correlation with human judgments (from ~0.51 to ~0.67–0.79 overall Pearson ρ). The framework also addresses self-enhancement bias through debiasing prompts and a multi-perspective evaluation strategy where multiple LEA models independently score the same interaction.

## Results

IQA-EVAL with GPT-4 as the LEA achieves the highest overall Pearson correlation with human evaluations (ρ=0.613), with Helpfulness at ρ=0.652 and Fluency at ρ=0.591. Assigning personas to LEAs significantly improves alignment: the Clarity-Seeker persona reaches ρ=0.690 overall, and the Expert persona reaches ρ=0.670, compared to 0.510 for the default (no-persona) GPT-3.5 LEA. In the LLM benchmarking on 1,000 complex questions (HotpotQA + AmbigQA), GPT-4 achieves the highest accuracy (0.66 on HotpotQA, 0.72 on AmbigQA) and best helpfulness (4.78) and fluency (4.96) scores, while Zephyr performs weakest (accuracy 0.40/0.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is human evaluation from Lee et al. (2023), which provides ground-truth fluency and helpfulness scores from 3,641 interactions by 331 annotators. IQA-EVAL with GPT-4 as LEA achieves the closest alignment to human judgments (overall ρ=0.613), outperforming Claude (ρ=0.551) and GPT-3.5 (ρ=0.510). Adding personas to the default GPT-3.5 LEA improves overall correlation by 0.12–0.18 (from 0.510 to 0.634–0.690), with the Clarity-Seeker persona yielding the best match (ρ=0.690). The multi-perspective variant (averaging scores from multiple LEA models) further improves GPT-4's correlation to ρ=0.624 overall, suggesting that self-enhancement bias has limited impact. On the benchmarking task, IQA-EVAL's accuracy-based ranking (GPT4 > Claude > GPT3.5 > Llama2 > Zephyr) aligns with Chatbot Arena's non-interactive ranking, but interaction-quality metrics (helpfulness, fluency) produce different rankings, showing that interactive evaluation captures dimensions missed by single-turn benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lee et al. (2023), which established the human-annotated dataset, interaction protocol, and evaluation metrics (fluency, helpfulness) for interactive QA, and on the LLM-as-judge paradigm (G-Eval, LLM-Eval) for automating response quality assessment. The key departure is combining an LLM-based agent that *simulates* the human user (drawing on LLM-agent simulation work like Park et al. and Gao et al.) with an LLM-based evaluator in a single automated pipeline, so that both interaction generation and scoring are handled without human involvement. The persona-assignment mechanism extends prior persona-based dialogue work (Li et al. 2016, Zhang et al. 2018) from response generation to evaluation, using crowdworker survey distributions to calibrate which user subgroups the LEA represents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IQA-EVAL offers a scalable, cost-effective (~$5k saved per evaluation run) alternative to human evaluation of interactive QA, with correlations of 0.6–0.7 with human judgments, making it practical for rapid model comparison and regression testing. The finding that interaction-quality rankings diverge from non-interactive accuracy rankings (e.g., Claude leads in interaction helpfulness but trails in standalone accuracy) suggests that current benchmarks systematically understate some models' interactive strengths. Open questions include generalization beyond multi-choice QA to open-ended and multi-document settings, resolving the self-enhancement bias when LEA and IQA share a backbone, and whether the persona framework can capture the full diversity of real user populations.
