## Problem

Sharded Data Parallelism (ShardedDP) reduces per-GPU memory by partitioning optimizer states, but introduces heavy communication overhead for both weights (AllGather) and gradients (ReduceScatter/All-to-All), especially across nodes with limited bandwidth. Prior attempts to compress this communication to 4-bit (QSDP, ZeRO++) degrade training accuracy, and no existing method achieves near-4-bit communication for both weights and gradients while preserving end-to-end training loss.

## Input / Output

Each GPU worker receives its shard of FP32 main weights and BF16 model weights, plus locally computed FP32 gradients. The method outputs 4-bit quantized weight differences (computed as main_weight − model_weight after the optimizer step) for AllGather, and two-level quantized gradients — INT8 for intra-node all-to-all and INT4 for inter-node all-to-all — for gradient reduction. A key structural detail is that the method is designed for Megatron-LM's ShardedDP, where each GPU retains a full copy of model weights throughout forward and backward passes, enabling buffer reuse for the difference computation without extra memory allocation.

## Method

SDP4Bit combines two quantization strategies with algorithm-system co-design. First, Quantization on Weight Differences (qWD) compresses the small, uniformly-distributed deltas between main and model weights to 4-bit rather than quantizing the full weights, which have a wide dynamic range that makes 4-bit quantization lossy. Second, Two-Level Gradient Smooth Quantization (TLq-HS) applies 8-bit quantization to intra-node all-to-all and 4-bit to inter-node all-to-all, with a 32×32 Hadamard transform applied before quantization to spread outlier energy across nearby elements and reduce quantization error. The implementation fuses the Hadamard transform with (de)quantization into a single CUDA kernel (requiring group size divisible by Hadamard matrix size), exploits H·H=I to eliminate redundant transforms, and reuses locally stored model weights to avoid extra buffers. Theoretically, the paper proves O(1/√T) convergence for arbitrary non-convex objectives under weaker assumptions than QSDP, and provides a counterexample showing that biased compressors applied directly to weights (as in QSDP/ZeRO++) can cause convergence failure.

## Results

SDP4Bit reduces both weight and gradient communication to nearly 4 bits while achieving a maximum validation loss increase of only 0.24% over the full-precision baseline across GPT models from 125M to 6.7B parameters. In end-to-end throughput, SDP4Bit achieves up to 4.08× speedup on 128 H800 GPUs (GPT-18B) and up to 3.44× on 64 A100 GPUs (GPT-6.7B). The training validation loss curve for GPT-6.7B using SDP4Bit is nearly indistinguishable from the full-precision baseline over 80,000 iterations (40B+ tokens on The Pile).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ZeRO++'s quantization strategy (qW for weights + ULq for gradients), implemented in Megatron-LM. On accuracy, ZeRO++'s direct 4-bit weight quantization (qW) degrades validation loss by up to 12% (e.g., 2.573 vs. 2.294 on GPT-125M), while SDP4Bit's weight-difference quantization (qWD) stays within 0.03% of the baseline. Similarly, ZeRO++'s uniform 4-bit gradient quantization (ULq) produces a visible loss gap, whereas SDP4Bit's two-level quantization with Hadamard smoothing (TLq-HS) narrows the gap to under 0.1%. On throughput, SDP4Bit is compared against the non-quantized BFloat16/Float32 mixed-precision baseline in Megatron-LM, achieving 2.39×–4.08× speedup depending on model size and hardware. The speedup is most pronounced on lower-bandwidth networks (A100/Slingshot10) and for larger models where communication overhead dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the ShardedDP communication compression line initiated by QSDP (Markov et al., ICML 2023) and ZeRO++ (Wang et al., ICML 2023), both of which attempted Int4 quantization of weights and gradients but suffered accuracy degradation at extreme compression ratios. SDP4Bit departs from both by (1) quantizing weight *differences* rather than raw weights, (2) replacing ZeRO++'s uniform 4-bit two-all-to-all gradient scheme with a two-level 8-bit/4-bit strategy, and (3) introducing Hadamard transform smoothing for gradient outliers. Theoretically, it generalizes QSDP's convergence analysis by removing the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition and the restriction to a specific 'random shift' quantizer, while showing that biased compressors are compatible with weight-difference compression but provably fail for direct weight compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDP4Bit is the first method to reduce both weight and gradient communication in ShardedDP to nearly 4 bits with negligible accuracy loss, achieving up to 4.08× end-to-end speedup on 128 GPUs — a practically significant gain for production LLM pre-training where communication is a dominant bottleneck. The convergence guarantee under weaker assumptions than prior work strengthens the theoretical foundation for using biased compressors in distributed training. Key limitations include the focus on LLM pre-training with GPT architectures, symmetric linear quantization only, and implementation tied to Megatron-LM's specific weight-retention strategy; open questions include extension to MoE models, fine-tuning, non-text modalities, and whether the weight-difference approach generalizes to other optimizer states beyond AdamW.
