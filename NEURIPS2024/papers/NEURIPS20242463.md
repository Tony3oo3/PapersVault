## Problem

Existing deep MIL methods for medical imaging are designed primarily to optimize bag-level classification, and their instance-level localization performance is comparatively poor. The paper argues that the natural local dependency among neighboring instances (the cluster assumption) has not been properly exploited to produce smooth, clinically meaningful attention maps that reflect true lesion boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes bags of pre-extracted instance features (512×512 WSI patches or CT slices, encoded by ResNet18/50, ViT-B-32, or a Barlow-Twins-pretrained ResNet50) along with a spatial adjacency matrix defining neighbor relationships between instances. It outputs a bag-level binary class prediction and per-instance attention scores that serve as localization estimates; the attention values are the direct proxy for instance labels used in both the localization AUROC/F1 evaluation and the attention map visualization.

## Method

The core contribution is a smooth operator Sm derived from Dirichlet energy minimization on the bag graph: Sm(U) = (I + γL)⁻¹U, approximated by T iterative steps of G(t) = α(I−L)G(t−1) + (1−α)U. A theoretical guarantee (Eq. 9) shows Sm reduces the Dirichlet energy of its input by a factor λ*_γ < 1 when the normalized Laplacian is used. The operator is integrated into two model variants: SmAP (applied within the attention pooling of ABMIL, modeling only local interactions) and SmTAP (applied both after a two-layer transformer encoder and within the attention pooling, modeling both global and local interactions). Spectral normalization is applied to layers immediately after Sm to keep weight norms constant, ensuring the Dirichlet energy bound in Eq. 4 holds. The operator is modular and can be placed at three levels (early on H, mid on F, late on f) or combined with other MIL backbones (CLAM, DSMIL, DFTD-MIL), making it a drop-in component rather than a fixed architecture.

## Results

On the localization task (instance-level AUROC), SmAP achieves 0.960 on CAMELYON16 (vs. 0.819 for ABMIL and 0.884 for DFTD-MIL), while SmTAP achieves 0.789 on CAMELYON16 (vs. 0.781 for TransMIL and 0.742 for CAMIL). On the classification task (bag-level AUROC), SmAP reaches 0.976 on CAMELYON16 and 0.943 on PANDA, and SmTAP reaches 0.976 on CAMELYON16 and 0.946 on PANDA, matching or exceeding all baselines. The proposed method ranks first in 8 out of 12 dataset-score pairs for localization and achieves the best overall mean rank (1.500) in both groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ABMIL [17], SmAP wins on PANDA (0.799 vs. 0.768 AUROC) and CAMELYON16 (0.960 vs. 0.819) for localization but loses narrowly on RSNA (0.798 vs. 0.806); on classification, SmAP matches ABMIL on RSNA (0.888 vs. 0.889) and wins on PANDA (0.943 vs. 0.933) and CAMELYON16 (0.976 vs. 0.956). Against DFTD-MIL [39], SmAP wins on RSNA (0.798 vs. 0.747) and CAMELYON16 (0.960 vs. 0.884) localization, while DFTD-MIL edges out SmAP on CAMELYON16 classification (0.983 vs. 0.976). Among transformer-based methods, SmTAP decisively outperforms SETMIL [40] and GTP [41] on CAMELYON16 localization (0.789 vs. 0.615 and 0.442 respectively), where both baselines collapse; it also beats TransMIL [32] on RSNA (0.767 vs. 0.732) and CAMIL [14] on CAMELYON16 (0.789 vs. 0.742). On classification, SmTAP leads on RSNA (0.906) and CAMELYON16 (0.976), though GTP [41] and SETMIL [40] slightly outperform it on PANDA (0.949/0.974 vs. 0.946). CLAM [25] and DSMIL [21] are consistently outperformed across all datasets and both tasks. The three additional baselines (DeepGraphSurv [24], PathGCN [11], IIBMIL [29]) evaluated in the appendix are also outperformed by the proposed methods on instance-level AUROC across all feature extractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ABMIL (attention-based MIL) and TransMIL (transformer-based MIL), positioning itself within a unified taxonomy of three families: independent-instance, global-interaction, and global+local-interaction models. Its key departure is the introduction of a smooth operator (Sm) derived from Dirichlet energy minimization on a bag graph, drawing on GNN theory (Zhou & Schölkopf, PPNP, p-Laplacian layers) to enforce local smoothness on attention values. Unlike prior local-dependency methods (CAMIL, GTP, SETMIL) that incorporate spatial structure primarily to improve bag-level classification, Sm is explicitly designed to improve instance-level localization, and it is modular enough to be combined with any global mechanism (e.g., transformers) or used standalone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical gap: MIL methods excel at bag classification but produce poor instance-level localization, which is critical for clinical deployment (pinpointing lesion locations). The smooth operator offers a simple, theoretically grounded, and plug-and-play solution that consistently improves localization across three medical imaging datasets and multiple feature extractors. Limitations include computational cost scaling with bag size (mitigable via subgraph sampling), uncertainty about optimal Sm placement, and the need for probabilistic formulations and broader clinical validation before real-world deployment.
