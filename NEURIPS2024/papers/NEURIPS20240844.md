## Problem

While fast samplers have been developed for unconditional diffusion and flow-matching models, solving inverse problems such as super-resolution, inpainting, and deblurring still requires hundreds to thousands of neural network evaluations to achieve high perceptual quality. Existing posterior-guided methods like ΠGDM also incur expensive Jacobian-vector products per step, making them impractically slow. This paper asks whether the specific algebraic structure of linear inverse problems can be exploited directly in the sampler design to reduce the number of sampling steps by an order of magnitude.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation y = Hx₀ (optionally plus Gaussian noise), the known linear degradation operator H (e.g., bicubic downsampling for 4× super-resolution, a binary mask for inpainting, or a Gaussian blur kernel), and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion or flow-matching model at 256×256 resolution. Sampling is initialized at a non-zero time τ using the pseudoinverse H†y plus scaled noise, rather than from pure noise, and the output is a single restored clean image. The framework is training-free and plug-and-play: no retraining or paired (x_t, y) data is required, and the same pre-trained checkpoint can be reused across different degradation operators.

## Method

The core contribution is Conditional Conjugate Integrators (C-ΠGDM for diffusions, C-ΠGFM for flows), which project the conditional reverse dynamics into a transformed space via an invertible time-dependent affine map x̄_t = A_t x_t. The key design choice is that A_t is parameterized to encode the degradation operator H and the guidance weight w directly: specifically, A_t = exp(κ₁(t)I + κ₂(t)P) where P = Hᵀ(HHᵀ)⁻¹H is the orthogonal projector onto the range of H. Because P is idempotent, the matrix exponential reduces to a closed-form scalar expression, making A_t and A_t⁻¹ trivially computable. The theoretical insight is that for large guidance weight w, near the start of sampling the projected dynamics are confined to the nullspace of P (i.e., the high-frequency components missing from the degraded observation), enabling early recovery of fine details, while near the end of sampling the transformation reverts to the identity, preventing over-sharpening artifacts. The projected ODE separates into a linear drift term (involving y and H†), a score-network term, and a Jacobian term, all with pre-computable time-dependent coefficients, allowing the entire sampling loop to run in as few as 5 Euler steps.

## Results

On the challenging 4× super-resolution task on ImageNet (256×256), C-ΠGDM achieves FID 37.31, KID 2.7, and LPIPS 0.220 at just 5 NFEs, outperforming ΠGDM (FID 49.06) at the same budget and surpassing DDRM (FID 51.64) and DPS (FID 38.18) which require 20 and 1000 NFEs respectively. For Gaussian deblurring on ImageNet, C-ΠGDM reaches FID 44.42 at 5 NFEs versus 63.94 for ΠGDM and 139.58 for DPS (1000 NFEs). On CelebA-HQ with flow models, C-ΠGFM achieves FID 26.95 for inpainting at 5 NFEs compared to 161.49 for ΠGFM, and FID 13.08 for 4× super-resolution versus 21.84 for ΠGFM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-ΠGDM dominates ΠGDM across all NFE budgets on both ImageNet super-resolution and deblurring: at 5 NFEs, C-ΠGDM achieves FID 37.31 vs. 49.06 (SR) and 44.42 vs. 63.94 (deblurring), a 24–31% FID reduction. Against DPS (1000 NFEs), C-ΠGDM at only 10 NFEs already matches or exceeds DPS on FID for super-resolution (34.22 vs. 38.18) and substantially outperforms it on deblurring (43.37 vs. 139.58). DDRM (20 NFEs) is also outperformed by C-ΠGDM at 10 NFEs on both tasks (FID 34.22 vs. 51.64 for SR; 43.37 vs. 62.53 for deblurring). For flow models on CelebA-HQ, C-ΠGFM shows the largest gains over ΠGFM on inpainting (FID 26.95 vs. 161.49 at 5 NFEs, a 6× improvement) and super-resolution (13.08 vs. 21.84), though ΠGFM slightly edges out C-ΠGFM at 20 NFEs on deblurring (FID 11.35 vs. 17.10) and super-resolution (10.23 vs. 11.07), suggesting diminishing returns of the conjugate projection at higher budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conjugate Integrator framework (Pandey et al., 2024), originally designed for fast unconditional diffusion sampling, to the conditional/guided setting for linear inverse problems. It builds directly on the ΠGDM posterior approximation (Song et al., 2022) and its flow counterpart ΠGFM (Pokle et al., 2024), but rather than treating the conditional score as a black box, it incorporates the algebraic structure of the degradation operator H into the projection matrix A_t itself. The key conceptual departure from prior guided samplers (DPS, DDRM, ΠGDM) is that the inverse problem's linearity is exploited at the integrator level, separating linear and non-linear drift terms and analytically solving for the linear coefficients, yielding a more well-conditioned projected ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving comparable or superior sample quality at 5 NFE versus 20–1000 NFE for competing methods makes high-fidelity image restoration feasible in near-real-time settings, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature means it applies to any pre-trained diffusion or flow model without retraining. However, the method still requires Jacobian-vector products of the score/velocity network, which remain expensive per step, and the Euler solver shows a performance plateau around NFE=20, suggesting stochastic solvers or higher-order methods could unlock further gains. Open directions include extending to blind (unknown H) inverse problems, latent diffusion models, and learning the transformation A_t during sampling rather than fixing it analytically.
