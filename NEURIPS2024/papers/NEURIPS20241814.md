## Problem

LLMs frequently generate untruthful or biased content, and while editing internal activations has shown promise for mitigating these behaviours, existing methods (e.g., ITI, contrastive decoding) require expensive iterative optimisation to learn a target editing module. This paper asks whether a closed-form, training-free spectral method can identify editing directions directly from the covariance structure between positive, negative, and neutral activations, eliminating the need for gradient-based training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three sets of demonstrations—positive (prompt + desirable completion), negative (prompt + undesirable completion), and neutral (prompt only, with the LLM's own completion)—from which it extracts last-token MLP-layer activations. It produces two orthogonal projection matrices (U⁺·U⁺ᵀ and U⁻·U⁻ᵀ) that are applied in parallel to the MLP outputs of the last L transformer layers at every token position during inference. A key practical detail is that only 25 demonstrations suffice for a noticeable improvement, and the offline projection computation is not bounded by the model's context window, unlike in-context learning.

## Method

SEA computes empirical cross-covariance matrices between neutral activations and positive/negative activations, then performs SVD on each. For the positive covariance, it retains the top-K% left singular vectors (directions of maximal co-variation to preserve); for the negative covariance, it retains the bottom-K% (directions of maximal co-variation to remove). During inference, both projection matrices are applied in parallel to MLP outputs of the last L layers, and the two edited activations are merged with a per-coordinate feature normalisation that preserves the original activation magnitude. To handle non-linearly separable behaviours (e.g., bias), Φ-SEA first maps activations through an invertible non-linear feature function (squared-exponential, tanh, or ELU), performs the same SVD-based editing in that transformed space, and then applies a numerically stabilised pseudo-inverse to return to the original activation space. The entire pipeline is training-free: no gradient updates are required, and the editing projections are obtained in closed form from a single SVD decomposition.

## Results

On TruthfulQA (LLaMA-2-Chat-7B), linear SEA (N=2000, K=99.8%, L=21) achieves MC1=39.41 and MC2=57.15, outperforming the ICL baseline (MC1=36.96, MC2=54.68) by +2.45 and +2.47 points respectively, while increasing inference time by only 3.67% (5.93s vs 4.90s per sample). On BBQ, non-linear SEA with the squared-exponential feature function boosts accuracy from 43.02% (ICL) to 56.17% (+13.15%), while reducing the unknown-answer rate from 19.6% to 8.1% and the stereotypical response rate from 52.0% to 42.6%. Across six LLMs (LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B, Gemma 2B/7B, Mistral 7B), linear SEA consistently improves TruthfulQA MC1, and Φ-SEA improves BBQ accuracy by 1.1–13.2 points depending on model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEA outperforms LoRA-FT on both MC1 (39.41 vs 35.74/35.01) and MC2 (57.15 vs 54.61/54.24) while requiring only 152s of offline projection computation versus 299–1190s of gradient-based training. Against ITI, SEA achieves higher MC1 (39.41 vs 37.01) and MC2 (57.15 vs 54.66) with comparable inference overhead (5.93s vs 5.82s). SEA substantially beats Contrastive Decoding (CD) on MC1 (39.41 vs 28.15) and MC2 (57.15 vs 54.87). DoLA is the one method that exceeds SEA on MC2 (60.84 vs 57.15), though it lags significantly on MC1 (32.97 vs 39.41). Compared to ICD, SEA achieves higher MC1 (39.41 vs 37.87) with 39% less inference time (5.93s vs 9.67s), while ICD edges out SEA on MC2 (57.77 vs 57.15). On BBQ, non-linear SEA (squared-exponential) dramatically outperforms both ICL (56.17% vs 43.02% accuracy) and LoRA-FT (56.17% vs 44.72%), while linear SEA provides only a modest +0.78% gain over ICL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEA sits within the growing family of inference-time activation editing methods for LLM alignment, which includes shifting-vector approaches (ITI [21]), contrastive decoding against weaker or induced models [22, 45], layer-contrast decoding (DoLa [7]), and optimal-transport-based non-linear editing (MIMIC [35]). The key conceptual departure is that SEA replaces iterative gradient-based optimisation (as in LoRA or ITI) with a closed-form spectral decomposition of cross-covariance matrices between neutral, positive, and negative activations, yielding editing projections in a single SVD pass. The non-linear extension via invertible feature functions (squared-exponential, tanh, ELU) with pseudo-inverses is a practical repurposing of kernel-style feature maps to the activation-editing setting, distinct from MIMIC's optimal-transport formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEA offers a training-free, data-efficient (as few as 25 demonstrations) alternative to gradient-based alignment that is not constrained by context length, making it attractive for practitioners who want to steer model behaviour without retraining or fine-tuning. The non-linear variant's 13-point accuracy gain on BBQ across multiple model families is practically significant for bias mitigation. However, the non-linear pseudo-inverse is not lossless, causing measurable degradation on control tasks (e.g., HellaSwag drops ~6 points), and the linear variant's improvements on TruthfulQA remain modest (~2.5 MC1). An open question is whether truly lossless non-linear editing can be achieved, and whether the covariance-based editing directions generalise to attributes beyond truthfulness and bias (e.g., style, safety).
