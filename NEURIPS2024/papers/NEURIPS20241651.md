## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) either require full model access (white-box), visual encoder access (gray-box), or surrogate models like CLIP (black-box), making them impractical for real-world LVLMs accessed solely through APIs. Moreover, these attacks are task-specific, necessitating separate perturbations for each downstream task (VQA, captioning, classification), incurring significant time and resource costs. This paper is the first to construct a universal, task-agnostic attacker that operates with only input-output queries to the LVLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241651/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker queries the LVLM with an RGB image (pasted with a 64×64 adversarial patch at a fixed location, constrained to ‖Δ‖∞ ≤ 16/255) paired with a text prompt from one of K downstream tasks, and receives only the generated text output as feedback. The output is a single universal adversarial patch that, when overlaid on any image at the learned position, steers the LVLM toward an attacker-chosen target label (e.g., "Unknown") across all tasks simultaneously. A Sentence-BERT encoder serves as a judge to compute cosine semantic distance between the LVLM's output and the target, replacing the unavailable logits or confidence scores.

## Method

The method proceeds in three stages: (1) random initialization of patch location and noise pattern, selecting the best combination by minimizing semantic distance to the target; (2) importance-aware gradient approximation, where Monte Carlo noise samples are weighted by an indicator function σ_t that measures whether each perturbation moves the LVLM output closer to the target, with helpful directions amplified via exp(Δσ_t) and unhelpful ones suppressed via ln(Δσ_t+3); (3) iterative patch update along the weighted gradient direction over T steps using 70k total queries. The key insight is that in the complex LVLM search space, naive Monte Carlo averaging cancels out opposing gradient directions, so the importance weighting selectively reinforces directions that consistently increase semantic distance to the target across diverse task inputs, enabling a single patch to be universally adversarial across tasks and images.

## Results

The proposed universal adversarial patch achieves semantic similarity scores of 0.81–0.87 (target: "Unknown") across four LVLMs (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, Flamingo, BLIP-2) and three datasets (MS-COCO, DALLE-3, VQAv2), compared to clean-image baselines of 0.37–0.48. The importance-aware gradient approximation consistently improves over the unweighted variant by 0.03–0.05 in overall similarity (e.g., 0.837 vs. 0.696 on LLaVA/DALLE-3). A single 64×64 patch, optimized with 70k queries in ~4.9 GPU-hours, is pasted at a fixed location and fools all three tasks (Image Classification, Image Captioning, VQA) simultaneously without access to model internals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MF-Attack [33] (a transfer-based black-box attack requiring a CLIP surrogate), the proposed method achieves 0.727 vs. 0.646 average similarity on ImageNet-1k VQA (Table 3), and in the re-implemented comparison (Table 14) wins by 0.15–0.21 across all four LVLMs and three datasets. Against CroPA [31] (a white-box cross-prompt attack), the proposed method achieves 0.69 vs. 0.65 overall ASR on OpenFlamingo/MS-COCO (Table 4), winning on Image Classification (0.73 vs. 0.70) and Image Captioning (0.51 vs. 0.34) but trailing on VQA (0.84 vs. 0.92); in the broader Table 14 comparison, the proposed method still leads CroPA by 0.02–0.05 overall across all models and datasets. Critically, the proposed attack operates in a strictly more constrained setting (no model weights, no logits, no surrogate model, no visual encoder access) while producing a single universal patch applicable to all tasks and images, whereas both baselines require per-sample or per-task perturbation generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on LVLMs by combining two previously separate concerns: universality (one perturbation across all tasks and images) and practical black-box access (only input/output queries, no model parameters, no logits, no surrogate models like CLIP). Prior white-box methods (CroPA, InstructTA) and transfer-based black-box methods (MF-Attack) each address one axis but not both simultaneously. The optimization strategy draws on zeroth-order Monte Carlo gradient estimation from hard-label black-box patch attacks (Tao et al., USENIX Security '23), adapted here with an importance-aware weighting scheme that up-weights noise directions increasing semantic distance to the target label, and a Sentence-BERT-based judge function borrowed from Zhao et al. (NeurIPS '24) to measure output-target alignment without access to model internals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical framing—attacking LVLMs through API-only access with a single reusable patch—directly mirrors how most deployed vision-language services (GPT-4V, Midjourney) are actually consumed, making the threat model more realistic than prior white-box or surrogate-based studies. The universal patch design also has physical-world implications (a printed sticker could fool any LVLM-driven task), though the authors acknowledge this remains untested. Key open questions include robustness in real-time camera-based deployments (robotics, autonomous driving), the query-budget trade-off (70k queries is non-trivial), and whether the approach generalizes to LVLMs with built-in safety filters that reject adversarial outputs entirely.
