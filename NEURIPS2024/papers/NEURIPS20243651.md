## Problem

Multi-task learning theory has produced risk bounds and transfer-distance analyses, but these remain largely theoretical, lacking practical algorithms to achieve the proposed bounds or precise performance evaluations that would enable hyperparameter tuning. This paper addresses that gap by deriving exact asymptotic training and testing risks for multi-task regression via random matrix theory, yielding data-dependent closed-form solutions for the regularization hyperparameters that balance multi-task benefit against negative transfer.

## Input / Output

The framework takes T tasks, each with a feature matrix X^(t) ∈ R^{d×n_t} and response matrix Y^(t) ∈ R^{q×n_t}, assuming feature vectors are concentrated random vectors (a generalization beyond Gaussian that covers Lipschitz transforms of real data). In the multivariate time series forecasting application, each of the T channels is treated as a separate task fed into a univariate architecture (PatchTST, DLinear, or Transformer), while a shared linear projection f_0 over the concatenated channel predictions captures cross-channel information at the final output layer. The method outputs per-task predicted response vectors and additionally produces closed-form optimal values for the regularization parameters λ and γ_t.

## Method

The core contribution is a regularized optimization problem that decomposes each task's weight matrix as W_t = W_0 + V_t (shared plus task-specific), with a global parameter λ controlling the shared component and per-task parameters γ_t controlling deviations. Using deterministic equivalents and concentration inequalities from random matrix theory under high-dimensional asymptotics (n/d → c_0), the paper derives closed-form asymptotic expressions for both training and test risk, decomposing the test risk into a signal term (amplified by cross-task covariance alignment) and a noise term (driving negative transfer). For the two-task identity-covariance case, this yields a closed-form optimal λ* = (n/d)·SNR − γ/2 that depends on raw data covariances, signal-generating hyperplanes, and noise levels. Practically, the method modifies the training loss of existing univariate forecasting models by adding a shared transformation f_0 with λ-weighted Frobenius regularization and per-task γ_t-weighted regularization on the univariate components, requiring no architectural changes beyond the final linear layer.

## Results

On multivariate time series forecasting benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather), adding MTL regularization to univariate models (PatchTST, DLinearU, Transformer) consistently reduces test MSE. For example, DLinearU+MTL achieves 0.267 MSE on ETTh2 (H=96) versus 0.294 for DLinearU without regularization, and 0.148 for PatchTST+MTL on Weather (H=96) versus 0.149 without. The MTL-regularized models match or surpass state-of-the-art multivariate models: DLinearU+MTL (0.267) beats both iTransformer (0.297) and SAMformer (0.295) on ETTh2 H=96, and PatchTST+MTL (0.148) beats iTransformer (0.174) and SAMformer (0.197) on Weather H=96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PatchTST without MTL regularization, the MTL-regularized version achieves lower MSE in 10 out of 12 dataset-horizon combinations (e.g., 0.430 vs 0.451 on ETTh1 H=720; 0.384 vs 0.393 on ETTh2 H=720), with statistically significant gains in most cases. Against DLinearU (univariate DLinear), MTL regularization yields consistent improvements (e.g., 0.267 vs 0.294 on ETTh2 H=96, a ~9% reduction), and also outperforms the multivariate DLinearM variant in most settings (e.g., 0.331 vs 0.477 on ETTh2 H=192). The univariate Transformer with MTL regularization beats its non-regularized counterpart in 9/12 cases and is competitive with SAMformer (the multivariate Transformer variant), outperforming it on ETTh2 H=720 (0.405 vs 0.391 is a loss, but 0.337 vs 0.340 on H=192 is a win). Against iTransformer, at least one MTL-regularized model outperforms it on every dataset-horizon combination (e.g., DLinearU+MTL 0.267 vs iTransformer 0.297 on ETTh2 H=96; PatchTST+MTL 0.148 vs iTransformer 0.174 on Weather H=96). Against SAMformer, MTL-regularized models win on most configurations but lose on ETTh1 H=336 (best MTL: 0.431 vs SAMformer 0.423) and ETTh1 H=720 (best MTL: 0.430 vs SAMformer 0.427).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random matrix theory (RMT) analysis of high-dimensional regression (Dobriban & Wager, Couillet & Liao) from single-task to multi-task settings, building directly on Tiomoko et al.'s RMT treatment of multi-task classification (Least Squares SVM, supervised PCA). The key departure is moving from broad risk bounds in transfer learning (contrasts, transfer distances) to precise asymptotic train/test risk expressions with a closed-form optimal regularization parameter λ, and providing consistent empirical estimators for the unknown quantities. The MTSF application repurposes the theoretical regularization as a practical loss modification for univariate neural architectures, a use case not explored in prior MTL-for-time-series work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first RMT-based precise performance estimation framework for multi-task regression with a ready-to-use hyperparameter optimization procedure, filling a gap where prior MTL theory offered only order-of-magnitude bounds. The finding that a simple shared-transformation regularization on the final linear layer of univariate models (PatchTST, DLinear) can match or exceed dedicated multivariate SOTA architectures (SAMformer, iTransformer) suggests that cross-channel information in MTSF may be more accessible than previously assumed. Limitations include the theory being strictly for linear models (non-linear application is heuristic via the final layer), the concentrated random vector assumption, and the oracle assumption on λ in the MTSF experiments. Open questions include extending the RMT analysis to deep non-linear architectures and to more complex task-dependence structures beyond the shared-plus-specific decomposition.
