## Problem

While safety-aligned chat models universally refuse harmful requests, the internal mechanisms underlying refusal remain poorly understood. This paper asks whether refusal can be reduced to a simple, low-dimensional representation, and if so, whether that representation can be surgically targeted to disable refusal without degrading other capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only 128 harmful instructions (from AdvBench, MaliciousInstruct, TDC2023) and 128 harmless instructions (from Alpaca) to extract the refusal direction—no harmful completions or gradient-based optimization are needed. It produces a single unit-norm direction vector in the model's residual stream space, and when applied as a rank-one weight edit, yields a modified model that retains its original capabilities while no longer refusing. The weight modification touches every matrix that writes to the residual stream (embedding, positional embedding, attention out-projections, MLP out-projections, and output biases).

## Method

The paper identifies the refusal direction via difference-in-means: for each post-instruction token position and layer, it computes the mean activation difference between harmful and harmless prompts, then selects the single most effective candidate by minimizing a bypass score (refusal metric under directional ablation on harmful prompts) subject to constraints on induction efficacy and KL divergence on harmless prompts. Directional ablation—projecting out the refusal direction from every residual stream activation at every layer and position—effectively disables refusal, while adding the direction to a single layer's activations induces refusal on harmless instructions. The key practical contribution is weight orthogonalization: by subtracting r̂r̂ᵀW from every matrix W that writes to the residual stream, the model is permanently prevented from ever representing the refusal direction, making the inference-time ablation equivalent to a one-time rank-one weight edit. A mechanistic analysis on Qwen 1.8B further shows that adversarial suffixes suppress the refusal direction by hijacking the attention of the top-contributing heads, shifting their focus from the harmful instruction tokens to the suffix tokens.

## Results

On HARMBENCH (159 standard behaviors), the proposed weight orthogonalization (ORTHO) method achieves attack success rates of 79.2–84.3% on QWEN models (7B/14B/72B) and 4.4–22.6% on LLAMA-2 models (7B/13B/70B) with default system prompts, rising to 61–80% without system prompts. Ablating the single refusal direction from residual stream activations reduces refusal rates from near 100% to near 0% on JAILBREAKBENCH across all 13 tested models. Model coherence is largely preserved: MMLU, ARC, and GSM8K scores shift by less than 1% on average, while TRUTHFULQA consistently drops by 1–3.5 points across all model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORTHO is a general (prompt-agnostic) jailbreak that modifies model weights once, and it is compared against both general and prompt-specific baselines. On QWEN models, ORTHO outperforms all general baselines: it beats GCG-M by 5.9–8.8 points, GCG-T by 31.4–41.6 points, HUMAN by 40.8–52.8 points, AP by 12.2–28.3 points, and PAIR by 19.5–25.8 points. On LLAMA-2 models, ORTHO is competitive with GCG-M (within 1–2 points) and clearly beats GCG-T, HUMAN, AP, and PAIR, though the prompt-specific GCG method still leads (e.g., 34.5% vs 22.6% on LLAMA-2 7B). Without the system prompt, ORTHO's ASR jumps to 61–80% on LLAMA-2, surpassing even prompt-specific GCG on LLAMA-2 7B (79.9% vs 34.5%). The paper also shows that ORTHO preserves model coherence far better than activation-addition-based steering, with CE loss increases of <0.05 nats on standard corpora versus >0.2 nats for activation addition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive-pair difference-in-means framework from Belrose (2023), Panickssery et al. (2023), and Zou et al. (2023a) by applying it specifically to the refusal behavior of chat models, showing that a single 1D direction mediates refusal across 13 diverse models. It departs from Zou et al. (2023a) and Zheng et al. (2024), who identified a 'harmfulness' direction and argued it was distinct from refusal; here the refusal direction is shown to be the actual causal mediator, and is significantly suppressed by adversarial suffixes (contradicting Zou et al.'s finding that the harmfulness representation is not altered). The weight orthogonalization technique is a novel practical contribution that converts an inference-time directional ablation into a permanent rank-one weight edit, requiring no gradient optimization or harmful completion examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single rank-one weight modification can nearly eliminate refusal across 13 models (1.8B–72B, multiple architectures, both APO and AFT alignment) while preserving general capabilities is a stark demonstration of the brittleness of current safety fine-tuning, with direct implications for the responsible release of open-source models. Key open questions include whether this single-direction structure generalizes to proprietary or future models, what the semantic content of the direction actually is (refusal vs. harm vs. danger), and whether understanding this mechanism can inform more robust alignment techniques. The adversarial suffix analysis (attention hijacking of specific heads) is preliminary, restricted to one model and one suffix, leaving the broader mechanism of suffix-based jailbreaks underexplored.
