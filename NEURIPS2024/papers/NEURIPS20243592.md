## Problem

Standard LoRA fine-tuning applies a single set of low-rank adaptation matrices uniformly across all user behavior sequences in LLM-based sequential recommendation, ignoring the substantial individual variability in user preferences and behavior patterns. This uniform treatment causes negative transfer between disparate sequences, as evidenced by misaligned gradient geometries across behaviorally distinct user groups. The paper reframes sequential recommendation as a multi-task learning problem and seeks a scalable way to customize LoRA per instance without deploying millions of separate modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's historical interaction sequence, item metadata rendered as text, and a holistic sequence representation extracted from a frozen sequential recommender (SASRec, 64-dim). These are assembled into hybrid prompts that concatenate LLM word-embedding tokens with behavioral tokens projected from the recommender's ID embeddings via a trainable cross-modal projector. The output is a single predicted next-item token generated autoregressively by the fine-tuned Llama-2-7B, evaluated via HitRatio@1 against a candidate set of 20 items.

## Method

iLoRA splits the standard LoRA up- and down-projection matrices (rank r) into K experts, each of rank r/K, so the total parameter count remains identical to vanilla LoRA. A gating network takes the SASRec sequence representation and produces softmax-normalized attention scores over the K experts, routing each instance to a personalized mixture. The instance-specific LoRA update is computed as a weighted sum of expert sub-matrices (ΔW(i<n) = Σ ω_k B_k A_k), which is then applied to the frozen Llama-2 transformer layers during instruction-tuning on hybrid prompts. This mechanism ensures that behaviorally similar sequences activate similar expert combinations while dissimilar sequences engage different experts, thereby mitigating negative transfer without increasing trainable parameters by more than 1% over standard LoRA.

## Results

iLoRA achieves HitRatio@1 scores of 0.5000, 0.5275, and 0.5264 on LastFM, MovieLens, and Steam respectively, with ValidRatio at or near 1.0 across all three datasets. This represents an average relative improvement of 11.4% over the closest LLM-based baseline (LLaRA) in HitRatio@1, while maintaining less than 1% relative increase in trainable parameters. The method also substantially outperforms traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, Caser, SASRec) by 20–90% in HitRatio@1, and far exceeds the unmodified Llama2-7B baseline (e.g., 0.5000 vs. 0.0246 on LastFM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iLoRA outperforms all seven baselines on HitRatio@1 across all three datasets. Against the strongest LLM-based competitor LLaRA (which uses uniform LoRA with hybrid prompting), iLoRA gains 8.5% on LastFM (0.5000 vs. 0.4508), 19.3% on MovieLens (0.5275 vs. 0.4421), and 6.4% on Steam (0.5264 vs. 0.4949). Over TALLRec, the second-best LLM-based method, improvements range from 13.5% (Steam) to 35.4% (MovieLens). Traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, Caser, SASRec) are surpassed by 20–124% in HitRatio@1, confirming that the LLM-based paradigm with instance-wise adaptation captures richer behavioral signals. The unmodified Llama2-7B baseline is dramatically outperformed (e.g., 20× on LastFM), highlighting the necessity of both fine-tuning and the MoE-based expert routing. Notably, iLoRA also achieves near-perfect ValidRatio (≥0.9891), indicating strong instruction-following capability comparable to or exceeding all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaRA pipeline (hybrid prompting + uniform LoRA fine-tuning on Llama-2 for sequential recommendation) by replacing the single shared LoRA module with an instance-specific one constructed via a Mixture-of-Experts decomposition of LoRA's up- and down-projection matrices. Conceptually, it reframes sequential recommendation as a multi-task learning problem where each user sequence is a distinct task, drawing on the gradient-conflict literature (Wang et al. 2021, 2023) to motivate why a uniform LoRA suffers negative transfer across heterogeneous behaviors. The gating mechanism that routes sequences to experts borrows directly from sparsely-gated MoE (Shazeer et al. 2017), but the routing signal is a dense sequence representation from a conventional recommender (SASRec) rather than the input tokens themselves, which is a notable departure from standard MoE designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides concrete gradient-similarity evidence that uniform LoRA fine-tuning in LLM-based recommenders suffers measurable negative transfer across behaviorally diverse sequences, and shows that a same-parameter-count MoE-LoRA can mitigate this with an 11.4% average HitRatio@1 gain. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets and a single base LLM (Llama-2-7B), and the authors acknowledge that hard routing, larger expert pools, and applicability beyond sequential recommendation remain unexplored. A key open question is whether the gating signal (a frozen SASRec representation) remains effective when the base LLM or the recommender backbone changes, and whether the 4-expert optimum generalizes to larger-scale deployments with millions of sequences.
