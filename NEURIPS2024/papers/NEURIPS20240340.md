## Problem

Existing 3D molecule generation methods rely on point clouds (processed by GNNs with quadratic scaling and limited expressivity) or discrete voxel grids (compatible with expressive architectures but scaling cubically with molecular volume). These limitations hinder scalability to larger molecules such as macrocyclic peptides. The paper asks whether a continuous, structure-agnostic representation can overcome both the expressivity and scalability bottlenecks simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240340/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from random noise in a 1024- or 2048-dimensional latent code space. During training, a 3D CNN encoder consumes low-resolution voxel grids (16×16×16 at 0.5–1.667 Å resolution) to produce the latent codes, while the neural field decoder evaluates atomic occupancy at arbitrary 3D coordinates. The final output is a set of atomic coordinates and types, extracted by rendering the field at 0.25 Å resolution, applying 3×3×3 peak detection, and refining coordinates via L-BFGS continuous optimization within a radius r around each detected peak.

## Method

FuncMol represents each molecule as a continuous atomic occupancy field v: R³→Rⁿ (one channel per atom type), parameterized by a conditional multiplicative filter network (MFN) with Gabor spatial bases and FiLM modulation by a per-molecule latent code z. A shared 3D CNN encoder maps low-resolution voxelized fields to these codes (auto-encoding), and the MFN decoder reconstructs the field at any coordinate, enabling training on free-form point samples with up-sampling near atom centers for efficiency. For generation, the model learns a single-level denoiser in the latent code space via neural empirical Bayes (Tweedie-Miyasawa relation), then samples clean codes using walk-jump sampling: Langevin MCMC (ABOBA discretization) walks through the Gaussian-smoothed code distribution, and a single denoising jump recovers the clean code, which is decoded into a molecule. This latent-space sampling strategy, combined with the continuous field representation, yields at least an order-of-magnitude speedup over point-cloud and voxel-based baselines while scaling to 74-atom macrocyclic peptides where voxel methods become infeasible.

## Results

On GEOM-drugs (the primary drug-like benchmark), FuncMol achieves 69.7% stable molecules, 100% validity, and 95.3% uniqueness, with a sampling time of 0.29 s/molecule — roughly 25× faster than VoxMol (7.55 s/mol) and 32× faster than EDM (9.35 s/mol). On QM9, FuncMol slightly improves over VoxMol on atom type TV (0.012 vs 0.029) and bond type TV (0.006 vs 0.009) while matching its stability. On CREMP (macrocyclic peptides, ~74 heavy atoms), FuncMol is the only method that can be trained at all: VoxMol requires ~10 hours per epoch on 4 A100 GPUs while FuncMol completes a neural field epoch in under 12 minutes, and generates a molecule in ~1.5 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the point-cloud diffusion baselines EDM and GeoLDM, FuncMol clearly wins on molecular stability (69.7% vs 40.3% and 57.9% stable molecules) and validity (100% vs 87.8% and 100%), while also producing molecules with far fewer multi-fragment artifacts (70.5% single-fragment vs 42.2% and 51.6%) and lower median strain energy (109.7 vs 951.3 and 461.5). Against the closest competitor VoxMol (also a score-based model using walk-jump sampling), FuncMol trades some quality for speed: VoxMol leads on stable molecules (75.0% vs 69.7%), atom type TV (0.033 vs 0.109), bond type TV (0.024 vs 0.052), and bond angle W₁ (0.64 vs 2.49), but FuncMol achieves 100% validity (vs 93.4%) and is ~25× faster to sample (0.29 vs 7.55 s/mol). On QM9, FuncMol slightly edges out VoxMol on atom and bond type distributions but both field-based methods trail the equivariant point-cloud models (EDM, GeoLDM) on most geometric metrics. The most striking comparison is on CREMP: VoxMol simply cannot be trained (10 h/epoch on 4 A100s), while FuncMol trains in minutes and generates macrocyclic peptides in ~1.5 s, demonstrating a clear scalability advantage for larger molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuncMol combines two previously separate lines of work: the walk-jump sampling (WJS) / neural empirical Bayes generative framework (Saremi & Hyvärinen, 2019; already used in VoxMol for voxel grids) and the neural field / implicit neural representation paradigm from 3D computer vision (MFNs, FiLM conditioning, Gabor basis functions). The key conceptual departure from prior 3D molecule generation is replacing discrete representations (point clouds in EDM/GeoLDM, voxel grids in VoxMol) with a continuous atomic occupancy field parameterized by a shared conditional neural network modulated by per-molecule latent codes, following the 'data-to-functa' idea of Dupont et al. (2022). Unlike VoxMol, which operates on fixed-resolution 3D grids whose cost scales cubically with molecular volume, the neural field representation is resolution-agnostic and scales linearly with the number of atoms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is scalability: FuncMol trains on macrocyclic peptides (CREMP, ~74 heavy atoms) in under 12 minutes per epoch where VoxMol required ~10 hours per epoch on 4 A100 GPUs and could not be trained to convergence, and samples molecules ~10× faster than point-cloud or voxel baselines. This opens the door to all-atom generative modeling of larger biomolecules (peptides, potentially proteins) that were previously intractable for score-based approaches. Limitations include competitive-but-not-clearly-superior quality on GEOM-drugs (atom TV and bond TV are worse than VoxMol), the absence of explicit bond modeling, and the fact that generation remains unconditional. Open questions include whether the field representation can be extended to conditional generation (protein pockets, fragments) and whether modeling bonds as additional field channels closes the remaining quality gap.
