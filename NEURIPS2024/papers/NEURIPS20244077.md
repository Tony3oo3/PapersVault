## Problem

Existing contrastive image-to-LiDAR distillation methods suffer from a 'self-conflict' dilemma: pixels and points that share the same semantic label but do not directly correspond are treated as negative samples, causing the contrastive loss to push apart semantically similar features and degrade learned representations. Additionally, conventional point-pixel pair sampling ignores significant category imbalances (e.g., bicycles at 1.47% vs. drivable surfaces at 37.66% in nuScenes) and non-uniform spatial point densities, leading to biased and unbalanced 3D representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds (N points in R³, voxelized at 0.1 m resolution over a 102.4 m × 102.4 m × 8 m range) and multi-view RGB images (e.g., 6 cameras at 416×224 after upsampling), along with text prompts fed to an off-the-shelf VFM (SAM, Grounded-SAM, or SEEM) to produce per-pixel weak semantic labels across 16 categories. The output is pre-trained 3D backbone weights (SR-UNet34 for segmentation, VoxelNet for detection) that are subsequently fine-tuned with a lightweight segmentation head or detection head (SECOND, PV-RCNN) on small fractions (1%–100%) of annotated data. Two decoupled projection heads operate in parallel: one for point-pixel-level InfoNCE distillation and another for category-level supervised contrastive distillation, both mapping features to a shared 64-dimensional L₂-normalized space.

## Method

OLIVINE addresses self-conflict by generating weak semantic labels via VFMs and redefining positive pairs in the contrastive loss as all point-pixel features sharing the same label (not just direct correspondences), using a dedicated set of projection heads (h_sem) separate from the point-pixel heads (h_pp). To stabilize class representations across batches, it models each class's point features as a von Mises-Fisher distribution on the unit hypersphere, updated via exponential moving average, and regularizes individual point features toward their class distribution through a KL-divergence loss between a Dirac delta and the vMF. A density-and-category-aware sampling strategy computes each point's sampling probability as inversely proportional to both its kernel-density-estimated spatial density and its category occurrence frequency, ensuring minority classes and sparse regions receive adequate representation. The total pre-training objective combines three terms: L = λ₁·L_PPNCE + λ₂·L_sup + λ₃·L_kl, where L_PPNCE is the standard point-pixel InfoNCE loss, L_sup is the weakly-supervised contrastive loss, and L_kl is the vMF-based consistency regularization.

## Results

On nuScenes-lidarseg, OLIVINE achieves 50.09% mIoU under linear probing, surpassing the previous best (Seal, 44.95%) by over 5 points. With only 1% of annotated data for fine-tuning, it reaches 50.58% mIoU on nuScenes and 49.38% on SemanticKITTI, outperforming Seal by 4.74% and 2.75% respectively. On 3D object detection (KITTI, moderate difficulty), OLIVINE achieves 68.3% mAP@40 with SECOND and 72.8% with PV-RCNN, the best among all pre-training initializations tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLIVINE consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor Seal [38] (NeurIPS'24), OLIVINE gains +5.14% mIoU on nuScenes linear probing, +4.74% at 1% fine-tuning, and +2.75% on SemanticKITTI 1%. The gap over PPKT [39] is even larger: +14.19% on nuScenes LP and +12.78% at 1% fine-tuning, demonstrating the substantial benefit of weakly-supervised semantic labels over purely self-supervised contrastive distillation. SLidR [50] and ST-SLidR [41] show progressively smaller gaps (e.g., +11.29% and +9.60% over SLidR and ST-SLidR respectively on nuScenes LP), confirming that superpixel-based clustering and semantically tolerant losses partially but incompletely address the self-conflict problem. On 3D object detection with PV-RCNN, OLIVINE achieves 72.8% mAP@40 versus 71.9% for SLidR and 70.8% for PPKT, with the largest per-class gain on pedestrians (+2.0 over SLidR). PointContrast [66] and DepthContrast [80], which are purely 3D self-supervised methods without image-to-LiDAR transfer, trail significantly (21.90% and 22.10% on nuScenes LP), underscoring the value of cross-modal distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLIVINE extends the image-to-LiDAR contrastive distillation lineage (PPKT → SLidR → ST-SLidR → Seal) by fundamentally redefining the positive/negative pair structure: instead of treating all non-corresponding pixels as negatives (the 'self-conflict' problem), it uses off-the-shelf VFMs (SAM, SEEM, Grounded-SAM) to generate weak semantic labels that identify same-class pixels as positives in a supervised contrastive loss. This departs from Seal's use of VFMs for superpixel generation and from ST-SLidR's heuristic semantic tolerance, replacing both with explicit label-based supervision. The method further introduces a von Mises-Fisher distribution for semantic consistency regularization and a KDE-based density/category-aware sampling strategy, neither of which has appeared in prior contrastive distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the self-conflict problem in contrastive distillation is not merely a tuning issue but a structural flaw that VFMs can address at near-zero annotation cost, yielding 5+ mIoU gains over the previous SOTA (Seal) on linear probing and consistent improvements across 10+ datasets. A key open question is the large gap between weak VFM labels (46.12) and ground-truth labels (57.72) on nuScenes 1% fine-tuning, suggesting that stronger or open-vocabulary VFMs could unlock substantially more performance. The method is pre-training-only (no inference overhead), making it practically deployable, but its dependence on VFM quality and the need for synchronized multi-view images with LiDAR limits applicability to settings where such paired data is unavailable.
