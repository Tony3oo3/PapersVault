## Problem

Existing Image Copy Detection (ICD) methods are trained on hand-crafted transformations (flips, rotations, crops) and fail to detect the fundamentally different tamper patterns produced by diffusion models, which the authors term a 'pattern gap.' No prior ICD dataset or method was designed to handle diffusion-generated replication, leaving a critical blind spot as diffusion models increasingly replicate content from existing images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 224×224 RGB images — a real image and a diffusion-generated replica — and produces a probability distribution over six discrete replication levels (0 = no replication to 5 = total replication). Rather than outputting a single similarity score, the model generates six feature vectors per image, and the cosine similarities between corresponding vectors yield six probability scores whose argmax determines the predicted replication level. The D-Rep dataset provides 40,000 manually annotated pairs with these six-level labels, split 90/10 for training and testing.

## Method

PDF-Embedding converts each ground-truth replication level into a probability density function (Gaussian, linear, or exponential) centered at the normalized level, encoding the intuition that neighboring levels should have smoothly varying probabilities. A ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet via DeiT) represents each image as a set of six class-token vectors rather than a single embedding, one vector per replication level. Training minimizes KL divergence between the target PDF and the predicted PDF formed by pairwise cosine similarities between corresponding vectors. At inference, the predicted level is the argmax over the six similarity scores. This distributional supervision outperforms protocol-driven objectives (directly optimizing PCC or RD), one-hot labels, and label smoothing, while adding only ~5.8% training overhead.

## Results

On the D-Rep test set (4,000 image-replica pairs), PDF-Embedding with an Exponential PDF achieves 56.3% PCC and 25.6% RD, substantially outperforming all publicly available models. The best public model, GPT-4V Turbo, reaches only 47.3% PCC and 38.7% RD, while the strongest self-supervised model (DINOv2) achieves 39.0% PCC and 32.9% RD. Among existing ICD models, the best (BoT) scores just 35.6% PCC and 53.8% RD, confirming that hand-crafted-transformation-based ICD methods are poorly suited to diffusion-generated replicas. The method also generalizes well across six different diffusion models (SD1.5, Midjourney, DALL-E 2, DeepFloyd IF, New Bing, SDXL, GLIDE), with average similarity scores of 0.77–0.83 on manually confirmed high-replication pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF-Embedding (Exponential PDF: 56.3% PCC, 25.6% RD) outperforms every baseline on both metrics. Against the strongest vision-language model GPT-4V (47.3% PCC, 38.7% RD), it gains +9.0% PCC and reduces RD by 13.1 points. Against the best self-supervised model DINOv2 (39.0% PCC, 32.9% RD), the improvement is +17.3% PCC and −7.3% RD. Among existing ICD models, the gap is even larger: SSCD (29.1% PCC, 62.3% RD) and BoT (35.6% PCC, 53.8% RD) are surpassed by +27.2%/+20.7% PCC and −36.7%/−28.2% RD respectively, confirming that invariance-to-transformation training is fundamentally mismatched to diffusion-generated replication. Among protocol-driven training objectives on D-Rep, 'Enlarging PCC' reaches 54.4% PCC but 40.1% RD, while 'Reducing RD' achieves 29.9% RD but only 15.1% PCC; PDF-Embedding dominates both (56.3% PCC, 25.6% RD). Compared to non-PDF supervision (One-hot Label: 37.6% PCC, 43.3% RD; Label Smoothing: 35.0% PCC, 36.1% RD), the PDF formulation yields +18.7% PCC and −17.7% RD over One-hot, validating the smoothness prior over neighboring replication levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established ICD paradigm (SSCD, BoT, CNNCL, EfNet) that learns invariance to hand-crafted transformations like flips and rotations, arguing that diffusion-generated replicas follow fundamentally different tampering patterns that these methods cannot capture. It also diverges from prior replication-detection studies (e.g., Somepalli et al. [10], Carlini et al. [22]) that repurpose off-the-shelf descriptors like CLIP or SSCD as feature extractors, instead training a specialized model on a purpose-built dataset. Methodologically, PDF-Embedding repurposes the ViT architecture but introduces a set-of-vectors representation and KL-divergence-based PDF supervision, moving away from the single-embedding-plus-similarity-score formulation standard in both ICD and metric learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The D-Rep benchmark and the finding that 10–20% of images from major diffusion models replicate existing gallery content provide the first systematic, quantified evidence of the replication problem at scale, which is critical for both copyright policy and model developers. The paper's failure-case analysis (common elements vs. true replication, semantic similarity without copying) highlights that the task is far from solved and that current detection remains imprecise. Open questions include whether the 6-level annotation scheme generalizes across domains, how detection scales to billion-image galleries, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D diffusion models. The relatively modest performance of even the best public models (GPT-4V at 47.3% PCC) underscores that this is an early-stage problem with substantial room for improvement.
