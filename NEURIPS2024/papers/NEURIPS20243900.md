## Problem

Loss-based active learning, which selects data points for labeling based on their predicted loss under the current model, has gained practical traction but lacked theoretical convergence guarantees. While uncertainty-based sampling (e.g., margin of confidence) had recent convergence results, no prior work established non-asymptotic convergence rates for SGD with loss-based point sampling, nor provided bounds on the expected number of labels acquired. This paper fills that gap by deriving convergence rate conditions for both loss-based and uncertainty-based sampling strategies under various step-size regimes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of unlabeled feature vectors x_t and, at each step, decides whether to acquire the corresponding label y_t based on a sampling probability function π that depends on the current model parameters and an estimated loss. In the active learning setting, the true loss is unavailable at sampling time, so a separate loss estimator (e.g., a Random Forest regressor) provides an unbiased estimate of the conditional expected loss. The output is a trained model parameter θ_n together with theoretical bounds on the expected average loss (O(1/n) for AWS, O(1/√n) for constant-step-size SGD) and the expected total number of sampled points (O(√n) for the linearly separable case).

## Method

The paper proposes Adaptive-Weight Sampling (AWS), which combines Bernoulli sampling with an adaptive step size: at each step, the algorithm samples a point with probability π(x,y,θ) and, if sampled, takes an SGD step of size ζ(x,y,θ)/π(x,y,θ), where ζ is the stochastic Polyak's step size (ratio of loss gap to squared gradient norm). This re-weighting ensures the expected step size equals the stochastic Polyak's step size, yielding an O(1/n) convergence rate for smooth convex losses under a condition linking π to the gradient-to-loss ratio. For constant step size, the paper establishes O(1/n) rates for linear classifiers on linearly separable data via a convergence rate lemma, and O(Π^{-1}(1/√n)) rates for general classifiers by showing the sampled SGD is equivalent to standard SGD on a transformed 'equivalent loss' function Π(ℓ). The sampling probability π can be loss-based (proportional to absolute error) or uncertainty-based (e.g., inverse of prediction confidence), and the framework extends to multi-class classification.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical convergence rate guarantees: O(1/n) for constant-step-size SGD with loss-based sampling on linearly separable data (Theorem 3.1), O(Π⁻¹(1/√n)) for general classifiers with smooth convex losses (Theorem 3.3), and O(1/n) for the proposed Adaptive-Weight Sampling (AWS) algorithm (Theorem 3.6). Empirically, AWS-PA (AWS with stochastic Polyak's step size and absolute-error-loss-based sampling) achieves faster convergence in average cross-entropy progressive loss than both random sampling and constant-step-size loss-based sampling across 5 of 6 benchmark datasets (Mushroom, MNIST 3-vs-5, Parkinsons, Splice, Tic-Tac-Toe, Credit). AWS-PA with a Random Forest loss estimator performs on par with the oracle version using true loss values, confirming robustness to estimation noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AWS-PA against two baselines: (1) random sampling and (2) loss-based sampling with a constant step size (the approach of Yoo and Kweon, CVPR 2019). AWS-PA outperforms the constant-step-size loss-based baseline in average cross-entropy progressive loss on most of the six datasets, demonstrating that the adaptive stochastic Polyak's step size yields faster convergence. The constant-step-size loss-based method in turn outperforms random sampling on five of the six datasets, confirming the value of loss-informed sampling over uniform sampling. All comparisons are conducted under hyper-parameter tuning that ensures comparable data sampling rates across algorithms, isolating the effect of the step-size strategy. Additionally, AWS-PA with estimated absolute error losses (via a Random Forest regressor retrained at each step) achieves convergence curves nearly identical to those using ground-truth losses, showing the method is robust to the bias and noise inherent in practical loss estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) stochastic Polyak step size for SGD (Loizou et al., 2021), which the authors extend by introducing a sampling component that preserves the Polyak step size in expectation; (2) the equivalent loss framework of Liu and Li (2023), which they generalize from proportional-to-loss sampling to any increasing function of conditional expected loss; and (3) the convergence analysis of uncertainty-based active learning by Raj and Bach (2022), whose conditions are subsumed by the paper's more general convergence rate lemma. The key departure from prior work is the combination of loss-based sampling with adaptive step sizes, yielding the novel Adaptive-Weight Sampling (AWS) algorithm, whereas prior convergence results for loss-based active learning (Yoo and Kweon, 2019) provided no theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first non-asymptotic convergence rate guarantees for loss-based active learning with SGD, filling a notable gap in the theoretical understanding of a strategy now deployed at industrial scale (e.g., Meta's integrity violation classifiers). The O(1/n) rate for AWS is a meaningful improvement over the O(1/√n) rate for constant-step-size loss-based sampling, and the empirical demonstration that noisy loss estimators do not degrade performance is practically reassuring. However, the analysis is restricted to convex losses and linear classifiers, and the empirical validation uses only small tabular datasets and binary MNIST, leaving open whether the guarantees and practical gains extend to deep non-convex models. The tightness of the convergence bounds and the theoretical treatment of biased loss estimators remain explicitly open problems.
