## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting has revolutionized real-time 3D reconstruction for perspective cameras, yet it has barely been extended to omnidirectional (360-degree) images because directly applying a perspective rasterizer to equirectangular images introduces severe geometric distortion. Existing NeRF-based omnidirectional reconstruction methods achieve reasonable quality but require over a second per frame to render, making real-time interaction impractical. The paper addresses this gap by designing a geometrically correct and fully differentiable omnidirectional rasterizer that enables 3DGS to operate natively in the equirectangular domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple equirectangular omnidirectional images (resolutions ranging from 1920×960 to 3840×1920, typically resized to half) along with a sparse point cloud and camera poses estimated via OpenMVG structure-from-motion. It produces an optimized set of 3D anisotropic Gaussian splats (position, color, scale, rotation, opacity) that can render novel omnidirectional views at approximately 0.026–0.032 seconds per frame on a single RTX A6000 GPU. Unlike perspective-based 3DGS which requires cubemap decomposition into six separate views, ODGS processes the full 360-degree image as a single unit, avoiding seam artifacts at cube-face boundaries.

## Method

The core contribution is a CUDA rasterizer that projects each 3D Gaussian onto a tangent plane of the unit sphere at the point where the ray from the camera origin to the Gaussian center intersects the sphere. A rotation matrix T_μ aligns the local perspective camera (unit focal length) with the Gaussian direction, simplifying the Jacobian to a diagonal scaling by 1/‖μ‖. The resulting 2D covariance on the tangent plane is then mapped to the equirectangular pixel space by applying a horizontal sec(θ) distortion factor (accounting for the cylindrical unrolling of the sphere) and a linear rescaling to pixel coordinates, yielding the final Jacobian J_omni = S_o Q_o J_o T_μ, which is verified by direct differentiation of the equirectangular projection function. A dynamic densification policy modulates the gradient threshold τ_μ = τ_min + (1−cos θ_μ)(τ_max − τ_min) to prevent excessive splitting of Gaussians near the poles where equirectangular stretching inflates apparent size. The entire pipeline is fully differentiable and parallelized in CUDA, achieving roughly 100× faster optimization and rendering compared to NeRF-based baselines while producing the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across six egocentric and roaming datasets.

## Results

ODGS achieves the best reconstruction quality across all six datasets (OmniBlender, Ricoh360, OmniPhotos, 360Roam, OmniScenes, 360VO) on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS at both 10-minute and 100-minute optimization budgets. At 100 minutes, ODGS reaches 33.05 dB PSNR / 0.0343 LPIPS on OmniBlender, 26.27 dB / 0.1051 on Ricoh360, and 26.68 dB / 0.1264 on 360VO, consistently outperforming the strongest NeRF-based baseline (EgoNeRF) by 1–3 dB PSNR and 0.1–0.25 LPIPS. Rendering speed is approximately 100× faster than NeRF-based methods (0.026–0.032 s per frame vs. 23–63 s), enabling real-time novel-view synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODGS outperforms all four baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across nearly all dataset–metric combinations. Against the strongest NeRF-based method, EgoNeRF, ODGS gains approximately 1–3 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.1–0.25 (e.g., 33.05 vs. 30.89 dB on OmniBlender; 26.68 vs. 23.78 dB on 360VO), while rendering ~800× faster (0.028 s vs. 23.78 s). Compared to 3DGS(P) (cubemap-decomposed perspective 3DGS), ODGS wins on all metrics; notably, 3DGS(P) suffers severe overfitting after 100 minutes (PSNR drops from 29.36 to 21.19 on OmniBlender), whereas ODGS continues to improve. TensoRF, which uses a Cartesian tensor grid, degrades significantly on large roaming scenes (e.g., 18.12 dB on 360Roam vs. ODGS's 20.85 dB), confirming that Cartesian coordinates are ill-suited for omnidirectional rays. NeRF(P) is the weakest baseline across all settings, with PSNR values 5–15 dB below ODGS, and requires ~62 s per frame for rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from perspective cameras to omnidirectional cameras by designing a tangent-plane projection rasterizer that handles the non-linear distortion of equirectangular projection. It departs from NeRF-based omnidirectional reconstruction (EgoNeRF, TensoRF) by replacing implicit neural radiance fields with explicit Gaussian primitives, and from the naive cubemap-decomposition approach (applying standard 3DGS to six perspective views) by performing rasterization natively in the omnidirectional domain. The dynamic densification policy, which scales the gradient threshold by elevation angle, is a targeted adaptation not present in the original 3DGS pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ODGS makes real-time 3D scene reconstruction from 360-degree content practical, achieving ~100× faster optimization and rendering than NeRF-based methods while matching or exceeding their quality. The finding that cubemap-decomposed 3DGS overfits severely after 100 minutes (degrading below its 10-minute performance) highlights a non-obvious failure mode of the naive perspective-decomposition strategy. The remaining limitation is the local affine approximation of the spherical projection, which produces artifacts for large Gaussians; adopting a more accurate distribution for spherically projected Gaussians is identified as the key direction for future improvement.
