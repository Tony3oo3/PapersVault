## Problem

Large-scale, high-quality TTS training data for Indian languages is severely limited: existing datasets cover at most 14 of 22 scheduled languages with only 1–2 speakers per language and predominantly read-speech, hindering zero-shot speaker and style generalization. Web-mined data is scarce and low-quality for these languages, and the available ASR corpora suffer from background noise, reverb, and low sampling rates that make them unsuitable for TTS training without substantial restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The TTS system consumes Indian-language text in grapheme form (no phonemizers exist for all 22 languages) along with a reference speech clip for speaker conditioning, and outputs synthesized speech at 44.1 kHz. The released dataset provides 690K speech-text pairs with per-utterance metadata (SNR, C50, pitch mean/std, speaking rate, gender, age group, scenario type), while the benchmark holds out 352 zero-shot speakers and stratifies few-shot/many-shot splits by gender, age group, and training duration to probe cross-speaker generalization.

## Method

The paper builds IndicVoices-R by applying a three-stage speech enhancement cascade to 44.1 kHz IndicVoices recordings: HTDemucs for source separation and noise removal, VoiceFixer for dereverberation, and DeepFilterNet3 for eliminating residual digital artifacts, followed by strict filtering on C50 ≥ 30 dB, SNR ≥ 25 dB, pitch, and speaking-rate thresholds. The key insight is that English-pretrained enhancement models generalize cross-lingually to Indian speech without degrading intelligibility. For the TTS model, they fine-tune the 830M-parameter VoiceCraft Transformer (a codec-token language model with delayed stacking) on the enhanced data, extending its tokenizer from 100 to 1089 grapheme tokens across all 22 languages and training for 50K+ steps on 8× A100 GPUs. This yields 1,704 hours from 10,496 speakers, and the resulting model achieves 88.18% speaker similarity on the zero-shot benchmark versus 78.93% for a model fine-tuned on the smaller IndicTTS corpus.

## Results

On the zero-shot speaker generalization benchmark, VoiceCraft fine-tuned on IndicVoices-R (IV-R) achieves a speaker similarity (S-SIM) of 88.18, substantially outperforming the same model fine-tuned on IndicTTS (78.93), while maintaining a competitive N-MOS of 3.64 versus 3.83. The IV-R dataset itself comprises 1,704 hours of speech from 10,496 speakers across 22 Indian languages, with quality metrics (SNR 60.47 dB, C50 53.45 dB) comparable to gold-standard TTS datasets like LJSpeech and LibriTTS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental comparison pits VoiceCraft fine-tuned on IV-R against VoiceCraft fine-tuned on IndicTTS. IV-R wins decisively on speaker similarity (88.18 vs. 78.93, a +9.25 point gain), demonstrating that the larger, more diverse speaker pool in IV-R (10,496 speakers vs. 27) translates directly into better zero-shot speaker generalization. On N-MOS, the IndicTTS model edges out IV-R by 0.19 (3.83 vs. 3.64), likely reflecting the studio-quality advantage of IndicTTS recordings over the restored ASR-quality audio in IV-R. In the dataset-level comparison (Table 1), IV-R trails LJSpeech (N-MOS 4.36), IndicTTS (4.29), and LIMMITS (4.30) on N-MOS but surpasses all of them in speaker count (10,496 vs. 1–2,456), total hours (1,704 vs. 24–585), and language coverage (22 vs. 1–14). Its SNR (60.47 dB) is competitive with LJSpeech (58.03) and LibriTTS (59.60), while its C50 (53.45 dB) is slightly below studio-recorded datasets (57–60 dB) but on par with Google-CS (53.24 dB), confirming that the restoration pipeline closes most of the quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper follows the LibriTTS-R paradigm of restoring ASR-quality audio into TTS-grade data, but applies it to Indian languages by chaining three English-pretrained speech enhancement models (HTDemucs, VoiceFixer, DeepFilterNet3) and demonstrating their cross-lingual generalization without degrading intelligibility. For the TTS side, it extends VoiceCraft—an English zero-shot TTS model trained on GigaSpeech—by expanding its tokenizer to 1,089 grapheme tokens across 22 Indian languages and fine-tuning on the restored corpus. The benchmark design draws on the zero/few/many-shot speaker-generalization evaluation framework established for English TTS (e.g., NaturalSpeech 3, VoiceCraft) but is the first to operationalize it with demographic stratification (age, gender) for Indian voices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IV-R is the first TTS dataset covering all 22 scheduled Indian languages with over 10,000 speakers, closing a critical data gap that has kept Indian TTS far behind English in scale and speaker diversity. The accompanying benchmark provides the community's first standardized way to measure cross-speaker generalization across demographics, which was previously absent. Key limitations temper the impact: the N-MOS (3.38) remains below studio-recorded datasets (4.29–4.36), the 8 kHz conversational subset could not be restored, and the single-model (VoiceCraft) evaluation leaves open whether the quality gap translates into perceptual degradation at scale. Future work could explore whether the pipeline generalizes to even lower-fidelity sources or whether the quality ceiling constrains downstream TTS naturalness.
