## Problem

Classical extended Kalman filter (EKF) inference in state-space models requires O(n³) matrix inversion per step and precise knowledge of underlying dynamics, making it impractical for high-dimensional, nonlinear, and unknown-dynamics settings. Existing deep learning approaches (VAEs, RKN, CRU) either lack the structured filtering-smoothing machinery for uncertainty-aware state estimation or still rely on expensive covariance operations. The paper asks: can one perform scalable, uncertainty-aware Bayesian inference in SSMs with entirely unknown nonlinear dynamics, without matrix inversion?

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frame sequences (24×24 grayscale for pendulum/polygon experiments; higher-dimensional RGB for KITTI odometry) distorted with time-correlated noise, and produces either latent state estimates (joint positions, velocities, accelerations) or imputed/denoised images depending on the task. A key design choice is the disentanglement: an encoder maps raw observations o_t to a lower-dimensional transferred observation w_t ∈ R^m, while the latent state x_t ∈ R^n (with n = 2m or 3m) captures dynamics. For imputation, half the frames are randomly removed and the model reconstructs them; for state estimation, the decoder outputs a Gaussian over physical states. On KITTI, a pretrained feature extractor (Zhou et al.) supplies the transferred observations rather than a learned encoder.

## Method

GIN structures inference as a Hammerstein-Wiener model: a nonlinear encoder/decoder wraps a linearized Gaussian SSM whose transition and emission matrices are learned as K state-dependent mixtures (via a softmax Dynamic Net), analogous to switching linear dynamical systems. The critical architectural innovation replaces the EKF's matrix inversion with compact GRU cells that directly parameterize the Kalman Gain and Smoothing Gain, reducing per-step cost from O(n³) to O(β²n²) where β<1 is a convolutional pooling ratio that exploits covariance sparsity. Training is supervised, maximizing a Gaussian likelihood over states or a Bernoulli likelihood over image pixels, with a Wishart prior on covariance matrices that asymptotically shrinks uncertainty toward zero. To prevent gradient explosion from the GRU's nonlinear dynamics, the paper proves (Theorem 3) that local stability at the zero fixed point is guaranteed when the largest singular value of the hidden-state weight matrix satisfies σ₁(U_h)<2, and enforces this via SVD projection or a cheaper Gershgorin-circle-based diagonal shift after each SGD step. A KL-divergence-based loss on the K transition matrices prevents mode collapse by encouraging each matrix to capture a distinct dynamical regime.

## Results

On state estimation, GIN (smooth, n=3m) achieves the lowest squared error across all three tasks: 0.069/0.073/0.075/0.067 (double pendulum joints), 0.049/0.047 (single pendulum), and 0.154/0.147 (polygon ball position), with log-likelihoods of 4.977, 7.445, and 4.342 respectively. On image imputation, GIN (uninformed smooth) attains log-likelihoods of −11.246 (single pendulum), −12.651 (double pendulum), and −14.923 (polygon), outperforming the next-best CRU (−12.554, −13.739, −15.972). On KITTI visual odometry, GIN achieves mean trel=7.03% and rrel=2.75°, beating LGSSM (7.55%/3.28°), AE-RNN (10.53%/3.87%), and KVAE (12.53%/5.63%), though DeepVO has lower trel (5.96%) at the cost of much higher rrel (6.12°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIN (smooth, n=3m) outperforms all 12 baselines on state estimation across all three environments. Against the strongest recurrent baselines, GIN reduces squared error by ~41% over CRU (0.069 vs 0.117 on double pendulum s1) and ~48% over RKN (0.069 vs 0.134), while also achieving higher log-likelihood (4.977 vs 4.269 and 4.176). On the polygon task with switching dynamics, GIN's advantage is most dramatic: SE of 0.154/0.147 vs CRU's 0.294/0.378 and LGSSM's 0.327/0.317, reflecting its ability to handle multiple dynamic regimes via learned switching matrices. On image imputation, GIN (uninformed) beats CRU by ~1.3 nats on single pendulum (−11.246 vs −12.554) and ~1.0 nats on polygon (−14.923 vs −15.972), and substantially outperforms VAE-based methods (e.g., VAE at −36.751). On KITTI visual odometry, GIN achieves the best rrel (2.75°) among all methods, beating LGSSM (3.28°) and AE-RNN (3.87°), though DeepVO achieves lower trel (5.96% vs 7.03%) at the expense of nearly 2× worse rrel (6.12° vs 2.75°). KVAE degrades substantially on KITTI (trel 12.53%) due to the need to reduce observation dimensionality to avoid matrix inversion, a limitation GIN avoids through its GRU-based gain approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

The GIN extends the classical EKF/Kalman smoother framework (Ljung [8], Deisenroth [33]) by replacing the O(n³) matrix inversion in the Kalman Gain and Smoothing Gain computations with compact GRU cells, reducing per-step cost to O(β²n²). It departs from model-based deep EKF approaches (BackpropKF [12], SIN [13]) and variational SSM methods (KVAE [19], EKVAE [21], RKN [24], CRU [25]) by operating entirely without known dynamics, learning transition and emission matrices end-to-end via a state-dependent Dynamic Net. The architecture is framed as a Hammerstein-Wiener model [34] with encoder-decoder transfer functions, and the multi-dynamics handling draws on switching linear dynamical systems [9], while the novel Lyapunov-stability constraint on GRU hidden-state weights (σ₁(U_h) < 2) has no direct precedent in prior SSM inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GIN offers a principled, computationally efficient alternative to EKF for high-dimensional state-space inference where dynamics are unknown, with a theoretical stability guarantee (Theorem 3) that directly addresses the gradient explosion problem plaguing RNN-based SSMs. However, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: the Wishart prior on covariance matrices forces them toward zero asymptotically, which constrains posterior uncertainty and limits the model's ability to maintain meaningful uncertainty estimates over long horizons. Open questions include whether the stability constraint generalizes beyond the zero-input fixed-point analysis, how the method scales to very long sequences, and whether the mode-collapse KL term can be replaced by more principled diversity objectives.
