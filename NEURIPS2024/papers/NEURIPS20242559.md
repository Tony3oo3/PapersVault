## Problem

Despite the proliferation of parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) algorithms for vision, no unified benchmark exists that enables fair, consistent comparison across diverse CV tasks. Existing benchmarks like ZhiJian cover only image classification with a handful of algorithms, lack open-sourced hyperparameters and training logs, and do not extend to video action recognition or dense prediction. Additionally, the field lacks a single metric that jointly captures both task performance and trainable parameter count.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB images (for classification, segmentation, and detection) and video clips (for action recognition) fed into pre-trained ViT-B/L/H or Swin-B/L backbones, with only a small subset of parameters (typically <1% of the full model) being updated. Outputs span class labels (24 image recognition datasets ranging from 800 to 240k training samples), action labels (Kinetics-400, SSv2, HMDB51), segmentation masks (COCO instance, ADE20K semantic), and bounding boxes (PASCAL VOC detection). The VTAB subset uses a fixed 800/200 train/val split per dataset, while FGVC datasets use their native splits or a 90/10 random split when no validation set is provided.

## Method

V-PETL Bench systematically implements 25 PETL algorithms (spanning 7 categories: adapter-based, prompt-based, low-rank, side-tuning, etc.) plus Full fine-tuning and Frozen baselines on 30 datasets across image recognition, video action recognition, and dense prediction, requiring ~310 A800 GPU days. The codebase is organized into four layers (Core, Algorithm, Extension, API) for modularity and extensibility, with all hyperparameters, configuration files, and model checkpoints open-sourced. The authors propose the Performance-Parameter Trade-off (PPT) metric, defined as M_t × exp(−log₁₀(P_M/C + 1)), which combines task performance with a logarithmic penalty on trainable parameters (normalized by C=10⁷) into a single comparable score. Key findings include: PETL methods match or exceed Full fine-tuning on data-limited tasks, NLP-origin algorithms (Adapter, LoRA, BitFit) degrade when applied to vision without task-specific design, and pre-training/downstream task similarity is a dominant factor in transfer success.

## Results

On FGVC (5 fine-grained datasets, ViT-B/16), GPS achieves the highest mean accuracy of 91.78% with only 0.66M trainable parameters (PPT 0.90), outperforming Full fine-tuning (88.54%, 85.8M params). On VTAB (19 diverse datasets), HST reaches 75.99% mean accuracy (0.78M params, PPT 0.74) and DTL reaches 74.58% (0.04M params, PPT 0.75), both substantially exceeding Full fine-tuning's 65.57%. On dense prediction, Mona is the only PETL method to surpass Full fine-tuning on COCO (53.4% vs 51.9% APBox), while on PASCAL VOC all PETL methods beat Full fine-tuning (e.g., LN TUNE: 85.8% vs 83.5% APBox). Across all 30 datasets, most PETL algorithms update less than 1% of pre-trained parameters while matching or exceeding Full fine-tuning performance, especially under data-limited settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FGVC, GPS (91.78%) and SNF (90.74%) clearly outperform Full fine-tuning (88.54%), while NLP-origin methods like LoRA (84.78%) and Adapter (85.66%) underperform Full fine-tuning, confirming that direct transfer from NLP without vision-specific design degrades results. On VTAB, nearly all 18 PETL algorithms surpass Full fine-tuning (65.57%), with HST (75.99%) and DTL (74.58%) leading; however, BitFit (62.05%) and VPT-Shallow (64.85%) struggle on Structured tasks due to the domain gap from ImageNet pre-training. On video recognition, BAPAT achieves the best HMDB51 score (71.93% with Video Swin-B, 71.93% vs Full FT 68.07%), while on data-rich SSv2, Full fine-tuning (53.97%) remains competitive with only AdaptFormer (59.02%) clearly surpassing it. On dense prediction, Mona is the sole PETL method to beat Full fine-tuning on COCO (53.4% vs 51.9% APBox), whereas on data-scarce PASCAL VOC all PETL methods win (LN TUNE: 85.8% vs 83.5%), and on data-rich ADE20K Full fine-tuning (52.10% mIoU) outperforms all PETL methods. The consistent pattern across tasks is that PETL advantages grow as training data decreases, and no single algorithm dominates all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PETL benchmarking effort initiated by ZhiJian [18], which covered only 5 algorithms on image classification with ViT, to a much broader scope: 25 algorithms across image recognition, video action recognition, and dense prediction with both ViT and Swin backbones. It is motivated by the survey [19] that identified the absence of a unified, reproducible evaluation framework for visual PETL. The paper also introduces the PPT metric to fill the gap of a single scalar that jointly captures performance and parameter efficiency, a dimension no prior benchmark addressed. Conceptually, it repurposes the NLP PETL evaluation paradigm (comparing adapter, prompt, and low-rank methods) to the vision domain while adding task diversity that prior visual benchmarks lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V-PETL Bench provides the first comprehensive, open-source, and reproducible evaluation infrastructure for visual PETL, reducing the barrier to fair comparison that has plagued the field (closed hyperparameters, underestimated baselines, task-limited benchmarks). Key open questions remain: whether the findings generalize beyond ViT/Swin to newer architectures (e.g., MAE, DINOv2, or diffusion-based backbones), whether the PPT metric's normalization constant C=10^7 remains appropriate as models scale, and how PETL methods behave in multimodal and generative settings, which the authors flag as future work. The finding that no single algorithm dominates across all tasks suggests the field still lacks a universally superior PETL strategy, keeping the design space open.
