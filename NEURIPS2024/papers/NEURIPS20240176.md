## Problem

Diffusion model sampling requires hundreds of sequential denoising steps, creating high latency that limits real-time applications. Existing speedups either reduce step counts (sacrificing sample quality) or parallelize via Picard iterations (e.g., ParaDiGMS) but suffer from O(N) memory requirements and communication-heavy synchronization. This paper asks whether parallel-in-time ODE integration can accelerate diffusion sampling while preserving exact sample quality and with lower memory/communication overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (e.g., StableDiffusion-v2 with a text prompt, or pixel-space models on LSUN/ImageNet/CIFAR), an initial Gaussian noise sample, and a target number of denoising steps N. It re-partitions the N-step trajectory into √N blocks and produces a final image that, upon convergence, is bit-identical to the sequential N-step solver's output. The convergence threshold τ (e.g., 0.1 in pixel space) controls when refinement stops, trading a negligible quality difference for early termination.

## Method

SRDS applies the Parareal parallel-in-time algorithm to the probability flow ODE of diffusion sampling. It first generates a rough trajectory via √N one-step DDIM evaluations (the coarse solver G), then iteratively refines by running √N-step DDIM fine solves (solver F) in parallel across √N trajectory blocks, updating the running solution via a predictor-corrector step: x_i^{p+1} = F(x_i^p, t_i, t_{i+1}) + (G(x_i^{p+1}) − G(x_i^p)). A key theoretical result guarantees convergence to the exact sequential N-step solution in at most √N iterations, though empirically only 3–6 iterations suffice. A pipelined implementation overlaps fine and coarse solves across iterations for an additional ~2× speedup, and the O(√N) memory footprint (versus O(N) for ParaDiGMS) enables better multi-GPU scaling with minimal inter-device communication.

## Results

On pixel-space diffusion (N=1024 steps), SRDS converges in 4–6 Parareal iterations, reducing effective serial evaluations to 147–212 (15–20% of sequential) while matching FID scores exactly (e.g., 12.8 on LSUN Church, 7.6 on CIFAR-10). On StableDiffusion-v2 (latent diffusion), Pipelined SRDS achieves 4.3× wallclock speedup at 961 DDIM steps (10.31s vs 44.88s serial), 3.2× at 196 steps, and 1.7× at 25 steps, while maintaining CLIP scores (31.9 vs 31.9 at 100 steps). The method requires only O(√N) memory and minimal inter-GPU communication, in contrast to the O(N) memory of ParaDiGMS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ParaDiGMS [Shih et al., NeurIPS 2024], Pipelined SRDS wins on wallclock time at every trajectory length tested: 4.3× speedup vs. ParaDiGMS's best 3.1× (at the most relaxed τ=1e-1) on 961-step DDIM, 3.2× vs. 2.7× on 196 steps, and 1.7× vs. 1.5× on 25 steps. SRDS also requires O(√N) memory versus ParaDiGMS's O(N), avoiding the sliding-window tricks and AllReduce communication that bottleneck ParaDiGMS at scale. Against the concurrent ParaTAA [Tang et al., 2024], SRDS achieves 2.73× speedup on DDIM-100 versus ParaTAA's 1.92×, and 1.72× on DDIM-25 versus ParaTAA's 1.17×, with the gap widening at shorter trajectories where parallelism is most valuable. Notably, SRDS uses only 4× 40GB A100s for these comparisons, while ParaDiGMS used 8× 80GB A100s and ParaTAA used 8× 80GB A800s.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRDS adapts the Parareal multi-resolution parallel-in-time integration algorithm (Lions et al., 2001) to diffusion model sampling, departing from the Picard-iteration-based ParaDiGMS (Shih et al., 2024) by introducing a coarse/fine solver hierarchy along the time axis rather than refining at a single resolution. It is also positioned against the concurrent ParaTAA (Tang et al., 2024), which tackles parallel sampling via fixed-point iteration on triangular nonlinear systems. Crucially, SRDS is orthogonal to existing solver improvements (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS, progressive distillation) and can be layered on top of any of them, framing the contribution as a parallelization wrapper rather than a new denoising formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By trading additional parallel compute for lower latency while provably converging to the exact sequential solution, SRDS opens a path toward real-time diffusion-based applications (interactive image editing, robotic trajectory planning) without the quality degradation inherent in few-step or distilled approaches. The counterintuitive finding that longer trajectories converge in fewer Parareal iterations (1 vs. 3) suggests the multi-resolution structure interacts favorably with the smoothness of the probability flow ODE at scale, though formal convergence guarantees specific to diffusion processes remain open. The main limitation is the increased total number of model evaluations, which may be prohibitive in compute-constrained settings, and the practical speedups (1.7×–4.3×) are meaningful but not transformative compared to the 10×+ reductions achievable via distillation.
