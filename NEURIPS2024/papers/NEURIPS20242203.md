## Problem

Zero-shot node classification suffers from prediction bias, where models systematically misclassify unseen-class nodes as seen classes. Existing methods mitigate this by leveraging external knowledge such as semantic vectors and inter-class relationships, but they inadequately exploit the implicit cluster structures already embedded within the unlabeled nodes of the graph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242203/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix), a set of class semantic vectors (text-based or label-based, extracted via BERT-Tiny), and labeled nodes from seen classes. It produces node embeddings in a latent space and assigns class labels to unseen nodes (ZNC) or all nodes (GZNC) via dot-product scoring against learned class prototypes. The adjacency matrix is reshaped as A = αA^l + βA to fuse label-relation and structural-adjacency information before spectral processing.

## Method

SpeAr establishes an approximate equivalence between minimizing a spectral contrastive loss L_scl and performing spectral decomposition on the reshaped graph (Theorem 3.1), so that learned node embeddings approximate the top-k eigenvectors of the normalized adjacency matrix. The loss decomposes into five terms (L1–L5) that pull together same-class labeled pairs and high-adjacency unlabeled pairs while pushing apart cross-class and non-adjacent pairs, with hyperparameters α and β controlling the relative weight of labeled versus unlabeled structure. Node embeddings are produced by a single-layer GCN with a residual skip connection (Eq. 2). Training proceeds in two stages: first an unsupervised spectral contrastive loss pre-trains the backbone, then a supervised variant refines both embeddings and class prototypes. Prototypes are initialized with class semantic vectors and iteratively updated via a momentum rule (Eq. 7) using pseudo-labeled nodes whose predicted probability exceeds a threshold q, enabling the model to progressively sharpen its understanding of unseen classes.

## Results

On the three standard ZNC benchmarks (Cora, Citeseer, C-M10M), SpeAr achieves 60.48%, 59.72%, and 54.22% accuracy under Class Split I, and 58.20%, 75.13%, and 79.00% under Class Split II, outperforming the strongest baselines (GraphCEN and DBiGCN) by 12–19 percentage points. For the generalized ZNC setting (Class Split III), SpeAr attains H-scores of 29.98 (Cora), 26.45 (Citeseer), and 22.19 (C-M10M), substantially exceeding DGPN (H=0) and DBiGCN (H≤8.50). On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv dataset (169K nodes), SpeAr reaches 30.45% (Split I) and 32.20% (Split II), surpassing GraphCEN by 6.5 and 3.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeAr outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and both class splits. Against the three ZNC-specific methods, the gains are most pronounced: on Class Split I, SpeAr beats GraphCEN (the strongest prior method) by 12.05 points on Cora (60.48 vs. 48.43) and 18.75 points on Citeseer (59.72 vs. 40.77); on Class Split II, it surpasses DBiGCN by 7.59 points on Cora and 14.66 points on Citeseer. Traditional zero-shot learning methods (DAP, ESZSL, ZS-GCN, WDVSc, Hyperbolic-ZSL) are consistently 15–30 points below SpeAr, confirming their inadequacy for graph-structured data. In the GZNC setting (Table 3), DGPN achieves 0% unseen-class accuracy (H=0) on all three datasets, DBiGCN reaches at most 4.83% unseen accuracy, while SpeAr attains 18.95–26.32% unseen accuracy, demonstrating a clear reduction in prediction bias. On the large-scale ogbn-arxiv benchmark, SpeAr leads GraphCEN by 6.49 points (Split I) and 3.84 points (Split II), validating scalability via the multi-round subgraph strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeAr builds directly on the spectral contrastive loss framework of HaoChen et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and the open-world semi-supervised learning theory of Sun et al. (NeurIPS 2023, ICML 2023), adapting their theorems to graph-structured data with a reshaped adjacency matrix that integrates label information. It departs from the dominant ZNC paradigm (DGPN, DBiGCN, GraphCEN) that relies primarily on external semantic vectors and inter-class relationships to mitigate prediction bias, instead mining implicit cluster structures from unlabeled nodes via spectral decomposition. The two-stage training with iteratively refined class prototypes (initialized from BERT-Tiny text embeddings) is a practical contribution layered on top of the theoretical spectral-contrastive connection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that unlabeled node cluster information, long underutilized in ZNC, can substantially reduce prediction bias—achieving 15–45% relative accuracy gains over strong baselines on ZNC. However, GZNC performance remains weak (H ≈ 22–30%), with seen-class accuracy dropping dramatically (e.g., 34% vs. 94% for DGPN on Cora), indicating the method still struggles to balance seen and unseen class recognition. The O(N²) computational complexity of the spectral contrastive loss limits scalability, though a multi-round subgraph strategy partially addresses this. Open questions include whether the spectral-prototype framework can be made more effective for GZNC and whether it generalizes to other zero-shot settings beyond graphs.
