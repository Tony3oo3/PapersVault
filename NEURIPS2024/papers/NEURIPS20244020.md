## Problem

Spectral methods for entrywise eigenvector estimation in low-rank signal-plus-noise models have estimation error that depends on the coherence parameter μ of the signal matrix, and the paper shows via the BBP phase transition that this dependence is intrinsic to the spectral estimator. However, the minimax lower bound under ℓ∞/ℓ2,∞ distance does not require such a dependence, indicating the spectral estimator is suboptimal in coherent regimes. The paper asks whether a non-spectral estimator can remove this coherence dependence while maintaining optimal estimation rates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy symmetric matrix Y = M* + W (where M* is rank-r with orthonormal eigenvectors U* and W has subgaussian entries), along with a debiased leading eigenvalue estimate λ̂ and a plug-in noise variance estimate σ̂². Rather than using all entries of Y uniformly, the algorithm identifies a data-driven subset of indices corresponding to large entries of the spectral estimate and uses only those entries for refined estimation. The output is an estimated eigenvector (rank-1) or eigenspace (rank-r) whose entrywise error is measured in ℓ∞ or ℓ2,∞ distance, with the key property that the error bound is independent of the coherence parameter μ.

## Method

The core insight is that any unit vector contains a subset of entries whose magnitudes are bounded below by a threshold α, and the signal-to-noise ratio is concentrated in the matrix entries corresponding to these large components. Algorithm 1 (rank-1) proceeds in two stages: it identifies a threshold α and index set Î from the spectral estimate u, then sign-flips Y using a diagonal matrix Q (matching signs of u on Î) and computes a row-sum estimator v_j = (Σ_{k∈Î} Ỹ_jk)/(Ŝ√λ̂) that leverages the concentrated signal. The final estimate combines the spectral estimate for small entries (where |u_j| ≤ (σ/λ̂)log n) with the row-sum estimate for large entries. For rank-r signals, Algorithm 2 conjugates Y by a Haar-distributed random orthogonal matrix H before applying the rank-1 procedure to each eigenvector, which randomizes the other signal eigenvectors so they behave like noise. The paper also derives new metric entropy bounds for rank-r singular subspaces under ℓ2,∞ distance, yielding an improved minimax lower bound that removes the √(r/n) ceiling present in prior results.

## Results

Theorem 1 establishes that Algorithm 1 achieves entrywise estimation error $d_\infty(\hat{u}, u^\star) \leq C\sqrt{\nu_W}(\log n)^{5/2}/|\lambda^\star|$ with probability at least $1-O(n^{-8}\log n)$, which is minimax-optimal up to log-factors and crucially has no dependence on the coherence parameter $\mu$. In contrast, the standard spectral estimator incurs an error of order $\sigma\sqrt{\mu}/|\lambda^\star|$ (Theorem 4.1 of [27]), which degrades significantly when $\mu$ is large. Theorem 2 improves the minimax lower bound for rank-$r$ eigenspace estimation under $\ell_{2,\infty}$ distance, removing the $\sqrt{r/n}$ cap that limited the previous bound (Equation 9) in the regime $|\lambda^\star|=O(\sigma\sqrt{n})$. Simulations (Figures 1–2) confirm that Algorithm 1/2 substantially outperforms the spectral estimator, with no visible $\mu$-dependence under Gaussian noise and markedly reduced sensitivity under Rademacher and Laplacian noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed Algorithms 1 and 2 against two baselines: (1) the naive spectral estimator (leading eigenvector/eigenspace of $Y$) and (2) approximate message passing (AMP). Against the spectral method, Algorithm 1 achieves substantially lower $d_\infty$ error, particularly when the coherence $\mu = a^2 n$ is large (e.g., $a=0.8$ gives $\mu \approx 0.64n$). Under Gaussian noise, Algorithm 1 shows essentially no dependence on $\mu$ across all tested coherence levels ($a \in \{0.3, 0.55, 0.8\}$), while the spectral estimator's error grows visibly with $\mu$. Under Rademacher noise, Algorithm 1's $\mu$-dependence is slightly reversed relative to the spectral estimator; under Laplacian noise, a small residual $\mu$-dependence remains but is asymptotically smaller than the theoretical rate. For the rank-$r$ case (Algorithm 2), the same pattern holds: it outperforms the spectral estimator across all noise types and coherence levels. Against AMP, the paper reports (in the appendix) that while AMP performs well in recovering signal eigenvectors under $\ell_2$ error, Algorithms 1 and 2 achieve better performance under the entrywise and $\ell_{2,\infty}$ metrics that are the focus of this work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the spectral perturbation theory of Chen et al. [27], whose Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 establish entrywise eigenvector error bounds that depend on the coherence parameter μ. The key departure is showing that this μ-dependence is an artifact of the spectral estimator rather than a fundamental barrier: via the BBP phase transition (Lemma 1), the authors prove the spectral rate is intrinsic to that estimator, then construct a non-spectral refinement (Algorithm 1) that removes the coherence dependence entirely. The debiased eigenvalue estimator from Chen et al. [26] and the Yang-Barron packing argument [59] serve as technical building blocks for the upper and lower bounds, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that entrywise eigenvector estimation can be coherence-free (up to log-factors) in the spiked Wigner model is a clean theoretical advance that reframes μ as a property of the estimator rather than the problem. The new metric entropy bounds for rank-r singular subspaces under ℓ_{2,∞} distance are of independent interest and tighten the minimax lower bound in the small-signal regime. Open questions include removing the technical gap condition (Assumption 2), providing a full theoretical analysis of the rank-r algorithm (currently empirical only), and understanding what happens in the sub-BBP regime where |λ*| ≤ σ√n and no polynomial-time spectral method is known to succeed.
