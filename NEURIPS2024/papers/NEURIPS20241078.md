## Problem

Efficient coding theory predicts that sensory circuits transform natural signals into representations with reduced statistical dependencies, but existing normative circuit models are limited to linear transformations (whitening, ICA) that cannot remove the higher-order dependencies prominent in sensory signals. The precise relationship between local interneuron properties—connectivity, activation functions, and response dynamics—and the resulting circuit-level response distribution remains unknown, leaving a gap between the computational goal of nonlinear redundancy reduction and a mechanistic circuit account.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D input signals consisting of pairs of oriented filter responses (matched to V1 receptive field selectivity) computed from natural images in the Kodak dataset, with spatial offsets ranging from d=2 to d=64 pixels. These filter responses exhibit sparse, heavy-tailed statistics well approximated by generalized Gaussian distributions with varying scale and shape parameters. The circuit produces approximately spherical Gaussian responses along with learned interneuron activation functions (gain g and shape parameter θ) and unit-norm synaptic weight vectors W, with K=2, 3, or 4 interneurons recurrently connected to N=2 primary neurons.

## Method

The paper derives an optimal transport objective that frames the circuit's input-response function as a transformation mapping the input distribution to a target (spherical Gaussian) distribution while minimizing L² distance. By replacing the distribution-matching constraint with a sum of marginal distances measured along learned directions and enforcing it via Lagrange multipliers, the authors obtain a minimax problem whose gradient-based solution maps directly onto a recurrent circuit with three complementary learning mechanisms: Hebbian synaptic plasticity performs projection pursuit to find signal axes least aligned with the target, while interneuron gain modulation and activation function adaptation reshape the marginal responses along those axes. The interneuron activation functions take the form f(θ,z) = a(θ)z + b(θ)sign(z)|z|^θ, where the linear term shapes the density near zero and the monomial term reshapes the tails, motivated by the generalized Gaussian statistics of natural image filter responses. Fast recurrent dynamics equilibrate the primary neuron responses before slow parameter updates, yielding an online algorithm that requires no access to the full response history.

## Results

On pairs of oriented filter responses from natural images (Kodak dataset), the learned circuit with K=3 interneurons reduces mutual information between coordinates by approximately 5–10× compared to raw filter responses at small spatial offsets (d=2, 8), e.g., from ~0.24 to ~0.02 at d=2 and from ~0.10 to ~0.01–0.03 at d=8. ZCA whitening (the linear baseline) provides only modest reduction at d=2 (e.g., 0.24→0.12) and negligible reduction at d≥8. K=3 and K=4 interneurons achieve comparable performance, while K=2 provides substantially less reduction. For spatial offsets d>32, raw filter responses already have low mutual information and the circuit offers no significant further reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the learned recurrent circuit (with K=2, 3, or 4 interneurons) against two baselines: raw local filter responses and ZCA (zero-phase component analysis) whitening, a standard linear decorrelation method. The circuit with K=3 interneurons substantially outperforms ZCA whitening at small spatial offsets: at d=2, the circuit reduces mutual information to ~0.026 nats versus ~0.122 for ZCA (a ~4.7× further reduction), and at d=8 it achieves ~0.018 versus ~0.081 for ZCA (~4.5× further reduction). At d=32, the circuit still provides a modest advantage (~0.010 vs. ~0.037). ZCA whitening is essentially ineffective at d≥8, sometimes even slightly increasing mutual information, confirming that linear transforms cannot remove the higher-order statistical dependencies present in natural image filter responses. The K=2 circuit is notably weaker than K=3 or K=4, suggesting that at least three interneurons are needed to capture the nonlinear structure of the 2D response distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of normative adaptive whitening circuits (Pehlevan & Chklovskii 2015; Duong et al. 2023; Lipshutz et al. 2023) from linear to nonlinear transformations by introducing interneuron activation function adaptation alongside Hebbian synaptic plasticity and gain modulation. The mathematical foundation draws on optimal transport (Wasserstein-2 distance) and projection pursuit, while the neuroscience motivation comes from efficient coding and redundancy reduction theory (Barlow, Laughlin, Simoncelli & Olshausen). The paper also connects to the Gaussianization and normalizing flows literature (Chen & Gopinath; Lyu & Simoncelli; Laparra et al.) by showing that its circuit algorithm is a nonlinear, biologically-constrained analogue of iterative marginal Gaussianization, but with non-orthogonal, potentially overcomplete weight vectors that are more realistic for neural circuits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, normative framework linking the physiological properties of local interneurons (connectivity, gain, nonlinear activation) to the statistical structure of population responses, addressing a gap that has persisted in efficient coding theory. However, the empirical validation is limited to 2D filter response pairs on the Kodak dataset, and it remains open how the number of interneurons required scales with signal dimensionality. The assumption of linear primary neurons and the absence of sign constraints on weights (violating Dale's law) limit direct biological applicability, and the connection to well-known phenomenological models like divisive normalization is suggested but not formally established.
