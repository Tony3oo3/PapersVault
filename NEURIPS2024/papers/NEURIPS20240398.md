## Problem

Distilled diffusion models (consistency models) achieve few-step image generation but lack the reversibility of undistilled models, meaning they cannot encode real images into their latent space for inversion-based editing. This gap prevents distilled models from supporting precise text-guided image editing, which requires faithfully reconstructing the source image after applying a new prompt. Additionally, the high classifier-free guidance (CFG) scales needed for quality generation further degrade inversion accuracy, creating a tension between editability and reconstruction fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image and a pair of text prompts (source and target) as input. It first encodes the image into latent noise via the forward consistency model (fCD) in 3–4 unguided steps, then decodes back to an edited image via the reverse consistency model (CD) in 3–4 guided steps, yielding a total of 6–8 model evaluations (~0.9 s on SD1.5). The encoding is always performed at w=1 to avoid out-of-distribution latents, while the decoding applies dynamic CFG (CADS schedule) with a step-function guidance scale that is zero above a threshold τ and the full CFG scale below it.

## Method

iCD introduces a forward consistency model (fCD) by flipping the boundary condition of standard consistency distillation: instead of mapping any trajectory point to the clean image (t₀), fCD maps it to the terminal noise (t_N), enabling deterministic image-to-noise encoding. Both fCD and CD are generalized to a multi-boundary formulation that segments the PF ODE trajectory into m intervals, allowing deterministic multistep inversion without the stochasticity of standard multistep consistency sampling. Two preservation losses (forward and reverse) are added to enforce mutual consistency between the forward and reverse models, closing the gap to teacher-level inversion quality. A key empirical finding is that dynamic CFG (deactivating guidance at high noise levels during decoding) dramatically reduces reconstruction error while preserving generation quality, and that encoding must remain unguided (w=1) since guided encoding produces out-of-distribution latents. The full pipeline is trained via LoRA adapters on top of CFG-distilled SD1.5 or SDXL, with the final objective combining two consistency losses and two preservation losses.

## Results

On the COCO editing benchmark with SDXL, iCD (8 steps) achieves a CLIP score (target) of 0.267, DinoV2 preservation of 0.472, and CLIP score (reference) of 0.748, outperforming all ReNoise variants (35–150 steps) on both preservation metrics. In human preference studies, iCD-XL is preferred over ReNoise Turbo (44 steps) 64% vs 36% on COCO and 63% vs 37% on PieBench, and overwhelmingly over ReNoise SDXL (150 steps) at 91% vs 9% on COCO. Inversion time is 1.56 s for iCD-XL versus 6.75 s for ReNoise LCM-XL and 116.4 s for NTI on SD1.5, making iCD roughly 4–120× faster depending on the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReNoise [25] (the primary SDXL baseline), iCD-XL wins on human preference across all three ReNoise configurations (64–95% vs 5–37%) and on both preservation metrics (DinoV2, CLIP-I) on COCO, while tying ReNoise Turbo on CLIP-T for PieBench (0.254 vs 0.254). For the SD1.5 editing experiments, iCD (8 steps) surpasses NTI [32], NPI [33], InfEdit [26], and Edit-friendly DDPM [43] in human preference on COCO, and outperforms InfEdit and Edit-friendly DDPM on PieBench, all while being 10–120× faster (0.96 s vs 9.95–116.4 s). In qualitative comparisons, iCD outperforms SDXL-Turbo + SDEdit [63] on reference image preservation (the stochastic SDEdit process degrades content fidelity) and beats InstructPix2Pix [7] on both content preservation and editing strength without requiring task-specific training. The one area where iCD is slightly weaker is PieBench automatic metrics, where ReNoise LCM achieves a marginally higher DinoV2 (0.736 vs 0.707) and CLIP-I (0.865 vs 0.858), which the authors attribute to the increased complexity of multi-type editing tasks requiring more steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Consistency Distillation (CD) framework [10, 12, 13] by introducing a forward consistency model (fCD) that maps trajectory points to the noise endpoint, enabling deterministic multistep inversion—a capability absent from all prior CD variants. The multi-boundary formulation is adapted from multistep consistency models [53, 13], but generalized to operate in both forward and reverse directions with preservation losses that couple the two models. The key conceptual departure from prior inversion-based editing work [32, 33, 31] is that those methods require 50+ DDIM steps, whereas iCD achieves comparable inversion in 3–4 steps. The dynamic CFG insight builds on CADS [45] and limited-interval guidance [46], which motivated unguided high-noise steps for diversity; here the same mechanism is repurposed to reduce inversion reconstruction error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unlocking accurate inversion in distilled models, iCD makes inversion-based text-guided editing practical at near-real-time speeds (~0.9 s per edit), a regime previously inaccessible without sacrificing fidelity. The finding that dynamic CFG is a free (zero-overhead) inversion booster is immediately applicable to any pretrained distilled model using step-wise guidance, broadening its utility beyond the specific iCD architecture. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other distillation paradigms (progressive, adversarial), whether boundary-step flexibility can be achieved without retraining, and whether the method generalizes to video or 3D generation where inversion is even more critical.
