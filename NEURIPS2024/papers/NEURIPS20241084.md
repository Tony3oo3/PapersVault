## Problem

Quantifying epistemic uncertainty for GNNs is challenging because uncertainty can arise at different structural scales (individual nodes, local neighborhoods, or clusters), yet existing methods either ignore this multi-scale nature or produce separate structure-aware and structure-agnostic measures that cannot be combined into a single score. Additionally, logit-based energy models suffer from provable overconfidence far from training data, causing their induced densities to be non-integrable and their uncertainty estimates to break under severe distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

GEBM is a post-hoc method that consumes the logits of any pre-trained logit-based GNN evaluated on an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X), with no retraining required. It produces a single per-node epistemic uncertainty score (the GEBM energy) that can be used directly for OOD detection, and optionally an evidential class prediction via a Dirichlet parameterization of the regularized joint energy. The only hyperparameters are the regularization strength gamma and the choice of diffusion operator P_A; the Gaussian regularizer parameters are fitted as MLEs from training-class logits.

## Method

GEBM defines three energy functions at different structural scales by interleaving a graph diffusion operator P_A with marginalization of the logit-based joint energy: Independent energy (no diffusion, captures node-level anomalies), Local energy (diffuse before marginalize, captures neighborhood-level evidence conflicts), and Group energy (marginalize before diffuse, captures cluster-level anomalies). These are aggregated via a logsumexp (soft maximum) into a single energy measure. A class-conditional Gaussian prior N(f_theta(x)_y | mu_y, Sigma_y) is added as a regularizer to the joint energy, which the paper proves ensures the induced density is integrable and that the energy diverges toward maximal uncertainty in the limit, mitigating the overconfidence of vanilla logit-based EBMs. The framework further reveals a correspondence between the regularized joint energy and Dirichlet evidence, enabling an evidential inference mode where predictions converge toward a prior under severe distribution shifts, improving robustness without additional training.

## Results

GEBM achieves the best average OOD detection rank across all 8 datasets (2.5–3.8), outperforming the next-best method GCN-EBM (4.0–5.2) by a wide margin. On epistemic AUC-ROC, GEBM improves over the vanilla logit-based EBM by 5.8–10.9 percentage points (16%–32% relative) on average across shifts. For example, on CoraML under the far-OOD N(0,1) shift, GEBM reaches 86.4% AUC-ROC versus 25.7% for GCN-EBM and 35.3% for GNNSafe. GEBM is the only estimator that remains effective across all 7 distribution shift types simultaneously, losing only on the Page-Rank centrality shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEBM dominates the vanilla logit-based EBM (GCN-EBM) across nearly all shifts, with the most dramatic gap on far-OOD N(0,1) perturbations (e.g., 86.4 vs. 25.7 on CoraML, 92.4 vs. 42.6 on Amazon Photo), confirming that Gaussian regularization is critical for severe distribution shifts. Against GNNSafe, which only diffuses energy at the cluster scale, GEBM wins on 3 of 4 shifts shown (e.g., 94.5 vs. 53.4 on Ber(0.5) for CoraML), demonstrating the benefit of multi-scale aggregation. GEBM also outperforms the evidential GPN on most shifts (e.g., 94.5 vs. 52.4 on Ber(0.5), 86.4 vs. 55.5 on N(0,1) for CoraML), while GPN retains an edge only on LoC(last) for Amazon Photo (87.3 vs. 83.6). Sampling-based methods (GCN-Ens, GCN-DE) are consistently weaker on epistemic OOD detection, with GCN-Ens reaching at most 80.3% AUC-ROC (Amazon Photo LoC) versus GEBM's 83.6%. The HEAT baseline, which learns multiple corrected EBMs via SGLD, is outperformed by GEBM on all shifts without requiring additional training. On the Page-Rank centrality shift (not shown in Table 1), GEBM is less competitive due to the inherent bias of symmetric diffusion toward high-degree nodes, yet it still achieves the best average rank overall across all 7 shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEBM extends the logit-based EBM framework for OOD detection (Liu et al., 2021) to graphs by aggregating energy at multiple structural scales that emerge from interleaving graph diffusion with energy marginalization. It departs from prior graph-specific EBM work (GNNSafe, HEAT) that only considers a single diffusion scale, and from GPN which separates structure-aware and structure-agnostic uncertainty without combining them into a single measure. The Gaussian regularizer is adopted from Lafon et al. (2024), but GEBM provides the first formal proof that it ensures integrability of the induced density for piecewise-affine classifiers. The evidential interpretation draws a novel bridge between EBMs and evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018; Charpentier et al., 2020), showing that the regularized joint energy directly yields Dirichlet evidence parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEBM provides a simple, training-free post-hoc method that yields a single epistemic uncertainty score sensitive to multiple distribution shifts simultaneously, which is essential for practical deployment where the type of shift is unknown a priori. The theoretical results (overconfidence of logit-EBMs with high probability, integrability guarantee under Gaussian regularization) give practitioners confidence that the uncertainty measure won't break under severe distribution shifts. Limitations include restriction to homophilic graphs, inability to improve aleatoric uncertainty or calibration, and weaker performance on centrality-based shifts. Open questions include extending the multi-scale energy paradigm to heterophilic settings and developing fully evidential GNN methods that leverage structural-scale aggregation during training rather than post-hoc.
