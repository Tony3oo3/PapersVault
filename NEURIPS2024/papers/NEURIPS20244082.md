## Problem

No large-scale curated dataset pairs CAD models with their corresponding G-code files for extrusion-based 3D printing, leaving a critical gap for training manufacturing-domain foundation models. The low-level, flavor-specific nature of G-code makes manual debugging infeasible and existing datasets (e.g., Greater G-code with only 860 files) far too small to support meaningful LLM-based research on G-code generation, translation, or manipulation.

## Input / Output

For G-code flavor translation, the model takes Sailfish-format G-code chunks (max 20 lines, with extrusion values converted to relative form) and produces the corresponding Marlin-format G-code chunk. For geometric transformation evaluation, a single G-code layer plus a natural-language prompt (e.g., 'scale by factor of 2') is fed to chat-based LLMs, which return the modified G-code layer. The dataset itself stores G-code in binary .bgcode format to minimize footprint, and each STL is sliced with one of four randomly selected infill patterns (Gyroid, Honeycomb, Cubic, Grid) to ensure structural diversity.

## Method

The dataset is built by slicing 100,068 STL models (from Objaverse-XL's Thingiverse branch and Thingi10K) using PrusaSlicer in both Sailfish and Marlin flavors, with renderings generated via Blender (10 views per object) and LVIS categories assigned by averaging CLIP-ViT-L-14 embeddings across views and matching to 1200+ category text embeddings. For the translation task, a key preprocessing pipeline aligns contours between flavors using a hash-based lookup on extrusion-coordinate representations (achieving >99.9% alignment), then adaptively creates matching line-index pairs to form semantically equivalent chunks. GPT-2 is finetuned with next-token prediction on these paired chunks, and evaluation uses a novel image-space IoU metric computed from top-down layer renderings produced by a custom open-source G-code renderer, since text-based metrics are insufficient for capturing geometric fidelity.

## Results

For G-code flavor translation (Sailfish→Marlin), finetuning GPT-2 on just 1 file (3,933 chunks) from Slice-100K boosts IOU@0.9 from 67% (unfinetuned GPT-2 Base) to 95%, and IOU@0.99 from 4% to 27%. Scaling to 5 files further improves IOU@0.95 to 91% and IOU@0.99 to 30%, while 25 files yields IOU@0.9 of 98% but no further gain at higher thresholds. For the geometric transformation (scaling) task, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 both fail on the S-shape, Claude-2 handles outer contours but struggles with infill, and open-source models (Llama-2-70b, Starcoder) fail across all test shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is between unfinetuned GPT-2 Base and GPT-2 finetuned on 1, 5, or 25 files from Slice-100K for Sailfish-to-Marlin G-code translation. Even a single file of training data (3,933 chunks) more than doubles IOU@0.99 (4%→27%) and lifts IOU@0.9 from 67% to 95%, demonstrating that the preprocessing pipeline reduces translation to a simple local mapping. Beyond 5 files, additional data yields diminishing returns (IOU@0.98 actually drops from 74% to 71% at 25 files), suggesting the task is data-efficient. For the geometric transformation task, the paper qualitatively compares GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude-2, Llama-2-70b, and Starcoder: GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 both fail on the S-shape, Claude-2 succeeds on outer contours of the cylinder and cube but fails on infill, and both Llama-2-70b and Starcoder fail on all three test shapes (cylinder, cube, S-shape), indicating that current LLMs—whether proprietary or open-source—remain unreliable for precise G-code coordinate manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale curated G-code dataset (100K+ files), departing from prior 3D datasets (Objaverse, ShapeNet, Thingi10K) that provide meshes, renderings, and categories but lack the manufacturing-specific G-code modality. The only prior G-code resource, Greater G-code, contains just 860 files. Methodologically, the paper repurposes existing tools—PrusaSlicer for slicing, CLIP for category assignment, Blender for renderings—into a novel pipeline, and introduces a preprocessing framework (contour flipping, adaptive pair creation, relative extrusion) that makes G-code amenable to LLM-based translation, a formulation not present in prior 3D or code-generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Slice-100K provides the foundational resource needed to train manufacturing-domain foundation models, a gap the authors identify as blocking progress in AI for additive manufacturing. The finding that GPT-2 finetuned on merely 5 G-code shapes achieves near-saturation translation quality (96% IOU@0.9) suggests the preprocessing reduces the task to a simple local mapping, potentially enabling lightweight on-device G-code tools. Key limitations include single-direction (Z-axis) slicing, difficulty verifying LVIS categories, and focus solely on extrusion-based printing, leaving open questions about multi-directional slicing, other AM techniques (SLA, SLS), and whether the data efficiency generalizes to more complex G-code structures or additional flavor pairs.
