## Problem

LLM alignment has been almost exclusively studied during instruction tuning or RLHF (post-alignment), leaving the pre-training stage unexplored for value alignment. This is especially problematic for low-resource languages like Arabic, where discarding unaligned web data is impractical and post-hoc corrections struggle with the sheer volume of problematic content. The paper asks whether aligning pre-training data from the outset ('native alignment') can produce safer, more culturally appropriate models without the instability and cost of post-alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw Arabic web-crawled text (ArabicText2022) alongside a structured code of conduct covering format correction, value fairness, content moderation, and knowledge preservation. GPT-4 first rewrites 10k samples as expert annotations; these pairs train Qwen1.5-4B-Chat as an alignment worker, which then rewrites 10 billion tokens of the corpus. The output is a native-alignment dataset used for continued pre-training on top of 100B tokens of mixed-source data, yielding LLaMA3-Tamed-8B and LLaMA3-Tamed-70B.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-step data-centric workflow: deduplication, expert annotation (GPT-4 rewrites data per a code of conduct), distillation into smaller alignment workers (Qwen1.5-4B-Chat trained on 10k expert pairs), and large-scale rewriting of 10B tokens. The aligned data is appended after conventional pre-training rather than replacing it, and ablations show the two data types are mutually beneficial. Pilot studies on 8k samples confirm toxicity reductions (up to 31.8% in sexual content) and lower perplexity after rewriting. Scaling experiments reveal that helpfulness gains accelerate with more aligned data while harmlessness improves gradually, suggesting no sharp plateau within the tested range. The approach is positioned as complementary to post-alignment (e.g., DPO), with the paper demonstrating that native alignment plus SFT outperforms SFT alone on cultural alignment benchmarks.

## Results

LLaMA3-Tamed-70B achieves a 69.88 average score across ArabicMMLU, EXAMS, ACVA, and AraTrust, surpassing Llama3-70B (68.96) and all other open-source baselines. The 8B variant (LLaMA3-Tamed-8B) reaches 62.14 average, outperforming Llama3-8B (60.25) and Qwen1.5-7B (54.59). On the trustworthiness benchmark AraTrust, native alignment yields a notable gain from 60.54 (Llama3-70B) to 63.41 (LLaMA3-Tamed-70B). GPT-4 remains the strongest closed-source model on knowledge benchmarks (72.50 ArabicMMLU, 57.76 EXAMS), but LLaMA3-Tamed-70B is the best open-source model overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama3-8B/70B [19], LLaMA3-Tamed consistently wins: the 70B model improves ArabicMMLU from 65.51 to 66.56, EXAMS from 54.78 to 55.49, and AraTrust from 60.54 to 63.41, while the 8B model lifts ArabicMMLU from 45.78 to 50.17 and ACVA clean from 77.49 to 80.17. Against Qwen1.5 [24], LLaMA3-Tamed-70B (69.88 avg) surpasses Qwen1.5-72B (66.62) and Qwen1.5-32B (63.45), and LLaMA3-Tamed-8B (62.14) beats Qwen1.5-7B (54.59) by a wide margin. Against Jais-30B-v3 [25], LLaMA3-Tamed-8B achieves a higher average (62.14 vs. 61.09) despite having fewer parameters, though Jais leads on ACVA clean (83.39 vs. 80.17). Against AceGPT [30], the BeaverTails evaluation (Figure 5) shows Llama-3-8B already surpasses AceGPT and Jais in both harmlessness and helpfulness relative to ChatGPT-4o, and native alignment further improves these ratios by 10.4% and 4.8% respectively. In the data-cleaning comparison (Appendix E), native alignment outperforms RefinedWeb-style cleaning [34] on MMLU (30.73 vs. 27.71 average for Qwen-1.5-0.5B), confirming that alignment rewriting provides gains beyond conventional quality filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard LLM alignment pipeline (instruction tuning → RLHF/DPO) by shifting alignment into the pre-training phase, which the authors term 'native alignment.' Conceptually, it builds on the 'superficial alignment hypothesis' from Lima et al. [5]—that alignment is a thin layer over pre-trained knowledge—and argues that embedding alignment deeply during pre-training can reduce the burden on post-hoc correction. Mechanically, it repurposes data rewriting and knowledge distillation (GPT-4-annotated pairs → smaller alignment workers) in a way that extends prior data-cleaning efforts (RefinedWeb, SlimPajama, WRAP) from quality filtering to value alignment. The authors explicitly position native alignment as complementary to, not a replacement for, post-alignment methods like DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of LLaMA3-Tamed-70B and -8B as open-source SOTA Arabic LLMs is a concrete contribution to the under-served Arabic NLP community, and the approach is particularly relevant for low-resource languages where discarding unaligned data is impractical. However, several open questions remain: the hallucination rate in rewritten data (acknowledged but not fully addressed), the absence of a fair apples-to-apples benchmark comparing native vs. post-alignment, and whether the modest empirical gains (e.g., +2.87 on AraTrust for 70B) would hold across languages and model scales. The finding that alignment data and standard pre-training data are mutually beneficial rather than conflicting is a useful practical insight for data-mixing strategies.
