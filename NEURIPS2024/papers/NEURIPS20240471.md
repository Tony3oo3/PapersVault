## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training methods align images with entire radiology reports, providing no pixel-level supervision for learning dense visual features critical to tasks like segmentation. Alignment-based approaches suffer from ambiguous token-to-region pairing, while reconstruction-based approaches capture only low-level texture rather than clinically meaningful high-level semantics, leaving a gap for dense prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 chest X-ray images (resized from 256×256, center-cropped, intensity-normalized to [0,1]) paired with free-text radiology reports, encoded by a ResNet-50 vision encoder and ClinicalBERT text encoder respectively. Beyond the standard global image and text embeddings used for contrastive alignment, the framework produces a dense C×H×W feature map from the encoder's final convolutional layer, which is decoded into a pixel-wise pseudo segmentation mask. The pseudo mask is generated on-the-fly via a parameter-free pipeline (attention aggregation → body-mask filtering → 85th-percentile thresholding → bilateral smoothing) and requires no additional trainable parameters or external annotations.

## Method

G2D is an end-to-end encoder-decoder medical VLP framework that jointly optimizes two losses: a vision-language contrastive loss (VLA) pulling paired image-report embeddings together in a shared space, and a pixel alignment loss (PA) that trains a U-Net decoder to predict a pseudo segmentation mask from the encoder's dense feature map. The pseudo mask is constructed parameter-free by aggregating multi-head self-attention scores from a [CLS] token over pixel embeddings, masking out background via histogram-based body segmentation, thresholding at the 85th percentile, and applying bilateral filtering to smooth boundaries. Because the attention map is derived from the text-aligned visual representation, the pseudo mask inherits clinical semantics (e.g., regions corresponding to 'pneumonia' or 'cardiomegaly' in the report), making it a meaningful dense supervision signal rather than a mere regularizer. The total loss is L_VLA + ½(L_Dice + L_BCE), and the entire system is trained for 50 epochs on 213,384 MIMIC-CXR image-report pairs across 16 A100 GPUs, with the decoder head enabling direct transfer to downstream segmentation without architectural modification.

## Results

On the SIIM segmentation task, G2D (encoder-decoder) achieves 65.6 Dice with only 1% of training data, surpassing the best baseline M-FLAG (52.5 Dice) trained on 100% of data. At 100% data, G2D reaches 68.4 Dice on SIIM and 76.9 on RSNA, outperforming M-FLAG (64.8/70.5) and MGCA (64.2/69.8). For object detection, G2D achieves 27.2 mAP on RSNA and 20.4 mAP on Object-CXR at 100% data, leading all baselines. On linear classification (CheXpert, 100%), G2D attains 91.1 AUC, exceeding MRM (88.7) and MGCA (88.2), and on CXR14 fine-tuned classification it reaches 83.1 mean AUC versus KAD's 82.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2D outperforms all eight baselines across every downstream task and data fraction. The closest competitor in segmentation is M-FLAG [6], which G2D (encoder-decoder) surpasses by 3.6 Dice on SIIM-100% (68.4 vs 64.8) and 5.6 Dice on RSNA-100% (76.9 vs 70.5); at 1% data the gap widens to 13.1 Dice on SIIM (65.6 vs 52.5). MGCA [9] and GLoRIA-MIMIC [8] trail M-FLAG by 0.6–1.1 Dice in segmentation and 0.5–1.0 mAP in detection. MedKLIP [5] and KAD [21], both of which leverage external disease-level annotations during pre-training, are still outperformed by G2D (which uses no extra annotations): on CXR14 fine-tuned classification at 100% data, G2D achieves 83.1 mean AUC vs KAD's 82.5 and MedKLIP's 80.1; on zero-shot CheXpert classification, G2D reaches 91.2 AUC vs KAD's 90.5. MRM [12], a reconstruction-based method, is the strongest linear-classification baseline (88.7 AUC on CheXpert-100%) but is exceeded by G2D by 2.4 AUC. BioViL [40] is the primary zero-shot grounding competitor, with G2D leading by 1.1 Dice on SIIM (5.1 vs 2.6) and 1.2 Dice on RSNA (47.7 vs 43.9). ConVIRT [4], the earliest alignment-based method, is consistently the weakest medical VLP baseline, trailing G2D by 8–14 Dice in segmentation and 9–19 mAP in detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2D extends the medical vision-language pre-training (VLP) line initiated by ConVIRT, GLoRIA, and MGCA, which align chest X-rays with radiology reports via contrastive learning but remain limited to global representations. It departs from both alignment-based methods (which suffer from ambiguous token-to-patch pairing) and reconstruction-based methods like MRM and PRIOR (which capture low-level texture rather than high-level semantics) by introducing a Pseudo Segmentation pretext task that derives pixel-level supervision from the model's own attention maps. Conceptually, the approach borrows the idea of extracting dense labels from CLIP-style attention (as in GLIP or PyramidCLIP for natural images) but makes it self-contained for the medical domain, where no well-trained object detector or large-scale aligned VLP model is available to provide such labels externally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that G2D's encoder-decoder architecture, trained end-to-end with the PS pretext task, narrows the gap between VLP pre-training and downstream dense prediction: with only 1% of segmentation training data, G2D outperforms baselines trained on 100%, suggesting the PS task acts as a strong inductive bias for pixel-level features. However, the authors acknowledge that pseudo masks provide only weak supervision and may not precisely associate individual pixels with specific report phrases, capping the method's ceiling for fine-grained grounding. Generalization beyond chest X-rays to other modalities (CT, MRI) or to multi-organ/multi-pathology settings remains an open question, as does whether the attention-derived pseudo masks remain meaningful when the VLP model is trained on more diverse or larger corpora.
