## Problem

Prior work characterized the expressive power of constant-iteration GNNs via graded modal logic relative to first-order logic, but no exact logical characterization existed for recurrent GNNs that terminate on their own. Additionally, the relationship between GNNs operating over reals (theoretical standard) and over floating-point numbers (practical standard) remained unexplored, leaving unclear whether the two settings have the same expressive power.

## Input / Output

The GNNs consume node-labeled directed graphs (with self-loops allowed) where each node carries a subset of label symbols from a countably infinite vocabulary. The GNN computes feature vectors in R^d (or a finite floating-point system S^d) over discrete rounds, aggregating neighbor states via sum-in-increasing-order and combining via a matrix-weighted affine map followed by truncated ReLU. Acceptance is signaled when a designated node visits a state in a predefined accepting set F ⊆ R^d (or S^d) at any round. The logical characterizations produce GMSC programs (finite rule-based modal logic with counting) or ω-GML formulas (infinitary disjunctions of graded modal formulas) that express exactly the same node properties.

## Method

The paper establishes exact equiexpressivity results by introducing counting message passing automata (CMPAs) as an intermediate model: bounded finite CMPAs (FCMPAs) match GNN[F]s and GMSC, while countable-state CMPAs match GNN[R]s and ω-GML. A key technical ingredient is Proposition 2.3, which shows that summing floats in increasing order stabilizes after a bounded number of additions, justifying the boundedness assumption for float GNNs. The translation from GMSC to R-simple GNN[F]s uses a 'clock' mechanism that simulates one logical iteration in D+1 GNN rounds, with feature vectors split into truth-value bits and depth-tracking bits. For the MSO-relative result, the paper leverages parity tree automata (via Janin–Walukiewicz) and the unraveling of graphs at a node to show that all models collapse to the same finitary logic (GMSC) on MSO-definable properties.

## Results

The paper establishes three main expressiveness equivalences as theorems rather than empirical metrics. Theorem 3.2 shows that recurrent GNNs with floats (GNN[F]), the graded modal substitution calculus (GMSC), and R-simple aggregate-combine GNN[F]s all have identical expressive power, without relativising to any background logic. Theorem 3.4 shows that recurrent GNNs with reals (GNN[R]) are exactly as expressive as the infinitary logic ω-GML (infinite disjunctions of graded modal logic formulas). Theorem 4.3 is the key practical result: relative to MSO-expressible properties, GNN[R] and GNN[F] (even in the R-simple setting) have the same expressive power, meaning the theoretical real-number model and the practical floating-point model coincide for this large class of properties.

**Table 1: Summary of main expressiveness results. ≡ means same expressive power; < means strictly less expressive. 'bnd.' = bounded, 'AC' = aggregate-combine.**

| Setting | Expressiveness Chain |
|---|---|
| Absolute (no background logic) | GNN[F] ≡ R-simple AC-GNN[F] ≡ GMSC ≡ bnd. FCMPA **<** GNN[ℝ] ≡ ω-GML ≡ CMPA |
| Relative to MSO | GNN[F] ≡ R-simple AC-GNN[F] ≡ GMSC ≡ bnd. FCMPA ≡ GNN[ℝ] ≡ ω-GML ≡ CMPA |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between formalisms. In the absolute (unrestricted) setting, GNN[F] is strictly less expressive than GNN[R]: for example, GNN[R] can express undecidable properties (via ω-GML) while GMSC/GNN[F] cannot, since GMSC programs on finite graphs eventually cycle. However, relative to MSO-expressible properties, this gap closes entirely (Theorem 4.3), so floats suffice for all MSO-definable graph properties. The paper also positions itself against Barceló et al. [5] (constant-iteration GNNs ≡ GML relative to FO) by extending to the recurrent setting, and against Pflüger et al. [26] (recurrent GNNs characterized via graded two-way μ-calculus relative to LocMMFP) by showing GMSC is orthogonal to the μ-calculus in expressivity and by using the more natural MSO background logic. A notable separation result (Proposition 4.5) shows that even constant-iteration GNN[R] is strictly more expressive than GNN[F], since depth-bounded ω-GML can express undecidable properties that GMSC cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logical characterization of GNN expressive power initiated by Barceló et al. (ICLR 2020), who matched constant-iteration GNNs to graded modal logic GML relative to first-order logic, by moving to recurrent GNNs and providing absolute (not background-logic-relative) characterizations. The float-side characterization via the graded modal substitution calculus (GMSC) builds directly on Kuusisto's (CSL 2013) connection between the modal substitution calculus and distributed message-passing automata, extended here with counting modalities. The MSO-relative result (Theorem 4.3) leverages the Janin–Walukiewicz/Walukiewicz correspondence between MSO and parity tree automata on tree-shaped graphs, a tool previously applied to the modal μ-calculus but not to GNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that for the large and natural class of MSO-expressible graph properties, recurrent GNNs using reals (the theoretical standard) and floats (the practical standard) have identical expressive power, closing a gap that exists in the unrestricted setting. This validates that theoretical analyses of GNNs do not diverge from practical floating-point implementations for a very significant class of properties. Open questions include extending the framework to global readout mechanisms (corresponding to counting global modalities), other acceptance conditions such as fixed-point-based termination, and the practically critical but theoretically unaddressed question of whether termination can be learned during training.
