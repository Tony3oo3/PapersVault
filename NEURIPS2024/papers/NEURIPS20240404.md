## Problem

Multi-modal diffusion models (e.g., Kosmos-G) enable zero-shot concept transfer from a single reference image, creating a new external-input threat where users can propagate copyrighted styles or harmful objects without any textual cue. Existing safe-generation methods—dataset filtering, adversarial perturbations, machine unlearning, and text-based inference-time refusals—either cannot scale to arbitrary user-supplied images or rely on textual descriptions of unsafe concepts that fail when the concept is purely visual. The paper addresses this gap by asking: can we surgically remove a specific unsafe concept from the generation process at inference time without retraining the model or degrading safe content?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference image (potentially containing one or more unsafe concepts such as a Van Gogh painting or a specific object) together with a free-form text prompt, both encoded by an MLLM into a unified token sequence that conditions a latent diffusion U-Net. A lightweight discriminator (ResNet-50 or ViT-base) first classifies the reference image to decide whether and which steering vectors to activate. The output is a generated image in which the targeted unsafe concept has been projected out of the cross-attention representations while all other prompt-specified content (backgrounds, other objects, composition) is preserved; no fine-tuning of the base diffusion model is required.

## Method

CRE generalizes the LLM representation-editing tuple ⟨F, L, P⟩ to diffusion models by adding a timestep dimension, yielding ⟨Φ, L, P, T⟩. The editing function Φ constructs a steering vector for each unsafe concept by running the concept's reference image through the same cross-attention computation (Q from the noisy latent, K/V from the concept embedding), then projects out the component of the attention output aligned with that steering vector; a trained discriminator gates the projection so that safe prompts pass through unmodified. The key novelty is the causal-period search: an 'assess-with-exclusion' algorithm walks backward from t=T to t=1, using the discriminator's confidence gap as a stopping criterion to identify the minimal interval [t_s, t_e] during which the unsafe concept actually manifests in the noisy image, so editing is applied only within that window. This reduces editing overhead by roughly half compared to editing at every timestep while maintaining removal fidelity. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play at inference time—steering vectors and causal periods are precomputed offline per concept category, and at serving time the only added cost is a discriminator forward pass plus a few projection operations in the cross-attention layers.

## Results

On safe object transfer (Table 1), CRE achieves 0% top-1 accuracy across all 10 ImageNet classes (average 0%), completely eliminating unsafe objects from generated images, compared to ProtoRe (1.66%), SLD (22.48%), and the unmodified Kosmos-G baseline (36.26%). On safe style transfer (Table 2), CRE reduces average top-1 style accuracy to 2.12% (ResNet-50) and 1.65% (ViT-base), versus ProtoRe at 13.97% and SLD at 24.93%. On the I2P safe generation benchmark (Table 4), CRE achieves the lowest average unsafe content rate of 0.06, outperforming ProtoRe (0.09) and SLD (0.19). Notably, CRE maintains near-identical performance (<0.1% degradation) when scaling from single to multiple unsafe concepts, while SLD and ProtoRe degrade by ~2% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRE decisively outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against ProtoRe [23], the closest competitor, CRE reduces object transfer accuracy from 1.66% to 0% (Table 1) and style transfer accuracy from 13.97% to 2.12% (Table 2), with the gap widening on large objects (church: 15.6% → 0%) and abstract styles (Disney: 47.59% → 11.39%). Against SLD [22], CRE is dramatically superior, reducing object transfer from 22.48% to 0% and style transfer from 24.93% to 2.12%, as SLD's reliance on textual descriptions of unsafe concepts fails in multi-modal zero-shot scenarios. The intuitive Kosmos-G-Neg baseline (adding 'without' prompts) actually performs worse than the unmodified model on both object (36.76% vs. 36.26%) and style transfer (47.31% vs. 32.59%), confirming that MLLM encoders cannot interpret negation commands. On the I2P benchmark, CRE achieves 0.06 average unsafe rate versus ProtoRe's 0.09 and SLD's 0.19. Critically, CRE scales to multiple simultaneous unsafe concepts with <0.1% performance degradation, while SLD and ProtoRe degrade by ~2% on average as prompt length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation editing framework from LLMs (Zou et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024), which operates on a <function, layers, positions> tuple, to diffusion-based generative models by introducing a causal timestep dimension, yielding <Φ, L, P, T>. It departs from prior inference-time safe generation methods (SLD, ProtoRe) that rely on textual descriptions of unsafe concepts via negative guidance, instead constructing steering vectors directly from example images of unsafe concepts and projecting them out of cross-attention representations. The causal period concept is adapted from causal tracing in knowledge editing (Basu et al. 2023) but repurposed to identify which diffusion timesteps are responsible for a given concept's generation, reducing editing overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRE addresses a practical gap in VL2I diffusion safety: existing methods fail when the unsafe concept is provided as a reference image rather than a text prompt, which is the primary attack vector in zero-shot concept transfer. The scalability result—maintaining <0.1% performance degradation when governing multiple unsafe concepts simultaneously, versus ~2% for SLD/ProtoRe—makes it viable for real-world deployment where governance rules are dynamic. Key limitations include dependence on discriminator accuracy (misclassification triggers unnecessary editing) and the requirement of at least one example image per unsafe concept category, leaving open the question of handling entirely novel or unseen unsafe concepts without exemplars.
