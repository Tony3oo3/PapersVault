## Problem

Existing domain adaptive 3D detection methods address the inter-domain gap by focusing on a single factor (object size, point density, or geometric shape), yet a substantial gap remains because multiple factors jointly determine how objects appear in an unseen domain. Additionally, the self-training pseudo-label collection is biased toward dominant object types that the source-trained detector already handles well, leaving underrepresented groups under-learned in the target domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240572/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (64-beam Velodyne for KITTI, 32-beam roof for NuScenes, 64+200-beam multi-sensor for Waymo) along with 7-parameter 3D box labels (center, size, vertical rotation). During training, foreground points extracted from existing boxes are encoded into fixed-dimension object descriptors (512-d) that drive GMM grouping; during inference, only the learned group means (512-d vectors) are needed as cross-attention queries against the backbone's BEV features, making the method a lightweight add-on that preserves the original detector's inference speed.

## Method

GroupEXP-DA extracts per-object descriptors from foreground points via MLP + global max pooling, then clusters them with a GMM (initialized by K-Means) into n_g groups whose parameters (mean, covariance, weight) are progressively updated during self-training. The key architectural contribution is a Group-Region Correlation module that uses cross-attention with each group mean as query and the BEV spatial feature as key/value, producing group-equivariant features that selectively detect objects similar to that group. An explorative update strategy redistributes source labels according to target-domain pseudo-label group assignments, reducing false negatives by ensuring the detector learns to find objects matching each group's target-domain characteristics. Training combines standard RPN detection loss with inter-group repel and intra-group attraction contrastive losses to keep groups distinctive yet cohesive, and the entire pipeline operates within the ST3D self-training loop where group-equivariant RPNs collect pseudo-labels while a shared baseline RPN handles final inference.

## Results

GroupEXP-DA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three adaptation scenarios (Waymo→KITTI, NuScenes→KITTI, Waymo→NuScenes) with both SECOND-IOU and PointPillars backbones. With SECOND-IOU, it outperforms the previous SOTA DTS by 1.14/2.21 AP_BEV/AP_3D on Waymo→KITTI (86.94/73.70 vs. 85.80/71.50), by 0.07/1.60 on NuScenes→KITTI (81.47/68.2 vs. 81.4/66.6), and by 2.64/1.42 on Waymo→NuScenes (43.84/24.42 vs. 41.2/23.0). With PointPillars, gains over DTS are 2.34/3.91, 2.39/1.04, and 2.11/0.65 respectively. The method also substantially outperforms the ST3D self-training baseline, e.g., by 4.75/11.88 on Waymo→KITTI (SECOND-IOU) and by 21.49/41.74 on NuScenes→KITTI (PointPillars).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupEXP-DA outperforms all seven compared baselines across all three adaptation scenarios and both backbones. Against the direct self-training baseline ST3D [44], gains are substantial: 4.75/11.88 AP_BEV/AP_3D on Waymo→KITTI (SECOND-IOU) and 21.49/41.74 on NuScenes→KITTI (PointPillars), where the baseline suffers severe degradation. Against the previous SOTA DTS [8], improvements are more modest but consistent: 1.14/2.21 (Waymo→KITTI, SECOND-IOU), 0.07/1.60 (NuScenes→KITTI, SECOND-IOU), and 2.64/1.42 (Waymo→NuScenes, SECOND-IOU). GPA-3D [17] is outperformed by 3.15/2.82 on Waymo→KITTI and 6.59/1.88 on Waymo→NuScenes (SECOND-IOU). The older methods SN [38] and 3D-CoCo [46] are significantly behind, with SN sometimes performing worse than source-only (e.g., 27.4/6.4 vs. 47.8/11.5 on Waymo→KITTI PointPillars). ST3D++ [43] is only reported for SECOND-IOU and is outperformed by 6.16/8.06 on Waymo→KITTI. In the multi-class setting (Appendix B.3), GroupEXP-DA also shows synergy with REDB [3], further improving over the multi-class baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-training paradigm for unsupervised domain adaptation in 3D detection (ST3D, ST3D++) by introducing a data-driven grouping mechanism that addresses the bias toward dominant object types in pseudo-label collection. It departs from prior methods (DTS, GPA-3D, SN) that each target a single inter-domain factor—density, geometric shape, or size—by instead learning groups via GMM that capture multiple factors jointly. The Group-Equivariant spatial feature, implemented via cross-attention between learned group queries and BEV features, is a new architectural component, while the explorative update strategy draws conceptual inspiration from prototype learning in GPA-3D but applies it to full GMM parameters (mean, covariance, weight) rather than class-level prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, modular add-on to existing voxel-based detectors that can be plugged into most current pipelines without architectural overhaul, making it broadly applicable. However, the method still fails on extremely sparse objects (e.g., distant vehicles with very few foreground points), which remains an open challenge. The multi-class results show synergy with class-balanced pseudo-labeling (ReDB), suggesting the grouping approach could complement other adaptation strategies. A key open question is whether the GMM-based grouping generalizes beyond the 'car' class focus of the main experiments, and whether the number of groups (optimal at 3–4) scales appropriately with scene complexity or class diversity.
