## Problem

Existing check-in sequence models optimize for narrow task-specific objectives (next location, time, or user linking) without deeply understanding the semantics of human visiting intentions and travel preferences embedded in mobility data. While LLMs excel at semantic understanding, they cannot directly interpret structured check-in sequences, creating a gap between the rich behavioral semantics in mobility data and the representational capacity of pre-trained language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241141/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a check-in sequence of POI visiting records (each containing POI ID, longitude, latitude, and category) ordered chronologically, along with timestamps and inter-record time intervals. Outputs are task-specific: a multi-class next-location prediction, a sampled arrival time from a learned Gaussian mixture distribution, and a user identity for trajectory-user linking. The LLM's output is partitioned into α (positions corresponding to VIMN inputs) and β (positions corresponding to HTPP and user embedding inputs), with separate projection heads applied to each part for different downstream tasks.

## Method

Mobility-LLM reprograms a pre-trained LLM (TinyLlama-1B) via three key components: (1) a POI Point-wise Embedding Layer (PPEL) that fuses POI ID embeddings with category semantics via a point-wise attention mechanism over LLM tokenizer tokens, augmented with GeoHash geographic encoding; (2) a Visiting Intention Memory Network (VIMN) built on an Imminent GRU that uses periodic sinusoidal encoding for timestamps and logarithmic time-interval encoding to modulate the forget gate, prioritizing recent check-in records over temporally distant ones; and (3) a Human Travel Preference Prompt (HTPP) pool spanning 3 domains (occupation, activity type, lifestyle) with 16 prompt words each, where cosine-similarity scoring against visiting intentions selects top-K domain-specific prompts as contextual cues. The LLM is fine-tuned with a Partially Frozen Attention (PFA) strategy—freezing early layers while unfreezing the last U attention layers—combined with LoRA adapters, keeping trainable parameters to ~3.4% of the total model size.

## Results

On the primary Next Location Prediction (LP) task, Mobility-LLM achieves MRR of 18.29 (Gowalla), 29.21 (WeePlace), 59.89 (Brightkite), and 26.47 (FourSquare), consistently outperforming all 12 baselines across all four datasets. On Trajectory User Link (TUL), the method achieves an average 47.3% improvement over all baselines, with MRR reaching 83.18–85.73 across datasets. For Time Prediction (TP), Mobility-LLM attains the lowest MAE on Gowalla (353.89 min), WeePlace (28.28 min), and FourSquare (309.78 min), though NSTPP edges it slightly on Brightkite RMSE (435.11 vs. 423.26). In the 5% few-shot setting, Mobility-LLM surpasses the best baselines by 23.6%–38.3% on average, matching full-data performance of other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mobility-LLM achieves the best result on every metric across all four datasets for all three tasks, with no losses to any baseline. On LP, it outperforms the strongest sequence-representation baselines CACSR (17.19% avg. MRR gain) and ReMVC (7.49% avg. MRR gain), and surpasses task-specific SOTA models PLSPL and LSTPM by 9.32% and 19.88% in average MRR respectively. The temporal point process baselines NSTPP and DSTPP are exceeded by over 14.86% and 13.22% in average MRR. On TUL, the gains are most dramatic: Mobility-LLM beats the prior best ReMVC by 17.17% on average and all other TUL-specific baselines (TULER, TULVAE, MoveSim, S2TUL) by an average of 47.3%. On TP, Mobility-LLM achieves the lowest MAE on three of four datasets (Gowalla 353.89, WeePlace 28.28, FourSquare 309.78 min), with NSTPP marginally ahead on Brightkite RMSE (435.11 vs. 423.26). In the 5% few-shot setting, the advantage widens substantially, with average improvements of 45–87% over the strongest baselines (CACSR, ReMVC, S2TUL), demonstrating that the LLM backbone's pre-trained knowledge compensates for limited training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM reprogramming paradigm (Time-LLM, LLM4TS, One-Fits-All) from time series and vision to human mobility check-in sequences, adapting the partially frozen transformer (PFA) strategy from Lu et al. (AAAI 2022) by selectively unfreezing attention layers to capture spatial-temporal dependencies. It departs from prior check-in sequence models (DeepMove, LSTPM, PLSPL, CACSR) that treat each task in isolation with task-specific architectures, instead proposing a unified LLM backbone with domain-specific reprogramming modules (VIMN for temporal intention, HTPP for preference prompts). The time prediction head borrows the intensity-free mixture distribution formulation from IFLTPP (ICLR 2020), while the overall architecture is novel in combining GRU-based memory, prompt-based preference extraction, and LLM contextual processing into a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework demonstrates that a single reprogrammed LLM can handle three distinct mobility tasks (location prediction, user linking, time prediction) with strong few-shot capability, achieving results comparable to full-data baselines with only 5% training data — a practical advantage for privacy-constrained or small-scale check-in datasets. The very large TUL improvement (47.3% average) suggests that semantic preference extraction via HTPP captures user identity signals that task-specific models miss. However, the paper acknowledges a significant limitation: POI embeddings are dataset-specific, preventing zero-shot cross-dataset transfer, and future work on universal POI/user embeddings is needed. The finding that scaling law does not strictly hold post-reprogramming (smaller backbones like Pythia-70M can outperform larger ones on specific tasks) is an interesting observation for practitioners choosing LLM backbones for reprogramming.
