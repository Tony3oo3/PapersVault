## Problem

Existing RLHF frameworks rely on a Bradley-Terry reward model that assumes transitive preferences, a property contradicted by evidence in human decision-making and by the ~70% accuracy of learned BT models in practice. This paper studies RLHF under a strictly more general preference oracle that can capture intransitive and AI-generated preferences, and provides the first finite-sample theoretical guarantees for learning in this setting under KL regularization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt distribution d₀, a reference policy π₀, and either a pre-collected offline preference dataset of tuples (x, a¹, a², y) or online access to a binary preference oracle that returns y ~ Ber(P*(x, a¹, a²)) for queried response pairs. It produces an ε-approximate Nash policy for the max-player — a stochastic LLM policy that is consistently preferred by the KL-regularized preference oracle over any competing policy up to relaxation ε. The preference model is constructed as a generative instruction-following task (predicting which of two responses is better) rather than a scalar reward head, and the KL coefficient η controls the trade-off between oracle alignment and proximity to π₀.

## Method

The paper formulates RLHF as a reverse-KL regularized minimax game between two LLMs (max-player and min-player) under a general preference function P*, and proposes two algorithms with finite-sample guarantees. In the offline setting, PELHF constructs a version space of preference models around the MLE and solves the minimax game under the worst-case preference in that space (pessimism), yielding a suboptimality bound governed by a coverage coefficient. In the online (iterative) setting, the algorithm uses a non-symmetric structure: the main agent exploits by computing the MLE and solving the Nash equilibrium via self-play IPO, while a separate enhancer policy explores by maximizing the information ratio (uncertainty) relative to the main agent, implemented practically via tournament-style rejection sampling. The online sample complexity depends on the eluder coefficient of the preference function class, and the paper shows the framework reduces to the BT-based reward formulation as a special case.

## Results

On the Ultra-feedback test set (3K prompts), the proposed Online ELHF-IPO achieves a gold win rate of 0.78 against the offline IPO reference, substantially outperforming offline IPO (0.50), offline DPO (0.41), and SFT (0.121). On AlpacaEval2 (GPT-4 Preview as judge), Online ELHF-IPO reaches 17.67 win rate, nearly doubling the offline IPO (9.36) and offline DPO (9.33) baselines, while SFT scores only 4.63. The preference model (vs. BT reward model) also shows improved reasoning accuracy on Reward-Bench (80.7% vs. 74.2% for Gemma-2B-it; 94.8% vs. 87.8% for LLaMA3-8B-it).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Online ELHF-IPO decisively outperforms all three baselines on both evaluation metrics. Against offline DPO [Rafailov et al., 2023], it improves the gold win rate from 0.41 to 0.78 (+0.37) and AlpacaEval2 WR from 9.33 to 17.67 (+8.34). Against offline IPO [Azar et al., 2023], it improves the gold win rate from 0.50 to 0.78 (+0.28) and AlpacaEval2 WR from 9.36 to 17.67 (+8.31). Against the SFT baseline (Zephyr-SFT-7B [Tunstall et al., 2023]), the gains are even larger: gold WR from 0.121 to 0.78 and AlpacaEval2 WR from 4.63 to 17.67. The results demonstrate that the iterative online exploration with a general preference oracle (rather than a BT reward model) yields substantially better alignment than purely offline methods, with the advantage most pronounced on the out-of-distribution AlpacaEval2 benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF formulation from the Bradley-Terry reward-based framework (Ouyang et al., Touvron et al.) to a general preference oracle that permits intransitive preferences, while simultaneously adopting the KL-regularized minimax game structure introduced by IPO (Azar et al.) and Nash learning (Munos et al.). The theoretical machinery draws on offline RL pessimism via version spaces (Jin et al., Rashidinejad et al.) for the offline setting and eluder-dimension-based exploration (Wang et al., Xie et al.) for the online setting. The key departure from prior work is the non-symmetric algorithmic design where the main agent exploits while a dedicated enhancer policy explores, which the authors argue is essential for practical implementability with approximate oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample guarantees for RLHF under a general (non-BT) preference oracle with KL regularization, addressing a gap between theory and practice since BT's transitivity assumption is contradicted by evidence of intransitivity in human preferences. The empirical results show the preference model outperforming the BT reward model on out-of-distribution reasoning tasks, and the online iterative approach (Online ELHF-IPO) achieves a substantial AlpacaEval2 improvement over offline baselines (17.67 vs. 9.36 WR). However, the empirical validation is limited to a 7B model with only 3 iterations, the enhancer policy lacks its own convergence guarantee, and the eluder coefficient's practical magnitude for large language models remains unclear—open questions that will determine whether these theoretical insights scale to frontier models.
