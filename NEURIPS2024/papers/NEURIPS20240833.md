## Problem

Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) — a framework encompassing natural policy gradient, TRPO, and PPO — performs regularized 1-step greedy policy improvement, yet multi-step greedy policies (as in AlphaGo and AlphaZero) are known empirically and theoretically to yield faster convergence. No prior work had incorporated multi-step lookahead into the PMD update rule while preserving dimension-free convergence guarantees, leaving a gap between the practical success of lookahead-based planning and the PMD convergence theory established for h=1.

## Input / Output

In the exact setting the algorithm requires full access to the value function V^{π_k} to compute the h-step lookahead Q-function Q_h^{π_k}(s,a) = r(s,a) + γ(PV_h^{π_k})(s,a) at every state-action pair. In the inexact setting it consumes M_0 Monte Carlo rollouts of length H per state at the base case plus M samples per state-action pair at each of the h−1 intermediate levels, yielding a total sample complexity of Õ(S/(hε²(1−γ)^6(1−γ^h)²) + SA/(ε²(1−γ)^7)). The function approximation variant replaces the tabular Q-function with a linear model Ψθ (Ψ ∈ R^{SA×d}), shifting the dependence from state-space size S to feature dimension d via a Kiefer-Wolfowitz target set of size O(d²).

## Method

The h-PMD update rule replaces standard PMD's 1-step greedy improvement with an h-step greedy policy: π_{k+1} = argmax_π {η_k T^π T^{h−1} V^{π_k} − D_φ(π, π_k)}, where T^{h−1} applies the Bellman optimality operator h−1 times before the greedy step, yielding a γ^h contraction factor on the suboptimality gap (versus γ for standard PMD). The stepsize condition η_k ≥ (1/c_k)‖min_{π∈G_h(V^{π_k})} D_φ(π, π_k)‖_∞ ensures iterates remain in the relative interior of the policy simplex. For the inexact setting, a recursive Monte Carlo planning procedure estimates Q_h^{π_k} by sampling transitions at each of h levels and taking max over actions at intermediate levels, with Hoeffding-based concentration bounds propagated through the recursion to control the additive error term 2b/((1−γ)(1−γ^h)). The linear function approximation extension regresses the lookahead Q-function onto features using a Kiefer-Wolfowitz target set, producing a performance bound that depends only on feature dimension d rather than state-space size.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a γ^h-linear convergence rate for the suboptimality gap ‖V* − V^{π_k}‖_∞ (Theorem 4.1), improving over the γ-rate of standard PMD [20] and h-PI [12]. In the inexact (Monte Carlo) setting, the sample complexity is Õ(S/(hε²(1−γ)⁶(1−γ^h)²) + SA/(ε²(1−γ)⁷)), which improves over the Õ(1/(ε²(1−γ)⁸)) of [20] by a factor of ~1/(1−γ) when h > 1/(A(1−γ)). Empirically on DeepSea (64×64 grid, γ=0.99), h-PMD converges in fewer iterations as h increases, and with h=100 the algorithm converges in a single iteration. The function approximation extension yields a bound depending only on feature dimension d rather than state space size S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against standard PMD (h=1) as analyzed in [20] (Johnson et al., NeurIPS 2023), which achieves a γ-linear convergence rate and Õ(1/(ε²(1−γ)⁸)) sample complexity. h-PMD strictly improves both: the convergence rate becomes γ^h (faster for any h≥2) and the sample complexity drops to Õ(1/(ε²(1−γ)⁷)) for sufficiently large h. Against h-PI [12] (Efroni et al., ICML 2018), h-PMD is a strict generalization — h-PI is recovered as the infinite-stepsizes limit — but h-PMD additionally provides stability guarantees in the stochastic/inexact setting where h-PI is unstable. Empirically on DeepSea, h-PMD with h=5,10,20,50 all outperform h=1 (standard PMD) in both iteration count and wall-clock runtime, with the gap widening as h increases. The paper notes that very large h (e.g., h=100) can be counterproductive in practice due to computational overhead, and intermediate values often perform best in more challenging settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper cross-fertilizes two previously separate lines of work: Policy Mirror Descent (PMD), a flexible framework encompassing natural policy gradient, TRPO, and PPO via Bregman divergence regularization [53, 20], and multi-step greedy policy improvement (h-PI) [12], which was previously studied only in the context of synchronous Policy Iteration. The h-PMD algorithm unifies these by replacing the 1-step greedy update in PMD with an h-step lookahead update, recovering standard PMD at h=1 and h-PI in the infinite-stepsize limit. The function approximation extension follows the generative-model analysis of Lattimore and Szepesvári [27], and the paper highlights a natural connection to AlphaZero, arguing that its tree-search Q-values correspond to the lookahead Q_h rather than the standard 1-step Q.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for lookahead-enhanced policy mirror descent, establishing a γ^h linear rate that strictly improves over the γ rate of standard PMD for h≥2, along with improved sample complexity under a generative model. The function approximation extension yields bounds depending only on feature dimension rather than state space size, making the framework applicable to large-scale problems. Open questions include extending to neural network function approximators, incorporating exploration mechanisms, designing adaptive lookahead strategies, and empirically validating the AlphaZero connection at scale. The practical tradeoff between faster convergence and the exponential-in-h computational cost of tree search remains an important consideration for deployment.
