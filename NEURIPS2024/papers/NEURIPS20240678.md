## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models generate inconsistent appearances of the same subject across different prompts due to their stochastic sampling. Existing consistent-subject-generation methods either require external reference images (personalization), perform expensive full-backbone tuning (TheChosenOne), or add auxiliary modules that double inference time (ConsiStory). No prior approach achieves prompt-only consistent generation with a lightweight, efficient tuning that preserves the original model's capacity and avoids increasing per-image inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a descriptive text prompt (e.g., 'a hobbit with robes') as the sole user input; the system generates N=11 base images from which the user selects one target image, and the remaining N-1 become auxiliary samples. At inference, subject-centric prompts (target prompt plus scene/context, e.g., 'a hobbit with robes + walking on the street') drive generation of 1024×1024 images via SDXL in 30 denoising steps. The output is a set of diverse images that all share the same subject identity as the user-selected target, with optional multi-subject support via spatially masked semantic offsets.

## Method

OneActor formalizes consistent subject generation as a clustering problem in latent space, deriving a cluster-guided score function that combines a target-attraction term and an auxiliary-exclusion term (Eq. 6). A lightweight projector φ (5-layer ResNet + linear + AdaIN) maps U-Net encoder intermediates and prompt embeddings to a semantic offset vector c_Δ, which is added to the base-word token embedding to steer denoising trajectories toward the target identity sub-cluster. Only this projector is trained (3–6 minutes on one A100) using three denoising losses over target, auxiliary, and average-auxiliary conditions, while the entire U-Net backbone remains frozen. At inference, semantic interpolation (scaling c_Δ by a controllable factor v) and cluster guidance scales (η₁, η₂) provide fine-grained control over the consistency–diversity trade-off, and setting η₂=0 eliminates any inference-time overhead relative to the base model.

## Results

On the subject consistency metrics (Table 1), OneActor achieves the highest DINO-fg score (0.756±0.073) and second-highest CLIP-I-fg (0.821±0.089), surpassing DreamBooth-LoRA (0.735, 0.833) and all other baselines. On prompt similarity (CLIP-T), OneActor scores 0.620±0.038, ranking second only to Textual Inversion (0.654) while substantially outperforming IP-Adapter (0.446) and BLIP-Diffusion (0.547). In the Pareto-front evaluations (Fig. 8), OneActor establishes a new Pareto front in both the TheChosenOne and ConsiStory settings, Pareto-dominating IP-Adapter, Textual Inversion, and BLIP-Diffusion in the first setting, and dominating ELITE, Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and two ConsiStory variants in the second. Tuning takes only 3–6 minutes (≈4× faster than TheChosenOne's 20 min), with inference at 8.3 s/image (η₂=0) or 11.0 s/image (η₂>0, steps 1–20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneActor Pareto-dominates IP-Adapter, Textual Inversion, and BLIP-Diffusion in the TheChosenOne evaluation setting, achieving simultaneously higher identity consistency and prompt similarity. Against DreamBooth-LoRA, OneActor wins on DINO-fg (0.756 vs 0.735) and CLIP-T prompt similarity (0.620 vs 0.594), while DB retains a narrow edge on CLIP-I-fg (0.833 vs 0.821) and LPIPS-fg (0.296 vs 0.299). In the ConsiStory setting, OneActor pushes the Pareto front forward, dominating ELITE, Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and two ConsiStory variants. TheChosenOne (TCO) is Pareto-dominated by OneActor in the first setting, and ConsiStory (CS) is dominated in the second setting. Qualitatively, OneActor preserves finer subject details (e.g., clothing colors) compared to TCO and CS, and is the only method alongside CS that successfully generates consistent multi-subject images, where DB and TCO fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the consistent subject generation task introduced by TheChosenOne (Avrahami et al., 2024) and the tuning-free approach of ConsiStory (Tewel et al., 2024), but departs from both by reframing the problem through a cluster-conditioned score function derived from a clustering perspective in latent space. Rather than tuning the full diffusion backbone (TCO) or attaching expensive auxiliary modules (ConsiStory), OneActor introduces a lightweight semantic-space projector (ResNet + AdaIN) that offsets a single base-word embedding, leaving the denoising U-Net entirely frozen. The method also draws on classifier-free guidance and latent diffusion model infrastructure, and extends the task to multi-subject generation, style transfer, and storybook visualization—capabilities absent from prior consistent-generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× tuning speedup (3–6 min vs. ~20 min) and the option to avoid any inference-time overhead make consistent subject generation practical for large-scale production workflows (animation, advertising, storybooks), where prior methods were prohibitively slow. The semantic-latent guidance equivalence, formally proven here, offers a general tool for fine-grained generation control beyond this specific task. Key open questions remain: the method struggles with more than ~4 simultaneous subjects, suffers from OOD failures when prompts push outside the target sub-cluster's learned distribution, and inherits entanglement biases from the base diffusion model's latent geometry—limitations the authors flag for future work.
