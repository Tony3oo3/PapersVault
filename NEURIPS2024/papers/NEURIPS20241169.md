## Problem

Existing zeroth-order (ZO) minimax algorithms for nonconvex-concave (NC-C) problems suffer from high overall complexity (e.g., O(d_x ε^{-8} + d_y ε^{-10})) and require very restrictive smoothing parameters (O(ε^4) or smaller) for their ZO gradient estimators, limiting practical robustness. Moreover, no ZO algorithm with convergence guarantees existed for the stochastic NC-C setting, despite stochastic losses being the more common scenario in practice.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only black-box function evaluations of the objective f(x,y) (no gradient or curvature access), with each ZO estimate requiring q_1 and q_2 directional perturbations along random unit vectors scaled by smoothing parameters μ_1 and μ_2. It outputs a point x̂ (drawn uniformly from the iterate sequence) that is an ε-stationary point of the regularized max function ψ(x) = g(x) + max_y{f(x,y) − h(y)} in the Moreau-envelope sense, i.e., ‖∇ψ_{1/2ℓ}(x̂)‖ ≤ ε. In application mode, the output is a perturbation δ (for data poisoning) or model parameters θ, a, b (for AUC maximization) under ℓ_∞ or ℓ_2 ball constraints.

## Method

ZO-GDEGA is a unified single-loop algorithm that pairs a proximal gradient descent step for x with a two-step extragradient (EG) ascent for y, all driven by one-point ZO randomized gradient estimators. The key design insight is interpreting the y-update through continuous-time dynamics: the first ascent step z_{t+1} = prox(y_t + η_y ∇̂_y f(x_t, y_t)) serves as a forward-Euler 'trial' step, while the second step y_{t+1} = prox(y_t + η_y ∇̂_y f(x_{t+1}, z_{t+1})) approximates a backward-Euler discretization, yielding greater robustness to noisy ZO estimates. The x-update uses the auxiliary variable z_t (not x_{t+1/2} as in standard EG), reducing per-iteration cost while preserving the EG benefit for y. The theoretical analysis is conducted in terms of the Moreau envelope of ψ (a stronger stationarity criterion than the coupling function f), and for NC-C problems the authors apply Nesterov smoothing to reduce to an NC-SC subproblem, achieving O((d_x+d_y)ε^{-6}) deterministic and O(d_x ε^{-6} + d_y ε^{-8}) stochastic complexity. Crucially, the smoothing parameters can be as large as O(ε) (or O(ε/√d) under continuity), far more tolerant than the O(ε^4) restrictions in prior ZO minimax methods.

## Results

On AUC maximization with MLP classifiers, ZO-GDEGA achieves the best AUC at larger smoothing parameters: 89.70 on MNIST and 88.66 on Fashion-MNIST at μ=0.05, outperforming Acc-ZOMDA (89.35 / 87.75) by 0.35 and 0.91 units respectively. At small μ=0.001, ZO-GDEGA is competitive (91.60 on MNIST vs. 92.45 for Acc-ZOMDA) but gains a clear robustness advantage as μ increases. In data poisoning attacks, poisoned data generated by ZO-GDEGA reduces logistic regression test accuracy by 2.1%–7.6% more than ZO-AGP on the synthetic dataset and by approximately 8.0% on the large-scale epsilon_test dataset (100K samples, 2000 dimensions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZO-AGP [50] (the only prior ZO algorithm for NC-C problems), ZO-GDEGA produces more effective poisoning data, reducing model accuracy by 2.1%–7.6% more on the synthetic dataset and ~8.0% more on epsilon_test, while also converging faster in both stationary gap and objective value. In AUC maximization, ZO-GDEGA ties or slightly trails Acc-ZOMDA [19] at small μ=0.001 (91.60 vs. 92.45 on MNIST) but clearly surpasses it at μ=0.05 (89.70 vs. 89.35 on MNIST; 88.66 vs. 87.75 on Fashion-MNIST), demonstrating superior robustness to rougher ZO estimations. ZO-SGDA [47] and ZO-Min-Max [33] degrade more sharply as μ increases (e.g., ZO-Min-Max drops to 83.23 on Fashion-MNIST at μ=0.05), confirming that ZO-GDEGA's tolerance for O(ε) smoothing parameters translates into practical gains. The white-box FO-AGP [52] reference shows that ZO-GDEGA's black-box attacks approach near-optimal poisoning effectiveness, validating the theoretical complexity improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extragradient (EG) method of Korpelevich (1976) into the zeroth-order (ZO) minimax setting, combining it with proximal gradient steps in the style of Boç & Böhm (2023) and Nesterov's smoothing (2005) to handle non-smooth regularizers. It departs from prior ZO minimax work (ZO-AGP [50,51], ZO-Min-Max [33], ZO-SGDA [47]) in three key ways: (i) it applies the EG structure only to the y-update rather than both variables, reducing per-iteration cost; (ii) it analyzes convergence in terms of the stronger regularized max function ψ rather than the coupling function f; and (iii) it tolerates smoothing parameters of O(ε) versus O(ε⁴) in prior methods, substantially relaxing robustness constraints. The ε-stationary point definitions follow Lin, Jin & Jordan (2020), and the NC-C-to-NC-SC reduction via smoothing follows Nesterov (2005).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ZO algorithm with theoretical guarantees for stochastic NC-C minimax problems, filling a gap that limits practical applicability in settings like AUC maximization and data poisoning where stochastic losses are standard. The robustness improvement—tolerating much rougher ZO gradient estimations—may be more practically impactful than the complexity reduction, as the AUC experiments show 0.4–5.4 unit gains at larger smoothing parameters where baselines degrade. Open questions include whether lower bounds exist for ZO minimax optimization (none are known), and the authors note extensions to non-smooth and federated settings as future directions. The analysis relies on concavity in y and bounded domain diameter, which may not hold in all practical minimax formulations.
