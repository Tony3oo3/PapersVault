## Problem

Decision-making in large-scale games, particularly auto-bidding in ad auctions, lacks realistic simulation environments that capture the full complexity of real-world multi-agent competition. Existing benchmarks like AuctionGym model the problem as a single-agent contextual bandit ignoring budget dynamics across rounds, while AdCraft approximates competitors with parameterized distributions rather than actual learned agents. AuctionNet addresses this gap by providing a benchmark derived from a real-world advertising platform with 48 diverse auto-bidding agents, 10 million ad opportunities, and over 500 million auction records.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a 175+ dimensional ad opportunity feature vector (user identity one-hot encodings, consumption behavior integers/floats), its own remaining budget, advertiser industry category, time step index, and historical auction logs. The action is a single scalar bid coefficient α_i per time step that uniformly scales the predicted value of every ad opportunity in that step (b_ij = α_i · v_ij), rather than per-impression bids. The environment returns auction results including winning status, assigned slot (out of 3), cost paid, and whether the impression was exposed and converted, with the final episode reward being total value obtained subject to budget and optional CPA constraints.

## Method

The environment comprises three interacting modules. The ad opportunity generation module uses a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) to synthesize realistic user feature vectors: a VAE encoder maps features to a latent space, a diffusion network denoises from N(0,1) in that latent space, and a decoder reconstructs features—avoiding the unreasonable outputs (e.g., negative consumption amounts) that direct diffusion on raw features produces. A separate Multi-Head Attention value prediction network integrates the generated ad features with advertiser category and time embeddings via alternating cross-attention and self-attention layers to predict pCTR, pCVR, and their product as the opportunity value. The bidding module populates the environment with 48 agents running different algorithms (PID Controller, Online LP, IQL, Behavior Cloning, Decision Transformer), where researchers control a subset while the rest remain uncontrollable to reflect real competitive dynamics. The auction module implements GSP with multiple slots (l=3), where slot exposure rates act as discount factors on both cost and value, increasing strategic complexity beyond single-slot auctions.

## Results

Performance is evaluated by normalizing all baselines against the heuristic Abid baseline (fixed bid rate), which is set to 1.0 on the Basic Task. Online LP achieves the best normalized performance among all baselines on both the Basic Task and Target CPA Task, attributed to its robustness without requiring task-specific adaptation. All baselines exhibit a drop in rewards on the Target CPA Task due to the CPA penalty for exceeding the cost-per-action constraint. The evaluation uses 48 agents of 7 types across 7 rounds with permuted agent assignments, reporting average performance over rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five algorithmic baselines plus a heuristic reference (Abid) on AuctionNet's Basic Task and Target CPA Task. Online LP [15] achieves the best normalized performance on both tasks, outperforming IQL [21], Behavior Cloning [30], Decision Transformer [8], and PID Controller [36]. IQL and BC perform notably below Online LP, though the authors note that optimized variants (AIGB [12], trajectory-wise iterative RL [22]) can significantly close this gap, suggesting these methods have untapped potential. The PID Controller [36], a traditional feedback-control approach, serves as a simple but effective baseline. Decision Transformer [8], which leverages sequence modeling for decision-making, also trails Online LP. On the Target CPA Task, all methods suffer reward degradation due to the sparse penalty function p(cpa_i; d_i) = min{(d_i/cpa_i)^β, 1} with β=3, making constraint satisfaction a harder challenge than the budget-only Basic Task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two prior ad-auction simulation environments: AuctionGym, which reduces bidding to a single-step contextual bandit and ignores multi-agent dynamics and budget carryover, and AdCraft, which models competitors via parameterized distributions rather than learned policies. For data generation, it adapts the Latent Diffusion Model (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and VAE reparameterization (Kingma & Welling, 2013) to synthesize realistic ad opportunity features without exposing sensitive platform data. The multi-agent POSG formulation and GSP auction mechanism are standard in the auto-bidding and mechanism-design literature; the paper's contribution is unifying these elements into a single, large-scale, data-driven benchmark rather than proposing a new algorithmic paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AuctionNet fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, multi-agent ad auction benchmark derived from a real advertising platform (Alibaba), with 10M ad opportunities, 48 diverse agents, and 500M+ auction records. Its practical impact is already demonstrated by powering the NeurIPS 2024 Auto-Bidding competition for over 1,500 teams. Key limitations include residual biases in the generative model (e.g., Taobao VIP level 7/8 distributions) and the fact that the environment remains a simulation rather than a live platform. Open questions include whether the benchmark generalizes to other large-scale game domains beyond ad auctions, and whether the generative pipeline can be improved to eliminate the observed distributional gaps.
