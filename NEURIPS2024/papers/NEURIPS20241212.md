## Problem

Existing world models do not guarantee that the effect of an agent's actions is represented in a systematic, predictable way within the latent state space. Performing the same action in two similar latent states can produce very different dynamics (deltas) simply due to how the encoder has structured the latent space, making action effects hard to generalize. The paper asks how to learn world models where actions have coherent, predictable effects on inferred latent states.

## Input / Output

The model takes an observation (RGB image or state vector) and an action as input, encoding the observation into a latent state z_t via a CNN or MLP encoder. A query network f_θ then maps the (z_t, a_t) pair to a compressed code h_t, which together with a_t feeds the dynamics network to produce the predicted delta Δ̃_t; the next latent state is z̃_{t+1} = z_t + Δ̃_t. Downstream, the model outputs predicted future latent states for planning (via Cross-Entropy Method), Q-values for model-free RL, or contrastive predictions for long-horizon state prediction. The key structural detail is that h_t is a bottleneck representation distinct from z_t, specifically designed to carry only the minimal state information needed to predict the action-induced change.

## Method

PLSM introduces a query network f_θ that maps (z_t, a_t) to a compressed code h_t, which then conditions the dynamics network d_θ to predict the latent delta Δ̃_t. The core regularizer penalizes the L2 norm of h_t (β||h_t||²), which the paper shows is equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between the deterministic h_t and a zero-mean isotropic Gaussian, thereby upper-bounding the mutual information I(z_t; Δ̃_t | a_t). This makes the dynamics 'softly state-invariant': the model is encouraged to represent states such that the same action produces similar deltas across different states, while still allowing state-dependent variation when necessary. The full loss combines next-latent-state prediction error (MSE or contrastive) with the h_t norm penalty, and the approach is combined with three model classes: Contrastive World Models (CWM) for long-horizon prediction, Self Predictive Representations (SPR) for model-based planning via TD-MPC, and RePo's GRU-based dynamics for model-free RL with visual distractors. A hybrid variant splits the latent space into a parsimonious half (governed by the bottleneck) and an unconstrained half to handle non-controllable dynamics in environments like Pong.

## Results

On the 26-game Atari benchmark, PLSM-augmented SPR achieves a mean human-normalized score of 67.1%, improving over SPR (61.5%) by 5.6 percentage points and substantially outperforming CURL (26.1%) and DrQ (46.5%). In long-horizon latent prediction, PLSM improves Hits@1 accuracy in 5 of 6 environments, reaching near-perfect accuracy (99.05%) on the 9-object cubes task at 10-step prediction versus 97.45% for C-SWM. In model-based RL on DMC, PLSM yields clear performance gains in 4 of 5 continuous control tasks (acrobot-swingup, finger-turn hard, quadruped-walk, humanoid-walk), and in the Distracting Control Suite it improves hopper-stand, walker-run, and finger-spin over RePo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Contrastive World Model (CWM, Kipf et al. 2019), PLSM improves long-horizon latent prediction accuracy in 5 of 6 environments, with the largest gains in the 9-object cubes/shapes task (near-perfect at 10-step horizon) and dSprites; PLSM underperforms only in Pong, where uncontrollable opponent dynamics make the parsimony assumption less suitable. Against TD-MPC (Hansen et al. 2022), PLSM yields clear return improvements in 4 of 5 DMC tasks (acrobot-swingup, finger-turn hard, quadruped-walk, humanoid-walk) but shows no gain in quadruped-run. Against RePo (Zhu et al. 2024) on the Distracting Control Suite, PLSM produces considerable improvements in the more challenging tasks (hopper-stand, walker-run, finger-spin) by compressing away unpredictable background dynamics. Against SPR (Schwarzer et al. 2020) on Atari, PLSM raises the mean human-normalized score from 61.5% to 67.1% (+5.6 pp), with large gains in UpNDown (10859→29572) and Kangaroo (3617→4887), but losses in games like ChopperCommand and BankHeist where important features are not action-controllable. Compared to CURL (Yarats et al. 2021), SPR+PLSM achieves a substantially higher mean human-normalized score (67.1% vs. 26.1%), confirming that the information bottleneck on dynamics complements the self-predictive representation objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLSM positions itself at the intersection of information bottleneck methods and latent dynamics modeling, departing from RSSM/Dreamer-style approaches that regularize the latent state z_t by instead applying the bottleneck to the dynamics term Δ_t. Architecturally, it inserts a query network f_θ between the latent state and the dynamics MLP, then penalizes the L2 norm of the query output h_t — a technique borrowed from deterministic autoencoder regularization (Ghosh et al., 2019) — to upper-bound the mutual information I(z_t; Δ_t | a_t). The paper demonstrates this as a modular add-on to four distinct world model families (TD-MPC/TOLD, RePo, SPR, and CWM), showing the regularization is architecture-agnostic. Conceptually, it is closest to the past-future information bottleneck (Creutzig et al., 2009; Bialek et al., 2001) but differs by treating the per-step dynamics operator as a random variable whose conditional dependence on the state is minimized, yielding 'softly state-invariant' transitions rather than a fixed low-dimensional dynamical system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, minimal architectural modification (one query network + one L2 penalty term) that consistently improves prediction accuracy, generalization, and control performance across model-based RL, model-free RL, and contrastive world models — suggesting that the systematic structure of action effects is an underexploited inductive bias in world modeling. A key limitation is the assumption that actions have predictable, deterministic effects; in environments with significant uncontrollable dynamics (e.g., Pong's opponent paddle), the regularization can hurt, motivating the hybrid split-latent variant. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to recurrent/partially-observable settings (where the bottleneck would be on history rather than state), to discrete latent spaces, and to controllable video generation systems like Genie and UniSim, where parsimonious action representations could improve generation fidelity.
