## Problem

Automated multi-object tracking and re-identification of wild animals from UAV footage lacks large-scale, ecologically relevant benchmark datasets with ground-truth annotations. Existing UAV animal datasets are either too short for meaningful tracking evaluation, lack MOT ground truth, or are restricted to controlled or semi-controlled settings, leaving a critical gap for tracking animals in their natural habitats across large spatial scales with multiple simultaneously-flying drones.

## Input / Output

The MOT pipeline consumes 5.4K-resolution RGB video frames (30 fps, captured at 80 m altitude with nadir-pointing cameras) and produces per-frame bounding boxes with class labels (male/female blackbuck) and persistent track IDs across sequences up to 193 seconds long. The Re-ID component takes a pair of simultaneously recorded videos from two overlapping UAVs and outputs identity mappings linking the same individual's track ID in one stream to its track ID in the other, restricted to the spatial overlap region. The detection sub-dataset provides 320 images (18.4K bounding boxes) in COCO and YOLOv8 formats, while MOT annotations follow the MOT challenge format and Re-ID uses a custom format with frame-level ID correspondences.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction and benchmarking pipeline rather than a new tracking architecture. Data were collected with three DJI Air 2S drones flying simultaneously over a ~250×300 m blackbuck lek, using a relay technique to achieve 30–120 minute continuous recordings. MOT annotations were generated via DarkLabel's auto-propagation followed by domain-expert verification and custom error-detection scripts; Re-ID ground truth was established through a novel ML-assisted workflow where YOLOv8 detections and ByteTracker IDs pre-label both video streams, and annotators then use movement continuity in the overlap region to link identities without requiring appearance-based individual recognition. For benchmarking, the authors train YOLOv8m (best mAP 0.624 at 5472 px) and Fast-RCNN detectors, then evaluate ByteTrack, OC-SORT, and BoT-SORT (with four Re-ID backbones) using TrackEval, finding that feature-based BoT-SORT achieves the highest HOTA (0.5378) and fewest ID switches (150) compared to localization-only trackers.

## Results

On the BuckTales MOT benchmark, BoT-SORT with the osnet_x1_0 Re-ID backbone achieves the best HOTA of 0.5378, the highest IDF1 of 0.9451, and the fewest ID switches (153). ByteTrack achieves HOTA 0.4950 with 777 ID switches, while OC-SORT trails at HOTA 0.4203 with 8,736 ID switches. Notably, MOTA scores are misleadingly high across all methods (0.87–0.94) because the metric does not penalize identity switches, making HOTA the more informative metric for this dataset. The best object detector (YOLOv8m at 5472 px) achieves mAP 0.624 on the detection subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoT-SORT with Re-ID (osnet_x1_0) outperforms ByteTrack by +0.043 HOTA (0.5378 vs. 0.4950) and reduces ID switches by 80% (153 vs. 777), while OC-SORT is substantially worse on both HOTA (0.4203) and ID switches (8,736). Even without a Re-ID backbone, BoT-SORT already surpasses ByteTrack on HOTA (0.5286 vs. 0.4950) and ID switches (171 vs. 777), indicating that its observation-centric association strategy is inherently more robust in this dense, high-interaction setting. Adding a Re-ID feature to BoT-SORT yields a modest but consistent gain in HOTA (+0.009) and a further ~10% reduction in ID switches. The paper highlights that MOTA is a poor discriminator here—all three trackers score above 0.87—because MOTA does not account for identity switches, a known limitation that becomes critical on long tracks with frequent close-proximity interactions. The authors recommend HOTA as the primary metric for biologists evaluating tracking quality over extended sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale UAV dataset purpose-built for multi-object tracking and re-identification of wild animals in natural habitats, rather than repurposing internet-scraped videos or controlled-environment recordings. It departs from prior UAV animal datasets (e.g., Barrios et al. 2024 for cattle, Desai et al. 2022 for crocodiles) in scale, resolution (5.4K), and by providing continuous video annotations for MOT rather than static detection labels. The Re-ID formulation is a deliberate extension of feature-based re-identification from single-video occlusion handling to a multi-sensor, same-time identity-matching problem between two simultaneously-flying UAVs, a setting previously explored only for vehicles (Wang et al., 2019). Methodologically, the paper repurposes the tracking-by-detection paradigm (YOLOv8 + ByteTrack/BoT-SORT/OC-SORT) as baselines rather than proposing new architectures, positioning itself as a benchmark and empirical study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BuckTales fills a critical gap in the wildlife CV literature by providing ground-truth MOT and Re-ID annotations at a scale (1.2M bounding boxes, 680 tracks, 5.4K resolution) that enables meaningful evaluation of tracker robustness over long sequences with dense social interactions. The lekking context introduces ecologically relevant challenges—rapid speed transitions, close-proximity interactions, and ID-switch-prone moments—that are absent from generic MOT benchmarks. Key open questions remain: cross-day Re-ID for animals without unique markings, multi-class-aware evaluation metrics (current HOTA/MOTA ignore class errors), and computational efficiency (best model runs at only 3 fps, insufficient for large-scale monitoring). The dataset is limited to a single species and behavioral context, so generalizability to other taxa and habitats is untested.
