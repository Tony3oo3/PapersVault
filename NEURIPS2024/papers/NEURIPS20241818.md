## Problem

Existing preference optimization methods (DPO, IPO, ROPO) assume binary, deterministic human preferences, ignoring the distributional nature of real-world preferences that vary across raters. The conservative DPO (cDPO) approach incorporates soft labels via linear interpolation of objectives but suffers from an objective mismatch between preference modeling and text generation, and performs poorly when modestly-confident labels dominate the training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241818/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x, a pair of candidate responses (y₁, y₂), and a soft preference label p̂ ∈ [0.5, 1.0] estimating the probability that y₁ is preferred over y₂. Soft labels are simulated via AI feedback from an instruction-tuned LLM (PaLM 2-L) that scores token log-probabilities under a Bradley-Terry model, with position bias mitigated by averaging over flipped orderings. The output is an updated LLM policy π_θ that generates aligned text responses, with the reference policy π_ref held fixed during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a weighted geometric averaging of LLM output likelihoods in the loss function: the winner likelihood π(y₁|x) is replaced by π(y₁|x)^p̂ · π(y₂|x)^(1-p̂) and the loser likelihood π(y₂|x) by π(y₁|x)^(1-p̂) · π(y₂|x)^p̂, applied to both the policy and reference models. This introduces a (2p̂−1) multiplicative scaling factor in the gradient, which naturally drives the gradient norm to zero when responses are equally preferred (p̂ ≈ 0.5) while preserving full gradient magnitude for confident preferences (p̂ → 1). The modification is applied uniformly to DPO, IPO, and ROPO to yield GDPO, GIPO, and GROPO, reducing to the original objectives under binary labels. A larger β is used in the geometric variants to compensate for the reduced gradient scale, acting as an implicit filter that amplifies learning from reliable preference pairs while suppressing updates from near-equal pairs.

## Results

On the six preference datasets, geometric-averaging methods (GDPO, GIPO, GROPO) achieve consistently higher binary winning rates than their non-geometric counterparts. The gains are most pronounced on Plasma Plan, Skewed, and Stairs (datasets dominated by modestly-confident labels): GDPO reaches 85.48% / 86.88% / 84.32% (v.s. PaLM 2-L) versus DPO's 83.16% / 84.79% / 82.81% and cDPO's 75.96% / 68.64% / 73.17%. GIPO achieves the strongest result on Anthropic Harmless (87.24% v.s. PaLM 2-L, vs. 75.40% for DPO). The average absolute improvement from adding geometric averaging (Ave.Δ) ranges from +0.69% (Reddit TL;DR v.s. GPT-4) to +8.44% (Plasma Plan Skewed v.s. PaLM 2-L), with all improvements statistically significant (p < 0.01, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDPO outperforms DPO on all six datasets (e.g., 85.48% vs. 83.16% on Plasma Plan, 88.90% vs. 86.21% on Anthropic Helpful), with the gap widening as modestly-confident labels become more prevalent. The most striking comparison is against cDPO: on Plasma Plan Skewed, cDPO drops to 68.64% (v.s. PaLM 2-L) while GDPO reaches 86.88%, a 18.2-point gap attributable to cDPO's objective mismatch between preference modeling and text generation. GIPO similarly dominates IPO and cIPO on Anthropic Harmless (87.24% vs. 80.36% and 81.85%), though on Plasma Plan the IPO family underperforms the DPO family overall. GROPO beats ROPO on every dataset, with the largest margin on Plasma Plan Stairs (85.13% vs. 82.81%). Across all comparisons, the geometric-averaging variants win or tie on every dataset, and the average improvement (Ave.Δ) is positive in all 24 evaluation settings (6 datasets × 2 judges × 2 metrics), confirming that the geometric averaging modification is universally beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO algorithmic family (DPO, IPO, ROPO) by replacing the binary preference assumption with distributional soft preference labels and introducing a weighted geometric average of LLM output likelihoods in the loss. The key conceptual departure is from cDPO (Mitchell, 2023), which handles soft labels via linear interpolation of objectives; the authors show this approach suffers from an objective mismatch between preference modeling and text generation. The geometric averaging mechanism draws on the noise-tolerant supervised learning literature (unhinged loss, reverse cross-entropy) but applies it in a novel way to the preference optimization setting, and the gradient analysis connects to the over-optimization concerns raised by IPO and the KL-regularized RLHF objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cDPO achieves the best preference label classification yet worst text generation quality exposes a previously underappreciated objective mismatch in soft-label preference optimization, suggesting that better reward modeling does not automatically yield better alignment. The geometric averaging technique is simple, drop-in applicable to any DPO variant, and shows consistent (if modest) gains, particularly when modestly-confident labels dominate the data distribution. Limitations include experiments on a single small model (PaLM 2-XS), reliance on AI feedback rather than actual human majority-voting labels, and single-seed runs; whether the benefits persist at larger scale or with genuine human soft labels remains open. The paper also raises the practical question of whether current RLHF datasets, which concentrate on high-confidence pairs, are missing valuable signal from the modestly-confident region.
