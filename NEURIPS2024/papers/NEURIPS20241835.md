## Problem

Simple bilevel optimization minimizes a convex upper-level function over the solution set of a convex lower-level problem, but the paper first proves that no zero-respecting first-order method can find an absolute optimal solution, establishing a fundamental intractability barrier. Among existing methods targeting the weaker notion of approximate optimality, none achieves near-optimal convergence rates under standard smoothness or Lipschitz continuity assumptions alone, typically requiring additional conditions like weak sharp minima or strong convexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two convex functions f and g (either L-smooth or C-Lipschitz on a compact convex set Z of diameter D), an initial point x₀, and a target accuracy ε. It outputs an (ε_f, ε_g)-weak optimal solution x̂ satisfying f(x̂) − f* ≤ ε_f and g(x̂) − g* ≤ ε_g. A key practical detail is that the method requires an initial interval [ℓ, u] bracketing the optimal value, obtained by running single-level first-order methods on f and g separately, and uses a warm-start strategy where the inner subroutine initializes from the previous outer-loop iterate.

## Method

FC-BiO is a double-loop algorithm built on a two-step reformulation: the bilevel constraint x ∈ argmin g is relaxed to the functional constraint g(x) − ĝ* ≤ 0, and the resulting constrained problem is further reduced (via Nesterov's reformulation) to finding the smallest root of the auxiliary function ψ*(t) = min_x max{f(x)−t, g(x)−ĝ*}. The outer loop performs bisection on t over N = O(log(1/ε)) iterations, while the inner loop solves the discrete minimax subproblem using either the Subgradient Method (Lipschitz setting) or a generalized Accelerated Gradient Method (smooth setting). In the smooth case, the gradient mapping step reduces to evaluating three projection candidates—onto Z, onto Z, and onto Z ∩ H where H is a hyperplane—making each inner iteration computationally tractable. The resulting iteration complexities of Õ(max{C_f²/ε_f², C_g²/ε_g²}·D²) and Õ(max{√(L_f/ε_f), √(L_g/ε_g)}·D) match the corresponding lower bounds up to logarithmic factors, establishing near-optimality under standard assumptions.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is theoretical: FC-BiO achieves Õ(max{√(L_f/ε_f), √(L_g/ε_g)}) iterations for smooth problems and Õ(max{C_f²/ε_f², C_g²/ε_g²}) for Lipschitz problems, matching the established lower bounds up to logarithmic factors. In experiments on the minimum-norm problem (Wikipedia Math Essential, ε=10⁻⁶), FC-BiO^sm converges fastest in both upper-level and lower-level objectives, with Bisec-BiO showing comparable performance as expected from matching theoretical rates. On the over-parameterized logistic regression problem (rcv1.binary, ε=10⁻³), FC-BiO converges faster than all other tested methods in both objectives, while Bisec-BiO is inapplicable due to the intractability of projecting onto the sublevel set of the logistic loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FC-BiO^sm outperforms all five baselines (a-IRG, Bi-SG, CG-BiO, AGM-BiO, PB-APG) on both the minimum-norm problem and the over-parameterized logistic regression problem, converging faster in both upper-level and lower-level objectives. The only competitive method is Bisec-BiO [28], which shows comparable convergence on Problem (11) as predicted by their identical theoretical rates, but is inapplicable to Problem (12) because it requires a sublevel set projection oracle for f that is intractable for logistic loss. Compared to AGM-BiO [6], which achieves O(max{1/√ε_f, 1/ε_g}), FC-BiO improves the lower-level rate from O(1/ε_g) to Õ(√(L_g/ε_g)) without requiring the weak sharp minima condition. Against R-APM [24], which needs the weak sharp minima assumption to reach O(1/√ε), FC-BiO achieves the same rate under only standard smoothness. The Lipschitz variant FC-BiO^Lip also outperforms a-IRG (O(1/ε⁴)) and Bi-SG (O(1/ε^{1/(1−α)})) by achieving the near-optimal Õ(1/ε²) rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Nesterov's functionally constrained optimization framework (Lectures on Convex Optimization, Section 2.3) and the bisection-based approach of Wang et al. (Bisec-BiO, AISTATS 2024), but departs from both by removing the strong convexity requirement that Nesterov's original algorithm needed and by avoiding the sublevel-set oracle that Bisec-BiO relies on. The key conceptual move is reformulating the simple bilevel problem as a functionally constrained problem with an approximate lower-level constraint, then applying a bisection procedure over an auxiliary minimax function. The hardness results (intractability of absolute optimal solutions for zero-respecting methods) extend the zero-chain constructions from Nesterov's single-level lower bounds to the bilevel setting, building on the general bilevel hardness framework of Chen, Xu, and Zhang (COLT 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intractability result for absolute optimal solutions is a fundamental negative finding that justifies the weak optimality criterion used throughout the bilevel optimization literature, and the near-optimal rates under only standard smoothness or Lipschitz assumptions (no strong convexity, no weak sharp minima, no Hölderian error bounds) represent the first such guarantee for this problem class. The remaining logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds, the restriction to deterministic first-order methods, and the open question of stochastic extensions are natural next steps. For practitioners in ML applications like lexicographic optimization and lifelong learning, this provides a clean, assumption-light algorithm with provable guarantees.
