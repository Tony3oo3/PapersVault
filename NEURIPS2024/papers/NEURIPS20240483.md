## Problem

Despite diffusion models' remarkable compositional generalization in text-to-image generation, the precise mechanism by which they learn factorized representations and acquire compositionality remains unclear. Prior studies on factorization in generative models have reached conflicting conclusions, and diffusion models specifically have been under-explored at the representation level. This paper uses a highly controlled 2D Gaussian toy setting to determine whether and when diffusion models learn factorized representations, and how that relates to compositional generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240483/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The conditional DDPM receives a (μx, μy) coordinate pair encoded via explicit positional encoding (concatenated with timestep encoding) at each UNet block, along with a noisy 32×32 grayscale image and diffusion timestep. The model outputs a denoised 32×32 grayscale image containing a 2D Gaussian bump or SOS (sum of stripes) centered at the specified location. Internal representations are extracted from layer 4 of the UNet (not the bottleneck, due to skip-connection signal dilution) and analyzed via PCA/UMAP dimension reduction.

## Method

The paper trains conditional DDPMs with a standard 3-block UNet (self-attention, group norm, GELU) on parametrically controlled 2D Gaussian datasets, varying grid spacing d and spread σ to tune data density and overlap. To distinguish factorized from coupled representations, the authors impose periodic boundary conditions so the latent manifold forms a torus, then use persistent homology to confirm torus topology, participation ratio to track effective dimension, and orthogonality/parallelism tests (adapted from Cueva et al.) to differentiate a Clifford torus (factorized, 4D) from a standard 3D torus (coupled). Composition vs. interpolation is probed via lesion experiments that remove contiguous ranges of x or y from training, and data efficiency is quantified by mixing full-range 1D Gaussian stripes with subsampled 2D compositional examples. Finally, the authors connect the threshold for manifold formation to continuum percolation theory (Gilbert's disk model), showing that the critical data fraction for learning a faithful representation scales with the overlap between neighboring Gaussians.

## Results

On the 2D Gaussian SOS compositional generalization task (held-out region μx,μy ∈ [13,19]), the 2D-only model achieves high accuracy on μy in the vertical section and high accuracy on μx in the horizontal section, but low accuracy on both coordinates in the intersection—confirming compositionality without interpolation. The 2D+1D model (full-range 1D stripes + 2D SOS with held-out region) achieves high accuracy on both μx and μy across all test regions, while the 1D-only model (no compositional examples) fails to generate the correct μx simultaneously with μy. On data efficiency, models trained on the augmented 2D+1D dataset require only a linear number of 2D examples in image size N to reach 60% OOD accuracy, versus a quadratic requirement for the 2D-only baseline (Fig. 5c). The percolation analysis shows that smaller Gaussian spread σ (less inter-sample overlap) delays the onset of faithful manifold learning, with σ=0.1 requiring λ≥0.8 to percolate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it ablates training data composition to isolate the mechanisms of compositionality versus interpolation. The 2D-only model (trained solely on 2D Gaussian SOS with a held-out square) demonstrates factorized compositionality—it correctly generates one coordinate independently of the other—but fails to interpolate unseen values, yielding low accuracy in the intersection region. Adding full-range 1D Gaussian stripes (2D+1D model) resolves the interpolation gap entirely, achieving high accuracy across all held-out regions, showing that the model needs the full extent of each factor plus a few compositional examples. The 1D-only model (stripes with no 2D compositional examples) factorizes x and y but cannot compose them, defaulting to generating a single stripe rather than their intersection. On data efficiency, the 2D+1D augmented dataset achieves 60% OOD accuracy with only ~20% of the 2D examples needed by the 2D-only baseline, and the scaling in image size N drops from quadratic to linear, indicating that isolated factors of variation plus few compositional examples yield sample complexity linear in the number of factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and conditional diffusion formulations (Chen et al., 2021; Saharia et al., 2023) to study factorization and compositionality in a highly controlled 2D Gaussian bump setting. It departs from prior factorization/compositionality studies in VAEs and GANs (citations 1–11) by using a low-dimensional, fully continuous dataset that enables explicit geometric analysis of learned representations via persistent homology and Clifford torus tests (borrowed from Cueva et al., 2021). Unlike Okawa et al. (2023), who showed multiplicative compositionality in toy diffusion models, this work focuses on the mechanistic representational structure and connects it to percolation theory from physics, drawing conceptual inspiration from factorized coding in biological neural systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of how diffusion models achieve compositional generalization, revealing that they learn 'hyper-factorized' representations that are orthogonal even across different values of the same continuous feature, which explains strong compositionality but poor interpolation. The percolation theory connection offers a physical explanation for the sudden emergence of factorized representations beyond a data threshold, and the finding that training with isolated factors plus few compositional examples yields linear (vs. quadratic) data scaling suggests a practical path toward more data-efficient diffusion training. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D grayscale Gaussian data with a small UNet, leaving open whether these representational properties and percolation dynamics persist in high-dimensional, multi-modal, or multi-compositionality settings.
