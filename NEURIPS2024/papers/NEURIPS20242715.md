## Problem

In Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning with Vision Transformers, the CLS token trained on the source domain encodes domain-specific information that consistently harms target-domain generalization—a phenomenon overlooked by prior CDFSL work. The paper identifies why this happens (the CLS token's structural role as a shared, slowly-adapting token at the input layer causes it to absorb low-frequency/domain information) and asks how to exploit this property rather than simply discarding the token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242715/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is a ViT-S backbone (DINO-pretrained on ImageNet) trained on 64 miniImageNet classes as the source domain, then applied to target-domain RGB images (CropDisease, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, ChestX) in K-way M-shot tasks. During source training, each image's input sequence is augmented with a pseudo-domain token added to a fixed random CLS token; during target-domain evaluation, only the CLS token and image patches are used. Classification is performed either via prototype distance (no parameter update) or by fine-tuning only the classifier head and CLS token on the support set, producing a class label for each query image.

## Method

The key insight is that the CLS token naturally absorbs domain information (manifested as low-frequency Fourier components) because it is shared across all image patches at the input layer and adapts slowly relative to patch tokens. To decouple this, the method fixes the CLS token at random initialization during source-domain training and introduces learnable domain tokens—one per pseudo-domain created by clustering the source class prototypes—that substitute for the CLS token in absorbing domain-specific information. An orthogonal loss (L_orth) explicitly encourages the domain tokens to be orthogonal to the fixed CLS token, reinforcing the separation. At target-domain time, the domain tokens are discarded and the previously fixed CLS token is made learnable, allowing it to efficiently absorb target-domain information with only a few parameters, akin to prompt tuning, while the rest of the backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the 5-way 5-shot CDFSL benchmark, CD-CLS achieves 66.10% average accuracy without finetuning and 67.54% with finetuning (68.05% in the transductive setting), outperforming all prior ViT-S-based methods. Under the more challenging 5-way 1-shot setting, CD-CLS reaches 53.68% (no FT), 54.62% (with FT), and 56.72% (transductive) average accuracy across CropDisease, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, and ChestX. The method consistently leads on all four target domains in every evaluation configuration, with the largest gains on EuroSAT (91.04% 5-shot, 78.41% 1-shot transductive) and the smallest on ChestX due to its extreme domain gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CLS outperforms all ViT-S-based baselines in every setting. In the 5-shot no-FT protocol, it leads FLoR (65.48%) by 0.62 points and StyleAdv (64.53%) by 1.57 points on average; in the 1-shot no-FT protocol, it beats FLoR (52.80%) by 0.88 points and MEM-FS (51.24%) by 2.44 points. With finetuning, CD-CLS surpasses FLoR by 0.71 points (5-shot) and 0.77 points (1-shot), and outperforms StyleAdv by 1.46 and 0.63 points respectively. In the transductive setting, CD-CLS* leads MEM-FS+RDA* by 2.35 points (5-shot) and 1.58 points (1-shot), and FLoR* by 2.60 and 1.10 points. The method is particularly strong on EuroSAT (91.04% 5-shot, 78.41% 1-shot transductive) where the domain gap is moderate, while the margin narrows on ChestX (the most dissimilar domain) where all methods struggle, though CD-CLS still leads by 0.2–0.5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot learning (CDFSL) and Vision Transformer architecture analysis. While prior CDFSL work (StyleAdv, FLoR, MEM-FS, SDT) has increasingly adopted ViT backbones, none had examined the role of the CLS token in domain transfer. The paper repurposes the prompt-tuning paradigm (learnable tokens that absorb specific information) by introducing pseudo-domain tokens that explicitly substitute for the CLS token's natural tendency to encode low-frequency domain information, a mechanism the authors trace to the CLS token's position at the input layer and its sharing across all image patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single learnable vector (the CLS token) silently encodes domain-specific low-frequency information that actively harms cross-domain generalization is a useful architectural insight for anyone deploying ViT in transfer-learning settings. The proposed decoupling strategy is simple (fix CLS, add domain tokens, orthogonal loss) and yields consistent gains across four diverse target domains. However, the absolute improvements over strong baselines are modest (~1–2%), the pseudo-domain clustering is somewhat ad hoc (each class as a domain works best for miniImageNet but not for constructed multi-domain settings), and the analysis is limited to ViT-S with DINO/iBOT pretraining, leaving open whether the phenomenon generalizes to other transformer variants or larger models.
