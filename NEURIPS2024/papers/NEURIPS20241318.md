## Problem

While prior work established a square-root lower bound for excess error bounds under strong local strong convexity and Lipschitz gradient conditions, no matching upper bound existed, and the analysis was restricted to the family of all measurable functions rather than hypothesis-set-specific H-consistency bounds. This paper asks whether both upper and lower bounds can be established under weaker regularity assumptions, and whether the result extends from binary margin-based losses to the broader families of comp-sum and constrained losses used in multi-class classification.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a surrogate loss parameterized by an auxiliary function Φ (convex, twice continuously differentiable, with sign and positivity conditions on its first and second derivatives) and a hypothesis set H (complete in binary; symmetric and complete in multi-class), and produces a tight characterization of the transformation function T(t) governing the H-consistency bound. The key output is T(t) = Θ(t²) near zero, implying the bound Γ(ε) scales as √ε, along with a detailed analysis of minimizability gaps that differentiate surrogate losses once the universal growth rate is fixed.

## Method

The proof proceeds by expressing the transformation T(t) as an integral involving the second derivative of Φ over an interval whose endpoint a*_t is the unique minimizer of a convex function f_t. A novel implicit function theorem with a compact set (Theorem 5.2) is introduced to handle the multi-class case where the minimizer depends on an additional parameter τ over a compact interval. This yields a*_t = Θ(t) near zero, and Taylor's theorem with integral remainder then gives T(t) = Θ(t²) by bounding the integral between constants times (a*_t)². The same argument applies uniformly across binary margin-based losses, multi-class comp-sum losses, and multi-class constrained losses, establishing a universal square-root growth rate. The paper then isolates minimizability gaps as the sole differentiating factor among smooth surrogates, providing conditions for zero or small gaps and comparisons across comp-sum loss families.

## Results

The paper establishes that for all smooth margin-based surrogate losses in binary classification (Theorem 4.2), comp-sum losses in multi-class classification (Theorem 5.3), and constrained losses (Theorem 5.5), the H-consistency bound transformation satisfies T(t) = Θ(t²), yielding a universal square-root growth rate Γ(ε) = O(√ε) near zero. The upper bound is entirely novel, while the lower bound requires weaker assumptions (convexity + C² + Φ′(0)>0, Φ″(0)>0) than the prior square-root lower bound of Frongillo & Waggoner [2021] (which required local strong convexity and locally Lipschitz gradient). This creates a sharp dichotomy: polyhedral (piecewise-linear) losses admit a linear growth rate, while all smooth losses follow the square-root rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a purely theoretical paper, this work does not run empirical comparisons against baseline methods. Instead, it provides theoretical comparisons across loss families: (1) Smooth vs. polyhedral losses — smooth losses (logistic, exponential, squared hinge) all share the square-root rate, while polyhedral losses (standard hinge, max-margin) achieve the strictly better linear rate, confirming and extending the dichotomy observed by Frongillo & Waggoner [2021]. (2) Across comp-sum losses parameterized by τ ∈ [0,2): the H-consistency bound scales as √(2n^{τ−1})·√ε, so the number of classes n enters as a multiplicative penalty that vanishes only at τ=1 (logistic/cross-entropy loss), making it optimal for large n despite slightly larger minimizability gaps at other τ values. (3) The paper shows that minimizability gaps are the sole differentiating factor among smooth surrogates near zero (since all share the same Θ(t²) growth), and provides conditions (e.g., deterministic distributions, realizable settings, or h* matching the pointwise minimizer) under which these gaps vanish, making the square-root bound tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growth-rate analysis of excess error bounds (Frongillo and Waggoner 2021, Bao 2023) to the more informative H-consistency bounds framework introduced by Awasthi et al. (2022) and Mao et al. (2023). While prior work established a square-root lower bound for excess error bounds under local strong convexity, this paper provides both matching upper and lower bounds for H-consistency bounds under weaker conditions (twice continuous differentiability with positive second derivative at zero), and extends the result from binary margin-based losses to multi-class comp-sum and constrained losses. The analysis of minimizability gaps as the key differentiator among surrogates builds on the H-consistency framework's decomposition but provides the first systematic comparison across loss families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal square-root growth rate for all smooth surrogate losses creates a clean theoretical dichotomy with polyhedral losses (linear rate), confirming that smoothness is the fundamental determinant of convergence behavior near the optimum. The minimizability gap analysis provides practical guidance for loss selection: while all smooth losses share the same asymptotic rate, the logistic loss (τ=1) is favored in multi-class settings because the class-count scaling factor n vanishes, partly explaining its dominance in practice. Open questions include whether the square-root rate is truly tight for all practical hypothesis sets (neural networks), and whether the minimizability gap conditions identified here can be verified or bounded for specific architectures and data distributions.
