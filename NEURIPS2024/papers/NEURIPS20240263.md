## Problem

Adaptive Risk Control (ARC) guarantees worst-case deterministic long-term risk control but distributes reliability unevenly across the input space, systematically favoring some data subpopulations at the expense of others (e.g., prioritizing one hospital's tumor images over another's). This paper asks whether one can retain ARC's distribution-free worst-case guarantees while simultaneously providing statistical risk control that is localized in the covariate space, interpolating between marginal and conditional coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step t, L-ARC receives a covariate X_t (an image, a time-series window, or a spatial coordinate), computes non-conformity scores s(X_t, y) for candidate outputs y, and selects a prediction set C_t = {y : s(X_t, y) ≤ g_t(X_t)} where g_t is a function-valued threshold rather than a scalar. After the set is produced, the algorithm receives a bounded loss L_t = L(C_t, Y_t) as feedback. The threshold function g_t(·) = f_t(·) + c_t lives in an RKHS determined by a chosen kernel (typically RBF with length scale l), and its coefficients grow linearly in t unless a truncated variant is used.

## Method

L-ARC replaces ARC's single scalar threshold with a threshold function g_t(·) = f_t(·) + c_t in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, updated online via two coupled recursions: the constant c_t follows the standard ARC update c_{t+1} = c_t − η_t(α − L_t), while the RKHS component f_t undergoes regularized kernel gradient descent f_{t+1} = (1 − λη_t)f_t − η_t(α − L_t)k(X_t, ·), with learning rate η_t = η_1 t^{−1/2}. The kernel choice (e.g., RBF with length scale l and amplitude κ) directly controls the degree of localization: smaller l and larger κ yield more localized thresholds that adapt independently to different regions of the input space. Theoretically, L-ARC achieves a localized statistical risk guarantee (Theorem 1) where the reweighting class W is all non-negative RKHS functions, with an approximation gap A(G, w) that grows with κ, and a worst-case deterministic long-term guarantee (Theorem 2) with a residual constant C(G) = κB that vanishes when κ = 0, recovering standard ARC. The key trade-off is that increasing localization improves fairness across subpopulations but slows convergence and widens the statistical risk gap.

## Results

On the tumor segmentation task (target FNR α=0.1), ARC produces highly uneven FNR across data sources, ranging from 0.08 (CVC-300) to 0.32 (ETIS-LaribPolypDB), while L-ARC equalizes FNR across all repositories and brings the overall test FNR closer to the target. In the beam selection experiment (T=25,000), all methods achieve the target long-term SNR regret, but L-ARC does so with smaller beam-sweep set sizes, reducing the time required for beam sweeping. On the Elec2 electricity demand dataset (T=45,312), both ARC and L-ARC converge to the target 0.9 coverage, but L-ARC reduces the weekend conditional miscoverage error as the kernel length scale l decreases, yielding more balanced coverage across weekdays and weekends.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-ARC is compared against two baselines: standard ARC (scalar threshold) and Mondrian ARC (two separate ARC instances for near/far transmitter regions in beam selection). Against ARC, L-ARC wins on localized risk control in all three main experiments: it equalizes FNR across tumor segmentation data sources (reducing the worst-case FNR from 0.32 to ~0.10), reduces weekend miscoverage error on Elec2, and ensures uniform SNR across the wireless deployment area. Against Mondrian ARC in beam selection, L-ARC achieves comparable long-term risk but with smaller beam set sizes and smoother spatial SNR coverage, since Mondrian ARC's hard region split still produces uneven guarantees at region boundaries. Both baselines match L-ARC on worst-case long-term risk convergence (all reach target α), confirming that L-ARC's advantage is specifically in the statistical localized guarantee rather than the deterministic long-term bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in conformal prediction: the online worst-case risk control of ARC (Feldman et al., 2022), which adapts a single scalar threshold via gradient descent, and the offline localized/conditional risk guarantees of Gibbs et al. (2023), which use reweighting functions to interpolate between marginal and conditional coverage. The key conceptual move is replacing ARC's scalar threshold with a function-valued threshold in an RKHS, where the kernel's length scale directly controls the degree of localization in the statistical guarantee. This departs from Gibbs et al. by operating in a fully online, sequential-feedback setting rather than requiring a fixed calibration set, and from ARC by abandoning the single-threshold structure in favor of a non-parametric, kernel-based threshold function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete fairness failure mode of ARC—uneven risk distribution across data subpopulations (e.g., FNR of 0.32 vs. 0.08 across tumor segmentation sources)—and provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy with a clear trade-off between localization strength and convergence speed. The main open questions are the linear memory growth (mitigated but not eliminated by the truncated variant in Appendix C.3) and the sensitivity to kernel selection, both of which the authors flag as directions for future work. For practitioners deploying calibrated models in high-stakes domains (medical imaging, wireless communications), L-ARC offers a drop-in replacement for ARC that can enforce per-subpopulation reliability without sacrificing worst-case guarantees.
