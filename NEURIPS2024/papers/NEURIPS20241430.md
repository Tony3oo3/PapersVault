## Problem

Prior to this work, the only known algorithm for reliably learning Gaussian halfspaces was the generic reduction to agnostic learning, yielding complexity d^{O(1/ε²)}. The paper asks whether reliable learning is computationally easier than agnostic learning for this natural concept class, and answers affirmatively by providing a significantly faster algorithm along with an SQ lower bound showing the d^{Ω(log(1/α))} dependence is essentially tight.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) where x is drawn from the standard Gaussian N(0, I) in R^d and y ∈ {±1}, along with the excess error parameter ε and the bias α of the optimal halfspace. It outputs a halfspace h(x) = sign(⟨w, x⟩ − t) that is ε-reliable, meaning its false-positive error is at most ε and its false-negative error is at most OPT + ε. The algorithm assumes α is known (handled by an outer guessing loop) and ε ≤ α/3; for larger ε, a trivial constant-hypothesis algorithm suffices.

## Method

The algorithm performs a random walk over a low-dimensional subspace to iteratively align the current weight vector w with the unknown optimal w*. The key structural insight is that although an adversary can corrupt negative labels to hide information in low-degree moments, the positive labels remain clean, guaranteeing that a degree-O(log(1/α)) sign-matching polynomial correlates with the negative (uncorrupted) labels. The algorithm estimates low-order Hermite (Chow-parameter) tensors conditioned on y = −1, extracts a low-dimensional subspace from their top singular vectors, and at each step conditions on a thin strip, projects onto the orthogonal complement of w, and updates w in a random direction from this subspace. A geometric step-size schedule (halving λ after O(1/ζ²) updates) ensures convergence in 2^{log(1/ε)^{O(log(1/α))}} iterations. The SQ lower bound constructs, via infinite-dimensional LP duality and Gaussian hypercontractivity, a label-corruption function that nullifies all moments up to degree log(1/α), proving no SQ algorithm can succeed with tolerance above d^{−Ω(log(1/α))}.

## Results

The main algorithm achieves sample and computational complexity $d^{O(\log(\min\{1/\alpha, 1/\epsilon\}))} \min\left(2^{\log(1/\epsilon)^{O(\log(1/\alpha))}},\; 2^{\mathrm{poly}(1/\epsilon)}\right)$ for reliably learning Gaussian halfspaces, where $\epsilon$ is the excess error and $\alpha$ is the bias of the optimal halfspace. When $\alpha > 0$ is a universal constant, this yields polynomial dependence on $d$ and quasi-polynomial dependence on $1/\epsilon$, a significant improvement over the prior $d^{\mathrm{poly}(1/\epsilon)}$ complexity from agnostic learning. A matching SQ lower bound of $d^{\Omega(\log(1/\alpha))}$ is established, showing the dependence on $\alpha$ is essentially tight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with the prior best reliable learner, which reduces reliable learning to agnostic learning via the reduction of Kalai et al. (2012) and uses $L_1$ polynomial regression, yielding complexity $d^{O(1/\epsilon^2)}$. The new algorithm strictly improves upon this: for constant $\alpha$, the dependence on $d$ drops from $d^{\mathrm{poly}(1/\epsilon)}$ to $d^{O(1)}$ (polynomial), while the dependence on $\epsilon$ becomes quasi-polynomial rather than polynomial in the exponent. The SQ lower bound of $d^{\Omega(\log(1/\alpha))}$ demonstrates that the new algorithm's dependence on $\alpha$ is near-optimal, establishing a strong computational separation between reliable agnostic learning and standard agnostic learning of Gaussian halfspaces. No empirical baselines are compared, as the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reliable agnostic learning framework of Kalai et al. (2012) and departs from the prior best approach—which simply reduced reliable learning to agnostic L1-regression (yielding d^{O(1/ε²)} complexity)—by developing a spectral algorithm over Chow parameter tensors conditioned on the uncorrupted negative labels. The algorithmic core borrows the sign-matching polynomial and random-walk structure from Diakonikolas et al. (STOC 2022) for Massart noise, while the SQ lower bound leverages the infinite LP duality technique of Diakonikolas et al. (COLT 2021) and the hidden-direction framework of Diakonikolas et al. (FOCS 2017). Conceptually, the paper positions reliable learning as computationally closer to semi-random noise models than to fully adversarial agnostic learning, a distinction not previously established for Gaussian halfspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is establishing a rigorous computational separation: reliable learning of Gaussian halfspaces is provably easier than agnostic learning, with the SQ lower bound showing the d^{Ω(log(1/α))} dependence is essentially tight. This challenges the prior assumption that the one-sided (non-convex) structure of reliable learning would make it at least as hard as agnostic learning. Key open questions include whether the quasi-polynomial dependence on 1/ε can be improved to poly(1/ε), whether similar separations hold for other concept classes or marginal distributions (e.g., log-concave or discrete), and whether the algorithmic ideas extend beyond the Gaussian setting.
