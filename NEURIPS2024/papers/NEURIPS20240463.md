## Problem

Mean-field variational inference assumes independence among latent variables across data instances, which is violated in scenarios like graph-structured data, constrained clustering, and multi-latent generative models where instances are inherently correlated. Existing structured VI methods model correlation among dimensions within a single latent variable but cannot scale to capture instance-level correlation across potentially millions of data points, and the closest prior work (CVAE) restricts both prior and posterior to identical tree structures, preventing high-order correlation modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data instances (28×28 images, tf-idf text vectors, or binarized rating vectors) along with optional pairwise must-link/cannot-link constraints or an undirected correlation graph specifying which instances should be correlated. It outputs correlated latent representations (D-dimensional, typically D=10 for clustering or D=100 for matching/prediction) whose joint distribution is a tree-structured or mixture-of-trees Gaussian, from which downstream tasks derive cluster assignments, user-matching rankings, or link-prediction scores. A key design constraint is that only O(N) edge-level correlation parameters are learned by the neural network, while all O(N²) entries of the reparameterization matrix are analytically computed from them.

## Method

TreeVI approximates the posterior with a multivariate Gaussian whose correlation matrix is induced by a tree: only edge-adjacent pairs have free correlation parameters γ_ij, and the correlation between any two non-adjacent nodes is the Hadamard product of edge parameters along the unique tree path. The central technical contribution is a matrix-form reparameterization (Theorem 1) derived from ancestral sampling on the tree, showing that the lower-triangular factor L_z^(T) has a closed-form structure where all non-zero blocks are explicit functions of the O(N) edge parameters, reducing neural network evaluations per epoch from O(N²) (naive Cholesky reparameterization) to O(N) — roughly 2× the cost of mean-field VI. The method extends to MTreeVI, a weighted mixture of M tree-structured posteriors, whose ELBO is lower-bounded by the weighted sum of per-tree ELBOs using the concavity of entropy. The tree structure itself is learned end-to-end via a continuous acyclicity constraint tr(A)exp(A²)=0 on a symmetric adjacency matrix, solved with an augmented Lagrangian method and hard thresholding, initialized by a similarity-weighted DFS spanning tree.

## Results

On constrained clustering, MTreeVI achieves the best performance across all four datasets: 97.5% ACC on MNIST, 82.1% ACC on Fashion MNIST, 96.1% ACC on Reuters, and 90.7% ACC on STL-10, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline DC-GMM by 0.5–1.6 percentage points. On user matching (MovieLens 20M), MTreeVI achieves a Reciprocal Rank of 0.7521, surpassing CVAE_corr (0.7129) and standard VAE (0.3498). On link prediction (Epinions), MTreeVI reaches a Normalized CRR of 0.0203, outperforming CVAE_corr (0.0171) and GraphSAGE (0.0115). Training cost is only 2–3× that of mean-field VI (e.g., ~5s vs ~2–3s per epoch on MNIST for TreeVI vs DC-GMM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On constrained clustering, both TreeVI and MTreeVI consistently outperform all five baselines (VaDE, SDEC, C-IDEC, DGG, DC-GMM) across all four datasets and three metrics. The closest competitor is DC-GMM [24], which also uses a generative model with instance-level constraints but relies on a mean-field posterior; MTreeVI improves over DC-GMM by 0.5–1.6% in ACC (e.g., 97.5% vs 96.5% on MNIST, 82.1% vs 80.5% on fMNIST). C-IDEC [44] is competitive on MNIST (96.3% ACC) but degrades significantly on fMNIST (68.1%) and STL-10 (81.6%), while DGG [42] and VaDE [14] trail by larger margins. On user matching, MTreeVI (RR=0.7521) beats CVAE_corr [36] (0.7129) by ~5.5% relative and standard VAE [18] (0.3498) by over 2×. On link prediction, MTreeVI (NCRR=0.0203) outperforms CVAE_corr (0.0171) and GraphSAGE [8] (0.0115), with the gap over GraphSAGE being particularly large (~77% relative improvement). The synthetic VAE experiment (Table 1) further confirms that tree-structured posteriors capture more correlation than mean-field: MTreeVI achieves ELBO −10.3586 vs −11.1535 for mean-field, approaching the ground-truth log-likelihood of −10.3417.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the correlated variational autoencoder line of work initiated by Tang et al. (CVAE, ICML 2019), which first introduced tree-structured distributions for instance-level latent correlation but restricted both prior and posterior to identical tree structures and could only model pairwise (adjacent-node) correlations. TreeVI departs by decoupling prior and posterior structures, enabling high-order correlations between non-adjacent nodes via path-product propagation, and—critically—providing an O(N)-complexity matrix-form reparameterization derived from ancestral sampling that makes training feasible at scale. The acyclicity constraint for learning the tree structure is inherited from the continuous structure-learning framework of Zheng et al. (DAGs with no tears, NeurIPS 2018), adapted here to undirected symmetric adjacency matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making correlated posteriors computationally accessible: the 2–3× overhead over mean-field VI (versus the O(N²) cost of a fully-connected correlation matrix) opens the door to instance-level correlation modeling in large-scale generative models where mean-field is known to be insufficient. The learned tree structure also offers interpretability, as demonstrated by same-category instances clustering together in the MNIST tree. Key limitations remain: the tree structure is a restrictive approximation of general graph-structured posteriors (mitigated but not eliminated by the mixture-of-trees extension), and the method assumes Gaussian latents with diagonal within-variable covariance. Open questions include whether richer structures (e.g., forests, loopy graphs with approximate inference) can further close the gap to the true posterior, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-Gaussian latent spaces.
