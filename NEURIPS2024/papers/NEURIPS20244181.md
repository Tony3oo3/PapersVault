## Problem

Existing adaptive rank allocation methods for LoRA-based PEFT, particularly AdaLoRA, rely on importance measurements that only consider parameters with minimal loss impact, neglecting the dominant role of singular values in SVD-based matrices and their temporal fluctuations during training. Additionally, these methods struggle to balance fine-tuning effectiveness and efficiency, resulting in a limited rank allocation space where increasing the initial rank nearly doubles training time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained weight matrix W₀ ∈ ℝ^{d₁×d₂} and standard training data, decomposing the incremental update Δ into PVQ where V is a diagonal matrix of singular values. During fine-tuning, it produces salience scores for each singular value within sliding time-series windows, and ultimately outputs a fine-tuned weight matrix where the least salient singular values are zeroed out, retaining only the target total rank r_t. The rank allocation space is controlled by the initial total rank r_i (set to 7.5× r_t in experiments) and the target rank r_t, with pruned singular vectors excluded from subsequent gradient updates.

## Method

SalientLoRA measures the salience of each singular value within a time-series using two complementary components: (1) orthogonality-aware singular value magnitudes, which weight each time-step's magnitude inversely proportional to the regularization loss of P and Q orthogonality, and (2) an influence domain derived from a dependency graph where edges represent significant correlations (Pearson coefficient > β) and slopes (linear regression > γ) between singular value trajectories, de-cycled via DFS to form a DAG. The final salience is a weighted combination s_a = λ·m_a + (1−λ)·I_a. An adaptive time-series window (starting at T_i=10 and expanding to T_f=200 via a cubic decay schedule) accelerates early rank reduction while ensuring cautious, stable allocation in later stages, enabling a higher initial rank without proportional increases in training time.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTaV3-base), SalientLoRA achieves an average score of 89.79 (r_t=144, 0.32M params) and 90.23 (r_t=276, 1.27M params), outperforming AdaLoRA by 0.96% and 1.17% respectively. On instruction tuning (LLaMA-7B/LLaMA2-7B, Alpaca), SalientLoRA scores 5.28 and 5.42 on MT-Bench, surpassing Full FT (5.12/5.25) by 0.12 and 0.17 points while using only 2.4% of the parameters. On NLG (BART-large, XSum), SalientLoRA achieves ROUGE-1/2/L of 43.93/20.81/36.04, exceeding AdaLoRA by 0.64%/0.86%/1.0%. The method also achieves a 94.5% speed improvement over AdaLoRA when the rank allocation space is expanded.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SalientLoRA consistently outperforms all six baselines across NLU, NLG, and instruction tuning tasks. On GLUE (0.32M params), it beats LoRA by 1.56%, DoRA by 0.91%, and AdaLoRA by 0.96% in average score; at 1.27M params, the margins are 1.67% over LoRA, 0.77% over DoRA, and 1.17% over AdaLoRA. On instruction tuning (LLaMA-7B), SalientLoRA (5.28) surpasses not only all PEFT baselines (LoRA 4.89, AdaLoRA 5.06, DoRA 5.17) but also Full FT (5.12) by 0.16 points, using only 2.4% of the parameters. On NLG (BART-large, XSum), it exceeds AdaLoRA by 0.64%/0.86%/1.0% in ROUGE-1/2/L and DoRA by 0.54%/0.36%/0.65%. SoRA is the closest competitor on GLUE at 1.27M params (89.36 vs. 90.23), but SalientLoRA still leads by 0.87%. The key efficiency advantage is that expanding the rank allocation space (initial rank 7.5× target) increases AdaLoRA's fine-tuning time by 108% on average, while SalientLoRA's time increases only 7–9% thanks to its adaptive time-series window.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive rank allocation line initiated by AdaLoRA (ICLR 2023), which introduced SVD-based dynamic rank distribution guided by gradient-based importance scores. SalientLoRA departs from AdaLoRA's point-in-time importance measurement by introducing a time-series salience framework that captures inter-rank dependencies through a correlation-based dependency graph and orthogonality-aware singular value weighting. It also relates to the broader LoRA family (LoRA, DoRA, SoRA, DyLoRA) by retaining the SVD decomposition of the incremental matrix while replacing the fixed or gradient-based pruning criterion with a multi-step, graph-structured salience score. The adaptive time-series window mechanism is a novel scheduling contribution not present in prior adaptive-rank methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94.5% speed improvement over AdaLoRA while simultaneously expanding the rank allocation space (7.5× initial rank) addresses a practical bottleneck: prior adaptive methods forced a tradeoff where larger initial ranks doubled training time, limiting how aggressively one could search for intrinsic ranks. The dependency-graph formulation for measuring inter-rank influence offers a more principled importance signal than per-parameter gradient magnitudes, and the ablation confirms both components (orthogonality-aware magnitude and influence domain) contribute meaningfully. Open questions include whether the dependency graph construction scales efficiently to models with tens of thousands of singular values (e.g., 70B+ LLMs), whether the Pearson-correlation-based dependency detection remains reliable under very noisy training dynamics, and whether the approach generalizes to non-SVD parameterizations such as DoRA's magnitude-direction split.
