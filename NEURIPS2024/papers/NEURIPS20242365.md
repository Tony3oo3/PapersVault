## Problem

ERM-trained classifiers exploit spurious correlations between target labels and bias attributes, causing poor generalization on bias-conflicting samples where these correlations are absent. Existing label-free debiasing methods upweight bias-conflicting samples but lack a principled objective for determining the optimal per-sample weight. This paper formulates a min-max training objective that enforces equal performance across bias-aligned and bias-conflicting groups, then derives a practical resampling algorithm from it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard classification inputs: RGB images at 28×28 (C-MNIST), 56×56 (MB-MNIST), or 224×224 (BAR, BFFHQ, CelebA), and text sequences capped at 300 tokens (CivilComments-WILDS), producing a single class label per sample. Unusually, the training pipeline is two-stage: a biased model is first trained to completion, and its per-sample softmax probabilities are then used to compute disagreement-based resampling weights before the debiased model is trained. The debiased model is initialized from the biased model's weights to satisfy the assumption that the model exhibits higher loss on the bias-conflicting group.

## Method

DPR derives a weighted-loss training objective from a min-max formulation that minimizes the worst-group average loss over bias-aligned and bias-conflicting groups. Under the assumption that the model has higher loss on the bias-conflicting group, this objective reduces to a weighted cross-entropy where each sample's weight is proportional to its bias-conflicting probability. Since bias labels are unavailable, the bias-conflicting probability is approximated by the disagreement probability 1 − p_bias(y|x), computed from a biased model trained with the generalized cross-entropy (GCE) loss, which amplifies reliance on spurious correlations. The debiased model is initialized with the biased model's parameters (ensuring the loss-gap assumption holds) and trained by resampling training examples according to the disagreement probability, combined with data augmentation (random color jitter, rotation) to increase diversity of upsampled bias-conflicting samples. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) shows this procedure simultaneously reduces the loss gap between groups and bounds the expected average loss.

## Results

DPR achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all six benchmarks without requiring bias labels. On the BFFHQ bias-conflicting test set (only 0.5% conflicting samples in training), DPR reaches 76.80% accuracy, improving over ERM by 20.87% and over the best baseline (LC, 70.60%) by 6.20%. On MB-MNIST with a 30% bias-conflicting ratio, DPR attains 94.04% unbiased accuracy, surpassing the next-best method PGD (90.76%) by 3.28%. On CelebA and CivilComments-WILDS, DPR achieves the highest worst-group accuracy (88.9% and 70.9% respectively) among all bias-label-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPR outperforms all six bias-label-free baselines on every dataset where a direct comparison is available. Against ERM, the gains are largest on BFFHQ (76.80% vs. 55.93% on the conflicting test set, +20.87%) and CelebA worst-group (88.9% vs. 47.7%). Against the strongest competing methods PGD and LC, DPR leads by 3.37% on BFFHQ unbiased (87.57% vs. 84.20%), 6.20% on BFFHQ conflicting (76.80% vs. 70.60%), and 3.28% on MB-MNIST at 30% ratio (94.04% vs. 90.76%). On CelebA, DPR edges out CNC and PGD on worst-group accuracy (88.9% vs. 88.8%) and average accuracy (82.9% vs. 81.7% for CNC). On CivilComments-WILDS, DPR achieves the best worst-group accuracy (70.9%) marginally ahead of PGD (70.6%). The consistent wins across synthetic and real-world benchmarks, particularly on the most challenging bias-conflicting test sets, support the claim that DPR's disagreement-probability-based resampling effectively reduces reliance on spurious correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage debiasing paradigm (train a biased model, then use it to identify and upweight bias-conflicting samples) established by LfF, DFA, JTT, PGD, and LC. The key departure is formulating a min-max training objective over bias-aligned and bias-conflicting groups—borrowing the spirit of Group DRO but without requiring bias labels—and deriving a closed-form resampling weight (the disagreement probability 1 − p_bias(y|x)) from this objective under a simple loss-ordering assumption. The use of GCE to amplify the biased model's reliance on spurious correlations follows Nam et al. (2020), while the resampling-over-reweighting choice aligns with An et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPR provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc upweighting strategies for bias-conflicting samples, with guarantees that the loss gap between groups shrinks and average loss decreases. Its consistent SOTA across six diverse benchmarks (synthetic and real-world, vision and text) without bias labels makes it a practical default for spurious-correlation mitigation. However, the two-stage pipeline introduces sensitivity to the biased model's quality and additional hyperparameters (τ, q), and the min-max objective is only approximately realized through resampling rather than directly optimized—open questions include whether a single-stage formulation or adaptive temperature could reduce this gap.
