## Problem

Previous visual table understanding (VTU) methods design task-specific architectures and objectives for individual subtasks (detection, structure recognition, querying, question answering), resulting in modal isolation and fragmented pipelines. The paper asks whether a single large vision-language model can unify all VTU tasks, but two cruxes make this nontrivial: the discrepancy between two-dimensional table structure and one-dimensional sequence representation, and the conflicting image resolution requirements across perception and comprehension tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a document image (resized to 2560×1920 for the high-resolution encoder and 224×224 for the low-resolution encoder, preserving aspect ratio) plus a natural-language instruction, and optionally normalized region coordinates for the table querying task. Outputs are serialized as LLM-generated text: detection yields normalized [x1,y1,x2,y2] boxes separated by '\n' without NMS; structure recognition and querying emit a sequence of '[object_class] [x1,y1,x2,y2]' tuples using five object classes (column, row, column header, projected row header, spanning cell); and question answering produces a free-form text answer. The detection-format representation avoids the verbosity and parsing fragility of HTML/Markdown markup.

## Method

TabPedia employs dual vision encoders—Swin-B at high resolution (2560×1920) for fine-grained local detail and a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 at low resolution (224×224) for global layout—whose features are projected into the LLM's token space via a 2D convolution and a linear layer, respectively. The core novelty is a concept synergy mechanism: 256 learnable meditative tokens are appended to the input sequence [instruction; low-res visual tokens; high-res visual tokens; meditative tokens] and fed into Vicuna-7B, where they adaptively attend to different regions of the multi-source visual embeddings and interpret task-specific instructions, enabling perception and comprehension tasks to share a single generative framework. Training proceeds in two phases: a text-aware pre-training stage that aligns the high-resolution encoder and projectors to the LLM (freezing the LLM and low-resolution encoder), followed by a table-aware instruction-tuning stage on ~2.1M samples across five datasets that unfreezes the LLM and fine-tunes the full pipeline except the low-resolution encoder. Ablations confirm that the meditative tokens contribute the largest share of attention-weighted information to generated answers and that the dual-encoder design is necessary, with high-resolution tokens dominating for structure tasks and both encoders contributing equally for detection and QA.

## Results

On the TQA task, TabPedia achieves 47.8% accuracy on WTQ, 71.3% on TabFact, and 53.5% on the newly introduced ComTQA benchmark, setting new state-of-the-art on WTQ and ComTQA among all compared LVLMs. For table structure recognition, TabPedia reaches 95.41 S-TEDS on PubTabNet and 95.11 on FinTabNet, surpassing the previous best end-to-end method OmniParser by 4.96 and 3.56 points respectively. On table detection, TabPedia achieves 98.4 F1 (IoU@0.75) without NMS, matching the DETR-based TATR baseline. The new ComTQA benchmark (9,070 QA pairs over 1,591 real-world images) reveals that existing LVLMs drop to 13.9–29.3% accuracy, while TabPedia maintains 53.5%, highlighting the benefit of jointly learning perception and comprehension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabPedia outperforms all open-source LVLMs (TextMonkey, Monkey, CogAgent, XComposer2) on WTQ and TabFact, with the largest margin over XComposer2 (+19.1 on WTQ, +9.0 on TabFact). Against closed-source models, TabPedia beats GPT-4V on all three TQA benchmarks (47.8 vs 45.5 WTQ, 71.3 vs 69.3 TabFact, 53.5 vs 27.2 ComTQA) and Gemini Pro on WTQ and ComTQA, though Gemini Pro edges ahead on TabFact (67.9 vs 71.3 is actually TabPedia winning; correction: TabPedia 71.3 > Gemini 67.9). The one notable loss is to DocOwl 1.5 on TabFact (71.3 vs 80.4), likely because TabFact's simpler yes/no verification format favors DocOwl's structure-learning approach. On TSR, TabPedia surpasses OmniParser by ~5 S-TEDS on PubTabNet and ~3.5 on FinTabNet, and far exceeds Donut (95.41 vs 25.28) and EDD (95.41 vs 89.90). For TD, TabPedia matches the DETR-based TATR (98.4 vs 98.4 F1) while eliminating the need for NMS post-processing, and clearly outperforms the Faster R-CNN variant (98.4 vs 89.5 F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

TabPedia builds directly on DocPedia's pre-training pipeline (text detection, recognition, spotting, long-text reading, captioning) and Donut's Swin-B high-resolution encoder, but departs fundamentally from the task-specific detection paradigm (DETR, Faster R-CNN) and markup-generation approaches (HTML/LaTeX) that dominate prior table recognition work. Its key architectural departure is the 'concept synergy' mechanism using meditative tokens, which draws on the broader transformer special-token literature (CLS tokens, DETR object queries, memory tokens) but applies it to harmonize multi-source visual embeddings with diverse task instructions within a single LLM. The Table Querying (TQ) subtask is explicitly positioned as underexplored, extending TSR from cropped table-centric images to full document images with region coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying table detection, structure recognition, querying, and question answering in one instruction-following LVLM, TabPedia eliminates the need for separate pipelines and post-processing (e.g., NMS), enabling compositional workflows like TD+TQ for full-document table parsing. The ComTQA benchmark (~9K QA pairs with multi-answer, calculation, and reasoning questions) addresses a real gap: existing TQA benchmarks use rendered Wikipedia tables that poorly reflect real-world document tables. Key limitations remain: the model cannot handle distorted/twisted tables, cannot answer questions directly from full document images (only table-centric crops), and suffers from slower autoregressive decoding compared to parallel detection. Whether the concept synergy mechanism generalizes beyond tables to other structured visual content is an open question.
