## Problem

Prior work established only an asymptotic O(n^{-1/3}) consistency rate for Contrastive Divergence (CD) on unnormalized exponential families, leaving open whether CD could achieve the parametric O(n^{-1/2}) rate and whether its asymptotic variance could approach the Cramér-Rao bound. This gap was particularly acute because the analysis was restricted to the full-batch setting and provided no non-asymptotic guarantees, making it unclear under what conditions on the number of MCMC steps and the learning rate schedule CD would be statistically competitive with methods like noise-contrastive estimation or score matching.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from an unnormalized exponential family p_ψ, a family of Markov kernels {k_ψ} with invariant distribution p_ψ, a fixed number of MCMC steps m, and a decreasing learning rate schedule η_t = Ct^{-β}. It produces the estimated natural parameter ψ_n together with explicit non-asymptotic error bounds: O(n^{-1/2}) for online CD, O((log n)^{1/2} n^{-1/2}) for offline CD, and an asymptotic variance bound within a factor of 4 of the Cramér-Rao lower bound for the Polyak-Ruppert averaged estimator. The bounds are expressed in terms of problem-specific constants (strong convexity μ, smoothness L, spectral gap α, chi-squared constant C_χ) that characterize the model class and the MCMC kernel.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-asymptotic analysis that decomposes the CD gradient error into two nearly independent sources: a bias term scaling as α^m (controlled by the spectral gap of the MCMC kernel applied only to the sufficient statistics φ and φ², a weaker condition than prior work) and an irreducible additional variance term from using a single MCMC sample to approximate E_{p_ψ}φ. For online CD, they derive a recursion on E||ψ_t − ψ*||² showing that when m exceeds the threshold log(σC_χ/μ)/log|α|, the effective strong convexity constant remains positive and the iterates converge at the parametric O(n^{-1/2}) rate. Applying Polyak-Ruppert averaging to the iterates then yields an estimator whose asymptotic variance is at most 4·tr(I(ψ*)^{-1}), i.e., within a constant factor of the Cramér-Rao bound. For offline CD, the analysis must additionally control a data-iterate correlation term arising from reusing samples across epochs; they bound this via a tail decomposition requiring a ν>2 moment condition, yielding the near-parametric O((log n)^{1/2} n^{-1/2}) rate, and further show that under a φ-discrepancy mixing condition the full parametric rate is recovered as m grows with n.

## Results

The paper establishes that online CD achieves the parametric convergence rate O(n^{-1/2}) (Theorem 3.2), improving over the prior asymptotic O(n^{-1/3}) rate of Jiang et al. [21], provided the number of MCMC steps satisfies m > log(σC_χ/μ)/log|α|. With Polyak-Ruppert averaging, the asymptotic variance of the averaged estimator is bounded by 4·tr(I(ψ*)^{-1}), i.e., within a factor of 4 of the Cramér-Rao lower bound (Theorem 3.3). For offline CD with full-batch updates, the paper proves a non-asymptotic near-parametric rate of O((log n)^{1/2} n^{-1/2}) (Theorem 4.2), a significant improvement over the O(n^{-1/3}) rate previously established.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Jiang et al. [21], who established asymptotic O(n^{-1/3}) consistency for full-batch CD under stronger assumptions (unrestricted spectral gap, subexponential tails via A6). This paper improves the rate to O(n^{-1/2}) for online CD and O((log n)^{1/2} n^{-1/2}) for offline CD, while requiring only a restricted spectral gap (A3) and a slightly stronger moment condition (ν > 2 in A4) instead of the full subexponential tail assumption. Compared to standard online SGD with unbiased gradients [22], CD introduces two additional error sources: a bias term (vanishing as m → ∞) and an irreducible variance term (from the single MCMC sample), which inflates the asymptotic variance by a factor of 4 relative to the Cramér-Rao bound. The paper also contrasts with Noise Contrastive Estimation [6] and Score Matching [7], noting that CD can be asymptotically competitive with these methods for training unnormalized models, though the near-optimality (factor-4 gap) is specific to the CD gradient structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the convergence analysis of Contrastive Divergence initiated by Jiang et al. (2018), who established an asymptotic O(n^{-1/3}) rate for full-batch CD under strong spectral gap and subexponential tail assumptions. The authors adopt the non-asymptotic stochastic approximation framework of Moulines and Bach (2011) to derive explicit finite-sample recursions, and they answer the open question left by Jiang et al. about whether CD can achieve the parametric O(n^{-1/2}) rate. A key departure from prior work is the use of a restricted spectral gap condition (only on the sufficient statistics and their squares) rather than a full L² spectral gap, and the treatment of both online and offline (multi-epoch, minibatch) settings with data-reuse correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that CD can be near-statistically-optimal (within a factor of 4 of the Cramér-Rao bound via Polyak-Ruppert averaging) provides practitioners with a theoretical justification for using CD in the 'long-run' MCMC regime, and clarifies the precise tradeoff between MCMC steps, bias, and irreducible variance. However, the analysis is restricted to unnormalized exponential families (linear in the natural parameter) and requires the number of MCMC steps to grow with sample size for the averaged estimator, limiting direct applicability to the short-run CD variants most commonly used in practice. Extending the framework to general unnormalized models (arbitrary energy functions) is identified as an important open direction, as is understanding whether the factor-of-4 variance inflation can be closed.
