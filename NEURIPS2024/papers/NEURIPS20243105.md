## Problem

The only prior result achieving sublinear bounds in both sampling complexity and query time for density estimation (Aamand et al., ICML 2023) improves over linear bounds by merely subpolynomial factors. This paper asks whether further improvements are possible and answers with the first statistical-computational trade-off lower bound for a data structures problem: any list-of-points data structure using n/s samples with polynomial space must incur query time at least k^{1-O(1)/log s}, showing the prior subpolynomial improvements are essentially tight.

## Input / Output

The data structure preprocesses k distributions over [n] (in the hard instance, each uniform over a random subset of size n/2) and then receives a query consisting of n/s samples drawn from one of the k distributions. The output is the index i identifying the distribution p_i that generated the samples. The query is inherently statistical—formed by Poisson-distributed samples rather than a deterministic vector—which distinguishes it from standard similarity search and is the key source of the trade-off. The lower bound holds in the realizable case (q equals one of the p_i) and for flat half-uniform distributions, making it a special case that extends to the general agnostic setting.

## Method

The lower bound proceeds by reducing the random GapSS (Gap Subset Search) problem to a Uniform Random Density Estimation (URDE) problem, where the statistical nature of the query (Poisson samples from a distribution) makes it strictly harder than the deterministic vector query of GapSS. The main technical contribution is explicitly solving the complex optimization problem inherited from Ahle and Knudsen's (FOCS 2020) GapSS lower bound in the regime w_q → 0, yielding the clean trade-off ρ_q ≥ 1 - O(ρ_u)/log s. For the matching upper bound, the paper presents a simple subset-based algorithm: during preprocessing it samples L random subsets of size ℓ and builds inverted lists mapping each subset to the distributions whose support contains it; at query time it finds a subset contained in the observed sample support, then scans the corresponding list, rejecting candidates by checking whether additional sampled elements fall outside their support. This 'bare-bones' algorithm achieves asymptotically matching bounds with a leading constant of log 2 ≈ 0.693 versus 1 in the lower bound, and experiments show it requires over 6× fewer operations than a tailored elimination baseline.

## Results

The paper establishes a statistical-computational lower bound for density estimation: any list-of-points data structure using n/s samples and polynomial space k^{1+ρ_u} must have query time at least k^{1-O(ρ_u)/log s}, i.e., ρ_q ≥ 1 - 1/s^{1-log 2-o(1)} - ρ_u/(log s - 1). The matching upper bound for half-uniform distributions (Theorem 4.2) achieves query time O(k^{1-ερ_u/(2log(2s))} + n/s) with space O(k^{1+ρ_u} + nk), yielding an asymptotic gap of only a log(2)≈0.693 factor in the leading constant. Experimentally, the proposed Subset algorithm achieves over 6× reduction in operations compared to the Elimination baseline on 100 random queries, and over 20× improvement over the FastTournament algorithm from [1] in a comparable setting (k=10000, n=500, S=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with [1] (Aamand et al., ICML 2023), whose algorithm uses n/log(k)^{1/4} samples with query time n + k^{1-1/log(k)^{1/4}}. This paper's lower bound shows that [1]'s subpolynomial improvement over linear query time (a factor of k^{1/log(k)^{1/4}}) is essentially tight: no data structure can improve the query time by more than a factor of k^{O(1)/log log k} while keeping samples below n/log^c(k). The lower bound is built on the GapSS lower bound of [3] (Ahle & Knudsen, FOCS 2020), which this paper adapts via a reduction from GapSS to URDE. The upper-bound algorithm in Section 4 is described as a 'bare-bones' simplification of [3]'s approach, trading a log(2) factor in the leading constant for significant simplicity. In experiments, the Subset algorithm outperforms the Elimination baseline (a natural half-uniform-specific method) by over 6× in operations across all tested parameter settings, and outperforms [1]'s FastTournament by over 20× in operations (≈20,000 vs. >400,000) while also having subquadratic O(kLℓ) preprocessing versus O(k²n) for the tournament-based approach. The classical results of [17, 11] (Scheffe; Devroye & Lugosi) and [2] (Acharya et al.) achieve logarithmic samples but require linear or worse query time, confirming the trade-off this paper formalizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly answers the open question posed by Aamand et al. [1] (ICML 2023), who gave the first sublinear-in-both-samples-and-query-time bounds for density estimation but only achieved subpolynomial improvements. The lower bound technique adapts the GapSS (Gap Subset Search) lower bounds of Ahle and Knudsen [3] (FOCS 2020) to the density estimation setting via a reduction from set-similarity search to sample-based distribution identification. The upper bound algorithm is a deliberately simplified 'bare-bones' version of the more complex hashing-based approach in [3], trading a small constant-factor gap in the exponent for significant practical simplicity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical-computational trade-off proven for a data structures problem, establishing that the subpolynomial improvements of [1] are essentially tight: one cannot improve query time over linear in k by more than a factor of k^{O(1)/log log k} while keeping samples below n/log^c(k). The lower bound holds even in the most favorable setting (realizable case, half-uniform distributions), making it a strong impossibility result. The key open question is whether a data structure for general (non-flat) distributions can match the lower bound, and more broadly whether similar statistical-computational trade-offs exist for other retrieval problems. The practical algorithm's 6× speedup over a tailored baseline validates the theoretical framework.
