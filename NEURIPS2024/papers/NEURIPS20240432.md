## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods incur high training and storage costs and cannot handle degradations unseen during training, while current parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods such as Adapter, LoRA, and FacT produce homogeneous frequency representations that fail to generalize stably across diverse restoration tasks. The paper identifies that different degradations require fundamentally different representations (e.g., high-pass for super-resolution vs. low-pass for denoising), and this mismatch between degradation-specific needs and the homogeneous output of PETL modules is the root cause of unstable performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image and a task-type indicator as input; the image passes through a task-specific head to produce shallow features, which are flattened into 1D token sequences for the frozen transformer body. AdaptIR internally restores the 2D spatial structure (C×H×W) at each insertion point to perform its multi-branch operations, then projects the result back to the 1D sequence space. The output is a restored high-quality image produced by the task-specific tail, with only ~0.6% of total parameters (≈697K for IPT) being trainable per downstream task.

## Method

AdaptIR is a heterogeneous Mixture-of-Experts module inserted parallel to the frozen MLP in each transformer layer, comprising three orthogonally designed branches that learn distinct representation bases: (1) a Local Interaction Module using depth-separable convolution with low-rank weight factorization for local spatial features, (2) a Frequency Affine Module that applies FFT, performs affine transformations on amplitude and phase maps via depth-separable 1×1 convolutions, and applies iFFT to capture global spatial structure, and (3) a Channel Gating Module that computes a spatially weighted channel vector via a 1×1 convolution + Softmax mask followed by an FFN for channel selection. An Adaptive Feature Ensemble then dynamically fuses the three bases using sigmoid-gated spatial weighting and channel gating to produce a degradation-specific representation, which is added as a residual to the frozen MLP output. Training freezes all pre-trained parameters (backbone, heads, tails) and optimizes only the AdaptIR modules with L1 pixel loss, requiring ~8 hours on a single GPU per task.

## Results

On hybrid degradation tasks (LR4&Noise30), AdaptIR achieves 26.48 dB PSNR on Set5 and 24.96 dB on Manga109 with only 697K trainable parameters (0.6% of the 119M full model), outperforming the best PETL baseline AdaptFormer by 0.38 dB on Set5 and 0.58 dB on Manga109. On single-task comparisons, AdaptIR reaches 37.81 dB on light deraining (Rain100L) and 29.70 dB on denoising σ=50 (Urban100), surpassing PromptIR by 0.77 dB and 0.31 dB respectively while using 139× fewer parameters. In the multi-task setting, AdaptIR outperforms PromptIR by up to 4.9 dB on light derain (41.27 vs 36.37 dB) with only ~10h training on 4×3080Ti GPUs versus ~48h on 8×3090 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptIR consistently outperforms all six PETL baselines (SSF, VPT, Adapter, LoRA, AdaptFormer, FacT) across both hybrid degradation types and all five SR benchmarks, with the largest margins over FacT (up to 1.78 dB on Urban100 for LR4&Noise30) and VPT (up to 2.37 dB on Set5). Against the all-in-one methods, AdaptIR surpasses PromptIR by 0.77 dB on light deraining and 0.31 dB on denoising σ=50 in single-task mode, and by a dramatic 4.9 dB on light derain in multi-task mode (41.27 vs 36.37 dB), while using 139× fewer parameters (697K vs 97M) and 16× less GPU memory. Compared to AirNet, AdaptIR gains 2.91 dB on light derain and 0.82 dB on denoising. Against classic single-task restoration models (RCAN, SAN, SwinIR, FFDNet, RDN), AdaptIR matches or exceeds their PSNR with 20–40× fewer parameters (e.g., 32.71 dB on Set5 SR with 370K params vs SwinIR's 32.74 dB with 11.9M). The simple MoE baseline (same-branch multi-branch structure) already outperforms all PETL methods, confirming that multi-branch design is key, but AdaptIR's orthogonal branch design (LIM/FAM/CGM) adds a further 0.13–0.25 dB over MoE. On real-world denoising (SIDD), AdaptIR leads LoRA by 0.13 dB, demonstrating generalization to unseen degradation types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) from NLP/vision (Adapter, LoRA, VPT, FacT, AdaptFormer, SSF) and all-in-one image restoration (AirNet, PromptIR). It departs from prior PETL work by identifying a fundamental limitation—homogeneous frequency representations across tasks—and proposing an orthogonal multi-branch MoE (local spatial via depth-separable convolution, global spatial via FFT-based frequency affine, channel via gating) to learn heterogeneous representation bases. It also departs from all-in-one methods by adopting a '1+N for N' paradigm (shared frozen backbone plus lightweight task-specific modules) rather than training a single monolithic model, thereby avoiding negative transfer while retaining per-task specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of PETL for image restoration, and the homogeneous-representation diagnosis provides a useful conceptual lens for why generic PETL modules struggle with low-level vision tasks that have diverse frequency/channel requirements. The practical efficiency (0.6% parameters, ~8h fine-tuning on 4×3080Ti) makes it attractive for deployment scenarios where per-task model storage is costly. However, the method is limited to transformer-based backbones (IPT, EDT), uses a fixed structure across all tasks, and the single-degradation gains over strong baselines like FacT are modest; the more compelling results appear on hybrid degradation. Open questions include whether the orthogonal branch design generalizes to CNN-based restoration models, whether task-aware dynamic architectures (as the authors themselves suggest) would further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to additional degradation types like blur and haze.
