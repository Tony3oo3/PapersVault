## Problem

Existing denoising diffusion models for 3D molecular conformer generation either ignore multiscale structure or apply hierarchy in very limited ways, because random noise corrupts both fine atomic details and coarse-grained structural information indiscriminately. This paper asks how to perform coarse-to-fine generation of molecular conformers—first placing fragments, then restoring atomic details—while maintaining SE(3) equivariance throughout the generative process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D molecular graph (atom types, bond types, SE(3)-invariant features) and approximate fragment coordinates produced by RDKit distance geometry (center-of-mass of each fragment's constituent atoms, matched to ground-truth conformers via Kabsch alignment). It outputs an ensemble of 3D atomic coordinate matrices (n×3) representing molecular conformers, conditioned jointly on the molecular graph and the fragment structure. A binary mapping matrix M (n×m) links each atom to its parent fragment, and all operations are performed in the zero center-of-mass subspace to enforce translational invariance.

## Method

EBD replaces the standard random-noise forward process with a deterministic blurring schedule: atom positions are linearly interpolated toward their respective fragment center-of-mass coordinates as t progresses from 0 to T, so that only fine atomic details are progressively removed while coarse-grained fragment geometry is preserved. The reverse process uses an SE(3)-equivariant deblurring network (hierarchical message passing over fragment and atom graphs with equivariant coordinate updates) that directly estimates the ground-truth atomic state x₀ rather than the previous less-blurred state. A key loss reparameterization—substituting μ_θ with (1−(t−1)/T)·f_θ + (t−1)/T·M x̂ᶠ and discarding the time-dependent weight—converts the training objective into a simple MSE between the network's ground-truth prediction and the true conformer after Kabsch alignment, which empirically avoids the degenerate conformers produced by the original previous-state estimator. The entire pipeline requires only T=50 diffusion steps (versus 5000 for GeoDiff) because the blurring schedule confines perturbation to high-frequency (fine-grained) spectral components, leaving low-frequency fragment structure largely intact.

## Results

On the GEOM-Drugs benchmark (δ=1.25 Å), EBD with only T=50 diffusion steps achieves COV-R 92.60%, MAT-R 0.8216 Å, COV-P 66.24%, and MAT-P 1.1237 Å, outperforming GeoDiff (T=5000) on every metric (89.40%, 0.8571 Å, 61.28%, 1.1642 Å) with 100× fewer time steps. On GEOM-QM9 (δ=0.5 Å), EBD similarly leads with COV-P 61.31% and MAT-P 0.3622 Å versus GeoDiff's 53.72% and 0.4362 Å. In chemical evaluation on QM9, EBD produces the most stable conformers with the lowest mean absolute error in energy (0.1812 eV) and HOMO-LUMO gap (0.1253 eV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBD outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric for GEOM-Drugs. The most direct comparison is against GeoDiff (the state-of-the-art denoising diffusion model), where EBD achieves higher COV-R (92.60% vs 89.40%), lower MAT-R (0.8216 vs 0.8571 Å), higher COV-P (66.24% vs 61.28%), and lower MAT-P (1.1237 vs 1.1642 Å) while using only 50 diffusion steps versus GeoDiff's 5000. Against the non-diffusion baselines (RDKit, CVGAE, GraphDG, CGCF, ConfVAE, GeoMol, ConfGF), EBD's advantage is even more pronounced, with COV-R exceeding 92% versus at most 67% for GeoMol. In the chemical evaluation on QM9, EBD achieves the lowest mean absolute error in energy (0.1812 eV) and HOMO-LUMO gap (0.1253 eV), beating GeoDiff (0.2597 eV, 0.3091 eV) and ConfGF (2.7886 eV, 0.4688 eV). The ablation against DecompDiff (which uses the same fragment-conditioned priors but random noise corruption) shows EBD produces more diverse conformers and better corrects inaccurate RDKit fragment coordinates, particularly when the initial fragment structure quality is poor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the blurring diffusion framework of IHDM (Rissanen et al., ICLR 2023), which models forward corruption via the heat equation in image space, to the 3D molecular conformer domain by replacing spectral blurring with a simple linear interpolation toward fragment center-of-mass coordinates. The equivariant deblurring network follows the E(n)-GNN message-passing architecture of Satorras et al. (ICML 2021), extended with a hierarchical atom-fragment interaction term. Conceptually, the paper departs from standard denoising diffusion (GeoDiff) by arguing that random noise indiscriminately corrupts both coarse and fine structure, whereas structured blurring toward a known coarse-grained prior preserves low-frequency geometry and focuses the generative process on fine details. It also extends the hierarchical conformer generation of Qiang et al. (ICML 2023), which applied diffusion only to the coarse fragment step, to a full coarse-to-fine pipeline where the diffusion model itself performs the fine-detail restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that structured blurring corruption enables 100× fewer diffusion timesteps (T=50 vs. T=5000) while matching or exceeding GeoDiff's quality, which is significant for the computational cost of conformer generation at scale. The PSD analysis provides a clear mechanistic explanation: blurring selectively preserves low-frequency (coarse) structure, so the reverse process only needs to restore high-frequency (fine) details. Open questions include how the RDKit fragment prior degrades for larger molecules (where the gap between approximate and ground-truth fragment coordinates widens), whether more expressive equivariant architectures (e.g., local complete frames, spherical harmonics) can further close this gap, and whether the blurring schedule generalizes to other hierarchical 3D generation tasks beyond small-molecule conformers.
