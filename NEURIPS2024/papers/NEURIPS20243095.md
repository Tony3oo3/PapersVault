## Problem

Existing Curriculum Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods struggle to generate suitable intermediate goals in complex environments, often relying on domain-specific knowledge, parametric distribution assumptions, Euclidean distance metrics, or pre-collected offline data. The paper addresses how to generate challenging yet achievable curriculum goals in an environment-agnostic, fully online manner that does not require prior expert demonstrations or task-specific inspection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243095/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model is conditioned on the agent's current state vector (2D position, velocity, orientation, angular velocity in mazes; 25-dimensional end-effector/object state in robotic tasks) and draws goal samples from the replay buffer during training. It outputs a set of curriculum goal candidates via the reverse diffusion process (10 timesteps), from which a single optimal curriculum goal is selected by solving a bipartite matching problem (Minimum Cost Maximum Flow) against the desired goal distribution. The selected goal then conditions the SAC policy for action selection in the next episode.

## Method

DiCuRL trains a conditional diffusion model (ε-prediction architecture) to generate curriculum goals, using a combined loss that simultaneously minimizes the standard diffusion denoising loss (capturing the visited-state distribution), maximizes the Q-function value at the generated goal (ensuring achievability under the current policy), and maximizes the Adversarial Intrinsic Motivation (AIM) reward between the generated goal and the desired goal (progressively shifting the curriculum toward the target). The inherent noising and denoising mechanism of the diffusion process promotes exploration beyond the currently visited state distribution. Goal selection from the generated set is performed via bipartite graph matching with a normalized Euclidean cost, solved with Minimum Cost Maximum Flow. The entire pipeline operates online with SAC as the off-policy backbone, requiring no pre-collected data or domain-specific reward shaping.

## Results

On the three maze environments, DiCuRL reaches a test success rate of 1.0 in 28,333±3,036 timesteps (PointUMaze), 108,428±34,718 (PointNMaze), and 305,833±43,225 (PointSpiralMaze). The closest competitor, OUTPACE, requires 29,800±4,166, 113,333±24,267, and 396,875±111,451 timesteps respectively, while HGG and GRADIENT fail to reach success rate 1.0 on PointNMaze and PointSpiralMaze within the 1M timestep budget. On the two MuJoCo robot manipulation tasks (FetchPush, FetchPickAndPlace) with sparse binary rewards, DiCuRL outperforms both HGG and HER baselines using DDPG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCuRL outperforms or matches all nine CRL baselines across the three maze environments. Against OUTPACE (the closest competitor, also using AIM reward), DiCuRL achieves faster convergence on all three mazes, with the largest gap on PointSpiralMaze (305,833 vs. 396,875 timesteps to 1.0 success, ~23% fewer). HGG and GRADIENT, which rely on Euclidean distance and optimal transport respectively, fail entirely on PointNMaze and PointSpiralMaze (NaN), while DiCuRL solves both. The remaining six baselines (ACL, GoalGAN, ALP-GMM, VDS, PLR, CURROT) are significantly worse: on PointUMaze they require 660K–1,000K timesteps to reach even 0.3 success rate (vs. 25K for DiCuRL), and none reach 0.3 on PointNMaze or PointSpiralMaze within the 1M budget. In the robot manipulation experiments (FetchPush, FetchPickAndPlace with DDPG and sparse rewards), DiCuRL surpasses both HGG and HER, confirming generalizability beyond 2D navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiCuRL sits at the intersection of curriculum reinforcement learning (CRL) and diffusion models, departing from prior CRL methods that rely on optimal transport (CURROT, GRADIENT), GANs (GoalGAN), or uncertainty-based selection (OUTPACE, VDS). While diffusion models have been applied to RL in offline settings for planning or policy generation (UniPi, Diffusion Policy, BESO), this paper is the first to use a conditional diffusion model online for curriculum goal generation, trained jointly with a Q-function and an Adversarial Intrinsic Motivation (AIM) reward in a unified loss. The AIM component is borrowed directly from OUTPACE, but here it is embedded within the diffusion training objective rather than used as a standalone classifier, and the Q-function provides a feasibility signal that prior CRL methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based goal generation can match or exceed nine state-of-the-art CRL baselines in an environment-agnostic, domain-knowledge-free manner, which is practically useful for sparse-reward navigation and manipulation tasks. However, the authors acknowledge two key limitations: the AIM reward may underperform in high-dimensional observation spaces (e.g., images), and the bipartite graph matching selection strategy is a heuristic that could be replaced by more principled alternatives. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based curriculum approach scales to high-dimensional visual inputs and whether the Q-function's critical role (the agent fails entirely without it) suggests a more fundamental dependency on value estimation than on the diffusion mechanism itself.
