## Problem

Density ratio estimation (DRE) methods can output the exact likelihood of any input in a single forward pass, a property lacking in most generative models, yet they have consistently failed to capture the distributions of complex high-dimensional data beyond MNIST. The paper identifies the root cause: a classifier trained only with cross-entropy loss achieves low loss without learning the correct probability vector globally, only near the true label, which is insufficient for the gradient-based denoising that DRE relies on.

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy image x_t (a 3×32×32 or 3×64×64 RGB tensor) as input and outputs a vector of T+2 logits (one per noise level from 0 to T+1), from which a SoftMax yields the probability distribution over noise levels. From this single forward pass, one can extract the denoised image via the input gradient, the exact log-likelihood of a clean image (by evaluating at t=0), or drive iterative sampling (DDPM/DDIM/DPM-Solver) to generate new images. The architecture is a U-Net with timestep-embedding layers removed and the final convolution replaced by a global-average-pooling + linear head producing T+2 outputs, followed by a fixed cumulative-sum layer.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that the MMSE denoiser E[ε_t|x_t] equals √(1−ᾱ_t)(∇_{x_t} F(x_t,t) + x_t), where F(x,t) = log p_{t|x̃}(T+1|x) − log p_{t|x̃}(t|x) is the log-ratio of the optimal classifier's output at the pure-noise and true-noise-level entries. Training therefore combines a cross-entropy loss on the T+2-way noise-level prediction with an MSE loss on the gradient-derived denoiser, computed via automatic differentiation; CE alone fails to enforce globally correct logits, while MSE alone leaves an arbitrary additive function of t unpenalized. At inference, each denoising step steers the sample in the direction that maximizes the probability of the current noise level while minimizing the probability of pure noise, analogous to Langevin dynamics. Likelihood is computed exactly in one forward pass via Theorem 3.2, which expresses p_{x_t}(x) as a product of the classifier's SoftMax outputs at entries t and T+1 and the known Gaussian reference density.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CDM(OT) achieves a state-of-the-art single-step NLL of 2.89 bits/dim, outperforming all other single-NFE methods (best prior: Residual Flow at 3.28) and even surpassing multi-step methods like Flow Matching (2.99, 142 NFEs) and DDPM++CONT. (2.99, ~200 NFEs). For image generation quality, CDM outperforms a same-architecture DDM on CelebA 64×64 (FID 2.51 vs 4.13 with DDPM 1000 steps) and conditional CIFAR-10 (FID 3.66 vs 4.70 with DDPM 1000 steps), while being comparable on unconditional CIFAR-10 (FID 4.74 vs 4.77). CDM computes exact log-likelihood in a single forward pass, whereas DDMs require 1K+ NFEs for ELBO-based NLL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDM(OT) (NLL 2.89, 1 NFE) decisively outperforms all single-NFE baselines: Residual Flow (3.28), Glow (3.35), and RealNVP (3.49), and also beats multi-step methods including Flow Matching (2.99, 142 NFEs), DDPM++CONT. (2.99, ~200 NFEs), DDPM(SDE) (3.28, ~200 NFEs), and DDPM(L) (≤3.70, 1K NFEs). The only method that beats CDM(OT) on NLL is VDM (2.65), but at a cost of 10K NFEs. For FID, CDM outperforms the same-architecture DDM on CelebA across all three samplers (e.g., 4.78 vs 8.47 with DDIM 50 steps) and on conditional CIFAR-10 (5.08 vs 5.92 with DDIM), while being marginally worse on unconditional CIFAR-10 with DDIM (7.56 vs 7.19) and DPM-Solver (7.29 vs 6.91) but matching it with DDPM (4.74 vs 4.77). Against TRE [35], the prior state-of-the-art DRE method, CDM is the first DRE-based approach to successfully generate images beyond MNIST, as TRE fails qualitatively on CIFAR-10 (Fig. 1). CDM also surpasses the DDM baseline in denoising MSE at high noise levels, which translates to better FID in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: density ratio estimation (DRE) methods like NCE and TRE, which learn distributions via classification but have only succeeded on MNIST, and denoising diffusion models (DDMs), which excel at image generation but require many network evaluations for likelihood computation. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) establishes that the MMSE denoiser is the gradient of the optimal noise-level classifier, connecting Tweedie's formula to cross-entropy classification. The method most directly extends Yair and Michaeli's proposal of a single noise-level classifier for DRE, but departs critically by showing that the CE loss alone is insufficient and that an MSE loss on the classifier's gradient is essential for global optimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDM is the first DRE-based method to generate images beyond MNIST, and it achieves state-of-the-art single-step NLL (2.89 bits/dim on CIFAR-10 with the OT scheduler), which is practically valuable for applications requiring fast likelihood evaluation such as out-of-distribution detection or image restoration ranking. The paper's diagnosis of why prior DRE methods failed—CE loss produces locally but not globally optimal classifiers—is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform other classification-based generative approaches. A key limitation is that CDMs require both a forward and backward pass per denoising step, making them more expensive than DDMs; the authors identify architecture design that amortizes this cost as an important open direction, and the method has only been validated on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA 64×64).
