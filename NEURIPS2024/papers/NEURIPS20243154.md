## Problem

Contextual Goal-Oriented (CGO) problems require an agent to reach a goal set specified by an abstract context, but no algorithm has been designed to solve them in the offline setting where only positive context-goal pairs (no negative examples) are available alongside unlabeled dynamics data. Existing approaches either predict a goal and navigate to it (risking infeasibility) or learn a reward model (requiring both positive and negative labels), both of which fail under the CGO data assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independently collected datasets: an unlabeled dynamics dataset of (s, a, s') transitions and a labeled context-goal dataset of (c, s) pairs where s belongs to the goal set G_c. Crucially, goal states in D_goal need not appear in D_dyn, and contexts are not paired with specific transitions. At test time, the agent receives a context c and initial state s_0, and the learned context-conditioned policy outputs actions over the original action space (the fictitious action a+ is never executed).

## Method

CODA constructs an action-augmented MDP by adding a fictitious action a+ that deterministically transitions to a terminal absorbing state s+ with reward 1, while all original actions retain their dynamics with reward 0. This transforms the two separate datasets into a single fully labeled transition dataset: each context-goal pair (s, c) becomes a fictitious transition (s, c, a+, 1, s+, c), and each dynamics transition (s, a, s') is paired with a randomly sampled context c and labeled reward 0. The resulting dataset is then solved by any standard offline RL algorithm (IQL, CQL, PSPI) via Bellman updates, which propagates the sparse positive supervision from goal examples back through the dynamics. The paper proves that under realizability, completeness, and concentrability assumptions, CODA+PSPI achieves a regret bound that decreases with both dataset sizes, requiring only positive data—no negative context-non-goal examples are needed.

## Results

On AntMaze-v2 (6 environments), CODA achieves an average success rate of 70.8%, outperforming PDS (50.5%), Goal Prediction (31.2%), RP (20.7%), and UDS+RP (21.2%). CODA also exceeds the Oracle Reward upper bound (66.6%) on average, demonstrating that the data augmentation strategy effectively leverages positive context-goal examples without needing a learned reward model. In the harder Four Rooms and Random Cells settings (Tables 2–4), CODA maintains a 15–45 point advantage over PDS and Goal Prediction baselines, and generalizes to shifted test context distributions (64.7% vs. 49.9% for PDS under distribution shift).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODA consistently outperforms all four baseline families across all three context-goal complexity levels. Against PDS (Hu et al., ICLR 2023), which learns a pessimistic reward model from context-goal pairs, CODA gains ~20 points on AntMaze-v2 (70.8% vs. 50.5%) and ~44 points on Four Rooms (75.0% vs. 30.6%), with the gap widening as the context-goal mapping becomes more complex. Against UDS+RP (Yu et al., ICML 2022), which assigns zero rewards to unlabeled transitions, CODA achieves a dramatic ~50-point improvement on AntMaze-v2 (70.8% vs. 21.2%), as UDS+RP collapses to near-zero success on medium and large mazes. The Goal Prediction baseline (combining HER [Andrychowicz et al., NeurIPS 2017] for goal-conditioned policy with classifier-free diffusion guidance [Ho et al., 2022] for goal generation) is the weakest at 31.2% average, suffering from insufficient goal examples and infeasible predicted goals. Notably, CODA matches or exceeds the Oracle Reward reference (which provides ground-truth rewards for all context-goal queries) on average, indicating that the action-augmented data augmentation is a more effective use of positive examples than even perfect reward labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bellman-consistent pessimism framework of PSPI (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2021) as its theoretical backbone, and on the contextual MDP formulation for multi-task RL. It departs from prior offline RL with unlabeled data (MAHALO, UDS, PDS) by showing that the specific structure of goal-oriented problems—where only positive context-goal pairs are available—allows a simpler, provably correct solution via an action-augmented MDP that avoids the need for negative examples or reward model learning. The method also subsumes goal-conditioned RL (GCRL) as a special case while extending it to abstract contexts that map to goal sets, a setting the authors note has not been formally studied before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CODA provides the first formalization and provable solution for offline contextual goal-oriented problems, showing that positive context-goal data alone suffices under natural coverage assumptions—a result that simplifies data collection requirements in practice (e.g., robotics play data + instruction datasets). The action-augmented MDP construction is a clean, general-purpose trick that converts a missing-label problem into a standard offline RL problem, making it compatible with any Bellman-based offline algorithm (IQL, CQL, etc.). However, experiments are limited to low-dimensional AntMaze simulations, and scaling to high-dimensional or visual settings (e.g., language-conditioned robot manipulation) remains an open question that the authors identify as future work.
