## Problem

Existing reversible instance normalization methods (RevIN, SAN, Dish-TS) handle non-stationarity by removing temporal statistical measures (mean, variance), which can only capture the dominant trend but are blind to evolving seasonal patterns. The paper shows that signals with identical mean and variance can have entirely different frequency content, making statistical normalization insufficient for time series with shifting periodicity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FAN takes a multivariate time series window of fixed length L=96 with D feature dimensions (z-score normalized) and produces a forecast of variable horizon H ∈ {96, 168, 336, 720}. The DFT-based normalization is applied independently per channel (channel independence), and the hyperparameter K (number of dominant frequencies to extract) is dataset-specific, ranging from 2 to 30 depending on the spectral richness of the data. The output is the full reconstructed forecast combining the backbone's stationary prediction with the MLP-predicted non-stationary component.

## Method

FAN performs instance-wise 1-D DFT on each input channel, selects the top-K dominant frequency components by amplitude, and removes them via inverse DFT to produce a stationary residual for the forecasting backbone. Rather than assuming the non-stationary pattern is unchanged (as RevIN does) or predicting only its statistics (as SAN/Dish-TS do), FAN uses a 3-layer MLP to directly predict the future values of the composite top-K frequency signal, conditioned on both the extracted non-stationary component and the full input. Training is multi-task: an MSE loss on the predicted non-stationary signal plus an MSE loss on the full forecast. The key distinction from prior Fourier-based methods (TimesNet, Koopa) is that the top-K frequency selection is instance-specific rather than fixed globally, allowing FAN to track evolving spectral content across different input windows.

## Results

FAN achieves 7.76%–37.90% average MSE improvements over the original backbones across eight benchmark datasets. On the five primary datasets (ETTm2, Electricity, Exchange, Traffic, Weather), average MSE improvements are 10.81%, 21.49%, 51.27%, 21.97%, and 21.55% respectively. FAN is model-agnostic and consistently improves all four tested backbones (DLinear, FEDformer, Informer, SCINet), with the largest gains on Informer at longer horizons (up to 36.91% MSE reduction at 720 steps on Exchange).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAN outperforms all three normalization baselines (SAN, Dish-TS, RevIN) on 6 out of 8 datasets with the DLinear backbone, with the largest margins on Exchange (0.149 vs. 0.172/0.265/0.190) and Traffic (0.432 vs. 0.514/0.591/0.652). SAN is the strongest competitor, ranking second on most datasets because its temporal-slicing approach partially captures evolving seasonality as trends; however, FAN still leads on 6/8 datasets. Dish-TS and RevIN, which rely solely on mean/variance statistics, consistently underperform FAN by 5–30% in MSE, particularly on datasets with strong seasonal variation (Traffic, ETTh1, ETTm1). On ETTh2 and ETTm1, SAN edges out FAN by a narrow margin (0.001–0.005 MSE), which the authors attribute to the low seasonal variation in those datasets limiting FAN's advantage. Across all four backbones, FAN maintains its lead, with the most dramatic wins on Informer (e.g., 0.168 vs. 0.265/0.472/0.238 on Exchange) where the backbone's sensitivity to non-stationarity is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAN extends the reversible instance normalization paradigm introduced by RevIN (Kim et al., ICLR 2021) and Passalis et al. (2019), which remove non-stationary signals from inputs and reinsert them into outputs. The key departure is replacing time-domain statistical measures (mean, variance) with instance-wise top-K Fourier components, enabling the method to capture both trend and evolving seasonal patterns that prior methods (RevIN, SAN, Dish-TS) cannot represent. Unlike frequency-based forecasting models such as TimesNet and Koopa that assume fixed principal frequencies across inputs, FAN performs per-instance, per-dimension frequency selection, and adds a lightweight MLP to predict how these non-stationary components evolve from input to output rather than assuming they remain unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAN provides a model-agnostic normalization layer that addresses a concrete gap in non-stationary time series forecasting: the inability of statistical-measure-based normalization to handle evolving seasonality. The theoretical proof that removing top-K frequency components reduces spectral variance (and thus enhances stationarity) gives the method principled grounding beyond empirical gains. Key open questions remain: the hyperparameter K is selected via a dataset-specific search heuristic rather than being learned autonomously, Fourier basis cannot represent all periodic waveforms (e.g., square waves), and the aggressive removal of non-stationary components risks over-stationarity for some backbones. Future work on adaptive K selection and richer waveform representations would strengthen the approach.
