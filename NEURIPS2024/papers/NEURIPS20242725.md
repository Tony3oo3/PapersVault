## Problem

Existing Federated Instruction Tuning (FIT) methods handle only client-aware data heterogeneity (variation across clients) while ignoring domain-aware data heterogeneity (variation between data from different domains). When scarce domain-specific data must be supplemented with data from related domains, conventional FIT treats intra- and inter-domain data equally, causing catastrophic forgetting of domain-specific information and sub-optimal performance on each individual domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242725/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen Llama2-7B (int8-quantized, 32 layers) and multi-domain instruction data (e.g., FinGPT for Finance, Alpaca-GPT4 for General, MedAlpaca for Medical) distributed across clients, and produces updated LoRA weights (rank 32, alpha 64) that are merged with the frozen LLM at inference time. A key structural detail is the two-level server hierarchy: intra-domain servers aggregate domain-specific LoRA weights, while a single inter-domain server aggregates only the top-k most important modules (by squared norm) across domains, adding a small S-Comm. overhead (~1M parameters) on the server side without increasing client-side memory or communication costs.

## Method

DoFIT introduces a two-level federated architecture with domain-aware fine-grained aggregation and a proximal perturbation initialization strategy. For aggregation, each intra-domain server first performs standard FedAvg over its domain's clients, then uploads only the top-k most important LoRA modules (ranked by squared norm) to an inter-domain server, where overlapping modules across domains are averaged—this excludes domain-specific interference while retaining shared domain-agnostic information. For initialization, rather than directly overwriting intra-domain weights with the inter-domain aggregated weights, DoFIT computes a proximal perturbation term (the element-wise difference between inter-domain and intra-domain modules, normalized by its L2 norm and scaled by α) and adds it to the original intra-domain modules. This projects inter-domain information into a parameter region least conflicted by intra-domain updates, preserving domain-specific knowledge and alleviating catastrophic forgetting. The approach requires minimal modification to the standard FIT pipeline, keeping the same client-side LoRA training and FedAgg aggregation while inserting the inter-domain server and perturbation-based initialization between rounds.

## Results

On the Finance&General (F&G) cross-domain setting, DoFIT (α=1.0) achieves an average accuracy of 0.849 and F1 of 0.858 across FPB, FiQA-SA, and TFNS (Avg:3), surpassing conventional FIT (0.801/0.813) and DoFIT-base (0.841/0.851). On the Medical&General (M&G) setting, DoFIT (α=1.2) reaches 0.261 accuracy on MedQA and 0.255 on MedMCQA (Avg 0.258), a substantial improvement over FIT (0.196 Avg) and DoFIT-base (0.205 Avg). The method also outperforms GPT-4 (Avg:3 Acc 0.731) and GPT-3.5 (0.725) on the Finance domain evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional FIT (OpenFedLLM, Ye et al. [35]), DoFIT consistently wins on cross-domain settings: on F&G, DoFIT (α=1.0) improves Avg:3 accuracy from 0.801 to 0.849 (+4.8 pp) and Avg:4 from 0.761 to 0.797 (+3.6 pp); on M&G, the Avg accuracy jumps from 0.196 to 0.258 (+6.2 pp). Against GPT-4 (Achiam et al. [1]) used as a reference on the Finance domain, DoFIT (α=1.0) achieves Avg:3 accuracy of 0.849 versus GPT-4's 0.731, a 11.8 pp margin, while also exceeding GPT-3.5 (0.725). Against federated domain adaptation methods FedGP and FedGP-g (Jiang et al. [8]), DoFIT (α=1.0) outperforms FedGP by 5.4 pp on Avg:3 accuracy (0.849 vs. 0.795) and by 15.8 pp over FedGP-g (0.849 vs. 0.739), demonstrating that fine-grained inter-domain aggregation and proximal perturbation initialization are more effective than gradient projection or prototype clustering for LLM instruction tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Federated Instruction Tuning (FIT) paradigm established by Zhang et al. (FederatedGPT, ICASSP 2024) and OpenFedLLM (Ye et al., 2024), which handle client-aware data heterogeneity via LoRA-based federated aggregation. The key departure is introducing domain-aware data heterogeneity as a distinct axis: rather than treating all clients uniformly, DoFIT separates intra-domain and inter-domain server sides, performing fine-grained top-k module aggregation across domains (drawing on LoRA-Drop's importance scoring) and a proximal perturbation initialization inspired by FedSol's orthogonal learning. The method retains LoRA as the PEFT backbone and FedAvg as the intra-domain aggregation rule, making it a minimal architectural modification to existing FIT pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoFIT addresses a practically important gap: when domain-specific instruction data is scarce, supplementing with related-domain data via conventional FIT actually degrades per-domain performance due to catastrophic forgetting, a problem the paper demonstrates empirically. The proximal perturbation initialization is a simple, low-overhead modification that could be adopted in existing FIT deployments. However, the evaluation is limited to two-domain settings (one specific + General), a single base model (Llama2-7B), and the Medical domain absolute accuracies remain very low (~0.26), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more than two domains, to more closely related domain pairs, or to larger models. The paper itself flags multi-domain extension and new optimization strategies as future work.
