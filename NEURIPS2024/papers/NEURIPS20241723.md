## Problem

In offline RL, Q-values for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions are systematically overestimated due to distribution shift, causing the learning policy to exploit unreliable estimates. Existing pessimistic methods either apply uniform penalties across all actions (leading to excessive conservatism) or rely on ensemble variance that lacks sensitivity to OOD actions. The key gap is a mechanism to selectively penalize Q-values only where uncertainty is genuinely high, while preserving optimistic learning in in-distribution regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset (state, action, reward, next-state tuples) generated by a behavior policy. A k-step sliding window of width T is applied over each trajectory to produce truncated Q-values, which form the training set for the consistency model. The outputs are a learned Q-value function Q(s,a), a conditional Q-value distribution p(Q|s,a) parameterized by the consistency model, and a stochastic policy π(a|s) that selects actions. The consistency model is trained via consistency distillation from a pretrained diffusion model, requiring only a single forward pass for sampling.

## Method

QDQ trains a conditional consistency model f_θ(x_T, T|(s,a)) on truncated Q-values (computed via a k-step sliding window over trajectories) to learn the behavior policy's Q-value distribution. Uncertainty for each (s,a) is estimated by drawing n prior noises, performing one-step denoising through the consistency model, and computing the variance of the resulting Q-samples. Actions whose uncertainty exceeds the upper β-quantile are flagged as OOD; their Q-targets are divided by the uncertainty estimate (1/H_Q), while in-distribution targets are scaled by β. The Q-network is trained with an uncertainty-aware loss that blends the standard optimistic Bellman residual (weight α) with a pessimistic Bellman residual using the uncertainty-penalized target (weight 1−α), yielding a cγ-contraction Bellman operator that converges to a fixed point close to the optimal Q-value in in-distribution regions while remaining pessimistic in OOD regions.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark (9 tasks, 5 seeds), QDQ achieves a total normalized score of 848.0±11.8, outperforming the strongest baseline EDAC (841.1) and IQL (698.5). On AntMaze (6 tasks), QDQ reaches 400.1±23.0, surpassing IQL (378) and CQL (229.8). QDQ's largest gains appear on medium and medium-replay datasets (e.g., halfcheetah-medium: 74.1 vs. 65.9 for EDAC; walker2d-medium-replay: 93.2 vs. 87.1), where its uncertainty-aware objective avoids over-penalizing in-distribution Q-values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDQ outperforms all 11 baselines in aggregate on Gym-MuJoCo (848.0 total), edging out EDAC (841.1) and MCQ (797.4), with the largest margins on medium and medium-replay datasets where over-conservative penalties hurt most (e.g., +8.2 over EDAC on ha-med, +6.1 on wa-med-r). On AntMaze, QDQ leads IQL (400.1 vs. 378) and CQL (229.8) overall, winning decisively on umaze (98.6 vs. 87.5) and medium-diverse (85.4 vs. 70.0), but trails CQL on umaze-diverse (67.8 vs. 84.0) and IQL on large-play/large-diverse (35.6/31.2 vs. 39.6/47.5), suggesting the consistency-model uncertainty estimate is less effective on very narrow, high-dimensional navigation tasks. Among uncertainty-based baselines, QDQ consistently beats UWAC (437.4 total) and PBRL (759.4), while EDAC and MCQ are the closest competitors, winning on 3 of 9 Gym-MuJoCo tasks (ho-med, wa-med, ha-med-e) where the action space is narrow or the behavior policy is already near-optimal. Policy-constraint methods (BC, AWAC, TD3+BC) and sequence-modeling approaches (DT, Onestep RL) are substantially outperformed across nearly all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

QDQ sits at the intersection of pessimistic Q-learning (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) and distributional RL, but departs from prior uncertainty estimation approaches (ensembles, bootstrapping, quantile regression) by learning the full conditional Q-value distribution via a consistency model trained through consistency distillation. The sliding-window construction of truncated Q-value datasets is a practical contribution that addresses sparsity in trajectory-level data, and the uncertainty-aware loss (Eq. 7) that blends optimistic and pessimistic Bellman residuals is a new formulation not present in prior pessimistic methods. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the consistency model's one-step sampling property—originally designed for efficient image generation—as a mechanism for robust, action-sensitive uncertainty quantification in RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded alternative to ensemble-based uncertainty in offline RL, with four theorems establishing convergence of the truncated Q-distribution, sensitivity of consistency-model variance to OOD actions, contraction of the modified Bellman operator, and proximity of the learned Q to the optimal Q. However, the method introduces multiple interacting hyperparameters (α, β, γ, window width, quantile threshold) that require task-specific tuning, and the performance gains, while consistent across D4RL, are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the consistency model's advantage over diffusion models for uncertainty estimation holds at larger scale, whether the approach extends naturally to goal-conditioned or multi-task settings, and whether the theoretical guarantees translate to more complex continuous control or real-robot domains.
