## Problem

Existing fine-tuning-free VLM debiasing methods apply a single global linear projection to all queries, assuming the bias direction is constant across inputs, while fine-tuning-based approaches suffer from catastrophic forgetting. This paper tackles the harder setting of online, open-set debiasing where the set of query classes is unknown at inference time and the interaction between protected attributes and query semantics is nonlinear and instance-dependent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241997/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a free-form text query (e.g., 'a photo of a nurse') and a pre-labeled reference image dataset with protected attribute annotations (gender, race), requiring no access to the downstream target dataset. It produces a unit-norm debiased text embedding in the same d-dimensional space as the original VLM encoder, ready for retrieval, zero-shot classification, or conditioning a captioning model. A 7B LLM (Mistral-7B-Instruct) is invoked at test time to generate attribute-augmented query variants, and n=100 nearest reference images per attribute group are selected for the equalization step.

## Method

BEND-VLM is a two-stage, fine-tuning-free test-time debiasing pipeline. Step 1 constructs a query-specific local attribute subspace by using an LLM to generate attribute-augmented variants of the query (e.g., 'a male nurse', 'a female nurse'), computing their embedding differences from the original query, and orthogonalizing the text embedding to this subspace (augmented with generic attribute embeddings for robustness). Step 2 then equalizes the debiased embedding's similarity to relevant reference images across attribute groups: it selects the n most similar reference images per attribute value and solves a constrained optimization that minimizes distance to the Step-1 embedding while enforcing equal average cosine similarity to each attribute group's image set. For binary attributes with unit-norm embeddings, this yields a closed-form solution via Lagrange multipliers; for multi-valued attributes, a general constrained solver is used. The key distinction from prior work is that the bias direction is computed per-query rather than globally, and the second stage uses image-based equalization rather than relying solely on text-space projection.

## Results

On UTKFACE with CLIP-ViT-B-P16, BEND-VLM reduces KL divergence from 0.114 (baseline) to 0.041 for race debiasing and from 0.120 to 0.008 for gender debiasing, while reducing MaxSkew from 0.451 to 0.371 (race) and 0.308 to 0.097 (gender). On FAIRFACE, BEND-VLM achieves KL divergence of 0.084 (race) and 0.006 (gender) versus baselines of 0.234 and 0.133. For zero-shot classification on CELEBA HAIRCOLOR queries, BEND-VLM achieves Worst Group AUC of 0.772 (ViT-B-P16) and 0.802 (ViT-L-P14) while maintaining the lowest KL divergence (0.016 and 0.011 respectively), matching or exceeding the accuracy of Orth-Proj. and Orth-Cal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEND-VLM consistently outperforms all four baselines on both bias metrics (KL Divergence and MaxSkew) across all three datasets and both CLIP model sizes. Compared to Orth-Proj. and Orth-Cal. (both from Chuang et al. [10]), BEND-VLM achieves substantially lower KL divergence — e.g., 0.041 vs. 0.259 for race on UTKFACE — while also matching or exceeding their Worst Group AUC (0.772 vs. 0.775 for Orth-Proj. on CELEBA HAIRCOLOR). DebiasCLIP [6] improves over the baseline on bias but still lags BEND-VLM by a wide margin (e.g., KL Div. 0.091 vs. 0.008 for gender on UTKFACE) and suffers a severe accuracy drop (Worst Group AUC 0.507 vs. 0.772 for BEND-VLM on CELEBA). The baseline CLIP model is the only method that BEND-VLM does not beat on Worst Group AUC in some settings, but BEND-VLM matches it on accuracy while reducing bias by an order of magnitude. Notably, Orth-Proj. and Orth-Cal. sometimes *increase* bias relative to the baseline (e.g., race KL Div. rises from 0.114 to 0.259 on UTKFACE), a failure mode never observed with BEND-VLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEND-VLM builds directly on the orthogonal-projection debiasing framework of Chuang et al. [10], which removes bias by projecting text embeddings orthogonal to a global attribute subspace. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces the single global subspace with query-specific local subspaces discovered on-the-fly via LLM-based attribute augmentation, making the debiasing nonlinear and input-dependent; and (2) it adds a second equalization step using a reference image dataset and a constrained optimization with a closed-form solution, which prior fine-tuning-free methods lack. The paper also explicitly positions against fine-tuning approaches (DebiasCLIP [6]) that suffer catastrophic forgetting, and against methods like FairVLM [11] that require known class sets at test time, framing its contribution as the first to handle truly open-set, streaming queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are rapidly deployed in retrieval, zero-shot classification, and text-guided generation, the ability to debias embeddings without retraining and without knowing the query distribution in advance is practically important for open-set, online systems. The method's requirement of a reference dataset with protected-attribute labels and a 7B LLM for attribute augmentation limits deployment in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings. The intersectional debiasing experiment reveals that the orthogonalization step specifically drives the 'whac-a-mole' effect (debiasing gender increases racial bias), while the equalization step does not — a useful diagnostic that may inform future multi-attribute debiasing strategies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-binary attributes without a solver, how it behaves with self-identified (rather than annotated) attributes, and whether the reference dataset requirement can be relaxed.
