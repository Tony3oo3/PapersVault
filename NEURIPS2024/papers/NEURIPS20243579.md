## Problem

Monocular depth estimation is inherently scale-ambiguous because perspective projection loses one dimension, forcing existing methods to either bake in dataset-specific scale biases (hurting cross-domain generalization) or require ground-truth depth at test time for alignment. This paper asks whether natural language descriptions of scene content can resolve the scale ambiguity, leveraging the observation that certain objects co-occur with scenes of characteristic scale (e.g., cars and trees imply outdoor scenes of tens of meters).

## Input / Output

RSA takes as input a text caption describing objects present in the image (generated via MaskDINO panoptic segmentation for structured captions or LLaVA VQA for natural-language captions, with 15 variants per image randomly sampled during training) and a frozen relative depth map from a pretrained monocular depth model (DPT, MiDas, or DepthAnything). It outputs two positive scalars—scale α and shift β—that are applied globally via ŷ = 1/(α·y + β) to convert inverse relative depth into metric depth in meters. The CLIP text encoder and the depth model are both frozen; only the RSA MLP parameters are learned.

## Method

RSA encodes the text description with a frozen CLIP text encoder, then passes the embedding through a 5-layer shared MLP projecting to 256 hidden dimensions, followed by two separate 5-layer MLP heads that predict the scale α and shift β parameters (both constrained positive for optimization stability). Training minimizes an L1 loss between the predicted metric depth and ground truth while keeping the depth model frozen, so RSA learns only the text-to-transformation mapping. The key design choice is using language rather than image features as the conditioning signal: because text is invariant to illumination, occlusion, and scene layout, it generalizes better across domains than image-based baselines. Text prompts are generated in two styles—structured templates listing object classes and instance counts from MaskDINO, and natural-language descriptions from LLaVA—and the model is trained with random caption sampling to improve robustness. When trained jointly on multiple datasets (NYUv2, KITTI, VOID), the unified RSA model achieves zero-shot transfer to unseen domains (SUN-RGBD, DDAD) without fine-tuning.

## Results

On NYUv2, RSA with DPT (trained on NYUv2+KITTI+VOID) achieves Abs Rel of 0.099 and RMSE of 0.355, approaching the oracle Linear Fit upper bound (Abs Rel 0.094, RMSE 0.332) while outperforming the image-based baseline (Abs Rel 0.100, RMSE 0.367). On KITTI, DPT+RSA (multi-dataset) reaches Abs Rel 0.064 and RMSE 2.335, again closing the gap to Linear Fit (0.052, 2.198) and beating the image baseline (0.068, 2.568). In zero-shot transfer to SUN-RGBD, DPT+RSA (NYUv2+KITTI+VOID) achieves Abs Rel 0.150, outperforming the image-based counterpart (0.153) and Adabins (0.159), while on DDAD it reaches Abs Rel 0.165 versus 0.191 for the image baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSA consistently outperforms image-based scaling baselines across all three depth models (DPT, MiDas, DepthAnything) on both in-domain and cross-domain settings, with the gap widening for zero-shot transfer (e.g., on DDAD, DPT+RSA achieves Abs Rel 0.165 vs. 0.191 for DPT+Image). Against existing relative-to-metric methods, RSA with DPT on NYUv2 (Abs Rel 0.099) beats DistDepth+DA+Median (0.158) and approaches ZeroDepth+DA+Median (0.081), while on KITTI it outperforms Monodepth2+Median (0.115) and ZeroDepth+DA+Median (0.105). However, RSA does not surpass ZoeDepth's per-pixel metric refinement (Abs Rel 0.077 on NYUv2, 0.054 on KITTI), which uses a learned depth decoder rather than a global linear transform. In zero-shot SUN-RGBD evaluation, DPT+RSA (0.150) edges out Adabins (0.159) but remains behind ZoeDepth-M12 (0.119) and DepthAnything+Linear Fit (0.113). On DDAD zero-shot, RSA with DPT (0.165) is competitive with Adabins (0.154) and NeWCRFs (0.119) but trails ZoeDepth-M12 (0.129), indicating that metric-depth methods with per-pixel decoders still hold an advantage in the most challenging cross-domain setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: relative-to-metric depth transfer (ZoeDepth, DepthAnything, MiDas, DPT, DistDepth, ZeroDepth) and vision-language models for 3D perception (DepthCLIP, WorDepth, Hu et al.). Prior relative-to-metric methods either fine-tune dataset-specific metric decoders (ZoeDepth, DepthAnything), require ground truth at test time for alignment (MiDas, DPT linear fit), or learn scale priors from geometric cues (ZeroDepth, DistDepth). RSA departs from all of these by using a frozen CLIP text encoder plus a lightweight MLP to predict global scale and shift from a text caption, requiring neither ground truth at inference nor dataset-specific fine-tuning of the depth model. The text descriptions themselves are generated by off-the-shelf tools (MaskDINO for structured captions, LLaVA for natural captions), making the pipeline fully automatable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSA provides a practical, lightweight module that converts any general-purpose relative depth model's output to metric scale without ground truth at test time, which is critical for real-world deployment in robotics, AR/VR, and autonomous driving. The zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (SUN-RGBD, DDAD) suggests that language's invariance to nuisance variables (illumination, occlusion, layout) makes it a more robust scale cue than visual features. Key limitations remain: the global scale-and-shift assumption may be insufficient when relative depth is inaccurate, the method is vulnerable to adversarial text input, and it has not been tested with human-written descriptions. Open questions include whether region-wise or pixel-wise scale prediction from language is feasible, and whether RSA can be extended to refine the relative depth itself rather than merely rescaling it.
