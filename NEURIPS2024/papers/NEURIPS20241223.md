## Problem

Zero-shot audio captioning without annotated audio-caption pairs remains challenging because existing approaches either rely on supervised audio encoders (e.g., CLAP trained on millions of audio-text pairs) or are constrained by finite word banks. Moreover, standard contrastive learning for multimodal alignment suffers from the modality gap, where audio and image tokens are mapped to disjoint subspaces, preventing seamless substitution of one encoder for another in a vision-language model. This paper asks whether the rich semantic knowledge embedded in a frozen image captioner (VLM) can be repurposed for audio captioning by aligning audio token distributions to image token distributions in an unsupervised manner.

## Input / Output

The method accepts raw audio waveforms processed by a CAV-MAE encoder (12-layer transformer, 768-dim hidden, mapped to 1024-dim) and optionally RGB video frames (10 frames per video, one randomly sampled during training) processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-L@336px encoder. The output is a free-form natural language caption describing audible events, generated by the frozen LLaVA 1.5 LLM. The system supports both audio-only and audio+image (visually-informed) inference modes, and the original image captioning capability is preserved by simply omitting the audio tokens and prefix.

## Method

The core contribution is DALI (Distribution ALIgnment), which matches the full token-level distributions of an audio backbone to those of a frozen image encoder, rather than aligning only mean statistics via contrastive loss. Two alignment variants are studied: DALI^MMD using Maximum Mean Discrepancy with a Gaussian RBF kernel, and DALI^OT using Earth Mover Distance (Optimal Transport), with an attentive variant (DALI^OT_Att) that learns per-token transport weights via cross-attention to handle partial audiovisual mismatches (e.g., occluded sound sources). The framework operates in two stages: Stage 1 performs prefix tuning on 16 image-audio-caption pairs to re-task the frozen LLaVA for audio-centric descriptions, while simultaneously aligning the audio backbone's token distribution; Stage 2 (audiovisual distillation) generates pseudo-captions from combined audio+image inputs to fine-tune only the audio-to-LLM MLP, enabling robust audio-only inference. The key insight is that full distribution matching avoids the modality gap inherent in contrastive learning, allowing the aligned audio tokens to be directly substituted into the image captioner's pipeline without degrading the LLM's generation quality.

## Results

On the AudioCaps test set, the best configuration (DALI_AttOT + Image, audio-visual input) achieves a SPIDEr score of 0.1946, ROUGE-L of 0.3061, and METEOR of 0.1257. The audio-only variant (DALI_AttOT) reaches SPIDEr 0.1592 and ROUGE-L 0.3106. On the out-of-distribution Clotho test set, DALI_MMD leads with METEOR 0.1067 and ROUGE-L 0.2993, while DALI_AttOT achieves SPIDEr 0.0625. All results are obtained in a fully unsupervised zero-shot setting using only 16 image-caption pairs for prefix tuning and no audio-caption training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unsupervised competitor, Shaharabany et al. (ImageBind backbone + GPT-2), the proposed DALI methods achieve substantially higher performance: DALI_AttOT reaches ROUGE-L 0.3106 versus 0.082, and SPIDEr 0.1592 versus no reported value, confirming that distribution alignment far outperforms contrastive-based modality bridging for captioning. Against the supervised baseline of Salewski et al. (CLAP backbone trained on millions of audio-text pairs), the results are mixed: on AudioCaps, DALI_AttOT+Image surpasses CLAP on SPIDEr (0.1946 vs. 0.183) but trails on ROUGE-L (0.3061 vs. 0.331); on Clotho, the paper's methods lead on METEOR (0.1067 vs. 0.094) and ROUGE-L (0.2993 vs. 0.254) but fall short on SPIDEr (0.0655 vs. 0.097). Notably, the paper's approach is fully unsupervised (no audio-caption pairs used in training), whereas Salewski et al. relies on a CLAP encoder pre-trained on large-scale supervised audio-text data, making the competitive SPIDEr result on AudioCaps particularly striking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a frozen VLM image captioner (LLaVA 1.5) for zero-shot audio captioning by aligning an audio encoder's token distribution to the image encoder's, departing from the standard contrastive-learning paradigm (CLIP, CLAP, ImageBind) that the authors argue creates a harmful modality gap. It builds on prior zero-shot audio captioning efforts (ZerAuCaps/Salewski et al. using CLAP + LLM prompting; Shaharabany et al. using ImageBind + GPT-2) but replaces their supervised or weakly-aligned audio backbones with a distribution-matching objective (MMD or Optimal Transport with cross-attention) that directly matches full token distributions rather than mean embeddings. The prefix-tuning mechanism for re-tasking the LLM and the two-stage audiovisual distillation pipeline are novel components not present in prior audio captioning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a VLM's image-captioning knowledge is general enough to describe audio without any audio-text supervision—opens a path to scaling audio captioning using the abundant, high-quality image-text data already available, sidestepping the chronic scarcity and noise of audio annotations. However, the method is fundamentally limited by its dependence on audiovisual co-occurrence: sounds with no visual correlate (wind, distant rumbling) cannot be captioned, and complete cross-modal mismatches degrade performance. Open questions include whether the distribution-alignment framework generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., haptic, olfactory), whether the image-bias benefit observed in MMD on out-of-domain Clotho data is robust across architectures, and whether the modest absolute scores (SPIDEr ≈ 0.15–0.19) can be closed with stronger base VLMs or additional supervision.
