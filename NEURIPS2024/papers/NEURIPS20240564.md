## Problem

Large-scale real-world multi-view datasets for 3D scene understanding are scarce because standard videos have fixed capture viewpoints, making it difficult to find corresponding frames from diverse angles at scale. Existing datasets rely on synthetic objects or manually recorded clips limited to a few hundred categories, leaving real-world scene synthesis and reconstruction largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ODIN takes a single RGB image of a scene and a target camera pose specified as a relative rotation R and translation t, and generates the corresponding novel-view image. Unlike prior methods that can only rotate the camera around a fixed center, ODIN supports free translational movement through the scene. For 3D reconstruction, the model generates images along a smooth camera trajectory and feeds them to Dust3R to produce a metric-scale point cloud of the scene.

## Method

The paper introduces 360-1M, a dataset of over 1 million 360° YouTube videos yielding 80M+ unique frames and 363M frame correspondences. Correspondences are found by projecting each equirectangular frame into four cardinal views, running Dust3R within a 20-frame window, filtering by confidence, and propagating matches through a connectivity graph to capture long-range pairs; scale ambiguity is resolved by fusing Dust3R point maps with monocular depth estimates. The ODIN model is a latent diffusion architecture (encoder, U-net denoiser, decoder) built on the Zero-1-to-3 design, extended to condition on both rotation and translation to enable free camera movement. A motion masking mechanism adds an auxiliary channel to the U-net that predicts a soft spatial mask, filtering dynamic objects from the diffusion loss while an auxiliary term prevents the mask from degenerating to zero, allowing training on in-the-wild video without manual filtering.

## Results

On MipNeRF 360, ODIN achieves LPIPS of 0.587, outperforming ZeroNVS (0.625), Zero-1-to-3 (0.667), and pixelNeRF (0.718), while also attaining the highest PSNR (16.84) and competitive SSIM (0.537). On DTU, ODIN achieves the best LPIPS (0.378) and PSNR (16.67) among all compared methods. For 3D reconstruction on Google Scanned Objects, ODIN achieves Chamfer Distance of 0.0697 and IoU of 0.5328, surpassing Zero-1-to-3 (CD 0.0717, IoU 0.5052) and all other baselines. On the held-out 360-1M set, ODIN improves over Zero-1-to-3 by 25% in Chamfer Distance (0.0799 vs 0.1059) and 66% in IoU (0.5267 vs 0.3178).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODIN outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. On MipNeRF 360, ODIN reduces LPIPS by 0.038 over ZeroNVS (0.587 vs 0.625) and by 0.080 over Zero-1-to-3 (0.587 vs 0.667), while also achieving substantially higher PSNR (16.84 vs 13.20 and 11.70 respectively). On DTU, ODIN achieves the best LPIPS (0.378) and PSNR (16.67), narrowly beating ZeroNVS on LPIPS (0.378 vs 0.380) and significantly on PSNR (16.67 vs 13.55), though SinNeRF retains a slight SSIM edge (0.560 vs 0.525). For 3D reconstruction on Google Scanned Objects, ODIN achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.0697) and IoU (0.5328), marginally beating Zero-1-to-3 (0.0717, 0.5052) and substantially outperforming Point-E (0.0804, 0.2944), MCC (0.1230, 0.2343), and SJC-I (0.2245, 0.1332). On the held-out 360-1M reconstruction benchmark, ODIN improves over Zero-1-to-3 by 25% in Chamfer Distance and 66% in IoU, demonstrating its advantage on complex real-world scenes where object-centric methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based novel view synthesis line (Zero-1-to-3, ZeroNVS, DreamFusion) from object-centric, rotation-only generation to real-world scenes with free camera translation, enabled by a new data source: 360° YouTube video. The key methodological departure is a scalable correspondence-search pipeline that uses Dust3R for fast relative pose estimation combined with equirectangular projection to align views across frames, plus a correspondence-propagation graph to find long-range pairs without exhaustive search. For handling dynamic content in in-the-wild video, the paper introduces motion masking (a learned soft mask in the U-Net), which departs from prior approaches that restricted training to static scenes or manually filtered data. The 3D reconstruction step reuses Dust3R on generated trajectory images, following the trajectory-sampling strategy of ZeroNVS but without the rotation-only constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 360-1M dataset (1M+ videos, 80M+ frames, 363M correspondences) is the largest real-world multi-view dataset to date and opens 360° video as a scalable, diverse data source for 3D understanding that bypasses the manual curation bottleneck of MVIImageNet/CO3D. The free-camera-movement capability (translation + rotation conditioning) is a meaningful generalization over prior diffusion NVS methods that could only rotate about a center point, and the motion-masking trick makes in-the-wild video usable without restricting to static scenes. Key open questions include modeling dynamic elements (4D generation) rather than merely masking them, and whether the approach generalizes to video generation or other 3D tasks. The quantitative gains over ZeroNVS on MipNeRF360 are notable but moderate, and the method has not been stress-tested on truly unstructured outdoor scenes beyond the held-out 360-1M split.
