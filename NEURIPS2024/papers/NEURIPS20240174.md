## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves fast, high-quality rendering for static scenes, but extending it to dynamic scenes is hindered by the very features that make it effective: dense 3D priors and explicit representations. Existing 4D extensions either introduce implicit temporal encodings (MLPs) that negate 3DGS's rendering speed advantage, or require dense per-frame point clouds that are expensive to compute and impractical for real-world or mobile deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240174/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (18–21 cameras at 2704×2028 or 2048×1088, 300 frames) and a sparse COLMAP point cloud from only the first frame as initialization—no per-frame point clouds or object masks are required. It produces a compact 4D Gaussian representation (115 MB for 300-frame scenes) storing keyframe positions, quaternion rotations, and temporal opacities, and renders novel views at 1352×1014 at 120.6 FPS on a 4090 GPU (62 FPS on a 2080Ti).

## Method

Ex4DGS separates static and dynamic Gaussians during training via motion-based triggers (top-2% by normalized image-space displacement), requiring no auxiliary supervision. Static Gaussians follow a simple linear motion model, while dynamic Gaussians are parameterized at sparse keyframes (uniform interval of 10 frames) and interpolated between them using a cubic Hermite spline for position, Slerp for quaternion rotation, and a two-component Gaussian mixture model for temporal opacity to handle object appearance and disappearance. A progressive training scheme starts with a short temporal window and extends it every 400 iterations, preventing local minima when only first-frame point clouds are available. A point-backtracking mechanism accumulates per-Gaussian rendering error (L1 and SSIM) across all training views over time, enabling targeted pruning of erroneous dynamic Gaussians without per-frame tracking.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video dataset with sparse COLMAP point cloud input, Ex4DGS achieves an average PSNR of 32.11 dB, outperforming STG (30.64), 4DGS (30.19), and 4DGaussians (28.63), while using only 115 MB of storage and rendering at 120.6 FPS with a training time of 0.6 hours. On the Technicolor dataset, Ex4DGS achieves the best PSNR of 33.62 dB, SSIM₁ of 0.916, and SSIM₂ of 0.962, surpassing STG (33.23 PSNR) and 4DGS (29.54 PSNR). The method also achieves 62 FPS on a single 2080Ti GPU for 300-frame scenes at 1352×1014 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ex4DGS outperforms all sparse-COLMAP baselines on the Neural 3D Video dataset by a clear margin: +1.47 dB over STG, +1.92 dB over 4DGS, and +3.48 dB over 4DGaussians in average PSNR, while also achieving the lowest storage (115 MB vs. 6057 MB for 4DGS) and fastest training (0.6 h vs. 4.2 h for 4DGS). On the Technicolor dataset, Ex4DGS achieves the best PSNR (33.62) and SSIM scores, narrowly beating STG (33.23 PSNR, 0.960 SSIM₂) by 0.39 dB in PSNR, while substantially outperforming 4DGS (29.54) and 4DGaussians (30.79). The key differentiator is robustness to sparse initialization: concurrent methods degrade significantly when given only the first-frame point cloud (e.g., 4DGaussians drops from 31.15 to 28.63 PSNR), whereas Ex4DGS is designed to work under this condition. In the occlusion and newly-appearing-object experiments (Appendix C), Ex4DGS again leads STG and 4DGS, with 4DGaussians failing catastrophically (23.31 PSNR on occluded objects, 21.51 on appearing objects) due to its inability to disentangle static and dynamic components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and Mip-Splatting to the 4D dynamic domain by replacing the per-frame or MLP-based temporal modeling used in 4DGS, 4DGaussians, and STG with explicit keyframe interpolation (Cubic Hermite Spline for position, Slerp for rotation, and a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model for opacity). It departs from the broader NeRF-based dynamic view synthesis lineage (D-NeRF, NeRFies, HexPlane, K-Planes) by maintaining a fully explicit, rasterization-based pipeline that avoids per-pixel ray sampling. The progressive training scheme and unsupervised static/dynamic point separation via motion-based triggers are novel contributions not present in prior 4D Gaussian methods, which typically require dense multi-frame point clouds or auxiliary masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~54× memory reduction (115 MB vs. 6270 MB for 4DGS) combined with 120+ FPS rendering makes dynamic novel view synthesis practical on consumer hardware, and the ability to initialize from a single sparse point cloud removes the dependency on expensive multi-frame 3D reconstruction. However, the method still struggles with objects that appear without any initial 3D Gaussian nearby, and monocular video remains challenging due to scale ambiguity. Open questions include whether the keyframe interpolation approach can handle highly non-rigid deformations (e.g., cloth, hair) and whether the progressive training strategy generalizes to unbounded or streaming video settings.
