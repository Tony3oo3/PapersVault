## Problem

Existing 3D planar reconstruction methods face a fundamental representation trade-off: 2D masks capture precise contours but suffer cross-view inconsistency and require complex fusion, while 3D point/surfel/voxel representations are discontinuous and struggle with irregular plane boundaries. Learning-based approaches like PlanarRecon produce incomplete reconstructions and fail to generalize beyond their training distribution. The paper asks whether a single representation can simultaneously provide continuous 3D surfaces, precise boundary delineation, and view consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a posed monocular RGB video as input, segmented into parts of 9 keyframes each via a NeuralRecon-style keyframe selection. It leverages off-the-shelf Metric3Dv2 depth and Omnidata normal estimators on each keyframe, combined with SLIC superpixels (~10k per 1296×968 frame), to initialize the 3D tablets. The output is a set of AlphaTablets—3D rectangles each carrying a learnable texture map, alpha channel, normal vector, and distance—yielding a complete 3D planar reconstruction with solid surfaces, clear boundaries, and interpolatable textures that also supports direct plane-based scene editing.

## Method

AlphaTablets represent each 3D plane as a rectangle with an alpha channel, texture map, and per-axis distance ratios, combining continuous 3D geometry with arbitrary boundary shapes. Differentiable rasterization is achieved by converting tablets into pseudo meshes for NVDiffrast, performing multi-layer depth peeling, and applying a novel alpha-aware anti-aliasing formula (c_aa = AA(αc)/AA(α)) that prevents incorrect transparency at overlapping boundaries. The bottom-up pipeline initializes dense, overlapping tablets from superpixel back-projection, then iteratively optimizes geometry (normal, distance), texture, and alpha via a composite loss (photometric, alpha-inverse, distortion, depth, normal) under a constraint that each tablet center stays on its initial camera ray. A hierarchical merging scheme using KD-tree neighborhood search and union-find with joint constraints on normal angle, projected distance, and color difference grows small tablets into coherent large planes, with an initial pre-merge reducing the ~10k-per-frame tablet count before optimization begins.

## Results

On ScanNet, AlphaTablets achieves an F-Score of 0.456 for 3D plane geometry reconstruction, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline PlanarRecon (0.372) and all reconstruct-then-fit methods. For plane segmentation, the method attains the best VOI (3.468), Rand Index (0.928), and Segmentation Covering (0.273), surpassing PlanarRecon (3.622, 0.897, 0.248). The method also achieves the highest Recall (0.481) and Precision (0.447) among all baselines, indicating more complete and accurate plane detection, particularly for smaller plane instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaTablets outperforms all seven baselines on F-Score (0.456 vs. best 0.372 for PlanarRecon), Recall (0.481 vs. 0.426 for Metric3D+Seq-RANSAC), and Precision (0.447 vs. 0.398 for PlanarRecon), demonstrating superior completeness and accuracy of detected planes. On segmentation metrics, it leads on all three: VOI (3.468 vs. 3.622 for PlanarRecon), Rand Index (0.928 vs. 0.897), and Segmentation Covering (0.273 vs. 0.248). The only metric where AlphaTablets trails is Accuracy (0.161 vs. 0.105 for PlanarRecon), which the authors attribute to PlanarRecon's tendency to reconstruct only large dominant planes and the incomplete ground-truth coverage in ScanNet. Volume-based methods (NeuralRecon, Atlas) and depth-based methods (ESTDepth, Metric3D) suffer from 3D inconsistency and RANSAC sensitivity, yielding lower F-Scores (0.231–0.331). SuGaR+Seq-RANSAC, the most recent Gaussian-based baseline, is degraded by poor COLMAP initialization and textureless regions in ScanNet, achieving only 0.327 F-Score. The method also generalizes qualitatively to TUM-RGBD and Replica datasets without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlphaTablets positions itself at the intersection of two prior lines of work: differentiable mesh rendering (building directly on NVDiffrast for pseudo-mesh rasterization and depth peeling) and 3D planar surface representation. It departs from 2D mask-based methods (which suffer view inconsistency), discrete 3D representations like points and voxels (which lose continuous geometry), and local primitives like surfels and 2D Gaussians (which cannot represent large coherent planes) by introducing semi-transparent rectangular primitives with per-tablet texture and alpha maps. The bottom-up pipeline—superpixel initialization, differentiable rendering optimization, and hierarchical merging—draws on ideas from SLAM (DPPTAM's superpixel grouping), NeRF-style distortion losses (mip-NeRF-360), and classical agglomerative clustering, but combines them in a way that treats plane reconstruction as iterative tablet growth rather than one-shot detection or volume-based tracking (as in PlanarRecon).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AlphaTablets representation is potentially reusable beyond planar reconstruction: its explicit, editable geometry with canonical texture maps enables direct 3D scene editing (texture swap, style transfer per plane) without re-optimization, which is a practical advantage over implicit or volumetric representations. However, the method is limited to planar regions (struggles with highly curved surfaces), lacks view-dependent effects (non-Lambertian surfaces degrade optimization), and requires ~2 hours per scene on a single A100, limiting real-time or large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the representation can be extended to handle view-dependent shading, whether hybrid representations (AlphaTablets + Gaussians) can cover both planar and non-planar geometry, and whether the initialization bottleneck (tens of thousands of Python-loop superpixel-to-tablet conversions) can be parallelized for practical use.
