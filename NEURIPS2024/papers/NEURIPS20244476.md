## Problem

Existing spatial audio rendering methods for 3D scenes either require explicit Room Impulse Response modeling (demanding detailed scene geometry and emitter locations) or depend on pre-rendered images as conditioning signals, which introduces visual-artifact sensitivity and sequential rendering lag. This paper addresses the gap of rendering high-quality spatial audio from arbitrary viewpoints without relying on pre-rendered visual cues, enabling real-time audio-visual synchronization for immersive virtual tourism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monaural reference sound waveform, the listener's 6-DoF viewpoint pose (rotation matrix and translation vector), and a pre-computed SfM point cloud (from COLMAP) as input. The SfM points are clustered into N=256 Audio-Visual Anchors, each carrying 3D coordinates, RGB features from K=50 nearest neighbors, and a learned latent audio embedding. The output is a pair of stereo spectrograms (left and right channels) in the STFT domain (257 frequency bands), which are inverse-transformed to produce viewpoint-specific spatial audio. No pre-rendered images or explicit emitter locations are required at inference time.

## Method

AV-Cloud constructs a point-based audio-visual scene representation from clustered SfM points (Audio-Visual Anchors) and renders spatial audio through a three-stage pipeline. First, Anchor Projection transforms each anchor's 3D position into the listener's head coordinate system with positional encoding. Second, the Audio-Visual Cloud Splatting (AVCS) module is a Visual-to-Audio Splatting Transformer where frequency-band indices serve as queries and anchor RGB+relative-vector features serve as keys/values; attention dynamically reweights anchor contributions per frequency band and viewpoint, producing a mixture mask (magnitude adjustment) and a difference mask (left-right energy distribution sensitive to head orientation). Third, the Spatial Audio Render Head (SARH) applies these masks to the monaural spectrogram and refines the stereo output through a residual structure containing Time Filters (1D convolutions conditioned on the integrated relative vector to model reverberation) and stacked Conv2D layers for time-frequency smoothing. Training is end-to-end supervised with MSE on log-magnitude spectrograms without phase supervision, and the entire pipeline operates without any rendered image input, decoupling audio from visual rendering for parallel execution.

## Results

On the RWAVS dataset, AV-Cloud achieves the best scores across all five metrics: MAG 0.351, LRE 0.936, ENV 0.145, RTE 0.074, and DPAM 0.276, with 3.91M parameters at 83 FPS. On the more challenging Replay-NVAS dataset (indoor apartment with multiple speakers), AV-Cloud again leads on every metric: MAG 0.180, LRE 0.600, ENV 0.052, RTE 0.065, and DPAM 0.234, at 103 FPS with 2.47M parameters. The lightweight AV-Cloud-sim-SH variant (0.51M params, 301 FPS) retains competitive accuracy, improving MAG and LRE by 20% and 17% respectively over the closest point-based baselines (NACF, INRAS, NAF) while reducing RTE by 45%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-Cloud outperforms all image-based methods (VAM, ViGAS, AVNeRF) on every metric for both datasets, with the largest gains in perceptual quality (DPAM: 0.276 vs. 0.357–0.459 on RWAVS) and reverberation accuracy (RTE: 0.074 vs. 0.079–0.098 on RWAVS; 0.065 vs. 0.137–0.159 on Replay-NVAS, a 53% reduction over ViGAS). Against point-based neural field methods (NACF, INRAS, NAF), AV-Cloud improves MAG by ~20%, LRE by ~17–38%, and DPAM by 18–45%, while also running faster (83–301 FPS vs. 41–180 FPS). The lightweight AV-Cloud-sim-SH variant (0.51M params) matches or exceeds NACF/INRAS/NAF on all metrics at roughly 3× their inference speed. Notably, AV-Cloud does not require pre-rendered images (unlike VAM, ViGAS, AVNeRF), enabling parallel audio-visual rendering and eliminating the sequential audio lag inherent to image-conditioned pipelines. In the in-the-wild human study (76 participants, fountain scene), AV-Cloud received 48% preference votes versus 37% for AVNeRF and 15% for NAF.

## Relation to Prior Work

AV-Cloud draws on three distinct research threads: (1) point-based neural rendering for visual scenes (3D-GS, splatting), which it repurposes for audio by clustering SfM points into Audio-Visual Anchors and applying a transformer-based splatting mechanism; (2) image-conditioned spatial audio synthesis (VAM, ViGAS, AV-NeRF), from which it deliberately departs by eliminating the dependency on pre-rendered images to avoid visual-artifact propagation and sequential rendering lag; and (3) implicit neural field methods for audio (NAF, INRAS, NACF), which it contrasts by introducing explicit viewpoint-adaptive reweighting of scene points rather than querying a continuous field at emitter/listener coordinates. The conceptual bridge is the observation that SfM points, already computed for visual rendering pipelines, can serve as a shared geometric substrate for both modalities, enabling parallel rather than sequential audio-visual synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time, synchronized audio-visual navigation (demonstrated via a WebGL platform at >25 FPS on Apple M2) without the architectural bottleneck of rendering an image before generating audio — a constraint that has limited prior image-based methods in interactive applications. The 53% reduction in reverberation time error on the challenging Replay-NVAS indoor dataset and consistent SOTA across all five metrics on both benchmarks suggest the point-based representation captures acoustic scene properties more faithfully than image-conditioned or implicit-field alternatives. Open questions include scalability to larger or more complex scenes, handling of multiple simultaneous dynamic emitters, extension beyond stereo to higher-order ambisonics, and robustness in acoustically noisy environments where the lightweight model architecture may degrade.
