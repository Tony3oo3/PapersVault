## Problem

Existing neural SAT solvers rely on CNF representation, which causes exponential clause blowup when encoding the XOR operations that dominate cryptographic problems, leading to scale explosion and loss of high-order operational semantics. No prior work has established an effective graph representation or learning-based solver for the XOR-friendly Algebraic Normal Form (ANF) that naturally captures these operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241317/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tripartite ANF graph (vanilla literals, second-order literals, and positive/negative clause pairs) constructed from a plaintext-ciphertext pair and encryption algorithm, where the graph is dramatically more compact than its CNF counterpart (e.g., 77 vs. 6340 nodes for SR(25)). For key-solving, the input is a pair of derived SAT instances differing only in one key bit assignment, and the output is a scalar satisfiability score in [0,1] per instance, with the key bit determined by which instance scores higher.

## Method

CryptoANFNet is a message-passing GNN over a tripartite ANF graph where second-order literal embeddings are derived by concatenating their constituent vanilla literal embeddings rather than stored independently, and clause updates use layer-norm LSTMs that incorporate both the complementary clause and aggregated literal messages. The architecture differs from NeuroSAT in two key ways: it retains only vanilla literal and clause-pair embeddings (halving memory), and it applies two separate classification MLPs to positive and negative clause embeddings before summing votes. For key-solving, the model is trained with an additional pairwise comparison loss (cross-entropy over softmax of paired scores) alongside standard BCE, enabling it to discriminate between the two derived instances for a given key bit. The ANF graph directly encodes XOR as polynomial addition over GF(2) and AND as multiplication, avoiding the 2^(k-1) clause explosion of CNF encoding.

## Results

On small-scale datasets, CryptoANFNet achieves 96% accuracy on SR(5) vs. 91% for NeuroSAT, and 72% vs. 55% on Speck3-8-16. On large-scale datasets, the gap widens: 72% vs. 57% on SR(25) and 66.5% vs. 51% on Scipher6-16-32. In terms of runtime, CryptoANFNet achieves a 50x average speedup over traditional heuristic solvers (e.g., 5 ms vs. 2470–5662 ms on SR(25) for WDSat). The key-solving algorithm further boosts accuracy from 76.5% to 82% on Scipher3-8-16 and from 72% to 75% on Speck3-8-16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryptoANFNet outperforms NeuroSAT [5] on all eight datasets, with the largest margins on large-scale instances (e.g., 72% vs. 57% on SR(25), 69% vs. 53% on Scipher6-8-16, 72% vs. 55% on Speck3-8-16), where NeuroSAT's CNF-based representation suffers from scale explosion due to XOR clauses. Against heuristic solvers, CryptoANFNet achieves a 50x average speedup: on SR(25), it runs in 5 ms per instance versus 2470–5662 ms for WDSat [42], 13491–35912 ms for CryptoMiniSat [44], and 4922–14856 ms for Kissat [45]. Among incomplete solvers in Appendix A, CryptoANFNet also dominates WalkSAT [46], RoundingSAT [47], and FourierSAT [48] in both speed and reliability. The key-solving algorithm (CryptoANFNet+key-solving) further improves over plain CryptoANFNet by 5.5 percentage points on Scipher3-8-16 (76.5%→82%) and 3 points on Speck3-8-16 (72%→75%), demonstrating that paired-instance scoring adds meaningful signal for key bit determination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuroSAT line of work (message-passing GNNs over CNF literal-clause graphs for satisfiability prediction) by replacing the CNF representation with an Arithmetic Normal Form (ANF) graph structure, motivated by the fact that cryptographic problems are XOR-heavy and CNF encoding of XOR causes near-exponential clause blowup. It also draws on the SAT-based cryptanalysis tradition (CryptoSAT, CryptoMiniSat, WDSat) and the learning-based cryptanalysis literature (Gohr's differential-neural distinguishers on SPECK), but departs from both by framing the problem as end-to-end supervised classification over ANF graphs rather than handcrafted differential/linear search or traditional solver heuristics. The key architectural departure from NeuroSAT is the tripartite graph (vanilla literals → second-order literals → complementary clause pairs) and the use of paired positive/negative clause embeddings with separate MLPs, replacing NeuroSAT's single clause embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ANF-based graph representation is a natural and potentially broadly applicable encoding for any XOR-dense Boolean problem, not just cryptography, and the 50× speedup over heuristic solvers (including CryptoMiniSat and Kissat) makes learning-based SAT solvers practically viable for cryptographic-scale instances where CNF solvers degrade to tens of thousands of milliseconds. However, the practical impact is currently limited: large-instance accuracy remains modest (66–76%), the evaluation covers only two cipher families (one undisclosed), no error bars or code are provided, and the MQ-problem restriction means the approach does not yet address higher-order algebraic structures. Open questions include whether the ANF graph generalizes beyond quadratic ANF, whether the key-solving algorithm scales to longer keys, and whether the approach transfers to other cryptographic tasks (differential trail search, linear approximation).
