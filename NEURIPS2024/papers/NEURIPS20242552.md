## Problem

In point cloud masked autoencoders like Point-MAE, the positional embeddings (patch center coordinates) carry rich geometric and semantic information, unlike the fixed patch indices in 2D images. The paper demonstrates that when 100% of patches are masked, the decoder can reconstruct the point cloud well using only the masked centers without any encoder output, revealing that the reconstruction objective is trivial and the encoder is not forced to learn semantic representations. Existing MAE-based methods for point clouds all directly leak these semantically rich centers to the decoder, undermining the purpose of self-supervised pre-training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw point cloud (1024 points for pre-training, 2048 for fine-tuning), divides it into 64 patches of 32 points each via FPS and KNN, and normalizes each patch by subtracting its center coordinates. The encoder receives visible patch tokens (embedded via a lightweight PointNet) plus sin-cos positional embeddings of visible centers processed through a learnable PEM. During pre-training, the output consists of predicted positional embeddings for masked centers and reconstructed normalized point coordinates; for downstream tasks, the encoder's latent representations are used to produce class labels or per-point segmentation masks.

## Method

PCP-MAE introduces a Predicting Center Module (PCM) that shares all Transformer block parameters with the encoder but replaces self-attention with cross-attention, where masked patch tokens serve as queries and the concatenation of visible and masked tokens serve as keys and values. The PCM predicts the sin-cos positional embeddings of masked centers, and these predictions (with a stop-gradient operation to prevent reconstruction shortcuts) replace the ground-truth centers fed to the decoder. The training objective combines an L2 center-prediction loss (weighted by η=0.1) with a Chamfer Distance reconstruction loss, forcing the encoder to encode enough inter-patch relational information to infer missing center positions. Because the centers are removed during patch normalization, the PCM cannot trivially recover them from masked patch tokens alone, making the prediction task non-trivial and compelling the encoder to learn richer semantic representations. The use of sin-cos positional embeddings (rather than raw 3D coordinates) as the prediction target provides a sparser, higher-dimensional representation that is easier for the network to learn.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, PCP-MAE achieves 95.52% (OBJ-BG), 94.32% (OBJ-ONLY), and 90.35% (PB-T50-RS) classification accuracy, surpassing Point-MAE by 5.50%, 6.03%, and 5.17% respectively. On ModelNet40, PCP-MAE reaches 94.0% (w/o voting) and 94.2% (w/ voting), improving over Point-MAE by 0.8% and 0.4%. In few-shot learning on ModelNet40, PCP-MAE achieves SOTA in 5-way 10/20-shot and 10-way 20-shot settings (e.g., 97.4% in 5-way 10-shot). For segmentation, PCP-MAE attains 84.9% Cls.mIoU on ShapeNetPart and 71.0% mAcc / 61.3% mIoU on S3DIS Area 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCP-MAE outperforms its direct baseline Point-MAE [24] by large margins of 5.50%, 6.03%, and 5.17% on the three ScanObjectNN variants, while using the same 22.1M parameter count. Among single-modal SSL methods, PCP-MAE surpasses Point-FEMAE [48] (the strongest single-modal competitor) on OBJ-BG (95.52 vs 95.18) and OBJ-ONLY (94.32 vs 93.29), and slightly on PB-T50-RS (90.35 vs 90.22), all with fewer parameters (22.1M vs 27.4M) and lower pre-training cost (1.4× vs 3.7× time). Against cross-modal methods, PCP-MAE beats ReCon [27] on OBJ-BG (95.52 vs 95.18) and OBJ-ONLY (94.32 vs 93.63) but trails by 0.28% on PB-T50-RS (90.35 vs 90.63); it also outperforms I2P-MAE [51] and ACT [9] across all ScanObjectNN variants. On ModelNet40, PCP-MAE matches Point-FEMAE and ReCon at 94.0% (w/o vote) while using substantially fewer parameters (22.1M vs 27.4M/43.6M). In few-shot learning, PCP-MAE achieves SOTA in 3 of 4 settings (5-way 10/20-shot, 10-way 20-shot), gaining 0.9–1.3% over Point-MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Point-MAE (Pang et al., ECCV 2022), which adapted the 2D masked autoencoder paradigm to point clouds by normalizing patches around their centers and using center coordinates as positional embeddings. The key conceptual departure is identifying that, unlike 2D patch indices which are fixed and information-poor, point cloud center coordinates carry rich geometric and semantic information, making the standard reconstruction objective trivial when centers are directly provided to the decoder. The proposed Predicting Center Module (PCM) introduces a cross-attention mechanism that shares parameters with the encoder to predict masked centers, replacing the ground-truth positional embeddings in the decoder. This positions PCP-MAE as a targeted correction to a shared flaw across all existing MAE-based point cloud methods (Point-M2AE, Point-FEMAE, ACT, I2P-MAE, ReCon), which all directly leak masked centers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 100%-masked Point-MAE decoder can reconstruct point clouds well using only center coordinates challenges the foundational assumption that MAE pre-training in 3D is as semantically meaningful as in 2D, and suggests that much of the field's progress may have been partially attributable to positional information leakage rather than learned representations. The practical impact is significant: PCP-MAE achieves 5+ point improvements on ScanObjectNN with only 1.02× parameters and 1.4× pre-training time, making it an efficient drop-in replacement for Point-MAE. Limitations include being single-modal (not leveraging cross-modal or contrastive signals) and being constrained by the limited size of available point cloud datasets. Open questions include whether the center-prediction objective generalizes to multi-modal settings and whether it can be combined with contrastive learning frameworks like ReCon.
