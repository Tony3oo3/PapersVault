## Problem

Existing deep neural network models of the visual cortex are predominantly feedforward, trained on static images, and lack physiological neuronal mechanisms, leaving a critical gap in understanding how the visual cortex encodes context-rich, temporally structured movie stimuli. No prior model simultaneously incorporates biologically plausible spiking dynamics and long-range top-down feedback connections to capture both dynamic and static cortical representations under natural video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240364/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives sequential movie frames (30 Hz, 224×224) one frame per time step; for UCF101-trained models the entire movie is fed continuously without interruption, while for ImageNet-trained models each frame is treated as an independent image repeated 4 times (T=4). Outputs are layer-wise spiking representations (membrane potentials and spike sequences from LIF neurons) extracted from all selected layers, which are compared against PSTH-processed Neuropixel recordings from six mouse visual cortical regions (VISp, VISl, VISrl, VISal, VISpm, VISam) using the proposed TSRSA metric, yielding a single Spearman rank correlation score per model–region pair.

## Method

LoRaFB-SNet is a deep spiking network whose backbone is a feedforward stack of SEW-ResNet residual blocks (convolution, pooling, batch normalization, LIF spiking neurons), with embedded recurrent modules that introduce long-range feedback between non-adjacent stages. Each recurrent module comprises three sub-components: a feedforward submodule for spatial feature abstraction, a long-range feedback submodule using depthwise transposed convolution (to upsample and reduce parameters) followed by BN and spiking neurons, and a fusion submodule that channel-concatenates the previous stage's output with the feedback signal and integrates them via pointwise convolution, BN, and spiking neurons. The network is pre-trained via supervised learning on UCF101 (video action recognition, 16-frame clips, T=16) and ImageNet (object recognition, T=4) using SpikingJelly with surrogate gradient backpropagation. For evaluation, the authors propose TSRSA, which extends standard RSA to temporal sequences by computing Pearson correlations between each frame's population response and all subsequent frames, concatenating these into a single similarity vector, and then measuring Spearman rank correlation between the model's and cortex's vectors—thereby capturing both static representational geometry and temporal dynamics in a single score.

## Results

LoRaFB-SNet achieves the highest TSRSA (Time-Series Representational Similarity Analysis) score among all compared models on the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding dataset. Under Movie1 (30 s), it scores 0.520 (63.3% of the neural ceiling of 0.821), and under the longer Movie2 (120 s), it scores 0.283 (45.9% of the ceiling of 0.616). The advantage over baselines grows substantially with stimulus duration: the gap over SEW-ResNet widens from +15.2% on Movie1 to +201.1% on Movie2, indicating that long-range feedback and spiking dynamics are especially critical for encoding extended temporal sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRaFB-SNet outperforms all three baselines on both movie stimuli. Against CORnet (recurrent but non-spiking), it gains +2.8% on Movie1 and +26.8% on Movie2, highlighting the added value of spike-sequential coding on top of recurrence. Against ResNet-2p-CPC (parallel-pathway CNN with fixed-length temporal filters), the margin is much larger: +49.3% on Movie1 and +64.4% on Movie2, showing that flexible spiking recurrence handles variable-length sequences far better than fixed filters. The most striking comparison is with SEW-ResNet (feedforward spiking network, same backbone): LoRaFB-SNet leads by +15.2% on Movie1 but by a staggering +201.1% on Movie2, demonstrating that long-range feedback connections are the key architectural ingredient for capturing dynamic, context-dependent representations over extended time scales. Under static natural scene stimuli, LoRaFB-SNet also achieves the highest TSRSA score (0.4130 vs. 0.3935 for SEW-ResNet), confirming that the gains are not solely due to temporal processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: recurrent deep networks for visual cortex modeling (CORnet, ResNet-2p-CPC) that capture top-down feedback but use conventional neurons, and deep spiking networks (SEW-ResNet) that employ biologically plausible LIF neurons but are purely feedforward. LoRaFB-SNet combines both mechanisms—long-range feedback connections and spiking neurons—for the first time in a single architecture, and extends the evaluation setting from static images to natural movie stimuli. The proposed TSRSA metric generalizes standard RSA to preserve temporal sequential relationships, addressing a gap where prior representational similarity analyses treated frames as independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the most biologically plausible computational model to date for mouse visual cortex representations under dynamic movie stimuli, with the advantage growing substantially for longer clips (up to 201% over the best feedforward spiking baseline on a 120s movie). The dynamic-vs-static experiments offer mechanistic insight: the model's sensitivity to temporal disruption (46.5% score drop) versus the feedforward baseline's near-immunity (<9%) suggests that context-dependent, temporally integrated encoding is a key feature of cortical movie processing. Open questions include the specific interplay between feedforward and feedback pathways over time, whether local (not just long-range) recurrent connections further improve fidelity, and whether the framework generalizes to other species or sensory modalities. Limitations include reliance on a single LIF neuron model, a single feedback topology, and evaluation on one mouse neural dataset.
