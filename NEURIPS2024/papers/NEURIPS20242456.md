## Problem

Reconstructing point coordinates from a minimal set of pairwise Euclidean distances (the Euclidean Distance Geometry problem) is NP-hard, and existing non-convex algorithms for this task lack convergence guarantees at the optimal sample complexity. A key obstacle is that the EDG measurement basis is non-orthogonal, preventing direct transfer of standard low-rank matrix completion analysis. This paper provides the first local convergence guarantee for a non-convex EDG algorithm at the optimal order Ω(νrn log n) random distance samples.

## Input / Output

The method receives m pairwise squared Euclidean distances selected via an index set Ω ⊂ {(i,j): i<j} sampled uniformly at random, along with a rank estimate r̃ (set to the true rank r for the theoretical guarantee). It outputs the centered Gram matrix X = P^T P ∈ S_n (symmetric, PSD, rank r, with X·1 = 0), from which the r×n coordinate matrix P is recovered by eigendecomposition. The constraint X·1 = 0 enforces translation invariance (centroid at origin), and the distance constraints X_ii + X_jj − 2X_ij = D_ij for (i,j) ∈ Ω are encoded via a custom measurement operator built on a non-orthogonal basis.

## Method

MatrixIRLS minimizes a sequence of quadratic majorizing models of a smoothed log-det rank surrogate F_ε(X) = Σ f_ε(σ_i(X)), where each iteration solves a weighted least squares subproblem X^(k+1) = argmin ⟨X, W_k(X)⟩ subject to the distance and centering constraints, with the weight operator W_k constructed from the SVD of the current iterate. The key technical contribution is a dual basis construction (Lemma 4.4) that extends the primal measurement basis to span the full space of symmetric matrices S_n, overcoming the non-orthogonality inherent in the EDG distance measurements. This enables proving a restricted isometry property (RIP) restricted to the tangent space of the manifold of symmetric rank-r matrices (Theorem 4.5), which in turn yields local quadratic convergence of MatrixIRLS at the optimal sample complexity m ≥ Cνrn log n (Theorem 4.3). The smoothing parameter ε_k is updated via the (r̃+1)-th singular value of the current iterate, ensuring only O(r) singular triplets are computed per iteration for scalability.

## Results

On the 1BPM protein reconstruction task (n=3672, r=3, oversampling factor ρ=3), MatrixIRLS achieves a relative Procrustes error of 2.7×10⁻¹² in 7.08 minutes, outperforming ALM (5.6×10⁻⁶, 23.4 min), ScaledSGD (2.4×10⁻², 20.1 min), and RieEDG (5.6×10⁻², 116.6 min) by 6+ orders of magnitude in accuracy while also being the fastest. On ill-conditioned synthetic data (condition number κ=10⁵), MatrixIRLS is the only method that recovers the ground truth to ~10⁻⁸ relative error (in ~35 iterations / ~200 s), whereas the other three methods fail to reach below 10⁻³ even after 100,000 iterations. Phase-transition experiments on Gaussian data (n=500, ranks 2–5) show MatrixIRLS and ALM have comparable success probabilities, while RieEDG and ScaledSGD require substantially higher oversampling factors to succeed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ALM (the augmented Lagrangian / Burer-Monteiro method from Tasissa & Lai 2018), MatrixIRLS wins decisively on accuracy (2.7×10⁻¹² vs. 5.6×10⁻⁶ relative Procrustes error) and runtime (7.08 vs. 23.4 min) on the 1BPM protein, and matches ALM on synthetic-data success probability at the same oversampling factors. Against ScaledSGD (Zhang et al. 2022), MatrixIRLS is superior on every metric: it achieves ~10⁻¹² error versus ScaledSGD's ~10⁻² on the protein, and on ill-conditioned data MatrixIRLS converges to ~10⁻⁸ in ~35 iterations while ScaledSGD stalls above 10⁻³ after 100,000 iterations. Against RieEDG (Smith et al. 2023), MatrixIRLS again dominates: RieEDG's relative error is 5.6×10⁻² on the protein (vs. 2.7×10⁻¹²) and it requires 116.6 min versus 7.08 min; on ill-conditioned data RieEDG also fails to converge below 10⁻³. Overall, MatrixIRLS is the only method that simultaneously achieves high-precision recovery, the lowest runtime, and robustness to ill-conditioning across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) framework from Kümmerle & Verdun (ICML 2021) for low-rank matrix completion to the Euclidean Distance Geometry (EDG) setting, where the measurement basis is non-orthonormal. The key technical departure is the construction of an extended dual basis (building on Tasissa & Lai 2018 and Lichtenberg & Tasissa 2024) that spans the full space of symmetric matrices, enabling a tangent-space restricted isometry property that was previously unavailable for EDG. Unlike prior non-convex EDG methods (Riemannian optimization, Burer-Monteiro factorization), which either lack convergence guarantees or provide only weaker ones, this work establishes local quadratic convergence at the optimal sample complexity Ω(νrn log n).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for any non-convex algorithm solving the EDG problem at the information-theoretically optimal sample complexity, closing a gap where only convex (nuclear norm) approaches had such guarantees. The tangent-space restricted RIP for the non-orthonormal EDG measurement basis is explicitly noted as potentially useful for analyzing other non-convex methods in this setting. However, the guarantee is only local (requiring initialization near the ground truth), and global convergence remains open. Practically, the method's robustness to ill-conditioned data (κ=10⁵) where all compared baselines fail completely suggests utility in noisy molecular conformation and sensor network localization tasks.
