## Problem

Existing faithfulness evaluation criteria for persona-driven role-playing (PRP) rely on coarse-grained LLM-based scoring without a clear mathematical formulation or per-constraint explanation, making it impossible to pinpoint which persona statements a response violates or to use the score as an optimization signal. This paper addresses the absence of a fine-grained, explainable, and quantified criterion for global PRP faithfulness that can both evaluate and guide improvement of AI characters.

## Input / Output

The system takes a set of atomic persona statements S (ranging from 8 to 599 facts about a character) and a single-turn user query q as input, and produces a role-play response r along with a per-statement APC score and an aggregate ΔAPC score. The ΔAPC score is a continuous value representing the expected number of entailed active (query-relevant) constraints minus the expected number of contradicted passive (query-irrelevant) constraints, regularized by subtracting the score a neutral-response baseline would achieve. In practice, the scoring is performed by two ~300M-parameter DeBERTa-V3 discriminators (one for statement-query relevance, one for statement-to-response NLI) that are symbolically distilled from GPT-4 with ~90% agreement.

## Method

The paper formulates PRP faithfulness as a MAX-CSP where each persona statement is an atomic constraint whose satisfaction depends on query-statement relevance: active constraints (relevant) require the response to be entailed by the statement, while passive constraints (irrelevant) only require non-contradiction. The APC score quantifies this by summing, over all statements, the NLI satisfaction probability marginalized by the relevance probability, yielding a continuous, constraint-wise explainable metric. The key practical contribution is building this scoring system via symbolic distillation: GPT-4 is prompted to generate labeled relevance and NLI datasets, which are then used to fine-tune small DeBERTa-V3 discriminators for efficient inference. The ΔAPC score is further leveraged as a reward signal in a novel APC-based DPO objective (Eq. 9) that combines active-constraint and passive-constraint DPO losses weighted by relevance probabilities, enabling global faithfulness optimization across all persona statements simultaneously. Experiments show APC-based DPO improves both active and passive constraint satisfaction across EU, RAG, and LCM baselines, with the largest gains on characters with more persona statements.

## Results

On six famous-figure characters with 77–599 persona statements, the ΔAPC score (expected entailed active constraints minus expected contradicted passive constraints) is the headline metric. Without APC-based DPO, RAG achieves the best average ΔAPC of 5.7, followed by Gemma-7B (3.5) and EU (−0.7); GPT-4 scores 4.1 on average. After APC-based DPO, RAG+CPO reaches 6.9 average, surpassing GPT-4 by 2.8 points, while EU+CPO improves only marginally to 0.2. On the smaller original characters (8–30 statements), APC scores from the distilled DeBERTa evaluator correlate near-perfectly with human evaluation (Spearman ≈ 99% for Bob and Eve).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Character-LLM's Experience Upload (EU), the paper shows EU is consistently the weakest PRP technique: it scores −0.7 average ΔAPC without DPO and only 0.2 with DPO, far below RAG+CPO (6.9). EU's failure is attributed to its per-statement fine-tuning, which violates passive constraints from other persona statements. Against the RAG baseline (Lewis et al., 2020), the paper confirms RAG is the strongest in-context method (5.7 avg without DPO), but APC-based DPO further boosts it to 6.9, a +1.2 gain that eliminates most passive-constraint violations. Against GPT-4 (OpenAI, 2023), the open-source RAG+CPO system (6.9 avg) surpasses GPT-4 (4.1 avg) by 2.8 points on average, while GPT-4 still outperforms all open-source methods without DPO. The paper also validates that the APC score itself is a reliable metric: DeBERTa-distilled APC scores correlate at ~90% accuracy with GPT-4's labels, and on small characters the APC score achieves near-perfect Spearman correlation (97–99%) with human evaluation, outperforming a coarse-grained GPT-4 scoring baseline (81–92%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the persona-driven role-playing (PRP) framework introduced by Character-LLM (Shao et al., 2023), which established experience upload (EU) as a training paradigm, and on retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020) as a context-management strategy. It departs from prior PRP evaluation, which relied on coarse-grained LLM-based scoring without formal definitions, by recasting faithfulness as a MAX-CSP over atomic persona statements and quantifying it through NLI and relevance probabilities. The optimization component extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by using the APC score as a structured, constraint-wise reward rather than a single scalar preference signal, and the evaluation pipeline borrows the symbolic distillation paradigm (GPT-4 → DeBERTa-V3) to make fine-grained scoring tractable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APC score provides the first explainable, constraint-level diagnostic for PRP faithfulness, enabling practitioners to pinpoint exactly which persona statements a response violates (active entailment failures vs. passive contradiction failures) rather than receiving an opaque scalar. The finding that EU can actively degrade faithfulness for characters already known to the base LLM challenges the assumption that more training data on a persona is always beneficial, and the demonstrated synergy between RAG and APC-based DPO offers a practical recipe for building more faithful characters. Open questions include extending the criterion to multi-turn interactions, handling statement importance weighting, and reducing the model-dependency of the GPT-4-distilled discriminators.
