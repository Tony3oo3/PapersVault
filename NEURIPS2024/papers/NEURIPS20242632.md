## Problem

Automated proving of Olympiad-level algebraic inequalities is severely limited by two bottlenecks: the absence of large-scale, high-quality training datasets (unlike Euclidean geometry, where AlphaGeometry synthesized millions of theorems), and the infinite reasoning space within finite conditions that makes proof search intractable. Existing LLMs and interactive theorem provers solve zero or near-zero problems on IMO-level inequalities, leaving a significant gap in machine intelligence for algebraic reasoning.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a ternary or quaternary algebraic inequality expressed symbolically (e.g., f(x,y,z) ≤ g(x,y,z)) along with variable conditions (e.g., x,y,z ≥ 0), and produces a sequence of theorem applications and transformation rules constituting a human-readable formal proof. The same deductive engine also operates in generation mode, starting from randomly sampled cyclically symmetric expressions to autonomously synthesize new inequality theorems with verified equality conditions. Proofs are structured as chains of matched fundamental inequalities (AM-GM, Cauchy, Jensen, Schur, Muirhead) connected by transitivity, rather than free-form natural language.

## Method

AIPS combines a symbolic deductive engine—performing expression-tree traversal, monotonicity labeling, and pattern matching against a library of fundamental inequality theorems integrated with SymPy—with a transformer-based value network that scores candidate subgoals during best-first search. The value network is trained via a two-stage curriculum: first pre-trained on tree-depth as a proxy for difficulty over 191,643 synthetically generated theorems, then fine-tuned sequentially on 1,200 filtered problems sorted by increasing tree-depth, where proof-path subgoals receive scaled-down values and off-path subgoals receive increased values as supervision. The theorem generator runs forward reasoning for 8 hours on 32 CPUs to produce the training corpus, and the self-evolving loop iteratively improves the value network as the prover tackles progressively harder problems. Best-first search is preferred over MCTS due to the extremely large action space and the high computational cost of each reasoning step, which makes MCTS simulations impractical within time limits.

## Results

On the MO-INT-20 benchmark of 20 IMO-level algebraic inequality problems, AIPS solves 10 out of 20 problems (50%), the best result among all tested methods. The full AIPS with curriculum-trained value network outperforms its own ablations: the pretrained value network alone solves 7/20, the tree-depth heuristic solves 7/20, MCTS solves 5/20, and BFS solves 4/20. Among the 10 problems from IMO or IMO shortlists, AIPS solves 5, reaching the average level of IMO contestants. Each problem is given a 90-minute time limit, consistent with standard IMO problem-solving time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIPS decisively outperforms all three external baselines on MO-INT-20. GPT-4 and GPT-4 Turbo (Achiam et al., 2023) solve 0 out of 20 problems, and Llemma-7b (Azerbayev et al., 2023) also solves 0, indicating that current LLMs lack the sustained algebraic reasoning required for Olympiad-level inequalities. LeanCopilot (Song et al., 2024), the open-source state-of-the-art interactive theorem prover in Lean, likewise solves 0 problems, confirming that tactic-based proof search in Lean struggles with the complex premise selection needed for algebraic inequalities. In contrast, AIPS solves 10/20, a 10-problem margin over every external baseline. Internally, the curriculum-trained value network adds 3 additional solved problems over the pretrained-only variant (10 vs. 7) and 5 over BFS (10 vs. 4), demonstrating the value of the iterative fine-tuning strategy on filtered synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIPS integrates the online-training paradigm of HyperTree Proof Search (Lample et al. 2022) with the value-network-guided backward search of GPT-f (Polu and Sutskever 2020), but departs from both by replacing the ITP-based action space with a custom symbolic deductive engine built on SymPy and hand-crafted inequality theorems (AM-GM, Cauchy, Jensen, Schur, Muirhead). Unlike AlphaGeometry, which synthesized geometry theorems to train a language model from scratch, AIPS uses a forward-reasoning engine to autonomously generate algebraic inequality theorems and then trains a transformer-based value network on them via curriculum learning. The system also diverges from LLM-based provers (GPT-4, LeanCopilot) by treating proof search as a sequential decision problem over a structured expression tree rather than a code-completion or natural-language generation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical data-scarcity bottleneck in algebraic inequality proving by demonstrating that a symbolic engine can autonomously generate Olympiad-level theorems—validated by professional contestants, with one selected for a 2024 city-level Mathematical Olympiad—thereby enabling training without human demonstrations. The 10/20 vs. 0/20 gap between AIPS and the strongest LLM baselines underscores that domain-specific symbolic reasoning remains essential for high-difficulty algebra, at least at current LLM capability. Key limitations include the restriction to ternary/quaternary inequalities (no n-variable generalization), reliance on hand-written matching rules that do not scale to new definitions, and the absence of code release, which limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether the value curriculum strategy transfers to other inequality classes and whether the symbolic engine can be augmented to discover new transformation rules autonomously.
