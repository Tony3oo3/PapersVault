## Problem

The Normalized Cut (NCut) clustering problem is NP-complete and fractionally structured, yet existing fractional programming tools are inadequate: Dinkelbach's transform handles only a single ratio (limiting it to two-class clustering), and the scalar quadratic transform, while accommodating multiple ratios, still leaves the inner subproblem nonconvex and intractable for the discrete NCut formulation. This paper closes that gap by extending the quadratic transform to the multidimensional (matrix/vector) ratio case, yielding a tractable reformulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes N data points represented as feature vectors and a target number of clusters K, and constructs a weighted undirected graph via a Gaussian kernel similarity matrix W (with diagonal degree matrix D). The output is a binary assignment matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{N×K} where each row contains exactly one 1, encoding which cluster each point belongs to. The key structural constraint is the hard 0-1 discreteness of X, which the method exploits to reduce the inner optimization to a row-wise argmax rather than a general combinatorial search.

## Method

The core contribution is a multidimensional quadratic transform (Proposition 3) that extends the scalar quadratic transform to matrix-valued ratios, enabling the NCut sum-of-ratios objective to be recast with auxiliary matrix variables Y_k. The critical insight is that under the discrete constraint x_ik ∈ {0,1}, the quadratic denominator x_k^T D x_k collapses to the linear form δ^T x_k, so the inner optimization over X for fixed auxiliary variables reduces to a simple row-wise argmax (equivalently, a bipartite matching). The resulting FPC algorithm alternates between updating score vectors μ_k (computed in closed form without requiring W^{1/2}) and reassigning each point to its argmax cluster, with per-iteration complexity O(KN²). For indefinite similarity matrices, a shift W̃ = W + αD restores positive semi-definiteness. The paper further proves the iteration is a majorization-minimization procedure, guaranteeing monotonic nondecrease of the original NCut objective at every step.

## Results

The proposed FPC algorithm achieves the lowest NCut objective on all 8 benchmark datasets, outperforming SC, FINC, and FCD. For example, FPC reduces the NCut objective by 0.19% over FCD on Office+Caltech10 (K=10) and by 0.07% on Epileptic (N=11500). In terms of clustering quality, FPC attains the best ACC, NMI, and ARI on most datasets (e.g., ACC 0.7225 on Splice vs. 0.6646 for SC and 0.5096 for FCD). FPC also runs 73% faster than FINC with a per-iteration complexity of O(KN²) compared to O(N³) for spectral clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPC strictly outperforms all three baselines (SC, FINC, FCD) on the NCut objective for 7 out of 8 datasets, tying SC only on Rice (K=2). Against FINC [7], FPC wins on every dataset, with the largest gap on Splice (0.997636 vs. 0.999867) and Landsat (2.994335 vs. 2.999986); FINC can even degrade performance when initialized by SC (e.g., Thyroid: 0.989329 vs. SC's 0.983144). Against FCD [17], FPC achieves lower NCut on all datasets, with margins of 0.19% on Office+Caltech10 and 0.07% on Epileptic. Against SC [10], FPC matches or improves the NCut objective and additionally runs 73% faster than FINC while matching SC's speed. On practical clustering metrics (ACC/NMI/ARI), FPC leads on most datasets but is slightly behind FCD on USPS (ACC 0.6920 vs. 0.7060; ARI 0.5679 vs. 0.5734) and behind SC on Epileptic ACC (0.3197 vs. 0.3609), indicating that NCut optimality does not always translate to the best external clustering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multidimensional quadratic transform from Shen & Yu (2018), originally developed for power control in communication systems, to the graph-partitioning domain. It departs from the classic Dinkelbach's transform (limited to single-ratio, hence two-class clustering) and the scalar quadratic transform used in FINC (which decouples multiple ratios but leaves a hard nonconvex subproblem). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the NCut's sum-of-ratios structure, when the numerator is factorized via the matrix square root of W, becomes a multidimensional ratio problem whose transformed subproblem reduces to a row-wise argmax (bipartite matching), sidestepping the nonconvexity that plagues prior FP-based NCut solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FPC algorithm provides the first FP-based NCut solver with provable monotonic convergence of the original NCut objective (via MM theory) and per-iteration complexity O(KN²) versus O(N³) for spectral clustering. However, the practical gains are modest: NCut objective improvements over FCD are on the order of 0.1–0.2%, and FPC does not consistently dominate on ACC/NMI/ARI. The paper acknowledges it only demonstrates per-iteration (not total) complexity advantage over SC, and convergence is to a local optimum. Open questions include whether the multidimensional FP framework extends to other graph-cut variants (ratio cut, min-max cut) and whether the approach scales to very large graphs where the O(KN²) per-iteration cost becomes prohibitive.
