## Problem

Despite LLMs being empirically well-calibrated, the internal mechanisms by which they regulate confidence in next-token predictions remain unexplored. Entropy neurons had been identified as high-norm, low-logit-composition components, but their operational mechanism was unclear, and no prior work had identified or characterized a second class of confidence-regulating components tied to the token frequency distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on the weights of pre-trained decoder-only transformers (GPT-2 Small/Medium, Pythia 410M/1B, Phi-2, Gemma 2B, LLaMA2 7B) and 256-token sequences sampled from the C4 corpus (25,600 tokens for effect quantification). Outputs are per-neuron decompositions of total effect vs. LayerNorm-mediated direct effect, the fraction ρ of each neuron's norm projected onto the unembedding's effective null space (bottom k singular vectors), and for token frequency neurons, the fraction of effect mediated by the centered log-frequency direction. The induction case study additionally uses 200-token duplicated sequences and BOS ablations on specific attention heads.

## Method

The paper uses causal mediation analysis to decompose each final-layer neuron's effect on model loss into a total effect (mean-ablating the neuron) and a direct effect (ablation with the LayerNorm denominator held fixed), revealing that entropy neurons exert most of their influence through LayerNorm re-scaling rather than direct logit modification. SVD of the unembedding matrix exposes an effective null space (bottom singular values near zero) onto which entropy neurons write almost exclusively, allowing them to inflate residual stream norm and modulate output entropy without altering token rankings. A parallel analysis identifies token frequency neurons by decomposing effects along the centered log-frequency direction, showing these neurons shift the output distribution toward or away from the unigram baseline. A case study on induction (duplicated 100-token sequences) demonstrates that entropy neurons activate in response to induction heads via an internal 'induction-has-occurred' signal, acting as a hedging mechanism that increases entropy to prevent loss spikes on confidently wrong predictions during repeated subsequences.

## Results

The paper identifies entropy neurons in the final layer of GPT-2 Small, Pythia 410M/1B, Phi-2, Gemma 2B, and LLaMA2 7B, showing that ~80% of their total effect on model output is mediated by the final LayerNorm (vs. ~30–40% in Pythia and Gemma). The unembedding matrix W_U exhibits an effective null space (sharp drop in singular values near index 755 in GPT-2), and entropy neurons write almost exclusively into this null space. In the induction case study, ablating entropy neuron 11.2378 in GPT-2 Small reduces output entropy by up to 70% during repeated sequences, confirming its role as a confidence-hedging mechanism. Token frequency neurons (first identified in Pythia 410M) show a token-frequency-mediated effect of ~0.6 and significantly shift the KL divergence between the model output and the unigram distribution upon ablation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method-comparison study, so it does not benchmark against competing approaches. Instead, the key comparisons are: (1) entropy neurons vs. 100 randomly selected final-layer neurons, where only entropy neurons show a large gap between total effect and direct (non-LayerNorm) effect on model loss; (2) cross-model universality, where the null-space + LayerNorm mechanism is confirmed in GPT-2, LLaMA2 7B, Phi-2, Pythia, and Gemma, though the fraction of effect explained by LayerNorm varies (≈80% in GPT-2/LLaMA2/Phi-2 vs. ≈30–40% in Pythia/Gemma); and (3) induction heads vs. baseline attention heads in BOS ablations, where ablating the top induction head L5H1 causes a substantial decrease in entropy neuron 11.2378 activation while baseline heads have negligible effect. The paper also contrasts entropy neurons (which modulate confidence via a global rescaling) with token frequency neurons (which shift the output toward/away from the unigram distribution), showing they operate through distinct mechanistic pathways.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identification of entropy neurons by Gurnee et al. (2024) and Katz & Belinkov (2023) by revealing their actual mechanism of action: writing to an effective null space of the unembedding matrix and leveraging the final LayerNorm to rescale the residual stream. It departs from prior mechanistic interpretability work (Elhage et al., 2021; Geva et al., 2022) by showing that standard logit attribution methods systematically miss this LayerNorm-mediated pathway. The causal mediation analysis framework from Pearl (2001) and Robins & Greenland (1992) is repurposed to decompose total vs. direct neuron effects, and the induction heads framework from Olsson et al. (2022) provides the case-study setting. The discovery of token frequency neurons is entirely new, with no prior identification of components that modulate the output distribution's distance from the unigram distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first thorough investigation of dedicated circuitry for confidence calibration in LLMs, revealing that LayerNorm — typically viewed as a training-stability technique — is being repurposed as a computational mechanism for logit scaling. The discovery that the unembedding matrix contains a deliberate effective null space (the model limits its own representational power) has broader implications for how interpretability methods should account for normalization layers. Key limitations include reliance on entropy and KL-divergence as confidence proxies, focus on only two neuron types in the final layer, and varying effect magnitudes across architectures (80% LayerNorm-mediated in GPT-2/LLaMA2 vs. ~40% in Pythia/Gemma). Open questions include whether dropout during training is a necessary condition for null-space formation, whether these mechanisms generalize to reasoning and question-answering tasks, and what other confidence-regulation components exist beyond these two families.
