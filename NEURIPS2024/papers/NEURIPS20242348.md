## Problem

Existing few-shot segmentation methods rely on cross attention to fuse support foreground features into query features, incurring quadratic complexity that limits image size and the number of fusion blocks. While Mamba (selective state space models) offers linear-complexity long-range dependency capture, it has only been applied to intra-sequence (self) modeling and not to the inter-sequence (cross) case required by FSS. A naive cross Mamba that concatenates support and query sequences suffers from two failure modes: support forgetting (support features in the fixed-size hidden state are gradually diluted by query features) and intra-class gap (query FG is more similar to itself than to support FG, so it resists fusing support information).

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image and K support images with binary foreground masks (1-shot or 5-shot setting). Support features are masked to retain only foreground pixels and downsampled by a factor of α=4, while query features are split into α² patches of matching spatial size for SRM and separately downsampled for QIM. A pretrained CNN backbone (VGG16 or ResNet50) extracts mid-level features at 1/8 resolution, which are then processed through 8 alternating Mamba blocks before a decoder produces the final binary segmentation mask at full resolution.

## Method

HMNet alternates self Mamba blocks (SMB, based on VMamba's 4-directional scanning) with hybrid Mamba blocks (HMB) to capture intra- and inter-sequence dependencies respectively. The HMB contains two key components: (1) a Support Recapped Mamba (SRM) that interleaves downsampled support features with query patches in the scan sequence (support, query patch, support, query patch, …), so the hidden state is periodically refreshed with support information, preventing the support forgetting issue; and (2) a Query Intercepted Mamba (QIM) that takes the averaged pure-support hidden state from SRM and applies it to all query pixels in parallel (equivalent to a length-1 sequence per pixel), thereby blocking query-query mutual interactions and forcing each query pixel to fuse support features rather than relying on its own context. QIM shares its Mamba parameters with the first SRM direction for better learning. The total complexity remains linear in sequence length, and the model is trained with episodic (meta-learning) training on base classes and evaluated on novel classes.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet50, HMNet achieves 70.4% mIoU (1-shot) and 74.1% mIoU (5-shot), surpassing the previous best AMNet by 0.3% and 0.9% respectively. On COCO-20^i with ResNet50, HMNet reaches 52.1% mIoU (1-shot) and 58.9% mIoU (5-shot), outperforming AMNet by 1.1% and 1.6%. The largest gains appear on COCO-20^i, where HMNet exceeds HDMNet by 3.2% (VGG16, 1-shot) and 2.9% (ResNet50, 5-shot), attributed to the more severe intra-class gap in that dataset. With VGG16 on PASCAL-5^i, HMNet achieves 67.3% mIoU (1-shot) and 71.1% mIoU (5-shot), with FB-IoU scores of 79.2% and 82.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMNet outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i across 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the strongest attention-based competitor HDMNet [31], HMNet gains +1.8% mIoU (VGG16, 1-shot: 67.3 vs 65.1) and +2.3% (ResNet50, 5-shot: 74.1 vs 71.8) on PASCAL-5^i, and +3.2% (VGG16, 1-shot: 49.1 vs 45.9) and +2.9% (ResNet50, 5-shot: 58.9 vs 56.0) on COCO-20^i. Against the most recent transformer-based AMNet [48], HMNet leads by +0.3% (ResNet50, 1-shot: 70.4 vs 70.1) and +0.9% (5-shot: 74.1 vs 73.2) on PASCAL-5^i, and by +1.1% and +1.6% on COCO-20^i. Prototypical methods (PFENet [40], SVF [39]) and earlier attention-based methods (CyCTR [60], VAT [11], DACM [52], ABCNet [49]) are all surpassed by 2–10% mIoU. Notably, HMNet achieves these gains while using linear-complexity Mamba blocks instead of quadratic-complexity cross attention, and is faster in inference (e.g., 2,666s vs 3,193s average testing time on COCO-20^i with 40,000 episodes), and does not run out of memory at 1113×1113 image sizes where HDMNet fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to introduce Mamba (selective state space models) into few-shot segmentation, replacing the quadratic-complexity cross-attention that dominates existing FSS methods (SCCAN, HDMNet, AMNet) with a linear-complexity alternative. It builds directly on Mamba's selective SSM formulation and VMamba's 4-directional scanning for self-attention, while departing from prior cross-Mamba work (which assumes pixel-wise aligned modalities) by addressing the fundamentally different FSS setting where support and query images contain different objects and cannot be aligned. The base architecture is tailored from SCCAN, and the two key design contributions—support recapped Mamba (periodic re-scanning of support features) and query intercepted Mamba (parallel scanning to block query-query interactions)—are motivated by two specific failure modes identified when naively concatenating support and query sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for FSS practitioners: HMNet handles 1113×1113 images where HDMNet runs out of memory, and scales gracefully with image size, enabling deployment on larger inputs without the quadratic blowup of attention. The well-motivated diagnosis of support forgetting and intra-class gap issues, paired with simple yet effective fixes, provides a clear template for adapting Mamba to other cross-sequence fusion tasks beyond FSS. Open questions include whether the meta-learning angle (generating query Mamba parameters directly from support sequences) can further close the gap, and whether the QIM's lack of a CUDA implementation limits its practical speed advantage at scale.
