## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses that rely on clean unlearning followed by vanilla fine-tuning suffer from two under-explored issues: the behavior of clean unlearning on backdoored models is not well understood, and vanilla fine-tuning unintentionally reactivates the backdoor effect. This paper investigates whether clean unlearning can reveal backdoor-related neurons without access to poisoned data, and whether the heightened neuron activity in backdoored models can be exploited to prevent backdoor reactivation during recovery.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a backdoored model and 5% of the original dataset as clean training samples (no poisoned data, no trigger knowledge). It operates on standard RGB image classification tasks (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64, GTSRB at 32×32) with CNN or ViT backbones. The output is a repaired model that retains clean-data accuracy while reducing the attack success rate to near zero, evaluated via ACC, ASR, and DER metrics.

## Method

TSBD is a two-stage post-training defense. Stage 1 (NWC-based Backdoor Reinitialization) performs clean unlearning (maximizing cross-entropy loss on clean data until accuracy drops to ~10%), computes the Neuron Weight Change (L1 norm of per-neuron weight differences) for every neuron, then zero-reinitializes the top-m% most-changing subweights among the top-n% highest-NWC neurons in the original backdoored model. Stage 2 (Activeness-Aware Fine-Tuning) recovers clean accuracy by fine-tuning the reinitialized model with a loss that adds an L2 gradient-norm penalty (λ‖∇L_ce‖₂), suppressing the elevated neuron activity characteristic of backdoored models and preventing backdoor reactivation. The gradient-norm term is computed via a Hessian-free approximation (two forward-backward passes with a perturbed parameter) to avoid second-order computation. The key empirical insight is that NWC from clean unlearning positively correlates with poison-unlearning weight changes, enabling backdoor neuron identification without any poisoned data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreAct-ResNet18 (10% poisoning ratio, 5% clean data), TSBD achieves an average ASR of 2.18% and DER of 97.09%, while maintaining a clean accuracy of 91.70%—higher than the undefended backdoored model (91.34%). On Tiny ImageNet, TSBD achieves an average ASR of 1.01% and DER of 97.89% with 53.83% ACC. The method is robust to hyperparameter choices, with ASR remaining below 5% across neuron ratios from 1% to 90% and weight ratios from 40% to 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSBD achieves the best average ASR (2.18%) and DER (97.09%) across all 8 attacks on CIFAR-10, outperforming every baseline by a wide margin on both defense metrics. Against the closest competitor i-BAU [21] (ASR 17.44%, DER 88.11%), TSBD reduces ASR by ~8× while also improving ACC by 3.8 points. RNP [22], the most directly related method (also using clean unlearning), achieves ASR 29.28% and ACC 88.99%, showing that NWC-based reinitialization is substantially more effective than RNP's unlearn-recovery pruning mask. CLP [38] and FP [37] fail catastrophically on strong attacks like Blended and SSBA (ASR > 65%), whereas TSBD keeps ASR below 5% on all attacks. On ACC, TSBD (91.70%) is slightly below FT (92.13%) and FP (91.84%) but exceeds the no-defense baseline, indicating minimal clean-accuracy cost. On Tiny ImageNet, TSBD similarly dominates with ASR 1.01% vs. i-BAU's 86.17% and RNP's 0.02% (but RNP sacrifices ACC to 32.24% vs. TSBD's 53.83%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the clean unlearning paradigm introduced by RNP, which uses only clean data to unlearn and then prunes backdoored neurons. The key departure is twofold: (1) it discovers that neuron weight changes during clean unlearning are positively correlated with those during poison unlearning, enabling backdoor neuron identification via a Neuron Weight Change (NWC) metric without ever accessing poisoned data; and (2) it replaces the vanilla fine-tuning step (which inadvertently reactivates the backdoor) with an activeness-aware fine-tuning that penalizes gradient norms, motivated by the observation that backdoored neurons exhibit systematically larger gradient norms than their clean counterparts. This positions the work between activation-based pruning (FP, ANP) and unlearning-based defenses (RNP, i-BAU), offering a more principled neuron-selection criterion and a theoretically grounded fine-tuning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two structural observations—positive correlation of unlearning weight changes and elevated backdoor neuron activeness—provide a mechanistic explanation for why clean unlearning exposes backdoor neurons, which had previously been treated as an empirical heuristic. The practical impact is a defense that consistently achieves the lowest average ASR (~2%) and highest DER (~97%) across 8 attacks, 3 datasets, and 2 architectures, while preserving or even exceeding the original clean accuracy. A notable limitation is degraded performance at 1% poisoning ratio, where backdoor-related weight changes become less distinguishable, and the method still requires 5% clean data. The authors identify data-free backdoor defense as the key open direction, suggesting that the NWC and activeness insights could be adapted to settings where no clean data is available.
