## Problem

Existing LM-based multi-agent planners assume perfect low-level action execution, rely on oracle or simulator feedback for subtask verification, and require full environment observability—assumptions that break down in real-world partially observable settings. Naive extensions of single-agent planning to multi-agent scenarios fail due to environment non-stationarity, where other agents' policies continuously change. The paper addresses how to enable robust, long-horizon multi-agent task planning with LMs that operate without privileged environmental knowledge or perfect execution guarantees.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural language task instruction, per-agent RGB images (1000×1000×3, 90° FOV, 1.5 m visibility range), and joint state information (location, rotation, inventory) from all agents at each high-level decision step. Outputs are free-form natural language action proposals per agent, which are mapped to executable high-level actions (NavigateTo, Pickup, Put, Open, Close, etc.) via a fine-tuned SentenceBERT cosine-similarity module. The system also produces a dynamically updated subtask list, corrective action suggestions on failure, and self-verified subtask completion status—all without querying the simulator for ground-truth state.

## Method

LLaMAR is a zero-shot cognitive architecture that iterates four specialized GPT-4V modules—Planner, Actor, Corrector, and Verifier—at every high-level decision step in a plan-act-correct-verify loop. The Planner decomposes the language instruction into feasible subtasks grounded in current observations and joint memory; the Actor selects synchronized high-level actions for all agents; the Corrector diagnoses execution failures from visual feedback and proposes corrective actions (e.g., navigating closer before picking up); and the Verifier confirms subtask completion purely from observations rather than simulator oracles. A CLIP-based exploration heuristic scores four cardinal directions by cosine similarity between image embeddings and open-subtask text embeddings, guiding agents toward semantically relevant regions. The centralized multi-agent formulation feeds all agents' observations into a single set of LM modules, enabling coordination and conflict avoidance, while a shared textual memory tracks past observations, actions, and failure reasons across the planning horizon.

## Results

On the 2-agent MAP-THOR benchmark, LLaMAR (GPT-4V with exploration) achieves a 66% success rate, 91% transport rate, 97% coverage, and 0.82 balance, substantially outperforming all baselines. The headline improvement is a 30% higher success rate over the next-best baseline (ReAct at 34%). In the Search & Rescue environment, LLaMAR with 5 agents reaches 74% success rate and 96% transport rate, demonstrating scalability to more complex multi-agent coordination tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaMAR outperforms all four baselines across every metric. Against ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), LLaMAR more than doubles the success rate (66% vs. 34%) and improves transport rate by 26 percentage points (91% vs. 72%), benefiting from its explicit Planner and Corrector modules that ReAct lacks. Against SmartLLM (Kannan et al., 2023), LLaMAR achieves 6× higher success rate (66% vs. 11%) and 4× higher transport rate (91% vs. 23%), as SmartLLM's plan-and-execute paradigm produces infinite loops and fails to handle low-level action failures. Against CoELA (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024), LLaMAR nearly triples the success rate (66% vs. 25%) and improves transport rate by 45 percentage points (91% vs. 46%), with the centralized architecture proving more effective than CoELA's decentralized approach for action selection. Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), LLaMAR achieves a 4.7× success rate advantage (66% vs. 14%), as CoT's static reasoning degrades over longer planning horizons. Notably, CoELA achieves the highest balance (0.73) among baselines, but LLaMAR surpasses it (0.82) while also leading on all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaMAR builds on the centralized multi-agent system (CMAS) framework of Chen et al. [16] and the verifier-based planning improvements of Arora & Kambhampati [17], but departs sharply from prior LM-based multi-agent planners (SmartLLM, CoELA, S-ATLAS, Two-Step) by operating without full observability, without oracle/simulator feedback for subtask verification, and without assuming perfect low-level action execution. The plan-act-correct-verify cognitive architecture is positioned as a grounded alternative to open-loop plan-and-execute paradigms and to LM self-verification methods that lack environmental grounding. The paper also introduces MAP-THOR as a new benchmark, extending SmartLLM's 36-task dataset to 45 tasks across 5 floor plans with four ambiguity levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the Corrector module achieves nearly identical performance with visual self-correction (SR 0.66) as with oracle-provided failure information (SR 0.67) is practically significant: it suggests real-world robots need not rely on privileged simulator access to recover from action failures. However, the approach's high LM query cost (4 modules per step), limited spatial reasoning, and performance ceiling set by the underlying VLM remain open challenges. The balance metric's sharp decline with more agents (0.82 → 0.54 from 2 to 5 agents) highlights an unresolved coordination problem that will matter for scaling to larger robot teams. MAP-THOR provides a useful standardized benchmark, but the evaluation is confined to AI2-THOR and a custom grid-world SAR environment, leaving open whether the architecture transfers to other simulators or physical robots.
