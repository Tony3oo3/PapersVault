## Problem

Existing generative models for 3D geometric structures (molecules, proteins, particle systems) only synthesize static equilibrium snapshots, ignoring that physical systems are inherently dynamic. Modeling the full temporal distribution of geometric trajectories is non-trivial because it must simultaneously preserve SE(3) physical symmetry, capture inter-frame temporal correspondence, and handle the substantially higher-dimensional and more diverse distribution compared to single-frame generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240806/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a geometric graph (node features such as atomic numbers or charges, and edges defined by bond connectivity or proximity) plus, in the conditional case, a partial trajectory of T_c observed frames as the conditioning signal. It produces a full geometric trajectory of T timesteps — a sequence of N×D coordinate matrices (D=3 for molecular/particle systems, D=2 for pedestrian scenes) — sampled from the learned distribution. For MD17, trajectories span 9–21 atoms with edges within three bond hops; for pedestrian forecasting, 8 input frames predict the next 12 frames of 2D coordinates.

## Method

GeoTDM is a diffusion model whose reverse process is parameterized by EGTN, a network that alternates SE(3)-equivariant graph convolution layers (EGCL) for spatial interactions and temporal self-attention layers with relative positional encoding for inter-frame dependencies. The key theoretical contribution proves that if the prior is SE(3)-invariant and all transition kernels are SE(3)-equivariant, the marginal at every diffusion step inherits the desired symmetry. For unconditional generation, the model operates in a translation-invariant subspace (zero-centered coordinates) with a restricted isotropic Gaussian prior; for conditional generation, it introduces a learnable equivariant prior that computes a point-wise linear combination of conditioning frames with learned per-node weights, and modifies both forward and reverse kernels to center diffusion around this prior. Training uses a standard noise-prediction objective, and the coordinate update in attention reuses attention coefficients weighted by coordinate differences to guarantee rotation equivariance.

## Results

On N-body conditional generation, GeoTDM achieves the lowest ADE/FDE across all three physics scenarios: Charged Particles (0.110/0.258), Spring Dynamics (0.0030/0.0079), and Gravity (0.256/0.613). On MD17 molecular dynamics, GeoTDM improves ADE/FDE by 23.1%/15.3% on average over the previous state-of-the-art EqMotion across all 8 molecules. On ETH-UCY pedestrian forecasting, GeoTDM achieves the best average minADE/minFDE of 0.23/0.40. For unconditional generation on Charged Particles, GeoTDM reaches a classification score of 0.556 (near 0.5 = indistinguishable from real), with a prediction score of 0.00978, far below all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoTDM outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. Frame-to-frame models (RF, TFN, SE(3)-Transformer, EGNN) suffer from error accumulation during roll-out and are consistently outperformed; for example, on Spring Dynamics GeoTDM achieves ADE 0.0030 vs. EGNN's 0.0101 (3.4× lower). The deterministic trajectory model EqMotion is the strongest single-trajectory baseline but is outperformed by GeoTDM on all 8 MD17 molecules (e.g., Aspirin ADE 0.107 vs. 0.185, a 42% reduction), attributed to GeoTDM's probabilistic modeling capturing MD stochasticity. The non-equivariant probabilistic model SVAE is significantly worse than equivariant methods, confirming the importance of SE(3) symmetry (e.g., on Gravity, SVAE ADE 0.582 vs. GeoTDM 0.256). On pedestrian forecasting (ETH-UCY), GeoTDM beats task-specific GANs (SGAN, SoPhie), VAEs (PECNet, Traj++, BiTraP, SVAE), and the diffusion-based MID, achieving the lowest average minADE/minFDE (0.23/0.40) without task-specific preprocessing or auxiliary losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion model framework (DDPM) to the geometric trajectory domain by combining it with SE(3)-equivariant graph neural networks (EGNN/EGCL) for spatial processing and temporal attention for inter-frame dependencies. It departs from prior equivariant diffusion work on static molecules (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) by operating on sequences of frames rather than single configurations, and from prior trajectory models (EqMotion, frame-to-frame GNNs) by adopting a generative diffusion formulation that enables unconditional generation, interpolation, and optimization. The learnable equivariant prior is a principled generalization that provably subsumes both CoM-based and fixed point-wise priors used in earlier equivariant diffusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoTDM provides the first theoretically grounded equivariant diffusion framework for geometric trajectories, with formal proofs that SE(3)-invariant/equivariant marginals are preserved throughout the denoising process. The unified framework's versatility (generation, interpolation, optimization, conditional forecasting) across N-body physics, molecular dynamics, and pedestrian trajectories without task-specific modifications suggests a general recipe for geometric dynamics modeling. Key limitations include multi-step sampling cost, evaluation only on small systems (≤21 atoms, ≤10 particles), and the open question of whether the approach scales to large biomolecular systems (protein MD) or high-dimensional motion synthesis, which the authors identify as future work.
