## Problem

While Vision Transformers have been extensively studied for In-Distribution accuracy, the impact of ViT architectural design choices (embedding dimension, depth, number of heads, MLP ratio) on Out-of-Distribution generalization remains largely unexplored. Prior work evaluated only 3–22 human-designed ViTs under OoD settings, and no systematic NAS benchmark or training-free proxy study existed for ViT OoD robustness, leaving it unclear whether ID-optimized architectural insights transfer to OoD performance.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 3,000 ViT sub-architectures sampled from pre-trained Autoformer-Tiny/Small/Base one-shot supernets (inheriting supernet weights without per-architecture training) and evaluates them on 8 large-scale OoD datasets spanning algorithmic corruptions (IN-C, IN-P), natural shifts (IN-R, IN-Sketch, Stylized IN), adversarial examples (IN-A, IN-O), and diffusion-generated images (IN-D). Outputs include per-architecture OoD accuracy, ID accuracy, AUPR (for IN-O detection), and Kendall τ rank correlations between OoD accuracy and various proxies (training-free NAS scores, #Param, #Flops, and individual architectural attributes).

## Method

The paper constructs OoD-ViT-NAS by leveraging one-shot NAS: three pre-trained Autoformer supernets are used to efficiently extract 1,000 sub-architectures each (total 3,000) across varying computational budgets, avoiding the prohibitive cost of training each candidate individually. The core empirical analysis proceeds in four stages: (1) quantifying the OoD accuracy range across architectures to demonstrate architectural impact; (2) computing Kendall τ correlations between ID and OoD accuracy to show the weak transfer of ID-based insights; (3) benchmarking 9 training-free NAS proxies (Grasp, SNIP, MeCo, CroZe, Jacobian, DSS, AutoProx-A, #Param, #Flops) for OoD prediction, revealing that simple proxies outperform complex ones; and (4) isolating the effect of each ViT structural attribute via controlled ablations and frequency-domain analysis, showing that increasing embedding dimension is the dominant factor for OoD improvement because it enables the model to capture more high-frequency components. This insight yields a practical design rule: scaling only the embedding dimension (e.g., ViT-B-32 → 840-dim) outperforms compound scaling to ViT-L-32 in OoD accuracy while being significantly more parameter- and latency-efficient.

## Results

On the OoD-ViT-NAS benchmark (3,000 ViT architectures, 8 OoD datasets), simple zero-cost proxies #Param (Kendall τ = 0.3600) and #Flops (0.3537) outperform all 9 complex Training-free NAS methods in predicting OoD accuracy, with the best ViT-specific methods DSS (0.3421) and AutoProx-A (0.3303) trailing behind. The average OoD accuracy range across architectures is 3.81%/4.86%/2.74% for Autoformer-Tiny/Small/Base search spaces, comparable to or exceeding the 1.9% improvement from SOTA domain-invariant training methods. Increasing embedding dimension alone (e.g., ViT-B-32 → 840-dim) achieves 48.28% IN-R OoD accuracy, surpassing compound-scaled ViT-L-32 (44.33%) and Swin-S (47.77%) at substantially lower latency and parameter cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 9 Training-free NAS proxies against OoD accuracy on 3,000 ViT architectures. All complex methods lose to the trivial #Param and #Flops proxies: the best ViT-specific methods DSS (0.3421) and AutoProx-A (0.3303) fall 0.018 and 0.030 below #Param (0.3600) respectively. CNN-based methods (Grasp, SNIP, MeCo, CroZe, Jacobian) perform even worse, with correlations ranging from 0.0841 to 0.2951. Notably, all methods consistently fail on ImageNet-D (Stable Diffusion-generated), where correlations drop to near zero or negative. In the architecture design comparison (Table 3), the paper's insight-driven approach (increasing only embedding dimension) achieves 48.28% IN-R OoD accuracy, beating ViT-L-32 (44.33%) by 3.95% and Swin-S (47.77%) by 0.51%, while using 67.8% fewer parameters than ViT-L-32 and 45.4% fewer than Swin-S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NAS-for-robustness line of work (NASOOD for CNNs, adversarial-robust NAS) to Vision Transformers and specifically to OoD generalization, a setting largely unexplored for ViTs. It builds directly on AutoFormer's one-shot search space and pre-trained supernets to efficiently sample 3,000 sub-architectures, and adapts the high-frequency component analysis from Bai et al. (ECCV 2022) to explain why embedding dimension matters. The training-free NAS evaluation draws on methods originally designed for CNNs (SNIP, Grasp, MeCo) and ViTs (DSS, AutoProx), as well as adversarial-robustness proxies (CroZe, Jacobian), positioning this as the first systematic study of these tools under OoD shifts for ViTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark (3,000 ViT architectures × 8 large-scale OoD datasets) fills a critical gap: prior ViT OoD studies examined only 3–22 human-designed models, making it impossible to isolate architectural attribute effects. The finding that ID accuracy poorly predicts OoD accuracy (and that Pareto-optimal ID architectures perform sub-optimally under OoD) challenges the implicit assumption in much of the ViT NAS literature that ID-optimized designs transfer to robustness. The negative result that trivial proxies (#Param, #Flops) outperform all sophisticated training-free NAS for OoD prediction is a significant open problem for the NAS community. Limitations include reliance on a single search space (AutoFormer), one-shot weight-sharing assumptions, and the unpredictable behavior on ImageNet-D, which may limit generalizability of some findings.
