## Problem

Existing jointly-differentially-private (JDP) resource allocation algorithms rely on Advanced Composition for privacy accounting (injecting unnecessarily large noise), fail to exploit the geometric structure of the dual space, and lack formal minimax lower bounds characterizing the fundamental privacy-optimality trade-off. This paper asks whether a generic algorithm can achieve tighter privacy guarantees, better optimality/constraint-violation bounds by leveraging primal-dual structure, and provable near-optimality.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n agents' private data (concave utility functions, convex consumption functions, convex feasible sets), a public resource availability vector nγb, and a privacy budget (ε, δ). It outputs the time-averaged allocation over T iterations, which may be infeasible—constraint violations are bounded but not eliminated. The public DP signal is the full sequence of noisy shadow prices; each agent computes its own allocation from this signal and its private data via the Billboard Lemma, ensuring JDP without revealing individual allocations.

## Method

Noisy Dual Mirror Descent dualizes the resource-coupling constraint into shadow prices and applies noisy Mirror Descent to the resulting dual problem, adding Gaussian noise to the dual gradient at each iteration. Privacy is accounted for via Rényi DP sequential composition, yielding a variance of T·‖b‖₂²·(2ln(1/δ)/ε² + 1/ε), substantially tighter than the Advanced Composition bounds used by prior work. Under strong duality, the optimal shadow price p* is shown to lie in an ℓ₁-bounded scaled simplex, motivating a parameterized negative entropy potential function Φ(w;b)=Σ b_j w_j ln(b_j w_j) that is strongly convex with respect to the ℓ₁ norm on this domain. This geometric alignment improves the optimality gap from O(√(m ln(1/δ))/ε) (weak duality, squared ℓ₂ potential) to Õ(√(ln(1/δ))/ε) (strong duality, negative entropy), while a matching minimax lower bound of Ω(m/ε) for feasible-output JDP algorithms confirms near-optimality up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

Theoretically, Noisy Dual Mirror Descent achieves a utility gap of O(√(m ln(1/δ))/ε) and per-constraint violation of O(√(m ln(1/δ))/ε) under weak duality, improving to Õ(√(ln(1/δ))/ε) and Õ(m√(ln(1/δ))/ε) under strong duality via negative entropy potential. A matching minimax lower bound of Ω(m/ε) is established for ε ≥ max{1, 1/(nγ)}, confirming near-optimality. Numerically, on large-scale assignment problems (n up to 3000), MD_l2 and MD_ne reduce the relative optimality gap from −29.4% (HHRW16 at ε=1) to 4.3% and 10.4% respectively, while cutting total constraint violation from >10³ to 193.9 and 134.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole numerical baseline is the dual gradient descent algorithm of Hsu et al. (HHRW16, SODA 2016). On the largest assignment instance (n=3000, m=30), HHRW16 produces negative optimality gaps (−29.4% at ε=1, −18.9% at ε=2), meaning it allocates less utility than the non-private optimum, and total constraint violations exceeding 10³ at low ε. In contrast, MD_l2 achieves a 4.3% gap and 193.9 violation at ε=1, and MD_ne achieves 10.4% and 134.5. At ε=10, both proposed methods reduce the gap to ~1.5–1.6% and violations to 18–22, versus 8.2% and 680.1 for HHRW16. MD_l2 tends to have lower optimality gaps at small ε, while MD_ne (negative entropy) nearly dominates at larger ε, consistent with the theoretical advantage of exploiting ℓ₁ geometry under strong duality. On the smaller workforce scheduling instance (7 workers, 14 days), the paper reports that private decisions visually converge to the non-private optimum as ε increases, with smaller optimality gaps at only slightly higher constraint violations compared to the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual decomposition paradigm for JDP resource allocation established by Hsu et al. (SODA 2016) and Huang & Zhu (SODA 2018), replacing their projected gradient descent / multiplicative weight updates with a general Noisy Mirror Descent framework that can exploit the geometry of the dual space. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (i) using Rényi DP for tighter privacy accounting instead of Advanced Composition, (ii) leveraging the ℓ1 geometric structure of the dual space under strong duality via parameterized negative entropy, and (iii) providing the first minimax lower bound for JDP resource allocation. The algorithm is a direct application of the Mirror Descent framework of Nemirovskij & Yudin (1983) to the private dual problem, connecting it to the broader non-Euclidean DP optimization literature (Asi et al. 2021, Bassily et al. 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first near-optimal algorithm with matching lower bound for convex resource allocation under JDP, resolving a fundamental open question about the privacy-optimality trade-off in this setting. The Rényi DP analysis reduces required noise by roughly 8× compared to prior work, making the algorithm substantially more practical. Key limitations include the lower bound only holding for ε ≥ max{1, 1/(nγ)}, and the feasibility guarantee requiring knowledge of ‖p*‖₁ (which in practice may require privacy-sensitive tuning of the domain W). Open directions include extending lower bounds to smaller ε, online settings, and finding a DP-safe way to tune the simplex radius constant K.
