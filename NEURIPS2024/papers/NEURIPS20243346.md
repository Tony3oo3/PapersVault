## Problem

3D Human Pose Estimation is limited by two scalability bottlenecks: existing annotated datasets suffer from 'popularity bias' (collected in controlled settings with limited motions and demographics), and the one-to-many depth ambiguity problem worsens as pose diversity increases. Prior pose augmentation methods inherit the same popularity bias because they derive knowledge from the source dataset, failing to generate truly out-of-distribution poses needed for real-world generalization.

## Input / Output

The estimation pipeline takes 2D joint positions (16-keypoint model, from HRNet or ground truth) and camera parameters (focal length, principal point) as input, and outputs 3D joint positions in camera space coordinates. Unconventionally, the BDC representation encodes each joint as a tuple of 2D position, bone length, a binary depth sign (front/back relative to the parent joint), and the parent joint's 3D coordinates, rather than a continuous depth value. The BPG generator produces paired 2D-3D poses entirely from biomechanical constraints with no source data dependency, using randomly sampled camera rotations (uniform on SO(3)) and translations.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary components. The Biomechanical Pose Generator (BPG) uses an osteo-kinematic model (17 joints, 16 bones, 25 angle parameters) constrained by Normal Range of Motion intervals and body proportion ratios to sample plausible 3D poses via forward kinematics, then projects them to 2D with randomly sampled camera parameters; a pose confidence metric rejects anatomically valid but physically impossible configurations (e.g., intersecting limbs). The Binary Depth Coordinates (BDC) reconceptualize depth estimation as a binary classification: given a predicted bone length and 2D joint position, a quadratic equation derived from the sphere constraint yields exactly two depth solutions, and a binary parameter s_i (predicted per anatomical segment via Gumbel-Softmax) selects the correct one. BDC integrates into existing lifting networks by replacing the final output layer with heads for 2D pose, bone length, root depth, and grouped binary depth, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Results

On the H36M benchmark (full training set, S9/S11 test), BPG+BDC achieves 44.0 mm MPJPE and 34.3 mm P-MPJPE, outperforming the best prior pose augmentation method CEE-Net (47.3/36.8) and the VPose baseline (52.7/40.6) by 3.3 mm and 8.7 mm respectively. In cross-dataset evaluation on 3DPW (in-the-wild, moving camera), BPG+BDC reduces P-MPJPE to 61.1 mm versus 94.6 mm for VPose and 76.8 mm for CEE-Net. On 3DHP, the method achieves 91.8% PCK and 62.3% AUC, surpassing the previous best (CEE-Net: 89.9%/58.2%). Integrating BDC into SOTA lifting models (GraphMDN, GFpose, Conpose, MixSTE) yields consistent 1.5–3.1 mm MPJPE improvements across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPG+BDC outperforms all compared pose augmentation baselines on every reported metric. On H36M (Table 1), it beats the strongest prior method CEE-Net by 3.3 mm MPJPE and 2.5 mm P-MPJPE, and the VPose baseline by 8.7 mm MPJPE. On the cross-dataset 3DHP benchmark (Table 3), BPG+BDC achieves 61.8 mm MPJPE, improving over CEE-Net (69.7 mm), PoseGU (75.0 mm), and RepNet (92.5 mm) by 7.9, 13.2, and 30.7 mm respectively. On the challenging 3DPW in-the-wild dataset (Table 4), BPG+BDC reaches 61.1 mm P-MPJPE, a 33.5 mm improvement over VPose (94.6 mm) and a 15.7 mm gain over CEE-Net (76.8 mm). BPG alone (without BDC) already surpasses all prior augmentation methods, confirming that the biomechanical generation strategy itself contributes meaningful diversity. The BDC component provides an additional 2.9 mm MPJPE gain on H36M and 5.4 mm on 3DPW, demonstrating that the binary depth formulation is particularly effective under high pose diversity and depth ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on osteo-kinematic models (Koritnik et al.) and forward kinematic formulations (Zhou et al.) to generate synthetic 3D poses, but departs fundamentally from prior pose augmentation methods (PoseAug, DH-AUG, CEE-Net, EvoSkeleton) by eliminating dependence on source data entirely, instead using biomechanical Normal Range of Motion constraints to sample poses autonomously. On the estimation side, the Binary Depth Coordinates (BDC) framework reinterprets the depth-lifting problem from continuous regression to a binary classification per joint, a conceptual departure from both Camera Space Coordinates and Normalized Device Coordinates used in prior lifting-based methods (VPose, GraphMDN, GFpose). The paper integrates BDC into existing architectures (VPose, GraphMDN, GFpose, Conpose, MixSTE) with minimal modifications, positioning itself as a plug-in enhancement rather than a new end-to-end model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks that limit real-world deployment of 3D HPE: the popularity bias in existing datasets and the one-to-many depth ambiguity that worsens with pose diversity. The finding that increasing data diversity *degrades* baseline performance (due to amplified depth ambiguity) but *improves* BDC-equipped models is a useful diagnostic for practitioners considering data augmentation strategies. Key limitations include single-frame generation (no temporal dynamics), a saturation point for synthetic data volume, and evaluation limited to three standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether BDC generalizes to multi-view or video settings, and whether the binary depth assumption holds under extreme perspective or occlusion.
