## Problem

Pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) have been shown to improve sample efficiency and generalization in model-free RL, but their utility in model-based RL (MBRL) remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether PVRs transfer their benefits to MBRL agents, and surprisingly finds that they do not: representations learned from scratch are equally or more data-efficient and generalize better to out-of-distribution visual shifts than any tested PVR.

## Input / Output

The agents receive 256×256 RGB images from three simulation domains (DMC, ManiSkill2, Miniworld) with no proprioceptive information; most PVRs internally crop to 224×224. The frozen PVR produces a fixed-dimensional embedding (ranging from 768 to 2048 dimensions depending on the model) that replaces the original CNN encoder, and the MBRL algorithm outputs continuous or discrete control actions along with predicted latent states and rewards from its learned world model. For TD-MPC2, a stack of the last 3 PVR embeddings is fed to the encoder rather than a single frame.

## Method

The paper benchmarks eight PVRs (CLIP, R3M, Taskonomy, VIP, DINOv2, OpenCLIP, VC-1, R2D2) plus in-domain autoencoders and from-scratch baselines by integrating them into two state-of-the-art MBRL algorithms, DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2, via a frozen PVR followed by a trainable linear layer. Agents are trained on 10 tasks across DMC, ManiSkill2, and Miniworld under randomized visual attributes, then evaluated on both in-distribution and held-out out-of-distribution splits. To explain the negative results, the authors analyze world model quality on a Pendulum Swingup task, finding that PVR-based agents produce comparable dynamics predictions but significantly worse reward predictions, and UMAP visualizations reveal that PVR embeddings fail to disentangle reward-relevant state structure. The study further ablates PVR properties (language conditioning, sequential data, data diversity, ViT architecture) and identifies data diversity and ViT architecture as the most important factors for OOD generalization.

## Results

Across 10 control tasks in DMC, ManiSkill2, and Miniworld, representations learned from scratch are in most cases equally or more data-efficient than all 8 PVRs (CLIP, R3M, Taskonomy, VIP, DINOv2, OpenCLIP, VC-1, R2D2), even when the PVRs are trained on hundreds of millions of image-text pairs. In OOD evaluation, the from-scratch baseline outperforms every PVR, with the notable exception of VC-1 which performs comparably. World model analysis reveals a significant negative correlation between reward prediction error and task performance (r = −0.66, p = 0.004), while dynamics prediction error shows no significant correlation (r = −0.22, p = 0.4), indicating that PVRs' inability to encode reward-relevant information is the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 8 PVRs against a from-scratch encoder baseline using DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 across DMC, ManiSkill2, and Miniworld. The from-scratch representation outperforms all PVRs in both in-distribution and OOD settings, contradicting the common assumption that pre-training accelerates MBRL. VC-1 (trained on Ego4D-MNI with MAE) is the only PVR that performs comparably to from-scratch, likely due to its diverse training data and ViT architecture. CLIP (both RN50x64 and ViT-L variants), R3M, VIP, DINOv2, OpenCLIP, Taskonomy, and R2D2 all underperform the from-scratch baseline, with DMC showing the largest gaps. Even an autoencoder pre-trained in-distribution on task-specific data performs worse than training from scratch, suggesting the objective mismatch in MBRL (simultaneously optimizing dynamics accuracy and agent performance) makes it harder to adapt to a fixed representation. Among PVR properties, data diversity and ViT architecture are the most important contributors to OOD generalization, while language conditioning and sequential training data play minor roles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extensive line of work on PVRs for RL (CLIP, R3M, DINOv2, VC-1, etc.) from model-free and imitation-learning settings into model-based RL, where the representation must simultaneously support dynamics prediction, reward prediction, and policy learning. It builds directly on DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 as the downstream MBRL algorithms, replacing their learned encoders with frozen PVRs plus a linear layer. The key conceptual departure from prior PVR-for-RL work is the identification of the objective mismatch in MBRL (Lambert et al., 2020) as a fundamental reason why PVR benefits observed in model-free settings do not transfer, and the extension of OOD evaluation to the downstream RL agent rather than only the PVR itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark challenges the widely-held assumption that pre-trained visual representations universally accelerate and improve RL agents, showing that for MBRL they are often worse than learning from scratch in both sample efficiency and OOD generalization. The mechanistic finding that PVRs fail to embed reward-relevant structure in their latent space (visualized via UMAP) provides an actionable explanation and suggests that future PVRs for MBRL must explicitly account for reward predictability. Open questions include whether the results hold in real-world settings, whether new PVRs designed with MBRL objectives (e.g., reward-aware pre-training) could overcome the limitation, and whether the objective mismatch can be mitigated through architectural changes.
