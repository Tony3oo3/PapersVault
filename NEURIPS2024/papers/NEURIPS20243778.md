## Problem

Existing RLHF methods align LLMs by comparing individual single-turn responses, which cannot capture the long-term, trajectory-level effects of actions in multi-turn interactions. A single action's quality may depend entirely on how the conversation subsequently unfolds, making turn-level feedback fundamentally insufficient for tasks requiring planning across multiple exchanges. This paper addresses the gap by developing preference-based RL that operates on entire multi-turn conversations rather than individual turns.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a context (e.g., a learning topic) and iteratively observes conversation history as state, generating one response per turn. Preference feedback is provided only at the end of a full H-turn trajectory, comparing two complete conversations rather than scoring individual turns. The preference-based Q-function is defined over pairs of policies (self-play), and the comparison policy always starts from the initial state even when evaluating a sub-trajectory, which differs from standard RL Q-functions. The output is a turn-level policy mapping (context, conversation history) → response distribution, implemented as an autoregressive T5 encoder-decoder model.

## Method

MTPO (Multi-turn Preference Optimization) is a mirror-descent policy optimization algorithm that uses self-play to converge to the Nash equilibrium of a regularized preference model over full trajectories. The key technical ingredient is a preference-based Q-function Q_α^{π,π'}(x_h, y_h) that measures the probability of the final state being preferred over a comparison policy's final state, minus accumulated KL divergence from a reference policy, enabling local per-state optimization to achieve the global minimax objective via a value difference lemma. The update rule π_{t+1}(y|x) ∝ μ(y|x)^{αη_t} · π_t(y|x)^{1-αη_t} · exp(η_t · Q_α^{π_t,π_t}(x,y)) improves the current policy in the direction of its self-play advantage while staying close to the reference. A variant MTPO-τ replaces the self-play Q-function with one computed under a geometric mixture policy π_t^α, introducing stochasticity that empirically improves performance. For deep architectures, the algorithm is implemented as an actor-critic policy gradient with GAE-based advantage estimation and turn-level (not token-level) value targets, dropping the inter-iteration KL penalty in favor of the fixed reference KL regularization.

## Results

On the Education Dialogue benchmark (preference-based, no explicit reward), MTPO-τ achieves a 0.907 average preference over SFT, 0.646 over single-turn RLHF-reward, and 0.758 over single-turn Nash-MD (Table 1, Flan-T5 XL judge). When evaluated with the same Gemini Ultra model that generated the preference data, MTPO-τ (T5-XL) reaches 0.959 over SFT and 0.951 over RLHF-reward (Table 2). In the reward-based Car Dealer environment, MTPO achieves a sale price of 58.6 (±0.3), matching the standard RL baseline of 58.4 (±0.3) despite using only preference feedback rather than explicit rewards (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-turn RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017), MTPO-τ wins decisively: it achieves 0.646 preference over RLHF-reward and 0.634 over RLHF-value, confirming that conversation-level preference feedback is superior to turn-level reward signals for multi-turn dialogue. Against single-turn Nash-MD (Munos et al., 2023), MTPO-τ also wins with 0.758 over Nash-reward and 0.730 over Nash-value, extending the Nash-equilibrium framework from single-turn to multi-turn settings. Among multi-turn methods, MTPO-τ outperforms multi-turn RLHF (0.588 preference) and MTPO (0.561), with the authors attributing MTPO-τ's edge to the stochasticity of its mixture policy providing more diverse self-play trajectories. In the reward-based Car Dealer environment, MTPO (58.6 ± 0.3) matches or slightly exceeds standard policy-gradient RL (58.4 ± 0.3) and clearly outperforms multi-turn RLHF (53.2 ± 0.3), demonstrating that preference-based learning can recover reward-based performance even with a weaker signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-turn preference-based RL framework of Nash-MD (Munos et al., 2023) and IPO-MD (Calandriello et al., 2024) to the multi-turn setting, where preference feedback is given between entire conversations rather than individual responses. The algorithmic core is mirror descent (Beck & Teboulle, 2003) combined with self-play, applied within a Contextual MDP formulation that captures the long-term consequences of individual actions. The paper also derives multi-turn RLHF as a corollary of the same mirror-descent framework, unifying the preference-based and reward-based approaches under a single optimization procedure. A key conceptual departure from prior work is the introduction of a preference-based Q-function that compares a policy's trajectory against a reference policy starting from the initial state, rather than the standard local Q-function formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide convergence guarantees for multi-turn preference-based RL, filling a gap between the well-studied single-turn RLHF literature and the practical need to align LLMs in extended interactions (multi-turn dialogue, tool use, multi-step reasoning). The Education Dialogue benchmark and the clear empirical separation between multi-turn and single-turn methods provide a useful testbed for future work. However, the experiments are limited to small T5 models (770M–3B) and LLM-generated environments, leaving open whether the advantages persist at the scale of frontier models and in more realistic, noisy human-preference settings. The paper also leaves unresolved the question of how to combine turn-level and token-level value estimates, which is critical for practical autoregressive LLM deployment.
