## Problem

Real-Time Recurrent Learning (RTRL) provides exact, untruncated gradients for recurrent networks in online settings, but its quartic computational complexity in hidden state size makes it intractable for standard RNNs. While linear recurrent units (LRUs) reduce RTRL to linear complexity via complex-valued diagonal recurrence, they underperform in online RL compared to gated architectures like GRUs. The paper asks whether a small architectural modification to LRUs can close this performance gap while retaining RTRL's efficiency.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a sequential stream of partial observations (e.g., masked joint positions or velocities in Mujoco, or discrete cues in Trace Conditioning) and maintains a fixed-size hidden state that compresses the entire interaction history. In control tasks, the output is an action distribution over continuous or discrete action spaces; in prediction tasks, the output is a scalar return estimate. Crucially, the agent updates parameters after every single environment step (incremental/online), not in batches, which is where RTRL's constant per-step cost provides its advantage over T-BPTT.

## Method

Recurrent Trace Units (RTUs) parameterize each hidden unit as a complex-valued diagonal recurrence using a cosine representation (r·cos(θ), r·sin(θ)) rather than the exponential form used by LRUs, yielding two real-valued vectors per unit that implicitly behave as complex numbers through rotation. Two key modifications over LRUs are: (1) a nonlinear activation (e.g., ReLU) is applied either inside or after the recurrence, and (2) log-parameterization (r = exp(−exp(ν^log)), θ = exp(θ^log)) constrains r ∈ (0,1] for stability, combined with an input normalization factor γ = (1−r²)^(1/2). The RTRL gradient update exploits the diagonal structure so that each unit's gradient trace depends only on its own two components, giving linear complexity in the hidden dimension with no approximation. The paper extends PPO to carry RTRL gradient traces across environment steps, showing that stale gradients (not recomputed after each epoch) actually improve performance by helping maintain the trust region.

## Results

In the Mujoco POMDP benchmark with ~24k recurrent parameters, RTUs outperform GRU, LRU, and Online LRU in all environments (Figure 4). In the Trace Conditioning prediction task, RTUs with RTRL achieve near-perfect prediction (MSRE approaching zero) while GRU and LRU with T-BPTT remain significantly worse even when given equal or greater computational budgets (Figure 2). In Reacher POMDP, non-linear RTUs achieve the best performance, with linear RTUs and GRU performing similarly, and both outperforming online LRUs (Figure 5). Across POPGym long-term memory tasks, RTUs consistently outperform all baselines (Figure 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRU (Cho et al., 2014) trained with T-BPTT, RTUs win in all Mujoco POMDP environments, the Trace Conditioning task, and POPGym; in Reacher POMDP, GRU achieves performance similar to linear RTUs but is outperformed by non-linear RTUs. Against LRU (Orvieto et al., 2023), RTUs win in most settings; notably, in Figure 2.d, when computation is not constrained to be equal, LRU can eventually match RTU error at very large parameter counts, but GRU cannot. Against Online LRU (Zucchet et al., 2023), RTUs consistently outperform in Mujoco POMDP, Reacher POMDP, and POPGym, confirming that the cosine representation and nonlinearity modifications provide meaningful gains over the original LRU formulation. Against the GPT2-transformer baseline (Ni et al., 2023, 200k parameters), results are reported in Appendix H; the paper notes that even when increasing the truncation length of GRU and LRU to use significantly longer history, they outperform RTUs in only one task, suggesting RTUs are competitive with much larger transformer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Linear Recurrent Units (LRUs) [Orvieto et al., 2023]—which demonstrated that complex-valued diagonal recurrence is representationally equivalent to dense linear recurrence—into the online reinforcement learning setting, where LRUs had only been explored for supervised tasks. The key architectural departure is the introduction of Recurrent Trace Units (RTUs), which replace the exponential complex representation with a cosine (rotation-matrix) representation and incorporate nonlinearity into the recurrence, while retaining the linear-complexity RTRL gradient update. The training side extends PPO to carry RTRL gradient traces across batch updates, building on the original RTRL formulation of Williams and Zipser (1989) and the online LRU work of Zucchet et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTUs offer a practically important efficiency win: they match or exceed GRU performance in partially observable control tasks while using linear (rather than truncation-length-dependent) computation per update, making them well-suited for continual online learning where T-BPTT's memory and compute grow with the chosen horizon. The finding that a minimal modification (cosine parameterization plus in-recurrence nonlinearity) substantially outperforms the otherwise-equivalent LRU in online RL is a useful design lesson for the broader recurrent-architecture community. A primary open limitation is the lack of a principled multi-layer RTRL formulation, and the authors note that parallel-scan training for non-linear RNNs remains an unexploited direction that could further improve throughput.
