## Problem

No existing surrogate loss for learning to defer simultaneously satisfies Bayes-consistency, realizable H-consistency, and H-consistency bounds. Mozannar et al. (2023) proposed a realizable H-consistent loss L_RS but could not prove or disprove its Bayes-consistency, while earlier losses (L_CE, L_OvA, L_general) are Bayes-consistent but fail realizable H-consistency. This paper resolves the open question and provides a unified family achieving all three guarantees at once.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input x (image, text, or structured data) and a fixed expert predictor g: X×Y→R, and produces a score vector h(x,·) over the augmented label space {1,…,n,n+1}, where n+1 denotes deferral. The prediction is argmax over this augmented space, and the deferral cost c(x,y) is incurred when h(x)=n+1. The cost function is general (bounded in [0,1]), not restricted to the expert's classification error, and the hypothesis set H must be symmetric and complete (satisfied by standard neural networks and linear models) for the H-consistency bound results.

## Method

The paper derives a new family of surrogate losses L_RL2D by rewriting the deferral loss as c(x,y)·1_{h(x)≠y} + (1−c(x,y))·1_{h(x)≠y ∧ h(x)≠n+1} and replacing each indicator with a comp-sum loss parameterized by a non-increasing function Ψ. The key design choice is that the second indicator is replaced by a modified comp-sum loss using e^{h(x,y)}+e^{h(x,n+1)} in the softmax numerator, which encourages the sum of two margins to be positive rather than requiring a single margin. This yields concrete losses for Ψ(t)=−log(t) (logistic), Ψ(t)=(1−t^q)/q (generalized cross-entropy), and Ψ(t)=1−t (MAE). The paper proves realizable H-consistency under mild conditions on Ψ (Theorem 4.1), H-consistency bounds with Γ(t)=√(2t) or √(2(n+1)^q·t) for classification-error costs (Theorem 4.2), and a linear bound Γ(t)=(n+1)t for general costs when Ψ(t)=1−t (Theorem 4.3), with Bayes-consistency following as corollaries.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets, L_RL2D with q=0.7 achieves the best system accuracy on HateSpeech (92.20%) and CIFAR-10H (96.80%), while L_RL2D with q=1 achieves the best on COMPASS (66.83%). Notably, on CIFAR-10H, both RL2D variants achieve substantially higher coverage (76.8% and 77.4%) compared to all baselines (64.3%–67.9%), indicating more aggressive deferral while maintaining or improving system accuracy. On the realizable Mixture-of-Gaussians dataset, L_RL2D converges to zero deferral loss as training data increases, confirming its realizable H-consistency, whereas L_CE, L_OvA, and L_general do not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HateSpeech, L_RL2D (q=0.7) achieves the highest system accuracy at 92.20%, narrowly beating L_OvA (92.18%) and L_general (92.05%), while L_RL2D (q=1) achieves the best accepted accuracy (96.57%). On COMPASS, L_RL2D (q=1) leads in system accuracy (66.83%) over L_general (66.67%), though L_general retains the best accepted accuracy (76.25% vs. 69.02%). On CIFAR-10H, L_RL2D (q=0.7) achieves the best system accuracy (96.80%) and both RL2D variants dramatically outperform all baselines in coverage (76.8%–77.4% vs. 64.3%–67.9%), suggesting the RL2D loss enables more effective deferral. In the realizable Mixture-of-Gaussians experiment (Figure 1a), L_RL2D converges to zero deferral loss with increasing training data, while L_CE, L_OvA, and L_general plateau above zero, empirically confirming the realizable H-consistency gap. In the general cost function setting (Figure 1b), L_RL2D (q=1) matches L_general in system accuracy but outperforms L_RS, which lacks Bayes-consistency for general costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of surrogate losses for learning to defer into a single family L_RL2D parameterized by a non-increasing function Ψ, derived by modifying comp-sum losses (Mao et al., 2023) to account for the deferral class. It subsumes the L_RS loss from Mozannar et al. (2023, AISTATS) as the special case Ψ(t) = -log(t), and generalizes the cross-entropy and one-vs-all surrogates from Mozannar and Sontag (2020) and Verma and Nalisnick (2022). The key departure from prior work is the 'modified comp-sum' construction where e^{h(x,y)} is replaced by e^{h(x,y)} + e^{h(x,n+1)} in the second loss term, which is what enables realizable H-consistency while preserving Bayes-consistency and H-consistency bounds simultaneously—something no prior surrogate achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open question from a notable AISTATS 2023 paper by proving L_RS is Bayes-consistent, and more broadly provides the first surrogate loss family that simultaneously satisfies all three consistency guarantees (Bayes-consistency, realizable H-consistency, H-consistency bounds) for both classification-error and general cost functions. A notable conceptual finding is that H-consistency bounds and realizable H-consistency do not imply each other in L2D, unlike in standard multi-class classification, revealing a structural difference in the deferral setting. Limitations include the restriction to single-expert, single-stage settings; multi-expert and two-stage extensions are left as future work, and empirical gains over strong baselines are modest rather than dramatic.
