## Problem

Current deep vision models predict behavioral accuracy but ignore reaction times, while classical decision-making models (drift-diffusion, Wong-Wang) capture RT dynamics but are restricted to simple parametric stimuli and require manual parameter tuning. No existing framework can simultaneously model the full temporal dynamics of human perceptual decisions—both the choice and the time it takes—on complex natural stimuli using end-to-end differentiable learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts random dot motion videos (75 Hz, up to 150 frames) or natural images (10-class object categorization with COCO and synthetic images) and produces two outputs: a class label and a reaction time defined as the discrete time step at which an accumulated evidence signal first crosses a learned threshold. For RDM, the RNN processes 150 timesteps; for object recognition, the Cornet-S backbone uses 20 timesteps in its IT layer. The RT output is a single integer per trial, and in the supervised setting, incorrect-trial RTs are negated so that a single histogram encodes both speed and accuracy.

## Method

RTify introduces a trainable scalar mapping f_w that converts a frozen RNN's hidden state at each timestep into an evidence value e_t, which is summed into an accumulator Φ_t; the model RT τ_θ is the first timestep where Φ_t exceeds a learnable threshold θ. Because τ_θ involves a non-differentiable min-and-inequality operation, the paper derives a first-order Taylor approximation of its gradient (∂τ_θ/∂w ≈ −(∂Φ_t/∂w)/(Φ_t − Φ_{t−1})), enabling backpropagation through the decision time. Two training regimes are supported: (i) supervised fitting of human RT histograms via MSE loss, and (ii) a self-penalty term λ·(l_y·τ_θ) added to cross-entropy that encourages an optimal speed–accuracy tradeoff without any human data. For feedforward architectures, the authors extend the Wong-Wang attractor circuit to an M-population, fully differentiable RNN module that can be stacked on top of any CNN (C3D, VGG-19), replacing the original model's scalar input with network activations and its two competing populations with M competing populations for multi-class decisions.

## Results

On the RDM task, the RTify model achieves lower MSE than both baselines across all coherence levels (0.8%–51.2%), with significance confirmed by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < .05) in both supervised and self-penalized settings. On the object recognition task, model RTs correlate with human RTs at r = .51 (p < .001) in the supervised setting and r = .40 (p < .001) in the self-penalized setting. The RTified WW module applied to a VGG-19 CNN achieves r = .49 (p < .001) correlation with human RTs on the same task. Notably, fitting the model solely on human RTs (without accuracy data) naturally yields human-like classification accuracy across all coherence levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior approaches for extracting reaction times from recurrent vision models. Against Spoerer et al. [29] (entropy-thresholding), RTify achieves significantly lower MSE on the RDM task across all coherence levels (Wilcoxon signed-rank p < .05) and superior correlation on the object recognition task (bootstrap probability of superiority = 99.9%). Against Goetschalckx et al. [30] (uncertainty proxy via evidential deep learning), RTify again achieves lower MSE on RDM (Wilcoxon p < .05) and higher correlation on object recognition (bootstrap 87.1%). Critically, the uncertainty proxy's correlation with human RTs (r = .36) collapses to a non-significant r = .05 (p = .32) when high-uncertainty trials are excluded, whereas RTify maintains a strong r = .41 (p < .001) under the same control, demonstrating that RTify captures a more robust and generalizable relationship between model dynamics and human behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wong-Wang (WW) recurrent circuit model [21, 22]—a biophysically grounded model of two-alternative forced-choice decisions—into a multi-class, fully differentiable RNN module that can be stacked on top of arbitrary feedforward vision architectures (CNNs). It departs from two prior approaches for extracting RTs from RNNs: Spoerer et al. [29], which uses entropy-thresholding (non-differentiable, requiring threshold search), and Goetschalckx et al. [30], which uses an evidential-uncertainty proxy. The key departure is the introduction of a trainable evidence-accumulation module (RTify) with a first-order Taylor approximation that makes the discrete threshold-crossing time differentiable, enabling direct gradient-based optimization of RTs without the non-differentiable min/inequality operations that limit prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a unified, differentiable mechanism to align deep vision models with the full temporal dynamics of human decisions (both choices and RTs), bridging the long-standing gap between image-computable vision models and decision-making models. A notable finding is that fitting only on human RTs (correct-response portion) naturally yields human-like classification accuracy, suggesting the speed-accuracy tradeoff is implicitly encoded in temporal dynamics. However, the evaluation relies on relatively small-scale psychophysics datasets (21 participants for RDM, 88 for object recognition) and primarily synthetic or constrained stimuli; the authors themselves note that internet-scale benchmarks with both choices and RTs are needed to properly validate the approach at scale.
