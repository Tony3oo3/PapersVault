## Problem

Traditional RL policies parameterized as diagonal Gaussians cannot represent multimodal action distributions, leading to mode-covering behavior where the policy concentrates density in low-Q-value regions between distinct optimal actions. While diffusion models can fit such complex distributions, integrating them into online RL is non-trivial: the diffusion policy's entropy has no closed-form expression, making standard maximum-entropy RL frameworks inapplicable, and prior online diffusion-RL methods (action gradient, Q-score matching) suffer from suboptimal performance or high training cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector (dimensionality ranges from 6 for InvertedDoublePendulum-v3 to 376 for Humanoid-v3) and produces a continuous action vector (dimensionality 1–17) bounded to [−1, 1]. Action generation requires sequentially sampling T=20 reverse denoising steps, where each step's noise prediction network receives the current noisy action, the state, and a 16-dimensional sinusoidal timestep embedding. During training, an additional Gaussian noise term scaled by a learned parameter α is added to the final action to regulate exploration; no noise is added at evaluation time.

## Method

DACER reconceptualizes the reverse DDPM process as a state-conditional policy π_θ(a|s), trained by backpropagating the Q-value gradient through the entire 20-step denoising chain to maximize expected Q-value. Because the resulting policy distribution lacks an analytical entropy, the paper estimates it by sampling N=200 actions per state, fitting a 3-component GMM via EM, and computing the mixture entropy; the batch-averaged estimate drives an adaptive update of a scalar parameter α (updated every 10,000 steps) that scales the variance of Gaussian noise added to the final action. The critic uses double Q-learning with DSAC's distributional tricks to mitigate overestimation, and the overall framework is off-policy with a 1M-transition replay buffer. This design decouples the diffusion policy from any specific entropy-maximizing RL algorithm, allowing it to plug into standard actor-critic pipelines while retaining the representational capacity of the diffusion model.

## Results

On the MuJoCo benchmark suite, DACER achieves the highest average final return on 6 out of 8 tasks. The most dramatic improvement is on Humanoid-v3, where DACER scores 11,888 ± 244, surpassing the next-best DSAC (10,829 ± 243) by 9.8% and DDPG (5,291 ± 662) by 124.7%. On Ant-v3, DACER reaches 9,108 ± 103 versus DSAC's 7,086 ± 261 (+28.5%), and on Hopper-v3 it scores 4,104 ± 49 versus DSAC's 3,660 ± 533 (+12.1%). On InvertedDoublePendulum-v3 and Pusher-v2, DACER ties with DSAC and SAC, as these tasks are near-saturated or trivially solvable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DACER outperforms all six baselines (DSAC, SAC, TD3, DDPG, TRPO, PPO) on 6 of 8 MuJoCo tasks, with the largest margins on Humanoid-v3 (+9.8% over DSAC, +124.7% over DDPG) and Ant-v3 (+28.5% over DSAC). On HalfCheetah-v3, DACER edges out DSAC by only 0.9% (17,177 vs 17,025), while both substantially outperform TD3 (8,632) and PPO (5,789). On InvertedDoublePendulum-v3, DACER, DSAC, and SAC all reach the near-optimal 9,360 return, indicating task saturation; TRPO lags significantly at 6,259. On Pusher-v2, DACER ties DSAC at −19, with all methods performing similarly due to the task's simplicity. The multimodal multi-goal experiment further demonstrates DACER's representational advantage: its Q-function learns four symmetric peaks matching the four goal locations, whereas DSAC, TD3, and PPO fail to capture this structure, and DACER's sampled trajectories exhibit clear multimodality at ambiguous states where DSAC produces unimodal, mode-covering behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reconceptualizes the reverse diffusion process of DDPM as a policy approximator in online RL, departing from the behavior-cloning framework that dominates prior diffusion-policy work in offline RL (Diffusion-QL, Decision Diffusion, EDP). It also diverges from the two prior online RL diffusion-policy attempts (action gradient method and Q-score matching) by directly optimizing the diffusion policy via Q-value maximization without requiring accurate ∇ₐQ estimation across the action space. The entropy-regulation mechanism borrows the adaptive α-update from SAC but replaces the analytically tractable Gaussian entropy with a GMM-based estimate, a necessary adaptation given that diffusion policies lack closed-form density expressions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for integrating diffusion policies into mainstream actor-critic online RL, addressing the multimodality limitation of diagonal Gaussian policies that can cause mode-covering and suboptimal convergence. The GMM-based entropy estimation, while effective, is computationally expensive (~40 ms per estimate, run every 10,000 iterations), which prevents seamless integration with per-step maximum-entropy RL and remains an open efficiency challenge. The multimodal multi-goal experiment cleanly demonstrates the representational advantage, but the modest margins over DSAC on most MuJoCo tasks (e.g., ~10% on Humanoid, ~28% on Ant) suggest the gains are real but not transformative, and the method's applicability beyond MuJoCo-scale continuous control remains untested.
