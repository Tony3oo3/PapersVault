## Problem

Stochastic second-order methods achieve superlinear convergence only when the Hessian noise diminishes, forcing per-iteration costs to grow over time. The prior Hessian-averaging approach of Sen Na et al. (2022) avoids this cost blow-up but suffers from slow global convergence, requiring Õ(κ²) iterations before entering the superlinear regime. This paper closes the gap by achieving a faster linear rate and reaching the same superlinear rate in Õ(κ) iterations.

## Input / Output

The method requires exact gradient evaluations ∇f(x) and a stochastic Hessian oracle Ĥ(x) constructed via subsampling (cost O(sd²)) or sketching (cost O(sd²)), along with a user-specified averaging weight sequence {w_t} (uniform w_t = t+1 or non-uniform w_t = (t+1)^{log(t+4)}). The output is an approximate minimizer x* with a four-phase convergence guarantee: warm-up, linear (rate 1−1/κ), initial superlinear, and final superlinear (rate Õ(Υ√(log t/t))^t). The per-iteration cost remains O(nd + d³) independent of t, unlike methods that increase sample size over time.

## Method

The core contribution replaces Newton's method with the Hybrid Proximal Extragradient (HPE) framework as the base algorithm, modified for strongly convex objectives. Each iteration computes an averaged stochastic Hessian H̃_t (reducing noise variance at the cost of bias), solves a linearized proximal subproblem (I + η_t H̃_t)(x̂_t − x_t) = −η_t ∇f(x_t), and applies a backtracking line search with warm-starting σ_{t+1} = η_t/β to allow step sizes to grow unboundedly—essential for superlinear convergence. The HPE extragradient update x_{t+1} = (1/γ_t)(x_t − η_t ∇f(x̂_t)) + (1−1/γ_t)x̂_t with γ_t = 1+2η_t μ yields a linear rate of 1−1/κ (improving over 1−1/κ² for stochastic Newton). The relaxed error condition in the proximal step (allowing error proportional to √γ_t ‖x̂_t−x_t‖ rather than a fixed fraction) is the key modification that enables the final superlinear rate without increasing per-iteration cost.

## Results

SNPE achieves a linear convergence rate of $1 - \kappa^{-1}$, improving over the $1 - \kappa^{-2}$ rate of Stochastic Newton [1], and reduces the warm-up phase $\mathcal{T}_1$ by a factor of $\kappa^2$ (from $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Upsilon^2)$ to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Upsilon^2/\kappa^2)$). The overall iteration complexity to find an $\epsilon$-accurate solution is $\mathcal{O}(\Upsilon^2 + \kappa + \frac{\log(1/\epsilon)}{\log\log(1/\epsilon)})$, improving the $\kappa^2$ dependence of Stochastic Newton [1] to $\kappa$. In experiments on a regularized log-sum-exp objective (d=500, n up to 150,000), SNPE outperforms Stochastic Newton in both iteration count and wall-clock runtime, while Damped Newton and exact-Hessian NPE converge in fewer iterations but at significantly higher per-iteration cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stochastic Newton [1] (Sen Na et al., Mathematical Programming 2022), SNPE strictly dominates on every transition point: the linear phase begins $\kappa^2$× earlier ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Upsilon^2/\kappa^2)$ vs $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Upsilon^2)$), the linear rate is $1-\kappa^{-1}$ vs $1-\kappa^{-2}$, and the final superlinear phase is reached in $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Upsilon^2+\kappa)$ iterations (non-uniform) vs $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^2)$ for [1]. The final superlinear rate $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}((\Upsilon\log t/\sqrt{t})^t)$ is matched, so the gain is purely in the number of iterations to reach it. Against exact-Hessian NPE [18] (Monteiro & Svaiter, SIAM J. Optim. 2010), NPE converges in fewer iterations due to zero Hessian noise, but its per-iteration cost is $\mathcal{O}(nd^2 + d^3)$ versus $\mathcal{O}(nd + d^3)$ for SNPE; in the large-data regime (n ≥ d²), SNPE's total wall-clock time is lower, and the gap widens with n. Against AGD, SNPE requires far fewer iterations (superlinear vs linear) and, when n ≥ d², has comparable per-iteration cost, yielding a clear overall advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hybrid Proximal Extragradient (HPE) framework of Solodov & Svaiter and Monteiro & Svaiter to the stochastic second-order setting, replacing the exact Hessian with a noisy estimate and a Hessian averaging scheme. The closest prior work is Sen Na et al. (2022), which achieves superlinear convergence for stochastic Newton via Hessian averaging but with a slower global linear rate (1−κ⁻²) and later transition to superlinear convergence. The key conceptual departure is using the HPE framework rather than plain Newton's method as the base algorithm, which the authors argue provides a principled path to superior global convergence guarantees, combined with a relaxed error condition in the proximal step tailored to strong convexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The κ² → κ improvement in iteration complexity for reaching superlinear convergence is practically meaningful in ill-conditioned problems common in machine learning, where condition numbers can be large. The paper also clarifies the trade-off between Hessian averaging bias and variance, providing a unified analysis for both uniform and non-uniform weighting schemes. A key limitation is the strong convexity requirement; extending the theory to merely convex objectives is explicitly left as future work, which would broaden applicability to many practical problems. The empirical finding that the extragradient step can slow convergence (consistent with observations in [19]) suggests the simpler Newton-type update may be preferable in practice, an open question for the HPE-based second-order literature.
