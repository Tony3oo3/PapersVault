## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of bias in machine learning focus on initial or asymptotic (converged) behavior, leaving the transient learning regime poorly understood. Since practical systems are often trained for limited steps and operate far from convergence, understanding how bias evolves over time—and which data properties drive it at different stages—is critical for designing effective mitigation strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240770/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical model takes a two-component Gaussian mixture (clusters with distinct mean vectors ±v/√d, variances Δ±, and mixing probability ρ) where each cluster has its own linear teacher for label assignment, and a linear student trained via online SGD on squared loss. The method produces closed-form ODE solutions for four order parameters (student–teacher alignments R±, student–shift alignment M, and student norm Q), from which per-sub-population generalisation error is computed as a degree-2 polynomial at any timestep. Empirical validation uses 2-layer ReLU networks (200 hidden units, sigmoidal readout) on rotated MNIST, grouped CIFAR-10, and CelebA, reporting per-group loss and accuracy trajectories.

## Method

The paper performs a high-dimensional (d→∞) mean-field analysis of online SGD on a linear classifier within the teacher-mixture framework. It identifies a finite set of order parameters that fully characterize the dynamics, proves (via concentration arguments) that their stochastic evolution converges in probability to a deterministic system of coupled ODEs, and then solves those ODEs analytically using Laplace transforms. The explicit solution reveals three distinct timescales—τ_M (spurious correlation/shift alignment), τ_R (teacher alignment/saliency), and τ_Q (student norm/representation)—each governed by different data properties. This yields a three-phase bias evolution: the classifier first favors the sub-population with higher representation and lower variance (spurious correlation phase), then the one with higher variance (saliency phase), and finally the one with higher representation × standard deviation (asymptotic phase), producing non-monotonic 'crossing' phenomena where the preferred sub-population changes during training.

## Results

The paper derives exact closed-form ODE solutions for the high-dimensional SGD dynamics of a linear classifier on a two-cluster Gaussian mixture, revealing a three-phase bias evolution: (1) initial alignment with spurious features (timescale τ_M = 1/(η(v+Δ^mix))), (2) preference for the higher-variance sub-population (timescale τ_R = 1/(ηΔ^mix)), and (3) asymptotic preference for the sub-population with higher ρ√Δ (timescale τ_Q = 1/(η(2Δ^mix − ηΔ²^mix))). The theory predicts a 'crossing' phenomenon where the classifier's bias reverses during training, which is empirically confirmed on MNIST (Fig. 5a), CIFAR-10 (Fig. 6b), and CelebA (Fig. 9). In the spurious correlation setting (v=16, ρ=0.5, Δ=0.1, T±=1, η=0.5), the student achieves near-perfect alignment with the shift vector before transitioning to the teacher, with the spurious feature correctly classifying ~90% of samples during the transient phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct method-to-method baselines. The primary validation is between the analytical ODE predictions and numerical simulations of online SGD at d=1000, where the generalisation errors and order parameters (R₊, R₋, M, Q) show remarkable agreement (Fig. 1a). The empirical experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA serve as out-of-model validation: the predicted crossing and double-crossing phenomena are reproduced in 2-layer ReLU networks trained with online SGD on MSE loss, and in a fine-tuned ResNet-18 on CelebA with cross-entropy loss. The theory's predictions about which sub-population is advantaged at each phase (variance-driven initially, representation-driven asymptotically) are confirmed across all three datasets, including cases where the network architecture and data structure are mismatched with the Gaussian mixture assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-mixture (TM) framework of Sarao Mannelli et al. [38] by providing the first exact closed-form solution for the transient SGD dynamics in the high-dimensional limit, building on the classical order-parameter methodology of Saad & Solla [34] and Biehl & Schwarze [8] and the rigorous convergence theorems of Goldt et al. [15] and Ben Arous et al. [6]. While prior theoretical work on fairness and spurious correlations (e.g., [37, 38]) characterized only asymptotic or initial-stage behavior, this paper unifies both domains under a single analytical framework that resolves the full time-dependent trajectory. It also connects to the simplicity bias literature [32, 39] by identifying interpretable data properties (variance, representation, shift) that make samples appear 'simple' at different training phases, and to the class imbalance literature [43, 12] by showing how group-level features drive non-monotonic preference shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The revelation that bias is a dynamical, non-monotonic quantity governed by distinct timescales—where the classifier's preferred sub-population can shift during training—has direct implications for designing bias mitigation strategies under limited compute budgets, since a model evaluated mid-training may exhibit a different bias profile than its converged counterpart. The crossing phenomenon, validated across MNIST, CIFAR10, and CelebA with architectures up to ResNet-18, suggests that existing mitigation methods relying on early-training signals [26, 42] may be confounded by the multi-phase structure. Key limitations include the restriction to linear students and Gaussian mixture data in the theory, with deeper networks validated only empirically; open questions include extending the exact ODE analysis to nonlinear activations and designing theory-informed dynamical protocols that exploit the identified timescales for targeted bias correction.
