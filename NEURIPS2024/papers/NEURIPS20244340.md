## Problem

Existing federated learning formulations treat models as point estimates, making them prone to overfitting on scarce client data and producing overconfident predictions. While Bayesian posterior inference offers a natural remedy, data heterogeneity across clients induces biased local posteriors that, when multiplicatively aggregated, yield a sub-optimal global posterior. The paper asks how to achieve a globally well-generalized posterior in federated heterogeneous settings without this bias.

## Input / Output

Clients hold locally distributed RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100) or character sequences (Shakespeare), split heterogeneously via Dirichlet distributions or role-based assignment. Rather than producing a single weight vector, the method outputs a posterior distribution over model weights; at inference, predictions are made by averaging samples drawn from the global posterior, yielding both a class label and a calibrated uncertainty estimate (measured by ECE). The server aggregates by averaging SGLD samples from participating clients rather than performing weighted averaging of point estimates.

## Method

FedMDMI introduces a global model-data mutual information term I(w;S) as a regularization constraint on the posterior, which compresses the information the model retains about heterogeneous data and thereby reduces sensitivity to client-specific bias factors. Using the chain rule of MI, the global term is upper-bounded by a sum of locally computable terms I(w;S_i), converting the global objective into per-client objectives. The optimal local posterior under this MI-regularized objective is shown to be a Gibbs distribution, which is sampled via stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) with a Gaussian prior initialized from the previous round's global model. At the server, a sample from the global posterior is approximated by averaging the SGLD samples uploaded by participating clients, leveraging non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for federated SGLD. The hyperparameter α controls the MI regularization strength, yielding a federated bias-variance trade-off formalized through a generalization bound of the form O(1/√(mn) + 1/√m) in terms of the global MI.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Dir(0.2)-L, 5% participation), FedMDMI achieves 83.42% top-1 test accuracy, outperforming the strongest Bayesian baseline FedEP (82.63%) and the best optimization-based method SCAFFOLD (82.11%). On CIFAR-100 (Dir(0.2)-L), FedMDMI reaches 49.46%, surpassing FedEP (48.89%) and FALD (48.43%). For uncertainty calibration, FedMDMI attains the lowest ECE across all settings (e.g., 0.115 on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.2)-L vs. 0.121 for FedEP and 0.169 for FedAvg), confirming better-calibrated predictive uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMDMI consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on top-1 accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Shakespeare under both low (5%) and high (10%) participation rates. Against the closest Bayesian competitor FedEP, FedMDMI gains 0.6–1.0% on CIFAR-10 and 0.5–0.7% on CIFAR-100, with the largest margin on Shakespeare (1.5–1.3%). Compared to the optimization-based SCAFFOLD, FedMDMI leads by 1.3–1.5% on CIFAR-10 and 1.7–2.7% on CIFAR-100, though SCAFFOLD converges faster in early rounds. Against FALD (the closest method, a special case of FedMDMI with α=1), FedMDMI gains 0.3–0.9% on CIFAR-10 and 0.5–1.2% on CIFAR-100, demonstrating the benefit of the tunable MI regularization. On uncertainty calibration (ECE), FedMDMI achieves the lowest error in all settings (e.g., 0.115 vs. 0.121 for FedEP on CIFAR-10 Dir(0.2)-L), while point-estimate methods like FedAvg and SCAFFOLD are consistently 0.05–0.08 worse. The one near-tie is CIFAR-10 Dir(0.7)-H where FedEP (83.79) marginally edges FedMDMI (83.76) within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMDMI extends the federated posterior inference framework established by FedPA (global posterior decomposition into a product of local posteriors) by introducing a global model-data mutual information regularization that decomposes into locally computable terms. The optimal local posterior under this MI-regularized objective is shown to be a Gibbs posterior (from PAC-Bayes theory, following Xu and Raginsky 2017), sampled via SGLD, and the global posterior is approximated by averaging local samples — a technique with convergence guarantees from FALD (Plassier et al., 2023), which is recovered as the special case α=1. The key departure from prior Bayesian FL methods (FedPA, FedEP) is that the MI constraint explicitly penalizes information the model extracts from heterogeneous local data, thereby reducing bias in local posteriors without requiring the extra storage/communication overhead of expectation propagation or auxiliary server-side data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled information-theoretic generalization bound for FL in terms of global model-data MI, yielding a federated bias-variance trade-off controlled by the hyperparameter α, and demonstrates that this regularization simultaneously improves accuracy, calibration (ECE), and provides client-level differential privacy as a byproduct. Limitations include the conditional independence assumption on the global likelihood (standard in FL but restrictive), the lack of convergence rate guarantees for non-convex objectives, and the observation that SGLD's stochastic gradient introduces slower convergence than optimization-based methods like SCAFFOLD. Open questions include whether the MI-based approach extends to settings where the conditional independence assumption fails, and whether the global-model-as-prior strategy remains effective under very low participation rates where the prior becomes stale.
