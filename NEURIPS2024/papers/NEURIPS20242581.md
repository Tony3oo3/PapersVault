## Problem

Multi-task learning in medical MLLMs suffers from the tug-of-war problem, where conflicting gradient signals across tasks degrade individual task performance. Prior work applying Mixture-of-Experts to mitigate this interference has focused almost exclusively on the LLM component, overlooking the connector that bridges visual and language embedding spaces. This paper identifies the connector as a critical site of multi-task conflict and is the first to address it directly.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 medical image and a text prompt formatted as '<Img> <ImageFeature> </Img> [Task Identifier] Instruction', where the task identifier (e.g., [vqa], [caption], [refer], [identify], [cls]) disambiguates the six supported tasks. Visual features are compressed by a factor of 4 via a resampler before entering the connector. Outputs are task-specific: free-form text for QA/VQA, structured radiology reports for report generation, normalized bounding-box coordinates in the format <X_min><Y_min><X_max><Y_max> (range 0–100) for referring expression comprehension, object names for referring expression generation, and class labels for image classification.

## Method

Uni-Med comprises a frozen EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14 visual encoder, a resampler (compression rate α=4), a Connector-MoE (CMoE) module, and a LLaMA2-7B LLM fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8). The CMoE replaces the standard two-layer MLP connector with five two-layer MLP projection experts and a soft router. The router is a lightweight MLP that receives the concatenation of per-token visual features and randomly initialized vision-level special task tokens, producing per-token routing weights via SoftMax; the aligned visual tokens are then a weighted sum of all expert outputs. This token-and-task-level routing strategy is shown to outperform token-only or task-only routing. Training is single-stage (100k iterations, batch size 4, one A800, ~10 hours) with per-task sample rates proportional to dataset size. The paper further provides interpretability analysis using gradient direction/magnitude interference metrics and per-parameter gradient sign purity scores, demonstrating that CMoE yields more balanced tug-of-war indexes and a higher proportion of low-conflict parameters compared to a shared MLP connector.

## Results

Uni-Med achieves leading performance across all six medical tasks through joint training on 12 datasets. On VQA, it reaches 82.12 BLEU-1 on Slake-VQA and 58.07 BLEU-1 on Path-VQA. For report generation on MIMIC-CXR, it attains 27.79 BLEU-1, 22.58 ROUGE-L, and 13.98 RadGraph-F1, substantially outperforming all baselines. On image classification, it achieves 76.96% accuracy on DermaMNIST and 78.07% on OrganSMNIST, far exceeding the next-best model (RadFM at 5.14% and 18.90% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Med outperforms all four baselines on nearly every reported metric. Against LLaVA-Med (the strongest general-purpose baseline), Uni-Med gains +5.17 BLEU-1 on Slake-VQA (82.12 vs 76.95), +11.65 BLEU-1 on Path-VQA (58.07 vs 46.42), and +7.89 BLEU-1 on MIMIC-CXR report generation (27.79 vs 19.90). Against RadFM, the gap is most dramatic on Path-VQA (+33.24 BLEU-1) and image classification (76.96% vs 5.14% on DermaMNIST), though RadFM is competitive on Slake-VQA (81.66 vs 82.12). XrayGPT, specialized for chest X-ray report generation, is the closest competitor on MIMIC-CXR BLEU-1 (27.11 vs 27.79) but trails significantly on MPx-Single (8.51 vs 15.80). Med-Flamingo is consistently the weakest baseline across all tasks. Notably, Uni-Med achieves these results through joint multi-task training without dataset-specific fine-tuning, whereas LLaVA-Med's reported numbers rely on per-dataset fine-tuning checkpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts lineage (Jacobs et al., 1991) from its recent applications in the LLM component of MLLMs (MoE-LLaVA, Octavius, MoCLE, MTLoRA, MOELoRA) to the previously overlooked connector that bridges visual and language embedding spaces. It also builds on the medical MLLM line (Med-Flamingo, LLaVA-Med, XrayGPT, RadFM) by moving from task-specific or few-task models toward a unified generalist architecture handling six heterogeneous medical tasks simultaneously. The interpretability framework draws on gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020) and gradient sign purity (Chen et al., 2020) to quantify multi-task interference, but applies these metrics specifically to the connector rather than the full model, which no prior work has done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying the connector as the primary locus of multi-task interference in MLLMs shifts where practitioners should apply capacity-allocating mechanisms, potentially simplifying the design of future multi-task MLLMs. The open-source release and the demonstration that joint training on 12 datasets in ~10 hours on a single A800 can match or exceed dataset-specific fine-tuning (as shown against LLaVA-Med in Appendix D.6) make this practically accessible. Key limitations remain: the model processes 3D medical images as 2D slices, is evaluated on only 12 datasets/6 tasks with LLaMA2-7B, and the theoretical understanding of why the connector is the bottleneck (versus the LLM) is still empirical rather than principled. Whether CMoE generalizes to larger LLM backbones, more modalities, or genuinely 3D inputs is an open question.
