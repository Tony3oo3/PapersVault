## Problem

Existing adversarial Bandits with Knapsacks (BwK) algorithms compare against weak static benchmarks and allow arbitrary per-round changes, while non-stationary BwK work requires a bounded global variation assumption that is violated even by a single change point. This paper studies the intermediate piecewise-stationary regime where parameters are constant within unknown-length segments, and provides the first competitive-ratio guarantee against a dynamic (fluid approximation) benchmark without requiring bounded global variation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives as input the total resource capacity B and known bounding constants η_min, η_max that bracket all per-round reward and consumption values. It operates under bandit feedback: after selecting an arm in each round, it observes only that arm's (reward, consumption) pair, and it does not know the horizon T, the number of stationary pieces L, the change-point locations, or the true (r, c) parameters. The output is a per-round arm selection (or null arm to reject) that maximizes cumulative reward subject to the hard budget constraint, with a provable competitive ratio of O(log(η_max/η_min)) against the fluid-approximation benchmark opt(FA), which allows a different optimal arm distribution in each stationary piece.

## Method

The core mechanism is an inventory-reserving strategy keyed to reward-consumption ratios: the algorithm partitions the ratio range [η_min/η_max, η_max/η_min] into 2M logarithmic intervals (M = ⌈log_α(η_max/η_min)⌉), reserves B/2M resource units per interval, and uses a round-robin scheme over M+1 candidate budget guesses to ensure that on each stationary piece, at least 1/(M+1) of rounds are assigned to the correct or adjacent ratio interval. In the full-feedback warm-up (IRES), the algorithm solves a linear program for each budget guess and commits to the corresponding fractional arm distribution. In the bandit-feedback setting (IRES-CM), it adds adaptive exploration (pulling a random arm for N consecutive rounds with probability γ_t) to estimate (r, c), and a change-monitoring mechanism that detects when the estimation window spans a change point, bounding the resulting 'failed exploitation' rounds. The analysis decomposes the fluid-approximation benchmark by ratio interval and shows that each interval contributes at least 1/O(M) of its optimal reward, yielding the O(log(η_max/η_min)) competitive ratio, which is shown tight via a matching lower bound.

## Results

The paper establishes a near-optimal competitive ratio of O(log(η_max/η_min)) for piecewise-stationary Bandits with Knapsacks, with a matching lower bound of Θ(1/log(η_max/η_min)) (Theorem 4.5). Specifically, IRES (deterministic full-feedback) achieves a reward of at least (1−(2log_α(η_max/η_min)+1)/B)·opt(FA)/(6α²·log_α(η_max/η_min)) (Theorem 3.1), while IRES-CM (bandit feedback) achieves (1−o(1))/(10α⁴·log_α(η_max/η_min))·(opt(FA)−Õ(L√(|K|NT))) (Theorem 4.2). In numerical experiments (L=2, T=20000, |K|=2, B=9360), IRES-CM consistently outperforms Immorlica et al. (2019) in both single-arm and mixed-arm benchmark settings, and outperforms Zhou et al. (2008) when the optimal FA solution involves mixed arms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Immorlica et al. (2019) (adversarial BwK, static benchmark), IRES-CM outperforms in both experimental configurations (Figure 1a and 1b). The paper attributes this to Immorlica's algorithm being designed for a fully adversarial setting and thus being significantly more conservative in inventory reservation; IRES-CM leverages the known η_min=0.5 to allocate resources more aggressively. Against Zhou et al. (2008) (full-feedback online knapsack, single best arm benchmark), the comparison is mixed: in Figure 1(a) where the FA optimum selects a single arm per piece, Zhou et al. (given extra full-feedback information) outperforms IRES-CM; in Figure 1(b) where the FA optimum uses mixed arms (x₂*(1)=0.128, x₂*(2)=0.872), IRES-CM clearly outperforms Zhou et al. This aligns with the theoretical distinction that Zhou et al.'s single-arm benchmark is strictly weaker than the best-distribution-over-arms benchmark (FA) used in this paper, as shown by Badanidiyuru et al. (2018) for the stationary case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two existing regimes of Bandits with Knapsacks: the fully stationary stochastic BwK of Badanidiyuru et al. (2018) and the fully adversarial BwK of Immorlica et al. (2019) and Kesselheim and Singla (2020). It also departs from the non-stationary BwK work of Liu et al. (2022), which requires a bounded global variation assumption that the authors show can be violated even with a single change point. The algorithmic core—reserving inventory per reward-consumption ratio interval via a round-robin guessing scheme—is a novel design that draws conceptual inspiration from the ratio-based competitive analysis of Zhou et al. (2008) for full-feedback online knapsack, but is adapted to the bandit-feedback and piecewise-stationary setting with a best-distribution-over-arms benchmark rather than a single-arm benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight O(log(η_max/η_min)) competitive ratio with a matching lower bound provides a clean characterization of the fundamental difficulty of piecewise-stationary BwK, and the removal of the bounded-global-variation requirement opens the door to more realistic non-stationary environments. However, the results are conditioned on η_min > 0 (all arms have strictly positive reward and consumption) and L = o(√T·η_min) (sublinear number of change points), which limits applicability to settings with very sparse changes. Open questions include whether the η_min > 0 assumption can be relaxed, whether the CR can be improved beyond the logarithmic dependence on the ratio range, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-resource or contextual variants without the d-fold degradation noted in the appendix.
