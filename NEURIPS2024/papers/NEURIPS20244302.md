## Problem

Train-time data poisoning attacks inject imperceptible perturbations into training images to create latent backdoors in deployed classifiers, yet existing defenses either degrade generalization accuracy, target only specific attack types, or impose prohibitive computational overhead. No prior method provides a universal, attack-agnostic preprocessing defense that removes poison signals while preserving classifier natural accuracy across diverse architectures and datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a potentially poisoned RGB training dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/CINIC-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs purified images ready for standard classifier training with no modifications to the downstream training pipeline. The generative models (EBM or DDPM) are trained on separate, non-overlapping datasets (e.g., the ImageNet portion of CINIC-10 for CIFAR-10 scenarios, CalTech-256 for Tiny-ImageNet) to ensure no clean copies of poisoned images leak into the purification process. Purification is applied once per dataset and the resulting purified set can be reused across multiple classifier training runs and poison scenarios.

## Method

PUREGEN applies a stochastic transform Ψ_T(x) as a preprocessing step, realized via iterative Langevin dynamics of a trained EBM (PUREGEN-EBM) or a limited forward/reverse DDPM process (PUREGEN-DDPM). The key insight is that poisoned images occupy a higher-energy region of the learned data distribution, and mid-run MCMC dynamics (150 EBM steps or 75 DDPM steps) transport them back toward the natural low-energy manifold while preserving class-relevant features due to metastable sampling. For PUREGEN-DDPM, the model is trained on only the first 250 steps of the standard 1000-step β schedule, deliberately sacrificing generative fidelity to dedicate more capacity to the 'restoration' direction. The EBM is trained using image-initialized convergent contrastive learning (following Nijkamp et al.), where negative samples are generated via short MCMC chains initialized from real images, producing the metastable mid-run dynamics that enable purification without full mixing. The purified dataset is then used for standard supervised classifier training (ResNet, MobileNetV2, DenseNet, etc.) with no architectural or training modifications.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), PUREGEN-EBM achieves 1.39% average poison success with 92.92% natural accuracy against Narcissus (1%), and 1.00% poison success with 92.98% natural accuracy against Gradient Matching (1%), outperforming all baselines. On CINIC-10, PUREGEN-DDPM reduces Narcissus poison success to 4.76% (vs. 62.06% undefended) while maintaining 79.35% natural accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-34), PUREGEN-EBM achieves 2.00% poison success with 63.27% natural accuracy on Gradient Matching (0.25%), the best natural accuracy among all defenses. PUREGEN maintains SoTA or near-SoTA performance even when its generative model training data is fully poisoned or from highly out-of-distribution sources (FID up to 152).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUREGEN-EBM dominates EPIC across all scenarios: on CIFAR-10 Narcissus, PUREGEN-EBM achieves 1.39% avg poison success with 92.92% natural accuracy versus EPIC's 27.31% PS and 82.20% natural accuracy — a 26-point reduction in poison success and 10.7-point gain in natural accuracy. Against FRIENDS, PUREGEN-EBM achieves lower poison success (1.39% vs. 8.32% on CIFAR-10 NS) while maintaining higher natural accuracy (92.92% vs. 91.01%); on CINIC-10, PUREGEN-DDPM dramatically outperforms FRIENDS (4.76% vs. 11.17% PS). Compared to JPEG, PUREGEN-EBM matches natural accuracy (92.92% vs. 92.94%) with slightly lower poison success (1.39% vs. 1.78%) on CIFAR-10 NS, but on CINIC-10 PUREGEN-DDPM is far superior (4.76% vs. 18.89% PS). On Tiny-ImageNet, PUREGEN-EBM achieves the best natural accuracy (63.27%) among all defenses while maintaining 2.00% poison success, whereas FRIENDS collapses natural accuracy to 42.74%. For the NTGA data-availability attack (not the paper's focus), PUREGEN-EBM reaches 85.22% natural accuracy, slightly below the SoTA AVATAR method (86.22%) but well above other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time adversarial purification paradigm of Hill et al. (EBM Langevin dynamics) and Nie et al. (diffusion-based purification) to the distinct threat model of train-time data poisoning, where the goal is to sanitize a training dataset before classifier training rather than clean individual test inputs. It builds directly on the MCMC-based EBM training procedure of Nijkamp et al. and the DDPM framework of Ho et al., but introduces a key modification: training DDPMs on only a subset of the noise schedule (250 of 1000 steps) so the forward process degrades images enough to remove perturbations without reaching the noise prior, trading generative quality for purification efficacy. The paper positions itself against the previous SoTA defenses EPIC (coreset selection) and FRIENDS (friendly noise), as well as the surprisingly competitive JPEG compression baseline, arguing that generative-model-based purification offers a universal, attack-agnostic preprocessing step that avoids the generalization trade-offs and attack-specificity of prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUREGEN provides a practical, one-time preprocessing defense that requires no knowledge of the attack type, poison percentage, or classifier architecture, making it broadly applicable across deployment scenarios. The demonstrated robustness to distributional shift (FID up to 152) and even fully poisoned generative-model training data is a strong practical property, reducing the need for a guaranteed-clean reference dataset. Key limitations include significant purification compute (923–4181 s on TPU V3 for CIFAR-10), the need for extensive hyperparameter search for the combined PUREGEN-REPS/FILT variants, and the fact that JPEG compression remains a surprisingly competitive and far cheaper baseline in many settings. Open questions include scaling to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), whether the EBM/DDPM synergy can be exploited more systematically, and how the defense holds against truly adaptive, defense-aware poisoning at scale.
