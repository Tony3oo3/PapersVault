## Problem

Prior data-driven algorithm design work for branch-and-cut established sample complexity bounds only for the classical Chvátal-Gomory and Gomory Mixed-Integer cut families, which are special single-row cases of the much broader cut generating function (CGF) framework. This paper asks whether rigorous sample complexity guarantees can be extended to learning the best CGF from larger parameterized families (both single-row and multi-row), and whether instance-dependent CGF selection via neural networks can be analyzed with similar guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an integer programming instance (A, b, c) in canonical form, extracts k rows from the optimal simplex tableaux with non-integer right-hand sides, and applies a parameterized CGF to produce a single cutting plane (α, β) added at the root node. The CGF parameters μ (two scalars for the 1D family, a point on a constrained simplex Δ_k^τ for the kD family) are selected via grid search or a neural network to minimize the truncated branch-and-cut tree size h(I, μ) ∈ [0, B] ∩ ℤ. For the neural network variant, the instance is encoded via a flattening function Enc: I → ℝ^d and passed through a ReLU network to predict the CGF parameters, enabling instance-dependent cut selection.

## Method

The paper introduces two CGF families with rigorous pseudo-dimension bounds: (1) a one-dimensional family π_{f,s₁,s₂}^{p,q} from Gomory and Johnson (2003) whose cutting plane coefficients are piecewise affine in the parameters (s₁, s₂), yielding Pdim = O(n² log((m+n)ϱ)); and (2) a novel k-dimensional family π_{f,μ} defined as a min-max over lattice translations of a simplex, computable in O(k) time per coefficient via a closed-form algorithm, with Pdim = O(kn² log((m+n)ϱ) + k² log(nτ)). The key technical mechanism is proving that the parameter space can be decomposed by a bounded number of hyperplanes into regions where each cutting plane coordinate is a fixed affine (1D) or rational (kD) function of μ, then composing with the degree-5 polynomial decomposition of the cutting plane space from Balcan et al. (2022) to invoke a piecewise-structure lemma for pseudo-dimension. Section 5 extends both results to ReLU neural networks mapping encoded instances to CGF parameters, giving instance-dependent Pdim bounds that scale with network width W and depth L.

## Results

The primary metric is average branch-and-cut tree size (lower is better) on 100 test instances. On single-knapsack problems, the learned 1-row cut dramatically outperforms GMI: e.g., Knapsack(30,1) drops from 832.16 (GMI) to 58.84 (1-row cut), a ~14× reduction. On multi-knapsack Knapsack(30,3), the 5-row cut achieves 2817.55 vs. GMI's 4963.91 (~43% reduction), and all multi-row cuts outperform the best single-row cut (3561.36). On packing problems, improvements over GMI are more modest (e.g., 1200.55 → 1123.9 for Packing(20,40)), but the 'best 1-row cut' column (738.58) indicates substantial unrealized potential for instance-dependent selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical Gomory Mixed-Integer (GMI) cut [Gomory, 1960]. The paper's 1-row cut (a parameterized family generalizing GMI) consistently beats GMI across all problem types: by ~45% on Knapsack(20,1) (87.0 vs. 158.88), ~93% on Knapsack(30,1) (58.84 vs. 832.16), and ~6% on Packing(20,40) (1123.9 vs. 1200.55). The k-row cuts (k=2,5,10) further improve over both GMI and the 1-row cut on multi-knapsack problems, with the 5-row cut achieving the best result on Knapsack(30,3) at 2817.55 vs. GMI's 4963.91. However, on packing problems, multi-row cuts do not consistently outperform the 1-row cut or GMI, suggesting the benefit of multi-row information is problem-structure-dependent. The 'best 1-row cut' column (oracle per-instance parameter selection) reveals a large gap between the fixed-parameter strategy and instance-dependent selection, motivating the neural network approach in Section 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven algorithm design framework of Balcan et al. (2021, 2022), which established sample complexity bounds for selecting Chvátal-Gomory and GMI cuts, to the much broader family of cut generating functions (CGFs) introduced by Gomory and Johnson (2003) and Basu and Sankaranarayanan (2019). It also generalizes the neural-network-based instance-dependent cut selection of Cheng et al. (2024) from CG/GMI to these more general CGF families. The key technical departure is that CGFs use information from multiple simplex tableau rows and have a more complex piecewise structure, requiring new analysis of how the cutting plane coefficients depend on CGF parameters to bound pseudo-dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CGFs are known to produce strictly stronger cuts than GMI but have remained largely unused in practice because the cut selection problem over this large parameterized family is intractable by classical means. This paper provides the first rigorous sample complexity guarantees for learning effective CGF parameters, establishing a theoretical foundation that could enable practical deployment of these powerful cuts via machine learning. Limitations include the restriction to a single cut at the root node, fixed row selection, and small-scale empirical validation (100 instances per problem type); open questions include scaling to multiple cuts per node, jointly learning row selection, and whether the theoretical bounds are tight enough to be practically useful for large-scale instances.
