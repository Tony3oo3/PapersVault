## Problem

Deep neural networks lose significant accuracy when test images suffer natural corruptions (noise, blur, weather effects, compression), yet incorporating specific corruptions into data augmentation can degrade clean-image performance and reduce robustness to other corruption types. The paper asks whether a single, corruption-agnostic training modification can broadly improve robustness without sacrificing clean accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at their native resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label, identical to any supervised classifier. What is unusual is that the perturbation is applied to the network's weight matrices during the forward pass rather than to the input image: each sub-batch is evaluated under a different multiplicative Gaussian noise draw ξ ~ N(1, σ²I) applied element-wise to the weights, so the input pipeline remains unchanged.

## Method

The key insight is that for a pre-activation neuron z = wᵀx, any input covariate shift ε can be exactly replicated by a multiplicative weight perturbation ξ = 1 + ε/x, moving the simulation of corruptions from input space to weight space. DAMP exploits this by dividing each mini-batch into M sub-batches, drawing an independent multiplicative Gaussian noise vector for each, computing the loss gradient under the perturbed weights (ω ⊙ ξ), and averaging the sub-batch gradients before a standard SGD update with L2 weight decay. This yields the same per-iteration cost as vanilla SGD (only two extra operations: Gaussian sampling and element-wise multiplication) while provably minimizing an upper bound on the expected corrupted-input loss. The paper further shows that ASAM's update rule is equivalent to optimizing under adversarial (worst-case) multiplicative weight perturbations, unifying the two methods under a common MWP framework.

## Results

On ResNet50/ImageNet (90 epochs, basic Inception-style preprocessing), DAMP achieves 23.8% clean error and 74.2% average corrupted error, outperforming Dropout (76.6%) and SAM (75.8%) while requiring only half the training cost of SAM/ASAM. On ViT-S16/ImageNet with only basic augmentations, DAMP trains from scratch in 200 epochs to reach 23.75% top-1 error—matching ResNet50's accuracy—while achieving the best average corruption robustness (72.15%) among all methods at the same compute budget. With MixUp and RandAugment, DAMP on ViT-B16 reaches 19.36% clean error and 59.19% average corrupted error, the best overall result in the paper.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dropout, DAMP consistently wins on corrupted images across all settings: on ResNet50/ImageNet it reduces average corrupted error from 76.6% to 74.2%, and on ViT-S16/ImageNet (200 epochs) from 79.63% to 72.15%, while maintaining comparable clean accuracy. Against SAM, DAMP achieves lower average corrupted error (74.2% vs. 75.8% on ResNet50/ImageNet; 72.15% vs. 73.42% on ViT-S16/ImageNet at equal compute budget of ~41h) despite requiring only half the per-iteration cost, since SAM needs two forward/backward passes per step. Against ASAM, the picture is mixed: ASAM achieves the best clean accuracy (22.8% vs. 23.8% on ResNet50/ImageNet) and a slightly lower average corrupted error (74.7% vs. 74.2%), but at twice the training cost; on ViT-S16/ImageNet at equal wall-clock time, DAMP (200 epochs, 41.1h) actually edges out ASAM (100 epochs, 41.1h) with 72.15% vs. 72.55% average corrupted error. The paper also shows that combining DAMP with MixUp and RandAugment on ViT-B16 (59.19% avg corrupted error) outperforms SAM/ASAM on the smaller ViT-S16 (61.02–61.66%) at the same compute budget, suggesting that scaling model size with DAMP is more effective than using adversarial perturbations on a smaller model.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMP repurposes the weight-perturbation idea from SAM/ASAM (which use adversarial additive perturbations) by switching to random multiplicative perturbations, and connects it to the input-augmentation literature (AugMix, AutoAugment, Patch Gaussian) through a theoretical equivalence between input corruptions and multiplicative weight shifts (Theorem 1). The paper explicitly positions Dropout and its variants (DropConnect, Variational Dropout) as special cases of DAMP with Bernoulli noise, while distinguishing its motivation: improving corruption robustness rather than preventing co-adaptation. It also contrasts with ensemble methods (Deep ensembles, BNNs) that achieve similar robustness gains but at linearly scaling inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAMP offers a zero-overhead training trick (identical cost to SGD) that broadly improves corruption robustness without sacrificing clean accuracy, making it attractive for safety-critical deployment where diverse corruptions are unavoidable. The demonstration that ViT-S/16 can be trained from scratch on ImageNet with only basic Inception-style preprocessing to match ResNet50 (23.7% top-1) is practically significant, as ViT typically demands extensive augmentation or sophisticated training to reach comparable performance. Key limitations include the theoretical proof assuming a simple feedforward architecture (no normalization or attention layers) and the exploration of only Gaussian multiplicative noise; open questions include whether richer noise distributions, application to NLP/RL, and formal guarantees for modern architectures can further extend the approach.
