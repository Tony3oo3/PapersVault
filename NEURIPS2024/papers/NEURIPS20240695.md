## Problem

In cooperative MARL under the CTDE paradigm, full parameter sharing boosts sample efficiency but forces all agents to adopt identical policies, limiting joint policy representational capacity. Non-parameter sharing avoids this homogeneity but incurs prohibitive training costs, especially as model sizes scale. Existing partial-sharing schemes (SePS, SNP) rely on static initializations set before training, which are difficult to design without prior knowledge of agent-specific dynamics.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local vector observation o_i^t and outputs an action a_i^t (discrete for QMIX-based MPE/SMACv2, continuous for MATD3-based MaMuJoCo). Internally, the method maintains a single shared parameter tensor θ₀ and N binary masks M_i (one per agent or agent type), producing agent-specific effective parameters θ_i = θ₀ ⊙ M_i via Hadamard product. For actor-critic settings, a second shared tensor φ₀ with K masks yields K critic ensembles Q(·; φ_j) whose mean is used for policy gradients while the minimum is used for target computation.

## Method

Kaleidoscope adapts soft threshold reparameterization (STR) so that each mask element m_i^(k) = 1[|θ₀^(k)| > σ(s_i^(k))] is learned end-to-end alongside the MARL objective, making the sharing level dynamic rather than fixed. A diversity regularization term maximizes the pairwise L1 distance between masked parameters ‖θ₀ ⊙ (M_i − M_j)‖₁, with gradients approximated via a tanh surrogate to handle the non-differentiable indicator. A periodic resetting mechanism reinitializes any parameter index masked out by all agents with probability ρ, recycling dead weights to preserve network capacity and mitigate primacy bias. The same masking scheme is extended to critic ensembles: K masks over a shared φ₀ produce heterogeneous critics, with cyclic mask resetting and a layer-weighted diversity term (w_l = 2^l) that gradually shifts value estimation from overestimation to underestimation across training.

## Results

Kaleidoscope achieves superior performance across all three benchmarks (MPE, MaMuJoCo, SMACv2) compared to six parameter-sharing baselines, with the advantage attributed to the flexibility of learnable masks and diversity regularization. In terms of inference cost, Kaleidoscope reduces FLOPs to 0.901× (MPE), 0.680× (MaMuJoCo), and 0.890× (SMACv2) relative to FuPS+ID, making it the most efficient method at test time. Ablation studies confirm that the diversity regularization term (Eq. 8) is the single most important component, while the periodic resetting mechanism primarily aids late-stage training and the critic-ensemble masking improves the update-to-data ratio from the start.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kaleidoscope outperforms both SePS (Christianos et al., 2021) and SNP (Kim and Sung, 2023) across all eleven scenarios in MPE, MaMuJoCo, and SMACv2, as shown in the learning-curve figures (Figs. 4–5). The key advantage over SePS is that Kaleidoscope's masks are learned end-to-end and adapt dynamically during training, whereas SePS fixes cluster-based sharing at initialization without access to agent-specific transition or reward knowledge. Compared to SNP, which uses fixed random pruning to create heterogeneous structures, Kaleidoscope's learnable weight-level masks provide finer-grained and more flexible heterogeneity, yielding consistently higher returns. Kaleidoscope also surpasses the simpler baselines (NoPS, FuPS, FuPS+ID, MultiH) by combining the sample-efficiency benefit of shared parameters (a single transition updates shared weights N times) with the representational capacity of diverse policies. Notably, Kaleidoscope achieves these gains while simultaneously reducing inference FLOPs (e.g., 0.680× on MaMuJoCo vs. 0.900× for SNP), making it both more performant and more efficient at deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the soft threshold reparameterization (STR) technique from Kusupati et al. (2020), originally designed for learnable network sparsity, as a mechanism for adaptive partial parameter sharing in MARL. It departs from prior partial-sharing methods like SePS (Christianos et al., 2021) and SNP (Kim and Sung, 2023), which fix the sharing structure at initialization based on clustering or random pruning, by making the sharing pattern itself a learnable, end-to-end optimized quantity. The method is integrated into the MATD3 actor-critic framework (Ackermann et al., 2019) and QMIX, extending the standard two-critic setup to a masked critic ensemble that improves the update-to-data ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in cooperative MARL—balancing sample efficiency (favored by full parameter sharing) against policy representational capacity (favored by independent parameters)—with a principled, adaptive solution that requires no manual design of sharing structures. However, the authors acknowledge that optimal hyperparameters (diversity coefficients, reset probability) vary across scenarios and that the current per-agent mask assignment does not scale beyond ~10 agents without additional clustering. Open questions include whether the approach extends to offline MARL or meta-RL, and whether automatic hyperparameter selection based on environmental signals could remove the need for per-scenario tuning.
