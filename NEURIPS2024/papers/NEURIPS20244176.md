## Problem

Directly extending short video diffusion models (trained on 16 frames) to generate longer videos (128+ frames) causes severe quality degradation due to high-frequency distortion: spatial high-frequency components decrease (blurring frames) while temporal high-frequency components increase (flickering). Existing training-free approaches like sliding-window attention and noise rescheduling improve local transitions but fail to maintain global temporal consistency across extended sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or multiple text prompts as input and produces long videos of 128 or more frames (demonstrated up to 512 frames) by modifying the temporal attention of pre-trained short video diffusion models (LaVie, VideoCrafter2) during inference. It operates entirely in the latent space of the base model's diffusion UNet, requiring no additional training data or parameters. Key hyperparameters include a local attention window α=8, a fusion cutoff at τ=25 denoising steps, and a Gaussian Low Pass Filter with normalized spatiotemporal stop frequency D₀=0.25.

## Method

FreeLong introduces SpectralBlend Temporal Attention (SpectralBlend-TA), a training-free replacement for the standard temporal attention in video diffusion UNets. It first decouples attention into two parallel streams: a local stream where each frame attends only to frames within a window of 2α (preserving high-fidelity spatial-temporal details) and a global stream where each frame attends to all frames (capturing long-range dependencies and narrative coherence). The two resulting feature maps are then blended in the frequency domain via 3D FFT: low-frequency components are taken from the global features and high-frequency components from the local features, separated by a spatial-temporal Low Pass Filter, then recombined via IFFT. This spectral blending is applied only during the first τ=25 denoising steps (where scene layout and object shapes are established), after which only local features are used for fine detail refinement. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only a swap of the temporal attention module, and supports multi-prompt generation with coherent scene transitions.

## Results

On VBench (200 prompts, 128-frame generation), FreeLong achieves the best scores across all five metrics: Subject Consistency 95.16, Background Consistency 96.80, Motion Smoothness 96.85, Temporal Flickering 96.04, and Imaging Quality 67.55. Inference time is 2.2s per video on an NVIDIA A100, faster than both FreeNoise and the sliding-window baseline (2.6s each) due to single-pass temporal attention. The method also scales to 512-frame generation while maintaining consistency and fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeLong outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against Direct sampling (128 frames from a 16-frame model), FreeLong gains +6.21 on Subject Consistency, +4.60 on Temporal Flickering, and +2.79 on Imaging Quality, confirming that the high-frequency distortion identified in the paper's frequency analysis is the primary failure mode. Against the Sliding window baseline (Gen-L-Video [20]), FreeLong leads by a large margin on Subject Consistency (+9.36) and Background Consistency (+3.97), showing that local windowing alone cannot capture global temporal coherence. Against FreeNoise [19], the closest competitor, FreeLong still wins on all five quality metrics (e.g., +2.86 Sub, +1.10 Flicker, +0.41 Imaging) while being 0.4s faster in inference, thanks to its single-pass SpectralBlend-TA design versus FreeNoise's repeated noise rescheduling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free long video generation line initiated by FreeNoise (noise rescheduling) and Gen-L-Video (sliding-window temporal attention), which both operate in the spatial/temporal domain to maintain consistency across extended sequences. It departs from these approaches by introducing a frequency-domain perspective: a diagnostic analysis showing that direct extension of short video models causes spatial high-frequency degradation and temporal high-frequency inflation, followed by a SpectralBlend Temporal Attention that blends low-frequency global features with high-frequency local features via 3D FFT. The method is built directly on the temporal attention modules of existing short video diffusion models (LaVie, VideoCrafter2) and is validated as model-agnostic across ModelScope, AnimateDiff, and OpenSora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of FreeLong makes it immediately deployable on any existing short video diffusion model without additional data or compute, which is practically significant given the cost of long video training. The frequency-domain diagnostic (spatial high-freq loss + temporal high-freq gain) provides a reusable analytical lens for understanding quality degradation in out-of-distribution-length generation. However, the paper acknowledges that temporal flickering persists in very long sequences and that rapidly changing scenes remain challenging, leaving open questions about whether frequency-domain blending alone can handle arbitrary scene transitions or whether more adaptive filtering is needed.
