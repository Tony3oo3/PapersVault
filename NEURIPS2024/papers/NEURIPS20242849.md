## Problem

Untrained neural network (UNN) methods like Deep Image Prior offer a training-free alternative for Snapshot Compressive Imaging, but they lack a theoretical framework characterizing their recovery performance, provide no guidance for optimizing the binary sensing masks, and suffer from overfitting and local-minima issues that degrade reconstruction quality relative to supervised approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D measurement of size 256×256 (obtained by optically encoding B=8 video frames through a binary coded-aperture mask) and the corresponding 3D sensing matrix H ∈ R^{n×nB} with n=256², and reconstructs the full 3D video data cube of 8 frames at 256×256 resolution. The system is highly underdetermined (n measurements for nB unknowns), and the mask entries are i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) random variables. No training data or pre-trained denoisers are required; all network parameters are optimized per-instance from the measurement alone.

## Method

SCI-BDVP solves the DIP-SCI optimization via an iterative projected-gradient-descent (or generalized-alternating-projection for noise-free) outer loop, where the projection step employs a bagged Deep Video Prior: K=3 independently trained DVPs operating at different patch sizes (64×64, 128×128, 256×256) are run in parallel, each minimizing a combined loss of fidelity to the GD-step output and the 2D measurement, and their outputs are averaged to reduce overfitting. A skip connection then blends the GD output with the bagged projection via a weighted average. The theoretical contribution establishes reconstruction error bounds for both noise-free and noisy measurements, derives an upper bound on the number of recoverable frames as a function of DIP dimensionality, and shows that the optimal mask density p* is below 0.5 for noise-free data but increases with noise variance, explaining when the algorithm effectively estimates the temporal mean of frames.

## Results

On noise-free video SCI (256×256, B=8 frames), SCI-BDVP (GAP) achieves an average PSNR of 29.41 dB and SSIM of 0.882, outperforming all other untrained methods by ~1 dB over the next-best UNN (Factorized-DVP at 28.21 dB). Under noisy measurements (σ=50), SCI-BDVP (GD) achieves 24.21 dB average PSNR, surpassing the best supervised baseline PnP-FastDVDnet (GAP) by 2.7 dB (24.21 vs. 21.49) and the best UNN baseline PnP-DIP by 3.0 dB. The method also demonstrates that the optimal mask non-zero probability p* is below 0.5 for noise-free measurements and increases with noise level, consistent with the paper's theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among untrained (UNN) methods, SCI-BDVP (GAP) achieves state-of-the-art performance on noise-free measurements, improving ~1.2 dB over Factorized-DVP (29.41 vs. 28.21 dB avg PSNR) and ~3.9 dB over PnP-DIP (29.41 vs. 25.56 dB). Against the supervised PnP-FastDVDnet [18], SCI-BDVP (GAP) trails by ~3.1 dB on noise-free data (29.41 vs. 32.54 dB), but under noisy measurements SCI-BDVP (GD) reverses the gap: at σ=50 it achieves 24.21 dB vs. 21.49 dB for PnP-FastDVDnet (GAP), a 2.7 dB advantage, and at σ=25 it leads by 2.65 dB (27.23 vs. 24.58). Against GAP-TV [9] and PnP-FFDnet [17], SCI-BDVP (GAP) wins by approximately 4 dB in PSNR on noise-free data (29.41 vs. 25.46 dB). The bagging mechanism is critical: the non-bagged SCI-DVP (E2E) variant achieves only 24.87 dB, a 4.5 dB gap to the full SCI-BDVP (GAP), confirming that bagging across multiple patch sizes substantially mitigates DIP overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bagged-DIP idea from 2D image denoising under speckle noise [28] to 3D video snapshot compressive imaging, constructing a bagged Deep Video Prior (BDVP) that operates at multiple patch scales in parallel. It builds on the DIP hypothesis [25] as the source model but departs from prior UNN-based SCI methods (PnP-DIP [27], Factorized-DVP [26]) by introducing a bagging mechanism to mitigate overfitting, a skip connection, and a theoretically-guided choice between GAP (noise-free) and GD (noisy) descent steps. The theoretical analysis of DIP-SCI recovery is novel, providing the first performance bounds connecting DIP architecture parameters to the number of recoverable frames, and it connects to prior mask-optimization work [34, 35, 36] by giving a principled explanation for the empirically observed optimal mask density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical framework characterizing UNN-based SCI recovery performance, offering practical guidance for hardware design (mask optimization) and system planning (maximum recoverable frames). The finding that an untrained, bagged method outperforms supervised plug-and-play approaches under noisy measurements is practically significant for real-world SCI systems where noise is unavoidable. Key limitations include the restriction to i.i.d. Bernoulli masks (practical systems use structured/shifted masks), evaluation on only six 256×256×8 benchmark videos, and consideration of only additive Gaussian noise. Open questions include information-theoretic lower bounds for SCI recovery, extension to structured masks, and parallelization of the bagged projections to reduce the substantial computational overhead.
