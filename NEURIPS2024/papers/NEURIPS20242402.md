## Problem

High-quality 3D scene creation via photogrammetry (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) requires hundreds to thousands of captured images, making it labor-intensive and inaccessible. Prior work addressed observation-limited settings with specialized solutions per input regime (single-image feed-forward models, text-to-3D distillation, sparse-view regularization), but each suffers in quality, efficiency, or generality. CAT3D instead reframes the ill-posed reconstruction problem as a generation problem: synthesize a large set of 3D-consistent novel views with a multi-view diffusion model, then apply standard reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 or 3 RGB images (512×512) with their camera poses as conditioning views, plus a set of target camera poses specifying where to generate novel views. For single-image input, it generates 80 views; for few-view (3-image) input, it generates 480–960 views. These synthetic views, together with the original observations, are fed into a Zip-NeRF-based reconstruction pipeline that produces a 3D scene representation renderable in real-time from any viewpoint. Text prompts are supported indirectly by first generating an input image with a text-to-image model. Non-square inputs are handled by combining square-cropped and padded model outputs.

## Method

CAT3D is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 uses a multi-view latent diffusion model (850M params) initialized from a text-to-image LDM, where 2D self-attention layers are inflated to 3D spatiotemporal self-attention at resolutions ≤32×32 to capture inter-view correlations, and camera poses are encoded as per-pixel raymaps (ray origin and direction) concatenated to the latents rather than using PixelNeRF or CLIP embeddings. The noise schedule is shifted by log(N) toward high noise to accommodate the higher dimensionality of multi-view generation. To exceed the model's 8-view training limit, target viewpoints are clustered into groups of 5 and generated independently; for single-image input, an autoregressive strategy first generates 7 anchor views (selected via k-means++ greedy initialization) before generating remaining groups in parallel. Stage 2 runs a modified Zip-NeRF reconstruction with an added LPIPS perceptual loss and a Gaussian-kernel distance weighting that progressively upweights views closer to the observed inputs, improving robustness to residual inconsistencies in the generated views.

## Results

On few-view 3D reconstruction, CAT3D achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five benchmark datasets and all view counts (3, 6, 9). The largest gains appear on the most challenging out-of-distribution datasets: on Mip-NeRF 360 with 3 views, CAT3D reaches 16.62 PSNR / 0.377 SSIM / 0.515 LPIPS, surpassing ReconFusion (15.50 / 0.358 / 0.585) and ZeroNVS (14.44 / 0.316 / 0.680). On CO3D with 3 views, CAT3D scores 20.57 PSNR vs. 19.59 for ReconFusion and 17.13 for ZeroNVS. For single-image-to-3D, CAT3D achieves a CLIP image score of 88.54±8.6 in ~1 minute, competitive with IM-3D (91.40±5.5, 3 min) while being an order of magnitude faster than ImageDream (83.77±5.2, 120 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReconFusion (the prior state-of-the-art few-view method), CAT3D wins on PSNR and LPIPS across all five datasets at every view count, with the largest margin on Mip-NeRF 360 (16.62 vs. 15.50 PSNR at 3 views, a ~1.1 dB gap) and CO3D (20.57 vs. 19.59). Against ZeroNVS, CAT3D leads by 1.5–2.2 dB PSNR on the harder out-of-distribution datasets (Mip-NeRF 360, CO3D) and by ~0.2–0.7 dB on in-distribution ones. Against Zip-NeRF (dense-view baseline with no generative prior), CAT3D improves by 3.85 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 3-view, demonstrating the value of the generative prior in the observation-limited regime. For single-image-to-3D (Table 2), CAT3D (CLIP 88.54, 1 min) trails IM-3D (91.40, 3 min) on CLIP score but is 3× faster and, unlike IM-3D, handles full scenes and objects-in-context rather than only segmented objects. CAT3D substantially outperforms ImageDream (83.77, 120 min) and One2345++ (83.78, 0.75 min) on CLIP while remaining competitive on speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT3D sits at the intersection of multi-view diffusion models (architecturally close to ImageDream and MVDream, using 3D spatiotemporal self-attention) and robust 3D reconstruction (building on Zip-NeRF with added LPIPS and distance-weighted losses). Its key conceptual departure from prior few-view methods like ReconFusion and ZeroNVS is the decoupling of the generative prior from 3D extraction: rather than iteratively distilling a 2D diffusion prior into a 3D representation via score matching, CAT3D first generates a large set of consistent novel views in parallel, then feeds them into a standard (but robustified) NeRF pipeline. This reframes the ill-posed sparse-view reconstruction problem as a generation problem, collapsing it to the fully-observed reconstruction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT3D provides a unified, practical pipeline that handles text-to-3D, single-image-to-3D, and few-view reconstruction in under a minute—roughly an order of magnitude faster than prior SOTA—while matching or exceeding quality across five benchmarks. Key limitations include reliance on manually designed camera trajectories (which may not generalize to open-ended environments), a fixed 8-view context window per diffusion call that limits global consistency at scale, and sensitivity to out-of-distribution content (e.g., human faces) inherited from the base text-to-image model. Open directions include automatic trajectory planning, initializing from pre-trained video diffusion models, and extending the number of jointly modeled views to improve long-range consistency.
