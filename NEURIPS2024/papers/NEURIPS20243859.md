## Problem

Shallow alignment methods (e.g., BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4) that freeze the LLM and map image features into its input embedding space suffer from a fundamental mismatch: visual tokens deviate from the expected distribution of deeper language layers, limiting cross-modal fusion. Directly training the LLM on image-text data causes catastrophic forgetting of NLP capabilities. The paper asks whether one can achieve deep visual-language fusion while keeping the pretrained LLM entirely frozen.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 224×224 or 490×490 resolution) and a text query as input. The image is encoded by a frozen EVA2-CLIP-E ViT and projected into the LLM's embedding space via a two-layer SwiGLU MLP adapter; all visual tokens share a single RoPE position ID to avoid excessive positional encoding for hundreds of image tokens. Two output variants exist: CogVLM-Chat produces natural-language captions or answers, while CogVLM-Grounding additionally outputs bounding boxes in the normalized format [[x0,y0,x1,y1]] with coordinates ranging 000–999.

## Method

CogVLM introduces a trainable visual expert module at every transformer layer of a frozen LLM (Vicuna-7B or LLaMA3-8B). Within each layer, image hidden states are processed by a dedicated QKV matrix and FFN (initialized from the LLM's own weights), while text hidden states continue through the original frozen parameters; the two streams are concatenated before the softmax attention and FFN outputs. This doubles the parameter count (6.5B trainable) without increasing FLOPs, and because the LLM weights are untouched, pure-text behavior is preserved exactly. A causal mask is applied to all attention operations including among image tokens. Pretraining proceeds in two stages—120K steps of next-token captioning on 1.5B LAION/COYO pairs, then 60K steps mixing captioning with referring-expression comprehension on 40M grounded images—followed by instruction SFT on VQA and grounding datasets.

## Results

CogVLM-Chat (Vicuna-7B backbone) achieves state-of-the-art results across all 7 LVLM benchmarks: 51.1 on MM-Vet, 72.5 on SEED-Bench, 77.6 on MMBench, 77.8 on LLaVA-Bench, 87.9 on POPE, 41.1 on MMMU, and 34.5 on MathVista. On image captioning, CogVLM-17B scores 132.6 CIDEr on NoCaps OOD (vs. 130.6 for GIT2) and 94.9 on Flickr Karpathy test (vs. 85.8 for Qwen-VL). On visual grounding, CogVLM-Grounding reaches 92.76 on RefCOCO val, 92.91 on RefCOCO+ test-A, and 90.79 on RefCOCOg test, surpassing specialist models. Swapping the backbone to LLaMA3-8B further boosts scores to 60.4 (MM-Vet), 80.5 (MMBench), and 44.3 (MMMU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogVLM-Chat (Vicuna-7B) outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (same backbone) by 20.6 points on MM-Vet (51.1 vs. 30.5), 13.3 on MMBench (77.6 vs. 64.3), and 10.9 on MathVista (34.5 vs. 23.6), demonstrating that deep fusion via the visual expert substantially exceeds shallow alignment. Against the larger SPHINX-2k (LLaMA2-13B), CogVLM wins on MM-Vet (51.1 vs. 40.2), MMBench (77.6 vs. 65.9), MMMU (41.1 vs. 32.9), and MathVista (34.5 vs. 27.8) despite using a smaller LLM. CogVLM also surpasses Emu2-Chat (LLaMA-33B) on MM-Vet (51.1 vs. 48.5), MMBench (77.6 vs. 63.6), and MMMU (41.1 vs. 34.1). On image captioning, CogVLM-17B leads GIT2 by 2.0 CIDEr on NoCaps OOD and outperforms Qwen-VL by 9.1 on Flickr, while on visual grounding it beats Qwen-VL by 3.4 on RefCOCO val (92.76 vs. 89.36) and even surpasses specialist models like UNINEXT-H and ONE-PEACE on 5 of 9 splits. Shallow-alignment baselines (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4) trail CogVLM by 25+ points on MMBench and 20+ on MM-Vet, confirming the paper's central claim that deep fusion is necessary for competitive multimodal performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogVLM carves a new architectural middle ground between two dominant VLM training paradigms: shallow alignment (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4), which maps image features into the LLM input space via a Q-Former or linear projection while freezing the LLM, and full LLM training (PaLI, Qwen-VL), which updates all language parameters on multimodal data at the cost of catastrophic NLP forgetting. The visual expert module—separate QKV matrices and FFNs for image tokens in every transformer layer—is explicitly motivated by the LoRA-vs-P-Tuning analogy from efficient fine-tuning, where per-layer weight adaptation outperforms input-space prefix embeddings. The model is built on standard components (EVA2-CLIP-E ViT, SwiGLU MLP adapter, RoPE positional encoding) but the per-layer expert design is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CogVLM demonstrates that deep vision-language fusion is achievable without sacrificing the pretrained LLM's NLP capabilities, a problem that has plagued prior VLMs (e.g., PaLME's 87.3% NLG degradation). The visual expert module is a practical, modular technique likely to be widely adopted, and the open-sourcing of weights and SFT data lowers the barrier for multimodal research. The paper acknowledges that VLM training is 'still in its infancy,' pointing to RLHF, anti-hallucination, and better SFT alignment as open directions. A notable limitation is the absence of statistical significance reporting and the reliance on a single training run per configuration, though the breadth of ablations and multi-backbone validation (Vicuna-7B, Vicuna-13B, LLaMA3-8B, GLM3-32B) partially mitigates this.
