## Problem

Offline RL methods face a constraint conflict issue when training data come from multiple behavior policies of varying quality: existing approaches either treat all samples equally or use advantage-weighted resampling that implicitly collapses diverse behavior policies into a narrow distribution, excluding critical transitions and imposing erroneous constraints on the learned agent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240915/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector s and a scalar advantage value ξ (computed as tanh(min_i Q_θi(s,a) − V_φ(s)), normalized to (−1,1)) as a joint condition c = s‖ξ. At deployment, the optimal action is produced by fixing ξ*=1; during training, the actual per-sample ξ from the critic is used for the BC regularization term. The output is a continuous action vector generated by the CVAE decoder given a latent code from the actor network.

## Method

A2PO alternates between two stages: (1) behavior policy disentangling, where a CVAE is trained with the state-advantage pair as condition to model separate action distributions for different quality levels, and (2) agent policy optimization, where an actor-critic agent (TD3+BC backbone) generates a latent code conditioned on c and feeds it to the frozen CVAE decoder to produce actions. The actor loss combines a Q-value maximization term (using the optimal condition c*=s‖1) with a behavior-cloning term (using the actual per-sample ξ), so suboptimal samples constrain only their corresponding advantage-conditioned policy rather than disrupting the optimal policy. The key distinction from prior advantage-weighted methods is that A2PO conditions the policy explicitly on advantage rather than reweighting the dataset, preserving full data diversity while still directing optimization toward high-advantage actions.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym benchmark (6 dataset types × 3 locomotion tasks), A2PO achieves a total normalized score of 1563.3, outperforming the next-best AW method CQL+AW (1376.9) by over 21% and the direct competitor LAPO (1026.8) by 52%. On the most challenging random-medium-expert mixed-quality dataset, A2PO scores 90.6/107.8/97.7 on halfcheetah/hopper/walker2d, while LAPO drops to 71.1/66.6/60.4 and CQL+AW to 76.8/71.8/58.3. Across Maze, Kitchen, and Adroit domains (Table 2), A2PO leads with totals of 609.5, 209.2, and 153.9 respectively, surpassing the next-best methods (Diffusion-QL at 485.0/207.1 and LAPO at 123.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2PO decisively outperforms the most direct AW competitor LAPO (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2022) by 52% on the Gym total (1563.3 vs. 1026.8), with the gap widening dramatically on high-diversity datasets: on random-medium-expert, A2PO scores 296.1 vs. LAPO's 198.1. Against the next-best AW method CQL+AW (Hong et al., 2023), A2PO leads by 21% (1563.3 vs. 1376.9), and against IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021) by 32% (1563.3 vs. 1183.9). Diffusion-QL (Wang et al., 2022) is the strongest non-AW baseline at 1302.1 on Gym and leads on Maze (485.0) and Kitchen (207.1), but A2PO still surpasses it on all three domains. On the additional baselines in Table 4, A2PO beats Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) and IQL+ReD (Yue et al., 2022) across nearly all mixed-quality settings, though PRDC (Ran et al., ICML 2023) slightly edges A2PO on the low-diversity medium and medium-replay datasets (e.g., 63.5 vs. 47.1 on halfcheetah-medium) while A2PO dominates on all high-diversity random-expert and random-medium-expert tasks. BC (Pomerleau, 1991), BCQ (Fujimoto et al., ICML 2019), TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, NeurIPS 2021), CQL (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2020), AWAC (Nair et al., 2020), and MOPO (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) are consistently outperformed by A2PO across all dataset types, with MOPO and AWAC showing particularly large gaps on mixed-quality data.

## Relation to Prior Work

A2PO builds on the TD3+BC actor-critic backbone and the CVAE architecture, but departs from both LAPO and prior advantage-weighted methods (AWAC, CQL+AW) in a key conceptual way: instead of reweighting or resampling transitions to implicitly favor high-advantage data, A2PO conditions the CVAE on both state and advantage, producing disentangled action distributions per advantage level. This shifts the problem from dataset redistribution (which the authors argue collapses diverse behavior policies into a narrow one) to explicit advantage-conditioned policy generation. The paper positions itself as the first dedicated advantage-aware policy optimization method for the constraint-conflict problem in mixed-quality offline datasets, distinguishing itself from both policy-constraint methods that treat all samples equally and AW methods that discard data diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constraint conflict issue is a practical bottleneck: real-world offline datasets are rarely collected from a single homogeneous policy, and existing methods degrade sharply when behavior policies have large quality gaps. A2PO's advantage-conditioned CVAE offers a principled mechanism to preserve all data while still guiding the agent toward high-advantage actions, and the consistent outperformance across D4RL's Gym, Maze, Kitchen, and Adroit domains (including newly constructed mixed-quality splits) strengthens the case. Limitations include the additional CVAE training overhead, reliance on accurate critic-based advantage estimation, and evaluation restricted to continuous-control benchmarks. The authors identify multi-task offline RL with greater policy diversity as a natural next step, and the advantage-conditioning paradigm could potentially transfer to other generative-policy frameworks beyond CVAE.
