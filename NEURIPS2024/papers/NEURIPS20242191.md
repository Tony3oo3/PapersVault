## Problem

Existing methods for two-player zero-sum Markov games with continuous state spaces either rely on tabular representations (scaling with state cardinality) or function approximation approaches that ignore the underlying dynamics, yielding sub-optimal Õ(ε⁻²) sample complexity. No prior method achieves near-optimal convergence independent of state space size while explicitly exploiting the transition dynamics structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242191/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires access to the parametric transition model s' = f(s,a,b) + ε with ε ~ N(0, σ²I_d) and the reward function r(s,a,b), operating on continuous state space S = R^d with finite action space (or continuous actions in the SDEPO-NN variant). It outputs a one-sided ε-optimal Nash equilibrium policy pair (π̄, π̲) and finite-dimensional linear Q-function approximations of the form Q(s,a,b) = φ(s,a,b)ᵀw, where φ is a truncated spectral embedding of dimension m. A tunable factorization parameter α ∈ [0,1) controls the kernel decomposition and can be adjusted for empirical performance.

## Method

SDEPO interprets the Gaussian transition kernel as a positive-definite kernel and decomposes it via Bochner or Mercer decomposition to construct a spectral dynamic embedding that explicitly encodes the environment's dynamics into a feature map φ(s,a,b). Finite-dimensional truncations are obtained through random features (Monte-Carlo approximation of Bochner) or Nyström features (eigendecomposition of an empirical Gram matrix), yielding linear Q-function representations. Policy evaluation is performed via iterative least-squares on these features, and policy improvement uses natural policy gradient with alternating ascent/descent updates in a two-stage procedure (Stage 1 finds π̲_k, Stage 2 derives π̄_k). The key theoretical contribution is a rigorous error decomposition showing the total policy evaluation error splits into an approximation term (Õ(1/√m) for random, Õ(1/n_nys) for Nyström) and a statistical term (Õ(m³/√n)), leading to Õ(1/((1-γ)³ε)) last-iterate convergence to an ε-optimal NE independent of state space cardinality. A practical variant SDEPO-NN replaces the finite-action natural policy gradient with Gaussian policies and neural-network Q-functions to handle continuous action spaces.

## Results

Theoretically, SDEPO achieves a last-iterate convergence rate of Õ(1/((1−γ)³ε)) to an ε-optimal Nash equilibrium, matching the best-known complexity for single-agent policy gradient methods and being independent of state space cardinality. In the continuous-state, finite-action experiment, SDEPO (both random and Nyström features) converges within 60 iterations, outperforming the tabular OFTRL method on a discretized version of the same environment. In the Simple Push experiment with continuous state and action spaces, SDEPO-NN_rf and SDEPO-NN_nys achieve 95.13%/99.37% and 95.38%/99.38% winning rates against NPG-NN (at σ=0.1/0.01), while NPG-NN wins only 5.23%/0.55% and 5.16%/0.59% of matches against the two SDEPO variants respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OFTRL (Zhang et al., 2022), a tabular method requiring known dynamics and a discretized state space, SDEPO demonstrates superior convergence on a randomly generated zero-sum Markov game: SDEPO converges within 60 iterations on the original continuous state space, while OFTRL operates on a 100-horizon discretized version and converges more slowly (Figure 1). In the Simple Push experiment, both SDEPO-NN variants (using spectral dynamic embedding in the Q-function) overwhelmingly dominate NPG-NN (natural policy gradient with neural networks but no spectral embedding): SDEPO-NN_rf wins 95.13% (σ=0.1) and 99.37% (σ=0.01) of matches, and SDEPO-NN_nys wins 95.38% and 99.38% respectively, while NPG-NN's win rate drops to 5.23%/0.55% and 5.16%/0.59%. The two SDEPO variants perform comparably to each other (≈50% win rate in head-to-head), confirming that both random and Nyström feature generation are effective. The results indicate that the spectral dynamic embedding, which explicitly encodes transition dynamics into the feature representation, is the key factor driving the performance gap over the embedding-free NPG-NN baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral dynamic embedding framework from single-agent stochastic nonlinear control (Ren et al., 2022, 2023) to the two-player zero-sum Markov game setting, combining it with the natural policy gradient and performance difference lemma machinery from Alacaoglu et al. (2022). It departs from prior kernel-based MG methods (Junchi Li et al., 2022; Qiu et al., 2021) by automatically generating finite-dimensional kernel features directly from the transition dynamics via Bochner and Mercer decompositions, rather than assuming a pre-specified kernel feature set. The approach also differs from tabular methods (Wei et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022) by operating on the original continuous state space without discretization, and from generic function-approximation MG methods (Xie et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2022) by explicitly leveraging dynamics information to achieve tighter convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-optimal O(1/((1-γ)³ε)) last-iterate convergence rate for continuous-state 2p0s-MGs matches the best-known single-agent policy gradient results, closing a gap that prior kernel-based MG methods (which achieved only O(ε⁻²)) could not. The automatic feature generation from dynamics is a practical advantage over methods requiring careful kernel feature selection. However, the theory requires known transition dynamics with Gaussian noise and finite action spaces, and the empirical validation is limited to two small-scale experiments (a synthetic game and a simple push variant), leaving open whether the approach scales to complex environments. The authors note that extending to independent learning (where players do not observe each other's policies) is a natural future direction.
