## Problem

In-Context Learning (ICL) in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) suffers from two compounding challenges: inference cost scales linearly with the number of prefixed demonstrations, and accuracy is highly sensitive to which demonstrations are selected. Existing non-learnable In-Context Vector (ICV) methods that compress demonstrations into a single vector work for simple NLP tasks but fail on complex multimodal tasks like VQA, where diverse question types (classification, counting, spatial reasoning) cannot be captured by a fixed, unlearned vector.

## Input / Output

At inference, LIVE takes only the query (an RGB image paired with a natural-language question) as input to the IDEFICS-9B model and produces a short text answer, requiring no demonstrations in the input sequence. The learned LIVE vectors (one per transformer layer, each of dimension d with a scalar weight α) are injected by adding α_l·v_l to the hidden representation at each layer l during the forward pass, so the input sequence length remains identical to zero-shot inference. This reduces FLOPs to 1/24.97 and wall-clock time to 1/8.25 of 32-shot ICL while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Method

LIVE is derived from a decomposition of the Self-Attention mechanism showing that ICL output equals a convex combination μ·h(X_D) + (1−μ)·h(x̂), where h(X_D) acts as a 'shift' on the query representation. The method learns a set of layer-specific shift vectors {v_1,…,v_L} and per-layer scalars {α_1,…,α_L} (totaling ~131K parameters) that approximate this shift without any demonstrations at inference. Training minimizes a combined loss: a KL-divergence term aligning the LIVE-shifted output distribution with the distribution produced by 32 randomly sampled demonstrations per query, plus a ground-truth cross-entropy term. The use of 32-shot random demonstrations per training sample forces the vectors to capture common task-level information rather than individual demonstration characteristics, and the per-layer design (motivated by evidence that different transformer layers play distinct roles in ICL) enables fine-grained task encoding. At inference, only the query is fed to the frozen LMM with the learned vectors added at each layer, achieving zero-shot-like speed with ICL-level or better accuracy.

## Results

On VQAv2, LIVE achieves 58.54% accuracy, surpassing 32-shot ICL (56.18%) by 2.36 points while requiring only 1/24.97 of the FLOPs and 1/8.25 of the inference time. On OKVQA, LIVE reaches 50.08% accuracy, improving over 32-shot ICL (48.48%) by 1.6 points. LIVE uses only 131,104 trainable parameters (1× baseline), compared to LoRA's 1,155,136 (8.8×), yet outperforms LoRA by 9.52/15.87 points on VQAv2/OKVQA. All non-learnable ICV methods (TV, FV, PCA-ICV) fail to match even 32-shot ICL, with the best (TV) trailing by 12.5/15.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIVE decisively outperforms all three non-learnable ICV baselines: TV (Hendel et al., 2023) achieves 43.68/32.68, FV (Todd et al., 2023) reaches 30.21/31.02, and PCA-ICV (Liu et al., 2023) scores 34.75/30.59 on VQAv2/OKVQA, all far below LIVE's 58.54/50.08. Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), LIVE wins by 9.52 points on VQAv2 and 15.87 on OKVQA while using 8.8× fewer trainable parameters. Compared to optimized ICD selection strategies, LIVE (58.54) edges out RICE (58.07) and MMICES (57.98) on VQAv2 at 32-shot, but trails RICE (51.11) and MMICES (50.61) on OKVQA by 1.03 and 0.53 points respectively. Notably, LIVE also surpasses 32-shot ICL itself on both datasets, demonstrating that the learned shift vector captures task information more effectively than raw demonstrations while eliminating inference-time token overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the In-Context Vector (ICV) paradigm from NLP—where non-learnable vectors like Task Vector, Function Vector, and PCA-ICV extract task information from demonstrations into a single vector—to the multimodal setting. The key conceptual departure is making the ICV learnable: rather than extracting a fixed vector via heuristics, LIVE trains per-layer shift vectors by distilling the output distribution of 32-shot ICL into a compact parameter set via KL divergence. The method is grounded in the 'shift vector' interpretation of ICL from Hendel et al. [20] and the finding that different Transformer layers play distinct roles in processing demonstrations [24], which motivates layer-specific vectors rather than a single shared one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIVE offers a practical path to deploying multimodal ICL at near-zero-shot inference cost (1/25th the FLOPs of 32-shot ICL) while actually exceeding ICL accuracy, addressing two deployment-critical bottlenecks: latency and demonstration sensitivity. The 'General LIVE' concept—averaging task-specific vectors to handle unknown test distributions—suggests a scalable recipe for multi-task deployment without retraining. However, the evidence base is narrow: experiments center on IDEFICS-9B and VQA, with generalization to other LMMs (IDEFICSv2) and tasks (COCO, OcrVQA) shown only in the appendix. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to autoregressive generation tasks beyond short-answer VQA, how it interacts with longer-context LMMs, and whether the per-layer vector design is truly necessary or an artifact of the specific architecture.
