## Problem

A widely held assumption in diffusion modeling is that the ELBO is invariant to the choice of noise schedule, meaning the forward noising process can be fixed without affecting likelihood. This paper shows that invariance only holds for univariate (scalar) Gaussian noise and breaks down for multivariate, input-conditioned schedules, opening the door to learning the diffusion process itself as a tighter variational posterior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243353/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32) and produces both generated images and log-likelihood estimates in bits-per-dimension. Unusually, the forward noising process is conditioned on a learned auxiliary latent z (a 15-hot binary vector of length 50) extracted from the input via a small 4-block ResNet encoder, so the noise applied to each pixel is a function of the specific image being processed. At generation time, z is sampled from a simple prior (uniform over k-hot vectors), making the model unconditional.

## Method

MULAN introduces three coupled innovations: (1) a per-pixel multivariate noise schedule parameterized as a degree-5 monotonic polynomial in time, so each dimension of the image receives noise at a different rate; (2) conditioning of the schedule on an auxiliary latent z via a two-layer MLP, making the noising process input-adaptive; and (3) lifting both the forward q and reverse p processes into an augmented space conditioned on z, which preserves the standard noise parameterization (ε_θ or v_θ) and enables efficient training. The key theoretical insight is that the diffusion loss becomes a line integral of the reconstruction-error vector field along the noise-schedule trajectory ν(z,t); because this field is non-conservative, different trajectories (schedules) yield different ELBOs, so jointly optimizing p and q over the schedule tightens the bound. Empirically, MULAN achieves state-of-the-art BPD of 2.55 on CIFAR-10 and 3.67 on ImageNet-32 while requiring only 2M training steps (vs. 10M for VDM) and adding ~10% parameters to the standard UNet.

## Results

MULAN achieves a new state-of-the-art in density estimation with 2.55 BPD on CIFAR-10 and 3.67 BPD on ImageNet-32, outperforming all prior methods without data augmentation. The method also reduces training time by 50%: it matches VDM's 2.65 BPD on CIFAR-10 in just 2M steps (vs. VDM's 10M steps) and reaches 3.71 BPD on ImageNet-32 in 1M steps (vs. VDM's 2M steps). The VLB bounds are ≤2.59 (CIFAR-10) and ≤3.71 (ImageNet), with true NLLs via probability flow ODEs being even lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MULAN outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric. On CIFAR-10, MULAN (2.55 BPD) beats the closest learned-diffusion competitor DiffEnc (≤2.62) by 0.07 BPD, VDM (≤2.65) by 0.10 BPD, and NDM (≤2.70) by 0.15 BPD. On ImageNet-32, MULAN (3.67 BPD) surpasses VDM (≤3.72) by 0.05 BPD, Reflected Diffusion Models (3.74) by 0.07 BPD, and ScoreFlow (3.76) by 0.09 BPD. Among autoregressive and flow-based methods, the gap is larger: MULAN improves over Image Transformer (2.90) by 0.35 BPD and over Flow Matching (2.99) by 0.44 BPD on CIFAR-10. The training efficiency gain is also notable: MULAN matches VDM's 2.65 BPD in 2M steps versus VDM's 10M steps, a 5× reduction in training steps, while adding only ~10% more parameters (a small encoder network for the auxiliary latent).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Variational Diffusion Models (VDM, Kingma et al. 2021), which first proposed learning the noise schedule but argued the ELBO is invariant to the choice of schedule for scalar noise. MULAN departs from VDM by showing this invariance breaks down for multivariate (per-pixel) noise schedules, and by combining three previously separate ideas—multivariate noise (as in blurring diffusion models), input-conditioned noising, and auxiliary latent variables (as in InfoDiffusion)—into a single learned forward process. It also positions itself against concurrent learned-diffusion work (NDMs, DiffEnc, DNFs) and optimal-transport approaches (MTC, Schrödinger bridges), distinguishing itself by optimizing log-likelihood rather than sampling speed or transport cost, and by remaining simulation-free with a tractable noise parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By dispelling the widely held assumption that diffusion-model likelihood is invariant to the noise schedule, this work opens a new axis of optimization (the forward process itself) that was previously considered irrelevant to density estimation. The 50% reduction in training steps and SOTA BPD on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32, achieved without modifying the UNet architecture, make the technique broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the learned multivariate schedules reveal interpretable structure (the authors found none with their current architecture), whether the approach transfers to improving sample quality (FID), and how it scales to higher-resolution or video data.
