## Problem

Large pre-trained Vision Transformers are typically deployed as one-size-fits-all initializations for downstream tasks, ignoring task-specific resource constraints such as memory, FLOPs, or latency. Existing Learngene approaches extract whole integral layers and manually stack them with randomly initialized layers, which preserves redundant weights within layers and cannot adapt to descendant models with custom dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240828/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT ancestry model (DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base) and produces initialized descendant model parameters with elastic scales—specifically, a configurable number of attention heads per layer (controlled by hyperparameter ω) and a configurable number of FFN layers. The clustering phase consumes the mean attention distance matrix of each head in each layer as a 1-D density metric, while the inheriting phase outputs weight tensors for MSA heads, FFN blocks, projection layers, and layer/patch/position embeddings that directly parameterize a smaller ViT ready for fine-tuning.

## Method

Cluster-Learngene adaptively clusters attention heads within each layer of the ancestry model using a DBSCAN-like density-based algorithm where the mean attention distance (Eq. 4) serves as the density metric, yielding head centroids that aggregate semantically similar heads without pre-specifying cluster counts. FFNs are clustered by comparing the average distance of head centroids between adjacent layers: when this distance falls below threshold ε, only the shallower FFN is retained as the FFN centroid. In the inheriting phase, head centroids are ranked by cluster size and distributed to descendant model attention heads via priority weight-sharing (evenly when H_d is divisible by the centroid count, with remainder heads appended from the highest-priority centroids otherwise). Learnable transformation parameters (Eq. 7) expand each FFN centroid into multiple FFNs, enabling initialization of descendant models at arbitrary elastic scales without retraining the ancestry model.

## Results

On 7 downstream classification tasks (DeiT-Small, 6-layer descendant models), Cluster-Learngene achieves top-1 accuracy of 71.09% on iNaturalist-2019, 89.53% on Food-101, 92.31% on Oxford Flowers, 89.87% on Stanford Cars, 97.79% on CIFAR-10, 85.38% on CIFAR-100, and 75.98% on CUB-200-2011, using only 7.5M inherited parameters. On ImageNet-1K, the Small-scale descendant model (H_d=6, L_d=12) reaches 78.43% top-1 accuracy, outperforming From-Scratch (68.56%) by 9.87% while requiring only half the training epochs. Cluster-Learngene also demonstrates 4× faster convergence and higher data efficiency, surpassing From-Scratch by 7.09% with only 25% of ImageNet training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cluster-Learngene outperforms all three Learngene/weight-transformation baselines across all 7 downstream datasets. Against Heuristic-Learngene [49] (which extracts the last 6 layers and stacks them with random lower layers), Cluster-Learngene gains 17.88% on iNaturalist-2019 (71.09 vs 53.21) and 12.74% on CIFAR-100 (85.38 vs 72.64). Against Auto-Learngene [50] (which extracts the first 6 layers and stacks random higher layers), the improvement is 11.17% on iNaturalist-2019 and 5.89% on CIFAR-100. Against Weight-Transformation [63] (MiniViT-style weight multiplexing), Cluster-Learngene leads by 11.26% on iNaturalist-2019 and 9.40% on CIFAR-100. Notably, Cluster-Learngene also surpasses the full Pretraining-Finetuning baseline on 6 of 7 datasets (losing only on CUB-200 by 2.15%) while inheriting fewer parameters (7.5M vs 10.5M), suggesting that the clustered learngene retains more universally transferable knowledge than the full pre-trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene framework (Wang et al., 2022/2023), which condenses knowledge from a large ancestry model into a compact subset to initialize downstream models. Prior Learngene methods extract integral layers and manually stack them with randomly initialized layers, but this work departs by performing adaptive density-based clustering *within* layers—clustering attention heads by mean attention distance and FFNs by inter-layer centroid proximity—thereby eliminating redundant weights that whole-layer extraction preserves. The clustering mechanism is directly adapted from DBSCAN (Schubert et al., 2017), repurposed to operate on scalar density metrics of attention heads rather than multi-dimensional data points, and the resulting centroids are expanded via priority weight-sharing and learnable parameter transformations to initialize descendant models at arbitrary scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: pre-trained foundation models are typically deployed at a fixed scale, but downstream tasks on edge devices or resource-constrained settings require elastic model sizes without retraining from scratch. By showing that a 7.5M-parameter clustered learngene can match or exceed full pretraining-finetuning (10.5M parameters) on most downstream tasks, the work suggests that *which* parameters are inherited matters more than *how many*. Key limitations include dependence on ancestry model quality (biases propagate to descendants), validation primarily on DeiT variants, and the open question of whether the density-based clustering principle generalizes to non-ViT architectures or to modalities beyond vision. The method's practical value is strongest for teams maintaining a single large pre-trained model and needing to deploy many size variants across heterogeneous hardware.
