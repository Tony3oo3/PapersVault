## Problem

Current LLM-based personalized teaching predominantly follows a passive 'Question-Answering' paradigm, directly delivering complete answers rather than engaging students in active problem-solving. This paper addresses the absence of a Socratic 'Thought-Provoking' teaching paradigm in LLMs, the lack of a large-scale dataset specifically designed for Socratic-style multi-round teaching dialogues, and the absence of a systematic evaluation framework for assessing LLM teaching quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242720/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem context, the accumulated dialogue history of prior teacher-student turns, and the student's latest response as input, and produces a single Socratic teacher turn (a guiding question, targeted explanation, or corrective prompt) as output. The output is constrained to follow a step-by-step guiding question structure without directly revealing the final answer, and terminates with an [END] token when the teaching sequence is complete. Training data consists of 35K multi-round dialogues (averaging 5.28 rounds, yielding ~208K single-round examples) plus 22K augmented single-round dialogues targeting four specific teaching abilities.

## Method

The paper constructs the SocraTeach dataset via a novel 'Dean-Teacher-Student' multi-agent pipeline where three GPT4-simulated agents interact: the Teacher generates Socratic instructions following pre-decomposed step-by-step guiding questions, the Student responds according to one of six cognitive state profiles (poor in problem understanding, instruction understanding, calculation, knowledge mastery, or thirst for learning; or strong in all), and the Dean supervises and revises Teacher outputs to enforce Socratic style compliance. Data augmentation adds 22K single-round dialogues specifically targeting four teaching abilities: rejecting irrelevant questions, answering student questions, identifying incorrect replies, and recognizing correct replies. SocraticLM is obtained by P-Tuning ChatGLM3-6b on SocraTeach with three ability-balancing strategies: separate training (dialogue data first, then 1/10-ratio problem-solving data to prevent catastrophic forgetting), differentiated instruction templates for teaching vs. problem-solving, and mixed zero-shot/one-shot prompt settings to preserve reasoning capacity.

## Results

SocraticLM (6B parameters) achieves an Overall Quality score of 0.62, outperforming GPT4 (0.50) by 12% and all other baselines. On the four objective teaching-ability metrics, SocraticLM scores 0.83 (IARA), 0.98 (CARA), 0.74 (SER), and 0.78 (SRR), surpassing GPT4 by 6%, 7%, 9%, and 23% respectively. On problem-solving, SocraticLM maintains GSM8K accuracy of 0.606 (vs. 0.624 for the base ChatGLM3-6b) and slightly improves MAWPS accuracy to 0.814 (vs. 0.798), confirming that the teaching fine-tuning does not catastrophically degrade reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SocraticLM dominates all eight baselines across every teaching metric. Against GPT4 (the strongest general-purpose baseline), SocraticLM leads by 12% on Overall Quality (0.62 vs. 0.50), 23% on Successful Rejection Rate (0.78 vs. 0.55), and 9% on Successful Explanation Rate (0.74 vs. 0.65). Against the education-specific EduChat-32b, SocraticLM achieves a 67% relative improvement on Overall Quality (0.62 vs. 0.37) and a 2600% improvement on SRR (0.78 vs. 0.03), highlighting that EduChat's 32B parameter scale does not compensate for the absence of Socratic-style training data. Among open-source LLMs, Llama3-8b is the closest competitor on IARA (0.75 vs. 0.83) and SRR (0.52 vs. 0.78), but still trails SocraticLM by 10% and 33% respectively. On problem-solving, SocraticLM's GSM8K accuracy (0.606) is only 1.8% below the un-fine-tuned ChatGLM3-6b (0.624), while its MAWPS accuracy (0.814) actually exceeds the base model (0.798), demonstrating that the three ability-balancing training strategies effectively prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based personalized teaching (EduChat, MathGPT, MATHDIAL) by shifting from the dominant 'Question-Answering' paradigm to a Socratic 'Thought-Provoking' paradigm, where the model actively provokes student reasoning rather than delivering answers. It builds on ChatGLM3-6b via P-Tuning and draws on prior teaching dialogue datasets (CIMA, AutoTutor, QuizBot, MATHDIAL), but departs from them by introducing a novel Dean-Teacher-Student multi-agent pipeline with a supervisory Dean role to correct the known inadequacy of LLMs as teachers, a five-dimensional student cognitive state system, and targeted data augmentation for four specific teaching abilities. The five-dimensional pedagogical evaluation system is positioned as the first comprehensive framework for assessing LLM teaching quality, going beyond BLEU/Rouge similarity metrics used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SocraTeach dataset (35K multi-round + 22K single-round dialogues) and the five-dimensional evaluation system provide the field with the first large-scale, fully automated resource specifically designed for Socratic teaching, addressing the scalability bottleneck of prior human-in-the-loop datasets like MATHDIAL (3K samples). The finding that fine-tuning on Socratic teaching dialogues can actually improve (not just preserve) problem-solving accuracy on MAWPS suggests a potential synergy between pedagogical and reasoning capabilities. However, the work is limited to primary-school mathematics, the SER metric (0.74) indicates substantial room for improvement in handling complex student questions, and generalization to other subjects or age groups remains an open question requiring additional data construction.
