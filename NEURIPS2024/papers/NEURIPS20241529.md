## Problem

Active 3D object detection faces two under-addressed challenges: (1) varying difficulty levels (size, occlusion, truncation) within each semantic category require selecting samples of diverse difficulty, and (2) highly imbalanced label distributions across point clouds (e.g., cars dominate over cyclists) degrade detector performance as labeled data accumulates. Existing methods like CRB balance labels only within individual point clouds without guaranteeing global balance, while KECOR ignores data imbalance entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of unlabeled LiDAR point clouds (KITTI: 3,712 train / 3,769 val; Waymo Open: 158,361 train / 40,077 test), a small initial labeled set, and a pre-trained 3D detector (PV-RCNN or SECOND). At each active learning query round, it outputs a subset of Γ₂ point clouds (300 for KITTI, 1,200 for Waymo) selected for human annotation of 7-DoF oriented bounding boxes and semantic labels. The selection is constrained by a fixed per-round bounding box budget N_q (100 for KITTI), and the process iterates until a total query budget N_Q is exhausted.

## Method

STONE formulates active 3D detection as a two-objective submodular optimization: maximizing representativeness via a gradient-based feature submodular function f₁ and preserving label-distribution entropy via f₂. Stage 1 (GBSSS) uses Monte Carlo dropout to generate hypothetical labels, computes class-reweighed regression loss (inverse-frequency scaling) and classification loss (label-distribution-aware margin subtraction m_{i,c}=1/√n_c), extracts gradients from the detector head's FC layer, and greedily selects Γ₁ diverse point clouds using a concave log-score submodular function. Stage 2 (SDMCB) first filters to the top-K point clouds with highest individual label-distribution entropy, then applies a second greedy pass that maximizes cumulative label entropy H(P̃ᵢ) computed over the combined labeled-plus-candidate set, ensuring the global label distribution remains balanced across all query rounds. This hierarchical two-stage design reduces GPU memory by 140% compared to KECOR while achieving state-of-the-art 3D AP on KITTI and Waymo Open.

## Results

On KITTI with PV-RCNN and 1% queried bounding boxes, STONE achieves average 3D AP of 82.17% (EASY), 68.75% (MODERATE), and 64.05% (HARD), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art KECOR by 0.54%, 0.08%, and 0.63% respectively. On Waymo Open, STONE reaches a 2% higher APH score than KECOR with fewer active learning loops, and achieves 1% higher APH than CRB with 24% fewer bounding boxes. On PASCAL VOC07 for 2D detection, STONE achieves 65.34% mAP in the first query, surpassing AL-MDN_gmm by 2.93% and AL-MDN_eff by 2.43%. STONE also requires 140% less GPU memory than KECOR (10 GB vs. 24 GB on KITTI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STONE outperforms all 12 baselines on KITTI 3D detection. Against the two strongest 3D-specific baselines, STONE improves average 3D AP over KECOR [34] by 0.54% (EASY), 0.08% (MODERATE), and 0.63% (HARD), and over CRB [35] by 1.47%, 0.84%, and 1.24% respectively. Generic active learning baselines (CORESET [44], BADGE [1], LLAL [62]) trail STONE by 5–10% in average HARD AP, while uncertainty-based 3D methods (MC-MI [11], MC-REG [35], CONSENSUS [43], LT/C [22]) show larger gaps, with MC-REG [35] lagging by over 12% in average HARD AP. On the SECOND backbone (Table 5), STONE achieves 58.75% 3D AP at HARD level, surpassing KECOR [34] (55.34%) by 3.4% and CRB [35] (53.09%) by 5.7%. On PASCAL VOC07 for 2D detection, STONE leads AL-MDN_gmm [5] by 2.93% and AL-MDN_eff [5] by 2.43% mAP in the first query, while remaining competitive with MC-DROPOUT [11] and ENSEMBLE [19] in later queries. Notably, STONE achieves these gains while consuming 140% less GPU memory than KECOR [34] (10 GB vs. 24 GB), and uses fewer active learning loops to reach comparable or higher APH on Waymo Open.

## Relation to Prior Work

STONE positions itself as the first submodular optimization framework specifically formulated for active 3D object detection, unifying two objectives—representativeness (via gradient-based feature coverage) and label-distribution balance—under a single diminishing-returns formulation. It builds directly on Monte Carlo dropout for hypothetical label generation, feature-based submodular score functions from speech data selection, and label-distribution-aware margin losses from imbalanced classification. The key departure from prior 3D-specific AL methods (CRB, KECOR) is that STONE explicitly models cross-point-cloud label balance as a submodular objective rather than relying on per-scene heuristics or kernel coding rate alone, and it introduces class-aware reweighing of both regression and classification losses to correct gradient distortion under severe class imbalance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the labeling budget needed to train competitive 3D detectors on KITTI and Waymo Open, STONE addresses a genuine cost bottleneck in autonomous driving deployment. The framework's generality—validated on 2D detection with SSD/VGG-16—suggests the submodular formulation could transfer to other detection or segmentation tasks with multi-class imbalance. However, the paper acknowledges it does not reduce the per-round running time of existing AL pipelines, and the modest AP gains (0.5–1.5% over KECOR) come at the cost of 37% more annotated bounding boxes, leaving open whether the added complexity is justified at scale. Future work on more efficient candidate screening and adaptive budget allocation would strengthen the practical case.
