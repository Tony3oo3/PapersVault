## Problem

Existing prompt-tuning methods for continual learning in Vision Transformers lack a theoretical guarantee against interference on previously learned tasks. While gradient orthogonal projection can eliminate forgetting in linear CNN layers, it cannot be directly transferred to ViT prompt tuning due to the high-order non-linear self-attention operation and the distribution drift introduced by LayerNorm, which fundamentally change the relationship between prompt updates and output features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240253/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and produces class labels through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k) with 4 trainable prompts inserted into each of the 12 layers (VPT-Deep). Only the prompts are updated; all other parameters (LayerNorm, QKV projections, MLP, and the classifier head) remain frozen. In the CLIP variant, prompts are inserted into the first 3 layers of the image encoder while the text encoder stays frozen, and a learnable logit scale plus a refined projection head handle cross-task confusion.

## Method

The paper derives two sufficient consistency conditions for eliminating interference in ViT prompt tuning: (1) the prompt update ΔP must be orthogonal to the normalized previous input image tokens projected by the second-order matrix W_q W_k^T (guaranteeing Affinity consistency), and (2) ΔP must be orthogonal to the activated attention map S_P generated by image queries and prompt keys (guaranteeing Aggregation consistency). An additional invariant prompt distribution constraint (equal mean and standard deviation before and after update) is imposed to handle LayerNorm, implemented via an L1 penalty loss. In practice, the candidate prompt update from the optimizer is projected into the null spaces of Q_X W_k^T and S_P via SVD-based null-space projection matrices B1 and B2, yielding ΔP = B2·P_G·B1. Nullities are determined adaptively by the maximum second derivative of the descending singular value curve, and stability–plasticity trade-off is controlled by blending each projection matrix with the identity via weights η1 and η2.

## Results

On the four class-incremental benchmarks using VPT with ViT-B/16, VPT-NSP² achieves 91.74% accuracy on 10-split CIFAR-100, 89.89% on 20-split CIFAR-100, 78.88% on 10-split ImageNet-R, and 83.54% on 10-split DomainNet, surpassing the second-best method by an average of 1.49% in accuracy. Forgetting is reduced to 3.28%, 4.91%, 5.06%, and 8.54% respectively, compared to 12.33%, 17.36%, 19.41%, and 25.65% for the sequential fine-tuning baseline. The approach also generalizes to CLIP, improving accuracy by 6.48%–9.31% and reducing forgetting by 2.68%–17.27% over the CLIP-Seq baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPT-NSP² achieves the highest accuracy on all four benchmarks, outperforming the strongest competitors by 2.39% on 10S-CIFAR-100 (vs. APG at 89.35%), 1.25% on 20S-CIFAR-100 (vs. APG at 88.64%), 1.74% on 10S-ImageNet-R (vs. CPrompt at 77.14%), and 0.57% on 10S-DomainNet (vs. CPrompt at 82.97%). Against the most closely related gradient-projection method PGP (DualP-PGP), VPT-NSP² gains 4.82% on 10S-CIFAR-100 and 3.13% on 10S-ImageNet-R, confirming that the full self-attention analysis (including Aggregation and LayerNorm) is critical. The method also substantially outperforms prompt-pool approaches (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) and expandable-backbone methods (EASE, InfLoRA) across all benchmarks. In terms of forgetting, VPT-NSP² is competitive but not always the lowest (e.g., CODA-P achieves 1.67% on 10S-CIFAR-100), which the authors attribute to a deliberate stability–plasticity trade-off that prioritizes overall accuracy. On long-sequence settings (50 and 100 tasks), VPT-NSP² still leads all baselines, surpassing L2P by up to 15.75% on 50S-CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the null-space projection framework for continual learning (Wang et al., CVPR 2021) from CNNs to visual prompt tuning in ViTs, building on the VPT-Deep architecture (Jia et al., ECCV 2022). The key departure is from prior orthogonal projection methods (OWM, GPM, Adam-NSCL) and the recent PGP (Qiao et al., ICLR 2024), which either assume linear operations or apply a simplified attention model yielding the condition X_t ΔP^T = 0. Here, the authors derive fundamentally different consistency conditions involving the second-order pre-trained transformation matrix W_q W_k^T and an activated attention map, accounting for the full self-attention non-linearity and LayerNorm distribution drift that are absent in CNN-based analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded explanation of why naive gradient orthogonal projection fails in ViT prompt tuning, and offers a practical null-space-based solution that achieves SOTA across four class-incremental benchmarks with both VPT and CLIP backbones. The invariant prompt distribution constraint, while effective empirically, is acknowledged as an approximation rather than an exact solution, leaving open whether the consistency conditions can be derived without this assumption. The approach's generalizability across pre-training paradigms (ImageNet-1k, DINO, MIIL, CLIP) suggests the theoretical conditions capture a structural property of ViT self-attention rather than a dataset-specific artifact.
