## Problem

Self-supervised representation learning is inherently less efficient than supervised learning due to sub-optimal self-generated targets, and existing dataset distillation methods are computationally expensive—often exceeding the cost of training on the full dataset. The paper asks how to leverage freely available, task- and architecture-agnostic pre-trained models (downloadable from the internet) to accelerate representation learning without additional computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled image dataset (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, or 224×224 ImageNet-1K) and any publicly available pre-trained model as the 'prior model'—which can be a ResNet, ViT, CLIP, or even a randomly initialized network, regardless of the architecture or dataset it was trained on. It produces an efficient dataset pairing the original samples (with weak augmentation) against one-time-generated pseudo-targets (transportable representations from the prior model), and a trained representation model that matches or exceeds full-budget performance at 50% or less of the original training cost.

## Method

RELA consists of two optimization-free components: RELA-D synthesizes an efficient dataset by running a single forward pass of the prior model over all samples to generate pseudo-targets (transportable representations), combined with weakly augmented samples; RELA-F adds a plug-and-play cosine-similarity loss between the student model's linear projection and these pseudo-targets to the existing SSL loss. A dynamic coefficient λ starts at 1 (fully emphasizing the RELA auxiliary loss) and adaptively decays to 0 as the model's self-generated targets become reliable, ensuring zero extra computational cost in later training. The key theoretical insight is that well-trained models across diverse architectures and tasks converge to a shared linear representation space, making their outputs valid pseudo-labels; this is formalized via convergence-rate theorems (smaller sample variance and higher relabeling intensity both accelerate convergence) and a generalization bound decomposed into sample-divergence, target-quality, and model-complexity terms.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 50% training budget, BYOL(RELA) using an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 prior achieves 61.6% linear-probing accuracy, surpassing full-budget BYOL (52.5%) by 9.1 points. On ImageNet-1K with 50% budget, BYOL(RELA) with CLIP-RN50 as prior reaches 62.9% top-1 accuracy, exceeding full-budget BYOL (61.9%) while halving computational cost. Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-1K), even a randomly initialized prior model improves over original BYOL at the same budget, and stronger priors (e.g., CLIP) yield larger gains. The method is plug-and-play: it improves all seven tested self-supervised algorithms (SimCLR, Barlow Twins, DINO, MoCo, SimSiam, SwAV, VicReg) at 10% budget, with DINO on CIFAR-10 jumping from 70.6% to 82.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RELA is evaluated as a plug-and-play accelerator on top of eight self-supervised baselines. Against BYOL (the primary baseline), BYOL(RELA) at 50% budget consistently outperforms full-budget BYOL on CIFAR-10 (87.3% vs. 82.7%), CIFAR-100 (61.6% vs. 52.5%), Tiny-ImageNet (46.4% vs. 43.6%), and ImageNet-1K (62.9% vs. 61.9%). In Table 3 (10% budget, CLIP-RN50 prior), RELA improves every method: DINO gains the most on CIFAR-10 (70.6%→82.4%, +11.8 pts), followed by SimSiam (66.7%→79.3%, +12.6 pts) and VicReg (71.3%→80.1%, +8.8 pts). On Tiny-ImageNet, MoCo benefits most (33.4%→37.1%, +3.7 pts) while SimSiam also improves substantially (25.4%→32.6%, +7.2 pts). Even with a randomly initialized prior (weakest setting), all methods improve over their original counterparts, confirming that the data-optimization component alone provides a meaningful boost. The method never degrades performance relative to the original algorithm at the same budget, and the gains scale with prior model strength (Rand. < dataset-specific BYOL\* < CLIP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dataset distillation and self-supervised learning, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than optimizing synthetic data to match training trajectories (as in gradient-matching or trajectory-matching distillation), RELA uses a pre-trained model's representations as fixed pseudo-targets, making the data generation process optimization-free and one-shot. It builds directly on BYOL as the base SSL algorithm and draws on domain adaptation theory (Ben-David et al.) and NTK analysis for its generalization and convergence bounds, while also connecting to the 'weak-to-strong generalization' literature. The conceptual departure from prior work is the reframing of self-supervised learning as a data-centric problem where the quality of generated targets (not just the architecture or loss) determines training efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% computational cost reduction on ImageNet-1K while matching full-budget BYOL accuracy is a practically significant result, and the plug-and-play design (a single additional loss term) makes adoption straightforward across any SSL method. However, the theoretical analysis is restricted to a simplified bimodal Gaussian case, limiting its explanatory power for real-world datasets. An open question is whether the 'transportable representation' property (that well-trained models across architectures converge to a shared linear representation space) holds universally or degrades with increasing task/architecture mismatch. The finding that even randomly initialized models provide substantial speedup suggests the mechanism may be more about data structure than label quality, which warrants further investigation.
