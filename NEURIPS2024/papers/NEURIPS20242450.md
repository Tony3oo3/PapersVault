## Problem

Personalized federated learning methods struggle to navigate the bias-variance trade-off in client classifier learning: relying solely on limited local data yields high variance, while naively incorporating global knowledge introduces bias that fails to generalize under real-world covariate shifts (e.g., weather, camera calibration) and data scarcity. Existing approaches either use fixed local classifiers, costly fine-tuning, or heuristic regularization that does not explicitly model the distributional mismatch between global and local feature spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) or grayscale images (28×28 for EMNIST) and outputs a class label via a closed-form generative classifier. Unusually, the per-client model is not a learned linear head but an explicit class-conditional Gaussian over the feature space, parameterized by per-class means and a shared covariance matrix. Each client additionally maintains a scalar interpolation coefficient β that controls how much of the global versus local distribution estimate is used at inference, making the personalization mechanism interpretable and data-driven.

## Method

pFedFDA decomposes the PFL objective into a shared feature extractor φ and a per-client generative classifier, modeling the feature space as a class-conditional Gaussian with tied covariance. During training, clients update φ by maximizing the likelihood of their local features under the *global* Gaussian distribution (i.e., the generative classifier acts as a fixed teacher that guides representation learning), which naturally aligns client features without requiring a separate regularization hyperparameter. At inference, each client estimates its own local feature distribution (means and covariance) from extracted features, then fuses it with the global estimate via a per-client interpolation coefficient β learned by k-fold cross-validation on the local data. The resulting personalized classifier is the closed-form Bayes rule of the interpolated Gaussian, which is linear in the features and requires no additional forward passes. A theoretical bias-variance bound (Theorem 1) characterizes how the optimal β depends on the local-global distribution shift, local data volume, and feature variances, providing a principled justification for the interpolation mechanism.

## Results

On CIFAR10-S (covariate shift, Dir(0.5)), pFedFDA achieves 72.4% average test accuracy with full training data, improving to a 6.9% margin over the second-best method (FedPAC, 67.9%) when only 25% of samples are available. On CIFAR100-S, the advantage grows from 0.1% (full data) to 6.5% (25% data). In standard non-i.i.d. settings (Table 2), pFedFDA remains within ~1% of the best method on EMNIST and CIFAR-10, while leading on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.5) with 38.5% vs. FedPAC's 37.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedFDA outperforms all 11 baselines across every covariate-shift and data-scarcity setting in Table 1, with the largest margins over FedAvg (up to +27.4% on CIFAR10-S 25%), FedBABU (+12.8%), and LG-FedAvg (+16.2%). Against the strongest baselines, pFedFDA beats FedPAC by 4.5–6.9% on CIFAR10-S and 0.1–6.5% on CIFAR100-S, with the gap widening as data becomes scarcer. In standard (non-covariate-shift) settings (Table 2), pFedFDA is competitive but not always best: FedPAC edges it by 0.4–1.2% on EMNIST, CIFAR-10 Dir(0.1), and CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1), while pFedFDA leads on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.5) (38.5% vs. 37.8%) and is within 0.4% of Ditto/pFedMe on TinyImageNet. FedAvgFT and Ditto are the closest competitors in moderate-heterogeneity settings, typically within 1% of pFedFDA. The paper also shows pFedFDA generalizes to unseen clients with novel corruptions (Table 4), maintaining the highest accuracy among all methods across 10 corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled representation-and-classifier paradigm in personalized federated learning (FedRep, FedBABU, FedRoD, FedPAC, LG-FedAvg) by replacing the discriminative classifier with a class-conditional Gaussian generative model of the feature space. The key conceptual departure is framing representation learning as a generative modeling task where the global classifier is derived from estimated feature distribution parameters (means and covariance), rather than learned through local fine-tuning or prototype alignment. The local-global interpolation of distribution parameters to obtain personalized classifiers is a novel mechanism for navigating the bias-variance trade-off, drawing on ideas from regularized estimation and prior fusion but applied in a new context. It is conceptually related to FedEM, which also estimates latent distributions, but pFedFDA operates on feature-space distributions with a single shared model rather than input-space mixtures with per-component models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in PFL: existing methods are primarily validated under label-imbalance (Dirichlet) heterogeneity, while real cross-device FL encounters covariate shift (sensor quality, weather, calibration) combined with severe data scarcity. The generative classifier provides a principled, low-overhead mechanism for personalization that scales better than fine-tuning-based approaches (FedRep, FedPAC) and avoids the mixture-model overhead of FedEM. Limitations include the Gaussian feature assumption (noted to potentially fail with ReLU activations), evaluation restricted to image classification, and the open question of scalability to very large client populations. Future work on cluster-aware distribution estimation and non-Gaussian feature models could extend the approach's applicability.
