## Problem

Complex visual reasoning requires both sequential multi-step composition (e.g., chaining spatial filters) and simultaneous processing of independent operations (e.g., counting multiple categories in parallel). Prior methods rely on either purely iterative architectures (MAC, NSM) that struggle with independent parallel stimuli, or purely parallel transformer attention that cannot explicitly compose operations step-by-step. No single lightweight mechanism had effectively unified both computational priors for complex VQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244380/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

IPRM consumes object-level visual tokens (bounding-box coordinates, predicted attributes, human poses, and relations projected to distributed representations, flattened across frames for video) and language tokens (question encoded by Distil-RoBERTa or Bi-LSTM). It produces a single reasoning-result vector y_s (obtained by attending over final operation states with a language summary query), a set of reasoning-result tokens Y_R (the final result states), and per-step language and visual attention maps that enable step-by-step reasoning visualization. The module is weight-tied, so its parameter count (≈4.4–5.5M) remains constant regardless of the number of iterative steps T or parallel operations N_op.

## Method

IPRM maintains an explicit latent memory of N_op parallel 'operation states' keyed to 'result states' and iterates for T steps. At each step it (1) forms N_op new latent operations by attending to language features conditioned on prior operation states, (2) executes all operations in parallel by attending to visual features using a feature-modulation mechanism that jointly conditions on both the new operations and prior result states, and (3) composes operations through an inter-operation attention unit with a lookback window W that allows each operation to attend to other current and recent past operations (with an identity mask preventing self-attention). This design lets independent operations be computed simultaneously (parallel) while their results are sequentially integrated into memory (iterative), and the operation composition unit mitigates redundancy across parallel slots. The entire module is fully differentiable, requires no auxiliary supervision (no bounding-box pre-training or functional programs), and can be plugged atop diverse vision-language backbones including frozen CLIP variants.

## Results

IPRM achieves 69.9% average accuracy on STAR (5% over prior SOTA SeViLA-BLIP2 at 64.9%) and 60.4% overall accuracy on AGQAv2 (5% over GF at 55.1%). On CLEVR-Humans, IPRM reaches 63.8% zero-shot and 85.5% finetuned accuracy, outperforming MDETR (59.9%/81.7%) by 3.9% and 3.8% respectively. On CLEVRER-Humans, IPRM improves zero-shot per-question accuracy by 7% (38.9% vs. ALOE's 26.9%) and finetuned accuracy by 18.8% (53.0% vs. 31.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPRM outperforms the strongest fully-neural baseline MDETR [36] on CLEVR-Humans by 3.9% zero-shot (63.8% vs. 59.9%) and 3.8% finetuned (85.5% vs. 81.7%), while requiring no bounding-box supervision. On CLEVR-CoGenT out-of-domain (ValB), IPRM achieves 80.3%, surpassing MDETR (76.7%) by 3.6% and MAC (78.3%) by 2%, though it trails NS-VQA and MDETR on in-domain ValA (99.1% vs. 99.8%) where bounding-box supervision helps. On CLEVRER-Humans, IPRM dominates all baselines across zero-shot, finetuned, and scratch settings, improving finetuned per-question accuracy from 31.7% (ALOE) to 53.0%. On STAR, IPRM beats SeViLA-BLIP2 by 5% average (69.9% vs. 64.9%) and MIST by 18.8%, but underperforms SeViLA-BLIP2 on the feasibility sub-task (59.1% vs. 62.4%), likely due to missing commonsense knowledge. On GQA, IPRM (60.3%) edges out LXMERT and 12-in-1 (both 60.0%) and MCAN (57.4%) but falls slightly short of OSCAR (61.6%), which benefits from 4.3M pretraining samples. On CLOSURE, IPRM (75.6%) is the best fully-neural method without extra supervision, approaching NS-VQA (77.2%) which uses ground-truth programs and bounding boxes.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPRM carves new ground by unifying two computational paradigms that prior visual reasoning work has treated separately: iterative step-by-step composition (as in MAC, FiLM, NSM) and parallel simultaneous processing (as in transformer cross-attention and concat-attention modules). Rather than choosing one paradigm or stacking them as independent blocks, IPRM maintains an explicit working memory of parallel 'operation states' and 'result states' that are iteratively updated, allowing the mechanism to both compose operations sequentially and explore independent reasoning paths simultaneously within a single lightweight module. The design draws on cognitive science concepts of working memory and on feature-modulation attention from recurrent visual reasoning methods, but the specific integration of inter-operation attention with a lookback window for dynamic composition is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IPRM provides a single, interpretable reasoning mechanism that achieves state-of-the-art across diverse complex VQA benchmarks (compositional, causal, spatiotemporal, situational) while being substantially more parameter-efficient than comparable transformer-based alternatives (4.4M vs. 17.4M for MDETR). The ability to visualize intermediate reasoning steps offers a practical diagnostic tool for identifying where models fail, which is valuable for both research and deployment. Open questions include whether IPRM can be effectively integrated into large-scale foundation video-language models, whether it generalizes beyond VQA to embodied or purely linguistic reasoning, and how to address the commonsense-knowledge gap it exhibits on feasibility-type questions that benefit from larger pretrained backbones.
