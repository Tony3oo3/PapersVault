## Problem

Existing multi-person pose estimation and tracking datasets are limited to well-lit, motion-free scenarios, leaving models vulnerable to the extreme conditions (low-light and motion blur) that commonly co-occur in real-world capture. No prior dataset provides real-captured multi-human pose and tracking annotations under these degradations, and no dataset pairs such data with event camera streams that are inherently immune to motion blur and robust in low light.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244283/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 16K RGB frames at 1373×928 resolution degraded by 16× extended exposure (motion blur) or ND-filter/aperture reduction (low-light), pixel-wise aligned and hardware-synchronized with event streams represented as voxel grids (bin size 10). Annotations consist of 14 skeletal keypoints, bounding boxes, and persistent track IDs across 158 sequences with 1–13 people per frame. A third, sharp RGB stream from the triplet camera system serves as the annotation reference, ensuring ground-truth accuracy even when the primary RGB is heavily degraded.

## Method

The core contribution is the EHPT-XC dataset built with a custom triplet camera system (two RGB + one Prophesee EVK4 event camera) aligned via 50/50 beam splitters and synchronized by an ATmega microcontroller trigger, enabling simultaneous capture of degraded RGB, sharp reference RGB, and event data. For benchmarking, the paper proposes a temporally-aggregated multi-modal fusion baseline that splits each event voxel into two temporal halves (E⁰, E¹), encodes them with shared-weight convolutional encoders, and merges them with the RGB feature through a module combining self-attention (with cross-covariance matrix computation following Restormer) and cross-attention between each event half and the combined representation. Three SOTA bottom-up pose estimators (HigherHRNet, DEKR, CID) and three trackers (ByteTrack, UniTrack, OC-SORT) are evaluated under RGB-only, Event-only, and RGB+Event settings, demonstrating that event data yields substantial mAR and MOTA gains in extreme conditions.

## Results

On the EHPT-XC dataset, the best pose estimation result is achieved by DEKR with the proposed RGB+Event fusion, reaching mAP@0.5:0.95 of 37.3 and mAP@0.5 of 42.0, compared to 25.1 for RGB-only DEKR and 33.1 for Event-only DEKR. The proposed temporally-aggregated fusion outperforms the existing cross-modal attention fusion [29] by +0.4 mAP@0.5:0.95 (37.3 vs. 36.9) for DEKR. For multi-person pose tracking, ByteTrack with RGB+Event achieves MOTA of 47.37, a +14.18 improvement over RGB-only (33.19), while UniTrack shows the largest relative gain at +21.22 MOTA (46.82 vs. 25.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three pose estimation backbones, DEKR consistently outperforms HigherHRNet and CID in mAP across all modalities, while CID achieves the highest mAR@0.5 (98.0) with the proposed fusion. The proposed temporally-aggregated multi-modal fusion marginally beats the existing cross-modal attention fusion of Sun et al. [29] for DEKR (37.3 vs. 36.9 mAP@0.5:0.95) and HigherHRNet (34.3 vs. 33.8), but CID with the existing fusion achieves slightly higher mAR@0.5 (98.2 vs. 98.0). For tracking, ByteTrack [51] is the strongest tracker in both RGB-only and RGB+Event settings, achieving MOTA of 47.37 with event data. UniTrack [36] benefits the most from event data (+21.22 MOTA) but remains the weakest tracker overall (IDF1 of 7.93). OC-SORT [6] underperforms relative to ByteTrack, likely due to the large inter-frame displacements in EHPT-XC that limit its long-range association strategy. Overall, all three pose estimation methods and all three trackers show substantial gains (10–21 MOTA points) when event data is fused with RGB, confirming the value of the event modality in extreme conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of event-based vision and human pose estimation/tracking. While prior datasets (ExLPose, WIHPD, M3FD) addressed low-light pose estimation and works like PoseTrack21 covered multi-person tracking, none combined real-captured motion blur, low-light conditions, and event camera data for multi-human pose estimation and tracking simultaneously. The triplet camera system with beam splitters is a novel data acquisition strategy that departs from prior single-camera or loosely-aligned multi-camera setups, enabling pixel-wise aligned annotation in degraded conditions. The proposed temporally-aggregated multi-modal fusion with cross-attention extends existing RGB-event fusion approaches (e.g., [29]) by splitting the event stream temporally and applying iterative attention-based merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EHPT-XC fills a critical gap by providing the first multi-human pose and tracking benchmark with real (not synthesized) extreme degradation and event data, enabling researchers to develop and evaluate methods that generalize beyond clean, well-lit scenarios. The significant performance gap between RGB-only and RGB+Event baselines (e.g., +14 to +21 MOTA in tracking) validates the practical value of event modality in deployment-relevant conditions like sports and rehabilitation. Key limitations include the simple fusion baseline, 2D-only annotations, and the absence of IRB approval, leaving open questions about whether more sophisticated fusion architectures, 3D pose estimation, and larger-scale data collection can further close the performance gap in extreme conditions.
