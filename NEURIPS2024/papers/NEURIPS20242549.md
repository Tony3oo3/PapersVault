## Problem

Existing warping-and-inpainting pipelines for single-shot novel view synthesis rely on explicit geometric warping followed by T2I inpainting, but this two-step strategy breaks down at large viewpoint changes: noisy monocular depth estimates produce unreliable warped images with artifacts that inpainting cannot fix, and semantic details from the input view are lost when only sparse pixels survive the warp. The paper asks whether a generative model can learn to implicitly perform warping and inpainting in a unified process, preserving input-view semantics without depending on a pixel-accurate warped image.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image (512×384 or 512×512), a relative camera pose P_{i→j}, camera intrinsics K, and a monocular depth map D_i (from ZoeDepth or DUSt3R). The input image is encoded by a CLIP image encoder to replace the text conditioning of Stable Diffusion, and the depth map is used solely to construct warped coordinate embeddings rather than to warp pixels. The output is a single novel-view RGB image at the target viewpoint, generated in approximately 2 seconds on one H100 GPU.

## Method

GenWarp uses a two-stream architecture—a semantic preserver network and a diffusion U-net, both initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5—that replaces explicit pixel warping with an implicit, attention-based warping mechanism. A canonical 2D coordinate map is Fourier-encoded and geometrically warped with the depth map to produce a warped coordinate embedding for the target view; these embeddings are injected into both streams via convolution, providing the geometric correspondence signal without committing to a pixel-level warp. The key architectural change augments the diffusion U-net's self-attention by concatenating the input-view features F_i (from the semantic preserver) to the keys and values, so the attention map becomes a concatenation of a self-attention part (generative prior) and a cross-view attention part (warping prior). By aggregating values under both attention components simultaneously, the model learns to rely on cross-view attention for regions with reliable depth-based correspondence and on self-attention for occluded or ill-warped regions. Training is a standard LDM denoising loss fine-tuned end-to-end on multi-view pairs from RealEstate10K, ScanNet, and ACID, with pseudo-depth from DUSt3R for datasets lacking ground-truth depth.

## Results

On the in-domain RealEstate10K test set, GenWarp achieves the best FID of 31.10 (mid range) and 32.40 (long range), along with the best PSNR of 14.55 and 13.55 respectively. On the out-of-domain ScanNet test set (mid range), GenWarp achieves FID 46.03 and PSNR 12.95, outperforming all baselines including SD-Inpainting (FID 52.20, PSNR 11.68) and GeoGPT (FID 85.52, PSNR 11.36). The method consistently leads on both generation quality (FID) and reconstruction fidelity (PSNR) across all evaluated settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenWarp outperforms all baselines on both FID and PSNR across every evaluated setting. Against the warping-and-inpainting baseline (SD-Inpainting following LucidDreamer), GenWarp improves FID by 6.17 points (out-of-domain) and 10.66 points (in-domain long range) while also gaining 1.27 and 0.57 dB in PSNR respectively, showing that implicit generative warping avoids the ill-warped artifacts that degrade inpainting quality. Against GeoGPT, GenWarp achieves substantially better PSNR (14.55 vs. 12.26 in-domain mid range) because it preserves input-view semantics, while also matching or beating GeoGPT's FID. Against Photometric-NVS, GenWarp wins on both metrics in-domain (FID 31.10 vs. 37.17, PSNR 14.55 vs. 12.05) and is the only method that generalizes to the out-of-domain ScanNet setting, where Photometric-NVS fails entirely. Notably, fine-tuning SD-Inpainting on the multi-view dataset actually degrades its out-of-domain performance (FID 72.90 vs. 52.20 pre-fine-tuning), whereas GenWarp's generative warping approach maintains strong generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenWarp departs from the dominant warping-and-inpainting paradigm (LucidDreamer, Text2Immerse) that explicitly warps pixels via monocular depth then inpaints occluded regions with a T2I model, and from direct NVS models (GeoGPT, Photometric-NVS) that learn view synthesis from scratch on multi-view datasets. Instead, it draws on the self-attention-sharing lineage (Text2Video-Zero, MVDream, Animate-Anyone, MagicAnimate) and the warped-coordinate-embedding idea from CoordGAN, repurposing both to let a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-net implicitly learn depth-based correspondence through an augmented self-attention that concatenates cross-view keys/values with self-attention keys/values. The key conceptual shift is replacing the two-step warp-then-inpaint pipeline with a unified generative process where the model decides per-region whether to rely on the input view (warp) or its own generative prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of existing warping-and-inpainting methods: noisy MDE depth maps produce ill-warped artifacts that inpainting cannot repair, and semantic details are lost under large viewpoint changes. The attention-based decomposition (self-attention for generative regions, cross-view attention for warpable regions) provides a clean, interpretable mechanism that could be adopted in other geometry-conditioned generation tasks. However, the method still struggles at extreme viewpoint changes where depth-based correspondence provides no signal, and the authors acknowledge that sequential generation (chaining pre-generated views) is needed in those cases. The practical demonstration of feeding generated views into InstantSplat for rapid 3DGS reconstruction suggests a useful pipeline for downstream 3D content creation.
