## Problem

While large vision-and-language models (LVLMs) show impressive reasoning on existing benchmarks, no systematic study has compared their mathematical and algorithmic reasoning against the cumulative knowledge-building progression of children across developmental stages. Prior benchmarks either compare AI models against each other or use limited human subjects lacking the diversity, scale, and grade-level stratification needed for a meaningful human-AI comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240504/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each SMART-840 problem consists of a text description and optionally a puzzle image, with five multiple-choice options (A–E) and a difficulty weight of 3, 4, or 5 points; approximately 69% require joint image-text reasoning, with the text-only share rising from <20% in grades 1–4 to ~52% in grades 11–12. The LVLM is queried via API with a prompt requesting step-by-step explanation ending in a formatted answer token (A1–E5), while children's performance data includes per-problem answer selections, time taken, and aggregate statistics from ~30,000 participants per year across all grades.

## Method

The paper introduces SMART-840, a dataset of 840 Math Kangaroo Olympiad problems from 2020–2024 spanning all grades 1–12, collected without omissions to avoid selection bias. Seven SOTA LVLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4v, Gemini-Pro, Gemini-Flash, Claude-3 Opus/Sonnet, and open-source models) are evaluated zero-shot with a standardized prompt, each problem queried at least twice to measure response variance. The core analytical contribution is a five-metric correlation analysis between AI and children's performance: difficulty index, discriminative index, problem weight, answer-choice entropy, and time-taken. The key finding is a counter-intuitive inverse grade trend—LVLMs perform worse on younger-grade problems than older-grade ones—and a consistent absence of significant positive correlation between what is hard for children versus what is hard for AI, suggesting fundamentally different reasoning mechanisms rather than a shared cumulative knowledge base.

## Results

On the SMART-840 benchmark (840 Math Kangaroo Olympiad problems, grades 1–12), the best-performing LVLM, Claude-3 Sonnet, achieves 49.7% mean accuracy, followed by GPT-4o at 42.4% (42.5% after manual validation), Claude-3 Opus at 38.3%, GPT-4v at 37.4%, Gemini-Pro at 31.7%, and Gemini-Flash at 29.4%. All closed-source LVLMs fall substantially below the human average of 64.2%, with the performance gap being largest (30–40%) on younger-grade problems and narrowing to 10–20% on grades 11–12. Open-source LVLMs (XGEN-MM-Phi3, InternVL-Chat, InternLM-XComposer2, LLaVa-Next) perform far worse, ranging from 8.0% to 19.1% accuracy. On text-only problems, GPT-4o reaches 92.3% on grades 1–2 (exceeding the human 64.5%), but on image-text problems its accuracy drops to ~36%, revealing a strong modality gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven compared LVLMs underperform the human average (64.2%) on SMART-840, with Claude-3 Sonnet leading at 49.7% mean accuracy—still 14.5 points below children. GPT-4o (42.4%) outperforms GPT-4v (37.4%) by ~5 points, confirming generational improvement, while Gemini-Pro (31.7%) and Gemini-Flash (29.4%) trail both GPT variants by 10+ points. Claude-3 Sonnet (49.7%) surpasses Claude-3 Opus (38.3%) by 11.4 points, particularly on grades 1–6 where it reaches 51.6% vs. 38.3%. Open-source models are dramatically weaker: XGEN-MM-Phi3 (8.0%), LLaVa-Next (15.6%), InternVL-Chat (17.6%), and InternLM-XComposer2 (19.1%) all fall below or near the random baseline (20.1%), despite reporting 47–60% on the MathVista leaderboard—a ~40-point gap the authors attribute to fundamentally different reasoning demands. A striking finding is that no LVLM shows a positive correlation with children's difficulty ratings (Pearson ρ is weak or negative across all metrics), meaning AI models find a different set of problems hard than children do, and they perform counter-intuitively better on higher-grade problems than lower-grade ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LVLM mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATHVISTA, GSM-8k, Math-Vision, SMART-101) by introducing SMART-840, a dataset drawn from the Math Kangaroo Olympiad spanning all K-12 grades with actual human performance statistics from ~30K students per year. The key departure from prior work is the systematic comparison of AI performance against children's developmental progression rather than against other AI models, using psychometric metrics (difficulty index, discriminative index, entropy, time-taken) to probe whether AI and children find the same problems hard. SMART-101 is the closest prior effort but covers only grades 1-2 with programmatically generated variations, whereas SMART-840 spans all grades with authentic Olympiad problems and rich human response data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings challenge a core assumption in the AI community: that scaling and training on diverse data should yield reasoning that mirrors human cumulative knowledge building. The consistent counterintuitive trend—LVLMs performing worse on younger children's problems than older children's—suggests AI models lack the foundational primitive concepts that underpin human mathematical development. Open questions include whether this gap is due to training data distribution (more Olympiad-style problems than early-grade puzzles), whether improved multimodal grounding would close the gap, and whether the negative correlations between AI and human difficulty reflect a fundamentally different reasoning mechanism. The small dataset size (840 problems) and zero-shot evaluation limit generalizability, but the consistency across seven models strengthens the conclusions.
