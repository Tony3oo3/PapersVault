## Problem

3D medical image segmentation models achieve high accuracy but suffer from exploding parameter counts and computational costs that scale cubically with spatial dimensions, making deployment on resource-limited hardware impractical. Existing approaches to multi-scale feature fusion (e.g., NAS-based topology search) require prohibitive search times (up to one GPU-year), while dense architectures waste computation on redundant feature connections. The paper asks whether a 3D segmentation method can be designed that adaptively trades off accuracy and efficiency under varying resource constraints without costly architecture search.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D volume of shape D×H×W (CT or MRI) and produces a per-voxel class map for multi-organ or multi-region segmentation (15 abdominal organs on AMOS-CT, 3 tumor sub-regions on BraTS, 13 organs on BTCV). Inference is patch-based (e.g., 1×128×128×128 for AMOS-CT, 4×128×128×128 for BraTS) with sliding-window aggregation. A key design choice is the feature sparsity level S (0.7, 0.8, or 0.9), which acts as a tunable knob: higher S yields fewer parameters and FLOPs with only marginal accuracy loss, enabling deployment at different resource budgets from the same trained topology.

## Method

E2ENet combines a CNN backbone (L=6 multi-scale levels) with a multi-stage fusion decoder that propagates features along three directions (downward, upward, forward). The core contribution is the Dynamic Sparse Feature Fusion (DSFF) mechanism: each fusion node uses a binary mask matrix to gate individual input-to-output channel connections, starting from a random sparse topology with sparsity S. Every ΔT epochs, the lowest-L1-norm active connections are pruned and an equal number of inactive connections are randomly reactivated, allowing the topology to evolve while maintaining constant sparsity. The second contribution is a restricted depth-shift 3D convolution: input channels are split into three groups shifted by {−1, 0, +1} along the depth axis before a 1×3×3 convolution, capturing inter-slice context at 2D-level parameter cost. Together, these components reduce parameters by ~69% and inference FLOPs by ~27% relative to nnUNet while matching its mDice on AMOS-CT.

## Results

On the AMOS-CT validation set, E2ENet (s=0.8) achieves 90.0% mDice with only 9.44M parameters and 778.74G inference FLOPs, matching nnUNet's 90.0% mDice while using ~3× fewer parameters and 27% fewer FLOPs. At higher sparsity (s=0.9), E2ENet retains 89.6% mDice with just 7.64M parameters and 492.29G FLOPs, yielding a PT score of 1.88 versus nnUNet's 1.30. On BraTS, E2ENet (S=0.7) achieves 74.5% mDice, outperforming nnUNet (74.1%), UNet++ (74.1%), and DiNTS (73.0%) while using 2–5× fewer parameters. On BTCV, E2ENet (s=0.7) reaches 88.3% mDice with 11.25M parameters, comparable to nnUNet's 88.4% but with ~2.8× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2ENet (s=0.8) matches nnUNet's mDice (90.0% vs 90.0%) on AMOS-CT while reducing parameters from 30.76M to 9.44M (69% reduction) and FLOPs from 1067.89G to 778.74G (27% reduction), achieving a substantially higher PT score (1.65 vs 1.30). Against transformer-based methods, E2ENet outperforms nnFormer (90.0% vs 85.6% mDice), Swin UNETR (90.0% vs 86.4%), and UNETR (90.0% vs 78.3%) on AMOS-CT while using 4–16× fewer parameters. On BraTS, E2ENet (S=0.7) beats UNet++ (74.5% vs 74.1% mDice) with 5× fewer parameters (11.24M vs 58.38M) and 3.7× fewer FLOPs, and outperforms DiNTS (74.5% vs 73.0%) without requiring costly NAS. In the multi-scale comparison on AMOS-CT, E2ENet (s=0.8) matches MedFormer's mDice (90.0% vs 90.1%) with 4× fewer parameters and 3× fewer FLOPs, and outperforms DeepLabv3 (89.6% vs 89.0% at s=0.9) with 10× fewer FLOPs. On BTCV, E2ENet (s=0.7) achieves 88.3% mDice, essentially matching nnUNet (88.4%) while using 2.8× fewer parameters (11.25M vs 31.18M), and clearly outperforms RandomPatch (88.3% vs 84.4%) and PaNN (88.3% vs 85.4%). The one case where a baseline has lower FLOPs is UNETR (391.03G vs 778.74G for E2ENet s=0.8), but UNETR's mDice is 11.7 points lower, making E2ENet's trade-off far more favorable.

## Relation to Prior Work

E2ENet sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) multi-scale feature fusion in 3D medical segmentation (UNet, UNet++, CoTr, DiNTS), (2) Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) from Mocanu et al. and subsequent prune-and-grow methods, and (3) shift-based efficiency strategies from video understanding (TSM, RubiksNet, AS-MLP). The key conceptual departure is from NAS-based topology search (DiNTS, C2FNAS), which the authors argue is prohibitively expensive; instead, E2ENet learns sparse feature-fusion topologies directly within a fixed architecture via the DSFF mechanism. The restricted depth-shift adapts temporal/axial shift ideas from video and MLP architectures to 3D medical convolutions, constraining shift magnitude to ±1 to suit sparse feature representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 69% parameter reduction and 27% FLOP savings on AMOS-CT while maintaining near-identical mDice, making 3D medical segmentation more deployable on resource-constrained hardware. The DSFF mechanism and restricted depth-shift are presented as plug-and-play components applicable to other 3D segmentation backbones. Key open questions include: whether sparse operations will translate to real wall-clock speedups as hardware support matures, whether learnable shift offsets could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond abdominal CT to other modalities and organ types. The paper acknowledges that current GPU/TPU support for unstructured sparsity limits practical speedup, leaving a gap between theoretical FLOP savings and actual latency reduction.
