## Problem

Existing analog hardware accelerators necessarily incorporate digital circuitry for non-weight-stationary operations (activations, normalization, attention) and to mitigate device imperfections, yet no theoretical framework exists for training such heterogeneous digital-analog systems end-to-end. Equilibrium Propagation (EP) has been demonstrated on purely analog energy-based circuits, but realistic near-term hardware will be hybrid, and the requirement to simulate full EB systems to convergence has limited EP proof-of-concepts to shallow (5–6 layer) models. This paper asks: how can gradient-based optimization be performed end-to-end through architectures that mix feedforward (digital) and energy-based (analog) blocks?

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, or ImageNet32 and produces a class-label logit vector via a final linear readout layer. During inference, each image passes through alternating feedforward transformations (batch normalization, pooling, convolution) and energy-based blocks (Deep Hopfield Networks of 2–3 layers each) that must relax to a fixed-point equilibrium before the next block can process the signal. The total architecture spans 6–15 layers depending on the experiment, with block sizes chosen to balance simulation speed against convergence quality.

## Method

The paper introduces Feedforward-tied Energy-based Models (ff-EBMs), where the inference pathway is a composition of feedforward blocks F^k and energy-based blocks E^k, and derives a BP-EP gradient chaining algorithm (Theorem 3.1, Alg. 2) that backpropagates error signals through feedforward blocks and 'eq-propagates' them through EB blocks. The key mechanism is a multi-level optimization formulation: each EB block's equilibrium state depends on the previous block's state, creating a nested constraint structure solved via Lagrangian multipliers that EP implicitly estimates through centered nudging (±β) of each block's energy. In practice, the algorithm computes two nudged equilibria per block using asynchronous fixed-point iteration (alternating odd/even layer updates) with GOE-initialized Deep Hopfield Network weights, then contrasts the resulting energy gradients to obtain parameter updates for both EB and feedforward parameters simultaneously. The approach reduces to standard backpropagation when EB blocks collapse to single layers (U^k=0), and experiments show that splitting a monolithic DHN into smaller ff-EBM blocks maintains or improves accuracy while reducing simulation time by up to 4×, achieving 46% top-1 on ImageNet32 with a 15-layer model.

## Results

On ImageNet32 with a 15-layer ff-EBM (block size 2), EP achieves 46.0% top-1 and 70.0% top-5 validation accuracy, establishing a new state-of-the-art for Equilibrium Propagation. On CIFAR-100 (15 layers), EP reaches 71.2% top-1 and 90.2% top-5, matching ID (71.1% top-1). In the splitting experiment on CIFAR-10, reducing block size from 6 to 2 layers (L=6) improves EP accuracy from 89.2% to 90.1% while reducing wall-clock time by up to ~4× (7:01 → 3:57). EP consistently matches or slightly exceeds ID performance across all datasets and depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EP (the proposed BP-EP chaining algorithm) matches ID (Implicit Differentiation / Recurrent Backpropagation) on every dataset and depth: e.g., 46.0% vs. 45.5% top-1 on ImageNet32 (L=15) and 71.2% vs. 71.1% on CIFAR-100 (L=15), confirming the theoretical equivalence (Theorem 4.1). Against the prior EP state-of-the-art, Holomorphic EP (Laborieux & Zenke, NeurIPS 2022: 36.5% top-1 on ImageNet32), the proposed method improves by ~10 percentage points. Against the best backprop-alternative, Dual Propagation (Høier et al., ICML 2023: 41.5% top-1 on ImageNet32), the improvement is ~5 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, EP with smaller blocks (bs=2) also slightly outperforms ID (90.1% vs. 90.0% for L=6; 92.5% vs. 91.5% for L=12) while being up to 4× faster in wall-clock time. Overall, the method ties or beats all baselines on accuracy while offering a principled path toward hardware-efficient training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Equilibrium Propagation (EP) from purely energy-based networks to hybrid architectures by introducing Feedforward-tied EBMs (ff-EBMs), where feedforward (digital) and energy-based (analog) blocks are interleaved. Theoretically, it builds on the bilevel optimization formulation of EP training (Scellier & Bengio, 2017) and the deeply-nested optimization perspective of Zach (2021), generalizing both to a multi-level setting. Algorithmically, it inherits the centered EP (C-EP) scheme of Laborieux et al. (2021) and the asynchronous fixed-point iteration and Deep Hopfield Network architecture from Scellier et al. (2024), but wraps them in a BP-EP gradient chaining procedure that has no direct counterpart in prior EP literature. The closest prior work, Zach (2021), treats feedforward learning as nested optimization with pairwise energy functions but provides no algorithmic prescription; this paper fills that gap with a concrete, implementable algorithm (Alg. 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, incremental roadmap for integrating self-trainable analog computational primitives into existing digital accelerators, addressing a key gap between the EP literature (which assumes fully analog systems) and realistic hardware (which requires digital circuitry for normalization, activation, and attention). The finding that a DHN can be arbitrarily split into smaller EB blocks with up to 4× simulation speedup while maintaining or improving accuracy is practically significant for EP's scalability, which has been limited to 5–6 layer models. Open questions include scaling to standard ImageNet and considerably deeper architectures, incorporating device non-idealities (ADC/DAC noise, write latency), and extending beyond vision tasks. The authors also note that ff-EBM learning partially inherits backpropagation's pitfalls (e.g., weight transport), leaving room for hybrid training schemes that combine EP in analog blocks with BP alternatives in digital blocks.
