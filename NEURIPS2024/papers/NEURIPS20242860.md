## Problem

Learning preference models from human feedback is costly, yet no principled method existed for deciding which questions to pose to annotators within a fixed budget to maximize ranking quality across all lists. The paper addresses this by formulating human preference elicitation as an optimal experimental design problem over lists of items, rather than over individual feature vectors, and provides the first matrix generalization of the Kiefer-Wolfowitz theorem to enable this.

## Input / Output

The method takes L lists of K items each, where each item is a feature vector in R^d, along with a query budget n. It produces a precomputed distribution π* over lists (the optimal design), then after n rounds of human feedback—either absolute (noisy scalar per item) or ranking (full permutation under the Plackett-Luce model)—outputs a preference model parameter θ̂_n and ranked permutations for all L lists. The list representation matrix A_i differs by feedback type: its columns are raw feature vectors for absolute feedback but pairwise differences x_{i,j}−x_{i,k} for ranking feedback, making the design problem a constrained vector design where items in a list are always observed together.

## Method

The core contribution is a matrix Kiefer-Wolfowitz theorem (Theorem 1) that establishes the equivalence between minimizing the worst-case prediction error g(π) = max_i tr(A_i^T V_π^{-1} A_i) and maximizing log det(V_π) over the probability simplex, where V_π = Σ π(i) A_i A_i^T is a design matrix built from list-level matrices rather than individual vectors. The resulting algorithm DOPE solves this convex D-optimal design (via CVXPY or Frank-Wolfe), samples lists i.i.d. from π*, and estimates θ̂_n using OLS (absolute feedback) or MLE under the PL model solved by IRLS (ranking feedback). The design is non-adaptive and precomputable, with support size at most d(d+1)/2 independent of L. Theoretical analysis yields prediction error bounds of Õ(d²/n) for absolute feedback and Õ(K⁶d²/n) for ranking feedback, plus exponentially decreasing ranking loss in the budget n and item gaps Δ_{i,j,k}.

## Results

On the Nectar dataset (L=2000, K=5, d=100), Dope achieves the lowest ranking loss among all methods, with APO's best ranking loss at n=500 being matched by Dope at n=150 — a more than three-fold reduction in sample size. On the Anthropic dataset (L=2000, K=2, d=36), Dope again achieves the lowest ranking loss, with baselines' best at n=1000 matched by Dope at n=300, also a three-fold reduction. On the synthetic ranking feedback experiment, Dope's ranking loss at n=100 is matched by the closest baselines only at n=60, a nearly two-fold sample efficiency gain. In Table 1, Dope achieves a maximum prediction error of 15.79±1.08 and ranking loss of 0.107±0.002, compared to the optimal solution's 9.22±0.82 and 0.092±0.002.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dope consistently outperforms all four baselines (Unif, Avg-Design, Clustered-Design, and APO) on ranking loss across all four experiments (synthetic absolute, synthetic ranking, Nectar, and Anthropic). The closest competitor is APO (Das et al. [20]), which greedily minimizes maximum pairwise ranking error; on the Nectar dataset, APO requires n=500 to reach the ranking loss that Dope achieves at n=150, and on the Anthropic dataset, APO needs n=1000 versus Dope's n=300. Unif, Avg-Design, and Clustered-Design are close to each other and to APO on the synthetic experiments but all trail Dope. The Avg-Design baseline (which uses the mean feature vector per list) confirms that Dope's list-level matrix representation provides a meaningful advantage over naive vector-level designs. Clustered-Design (k-medoids) shows that simple diversity via clustering is inferior to the principled optimal design approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical Kiefer-Wolfowitz optimal design theorem [41] from feature vectors to matrices representing lists of items, enabling a unified framework for computing information-gathering policies over ranked lists. It builds directly on the optimal design machinery from Lattimore and Szepesvári [45] and borrows the Plackett-Luce MLE estimator and concentration bounds from Zhu et al. [102] for the ranking feedback setting. The closest prior work, APO (Das et al. [20]), greedily minimizes maximum pairwise ranking error for K=2 lists; this paper extends the problem to K≥2, provides a non-adaptive (precomputable) policy rather than a greedy one, and covers both absolute and ranking feedback in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-adaptive nature of the optimal design means the data-collection policy can be precomputed offline, making it practical for expensive human annotation pipelines in RLHF and search. The paper provides matching upper bounds on prediction error and ranking loss for both feedback models, but does not derive lower bounds, leaving open whether Dope is truly minimax-optimal. Key open directions include extending to the fixed-confidence setting, applying the method to actual LLM reward model training, and handling other feedback models (e.g., partial rankings, pairwise comparisons) within the same optimal design framework.
