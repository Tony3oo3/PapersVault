## Problem

Convex reformulations of two-layer ReLU networks provide global optimality guarantees, but prior algorithms (r-FISTA, exact ADMM) could only solve them on downsampled MNIST or CIFAR-10, failing to scale to high-dimensional datasets like ImageNet. Standard non-convex optimizers (SGD, Adam) offer no global convergence guarantees and require extensive learning-rate tuning, with performance highly sensitive to hyperparameter selection. The paper asks whether convex neural networks can be made practical at the scale of modern deep learning.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32×3, ImageNet at 512×512×3, Food-5k at 256×256×3) and outputs binary or multi-class labels. For language tasks, IMDb reviews are tokenized and padded to 60 words, passed through a pretrained GPT-2 (12 transformer blocks, 768-dim embeddings), and the resulting features are reshaped into batches of shape (1500×60×768) before entering CRONOS. The convex program operates on P sampled ReLU activation patterns (diagonal 0/1 matrices) rather than the full exponential set, with P typically set to m/2 where m is the number of neurons.

## Method

CRONOS reformulates two-layer ReLU network training as a linearly constrained generalized linear model with group lasso regularization, then solves it via ADMM where the u-subproblem is handled by Nyström Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (NysPCG). The key insight is that the Hessian matrix inherits approximate low-rank structure from the data matrix X, so a rank-r Nyström preconditioner (r≈20) reduces the condition number to a constant, yielding O(log(1/δ)) PCG iterations independent of the data condition number. CRONOS-AM extends this to arbitrary-depth networks via alternating minimization: CRONOS optimizes the last two layers in convex form while DAdapted-Adam (learning-rate-free) updates the earlier non-convex layers for one epoch per outer iteration. All operations exploit the diagonal-matrix structure of F and G to avoid instantiating large matrices, and JAX/XLA JIT compilation enables GPU-parallel matvecs that scale to ImageNet-sized data on a single RTX-4090.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 binary classification with a 4-layer ReLU MLP, CRONOS-AM achieves 90.5% peak validation accuracy without any learning rate tuning, outperforming DAdam (90.15%) and matching or exceeding the best-tuned AdamW (90.25%). On ImageNet binary classification, CRONOS-AM reaches 88.47%, surpassing DAdam (87.96%) and AdamW (87.46%), though trailing Shampoo (89.72%). On IMDb sentiment classification with GPT-2 fine-tuning, CRONOS achieves 93.91% validation accuracy, slightly exceeding tuned AdamW (93.69%). On Fashion-MNIST multi-class classification, CRONOS achieves 85.8%, substantially outperforming all non-convex baselines whose best results range from 71.54% to 78.62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRONOS-AM outperforms DAdam on both CIFAR-10 (90.5% vs. 90.15%) and ImageNet (88.47% vs. 87.96%), and exceeds the best-tuned AdamW on ImageNet (88.47% vs. 87.46%) while matching it on CIFAR-10 (90.5% vs. 90.25%). On ImageNet, Shampoo is the sole method that surpasses CRONOS-AM (89.72% vs. 88.47%), which the authors attribute to Shampoo's approximate second-order nature. On Fashion-MNIST multi-class classification, CRONOS achieves 85.8%, a 7+ point margin over the best non-convex baseline (Adam at 78.62%), with all stochastic optimizers showing extreme sensitivity to learning rate (ranges spanning 40+ percentage points). On IMDb sentiment classification with GPT-2, CRONOS reaches 93.91% in the fine-tuning setting, marginally beating tuned AdamW (93.69%), and in the no-training setting (GPT2-NFT) CRONOS achieves 77.66% versus AdamW's best of 73.69%. A critical practical advantage is that CRONOS-AM requires no learning rate hyperparameter, whereas all competing optimizers exhibit non-convergent behavior for poorly chosen learning rates, with validation accuracy dropping as low as 50% (chance level) on ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks established by Pilanci and Ergen (2020) and the subsequent solvers of Mishkin et al. (2022, r-FISTA) and Bai et al. (2023, exact ADMM), all of which were restricted to downsampled MNIST or CIFAR-10. The key methodological departure is combining ADMM with Nyström preconditioned conjugate gradient (from Frangella et al., 2023a) and JAX GPU acceleration to eliminate the exact linear-system bottleneck that made prior convex neural network solvers intractable at scale. CRONOS-AM further extends the two-layer convex primitive to arbitrary-depth networks via alternating minimization with DAdapted-Adam for the non-convex layers, a combination not present in prior convex neural network literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that convex reformulated neural networks can handle ImageNet-scale and large language modeling tasks, opening a path toward globally optimal training with O(1/K) convergence guarantees versus SGD's O(1/ε⁴) stationary-point guarantee. The near-zero hyperparameter sensitivity (no learning rate) is a practical advantage in expensive training pipelines. However, open questions remain: no convergence guarantee exists for CRONOS-AM on deep networks, the ImageNet experiments use binary rather than 1000-class classification, CNN results are mixed, and scaling to multi-GPU/TPU or harder NLP benchmarks is unexplored.
