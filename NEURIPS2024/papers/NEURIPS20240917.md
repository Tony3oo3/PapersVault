## Problem

Prior work claims that chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting teaches LLMs generalizable algorithmic procedures via in-context demonstrations, but it remains unclear how similar the examples must be to the query, how broadly any given CoT prompt applies, and how much human labor is required to craft effective prompts. This paper investigates whether CoT induces genuine in-context learning of procedures or merely pattern matching, using classical planning (Blocksworld) as a testbed where problem instances can be systematically scaled in difficulty.

## Input / Output

For the planning experiments, input is a natural-language rendering of a PDDL Blocksworld problem (initial state predicates, goal predicates, and action constraints), optionally preceded by 0–3 annotated CoT examples of varying specificity. Output is a sequence of PDDL actions (pick up, unstack, put down, stack) that must be verified as a valid plan using the VAL checker. For the synthetic benchmarks, inputs are natural-language questions (coin-flip parity, letter concatenation, parenthesized arithmetic) and outputs are binary yes/no answers, concatenated character strings, or single integer values.

## Method

The paper constructs a five-level hierarchy of CoT prompts ranging from the most general (zero-shot 'let's think step by step') to the most specific (lexicographic stacking with a fixed syntactic goal form), and evaluates each on Blocksworld table-to-stack problems with 3–20 blocks, thereby decoupling prompt generality from problem complexity. The same two-axis design (prompt specificity × problem size) is replicated on three scalable synthetic benchmarks—CoinFlip, LastLetterConcatenation (plus LastVowel and FoomLetter variants), and multi-step single-digit arithmetic—using the original prompts from Wei et al. (2022) and Kojima et al. (2022). Experiments are run on GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo, and Claude-3-Opus, with self-consistency (5 samples, temperature 0.7) tested as a state-of-the-art CoT extension. The key empirical finding is that CoT yields meaningful gains only when the prompt is highly specific to the exact problem subclass, and accuracy collapses as the query's stack height or step count exceeds what appears in the examples, indicating pattern matching rather than algorithmic generalization.

## Results

On 261 table-to-stack Blocksworld instances, GPT-4's zero-shot accuracy is only 3.83%, rising to 59.3% with the most specific (Stacking) CoT prompt, but dropping to 51.3% with the more general Blocksworld Universal Algorithm prompt and 6.89% with the Progression Proof prompt. On synthetic benchmarks, Zero-shot CoT lifts CoinFlip accuracy from 56.38% to 95.71% and LastLetterConcatenation from 10.00% to 52.54%, but these gains collapse as problem length increases (e.g., CoinFlip drops below 90% only at 31 steps, while LLC and Arithmetic degrade sharply past a handful of steps). Self-consistency, a state-of-the-art CoT extension, actually *reduces* accuracy on Blocksworld (e.g., GPT-4-Turbo Stacking Prompt drops from 40.6% to 24.5%), confirming that CoT gains are fragile and pattern-matching-driven rather than algorithmic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al. [26], 'let's think step by step'), the paper shows only marginal gains: on table-to-stack Blocksworld, GPT-4 improves from 3.83% to 4.98%, and GPT-4-Turbo from 19.1% to 21.0%, far below the 59.3% achieved by the most specific manual CoT prompt. Against the original Manual CoT (Wei et al. [50]), the paper replicates its prompts on CoinFlip (98.89% accuracy) and LastLetterConcatenation (51.06%), confirming the reported gains on small instances, but demonstrates that these gains do not generalize to longer sequences or minor domain modifications (LastVowelConcatenation drops to 27.00%, FoomLetterConcatenation to 26.00%). Against Self-consistency (Wang et al. [49]), the paper finds it is consistently *worse* than single-sample CoT on Blocksworld: GPT-4-Turbo's Stacking Prompt accuracy falls from 40.6% to 24.5%, and Claude-3-Opus from 24.5% to 26.4% (a slight gain only for Claude), because the large solution space produces five unique plans per query, forcing random tie-breaking. Overall, the paper's central finding is that no CoT variant generalizes beyond the narrowest problem class, and the more specific the prompt, the more human labor is required to craft it—undermining the claim that CoT teaches generalizable algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CoT prompting framework of Wei et al. [50] and the LLM planning evaluation methodology of Valmeekam et al. [47], but departs from both by systematically varying prompt granularity (from universal zero-shot CoT to highly specific lexicographic stacking) and scaling problem complexity beyond what examples demonstrate. While prior CoT work treated the demonstrated procedure as a generalizable algorithm the model 'learns in context,' this paper reframes the question as one of pattern-matching fidelity: how similar must the query be to the examples for the prompt to work? The synthetic benchmark extensions (scalable CoinFlip, LastLetterConcatenation, and arithmetic) also depart from prior static evaluations by enabling controlled difficulty scaling, addressing a gap the authors identify in the original CoT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-held assumption that CoT prompting enables in-context learning of general algorithmic procedures, arguing instead that observed improvements stem from syntactic pattern matching that collapses as problems deviate from the examples. This has direct practical implications: the performance gains from CoT come at the cost of increasingly labor-intensive, problem-specific prompt engineering, and those gains are fragile to even modest increases in problem size. Open questions include whether newer or larger models might exhibit genuine in-context algorithm learning, whether the pattern-matching explanation fully accounts for all CoT effects, and whether alternative prompting strategies (beyond the ones tested here) could achieve the generalization that CoT was claimed to provide.
