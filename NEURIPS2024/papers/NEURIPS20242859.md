## Problem

Worst-case (minimax) analysis of differentially private density estimation in Wasserstein distance yields pessimistic bounds that fail to adapt to easy instances such as concentrated or sparsely supported distributions. This paper introduces a principled notion of instance optimality for this setting—competitive with an algorithm told the distribution lies in a D_∞-ball neighborhood—and shows it is uniformly achievable up to polylogarithmic factors, closing the gap between worst-case and instance-specific performance.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution P over a metric space (R, [0,1]², or an arbitrary finite metric space) and a privacy budget ε, and outputs a distribution estimate. The guarantee is that the Wasserstein distance between the output and P is within a polylogarithmic factor of the best ε-DP algorithm that is additionally told P belongs to a specific D_∞-ball neighborhood. In the 1D case the neighborhood is restricted to a pair {P, Q} with D_∞(P,Q) ≤ ln 2, giving a stronger guarantee; in higher dimensions the neighborhood is the full D_∞-ball.

## Method

For distributions over R, the algorithm privately estimates ~εn equally spaced quantiles of the empirical distribution using a DP CDF/quantile estimation subroutine, then places 1/(εn) mass at each estimated quantile point, yielding a rate that decomposes into a non-private sampling term, an interquantile privacy term, and a tail-truncation term. For R² and general finite metric spaces, the key technical contribution is a randomized embedding into hierarchically separated trees (HSTs), which avoids the high distortion of deterministic hierarchical decompositions near cell boundaries. On the HST, the algorithm operates in two stages: it first identifies κ-active nodes (those with mass > log(n)/(εn)) and then privately estimates their weights, assigning zero mass to all other nodes before projecting back to the original metric space. Lower bounds are established via a generalized DP Assouad's lemma, constructing nets of distributions within each D_∞-neighborhood that are pairwise far in Wasserstein/TV distance yet mutually indistinguishable under n samples.

## Results

The paper proves instance-optimal differentially private density estimation rates in Wasserstein distance, matching lower bounds up to polylogarithmic factors. For 1D distributions on [0,1], the achievable rate is Õ(E[W(P,P̂_n)] + (1/εn)(q_{1−1/εn} − q_{1/εn}) + W(P, P|_{q_{1/εn}, q_{1−1/εn}})), which adapts to the distribution's concentration and support. For 2D and general finite metric spaces, the algorithm achieves a (log n)^{O(1)} (or (log|X|·log n)^{O(1)}) competitive ratio against the best algorithm given a 2× multiplicative approximation of the density. As a corollary for discrete distributions in TV distance, the rate is Σᵢ min{pᵢ(1−pᵢ), √(pᵢ(1−pᵢ)/n)} + Σᵢ min(pᵢ, 1−pᵢ, 1/(εn)), which for power-law distributions gives Õ(min{k/(εn), 1/√(εn)}) versus the worst-case Θ(k/(εn)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against worst-case minimax rates. For Wasserstein estimation over [0,1]^d, the minimax rate is Θ((εn)^{−1/d}), which suffers a curse of dimensionality; the instance-optimal rate adapts to the effective dimension of the distribution (e.g., if P lies on a k-dimensional subspace, the rate improves to Õ((εn)^{−1/k})). For discrete distributions in TV distance, the minimax rate is Θ(k/(εn)) linear in support size, while the instance-optimal rate for a power-law distribution p(i)∝i^{−2} is Õ(min{k/(εn), 1/√(εn)}), a significant improvement for large k. The paper also compares to [GHK+23] (Differentially Private Heatmaps), which achieves additive error √k/n but requires the sparsity parameter k as input and uses a deterministic hierarchical decomposition; the present work is automatically adaptive to sparsity, achieves polynomially small additive error at only logarithmic multiplicative cost, and extends to all finite metric spaces via randomized HST embeddings. In a concrete 1D experiment (Figure 1), the instance-optimal DP algorithm achieves W₁ error of 0.86 versus 13.4 for a minimax-optimal algorithm on a sparsely supported distribution, demonstrating the practical gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends worst-case Wasserstein distance density estimation (Dudley 1969; Boedihardjo et al. 2022; He et al. 2023) to the instance-optimal regime, introducing a D-infinity neighborhood notion that captures constant-factor density approximation as the relevant prior knowledge. For the high-dimensional and general metric space settings, it departs from prior practical private density estimation work (Cormode & Bharadwaj 2022; Zhang et al. 2016; Ghazi et al. 2023) that relies on deterministic hierarchical decompositions, replacing them with randomized HST embeddings (Bartal 1996; Fakcharoenphol et al. 2003) to achieve the tighter distortion needed for instance optimality. The lower bounds build on a generalized DP Assouad's lemma (Acharya et al. 2021) extended to product-of-hypercubes indexing, while the one-dimensional upper bound leverages DP quantile estimation (Kaplan et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to characterize and achieve instance-optimal rates for private density estimation in Wasserstein distance, showing that minimax-optimal algorithms can be dramatically outperformed on structured distributions (e.g., sparse or concentrated ones) and providing a principled D-infinity neighborhood definition that is arguably the right notion of instance optimality for geometric estimation problems. The HST-based approach extends naturally to arbitrary finite metric spaces, yielding new instance-optimal bounds for private TV estimation of discrete distributions that improve over worst-case rates for power-law and other structured distributions. Open questions include removing the polylogarithmic factors, extending to higher-dimensional Euclidean spaces with tighter dependence on dimension, and designing instance-optimal algorithms for other private learning tasks.
