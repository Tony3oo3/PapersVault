## Problem

The original Kernel Kullback-Leibler (KKL) divergence introduced by Bach (2022) is undefined when the source distribution's support is not contained in the target's support, mirroring the well-known limitation of standard KL divergence. This makes KKL impractical for discrete measures with disjoint supports, a common scenario in generative modeling and distribution matching. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a regularized variant and providing the missing theoretical analysis (skewness, concentration) and a tractable closed-form implementation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two finite point sets (source X^n and target Y^m) along with a Gaussian kernel bandwidth σ and a regularization parameter α ∈ (0,1). It produces a scalar divergence value computed via kernel Gram matrices K_p, K_q, and K_{p,q}, and when used as an optimization objective, outputs a transported point set obtained by iterating Wasserstein gradient descent. The closed-form expression requires an SVD of the (n+m)×(n+m) block matrix K, giving O((n+m)³) per-iteration cost, and the gradient is available analytically, enabling hyperparameter-free optimizers like L-BFGS.

## Method

The regularized KKL is defined as KKL_α(p||q) = KKL(p||(1−α)q + αp), mixing the target with the source to guarantee finiteness for all distribution pairs. The paper derives a closed-form expression (Proposition 6) writing the divergence entirely in terms of kernel Gram matrices, and a closed-form first variation (Proposition 7) involving the matrix logarithm of a block Gram matrix K and its eigendecomposition. Optimization proceeds via explicit time-discretization of the Wasserstein gradient flow, which for discrete measures reduces to standard Euclidean gradient descent on particle positions. Theoretical contributions include a monotonicity result in α, a quantitative deviation bound between regularized and unregularized KKL under a density-ratio condition, and finite-sample concentration bounds for empirical measures that extend Bach's (2022) entropy-only bound to the full cross-term.

## Results

This paper is primarily theoretical, with qualitative empirical validation rather than quantitative benchmark tables. The key theoretical contributions are: (1) a monotonicity result showing KKL_α decreases in α (Proposition 2), (2) a deviation bound quantifying |KKL_α − KKL| ≤ (α(1+1/μ) + α²/(1−α)(1+1/μ²))|Tr(Σ_p log Σ_q)| (Proposition 3), and (3) a finite-sample concentration bound of order O(log n / √(m∧n)) + O((log n)²/(m∧n)) (Proposition 4). Empirically, the KKL gradient flow with L-BFGS robustly recovers target shapes (3-rings, heart-to-spiral) with n=100 particles and σ=0.1, outperforming MMD on support-sensitive tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares KKL gradient descent against MMD (Arbel et al., 2019) and KALE (Glaser et al., 2021) on two shape-transfer tasks. On the 3-rings target (zero-volume support), KKL and KALE both keep particles closely following the ring structure, while MMD particles drift off the rings because MMD is insensitive to support differences. On the heart-to-spiral task (disjoint positive-volume supports), both KKL and KALE recover the spiral shape substantially faster than MMD, though all three methods struggle with disconnected outlier points. A key practical advantage of KKL over KALE is that both the divergence and its gradient are available in closed form via kernel Gram matrices, enabling hyperparameter-free optimization with L-BFGS, whereas KALE requires solving an inner n-dimensional convex optimization problem at each gradient step (scaling as O(n³) with Newton's method). The paper does not report quantitative metrics (e.g., final W₂ distance or MMD value), so comparisons are based on visual trajectory quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bach's (2022) kernel Kullback-Leibler (KKL) divergence, which evaluates quantum divergences on kernel covariance operators (second-order RKHS embeddings), by introducing a regularized variant that is well-defined for all distribution pairs, including those with disjoint supports. It positions KKL as a middle ground between the standard KL divergence (which is displacement-convex but intractable with sample-only targets) and MMD (which is tractable but non-convex in Wasserstein geometry and based on first-order mean embeddings). The regularization strategy parallels skewed divergences for standard KL (Lee 2000; Kimura and Hino 2021), while the closed-form optimization contrasts with KALE (Glaser et al. 2021), which requires solving an inner variational optimization at each gradient step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form expression for the regularized KKL and its gradient enables practical Wasserstein gradient descent with hyperparameter-free optimizers like L-BFGS, removing the inner-optimization bottleneck that limits KALE's scalability. The finite-sample bounds and skewness analysis provide the first complete statistical characterization of KKL for empirical measures, going beyond Bach's (2022) entropy-term-only guarantee. However, convergence guarantees for the KKL flow remain unestablished, and the O((n+m)³) cost from the SVD of the combined Gram matrix limits applicability to large-scale problems; the authors suggest random features as a promising direction for efficiency.
