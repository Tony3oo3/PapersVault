## Problem

General language-image pre-training methods like CLIP perform poorly on person-centric tasks because they operate at the instance level (treating each image-text pair independently) and perform only global cross-modal alignment, neglecting two critical person-specific characteristics: fine-grained body-part attributes that distinguish individuals, and the identity-level concordance among multiple images and descriptions of the same person.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241451/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes triplets of (grayscale person image, color person image, textual description) along with identity labels from the SYNTH-PEDES dataset (312K identities, 4.8M images, 12.1M auto-generated descriptions). The visual encoder (ResNet50/101/152 or Swin-B) and textual encoder (BERT with last 5 layers unfrozen) produce 768-dim global embeddings. The three pretext tasks jointly output a colorized image (pixel-wise regression), predicted masked attribute tokens (cross-entropy over vocabulary), and aligned cross-modal embeddings in a shared feature space, all of which are discarded after pre-training in favor of the learned backbone weights.

## Method

PLIP is a dual-branch (visual + textual) encoder trained jointly on three pretext tasks designed around person-specific structure. (1) Text-guided Image Colorization (TIC) restores a color image from its grayscale version conditioned on a full text description, using multi-modal SE-blocks to fuse the textual global embedding into visual feature channels, thereby binding color-part phrases to corresponding body regions. (2) Image-guided Attributes Prediction (IAP) performs multi-modal masked language modeling where only attribute phrases (not arbitrary words) are masked, and a self-attention fusion module combines the visual global embedding with textual hidden states to predict the masked tokens. (3) Identity-based Vision-Language Contrast (IVLC) replaces the standard instance-level InfoNCE with an identity-level contrastive objective (implemented via CMPM loss) that pulls together all image-text pairs sharing the same identity and pushes apart those from different identities. The overall loss is a weighted sum of the three tasks, and the pre-trained backbone is transferred to downstream tasks by simple replacement without architectural modification.

## Results

On text-based person Re-ID, PLIP with Swin-B achieves 75.36% R@1 and 94.87% R@10 on CUHK-PEDES and 66.17% R@1 and 88.94% R@10 on ICFG-PEDES, outperforming all compared pre-trained models by large margins (e.g., +17.3% R@1 over SOLIDER Swin-B on CUHK). On unsupervised image Re-ID, PLIP applied to ISE achieves 87.6% mAP and 95.3% R@1 on Market1501, and 46.4% mAP and 74.9% R@1 on MSMT17, setting new state-of-the-art. On person search, PLIP with Swin-B reaches 96.6% mAP on CUHK-SYSU and 90.3% R@1 on PRW. On human parsing, PLIP Swin-B achieves 63.52 mIoU on LIP and 73.93 mIoU on PASCAL-Person-Part.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLIP achieves the most dramatic gains on text-based person Re-ID, where it outperforms CLIP (RN50) by +13.2% R@1 on CUHK-PEDES (71.13 vs 57.92) and +13.6% on ICFG-PEDES (64.88 vs 51.30), and surpasses the strongest person-domain pre-trained model SOLIDER (Swin-B) by +17.3% R@1 on CUHK (75.36 vs 58.06). On image-based Re-ID, PLIP (Swin-B) achieves 93.2% mAP on Market1501, exceeding LUP-NL (RN50) by +1.3% and LUP (RN50) by +2.2%, while on MSMT17 the improvement is even more pronounced (+11.4% mAP over LUP-NL when applied to ISE). On person search, PLIP (Swin-B) leads on CUHK-SYSU (96.6% mAP vs 94.9% for SOLIDER) but trails SOLIDER on PRW mAP (56.6% vs 59.7%), though it wins on PRW R@1 (89.9% vs 86.8%). For human parsing, PLIP (Swin-B) achieves the best mIoU on both LIP (63.52) and PASCAL (73.93), surpassing LUP-NL by +1.54 and +0.81 respectively. Overall, PLIP consistently outperforms all general-domain pre-trained models (MoCov3, CLIP, BLIP) and person-domain pre-trained models (LUP, LUP-NL, SOLIDER) across the majority of evaluated tasks, with the largest margins on cross-modal text-based Re-ID.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLIP extends the CLIP-style language-image contrastive pre-training paradigm to the person domain, but departs from it in two key ways: it replaces instance-level matching with identity-level contrast (IVLC), and it adds two person-specific pretext tasks (text-guided colorization and attribute-focused masked language modeling) that explicitly target fine-grained person attributes. On the person-domain side, it builds on the LUP/LUP-NL line of large-scale person pre-training but introduces the language modality, which prior person pre-training works (LUP, LUP-NL, SOLIDER) entirely omitted. The SYNTH-PEDES dataset is constructed atop LUPerson-NL and LPW images with automatic captions generated by the proposed SPAC captioner, filling a gap left by the limited scale of manually annotated person-text datasets like CUHK-PEDES and ICFG-PEDES.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SYNTH-PEDES dataset (312K identities, 4.8M images, 12M descriptions) is the largest real-person image-text corpus to date and provides a practical resource for the community. The identity-level contrast insight is a simple but important correction: treating multiple images of the same person as negatives (as CLIP does at the instance level) is fundamentally misaligned with person-centric tasks where identity concordance is the signal. Open questions remain around explicit local cross-modal alignment (the authors acknowledge the model blends adjacent body-part colors), computational efficiency from multiple forward passes per sample, and privacy implications of pre-training on internet video data. Whether the gains transfer to truly unseen person attributes or novel camera domains beyond the tested benchmarks is not fully explored.
