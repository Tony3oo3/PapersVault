## Problem

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are constrained by the fact that the initial membrane potential (IMP) is always set to zero, which limits the diversity of firing patterns and pattern mappings a neuron can produce. Additionally, existing training losses (SDT and TET) exhibit a fundamental mismatch: TET converges slowly on static tasks because it forces all time steps to produce correct outputs despite differing membrane potential states, while SDT underperforms on neuromorphic event-based tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242942/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (fed identically at each of T time steps, e.g., 4 steps for ImageNet/CIFAR) or asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras (naturally time-varying, e.g., 10–16 steps for CIFAR10-DVS and N-Caltech101 at 48×48 or 128×128 resolution). The output is a single class label; for static tasks the paper uses only the last time step's logits (LTS), while for neuromorphic tasks it aggregates per-time-step cross-entropy losses. No additional architectural changes or inference-time overhead are introduced beyond storing the learned IMP values already allocated for membrane state.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary modifications to standard LIF/IF spiking neurons. First, a learnable IMP replaces the fixed zero initialization: each neuron's initial membrane potential is a trainable parameter initialized from Uniform(−λ, λ), which generates novel spike patterns and additional input-to-output pattern mappings, accelerating the membrane potential toward a useful state within few time steps. Second, for static tasks, a Last Time Step (LTS) post-processing discards all intermediate outputs and computes loss only on the final time step, eliminating the TET conflict where the network must produce correct predictions at every membrane potential state. Third, for neuromorphic tasks, a label-smoothed TET loss (TET-S) removes the MSE firing-level regularization from vanilla TET and instead applies label smoothing to the cross-entropy target, allowing the training loss to converge to zero without the trade-off between firing-rate control and classification accuracy. Together, these changes yield up to 4.05% improvement on ImageNet-1k and SOTA results of 87.80% on CIFAR10-DVS and 87.86% on N-Caltech101 with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On neuromorphic datasets, IMP+TET-S achieves SOTA accuracy of 87.80% on CIFAR10-DVS (48×48, T=8) and 87.86% on N-Caltech101 (128×128, T=16), surpassing the previous best by 1.9% and 1.95% respectively. On the large-scale static dataset ImageNet1k, IMP+LTS with SEW-ResNet50 reaches 71.83% top-1 accuracy, exceeding the vanilla SEW-ResNet152 (69.26%) by 2.57% while using fewer parameters (36.67M vs. 60.19M). The LTS post-processing alone improves SEW-ResNet50 by 3.46% over the baseline, and adding learnable IMP further boosts accuracy by an additional 0.59%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR10-DVS (48×48), IMP+TET-S at T=8 achieves 87.80%, outperforming PSN (85.90%) by 1.90%, TEBN (84.90%) by 2.90%, and the original TET (83.17%) by 4.63%. On N-Caltech101 (128×128), IMP+TET-S reaches 87.86%, beating STR (85.91%) by 1.95% and EventRPG (85.62%) by 2.24%. On ImageNet1k, IMP+LTS with SEW-ResNet50 (71.83%) surpasses the much larger SEW-ResNet152 (69.26%) by 2.57% and improves over the SEW-ResNet50 baseline (67.78%) by 4.05%, though it remains below Spikformer-8-512 (73.38%) which uses a transformer architecture. The LTS method alone provides consistent gains across SEW-ResNet variants (1.06–3.46%), while learnable IMP adds a further 0.59–1.15% on top. On neuromorphic tasks, TET consistently outperforms SDT (e.g., 85.6% vs. 84.3% on CIFAR10-DVS), validating the paper's recommendation to use TET-S for event-based data and LTS for static data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TET (Temporal Efficient Training) framework of Deng et al. (ICLR 2022) and the broader surrogate-gradient BPTT training paradigm for SNNs, but reframes the problem around the initial membrane potential (IMP), which prior work has uniformly set to zero as a neutral convention. Rather than modifying neuron architecture (as PLIF, GLIF, or PSN do), the authors show that the IMP is a non-trivial degree of freedom that constrains achievable spike patterns and pattern mappings, and that making it learnable is a minimal yet effective intervention. The LTS post-processing and label-smoothed TET loss (TET-S) further depart from prior training objectives by decoupling the optimization target from the regularization term that TET originally introduced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable IMP is a drop-in modification requiring no architectural changes and incurring negligible computational overhead (±1.03% execution time), making it immediately adoptable across existing SNN pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that IMP is sensitive to initialization, that LTS loses effectiveness at larger time steps, and that no unified loss function yet handles both static and neuromorphic tasks optimally. The finding that TET converges slower than SDT on static tasks—despite outperforming it on neuromorphic data—highlights an unresolved tension in SNN training objectives that remains an open question for the field.
