## Problem

While the geometry of neural network loss landscapes has been extensively studied, it remained unclear whether gradient flow trajectories in shallow ReLU networks can freely navigate the parameter space or are fundamentally constrained by the network's symmetries. This paper asks whether the rescaling invariance inherent to homogeneous activations like ReLU creates topological barriers that prevent a training trajectory from reaching the global optimum from certain initializations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241114/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-layer ReLU network architecture (input dimension d, output dimension e, l hidden neurons) and an initialization θ₀, from which it extracts the conserved quantities c_k = ‖W_k^(1)‖²_F − ‖W_k^(2)‖²_F for each hidden neuron. It produces the full Poincaré polynomial of the invariant set H(c), the 0-th Betti number (number of connected components), and a sign vector s(θ) that identifies which connected component a given parameter occupies. The key structural constraint is that the invariant set factors as a Cartesian product of per-neuron hyperquadrics, a decomposition that holds only for two-layer architectures.

## Method

The paper exploits the rescaling symmetry of homogeneous activations to show that gradient flow conserves the quantity ⟨⟨θ,θ⟩⟩_k = ‖W_k^(1)‖²_F − ‖W_k^(2)‖²_F for each hidden neuron k, confining trajectories to an invariant set H(c) that factors as the product of per-neuron quadric hypersurfaces Q(c_k). Each Q(c_k) is shown to be homeomorphic to S^{d−1}×R^e (if c_k>0), S^{e−1}×R^d (if c_k<0), or contractible (if c_k=0), and the Künneth formula then yields the Poincaré polynomial (1+x^{d−1})^{l_+}(1+x^{e−1})^{l_-}. When e=1 and c_k<0, the hyperquadric splits into two sheets, producing 2^{l_-} connected components and a genuine topological obstruction: gradient flow cannot flip the sign of the output weight of a 'pathological' neuron. The paper further shows that accounting for neuron permutation symmetries reduces the number of effective components from exponential to linear (1+l_-), and validates the obstruction empirically on a toy regression task and the breast cancer dataset.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is Corollary 1, which gives the exact 0-th Betti number (number of connected components) of the invariant set: β₀ = 2^{l₋} when d > 1 and e = 1, meaning the space fragments exponentially in the number of pathological neurons. The Poincaré polynomial (Theorem 1) is p_{H(c)}(x) = (1 + x^{d-1})^{l₊}(1 + x^{e-1})^{l₋}, encoding all Betti numbers. In the toy experiment, initializing with c = (−0.1, −0.1) and s = (+1, +1) (4 connected components) traps the trajectory in a poor local region, while c = (−0.1, +0.1) (2 components) allows the network to learn the target function. On the breast cancer dataset, increasing the number of non-pathological neurons l₊ from 0 to l monotonically decreases the average test BCE loss across 100 random initializations for each l = 2,…,9.

**Corollary 1: Number of connected components (0-th Betti number) of the invariant set H(c).**

| Condition | β₀ (connected components) |
|---|---|
| d > 1, e > 1 | 1 |
| d = 1, e > 1 | 2^{l₊} |
| d > 1, e = 1 | 2^{l₋} |
| d = 1, e = 1 | 2^{l₊ + l₋} |

where l₊ and l₋ are the numbers of neurons with c_k > 0 and c_k < 0, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper with no direct method-to-method comparisons. The empirical validation contrasts two initialization configurations on a toy regression task: a 'bad' initialization with c = (−0.1, −0.1) (all neurons pathological, 4 connected components) where the gradient descent trajectory is confined to the positive sheet of the invariant hyperquadric and fails to approximate the target function, versus a 'good' initialization with c = (−0.1, +0.1) (one non-pathological neuron, 2 components) where the network successfully learns by flipping the sign of the second neuron's output weight. The breast cancer experiment further shows that the obstruction's impact depends on the absolute number of non-pathological neurons l₊ rather than their fraction l₊/l, meaning simply adding more hidden neurons without ensuring some are non-pathological does not resolve the obstruction. The paper also notes that under Xavier and Kaiming initialization, the probability of any pathological neuron becomes negligible for large input dimension d, making the obstruction unlikely in practice for standard wide networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conservation-law literature for homogeneous-activation networks (Du et al. 2018, Liang et al. 2019, Tanaka et al. 2020, Kunin et al. 2020), which established that rescaling invariance yields conserved quantities ⟨⟨θ,θ⟩⟩_k under gradient flow. It departs from the broader loss-landscape topology literature (Freeman & Bruna 2016, Cooper 2018, Nguyen 2019, Simsek et al. 2021) by shifting focus from sublevel-set connectedness to the exact topology of the invariant set itself, computing its full Poincaré polynomial rather than bounding Betti numbers. The closest prior work is Boursier et al. (2022), which observed sign-locking of output weights for pathological neurons; this paper generalizes that observation into a complete topological classification and quantifies the obstruction via Betti numbers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a precise, initialization-dependent criterion for when gradient flow training is fundamentally incapable of reaching the global optimum, reframing a practical training failure as a topological invariant of the parameter space. The reduction from 2^{l_-} connected components to only 1+l_- effective components via permutation symmetry is a useful structural insight that tempers the worst-case picture. Key limitations confine the result to one hidden layer and idealized gradient flow; extending the product decomposition to multi-layer networks (where neurons share weights) and understanding how finite step-size or SGD breaks the conservation laws remain open. The probability analysis showing that Xavier/Kaiming initializations make obstruction vanishingly unlikely for wide networks suggests the effect is most relevant in narrow or adversarially initialized settings.
