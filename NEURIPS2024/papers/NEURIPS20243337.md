## Problem

Neural mutual information estimators based on variational lower bounds (MINE, NWJ, SMILE) suffer from either high bias or exponentially growing variance as the true MI increases, due to the partition function term. Additionally, the standard practice of using a random permutation to construct marginal samples from joint data is shown to be theoretically invalid: it introduces fixed points that bias the estimator and impose an upper bound of log(N) on the MI estimate, a limitation previously unaddressed in the discriminative MI estimation literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N paired samples (x_i, y_i) drawn from the joint distribution p_XY and produces a single scalar MI estimate computed as E_{p_XY}[log((f*)'(T̂(x,y)))] over joint samples only, requiring no separate marginal expectation at inference time. The deranged architecture reduces the per-iteration sample requirement from N(N−1) marginal pairs (joint architecture) to N deranged pairs, cutting computational complexity from Ω(N²) to Ω(N). The discriminator is a feed-forward network with two hidden layers of 256 ReLU neurons, taking concatenated (x,y) of dimension 2d as input.

## Method

f-DIME generalizes the GAN discriminator framework by maximizing the variational representation of an arbitrary f-divergence D_f(p_XY ‖ p_X p_Y), where the optimal discriminator T̂ equals f'(p_XY/(p_X p_Y)) and the MI is recovered as E[log((f*)'(T̂))], eliminating the partition function entirely and yielding lower variance than MINE/NWJ/SMILE. The paper proves that a naive random permutation of y to form marginal pairs introduces K fixed points (K=1 on average) that corrupt the value function, biasing the discriminator output to NR/(KR+N−K) and capping the estimate below log(N); a derangement (permutation with no fixed points) resolves this by guaranteeing all N shuffled pairs are genuine marginal samples. Three concrete f-divergences are instantiated—KL, GAN (Jensen-Shannon), and Hellinger distance squared—each producing a distinct estimator with different bias-variance profiles: KL-DIME has the lowest variance but higher bias at large MI, while GAN-DIME achieves the best overall bias-variance trade-off. The deranged architecture feeds a single network with N joint pairs and N deranged pairs per iteration, achieving Ω(N) complexity versus Ω(N²) for the joint architecture.

## Results

On the standard Gaussian setting (d=20, N=64), GAN-DIME achieves the best overall bias/variance trade-off, with bias of 0.26 and variance of 0.55 at MI=10, compared to SMILE (bias 0.52, variance 0.51) and CPC (bias 5.88, variance 0.00). KL-DIME attains the lowest variance across all MI levels (0.05–0.12) but suffers from the highest bias at high MI (1.44 at MI=10). The f-DIME class avoids the exponentially growing variance of MINE and NWJ (e.g., NWJ variance reaches 9.48 at MI=10 vs. 0.69 for GAN-DIME), and the deranged architecture reduces training time from ~55 min (separable) to ~15 min (random derangement) or <9 min (shift-based) at N=30k, d=20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MINE and NWJ, the f-DIME estimators dramatically reduce variance: at MI=10, NWJ variance reaches 9.48 and MINE 17.10, while GAN-DIME stays at 0.69 and KL-DIME at 0.11, confirming the theoretical result that f-DIME avoids the partition-function-driven exponential variance growth. Compared to SMILE (τ=1), GAN-DIME achieves lower bias at high MI (0.26 vs. 0.52 at MI=10) with comparable variance, and the paper shows that SMILE's training is effectively guided by the same GAN divergence, explaining their similar behavior. Against CPC, f-DIME is not upper-bounded by log(N); CPC's bias grows to 5.88 at MI=10 (N=64) while GAN-DIME remains at 0.26, though CPC does achieve the lowest variance at high MI due to its contrastive structure. Versus NJEE, f-DIME is both more accurate (lower bias at most MI levels) and far more computationally efficient, since NJEE requires training 2d−1 separate networks and fails at d>30 on the authors' hardware. Overall, GAN-DIME is identified as the best single estimator, balancing low bias, moderate variance, and robustness to changes in d and N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the variational f-divergence framework of Nguyen et al. [18] and the f-GAN training objective of Nowozin et al. [25] from generative modeling to discriminative mutual information estimation. While prior discriminative MI estimators (MINE, NWJ, SMILE) are all tied to the KL divergence and require a partition function at estimation time, f-DIME decouples the training objective (any f-divergence) from the estimation step (density ratio extraction), eliminating the partition term. The paper also departs from the standard random-permutation strategy for constructing marginal samples, proving it introduces a log(N) upper bound and proposing derangement as a correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The derangement result is a practical correction that affects all discriminative MI estimators relying on random shuffling: the standard technique produces biased, upper-bounded estimates, and the fix (derangement) is simple and O(N). The f-DIME framework offers practitioners a tunable bias/variance knob via the choice of f-divergence, with GAN-DIME recommended for general use and KL-DIME for low-MI regimes. Limitations include the restriction to three tested f-divergences, the absence of a guaranteed lower bound by default, and reduced precision at high MI values typical of discriminative approaches. Open questions include whether an optimal f-divergence exists for arbitrary data distributions and whether the derangement insight extends to other contrastive or skew-divergence estimators.
