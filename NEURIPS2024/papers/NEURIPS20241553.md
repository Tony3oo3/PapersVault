## Problem

Spatial Message Passing GNNs (MPGNNs) are fundamentally limited by their finite ℓ-hop receptive field and over-squashing, which causes exponential decay of information sensitivity between distant nodes. While spectral graph filters could in principle provide global information exchange, their parametrization, composition with spatial message passing, and extension to directed graphs remain virtually unexplored in the GNN literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241553/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} (or weighted) and node features X ∈ R^{n×d}, and produces node embeddings H ∈ R^{n×d'} usable for node- or graph-level predictions. A key implementation requirement is a partial eigendecomposition of the graph Laplacian (truncated to k eigenvectors with eigenvalue cutoff λ_cut), computed once per graph and shared across all layers. For directed graphs, the Laplacian is replaced by the Hermitian Magnetic Laplacian, requiring feature transformations to map between real and complex domains before and after the spectral convolution.

## Method

S²GNNs synergistically compose spatial message passing (spatially bounded to p_cut hops, spectrally unbounded) with spectral filters (spatially unbounded, spectrally bounded to λ_cut) in either additive or sequential form. The spectral filter is parametrized as a channelwise linear combination of translated Gaussian basis functions (Gaussian smearing) multiplied by a Tukey window to suppress Gibbs ringing, applied via the partial eigendecomposition. The paper proves this combination eliminates over-squashing (uniformly lower-bounded Jacobian sensitivity) and yields strictly tighter approximation-theoretic error bounds than purely spatial polynomial filters. Additional design-space contributions include the first neural network operating in the spectral domain (preserving permutation equivariance via sign and permutation equivariance), the first spectral filter for directed graphs via the Magnetic Laplacian, and parameter-free positional encodings derived from the partial EVD that provably exceed 1-WL expressivity.

## Results

On the peptides-func long-range benchmark, S²GCN achieves 0.7275±0.0066 AP (0.7311 with positional encodings), outperforming the best graph transformer (GPS+HDSE, 0.7156) by ~1.2 AP points while using ~35% fewer parameters. On the 30k-token associative recall sequence task, S²GCN reaches 0.97±0.05 accuracy, competitive with Hyena (1.000) and far exceeding Transformer (0.324) and H3 (0.084). On OGB Products (2.5M nodes), S²GAT improves test accuracy from 0.798 (GAT) to 0.811 and F1 from 0.347 to 0.381. On TPUGraphs (10M-node directed graphs), S²GCN achieves 63.62 Kendall tau vs. 60.25 for GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²GCN decisively outperforms all graph transformer baselines on peptides-func: GPS+HDSE (0.7156), GRIT (0.6988), Graph ViT (0.6942), G.MLPMixer (0.6921), MGT+WPE (0.6817), and TIGT (0.6679), with the largest gap over TIGT at ~6 AP points. It also beats the graph rewiring method DREw-GCN (0.7150) and both state-space-model baselines (GRED 0.7133, Graph Mamba 0.7071). Among spatial GNNs, S²GCN surpasses NBA-GIN (0.7071), GCN (0.6860), PathNN (0.6816), and CIN++ (0.6569) on peptides-func, though NBA-GIN retains a slight edge on peptides-struct (0.2424 vs. 0.2467). On the 30k-token associative recall task, S²GCN (0.97) is competitive with Hyena (1.000) but dramatically outperforms Transformer w/ FlashAttention (0.324, which also OOMs) and H3 (0.084). On large-scale benchmarks, S²GAT improves over GAT on OGB Products (0.811 vs. 0.798 test accuracy) and S²GCN beats GCN on TPUGraphs (63.62 vs. 60.25 Kendall tau), confirming scalability to millions of nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²GNNs build on the spectral graph convolution framework of Hammond et al. (2011) and the Gaussian smearing parametrization from Schütt et al. (2017), but depart fundamentally from prior spectral GNNs (Bruna et al., 2014; Stachenfeld et al., 2020) by explicitly combining spatial message passing with a spectrally parametrized filter in a single architecture, rather than treating them as alternatives. The virtual node concept from Gilmer et al. (2017) is shown to be a special case of the spectral filter, and the Magnetic Laplacian from Forman (1993) is repurposed to extend spectral filters to directed graphs—a setting where prior spectral GNNs were inapplicable due to the lack of a real eigendecomposition. Unlike graph transformers or rewiring approaches that address long-range interactions through global attention or topology modification, S²GNNs achieve global information flow through a truncated eigendecomposition, maintaining the O(nm) complexity of standard message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable elimination of over-squashing in a GNN architecture (Theorem 2) and strictly tighter approximation-theoretic error bounds than pure spatial filters (Theorem 4), giving practitioners a principled reason to adopt spectral components. Practically, S²GNNs set new SOTA on long-range graph benchmarks while remaining ~35% below the 500k parameter threshold and scaling to millions of nodes on a single 40 GB GPU, making them a viable alternative to graph transformers for large-scale graph learning. Open questions include the tendency of global models to overfit (noted in the paper's limitations), whether the fixed low-frequency band assumption generalizes to all domains, and how to handle real-valued weighted directed graphs where the Magnetic Laplacian's injectivity is not yet guaranteed.
