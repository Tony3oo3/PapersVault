## Problem

All prior work on online weighted paging assumes page weights (fetching costs) are known in advance, an assumption that fails in realistic multi-level caching architectures where a page's expected cost depends on its unknown probability of residing in a mid-level cache. This paper is the first to study online weighted paging with unknown weights, where the algorithm only observes stochastic samples of each page's cost upon eviction, and shows that the optimal O(log k) competitive ratio can still be achieved with an added bandit-style regret term.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of T page requests arriving online into a cache of k slots, and upon evicting a page it observes one independent sample from that page's unknown distribution (supported in (0,1]) whose expectation equals the page's true weight. The algorithm outputs eviction decisions and maintains a probability distribution over integral cache states (anti-caches), with the constraint that the cache never exceeds k pages. The performance guarantee is E[ON] ≤ O(log k)·OPT + Õ(√(nT)), combining a competitive ratio with an additive regret term that is tight for both the paging and multi-armed bandit components.

## Method

The algorithm has two coupled components: a deterministic fractional solver (ONF) that allocates anti-servers using optimistic lower confidence bounds (LCBs) via multiplicative updates proportional to (y_p + η)/LCB_p, and a randomized rounding scheme that maintains a probability distribution over integral cache states using pessimistic upper confidence bounds (UCBs). A key technical contribution is a sampling interface: the rounding scheme fills a queue of page samples, and the fractional algorithm pops a sample whenever the total fractional eviction of a page reaches an integer, ensuring the fractional solver remains deterministic conditional on the samples. The rounding scheme maintains two invariants—consistency (marginal anti-cache probabilities match the fractional solution) and a balanced property (the count of pages per UCB class in each integral state matches the fractional count up to rounding)—restored by a REBALANCESUBSETS procedure that transfers pages between matched anti-cache states. The analysis adapts the potential function of Bansal et al. [2010] with an additional exploration-budget term that is recharged upon receiving samples, yielding the combined competitive-plus-regret bound.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) establishes that the proposed randomized algorithm ON for Online Weighted Paging with Unknown Weights (OWP-UW) achieves E[ON(Q)] ≤ O(log k) · OPT(Q) + Õ(√(nT)) for every input Q. The O(log k) competitive ratio matches the known optimal bound for randomized weighted paging (Bansal et al., 2012), while the additive Õ(√(nT)) regret term matches the optimal multi-armed bandit regret bound. The paper further shows that OWP-UW does not admit sublinear regret without a competitive ratio term, as a simple lower bound construction yields Ω(T/k) linear regret in that case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The theoretical result is positioned against two known optimal bounds: the Ω(log k) competitive ratio lower bound for randomized online (weighted) paging (Fiat et al., 1991) and the Õ(√(nT)) regret lower bound for multi-armed bandits (Lattimore and Szepesvári, 2020). Since OWP-UW generalizes both standard OWP and MAB, both terms in the bound are individually tight. The paper also contrasts its approach with the classical Bansal-Buchbinder-Naor framework (2010, 2012), noting that the standard fractional-then-round paradigm breaks down when weights are unknown because the fractional solver needs samples that only the rounding scheme can provide, necessitating the novel sampling interface between the two components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bansal–Buchbinder–Naor framework for randomized weighted paging (which achieves the optimal O(log k) competitive ratio under known weights) to a setting where page weights are unknown and must be learned through stochastic samples. The key technical departure is a sampling interface between the fractional solver and the rounding scheme: the fractional algorithm uses optimistic lower confidence bounds (LCBs) to drive eviction decisions, while the rounding scheme uses pessimistic upper confidence bounds (UCBs) to maintain its balanced distribution over cache states. This optimistic-pessimistic split, combined with a queue-based mechanism ensuring the fractional solver always receives samples when needed, is the paper's central methodological contribution, drawing on multi-armed bandit confidence-bound techniques (Hoeffding-based) to handle the learning component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is notable because it shows the optimal O(log k) competitive ratio is preserved even when weights are unknown, with only an additive Õ(√(nT)) regret term — matching the best-known MAB regret lower bound. The authors argue this bound is tight in both components, making the solution essentially optimal for the new problem. Open questions include extending the approach to non-stationary weights (where page popularity shifts over time, as in real multi-core architectures) and adapting the optimistic-pessimistic fractional/rounding interface to other online problems with cost sampling. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the practical relevance is limited to caching and memory-hierarchy management scenarios.
