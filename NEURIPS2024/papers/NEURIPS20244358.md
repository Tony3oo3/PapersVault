## Problem

Adversarial imitation learning from observation (AILfO) methods, which use MLP discriminators to distinguish expert from agent state transitions, are brittle to train and highly sensitive to hyperparameters. The MLP discriminator maps high-dimensional inputs to a single logit, making it prone to overfitting and producing reward landscapes that drop sharply outside the expert distribution, hindering policy learning when the agent is far from expert behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244358/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state-only expert demonstrations (sequences of states without action labels) and online agent-environment interactions. It supports both vectorized state spaces (up to 59 dimensions, e.g., AdroitDoor, OpenMicrowave) and image-based observations (64×64×3 grayscale frames for CarRacing, 64×64×3 RGB for CloseDrawer). The output is a learned agent policy that produces continuous actions; the diffusion discriminator computes a scalar 'realness' score per transition using a single denoising step, which is converted into a GAIL-style reward log(1−D_φ(s,s′)) for RL-based policy optimization.

## Method

DIFO replaces the MLP discriminator in AILfO with a conditional denoising diffusion model (DDPM) that, given current state s and a binary label c∈{c_E, c_A}, predicts the noise added to a corrupted next state s′_t. The discriminator score is the sigmoid of the difference between agent-conditioned and expert-conditioned denoising losses, σ(λ_σ(L_d^A − L_d^E)), turning the diffusion model into a binary classifier trained with BCE loss on expert (positive) and agent (negative) transitions. A separate MSE loss on expert data ensures the diffusion model faithfully approximates the expert distribution (ELBO). The policy is trained with SAC or PPO using the GAIL reward log(1−D_φ(s,s′)), where D_φ is evaluated with a single denoising step for efficiency. The adversarial loop—alternating discriminator updates (BCE + MSE) and policy updates—prevents the policy from exploiting a frozen diffusion model, while the high-dimensional noise-prediction objective is inherently harder to overfit than a scalar MLP logit.

## Results

DIFO consistently outperforms or matches the best-performing baseline across all eight continuous control tasks (navigation, locomotion, manipulation, and image-based games), with notably faster convergence and lower variance in ANTMAZE, ADROITDOOR, and CARRACING. In the data-efficiency study on ANTMAZE, DIFO maintains ~70% success rate with only 50 expert trajectories while the best baseline (WAIfO) drops to ~20%, and with 25 trajectories DIFO still achieves ~20% while all other baselines fall to near zero. The learned reward function (visualized on the SINE toy task) is smoother outside the expert distribution compared to GAIfO, facilitating policy learning from far-off states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFO outperforms all eight baselines (BC, BCO, GAIfO, WAIfO, AIRLfO, DePO, IQ-Learn, OT) across the majority of tasks, with the largest margins in high-dimensional state/action spaces (ANTMAZE, ADROITDOOR) and image-based tasks (CARRACING). BC, despite having privileged access to ground-truth action labels, underperforms most LfO methods in randomized settings due to covariate shift. OT is the weakest baseline, only succeeding in environments with limited trajectory variety (ADROITDOOR, WALKER, CLOSEDRAWER) because it computes trajectory-level rather than transition-level distances. Among adversarial baselines, WAIfO is the strongest competitor but degrades sharply with reduced expert data (from ~75% to ~20% success on ANTMAZE when going from 200 to 50 trajectories), while DIFO degrades only modestly (~80% to ~70%). DIFO's own ablation variants (DIFO-NA without adversarial training, DIFO-Uncond without state conditioning) perform substantially worse, confirming that both the adversarial loop and the conditional diffusion architecture are necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFO extends the adversarial imitation learning from observation (AILfO) framework—exemplified by GAIfO, WAIfO, and AIRLfO—by replacing the standard MLP discriminator with a conditional diffusion model that generates next states given the current state. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the diffusion denoising objective as a binary classification task: the difference between expert-conditioned and agent-conditioned denoising losses (via a sigmoid) serves as a 'realness' score, drawing on the observation that denoising loss approximates the ELBO of the data likelihood (Li et al., 2023). This positions DIFO at the intersection of diffusion-based generative modeling and GAN-style adversarial training, but unlike prior diffusion-for-imitation work (Diffusion Policy, Diffusion-RAIL), it operates entirely in state space without requiring action labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIFO addresses a practical pain point in AILfO—training brittleness and hyperparameter sensitivity—by leveraging the diffusion model's capacity to model high-dimensional noise patterns, which is inherently harder to overfit than an MLP mapping to a single logit. The demonstrated data efficiency (maintaining ~20% success rate with only 25 expert trajectories in AntMaze while all baselines collapse) is practically significant for real-world deployment where collecting demonstrations is costly. Key limitations remain: the method assumes identical state spaces for agent and expert (limiting cross-embodiment), relies heavily on expert demonstration quality, and may not integrate well with accessible environmental rewards. Open questions include whether the diffusion discriminator can be extended to handle delayed action effects or heterogeneous embodiments.
