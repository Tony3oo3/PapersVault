## Problem

Existing online batch selection methods for LLM training either depend on computationally expensive reference models or rely on simple heuristics (e.g., high loss, large gradient norm) that fail to capture true data informativeness and can even underperform uniform sampling. There is no principled, scalable method that dynamically selects the most informative training subsets at each iteration while maintaining the runtime of regular training.

## Input / Output

At each training iteration, GREATS consumes a sampled training batch (size 4–32), a small set of validation points (2–16 examples from the target domain), and the current model parameters. It outputs a selected subset (50% of the batch) used for the gradient update, producing updated model parameters. Unusually, the validation data is appended to the training batch during backpropagation so that all required gradient inner-products are computed in a single forward-backward pass, and no model-sized per-sample gradient vectors are ever materialized.

## Method

GREATS formulates online batch selection as maximizing a set utility function (validation loss reduction after a one-step update) and applies a greedy algorithm that iteratively picks the example with the largest marginal gain. The key approximation replaces the expensive utility evaluation with a first-order Taylor expansion, reducing the marginal gain to a gradient inner-product between the training point and the validation point, minus a Hessian-weighted correction term that penalizes redundancy with already-selected points. To make this tractable at scale, the paper introduces the 'ghost inner-product' technique (inspired by differential privacy's ghost clipping), which decomposes per-sample gradients through the chain rule at each layer so that all pairwise gradient inner-products are computed from layer-level activations and output gradients without instantiating any full gradient vector. Finally, a book-keeping trick reuses the output gradients from the selection backpropagation to perform the actual parameter update, merging batch selection and gradient descent into a single pass and achieving throughput within ~6% of regular training.

## Results

On MMLU (9 subjects), GREATS achieves 54.2% average accuracy, outperforming Regular training (50.4%), MaxLoss (50.8%), and GradNorm (50.4%) by at least 3.4 percentage points. On TYDIQA, GREATS reaches 55.0% F1, surpassing Regular (54.3%), MaxLoss (54.7%), and GradNorm (53.4%). In terms of convergence speed, GREATS consistently achieves faster reduction in test perplexity across all four model-dataset configurations (LLaMA-2/MMLU, Mistral/TYDIQA, LLaMA-3/SAMSum, GPT-Small/OpenWebText). With the ghost inner-product technique, GREATS achieves a throughput of 71.3 tokens/sec versus 76.2 for regular training (only ~6% overhead), while the direct per-sample gradient implementation is ~18× slower (4.2 tokens/sec).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GREATS consistently outperforms all four baselines across both evaluation benchmarks. Against GradNorm (gradient-norm heuristic, [23]), GREATS gains +3.8 pp on MMLU average (54.2% vs 50.4%) and +1.6 pp on TYDIQA (55.0% vs 53.4%), indicating that simply selecting high-gradient-norm samples is insufficient and can even select corrupted data. Against MaxLoss (high-loss heuristic, [28]), GREATS improves MMLU by +3.4 pp and TYDIQA by +0.3 pp; MaxLoss is the closest heuristic baseline but still lags significantly on MMLU. Against the reference-model-based RHO-Loss [30] and the SBERT semantic-similarity method [35], GREATS achieves faster perplexity reduction throughout training (Figures 2–4) without requiring an additional reference model or pre-computed embeddings. In the pretraining setting (GPT-Small on OpenWebText), GREATS still improves test perplexity over regular training, though the gain is smaller since validation and training data share the same distribution. On runtime, GREATS with ghost inner-product (71.3 tok/s) is nearly as fast as regular training (76.2 tok/s), whereas the direct per-sample gradient implementations of both GREATS (4.2 tok/s) and GradNorm (6.8 tok/s) are 11–18× slower, confirming the practical feasibility of the proposed approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

GREATS builds on the TracIN influence-function framework [33] for the first-order importance score and repurposes the 'ghost clipping' technique from differential privacy [5, 6] into a 'ghost inner-product' method for computing pairwise gradient inner-products without materializing model-sized vectors. It departs from the dominant 'scoring and top-k paradigm' in online batch selection (MaxLoss [28], GradNorm [23], RHO-Loss [30]) by formulating selection as a submodular set-utility optimization and applying a greedy algorithm with Taylor-expansion approximations, thereby capturing interactions among selected points rather than treating each sample's value independently. The book-keeping trick for merging selection and gradient update in a single backpropagation is adapted from [6].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GREATS provides the first principled (non-heuristic) online batch selection method that runs at near-regular-training speed (71.3 vs. 76.2 throughput), making adaptive data selection practical for large-scale LLM fine-tuning and pretraining. The ghost inner-product technique is of independent interest beyond data selection, as gradient inner-products arise in influence functions, meta-learning, and differential privacy. Key open questions include extending the method to Adam-family optimizers (currently SGD-only), developing a validation-free variant, and directly optimizing downstream task performance rather than perplexity. The moderate but consistent gains (~3.4% on MMLU) and robustness to as few as 2 validation points suggest the approach is reliable, though the pretraining gains are marginal when validation and training data share the same distribution.
