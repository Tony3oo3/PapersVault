## Problem

Softmax gating in mixture-of-experts models introduces unnecessary competition among experts, leading to representation collapse, and sigmoid gating has been proposed as an empirically superior alternative. However, no rigorous theoretical analysis existed to explain why sigmoid gating is better or to characterize the convergence rates of expert estimation under sigmoid gating, leaving the choice of gating function without statistical justification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. regression samples (X_i, Y_i) with X_i ∈ R^d drawn from a distribution μ and Y_i = f_{G_*}(X_i) + ε_i where ε_i is Gaussian noise, and the true regression function is a sigmoid-gated MoE with k_* experts of parametric form h(x, η). The output is the least squares estimator Ĝ_n of the mixing measure (fitted with k > k_* experts in the over-specified setting), from which expert parameters (β_{0i}, β_{1i}, η_i) are recovered. The primary deliverable is convergence rates for expert estimation under two regimes of over-specification, compared against the corresponding softmax gating rates from prior work.

## Method

The paper analyzes least squares estimation in an over-specified sigmoid-gated MoE by identifying two regimes: Regime 1 where all over-specified gating parameters β_{1i}^* equal 0_d, and Regime 2 where at least one is nonzero. Under Regime 1, a strong identifiability condition (linear independence of second-order Taylor derivatives of the product σ(x,β_1,β_0)·h(x,η)) yields polynomial rates O_P(n^{-1/4}) for ReLU/GELU experts via a Voronoi loss, while input-independent or polynomial experts suffer O_P(1/log n) rates due to parameter interactions expressed as PDEs. Under Regime 2, a weaker identifiability condition (first-order derivative independence) suffices, yielding parametric O_P(n^{-1/2}) rates for both ReLU/GELU and polynomial experts—substantially faster than the O_P(n^{-1/4}) and O_P(1/log n) rates under softmax gating. The key mechanism is that sigmoid gating decouples expert weights, eliminating the multiplicative parameter interactions that plague softmax gating and enabling faster identification of individual expert parameters.

## Results

Under Regime 2 (where over-specified gating parameters are non-zero), sigmoid gating achieves expert estimation rates of O_P(n^{-1/2}) for ReLU/GELU experts and polynomial experts, compared to O_P(n^{-1/4}) and O_P(1/log(n)) respectively for softmax gating. Under Regime 1 (all over-specified parameters zero), both gating functions yield identical rates: O_P(n^{-1/4}) for ReLU/GELU experts and O_P(1/log(n)) for polynomial and input-independent experts. Empirically, the Voronoi loss for sigmoid gating converges at O(n^{-0.51}) for ReLU experts and O(n^{-0.40}) for linear experts, versus O(n^{-0.24}) and O(n^{-0.11}) for softmax gating on the same data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is softmax gating MoE analyzed in Nguyen et al. [26] (ICML 2024), which studies the same regression framework with least squares estimation. Under Regime 1, sigmoid and softmax gating produce identical expert estimation rates across all expert types, so neither method has an advantage. Under Regime 2, sigmoid gating strictly dominates: for ReLU/GELU experts it achieves the parametric rate O_P(n^{-1/2}) versus O_P(n^{-1/4}) for softmax, and for polynomial experts it achieves O_P(n^{-1/2}) versus the sub-polynomial O_P(1/log(n)) for softmax. The empirical experiments confirm this gap: sigmoid gating's Voronoi loss decays at O(n^{-0.51}) for ReLU experts and O(n^{-0.40}) for linear experts, while softmax gating decays at O(n^{-0.24}) and O(n^{-0.11}) respectively on identical synthetic data. The paper concludes that sigmoid gating is more sample efficient because Regime 2 (input-dependent gating) is the practically relevant setting, and sigmoid gating is also compatible with a broader class of expert functions (including polynomial activations) at fast rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regression-based expert estimation framework of Nguyen et al. [26], which analyzed softmax gating MoE, to the sigmoid gating setting proposed by Csordas et al. [4] as a remedy for representation collapse. The analytical toolkit builds on the Voronoi loss methodology from Manole and Ho [21] and the convergence rate analyses for Gaussian MoE by Ho et al. [14] and Nguyen et al. [27], but requires fundamentally different identifiability conditions (strong vs. weak) and a two-regime decomposition of over-specified gating parameters that has no analogue in the softmax case. The key departure from [26] is that sigmoid gating eliminates the normalization constraint among expert weights, which changes the algebraic structure of parameter interactions and yields faster rates for polynomial and ReLU/GELU experts under Regime 2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that sigmoid gating is provably more sample efficient than softmax gating—particularly the dramatic improvement for polynomial experts from O_P(1/log(n)) to O_P(n^{-1/2}) under Regime 2—provides a theoretical justification for the empirical preference for sigmoid gating observed in practice. This is practically relevant as MoE architectures scale to increasingly large language and vision models where sample efficiency directly impacts training cost. Key limitations include the assumption of a parametric ground-truth MoE (rather than an arbitrary regression function), pointwise rather than uniform rates, and the fact that Regime 2, where the advantage is most pronounced, involves model misspecification. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-parametric settings and developing uniform rate guarantees for sigmoid gating MoE.
