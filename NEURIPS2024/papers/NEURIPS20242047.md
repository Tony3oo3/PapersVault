## Problem

The sample complexity of full-batch Gradient Descent in stochastic convex optimization was an open problem: prior lower bounds only established logarithmic or square-root dimension dependence, while dimension-independent upper bounds required non-standard hyperparameters that made running time quadratic in sample size. This paper resolves the gap by proving that linear dimension dependence is necessary, showing that GD with standard hyperparameters has no generalization advantage over worst-case empirical risk minimizers.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a convex L-Lipschitz function f(w,z) over the unit ball in R^d, given a finite i.i.d. sample S of m examples from an unknown distribution D, with algorithm hyperparameters η (learning rate) and T (iterations). The algorithm outputs the averaged iterate w_S^GD, and the paper's result is a lower bound on the generalization gap F(w_S^GD) − min_w F(w) that scales as min{d/m, 1} · min{η√min{⌊d³/136⌋, T}, 1}. Crucially, the bound holds for any first-order oracle (not a specific subgradient choice) and any suffix-averaging scheme, including the last iterate.

## Method

The proof constructs a distribution D and a 4-Lipschitz convex function f(w,z) = g(w,z) + αN(w) + h(w,z), where g is Feldman's function (yielding the d/m overfitting term), N is a modified Nemirovski function operating on O(T^{1/3}) blocks of coordinates (yielding the η√T stability term even when T ≈ d³, which is the key innovation over prior work), and h is a strongly convex quadratic in an extra coordinate that encodes the sample via a hash-like mapping. The central technical challenge is that the natural subgradient oracle depends on the full sample; the paper overcomes this with a reduction (Lemma 9) that constructs a standard first-order oracle inducing the identical GD trajectory by reading the sample from coordinate d+1. A convex interpolation lemma (Lemma 7) then guarantees the final function is differentiable at all visited points, ensuring the lower bound holds for any first-order oracle rather than just a particular subgradient selection.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that for any first-order oracle, the generalization gap of full-batch GD is at least Ω(min{d/(1032m), 1} · min{η√(min{⌊d³/136⌋, T}), 1}), with probability 1/2. Under standard hyperparameters η = Θ(1/√T) and T = O(m^1.5), Corollary 3 yields a sample complexity of Ω(d/m + 1/√m), which matches the known upper bound for worst-case ERMs (Carmon et al. [11]) up to logarithmic factors. This resolves the open problem of whether GD's dimension dependence is linear (as opposed to the previously known √d lower bound).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no experimental baselines, but it provides a clear progression of theoretical lower bounds on GD's dimension dependence. Amir, Koren, and Livni [3] (COLT 2021) first showed a logarithmic dependence Ω(log d) on the dimension for GD's sample complexity. Schliserman, Sherman, and Koren [20] (2024) improved this to Ω(√d). The present paper closes the gap by proving a linear Ω(d) dependence, matching the worst-case ERM lower bound of Feldman [12]. On the upper bound side, Bassily et al. [6] (NeurIPS 2020) showed dimension-independent generalization for GD, but only under non-standard hyperparameters (quadratic number of rounds in sample size). Under standard hyperparameters, this paper shows GD achieves no better than the worst-case ERM rate of Carmon, Livni, and Yehudayoff [11], meaning GD has no algorithmic advantage over naive empirical risk minimization in the non-smooth SCO setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in Stochastic Convex Optimization: (1) lower bounds showing that GD can overfit with dimension-dependent sample size, progressing from logarithmic dependence (Amir, Koren, Livni 2021) to square-root dependence (Schliserman, Sherman, Koren 2024), and (2) upper bounds from stability analysis (Bassily et al. 2020) and ERM theory (Carmon, Livni, Yehudayoff 2023; Feldman 2016). The key technical departure is replacing the Nemirovski function with a block-coordinate variant that achieves a d³ iteration budget, combined with a convex interpolation argument (Lemma 7) to eliminate dependence on a sample-dependent subgradient oracle. The construction builds on Feldman's overfitting function [12] rather than the earlier Shalev-Shwartz et al. [22] construction, which is what enables the jump from logarithmic to linear dimension dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a central open problem by showing that full-batch GD in the non-smooth SCO setting has sample complexity Θ̃(d/m + 1/√m), exactly matching the worst-case ERM — meaning the optimization procedure provides no generalization advantage over simply minimizing the empirical risk. This sharpens the understanding of *which* algorithms avoid overfitting in overparameterized regimes (SGD, stable-GD, regularized ERM do; full-batch GD does not). Several open questions remain: whether stability in low dimension (d < m) can yield improved bounds, whether there exist hyperparameter regimes where GD is unstable yet still generalizes, and what happens under smoothness assumptions when ηT/m = Ω(1). For practitioners, the result reinforces that the choice between full-batch GD and SGD is not merely computational but fundamentally affects generalization in the non-smooth regime.
