## Problem

Existing non-asymptotic analyses of distributed SGD under Markovian sampling characterize convergence through the worst-performing agent's mixing rate, masking how individual agents' sampling strategies collectively shape system performance. This paper asks whether an asymptotic (CLT) analysis can isolate each agent's contribution to convergence and whether a small subset of agents adopting highly efficient sampling can outperform the majority using only moderately improved strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242315/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent i holds a local dataset X_i (potentially non-iid) and samples via its own ergodic Markov chain (i.i.d., single shuffling, SRW, NBRW, or SRRW), while agents communicate through a doubly-stochastic matrix W_n encoding DSGD, LSGD-FP/PP/RC, HLSGD, or DFL topologies with optionally increasing communication intervals K_l. The algorithm produces the consensus parameter θ_n = (1/N)Σθ_n^i converging a.s. to θ*, with the CLT specifying γ_n^{-1/2}(θ_n − θ*) → N(0, V) where V = (1/N²)ΣV_i and each V_i is governed by agent i's sampling strategy; the trace of V determines the asymptotic MSE.

## Method

The paper derives a CLT for UD-SGD under heterogeneous Markovian sampling and general communication patterns by reformulating the two-step local-update/aggregation iteration as a stochastic approximation recursion and decomposing the Markovian noise via the Poisson equation into a martingale-difference term plus bias terms that vanish asymptotically. The limiting covariance matrix takes the closed form V = ∫₀^∞ e^{Mt} U e^{Mt} dt with U = (1/N²)ΣU_i, where U_i is the asymptotic covariance matrix of agent i's Markov chain applied to ∇F_i(θ*,·), yielding linear speedup (V = V̄/N), asymptotic network independence, and the key Corollary 3.4: if any single agent improves its sampling (U_i^X ≽ U_i^Y in Loewner order), the overall V decreases. Empirically, the authors validate these results on L2-regularized logistic regression (ijcnn1, 100 agents) and CIFAR-10 image classification (ResNet-18, 10 agents), showing that 10 agents using SRRW achieve smaller MSE than 50 agents using NBRW, and that four distinct communication patterns (Centralized SGD, LSGD-FP, DSGD-VT, DFL) converge to the same asymptotic regime.

## Results

The paper establishes a CLT for UD-SGD showing γ_n^{-1/2}(θ_n − θ*) → N(0, V) where V = (1/N²)Σᵢ Vᵢ, yielding linear speedup (V scales as 1/N) and asymptotic network independence under Markovian sampling. Empirically on ijcnn1 (100 agents, binary classification), SRRW sampling achieves significantly smaller asymptotic MSE than NBRW, which in turn outperforms SRW. A striking finding: only 10 out of 50 agents upgrading from SRW to SRRW already produces smaller MSE than all 50 agents using NBRW. On CIFAR-10 (10 agents, ResNet-18), SRRW similarly outperforms NBRW and SRW for image classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical framework (CLT with per-agent limiting covariance) directly challenges the worst-agent paradigm used in prior non-asymptotic analyses. Compared to NBRW [Lee et al., 48], SRRW [Doshi et al., 25] achieves substantially smaller asymptotic MSE in all experimental settings (Figures 2a, 2c, 2e, 2f), with the efficiency ordering SRRW ≫ NBRW > SRW. In the partial-adoption experiment (Figure 2c), just 10 agents using SRRW outperform all 50 agents using NBRW, and 20 SRRW agents widen the gap further—demonstrating that a minority of highly efficient agents can dominate system performance. For the asymptotic network independence comparison (Figure 2d), Centralized SGD, LSGD-FP [Woodworth et al., 76], DSGD-VT [Koloskova et al., 43], and DFL [Lalitha et al., 46] all converge to the same asymptotic MSE around 1000 iterations, confirming that communication topology becomes irrelevant in the CLT regime. The paper's key advantage over all listed baselines is that it differentiates individual agent behavior (D.A.B. = ✓) while simultaneously proving linear speedup and asymptotic network independence under Markovian sampling—a combination no prior work achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends three prior lines of work: (1) asymptotic CLT analyses of distributed SGD under i.i.d. sampling (Morral et al. [58], Koloskova et al. [43], Li et al. [51]), (2) single-agent CLT with Markovian noise via the Poisson equation (Fort [29], Chen et al. [17], Hu et al. [38]), and (3) non-asymptotic convergence bounds for DSGD/LSGD under Markovian sampling (Wai [71], Zeng et al. [80], Khodadadian et al. [42]). The key departure is from the non-asymptotic 'worst-performing agent' paradigm, where bounds depend on the maximum mixing rate across all agents, to an asymptotic CLT where each agent's sampling strategy contributes independently and equally to the limiting covariance matrix. The paper also extends the increasing-communication-interval analysis of Li et al. [51] from i.i.d. to Markovian sampling, requiring a different step-size schedule (γ_n = 1/n rather than 1/n^a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a small subset of agents adopting highly efficient sampling (e.g., SRRW) can outperform all agents using moderately improved strategies (e.g., NBRW) directly challenges the dominant 'worst-agent-bottleneck' narrative in distributed learning and has practical implications for large-scale FL where not all agents can be upgraded simultaneously. The asymptotic framework also confirms linear speedup and asymptotic network independence under Markovian sampling, extending results previously known only for i.i.d. settings. Open questions include developing fine-grained finite-time bounds that individually characterize each agent's contribution, theoretically analyzing SRRW within the UD-SGD framework, and resolving the stability assumption (Assumption 2.4) which remains unverified for UD-SGD. The analysis is asymptotic, so practitioners working in the finite-time regime may need complementary non-asymptotic results to fully leverage these insights.
