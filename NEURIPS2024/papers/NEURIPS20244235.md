## Problem

Diffusion transformers require full model evaluation at every denoising step, making inference slow and expensive. While cache-based acceleration methods exist for U-Net architectures, they cannot be directly applied to transformer-based diffusion models due to their fundamentally different structure. The paper asks whether and how layer-level redundancy across timesteps can be exploited in diffusion transformers to skip computation without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244235/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion transformers (DiT-XL/2, DiT-L/2, U-ViT-H/2) and takes a noisy latent image plus a class label as input, producing a generated RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The only learned component is a small router vector β (e.g., 560 parameters for DiT-XL/2 with 20 steps) that is time-variant but input-invariant, meaning the same layer-caching schedule applies to all inputs at a given timestep. Caching is applied every two steps: one full inference step followed by one cached step where selected layers reuse outputs from the previous step.

## Method

Learning-to-Cache (L2C) formulates layer selection as a differentiable optimization problem by interpolating between two extremes: a fast configuration where all layers are cached (reusing previous-step outputs) and a slow configuration where all layers are fully computed. For each transformer layer, a continuous router coefficient β_i controls the blend between the current-step computation f(h_i^m) and the cached previous-step computation f(h_i^s), with β_i=0 meaning the layer is fully cached. The router is optimized via a Lagrangian objective that minimizes the L2 approximation error between the cached and full model outputs while penalizing the number of active layers, requiring no updates to the diffusion model parameters. At inference, β is discretized through a threshold to produce a static computation graph. The learned patterns reveal architecture-specific sparsity: U-ViT caches up to 93.68% of layers in cache steps (middle layers are highly redundant), while DiT-XL/2 caches about 47.43%, with distinct patterns between attention and feedforward blocks.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (256×256, ImageNet), L2C at 50 NFE achieves FID 2.27 (vs. DDIM 2.26) with a 1.30× speedup, and at 20 NFE achieves FID 3.46 (vs. DDIM 3.48) with 1.27× speedup, matching unaccelerated quality while reducing latency. On U-ViT-H/2 (256×256), L2C at 50 NFE achieves FID 2.3625, slightly *better* than DPM-Solver-2 (2.3728), with a 1.74× speedup. The maximum cacheable layer ratio reaches 93.68% for U-ViT-H/2 and 47.43% for DiT-XL/2 with less than 0.01 FID degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM (Song et al., 2020), L2C matches or slightly exceeds quality at the same NFE while delivering 1.10–1.30× speedup: at 50 NFE on DiT-XL/2, FID is 2.27 vs. 2.26, and at 20 NFE, FID is 3.46 vs. 3.48. Against DPM-Solver-2 (Lu et al., 2022) on U-ViT-H/2, L2C at 50 NFE achieves a marginally better FID (2.3625 vs. 2.3728) with 1.74× speedup, and at 20 NFE achieves FID 2.5809 vs. 2.5739 with 1.35× speedup. Against DeepCache (Ma et al., 2023) on U-ViT at 20 NFE, L2C improves FID from 2.70 to 2.64 at a comparable 1.67× speedup. Against Faster Diffusion (Li et al., 2023) on U-ViT at 20 NFE, L2C substantially improves FID from 2.82 to 2.57 at a similar 1.30× speedup. DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) serves only as the unaccelerated reference (250 NFE, FID 2.27); L2C at 50 NFE matches this quality with a 6.6× reduction in latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-timestep caching paradigm from U-Net-based diffusion models (DeepCache, Faster Diffusion, Cache Me If You Can) to transformer-based diffusion models, where the fundamental unit of caching shifts from U-Net feature maps to individual transformer layers. It departs from prior transformer compression work (layer pruning, structured dropout) by showing that static layer removal is infeasible for diffusion transformers; instead, the redundancy is temporal—layers at the same depth produce highly similar outputs across adjacent timesteps. The key methodological contribution is formulating layer selection as a differentiable optimization problem via interpolation between a 'fully cached' and 'fully computed' endpoint, enabling gradient-based learning of a timestep-variant, input-invariant router without updating any diffusion model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that up to 93.68% of U-ViT-H/2 layers are cacheable with <0.01 FID degradation reveals a previously unexploited form of redundancy in diffusion transformers, suggesting that current architectures may be over-parameterized in a temporal sense. The method is highly practical since it requires training only a small router (e.g., 560 parameters for DiT-XL/2 with 20 steps) and leaves the diffusion model frozen. However, the 2× speedup cap (one full step + one cached step per pair), the strong architecture dependence (DiT-XL/2 only achieves ~47% cacheability), and the requirement for a per-model training phase limit broader applicability. Open questions include whether the caching window can be extended beyond two steps, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D diffusion transformers, and whether architectural co-design could further amplify the temporal redundancy that this method exploits.
