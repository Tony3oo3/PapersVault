## Problem

Conditional Flow Matching constructs probability paths using straight-line interpolants designed under Euclidean geometry, which can lie outside the data manifold and fail to capture nonlinear dynamics. This is particularly restrictive for trajectory inference tasks such as single-cell RNA-seq, where the true dynamics are supported on a curved low-dimensional manifold and straight paths produce reconstructions with high uncertainty in regions devoid of data.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired samples (x_0, x_1) from source and target marginals along with a dataset D used to construct a task-agnostic diagonal Riemannian metric (LAND in low dimensions, RBF in high dimensions). It produces a learned vector field v_θ whose flow generates the probability path, yielding reconstructed intermediate marginals at arbitrary time t. For the image translation task, the method operates in the Stable Diffusion v1 VAE latent space and outputs translated images; for single-cell data, it operates on 5- or 100-dimensional PCA representations and outputs Wasserstein distances to held-out timepoints.

## Method

MFM introduces a two-stage simulation-free training procedure. In stage one, a neural network φ_{t,η} parameterizes nonlinear corrections to straight interpolants via x_{t,η} = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1 + t(1-t)φ_{t,η}(x_0,x_1), and η is trained by minimizing the geodesic kinetic energy E[(ẋ_{t,η})^T G(x_{t,η};D) ẋ_{t,η}], where G is a data-dependent diagonal metric (LAND or RBF) that assigns lower cost near the data support. In stage two, the vector field v_{t,θ} is regressed using the standard CFM objective but evaluated at the learned geodesic interpolants x_{t,η*} with the Riemannian norm ‖·‖_g. The OT-MFM variant further uses the 2-Wasserstein optimal transport plan as the coupling q. Unlike Riemannian Flow Matching, MFM avoids simulating second-order Euler-Lagrange equations for exact geodesics, instead approximating them through the parameterized interpolants trained on first-order time derivatives alone.

## Results

On the primary single-cell trajectory inference task (5-dim PCA, leave-one-out Wasserstein-1), OT-MFM_LAND achieves the best scores across all three datasets: 0.724 on CITE-seq, 0.713 on Embryoid Body, and 0.890 on Multiome. On the 100-dim PCA setting, OT-MFM_RBF similarly leads with 41.78 on CITE-seq and 50.91 on Multiome. On the synthetic Arch task, OT-MFM_LAND reduces the EMD from 0.608 (OT-CFM) to 0.081, and on AFHQ unpaired image translation, OT-MFM_RBF improves FID from 41.42 to 37.87 and LPIPS from 0.512 to 0.502 over the Euclidean OT-CFM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OT-MFM_LAND outperforms the direct Euclidean counterpart OT-CFM by a substantial margin on all three single-cell datasets (e.g., 0.724 vs. 0.882 on Cite, 0.713 vs. 0.790 on EB, 0.890 vs. 0.937 on Multi), confirming that the Riemannian metric learning provides meaningful gains beyond optimal transport coupling alone. Against the strongest competing baselines, OT-MFM_LAND beats WLF-UOT (0.733/0.738/0.911) and WLF-OT (0.802/0.742/0.949) on all three datasets, as well as SF²M-Exact (0.920/0.793/0.933), which adds stochasticity as a bias. The Schrödinger Bridge baselines DSB (0.953/0.862/1.079) and DSBM (1.705/1.775/1.873) are consistently outperformed, with DSBM being the weakest overall. On the synthetic Arch task, OT-MFM_LAND achieves an EMD of 0.081 versus 0.608 for OT-CFM, a ~7.5× improvement, and on AFHQ image translation, OT-MFM_RBF reduces FID by ~3.5 points (37.87 vs. 41.42) and LPIPS by 0.01 over OT-CFM. Notably, even the independent-coupling variant I-MFM_LAND (without OT) outperforms OT-CFM on EB (0.822 vs. 0.790 is a loss, but on Cite 0.916 vs. 0.882 is also a loss), showing that the metric learning benefit is most pronounced when combined with OT coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) by replacing its straight-line interpolants with geodesic approximations under a data-dependent Riemannian metric on the ambient space. It departs from Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM, Chen & Lipman 2023) in two key ways: the metric is learned from data rather than assumed, and interpolants are trained to approximate geodesics without requiring expensive simulation of Euler-Lagrange equations. The metric family (LAND, RBF) is borrowed from the metric-learning literature (Arvanitidis et al. 2016, 2021) but repurposed here as a geometric regularizer for flow matching, and the OT coupling follows Tong et al. 2023. The paper also positions itself against energy-minimizing interpolant methods (Shaul et al. 2023, GSBM/Liu et al. 2024) by showing the geodesic loss subsumes kinetic energy plus a data-dependent potential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a simulation-free, task-agnostic recipe for making flow matching respect the data manifold, which is directly relevant to trajectory inference in single-cell genomics where the manifold hypothesis is well-supported. Achieving SOTA on single-cell trajectory prediction without adding stochasticity or mass teleportation suggests that geometric structure alone can capture much of the dynamics. Open questions include whether task-specific biases can be encoded into the metric, how the approach extends to non-Euclidean ambient spaces, and whether the geodesic-interpolant principle transfers to score-based/diffusion models. The reliance on OT coupling also inherits known scalability limitations in high dimensions.
