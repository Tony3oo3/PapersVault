## Problem

Transformer-based local feature matching methods (e.g., LoFTR) achieve superior accuracy over CNN-based approaches but suffer from quadratic computational complexity in the number of image tokens, making them too slow for latency-sensitive applications like tracking and large-scale mapping. The core gap is how to retain transformer-level matching quality while drastically reducing the number of tokens processed and the attention operations performed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images at identical resolution (e.g., 640×480) and produces approximately 4800 semi-dense point correspondences with per-match confidence scores. Internally, the pipeline operates at three resolutions: H/32×W/32 for coarse homography estimation, H/8×W/8 for segmentation-based hypothesis re-selection, and H/2×W/2 for sub-pixel refinement. The output coordinates are (P_j^s, P_j^{t*}) pairs on the source and target images, where the target coordinate is refined from the homography-projected position.

## Method

ETO replaces per-unit bipartite matching with multiple homography hypotheses: each unit on a low-resolution (H/32) feature map predicts a local homography (rotation, scale, perspective, translation) covering a 3×3 patch region, reducing transformer tokens from 80×60 to 20×15. A segmentation stage at H/8 resolution then classifies each sub-unit into one of 9 neighboring hypotheses by minimizing projection error, correcting boundary artifacts of the regular grid. For refinement, ETO eliminates self-attention and reduces cross-attention from bidirectional to uni-directional (querying only the target neighborhood), which the authors show converges faster and is substantially cheaper while preserving accuracy. Training is two-stage: the coarse stage (homography estimation) is supervised via indirect correspondence loss with a classification fallback for grossly misidentified matches, then the fine stage (segmentation + refinement) is trained with focal loss and L1+BCE losses while freezing coarse parameters.

## Results

On YFCC100M (harder outdoor scenes), ETO achieves 44.8%/64.0%/78.8% pose AUC at 5°/10°/20° in 22.1 ms, matching LoFTR (44.7%/63.6%/78.3%) while running ~4.3× faster (22.1 ms vs 96.3 ms). On MegaDepth (easier outdoor scenes), ETO reaches 51.7%/66.6%/77.4% in 21.0 ms, slightly below LoFTR (57.5%/71.2%/80.8% in 93.2 ms) but 4.4× faster. On ScanNet (indoor), ETO achieves 20.1%/40.4%/59.8% in 24.2 ms, close to LoFTR (21.4%/40.3%/57.2% in 94.2 ms) while being ~3.9× faster. On HPatches, ETO runs in 53 ms vs 218 ms for LoFTR and 101 ms for SuperPoint+LightGlue, with competitive homography estimation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoFTR (the closest detector-free Transformer baseline), ETO matches accuracy on YFCC100M (44.8 vs 44.7 at 5°) and ScanNet (40.4 vs 40.3 at 10°) while running 4.3× and 3.9× faster respectively; on the easier MegaDepth set ETO trails by ~5.8 points at 5° (51.7 vs 57.5) but is still 4.4× faster. Against detector-based pipelines (SuperPoint+SuperGlue, SuperPoint+LightGlue), ETO substantially outperforms on YFCC100M (44.8 vs 29.7–36.1 at 5°) and MegaDepth (51.7 vs 43.2–51.5 at 5°), while also being 2–3× faster than LightGlue. Against ASpanFormer and Quadtree (other detector-free Transformer variants), ETO is comparable in accuracy on YFCC100M but 7× faster (22.1 ms vs 155–159 ms). Against RoMa (dense matching), ETO is 33× faster (21.0 ms vs 689 ms) at the cost of ~13 points at 5° on MegaDepth. On HPatches, ETO achieves 42% 1-px corner accuracy in 53 ms, beating SuperPoint+SuperGlue (41% in 79 ms) and SuperPoint+LightGlue (44% in 101 ms) on speed while remaining competitive on accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETO extends the detector-free transformer matching pipeline established by LoFTR (Sun et al., CVPR 2021) and the parameterized-unit concept from PATS (Ni et al., CVPR 2023), which only modeled scale. The key departure is replacing per-unit bipartite matching with multiple homography hypotheses that cover groups of patches, compressing the token count fed to the transformer from 80×60 (1/8 resolution) to 20×15 (1/32 resolution). In the refinement stage, ETO departs from the standard bidirectional self- and cross-attention used by LoFTR and its successors, substituting a single unidirectional cross-attention that the authors argue is sufficient given the propagation already achieved at the coarse stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–5× speedup over LoFTR and 2–3× over LightGlue at comparable accuracy makes transformer-based matching viable for latency-sensitive applications (real-time tracking, large-scale mapping) that were previously locked to slower CNN-based pipelines. However, matching accuracy on harder benchmarks (YFCC100M, ScanNet) still trails the best detector-free methods like RoMa, and the authors acknowledge the parametric homography scheme may be suboptimal and that end-to-end fine-grained training could close the gap. An open question is whether the homography-hypothesis token compression generalizes beyond the 3×3 local neighborhood assumption or to more complex scene geometry.
