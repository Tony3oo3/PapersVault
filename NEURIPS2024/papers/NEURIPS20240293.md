## Problem

Existing compression-based generalization bounds for LLMs treat entire documents as IID samples, but the small number of documents forces extremely restrictive compression (e.g., SubLoRA) to achieve non-vacuous bounds, resulting in models that generate ungrammatical text and bounds that are vacuous at the billion-parameter scale. The vast number of constituent tokens in training corpora remains untapped, leaving a gap between theoretical guarantees and the performance of deployed, high-quality LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM checkpoint (GPT2, LLaMA1/2, or Mistral-based antibody models), a long token sequence formed by concatenating IID documents with end-of-text (EOT) tokens, and compression parameters (quantization bit-width, LoRA rank, Monarch/Kronecker block structure). It produces a non-vacuous upper bound on the expected next-token negative log-likelihood (BPD) and Top-k error, where the expectation is conditioned on contexts observed during training but the next token is resampled from the data-generating process. The model is explicitly masked so it cannot attend across EOT boundaries, ensuring the autoregressive factorization resets at each document.

## Method

The core contribution is a token-level generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) derived by constructing a martingale difference sequence from the per-token NLL and its conditional expectation, then applying Azuma's inequality with a union bound over the hypothesis space weighted by a Solomonoff prior. This replaces the IID document assumption with a valid concentration inequality over the non-IID token sequence, increasing the effective sample size from the number of documents to the number of tokens (billions to trillions). The larger sample size shrinks the complexity term, permitting far less restrictive compression: Monarch matrices, Kronecker factorizations, and post-training quantization via QuIP/QuIP# (2–4 bits) suffice to achieve non-vacuous bounds for models up to LLaMA2-70B without altering pretraining. A token-level prediction smoothing head learns a per-token α that balances empirical risk against the bounded-interval width Δᵢ, further tightening the bound. The approach yields the first non-vacuous bounds for deployed LLMs generating high-quality text, and demonstrates that the bounded quantity correlates at >98% with downstream perplexity and accuracy.

## Results

On OpenWebText (9B tokens), the best token-level BPD generalization bound is 7.65 (Monarch parametrization) with 75.87% Top-1 error, compared to 15.62 BPD for random guess. Pretrained GPT2-small (124M) quantized post-training achieves a BPD bound of 7.61 without any pretraining modification. On the Amber dataset (1.2T tokens), 2-bit QuIP-quantized LLaMA2 models achieve non-vacuous BPD bounds ranging from 4.28 (7B) to 6.39 (70B), all well below the 14.97 random-guess threshold. The token-level bounds correlate at 97.9% with downstream accuracy and 99.1% with downstream perplexity across GPT2 model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SubLoRA from Lotfi et al. [32], which achieved the first non-vacuous document-level bounds for LLMs. The proposed token-level bounds with Monarch parametrization reduce the BPD bound from 10.49 (SubLoRA) to 7.65, a 27% improvement, while also reducing Top-1 error from 90.44% to 75.87%. Critically, the quantized GPT2-small model (no pretraining modification whatsoever) achieves a BPD bound of 7.61, outperforming SubLoRA's 10.49 while generating grammatically correct text rather than the ungrammatical output of SubLoRA. The paper further extends non-vacuous bounds to LLaMA2-70B (BPD 6.39 on Amber), a scale entirely unreachable by the document-level approach of Lotfi et al. [32], which was limited to compressed GPT2 variants producing low-quality text. The token-level framework also reveals that LLaMA2-Chat models have looser bounds than their base counterparts (e.g., 4.54 vs 4.28 BPD for 7B), showing that dialogue fine-tuning degrades next-token prediction generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the document-level PAC-Bayes compression bounds of Lotfi et al. [32] to the token level by replacing the IID document assumption with a martingale-based concentration inequality (Azuma's inequality), thereby exploiting the vastly larger number of tokens in LLM training corpora. The compression framework builds on the Kolmogorov-complexity-based coding of [31], but the key conceptual departure is that the increased sample count (billions of tokens vs. thousands of documents) relaxes the complexity budget enough to permit far less restrictive compression—post-training quantization alone, Monarch matrices, or Kronecker factorizations—rather than the aggressive SubLoRA subspace training that prior work required. The paper also draws on recent post-training quantization methods (QuIP/QuIP#) and structured matrix parametrizations (Monarch, Kronecker) as practical compression tools, positioning itself at the intersection of generalization theory and model compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to obtain non-vacuous generalization bounds for deployed, high-quality LLMs (up to LLaMA2-70B) without altering the pretraining procedure, closing a significant gap between theoretical guarantees and practical models. The finding that LLaMA2-Chat models exhibit looser bounds than their base counterparts suggests that instruction fine-tuning degrades next-token prediction generalization, a practical insight for model deployment. The memorization-vs-reasoning experiment (structured sequences retained under compression while random sequences are forgotten) provides a theoretical lens on a widely observed but poorly understood phenomenon. Open questions include the remaining gap between bound values and validation BPD, whether combining document-level independence with token-level non-independence can yield tighter bounds, and whether these results extend to downstream tasks beyond next-token prediction.
