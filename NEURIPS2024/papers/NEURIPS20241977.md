## Problem

Existing binarized neural networks (BNNs) train binary weights through full-precision latent weights and gradient descent, meaning they offer no training-time memory or energy savings and suffer accuracy loss from arbitrary gradient approximations via sign-function proxies. No scalable algorithm existed for natively training deep models entirely in the Boolean domain without relying on FP latent weights or real-arithmetic optimizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard inputs—RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet/Cityscapes, low-res images for super-resolution) and tokenized text for NLU—and produces class labels, segmentation masks, high-resolution images, or NLU predictions respectively. Internally, all intermediate activations and weights are 1-bit Boolean values (T/F), with only the first and last layers retained in full precision and optimized via Adam. The framework is architecture-agnostic: it drops into standard CNNs (VGG-Small, ResNet-18), segmentation heads (DeepLabV3), super-resolution networks (EDSR), and Transformers (BERT) without requiring specialized binary-only blocks.

## Method

The paper introduces Boolean variation, a derivative-like operator on Boolean functions defined via XNOR of directional deltas, which satisfies a chain rule analogous to the continuous gradient (Theorem 3.12). This enables Boolean backpropagation: the loss variation with respect to each Boolean weight is computed as xnor(δLoss/δx^{l+1}, x^l_i) and aggregated over the batch, while upstream signals propagate via xnor(δLoss/δx^{l+1}, w^l_ij). Weight updates follow a simple flipping rule—negate the weight when xnor(aggregated variation, current weight) = T—replacing gradient descent entirely. An accumulator with an auto-regularizing decay factor β^t (the fraction of unchanged weights per layer) provides adaptive, Hebbian-inspired learning without hyperparameter tuning. The result is a training procedure that operates solely with Boolean logic (XNOR, NOT, counting), eliminating FP latent weights, gradient computation, and real-arithmetic optimizers, while a mixed Boolean-Real neuron formulation permits selective co-existence with FP modules like batch normalization.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-18, B⊕LD achieves 51.8% top-1 accuracy (Base 64) and 70.0% (Base 256 + KD with ResNet-34 teacher), surpassing the FP baseline (69.7%) while consuming only 24.45% of FP energy on Tesla V100. On CIFAR-10 with VGG-SMALL, B⊕LD reaches 92.37% (with BN) vs. 93.80% FP, at just 3.71% of FP energy. For semantic segmentation on Cityscapes, B⊕LD achieves 67.4% mIoU, approaching the FP baseline (70.7%). On the GLUE benchmark with BERT, B⊕LD attains 70.9% average accuracy, comparable to the SOTA BIT (71.0%) and substantially outperforming BiBERT (63.2%) and BinaryBERT (41.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

B⊕LD decisively outperforms early BNN baselines: on ImageNet it surpasses BINARYNET (42.2%) by 9.6–27.8 points and XNOR-NET (51.2%) by 0.6–18.8 points, while consuming an order of magnitude less energy. Against architecture-modified BNNs, B⊕LD (Base 256) beats BI-REALNET:18 (56.4%) by 10.5 points and MELIUS-NET:29 (65.8%) by 1.1 points without requiring specialized blocks or FP shortcuts. Under knowledge distillation with a ResNet-34 teacher, B⊕LD (Base 256) achieves 70.0%, outperforming BNEXT:18 (68.4%) by 1.6 points and POKEBNN (65.2%, using a stronger ResNet-50 teacher) by 4.8 points, while using 37–53% less energy. On Cityscapes segmentation, B⊕LD (67.4% mIoU) surpasses Binary DAD-Net (58.1%) by 9.3 points. For NLU, B⊕LD BERT (70.9% avg GLUE) ties BIT (71.0%) and outperforms BiBERT (63.2%) by 7.7 points and BinaryBERT (41.0%) by 29.9 points, all while using native Boolean weights during training rather than FP latent weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant BNN paradigm (BinaryConnect, Binarized Neural Networks, XNOR-Net, Bi-Real Net, BNEXT, PokeBNN) in which binary weights are learned through full-precision latent weights updated by gradient descent with differentiable proxies. Instead, it introduces a novel mathematical object—Boolean variation—that serves as a discrete analog of the continuous gradient, complete with a chain rule (Theorem 3.12) and a convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.17), enabling training entirely in the Boolean domain. The approach is conceptually related to earlier discrete-weight training ideas (Expectation Backpropagation, statistical physics-inspired algorithms, Belief Propagation) but extends them to deep architectures with a principled calculus. It is also orthogonal to logic gate networks, which restrict each neuron to two inputs, and to existing BNN methods, with which it can be combined (e.g., adding batch normalization or FP first/last layers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOLD provides the first scalable framework for natively training deep models with 1-bit weights and activations without any FP latent parameters, yielding up to 36× energy reduction versus full-precision training while remaining competitive on classification, segmentation, super-resolution, and NLU tasks. The convergence analysis reveals an irreducible error floor (2Ldκ) inherent to discrete weights, and the authors explicitly note that the approximation capacity of Boolean neural networks lacks a universal approximation theorem, leaving a fundamental theoretical gap. The absence of native Boolean accelerators means current energy estimates are analytical rather than measured, and the practical impact will depend on whether future hardware can exploit the Boolean dataflow. The work could catalyze new logic-circuit and in-memory-computing architectures designed around Boolean arithmetic rather than real-valued MACs.
