## Problem

Natural gradient descent (NGD) offers superior convergence for training deep neural networks by preconditioning gradients with the Fisher information matrix, but computing the inverse of this high-dimensional matrix is intractable for models with millions of parameters. Existing Kronecker-factored approximations like KFAC still require a complete backpropagation pass to compute each layer's block matrix, and their factor matrices are both full, limiting further computational savings. This paper asks whether the Fisher information matrix can be approximated more cheaply while retaining the curvature information that drives NGD's fast convergence.

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes per-layer inputs (activations a_{l-1}), per-layer outputs (activations a_l), and backpropagated gradients ∇_{θ_l}h(θ) at each training step. Rather than requiring the full backpropagation chain to compute all block matrices simultaneously, LNGD computes each layer's Fisher block independently during the forward pass using a Gaussian sampling assumption on activations. The output is updated parameters θ_{l+1} = θ_l + m, where m is a momentum-augmented step scaled by a per-layer adaptive learning rate α_l, so different layers update at different effective step sizes within the same iteration.

## Method

LNGD approximates the Fisher information matrix in two stages. First, a layer-wise sample method computes each block F_l using the l-th layer's local prediction distribution P(a_l|a_{l-1}) under a Gaussian assumption, eliminating the need for a full backpropagation pass. Second, each block is factored as F_l ≈ Φ_l ⊗ Ψ_l where Ψ_l is diagonal (not full), with the trace of F_l preserved before and after approximation; this makes the inverse trivially cheap since only one full matrix inversion per layer is needed. An adaptive layer-wise learning rate α_l is then computed by minimizing a quadratic model per layer, with a fast vector-based computation that avoids forming the full F_l d_l^T product. A trace-scaled damping term is added to each Kronecker factor to stabilize training, and exponential moving averages smooth the factor estimates across mini-batches. Global linear convergence is proven for a two-layer ReLU network under standard over-parameterization conditions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, LNGD reaches 91% top-1 accuracy in 36 epochs (189.69 s), outpacing SGD (79 epochs), ADAM (72 epochs), and KFAC (45 epochs), while also achieving the highest final test accuracy of 93.61%. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, LNGD attains 75.9% top-1 accuracy in 36 epochs (6.46 h) with a best accuracy of 76.73%, versus 78 epochs for SGD and 39 epochs for KFAC. On WMT English-German with Transformer-Big (213.7M params), LNGD reaches 32% BLEU in 19K steps (2.85 h) with a best BLEU of 33.55%, compared to 25K steps for ADAM and 22K steps for KFAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LNGD consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three tasks. Against SGD, LNGD reduces the number of epochs to reach 91% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 by 54% (36 vs. 79) and achieves a 1.73-point higher final test accuracy (93.61% vs. 91.88%); on ImageNet, SGD requires 100% more epochs and 75% more time, and on WMT, SGD fails to reach 32% BLEU at all (best 31.87%). Against ADAM, LNGD saves 50% fewer epochs on CIFAR-10 (36 vs. 72) and 24% fewer steps on WMT (19K vs. 25K), while ADAM never reaches 75.9% top-1 on ImageNet (best 74.05%). Against KFAC, the closest second-order competitor, LNGD reduces epochs by 20% on CIFAR-10 (36 vs. 45), by 8% on ImageNet (36 vs. 39), and by 14% on WMT (19K vs. 22K), while also achieving the highest final accuracy in every setting (e.g., 93.61% vs. 93.34% on CIFAR-10, 76.73% vs. 76.58% on ImageNet, 33.55% vs. 33.45% BLEU on WMT). Against EKFAC (reported in the appendix), LNGD similarly achieves fewer epochs and lower total training time on CIFAR-10, confirming that the layer-wise sampling and diagonal Kronecker factorization provide a more efficient approximation than eigenvalue-corrected factorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

LNGD extends the KFAC/TKFAC line of Kronecker-factored natural gradient methods by introducing two key departures: (1) a layer-wise Gaussian sampling strategy that computes each Fisher block independently without full backpropagation, and (2) a trace-preserving Kronecker factorization where one factor is constrained to be diagonal, reducing inversion cost. The adaptive layer-wise learning rate draws on the observation that parameters within a layer share similar gradient magnitudes, bridging the gap between global and element-wise adaptive rates. Conceptually, the paper sits between KFAC (which requires full backprop for the gradient factor) and diagonal approximations (which discard too much curvature information), aiming to retain the most informative diagonal structure at lower computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a consistent ~20% reduction in training time over KFAC and ~30% over Adam across CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and WMT, which is meaningful for large-scale training pipelines where second-order methods are otherwise prohibitively expensive. However, the convergence guarantee is limited to a two-layer ReLU network under overparameterization, and the Gaussian assumption on per-layer activations, while empirically supported, lacks a rigorous justification for deep or attention-based architectures. Open questions include whether the layer-wise sampling approximation degrades in very deep networks, how the method interacts with modern architectural components (batch normalization, layer normalization, attention), and whether the diagonal constraint on Ψ can be relaxed to capture more off-diagonal structure without losing the computational advantage.
