## Problem

Open-world semi-supervised learning (OwSSL) requires a model to classify seen-class instances while simultaneously discovering novel-class instances in unlabeled data, but existing self-labeling approaches suffer from confirmation bias (predicting novel instances as seen classes) and clustering misalignment due to reliance on inaccurate prior distributions. TRSSL, the prior state-of-the-art, performs unconditional self-labeling on unlabeled data alone with a uniform distribution assumption, yielding a biased estimator of the true unlabeled class distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled images from seen classes (e.g., 10% or 50% label ratio) and a large pool of unlabeled images spanning both seen and novel classes, processed through ResNet-18 (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) or ResNet-50 (224×224 ImageNet-100) initialized from SimCLR pre-training. Outputs are standard class labels for seen classes and cluster assignments for novel classes, evaluated via the Hungarian algorithm to align predicted clusters with ground-truth novel labels. The framework also supports multi-crop local views (4 low-resolution crops per image) for the clustering objective in the OwMatch+ variant.

## Method

OwMatch introduces conditional self-labeling, which augments the standard transportation polytope constraint (aligning self-labels to a prior class distribution) with a second constraint that pins self-labels of labeled samples to their ground-truth one-hot vectors; the joint optimization is solved via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, yielding an unbiased estimator of the unlabeled class distribution (proven via a novel Expectation of Chi-Square Statistics metric). For the confidence objective, the paper proposes open-world hierarchical thresholding: a group-level threshold separates seen from novel classes based on their distinct learning paces (η), while a class-level adjustment (ζ) further modulates thresholds within each group, producing class-specific thresholds τ(c) that retain high-quality pseudo-labels despite the slower convergence of novel clusters. The total training objective sums a standard supervised cross-entropy, a clustering cross-entropy between optimized self-labels and model predictions, and a consistency loss gated by the hierarchical threshold, all optimized jointly with SGD on weakly and strongly augmented views.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 50% novel class ratio and 50% label ratio, OwMatch+ achieves 80.1% seen-class accuracy, 63.9% novel-class accuracy, and 71.9% all-class accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art TRSSL (80.0/49.3/64.7) by 14.6% on novel classes and 7.2% on all classes. On CIFAR-10, OwMatch+ reaches 96.5/97.1/96.8 (seen/novel/all), outperforming FixMatch by up to 47.3% in all-class accuracy. On ImageNet-100, OwMatch+ attains 85.5% all-class accuracy, exceeding the prior best OpenCon (83.8%) by 1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OwMatch+ achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three main benchmarks. On CIFAR-100, it outperforms TRSSL (the prior OwSSL SOTA) by 14.6% on novel-class accuracy (63.9 vs. 49.3) and 7.2% on all-class accuracy (71.9 vs. 64.7), while matching TRSSL on seen-class accuracy (80.1 vs. 80.0). On CIFAR-10, OwMatch+ surpasses OpenLDN by 2.0% on novel classes (97.1 vs. 95.1) and 1.4% on all classes (96.8 vs. 95.4), and exceeds NACH by 4.9% on novel classes. On ImageNet-100, OwMatch+ beats OpenCon by 1.7% on all-class accuracy (85.5 vs. 83.8) and NACH by 5.9% on all-class accuracy (85.5 vs. 79.6). Against traditional SSL baselines, OwMatch+ outperforms FixMatch by up to 47.3% in all-class accuracy on CIFAR-10 (96.8 vs. 49.5) and 51.6% on CIFAR-100 (71.9 vs. 20.3), demonstrating the critical advantage of OwSSL-specific methods over closed-world SSL. In the GCD comparison (Table 5), OwMatch+ with ResNet-50 achieves 85.5% all-class accuracy on ImageNet-100, surpassing SimGCD (83.9%) and PromptCAL (83.1%) which use the larger ViT-B/16 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-labeling framework of Asano et al. (ICLR 2020) and TRSSL (ECCV 2022) by adding a conditional constraint that anchors labeled data to their ground-truth labels within the optimal-transport polytope, converting an unconditional clustering problem into a supervised one. The confidence objective adapts FixMatch's consistency regularization and FreeMatch's self-adaptive thresholding into a two-level hierarchical scheme that separately calibrates thresholds for seen and novel class groups. The theoretical contribution—proving the unconditional estimator is biased while the conditional one is unbiased via a novel Expectation of Chi-Square Statistics (ECS) metric—provides a rigorous statistical justification for the conditional formulation that prior OwSSL work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where SSL methods fail when unlabeled data contains novel classes, a common scenario in medical imaging and open-domain recognition. The 14.6% novel-class improvement over TRSSL on CIFAR-100 and the theoretical guarantee of unbiased estimation make this a meaningful step toward deployable open-world SSL. However, the method still relies on a prior class distribution (or an adaptive estimation that costs ~3% accuracy), and the authors acknowledge that convergence guarantees for the adaptive scheme remain open. Future work on handling truly unknown distributions and imbalanced long-tailed settings would strengthen the practical applicability.
