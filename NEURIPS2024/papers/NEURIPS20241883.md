## Problem

Identifiability of linear ODE systems has been well studied under full observability, but the conditions under which system parameters can be uniquely recovered from observations remain unexplored when latent confounders interact with the system. This gap is critical because unidentifiable parameters lead to fundamentally different causal structures and misleading intervention predictions. The paper addresses this by deriving necessary and sufficient identifiability conditions for two settings: independent latent confounders with prescribed functional evolution, and causally related latent confounders whose structure follows a DAG.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the observable trajectory x(t) (either as a continuous function or as n discrete error-free samples at arbitrary time points), since the latent state z(t) is never directly observed. From a single trajectory, the identifiable quantities are the composite parameters (x_0, A, Bz_0, BGz_0, …, BG^{p-1}z_0), which suffice to determine causal relationships among observable variables but not B and G individually. When p controllable latent initial conditions z_0^{*i} are available (each yielding a separate trajectory), the output extends to full identification of matrices B and G, enabling reliable causal inference under interventions on latent variables.

## Method

The core technique is an augmented-state transformation that converts the partially observable ODE system into a fully observable homogeneous linear ODE, to which existing single-trajectory identifiability results (Lemma 2.1) apply directly. For the independent-confounder case with polynomial f(t), the state is augmented to y(t) = [x(t), 1, t, …, t^{r+1}]^T; for the DAG case, the nilpotency of G (guaranteed by the strictly upper-triangular structure) ensures z(t) = e^{Gt}z_0 is a polynomial of degree p−1, so the augmentation is y(t) = [x(t), 1, t, …, t^{p-1}]^T. The identifiability condition then reduces to the linear independence of {γ, Aγ, …, A^{d-1}γ} for a specific vector γ encoding the system parameters. For discrete observations, identifiability is characterized by the existence of d+p linearly independent augmented observations y_j. For the p-controllable-trajectory setting, B is recovered via B = SZ^{-1} (where Z stacks the known z_0^{*i}), and G is recovered by exploiting the rank-p condition on the stacked matrix [B; BG; …; BG^{p-1}], yielding G = W_p^{-1} V_p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a purely theoretical contribution and does not compare against competing identifiability methods or baselines. The simulations serve solely to validate the proposed sufficient-and-necessary identifiability conditions (Theorems 3.1, 4.1–4.4) by demonstrating that parameter estimation succeeds (MSE → 0) when the conditions hold and fails (MSE remains high) when they are violated. The closest prior work is Wang et al. [42] on identifiability of fully observable linear ODE systems from discrete observations, which this paper extends to the hidden-confounder setting; however, no head-to-head numerical comparison is performed since the problem settings differ. The paper's contribution is thus in establishing new theoretical conditions rather than in empirical benchmarking against existing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability analysis of homogeneous linear ODE systems established by Stanhope et al. [34] and Wang et al. [42], which assumed full observability of all state variables, to the practically important setting where latent confounders interact with the observable system. The key methodological bridge is an augmented state transformation that converts the partially observable system into a fully observable homogeneous linear ODE, thereby inheriting the linear-independence conditions from prior work. The paper also situates itself within the broader causal-inference literature connecting ODEs to structural causal models (Mooij et al. [25], Rubenstein et al. [30]), emphasizing that identifiability is a prerequisite for reliable causal interpretation and intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic identifiability analysis of linear ODE systems with hidden confounders, filling a clear gap between the fully-observable theory and real-world systems where unmeasured variables are ubiquitous. The sufficient-and-necessary conditions (A1, B1, C1, etc.) give practitioners concrete, checkable criteria for designing experiments—e.g., how many controllable trajectories are needed to identify matrices B and G. A notable limitation, acknowledged by the authors, is that verifying these conditions requires knowledge of the true parameters, which are unavailable in practice; the paper suggests constrained estimation as a workaround but leaves the practical verification problem open. Extending the framework to nonlinear ODEs, stochastic settings, or more general latent structures (beyond DAGs) remains future work.
