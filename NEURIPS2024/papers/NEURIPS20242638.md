## Problem

In latent diffusion models, the encoder is only a right-inverse of the decoder, so recovering the exact latent code from a generated pixel-space image requires solving a decoder inversion problem. Prior approaches used gradient-based optimization (analogous to GAN inversion), which demands large GPU memory and long runtimes that become infeasible for video LDMs generating dozens of frames on a single 24 GB GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242638/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a generated image x in pixel space along with the frozen LDM encoder E and decoder D, and iteratively refines a latent vector z initialized as E(x). A key practical detail is that all operations (encoder and decoder forward passes) can be executed in 16-bit half-precision, whereas gradient-based baselines require 32-bit to avoid underflow in backpropagated gradients. The output is the inverted latent z that, when passed through D, reconstructs x with minimal NMSE.

## Method

The core substitution replaces the gradient of the pixel-space loss ∇_z ℓ(x, D(z)) with the encoder-residual signal E(D(z)) − E(x), yielding the forward step iteration z^{k+1} = z^k − ρ(E(D(z^k)) − E(x)). This avoids any backpropagation through the decoder, reducing memory from O(decoder activations) to O(1) extra storage. The paper proves convergence to the true latent under a β-cocoercivity condition on T(·) = E∘D(·) − E(x) for both the vanilla forward step method (Theorem 1) and the inertial Krasnoselskii–Mann iteration with momentum (Theorem 2), and experimentally validates that the cocoercivity assumption holds for Stable Diffusion 2.1, LaVie, and InstaFlow. In practice, Adam with a cosine warm-up/annealing schedule is layered on top, achieving up to 5× faster runtime, 89% less peak memory, and comparable or better NMSE than gradient-based baselines.

## Results

The gradient-free decoder inversion achieves up to 5× faster runtime (1.89 s vs 9.51 s at −16.4 dB NMSE on InstaFlow) and up to 89% GPU memory reduction (7.13 GB vs 64.7 GB on LaVie), while uniquely supporting 16-bit precision (infeasible for gradient-based due to underflow). On Stable Diffusion 2.1, the method reaches −21.85 dB NMSE in 34.5 s (200 iterations, 32-bit) with 3.81 GB memory, compared to −24.82 dB for the gradient-based baseline at 100 iterations (31.7 s, 8.83 GB). For tree-rings watermark classification on InstaFlow, the gradient-free method matches the gradient-based accuracy (227/300) while using 64% less memory (3.15 vs 8.84 GB) and 62% less runtime (13.6 s vs 35.9 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the gradient-based decoder inversion of Hong et al. [14], the proposed gradient-free method wins decisively on efficiency: up to 5× faster runtime and up to 89% less GPU memory (7.13 GB vs 64.7 GB on LaVie), while also enabling 16-bit inference that is infeasible for the gradient-based approach. On raw accuracy, the gradient-based method retains a modest edge at high iteration counts (e.g., −24.82 dB vs −21.85 dB on SD 2.1 at 100 vs 200 iterations), but the gradient-free method reaches comparable accuracy (−21.71 dB at 100 iterations) in roughly half the time (17.7 s vs 31.7 s) with 57% less memory. In the watermark classification task (Table 2), the gradient-free method ties the gradient-based method on InstaFlow (227/300 accuracy) while cutting memory by 64% and runtime by 62%, and on SD 2.1 it achieves 202/300 vs 207/300 with 44% less memory. Against the Oracle baseline [32] in background-preserving editing (Figure 4), the gradient-free 16-bit method (100 iter, 9.56 s) achieves NMSE of 0.0080–0.0082, substantially closer to the oracle (0.0016–0.0060) than the gradient-based method at a similar runtime (0.0119–0.0126 at 9.31 s), demonstrating that the gradient-free inversion better recovers the original latent trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hong et al. [14], which formulated decoder inversion in LDMs as a gradient-based optimization problem (min_z ℓ(x, D(z))) and solved it via backpropagation through the decoder. The key departure is replacing the expensive gradient ∇_z D(z) with the encoder-based residual E(D(z)) − E(x), yielding a forward step method that requires only forward passes through both encoder and decoder. The theoretical analysis draws on the Krasnoselskii-Mann iteration literature [19, 28, 29] and the cocoercivity framework from monotone operator theory [39], while the practical algorithm integrates Adam and cosine learning rate scheduling following conventions from GAN inversion [1, 54] and prior LDM inversion work [14].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method makes decoder inversion feasible for video LDMs (e.g., LaVie) on a single GPU by reducing peak memory from 64.7 GB to 7.13 GB and cutting runtime by ~5×, unblocking applications like noise-space watermarking and background-preserving editing that were previously intractable at video scale. A notable practical finding is that the gradient-free approach supports 16-bit inference (avoiding the underflow issues that make 16-bit gradient-based methods infeasible), which aligns with how modern LDMs are deployed. The main limitation acknowledged is that gradient-based methods still achieve lower NMSE given unlimited compute, and the cocoercivity assumption underlying the convergence theorems is validated only empirically rather than proven for specific architectures. Open questions include whether the forward step method extends to LDMs without a well-trained encoder, and whether the theoretical guarantees can be tightened to remove the cocoercivity assumption.
