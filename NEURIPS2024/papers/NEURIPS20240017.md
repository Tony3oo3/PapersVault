## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image models struggle to generate images in a specific domain (e.g., a particular artist's sketch style) when only a handful of example images are available, and existing finetuning approaches for T2I models have focused on subject-driven generation or require tens-to-hundreds of images. Few-shot domain-driven generation faces four unresolved issues: prior attributes of the identifier word persisting in outputs, domain and categorical attributes leaking into each other during cross-category generation, overfitting on small datasets, and insufficient domain attribute strength when the category changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes approximately 10 RGB images (512×512) from a target domain and a rarely-used identifier word [V] paired with a category word [N] (e.g., 'a sks face'). At inference, standard text prompts control the generated content, enabling four scenarios: intra-category (same category as training data), cross-category (different category via text), extra-attribute (additional text-specified details), and personalization (combined domain + subject). All experiments use Stable Diffusion v1.4 as the base model with LoRA (rank 4) on the UNet, and generation uses DDIM with 50 steps and CFG scale 7.5.

## Method

DomainGallery is a three-stage pipeline (prior attribute erasure → finetuning → inference-time enhancement) that binds non-categorical domain attributes to an identifier word [V] via LoRA finetuning of Stable Diffusion. Four attribute-centric losses address the key challenges: (1) L_erase pre-erases prior attributes of [V] by matching predicted noise with and without the identifier; (2) L_disen enforces domain-category disentanglement by penalizing deviation of the LoRA-augmented UNet from the frozen base UNet on source-conditioned inputs, preventing attribute leakage between [V] and [N]; (3) L_sim regularizes against overfitting by performing 5-step DDIM denoising from shared noisy latents under source/target conditions to create paired latents, then computing KL divergence on multi-layer UNet encoder features (avoiding pixel-level decoding); (4) at inference, a CFG-like additional guidance term on [V] (V-uncond mode) boosts domain attribute strength for cross-category generation. For personalization, DreamBooth-style subject finetuning is added in a single joint stage.

## Results

On the five 10-shot domain-driven generation datasets, DomainGallery achieves the best FID on CUFS sketches (44.86) and the best KID on Van Gogh houses (32.20) and wrecked cars (23.63), while also attaining the highest intra-clustered LPIPS (diversity) scores across all five datasets (e.g., 0.7255 on Van Gogh houses vs. 0.6367 for DomainStudio). On FFHQ sunglasses, DomainGallery trades a small FID gap (43.10 vs. 41.22 for DreamBooth+LoRA) for substantially better diversity (0.6924 vs. 0.6452). The paper notes that DreamBooth's competitive KID on watercolor dogs (32.60) is accompanied by severe overfitting, which DomainGallery avoids through its transfer-based similarity consistency loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DomainGallery is compared against DreamBooth, DreamBooth+LoRA, and DomainStudio (the only prior few-shot domain-driven method). Against DomainStudio, DomainGallery wins on FID for CUFS sketches (44.86 vs. 51.73), KID for Van Gogh houses (32.20 vs. 41.06) and wrecked cars (23.63 vs. 32.31), and I-LPIPS on all five datasets, while DomainStudio only edges ahead on FFHQ FID (66.66 vs. 43.10 is actually DomainGallery winning; DomainStudio's 66.66 is worse). Against DreamBooth+LoRA, DomainGallery achieves better FID on CUFS (44.86 vs. 52.80), better KID on Van Gogh (32.20 vs. 44.51) and wrecked cars (23.63 vs. 26.68), and higher diversity on all datasets, though DreamBooth+LoRA has a slightly lower FID on FFHQ (41.22 vs. 43.10). DreamBooth's nominally best KID on watercolor dogs (32.60) and wrecked cars (8.81) is attributed to severe overfitting rather than genuine generalization, as confirmed by qualitative analysis in the appendix. Qualitatively, DomainGallery avoids the prior-attribute leakage (military elements from the identifier 'sks') and domain-category attribute leakage that plague all three baselines in cross-category generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DomainGallery extends the DreamBooth finetuning paradigm from subject-driven to domain-driven generation, where the domain is defined by shared non-categorical attributes rather than a single subject identity. It builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient finetuning of Stable Diffusion and positions itself against DomainStudio as the sole prior work in few-shot domain-driven generation, while drawing on model-transfer regularization ideas (similarity consistency losses) from the GAN literature. The key departure is an attribute-centric decomposition of the finetuning problem into four targeted techniques—prior attribute erasure, domain-category disentanglement, transfer-based similarity consistency, and inference-time attribute enhancement—each addressing a specific failure mode that subject-driven methods do not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated recipe for adapting pretrained T2I models to new visual domains with only ~10 images, covering intra-category, cross-category, extra-attribute, and personalization scenarios in a unified framework. Its attribute-centric diagnostic lens (erasure, disentanglement, regularization, enhancement) gives practitioners a clear checklist for debugging domain finetuning failures. Open questions remain: the method assumes a single shared category word, struggles with content-based domains (acknowledged cherry-picking on FFHQ sunglasses), and the quantitative gains over DomainStudio are modest, leaving room for stronger regularization or multi-category extensions.
