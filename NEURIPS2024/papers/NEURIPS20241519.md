## Problem

Consistency models enable fast one- or two-step image generation but produce samples of lower perceptual quality than their diffusion origins. While BIGROC demonstrated that a robust classifier can post-process generated images via Perceptually Aligned Gradients, it is entirely unaware of the synthetic data distribution it aims to improve. This paper addresses that gap by introducing an energy-based discrimination component that is jointly trained with the classifier, making the boosting process aware of both the real and synthetic distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single synthesized RGB image (64×64, 128×128, or 256×256) produced by a Consistency model (or other generative model such as BigGAN or ADM-G) together with its target class label, and outputs a refined image of the same resolution. Refinement is performed via 7–35 iterative PGD or SGLD steps (depending on the source generative model), each step requiring only ~0.02 s, so the total post-processing cost is comparable to a single Consistency inference step. An image-dependent early-stopping mechanism selectively refines images that diverge from the real data manifold while leaving already-realistic images largely untouched.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint classifier-discriminator network (initialized from a robust RN50 or Wide-RN50-2 backbone) trained with a combined adversarial loss: a Cross-Entropy term that enforces correct class assignment and a Binary-Cross-Entropy term on the c-th logit (via sigmoid) that acts as an energy function distinguishing real from synthetic images. Both losses are evaluated on adversarially perturbed inputs—real images are pushed toward looking fake (negative α in PGD) while synthetic images are pushed toward looking real (positive α)—with the number of PGD attack steps gradually increased during training. At inference, the joint model's Perceptually Aligned Gradients guide an iterative refinement loop that minimizes a composite loss ℓ(x,c) = ℓ_CE(x,c) − ½(ℓ_BCE(x,c) − ℓ₀)², where ℓ₀ is the mean BCE loss of real images, simultaneously pulling the image toward its target class and toward the real data manifold. The discriminator's sigmoid-based energy avoids the instability and MCMC dependence of classical EBMs while retaining the distribution-awareness that a plain classifier lacks.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, the proposed Wide-RN Robust Joint model with PGD boosting achieves FID 8.83 and IS 55.67 for 1-step Consistency Training (vs. 13.00/28.83 unboosted), and FID 3.78 and IS 55.13 for 2-step Consistency Distillation (vs. 4.69/42.28 unboosted). The method also generalizes to other generative models, improving ADM-G 256×256 from FID 4.58 to 3.17 and BigGAN 256×256 from FID 7.03 to 6.16. Each PGD step costs only 0.02 s, making 25 boosting steps comparable in compute to a single Consistency inference step (0.55 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Wide-RN Robust Joint model consistently outperforms all baselines across every configuration. Compared to the unboosted Consistency Training baseline (FID 13.00, 1-step), the joint model reduces FID to 8.83 (−32%), and against BIGROC (RN50 Robust Classifier, FID 10.71) it further improves to 8.83 (−17.6%), confirming that the adversarial discriminator component adds substantial value over classifier-only boosting. The Wide-RN Non-Robust Joint (FID 12.30) performs worse than even the unboosted model, demonstrating that adversarial robustness is essential for the Perceptually Aligned Gradients property to function. Against ACT [17], which achieves FID 10.6 on CT via a GAN-based adversarial training approach, the proposed method reaches FID 8.83 with a much lighter training procedure. For Consistency Distillation, the joint model improves FID from 6.20 (unboosted) to 4.84 (1-step) and 3.78 (2-step), surpassing BIGROC's 5.55 and 4.20 respectively. The method also generalizes to non-Consistency generators (ADM-G, BigGAN, iDDPM) at 64–256 px without retraining, improving FID by 12–21% across those models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends BIGROC [6], which uses Perceptually Aligned Gradients from a robust classifier to refine generated images, by augmenting the classifier with an adversarially-trained discriminator that encodes both the real-data and generated-data distributions via a Binary Cross-Entropy energy. It also draws on the classifier-as-EBM connection [11] and the Consistency Models framework [26], but departs from ACT [17] by treating the discriminator as a post-processing guide rather than integrating it into the generative model's training loop. The gradual adversarial training schedule and the image-dependent early-stopping mechanism are novel training details not present in prior robust-classification or EBM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a lightweight, model-agnostic post-processing step (25 PGD iterations ≈ one Consistency inference step) that yields substantial FID gains across Consistency Training, Consistency Distillation, and even unrelated generators (BigGAN, ADM, IDDPM) at multiple resolutions without retraining. A key open question is whether the gains are bounded by the relatively small RN50/Wide-RN backbones used here, and whether training on a diverse mixture of generative models (rather than Consistency outputs alone) would further improve the discriminator's energy landscape. The finding that a non-robust joint model underperforms even the plain BIGROC classifier underscores that adversarial training—not architectural complexity—is the critical ingredient for meaningful perceptual refinement.
