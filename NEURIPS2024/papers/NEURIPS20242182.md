## Problem

Evaluating embedding models typically requires running expensive, domain-specific downstream tasks with labeled data, which does not scale to large pools of candidate embedders. The paper asks whether embedders can be ranked in a task-agnostic, label-free manner by leveraging information-theoretic ordering principles from statistical experiment comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242182/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of pre-trained embedding models (34 in NLP, ~15 in molecular) and a representative unlabeled dataset (MTEB English datasets or ZINC 250k) from which embeddings are extracted for all models. It produces a directed pairwise information sufficiency matrix (forming a graph of embedders), a scalar IS score per embedder (the median of its outgoing pairwise IS values), and a resulting ranking. The pairwise IS is estimated by fitting a Gaussian mixture model to simulate one embedder's output distribution from another's, so the computation is O(N²) in the number of embedders but requires no labels or downstream task training.

## Method

The paper grounds embedder comparison in Blackwell's sufficiency ordering and Le Cam's deficiency: if embedder U can perfectly simulate embedder V via a Markov kernel, then U's Bayes risk is no worse than V's on every classification task. Since deficiency is intractable to estimate, the authors introduce Information Sufficiency (IS) as a tractable proxy — the reduction in differential entropy of V when conditioned on U, estimated by learning a Gaussian mixture model M(V|U) that simulates V from U. The IS score for each embedder is the median of its pairwise IS values against all others, yielding a single scalar that ranks models without any downstream task. Theoretically, Corollary 1 shows that small deficiency (and hence large IS) bounds the gap in expected Bayes risk across all classification tasks, providing a task-agnostic guarantee. Empirically, the IS score achieves Spearman correlations of 0.90 (NLP, 56 tasks) and 0.94 (molecular ADMET, 31 tasks) with downstream performance, and the pairwise IS graph naturally clusters embedders by architecture and training objective.

## Results

The proposed Information Sufficiency (IS) score achieves a Spearman ranking correlation of 0.90 with downstream task performance across 56 NLP tasks (Pearson 0.94) and 0.94 across 31 molecular ADMET tasks (Kendall-tau 0.80). Within NLP subcategories, correlations range from 0.78 (Reranking, 4 datasets) to 0.89 (Retrieval, 15 datasets) in Spearman terms. In molecular modeling, the strongest correlations appear for Metabolism (0.94) and Toxicity (0.92), while Excretion tasks show the weakest (0.77) due to their difficulty as regression problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces a new evaluation methodology rather than competing against existing embedder evaluation baselines in a head-to-head sense. The IS score is validated by measuring how well its induced ranking of 34 NLP embedders (and 12 molecular embedders) correlates with actual downstream task performance rankings from the MTEB benchmark and Therapeutic Data Commons. The IS score consistently ranks models in the same order as their MTEB average scores (Spearman 0.90), correctly identifying top performers like SFR-Embedding-Mistral (IS 0.59, MTEB 67.56) and bottom performers like allenai-specter (IS 0.38, MTEB 40.28). In molecular modeling, the IS score correctly identifies (Chem)Bert-MTR and MolR as the most promising embedders and flags attribute-masking GNNs as the least informative, consistent with prior observations by Sun et al. (2022). The authors note that STS, Clustering, and Reranking tasks show slightly lower correlations (Spearman 0.78–0.85) because these tasks use embeddings directly via dot products rather than training a probe on top, suggesting the IS score captures extractable information rather than geometric alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Blackwell's sufficiency ordering of statistical experiments, Körner-Marton's less-noisy channel comparison, and Le Cam's deficiency into a single practical framework for ranking embedding models. It departs from prior embedding evaluation work (MTEB, probing, geometric metrics like α-req and RankMe) by requiring no labeled downstream data and no task-specific model fitting, instead estimating a tractable proxy for Le Cam deficiency via Gaussian mixture simulation of one embedder from another. The information-theoretic lineage (Shannon → Blackwell → Le Cam → Körner-Marton) is reframed specifically for the embedding-model comparison setting, with the key practical innovation being the Information Sufficiency (IS) score as a label-free, task-agnostic ranking criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IS score offers practitioners a computationally cheap way to prioritize which embedders to invest in expensive downstream evaluation, with Spearman correlations of 0.90 (NLP, 56 tasks) and 0.94 (molecular, 31 tasks) validating the approach. Key limitations include dependence on the pool of available embedders (the score is inherently relative), weaker correlations on dot-product-based tasks (STS, clustering, reranking) where embeddings are used directly rather than through a trained probe, and the Gaussian mixture proxy's inability to capture all aspects of the true deficiency. Open questions include whether better f-divergence-based estimation of the Markov kernel would tighten the proxy, whether random projections could serve as universal baselines, and whether the method extends to settings with very few candidate embedders.
