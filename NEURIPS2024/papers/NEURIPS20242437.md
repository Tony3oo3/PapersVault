## Problem

Existing Neural Field (NeF)-based PDE solvers, such as DINo, learn solutions as flows in a latent space that does not preserve the known geometric symmetries of the underlying PDE (e.g., rotation equivariance, periodic translation). This causes them to fail when generalizing to unseen spatial locations, beyond the training time horizon, or under geometric transformations of the initial condition. The paper asks whether incorporating equivariance constraints into both the NeF decoder and the latent-space dynamics model can restore these generalization properties while retaining the grid-agnostic, space-time-continuous nature of NeF-based solving.

## Input / Output

The method consumes coordinate-value pairs of the PDE initial condition ν₀, which may be sparsely observed (down to 5% of the domain), irregularly sampled, or defined on non-grid geometries (R², T², S², B³). It produces a continuous spatiotemporal solution ν(x,t) that can be evaluated at arbitrary coordinates and times, including zero-shot super-resolution (trained at one resolution, evaluated at 2×) and extrapolation beyond the training time horizon. The initial latent is obtained in only 3 meta-learning SGD steps, making inference fast compared to the 300–500 autodecoding steps used by prior NeF-based PDE solvers.

## Method

The framework represents each PDE state as a set of latents z = {(pᵢ, cᵢ)} (pose + context vector) decoded by an Equivariant Neural Field (ENF) whose cross-attention operates over bi-invariant attributes a(zᵢ, x) that are invariant under the PDE's symmetry group G. The PDE solution is modelled as an equivariant neural ODE F_ψ over this latent space, implemented as a message-passing network that updates poses via logarithmic/exponential maps on the group manifold and contexts via weighted aggregation, with weight-sharing over the same bi-invariant attributes to enforce G-equivariance. Meta-learning (Meta-SGD) is used to fit the initial latent z₀ in 3 gradient steps, which both accelerates inference and forces a more structured latent geometry that simplifies the neural ODE's learning task. The key design choice is selecting bi-invariants matched to each PDE's specific symmetry group (SE(2) for planar diffusion, T²/π for periodic Navier-Stokes, axis-rotation for shallow-water on S², SO(3) for 3D ball convection), enabling the framework to respect boundary conditions and domain geometry that break full SE(n) equivariance.

## Results

On Navier-Stokes over the 2-Torus (100% observed, t_OUT TEST), the equivariant model (a^T²/π) achieves 1.16E-02 MSE, outperforming DINo (9.08E-02) by ~8×, though grid-based FNO (1.60E-03) and G-FNO (3.52E-04) remain stronger on fully observed regular grids. Under 5% sparse observations, the proposed method achieves 1.61E-01 MSE at t_OUT TEST, dramatically outperforming FNO (7.07E-01), G-FNO (6.47E-01), and DINo (2.91E-01). On the heat equation over R² with disjoint roto-translations between train and test, the SE(2)-equivariant model achieves 2.53E-05 MSE at t_OUT TEST versus 5.12E-03 for DINo, while the non-equivariant variant fails entirely. On internally-heated convection in a 3D ball, the proposed method achieves 7.97E-03 MSE at t_OUT TEST, outperforming DINo (7.78E-02) by ~10× and Transolver (2.09E-02) by ~2.6×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINo [51] (the prior NeF-based PDE solver), the proposed method wins decisively on all equivariance-sensitive tasks: on the heat equation with disjoint roto-translations, DINo fails (5.12E-03) while the equivariant model achieves 2.53E-05; on Navier-Stokes at 5% observation, DINo degrades to 2.91E-01 while the proposed method holds at 1.61E-01; and on IHC in a 3D ball, DINo scores 7.78E-02 versus 7.97E-03 for the proposed method at t_OUT TEST. Against grid-based FNO and G-FNO, the proposed method loses on fully observed regular grids (FNO: 1.60E-03 vs. 1.16E-02) but wins by 4–5× under 5% sparse observations (1.61E-01 vs. 7.07E-01 for FNO), highlighting the advantage of continuous NeF representations for irregular data. Against Transolver [50] on IHC, Transolver achieves lower MSE within the training horizon (1.88E-02 vs. 7.72E-03 at t_OUT TRAIN) but overfits and generalizes poorly beyond it (2.09E-02 vs. 7.97E-03 at t_OUT TEST). On CFDBench (no global symmetries), the proposed translation-equivariant model beats U-Net on Cavity (1.10E-02 vs. 1.58E-02) and Cylinder (1.42E-05 vs. 5.49E-05), and is competitive with FNO on Dam (8.19E-05 vs. 8.75E-05). Against CNODE [2], the proposed method dominates in all sparse-observation settings, where CNODE degrades to O(1) MSE at 5% observation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINo framework [51], which models PDE solutions as flows in the latent space of a conditional Neural Field, by replacing the standard NeF decoder with an Equivariant Neural Field (ENF) [49] that preserves geometric symmetries of the PDE. The equivariant message-passing neural ODE is built on PTHITA [5], which achieves SE(n) equivariance through weight-sharing over bi-invariant attributes, and is extended here to PDE-specific symmetry groups (torus translations, axis rotations on S², SO(3) in a ball). Meta-learning (Meta-SGD [28], following [13, 45]) is integrated to obtain initial latents in 3 gradient steps rather than 300–500, and the paper shows this also structures the latent space, complementing the structural benefit of equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that imposing the correct equivariance constraints as an inductive bias is not merely a theoretical nicety but a practical necessity for continuous PDE solvers to generalize to unseen spatial locations, geometric transformations, and sparse observations—settings where prior NeF-based solvers (DINo) and grid-based methods (FNO, G-FNO) fail. Key open questions include scaling the global cross-attention in the ENF backbone to higher-resolution or larger-domain PDEs (the authors note computational cost grows with latent×coordinate pairs), reducing error accumulation beyond the training horizon for volatile dynamics, and whether the framework can handle PDEs with no global symmetries more effectively than the translation-equivariant fallback used on CFDBench.
