## Problem

Finding preference-guided Pareto solutions that represent specific tradeoffs among multiple objectives is critical in multi-objective learning, yet existing methods are restrictive in how they define preferences (weights, rays, or simple threshold constraints) and/or lack non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether a unified framework can capture both relative preferences (via a general polyhedral cone partial order) and absolute preferences (via linear constraints on objectives) while admitting provably convergent single-loop deterministic and stochastic algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vector-valued objective F(θ) = (f_1(θ), …, f_M(θ)), preference constraints expressed as linear functions of the objectives (B_g F(θ) + b_g ≤ 0 and B_h F(θ) + b_h = 0), and an ordering cone matrix A defining the partial order C_A = {y | Ay ≥ 0}. It produces model parameters θ that are C_A-optimal and satisfy the preference constraints, yielding objective values aligned with the specified tradeoff. Unlike scalarization methods that collapse objectives into a single scalar, FERERO operates directly in the vector objective space, and the constraints are defined in objective space as intersections of half-spaces rather than in parameter space.

## Method

FERERO casts preference-guided multi-objective learning as a constrained vector optimization problem and solves it via an adaptive subprogram that simultaneously handles objective improvement (under a general cone-induced partial order) and constraint satisfaction without requiring feasibility at initialization or at each iterate. The subprogram yields an update direction d* = −∇F(θ)A_ag^T λ* as a weighted gradient combination, where λ* is obtained from a convex dual problem; this unified formulation eliminates the need for different subprograms at different constraint-satisfaction stages. The key algorithmic contribution is a single-loop approximate variant (FERERO-SA) that updates λ via one projected gradient step per iteration rather than solving the subprogram exactly, making it the first single-loop primal algorithm for constrained vector optimization with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees (O(T^{-1}) deterministic, O(T^{-1/2}) stochastic, and a sharper O(T^{-1}) under a proximal PL condition). The theoretical analysis overcomes the failure of standard constraint qualifications by proving the calmness condition holds due to the linear-in-F structure of the constraints.

## Results

On multi-patch image classification (Multi-MNIST, Multi-Fashion, Multi-F+M), FERERO achieves the highest hypervolume across most settings, e.g., 1.97±0.21 vs. 1.68 (LS) and 1.35 (EPO) on Multi-MNIST loss, and 7.76±0.18 vs. 6.77 (XWC-MGDA) on Multi-Fashion loss. On multi-lingual speech recognition, FERERO-FT achieves 5.1% average WER on Librispeech/AISHELL-1, outperforming LS-FT (6.4%) and the initial M2ASR model (6.7%), while also reducing the English-Chinese WER gap from 0.9% (LS-FT) to 0.5% (FERERO-FT). On synthetic nonconvex Pareto fronts, FERERO converges in as few as 10 iterations (vs. 60 for EPO and 100 for PMTL) while exactly aligning with preference vectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FERERO outperforms all four image-classification baselines (LS, PMTL, EPO, XWC-MGDA) on hypervolume in 5 out of 6 settings, with the largest margin on Multi-Fashion loss (7.76 vs. 6.77 for XWC-MGDA, +14.6%). On Multi-F+M loss, FERERO (3.82) is competitive with XWC-MGDA (3.89) but slightly behind EPO (3.76). In the speech recognition task, FERERO-FT reduces average WER by 1.3 points over LS-FT (5.1% vs. 6.4%) and 1.6 points over the M2ASR initialization (6.7%), while also narrowing the English–Chinese gap. A key qualitative advantage over PMTL is exact alignment with preference vectors (PMTL only approximates them), and over EPO is the ability to handle more flexible preference definitions (e.g., shifted rays) and perform controlled ascent to trace the Pareto front. On the harder synthetic setting where initial objectives are near the Pareto front, FERERO converges in 10 iterations versus 60 for EPO and 100 for PMTL, and succeeds in all 4 preference cases where PMTL fails in 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—preference-guided multi-objective optimization (PMTL, EPO, XWC-MGDA) and vector optimization with general cone-induced partial ordering—into a single constrained vector optimization framework. It builds directly on MGDA and its stochastic variants for finding common descent directions, but departs from them by incorporating both relative preferences (via a polyhedral cone defining the partial order) and absolute preferences (via linear equality/inequality constraints on objectives) simultaneously. The key methodological departure from prior constrained MOO work (which typically assumes LICQ or uses double-loop SQP-style algorithms) is the adaptive single-loop primal subprogram that handles constraints without stage-wise switching, combined with a proof that the calmness constraint qualification holds under the specific linear-in-F structure of the constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, flexible way to specify arbitrary trade-off preferences in multi-objective learning—useful for fairness-accuracy balancing, drug design, and multilingual speech recognition—while the single-loop algorithm with O(T^{-1}) convergence under proximal PL offers a practical efficiency gain over double-loop alternatives. Limitations include higher per-iteration complexity compared to scalarization, reliance on standard smoothness and boundedness assumptions, and the proximal PL condition whose sufficient conditions remain partially open. The broader question of whether the calmness CQ extends to more general (nonlinear-in-F) constraint structures, and whether the framework scales to many-objective settings (M >> 3), remains open.
