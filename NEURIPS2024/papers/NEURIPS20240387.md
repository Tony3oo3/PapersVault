## Problem

Releasing graph diffusion vectors (e.g., Personalized PageRank) can leak sensitive edge information through link disclosure attacks, yet existing differentially private graph algorithms rely on output perturbation that yields suboptimal privacy-utility tradeoffs. Privacy Amplification by Iteration (PABI), which exploits the contractive structure of iterative processes to achieve non-divergent privacy bounds, had not been adapted to graph diffusions, and the existing PABI analysis was limited to Gaussian noise in ℓ₂ space with impractical set-diameter bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph's adjacency matrix, a single seed node (or neighborhood set), a privacy budget (ε, δ) with δ = 1/#edges, and diffusion hyperparameters (teleport probability β, number of steps K, threshold parameter η). It outputs a noisy personalized PageRank vector over all nodes, from which a top-R ranking list is derived. The privacy guarantee is personalized edge-level RDP: only edges not incident to the seed node are protected, since the seed user already knows their own connections. A projection onto the unit ℓ₁ ball is applied after each noise injection to preserve the stochastic property of the diffusion vector.

## Method

The framework injects dual Laplace noise (ξₖ⁽¹⁾ + ξₖ⁽²⁾) at every diffusion iteration rather than perturbing only the final output, leveraging the fact that graph diffusion maps are strict contractions in ℓ₁ space. A degree-based thresholding function f(x) = min(max(x, −η·d), η·d) clips diffusion values proportionally to node degrees, preventing low-degree nodes from inducing disproportionately large distortion per step. The privacy analysis is the first PABI treatment with Laplace noise in ℓ₁ space, and introduces a novel ∞-Wasserstein distance tracking technique that replaces the infeasible set-diameter bound D with a tracked quantity w_τ = ρ_diff·(1−γ_max^τ)/(1−γ_max), yielding orders-of-magnitude tighter privacy accounting. The resulting RDP bound is non-divergent in the number of iterations K, in contrast to the linear growth from naive composition, and the optimal noise scale σ is calibrated via binary search over the τ parameter.

## Results

On NDCG@100 and Recall@100 for PPR-based node ranking, the proposed noisy graph diffusion framework outperforms both baselines (DP-PUSHFLOWCAP and Edge-Flipping) across all three benchmark datasets (BlogCatalog, Flickr, TheMarker) for privacy budgets ε ranging from 10⁻² to 1. In the strong privacy regime (ε ≤ 0.5), the method demonstrates significant improvement over DP-PUSHFLOWCAP, validating that process-level noise injection achieves a superior privacy-utility tradeoff compared to output perturbation. The calibrated noise scale under the PABI-based Theorem 1 is approximately 10× smaller than that required by the standard RDP composition theorem, and the degree-based thresholding function provides a 0.15–0.2 NDCG margin over uniform thresholding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DP-PUSHFLOWCAP [Epasto et al., NeurIPS 2022], which applies Laplace output perturbation to approximate PPR, and Edge-Flipping, which uses a randomized response mechanism on the adjacency matrix. The proposed method wins on both NDCG@100 and Recall@100 against DP-PUSHFLOWCAP across all three datasets, with the gap being most pronounced in the strong privacy regime (ε ≤ 0.5), where output perturbation degrades sharply. Against Edge-Flipping, the proposed method also dominates on both metrics, and Edge-Flipping is impractical at scale due to its O(|V|²) runtime (it cannot complete on Flickr within the 12-hour limit). Additionally, the proposed method is significantly more robust to the thresholding hyperparameter η, maintaining consistent outperformance over DP-PUSHFLOWCAP across η values from 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁶, whereas DP-PUSHFLOWCAP's performance is more sensitive to this choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Privacy Amplification by Iteration (PABI) framework from Feldman et al. [26] and Altschuler et al. [27], which was previously developed for noisy gradient descent in ℓ₂ space with Gaussian noise, to graph diffusions in ℓ₁ space with Laplace noise. It departs from the dominant output-perturbation approach for private PPR (Epasto et al. [20]) by injecting noise during the diffusion process rather than at the output, and introduces a novel ∞-Wasserstein distance tracking method that replaces the impractical set-diameter bound in prior PABI analyses. The degree-based thresholding function is a theory-informed design that addresses the high sensitivity of low-degree nodes, a problem not handled by uniform clipping in prior private graph algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practically usable privacy-utility tradeoff for graph diffusion tasks, with the ∞-Wasserstein tracking yielding orders-of-magnitude tighter bounds than diameter-based alternatives and a 10× reduction in required noise scale compared to naive composition. However, the results are demonstrated primarily on PPR diffusion under edge-level (and personalized edge-level) privacy; extending to other diffusion types (e.g., heat kernel) and to node-level DP remain open. The degree-based thresholding and Laplace-in-ℓ₁ PABI analysis are transferable tools that could benefit other iterative graph algorithms requiring privacy guarantees.
