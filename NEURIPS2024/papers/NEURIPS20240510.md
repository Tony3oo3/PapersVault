## Problem

Makeup transfer is inherently unsupervised, so prior methods rely on pseudo-paired data synthesized via histogram matching or geometric warping, which produces over-smoothed or artifact-laden targets that misguide training and yield low makeup fidelity. Additionally, different makeup styles impose different demands on source content details (e.g., freckles should be preserved in natural looks but obscured in dramatic ones), yet existing methods offer no mechanism to flexibly control this tradeoff.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image and a reference face image, both processed at 256×256 resolution. The source image is decomposed via BiSeNet face parsing (foreground/background split), 3DDFA-V2 3D shape reconstruction, and a Laplacian pyramid decomposition of the grayscale foreground into L+1 hierarchical texture bands. The reference image provides the makeup representation after random spatial distortions. The output is a single makeup-transferred facial image where the background is inpainted and the facial region is generated by the diffusion model, with the texture detail level (h0–h4) selectable at inference to tune the content-preservation vs. makeup-fidelity tradeoff.

## Method

SHMT is built on a Latent Diffusion Model (downsampling factor 4, UNet denoiser) and trained self-supervised via a decoupling-and-reconstruction paradigm: a single face image is split into content (3D shape + one Laplacian-pyramid texture band) and makeup (spatially distorted foreground) representations, and the model learns to reconstruct the original image from these components, eliminating the need for pseudo-paired data. The Laplacian pyramid hierarchically decomposes texture from fine (h0) to coarse (h4), so selecting a finer band forces the model to distill only low-frequency makeup information (suitable for simple styles) while a coarser band requires high-frequency makeup transfer (suitable for complex styles). The Iterative Dual Alignment (IDA) module computes a spatial-attention-based deformation mapping between content and makeup features, then at each denoising step blends this alignment with a second alignment computed between the noisy intermediate result and the makeup representation, using a timestep-conditioned MLP weight to progressively correct domain-gap alignment errors as the intermediate result approaches the real image domain.

## Results

On the LADN dataset (complex makeup), SHMT-h4 achieves the best FID of 27.01 and CLS of 0.786, outperforming the strongest baseline Stable-Makeup (FID 37.33, CLS 0.767). On the MT dataset, SHMT-h4 reaches FID 24.93 and CLS 0.715, while SHMT-h0 achieves the highest Key-sim of 0.976 for content preservation. Across all three datasets (MT, Wild-MT, LADN), SHMT-h4 consistently attains the lowest FID and highest CLS, while SHMT-h0 consistently attains the highest Key-sim, demonstrating a controllable trade-off between makeup fidelity and content preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHMT-h4 (coarse texture, optimized for complex makeup) outperforms all seven baselines on FID and CLS across all three datasets. On LADN, it reduces FID from 37.33 (Stable-Makeup, the strongest baseline) to 27.01 and improves CLS from 0.767 to 0.786. SHMT-h0 (fine texture, optimized for simple makeup) achieves the best Key-sim on all datasets (0.976 on MT, 0.976 on Wild-MT, 0.978 on LADN), surpassing Stable-Makeup (0.973, 0.968, 0.965) and all GAN-based methods. Among GAN-based baselines, LADN [11] performs worst overall (FID 73.91 on MT, 104.91 on Wild-MT) due to artifacts and content distortions, while CPM [29] is competitive on LADN (FID 40.57) but still trails SHMT-h4 by 13.56 FID points. In the user study, SHMT is preferred 61.5% of the time for simple styles and 86.75% for complex styles, far exceeding the next-best method (EleGANt at 20.25% and CPM at 11.5% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

SHMT builds on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) as its generative backbone but departs fundamentally from the pseudo-paired data synthesis paradigm (histogram matching, geometric warping) that has dominated GAN-based makeup transfer since BeautyGAN. Its self-supervised 'decoupling-and-reconstruction' strategy—reconstructing the input from its own content and makeup components—eliminates the need for any synthetic supervision signal. The hierarchical texture decomposition via Laplacian pyramid and the Iterative Dual Alignment module are novel contributions not present in prior makeup transfer or general style transfer literature, positioning SHMT as the first diffusion-based makeup transfer method to explicitly handle the diversity of makeup styles through a controllable content-preservation knob.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the reliance on pseudo-paired data, SHMT addresses a root cause of low makeup fidelity in prior methods, and the hierarchical texture details provide a practical, interpretable control for the content-preservation vs. makeup-fidelity trade-off that has been implicitly handled (or ignored) by all previous approaches. The method's dependence on pre-trained face parsing and 3D reconstruction models remains a vulnerability, and the computational cost of diffusion-based inference (several seconds per image) limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the self-supervised decoupling-and-reconstruction paradigm generalizes to other appearance-transfer tasks (e.g., hairstyle, clothing) and whether the content-fidelity trade-off can be resolved rather than merely parameterized.
