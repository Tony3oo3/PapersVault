## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods predominantly target CNN-to-CNN pairs with small scale gaps and are evaluated on small datasets, leaving open whether well pre-trained large ViTs can effectively serve as teachers for heterogeneous student architectures (CNN, MLP, ViT) on mainstream large-scale benchmarks. The core challenge is aligning three intertwined differences between teacher and student: feature computing paradigm (self-attention on patches vs. convolution on pixels), model scale, and knowledge density arising from the teacher's massive pre-training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for most experiments, 384×384 for EVA-02-S/14) and produces class labels for ImageNet-1K classification or intermediate feature maps for downstream dense prediction (object detection, instance segmentation, semantic segmentation). A key constraint is that the student backbone is trained from scratch on IN-1K alone—no upstream pre-training data is viewed—while the frozen ViT teacher carries knowledge from datasets up to LAION-2B, making the knowledge density gap the central practical challenge.

## Method

ScaleKD comprises three tightly coupled components. (1) Cross Attention Projector (CAP) bridges the feature computing paradigm gap by patchifying CNN/MLP pixel grids, adding positional embeddings, and applying a transformer decoder block with trainable queries matching the teacher's feature resolution, so that cross-attention models global dependencies on projected student features. (2) Dual-view Feature Mimicking (DFM) runs two complementary distillation paths through CAP: one in the original spatial space to capture global features, and one in the frequency domain where a DCT-based operator removes the dominant direct (zero-frequency) component, forcing the student to mimic the subtle alternative responses that standard feature distillation neglects. (3) Teacher Parameter Perception (TPP) constructs a proxy path by splicing the student's first n−1 stages with the teacher's last stage via CAP, so that feature mimicking in this hybrid path gradually aligns the student's parameter space with the teacher's, transferring pre-training knowledge without access to the pre-training data. The final loss (Eq. 5) combines cross-entropy, DFM losses applied within both TPP sub-paths, and a vanilla logits KD term, with a single balancing hyperparameter β (default 0.6).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ScaleKD achieves a mean top-1 accuracy improvement of 3.94% across 11 teacher-student pairs, with a maximum gain of 6.27% (Mixer-B/14 with BEiT-L/14 teacher). Using Swin-L as teacher, ScaleKD brings ResNet-50 to 82.03% (+3.39%), ViT-B/16 to 85.53% (+3.73%), and Mixer-B/16 to 81.96% (+5.52%) top-1 accuracy. On downstream tasks, ScaleKD-enhanced backbones transfer well: +2.13% mIOU on ADE20K (ResNet-50) and +2.1% AP on MS-COCO object detection (ResNet-50). Critically, ScaleKD outperforms all four pre-training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, cross-modal, hybrid) while viewing up to 195× fewer training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional feature distillation (FitNets [15]), ScaleKD achieves +2.75% and +3.22% top-1 gains on ResNet-50 and Mixer-S respectively, versus only +0.88–1.30% for FD. Against recent KD methods (Table 7), ScaleKD outperforms DIST [50] by 1.30% (83.80 vs 82.50) and DiffKD [51] by 1.30% on Swin-T, and outperforms OFA [36] by 0.70% (82.03 vs 81.33) on ResNet-50, all with fewer training epochs. Against FunMatch [52], ScaleKD achieves 82.55% at 600 epochs vs. FunMatch's 82.31% at 9600 epochs (16× fewer). Against pre-training paradigms (Table 4), ScaleKD-trained ViT-B/16 reaches 85.53% (supervised) and 85.64% (self-supervised) top-1, beating CLIP [13] (85.47%) and EVA-02 [49] (85.80% at 384×384) while viewing 5.58×–195× fewer samples. Against model engineering (Table 8), ScaleKD-enhanced ResNet-50 (82.55%) matches RepViT-2.3M (82.50%) and outperforms ConvNeXt-T (82.14%), while ScaleKD-enhanced Mixer-B/16 (81.96%) surpasses gMLP-B (81.60%) and ResMLP-B24 (81.00%). On CIFAR-100 linear probing (Table 12), ScaleKD-trained ViT-B/14 achieves 89.11% full-shot accuracy, exceeding CLIP OpenAI (88.92%) without viewing any pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation framework (building on Hinton et al.'s logits-based KD loss) from its traditional CNN-to-CNN, small-scale-gap setting to a cross-architecture formulation where large pre-trained ViTs serve as teachers for heterogeneous CNN, MLP, and ViT students. It departs from recent ViT-teacher KD efforts (OFA, ViTKD) that used small, non-pre-trained ViTs, and from frequency-based KD methods (FAM, FreeKD) that operated on CNN pairs, by introducing three coupled components—cross-attention projector, dual-view feature mimicking via DCT, and teacher parameter perception—that jointly address feature paradigm, model scale, and knowledge density gaps. The parameter-space alignment idea (TPP) is a novel perspective distinct from prior teacher parameter reuse methods, which focused on classifier transfer or shortcut removal rather than progressive parameter-space alignment for pre-training knowledge transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes knowledge distillation from a model-compression tool to a training-acceleration technique, demonstrating that a single well-pre-trained ViT teacher can replace the need for expensive upstream pre-training (supervised, self-supervised, cross-modal, or hybrid) on any target student architecture, reducing viewed training samples by up to 195× while matching or exceeding pre-training-based baselines. This has direct practical implications for resource-constrained settings where a strong pre-trained ViT is available but the pre-training data is not. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large teachers (e.g., ViT-22B) or large students (e.g., ViT-L), how it behaves when the teacher and student share the same architecture family, and whether the frequency-domain insight generalizes beyond the specific DCT-based formulation used here.
