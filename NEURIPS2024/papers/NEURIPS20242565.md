## Problem

Existing pessimistic off-policy evaluation approaches analyze concentration properties of pre-defined risk estimators chosen to simplify the analysis, rather than deriving general bounds and then identifying the optimal estimator. This ad hoc design leaves practitioners without a principled way to determine which regularized IPS estimator yields the tightest worst-case risk bound, and existing bounds (clipping, Implicit Exploration) are provably looser than necessary for critical decision-making applications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged bandit feedback as i.i.d. (context, action, cost) tuples with costs in [-1,0], along with a target policy and behavior policy defined over a finite action set. It produces a high-probability risk upper bound parameterized by a smoothing parameter λ (typically set to O(1/√n)), a selected policy from a finite candidate set, and a learned policy via PAC-Bayesian optimization. A key structural detail is that the LS estimator is unbounded yet achieves finite variance for any λ>0, and the PAC-Bayesian variant LS-LIN linearizes the estimator in the target policy to enable first-order optimization over arbitrary policy classes.

## Method

The paper derives a unified family of empirical concentration bounds (Proposition 1) for all regularized IPS estimators whose regularization function h satisfies a mild ordering condition (C1), parameterized by a moment order L and a free parameter λ. Minimizing the L→∞ bound over h reveals that the optimal regularizer is standard IPS, but the bound itself defines a novel estimator—Logarithmic Smoothing (LS)—that applies -log(1-λwc)/λ to each importance-weighted cost, acting as a soft differentiable clipping with parameter 1/λ. LS is proven to concentrate at a sub-Gaussian rate with a variance proxy that improves on the Harmonic estimator, and its upper bound is shown to dominate the Implicit Exploration bound in all scenarios (Proposition 8). For off-policy learning, a linearized variant LS-LIN smooths the behavior propensity rather than the full importance weight, enabling a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound that surpasses all prior PAC-Bayesian OPL bounds for Clipping, Exponential Smoothing, and IX.

## Results

On the kropt dataset, the LS bound achieves a relative radius of 0.362 at 2^8 samples, outperforming SN-ES (1.000), cIPS-EB (0.917), and IX (0.373), with the gap widening as sample size increases (0.035 vs 0.041 for IX at 2^14). In off-policy learning over 200 scenarios, LS-LIN achieves the best guaranteed risk improvement of 26.31% (vs. 24.74% for IX, 21.28% for cvcIPS, 14.48% for cIPS, and 7.78% for ES) while matching IX on true risk improvement (36.76% vs. 36.70%). In off-policy selection, LS identifies the best policy as often as SN while never selecting a policy worse than the behavior policy, unlike point estimators IPS and SN which can select catastrophically worse policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LS bound is provably and empirically tighter than all competitors across every sample size tested. Against IX (Gabbianelli et al., 2024), the closest competitor, LS achieves a 1.6× tighter bound at 2^14 samples (0.035 vs. 0.041) and a 3.3× tighter bound at 2^8 (0.362 vs. 0.373), with the gap widening for stochastic policies and small n. Against cIPS-EB (Swaminathan & Joachims, 2015) and SN-ES (Kuzborskij et al., 2021), LS is dramatically tighter (0.035 vs. 0.138 and 0.257 at 2^14). In OPL, LS-LIN substantially outperforms all PAC-Bayesian baselines on guaranteed risk (26.31% vs. 24.74% for IX, 21.28% for cvcIPS, 14.48% for cIPS, 7.78% for ES) while matching IX on true risk (36.76% vs. 36.70%). In OPS, point estimators IPS and SN can select policies worse than the behavior policy (a catastrophic failure), whereas LS-based pessimistic selection never does while still finding the best policy at a rate comparable to SN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard approach of first choosing a regularized IPS estimator (clipping, exponential smoothing, implicit exploration) and then analyzing its concentration properties. Instead, it derives a unified family of high-probability empirical moment bounds for the entire class of regularized IPS estimators satisfying a mild condition (C1), then identifies the estimator that minimizes the bound. This 'bound-first, estimator-second' philosophy yields Logarithmic Smoothing (LS) as the minimizer of the infinite-moment bound, and recovers clipping as the minimizer of the second-moment bound. The PAC-Bayesian OPL analysis follows the linearized-estimator framework established by Sakhi et al. (2023) and Aouali et al. (2023), with LS-LIN as a new linearized variant that provably dominates the IX PAC-Bayesian bound of Gabbianelli et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, unified theoretical lens for understanding why different regularized IPS estimators concentrate at different rates, and shows that the tightest achievable bound within the regularized IPS family is attained by a simple logarithmic smoothing of importance weights. The practical impact is a drop-in estimator with provably tighter pessimistic bounds for evaluation, selection, and learning, which matters in safety-critical deployment settings. Key limitations include the restriction to negative costs (excluding doubly robust estimators), the i.i.d. assumption precluding sequential RL, and the discrete action space requirement. Extending the framework to doubly robust estimators and the general offline RL setting are identified as nontrivial open problems.
