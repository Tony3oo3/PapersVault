## Problem

Standard diffusion training diffuses each image to any point in the noise space, creating a fully miscible mixture where every noise point is equally associated with every image. This random image-noise mapping makes the denoising function extremely difficult to optimize, resulting in slow convergence (e.g., 10 days on 4 A6000 GPUs for Consistency Models on CIFAR-10). The paper identifies this miscibility as an under-explored bottleneck and shows it dominates over flow-straightening (optimal transport) effects in explaining training inefficiency.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard diffusion model inputs: random Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on class labels or text prompts) at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (ImageNet), producing synthetic RGB images. During training, the key modification occurs before noise is added: within each mini-batch, noise samples are permuted via linear assignment so that each image receives a spatially nearby noise sample in the shared image-noise space, while the overall batch noise distribution remains exactly Gaussian. No changes are made to the model architecture, noise scheduler, sampler, or inference procedure.

## Method

Immiscible Diffusion introduces an assignment-then-diffusion training strategy: before adding noise to images, a batch-wise linear assignment (solved via the Hungarian algorithm) minimizes the total L2 distance between image and noise samples, so each image is only diffused to nearby noise regions rather than arbitrary ones. This creates 'immiscibility' in the noise space, making the denoising target locally coherent instead of a uniform average over all images. To handle the O(N³) complexity of the Hungarian algorithm, the authors quantize images and noise from fp32 to fp16 before computing the distance matrix, reducing overhead to ~22.8 ms for a batch of 1024 on an A6000. The entire modification is a single line of code (scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment) applied only during training. Ablations confirm that the immiscibility property—not the optimal-transport straightening—is the dominant factor: a non-OT variant that preserves immiscibility while breaking the transport structure achieves nearly identical training speedups.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Immiscible Consistency Model achieves FID 10.0 in 190k training steps versus >800k for the baseline, yielding a ~3x training speedup. For DDIM on CIFAR-10, Immiscible DDIM reduces FID from 10.03 to 7.46 at 20 inference steps (ΔFID −2.57), with gains growing as inference steps decrease. For class-conditional Stable Diffusion on ImageNet (20k steps), Immiscible Diffusion achieves FID 16.43 vs. 17.92 for the baseline, and for fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v1.4 (5k steps), FID drops from 11.45 to 10.28. The assignment overhead is negligible at 22.8 ms per step for a batch size of 1024 on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Consistency Model baseline [Song et al., 2023], Immiscible Diffusion achieves a 3× training speedup on CIFAR-10 (190k vs. >800k steps for FID 10) and similar gains on CelebA (~1.3×) and Tiny ImageNet (~1.2×), while also reaching lower final FIDs. Against DDIM [Song et al., 2021], Immiscible DDIM wins at every inference step tested, with the largest margin at 20 steps (FID 7.46 vs. 10.03, a 2.57-point improvement) and a smaller but consistent gap at 1000 steps (3.67 vs. 3.82). Against Stable Diffusion [Rombach et al., 2021], Immiscible Diffusion improves class-conditional FID by 1.49 points (16.43 vs. 17.92) at 20k steps on ImageNet and reduces fine-tuning FID from 11.45 to 10.28 at 5k steps, without degrading CLIPScore (28.55 for both). The paper also ablates the optimal-transport component and shows that a non-OT variant of Immiscible Diffusion still achieves comparable training speedup, confirming that the immiscibility property—not path straightening—is the dominant factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying the 'miscibility problem' in diffusion training—where random image-noise mapping creates a jumbled mixture that hinders denoising optimization—and proposing a physics-inspired solution. While conceptually adjacent to batch-wise optimal transport in flow matching (Pooladian et al., Tong et al.), the paper explicitly argues that the dominant mechanism is not path straightening (OT) but rather making the image-noise assignment distinguishable (immiscibility), supported by a non-OT ablation. It is orthogonal to prior training-efficiency work on activation magnitudes (Karras et al.), consistency model design (Song & Dhariwal), and patch strategies (Wang et al.), and repurposes the classical Hungarian assignment algorithm in a novel quantized form for this purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 3x training speedup achievable with a single line of code is practically significant for the iterative development cycle of diffusion models, and the method requires no architectural changes, scheduler modifications, or inference-time overhead. However, validation is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA, tiny ImageNet, 10% ImageNet) and the authors acknowledge the lack of validation on large-scale corpora like LAION. Open questions include whether the assignment strategy generalizes to text-to-image/video at full scale, whether more sophisticated (non-Hungarian) assignment schemes yield further gains, and how the method interacts with latent-space diffusion at higher resolutions where the 2% distance reduction may behave differently.
