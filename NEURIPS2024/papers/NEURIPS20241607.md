## Problem

Cross-modal hashing with incomplete multi-label annotations suffers from pairwise uncertainty: as unknown labels increase, negative pairs vanish entirely (e.g., 35% unknown on Flickr removes all negatives), collapsing similarity-preserving hash learning. While CLIP's descriptive completeness prior could in principle recover missing labels, its zero-shot class-sample similarity scores achieve only 68% recovery precision, and no prior work has applied CLIP-based label recovery to cross-modal hashing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs whose label vectors contain three states per class (positive, negative, unknown), evaluated at 30%, 50%, and 70% known-label ratios. It produces 32-bit binary hash codes for both modalities via 4-layer MLPs on frozen ViT-B-32 CLIP features, along with recovered positive labels from the unknown set and soft pseudo-labels (via a hard-sigmoid window) for remaining unknowns. The similarity matrix used for hash training is real-valued in [0,1] rather than binary, reflecting the estimated confidence from recovered and augmented labels.

## Method

PCRIL introduces a learnable multi-label prompt that encodes an anchor subset of positive classes into CLIP text embeddings, then constructs three types of negative variants (deletion, addition, replacement) and trains a margin-based contrastive loss so that the anchor set's matching score exceeds all negatives. A greedy Potential Label Tree Search (PLTS) then iteratively adds the unknown class that maximizes the set score until a monotonic-increase termination condition is met, recovering positive labels and assigning pseudo-labels to the rest. Two augmentation strategies handle extreme cases: an asymmetric complementary mixup that blends a sample's features with a counterpart whose positive labels cover its unknowns, and an adaptive negative masking scheme that randomly reassigns a controlled fraction of unknown pairs as negatives to restore the positive-to-negative ratio. The hash objective uses KL divergence between the estimated real-valued similarity and the sigmoid of hash inner products, replacing the standard binary cross-entropy used in conventional CMH.

## Results

PCRIL achieves mean mAP of 81.4 on MIRFlickr-25k, 69.9 on NUS-WIDE, and 65.3 on MS COCO (32-bit codes, averaged across 30%/50%/70% known-label ratios and both retrieval directions). This represents an average 12.13% mAP improvement over the closest CLIP-based baseline DCHMT and 8.96% over the best deep CMH method (AGAH) across all three datasets. The gains are most pronounced under high incompleteness: at 30% known labels, PCRIL outperforms the second-best method by 4.8 mAP on Flickr, 3.1 on NUS, and 2.2 on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCRIL consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and incompleteness level. Against the strongest non-CLIP deep method AGAH, PCRIL gains +7.8 mAP on Flickr (81.4 vs. 73.6), +13.4 on NUS (69.9 vs. 56.5), and +6.8 on COCO (65.3 vs. 58.5). Against DCHMT, the only other CLIP-based CMH method, the improvement is +7.6 on Flickr, +16.8 on NUS, and +12.9 on COCO, confirming that simply using a CLIP backbone is insufficient without the proposed prompt contrastive recovery. Shallow methods (DCH, SDMCH, SCRATCH) and earlier deep methods (DCMH, SSAH) degrade severely under high incompleteness—DCMH drops to 30.4 mAP on NUS at 30% known labels—while PCRIL maintains 68.7, demonstrating robustness to pairwise uncertainty. The WCHash baseline (cited at 70% known only) scores just 62.6 on Flickr, far below PCRIL's 84.9 at the same setting. On the additional IAPR TC-12 dataset, PCRIL achieves 45.5/49.0/56.2 mAP at 30%/50%/70% known, surpassing DCHMT (39.2/47.9/56.3) and SSAH (33.4/39.2/45.3) at the two hardest settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: cross-modal hashing (DCMH, SSAH, AGAH, DCHMT), learning with incomplete labels (Bucak, Durand, Cole, Sun, Ding), and CLIP prompt tuning (Zhou et al., Sun et al.). It departs from prior CMH methods that assume complete annotations by introducing a CLIP-based label recovery stage, and from prior incomplete-label methods (which focus on single-modality image recognition or shallow hashing) by formulating a multi-label set prompt with contrastive margin learning over deletion/addition/replacement negatives. The greedy tree search (PLTS) for recursive label completion and the asymmetric complementary mixup are novel architectural choices not present in either the CMH or the incomplete-label literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: large-scale multi-modal datasets cannot be exhaustively annotated, and the paper demonstrates that even 20–35% unknown labels can eliminate all negative pairs, collapsing similarity learning in standard CMH. The 12% mAP gap over the closest CLIP-based baseline (DCHMT) is substantial, though partly inflated by the absence of a dedicated deep CMH method for incomplete labels. Key limitations include CLIP's 77-token text budget (restricting very richly labeled samples), dependence on multi-labeled training samples (single-positive cases remain unaddressed), and evaluation limited to image-text pairs. Open questions include whether the prompt-contrastive paradigm transfers to other modalities (audio, video) or to single-positive-label regimes, and whether the tree search can be replaced by differentiable alternatives for end-to-end training.
