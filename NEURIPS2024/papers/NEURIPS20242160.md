## Problem

Sparse-reward reinforcement learning requires long, coordinated action sequences to obtain any reward signal, making exploration extremely difficult. Existing skill-learning approaches that address this by extracting temporally extended actions from demonstrations rely on neural networks (VAEs, sequence models) that are expensive to train and slow at inference. The paper asks whether a far simpler, non-parametric method can extract useful skills that accelerate sparse-reward RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242160/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The skill-extraction stage consumes only action sequences from demonstrations (no observations or rewards), discretizing continuous actions via k-means into k=2×d_act tokens before tokenization. The output is a finite vocabulary of N_min=16 skills, each a fixed-length (L=10) sequence of primitive actions, which becomes the discrete action space for a downstream SAC-discrete policy. Because skills are observation-free, they transfer across environments that share the same action space, even when extracted from as few as 1% of demonstrations in a related task.

## Method

The core mechanism applies Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), a subword tokenization algorithm from NLP, to discretized action sequences: after k-means clustering of continuous actions, BPE iteratively merges the most frequent neighboring token pairs until a large intermediate vocabulary (N_max=10^6) is built, then prunes to the N_min most frequent subwords of length L=10. This yields a finite, interpretable set of skills (e.g., linear motion and turns in AntMaze, reaching and pulling in Kitchen) without any neural network for extraction or rollout. A standard 4-layer MLP policy trained with SAC-discrete selects among these skills at each decision point, eliminating the need for a generative model at inference. The result is ~1000× faster skill generation and ~100× faster rollouts compared to VAE-based baselines like SSP, while achieving competitive or superior performance on AntMaze, Kitchen, and CoinRun.

## Results

On AntMaze (U/M/L) and Kitchen, SaS achieves strong returns while all baselines (SAC, SAC-discrete, SSP, SFP) mostly score 0, with SaS reaching near-optimal performance on AntMaze-M (score 1/1) and Kitchen (score ~3-4/4). On CoinRun, SaS is competitive with baselines rather than dominant. The method achieves a 1000× speedup in skill extraction (3 s vs. 130,000 s for SSP) and a 100× speedup in policy rollout (0.007 s vs. 0.9 s for SSP), translating to ~10 h total training vs. 24 h (SSP) and ~1 week (SFP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC and SAC-discrete, SaS decisively wins on AntMaze and Kitchen, where both baselines fail to find any reward (score 0) due to poor exploration, while SaS achieves near-optimal returns; on CoinRun the gap narrows and SaS is roughly competitive. Against SSP (Skill-Space Policy, a VAE-based skill prior [57]), SaS outperforms on all sparse-reward tasks where SSP scores 0, and is ~1000× faster in skill generation and ~100× faster per rollout. Against SFP (State-Free Priors [6]), SaS again wins on performance and is dramatically faster (SFP requires ~2 h for skill generation and 4.1 s per rollout vs. 3 s and 0.007 s for SaS). Against the observation-conditioned SPiRL [57], the comparison is task-dependent: SPiRL achieves stronger results on Kitchen (where demonstrations exactly overlap the downstream task) but struggles on AntMaze where demonstrations are random trajectories; SaS wins on AntMaze. OPAL [2] is a closed-source concurrent method; the paper reports its published numbers for reference but does not re-run it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), a subword tokenization algorithm from NLP, for skill discovery in sparse-reward RL, departing from the dominant neural-network-based approaches (VAEs in SSP/SPiRL, sequence models in SFP/OPAL) that learn continuous latent skill representations. It builds on the action-discretization idea from Shafiullah et al. (Behavior Transformers) and Dadashi et al., applying k-means clustering to form a seed vocabulary before running BPE. The conceptual lineage connects to the options framework and hierarchical RL, but the key departure is replacing learned generative skill models with a deterministic, interpretable finite vocabulary of temporally extended actions extracted purely from action sequences without observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× speedup in skill extraction (seconds vs. hours) and 100× speedup in rollout make skill-based RL dramatically more accessible, and the finite vocabulary yields interpretable, inspectable skills — a practical advantage over continuous latent skill spaces. The finding that skills extracted from 1% of demonstrations in one AntMaze variant transfer to another suggests a path toward lightweight, cross-task skill libraries. Key limitations remain: open-loop execution of subwords limits safety and adaptivity, discretization loses action resolution (hurting fast locomotion), and the method's performance advantage is partly inflated by baselines (SAC, SSP, SFP) mostly achieving zero return. Open questions include whether closed-loop or residual-corrected subwords can close the gap to continuous-action performance, and whether the BPE hierarchy generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-agent settings.
