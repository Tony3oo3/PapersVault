## Problem

Transformers fail at multi-digit arithmetic primarily because they cannot reliably represent the exact position of each digit within a long number, making it impossible to align digits of the same significance. Prior work identified addition as difficult for transformers but did not isolate this positional bottleneck, and existing positional embeddings (FIRE, NoPE, RoPE) limit length generalization to at most 2.5× the training operand length. This paper asks whether fixing digit-position representation unlocks both in-distribution accuracy and extreme-length logical extrapolation.

## Input / Output

Inputs are arithmetic problems (addition, subtraction, multiplication) formatted least-significant-digit-first with no inter-digit padding and no zero-padding of shorter operands, e.g. '9 8 2 8 2 + 3 8 5 9 1 7 2 ='. For sorting, inputs are arrays of reversed numbers tagged with alphabetical indices (e.g. 'a:64957, b:99963, ...'). The model is trained with loss computed only on the answer digits (the question is masked), and outputs are generated via greedy decoding with a character-level tokenizer. Evaluation uses strict exact-match accuracy across all output digits, with separate in-distribution, out-of-distribution (up to 100 digits), and extreme OOD (100–160 digits) categories.

## Method

The core contribution is Abacus Embeddings: learned positional embeddings applied per-digit that encode each digit's position relative to the start of its number, so all digits of the same significance share the same embedding. During training, consecutive positional indices start from a random offset β ~ U[1,k] (default k=100), shared across all numbers in a batch, exposing the model to a wide range of positional codes even on short sequences; at test time β is fixed to 1. These embeddings are additive and compatible with standard positional schemes (NoPE, FIRE, RoPE). On top of this, the paper combines Abacus Embeddings with two architectural modifications: input injection (skip connections adding the embedded input to every decoder layer) and looped transformers (a small recurrent block of shared-weight decoder layers repeated multiple times with progressive loss). Trained from scratch on 20-digit operands with 8 exaFLOP (one GPU, 24 hours), the resulting models achieve 99% exact-match accuracy on 100-digit addition—a 6× length generalization factor—versus 2.5× for prior state-of-the-art.

## Results

Trained on 20-digit operands with a single GPU for 24 hours, Abacus Embeddings enable a depth-16 decoder-only transformer to achieve up to 99% exact match accuracy on 100-digit addition, a 6× length generalization factor (previous SOTA was 2.5×). Combining Abacus Embeddings with input injection and a looped transformer architecture further improves OOD accuracy from 92.9% to 99.1%, an 87% reduction in error. For 15-digit integer multiplication, Abacus Embeddings with a looped transformer achieve near-perfect in-distribution accuracy, outperforming FIRE embeddings on the hardest in-distribution problems. On array sorting (30×30 OOD), the Abacus+FIRE combination achieves 4.48% exact match accuracy versus 3.73% for FIRE alone.

| Embedding | OOD (number length–30) | OOD (array length–30) | All OOD (30×30) | All OOD (20×20) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIRE | 55.32 | 21.35 | 3.73 | 14.65 |
| Abacus | **68.63** | 9.67 | 2.65 | 9.78 |
| Abacus + FIRE | 67.28 | 21.11 | **4.48** | **16.91** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FIRE embeddings (Li et al., 2023), Abacus Embeddings dramatically outperform on addition: FIRE achieves only 2.5× length generalization (to ~50 digits from 20-digit training), while Abacus reaches 6× (to 120 digits), with 99% accuracy at 100 digits versus FIRE's much lower OOD accuracy (Figure 1, Figure 3). On sorting, Abacus wins on number-length extrapolation (68.63% vs. 55.32%) but loses on array-length extrapolation (9.67% vs. 21.35%), while the Abacus+FIRE combination is best on the joint 30×30 OOD setting (4.48% vs. 3.73%). Against NoPE (Kazemnejad et al., 2023), Abacus Embeddings consistently improve both in-distribution and OOD addition accuracy across all architecture variants (Figure 3, Figure 17). Against RoPE (Su et al., 2024), combining Abacus with RoPE improves operand-length generalization over RoPE alone (Figure 7), though the gain is smaller than with FIRE. Against the index-hints + randomized FIRE method of Zhou et al. (2024), Abacus Embeddings are substantially more robust: Zhou et al. report variance from near-perfect to 0% accuracy depending on random seed, while Abacus Embeddings maintain consistent high accuracy across multiple runs (Figure 21, Section 3.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Randomized Positional Encodings (Ruoss et al., 2023), modifying the scheme to use consecutive ascending indices with a random starting offset rather than fully random indices, yielding what the authors call Abacus Embeddings. It sits within the broader line of work on positional embeddings for length generalization (FIRE, NoPE, RoPE, Alibi) and on teaching transformers arithmetic via least-significant-digit-first formatting (Zhou et al., 2023, 2024; Lee et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2023). The architectural side draws on looped transformers and input injection from the algorithmic reasoning literature (Bansal et al., 2022; Giannou et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023a), combining these with the new embeddings in a way not previously explored for arithmetic. The key conceptual departure from prior work is identifying digit alignment within numbers as the primary bottleneck rather than the addition operation itself, and addressing it with a targeted, lightweight embedding rather than more complex architectural changes or index hints in the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× length generalization factor (vs. 2.5× prior SOTA) and near-perfect 100-digit addition accuracy from 20-digit training represent a substantial practical improvement, and the Abacus Embeddings technique is simple enough to integrate into general-purpose models alongside RoPE or FIRE. The finding that a 12M-parameter looped transformer outperforms a 122M-parameter standard transformer on this task has broader implications for efficient algorithmic reasoning. However, the paper explicitly notes it does not test natural language tasks, leaving open whether Abacus Embeddings degrade or help on heterogeneous workloads. The sorting results are mixed (Abacus helps number-length generalization but hurts array-length generalization), suggesting the technique's transferability to non-arithmetic algorithmic tasks is not yet fully characterized.
