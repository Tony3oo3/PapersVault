## Problem

Achieving interventional fairness from observational data when the true causal DAG is unknown remains challenging. Existing approaches either discard descendant variables (hurting prediction accuracy) or rely on strong identification assumptions (e.g., linearity, additive noise) to estimate causal effects from partially directed graphs. This paper asks whether one can exploit all available variables while guaranteeing interventional fairness using only a CPDAG or MPDAG, without global enumeration of all equivalent DAGs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational tabular data (covariates X, sensitive attribute A, outcome Y), a CPDAG or MPDAG learned via constraint-based causal discovery (e.g., PC algorithm), and optionally a set of directed background-knowledge edges to refine the CPDAG into an MPDAG via Meek's rules. It outputs a prediction model Ŷ trained under a worst-case fairness constraint, the enumerated set of possible adjustment sets for A, and per-individual estimated causal effects of A on Ŷ for each adjustment set. The local nature of the algorithm means only the neighborhood of A in the graph is needed, avoiding infeasible global DAG enumeration.

## Method

The core insight is that all possible parental sets of the sensitive attribute A can be identified locally on a CPDAG/MPDAG: a subset S(A) of A's undirected neighbors is a valid parent set iff the induced subgraph over S(A) is complete (no new v-structure), and for MPDAGs additionally no directed triangle through A is formed. For each valid parental set, the method estimates a propensity score via logistic regression and then computes the post-intervention distribution P(Ŷ|do(A=a)) through the back-door adjustment formula. The prediction model is then trained via a min-max joint optimization that alternately updates (i) treatment-effect estimation models for each possible adjustment set and (ii) the predictor itself, which minimizes prediction error plus a worst-case fairness penalty γ·max_m (1/N)Σξ_i^(m) where slack variables ξ_i^(m) penalize estimated causal effects exceeding a threshold C. This avoids discarding any variables and does not require the true DAG to be uniquely identifiable.

## Results

On the real-world COMPAS dataset, the proposed method achieves the best RMSE (0.219±0.020) and lowest unfairness (0.179±0.011) among all baselines. On the Adult dataset, it achieves the best RMSE (0.375±0.019) while ε-IFair achieves slightly lower unfairness (0.155 vs. 0.171). On OULAD, the method attains the best RMSE (0.491±0.040) and lowest unfairness (0.024±0.018). On synthetic benchmarks (e.g., NODE=20, EDGE=40, Noise=1.5), the method achieves RMSE 0.561±0.126 and unfairness 0.049±0.073, outperforming all fairness-aware baselines on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of prior methods: variable-selection approaches (Fair and FairRelax from Zuo et al., NeurIPS 2022) and a constrained-optimization approach (ε-IFair from Zuo et al., ICLR 2024). Against Fair (which uses only definite non-descendants of the sensitive attribute), the proposed method consistently achieves lower RMSE and lower unfairness across all synthetic and real-world settings, because Fair discards too many informative variables. Against FairRelax (which adds possible descendants but imposes no explicit fairness constraint), the proposed method again wins on both metrics, as FairRelax's relaxed variable set can still encode sensitive-attribute information without correction. Against ε-IFair, the proposed method achieves lower RMSE in nearly all settings (e.g., 0.375 vs. 0.413 on Adult; 0.219 vs. 0.235 on COMPAS) and lower unfairness on COMPAS and OULAD, though ε-IFair edges out slightly on Adult unfairness (0.155 vs. 0.171). The advantage over ε-IFair is attributed to the local enumeration of all possible adjustment sets rather than relying on a single identified causal effect, making the method more robust when the learned CPDAG is imperfect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the IDA framework (Maathuis et al., 2009) and its extension to background knowledge (Fang and He, 2020) to locally enumerate possible parental sets of a sensitive attribute on a CPDAG/MPDAG, avoiding the infeasible global enumeration of all DAGs in a Markov equivalence class. It departs from two prior lines of work on interventional fairness with partially known causal graphs: variable selection methods (Fair/FairRelax, Zuo et al. 2022) that discard descendant variables at the cost of prediction accuracy, and causal effect identification methods (ε-IFair, Zuo et al. 2024) that require strong identification assumptions. The key conceptual shift is reframing the problem from 'identify the causal effect' to 'bound all possible causal effects and control the worst case' via a min-max optimization, enabling the use of all variables without additional identification assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the practically critical scenario where the true causal DAG is unknown and only a CPDAG or MPDAG is available, providing a method that achieves interventional fairness while retaining prediction accuracy by using all covariates. The local enumeration approach is computationally efficient compared to global DAG enumeration, making the method scalable. Key limitations include reliance on the accuracy of the learned CPDAG and propensity estimates (inaccuracies lead to mild fairness violations), and the method does not handle hidden confounders. An important open question, noted by the authors, is extending the framework to settings with unobserved variables and partially known DAGs.
