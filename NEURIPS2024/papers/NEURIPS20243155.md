## Problem

Classical inverse optimization (IO) methods learn the objective function of an expert's forward optimization problem from observed signal-decision pairs, but they rely on finite-dimensional feature spaces that oversimplify complex continuous control tasks. While kernel methods could extend the hypothesis class to infinite-dimensional RKHS spaces, the resulting semidefinite program scales quadratically with dataset size, making it intractable for the large datasets typical in imitation learning (e.g., 100k+ samples). This paper addresses both the expressiveness gap and the scalability bottleneck simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a dataset of exogenous signals (continuous states) paired with observed decisions (continuous actions), along with known linear constraint matrices M and W that define the feasible set for each state. The output is a nonparametric quadratic forward optimization problem whose objective coefficients are expressed as finite linear combinations of kernel evaluations (Gaussian RBF) over the training set, rather than as a fixed parameter vector. At inference time, given a new state, the learned FOP is solved to produce the action that best replicates the expert's behavior, with the number of kernel terms growing with the training set size.

## Method

The paper derives a Lagrangian dual reformulation (Theorem 1) of the suboptimality-loss IO problem in which the learned objective depends only on scalar kernel evaluations κ(s_i, s_j) rather than explicit feature mappings, yielding a form consistent with the representer theorem. A simplified variant (Corollary 1) fixes θ_uu = I_n, reducing the problem to a semidefinite program with per-sample PSD constraints. To solve this at scale, the Sequential Selection Optimization (SSO) algorithm performs coordinate descent over the per-sample dual variables (Λ_i, Γ_i), selecting batches of p coordinates at each iteration using a KKT-violation heuristic inspired by SMO, and initializing via a warm-up trick that solves small subproblems on data partitions. This reduces per-iteration cost from O(N²) to O(p²) while provably converging to the same solution as the centralized SDP solver.

## Results

On six MuJoCo continuous control tasks from D4RL, KIO trained with only 5k–10k data points achieves normalized returns close to or exceeding the teacher agent in 4 out of 6 tasks (e.g., 109.9 on Hopper-expert vs. teacher 108.5; 74.6 on Walker2d-medium vs. teacher 68.1). The non-kernel IO baseline collapses to near-zero scores on Walker2d and HalfCheetah tasks, confirming the necessity of the kernelized hypothesis class. The SSO algorithm matches the centralized SCS solver's objective value to within 0.001 while achieving slightly higher task scores in 5 of 6 tasks after just 20 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-kernel IO baseline [Akhtar et al., 2021], KIO dominates across all six tasks: the gap is most dramatic on Walker2d-medium (74.6 vs. 0.0) and Halfcheetah-expert (84.4 vs. −1.7), where IO essentially fails to learn a useful policy. Against BC(TD3+BC) [Fujimoto & Gu, 2021], which uses 1M samples, KIO wins on Hopper-expert (109.9 vs. 111.5, near-tie), Hopper-medium (50.2 vs. 30.0), and Walker2d-medium (74.6 vs. 11.4), but loses on Walker2d-expert (108.5 vs. 56.0 — actually KIO wins here) and Halfcheetah-expert (84.4 vs. 105.2). Against BC(CQL) [Kumar et al., 2020], KIO wins on Hopper-expert (109.9 vs. 109.0), Hopper-medium (50.2 vs. 29.0), and Walker2d-medium (74.6 vs. 6.6), but loses on Walker2d-expert (108.5 vs. 125.7) and Halfcheetah-expert (84.4 vs. 107.0). The SSO algorithm [proposed] matches the SCS centralized solver [O'Donoghue et al., 2016] in objective value (e.g., 185.220 vs. 185.219 on Hopper-expert) while achieving marginally higher task scores in 5 of 6 tasks, validating its effectiveness as a scalable alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse optimization framework of Akhtar et al. [2], which learns a quadratic forward optimization problem from observed signal-decision pairs, by lifting the hypothesis class from a finite-dimensional feature space to an infinite-dimensional RKHS via kernel methods. The suboptimality loss from Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn [25] provides the training objective, while the SSO algorithm draws on coordinate descent and SMO-style heuristics [30, 37] to address the quadratic scaling of kernel methods. Prior kernelized IO work [35, 8] demonstrated the potential of kernels in this setting, but this paper provides a clean Lagrangian dual reformulation (Theorem 1) that decouples the problem from the feature dimensionality and yields a nonparametric FOP consistent with the representer theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic performance gap between kernel and non-kernel IO (e.g., Walker2d-expert: 108.5 vs. 0.9) suggests that finite-dimensional feature spaces are fundamentally insufficient for complex continuous control tasks, positioning kernel IO as a more expressive alternative to behavior cloning in low-data regimes. However, the model remains nonparametric—adding a new data point requires recomputing all coefficients—and the SSO algorithm still demands up to 256 GB of memory at 100k samples, limiting practical deployment. Open questions include establishing convergence rate guarantees for SSO, developing online/incremental variants to avoid full retraining, and reducing memory footprint through sparsification or approximation.
