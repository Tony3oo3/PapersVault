## Problem

DeltaNet, a linear transformer that replaces the additive memory update with a delta rule for improved associative recall, was originally trained with a strictly sequential algorithm that cannot parallelize over sequence length. This made it hardware-inefficient on modern GPUs and prevented scaling to standard language modeling settings (e.g., 1B+ parameters on 100B+ tokens), leaving it unclear whether its recall advantages would hold at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence and produces next-token predictions in a standard autoregressive fashion. Internally, each token is projected into query, key, and value vectors (with SiLU activation and L2 normalization on keys/queries), and the hidden state is a d×d matrix updated via a generalized Householder transformation at each step. The output at each position is o_t = S_t q_t, where S_t is the matrix-valued recurrent state. The chunkwise algorithm processes sequences in blocks of size C (typically 64–128), propagating state chunk-to-chunk while computing intra-chunk outputs in parallel via matrix multiplications.

## Method

The key insight is that DeltaNet's recurrence S_t = S_{t-1}(I − β_t k_t k_t^T) + β_t v_t k_t^T can be viewed as applying a generalized Householder transformation, enabling the use of the WY representation to express products of these transformations in O(d) memory rather than materializing the full d×d state at each step. This yields a 'pseudo value' u_t = β_t(v_t − Σ_{i<t} u_i(k_i^T k_t)) that replaces the original value vector, reducing the problem to a form analogous to standard linear attention. The chunkwise parallel form then propagates state between chunks via S_{[t+1]} = S_{[t]} + (U_{[t]} − W_{[t]} S_{[t]}^T) K_{[t]}, while intra-chunk outputs are computed as O_{[t]} = Q_{[t]} S_{[t]}^T + (Q_{[t]} K_{[t]}^T ⊙ M)(U_{[t]} − W_{[t]} S_{[t]}^T). A UT transform converts the remaining sequential computation of W and U into a single lower-triangular matrix inverse, making the entire forward pass expressible as matrix multiplications suitable for tensor cores. The model follows a LLaMA-style architecture with the DeltaNet layer replacing self-attention, and hybrid variants interleave DeltaNet with sliding-window or global attention layers.

## Results

At the 1.3B/100B-token scale, DeltaNet achieves a Wikitext perplexity of 17.25 and LAMBADA perplexity of 14.92, outperforming Mamba (17.06/13.89) on recall-intensive tasks (SWDE 52.4 vs 41.4, SQuAD 37.4 vs 35.2, FDA 22.3 vs 6.2) while trailing GLA (17.22/14.47) on perplexity and SQuAD. The hybrid DeltaNet + Global Attention model achieves the best overall performance with Wikitext ppl 16.55, LAMBADA ppl 12.40, and an average common-sense score of 51.8, surpassing the Transformer++ baseline (16.85/13.44/50.9). On the synthetic MAD benchmark, DeltaNet scores 71.8 average accuracy, outperforming Mamba (69.3) and GLA (60.0) but trailing Transformer (74.5), with perfect scores (100%) on In-Context Recall, Noisy Recall, and Selective Copy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba, DeltaNet clearly wins on all three recall-intensive tasks (SWDE 52.4 vs 41.4, SQuAD 37.4 vs 35.2, FDA 22.3 vs 6.2) and on average common-sense accuracy (50.4 vs 50.0), though Mamba achieves slightly better Wikitext perplexity (17.06 vs 17.25) and LAMBADA perplexity (13.89 vs 14.92). Against GLA, the comparison is mixed: DeltaNet wins on SWDE (52.4 vs 50.6) and FDA (22.3 vs 19.9), but GLA wins on Wikitext ppl (17.22 vs 17.25), LAMBADA ppl (14.47 vs 14.92), SQuAD (42.6 vs 37.4), and average common-sense (51.0 vs 50.4); the authors attribute DeltaNet's SQuAD gap to its poorer state-size scalability at 1.3B. Against Transformer++, pure DeltaNet is competitive but slightly behind on perplexity, while the hybrid DeltaNet + Global Attention model decisively outperforms Transformer++ on every metric (Wiki ppl 16.55 vs 16.85, LMB ppl 12.40 vs 13.44, SWDE 71.0 vs 66.6, SQuAD 43.0 vs 31.5, FDA 29.8 vs 27.4). On the synthetic MAD benchmark, DeltaNet (71.8 avg) beats Mamba (69.3) and GLA (60.0) but trails Transformer (74.5), with its strongest advantage on Fuzzy Recall (35.7 vs 6.7 for Mamba). At the 3B scale, DeltaNet-3B (59.8 avg) slightly underperforms PowerLM-3B (62.3) and Llama-3.2-3B (62.8) but outperforms RecurrentGemma-2B (57.9), RWKV-6-3B (54.9), and Mamba-2.7B (53.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DeltaNet (Schlag et al., 2021), which replaced the additive update in linear transformers with a delta-rule update for improved associative recall, by deriving a hardware-efficient chunkwise parallel training algorithm. The key algorithmic insight is reparameterizing the DeltaNet recurrence as a product of generalized Householder transformations and applying the WY representation (Bischof & Loan, 1985) to avoid materializing d×d hidden states, enabling the chunkwise parallel strategy from FlashLinearAttention (Yang et al., 2023) and Hua et al. (2022) to be extended to the delta-rule case. The UT transform (Joffrain et al., 2006) is further leveraged to express the intra-chunk computation as matrix multiplications suitable for tensor cores. The paper also builds on the broader linear-attention/linear-RNN literature (Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2023; Gu & Dao, 2023) and the hybrid architecture paradigm (De et al., 2024; Lieber et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the primary practical barrier to using DeltaNet at scale, enabling competitive 1.3B and 3B language models that outperform Mamba and GLA on perplexity and zero-shot downstream tasks, with hybrid variants (DeltaNet + sliding-window or global attention) surpassing Transformer++ baselines. However, the paper identifies a key limitation: DeltaNet's state size does not scale as effectively as GLA's for recall-intensive tasks at larger scales, and its length generalization is weaker due to the absence of explicit decay factors. Open questions include whether block-diagonal Householder transition matrices can recover larger effective state sizes, and how to further enhance the delta rule's expressiveness (e.g., via recurrent or self-referential extensions) without sacrificing the sequence-level parallelism that this paper establishes.
