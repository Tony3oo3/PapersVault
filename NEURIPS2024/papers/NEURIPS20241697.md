## Problem

The NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track was created to elevate dataset quality, yet no systematic framework existed to assess whether published datasets actually met rigorous documentation and curation standards. This paper asks how well curated NeurIPS datasets are and identifies where documentation practices fall short of established data curation norms drawn from library, archival, and information sciences.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes the full documentation of a NeurIPS D&B paper (main text, datasheets, data statements, and supplementary materials) and produces structured scores for 18 rubric elements across five categories. Each element receives a binary minimum-standard score (pass/fail) and a three-level excellence score (full/partial/none), yielding a per-dataset documentation quality profile and per-element completion rates that can be aggregated across the track to identify systemic gaps.

## Method

The core contribution is a rubric-and-toolkit evaluation framework that translates data curation lifecycle models, FAIR principles, and environmental sustainability considerations from library and archival sciences into a practical ML dataset documentation assessment tool. The rubric comprises 18 elements across five categories (scope, ethicality and reflexivity, data pipeline, data quality, and data management), each with minimum-standard and excellence criteria, supplemented by a toolkit providing interpretive guidance, glossary, and sample evaluations. The framework was iteratively refined over five evaluation rounds with three to four reviewers per round, using inter-rater reliability (ICC) measurements and structured disagreement reviews to ensure scoring consistency. Applied to 60 randomly selected NeurIPS D&B datasets from 2021–2023, the framework reveals that documentation quality varies widely (39%–86% minimum-standard pass rates), that model-work-adjacent elements are well documented while context awareness and environmental footprint receive 0% pass rates, and that no measurable improvement occurred over the three-year period despite increasing submission requirements.

## Results

Across 60 NeurIPS D&B datasets (2021–2023), documentation quality varies widely: the best dataset fulfilled 86% of minimum-standard criteria while the worst fulfilled only 39% (a 47-point gap). At the standard of excellence, the best paper scored 'full' on 50% of criteria, while the two worst received no 'full' scores at all. The rubric elements 'context awareness' and 'environmental footprint' achieved 0% pass rates, whereas 'context, purpose, motivation', 'suitability', and 'reliability' reached 100% pass. Inter-rater reliability improved across five evaluation rounds, reaching a median ICC of 0.90 in the final round (range 0.83–0.98 across rubric categories), confirming the framework's consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing evaluation methods or baselines; it is a single-framework empirical audit. The most meaningful comparison is temporal: despite increasingly stringent NeurIPS submission requirements (adding persistent identifiers, long-term preservation, and metadata access from 2022 onward), median minimum-standard pass rates declined from 78% (2021) to 67% (2022) to 61% (2023), and median 'full' excellence scores dropped from 29% to 25% to 13%. Structured documentation (datasheets, data statements) also fell from 80% in 2021 to 50% in 2023. The contrast between minimum-standard and excellence performance is stark: 14 of 18 elements clear the 50% pass threshold, yet only 1 of 18 achieves >50% 'full' scores, indicating that even well-documented datasets fall short of rigorous curation standards. The framework's reliability is validated through iterative IRR improvement (from 'fair' to 'excellent' ICC across five rounds), with the final round's median ICC of 0.90 supporting its use as a consistent auditing tool.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the authors' prior FAccT 2024 work [11], which introduced the initial data curation evaluation framework and completed rounds 1–3 of iterative refinement; here the rubric is finalized (13 elements updated, toolkit expanded) and applied to a larger, independent sample. Conceptually, it translates lifecycle models from digital curation and archival science (Higgins, Palmer et al.) and FAIR data principles into a concrete, validated rubric for ML dataset documentation, building on earlier documentation frameworks (datasheets, data statements, data cards) but adding dimensions those frameworks omit—environmental footprint, positionality, and structured data management. It also engages with prior NeurIPS-specific analyses of dataset harms and documentation gaps [84, 53, 4] by providing a systematic, reliability-tested auditing tool rather than a qualitative survey.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that documentation quality is *declining* over 2021–2023 (median pass rate 78%→61%; excellence 29%→13%) despite increasingly stringent submission requirements is a concrete signal that current peer-review mechanisms are insufficient, motivating the paper's proposal for a dedicated dataset-documentation reviewer role. The 0% pass rates on environmental footprint and context awareness highlight blind spots that existing checklists (datasheets, NeurIPS ethics checklist) do not capture. Key limitations include the modest sample (60 datasets, ~10/year for temporal claims), reliance on documentation rather than process observation, and human-evaluator subjectivity; an ethnographic study of actual curation workflows and a larger-scale application of the rubric remain open.
