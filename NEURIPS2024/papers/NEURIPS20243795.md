## Problem

When synthetic data instances are optimized individually (as in SRe2L) to reduce GPU memory and enable parallelization, they tend to cluster in a crowded region of latent space rather than covering the full feature distribution of the original dataset. The paper identifies that the Batch Normalization loss's variance component is the primary diversity driver in this isolated synthesis paradigm, but the coupled mean regularization within the same BN loss actively constrains diversity, creating an unresolved tension that limits the representativeness of the distilled dataset.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large original dataset T (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at 224×224), a pre-trained teacher network (e.g., ResNet-18), and for each batch of synthesis, a randomly sampled subset B ⊂ T used to compute the directed weight perturbation. It produces a synthetic dataset S with a user-specified number of images per class (ipc), where each instance is optimized in latent space against the perturbed teacher. Synthetic instances are initialized from real data samples (not random Gaussian noise) to ensure uniform projection in latent space, and the entire pipeline incurs only ~7% additional computational overhead over the base SRe2L method.

## Method

The core contribution is Directed Weight Adjustment (DWA), which replaces undirected random perturbation of teacher weights θ_T with a directed perturbation Δθ̃ obtained by maximizing the loss over a random subset B of the original dataset via K gradient steps. This directed perturbation provably decreases the loss on T\B (the complement), ensuring each batch of synthetic data captures features from a different, informative region of the original distribution without introducing noise. Additionally, the BN loss is decoupled into separate mean and variance coefficients (λ and λ_var), allowing the variance regularizer to be strengthened independently to promote diversity without the conflicting pull of the mean regularizer. The final synthesis objective minimizes ℓ(f_{θ_T+Δθ̃}, s_i) + λ·L_mean(f_{θ_T}, s_i) + λ_var·L_var(f_{θ_T}, s_i) for each instance s_i, with the teacher weights re-perturbed for every new batch of ipc instances. Theoretical analysis via first-order Taylor expansion shows that the directed perturbation increases Var(S) by a factor proportional to σ_θ² while guaranteeing no net noise injection, and the overall overhead is negligible (K=12 perturbation steps vs. 1000 synthesis iterations per instance).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ipc=10 and ResNet-18, DWA achieves 37.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing SRe2L (21.3%) by 16.6 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 with ipc=50 and ResNet-18, DWA reaches 60.9% accuracy versus SRe2L's 52.2% (8.7% gain). On Tiny-ImageNet with ipc=50, DWA achieves 52.8% (ResNet-18) and 54.7% (ResNet-101), outperforming SRe2L by 11.7 and 12.2 points respectively. The method incurs only 7.32% additional computational overhead over SRe2L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWA outperforms all seven baselines (DC, DM, CAFE, MTT, TESLA, SRe2L, DataDAM) across every dataset and ipc setting tested. The most dramatic gains are against SRe2L, the primary large-scale baseline: on ImageNet-1K at ipc=10, DWA achieves 37.9% vs. SRe2L's 21.3% (a 16.6-point margin), and at ipc=50, 55.2% vs. 46.8%. On CIFAR-100 (ipc=50, ResNet-18), DWA reaches 60.9% compared to SRe2L's 52.2%, MTT's 47.7%, and TESLA's 47.9%. Against trajectory-matching methods (MTT, TESLA), DWA leads by 12–13 points on CIFAR-100 and by 27+ points on ImageNet-1K at ipc=10. DataDAM, which uses attention matching, is competitive only on Tiny-ImageNet (28.7% at ipc=50) but collapses to 6.3% on ImageNet-1K, while DWA maintains strong performance at 37.9%. The consistent superiority across ResNet-18/50/101 validation backbones confirms that the diversity gain translates to cross-architecture generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SRe2L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced the Squeeze-Recover-Relabel paradigm for parallelizable, per-instance dataset distillation at ImageNet scale. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical decomposition of SRe2L's BN loss into mean and variance components, revealing that the variance term—not random initialization—is the actual source of diversity, while the mean term inadvertently constrains it. Building on this insight, the paper introduces Directed Weight Adjustment (DWA), a lightweight mechanism that perturbs the teacher model's weights in a data-directed manner (by maximizing loss on a random subset) to inject controlled randomness into each synthesis batch, going beyond the undirected random perturbation that would introduce noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of the BN loss's mean and variance regularizers provides a clear theoretical explanation for why naively increasing the BN loss coefficient in SRe2L yields diminishing returns, offering practitioners a principled way to tune diversity. The directed weight adjustment adds less than 0.1% computational overhead while delivering 5–16% accuracy gains over SRe2L across CIFAR, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-1K, making it a practical drop-in improvement for the efficient distillation pipeline. However, the method is tied to architectures with BN layers (requiring a ViT-BN workaround for transformers), and the authors acknowledge that increasing the perturbation variance introduces noise that bottlenecks performance. An open direction they identify is sequential synthesis where later instances explicitly distinguish themselves from earlier ones, which could further enhance diversity without relying on stochastic weight perturbation.
