## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models (VLMs) such as GPT-4V, Flamingo, and Med-PaLM-2 are architecturally constrained to 2D image input, yet radiological data like brain MRI and CT are inherently 3D volumetric. No efficient, unsupervised method existed to select the most informative 2D slices from 3D volumes so that these powerful 2D VLMs could be applied to 3D medical imaging without costly fine-tuning or computationally expensive slice-by-slice analysis.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 3D MRI brain scans in DICOM format (2,453 cases from 561 patients) and produces a small set of representative 2D slices per volume, which are then paired with text prompts and fed to VLMs. Downstream outputs are structured labels: binary cancer presence (yes/no/uncertain), multi-class cancer type (7 tumor categories from GBM to gangliocytoma), and prognostic cancer status (improving/progressing/no change). The VAE operates on 2D patches extracted from the 3D volume, and the selection budget is constrained by the VLM's input capacity (2, 4, or 8 examples for few-shot).

## Method

Vote-MI is a one-pass, unsupervised slice selection method with two core components: (1) a patch-wise Variational Autoencoder trained without labels to map each 2D slice into a low-dimensional feature descriptor, and (2) a mutual-information-based criterion that scores candidate slices for both diversity (coverage of the feature space) and representativeness (centrality to the data distribution). The algorithm performs clustering over the VAE latent space and selects slices that maximize mutual information between the selected subset and the full volume, yielding a diverse yet representative set in a single pass. Selected slices are then used as inputs to frozen VLMs (Flamingo, Med-Flamingo, MedPaLM-2) under zero-shot and few-shot protocols; in the few-shot setting, a bifurcated retrieval step matches test samples to the most pertinent examples from the Vote-MI-curated pool. This approach achieves 14.6% and 16.6% absolute accuracy gains over random selection for zero-shot and few-shot tasks respectively, while also reducing variance across repeated trials.

## Results

On the BrainMD-select benchmark, Vote-MI achieves 59.4% (±4.2%) slice selection accuracy, outperforming all baselines (Uncertainty 52.2%, Core-set 53.5%, K-center 47.6%, Random 28.2%). In zero-shot learning on BrainBench, Vote-MI yields a 14.6% average absolute gain over random selection across two diagnostic tasks. In few-shot learning (budget of 8), Vote-MI achieves 55.3/46.0/46.7% accuracy on W/o cancer, Cancer types, and Cancer status respectively, a 16.6% average absolute gain over random (30.1/28.7/30.4%). Notably, two Vote-MI-selected examples outperform eight randomly chosen ones across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vote-MI consistently outperforms all three algorithmic baselines on the BrainMD-select slice selection task: it beats Uncertainty (52.2%) by 7.2 points, Core-set (53.5%) by 5.9 points, and K-center (47.6%) by 11.8 points, while Random sampling lags far behind at 28.2%. In downstream few-shot learning, Vote-MI achieves 15–23% absolute gains over random selection across all three tasks and all budget sizes (2, 4, 8), with the largest margin at budget 2 (e.g., +22.2 on W/o cancer). However, Vote-MI still trails radiologist-selected slices by 10–15 points at each budget size (e.g., 55.3% vs. 70.1% at budget 8 for W/o cancer), indicating a remaining gap between automated and expert selection. Vote-MI also substantially reduces variance compared to random selection (e.g., ±3.1 vs. ±6.1 at budget 8), making downstream VLM performance more stable across repeated runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of 3D medical imaging and 2D vision-language models by framing representative slice selection as a bridge problem. Conceptually, Vote-MI repurposes active learning and data selection ideas (uncertainty sampling, core-sets, K-center) into a one-pass, unsupervised pipeline that uses a patch-wise VAE for feature extraction and a mutual-information-based criterion for diversity and representativeness. The VLMs evaluated (Flamingo, Med-Flamingo, MedPaLM-2) are existing models; the contribution is entirely in the data curation layer and the BrainMD dataset, which fills gaps identified in prior multimodal brain tumor datasets (BRATS lacks text, BMs/TCIA are small-scale). The paper does not build on a specific prior slice-selection method but rather introduces a new formulation tailored to the 3D-to-2D VLM gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D-input limitation of SOTA VLMs is a genuine practical bottleneck for radiology, and Vote-MI offers a lightweight, training-free way to partially close that gap without requiring 3D model retraining. The BrainMD dataset (2,453 multimodal cases with radiology reports and EHR data) is a valuable resource for the community. However, Vote-MI still trails radiologist-selected slices by 10–15 percentage points across all tasks, and the single-site cohort limits generalizability. Open questions include whether better feature extractors (diffusion-based, contrastive) or alternative diversity criteria (graph-based, confidence-based) can close the remaining gap, and whether the approach transfers to other 3D modalities (CT, ultrasound) and multi-site settings.
