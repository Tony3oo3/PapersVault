## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers are designed exclusively for either image or video inputs, forcing downstream generative models to rely on separate, modality-specific encoders. Even approaches like MAGVITv2 that explore causal 3D convolution still train distinct models for each modality without achieving cross-modal synergy. This limits data scalability—particularly for video, which is scarcer than images—and prevents a unified tokenization framework that could benefit both image and video generation.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts a single RGB image (T=0) or a video clip of 17 frames at spatial resolutions from 128 to 384 pixels. The first frame is patchified independently (p×p patches) from subsequent frames (t×p×p patches), then concatenated into a unified spatial-temporal sequence. The VQVAE variant outputs discrete codebook indices (8192 entries, latent dimension 8), while the VAE variant outputs a continuous Gaussian latent vector; both are subsequently decoded back to pixel space or consumed by autoregressive LMs and diffusion transformers for generation.

## Method

OmniTokenizer employs a spatial-temporal decoupled transformer with 4 window-attention spatial layers (window size 8) and 4 causal-attention temporal layers (hidden dim 512), where window attention captures local spatial structure and causal attention models temporal dynamics while remaining compatible with autoregressive generation. A progressive two-stage training strategy is central: Stage 1 pretrains on fixed-resolution (256×256) image data to establish spatial encoding, then Stage 2 jointly trains on image and video at multiple random resolutions (128–384) to learn temporal dynamics. The VQVAE variant is trained with a vector-quantization objective plus L2 reconstruction and GAN losses, and is subsequently fine-tuned with KL divergence to produce the VAE variant for diffusion-based generation. This design allows a single set of weights to tokenize both images and videos, with the progressive curriculum ensuring that spatial quality is not degraded by the introduction of temporal modeling.

## Results

OmniTokenizer-VQVAE achieves a reconstruction FID of 1.11 on ImageNet (256×256, 32×32 latent, 8192 codebook), beating the previous SOTA ViT-VQGAN (1.28) by 13% and VQGAN (1.49) by 25%. When fine-tuned as OmniTokenizer-VAE, the FID drops further to 0.69. On video reconstruction, OmniTokenizer-VQVAE achieves 42 rFVD on UCF-101 and 20 rFVD on Moments-in-Time, outperforming MAGVIT (58/–) and TATS (162/–) by 26% and 74% respectively. For downstream generation, the 227M-parameter autoregressive model reaches 10.13 FID / 94.5 IS on ImageNet class-conditional generation, and the 650M model achieves 191 FVD on UCF-101 video generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image reconstruction, OmniTokenizer-VQVAE decisively outperforms ViT-VQGAN [64] (1.11 vs 1.28 rFID on ImageNet, 1.93 vs 4.66 on CelebA-HQ, 1.91 vs 3.13 on FFHQ) and VQGAN [12] (1.11 vs 1.49), with the VAE variant further reducing FID to 0.69. On video reconstruction, OmniTokenizer-VQVAE beats MAGVIT [66] (42 vs 58 rFVD on UCF-101), TATS [13] (42 vs 162), MaskGIT [7] (42 vs 240), and CViViT [55] (20 vs 66 on MiT) by large margins. For autoregressive image generation on ImageNet, the 227M model achieves 10.13 FID, surpassing VQGAN [12] (18.65) by 32% and RQ-Transformer [26] (15.72); the 650M model reaches 7.45 FID, beating ViT-VQGAN [64] (8.81). For video generation on UCF-101, the 650M model (191 FVD) outperforms TATS [13] (332) and CogVideo [19] (626) but trails MAGVIT [66] (76) and MAGVITv2 [67] (58). On Kinetics-600 frame prediction, the method (32.9 FVD) beats all autoregressive baselines including ViVQVAE [56] (64.3) and Phenaki [55] (36.4) but is outperformed by the non-autoregressive MAGVITv2 [67] (4.3). For diffusion-based generation, OmniTokenizer-VAE with DiT-XL/2-CFG achieves 3.48 FID on ImageNet, competitive with but slightly behind the original DiT-XL/2-CFG (2.27), while on UCF-101 unconditional video generation, Ours-Latte (209.2 FVD) substantially outperforms Latte [29] (478.0) and all GAN-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniTokenizer builds on the spatial-temporal decoupled transformer architecture (Bertasius et al., ICML 2021) by combining window attention for spatial modeling with causal attention for temporal modeling, and adopts the VQVAE codebook mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and VAE KL formulation (Kingma & Welling, 2014) for its two tokenizer variants. It inherits the first-frame/following-frames patchification strategy from MAGVITv2 (Yu et al., ICLR 2024), which itself explored causal 3D convolutions for joint image-video processing but still required separate models. The key departure is unifying both modalities under a single shared architecture and weight, coupled with a progressive training strategy (image pretraining at fixed resolution, then joint multi-resolution training) that no prior tokenizer has employed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single tokenizer that handles both images and videos, OmniTokenizer removes a practical bottleneck for generative model developers who previously needed separate tokenizers per modality, and the progressive training recipe offers a transferable strategy for multi-modal representation learning. The paper demonstrates that the unified tokenizer improves image reconstruction beyond image-only training (1.11 vs. 1.28 rFID), suggesting genuine cross-modal synergy rather than mere compatibility. Open questions include whether the synergy persists at larger scales (the authors note scaling laws suggest further gains), whether the approach extends to longer video sequences or higher resolutions, and whether the specific window/causal attention combination is optimal or merely sufficient.
