## Problem

Current backdoor purification methods achieve low Attack Success Rates (ASR), suggesting successful defense, but it remains unclear whether learned backdoor features are truly eliminated or merely suppressed. This paper reveals that purified models are vulnerable to rapid backdoor reactivation with only a handful of poisoned samples, exposing a 'superficial safety' gap that standard ASR metrics fail to capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242476/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The models process RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet) and output class labels over 10, 100, or 200 classes. PAM consumes a backdoored model checkpoint, BTI-reversed synthetic backdoored samples, and a small clean tuning set to produce a purified model. QRA, by contrast, requires only black-box query access to the purified model and outputs sample-specific ε-bounded perturbations (ε=16/255) that reactivate the backdoor without modifying model weights.

## Method

The paper introduces two evaluation attacks—Retuning Attack (RA), which fine-tunes purified models on <1% of original poisoned samples for 5 epochs, and Query-based Reactivation Attack (QRA), which learns an MLP perturbation generator to translate RA-induced parameter changes into input-space perturbations via KL-divergence alignment with a regularization term against a surrogate EP model. Using Linear Mode Connectivity analysis, the authors show that existing purification methods converge to solutions that remain close to the backdoored model along the backdoor-connected path, whereas Exact Purification pushes the solution far enough to create a stable high-error barrier. The proposed defense, Path-Aware Minimization (PAM), computes gradients from an interpolated model W + ρ·(W₀−W)/‖W₀−W‖₂ (where W₀ is the backdoored model) and uses those gradients to update the purified model, actively promoting deviation along the backdoor-connected path and yielding post-purification ASR below 5% after RA across all tested datasets and architectures.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 5% poisoning), all existing purification methods (ANP, BAU, SAM, FST, BTI) suffer catastrophic post-purification robustness failure: their ASR jumps from ~1–5% (O-ASR) to 50–100% (P-ASR) after just 5 epochs of retuning with fewer than 10 poisoned samples. The proposed PAM method achieves an average P-ASR of 4.55% on CIFAR-10, 0.95% on CIFAR-100, and 7.48% on Tiny-ImageNet, approaching the upper-bound performance of Exact Purification (EP) which achieves 2.22%, 0.50%, and 0.88% respectively. The Query-based Reactivation Attack (QRA) further demonstrates practical threat by increasing P-ASR by an average of 66.13% on purified models using only model queries, while maintaining low C-ASR on clean inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAM decisively outperforms all five purification baselines on post-purification robustness (P-ASR after RA). ANP recovers to ~84% average P-ASR, BAU to ~98%, SAM to ~87%, FST to ~94%, and BTI to ~92%—all essentially fully reactivated. In contrast, PAM maintains an average P-ASR of only 4.55% on CIFAR-10, within 2.3 percentage points of the EP upper bound (2.22%). On CIFAR-100, PAM achieves 0.95% average P-ASR versus EP's 0.50%, while SAM and FST collapse to 78–100%. On Tiny-ImageNet, PAM reaches 7.48% P-ASR (vs. EP's 0.88%), still far below the 60–99% P-ASR of SAM, FST, and BTI. PAM also matches or exceeds baselines on O-ASR (original purification quality), achieving <2% ASR on all three datasets while preserving clean accuracy within ~2% of the backdoored model. The paper additionally shows that simply combining BTI with SAM (BTI+SAM) does not recover PAM-level robustness (P-ASR still reaches 92–100%), confirming that the path-aware gradient mechanism is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the backdoor purification literature (ANP, I-BAU, FT-SAM, FST, BTI) and repurposes Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) from transfer learning analysis [Frankle et al., Neyshabur et al.] to diagnose why purified models remain vulnerable. It draws a direct parallel to the 'superficial safety' phenomenon observed in LLM alignment [Christiano et al., Lubana et al.], where fine-tuning appears to remove harmful behavior but residual capabilities persist. The proposed PAM defense leverages BTI's trigger-reversing samples [Xu et al., ICLR 2023] as a proxy for the backdoor-connected path, but departs from BTI's standard fine-tuning by computing gradients on interpolated models between the purified and backdoored checkpoints rather than on the purified model alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art backdoor purification methods achieve low ASR yet are trivially reactivated by a handful of poisoned samples and 5 epochs of fine-tuning challenges the field's primary safety metric and suggests that deployed 'purified' models may be far less secure than reported. The QRA attack is particularly concerning because it requires only model queries (no weight access), making it applicable to API-based deployments. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with CNNs; the authors explicitly note that extending the framework to LLMs, diffusion models, and unlearning remains open. The post-purification robustness criterion introduced here could become a standard additional evaluation metric alongside ASR for future backdoor defense work.
