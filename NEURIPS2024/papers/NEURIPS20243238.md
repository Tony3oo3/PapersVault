## Problem

Prevalent PEFT methods for ViTs (LoRA, Adapter) decompose the adaptation matrix into a fixed-rank product of down- and up-projection matrices, imposing the same bottleneck dimensionality across all layers. This fixed rank cannot accommodate the varying representational needs of different layers, limiting adaptation flexibility. The paper asks whether one can construct adaptation matrices whose effective rank varies per layer while keeping the parameter count low.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243238/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, or Swin-B) initialized on ImageNet-21K and a small set of downstream RGB images resized to 224×224, and outputs class labels for fine-grained or general visual classification. During fine-tuning, only the Householder vectors, diagonal scaling coefficients, and a rank-1 low-rank pair are learned per layer; at inference the entire HTA module is re-parameterized into the frozen weight matrices, so the forward pass incurs no additional latency or memory overhead beyond the original backbone.

## Method

HTA reinterprets the adaptation matrix through an SVD lens (W_left · D · W_right) and replaces the two unitary factors with Householder reflections H = I − vvᵀ, each derived from a single learnable vector of length D^(l), making them orthogonal yet extremely parameter-efficient. A layer-wise learnable diagonal matrix D_H scales the transformation, allowing the effective rank of the composed adaptation to vary across layers without changing the parameter budget. Because a single Householder vector cannot span a general orthogonal basis, a rank-1 low-rank term (W_down W_up) is added in parallel to restore expressiveness. The resulting module can be plugged in either LoRA-style (additively to Q, K, V, O, FC1, FC2 projections) or Adapter-style (inserted after MHA and FFN blocks), and in both cases the combined weights are merged into the original matrices at inference time.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 tasks) with ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, HTA achieves a mean total accuracy of 74.7% with only 0.22M trainable parameters, outperforming the previous best PEFT method RLRR (73.4%, 0.33M params) by 1.3 points while using 33% fewer parameters. With the AugReg-enhanced backbone (HTA*), the mean total rises to 75.1%, matching RLRR* (75.1%) but again with fewer parameters. On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained datasets), HTA achieves 90.6% mean accuracy (0.36M params), surpassing LoRA (89.5%) and ARC (90.1%), while HTA* reaches 91.1%. On Swin Transformer, HTA achieves 74.2% mean total with 0.23M params, outperforming RLRR (73.0%, 0.41M) by 1.2 points with half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTA outperforms all compared PEFT methods on the VTAB-1k benchmark. Against the closest competitor RLRR [29], HTA gains +1.3 points in mean total accuracy (74.7 vs. 73.4) while reducing parameters from 0.33M to 0.22M (−33%); with AugReg, both reach 75.1% but HTA* still uses fewer parameters. HTA surpasses LoRA [1] by +2.4 points (74.7 vs. 72.3) with 24% fewer parameters, and beats ARC [3] by +1.3 points (74.7 vs. 73.4) with 69% more parameters (0.22M vs. 0.13M) — a trade-off the authors justify via the added rank-1 low-rank component. On FGVC, HTA (90.6%) edges out LoRA (89.5%) and ARC (90.1%) but is slightly behind SSF (90.7%) and ARC* (90.7%), with the gap narrowing to 0.0 points for HTA* (91.1%) vs. RLRR* (91.0%). On Swin Transformer, HTA achieves 74.2% vs. RLRR's 73.0% (+1.2 points) with only 0.23M vs. 0.41M parameters, and on ViT-Large it reaches 75.4% vs. RLRR's 75.2% with 0.54M vs. 0.82M parameters, confirming consistent gains across backbone scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA and Adapter lineage of parameter-efficient fine-tuning for Vision Transformers by reinterpreting the adaptation matrix through the lens of Singular Value Decomposition. Rather than learning full low-rank factor matrices with a fixed bottleneck dimension, it replaces the SVD unitary factors with Householder transformations—orthogonal matrices derivable from a single vector—while learning the diagonal scaling coefficients per layer. This departs from prior SVD-informed PEFT work (e.g., AdaLoRA, which prunes singular values) by using Householder reflections as a parameter-efficient surrogate for the rotation components, and from ARC/RLRR by allowing the effective rank to vary across layers rather than being locked to a fixed bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled alternative to the empirically-chosen fixed bottleneck dimension in LoRA/Adapter, theoretically enabling layer-adaptive rank without additional parameters. However, the authors acknowledge that a single Householder vector cannot span a general orthogonal basis, necessitating an additive rank-1 LoRA component that somewhat compromises the conceptual elegance. An open question is whether richer orthogonal constructions (e.g., products of multiple Householder reflections) could eliminate this auxiliary component while preserving parameter efficiency. The approach's generality across ViT-B, ViT-L, and Swin Transformer backbones suggests it could serve as a lightweight drop-in module for PEFT pipelines, though the performance margins over strong baselines like RLRR remain modest.
