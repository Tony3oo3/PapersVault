## Problem

Existing post-hoc explanation methods for GNNs (e.g., GNNExplainer, PGExplainer) are designed exclusively for classification tasks, leaving graph regression unaddressed. Two fundamental obstacles prevent direct extension: the Graph Information Bottleneck objective relies on cross-entropy to approximate mutual information, which is intractable for continuous regression labels, and the distribution shifting problem is far more severe in regression because continuous decision boundaries amplify small distributional shifts into large prediction errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen trained GNN model f (e.g., a 3-layer GCN), a target graph G with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n}, and a pool of graphs from the dataset for positive/negative neighbor sampling. It outputs a sub-graph explanation G* parameterized as a soft edge-weight mask M* ∈ [0,1]^{n×n} applied element-wise to A, highlighting the edges most responsible for f's regression prediction. The framework is explainer-agnostic, plugging into either GNNExplainer or PGExplainer as the mask generator without modifying the underlying GNN architecture.

## Method

RegExplainer replaces the cross-entropy approximation of I(G*;Y) in the GIB objective with an InfoNCE-based lower bound on I(Y*;Y), where Y* is the prediction of the explanation sub-graph, making the objective well-defined for continuous labels. To handle distribution shifting, instead of evaluating f(G*) directly, the method constructs two mix-up graphs G^(mix)+ and G^(mix)− by combining G* with the label-irrelevant sub-graphs (G+ − (G+)*) and (G− − (G−)*) from a positive and negative neighbor sampled by embedding similarity, keeping the mixed graphs in-distribution while preserving the label-relevant structure of G*. The explainer is then trained with a self-supervised contrastive objective: an InfoNCE loss on the triplet ⟨G, G+, G−⟩ that pulls the embedding of G^(mix)+ toward G and pushes G^(mix)− away, combined with a KL-divergence size loss and an MSE loss between f(G^(mix)+) and f(G). This design is model-agnostic and requires no retraining of the target GNN.

## Results

The primary metric is AUC-ROC on edges measuring explanation faithfulness against ground-truth sub-graphs. RegExplainer (with PGExplainer backbone) achieves the best AUC-ROC across all four datasets: 0.758 on BA-Motif-Volume, 0.989 on BA-Motif-Counting, 0.739 on Triangles, and 0.553 on Crippen. The paper reports up to 48.0% relative improvement (0.246 absolute) over the second-best baseline on BA-Motif-Volume, and an average improvement of 34.3% (0.175 absolute) across datasets. The GNNExplainer+RegExplainer variant also outperforms standalone baselines on all four datasets, confirming the framework's model-agnostic applicability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegExplainer (PGExplainer backbone) dominates all five baselines on every dataset. Against GRAD (gradient-based), it improves AUC-ROC by 0.340 on BA-Motif-Volume (0.758 vs 0.418) and by 0.127 on Crippen (0.553 vs 0.426). Against ATT (GAT attention weights), the gap is 0.246 on BA-Motif-Volume (0.758 vs 0.512), representing the paper's headline 48% relative improvement. Against MixupExplainer, which already addresses distribution shift for classification, RegExplainer wins on BA-Motif-Volume (0.758 vs 0.471), Triangles (0.739 vs 0.663), and Crippen (0.553 vs 0.499), but MixupExplainer retains a narrow edge on BA-Motif-Counting (0.868 vs 0.989 — actually RegExplainer still wins here). Against standalone GNNExplainer and PGExplainer, RegExplainer consistently outperforms, with the largest gains on BA-Motif-Volume (+0.257 over GNNExplainer, +0.288 over PGExplainer) and BA-Motif-Counting (+0.484 over GNNExplainer, +0.191 over PGExplainer), confirming that the InfoNCE-based GIB objective and positive/negative mix-up strategy are critical for regression tasks where continuous decision boundaries amplify distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) framework for post-hoc GNN explanation—established by GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, and unified by Wu et al.—from classification to regression tasks. It departs from prior work in two key ways: replacing the cross-entropy approximation of mutual information with an InfoNCE-based estimator (borrowed from Contrastive Predictive Coding) to handle continuous labels, and adapting the mix-up strategy from MixupExplainer by introducing positive/negative neighbor sampling with self-supervised contrastive learning to address the more severe distribution shifting that arises with continuous decision boundaries. The paper also introduces four regression-specific datasets (BA-Motif-Volume, BA-Motif-Counting, Triangles, Crippen) to fill the absence of ground-truth-explanation benchmarks for graph regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Graph regression underpins critical applications (molecular property prediction, traffic flow forecasting), yet the explainability gap left by classification-only methods limits trust in these models for sensitive domains like drug discovery. The paper's demonstration that distribution shifting is dramatically more damaging in regression (RMSE jumping from ~1 to ~1400 on BA-Motif-Volume) provides a concrete motivation for the mix-up-with-contrastive-learning design. Limitations include evaluation only on GCN as the backbone GNN, reliance on relatively small synthetic datasets, and an acknowledged inability to extend to spatio-temporal graphs with dynamic topology—open questions that constrain the method's immediate practical reach.
