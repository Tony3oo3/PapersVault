## Problem

Existing vector quantization methods learn single-modal codebooks that capture primarily low-level visual features (pixels, edges, textures), leaving them poorly aligned with text semantics. This modal gap causes suboptimal performance when the codebook is applied to cross-modal downstream tasks such as text-to-image generation, image captioning, and visual question answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images and their paired text descriptions as input, with the text encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder (not jointly trained). It produces a discrete code sequence of 256 tokens (codebook size 1024, down-sampling factor 16) that serves as a unified representation for reconstruction, text-conditioned generation, captioning, VQA, and visual grounding. The approach is model-agnostic: the language-guided losses can be attached to any existing VQ backbone (VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN, CVQ) without modifying its architecture.

## Method

LG-VQ introduces pre-trained CLIP text semantics as supervisory signals for codebook learning through two alignment modules. The Semantic Alignment Module enforces global consistency via an InfoNCE loss between a ViT-encoded [CLS] token of the discrete codes and the CLIP [EOT] text embedding, plus a masked text prediction loss where a cross-attention decoder reconstructs randomly masked words from the visual code sequence. The Relationship Alignment Module transfers word-level cosine similarity structure into the code space: for each word pair, it identifies the most similar codes via Z_vt alignment and penalizes the squared difference between word-pair and code-pair cosine similarities. The total objective adds three weighted losses (L_gsa, L_mtp, L_ras) to the standard VQ reconstruction and commitment losses, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying VQ model.

## Results

On image reconstruction, LG-VQ consistently improves FID across all four datasets when integrated into VQ-GAN: TextCaps (20.35 vs. 24.08), CelebA-HQ (5.34 vs. 5.66), CUB-200 (3.08 vs. 3.63), and MS-COCO (10.72 vs. 14.45), with the largest relative gain (~26%) on MS-COCO. On text-to-image generation (CelebA-HQ), CVQ+LG achieves FID 12.33, outperforming VQ-GAN (15.29) and LAFITE (12.54). On VQA (COCO-QA), LG-VQ reaches 40.97% accuracy, improving over VQ-GAN by ~8.32% absolute. On image captioning (CUB-200), LG-VQ achieves BLEU-4 1.69, ROUGE-L 34.73, METEOR 25.78, and CIDEr-D 102.77, surpassing VQ-GAN and VQCT on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LG-VQ outperforms all three backbone baselines (VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN, CVQ) on FID across all four reconstruction datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on CUB-200 (VQ-VAE: 54.92→36.55, a 33% reduction) and MS-COCO (VQ-GAN: 14.45→10.72, a 26% reduction). In text-to-image generation on CelebA-HQ, CVQ+LG (FID 12.33) beats UNITER (26.09), Corgi (19.74), and LAFITE (12.54), while VQ-GAN+LG (12.61) also surpasses LAFITE. For semantic synthesis, CVQ+LG (FID 11.03) outperforms Reg-VQ (15.34) and VQCT (14.47). On VQA (COCO-QA), LG-VQ (40.97% accuracy) edges out VQCT (40.42%) and substantially outperforms VQ-GAN (37.82%). In image captioning on CUB-200, LG-VQ leads VQ-GAN and VQCT on BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, and METEOR, but trails V2L Tokenizer slightly on CIDEr-D (102.77 vs. 104.14). For unconditional generation, LG-VQ (FID 9.1) significantly outperforms both Style ALAE (19.2) and DC-VAE (15.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE/VQ-GAN/CVQ lineage of discrete codebook learning by introducing pre-trained text semantics (via CLIP) as supervisory signal during codebook training, departing from prior methods that optimize purely on image reconstruction or adversarial losses. It contrasts with VQCT, which transfers codebook entries via a part-of-speech-based network, and with V2L Tokenizer, which maps images directly to LLM word tokens; LG-VQ instead aligns the existing visual codebook with text in a model-agnostic fashion using two novel losses (global semantic alignment via InfoNCE and relationship alignment via cosine-similarity matching). The Relationship Alignment Module draws on VQA techniques that exploit inter-word semantic relations, repurposing them to inject relational structure into discrete visual codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a text-aligned codebook can simultaneously improve reconstruction quality and cross-modal downstream tasks (text-to-image, captioning, VQA, visual grounding) without architectural changes, offering a drop-in enhancement for existing VQ pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that the one-to-one word-to-code assumption limits expressiveness, and that visual text reasoning performance remains far below dedicated captioning or VQA models. Open questions include whether richer code-word mappings (one code to multiple words) and integration with LLMs could close the remaining gap toward truly unified multi-modal generation and understanding.
