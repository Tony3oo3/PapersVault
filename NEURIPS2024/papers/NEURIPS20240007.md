## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient transfer learning methods for audio-visual tasks (e.g., LAVisH, DG-SCT) inject only cross-modal adapters into frozen pre-trained models, which can introduce irrelevant or disturbing information when audio and visual content do not align (e.g., off-screen sounds, missing modality). The Mixture of Experts strategy, which dynamically routes inputs to specialized sub-networks, had not been explored for audio-visual learning, leaving no mechanism to flexibly balance intra-modal detail against inter-modal interaction on a per-token basis.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames sampled at 1 fps (decomposed into non-overlapping patches at 192×192 spatial resolution) and audio waveforms converted to 192-bin mel-spectrograms via STFT (also patchified). Outputs are task-specific: per-segment event category labels (AVE), temporal event segments with modality-specific F-scores (AVVP), pixel-level sounding-object masks (AVS), and multiple-choice answers (AVQA). Notably, the same trained model accepts visual-only input and degrades gracefully, since the router can suppress the cross-modal expert when audio is absent.

## Method

AVMoE injects two types of lightweight adapters as experts into each layer of frozen Swin-V2-L (visual) and HTS-AT (audio) backbones: a Cross-Modal Adapter (CMA) that compresses modality tokens into latent vectors, fuses them via cross-attention, and refines through a bottleneck; and a Unimodal Adapter (UA) that performs the same compression but applies self-attention instead of cross-attention, preserving intra-modal structure. A modality-agnostic MLP router ingests the concatenated visual and audio embeddings and outputs softmax weights over the two expert types, with Gaussian noise added during training to prevent expert collapse. The final adapter output is the weighted sum of CMA and UA predictions, allowing the model to shift reliance toward unimodal processing when modalities mismatch or one modality is missing, and toward cross-modal fusion when they are complementary. Only the adapters and router are trained (≈35–55% of total parameters depending on backbone), keeping the pre-trained weights frozen.

## Results

On the AVE task, AVMoE achieves 82.6% segment-wise accuracy with Swin-V2-L + HTS-AT backbones, outperforming DG-SCT (82.2%) and LAVisH (78.6%) while using fewer trainable parameters (34.9% vs. 43.6%). On AVVP, AVMoE reaches 58.8 Type and 59.0 Event F-scores at segment level, exceeding DG-SCT by 1.7 and 2.0 points respectively. For AVS under the multi-source MS3 setting, AVMoE attains 54.5 mJ and 68.7 mF, surpassing DG-SCT (53.5/64.2) by a notable 4.5 points in mF. On AVQA, AVMoE achieves 75.7% average accuracy (77.6 AQ / 82.7 VQ / 71.9 AVQ), setting new SOTA across all three question types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVMoE consistently outperforms the two most relevant parameter-efficient baselines, LAVisH [30] and DG-SCT [11], across all four audio-visual tasks. On AVE, AVMoE (82.6%) beats DG-SCT (82.2%) by 0.4% and LAVisH (78.6%) by 4.0% while using fewer trainable parameters (34.9% vs. 43.6% and 30.6% respectively). On AVVP, AVMoE surpasses DG-SCT by 1.7 points in segment-level Type F-score (58.8 vs. 57.1) and also outperforms MGN [40] (55.6) and HAN [53] (54.0). For AVS, the advantage is most pronounced under the challenging multi-source MS3 setting, where AVMoE improves mF by 4.5 points over DG-SCT (68.7 vs. 64.2) and by 9.4 points over LAVisH (68.7 vs. 59.9). On AVQA, AVMoE achieves the best average accuracy (75.7%) with fewer total parameters (456.6M) than DG-SCT (520.2M), and notably gains 1.2% on the most difficult AVQ category (71.9% vs. 70.7%). The modality ablation further shows that AVMoE degrades far less than DG-SCT when audio is removed (e.g., AVE: 78.4% vs. 72.1% visual-only), confirming the robustness benefit of the unimodal expert.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning line for audio-visual learning initiated by LAVisH (cross-modal adapters in frozen ViTs) and DG-SCT (dual-guided spatial-channel-temporal attention prompts). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a Mixture-of-Experts framework that treats both cross-modal and unimodal adapters as distinct experts, with a lightweight MLP router (borrowed from Switch Transformers) dynamically allocating their contributions per token. While MoE has been explored in NLP and vision-language models, this is the first application of the MoE paradigm specifically to audio-visual learning, addressing the limitation that prior adapter-based methods exclusively inject cross-modal information and can introduce irrelevant signals when modalities are misaligned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE-based adapter selection provides a principled mechanism for handling real-world audio-visual mismatches (off-screen sounds, occluded events, missing modalities) without task-specific architectural redesigns, as demonstrated across four diverse downstream tasks. The visual-only ablation revealing that cross-modal-only adapters actively degrade performance when audio is absent (DG-SCT drops 10+ points on AVE) is a practically important finding for robust deployment. However, the paper is limited to at most 2 experts per type due to GPU memory constraints, leaving open whether scaling to more experts yields further gains, and the overall performance margins over DG-SCT remain modest (0.4–1.2% on most tasks), suggesting the MoE routing may not yet be fully exploited.
