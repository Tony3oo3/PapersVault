## Problem

Existing kernel k-means and multiple kernel k-means methods optimize clustering quality but ignore fairness, potentially producing partitions that disproportionately concentrate or exclude members of protected groups. No prior work has integrated group fairness into the kernel k-means or multiple kernel k-means framework, leaving a gap for fair clustering in the kernel setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{d×n}, pre-given protected group indicator matrix G ∈ {0,1}^{n×t}, and one (FKKM) or multiple (FMKKM) base kernel matrices. It directly outputs a discrete partition matrix Y ∈ {0,1}^{n×c} (one-hot cluster assignments) without the conventional two-step continuous-embedding-then-discretize pipeline. The fairness is enforced entirely at the data level by modifying the kernel matrix, so the downstream optimization remains a standard kernel k-means on the modified 'fair kernel.'

## Method

The paper introduces a fairness regularization term Tr(Y^T GG^T Y (Y^T Y)^{-1}) and proves (Theorem 1) that minimizing it achieves the optimal group fairness of Bera et al. The key insight is that this term has the same algebraic form as the KKM objective, so it can be absorbed into the kernel matrix, yielding a fair kernel K̃ = K + αI − λGG^T where α = λ|G_max| ensures positive semi-definiteness. This means FKKM requires no modification to the KKM loss—only the kernel is replaced—and FMKKM extends this by constructing a fair kernel for each base kernel before forming the consensus. Optimization proceeds via row-by-row discrete updates for Y (reducing to O(nc) per row) and a closed-form Cauchy-Schwarz solution for kernel weights γ in the multiple-kernel case. A Rademacher-complexity-based generalization error bound is derived, from which a practical λ-selection strategy (increment λ until MNCE stabilizes) is obtained.

## Results

On the multiple kernel setting, FMKKM achieves the best fairness (Bal and MNCE) across all six datasets, with MNCE reaching 0.985–1.0 and Bal reaching 0.263–0.935, while all nine non-fair MKKM baselines yield Bal = 0 on D&S, HAR, and MNIST-USPS. FMKKM also attains the best or near-best clustering accuracy: ACC 0.791 / NMI 0.752 on HAR, ACC 0.995 / NMI 0.991 on Jaffe, and ACC 0.495 / NMI 0.454 on MNIST-USPS. In the single kernel setting, FKKM similarly dominates on fairness (e.g., MNCE 0.991 on D&S, 0.989 on HAR) while maintaining competitive ACC and NMI (e.g., ACC 0.771 / NMI 0.710 on HAR, ACC 1.0 / NMI 1.0 on Jaffe).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMKKM decisively outperforms all nine MKKM baselines (ONKC, MKCSS, DPMKKM, LFLKA, EMKC, OSLR, ASLR, CSAMKC, FAMKKM) on fairness: it is the only method achieving non-zero Bal on D&S, HAR, and MNIST-USPS, where every baseline scores 0, and it attains MNCE ≥ 0.985 on all six datasets versus 0–0.998 for baselines. On clustering accuracy, FMKKM achieves the best ACC and NMI on HAR (0.791/0.752 vs. best baseline EMKC 0.732/0.656), MNIST-USPS (0.495/0.454 vs. best MKCSS 0.457/0.442), and Jaffe (0.995/0.991 vs. best FAMKKM 0.985/0.971), while remaining competitive on D&S, Credit Card, and K1b. The ablation FMKKM-f (without the fairness term) confirms the contribution of the regularization: it matches or exceeds FMKKM on ACC/NMI (e.g., 0.645 vs. 0.616 ACC on D&S) but collapses to Bal = 0 and MNCE 0.585–0.986, showing that the fairness gain is entirely due to the proposed term. In the single-kernel setting, FKKM similarly dominates on Bal and MNCE against K-means, KKM, SC, SpFC, VFC, and FFC, while maintaining top-1 or top-2 ACC/NMI on five of six datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: kernel k-means (Dhillon et al., 2004) and fair clustering (Bera et al., 2019; Chierichetti et al., 2017). Prior fair clustering methods (SpFC, VFC, FFC) operate in input space or impose linear constraints on spectral embeddings, while kernel k-means methods ignore group fairness entirely. The key departure here is the observation that a fairness regularization term can be algebraically absorbed into the kernel matrix itself (K̃ = K + αI − λGGᵀ), reducing fair kernel k-means to standard kernel k-means on a modified kernel — a formulation the authors claim is the first for both single and multiple kernel settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that any existing kernel k-means or multiple kernel k-means pipeline can be made fair by simply swapping in the fair kernel, with no algorithmic changes required. The theoretical generalization bound and the resulting λ-setting strategy (incrementally increase λ until MNCE stabilizes) make the method straightforward to deploy without ground-truth labels. A key limitation is that protected groups must be pre-specified by humans, and the authors explicitly flag automatic identification of protected groups as an open problem. The method also assumes the fair kernel remains positive semi-definite, which constrains how large λ can be relative to the kernel's spectral properties.
