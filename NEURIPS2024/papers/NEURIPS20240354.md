## Problem

Current computable RDP guarantees for DP-SGD with fixed-size subsampling are not tight (off by a factor of 4 at leading order) and do not uniformly cover both add/remove and replace-one adjacency relations. Additionally, no non-asymptotic RDP analysis existed for fixed-size subsampling with replacement, which is widely used in practice for class imbalance and federated learning.

## Input / Output

The accountant takes the dataset size |D|, fixed minibatch size |B|, per-step noise level σ_t, clipping threshold C, total number of training steps T, and the chosen adjacency type (add/remove or replace-one) as parameters. It outputs a closed-form, non-asymptotic upper bound on the RDP privacy loss ε_α for any order α > 1, which can then be converted to (ε, δ)-DP via standard RDP-to-DP conversion. The user selects the Taylor expansion order m (typically m=3 or 4) to trade off computational cost against bound tightness, with the remainder term rigorously bounded to prevent privacy leakage from truncation.

## Method

The core technique is a Taylor expansion of the Rényi divergence integrand with an explicit integral-form remainder bound, applied after a probabilistic decomposition (Lemma A.1) that expresses the fixed-size subsampling mechanism as a Gaussian mixture with mixing parameter q = |B|/|D|. For add/remove adjacency, this yields a sensitivity of 2C (twice Poisson's C), and the expansion at m=3 gives a computable bound that is 4× tighter than Wang et al. (2019). For replace-one adjacency, both arguments of the Rényi divergence become Gaussian mixtures, requiring a more involved expansion where the leading-order term is captured exactly and higher-order terms are bounded via χ^β divergences and Hölder's inequality. The paper further shows that FSwoR and Poisson subsampling achieve identical leading-order privacy under replace-one adjacency, and provides the first non-asymptotic upper and lower RDP bounds for FSwR, revealing a critical α threshold beyond which larger minibatches cause a sharp privacy degradation due to repeated sampling of the distinguishing element.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a 6-layer CNN (σ=6, |B|=120, |D|=50,000, C=3.0, 250 epochs), FSwoR-RDP achieves 63.87% test accuracy versus 61.57% for Poisson subsampled RDP in Opacus, while providing tighter (ε,δ)-DP guarantees. Theoretically, the new FSwoR-RDP bound under replace-one adjacency improves on Wang et al. (2019) by a factor of 4 at leading order in the sampling probability q. In terms of memory, FS-RDP maintains a constant GPU footprint of 14,826 MB across all epochs, whereas Poisson-based accountants (RDP, f-DP, PRV) fluctuate between 7,984 MB and 23,280 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang et al. (2019), the new FSwoR-RDP bound under replace-one adjacency is tighter by a factor of 4 at leading order in q; numerically (Figure 2), the m=4 bound stays close to Wang's theoretical lower bound across the full α range, while Wang's upper bound is significantly looser. Against Poisson subsampled RDP (Mironov et al., 2019, as implemented in Opacus), FSwoR-RDP matches privacy to leading order under replace-one adjacency but achieves 2.3 percentage points higher CIFAR-10 accuracy (63.87% vs. 61.57%), attributed to lower gradient estimator variance from fixed-size sampling. Under add/remove adjacency, Poisson subsampling retains an approximate factor-of-2 privacy advantage over fixed-size methods. Against f-DP (Dong et al., 2022) and PRV (Gopi et al., 2021), the comparison is primarily on memory: FS-RDP's constant 14,826 MB footprint avoids the variable 7,984–23,280 MB swings of these Poisson-based accountants, which is critical for resource-constrained and federated learning settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RDP accounting framework for DP-SGD from Poisson subsampling (Abadi et al., 2016; Mironov et al., 2019) to fixed-size minibatches, building directly on Wang et al. (2019), which provided the best prior computable bounds for fixed-size subsampling under replace-one adjacency. The key methodological departure is a Taylor expansion approach with explicit integral remainder bounds, applied specifically to the DP-SGD Gaussian mechanism rather than to general mechanisms as in Wang et al. (2019). The paper also unifies add/remove and replace-one adjacency in a single framework and, for the first time, provides non-asymptotic RDP bounds for fixed-size subsampling with replacement, a setting previously unaddressed in the DP-SGD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× tighter RDP bounds and the demonstrated equivalence between FSwoR and Poisson subsampling under replace-one adjacency give practitioners a principled reason to adopt fixed-size minibatches, which offer constant GPU memory usage and lower gradient variance—practical advantages particularly relevant for federated learning and resource-constrained settings. The FSwR analysis reveals a 'phase transition' where larger minibatches can dramatically degrade privacy due to repeated sampling of the distinguishing element, implying that with-replacement subsampling requires much smaller batch sizes than without-replacement. Open questions include whether the Taylor expansion approach extends to other noise distributions or non-Gaussian mechanisms, and whether the accuracy advantage of fixed-size subsampling (observed on CIFAR-10) persists at scale or with more complex architectures.
