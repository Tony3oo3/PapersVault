## Problem

Real-world images often suffer from multiple simultaneous degradations (noise, blur, rain, haze, JPEG artifacts, low light), yet existing restoration approaches either rely on all-in-one models with limited task scope and compromised per-task performance, or apply fixed/random execution orders with a single model per task. The optimal task sequence and model selection are highly image-specific and combinatorially complex (e.g., 287 possible pipelines for four degradation types with multiple models each), making manual or rule-based selection impractical and suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243511/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a degraded RGB image alongside a text prompt (e.g., 'How to enhance the quality of this image?') and, in iterative mode, an execution history string describing prior steps. It outputs a structured, interpretable pipeline in the format '1.<task name><model name>. 2.<task name><model name>…' that specifies both the ordered sequence of restoration tasks and the specific model to invoke for each. The model pool contains multiple candidates per task (e.g., three Restormer variants trained at different noise levels, two deJPEG models for mild vs. severe compression), and the system can also emit 'Rollback' or 'Stop' tokens to control iterative execution.

## Method

RestoreAgent fine-tunes LLaVA-Llama3-8B (CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder + Llama3-8B language model) with LoRA (rank 16) applied to all self-attention projection layers in both modules, using the Xtuner framework. Training data (~23k pairs) is constructed by exhaustively enumerating all permutations of task sequences and model combinations for each degraded image, scoring every pipeline with a user-defined balanced metric (z-score-normalized sum of PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS), and retaining only the optimal pipeline as the target. Five training scenarios are incorporated: initial full planning, mid-pipeline re-planning with execution history, rollback detection from erroneous paths, post-rollback guidance, and stop detection for already-restored images. The key insight is that the MLLM learns the mapping from visual degradation patterns to the optimal (task order, model choice) pair end-to-end from data, replacing hand-coded rules and enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks (e.g., desnowing added in ~30 minutes of fine-tuning) and new optimization objectives.

## Results

RestoreAgent achieves the best performance across all four multi-degradation test sets, outperforming every all-in-one baseline on all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS). On noise+JPEG, it reaches 25.32 dB PSNR and 0.7806 SSIM, surpassing the strongest baseline Real-ESRGAN (23.43 dB) by ~1.9 dB. On the most complex combination (rain+haze+noise+JPEG), RestoreAgent achieves 19.72 dB PSNR vs. 15.30 dB for the best all-in-one method (DA-CLIP), a gap of over 4.4 dB. In the decision-making comparison (Table 1), RestoreAgent ranks in the top 12.9% of all possible strategies on average, beating human experts (19.5%) and pre-defined rules (22.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RestoreAgent outperforms all eight all-in-one baselines on every metric across all four degradation combinations. Against Real-ESRGAN (the strongest noise+JPEG baseline), it gains +1.89 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.3022 to 0.2308. Against StableSR, the margin is even larger at +7.71 dB PSNR, reflecting StableSR's poor generalization to multi-degradation. On haze+noise, RestoreAgent beats AirNet by +2.91 dB PSNR and PromptIR by +4.34 dB, with LPIPS dropping from 0.5569 (AirNet) to 0.2193. On the three-degradation rain+haze+noise set, InstructIR is the closest competitor at 19.45 dB PSNR, but RestoreAgent still leads by +0.08 dB PSNR while substantially outperforming on SSIM (0.8237 vs. 0.6897) and LPIPS (0.2166 vs. 0.3994). On the most complex four-degradation set (rain+haze+noise+JPEG), RestoreAgent leads DA-CLIP by +4.42 dB PSNR and MiOIR by +5.92 dB, with DISTS improving from 0.2939 (MiOIR) to 0.1903. AutoDIR is competitive on rain+haze+noise (19.22 dB) but still trails RestoreAgent by +0.31 dB PSNR and +0.0877 LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant all-in-one restoration paradigm (AirNet, InstructIR, AutoDIR, MiOIR, DA-CLIP) by arguing that a single model cannot match specialized models on individual tasks, and instead positions an MLLM (LLaVA-Llama3-8B fine-tuned with LoRA) as an orchestration layer that delegates actual restoration to a pool of task-specific models (Restormer, DeblurGANv2, Retinexformer, RIDCP). It also extends prior tool-use agents (RL-Restore, Clarity ChatGPT) that either used reinforcement learning for a narrow 3-task toolbox or identified degradations but applied fixed execution orders, by formulating the full joint optimization of task sequence and model selection as a learnable decision problem solvable by a multimodal LLM. The conceptual lineage is: single-task SOTA models → all-in-one models → tool-use agents → MLLM-as-planner with a modular model pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular architecture means the system's upper bound is set by the best available specialized model for each task, and new tasks or models can be integrated with ~30 minutes of LoRA fine-tuning rather than retraining a monolithic restoration network. This is practically significant for a field where new SOTA restoration models appear frequently. Key limitations include the narrow scope tested (6 tasks, 200 test images, a single human expert baseline) and dependence on the generalization quality of the underlying restoration models, which the authors acknowledge as a field-wide bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 10+ tasks and dozens of models per task, how it handles truly unseen degradation types outside the training distribution, and whether the exhaustive-search-based training data construction remains feasible as the combinatorial space grows.
