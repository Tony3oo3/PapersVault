## Problem

Existing definitions of LLM honesty are inconsistent across models due to differing pre-training knowledge boundaries and exclude important dimensions like sycophancy. More critically, a tension exists between honesty and helpfulness: when LLMs must decline to answer queries beyond their capabilities, they often produce responses that are either dishonest (hallucinating plausible-sounding answers) or honest but unhelpful (refusing without any guidance). The paper asks whether helpfulness can be prioritized while preserving honesty, and identifies six concrete categories of LLM-unable queries where this tension manifests.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text queries from the HONESET dataset (930 queries across six categories: latest information, user input errors, professional capability, modality mismatch, interactivity sensory processing, and self-identity cognition) and produces structured text responses that include disclaimers, rational explanations for limitations, and actionable guidance. The training-free pipeline operates in two sequential LLM calls per query (curiosity elicitation followed by answer optimization), while the fine-tuning pipeline consumes preference pairs of (query, honest response, dishonest response) or (query, less-helpful honest response, more-helpful honest response) scored by an LLM-as-a-Judge on a 1–10 scale.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary approaches. The training-free method uses curiosity-driven prompting: a first prompt elicits the LLM's internal confusion and uncertainty about the query, then a constitution-guided second prompt combines the original query, the raw answer, and the expressed confusion to generate an optimized response that conveys limitations as disclaimers while providing practical alternatives. The fine-tuning method employs a two-stage DPO curriculum: Stage 1 trains on preference pairs with contrasting honesty labels (one honest, one dishonest) and overall scores below a threshold β, teaching the model to distinguish honest from dishonest responses; Stage 2 trains on pairs where both responses are honest but differ in overall quality (minimum score above β), refining helpfulness among already-honest outputs. Both stages use LoRA fine-tuning with the DPO loss, and the curriculum ordering (easy-to-hard, honesty-first-then-helpfulness) is the key design choice that outperforms direct fine-tuning on a combined dataset.

## Results

On the HONESET benchmark, the two-stage DPO fine-tuning achieves a 65.3% H² score gain for Llama3-8b (4.975 → 8.225) and a 124.7% gain for Mistral-7b (3.308 → 7.433), while honesty rates rise from 49.2% to 91.7% and 32.5% to 85.8% respectively. The training-free curiosity-driven prompting pushes honesty rates above 60% for all nine evaluated LLMs, with GPT-4 and Claude3-Opus reaching 100% and Llama2-7b improving from 43.0% to 83.7%. Fine-tuning also preserves safety (refusal rate on TrustLLM increases from 94.79% to 98.43%) and causes only a modest 5% drop on MTBench alongside a 0.7% MMLU gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external alignment methods; instead it benchmarks its own variants against the raw (unmodified) model and against a 'direct' fine-tuning baseline that combines both stages into a single DPO pass. The two-stage curriculum approach consistently outperforms direct fine-tuning: for Llama3-8b, Stage-2 yields 91.7% honesty vs. 82.5% for direct (+9.2 pp) and H² 8.225 vs. 6.575 (+1.65); for Mistral-7b, Stage-2 reaches 85.8% vs. 79.2% (+6.6 pp) and H² 7.433 vs. 6.733 (+0.70). Stage-1 alone (honesty discrimination) provides only modest gains (10.9% H² for Llama3-8b, 40.3% for Mistral-7b), confirming that the second stage (helpfulness refinement among honest responses) is essential. The training-free curiosity-driven prompting, applied to all nine LLMs, also outperforms raw responses in pairwise H² comparisons (e.g., 40.1% win rate for Llama2-7b) and raises GPT-4's H² score from 8.094 to 8.604 (+6.3%) and Mistral-7b's from 3.885 to 6.046 (+55.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the honesty alignment literature (Yang et al. 2023, Yin et al. 2023) by broadening the definition of honesty to include non-sycophancy as a fourth dimension beyond accuracy, capability awareness, and self-identity. For the fine-tuning approach, it builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023) but wraps it in a curriculum-learning-inspired two-stage schedule that first teaches honesty discrimination before refining helpfulness. The training-free method repurposes self-alignment and uncertainty-elicitation ideas (Sun et al. 2023, Xiong et al. 2024) into a curiosity-driven prompting pipeline, positioning it as a lightweight alternative to parameter updates. The HONESET dataset is positioned as the first benchmark specifically targeting LLM-unable queries across six structured categories, filling a gap left by prior honesty evaluations that focused narrowly on knowledge-boundary calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit framing of honesty and helpfulness as a joint optimization objective (via the H² assessment) addresses a tension that prior work identified but did not systematically resolve, and the HONESET dataset provides the community with a reusable benchmark for LLM-unable scenarios. However, the fine-tuning experiments are limited to two small open-source models (Llama3-8b, Mistral-7b), and the evaluation relies heavily on LLM-as-a-Judge, which the authors validate at 91.43% agreement but which may still introduce systematic biases. The 5% MTBench degradation highlights an unresolved trade-off between honesty alignment and general capability preservation. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum generalizes to larger models or proprietary APIs, and whether the curiosity-driven prompting approach remains effective under adversarial or multi-turn settings.
