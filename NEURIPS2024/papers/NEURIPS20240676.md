## Problem

In black-box optimization, the Order Oracle provides only pairwise comparisons of function values (with bounded noise) rather than actual values or gradients, yet no accelerated algorithm existed for this oracle concept. The paper asks whether acceleration is even achievable when the optimizer can only determine which of two points has a lower objective value, and whether coordinate-descent-based methods can match first-order oracle complexity under this severely restricted feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a starting point x₀ ∈ ℝ^d and query an Order Oracle that returns sign[f(x) − f(y) + δ(x,y)] with |δ| ≤ Δ, along with per-coordinate smoothness constants L_i and (for the accelerated variant) the strong convexity parameter μ_{1-α}. The output is an approximate minimizer x_N after N iterations, with oracle complexity of Õ(S_α F₀/ε²) in the non-convex case, Õ(S_α R²_{[1-α]}/ε) in the convex case, and Õ(S_{α/2}/√μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε)) in the strongly convex accelerated case. Notably, the non-accelerated OrderRCD is fully adaptive (requiring no knowledge of L_i or μ), while OrderACDM requires μ_{1-α} and L_i to set momentum parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism integrates the Order Oracle into coordinate descent via the Golden Ratio Method (GRM) as a one-dimensional line search: at each iteration, a random coordinate i_k is sampled with probability p_α(i) = L_i^α/S_α, and GRM uses only pairwise comparisons to simultaneously determine both the optimal step size and the gradient coordinate value along that direction, yielding a fully adaptive step. For the accelerated variant (OrderACDM), the authors adapt the ACDM framework by inserting a second GRM call to handle the momentum (z_k) update, resolving the challenge that the linear-search step may overshoot relative to the original 1/L_i · ∇_i f(y_k) step in the momentum recursion. The stochastic variant replaces GRM with a sign-based direction: the algorithm takes a step proportional to sign[⟨∇f(x_k,ξ_k), e_k⟩]·e_k where e_k is uniform on the unit sphere, which Lemma 5.2 shows is equivalent in expectation to normalized SGD, yielding asymptotic normality of √N(x_k − x*). The key theoretical contribution is proving that the accelerated rate O(S_{α/2}/√μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε)) is achievable with only comparison feedback, matching the best known first-order accelerated coordinate descent rates up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

In the non-convex setting, OrderRCD achieves oracle complexity Õ(S_α F_0/ε²), matching the best first-order coordinate descent rate up to a logarithmic factor and improving over Gorbunov et al. (O(dS_α F_0/ε²)) by removing the dimension d. In the strongly convex setting, the accelerated OrderACDM achieves Õ(S_{α/2}/√μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε)) oracle calls — the first accelerated rate under the Order Oracle — compared to Õ(S_α/μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε)) for all existing Order Oracle methods. Numerically, OrderRCD outperforms its first-order counterpart RCD despite having access only to comparisons, and OrderACDM (with one GRM call) matches the convergence speed of the first-order ACDM on a quadratic test problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nesterov (2012) RCD, OrderRCD matches the iteration complexity in all three settings (non-convex, convex, strongly convex) up to a logarithmic factor from the Golden Ratio Method, while requiring only comparison access rather than function values. Against Gorbunov et al. (2019), OrderRCD removes the dimension factor d in the non-convex and convex cases (e.g., Õ(S_α F_0/ε²) vs. O(dS_α F_0/ε²)), and matches the strongly convex rate. Against Saha et al. (2021), OrderRCD improves from O(dLR²/ε) to Õ(S_α R²_{[1-α]}/ε) in the convex case by exploiting coordinate-wise smoothness. Against Tang et al. (2023), OrderRCD provides the first convex and strongly convex guarantees under the Order Oracle, whereas Tang et al. only cover the non-convex case. In the accelerated strongly convex setting, OrderACDM (Õ(S_{α/2}/√μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε))) strictly improves over all prior Order Oracle methods (which achieve at best Õ(S_α/μ_{1-α} · log(1/ε))), and numerically matches the first-order ACDM of Nesterov and Stich (2017) when using a single GRM call per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates the Order Oracle (a comparison-only oracle that returns sign[f(x)−f(y)] with bounded noise) into the coordinate descent framework via the Golden Ratio Method for linear search, departing from prior Order Oracle algorithms (Gorbunov et al. 2019, Saha et al. 2021, Tang et al. 2023) that relied on stochastic three-point methods or sign-SGD. The non-uniform coordinate sampling p_α(i)=L_i^α/S_α follows Nesterov (2012), while the accelerated variant (OrderACDM) directly adapts the ACDM scheme of Nesterov and Stich (2017) with the non-uniform sampling improvement of Allen-Zhu et al. (2016). The stochastic Order Oracle algorithm's convergence proof is grounded in the normalized SGD analysis of Polyak and Tsypkin (1980), establishing a connection between comparison-based feedback and classical stochastic approximation theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that acceleration—a property previously thought to require richer oracle information—is achievable with only pairwise function comparisons, opening a new direction for derivative-free optimization under severe information constraints. Practical limitations include the need for smoothness constants L_i and strong convexity parameter μ in the accelerated algorithm, a logarithmic oracle overhead from the Golden Ratio Method, and numerical validation restricted to quadratic test problems. Open questions include whether acceleration extends to non-convex settings under the Order Oracle, whether the logarithmic gap to first-order methods is fundamental, and how the methods perform under realistic (non-quadratic) objectives with structured noise.
