## Problem

Adam and RMSprop are not guaranteed to converge for smooth nonconvex objectives unless β₂ is tuned in a problem-dependent manner, and existing fixes like AMSGrad require the impractical assumption that stochastic gradients are uniformly bounded almost surely. This paper identifies the root cause of non-convergence as the statistical correlation between the current gradient and the second moment estimate, and asks whether a simple algorithmic modification can eliminate this correlation while preserving the optimal O(1/√T) convergence rate for any β₂ under only a finite second-moment assumption.

## Input / Output

ADOPT consumes the same inputs as Adam: a stochastic gradient g_t and the current parameter vector θ_{t-1}, along with hyperparameters β₁, β₂, and ε. The key structural difference is that the second moment estimate v_{t-1} (computed from past gradients only) is used to normalize the current gradient *before* the momentum update, rather than normalizing the momentum after it is computed. The output is the standard updated parameter θ_t, making ADOPT a drop-in replacement for Adam in any training pipeline.

## Method

ADOPT makes two targeted changes to Adam's update rule. First, it decorrelates the second moment estimate from the current gradient by using v_{t-1} (which contains no information about g_t) instead of v_t, eliminating the correlation that causes the non-vanishing constant term in the convergence bound. Second, it reverses the order of operations: the current gradient g_t is first normalized by max{√v_{t-1}, ε}, and then the momentum is applied to this normalized gradient (m_t = β₁ m_{t-1} + (1-β₁) g_t / max{√v_{t-1}, ε}), rather than applying momentum to the raw gradient and then normalizing. This ordering ensures that the second moment estimate only interacts with the current gradient through the normalization step, preserving conditional independence. Under Assumptions 2.1–2.3 and 2.5 (finite second moment, not bounded gradient), ADOPT achieves the minimax-optimal O(1/√T) rate for smooth nonconvex objectives with any choice of β₂, without requiring the bounded-noise assumption (Assumption 2.6) that AMSGrad and AdaShift depend on.

## Results

On ImageNet classification with SwinTransformer-tiny, ADOPT achieves 79.62% top-1 accuracy at 200 epochs and 81.50% at 300 epochs, outperforming AdamW (79.29% / 81.26%) and AMSGrad (78.91% / 81.17%). For NVAE density estimation on MNIST, ADOPT achieves a negative log-likelihood of 78.88 at 300 epochs, beating Adamax (79.41). On LLaMA-7B instruction fine-tuning, ADOPT improves the MMLU score to 42.13 versus 41.2 for Adam (baseline without fine-tuning: 35.1). In GPT-2 pretraining with a small batch size of 96, Adam suffers loss spikes and fails to converge, while ADOPT trains stably throughout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADOPT consistently outperforms Adam/AdamW across all practical benchmarks: on ImageNet it gains +0.33 pp at 200 epochs and +0.24 pp at 300 epochs; on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 it converges slightly faster; on LLaMA-7B fine-tuning it improves MMLU by +0.93 points (42.13 vs 41.2); and in GPT-2 pretraining with batch size 96, Adam diverges due to loss spikes while ADOPT remains stable. Against AMSGrad, ADOPT achieves +0.71 pp on ImageNet at 200 epochs and converges substantially faster in the toy problem with high gradient noise (k=50), where AMSGrad's reliance on the bounded-gradient assumption (Assumption 2.6) causes slow convergence. On MNIST MLP classification, ADOPT slightly outperforms AdaShift in both convergence speed and final test accuracy. For generative modeling (NVAE on MNIST), ADOPT beats Adamax by 1.17 NLL points at 200 epochs (79.02 vs 80.19) and 0.53 points at 300 epochs (78.88 vs 79.41). The ablation study confirms that both algorithmic changes (decorrelation via v_{t-1} and reordering of momentum/normalization) are necessary—removing either one causes ADOPT to fail to converge in the toy problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2014) and RMSprop (Hinton, 2012), addressing the well-known non-convergence issue identified by Reddi et al. (2018). It departs from prior fixes in two key ways: unlike AMSGrad, which requires the impractical bounded-gradient-noise assumption (Assumption 2.6), and unlike AdaShift (Zhou et al., 2019), which only analyzed a single convex example, ADOPT provides a general smooth nonconvex convergence guarantee under only the bounded second-moment assumption. The paper also contrasts with a line of work (Shi et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023) that achieves convergence by tuning β₂ in a problem-dependent manner, whereas ADOPT converges for any β₂ without such tuning. The core insight unifies and extends the decorrelation observation from AdaShift by showing that a second, independent source of non-convergence arises from the ordering of the momentum update relative to the second-moment normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADOPT provides the first Adam-like optimizer with a problem-independent O(1/√T) convergence guarantee that does not require bounded gradient noise, making it a theoretically safe drop-in replacement for Adam across a wide range of ML tasks including VAEs and diffusion models where unbounded Gaussian noise is standard. The practical impact is significant: the algorithm requires only two small modifications to Adam's update rule, and the paper validates it across image classification, language model pretraining/finetuning, generative modeling, and reinforcement learning. A key limitation is that the analysis still relies on Assumption 2.5 (bounded second moment of the stochastic gradient) rather than the weaker bounded-variance assumption (Assumption 2.4) used for vanilla SGD; relaxing this is identified as future work. Additionally, the authors note a counterexample showing ADOPT can fail under without-replacement sampling, though this is unlikely to matter in practice for large datasets.
