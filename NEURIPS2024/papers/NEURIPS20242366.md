## Problem

Existing single-image human reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: per-instance optimization approaches (e.g., TeCH, HumanSGD) achieve high fidelity but require hours of optimization per subject, while generalizable feed-forward methods either ignore human structural priors or demand multi-view inputs. No prior work combines a 2D generative diffusion model with human geometry priors in an end-to-end 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that generalizes from a single image without any per-instance fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a clothed human and, at inference, uses PIXIE to estimate SMPL-X parameters and CLIP to extract an image embedding—no additional views or optimization are needed. It outputs pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting attributes (position, scale, rotation quaternion, color, opacity) for N_p Gaussians, which are rendered via differentiable rasterization into novel-view images at over 150 FPS on an A100 GPU. The full reconstruction pipeline (diffusion + latent reconstruction) completes in approximately 9.3 seconds, with the diffusion stage taking ~9 s and the Transformer decoding ~0.3 s.

## Method

HumanSplat integrates three components in a unified end-to-end architecture: (1) a fine-tuned SV3D video diffusion model that hallucinates N multi-view latent features from the single input, conditioned on CLIP embeddings and Plücker ray embeddings; (2) a latent reconstruction Transformer with two interaction stages—an intra-attention module that extracts spatial correlations within each view's latent tokens, followed by a geometry-aware inter-attention module that performs projection-aware cross-attention between latent tokens (queries) and SMPL-derived geometric tokens (keys/values) within a K_win × K_win window, reducing complexity from O(L_F × L_H) to O(L_F × K_win²) while tolerating SMPL estimation errors for loose clothing; and (3) a semantics-guided hierarchical loss that assigns higher weights (λ_j = 2) to visually sensitive body parts (head, hands, arms) and supervises at multiple resolution levels, combined with a standard reconstruction loss over rendered multi-view images. The model is trained on 3,500 high-fidelity human scans (THuman2.0, 2K2K, Twindom) with 36 cameras across three hierarchical cycles, using a 256-res pretraining phase followed by 512-res fine-tuning.

## Results

On THuman2.0, HumanSplat achieves PSNR 24.033, SSIM 0.918, and LPIPS 0.055; on the more challenging Twindom dataset it reaches PSNR 23.346, SSIM 0.913, and LPIPS 0.125. The method completes reconstruction in approximately 9.3 seconds on a single A100 GPU, compared to 4.5 hours for the closest quality competitor TeCH. HumanSplat outperforms all generalizable baselines (LGM, GTA, SIFU) on both datasets and also surpasses per-instance optimization methods (TeCH, HumanSGD, Magic123) on LPIPS and runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest per-instance baseline TeCH (4.5h optimization), HumanSplat trades a small PSNR gap on THuman2.0 (24.033 vs 25.211) for a large LPIPS advantage (0.055 vs 0.083) and a ~175× speedup (9.3s vs 4.5h); on Twindom, HumanSplat clearly wins on all three metrics (PSNR +2.15, SSIM +0.029, LPIPS −0.063). Against the best generalizable baseline SIFU, HumanSplat gains +2.01 PSNR and −0.029 LPIPS on THuman2.0, and +3.63 PSNR and −0.187 LPIPS on Twindom, while being ~5× faster (9.3s vs 44s). Against LGM* (fine-tuned on the same data), HumanSplat improves PSNR by +4.02 on THuman2.0 and +3.51 on Twindom with comparable runtime (9.3s vs 9.5s). Against GTA, which uses human priors but no diffusion, HumanSplat achieves +5.98 PSNR on THuman2.0 and +5.68 PSNR on Twindom. The per-instance diffusion baselines Magic123† and HumanSGD† are substantially outperformed on both quality and speed, confirming that the end-to-end generalizable framework with structure priors is both more accurate and far more efficient than iterative SDS-based optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumanSplat sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 2D diffusion-based multi-view synthesis (SV3D), (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a renderable 3D representation, and (3) parametric human body models (SMPL) as geometric priors. It departs from SDS-based methods (TeCH, HumanSGD) that require hours of per-instance optimization, and from generalizable LRM-based approaches (LGM, GTA, HumanLRM) that either ignore human structure or require multi-view inputs. The key architectural departure is the end-to-end latent reconstruction Transformer that fuses diffusion latent features with SMPL-derived geometric tokens via a projection-aware windowed cross-attention, addressing the robustness-flexibility trade-off inherent in using SMPL for clothed humans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9.3-second feed-forward inference (vs. 4.5 hours for TeCH) while matching or exceeding quality on LPIPS makes single-image-to-3D-human practically deployable in interactive applications like e-commerce, gaming, and telepresence. The semantic-guided hierarchical loss and projection-aware attention are reusable design patterns for any task combining generative appearance models with parametric structure priors. Open questions remain around handling intricate garments and accessories, direct animation of the reconstructed Gaussians (currently requiring post-processing), and further speedups for mobile deployment.
