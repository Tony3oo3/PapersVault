## Problem

Fully-connected Graph Transformers lose both structural and temporal information when applied to dynamic graphs, since self-attention across all nodes at multiple snapshots discards the spatio-temporal structure. Existing dynamic Graph Transformer methods rely on partial attention over sampled sub-graphs and independently computed spatial and temporal encodings, failing to capture global long-range interactions. The paper asks how to design a unified spatio-temporal encoding that preserves the full graph structure while exploiting the spectral properties of multi-layer networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Discrete Time Dynamic Graph as a sequence of adjacency matrices over a fixed set of N nodes, along with fixed node feature vectors of dimension F. A time window w (default 3) determines how many consecutive snapshots are stacked into the supra-adjacency matrix; isolated nodes are removed and virtual nodes inserted to guarantee connectivity before spectral decomposition. For a target node pair (u, v) at time t+1, the model outputs a single scalar link-existence probability via a sigmoid-MLP applied to a cross-attended edge embedding of dimension d=128.

## Method

SLATE casts a DTDG as a connected multi-layer graph by (1) removing isolated nodes per snapshot, (2) adding a virtual node to bridge disconnected clusters, and (3) inserting temporal self-connections between a node and its past self. The spectral decomposition of the resulting supra-Laplacian yields k eigenvectors and eigenvalues that form a unified spatio-temporal positional encoding, replacing the separate Laplacian + sinusoidal temporal encodings used by prior methods. A single-layer fully-connected transformer then processes all N·w node-time tokens jointly, capturing long-range spatio-temporal dependencies without sub-graph sampling. A lightweight cross-attention edge module aggregates the w temporal representations of each node pair into a pairwise embedding, which is mean-pooled and passed through an MLP for binary link prediction. Training uses standard binary cross-entropy with one positive and one negative neighbor sampled per node per step.

## Results

On the DTDG protocol (Table 1), SLATE achieves an average ROC-AUC of 95.12 across 5 datasets, outperforming the best discrete baseline HTGN (93.62) by +1.50 points, with the largest margin on SBM (+2.89). On the broader CTDG protocol (Table 2), SLATE reaches 96.88 average AUC across 6 datasets, surpassing the strongest competitor DyGformer (83.41) by +13.47 points. The most dramatic gains appear on UNVote (+42.82 over DyGformer) and Trade (+26.53), while SLATE is slightly behind DyGformer on CanParl (−5.39) and Contact (−0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest DTDG baseline HTGN, SLATE wins on 4 of 5 datasets (HepPh +2.08, Enron +2.22, Colab +1.58, SBM +2.89) but trails by 1.29 on AS733. Against the best CTDG baseline DyGformer, SLATE dominates on USLegis (+17.90), Trade (+26.53), and UNVote (+42.82), while DyGformer retains a narrow edge on CanParl (−5.39) and Contact (−0.41). Compared to the closest transformer-based dynamic method TCL, SLATE gains +21.77 points on average, primarily due to its global fully-connected attention versus TCL's sub-graph sampling. Against the ROLAND-GT baseline (separate spatial GT + LSTM temporal model), SLATE's unified spatio-temporal attention yields +6.29 average AUC, confirming the benefit of joint encoding. SLATE also outperforms sparse-attention methods like DySat by +6.45 on average, and MP-GNN-based models (EvolveGCN, GRUGCN, VGRNN) by 5–8 points, validating the fully-connected transformer architecture for dynamic link prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLATE extends the Laplacian positional encoding paradigm for static Graph Transformers (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) to the dynamic setting by replacing the per-snapshot Laplacian with the supra-Laplacian of a multi-layer graph constructed from a DTDG, drawing on the multi-layer network formalism of Kivelä et al. (2014). It departs from the dominant DTDG architecture of stacking a spatial MP-GNN with a temporal RNN (e.g., ROLAND, EvolveGCN, DySat) by using a single fully-connected spatio-temporal transformer, and from prior Dynamic Graph Transformers (TCL, DyGFormer) that sample local sub-graphs and concatenate independent spatial and temporal encodings. The cross-attention edge module for pairwise link prediction is inspired by TCL but operates on globally computed node representations rather than locally sampled substructures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified supra-Laplacian encoding offers a principled, single-source spatio-temporal positional signal for dynamic graph transformers, eliminating the ad-hoc concatenation of separate spatial and temporal encodings that plagues prior DGTs. The large and consistent performance gains (13+ points over DyGFormer, 21+ over TCL across six CTDG-protocol datasets) suggest that global spectral structure, properly connected, is a significantly underexploited signal for dynamic link prediction. Key limitations remain: SLATE is transductive (cannot generalize to unseen nodes), and while Performer-based linear attention helps, scaling to very large graphs (beyond ~13K nodes) is still challenging. Open directions include hybridizing with MP-GNNs for inductive settings and extending the supra-Laplacian framework to continuous-time or heterogeneous dynamic graphs.
