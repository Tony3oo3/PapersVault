## Problem

Zero-1-to-3 and its extended variant Zero123-XL can synthesize novel views from a single source image, but frequently produce implausible shapes, inconsistent geometry, and visual artifacts that limit practical usability. Existing remedies—retraining on larger 3D datasets or aggregating multiple generated views through 3D representations—are resource-intensive and often introduce blurriness. This paper identifies self-attention maps within the denoising UNet as the primary source of these artifacts and proposes a training-free, test-time filtering mechanism to correct them without any retraining or external models.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained Zero-1-to-3 or Zero123-XL checkpoint, requiring no additional training data or parameters. The source image is consumed in two ways: its CLIP embedding is concatenated with the relative camera transformation [R|T] to form a global pose-CLIP condition fed through cross-attention, and the image itself is channel-concatenated with the denoised latent to preserve object identity. The output is a single RGB image at the requested target viewpoint, generated in the 32×32 latent space of the underlying Stable Diffusion UNet.

## Method

Drawing an analogy between SGD weight updates and attention map updates during denoising, the paper treats self-attention maps as 'parameters' of a score-prediction model and applies gradient/weight-averaging techniques to them. At each denoising step the latent is re-noised and denoised R times (per-step resampling), producing R attention maps that are aggregated via element-wise min-pooling (in-step update) and then blended with a history buffer using exponential moving average (cross-step update) to preserve fine details that resampling alone tends to erode. A complementary early-stage mutual self-attention mechanism injects the source view's keys and values into the target's self-attention layers during the first third of denoising to anchor shape consistency before the structure is locked in. An hourglass DDIM schedule allocates denser sampling to the early (shape-determination) and late (detail-refinement) stages while sparsely sampling the middle, keeping total NFE at 66 for 26 timesteps.

## Results

On the challenging GSO subset, Zero-to-Hero applied to Zero123-XL achieves PSNR 18.35, SSIM 0.864, LPIPS 0.153, and IoU 78.3% using only 26 timesteps (66 NFE with resampling), outperforming the base Zero123-XL (PSNR 17.72, SSIM 0.854, LPIPS 0.163, IoU 76.4%) across all metrics. On the RTMV dataset, the same method yields PSNR 11.15, SSIM 0.618, LPIPS 0.372, and IoU 72.8%, again surpassing Zero123-XL (PSNR 10.64, SSIM 0.595, LPIPS 0.391, IoU 71.9%). Notably, the base models show virtually no improvement when increasing DDIM steps from 25 to 100, while Zero-to-Hero achieves its gains with fewer nominal steps than the 50-step baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against Zero-1-to-3 and its extended variant Zero123-XL, both evaluated at 25, 50, and 100 DDIM steps. Zero-to-Hero wins on every metric for both base models and both datasets. On GSO, applying the method to Zero123-XL yields +0.63 PSNR, +0.010 SSIM, −0.010 LPIPS, and +1.9% IoU over the best Zero123-XL configuration, while using only 26 timesteps (66 NFE) versus 50 or 100 steps for the baseline. On RTMV, the gains are +0.51 PSNR, +0.023 SSIM, −0.019 LPIPS, and +0.9% IoU over Zero123-XL. The base models show essentially flat performance when scaling from 25 to 100 steps (e.g., Zero123-XL PSNR drops from 17.72 to 17.68), confirming that simply adding more denoising steps does not help, whereas Zero-to-Hero's attention map filtering provides consistent improvements at a comparable or lower step count. The method also narrows the gap to the ground-truth attention map oracle (PSNR 21.79, IoU 88.6%), closing roughly 30% of the remaining performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Zero-1-to-3 and Zero123-XL, diffusion-based novel view synthesis models that condition on a source image and target camera pose. Its core novelty is a conceptual analogy between SGD weight updates and attention map evolution during denoising, which motivates a filtering pipeline (in-step min-pooling, cross-step EMA) borrowed from gradient aggregation and stochastic weight averaging literature. The method also adapts mutual self-attention from MasaCtrl but departs from prior practice by applying it at early denoising stages rather than late, and incorporates per-step resampling inspired by Repaint and Restart sampling. The hourglass scheduling scheme is a practical contribution that concentrates compute where it matters most.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play technique with modest overhead (~1–1.5 s extra), Zero-to-Hero is immediately applicable to the widely-used Zero-1-to-3 ecosystem and generalizes to other diffusion-based tasks (ControlNet, MVDream), making it a practical tool for practitioners who cannot afford retraining on large 3D datasets. The identification of cross-attention degeneracy in Zero-1-to-3 (reducing to a global bias) and the demonstration that self-attention maps are the critical lever for geometric consistency provide useful architectural insights. Key limitations include being bounded by the base model's generative ceiling and the trade-off between filtering strength and generation diversity. Open directions include trainable filtering, cross-attention manipulation for pose fidelity, and extension to video/4D generation.
