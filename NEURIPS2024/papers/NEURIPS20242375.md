## Problem

Ligand-based virtual screening (LBVS) relies on structural or charge similarity to find bioactive molecules but ignores binding affinity information, and the limited, noisy affinity data available cannot cover the full chemical space. A key unresolved challenge is how to leverage large-scale unlabeled molecular data to enhance LBVS without a reliable inductive bias for 3D molecular data augmentation, since standard contrastive learning techniques from vision or language do not transfer to molecules with their inherent 3D structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D molecular conformations (atom positions and types) as input for both a query molecule and a large molecular database, processing them through a Uni-Mol backbone that uses self-attention with distance bias to produce L2-normalized CLS-token embedding vectors. At inference, the full-dataset encoder g_ψ encodes all database molecules, and candidates are ranked by cosine similarity to the query embedding, returning a top-k list of bioactive molecules. The framework is framed as dense retrieval, requiring no protein structure at test time.

## Method

S-MolSearch employs two simultaneously trained encoders initialized from Uni-Mol: f_θ processes labeled molecule-protein binding data (from ChEMBL, organized into target-specific clusters) using InfoNCE contrastive loss, while g_ψ processes the full labeled+unlabeled dataset (~19M molecules) using soft pseudo-labels. The soft labels are generated by computing a smooth and sparse optimal transport plan Γ between f_θ's embedding similarity matrix and the full dataset, formulated as min <Γ,C> + λ/2‖Γ‖² subject to uniform marginal constraints, solved via the POT library. A KoLeo regularizer promotes uniform embedding dispersion. The total loss L_total = L_sup + L_soft + μL_reg is minimized jointly, and the paper provides a theoretical grounding (Propositions 1–2) showing the entire training procedure is equivalent to solving an inverse optimal transport problem that transfers supervised knowledge to the unsupervised encoder.

## Results

On DUD-E (zero-shot), S-MolSearch with a 0.9 similarity threshold achieves 92.56% AUROC, 75.37% BEDROC, and 51.50% EF@0.5%, surpassing the best baseline DrugCLIP (80.93% AUROC, 50.52% BEDROC, 38.07% EF@0.5%) by over 49% on BEDROC and over 30% on EF. On LIT-PCBA, S-MolSearch (0.9) reaches 61.78% AUROC, 8.48% BEDROC, and 11.97% EF@0.5%, outperforming all structure-based and ligand-based baselines. The 0.4-threshold variant (stricter protein filtering) also beats all baselines on DUD-E with 84.61% AUROC and 54.22% BEDROC, confirming robustness across data curation choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-MolSearch outperforms all 14 baselines on every reported metric across both DUD-E and LIT-PCBA. Among ligand-based methods, SHAFTS (78.20% AUROC, 32.49% EF@1%) is the strongest, yet S-MolSearch (0.9) exceeds it by 14.4 AUROC points and 15.5 EF@1% points. Among structure-based docking methods, Glide-SP (76.70% AUROC, 40.70% BEDROC) is the best, trailing S-MolSearch by 15.9 AUROC and 34.7 BEDROC points. The closest competitor overall is DrugCLIP (80.93% AUROC, 50.52% BEDROC, 38.07% EF@0.5%), a contrastive protein–molecule model; S-MolSearch (0.9) surpasses it by 11.6 AUROC, 24.9 BEDROC, and 13.4 EF@0.5% points. On LIT-PCBA, where all methods degrade due to cell-based assay noise, S-MolSearch (0.9) still leads with 61.78% AUROC and 8.48% BEDROC, beating Gnina (60.93% AUROC, 5.40% BEDROC) and DrugCLIP (57.17% AUROC, 6.23% BEDROC). Notably, S-MolSearch requires no protein structure at inference, unlike all SBVS baselines, making it applicable to targets without solved structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical connection between contrastive learning and inverse optimal transport (IOT) established by Shi et al. (ICML 2023) to a semi-supervised setting, introducing a two-encoder framework where a supervised encoder on affinity-labeled data generates soft labels via smooth OT for a second encoder trained on the full molecular dataset. It builds on Uni-Mol as the 3D molecular backbone and departs from prior ligand-based virtual screening methods (ROCS, SHAFTS, LIGSIFT) that rely solely on structural or shape overlap by incorporating binding affinity information into the contrastive objective. The semi-supervised design also draws on pseudo-labeling and teacher-student paradigms from the broader semi-supervised learning literature, but the specific use of IOT to transfer knowledge between the two encoders is novel in the molecular domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-MolSearch addresses a core bottleneck in virtual screening: the scarcity and noise of binding affinity data, by showing that a small amount of labeled data can effectively guide representation learning over ~19M unlabeled molecules. The substantial DUD-E gains (49% BEDROC improvement) demonstrate that semi-supervised contrastive learning with 3D structure can rival or exceed structure-based methods without requiring protein structures, which is critical for undruggable or disordered targets. However, the LIT-PCBA improvements are more modest, the method lacks interpretability compared to traditional shape-based search, and the dual-encoder design increases memory overhead. Open questions include whether the framework extends to broader biochemical interactions beyond affinity, how it scales to even larger chemical spaces, and whether the IOT formulation can be simplified to a single-encoder architecture.
