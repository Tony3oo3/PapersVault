## Problem

Existing positional-encoding-based context extension methods for LLMs require fine-tuning at the full target length (e.g., 256K tokens), imposing substantial computational overhead, and they exhibit a pronounced 'Lost-in-the-Middle' degradation where retrieval accuracy drops sharply for information located in the middle of long contexts. The paper asks whether one can extend context efficiently (fine-tuning only within the pre-trained 4K window) while simultaneously improving middle-position understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241793/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama-2-7B/Chat with a 4K native window) and fine-tunes it exclusively on 4K-length training sequences to unlock inference at target lengths up to 256K. At inference time, the extended model accepts a long context text sequence (up to 256K tokens) paired with a query prompt and produces a standard autoregressive text response. The critical constraint is that all training samples are truncated to the pre-trained window size N, so the model never sees a sequence longer than N during fine-tuning.

## Method

CREAM manipulates RoPE position indices by partitioning each training sample into three segments—Head, Middle, and Tail—whose relative distances collectively span the full target range [0, L-1] while each sample remains within the pre-trained length N. Two segmentation strategies are sampled with equal probability: a continuity strategy (head/tail length k=32, keeping the middle close to the pre-trained window) and a relativity strategy (head/tail length N/3, maximizing the number of distinct relative positions learned). To counteract Lost-in-the-Middle, the middle segment's start and end positions are drawn from a truncated Gaussian distribution centered on the expansion factor, biasing the model's attention toward middle positions during fine-tuning. The approach is interpolation-agnostic and composes with any RoPE frequency scaling (Linear, NTK, YaRN), and requires only 100 steps of instruction-tuning for the Chat variant to achieve near-perfect Needle-in-a-Haystack performance.

## Results

On the 'Lost in the Middle' task, CREAM-YaRN achieves 73.4% average accuracy (5K tokens) and 45.2% (10K tokens), outperforming PoSE-YaRN (50.0% and 24.4%) by over 20% on average. On LongBench, CREAM-7B-32k (instruction-tuned for only 100 steps on 4K data) achieves 35.9% average, surpassing the strongest baseline LongChat-v1.5-7B-32k (34.3%) by 1.6%. On LongChat-Lines, CREAM outperforms PoSE by 16% on average across context lengths up to 32K. CREAM also extends Llama-2-7B to 256K context with minimal perplexity degradation (e.g., 5.5 on PG-19 at 256K with YaRN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CREAM consistently outperforms PoSE (Zhu et al., 2023) across all interpolation methods on the Lost-in-the-Middle task, with the largest gap under YaRN (73.4% vs 50.0% at 5K; 45.2% vs 24.4% at 10K). On LongChat-Lines, CREAM beats PoSE by 16% on average and RandPos (Ruoss et al., 2023) by a wider margin, as RandPos's discontinuous position indices cause significant perplexity degradation. For the Chat model, CREAM-Chat surpasses SkipAlign (Wu et al., 2024) on Needle-in-a-Haystack, maintaining perfect recall until 29K tokens while SkipAlign degrades from 18K. On LongBench, CREAM-7B-32k (35.9% avg) outperforms LongChat-v1.5-7B-32k (34.3%) by 1.6% despite using only 100 steps of 4K-length instruction tuning. In the LongBench subtask comparison (Table 9), CREAM (39.6% avg, trained on 3.2K/4K data) beats EABF (39.1%), ABF (38.6%), YaRN (35.7%), PI (36.3%), and NTK-By-Parts (34.7%), all of which were trained on 3.5K/16K data. On standard NLU benchmarks, CREAM nearly matches the unmodified Llama-2-7B baseline, confirming that fine-tuning does not degrade pre-trained capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

CREAM sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work on positional encoding manipulation for context extension: RandPos (Ruoss et al., 2023), which exploited the relativity of RoPE to enable fine-tuning at shorter lengths, and PoSE (Zhu et al., 2023), which emphasized continuity of position segments during training. CREAM unifies both principles by sampling two types of segment configurations (small head/tail for continuity, N/3 head/tail for relativity) with equal probability, and adds a truncated Gaussian sampling strategy specifically targeting the middle of the context. It operates within the broader positional interpolation framework (Linear, NTK, YaRN) as a composable recipe rather than a new interpolation scheme, and is compatible with PEFT methods like LoRA and QLoRA without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: CREAM requires only 100 steps of instruction tuning (on 4K-length data) to achieve near-perfect Needle-in-a-Haystack performance and outperforms LongChat-v1.5-7B-32k on LongBench, while extending Llama-2-7B to 256K with minimal perplexity degradation. The truncated Gaussian middle sampling provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy for the 'Lost in the Middle' problem, yielding >20% average improvement over PoSE on that task. However, the paper acknowledges that decoder-only architectures inherently exhibit U-shaped performance curves, so the middle-position problem is mitigated rather than solved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-RoPE positional encodings, whether the 256K perplexity stability translates to downstream task quality at that extreme length, and whether the method's gains persist as base models natively support longer contexts (e.g., Llama 3's 8K).
