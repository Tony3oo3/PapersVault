## Problem

While LLMs excel at text-based conversation, integrating the speech modality end-to-end to generate spoken responses with natural prosody and paralinguistic features (emotion, pitch) remains unsolved. Cascaded ASR→LLM→TTS pipelines suffer from error propagation and require explicit emotion labels, while prior end-to-end speech-text models rely on limited cross-modal objectives that fail to fully leverage pretrained LLM capabilities for spoken dialog generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a spoken dialog turn as input, encoded as a sequence of 10,000-vocabulary acoustic unit tokens (50Hz, extracted via k-means clustering of XLS-R intermediate representations), along with the previous turn's speech as a reference for zero-shot voice adaptation. It produces a spoken response waveform at 22,050Hz via a flow-matching decoder (unit-Voicebox + BigVGAN), with an intermediate text response generated as part of the autoregressive decoding chain. The multi-step template means the model internally transcribes the input speech, generates a text response, then synthesizes the corresponding speech in a single forward pass.

## Method

USDM extends Mistral-7B with 10,000 acoustic unit tokens and is trained in two stages. First, a unified speech-text pretraining scheme on ~87K hours of English ASR data constructs interleaved sequences that model both correspondence (speech↔text alignment) and continuation (sequential cross-modal generation) relationships using dynamic segmentation (N = ⌊S/10⌋+1) and special tokens <|correspond|> and <|continue|>. Second, fine-tuning on DailyTalk uses a multi-step spoken dialog template (input speech → transcript → response text → response speech) that exploits the LLM's chain-of-reasoning over the intermediary text modality. A key empirical finding is that the high-vocabulary acoustic units (k=10,000) encode paralinguistic information—verified by 60.8% emotion classification accuracy and preserved pitch contours in reconstruction—enabling the model to generate prosodically appropriate responses without explicit emotion labels.

## Results

On DailyTalk, USDM achieves a MOS of 4.31±0.07 and P-MOS of 4.31±0.06, approaching the ground truth (4.51/4.35) and surpassing all baselines. In semantic evaluation, USDM scores 13.1 METEOR and 15.7 ROUGE-L, with a TTS WER of 2.0% and STT WER of 7.4%. In GPT-4-based preference tests, USDM is preferred over the Cascaded baseline 42.7% of the time (vs. 32.7% for Cascaded), and overwhelmingly preferred over From Scratch (79.7%) and SpeechGPT (61.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USDM outperforms all three baselines on prosody (P-MOS) and semantic coherence (METEOR, ROUGE-L). Against the Cascaded baseline (whisper-large-v3 + Mistral-7B + Voicebox), USDM achieves a higher P-MOS (4.31 vs. 4.22) and better semantic scores (METEOR 13.1 vs. 12.5, ROUGE-L 15.7 vs. 15.0), though Cascaded has lower STT WER (3.8% vs. 7.4%) due to its dedicated whisper-large-v3 ASR trained on 5M hours. Against SpeechGPT, USDM leads on every metric: MOS (4.31 vs. 4.08), P-MOS (4.31 vs. 4.04), METEOR (13.1 vs. 9.9), ROUGE-L (15.7 vs. 11.8), STT WER (7.4% vs. 12.4%), and TTS WER (2.0% vs. 23.2%). The From Scratch baseline (Mistral-7B fine-tuned directly on dialog data without cross-modal pretraining) performs worst, with STT WER of 58.1% and TTS WER of 64.0%, confirming that the unified speech-text pretraining is essential for transferring LLM capabilities to spoken dialog. In GPT-4 preference tests, USDM is preferred over Cascaded 42.7% of the time (p<0.05), and decisively over From Scratch (79.7%) and SpeechGPT (61.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based spoken language modeling (SpeechGPT, AudioLM, SpiRit-LM) by proposing a unified speech-text pretraining scheme that simultaneously models both correspondence and continuation relationships between modalities, rather than the single-task objectives (ASR or TTS) used in prior work. It builds on Voicebox's flow-matching architecture for the speech decoder and XLS-R-based acoustic units for speech tokenization, but departs from cascaded pipelines (ASR → LLM → TTS) by performing end-to-end speech-to-speech dialog generation with an intermediate text-bridging template inspired by chain-of-thought reasoning. A key empirical departure is the demonstration that high-vocabulary acoustic units (k=10,000), previously assumed to encode primarily semantic content, also carry substantial paralinguistic information such as emotion and pitch variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USDM establishes a practical blueprint for speech-enabled conversational LLMs that preserve prosody without requiring explicit emotion labels or separate ASR/TTS modules, addressing a gap the authors identify as 'elusive' in prior LLM-based dialog work. The unified pretraining scheme is a reusable technique that could transfer to other speech-text tasks beyond dialog. However, significant limitations remain: the model is evaluated only on a single 20-hour English dataset (DailyTalk), relies on an intermediate text step that introduces error propagation, and the paralinguistic findings are validated on a small emotion dataset (CREMA-D). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual settings, multi-turn dialogs with unseen speakers, and whether direct speech-to-speech generation without text bridging is feasible.
