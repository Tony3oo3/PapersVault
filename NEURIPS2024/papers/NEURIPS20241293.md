## Problem

Existing lane detection methods rely primarily on finer-scale local information to regress key points of lane lines, causing large deviations when local regions are occluded by vehicles or degraded by poor lighting. Additionally, prior supervision losses (e.g., LIoU) fail to properly penalize predictions that are far from ground truth on curved lanes, ignoring the thin-and-long geometric structure of lane lines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241293/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 800×320 pixels and produces 150 lane anchors per image, each parameterized by a start point (x₀, y₀), an angle of inclination θ, a length l, and a P-point offset map. Final key points are computed analytically via x_i = tan(θ)·(y_i − y₀) + x₀ + o_i with y-coordinates sampled at equal intervals, and bi-linear interpolation connects them into continuous lane curves. At inference, Non-Maximum Suppression filters the 150 anchors down to the detected lane lines.

## Method

The core architecture is a Siamese Transformer (LATR) with shared parameters across pyramid levels, where a lane query is progressively refined from high-level (global semantics) to low-level (finer-scale) features extracted by a Swin Transformer backbone. The lane query is integrated into the encoder (unlike DETR) to unify input scales across levels, enabling the shared-parameter Siamese design; Deformable Attention is used for cross-attention in the decoder. A novel Curve-IoU (CIoU) loss replaces standard IoU by adding a ReLU-penalized L1 distance term to the overlap computation, so that spatial separation between predicted and ground-truth lane lines is accurately captured even at large offsets on curved segments. Training uses AdamW with cosine decay (lr=6e-4), a combined cross-entropy classification loss and CIoU regression loss, and 150 lane anchors per image.

## Results

On the OpenLane dataset, the proposed method (Swin-base backbone) achieves an F1 score of 69.7, surpassing the strongest prior method CondLSTR (ResNet101, F1=67.8) by 1.9 points and CondLaneNet (ResNet101, F1=59.1) by 10.6 points. On CULane, the method reaches 80.85 F1, outperforming CLRNet (DLA34, 80.47) by 0.38 points. On TuSimple, the method achieves 98.01 F1 (small backbone), edging out CLRNet (ResNet101, 97.68) by 0.33 points. The tiny variant runs at 172 FPS with only 10.2 GFlops, matching the efficiency of CondLaneNet-ResNet18 while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OpenLane, the proposed method decisively outperforms all CNN-based baselines: CondLaneNet (ResNet101, F1=59.1) and CLRNet (ResNet101, F1=59.1) trail by over 10 points, while the strongest Transformer-based competitor CondLSTR (ResNet101, F1=67.8) is surpassed by 1.9 points (base) to 5.0 points (tiny vs. ResNet18). On CULane, the method achieves 80.85 F1 (base), beating CLRNet with DLA34 (80.47) by 0.38 points and CondLaneNet (ResNet101, 79.48) by 1.37 points; it also leads in five of eight challenging sub-scenarios including Crowded (80.21 vs. 79.59) and Hlight (76.04 vs. 75.30). On TuSimple, the small variant reaches 98.01 F1, marginally ahead of CLRNet (ResNet101, 97.68) and Laneformer (ResNet50, 97.56), though differences are small given the dataset's simpler highway conditions. Notably, the tiny variant (172 FPS, 10.2 GFlops) matches the speed of CondLaneNet-ResNet18 while achieving 16 points higher F1 on OpenLane, demonstrating a significant accuracy-efficiency tradeoff advantage. The method loses to anchor-based methods (e.g., ADNet, PriorLane) on specific CULane sub-scenarios like Shadow and Arrow, which the authors attribute to the smaller CULane training set (88K vs. 160K images on OpenLane).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR-style query-based detection framework and the LIoU loss from CLRNet, but departs from both in key ways: it integrates the lane query into the encoder (rather than the decoder) and employs a Siamese structure with shared parameters across multi-scale features, enabling a high-to-low hierarchical refinement that progressively incorporates finer-scale information. The Curve-IoU loss extends LIoU by adding an L1-distance penalty term specifically designed for the thin-and-long geometry of lane lines, addressing a known failure mode where LIoU cannot distinguish predictions at different distances from ground truth. The overall positioning is within the attention-based lane detection lineage (LSTR, CLRNet, PriorLane), but the Siamese multi-scale refinement and the geometry-aware loss represent the primary conceptual departures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.0-point F1 improvement on OpenLane and consistent SOTA across three benchmarks demonstrate that hierarchical multi-scale refinement in a Siamese Transformer is a practical recipe for robust lane detection under occlusion and poor lighting. The paper's observation that Transformer-based methods benefit more from larger, more diverse datasets (OpenLane 160K vs. CULane 88K) than anchor-based CNN methods suggests a scaling advantage that could widen with future data. However, the method still trails anchor-based approaches in specific CULane sub-scenarios (Shadow, Noline, Arrow), and the authors explicitly call for larger, more diverse datasets to fully realize the potential of this architecture. The shared-parameter Siamese design also offers a useful efficiency template for other multi-scale detection tasks.
