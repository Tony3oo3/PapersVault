## Problem

LoRA fine-tuned diffusion models suffer from distribution collapse (data copying) at higher adapter strengths and ranks, especially when trained on small datasets, while reducing rank causes underfitting. Existing adaptive rank methods like SoRA fix the effective rank at inference time, lacking the flexibility needed for vision tasks where the required rank varies across diffusion timesteps. Additionally, all prior low-rank adaptation operates in the feature space, overlooking the compact, decorrelated representations available in the frequency domain.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image stylization, the method takes a text prompt and a trained FouRA adapter (rank 64, applied to self- and cross-attention layers of a Stable Diffusion v1.5 UNet) and produces 512×512 stylized images, with adapter strength α ∈ [0,1] controlling the blend between base model and adapter outputs. For concept editing, it takes a reference image plus positive/negative concept prompts and produces an edited image via SDEdit-style inference. For language tasks, it takes text input and produces classification labels on GLUE and commonsense reasoning benchmarks. Multi-adapter merging linearly combines the outputs of two independently trained FouRA adapters at inference without joint training.

## Method

FouRA applies the LoRA down-projection A and up-projection B in the frequency domain (1D DFT or DCT along the embedding dimension k₁) rather than the feature space, folding the blending scalar α into the low-rank subspace. A learned adaptive gating function G(·), implemented as an MLP followed by sigmoid and entropy thresholding, operates inside the low-rank frequency subspace to dynamically select which frequency basis components to retain, effectively varying the rank per layer, per input, and across diffusion timesteps at inference. The frequency transform yields a more compact singular value spread (proven via Lemma 4.1), which reduces the generalization error upper bound (Lemma 4.2) and makes the learned adapter subspaces more decorrelated from the base weights (amplification factor >2× vs. LoRA). This decorrelation also enables training-free multi-adapter merging via simple linear combination, as the projection norms between different FouRA adapters are significantly lower than for LoRA.

## Results

On text-to-image stylization (rank 64, Realistic Vision-v3.0), FouRA achieves HPSv2 scores of 30.6 (Paintings) and 34.1 (Blue-Fire) at α=0.8, outperforming LoRA (27.7 and 33.8) by up to 3.1 points, while also improving LPIPS diversity by 5–7 points. On commonsense reasoning with Llama-3-8B, FouRA at rank 16 (28.31M params) achieves 85.1% average accuracy, surpassing LoRA at rank 32 (56.60M params) at 82.9% with half the trainable parameters. On GLUE benchmarks with DeBERTa-V3, FouRA outperforms SoRA on 4 of 6 tasks (e.g., CoLA 70.6 vs 69.9, STS-B 91.6 vs 91.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA [17], FouRA consistently wins on all evaluated metrics: HPSv2 improves by 2.9–3.1 points on Paintings (RV-v3.0, α=0.8) and by 0.3–1.1 points on Blue-Fire, while LPIPS diversity gains 5–7 points, indicating substantially reduced distribution collapse. The advantage grows with adapter strength α, confirming that FouRA mitigates the overfitting/data-copying artifacts that plague LoRA at high α. Against SoRA [3] (adaptive rank fixed at inference), FouRA's inference-adaptive masking in the frequency domain achieves higher HPSv2 across all α values (Fig. 9) and outperforms SoRA on 4 of 6 GLUE benchmarks (CoLA +0.7, STS-B +0.2, SST-2 +0.3, QNLI +0.3), while matching on MNLI and slightly trailing on MRPC (−0.2). On commonsense reasoning, FouRA at rank 16 (85.1% avg) beats LoRA at rank 32 (82.9% avg) by 2.2 points with half the parameters, and FouRA at rank 32 (85.3%) further extends this gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

FouRA extends LoRA by relocating the down-projection and up-projection matrices into the frequency domain (DFT or DCT along the embedding dimension), motivated by the known decorrelation and energy-concentration properties of spectral transforms. It departs from prior adaptive-rank methods (SoRA, AdaLoRA) by making the effective rank input-dependent and varying across diffusion timesteps at inference, rather than freezing it after training. The concept-slider editing experiments build directly on Gandikota et al.'s disentangled objective, and the multi-adapter merging capability is positioned against joint-training approaches like MoLE and ZipLoRA, offering a training-free alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FouRA addresses a practical and well-documented failure mode of LoRA fine-tuning—distribution collapse and data copying—by showing that frequency-domain low-rank adaptation yields more compact singular value spectra, which theoretically tightens generalization bounds and empirically produces more diverse outputs. The training-free multi-adapter merging and the inference-adaptive rank selection are practically useful for style composition workflows. Limitations include modest hardware support for frequency transforms, validation primarily on SD 1.5, and the adaptive masking mechanism remaining somewhat opaque. Open questions include whether frequency-domain PEFT generalizes to transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., SDXL, Flux) and whether the learned spectral masks can be made interpretable for targeted frequency-band control.
