## Problem

Existing GNN-based rigid-body simulators rely on discrete autoregressive rollout, making them unable to capture long-term continuous evolution, and they typically ignore the instantaneous state changes caused by collisions. Additionally, these methods treat all mesh nodes at a single level, neglecting the intrinsic hierarchical structure between individual mesh points and the rigid objects they compose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241884/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives only the initial frame X^0 (positions, velocities, and static attributes like friction for each mesh node) along with a dynamically constructed mesh graph where edges connect nodes within a distance threshold d=0.08. It produces continuous trajectories at arbitrary time steps for both individual mesh nodes and aggregated object centroids, plus predicted collision event times used to compute contact prediction accuracy. The object-level representation is initialized as the mean of its constituent node observations, and the graph topology is updated at each time step as nodes move.

## Method

EGODE introduces a two-level coupled graph ODE where mesh node states evolve under the influence of neighboring nodes and their parent object, while object states evolve under the influence of all other objects and the summarized local node information, enabling joint continuous-time modeling of hierarchical dynamics. A learnable event function g(t, [x_i, v_i, x_i', v_i']) is evaluated between nearby node pairs during ODE integration to detect the zero-crossing that marks a collision time t*. At each detected collision, both node-level and object-level states are instantaneously updated via separate message-passing functions that aggregate information from the colliding object's nodes. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a combined MSE loss at both the node and object levels, and the ODE is solved with a standard Euler solver, with the event module iteratively interrupting and resuming integration at each collision.

## Results

On the RigidFall dataset, EGODE achieves the lowest prediction MSE across all training set sizes (500, 1000, 5000) and both prediction horizons (t=20, t=40), with a best MSE of 0.12×10⁻² at |Train|=5000, t=20. On the Physion dataset, EGODE attains the highest contact prediction accuracy across all 8 scenarios, reaching 94.7% on Dominoes and 90.0% on Collide. Compared to the strongest baseline SGNN, EGODE improves accuracy by 5.9% on Dominoes and 4.5% on Collide, and reduces MSE by 10–15% on RigidFall long-horizon predictions (t=40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGODE outperforms all seven baselines on both RigidFall (MSE) and Physion (contact accuracy). The closest competitor is SGNN [20], which uses a discrete subequivariant GNN with object-level and particle-level message passing; EGODE reduces its MSE by ~37% at |Train|=5000, t=40 (0.46 vs. 0.74) and gains 5.9% accuracy on Physion Dominoes. SEGNO [32], the only other continuous (Neural ODE) baseline, is outperformed by EGODE by a wide margin (e.g., 0.46 vs. 1.44 MSE at |Train|=5000, t=40), suggesting that the coupled architecture and event module provide substantial gains over a single-level ODE. Among discrete GNN baselines, GNS [52] and EGNN [53] are competitive at short horizons but degrade sharply at t=40, while DPI-Net [30] and GMN [22] show larger variance. SocialODE [62], designed for multi-agent trajectory forecasting rather than rigid-body collision, performs the weakest among learned methods, confirming that domain-specific inductive biases (hierarchical structure, collision events) are critical for rigid dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGODE sits at the intersection of three research threads: neural ODEs for continuous-time dynamics (Chen et al., 2018), coupled graph ODEs for hierarchical interacting systems (Huang et al., 2021), and neural event functions for detecting discontinuities within ODE integration (Chen et al., 2020). It departs from the dominant GNN-based physics simulator paradigm (GNS, MeshGraphNet, SGNN, EGNN) that relies on discrete autoregressive rollout, and from prior continuous GNN approaches (SEGNO, SocialODE) that model smooth evolution but ignore instantaneous collision events. The key architectural contribution is unifying a two-level coupled graph ODE (mesh-node and object representations) with a learnable event module that detects collision times and applies instantaneous state updates within the ODE solver loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in data-driven rigid-body simulation: how to simultaneously capture smooth continuous evolution and abrupt discontinuous changes from collisions within a single differentiable framework. The coupled hierarchical design (node-level + object-level ODEs) provides a principled way to exploit the intrinsic multi-scale structure of rigid systems, and the event module offers a general mechanism for incorporating discontinuities into ODE-based simulators. However, the approach is limited to rigid bodies without hinges or deformable components, and the generalization to fluid dynamics or human trajectory forecasting remains future work. Open questions include whether the event module scales to systems with many simultaneous collisions and whether the coupled ODE formulation can be extended to soft-body or articulated systems.
