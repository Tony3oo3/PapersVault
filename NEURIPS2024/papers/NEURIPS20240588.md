## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models require hyper-detailed image-text data to achieve comprehensive visual perception, but existing caption engines (e.g., ShareGPT4V) produce descriptions that miss small objects, text elements, spatial relations, and external knowledge due to their limited perceptual capabilities. This scarcity of dense, accurate annotations hinders MLLM progress on fine-grained understanding tasks, especially for high-resolution images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240588/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes high-resolution images (short-edge ≥ 448 px) from LAION-2B alongside four types of expert-derived priors: closed-set and open-set object detection boxes (EVA02 + OWL-ViTv2), full OCR text (PaddleOCR), scene-level tags (RAM++), and the original LAION short caption as world knowledge. The output is a 1-million-sample dataset of dense descriptions averaging ~190 words and 11 sentences per caption, covering objects, attributes, spatial positions, text content, and contextual knowledge, plus a 100K-sample GPT-4V-annotated meta dataset used for supervision.

## Method

Perceptual Fusion integrates diverse vision experts as image priors and uses a low-budget MLLM (LLaVA-1.6 7B) as a central pivot for information fusion. The key insight is that specialized perception models outperform generalist MLLMs within their respective domains, so feeding their structured outputs (detection boxes, OCR strings, tags) as contextual information into a captioning prompt compensates for the engine's perceptual blind spots. The authors first prompt GPT-4V with this expert-augmented context to generate 100K gold-standard descriptions (DenseFusion-4V-100K), then supervised-fine-tune LLaVA-1.6 on this meta dataset (~5.5 h on 32×A100, 2 epochs) to obtain a scalable, open-source caption engine. Data curation from LAION-2B involves high-resolution filtering (≥448 px), semantic deduplication via k-means clustering on EVA-CLIP features (50K clusters, ε=0.4), and selecting the 20 images closest to each cluster center to maximize diversity, yielding the final 1M-sample DenseFusion-1M dataset.

## Results

Using DenseFusion-1M in pre-training, LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B) improves from 66.8→69.3 on ScienceQA, 58.2→62.0 on TextVQA, 64.3→69.2 on MMBench, and 30.5→37.8 on MM-Vet, outperforming ShareGPT4V on all 10 benchmarks. Under the high-resolution S² setting, LLaVA-S² (Ours) achieves 72.1 on ScienceQA, 67.4 on TextVQA, and 70.7 on MMBench, surpassing LLaVA-1.6 (70.1, 64.9, 67.4) while using only a fifth of its visual tokens. The largest gains appear in text-recognition (TextVQA +3.8 over baseline) and holistic understanding (MM-Vet +7.3), confirming the value of hyper-detailed captions for fine-grained perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ShareGPT4V (the closest dataset competitor), DenseFusion-1M wins on all 10 benchmarks with LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B), with the largest margin on TextVQA (+1.6) and MMBench (+0.4), despite ShareGPT4V using images from COCO/VG that overlap with evaluation domains while DenseFusion-1M uses generic LAION images. Under the high-resolution S² setting, LLaVA-S² (Ours) outperforms ShareGPT4V-S² on every benchmark (e.g., TextVQA 67.4 vs 64.4, MMBench 70.7 vs 68.0, MM-Vet 37.5 vs 35.0) and also beats LLaVA-1.6 on 8 of 10 benchmarks (losing only on VQA-v2 by 0.2 and MM-Vet by 6.4), while using roughly one-fifth the visual tokens. Compared to other low-resolution MLLMs (InstructBLIP, QwenVL-Chat, mPLUG-Owl2, InternVL-Chat, LVIS-4V), the DenseFusion-1M-trained model leads on TextVQA (62.0 vs. 34.5–61.5) and MM-Vet (37.8 vs. 26.2–36.5). The consistent gains across all three LLM backbones (Vicuna-7B, LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2-7B) confirm that the improvement stems from data quality rather than a specific model architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on large-scale image-text dataset construction for MLLM pre-training (BLIP-LAION, ShareGPT4V, ALLaVA) by introducing a 'Perceptual Fusion' pipeline that integrates specialized vision experts—RAM++ for tagging, EVA02/OWL-ViTv2 for detection, PaddleOCR for text recognition, and LAION web captions for world knowledge—as image priors into a low-budget LLaVA-1.6 caption engine. Conceptually, it departs from prior approaches that rely solely on a single generalist MLLM (GPT-4V or open-source LLaVA) for captioning, instead treating expert outputs as intermediate perceptual signals that compensate for the generalist's blind spots in small-object detection, OCR, and open-vocabulary recognition. The data curation pipeline builds on SemDeDup for semantic deduplication and the S² (Scaling on Scales) framework for high-resolution training, positioning the work at the intersection of dataset engineering and high-resolution MLLM design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DenseFusion-1M provides a 1M-scale, hyper-detailed image-text dataset generated at a fraction of the cost of GPT-4V-based pipelines, making comprehensive visual perception training more accessible to the broader MLLM community. The finding that a 7B model augmented with expert priors can rival frontier-model captioning quality suggests a practical recipe for scaling detailed annotations without frontier-API budgets. However, limitations remain: expert outputs are inherently noisy (only high-confidence predictions are retained), the approach is bounded by the caption engine's context window, and the paper acknowledges that perfectly describing over-complicated scenes is still infeasible. Open questions include whether the expert-fusion paradigm generalizes to video or 3D perception, and whether the quality gap versus true GPT-4V captions narrows as base MLLMs improve.
