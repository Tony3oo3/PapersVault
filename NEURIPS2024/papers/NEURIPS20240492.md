## Problem

Pretrained model embeddings used for k-nearest neighbor classification do not exploit data-specific information, and traditional feature engineering strategies require task-specific expertise and tuning per backbone. This paper asks whether self-supervised gradients, which encode first-order learning signals adapted to the downstream data, can enhance frozen pretrained representations without any parameter updates or training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes any frozen pretrained backbone (ViT, BERT, T5, SSAST) and its standard input (RGB image resized to 224×224, text sequence, or audio spectrogram), and produces a d-dimensional enhanced feature vector. Gradients are extracted from the attention output projection of the last transformer block, vectorized, and projected via a binary random projection matrix to match the embedding dimensionality before concatenation. The final FUNGI feature is PCA-reduced to d dimensions, enabling iso-storage kNN retrieval regardless of how many gradient sources are combined.

## Method

FUNGI computes gradients from three self-supervised objectives—KL divergence against a uniform distribution, DINO self-distillation, and a patch-based SimCLR contrastive loss—on top of a frozen backbone with a randomly initialized linear projection head. The per-sample gradients are vectorized, downsampled via binary random projections (Achlioptas) to the embedding dimension, L2-normalized alongside the L2-normalized model embeddings, and concatenated. A final PCA step reduces the combined feature back to d dimensions, allowing multiple losses to be combined at no additional storage cost. The key insight, validated via CKA similarity analysis, is that gradients from different SSL objectives encode complementary information to the embeddings, and their combination consistently improves kNN accuracy across 20 backbones, 11 image datasets, 5 text datasets, and 2 audio datasets, as well as retrieval-based semantic segmentation (e.g., +17% mIoU over DINO on Pascal VOC).

## Results

FUNGI features consistently improve kNN classification accuracy across 11 image datasets, with mean accuracy gains of +4.4% (IN1K backbone) and +2.9% (IN21K backbone) over plain embeddings in the full-dataset setting, and +2.8% and +2.4% in the 5-shot setting. For retrieval-based semantic segmentation on Pascal VOC, FUNGI boosts DINO ViT-B/16 by +17.2 mIoU (44.9→62.1) at the smallest memory bank size, and by +11.3 mIoU at the largest. In the text domain, FUNGI improves BERT Banking-77 kNN accuracy from 55.4% to 67.9% (+12.5) with KL+SimCLR gradients, and in audio, ESC-50 accuracy rises from 42.8% to 47.0% (+4.2) with KL gradients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is FUNGI features against the frozen model's own embeddings, and FUNGI wins in the vast majority of settings: across 7 backbones in Table 2, the full K+D+S combination improves mean kNN accuracy on all 7 models in the full-dataset setting (gains range from +1.8% for DINOv2 to +5.6% for DeIT ViT-B/32) and on 6 of 7 in the 5-shot setting. The single external baseline is HummingBird (Balazevic et al., 2024), a self-supervised model specifically pretrained for retrieval-based dense prediction. On Pascal VOC semantic segmentation with a 1024×10⁴ memory bank, FUNGI-enhanced DINO ViT-B/16 achieves 67.0 mIoU versus HummingBird's 70.5 mIoU (a 3.5-point gap), and on ADE20K it reaches 25.2 versus 28.3 mIoU (3.1-point gap). Crucially, FUNGI requires no training whatsoever, whereas HummingBird employs a specialized self-supervised pretraining strategy. In the data-efficient segmentation setting (Table 4), FUNGI with DINO ViT-B/16 actually surpasses end-to-end fine-tuning of DINO on Pascal VOC (46.8 vs. 44.3 mIoU at 1/64 data), demonstrating that the gradient signal can be more effective than parameter updates in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUNGI repurposes self-supervised gradients as retrieval features, departing from both classical feature engineering (SIFT, HOG, Fisher vectors) and modern augmentation strategies (Head2Toe, kNN-prompting) that require task-specific tuning. Conceptually, it is closest to Mu et al. (2020) and Wei et al. (2022), who showed that Jacobian/eNTK features can enhance linear classifiers, but FUNGI is computationally cheaper (single-layer gradient vs. full Jacobian) and requires no annotations. It also relates to test-time training (Sun et al., 2020; Hardt & Sun, 2024), which uses SSL objectives to update parameters on test data, but FUNGI keeps all parameters frozen and instead uses the gradient signal as a feature for kNN retrieval. The method builds directly on DINO, SimCLR, and binary random projections (Achlioptas, 2003) as its loss and dimensionality-reduction primitives, and extends the retrieval-based in-context scene understanding framework of Balazevic et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUNGI offers a genuinely plug-and-play enhancement to any frozen pretrained backbone, with no training or task-specific tuning, which is practically attractive for retrieval-augmented systems and few-shot settings where labeled data is scarce. The +17% mIoU gain over DINO on Pascal VOC semantic segmentation without any training is a strong result, and the consistent (if more modest) 2–5% kNN improvements across 20 backbones and 18+ datasets in three modalities demonstrate broad applicability. Key limitations include diminishing returns as pretraining dataset size grows (IN21K vs. IN1K, EVA-CLIP vs. CLIP), the failure of SimCLR gradients for contrastively-pretrained models, and non-trivial computational overhead from multi-view losses. Open questions include identifying optimal SSL objectives per modality (e.g., masked language modeling for text) and whether the approach scales to even larger backbones or benefits from learned (rather than random) projections.
