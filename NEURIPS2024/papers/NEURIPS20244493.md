## Problem

Existing local solvers for Personalized PageRank (APPR, ISTA, FISTA, ASPR) all rely on monotonicity properties of residuals or active sets for their runtime analysis, which blocks the development of faster accelerated local methods. Fountoulakis & Yang posed the open question of whether a local solver with Õ(1/(√α·ε)) operations exists, but no prior work demonstrated that standard accelerated iterative solvers (Chebyshev, Heavy-Ball) could be effectively localized while maintaining provable local runtime bounds.

## Input / Output

The methods take an undirected graph G, a source node s, a damping factor α ∈ (0,1), and a precision tolerance ε, and produce a local estimate π̂ of the PPR vector satisfying ‖D⁻¹(π̂−π)‖∞ ≤ ε. Crucially, each solver only accesses neighbors of currently active nodes via a queue-based evolving set process, so the total number of operations is independent of the full graph size. The symmetrized system Qx = b is solved where Q = I − ((1−α)/(1+α))D⁻¹/²AD⁻¹/² is a symmetric positive-definite M-matrix with condition number 1/α, and the PPR vector is recovered as π = D¹/²x.

## Method

The paper introduces a locally evolving set process framework that characterizes algorithm locality through the running average volume vol̄(S_t) and residual ratio γ̄_t of active node sets, proving the ratio vol̄(S_t)/γ̄_t ≤ 1/ε. This framework reveals that APPR is a local variant of Gauss-Seidel SOR, enabling the derivation of a tighter runtime bound Õ(vol̄(S_t)/(α·γ̄_t)) that mirrors actual performance. For accelerated methods, the key technical novelty is rewriting residual updates as nonhomogeneous second-order difference equations with parameterized coefficients (via Chebyshev polynomials), which yields a runtime bound of Õ(vol̄(S_t)/(√α·(2−c))) for both local Chebyshev (LocCH) and local Heavy-Ball (LocHB) without requiring any monotonicity of residuals or active sets. A parallelizable local gradient descent (LocGD) is also derived with the same Õ(vol̄(S_t)/(α·γ̄_t)) bound, and all methods are shown to achieve up to 100× speedup over their standard counterparts on 17 real-world graphs.

## Results

On local clustering across 5 graphs, LocSOR achieves 10–15× fewer operations than APPR (e.g., 6.5×10⁴ vs. 6.9×10⁵ on G₁) and 6–9× fewer than FISTA, while also being the fastest in wall-clock time (0.043 s vs. 0.127 s for APPR on G₁). On two huge-scale graphs (ogbn-papers100M with 111M nodes/1.6B edges and com-friendster with 65M nodes/1.8B edges), the proposed local methods are several times faster than APPR. The paper reports up to a hundredfold speedup of local solvers over their standard (non-local) counterparts, with the advantage most pronounced around ε = 1/n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against APPR [2], LocSOR consistently uses an order of magnitude fewer operations (10–15× reduction) and runs 2–5× faster in wall-clock time across all 5 clustering graphs; LocCH and LocHB also outperform APPR in estimation error per operation (Fig. 5). Against FISTA [22] (momentum-based local method), LocSOR uses 6–9× fewer operations and is roughly 2× faster, while FISTA's nondecreasing active-set requirement inflates its operation count. Against ISTA [13], the locally evolving set process methods again require fewer operations because they do not enforce monotonic sparsity growth. Against ASPR [37] (nested subspace pursuit), the paper shows ASPR actually requires more operations than APPR (Fig. 3), so LocSOR dominates both. Finally, compared to non-local standard solvers (GD, SOR, HB, CH) and CGM, the localized versions achieve up to 100× speedup, with the gap widening as ε decreases below 1/n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the classic APPR algorithm (Andersen et al., 2006) as a local variant of Gauss-Seidel Successive Over-Relaxation, building on the variational reformulation of the PPR system by Fountoulakis et al. (2019) and directly addressing the open problem posed by Fountoulakis & Yang (2022) about achieving √α-rate local solvers. The locally evolving set process framework is inspired by stochastic evolving sets (Morris & Peres, 2003) but applied to deterministic iterative solvers. Unlike prior local methods (APPR, ISTA, FISTA, ASPR) whose runtime analyses critically depend on monotonicity of residuals or active sets, this work circumvents that barrier by characterizing residuals of accelerated methods via parameterized second-order difference equations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled way to localize any standard iterative solver (GD, SOR, Chebyshev, Heavy-Ball) for graph-induced linear systems, with the accelerated methods achieving the same √α convergence rate as their global counterparts while remaining local. The up-to-100x speedup over standard solvers and significant gains over APPR on 100M+ node graphs have direct practical implications for GNN training (APPNP, PPRGo) and large-scale graph analytics. Key limitations include the unproven assumption that the geometric mean of residual reduction is Θ(√α) for LocCH, and the partial loss of acceleration benefit when α is very small. Open questions include whether all standard iterative methods (e.g., conjugate gradient) can be effectively localized, and whether higher-order neighbor exploration or stochastic subsampling of active nodes could further improve the framework.
