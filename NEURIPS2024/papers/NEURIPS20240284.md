## Problem

Out-of-vocabulary (OOV) items lack behavioral embeddings, so current models generate 'makeshift' embeddings from content features alone, creating a substantial content-behavior gap that degrades recommendation quality. Existing OOV recommendation methods focus exclusively on improving the one-shot embedding generation (via GANs, distillation, or dropout) but overlook the sequential backpropagation process that shapes IV item embeddings, leaving OOV embeddings suboptimally tuned to the deployed recommender system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 200-dimensional content feature vectors for OOV items (e.g., product descriptions embedded via LLM in production), the full user embedding matrix E_u, and the IV item embedding matrix E_iv from a pre-trained recommender backbone (MF or NGCF). It produces a refined OOV item embedding in the same d-dimensional space as IV embeddings, which is then scored against user embeddings via dot product for recommendation. The state representation includes a countdown timer l_t = N - t alongside the evolving embedding h_t, and the policy outputs a probability distribution over a sampled exploration set of users plus a termination action rather than the full user space.

## Method

USIM formulates OOV embedding refinement as a Markov Decision Process where an agent sequentially 'imagines' user interactions and updates the OOV embedding via gradient steps (h_{t+1} = h_t + λ·e_{a_t}), mimicking the backpropagation that trains IV embeddings. The reward combines three signals: embedding alignment (reduction in Euclidean distance to the target IV embedding), recommendation performance (reduction in prediction error over the item's true interacted users), and a per-step penalty to encourage efficient convergence. Training uses RecPPO, a modified PPO whose value network loss includes a recommendation-oriented supervision term that directly anchors V(ω) to zero when the state embedding equals the target item embedding. To handle the massive user action space, USIM constructs a per-state exploration set comprising top-k users by cosine similarity to the residual (e_i − h_{i,t}), k random users, and the termination action, which the policy network scores via a softmax over user embeddings projected through learned weight matrices.

## Results

On the MF backbone with CiteULike, USIM achieves OOV Recall@K of 0.2753 and NDCG@K of 0.1647, outperforming the best baseline ALDI (0.2622/0.1542) by 5.0% and 6.8% respectively. On the GNN backbone, USIM reaches OOV Recall@K of 0.2493 (CiteULike) and 0.1564 (MovieLens), surpassing ALDI by 0.93% and 1.45%. For overall recommendation, USIM achieves 0.1926 Recall and 0.1530 NDCG on MF/CiteULike (19.0% and 11.0% improvement over the best baseline), while maintaining IV item performance unchanged. Online A/B testing on an e-commerce platform shows USIM improves OOV item GMV by 20.3% over random and 13.6% over ALDI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USIM outperforms all 8 OOV recommendation baselines on both MF and GNN backbones across both datasets. The closest competitor is ALDI (knowledge distillation), which USIM beats by 5.0% OOV Recall on MF/CiteULike (0.2753 vs 0.2622) and by 19.0% on overall Recall. Dropout-based methods (DropoutNet, MTPR, Heater, CLCRec) consistently underperform on OOV MovieLens (e.g., Heater drops to 0.0601 OOV Recall) and also degrade IV item performance, whereas USIM preserves IV metrics. Generative methods GAR and DeepMusic show severe instability on MovieLens OOV (GAR: 0.0348 Recall), while USIM maintains consistent gains. Against RL baselines (Wolpertinger Policy and Hierarchical RL), USIM significantly outperforms both in OOV and overall recommendation, with the other two methods showing no apparent improvement over their initial states, validating the tailored exploration set and RecPPO design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OOV/cold-start item recommendation literature, which has been dominated by one-shot embedding generation (generative models like GAR and ALDI) and robustness training (dropout models like Heater and CLCRec). Its key departure is framing the embedding refinement as a sequential decision process: rather than producing a final embedding in a single forward pass, USIM formulates the optimization as a Markov Decision Process where an RL agent (RecPPO, a modified PPO) iteratively imagines user interactions and updates the OOV embedding via backpropagation. The method is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated on both MF and NGCF, and builds directly on PPO's value-network training by injecting recommendation-specific supervision signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: USIM has been deployed on Alibaba's e-commerce platform for months, serving millions of OOV items to billions of users, with online A/B tests showing up to 20% GMV improvement over random baselines. However, the method introduces four key hyperparameters (k, N, p, λ) that require careful tuning, and the autoregressive imagination process is slower than one-shot generators. Open questions include whether the RL formulation scales to user spaces orders of magnitude larger than the benchmark datasets, and whether the reward design generalizes beyond dot-product-based scoring functions used in MF and GNN backbones.
