## Problem

While scaling laws have well-characterized how parameter count, data volume, and training tokens drive base capabilities, the influence of architecture on these capabilities remains poorly understood. FFN-Wider Transformers—a simple modification that widens the FFN intermediate dimension from 4d to 32d—exhibit a significant decline in out-of-distribution generalization, transfer learning, and few-shot performance at matched pre-training loss, yet the underlying mechanism linking this architectural change to the capability drop is unexplained.

## Input / Output

The models consume English text sequences (max length 128 for BERT/GPT experiments, 2048 for the MoE experiment) and produce token-level predictions for language modeling, fine-tuned classification scores for transfer learning, and few-shot multiple-choice answers. A critical evaluation constraint is that all cross-architecture comparisons are made at aligned in-distribution pre-training loss to isolate architectural inductive bias from scale effects; the 14B MoE experiment additionally aligns parameter count, compute, and training steps (100B tokens on SlimPajama CC+C4), with OOD evaluation on the remaining SlimPajama subsets.

## Method

The paper identifies MHA as a 'combination function' (many-to-one, context-dependent) and FFN as a 'transformation function' (one-to-one, context-insensitive), and shows via mutual information and token-prediction analyses that FFN-Wider models shift the actual contribution ratio away from combination, degrading base capabilities. To reverse this, the proposed Combination Enhanced Architecture (CEA) splits the wider FFN into an Outer-FFN (retaining transformation) and an Inner-FFN relocated inside the MHA layer, where a direct pathway ensures the current position's query, key, and value bypass the Inner-FFN so that only context representations are transformed—forcing all Inner-FFN output through the attention combination. The optimal Outer-FFN width ratio is 0% for BERT and 12.5% for GPT. The same principle extends to MoE Transformers by converting the MoE layer into an Inner-MoE within MHA (with the current position masked to prevent leakage), yielding significant OOD and few-shot gains on a 14B-parameter model trained on 100B tokens.

## Results

On the 14B-parameter MoE model (trained on 100B SlimPajama tokens), CEA reduces OOD language modeling loss across all five held-out domains, e.g., from 2.761 to 2.585 on Book and from 3.155 to 2.964 on Wiki, while also improving LAMBADA PPL from 24.8 to 19.3 and SCIQ 5-shot accuracy from 72.7% to 84.1%. On the FFN-Wider BERT (H=768) setting, CEA recovers Pile OOD loss from −2.196 (FFN-Wider) to −2.149, surpassing the vanilla BERT (−2.158), and lifts GLUE average from 82.60 to 83.86 (vanilla: 83.89). For FFN-Wider GPT (H=768), CEA improves 0-shot average from 44.60 to 45.52 (vanilla: 45.82) and 1-shot from 44.84 to 45.84 (vanilla: 46.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Vanilla BERT baseline (Devlin et al., 2019), the FFN-Wider BERT w/ CEA nearly matches vanilla performance on all metrics (Pile −2.149 vs −2.158; GLUE 83.86 vs 83.89; SuperGLUE 64.22 vs 64.41), while the unmodified FFN-Wider BERT lags by 0.038 on Pile and 1.29 on GLUE. Against the Vanilla GPT baseline (Radford et al., 2018), the FFN-Wider GPT w/ CEA closes most of the gap (0-shot 45.52 vs 45.82; 1-shot 45.84 vs 46.09), whereas the plain FFN-Wider GPT trails by 1.22 (0-shot) and 1.25 (1-shot). Against the GPT-3 1.3B backbone (Brown et al., 2020), the MoE 14B w/ CEA outperforms on 14 of 17 few-shot tasks, with the largest gains on SCIQ (+6.0), LAMBADA PPL (−4.4), and HellaSwag (+3.8). Against the Vanilla MoE 14B (Switch Transformer, Fedus et al., 2022), CEA wins on every OOD domain and 15 of 17 few-shot tasks, with the most dramatic improvement on SCIQ (84.1 vs 72.7, +11.4 points) and LAMBADA PPL (19.3 vs 24.8, −5.5), confirming that the base-capability decline observed in MoE models is directly attributable to the reduced combination-function contribution ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant scaling-laws literature (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.) that attributes base capabilities primarily to parameter count, data volume, and compute, arguing instead that architectural inductive bias is an equally decisive but underexplored factor. It builds directly on the BERT and GPT architectures as baselines and extends its proposed Combination Enhanced Architecture (CEA) to the Switch-Transformer-style MoE design, connecting to Tay et al.'s (2023) observation that architecture variants differ in converting pre-training loss into downstream capability without providing a mechanistic explanation. The key conceptual departure is the reframing of MHA as a 'combination function' and FFN as a 'transformation function,' with the actual contribution ratio of the former identified as the critical architectural variable governing base capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CEA provides a practical, drop-in architectural fix for MoE models that suffer base-capability decline relative to dense models of equivalent compute, demonstrated at 14B parameters with 100B tokens of training. The 'combination function contribution ratio' framework offers a diagnostic lens for future architecture design: any modification that shifts capacity away from context-sensitive combination (e.g., wider FFNs, certain MoE routing schemes) may silently degrade generalization even while improving in-distribution loss. Key limitations include testing only language-modeling pre-training objectives, a single MoE scale point, and relatively small FFN-Wider experiments (≤506M params), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to other objectives (e.g., contrastive, DPO) and to architectures where the MHA/FFN dichotomy is less clean (e.g., linear attention, state-space models).
