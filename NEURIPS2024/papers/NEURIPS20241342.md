## Problem

Detection-based character localization, once a standard OCR paradigm, has been largely abandoned for handwritten text recognition in favor of autoregressive or CTC-based sequence models, primarily because handwritten characters are often poorly separated and character-level bounding box annotations are expensive to obtain. This paper asks whether a modern transformer-based detector can be made general enough to handle diverse scripts (Latin, Chinese, ciphers) using only line-level supervision on real data, without requiring script-specific architectures or character-level ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241342/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped text-line image as input and outputs a set of character detections, each consisting of a 2D bounding box and an independent probability vector over the alphabet (including a 'no object' class). Detections are ordered by the minimum x-coordinate of their boxes to form the transcription sequence. Unlike autoregressive decoders that emit one token at a time, all characters are predicted in parallel, and the independent per-class probabilities must be converted to a joint distribution (via a thresholding and renormalization step) before CTC-based decoding or N-gram refinement can be applied.

## Method

DTLR builds on the DINO-DETR architecture (CNN backbone + 6-layer deformable-attention transformer encoder + 6-layer transformer decoder with 900 character queries) to simultaneously detect all characters in a line. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) fully supervised pre-training on 100k synthetic text lines with character-level bounding boxes, where random erasing of vertical and horizontal blocks encourages the model to learn an implicit language prior and prevents over-reliance on discriminative local features; (2) fine-tuning on real data using only line-level transcriptions, where an adapted CTC loss (with the blank-merging behavior removed, since duplicates are handled by non-max suppression) provides sequence-level supervision without requiring bounding box ground truth. When the target alphabet differs from the pre-training alphabet (e.g., ciphers), the classification head is re-initialized and trained in two sub-steps—first freezing the backbone, then unfreezing for end-to-end fine-tuning—preserving the learned localization capability.

## Results

On standard Latin HTR benchmarks, DTLR achieves 5.46% CER on IAM, 5.35% on READ, and 2.53% on RIMES (with N-gram), competitive but below the best autoregressive methods (DTrOCR: 2.38% on IAM). For Chinese HTR on CASIA v2, DTLR sets a new state of the art with 96.83% Accurate Rate and 97.34% Correct Rate, surpassing Yu et al. (96.77%/96.77%). On cipher recognition, DTLR achieves 2.2% SER and 84.3% Word Accuracy on Copiale, and 8.5% SER and 59.8% WA on Borg, both outperforming prior best methods. Inference speed is 67 ms per line on an A6000 GPU, faster than TrOCR (284 ms) and FasterDAN (140 ms) at batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Latin HTR, DTLR trails the strongest autoregressive baselines: DTrOCR (2.38% CER) and TrOCR (2.89%) lead on IAM, while Diaz et al. with a large-scale LM (1.99% on RIMES) and DAN (4.10% on READ) outperform DTLR on those datasets. However, DTLR surpasses PyLaia (8.44%→5.46% on IAM), VAN (3.04%→2.53% on RIMES), and Michael et al. (3.04%→2.53% on RIMES). For Chinese HTR on CASIA v2, DTLR (96.83% AR / 97.34% CR) edges out the prior SOTA of Yu et al. (96.77%/96.77%) and substantially outperforms Peng et al. (90.52%/89.61%) and Wang et al. (92.18%/90.77%). On ciphers, DTLR improves over all Souibgui et al. baselines: on Copiale it reduces SER from 3.3% (LSTM-VGG) to 2.2% and raises WA from 82.8% to 84.3%; on Borg it lowers SER from 9.4% to 8.5% and matches the best WA (59.8% vs. 59.4% for LSTM-VGG). DTLR also ranked first in the ICDAR 2024 cipher competition on Borg and Ramanacoil ciphers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTLR revives the early-OCR paradigm of explicit character localization and classification, which was largely abandoned for handwritten text recognition in favor of implicit segmentation (CTC, HMM) and, more recently, autoregressive transformer decoders (TrOCR, DTrOCR, DAN). Architecturally, it builds directly on DINO-DETR's transformer-based detection framework with deformable attention, repurposing object detection machinery for character-level detection in text lines. The synthetic data generation pipeline is adapted from Monnier and Aubry's docExtractor, and the fine-tuning stage introduces an adapted CTC loss that removes the blank-merging behavior (unnecessary when characters are independently detected) while the N-gram post-processing follows Tarride and Kermorvant. Conceptually, the key departure from prior HTR work is treating a full line as a parallel detection problem rather than a sequential generation problem, enabling all characters to be processed simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single general detection-based architecture can handle Latin, Chinese, and cipher scripts without task-specific modifications, achieving state-of-the-art on CASIA v2 (Chinese) and both Borg and Copiale ciphers. This challenges the field's assumption that autoregressive decoding with large-scale language pre-training is necessary for competitive HTR, and offers interpretability (explicit character positions) and parallel inference (67 ms vs. 284 ms for TrOCR). However, on standard Latin HTR benchmarks (IAM, READ), DTLR remains notably behind the best autoregressive methods (5.46% vs. 2.38% CER on IAM), suggesting the detection paradigm has not yet closed the gap where large language priors dominate. Open questions include whether the approach can scale to full-page recognition, whether bounding-box degeneration during long fine-tuning can be mitigated, and whether the generality advantage persists as datasets grow larger.
