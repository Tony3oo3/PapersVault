## Problem

Existing approximation algorithms for the Column Subset Selection (CSS) problem with exactly k columns either have running times that are quadratic in n or d, or achieve large approximation ratios such as (k+1)! in linear time. The local search heuristic proposed by Ordozgoiti et al. [23] improves solution quality but lacks theoretical guarantees on convergence, and its O(nd²k²) per-iteration cost makes it impractical for large-scale data. This paper addresses the gap of achieving both linear running time in n and d and a provable O(k) approximation ratio using a theoretically grounded local search strategy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an n×d real matrix A (normalized so ||A||_F² = 1/4) and a positive integer k ≪ min{n,d}, and returns a submatrix S consisting of exactly k columns of A along with their index set I. Internally, the algorithm operates on a perturbed full-rank matrix A′ = A + D where D is a diagonal matrix with entries scaled by ||A − SS†A||_F / (52·min{n,d}·(k+1)!)^{1/2}, ensuring the full-rank property needed for the theoretical analysis. The local search runs for T = O(k² log k) iterations, and the final column indices are mapped back to the original matrix A.

## Method

The LSCSS algorithm combines two key techniques to achieve linear-time local search with theoretical guarantees. First, a two-step mixed sampling method replaces exhaustive O(dk) swap-pair enumeration: it samples 10k candidate column indices with probability proportional to their residual error ||E_:i||_2²/||E||_F², then uniformly selects one as the swap-in column, reducing per-iteration cost from O(nd²k²) to O(ndk²). Second, a matched swap-pair construction method partitions the current solution's columns into set H (each capturing exactly one optimal column via a mapping function φ) and set L (capturing none), and proves that with constant probability ≥ 1/1375, the sampled column reduces the residual error by a multiplicative factor of (1 − 1/(100k)) per iteration. After O(k² log k) iterations, this yields a 26(k+1)-approximation on A′, which translates to a 53(k+1)-approximation on the original A, all in O(ndk⁴ log k) total running time.

## Results

On 8 large-scale datasets (ranging from 180 to 8,000,000 rows), LSCSS achieves the best error ratio on nearly all dataset-k combinations, e.g., 1.1348 on CMHS (k=5) vs. 1.2494 (ILS) and 56.96 (TwoStage), and 1.1451 on Mnist8m (k=10) vs. 1.2357 (ILS) and 3.0365 (TwoStage). In terms of speed, LSCSS is at least 10× faster than all competing algorithms across all large datasets, completing CMHS (k=5) in 2.82 s versus 18.43 s (ILS), 493.49 s (Greedy), and 354.91 s (TwoStage). On the largest dataset Mnist8m (8M rows, k=100), LSCSS finishes in ~4.7 hours while TwoStage, Greedy, and ILS all exceed the 48-hour limit. The theoretical guarantee is a 53(k+1)-approximation with O(ndk⁴ log k) running time, linear in both n and d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TwoStage (leverage score sampling + RRQR, [5]), LSCSS dominates on both metrics: error ratios are 1–3 orders of magnitude lower (e.g., 1.13 vs. 56.96 on CMHS k=5) and running times are 10–100× faster. Against Greedy ([1]), LSCSS achieves lower error ratios on most datasets (e.g., 1.1348 vs. 1.7446 on CMHS k=5; 1.1451 vs. 1.9146 on Mnist8m k=10) and is at least 15× faster on large datasets. Against ILS (heuristic local search, [23]), LSCSS matches or slightly improves error ratios (e.g., 1.5042 vs. 1.5681 on CMHS k=100) while being 10–40× faster (e.g., 2.82 s vs. 18.43 s on CMHS k=5; 16,910 s vs. >48 h on Mnist8m k=100). Against VolumeSampling ([13]), tested only on 14 small datasets due to O(n³) complexity, LSCSS is at least 2× faster and achieves lower error ratios across all settings. Against QR with Column Pivoting ([24]), LSCSS achieves lower error ratios on all five tested datasets and runs faster, confirming that the local search refinement provides meaningful gains over a single QR-based selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the heuristic local search framework for Column Subset Selection (CSS) from Ordozgoiti et al. [23] by providing the first theoretical guarantees on convergence, and builds on the adaptive sampling initialization of Deshpande and Vempala [15] which yields a (k+1)!-approximation. The key departure is the two-step mixed sampling method that reduces swap-pair enumeration from O(dk) to k per iteration, enabling linear time in both n and d, combined with a matched swap-pair construction that bounds the residual error improvement in each local search step. Prior (k+1)-approximation algorithms (volume sampling, fast volume sampling) require quadratic time in n or d, while the only prior linear-time algorithm [15] has a (k+1)! approximation ratio; this paper occupies the middle ground with a 53(k+1) approximation in O(ndk⁴ log k) time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first local search algorithm for CSS with provable approximation guarantees, bridging the gap between heuristic local search (no guarantees) and sampling-based methods (quadratic time). The 10× speedup over state-of-the-art on large-scale datasets (up to 8M rows) makes CSS practically viable for very large feature selection problems where prior methods hit memory or time limits. The 53(k+1) approximation ratio is notably worse than the (k+1) lower bound achievable by volume sampling, so the trade-off is linear time at the cost of a constant-factor approximation gap. The authors identify multi-swap local search as a natural future direction that could potentially close this gap while preserving linear time.
