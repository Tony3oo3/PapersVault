## Problem

Existing multi-person pose estimation methods predict poses for all detected individuals and require hand-engineered selection strategies (e.g., IoU or distance-based matching) to identify a target person at deployment, which can yield false negatives or suboptimal results. Moreover, these methods do not jointly estimate pose and mask, and they lack any mechanism for user-driven, prompt-conditioned prediction. This paper introduces Referring Human Pose and Mask Estimation (R-HPM), a new task that requires a single model to simultaneously predict identity-aware keypoints and a segmentation mask for a specified individual using natural text or positional prompts.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (resized to a maximum of 640 px on the longer side) paired with one of three prompt types: a free-form text description (encoded by RoBERTa into word-level and sentence-level features), a scribble (12 points sampled from a cubic Bézier curve within the target mask), or a single point. For positional prompts, embeddings are retrieved directly from visual features at stride 16. The model outputs N=17 keypoint positions, a binary segmentation mask at full resolution, a bounding box, and a confidence score for the referred person, produced by the highest-scoring query group out of 20 without any post-processing.

## Method

UniPHD is a fully end-to-end promptable transformer comprising a Swin Transformer visual backbone, a multimodal encoder (modality-specific cross-attention followed by a deformable transformer encoder), a Pose-centric Hierarchical Decoder (PHD), and four lightweight prediction heads. The key architectural contribution is the PHD, which first aggregates local details via deformable attention for each query independently, then models global dependencies by treating each query group (one instance query + k keypoint queries) as a bipartite graph with a learnable soft adjacent matrix that encodes keypoint-to-keypoint, keypoint-to-instance, and instance-to-keypoint relations. Prompt-conditioned query initialization enriches queries with dynamic spatial priors from the multimodal features, and at inference the highest-scoring query group directly yields the target's pose and mask. The framework is modular: its promptable paradigm can wrap the decoders of existing methods (GroupPose, ED-Pose) to convert them into R-HPM models, and training uses Hungarian matching with a composite loss of L1, GIoU, focal, OKS, Dice, and cross-entropy terms.

## Results

On RefHuman val, UniPHD with scribble prompts achieves 74.7 pose AP, 90.4 PCKh@0.5, 85.7 oIoU, and 70.0 mask AP, outperforming the nearest competitor Uni-GroupPose (73.0 AP) by 1.7 AP. On MS COCO val2017, UniPHD with scribble prompts reaches 73.9 pose AP (93.9 AP50, 81.9 AP75), surpassing ED-Pose (68.9 AP) and GroupPose (68.5 AP) by over 5 AP. For text-based segmentation, UniPHD achieves 76.3 oIoU, exceeding SgMg and ReLA (both 75.9 oIoU). The model runs at ~35 FPS on an RTX 3090 with 184M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RefHuman val, UniPHD with scribble prompts (74.7 AP) outperforms both Uni-GroupPose (73.0 AP) and Uni-ED-Pose (72.9 AP) by 1.7 and 1.8 AP respectively, and also exceeds the non-promptable GroupPose† with BBox (73.4 AP) and ED-Pose† (72.8 AP) despite using no ground-truth box-based result selection. On MS COCO val2017, UniPHD with scribble (73.9 AP) surpasses PETR (66.4 AP) by 7.5 AP, ED-Pose (68.9 AP) by 5.0 AP, and GroupPose (68.5 AP) by 5.4 AP, all with a smaller Swin-T backbone. For text-based segmentation on RefHuman, UniPHD (76.3 oIoU) beats LAVT (74.5), CGFormer (75.3), ReLA (75.9), and SgMg (75.9) by 0.4–1.8 oIoU. The paper also shows that integrating ED-Pose or GroupPose decoders into the promptable paradigm (Uni-ED-Pose, Uni-GroupPose) already rivals the original models trained with 3× more data and hand-engineered selection strategies, validating the promptable framework itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of end-to-end transformer-based pose estimation (PETR, ED-Pose, GroupPose) and referring image segmentation, but departs from both in a key way: prior pose estimation methods predict all people and require hand-crafted post-processing to select a target, while prior RIS methods only produce masks without keypoints. UniPHD unifies these by treating prompts (text, scribble, or point) as instance queries that coordinate with keypoint queries in a pose-centric hierarchical decoder, combining deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) for local detail with a novel graph attention over a bipartite keypoint-instance graph for global dependency modeling. The conditional segmentation head follows the dynamic-filter paradigm from SgMg, and the overall training protocol (bipartite matching, Hungarian assignment) is inherited from the DETR family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of R-HPM as a unified promptable task eliminates the fragile post-processing selection step that plagues current multi-person pose estimation in deployment, and the RefHuman dataset (50K+ instances with text, scribble, and point prompts) provides a much-needed benchmark for identity-aware human understanding. A key open question is the persistent gap between text and positional prompts (text lags by ~8 AP), which the authors attribute to vision-language misidentification in crowded scenes; closing this gap likely requires stronger cross-modal alignment. The task has direct practical relevance for assistive robotics and sports analysis where a user must specify *which* person to track, but scalability to video and 3D settings remains unexplored.
