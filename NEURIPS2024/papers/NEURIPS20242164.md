## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models fall into two disconnected camps: training-required approaches like TPT that perform entropy minimization over augmented views (computationally expensive due to gradient descent), and training-free approaches like TDA that use cache-based feature retrieval but ignore the fine-grained information within the test sample itself. The paper asks how these two paradigms are theoretically connected and whether combining them can yield a method that is both efficient and effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242164/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test image (processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 or RN-50 encoder) and the corresponding CLIP text embedding clusters as input. It generates 64 augmented views via random cropping followed by random horizontal flipping, filters them by an entropy threshold (percentile p=0.1) to select boosting samples, and combines them with up to k_t historical samples from the test stream in a joint key-value cache with per-class shot capacity. The output is a class label produced by a non-parametric cache classifier that computes weighted similarity between the test feature and cached features, requiring no gradient updates.

## Method

BoostAdapter extends the Tip-Adapter/TDA cache classifier by augmenting the memory bank with instance-aware boosting samples drawn from regional bootstrapping of the current test sample, alongside instance-agnostic historical samples filtered from the test data stream. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that cross-entropy optimization over well-clustered boosting samples converges to the same classifier as feature retrieval from a cache of those samples, establishing an equivalence between entropy-based and cache-based adaptation. The final prediction is p_boost(x) = A(g(x) G̃_cache^T) Ỹ, where G̃_cache concatenates features from both historical and boosting samples and A is a scaling function. The paper derives excess error bounds (Propositions 2–3) showing that incorporating boosting samples strictly reduces the empirical risk because the boosting distribution is closer to the test sample than the target distribution (c_b > c_t). In practice, the method requires no gradient descent, runs at ~11 fps on a single GPU, and outperforms TDA by 1.68% on OOD and 1.15% on Cross-Domain benchmarks.

## Results

On the OOD benchmark (ViT-B/16), BoostAdapter achieves 65.57% average top-1 accuracy across four ImageNet variants, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline TDA (63.89%) by 1.68% and the best training-required method PromptAlign (63.55%) by 2.02%. On the Cross-Domain benchmark (10 datasets), BoostAdapter reaches 68.68% average accuracy, surpassing TDA (67.53%) by 1.15% and PromptAlign (66.92%) by 1.76%. The method also achieves state-of-the-art results on 7 out of 10 cross-domain tasks, with the largest gain on fine-grained Aircraft (27.45% vs. 23.91% for TDA). Inference speed is 11.23 fps with 1.2 GB memory, comparable to TDA (11.89 fps, 1.2 GB) and far faster than TPT (0.29 fps, 4.5 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoostAdapter consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on both benchmarks. Against the closest training-free competitor TDA, BoostAdapter gains +1.68% on OOD average and +1.15% on Cross-Domain average, with the largest per-dataset margin on ImageNet-A (+4.42%) and Aircraft (+3.54%), indicating that self-bootstrapping is most beneficial when historical samples lack fine-grained discriminative information. Against training-required methods, BoostAdapter surpasses PromptAlign (the strongest gradient-based baseline) by +2.02% on OOD and +1.76% on Cross-Domain while requiring no gradient descent, running at 11.23 fps versus TPT's 0.29 fps. Against prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) combined with TPT, BoostAdapter leads by 2.73% on OOD average. On the RN-50 backbone, the advantage over TDA narrows to +1.57% (OOD) and +0.49% (Cross-Domain), confirming consistent but backbone-dependent gains. BoostAdapter also outperforms TDA across all 15 corruption types on ImageNet-C at severity level 5, with an average improvement of +0.59%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate families of test-time adaptation (TTA) for vision-language models: training-required methods (TPT, DiffTPT, PromptAlign) that minimize entropy via gradient descent over augmented views, and training-free methods (TDA, Tip-Adapter) that perform non-parametric feature retrieval from a cache of historical samples. The key conceptual move is Proposition 1, which shows that cross-entropy optimization over well-clustered boosting samples converges to the same classifier as a cache-based retrieval over those same samples, thereby unifying the two paradigms. BoostAdapter then exploits this equivalence by populating a single cache with both instance-agnostic historical samples (TDA-style) and instance-aware boosting samples generated via regional bootstrapping (TPT-style), maintaining the efficiency of training-free inference while capturing the self-information mining that training-required methods achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical error bounds (Propositions 2–3) provide a principled justification for why combining historical and boosting samples reduces excess risk, with the key insight that boosting samples are closer to the test sample (c_b > c_t) and thus contribute a tighter local approximation. Practically, BoostAdapter achieves consistent gains over TDA (~1.7% on OOD, ~1.15% on Cross-Domain) at comparable inference speed (11.23 vs 11.89 fps), making it a drop-in upgrade for training-free TTA pipelines. The unification perspective also opens a path for grafting other training-required techniques (TSD, DEYO) onto training-free adapters without gradient descent. A remaining limitation is the fixed 64-view augmentation overhead, and the authors note that more efficient augmentation strategies for generating boosting samples would be a natural next step.
