## Problem

Previous analyses of kernel ridge regression learning curves relied on strong and often incompatible assumptions (Gaussian Design, Embedding Condition, MaxDoF), leaving three fundamental questions open: when does the Gaussian Equivalence Property (GEP) actually hold, under what conditions is generalization fully determined by the kernel eigen-spectrum alone, and whether a unifying theory exists under minimal assumptions. This paper shows that GEP is valid only under strong ridge regularization and breaks down under weak ridge, where dependent and independent features yield qualitatively different variance behavior (tempered vs. catastrophic overfitting).

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a kernel with either polynomial (λ_k = Θ(k^{-1-a})) or exponential (λ_k = Θ(e^{-ak})) eigen-decay, target coefficients with decay rate r, a ridge parameter λ = Θ(n^{-b}) or Θ(e^{-bn}), and feature vectors satisfying either Assumption (GF) for generic/dependent features or Assumption (IF) for independent sub-Gaussian features. The output is a non-asymptotic test error bound decomposed into bias B and variance V, with explicit asymptotic rates in the overparameterized (p > n) and underparameterized (p ≤ n) regimes, expressed as functions of n, a, r, b, and noise level σ². The bounds are tight (Θ) for independent features in most settings and for generic features in specific source-coefficient ranges (1 ≤ s ≤ 2).

## Method

The proof strategy proceeds in four steps: (1) establishing concentration results on the whitened feature matrix Z = XΣ^{-1/2} under both Assumption (GF) and (IF); (2) bounding the condition number of truncated kernel matrices A_k = X_{>k}X_{>k}^T + nλI_n; (3) plugging these into the 'Master inequalities' from Bartlett et al. [6] and Tsigler & Bartlett [46], which express bias and variance in terms of concentration coefficients (ρ, ζ, ξ) and effective ranks (r_k, R_k); and (4) deriving asymptotic learning rates by specializing the non-asymptotic bounds to specific eigen-decay and ridge regimes. The key insight is that under strong ridge, the 'probably constant' part of the Master inequalities is bounded identically for both independent and generic features, yielding GEP; under weak ridge, the variance term diverges for kernels like the NTK while remaining bounded for the Laplacian kernel, revealing that GEP fails. A novel improved bias bound under weak ridge with polynomial decay (Theorem 3.1) is obtained by combining techniques from Li et al. [31] with the generic-feature framework of Barzilai & Shamir [7].

## Results

In the over-parameterized regime (p > n), the paper establishes that under strong ridge, both bias and variance achieve identical rates for independent (IF) and generic (GF) features: bias decays as Θ(n^{-b s̃}) and variance as Θ(σ² n^{-1+b/(a+1)}) for polynomial eigen-decay. Under weak ridge, the bias bound is improved to O(n^{-(1+a)s̃}) for s > 1 (a novel result), while the variance exhibits a qualitative split: Θ(σ²) for independent features versus Õ(σ² n^{2a}) for generic features, revealing that the Gaussian Equivalence Property (GEP) fails under weak ridge. For exponential eigen-decay, the bias decays exponentially as Θ(e^{-b s̃ n}) under strong ridge, but catastrophic overfitting (diverging variance) occurs under weak ridge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Barzilai & Shamir [7], the paper's bias bound under weak ridge + polynomial decay + generic features is strictly tighter: Figure 1's phase diagram shows the new bound (Propositions D.5+D.6+E.1) decays faster across a wider range of (a, r) parameters, particularly in the s ≤ 1 regime where [7] gives a loose bound regardless of s. Against Cui et al. [16], the paper matches the upper bound rate under a weaker assumption (independent sub-Gaussian features IF) and additionally proves a matching lower bound, upgrading the result from O(·) to Θ(·) and validating the Gaussian Equivalence Property beyond the Gaussian Design assumption. Relative to Long et al. [33], the paper recovers their tight bias bounds for both polynomial and exponential eigen-decay under the weaker Assumption (GF) instead of the Maximal Degree-of-Freedom (MaxDoF) assumption, broadening the class of applicable kernels. Compared to Cheng et al. [14], the paper extends the tempered overfitting characterization (variance bounded by constants) from the independent-features setting to the broader generic-features setting, while also revealing that under GF the variance can diverge (as with the neural tangent kernel), a phenomenon not captured by [14].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads in KRR learning curve analysis: the Gaussian Design (GD) / replica method literature (Bordelon et al., Cui et al., Loureiro et al., Simon et al.) and the realistic-kernel literature (Li et al., Barzilai & Shamir, Long et al., Misiakiewicz & Saeed). It builds on the Master inequalities of Bartlett et al. [6] and Tsigler & Bartlett [46] as the core analytical engine, and on the concentration/conditioning framework of Barzilai & Shamir [7], but replaces their Assumption (GF) with a more minimal formulation and extends the analysis to both polynomial and exponential eigen-decay under both strong and weak ridge. The key conceptual departure is showing that the Gaussian Equivalence Property (GEP), which had been informally invoked to justify GD-based analyses, holds only under strong ridge and provably fails under weak ridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conditional GEP result explains why a large body of replica-method work under unrealistic Gaussian assumptions produced correct learning curves for realistic kernels, while simultaneously delineating the regime (weak ridge / interpolation) where those analyses break down — a distinction that has practical implications for understanding overfitting in NTK and other deep kernel limits. The unifying framework under minimal assumptions (no explicit input space, no continuity requirement) broadens the applicability of KRR generalization theory. Open questions include: why the Laplacian kernel exhibits tempered overfitting (V=Θ(1)) while the NTK exhibits catastrophic overfitting (V→∞) under the same weak-ridge regime; whether the upper bounds in certain regions of the phase diagram (Figure 1) are sharp; and how to further refine Assumption (GF) to capture these qualitative differences.
