## Problem

Existing fairness-aware training methods, particularly Lagrangian regularization, combine accuracy and fairness into a single weighted objective, which can obscure the complex interdependencies between the two goals and lead to suboptimal trade-offs. The paper asks whether a hierarchical (bilevel) formulation of the fairness-accuracy problem can yield provably Pareto-optimal solutions while maintaining or improving accuracy relative to the standard Lagrangian approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243354/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular features, a binary (or multi-valued) sensitive attribute, and a target label per sample, with no data preprocessing or augmentation applied before training. The neural network is parameterized by two distinct sets of weights—accuracy parameters θ_a and fairness parameters θ_f—embedded as separate fully-connected layers at a chosen position within the MLP. The output is a standard class prediction, and the training procedure produces a Pareto-optimal point on the accuracy–demographic-parity frontier, with the trade-off controlled by the fairness learning rate η_f.

## Method

FairBiNN formulates the fairness-accuracy trade-off as a Stackelberg (bilevel) optimization problem: the upper-level (leader) minimizes the primary accuracy loss over θ_a, while the lower-level (follower) minimizes a fairness loss (e.g., demographic parity) over θ_f conditioned on θ_a. Under assumptions of Lipschitz continuity of network layers, strict convexity of the primary loss near its optimum, and sufficiently small follower step sizes, the paper proves (Theorem 3.8) that the resulting Stackelberg equilibrium is Pareto optimal for the bi-criteria problem. Theorem 3.9 further shows that, when the primary parameters are overparameterized and step sizes are matched, the bilevel solution's primary loss is upper-bounded by the Lagrangian solution's loss. Practically, the network is trained by alternating gradient updates: θ_a is updated on the accuracy loss with θ_f held fixed, then θ_f is updated on the fairness loss with θ_a held fixed, using separate optimizers and learning rates for each parameter group.

## Results

On the UCI Adult dataset, FairBiNN significantly outperforms all seven baseline methods in the accuracy–demographic parity (DP) trade-off, occupying the lower-right region of the Pareto front (high accuracy, low DP). On Heritage Health, FairBiNN closely competes with the best state-of-the-art methods. In the direct bilevel-vs.-Lagrangian comparison, FairBiNN achieves a lower BCE loss of approximately 0.23 by epoch 200 on Adult versus ~0.26 for the Lagrangian approach, while incurring negligible additional runtime (0.62 s/epoch vs. 0.60 s on Adult; 1.03 s vs. 1.05 s on Health).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairBiNN is compared against seven in-process fairness baselines: CVIB [47], MIFR [61], FCRL [25], MaxEnt-ARL [56], Adversarial Forgetting [30], FCR [4], and RFR [31]. On UCI Adult, FairBiNN dominates all seven methods, achieving higher accuracy at every level of demographic parity (the red diamond markers sit strictly in the lower-right of the scatter plot). On Heritage Health, FairBiNN is competitive with the top-performing baselines but does not uniformly dominate, with some methods (notably FCRL and RFR) achieving comparable or slightly better DP at similar accuracy levels. Against the Lagrangian regularization approach specifically, FairBiNN consistently achieves higher accuracy for any given Statistical Parity Difference (SPD) on both datasets, with the gap being more pronounced on Heritage Health where the Lagrangian curve degrades rapidly as fairness constraints tighten. Computational cost is essentially identical between FairBiNN and the Lagrangian method (Table 1), confirming that the bilevel formulation does not introduce meaningful overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Lagrangian/regularization paradigm for fairness in neural networks (Zafar et al., Agarwal et al., Cotter et al.) by recasting the accuracy-fairness trade-off as a bilevel Stackelberg game, drawing on Migdalas (1995) for the Stackelberg-to-Pareto-optimality connection and Dempe (2002) for the bilevel programming framework. Rather than combining both objectives into a single weighted loss, FairBiNN separates them into a leader (accuracy) and follower (fairness) hierarchy with dedicated parameter sets, enabling independent optimization of each objective. This positions the work as a structural alternative to in-process fairness methods that modify the training objective, while remaining compatible with standard neural network architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that the bilevel solution is Pareto optimal and upper-bounds the Lagrangian loss (Theorem 3.9) provides a principled justification for the hierarchical approach that regularization-based methods lack. However, the practical applicability is currently constrained: the Lipschitz continuity requirement excludes softmax (limiting to binary classification) and standard attention mechanisms, and the theory offers no absolute fairness guarantees for the final model. Open questions include extending the framework to multiclass settings, multiple simultaneous fairness criteria, and interaction with data augmentation or overparameterized architectures beyond MLPs.
