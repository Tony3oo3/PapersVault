## Problem

The Procrustes-Wasserstein problem of aligning two point clouds up to an unknown orthogonal transformation and relabeling lacked information-theoretic guarantees in the high-dimensional regime (d ≫ log n), and existing low-dimensional results relied on the overlap metric, which imposes far stricter noise conditions (σ = o(n^{-1/d})) than the more natural L² transport cost. Additionally, practical algorithms such as Grave et al.'s Wasserstein Procrustes had no theoretical guarantees, particularly regarding their mini-batch steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds X, Y ∈ R^{d×n} drawn from a Gaussian planted model where Y = Q*XP*ᵀ + σZ with i.i.d. N(0, I_d) entries, and produces the planted permutation π* and orthogonal matrix Q*. Performance is measured by the L² transport cost c²(π̂, π*) for the relabeling and the Frobenius norm ℓ²(Q̂, Q*) for the rotation, rather than the discrete overlap used in prior work. The key distinction is that in low dimensions, c² recovery is feasible at σ = o(d^{-1/2}) (independent of n), whereas overlap recovery requires σ to decay polynomially in n.

## Method

The paper establishes information-theoretic thresholds via ML analysis (high-d) and a conical alignment loss estimator (low-d) that counts points in directional cones to estimate Q* over an ε-net of O(d), then recovers π* via a linear assignment problem. Computationally, the Ping-Pong algorithm initializes P via Frank-Wolfe on the relaxed QAP over the bistochastic polytope, then alternates between an SVD step (Ping: update Q from current P) and a Hungarian/LAP step (Pong: update P from current Q). This differs from Grave et al.'s approach by using exact per-variable minimization rather than projected gradient descent, and by operating on full batches. Sufficient conditions are given for one single Ping-Pong step (K=T=1) to recover the planted signal with overlap error δ, requiring σ ≤ n^{-13/δ} in general and σ polynomially small in n in the high-dimensional regime.

## Results

In the high-dimensional regime (d ≥ 2 log n), the ML estimators achieve overlap ov(π*, π̂) ≥ 1 − max(60σ², c₁d/n, c₂ log n/(d log d)) and Frobenius error ℓ²(Q*, Q̂)/(2d) ≤ c₁d/n + c₂σ² + c₃ max(d log n/n, √(log n/n)), establishing that almost exact recovery is feasible whenever σ → 0. In the low-dimensional regime (d ≪ log n), the paper proves the existence of estimators with c²(π̂, π*)/(2d) ≤ δ₀ and ℓ²(Q̂, Q*)/(2d) ≤ δ₀ under the much weaker condition σ ≤ C₁δ₀²d^{-1/2}, compared to σ ≪ n^{-1/d} required by prior work for overlap-based recovery. Experimentally, the Ping-Pong algorithm (initialized via Frank-Wolfe relaxed QAP) consistently outperforms Grave et al.'s method across varying dimensions, sample sizes, and noise levels in terms of overlap accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Ping-Pong algorithm against Grave et al. [2019] (Wasserstein Procrustes) in Figure 1, where the Ping-Pong method (red curve) consistently achieves higher overlap accuracy than Grave et al.'s projected gradient approach (purple curve) across all tested parameter sweeps (varying d, n, and σ). The relaxed QAP initialization alone (blue curve) serves as a lower bound, and both iterative methods improve upon it, but Ping-Pong's greedy alternating minimization (SVD for Q, LAP for P) yields better final accuracy than Grave et al.'s projected gradient steps. On the informational side, the paper's low-dimensional result (σ ≪ d^{-1/2} suffices for c² and ℓ² recovery) is substantially weaker than the σ ≪ n^{-1/d} threshold from Wang et al. [2022] and Kunisky & Niles-Weed [2022] for overlap-based recovery, because the L² transport cost metric is less stringent than exact matching in small dimensions. In the high-dimensional regime, the paper's σ ≪ 1 condition for the full PW problem (recovering both P* and Q*) is the first result of its kind, complementing Kunisky & Niles-Weed's σ < (1/2−ε)(d/log n)^{1/2} which only applies when Q* is known.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Procrustes-Wasserstein problem formulated by Grave et al. [2019] by establishing the first information-theoretic guarantees in both high- and low-dimensional regimes, and by proving an informal equivalence between PW and geometric graph alignment (Wang et al. [2022]). It deliberately departs from prior work on geometric graph alignment by adopting the L² transport cost as the performance metric rather than overlap, which yields substantially looser recovery conditions in low dimensions. Computationally, the Ping-Pong algorithm modifies Grave et al.'s alternating scheme by replacing projected gradient steps with exact per-variable minimization (SVD for Q, LAP for P), initialized via a Frank-Wolfe convex relaxation of the QAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that in low dimensions, recovering the permutation in the transport-cost sense requires only σ = o(d^{-1/2}) (noise need not vanish with n), whereas overlap-based recovery demands σ = o(n^{-1/d}) — a gap that widens dramatically as d grows. This reframes what is 'achievable' for embedding alignment in small-dimensional latent spaces. Open questions include: (i) whether the Ping-Pong algorithm's one-step guarantee can be extended to full convergence (the contraction argument is explicitly left open), (ii) whether a computational/statistical gap exists in the low-dimensional regime, and (iii) how these results extend beyond the Gaussian planted model to real-world non-isotropic embeddings.
