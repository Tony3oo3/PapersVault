## Problem

Existing pre-training methods for electricity time series (ETS) data rely on small-scale datasets with few instances, and LLM-based approaches lack sufficient domain-specific knowledge for power systems. No prior model simultaneously captures the three defining characteristics of ETS data: hierarchical dependencies across city/district/user levels, temporal dependencies modulated by exogenous variables (weather, temperature), and behavioral discrepancies across instances and time windows. This leaves a significant gap in foundation-model-level ETS modeling for power system applications.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 672-point electricity time series windows sampled at 15-minute intervals, paired with K exogenous variable sequences (e.g., weather type, temperature) over the same window, and a directed hierarchical graph linking city, district, and user-cluster nodes. Pre-training produces a d-dimensional latent representation per window; after fine-tuning, task-specific heads map this to forecast values (horizons of 4–672 steps), imputed values (mask ratios 12.5%–50%), binary anomaly labels, or multi-class labels (gender, age, family structure). The full model contains ~250M parameters and was pre-trained on 987.42 GB of hierarchical ETS data spanning over 3 years.

## Method

PowerPM combines a 26-layer Transformer temporal encoder (model dim 1024, 16 heads) with a 2-layer R-GCN hierarchical encoder. The temporal encoder first patches each window into segments (patch length 48, stride 24), adds learnable embeddings for exogenous variables, and processes the result through standard self-attention. The hierarchical encoder operates on a directed graph where users are sparsified into 12 K-means clusters (using DTW distance) as intermediary nodes between districts and cities, enabling efficient message passing across hierarchy levels. Pre-training uses a novel self-supervised objective that jointly optimizes (a) masked ETS reconstruction with a 50/50 random/causal masking strategy—forcing the model to predict future patches from past context, not just fill random gaps—and (b) dual-view contrastive learning that pulls representations of temporally adjacent windows from the same instance closer while pushing apart windows from different instances or distant time points, thereby encoding both intra-window temporal structure and inter-window behavioral discrepancy.

## Results

On the private ETS dataset, PowerPM (250M params) achieves the best average MSE across all 44 downstream tasks. For load forecasting, PowerPM attains 0.2423 MSE on City Forecasting (vs. 0.3109 for UniTime, 0.3363 for PatchTST) and 0.1907 on Solar Generation Forecasting (vs. 0.2084 for GPT4TS). On grid stability tasks, PowerPM achieves 0.1433 MSE on Electricity Theft Detection and 0.4540 on Clock Anomaly Detection, outperforming all baselines. On public datasets (CAISO, NYISO, ISONE, PJM), PowerPM maintains its lead, e.g., 0.1193 MSE on NYISO State Forecasting vs. 0.1434 (UniTime) and 0.1747 (PatchTST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PowerPM outperforms all 8 baselines on the majority of 44 downstream tasks. Among LLM-enhanced models, GPT4TS is the strongest competitor, achieving near-parity on Solar Generation Forecasting (0.2084 vs. 0.1907) and High-Power Appliance Detection (0.7426 vs. 0.7427), but PowerPM leads by 7–15% MSE on forecasting tasks (e.g., City Forecasting: 0.2423 vs. 0.3192) and by ~25% on Electricity Theft Detection (0.1433 vs. 0.1914). Among pre-trained time series models, PatchTST is the closest baseline on Exclusive User Forecasting (0.4762 vs. 0.4452) and Public User Forecasting (0.3546 vs. 0.3000), while CoST and TS2Vec trail significantly on all tasks. Supervised models (TimesNet, DLinear) are consistently the weakest, with DLinear lagging by 20–40% MSE on forecasting. On public datasets, PowerPM generalizes well: it beats the best baseline (UniTime) by 17% on NYISO State Forecasting (0.1193 vs. 0.1434) and by 18% on ISONE Region Forecasting (0.2481 vs. 0.2918), confirming cross-domain transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PowerPM sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: self-supervised time series pre-training (PatchTST's patching, CoST/TS2Vec's contrastive objectives) and graph-based relational modeling (R-GCN). It departs from the LLM-enhanced time series line (GPT4TS, Time-LLM, UniTime) by arguing that domain-specific pre-training on hierarchical ETS data with exogenous variables is more effective than repurposing general-purpose language models. The key architectural departure from all prior ETS pre-training work is the simultaneous modeling of temporal dependencies (via a 26-layer Transformer) and hierarchical dependencies (via a 2-layer R-GCN over a city-district-user-cluster graph), combined with a novel dual-view contrastive loss that explicitly encodes temporal and instance-level proximity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first foundation model purpose-built for power systems ETS data, providing an off-the-shelf 250M-parameter model that generalizes across 44 downstream tasks (forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection, classification) and transfers to four public grid datasets (CAISO, ISONE, NYISO, PJM). The ablation showing the hierarchical encoder contributes the largest performance drop suggests that macro-micro information flow is underexploited in current ETS modeling. A key limitation is that pre-training uses only load data; the authors acknowledge that system-operation and sensor-detection ETS remain unaddressed, and scaling to those modalities is left as future work. Open questions include whether the hierarchical graph structure generalizes to non-administrative topologies (e.g., feeder-level distribution networks) and whether the 250M scale is sufficient or whether larger models would yield further gains.
