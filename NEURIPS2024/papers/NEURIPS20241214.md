## Problem

Standard deep RL policies parameterized as deterministic networks or Gaussian distributions are restricted to a single behavioral mode, and the greedy RL objective further collapses exploration to one solution. While diffusion models can represent multimodal action distributions, their use in online RL is hindered by intractable policy likelihood approximation and the same greediness that skews learning to a single mode. No prior work explicitly discovers, preserves, and improves multiple behavioral modes from scratch in online continuous control.

## Input / Output

The agent receives high-dimensional continuous state vectors (e.g., 8-DOF ant joint states in AntMaze or 7-DOF Franka arm joint states in manipulation tasks) and optionally a 5-dimensional mode-specific latent embedding that conditions which behavioral mode to execute. The policy outputs a continuous action vector sampled via a reverse diffusion process (5 steps suffice). Unusually, the diffusion model is trained with a denoising/BC objective on target actions generated by gradient ascent on Q-values rather than on ground-truth actions, and the target actions are stored in the replay buffer and iteratively refined across updates.

## Method

DDiffPG introduces a diffusion policy gradient that avoids vanishing gradients by computing ∇_a Q(s,a), generating a target action a^target = a + η∇_a Q(s,a), and training the diffusion model with a standard denoising MSE loss on (s, a^target) pairs, decoupling the RL objective from the diffusion training loop. For mode discovery, novelty-based intrinsic motivation (RND) drives exploration while unsupervised hierarchical clustering with DTW distances over goal-reached trajectories identifies behavioral modes without requiring a predefined count. To prevent mode collapse, each discovered mode is assigned its own Q-function for independent improvement, and a separate exploration Q-function trained solely on intrinsic rewards maintains ongoing exploration. Training batches are constructed by concatenating (s, a^target) pairs sampled from all modes to preserve the multimodal action distribution, and re-clustering every 100 iterations inherits Q-functions and target actions from overlapping prior clusters for learning continuity. Finally, mode-specific latent embeddings condition the diffusion policy during training, enabling explicit mode selection at test time for applications like online replanning in non-stationary environments.

## Results

DDiffPG is the only method that discovers multiple behavioral modes (2–4.3 modes) across all eight tasks while maintaining competitive success rates. On AntMaze-v2, DDiffPG achieves 100% success rate (episode length 66.8) versus 75% for TD3/DIPO and 53% for SAC, by learning both the high-reward and easy-to-reach goals. In the randomized-obstacle replanning test, DDiffPG achieves 100% success rate (episode length 162.3) compared to 50% for TD3/DIPO and 45% for SAC/Diffusion-QL. On robotic manipulation tasks, DDiffPG matches the best success rates (100% on Reach, Peg-in-hole, Drawer-close, Cabinet-open) while discovering 2–3.5 modes, whereas all baselines collapse to a single mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDiffPG decisively outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric of multimodal behavior discovery: it finds 2–4.3 distinct modes while every baseline (TD3, SAC, DIPO, Diffusion-QL, Consistency-AC, RPG) collapses to at most 1 mode. On success rate, DDiffPG ties TD3 and DIPO on AntMaze-v1/v3/v4 (all ~1.0) but clearly wins on AntMaze-v2 (1.0 vs. 0.75 for TD3/DIPO, 0.53 for SAC) by escaping the local minimum of the easier lower-reward goal. In the randomized-obstacle replanning test, DDiffPG's mode-conditional policy achieves 100% success versus 50% for TD3/DIPO and 45% for SAC/Diffusion-QL, a 2× improvement. On episode length (efficiency), TD3 is consistently faster in AntMaze tasks (e.g., 59.2 vs. 75.7 in v1; 83.4 vs. 142.5 in v3), reflecting DDiffPG's deliberate multi-mode exploration overhead. Diffusion-QL and Consistency-AC underperform across the board, with Consistency-AC failing entirely (0% success) on AntMaze-v1, v2, and v4. On robotic manipulation tasks, DDiffPG matches the best success rates (100%) while discovering 2–3.5 modes, with only a modest episode-length penalty (e.g., 23.8 vs. 18.0 for TD3 on Reach).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion policy gradient lineage from DIPO (Yang et al., 2023) by introducing a persistent target-action mechanism that preserves MDP consistency, and departs from it by decoupling exploration from exploitation through mode-specific Q-functions. It builds on the DDPM architecture (Ho et al., 2020) for policy parameterization and RND-based intrinsic motivation (Burda et al., 2018) for exploration, but positions itself against both offline diffusion RL methods (Diffusion-QL, IDQL) and latent-conditioned multimodal skill discovery (RPG, DIAYN-style approaches) by explicitly discovering and preserving behavioral modes via hierarchical trajectory clustering rather than relying on learned latent variables. The key conceptual departure is treating multimodality as an explicit, clusterable property of trajectories rather than an emergent property of a latent-conditioned policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: diffusion policies have shown strong multimodal expressivity in offline and imitation settings, but no prior work demonstrated how to learn and *maintain* multiple behavioral modes from scratch in online RL without mode collapse. The mode-specific Q-function design and multimodal batch construction offer a reusable recipe for any diffusion-based actor-critic method that needs to preserve diversity. Limitations include ~5× computational overhead over TD3/SAC, dependence on task-specific distance metrics for clustering (2D/3D positions rather than full state), and exploration challenges in very large state spaces. Open questions include scaling to long-horizon planning with multimodal policies, offline-to-online fine-tuning that preserves learned modes, and open-ended skill discovery beyond goal-reaching.
