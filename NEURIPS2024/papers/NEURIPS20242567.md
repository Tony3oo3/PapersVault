## Problem

Graph neural networks are the standard way to exploit structural information in recommender systems, but they act as intermediate modules that add significant training and inference overhead, making them hard to scale to millions of nodes and difficult to combine with sequential modeling. The paper asks whether graph structural information can be injected directly into the embedding update rule (i.e., the optimizer) with minimal computational cost, while still guaranteeing convergence—a property that prior graph-regularization frameworks for feature/label smoothing do not provide when applied to gradient variations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user interaction sequences to build a weighted adjacency matrix (entries w_ij count consecutive co-occurrences of items v_i and v_j across all sequences), along with the per-step embedding gradients (or moment estimates) produced by the backbone model's loss. It outputs updated item embeddings at each training step and, at inference, standard top-N recommendation lists. Crucially, SEvo modifies only the optimizer's update step for the embedding matrix; the model's forward pass, architecture, and inference logic remain completely unchanged, so inference time is identical to the unmodified backbone.

## Method

SEvo applies a structure-aware transformation ψ̂ to the embedding variation ΔE at every update: ψ̂(ΔE; β) = (1−β)/(1−β^{L+1}) · Σ_{l=0}^{L} β^l Ã^l ΔE, a rescaled L-layer Neumann series of the normalized adjacency matrix. The transformation is derived by solving a graph-regularization objective that trades off smoothness (related nodes' variations become similar) against direction preservation (the update still descends the loss), with β controlling the trade-off. The paper proves that the commonly used iterative approximation (APPNP-style) is not direction-aware for β ≥ 1/2, whereas the Neumann series form is both structure-aware and direction-aware for all β ∈ [0,1), and that the 1/(1−β^{L+1}) rescaling improves the convergence rate from O(C/((1−β)²T)) to O(C/((1−β)T)). For practical integration, SEvo is plugged into AdamW with a moment-estimate correction: when a node's gradient is zero (not sampled in the mini-batch), the first and second moments are updated using bias-corrected self-referencing formulas rather than simply decaying, preventing the auto-attenuation that would otherwise interfere with SEvo's smoothing of inactive nodes.

## Results

SEvo-enhanced AdamW achieves average relative improvements of 9%–23% across six backbone models (MF-BPR, GRU4Rec, SASRec, BERT4Rec, STOSA) on four standard datasets (Beauty, Toys, Tools, MovieLens-1M), with the best SEvo-enhanced model (STOSA+SEvo) outperforming all GNN-based baselines (LightGCN, SR-GNN, LESSR, MAERec) on most metrics. On large-scale datasets with millions of nodes, gains are substantially larger: SASRec+SEvo improves HR@1 by +92.5% on Electronics and +139.1% on Clothing, with NDCG@10 gains of +53.9% and +89.1% respectively. Training overhead is minimal (e.g., +37s per epoch for SASRec on Beauty) and inference time is unchanged compared to the unmodified backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEvo-enhanced sequence models consistently outperform all four GNN-based baselines (LightGCN, SR-GNN, LESSR, MAERec) on the four standard datasets, with the gap being largest on sparser datasets (Tools: +33.8% HR@1; Beauty: +30.2% HR@1) and smallest on the denser MovieLens-1M (+7.6% HR@1). Among the GNN baselines, MAERec is the strongest on Beauty and Toys, while LESSR dominates on MovieLens-1M; however, SEvo-enhanced BERT4Rec surpasses even LESSR on MovieLens-1M (e.g., NDCG@10: 0.1743 vs. 0.1373). The SEvo-enhanced models also outperform their own unmodified backbones across the board, with improvements ranging from ~2% (MovieLens-1M) to ~30% (Beauty/Tools), confirming that the gains come from SEvo rather than backbone choice. On large-scale datasets (Electronics, Clothing), GNN-based baselines fail entirely due to prohibitive training costs, while SASRec+SEvo achieves 40%–139% improvements over the unmodified backbone with only a few extra seconds per epoch. The moment estimate correction in SEvo-enhanced AdamW provides additional gains for RNN-based backbones (GRU4Rec, STOSA) where sparse gradients are more prevalent, but has minimal effect on BERT4Rec whose scoring layer is fully updated at each step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the graph regularization framework from Zhou et al. (NeurIPS 2003) and the Neumann series approximation, but applies them to embedding *variations* (optimizer updates) rather than to node features or labels as in prior GNN smoothing work (APPNP, C&S). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of using GNNs as intermediate architecture modules (LightGCN, SR-GNN, MAERec) by instead modifying the optimizer itself—SEvo-enhanced AdamW—so that structural information is injected at the update level with no architectural change. The moment estimate correction for sparse gradients extends AdamW in a way that is specific to the interaction between SEvo's smoothing and the auto-attenuation of inactive nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEvo offers a practically attractive alternative to GNN modules: zero inference overhead, minimal training cost, and the ability to scale to millions of nodes where dynamic-graph GNNs (SR-GNN, LESSR) simply fail to train. The 40–139% gains on large-scale datasets, while partly attributable to GNN baselines not completing training, still demonstrate that optimizer-level structural injection can be highly effective. Open questions include extending SEvo to multiplex heterogeneous graphs, dynamic adjacency matrices, and combining it with multiple types of prior knowledge (categories, knowledge distillation) without prohibitive efficiency costs. The training-free nature of SEvo also limits its expressive power compared to a task-specific GNN module.
