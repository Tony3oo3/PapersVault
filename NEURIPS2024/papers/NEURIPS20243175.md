## Problem

The computational complexity of parameter identification in linear structural causal models was largely unresolved: the best-known complete algorithm for generic identification relied on Gröbner basis computation with double-exponential worst-case time, and no hardness results existed for any notion of identifiability. This paper closes both gaps by establishing a PSPACE upper bound for generic identification and proving ∃R-hardness (hence coNP-hardness) for numerical identification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is an acyclic mixed graph G = (V, D, B) with n topologically sorted nodes, a set of directed edges D, and a set of bidirected edges B, together with an n×n symmetric positive-definite covariance matrix Σ. For generic identification, only the graph G is needed and the output is a yes/no decision on whether the parametrization map is injective on a Zariski-dense open set. For numerical identification, Σ must additionally be feasible (in the image of the parametrization map), and the output is whether the fiber over Σ has exactly one element, i.e., whether all structural parameters are uniquely determined by the observed covariances.

## Method

For the PSPACE upper bound on generic identification, the authors formulate the condition dim(R^D) + dim(PD(B)) > dim(S_G) as a first-order formula over the reals with alternating quantifiers, then apply Renegar's quantifier-elimination algorithm, yielding an ∃∀R / ∀∃R membership and hence exponential time. For the hardness of numerical identification, they reduce from the complement of QUAD++ by constructing a bipartite mixed graph whose missing bidirected edges on the top layer encode arbitrary quadratic polynomial equations via the fiber characterization of Drton: each missing edge i↔j contributes one equation of the form σ_{i,j} − Σ σ_{a_ℓ,j}λ_{a_ℓ,i} − Σ σ_{b_k,i}λ_{b_k,j} + Σ σ_{a_ℓ,b_k}λ_{a_ℓ,i}λ_{b_k,j} = 0, and an observation guarantees that σ-values in different missing-edge equations are independent, allowing free 'programming' of the system. The graph is built from gadgets that store variable values, enforce affine-linear constraints, and enforce multiplicative constraints (x_a = x_b · x_c), with the resulting Σ made positive definite via strict diagonal dominance. The upper bound for numerical identification follows by expressing 'all parameter pairs producing Σ are identical' as a ∀R formula, again decidable in PSPACE via Renegar.

## Results

The paper proves that generic identifiability in linear SCMs is decidable in PSPACE (exponential time), improving the previous state-of-the-art double-exponential time bound from Gröbner basis computation. More precisely, generic identifiability is shown to lie in both ∃∀R and ∀∃R, intermediate classes between NP and PSPACE. For numerical identifiability, the paper establishes ∀R-hardness (hence coNP-hardness), providing the first hardness result for any notion of identifiability, while also showing a matching (promise) ∀R upper bound and PSPACE decidability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the Gröbner basis approach [22], which has double-exponential worst-case time complexity for generic identification. The new algorithm based on Renegar's quantifier elimination [33] reduces this to PSPACE (single exponential time), a substantial improvement. For numerical identifiability, no prior complexity results existed; the paper's ∀R-hardness (Theorem 2) is the first hardness result for any identifiability notion, placing the problem at least as hard as coNP. The upper and lower bounds nearly match: numerical identifiability is in (promise) ∀R and is ∀R-hard, giving an almost tight characterization. The paper also positions its results relative to the broader complexity hierarchy NP ⊆ ∃R ⊆ PSPACE and coNP ⊆ ∀R ⊆ PSPACE, showing that identifiability sits at the ∃∀R/∀∃R level for the generic case and at the ∀R level for the numerical case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the algebraic-geometric framework for linear structural equation models established by Drton (2018) and Foygel, Draisma, and Drton (2012), which characterize identifiability via fibers of the parametrization map. It departs from the state-of-the-art Gröbner-basis approach (doubly exponential time) by formulating generic identifiability as a sentence in the first-order theory of the reals and applying Renegar's quantifier-elimination algorithm, yielding a PSPACE (exponential) upper bound. The hardness proof for numerical identifiability draws on the ∃R-completeness of the dimension problem (Koiran, 1999) and the fiber characterization of Drton, but the reduction from QUAD++ to a mixed-graph construction is entirely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ∃R-hardness (hence coNP-hardness) of numerical identifiability is, to the authors' knowledge, the first hardness result for any notion of identifiability in causal inference, fundamentally changing the field's understanding of what can be efficiently computed. The PSPACE upper bound for generic identifiability is a substantial improvement over the prior doubly-exponential Gröbner-basis method, though the paper acknowledges the algorithm is purely theoretical and not yet implemented. Open questions include whether the ∃∀R/∀∃R bounds can be tightened to ∃R or ∀R, and whether practical implementations of the quantifier-elimination approach can handle instances of reasonable size.
