## Problem

Discrete disease progression models (e.g., EBM, ALPACA) infer a latent permutation of events from cross-sectional data, but maximum-likelihood inference over N! permutations becomes computationally intractable beyond a few hundred features, precluding pixel-level modelling of imaging data. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating permutation inference as an optimal transport problem over the Birkhoff polytope, enabling models with orders of magnitude more features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242022/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes cross-sectional feature data (one time-point per individual) from patient and control groups, where features can be pixel-level imaging maps (TBM Jacobian maps for brain, OCT thickness maps for retina) or clinical test scores, and may be incomplete. It produces a soft doubly-stochastic permutation matrix S encoding the full distribution over event orderings, a hard permutation s (the disease event sequence) extracted via the Hungarian algorithm, and per-individual stage likelihoods. The pixel-level output is the key novelty: each of thousands of voxels/pixels is assigned a position in the progression sequence, yielding spatial maps of when pathology appears.

## Method

The vEBM reformulates disease progression as the optimal transport of latent event likelihoods to their optimal positions in a continuous latent permutation, with the permutation matrix S constrained to the Birkhoff polytope. Inference proceeds by optimising a differentiable ELBO where both the variational posterior and prior are Gumbel-Sinkhorn distributions, implementing the reparameterisation trick for discrete permutations; the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm with entropy regularisation solves the doubly-stochastic constraint at each iteration. The classical EBM is recovered as the τ→0 limit of the soft permutation, and the hard event sequence is extracted post-hoc via the Hungarian algorithm. This yields a factor of 1000× speedup over MCMC/EM baselines, enabling pixel-level models (1344 events for AD, 537 for AMD) that were previously intractable, while also providing direct access to posterior uncertainty over event orderings.

## Results

The vEBM achieves a 1000× speedup over the EBM baseline at J=200 features (I=2000), while the ALPACA baseline becomes computationally intractable at that scale. On synthetic data, the vEBM matches or exceeds baseline accuracy (Kendall's τ) across all noise levels and model sizes, with statistical significance at p<0.001 (unpaired t-tests over 10 repeats). On real data, the vEBM infers 1344 pixel-level disease events in AD (ADNI, 816 subjects, 5 min training) and 537 pixel-level events in AMD (Duke University, 384 subjects, 1 min training), both for the first time at pixel resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the original Event-Based Model (EBM) [1], which uses gradient descent plus MCMC sampling, and ALPACA [33], which uses EM with Gibbs sampling over a Mallows model. On speed, the vEBM is a factor of 1000× faster than the EBM at J=200, and ALPACA is entirely intractable at that scale (no data point reported). On accuracy (Kendall's τ), the vEBM outperforms or matches both baselines across all tested noise levels (σ=0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and model sizes (J=10, 100, 200), with the sole exception being I=100, J=1000 at σ=0.1, where the EBM's MCMC sampling is expected to find the global minimum while the vEBM retains some variational approximation error. The vEBM's advantage grows with model size: at J=200 the EBM is still tractable but 1000× slower, while ALPACA cannot run at all. On real-world data (ADNI, Duke University), neither baseline is included due to computational intractability at pixel-level resolution (J=1344 and J=537 respectively), underscoring the vEBM's unique scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the Event-Based Model (EBM) of disease progression [Fonteijn et al., 2012] and its probabilistic cascade variant (ALPACA) [Huang & Alexander, 2012] as an optimal transport problem over the Birkhoff polytope, borrowing the Gumbel-Sinkhorn machinery from Mena et al. (2018) for differentiable permutation inference. The key conceptual departure is replacing the combinatorial search over discrete permutations (via MCMC or EM) with a continuous variational inference over soft permutation matrices, which the authors show recovers the original EBM as a temperature limit. This positions the work at the intersection of computational optimal transport and longitudinal disease modeling, bridging two communities that had previously addressed permutation inference through entirely different tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: by removing the combinatorial bottleneck, the vEBM enables pixel-level (thousands-of-features) disease progression models that were previously intractable, demonstrated for the first time in Alzheimer's disease and age-related macular degeneration. The 1000× speedup and single-CPU tractability make the method accessible to clinical and research labs without GPU infrastructure. Open questions include whether the single-permutation assumption generalises to heterogeneous populations (subtypes), how to incorporate feature-wise spatial covariance (e.g., Markov random fields for neighbouring pixels), and whether the memory bottleneck from dense matrix operations can be resolved with sparse representations to push beyond the current ~1300-event scale.
