## Problem

Two-stage recommender systems with tree-structured retrievers are widely deployed in industry, yet their generalization properties remain theoretically uncharacterized. No prior work provides generalization error bounds for tree-based retrievers using beam search, nor for rankers trained under the distribution shift inherent to the two-stage pipeline. This paper fills that gap by deriving Rademacher-complexity-based upper bounds for both stages.

## Input / Output

The retriever consumes a user representation (a d-dimensional embedding vector or a sequence of T item embeddings) and traverses a B-ary tree of learnable node parameters via beam search of width K, outputting a candidate set of K leaf items. The ranker then scores only those K candidates using a richer model (e.g., target attention over the full interaction history) and returns the single highest-scoring item. A critical structural detail is that the ranker's training distribution is the conditional distribution over items that the retriever successfully surfaces, which differs from the full item distribution used at inference time.

## Method

The paper decomposes two-stage classification error into a retrieval error term P(y ∉ B(x)) and a conditional ranking error term P(h(x)≠y | y∈B(x)), then bounds each via Rademacher complexity. For the retriever, they define a function space G_F parameterized by the score function f(x,v) and the beam-search max_K operator, proving that the Rademacher complexity scales as O(BN/√(B²−1)) for linear scores, O(c_σ^{L−1} B_1^L (B_0+B_x)) for MLPs, and O(c_σ^{L−1} B_1^L (B_w T + B_0)) for target attention, all multiplied by the tree-structure factor. For the ranker, they derive a bound under the shifted distribution D' that includes an explicit distributional-disparity term E|1 − P'(x,y)/P(x,y)|, showing that training the ranker on the retriever-filtered subset (harmonized distribution) eliminates this term at the cost of fewer samples. Experiments on Mind and MovieLens-10M confirm that increasing tree branches improves recall and that distribution harmonization improves top-1 accuracy when retriever recall exceeds ~10%.

## Results

The paper's empirical validation focuses on two theoretical insights. First, increasing the number of tree branches (B) in the TDM-based retriever consistently improves Recall@20 on both Mind and Movie datasets (Figure 1), confirming the theoretical bound's O(B/√(B²−1)) term. Second, training the DIN ranker on a harmonized distribution (only items successfully retrieved by the fixed retriever) yields consistent Precision@1 gains over the standard two-stage setup: on Mind, H-TS achieves 0.6565 vs. 0.6500 at K=40 and 0.5644 vs. 0.5609 at K=120; on Movie, H-TS achieves 0.3555 vs. 0.3516 at K=40 and 0.3488 vs. 0.3453 at K=120.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no external recommender system baselines. The sole empirical comparison is internal: the standard two-stage model (TS), where the ranker is trained on the full logged training data, versus the harmonized two-stage model (H-TS), where the ranker is trained only on the subset of training samples whose target items were successfully retrieved by the fixed TDM retriever. H-TS consistently outperforms TS across all K values on both datasets, with gains of approximately 0.6–0.9 percentage points on Mind and 0.3–0.4 percentage points on Movie. The authors note that this improvement requires the retriever to achieve a recall rate above ~10%, since filtering training data reduces the effective sample size m′ and weakens the generalization guarantee. The branch-number experiment (Figure 1) similarly has no external baseline; it simply demonstrates monotonic improvement in Recall@20 as B increases, validating the theoretical prediction that flatter trees generalize better.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical classification generalization bound of Babbar et al. (JMLR 2016) to the beam search setting used in tree-based recommenders, introducing a max_K operator in the function space to capture the top-K selection at each tree level. While prior work on two-stage recommenders (TDM, JTM, DeFoRec) focused on empirical improvements to tree-structured retrieval, and off-policy learning work [18] addressed ranker training, no prior study provided formal generalization guarantees for either the tree-based retriever or the ranker under distribution shift. The paper also connects to extreme multi-class classification theory [17] and component-interaction analysis of two-stage systems [12], but is the first to unify these threads under a single Rademacher-complexity-based error decomposition for the full two-stage pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error decomposition framework (Proposition 4.1) gives practitioners a clear diagnostic: total two-stage error splits into retrieval miss probability and conditional ranking error, and the Corollary 4.2 threshold quantifies when the two-stage system beats a single ranker. The finding that increasing tree branches improves generalization (via the O(B/√(B²−1)) term) provides a principled tradeoff knob between inference efficiency and accuracy, though the extreme case (B = N) degenerates to full ranking. The distribution harmonization result is practically useful but carries an important caveat: it only helps when the retriever achieves >10% recall, and the empirical gains are modest (e.g., +0.0065 Precision@1 on Mind). Open questions include whether tighter, data-dependent bounds are achievable, whether the analysis extends to multi-tree forests or learned tree structures, and how these bounds behave under the non-i.i.d. conditions common in production recommender logs.
