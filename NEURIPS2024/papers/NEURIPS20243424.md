## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods can render scenes but make it difficult to independently edit individual object parts over time. Approaches like SC-GS require manually designating static and dynamic control points, and editing one part (e.g., a hand) inadvertently deforms connected regions (e.g., the head), limiting reproducibility and fine-grained control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view video frames with corresponding camera poses and time labels, processed in batches of 4 or 8 views per iteration (400×400 resolution with black background on D-NeRF). At inference and editing time, the user manipulates triangle vertices in the Sub-Triangle Soup or a derived mesh, and the system produces rendered novel-view images at any selected time point. Each Core-Gaussian carries approximately 25 Sub-Gaussians, and the full scene is stored as a compact Triangle Soup (disconnected triangle cloud) rather than a dense Gaussian set.

## Method

D-MiSo introduces a two-level Multi-Gaussian hierarchy: large flat Core-Gaussians (parameterized by triangle faces via an invertible GaMeS-style transformation) handle global object motion, while smaller Sub-Gaussians (positioned in the Core-Gaussian's local coordinate system via trainable offset vectors α) handle rendering detail. Two deformation networks operate at different scales: a Deform Network ψ updates Core-Gaussian triangle vertices (translation + rotation) over time for global motion, and a lightweight single-layer Sub-Rot Network φ applies small per-Sub-Gaussian rotation changes for local effects like shadows. Training is two-stage—Stage 1 fits Core-Gaussians with batched views and pruning to establish motion, then Stage 2 attaches and refines Sub-Gaussians for render quality. For editing, three strategies are provided: (1) extracting a connected mesh from Core-Gaussians via Alpha Shape and reassigning Sub-Gaussians to mesh faces, (2) directly transforming groups of Sub-Triangle Soup vertices (enabling independent part edits like raising a thumb), and (3) applying spatial functions (e.g., sinusoidal) to selected regions for fluid deformations. Because Sub-Gaussians inherit their position from the Core-Gaussian's local frame, modifying one Core-Gaussian rigidly transforms all its children, eliminating the need for static/dynamic markers and enabling independent, reproducible edits.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset, D-MiSo achieves an average PSNR of 42.27 across 7 scenes, competitive with the primary editable baseline SC-GS (43.31) and within ~1 dB on most scenes. On NeRF-DS, D-MiSo attains SOTA PSNR on Bell (25.3) and Sieve (26.5) among editable methods. On PanopticSports, D-MiSo achieves SOTA PSNR on 5 of 6 scenes (mean 29.04) and SOTA LPIPS on all 6 scenes (mean 0.14), outperforming both 3DGS and Dyn3DG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SC-GS, the primary editable baseline, D-MiSo is within 1–3.5 dB PSNR on most D-NeRF scenes but wins on Jumpingjacks (42.05 vs. 41.13) and is nearly tied on Standup (47.66 vs. 47.89). On NeRF-DS, D-MiSo surpasses SC-GS on Bell (25.3 vs. 25.1) and Sieve (26.5 vs. 26.0) while remaining competitive elsewhere. On PanopticSports, D-MiSo outperforms both 3DGS (mean PSNR 28.21) and Dyn3DG (28.70) with a mean PSNR of 29.04, and achieves the best LPIPS (0.14) across all six scenes. Compared to non-editable methods like Deform-GS (mean ~41.5 on D-NeRF) and DynMF (~37.5), D-MiSo is competitive in reconstruction quality while uniquely supporting per-object editing, scaling, and rotation without requiring static/dynamic node selection. The key trade-off is that D-MiSo sacrifices ~1 dB PSNR on some D-NeRF scenes (e.g., Hook: 38.13 vs. SC-GS 39.87) in exchange for more flexible, mesh-like editing of individual object parts.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-MiSo builds directly on two prior works: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the rendering backbone, and GaMeS (Waczyńska et al., 2024) for the invertible parameterization of flat Gaussians by triangle faces. The key departure is from SC-GS, the primary editable dynamic-scene baseline, which relies on sparse control nodes and requires manual designation of static vs. dynamic points. D-MiSo replaces this node-based paradigm with a hierarchical Multi-Gaussian structure (Core-Gaussians for global motion, Sub-Gaussians for rendering detail) whose parameters are expressed as a Triangle Soup, enabling mesh-like vertex editing without the coupling artifacts and scaling issues inherent to node-based control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, real-time pipeline for editing dynamic 3D scenes that is more intuitive than node-based alternatives, with applications spanning VR/AR, robotics, and medical imaging. A notable limitation is that regions poorly represented in the training data (e.g., individual fingers) cannot be reliably edited, as the Triangle Soup lacks the fidelity of a well-fitted mesh in those areas. Open questions include how the approach scales to scenes with many independently moving objects, whether the two-stage training (Core then Sub) can be unified, and whether the editing quality can be quantitatively benchmarked beyond qualitative comparisons.
