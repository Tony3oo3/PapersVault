## Problem

While adversarial training and multi-task representation learning are each well-studied, there was no theoretical guarantee that learning a shared representation through adversarial training on diverse source tasks would transfer robustness to a previously unseen target task with limited data. Existing theoretical results on adversarial transfer either required restrictive assumptions (e.g., Lipschitzness of the adversarial loss class, specific linear models) or could not separate the statistical cost of the function class from the cost of the attack model, leaving the dimensional dependence of adversarial robustness unclear in general settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labeled samples from each of t source tasks and m labeled samples from the target task, all drawn from a common input space X ⊆ R^d with labels in Y ⊆ R. The representation class H maps R^d to a lower-dimensional R^k space, and the predictor class F maps R^k to Y. The output is a composite predictor f̂₀ ∘ ĥ that minimizes adversarial risk over an attack function class A (e.g., bounded ℓ∞ perturbations), with the representation ĥ fixed from Stage 1 and only the target predictor f̂₀ adapted in Stage 2. The attack class A is assumed to be totally bounded and to map X into itself (within-domain attacks).

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage adversarial MTRL algorithm: Stage 1 performs ERM over the adversarial loss class jointly over t task-specific predictors and a shared representation, and Stage 2 fixes the learned representation and performs ERM for the target task predictor. The core theoretical contribution is bounding the sample-dependent Rademacher complexity of the adversarial loss class by the worst-case Rademacher complexity of the non-adversarial loss class times a multiplicative attack factor Λ_A, achieved via a novel application of the Rudelson–Vershynin fat-shattering comparison inequality that decouples function class complexity from attack complexity without parametric assumptions. This yields excess adversarial transfer risk rates of Õ(√(dC(F)/n + dC(H)/nt) + √(kC(F)/m)) for Lipschitz losses and optimistic (fast-rate) bounds for smooth nonnegative losses, with the adversarial cost contributing a √d log d factor for source tasks and √k log k for the target task. A key reduction (Theorem 3) shows the multi-task adversarial Rademacher complexity equals a single-task complexity on an augmented dataset with an immutable task index, simplifying prior MTRL analyses that relied on worst-case complexity.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline result is a bound on the excess adversarial transfer risk: for Lipschitz losses, the rate is Õ(√(dC(F)/n + dC(H)/nt) + √(kC(F)/m)), showing that pooling nt source samples to learn a robust representation reduces the target-task cost. For smooth nonnegative losses, the paper provides an optimistic rate that achieves fast O(1/n) decay in both source and target samples when adversarially robust predictors exist in the hypothesis class. The adversarial attack contributes a multiplicative √d log d factor (source tasks) and √k log k factor (target task) over the non-adversarial rate, establishing the dimensional cost of robustness in general settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are entirely theoretical, benchmarking its rates against prior adversarial and MTRL analyses. Compared to Deng et al. [13], who obtain √(k/m) + √(k²d/nt) for linear classifiers with orthonormal representations, this paper's rate has a √d gap on source tasks and a √k gap on the target task, which the authors attribute to the Gaussian chain rule decomposition. Compared to Mustafa et al. [25], the covering number lemmas (Lemmas 1 and 3) remove a Lipschitzness assumption on the adversarial loss class, extending applicability to discontinuous attack models (e.g., normalized perturbation attacks). Compared to Yin et al. [44], the dimensional dependence is Õ(√d) versus d^(1/q), matching when p=2 but worse by √d when p=1, though this paper's attacks are constrained to remain within the input domain (Assumption 4.B). The non-adversarial MTRL rates of Tripuraneni et al. [37] and Watkins et al. [39] are recovered as special cases when the attack class contains only the identity, confirming the adversarial overhead is isolated to the Λ_A factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-adversarial MTRL theory of Tripuraneni et al. [37] and Watkins et al. [39] to the adversarial setting, introducing a 'robust task diversity' condition (Definition 1) that generalizes the original task diversity to account for adversarial perturbations. It also builds on the single-task adversarial generalization analysis of Mustafa et al. [25] and Yin et al. [44], but removes their Lipschitzness assumption on the adversarial loss class and handles the resulting Dudley integral more generally via a novel application of the Rudelson–Vershynin fat-shattering comparison inequality [31]. The multi-task-to-single-task reduction (Theorem 3) simplifies prior MTRL analyses that relied on worst-case complexity, and the optimistic-rate machinery from Srebro et al. [34] is adapted to the adversarial MTRL context for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general (non-linear) theoretical guarantees for adversarially robust transfer learning via representation learning, showing that pooling nt source-task samples yields a √d log d dimensional cost for adversarial robustness and that optimistic (fast) rates are achievable when robust in-class predictors exist. This gives practitioners a theoretical justification for adversarial pre-training on diverse tasks in data-scarce, high-stakes domains. Open questions include whether the within-domain attack assumption (Assumption 4.B) can be relaxed while retaining the √d rate, how to optimally select source tasks for robustness transfer, and whether the log-log coupling between function-class and attack complexity can be fully decoupled (as conjectured by Rudelson and Vershynin).
