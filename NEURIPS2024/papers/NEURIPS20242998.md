## Problem

Continual test-time adaptation methods for non-stationary environments rely heavily on pseudo-label generation or entropy minimization, but the challenge of adaptively aligning the evolving unlabeled data representation back to the source domain remains underexplored. The core difficulty is that each round provides only a small batch of unlabeled data, so using too little data introduces high variance while using the entire history introduces high bias as the distribution shifts over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242998/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method receives a small batch of N_t unlabeled RGB images (e.g., 10 per round) drawn from an unknown, time-varying distribution, along with a pre-computed label-free sketch of the source data consisting of the mean μ_0 and covariance Σ_0 of the source representation. The output is a class prediction for each input image and updated model parameters—specifically the affine parameters of normalization layers in the ResNet-50 representation module and the linear classifier weights—while the rest of the network remains frozen.

## Method

Ada-ReAlign employs a two-layer online ensemble: K base learners, each with a geometrically increasing restart window of 2^i rounds, perform online gradient descent on a Gaussian representation alignment loss (squared mean difference plus Frobenius-norm covariance difference relative to the source sketch), and an AdaNormalHedge meta learner with geometric covering adaptively weights their outputs based on recent loss performance. After combining base-learner representations, the linear classifier is refined via entropy minimization with L2 regularization toward the initial classifier. Only the affine parameters of normalization layers (less than 1% of total parameters) are updated, keeping computation efficient. Under convexity assumptions, the algorithm achieves O(T^{2/3} V_T^{1/3}) dynamic regret, where V_T captures the cumulative distribution shift, improving over the O(T) regret of per-round optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C under sequential shift (N_t=10, M=10), Ada-ReAlign achieves a mean classification error of 21.1%, outperforming the closest competitor SAR (24.9%) by 3.8 percentage points and the non-adapt baseline (32.7%) by 11.6 points. Under gradual shift, Ada-ReAlign reaches 11.8% mean error versus SAR's 12.6% and CoTTA's 14.1%. On the real-world iWildCam dataset, Ada-ReAlign achieves 23.6% error, beating TTAC (27.3%), CoTTA (29.3%), and SAR (31.5%). On ImageNet-C sequential shift, Ada-ReAlign attains 54.7% mean error, the best among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ada-ReAlign outperforms all six baselines across both gradual and sequential shift settings on CIFAR-10C and ImageNet-C. The closest competitor is SAR (24.9% mean error on CIFAR-10C sequential shift), which Ada-ReAlign beats by 3.8 points; SAR uses a single model with entropy-based reset, while Ada-ReAlign's meta-base ensemble adaptively selects the appropriate historical window. CoTTA (32.5%) and TTAC (28.0%) both underperform significantly under sequential shift, as their reliance on all historical data or pseudo-labels introduces bias when the distribution changes. TENT-CT (34.2%) and TENT-RE (28.2%) show that neither continuous adaptation nor per-round restart alone is sufficient for non-stationary streams. MEMO (30.4%) with data augmentation also lags behind. On the real-world iWildCam dataset, Ada-ReAlign (23.6%) beats TTAC (27.3%) by 3.7 points and SAR (31.5%) by 7.9 points, confirming generalizability beyond synthetic corruption shifts. AdaNPC is excluded from main accuracy comparisons because it requires access to labeled source data during adaptation, but in the efficiency comparison it is faster (single forward pass) yet not directly comparable on accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual test-time adaptation (TTA) literature by introducing non-stationary representation alignment as the adaptation mechanism, departing from the dominant entropy-minimization and pseudo-labeling approaches (TENT, CoTTA, SAR). The algorithmic core is directly inspired by the online ensemble framework of Zhao et al. (JMLR 2024), which uses base learners with geometrically increasing window sizes and restarts to handle non-stationary online learning, combined with the AdaNormalHedge meta-learner (Luo & Schapire, COLT 2015) to adaptively weight base learners. Prior representation alignment methods (TTT++, TTAC) target fixed distributions with abundant unlabeled data; this work is the first to formulate and solve representation alignment as a sequential, data-scarce, distribution-shifting problem using only a source sketch (mean and covariance).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap in continual TTA by showing that adaptive representation alignment to a source sketch can outperform pseudo-label-based methods, particularly under abrupt sequential shifts where pseudo-labels become unreliable. The dynamic regret guarantee of O(T^{2/3} V_T^{1/3}) provides the first theoretical grounding for a TTA method in non-stationary settings, though it holds only under convexity assumptions that do not apply to the ResNet-50 used empirically. Open questions include whether the approach extends to class-specific prototype alignment, whether non-convex theoretical guarantees can be established, and whether the ~5× computational overhead (11 base learners) is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment. The method's reliance on only a source sketch (not full source data) is a practical advantage for privacy-constrained settings.
