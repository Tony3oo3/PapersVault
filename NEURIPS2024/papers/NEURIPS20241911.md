## Problem

Off-dynamics RL aims to transfer policies across domains that differ only in transition dynamics, but the field lacks a standard benchmark: existing methods (DARC, VGDF, PAR, H2O, etc.) each construct custom environments with hand-crafted dynamics shifts, making cross-method comparison unreliable and progress difficult to track. ODRL introduces the first unified benchmark that covers multiple task domains, a broad spectrum of dynamics shift types, and all four combinations of online/offline source and target domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241911/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives state observations and transitions from a source domain (with known dynamics) and a limited budget of target-domain data (5,000 transitions for locomotion/dexterous tasks, 10,000 for AntMaze), where the two domains share identical state spaces, action spaces, and rewards but differ in transition probabilities. The source domain may be online (interactive) or offline (fixed D4RL-quality datasets), and the target domain may likewise be online or offline, yielding four experimental settings. Performance is evaluated solely in the target domain via achieved return and a normalized score (NS = (J_π − J_r)/(J_e − J_r) × 100), with reference random and expert returns provided for all tasks.

## Method

ODRL organizes 122+ tasks across three domains—locomotion (Ant, Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d with friction/gravity/kinematic/morphology shifts), navigation (AntMaze with 18 map layouts), and dexterous manipulation (Adroit pen/door/relocate/hammer with broken-joint and shrink-finger shifts)—and implements 15+ algorithms in a unified single-file framework spanning all four online/offline settings. The benchmark includes recent off-dynamics methods (DARC with domain-classifier reward penalty, VGDF with value-guided data filtering, PAR with representation-mismatch penalty, H2O with importance-weighted CQL, DARA, BOSA) alongside baselines that treat both domains as one mixed domain (SAC, CQL_SAC, MCQ_SAC, BC_SAC, IQL, TD3_BC, RLPD). Extensive experiments across 5 seeds reveal that no single algorithm dominates all dynamics shift types: PAR excels on locomotion but fails on dexterous manipulation, VGDF struggles where dynamics models are hard to train, and surprisingly, vanilla SAC trained on mixed source+target data often performs competitively, while source-domain performance does not reliably predict target-domain performance.

## Results

The benchmark evaluates methods using normalized score (NS) in the target domain across locomotion (Ant, Walker2d), navigation (AntMaze), and dexterous manipulation (Pen, Door) tasks under four experimental settings. In the Online-Online setting, PAR achieves the highest aggregated NS on locomotion tasks, while DARC and SAC_IW are the only off-dynamics methods that achieve competitive performance on dexterous manipulation. Surprisingly, vanilla SAC trained on mixed source-target data without any dynamics-aware component (Obs 5) matches or exceeds dedicated off-dynamics methods on numerous dynamics shift types. AntMaze navigation proves extremely challenging, with no algorithm achieving meaningful returns across all maze layouts, and a larger dynamics gap does not necessarily make learning harder (Obs 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAR [44] achieves the best locomotion performance across friction, gravity, kinematic, and morphology shifts but fails on AntMaze and Adroit due to poor reward penalty terms and severe value overestimation. DARC [16] is one of only two methods (alongside SAC_IW) that achieves competitive performance on dexterous manipulation, while VGDF [76] performs on par with PAR on locomotion but falls short on Adroit because it requires training target-domain dynamics models that are difficult for high-dimensional tasks. Vanilla SAC [26] trained on mixed source-target data is surprisingly competitive, matching or exceeding dedicated off-dynamics methods on many shift types (Obs 5), and CQL_SAC can outperform BC_PAR when expert source datasets are available (Obs 8). H2O [52] remains competitive in both Offline-Online and Online-Offline settings by combining domain-classifier-based importance weighting with CQL conservatism. In the Offline-Offline setting, DARA [41], BOSA [42], IQL [39], and TD3_BC [20] are evaluated, with no single method dominating. A critical finding is that no method exhibits universal advantages across all dynamics shifts (Obs 1), and there is no definitive correlation between source-domain and target-domain performance (Obs 11), meaning source-domain performance is not a reliable proxy for target-domain success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the off-dynamics RL formulation of Eysenbach et al. (2021), which was restricted to the online-online setting, by formally defining a general setting where both source and target domains can be independently online or offline (yielding four experimental paradigms). It positions itself against existing RL benchmarks (D4RL, DMC, Meta-World, RLBench, CARL, Gym-extensions, Continual World), none of which support single-task dynamics shift evaluation with offline datasets. The key methodological departure is from prior off-dynamics RL papers that each constructed their own bespoke environments; ODRL unifies algorithms like DARC, VGDF, PAR, H2O, DARA, and BOSA in a single-file, single-framework implementation for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ODRL fills a critical gap by providing the first standardized benchmark for off-dynamics RL, enabling meaningful cross-method comparison that was previously impossible due to heterogeneous self-constructed testbeds. The finding that no existing method dominates across all dynamics shift types, combined with the observation that simple mixed-domain SAC training often matches sophisticated off-dynamics algorithms, suggests the field needs fundamentally new approaches rather than incremental refinements. A key limitation is that ODRL is restricted to sim-to-sim with a single dynamics shift per task, whereas real-world deployment may involve compound shifts. The open question of whether a truly general dynamics-aware algorithm exists remains unresolved, and the benchmark's constrained target-domain data budget (5K–10K transitions) makes it a realistic but challenging testbed.
