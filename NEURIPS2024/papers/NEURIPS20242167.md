## Problem

State-of-the-art audio deepfake detection models suffer from severe performance degradation on unseen generative attacks (out-of-domain data) and operate as black boxes whose decisions cannot be explained to end users. Existing approaches either rely on costly end-to-end finetuning of SSL frontends or post-hoc saliency maps that are sensitive to training configurations and inconsistent across runs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242167/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes raw audio speech samples (5-second segments during training, full duration at evaluation with only amplitude normalization) and produces a binary real/fake prediction along with a quantifiable style-linguistics mismatch score derived from the cosine distance between learned dependency features. The input is processed through two separate frozen Wav2vec-XLSR encoders with distinct layer selections (layers 0–10 for style, layers 14–21 for linguistics), making the pipeline sensitive to the specific disentanglement of paralinguistic and linguistic content within the SSL representations.

## Method

SLIM is a two-stage framework that explicitly models the style-linguistics mismatch introduced by TTS/VC systems, which artificially combine verbal content with target-speaker vocal attributes. Stage 1 performs one-class self-supervised contrastive training on real speech only, using a cross-subspace loss to pull compressed style and linguistics embeddings together and an intra-subspace loss to remove redundancy within each subspace, yielding 256-dim dependency features that capture the natural style-linguistics coupling. Stage 2 freezes all Stage 1 modules and trains a lightweight classifier (ASP + MLP + two FC layers) on the concatenation of the dependency features and the original subspace embeddings projected to 256 dims each, using binary cross-entropy on ASVspoof2019 LA data. The key design choice is that the dependency features are complementary to the raw subspace embeddings: the former flag samples whose style-linguistics relationship deviates from the real-speech norm, while the latter remain sensitive to signal-level synthesis artifacts, and their fusion captures a broader range of deepfake indicators.

## Results

SLIM (frozen frontend, full features) achieves EER of 0.6% on ASVspoof2019, 8.3% on ASVspoof2021, 12.9% on In-the-wild, and 13.5% on MLAAD-EN. On the two out-of-domain datasets (In-the-wild and MLAAD-EN), SLIM substantially outperforms all frozen-frontend baselines, with the next-best SSL-Fusion reaching only 24.2% and 26.5% EER respectively. The finetuned variant of SLIM further improves to 12.5% and 10.7% EER on In-the-wild and MLAAD-EN, beating the strongest finetuned baseline W2V-AASIST (17.5% and 14.5%). On in-domain ASVspoof2019, SLIM is competitive (0.6% EER) with top methods like WLM-ASP (0.3%) and SSL-Fusion (0.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among frozen-frontend baselines, SLIM (Full) ties W2V-LCNN on ASVspoof2019 (0.6% vs 0.6% EER) and is within 0.2% on ASVspoof2021 (8.3% vs 8.1%), but dramatically outperforms all frozen baselines on out-of-domain data: 12.9% vs 24.2% (SSL-Fusion) on In-the-wild and 13.5% vs 26.5% (SSL-Fusion) on MLAAD-EN — a gap of over 11 percentage points. Against the strongest finetuned baselines, SLIM (finetuned) achieves 12.5% EER on In-the-wild vs 17.5% for W2V-AASIST and 12.5% vs 18.6% for W2V-ASP-ft, while on MLAAD-EN it reaches 10.7% vs 14.5% (W2V-AASIST). On ASVspoof2021, W2V-AASIST (3.6%) slightly edges out finetuned SLIM (4.4%), but the gap narrows substantially on the harder out-of-domain sets. The paper also shows that SLIM's dependency features alone (without subspace embeddings) achieve 25.8% and 19.8% EER on In-the-wild and MLAAD-EN, outperforming the concatenated style+linguistics embeddings (25.1% and 23.9%), confirming that the explicit mismatch signal is the key driver of generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: SSL-based audio deepfake detection (Wav2vec/WavLM/HuBERT frontends with supervised backends) and style-linguistics decomposition in speech (x-vectors, ECAPA-TDNN for style; ASR-tuned SSL layers for linguistics). It departs from prior ADD systems by explicitly modeling the *dependency* between style and linguistics subspaces via a one-class self-supervised contrastive objective (Stage 1) rather than treating the SSL representation as a monolithic feature. The two-stage design—self-supervised pretraining on real speech only, followed by lightweight supervised classification—contrasts with the dominant fully-supervised end-to-end paradigm, and the interpretability mechanism (quantifying style-linguistics mismatch) replaces post-hoc xAI tools like SHAP or GradCAM that prior work has found sensitive to training configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Generalization to unseen generative attacks and model interpretability are the two most pressing open problems for deploying ADD in real-world settings (legal evidence, financial security, journalism). SLIM addresses both simultaneously by grounding detection in a principled, human-understandable signal (style-linguistics alignment) rather than opaque spectral artifacts. Key limitations include English-only evaluation, potential misclassification of atypical speech (dysarthric, children's, multi-speaker), and the fact that frontend finetuning—which further improves EER—undermines the interpretability guarantee. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multilingual deepfake datasets (e.g., MLAAD-v4), whether more diverse pretraining corpora can close the gap on atypical speakers, and whether interpretability can be preserved under end-to-end finetuning.
