## Problem

State-of-the-art knowledge distillation for LLMs relies on minimizing explicit distribution distances (KL, RKL, JS, TV) between teacher and student token-level predictions, which is essentially behavior cloning. This approach cannot capture the full knowledge of a teacher because multiple valid outputs exist for a given input and there is no standardized quality metric for generated text, leading to sub-optimal generalization of the distilled student. The paper asks whether a more principled imitation-learning objective—matching action-value moments rather than raw distributions—can close the imitation gap more effectively.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen teacher LLM (e.g., OpenLLaMA-7B or T5-XL), a pre-trained student LLM (e.g., OpenLLaMA-3B or T5-Large/Base/Small), and a supervised training dataset (e.g., Dolly-15K, SAMSum, IWSLT'17). During training, both teacher-generated (off-policy) and student-generated (on-policy) trajectories are sampled per step, and two lightweight single-layer MLP Q-value estimators (adding O(H|V|) parameters each) are jointly trained. The output is a distilled student model that can be deployed independently for instruction-following, summarization, translation, or reasoning tasks.

## Method

The paper formulates auto-regressive text generation as a finite-horizon MDP and applies the Performance Difference Lemma to derive off-policy and on-policy moment-matching upper bounds on the imitation gap between teacher and student policies. Instead of minimizing a fixed distribution distance, it introduces a two-player minimax game: two Q-value networks (one for off-policy, one for on-policy) are trained via stochastic gradient ascent to maximize the moment-matching discrepancy, while the student policy is updated via policy gradient to minimize it. The Q-value estimator is a compact linear module f_φ(y≤t, y) = (h_t^{π_θ} + v_y)^T w_y that combines the policy's hidden state with token embeddings. The joint objective β·L^off + (1−β)·L^on is optimized with an inner loop of N Q-value ascent steps followed by one policy gradient descent step per outer iteration, and the paper proves O(1/√K) convergence to an ε-accurate stationary point under standard smoothness and boundedness assumptions.

## Results

On the instruction-following benchmark (OpenLLaMA-7B → OpenLLaMA-3B), the proposed adversarial moment-matching method achieves 59.8 GPT-4 score and 30.7 ROUGE-L on DollyEval, 54.2/21.7 on SelfInst, and 47.8/21.4 on VicunaEval, outperforming the strongest baseline DistiLLM (59.2/29.5, 53.4/20.8, 46.3/20.4) across all five evaluation sets. On task-specific experiments (T5-XL → T5-Large), the method reaches 52.7 ROUGE-L on SAMSum, 36.0 BLEU on IWSLT'17 (en-de), and 65.3% accuracy on StrategyQA, again surpassing all baselines. The method also improves over the SFT baseline by 13.0 GPT-4 points on DollyEval and 8.3 ROUGE-L on SAMSum (T5-Large).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both instruction-following and task-specific experiments. The closest competitor is DistiLLM [21], which the method surpasses by 0.6 GPT-4 points on DollyEval (59.8 vs. 59.2), 0.8 on SelfInst (54.2 vs. 53.4), and 1.5 on VicunaEval (47.8 vs. 46.3), with consistent 0.5–1.5 point gains on ROUGE-L metrics. MiniLLM [14] and GKD [2] trail by 1.0–3.6 GPT-4 points depending on the dataset, with GKD showing a notable weakness on VicunaEval R-L (19.3 vs. 21.4). The older baselines—KD [16], SeqKD [20], and ImitKD [24]—are substantially behind, with KD actually underperforming SFT on DollyEval GPT-4 (43.9 vs. 46.8), confirming that simple distribution-matching or teacher-output cloning is insufficient for LLM distillation. On task-specific tasks (Table 2), the method leads DistiLLM by 0.6 ROUGE-L on SAMSum, 0.5 BLEU on IWSLT'17, and 2.5% accuracy on StrategyQA (T5-Large), with similar margins holding for T5-Base and T5-Small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes LLM knowledge distillation from the dominant distribution-matching paradigm (KL, RKL, JS divergences, TV distance as used by MiniLLM, GKD, DistiLLM) into a moment-matching imitation learning framework, building directly on Swamy et al.'s game-theoretic formulation of the imitation gap. While prior on-policy/off-policy distillation methods minimize explicit probability distribution distances between teacher and student token predictions, this work instead matches moments of the Q-value function (action-value) via an adversarial two-player minimax game. The approach inherits the on-policy/off-policy combination strategy from recent KD methods but replaces the well-studied distribution-matching metrics with a learned Q-value function optimized through policy gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that step-wise TV distance upper-bounds the moment-matching objective provides a principled explanation for why moment-matching can in principle outperform distribution-matching, and the empirical finding that TV distance is consistently the second-best step-wise metric corroborates this connection. However, practical adoption is limited by the additional inner-loop gradient ascent steps and auxiliary Q-value networks that increase both time and memory overhead, and experiments are restricted to OpenLLaMA and T5 architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model families (e.g., LLaMA-2/3, Mistral), whether the Q-value estimation can be made more parameter-efficient, and whether the modest empirical gains over strong baselines like DistiLLM persist at larger scale.
