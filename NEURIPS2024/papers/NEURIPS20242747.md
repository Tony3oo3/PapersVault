## Problem

All existing state-of-the-art offline preference optimization algorithms (DPO, SLiC, IPO, KTO) were designed by human experts, inherently constraining the search space of possible loss functions f(βρ) to what human creativity can produce. The paper asks whether LLMs can be used to automatically discover novel, high-performing preference optimization objectives without expert human intervention, exploring the vast space of scalar loss functions over the log-ratio difference that remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The discovery pipeline consumes the log-probabilities of chosen and rejected completions under both the policy π_θ and reference model π_ref, from which the log-ratio difference ρ is computed. The LLM must output PyTorch code conforming to a fixed four-tensor interface (policy_chosen_logps, policy_rejected_logps, reference_chosen_logps, reference_rejected_logps) that returns a scalar loss. The output is a validated, trainable loss function; the best discovered one (DiscoPOP) is a non-convex, dynamically weighted blend of logistic and exponential losses whose mixing coefficient is a sigmoid of βρ/τ, with τ=0.05 matching the fixed KL-regularization scale used during discovery.

## Method

The paper introduces an iterative LLM-driven objective discovery loop in which GPT-4 is prompted with established loss functions (DPO, SLiC, IPO, KTO) and their MT-Bench scores as in-context examples, then asked to propose a new PyTorch loss function. Each proposal is validated via unit tests, used to fine-tune a 7B Gemma model (zephyr-7b-gemma-sft) on the Argilla DPO Mix 7K preference dataset with fixed hyperparameters (β=0.05, lr=5e-7, 2 epochs, 8×A100), and evaluated on MT-Bench; the resulting score is fed back as fitness for the next generation over ~100 iterations. The best discovered loss, DiscoPOP (Log Ratio Modulated Loss), adaptively blends the DPO logistic loss and an exponential loss via a sigmoid gate on βρ/τ, producing a non-convex objective with negative gradients at ρ=0. This non-convexity and adaptive weighting allow DiscoPOP to emphasize exponential loss when the policy diverges positively (chosen preferred) and logistic loss when it diverges negatively, yielding consistent improvements over DPO on held-out tasks including Alpaca Eval 2.0 (13.21% vs 11.23% win rate vs GPT-4), TL;DR summarization, and IMDb sentiment generation.

## Results

On the held-out Alpaca Eval 2.0 benchmark, DiscoPOP (LRML) achieves a 13.21% win rate against GPT-4 and 14.78% length-controlled win rate against the SFT base model, outperforming DPO (11.23% / 12.81%) and SLiC (10.67% / 13.16%). On the discovery task (MT-Bench), DiscoPOP scores 7.916/10, surpassing DPO (7.888), SLiC (7.881), and KTO (7.603). On the TL;DR summarization task, DiscoPOP achieves 87.63% win rate vs. human preference, slightly below DPO (88.27%) but well above SLiC (83.02%) and KTO (85.34%). On IMDb positive sentiment generation, DiscoPOP achieves higher reward at lower KL-divergence than DPO for β ∈ {0.025, 0.05, 0.1}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiscoPOP consistently outperforms DPO across held-out tasks: on Alpaca Eval 2.0 it improves the win rate vs. GPT-4 from 11.23% to 13.21% (+1.98 pp) and the LC win rate vs. SFT from 12.81% to 14.78% (+1.97 pp), while on MT-Bench it scores 7.916 vs. DPO's 7.888. Against SLiC, DiscoPOP wins on all three held-out benchmarks (Alpaca Eval: 13.21% vs. 10.67%; MT-Bench: 7.916 vs. 7.881; TL;DR: 87.63% vs. 83.02%). Against KTO, DiscoPOP also leads on all tasks (Alpaca Eval: 13.21% vs. 12.57%; MT-Bench: 7.916 vs. 7.603; TL;DR: 87.63% vs. 85.34%). The one exception is the TL;DR summarization task, where DPO narrowly edges out DiscoPOP (88.27% vs. 87.63% vs. human preference), though the difference is within standard error. On IMDb, DiscoPOP achieves a better reward–KL tradeoff than DPO at low β values (0.025–0.1), but both methods degrade at high β (≥0.5), and DiscoPOP struggles to converge at extreme β values (≤0.01 or ≥2.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: offline preference optimization (DPO, SLiC, IPO, KTO) and meta-optimization for automated algorithm discovery. Prior meta-optimization work (Lu et al. 2022, Houthooft et al. 2018, Jackson et al. 2024) parameterized objectives with neural networks or domain-specific languages, while recent LLM-driven discovery efforts (Eureka, Romera-Paredes et al. 2024) targeted reward functions or coding tasks. Here, the authors use GPT-4 to directly propose code-level preference optimization loss functions in an iterative loop, departing from both the parameterized-objective paradigm and the task-specific reward design of Eureka by discovering general-purpose scalar loss functions f(βρ) that transfer across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can discover preference optimization losses that outperform all human-designed baselines across multiple held-out tasks, and that the best discovered loss (DiscoPOP) is non-convex—challenging the implicit assumption that convexity is necessary for well-behaved preference optimization. However, the approach is limited by its dependence on closed-source GPT-4, the repurposing of β as both a KL-regularization and a functional-shape parameter, and convergence failures at extreme β values. Open questions include whether multi-parameter objectives, open-source model self-improvement, and richer training-curve feedback (e.g., via VLMs) can scale the discovery process to larger models and more diverse tasks.
