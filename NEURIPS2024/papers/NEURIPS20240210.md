## Problem

While Adversarial Training (AT) is the mainstream approach for improving adversarial robustness in CNNs and Transformers, its effectiveness on Deep State Space Models (SSMs) remains largely unexplored. It is unclear whether SSM architectural components that boost standard training performance (e.g., data-dependent parameterization, MIMO extensions, Attention) similarly benefit under AT, and how they affect the robustness-generalization trade-off and Robust Overfitting (RO). This paper systematically investigates these questions across five SSM structures and four AT frameworks.

## Input / Output

Images are flattened into 1D sequences for SSM processing: MNIST as (784,1), CIFAR-10 as (1024,3), and Tiny-ImageNet as (4096,3), rather than being treated as 2D spatial inputs. The models produce class labels (10 classes for MNIST/CIFAR-10, 50 for Tiny-ImageNet) via a mean-pooling reduction followed by a linear head. This sequence-image formulation is essential to the study, as SSMs inherently model temporal/sequential dependencies and the paper aims to understand how adversarial perturbations propagate through state transitions.

## Method

The paper first empirically evaluates S4, DSS, S5, Mega, and Mamba under PGD-AT, TRADES, FreeAT, and YOPO, revealing that pure SSM structures struggle under AT, data-dependent parameterization (Mamba) does not help, and only Attention (Mega) yields a favorable robustness-generalization trade-off—albeit with severe Robust Overfitting. Theoretically, the authors prove (Theorem 4.1.1) that fixed-parameterized SSMs have output error bounds strictly determined by their state-transition eigenvalues, creating a fixed lower bound that limits AT's ability to reduce adversarial output error, while data-dependent SSMs (Mamba) risk error explosion due to ill-conditioned inverse operations in ZOH discretization. They show that Attention effectively provides adaptive scaling of the SSM output error, aligning clean and adversarial outputs but introducing RO through high model complexity. Inspired by this, they propose a simple Adaptive Scaling (AdS) module—y_k = σ(L₁(y_k)) ⊙ y_k + σ(L₂(y_k)) with learnable linear maps and a nonlinear activation—that replicates Attention's scaling benefit at minimal complexity, improving CA and RA by 3–4% without inducing RO.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PGD-AT, Mega (SSM+Attention) achieves the best clean accuracy (72.54%) and robust accuracy (40.53% PGD-10, 25.79% AA), outperforming DSS (64.92% CA, 37.31% RA), S4 (64.67% CA, 36.19% RA), Mamba (58.69% CA, 35.98% RA), and S5 (53.93% CA, 31.49% RA). Under TRADES, Mega again leads with 71.01% CA and 43.65% PGD-10 RA. However, Mega exhibits severe Robust Overfitting (RO), with its PGD-AT RA dropping from a best of 40.53% to 30.68% at the final epoch (a ~10% gap). The proposed AdS mechanism improves S4 and DSS by over 3% CA and 2–4% RA (e.g., DSS+AdS with TRADES reaches 69.58% CA and 41.24% RA), approaching Mega's performance without the RO issue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five SSM architectures under four AT frameworks. Mega (SSM+Attention) [19] consistently achieves the highest CA and RA after AT on CIFAR-10 (72.54% CA / 40.53% RA under PGD-AT; 71.01% CA / 43.65% RA under TRADES), outperforming DSS [28] by ~7–8% CA and ~3–6% RA, S4 [3] by ~8–9% CA and ~4–8% RA, Mamba [20] by ~14% CA and ~5–9% RA, and S5 [27] by ~19% CA and ~9–12% RA. However, Mega suffers from pronounced Robust Overfitting, with its PGD-AT RA declining from 40.53% (epoch 39) to 30.68% (final epoch). DSS [28] and S4 [3] (fixed-parameterized SISO SSMs) perform comparably to each other, with DSS slightly ahead in RA. Mamba [20], despite its data-dependent parameterization, underperforms both S4 and DSS in RA after AT, confirming that selective SSM parameters do not aid adversarial robustness. S5 [27] (MIMO SSM) shows the worst robustness-generalization trade-off, with the largest CA drop (16.62% under PGD-AT) and lowest RA among all structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the SSM architecture literature (S4, DSS, S5, Mamba, Mega) and the adversarial training literature (PGD-AT, TRADES, FreeAT, YOPO), but carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate how different SSM structural components interact with AT. The only prior robustness study on SSMs (Du et al., 2024) examined a single visual SSM (VMamba) without AT, leaving the question of whether AT can effectively harden SSMs entirely open. The paper's theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1.1) provides a novel output-error bound for SSMs under perturbation that unifies fixed-parameterized and data-dependent cases, and the proposed AdS mechanism repurposes the adaptive-scaling insight from Attention into a two-linear-layer module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSMs (especially Mamba) gain traction as alternatives to Transformers, understanding their adversarial vulnerability is critical before deployment in safety-sensitive settings. The finding that data-dependent parameterization (Mamba) does not confer AT advantages over simpler fixed-parameterized SSMs, and that a trivial adaptive scaling module recovers most of Attention's AT benefit without robust overfitting, gives practitioners a concrete, low-cost design principle for robust SSMs. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 2D image classification (treated as 1D sequences), modest absolute robustness numbers, and no exploration of whether AdS generalizes to NLP, video, or deeper SSM backbones.
