## Problem

Frontier large multimodal models (LMMs) require large-scale interleaved image-text datasets for pre-training, yet the open-source landscape lags significantly behind closed-source efforts. The previous leading open-source dataset, OBELICS, contains only 115B text tokens and 353M images sourced exclusively from HTML, creating a 10x scale gap and limiting domain diversity. This disparity hampers the development of competitive open-source LMMs and restricts the community's ability to study multimodal data engineering at scale.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes CommonCrawl WARC dumps (HTML, May 2017–April 2024), CommonCrawl WAT dumps (PDFs, February 2023–April 2024), and ArXiv LaTeX source code, producing 1.02T text tokens across 1.05B interleaved documents with 3.42B images. For HTML, the output is document text paired with image URLs; for PDFs and ArXiv, it is text paired with a source PDF URL and image reference numbers for local extraction. Images are constrained to 150–20,000 pixels per side, and the final dataset exceeds 300TB, relying on external image URLs rather than being self-contained.

## Method

MINT-1T's core contribution is a multi-source data engineering pipeline that preserves the original interleaved ordering of images and text across three distinct document types. The HTML pipeline extends OBELICS's DOM-tree parsing over a wider crawl window; the PDF pipeline uses PyMuPDF to extract reading order via bounding-box column clustering and anchors images by spatial proximity to text blocks; the ArXiv pipeline parses LaTeX with TexSoup to interleave figure tags with paper text. Quality filtering combines FastText language identification, RefinedWeb n-gram and MassiveText rules, image size/aspect-ratio thresholds (relaxed to 3:1 for PDFs to preserve scientific figures), and an NSFW image classifier that discards entire documents containing a single flagged image. Deduplication uses a Bloom filter on 13-gram paragraphs (discarding documents >80% duplicate) and SHA256 hashing to remove images appearing more than 10 times per snapshot. Model validation trains 4.6B XGen-MM (ViT-H + 6-layer perceiver resampler + Phi-3 mini) and 7B Idefics2 (Mistral-7B + LoRA) architectures on 10B multimodal tokens with a 50/50 image-text-to-interleaved batch mixture, demonstrating that the full MINT-1T mixture outperforms OBELICS on MMMU and VQA benchmarks.

## Results

On in-context learning benchmarks (4-shot and 8-shot), the full MINT-1T model achieves an average score of 62.3 (4-shot) and 64.6 (8-shot), outperforming OBELICS (61.2 and 63.3) and MINT-1T-HTML-only (60.9 and 63.0). On multi-image reasoning, MINT-1T scores 41.3 on MMMU (vs. OBELICS's 37.6) but 43.3 on Mantis-Eval (vs. OBELICS's 44.2). The HTML-only subset of MINT-1T performs roughly on par with OBELICS, indicating that the performance gains come primarily from the addition of PDF and ArXiv documents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is OBELICS, the previous largest open-source interleaved dataset (115B tokens, HTML-only). The full MINT-1T model (HTML + PDF + ArXiv) outperforms OBELICS on average in-context learning by 1.3 points at 8-shot (64.6 vs. 63.3), with notable gains on VQAv2 (+3.8), TextVQA (+1.8), and OKVQA (+1.5). On MMMU, MINT-1T achieves 41.3 versus OBELICS's 37.6, a 3.7-point improvement attributed to the Science and Technology content in PDF/ArXiv documents. However, MINT-1T underperforms OBELICS on Mantis-Eval (43.3 vs. 44.2), suggesting the multi-image reasoning gains are not uniform. The HTML-only subset of MINT-1T is essentially on par with OBELICS (63.0 vs. 63.3 average at 8-shot), confirming that the diversity of data sources—not just scale—drives the performance improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINT-1T extends the line of open-source multimodal interleaved datasets initiated by Multimodal-C4 and OBELICS, which were sourced exclusively from HTML documents. The key departure is the inclusion of PDFs (from CommonCrawl WAT files) and ArXiv papers as data sources, a first for open-source interleaved datasets at this scale. The filtering pipeline adapts text-quality heuristics from RefinedWeb/Dolma/FineWeb and image filtering from OBELICS, while the model experiments follow the OpenFlamingo training paradigm with XGen-MM and Idefics2 architectures. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a data-engineering contribution that closes the scale and diversity gap between open-source and proprietary multimodal pre-training corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINT-1T provides the research community with a 10x larger and more domain-diverse open-source interleaved dataset, directly addressing the data scarcity that has limited open-source LMM development relative to frontier closed models. The finding that PDF/ArXiv sources shift domain coverage toward Science and Technology (whereas OBELICS is biased toward Humanities) suggests that source diversity, not just scale, matters for generalization. Key limitations include PDF reading-order errors from PyMuPDF, the reliance on external image URLs subject to link rot, and the fact that the full 1T-token dataset was only partially utilized in experiments (10B tokens). Open questions include whether training on substantially larger fractions of MINT-1T yields further gains, whether better PDF parsing would improve quality, and whether additional document sources (e.g., books, slide decks) could further diversify coverage.
