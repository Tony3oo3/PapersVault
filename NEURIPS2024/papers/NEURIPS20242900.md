## Problem

Existing dynamic graph benchmarks lack raw text attributes on nodes and edges, while text-attributed graph benchmarks ignore temporal dynamics, leaving no unified testbed for studying how structural and textual co-evolution in real-world graphs affects downstream tasks. This gap hinders both methodological advances in semantic modeling within dynamic graphs and the evaluation of LLMs' temporal reasoning over graph-structured text.

## Input / Output

Each DyTAG provides edges as (source, target, relation_id, timestamp, category) tuples alongside raw text descriptions for both nodes and edges. For dynamic graph models, text is pre-encoded via BERT-base-uncased (768-dim, 512-token max) to initialize node and edge embeddings; for LLMs, raw text is formatted into prompts containing node descriptions and recent one-hop interactions. The four tasks produce: binary link scores (AP/AUC-ROC), ranked node lists over 1000 candidates (Hits@k), multi-class edge category predictions (weighted P/R/F1), and free-form generated relation text scored by BERTScore.

## Method

DTGB constructs eight large-scale DyTAGs spanning e-commerce, social networks, multi-round dialogue, and knowledge graph domains, with nodes and edges carrying raw text and edges annotated with meaningful categories (e.g., 1–5 star ratings, political relation types, e-mail folders). The evaluation protocol defines four complementary tasks—future link prediction, destination node retrieval, edge classification, and textual relation generation—each probing different facets of structural-textual co-evolution. Seven dynamic graph models (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, CAWN, TCL, GraphMixer, DyGFormer) are benchmarked with BERT-encoded text initialization under both transductive and inductive settings, while six LLMs (Mistral-7B, Vicuna-7B/13B, Llama-3-8B, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o) are evaluated on textual relation generation with optional LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning. Key empirical findings: text consistently improves performance but degrades memory-based models (JODIE, DyRep) whose incremental update mechanism is misled by high-dimensional BERT embeddings; inductive settings benefit most from text for cold-start nodes; and existing models struggle with edge classification (especially on high-cardinality datasets like GDELT) and semantic-aware node retrieval under historical sampling.

## Results

On the edge classification task, the best weighted F1 scores are 0.648 (JODIE, Enron), 0.134 (CAWN, GDELT), 0.316 (CAWN, ICEWS1819), 0.623 (TCL, Googlemap CT), 0.650 (TGAT, Stack elec), 0.722 (TCL, Stack ubuntu), 0.605 (DyGFormer, Amazon movies), and 0.636 (DyGFormer, Yelp), showing that models struggle significantly on datasets with many edge categories. For future link prediction (AUC-ROC), transductive performance reaches up to 0.992 (TCL, ICEWS1819 with text), but JODIE and DyRep hit OOM on all graphs with >100K nodes. In the inductive node retrieval task (Hits@10), text attributes yield dramatic gains (e.g., Googlemap CT: 0.099→0.371 for TGAT), yet absolute scores remain low, exposing models' weakness in capturing semantic relevance. For textual relation generation, all six LLMs achieve BERTScore F1 of 80.3–82.1, with supervised fine-tuning improving Vicuna-7B by +3.82 points (to 85.67 on Googlemap CT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven dynamic graph models, TCL and DyGFormer are the most consistently competitive: TCL achieves the best AUC-ROC on ICEWS1819 (0.9923 transductive, 0.9778 inductive with text) and the best F1 on Stack ubuntu edge classification (0.722), while DyGFormer leads on Enron (0.9779) and GDELT (0.9662) link prediction and Yelp edge classification (0.636). JODIE and DyRep, the memory-based models, suffer severe scalability issues (OOM on all graphs with >100K nodes) and their performance degrades when BERT-encoded text is used for initialization, as the incremental RNN update is misled by high-dimensional embeddings. TGAT and CAWN are mid-tier performers that benefit consistently from text attributes; CAWN edges out others on GDELT and ICEWS1819 edge classification (F1 0.134 and 0.316 respectively). GraphMixer is the weakest overall, ranking last or near-last on most datasets and showing the largest gap between transductive and inductive settings. For textual relation generation, open-source LLMs (Mistral-7B F1=82.07, Vicuna-7B F1=81.85) match or slightly exceed GPT-3.5-turbo (F1=81.91) and GPT-4o (F1=81.07) on Googlemap CT, and Vicuna-7B benefits the most from LoRA-based SFT (+3.82 F1), suggesting that domain-specific fine-tuning is more impactful than raw model scale for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of dynamic graph learning and text-attributed graph (TAG) research, two areas that have developed largely in parallel. Existing dynamic graph benchmarks (TGB, DyGLib) provide temporal interaction data but lack raw text descriptions, while TAG benchmarks (CS-TAG) offer rich node text but ignore temporal evolution. DTGB unifies both by introducing eight large-scale datasets where nodes and edges carry raw text, edge categories, and timestamps, and by standardizing four evaluation tasks that require models to jointly reason over structural dynamics and natural language. The text integration strategy (BERT-encoded embeddings as initialization) is deliberately simple, positioning the benchmark as a testbed for future, more sophisticated text-structure fusion methods rather than a methodological contribution itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTGB is the first comprehensive benchmark for DyTAGs, and its empirical results expose concrete weaknesses in current models: memory-based architectures (JODIE, DyRep) suffer scalability failures on large graphs and paradoxically degrade when text is added; all evaluated models rely heavily on structural co-occurrence rather than semantic relevance (evidenced by large performance drops under historical sampling in node retrieval); and edge modeling is largely neglected in existing architectures. Open questions include how to design temporal graph tokens that natively encode dynamic structure for LLMs, how to scale text-attribute integration to graphs with millions of nodes and long text, and whether the simple BERT-initialization strategy can be replaced by more adaptive mechanisms. The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for DyTAG research given its domain diversity and unified protocol.
