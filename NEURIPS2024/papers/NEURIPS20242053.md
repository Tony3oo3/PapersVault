## Problem

Memory-efficient LLM training methods like GaLore and Sketchy project optimizer states into a low-rank subspace using SVD-derived projection matrices, but no convergence guarantee existed for arbitrary projection matrix update rules on non-convex objectives. Additionally, periodic SVD is computationally expensive for large models and fails to incorporate gradient information from all recent data in a timely fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242053/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes model weight matrices W ∈ R^{n×m} and their gradients G_t at each iteration, along with a projection matrix P_t ∈ R^{n×k} (k ≪ n). It produces updated weights W_{t+1} and an updated projection P_{t+1}, while the optimizer state (momentum, variance) is stored only in the reduced k×m subspace rather than full n×m size. The projection update requires only a single backward pass on a simple PCA loss, making it fast enough to run in parallel with the weight update without adding wall-clock overhead.

## Method

The paper provides the first convergence guarantee for subspace descent with arbitrary projection matrix update rules by showing that for any optimizer admitting a Hamiltonian+descent structure (Adam, Lion, Momentum), the Lyapunov function H(W,Ŝ) remains monotonically non-increasing regardless of how P_t evolves, because the adjoint property ⟨P_t X, Y⟩ = ⟨X, P_t^T Y⟩ cancels cross terms in the time derivative. Building on this theory, Online Subspace Descent updates P_t via one step of an optimizer (e.g., Adam or SGD) on the online PCA objective L_{G_t}(P) = ‖PP^T G̃_t − G̃_t‖² + λ‖P^T P − I‖², where G̃_t is the normalized gradient, replacing the expensive periodic SVD of GaLore with a cheap continuous update. The framework generalizes to arbitrary linear operators P_t with adjoints P_t*, enabling two-sided projections P_t X Q_t. Experiments pretraining LLaMA (60M–7B) on C4 show lower perplexity and better GLUE scores than GaLore at comparable memory, with up to 1.3× speedup at 7B scale.

## Results

On LLaMA pretraining (C4, sequence length 256, 10K steps, rank 512), Online Subspace Descent achieves perplexity of 56.12 (60M), 43.67 (350M), and 31.30 (1B), compared to GaLore's 57.03, 44.34, and 35.52 respectively, while the full-rank 8-bit AdamW baseline reaches 32.75, 30.43, and 29.40. At 7B scale, Online Subspace Descent achieves 43.72 perplexity in 7.14 hours versus GaLore's 51.21 in 9.74 hours (1.3× faster). On GLUE downstream evaluation of 7B checkpoints, Online Subspace Descent scores 0.4951 average versus GaLore's 0.4862.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaLore (the primary low-rank baseline), Online Subspace Descent consistently achieves lower perplexity across all model sizes: 56.12 vs. 57.03 (60M), 43.67 vs. 44.34 (350M), 31.30 vs. 35.52 (1B), and 43.72 vs. 51.21 (7B), with the gap widening substantially at larger scales. Against the full-rank 8-bit AdamW baseline, Online Subspace Descent narrows the perplexity gap from 4.12 points (GaLore at 1B: 35.52 vs. 29.40) to just 1.90 points (Ours at 1B: 31.30 vs. 29.40). On GLUE downstream tasks for 7B checkpoints, Online Subspace Descent outperforms GaLore on 5 of 6 tasks (MRPC, SST2, MNLI, QNLI, QQP) with a 0.009 average improvement. When paired with alternative base optimizers (Lion, Adafactor), Online Subspace Descent with AdamW for P_t updates (e.g., Lion+AdamW: 44.76, Adafactor+AdamW: 34.15) outperforms the corresponding GaLore variants (GaLore+Lion: 46.90, GaLore+Adafactor: 34.32), though same-optimizer pairings (Lion+Lion: 57.97) can underperform GaLore.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory-efficient LLM training line initiated by GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024) and Sketchy (Feinberg et al., 2024), which project optimizer states into a low-rank subspace via periodic SVD. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the expensive, infrequent SVD with a continuous online PCA update of the projection matrix, and (2) providing the first convergence guarantee for arbitrary projection update rules by embedding the analysis in the Hamiltonian+descent framework (Maddison et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2023). The theoretical machinery draws on the Lyapunov analysis of LION and Adam in continuous time, showing that the adjoint structure of the projection operator preserves the descent property regardless of how the subspace evolves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence guarantee fills a notable theoretical gap: prior subspace-descent methods (GaLore, Sketchy, Stochastic Subspace Descent) lacked any non-convex convergence analysis, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on projection update rules. Practically, eliminating SVD reduces per-step overhead by up to 142× and narrows the perplexity gap with full-rank AdamW8bit across model scales (60M–7B). Open questions include whether the continuous-time Lyapunov result transfers to finite-step discrete training, the role of weight decay in the projected dynamics, and whether the approach extends to architectures with inherently non-low-rank weight structures (e.g., MoE). The empirical evidence is limited to a single dataset (C4), one architecture family (LLaMA), short 10K-step runs, and no error bars, so the generalizability of the perplexity gains remains to be established.
