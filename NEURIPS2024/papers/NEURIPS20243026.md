## Problem

DDPM and related diffusion inference methods decompose the denoising process into O~(1/ε²) tiny-step Gaussian transitions, each trivially easy but collectively requiring a large number of subproblems to bound the cumulative sampling error. This creates a severe imbalance between subproblem quantity and per-subproblem complexity. The paper asks whether a more balanced decomposition—fewer subproblems with harder but tractable targets—can provably accelerate diffusion inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard Gaussian noise sample as initialization and a pretrained score network s_θ,t that provides score estimates at each diffusion time; each inner MCMC iteration consumes exactly one score query, so total cost is measured in number of function evaluations (NFE). The output is a single sample from the learned data distribution, evaluated on MNIST (28×28 grayscale images) and synthetic 10-dimensional mixture-of-Gaussians distributions. Unlike DDPM which uses a fixed small step size η=O(ε²), the RTK framework uses a constant step size η=½·log(1+1/2L), reducing the outer-loop count to O~(1) while shifting computational effort into the inner MCMC loop.

## Method

The core contribution is the Reverse Transition Kernel (RTK) framework, which reformulates diffusion inference as a sequence of sampling subproblems targeting the conditional p_{t'|t}^←(x'|x) ∝ exp(−f_t(x) − ‖x' − x·e^{−η}‖²/(2(1−e^{−2η}))), a perturbation of the noised distribution with an L2 regularization term. By choosing step size η = ½·log(1+1/2L), the number of outer-loop subproblems becomes O~(1) while each target is provably L-strongly log-concave, enabling efficient MCMC sampling. Two inner-loop solvers are developed: RTK-MALA, which uses Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin steps and achieves linear convergence O(log(1/ε)) in the sampling error (requiring an energy difference estimator), and RTK-ULD, which uses Underdamped Langevin Dynamics and achieves O~(d^{1/2}ε^{−1}) gradient complexity under only score estimation assumptions. The RTK score is computable with a single score network query plus a closed-form quadratic term, making the framework directly compatible with pretrained diffusion models without architectural changes.

## Results

Theoretically, RTK-ULD achieves a gradient complexity of Õ(L²d^{1/2}ε^{-1}) to reach ε TV error, improving upon DDPM's Õ(L²dε^{-2}) by a factor of Õ(d^{1/2}ε^{-1}). RTK-MALA achieves linear convergence in ε with complexity O(L⁴d²log(d/ε)), the first linear-in-ε result for diffusion inference. Empirically, on a 12-component 10D Mixture of Gaussians, RTK-MALA attains the highest marginal accuracy among all tested methods, while on MNIST (28×28 grayscale), RTK-based methods achieve lower FID than DDPM at 20 NFE, with RTK-MALA producing visibly sharper digit reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM [17], all RTK variants (ULA, ULD, MALA, score-only MALA) achieve substantially higher marginal accuracy on the 10D MoG benchmark at the same NFE, with the gap most pronounced at low NFE (<200); on MNIST at 20 NFE, RTK-ULD and score-only RTK-MALA produce lower FID and visually sharper digits than DDPM. Against the SDE-based DDPM theory of Chen et al. [8], RTK-ULD improves the dimension dependence from d to d^{1/2} and the error dependence from ε^{-2} to ε^{-1}, while requiring only score estimation (no energy Hessian). Against the ODE-based DPUM of Chen et al. [9], RTK-ULD matches the Õ(L²d^{1/2}ε^{-1}) rate but under milder assumptions—DPUM additionally requires Lipschitz smoothness of the score network s_θ, whereas RTK-ULD only needs [A1]–[A2] and [E1]. Against the ODE-based sampler of Li et al. [24], RTK-ULD improves the dimension dependence from d³ to d^{1/2} and removes the need for energy Hessian estimation. RTK-MALA requires the additional energy-difference estimation assumption [E2], making it slightly more restrictive than RTK-ULD, but in exchange achieves the first linear convergence in ε (O(log(1/ε)) inner iterations), which is advantageous for high-accuracy sampling tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion inference (as in DDPM) through the lens of subproblem decomposition: DDPM uses many tiny step sizes (η = O(ε²)), each solved by a one-step Gaussian, yielding O~(1/ε²) trivial subproblems. The RTK framework generalizes this by allowing larger step sizes (η = Θ(1)), producing only O~(1) subproblems whose targets are provably strongly log-concave, solvable via MCMC (MALA, ULD). Theoretically, it builds on the convergence analysis of Chen et al. (ICLR 2023) and Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2024), improving the state-of-the-art complexity from O~(dε^{-2}) to O~(d^{1/2}ε^{-1}) for RTK-ULD and achieving linear convergence O(log(1/ε)) for RTK-MALA under slightly stricter assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RTK framework provides a principled way to trade off the number of diffusion inference steps against per-step computational cost, potentially enabling faster sampling with fewer NFEs. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the best theoretical result (RTK-MALA's linear convergence) requires energy difference estimation unavailable in standard pretrained diffusion models, and experiments are confined to small-scale MoG distributions and MNIST. Open questions include whether the theoretical speedups materialize at image-generation scale, how to practically approximate energy differences from score networks, and whether the O~(1) outer-loop structure remains advantageous when inner MCMC chains must mix in high dimensions.
