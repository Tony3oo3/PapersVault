## Problem

Despite the widespread use of Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) for PDE solving, there is no comprehensive theoretical analysis of their expressivity or trainability. While universal approximation is established, the exponential expressivity governed by weight initialization (analogous to the ordered-chaos phase transition in fully connected and convolutional networks) remains unexplored, and the empirically observed training instability in deep FNOs lacks a theoretical explanation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The FNO operates on scalar-valued spatial fields defined on a regular grid of size N (e.g., 64 for 1D advection/Burgers, 64×64 for 2D Darcy Flow and Navier-Stokes) with D hidden features (D=32 in experiments) and K Fourier modes (K=12). The input is a PDE initial condition or coefficient field, and the output is the corresponding solution field at a later time or steady state. The theoretical analysis tracks the N×N covariance matrix of pre-activations across spatial positions, revealing that only periodic (shift-invariant) spatial correlations with frequencies below the mode-truncation cutoff K are propagated through layers.

## Method

The paper establishes a mean-field theory for the FNO by deriving an iterated map C for the spatial covariance of pre-activations in a simplified 1D FNO, then showing that fixed points of the fully connected network (DCN) iterated map (with correlation c*=1) are also fixed points of the FNO map. Linearizing the Jacobian around this fixed point reveals a K-dimensional eigenspace (rather than the N(N−1)/2 dimensional space of DCN), with eigenvalues χ for the zero-frequency mode and χ_{c*} for periodic modes, where mode truncation modifies the zero-frequency convergence rate from χ_{q*} to a blended χ. The ordered-chaos phase transition is characterized by whether χ and χ_{c*} are below or above 1, and the gradient covariance during backpropagation scales as χ_{c*}^{L−ℓ}, directly linking the expressivity phase to gradient vanishing (ordered) or explosion (chaotic). This yields a practical initialization criterion: setting σ² so that χ_{c*}≈1 places the network at the edge of chaos, which for ReLU reduces to the FNO analogue of He initialization (σ²=2, zero bias).

## Results

The paper establishes that the FNO's expressivity is governed by an ordered-chaos phase transition, with the periodic spatial correlation components decaying/growing at rate χ_{c*} (identical to DCN and CNN) and the zero-frequency component at a modified rate χ that depends on the mode truncation parameter K. Empirically, across all four PDE benchmarks (advection, Burgers', Darcy Flow, Navier-Stokes), the training loss heatmaps (Figs. 3–4) show that as depth L increases from 4 to 32, the acceptable range of initial σ² narrows sharply, and σ² ≈ 2 (the edge-of-chaos / He initialization) is required for stable training. For σ² < 2 the gradient norm vanishes exponentially with depth, while for σ² > 2 it explodes, confirming the theoretical prediction from Theorem 3.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper and does not compare the FNO against alternative architectures or methods. Instead, the experiments compare different initialization parameters (σ² ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0}) and network depths (L ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}) for both the simplified FNO (Tanh and ReLU activations) and the original FNO (ReLU) across four PDE datasets. The key empirical finding is consistent across all settings: at shallow depths (L=4), a wide range of σ² values yields acceptable training loss, but at L=32 only σ² ≈ 2 produces stable convergence, while σ² = 0.1 or 0.5 leads to vanishing gradients and σ² = 3.0 or 4.0 leads to exploding gradients. The original FNO with the DC module (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3e–h) exhibits the same initialization sensitivity as the simplified FNO, validating that the mean-field theory transfers to the full architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field theory framework established for densely connected networks (Poole et al., 2016; Schoenholz et al., 2016) and CNNs (Xiao et al., 2018) to the Fourier Neural Operator architecture (Li et al., 2020c), which had previously lacked any theoretical analysis of its expressivity and trainability. The key conceptual departure is showing that while FNO shares the same fixed-point structure and edge-of-chaos initialization condition as DCN/CNN, mode truncation introduces FNO-specific eigenspace geometry: the deviation space is K-dimensional (at most N/2+1) rather than the full N(N-1)/2 of DCN, and the zero-frequency convergence rate χ differs from χ_{q*}. The paper also provides a theoretical explanation for the training instability in deep FNOs empirically reported by Tran et al. (2022), connecting it to the ordered/chaotic phase structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FNO is one of the most widely deployed architectures for PDE surrogate modeling, yet practitioners had no principled guidance on initialization for deep networks beyond trial-and-error. This paper provides that guidance: σ² ≈ 2 with zero bias for ReLU (analogous to He initialization) places the network at the edge of chaos, and the acceptable σ² range narrows as depth increases. The mode-truncation effect on the eigenspace is a genuinely FNO-specific finding that has no counterpart in DCN or CNN theory. A key limitation is that the analysis is restricted to initialization and does not address the full optimization trajectory; open questions include extending the theory to FNO with skip connections, normalization layers, and dynamical isometry conditions.
