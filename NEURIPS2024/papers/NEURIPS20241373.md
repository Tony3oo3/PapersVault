## Problem

Existing Learngene methods for initializing variable-sized Vision Transformers rely on manually designed transformation mechanisms (e.g., stacking randomly-initialized layers) that lack a learnable component, and they only transform the compact learngene along the depth dimension, ignoring the width dimension. This constrains both the quality of initialization and the flexibility of the resulting model family.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241373/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compact learngene module (15M parameters, 6 heads, 8 layers) and a set of learnable transformation matrices (width matrices F and depth matrix G) that are trained once via distillation from a large ancestry model (LeViT-384). Given a target model size specification (e.g., 12 heads × 12 layers), specific rows of the trained matrices are selected via continuous or magnitude-wise strategies to produce initialized Des-Net weights. The output is a fully initialized ViT of arbitrary width and depth that can be fine-tuned on downstream classification or segmentation tasks without further distillation.

## Method

LeTs introduces a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, an auxiliary model (Aux-Net) is constructed by applying width transformation matrices F (in-dimension F^in and out-dimension F^out, which expand each learngene layer via concatenation) and a depth transformation matrix G (which linearly combines width-transformed layers within groups to form new layers) to the compact learngene, then trained via prediction-based distillation from the ancestry model so that F and G capture structural knowledge about adding neurons and layers. In stage 2, specific parameters are selected from the well-trained matrices—using continuous row selection, top-n L1/L2-norm magnitude selection for width, and sequential group selection for depth—to construct target transformation matrices T^des that adaptively transform the learngene into variable-sized Des-Nets. The 'first-width-then-depth' ordering keeps the transferred parameter footprint compact (only ~37.7M total for the learngene plus matrices), and parameter sharing across model components (MHA, MLP, LN) further reduces redundancy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, LeTs-initialized Des-Nets outperform 100-epoch from-scratch training after only 1 epoch of fine-tuning, reducing total training cost by ~7× across 24 model variants. In direct evaluation without any tuning, LeTs achieves 80.1% Top-1 on Des-H12-L12 (86.6M), surpassing TLEG by 3.5% and Scratch by 0.5%. On downstream CIFAR-100, LeTs (Des-H12-L12) reaches 90.0% Top-1, outperforming Pre-Fin by 1.5% while reducing initialization parameters by ~20× (37.7M vs. 758M). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, LeTs(0ep) with Des-H6-L12 backbone outperforms Pre-Fin by 3.4% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeTs consistently outperforms all five Learngene and expansion baselines. Against TLEG [26], LeTs gains 3.0–4.3% Top-1 in direct evaluation on ImageNet-1K (e.g., 80.1% vs. 76.6% on Des-H12-L12) and 1.3–1.7% after 5-epoch fine-tuning across model sizes. Against SWS [41], LeTs (5ep) surpasses SWS (10ep) by 1.2–3.7% on ImageNet-1K, indicating that learnable transformation matrices provide substantially better initialization than stage-wise weight sharing. Against Grad-LG [24], the gap is dramatic: Grad-LG scores near-random (0.1%) in direct evaluation and 70.8% on CIFAR-100, while LeTs reaches 80.1% and 90.0% respectively. Against IMwLM [39] (larger-model initialization), LeTs outperforms by 2.5–32% in direct evaluation depending on model size, with the largest gap on Des-H12-L12 (80.1% vs. 48.1%). Against MatFormer [40], LeTs demonstrates superior initialization efficiency in Figure 5, requiring fewer parameters to initialize the same range of model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene paradigm (Wang et al., 2022/2023), which learns a compact module from a large pretrained model and transforms it to initialize variable-sized descendants. Prior Learngene methods (Grad-LG, TLEG, SWS, LearngenePool) relied on manually-designed, depth-only transformation mechanisms, while network expansion work (Net2Net, LiGO, Bert2Bert) focused on incremental training acceleration rather than flexible initialization from a single compact source. LeTs departs from both lines by introducing learnable transformation matrices that operate along both width and depth dimensions, replacing hand-crafted rules with trained structural knowledge, and by adding a parameter selection stage to adapt the transformation to arbitrary target sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LeTs reduces initialization parameters by ~20× and total training costs by ~7× compared to pre-training each model size individually, while matching or exceeding from-scratch quality after minimal fine-tuning. This makes variable-sized model deployment substantially more accessible for resource-constrained settings. However, the approach still requires a well-trained ancestry model, uses only prediction-based distillation (acknowledged as a limitation), and has been validated exclusively on ViT-based architectures. Open questions include whether the learnable transformation generalizes to non-ViT backbones, language models, or diffusion models, and whether more advanced distillation objectives would further improve the quality of the learned transformation matrices.
