## Problem

It was an open question (raised in ICM 2022 and by Koehler–Mossel) whether the Kesten-Stigum threshold dλ²=1 is sharp for low-degree polynomial reconstruction in broadcasting on trees. Prior work only established polynomial hardness in the special case λ=0, leaving the general sub-KS regime unresolved. This paper proves that below the KS bound, any polynomial of O(log N) degree (where N is the number of leaves) has vanishing correlation with the root, for arbitrary Markov chains on trees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rooted tree T of depth ℓ whose degree is dominated by d with growth parameter R, a q×q ergodic transition matrix M with second eigenvalue λ satisfying dλ²<1, and a function f of the leaf variables X_L whose Efron-Stein degree (or more generally, fractal capacity) is at most c·ℓ/(1+log R). It produces an upper bound of the form Var(E[f(X_L)|X_ρ]) ≤ (max{dλ²,λ})^{ℓ/4} · Var(f(X_L)), which implies the correlation between f and any function of the root decays exponentially in ℓ. The result holds uniformly over all such trees and channels, not just regular d-ary trees.

## Method

The proof is an induction on a newly introduced notion called fractal capacity, which generalizes Efron-Stein degree by exploiting the tree's branch structure: a function has fractal capacity ≤ k if it is a sum of functions each depending on a subset of leaves whose nearest common ancestor is at height at most k. The inductive step shows that if A_k-polynomials satisfy a second-moment decay bound with penalty constant h*_k, then A_{k+1}-polynomials satisfy the same bound with penalty h*_k + C. The key technical ingredient is decomposing an A_{k+1}-polynomial into products of A_k-polynomials on sibling subtrees of a pivot vertex, then bounding the resulting covariance using the Markov property (conditional independence given the parent) and the variance decay of the underlying Markov chain. A critical challenge is handling the case where transition matrix entries can be zero, which breaks naive independence arguments; this is resolved via a careful Markov-chain analysis (Proposition C.3) showing that conditional variance is bounded below by a constant times unconditional variance.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.6) establishes that below the Kesten-Stigum threshold dλ² < 1, for any function f(x_L) of Efron-Stein degree ≤ c·ℓ/(1+log R), the variance of the conditional expectation satisfies Var(E[f(X_L)|X_ρ]) ≤ (max{dλ², λ})^(ℓ/4) · Var(f(X_L)), where ℓ is the tree depth. Equivalently (Corollary 1.8), the correlation between any such f and any function of the root decays as (max{dλ², λ})^(ℓ/8). For a d-ary tree with N = d^ℓ leaves, this means all O(log N)-degree polynomials have exponentially vanishing correlation with the root below the KS bound, confirming the conjecture posed in the ICM 2022 talk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The key prior result it extends is Koehler and Mossel (NeurIPS 2022) [23], which proved polynomial hardness only in the special case λ = 0 (requiring degree N^Ω(1) polynomials). The present work generalizes this to the full regime dλ² < 1, showing that O(log N)-degree polynomials suffice to capture the hardness. The result also complements the NC1-completeness lower bound of Moitra, Mossel, and Sandon, which showed that estimating the root better than random is NC1-complete for certain chains. The paper's Theorem 1.16 further shows the bound is optimal in the fractal-capacity sense: correlation decay persists up to fractal capacity proportional to ℓ, while all functions of the leaves have fractal capacity at most ℓ+1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-degree polynomial hardness framework from Koehler and Mossel [23], who proved that for the special case λ=0, even polynomials of degree N^c cannot correlate with the root, to the full regime below the Kesten-Stigum threshold (dλ² < 1) for arbitrary Markov chains. The proof introduces a novel notion of 'fractal capacity' that generalizes Efron-Stein degree, enabling a recursive decomposition argument that works in the highly correlated (non-product-measure) setting of tree broadcasting. The result also connects to the broader low-degree polynomial program [19, 18, 25, 4, 15] for predicting computational-to-statistical gaps, and to the Kesten-Stigum threshold [22] that governs reconstruction in stochastic block models and phylogenetic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a central open question posed by Koehler and Mossel and highlighted in the ICM 2022 survey: whether the Kesten-Stigum threshold is sharp for low-degree polynomial reconstruction in the general (non-linear) regime. The result is notable as one of the first low-degree lower bounds proved in a setting that is not based on or reducible to a product measure, opening the door to similar hardness results for other correlated inference problems. A key limitation is that the bound applies to O(log N)-degree polynomials (or fractal capacity O(ℓ)), leaving open whether higher-degree polynomials or other algorithmic classes (e.g., Belief Propagation, which is known to be Bayes-optimal) can succeed below KS in channels where information-theoretic recovery is possible. The connection to the stochastic block model and phylogenetic reconstruction suggests broader implications for understanding algorithmic phase transitions in structured inference problems.
