key_contribution: InstructMol, a semi-supervised learning framework that introduces an auxiliary instructor model to predict pseudo-label reliability and applies signed confidence-weighted loss reweighting (2p−1) to the target molecular model, enabling robust utilization of large-scale unlabeled molecular data for both classification and regression property prediction without requiring domain transfer between pretraining and fine-tuning stages.
limitations: The combination of InstructMol with self-supervised pretraining relies on existing methodologies (GEM, Uni-Mol, GROVER) rather than a co-designed pretraining objective, and the authors explicitly note that developing a more tightly integrated self-supervised learning algorithm better aligned with InstructMol remains future work. Additionally, the method has been validated primarily on 2D molecular graphs at MoleculeNet scale (thousands of labeled samples), and scalability to 3D structure prediction or binding affinity tasks with more severe label noise is not explored.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that OOD improvements (up to ~10% on GOOD-PCBA) substantially exceed in-domain gains, suggesting pseudo-labeling with confidence weighting specifically helps distributional shift rather than just in-sample fitting. The extreme low-data LogP result (RMSE 0.63 with only 14 training molecules vs. 5.63 without InstructMol) is striking, though it is a single-task demonstration. Overall, the conceptual contribution—a separate critic model for pseudo-label reliability enabling signed loss weighting—is a reasonable and well-motivated extension rather than a paradigm shift.

## Problem

Molecular property prediction is severely limited by the scarcity of labeled data, as wet-lab annotation is prohibitively expensive. Existing semi-supervised approaches either suffer from a distributional gap between pretraining and fine-tuning stages, or rely on naive pseudo-labeling that accumulates errors through confirmation bias. Critically, prior confidence-based pseudo-label selection methods (e.g., UPS) are restricted to classification tasks and cannot handle the regression problems that dominate biochemical property prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D molecular graphs (converted from SMILES via RDKit) in two tiers: a small labeled set (typically 600–8,500 molecules) and a large unlabeled set (up to 1M molecules from ZINC15). The target model f outputs a continuous or discrete molecular property (e.g., LogP, Ki, toxicity scores), while the instructor model g outputs a scalar confidence score p_i ∈ [0,1] per sample. Unusually, g's input includes not just the molecule and its label but also the target model's loss value H_f(f(x), y) as an explicit feature, coupling the critic to the main prediction task's difficulty.

## Method

InstructMol introduces an auxiliary instructor model g that acts as a binary classifier distinguishing real labels from pseudo-labels, trained with focal loss to handle the extreme class imbalance (M ≫ N). The key mechanism is a cost-sensitive reweighting of the target model's loss: for each pseudo-labeled sample, the loss is multiplied by (2p_j − 1), so the instructor's trust (p→1) amplifies the learning signal while distrust (p→0) inverts it, actively pushing the target model away from erroneous pseudo-labels. Pseudo-labels are generated by the target model itself and refreshed every k epochs with an adaptive decay schedule (k halves until k_min=3). The instructor is pretrained on pseudo-labels from a supervised target model before joint SSL training begins, ensuring stable initialization. This design disentangles confidence estimation from property prediction, avoiding the miscalibration problems inherent in using the target model's own output as a reliability indicator.

## Results

On the six MoleculeNet classification benchmarks, GEM+InstructMol achieves ROC-AUC of 95.8% (BBBP), 93.2% (BACE), 97.8% (ClinTox), 83.7% (Tox21), 74.2% (ToxCast), and 67.0% (SIDER), outperforming the GEM-only pretraining baseline by 0.1–0.7 percentage points across all tasks. For the three regression tasks, the method attains RMSE of 1.029 (ESOL), 0.681 (FreeSolv), and 0.681 (Lipo), matching or exceeding the best supervised baselines. The gains are most pronounced on the harder tasks (ClinTox, ToxCast, SIDER), where the instructor model's confidence weighting provides the largest margin over unweighted pseudo-labeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructMol consistently outperforms all 24 baselines across every benchmark. Against the GEM-only pretraining baseline (the closest competitor), the instructor model adds 0.1–0.7% ROC-AUC, with the largest gains on the hardest tasks (ClinTox: +0.1%, ToxCast: +0.5%, SIDER: +0.7%). Compared to the best supervised method (D-MPNN), InstructMol improves by 3.9% on BBBP, 8.0% on BACE, and 8.1% on ClinTox, demonstrating that the confidence-weighted pseudo-labeling strategy extracts substantially more signal from unlabeled data than direct supervision alone. The method also surpasses the other pretraining approaches (GROVER, PretrainGNN, N-Gram variants) by 1.8–3.5% on average, confirming that the instructor's role in filtering unreliable pseudo-labels is critical for robustness. On the regression tasks, InstructMol matches the best supervised baselines on ESOL and FreeSolv while slightly exceeding them on Lipo, indicating no degradation in performance when the task shifts from classification to continuous prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructMol extends the pseudo-labeling line of semi-supervised learning (self-training, UPS, curriculum labeling) by introducing a separate instructor model that predicts pseudo-label reliability, decoupling confidence estimation from the target model's property prediction. This departure is motivated by the observation that prior proxy-labeling methods (especially UPS) rely on the target model's own output as a confidence signal, which fails for regression tasks and suffers from poor calibration. The method also positions itself as complementary to self-supervised pretraining (GEM, GROVER, Uni-Mol) rather than a replacement, and its cost-sensitive loss design (2p−1 weighting) generalizes beyond the binary select/discard paradigm of earlier proxy-labeling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in molecular ML—extreme label scarcity—by showing that a lightweight instructor model can make pseudo-labeling safe for both classification and regression, and that this approach improves OOD generalization more than in-domain accuracy, suggesting pseudo-labels inject genuinely new structural information. The real-world validation on 9 patent molecules (ZA202303678A) adds practical credibility. Open questions include whether the instructor model's added complexity is justified at scale, whether a more tightly integrated self-supervised objective (rather than bolting InstructMol onto GEM) could yield further gains, and whether the approach transfers to 3D structure prediction or binding affinity tasks where label noise is even more severe.
