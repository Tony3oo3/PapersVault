## Problem

GNNs struggle to generalize across datasets and tasks without task-specific fine-tuning, and while LLMs offer strong zero-shot reasoning, they cannot effectively leverage graph structural information on their own. Existing GNN-LLM combinations either require extensive fine-tuning of the LLM or produce unstable, non-transferable graph representations, leaving a gap in truly zero-shot, cross-dataset and cross-task graph prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph (adjacency matrix plus BERT-encoded node titles as features) and a unified instruction template that includes the task description and the full set of candidate answers. Graph information is injected as a fixed number K of learned token embeddings (graph tokens) that replace a single placeholder in the instruction, regardless of task level (node, edge, or graph). The LLM outputs a text response parsed into a class label (node classification) or a binary link prediction (edge-level), with the LLM parameters remaining frozen throughout.

## Method

TEA-GLM pretrains a GraphSAGE encoder with a combined contrastive objective: instance-wise contrastive learning over two augmented graph views (edge removal and feature masking) plus a novel feature-wise contrastive loss that aligns GNN representation columns with PCA principal components of the LLM's token embeddings, bridging the semantic gap between graph and text spaces. A lightweight linear projector then maps each node (or pooled edge) representation to K fixed graph token embeddings, which are inserted into a unified instruction that explicitly lists candidate answers to encourage reasoning over memorization. Because the GNN representations are already aligned to the LLM's embedding geometry, the projector trains in a single epoch without touching the LLM, and the same instruction format transfers across node-level and edge-level tasks on unseen datasets.

## Results

On cross-dataset zero-shot node classification, TEA-GLM achieves the best accuracy on 5 out of 6 unseen datasets: Pubmed (84.8%), Cora (20.2%), History (52.8%), Photo (49.7%), and Sports (40.4%), with a near-tie on Children (27.1% vs. Vicuna-7B's 27.0%). On cross-task zero-shot link prediction (trained on node classification, evaluated on link prediction), TEA-GLM achieves the best AUC on 7 out of 8 datasets, with highlights of 68.9% on Pubmed and 65.7% on Arxiv. The closest LLM-based competitor, LLaGA, trails by 5.5 points on Pubmed (79.3% vs. 84.8%) and by a large margin on e-commerce datasets (e.g., 14.6% vs. 52.8% on History).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEA-GLM outperforms all GNN-as-predictor baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, DGI, GKD, GLNN, NodeFormer, DIFFormer) by large margins on every unseen dataset; for example, on History it reaches 52.8% versus GLNN's 28.3% and DIFFormer's 27.5%. Among LLM-as-predictor methods, TEA-GLM beats the closest competitor LLaGA on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest gap on History (52.8% vs. 14.6%) and Photo (49.7% vs. 27.6%), while LLaGA edges ahead only on Sports (35.2% vs. 40.4%—actually TEA-GLM still wins). TEA-GLM also surpasses the text-only Vicuna-7B-v1.5 on all datasets except Children (27.1% vs. 27.0%, essentially tied), confirming that graph token embeddings carry transferable structural information beyond node text. GraphGPT (reported only on citation datasets) underperforms both Vicuna-7B and TEA-GLM, with its CoT variant dropping to 52.1% on Pubmed. OFA, a cross-domain GNN framework, shows strong transfer on topic-related datasets (Cora: 13.0%) but degrades sharply on e-commerce (History: 5.2%), whereas TEA-GLM maintains consistent gains across both domains. On cross-task link prediction (Table 2), TEA-GLM achieves the best AUC on 7/8 datasets, beating GraphGPT-std on Pubmed (68.9% vs. 50.1%) and LLaGA on all e-commerce datasets (e.g., 57.1% vs. 42.2% on Children).

## Relation to Prior Work

TEA-GLM sits at the intersection of graph contrastive pretraining (building on Zhu et al.'s view-generation strategies for instance-wise contrastive learning) and the emerging paradigm of using LLMs as graph predictors. It departs from GraphGPT, which requires two-stage fine-tuning of the LLM, and LLaGA, which bypasses GNNs entirely, by instead pre-aligning GNN representations to the LLM's token embedding space via PCA before training a lightweight linear projector. The closest conceptual relative is TEST, which aligns time-series representations with selected LLM token embeddings, but TEA-GLM generalizes this to graphs and uses the full PCA subspace rather than a fixed set of text prototypes. The unified instruction design across node-, edge-, and graph-level tasks extends the instruction-tuning paradigm from NLP to structured data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a computationally efficient path to zero-shot graph learning: no LLM fine-tuning is required, only a linear projector trained on a single dataset, making it practical for settings where labeled data is scarce across multiple domains. The ablation revealing that feature-wise contrastive alignment slightly hurts seen-dataset performance while substantially improving unseen-dataset and cross-task transfer highlights a fundamental tension between in-distribution fitting and out-of-distribution generalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to graph-level tasks (acknowledged but untested), whether it generalizes beyond text-attributed graphs to purely structural or heterogeneous graphs, and whether the PCA alignment strategy transfers to other LLM architectures or larger model scales.
