## Problem

Existing regularized adaptive methods for training structured neural networks (e.g., ProxGen, ProxSSI) lack theoretical guarantees that the output model actually attains the ideal structure induced by the regularizer at the stationary point. The only method with such manifold identification guarantees, RMDA, is non-adaptive and thus underperforms on modern architectures like transformers and LSTMs. This paper bridges the gap by proposing the first regularized adaptive method with both convergence and finite-iteration structure identification guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised training data (RGB images, text corpora, or audio waveforms), a neural network architecture, and a nonsmooth regularizer (group LASSO for channel/input-wise sparsity, or nuclear norm for low-rank structure). It outputs a trained neural network whose parameters are guaranteed to lie on the active manifold of the regularizer after finitely many iterations, yielding a model with locally optimal structured sparsity or low-rank structure. Unlike unregularized adaptive methods where the subproblem has a closed-form coordinate-wise solution, RAMDA's subproblem combines a diagonal preconditioner with a nonsmooth regularizer and must be solved approximately via a few proximal gradient steps.

## Method

RAMDA is a double-dual averaging algorithm that updates both the stochastic gradient estimator and the diagonal preconditioner via weighted historical sums (with step size s_t = η_t√t), achieving variance reduction in both components—essential for manifold identification. The preconditioner uses the cubic root of accumulated squared gradient norms (MADGRAD-style) for adaptiveness, and the update direction is obtained by approximately solving a subproblem involving the regularizer, a linear term, and a quadratic proximal term. A checkable subgradient-norm-based inexactness condition (min‖s‖ ≤ ε_t over the subdifferential, plus an objective decrease requirement) governs the approximate subproblem solution, and a proximal gradient solver with early stopping attains this condition in O(log(1/ε_t)) iterations for convex regularizers. The iterate update averages the previous iterate with the subproblem solution using a momentum coefficient c_t, and the paper proves via partial smoothness and prox-regularity theory that iterates eventually lie on the active manifold, identifying the locally optimal structure.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet50, RAMDA achieves 74.53% top-1 accuracy and 29.19% weighted group sparsity, simultaneously outperforming all baselines on both metrics. On Transformer-XL (WikiText-103), RAMDA attains 26.97 validation perplexity with 36.2% group sparsity, and on Tacotron2 (LJSpeech), it reaches 0.44 validation loss with 52.9% group sparsity. The PG subproblem solver is approximately 3× faster than ProxSSI's Newton-Raphson solver while producing comparable model quality, making regularized adaptive methods practically feasible at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMDA outperforms RMDA [16] on all three large-scale tasks: on ImageNet it gains +0.06% accuracy and +3.99% sparsity; on Transformer-XL it achieves 0.13 lower perplexity and +0.2% sparsity; on Tacotron2 it reaches 0.02 lower loss and +7.0% sparsity. Against ProxGen [51], RAMDA consistently wins on both prediction quality and sparsity (e.g., +0.36% accuracy and +8.9% sparsity on ImageNet; +0.52 lower perplexity and +5.7% sparsity on Transformer-XL). ProxSGD [49] is the weakest structured baseline, trailing RAMDA by 1.03% accuracy and 11.65% sparsity on ImageNet. ProxSSI [9] achieves similar model quality to ProxGen but requires ~3× more time per epoch, so it is excluded from the main comparisons. The unregularized baselines (Adam, AdamW, MSGD) achieve the best raw prediction performance but provide no structured sparsity, confirming the trade-off that RAMDA navigates by retaining most of the accuracy while inducing substantial structure. On smaller datasets (CIFAR-10/100), RAMDA can underperform RMDA on sparsity (e.g., 65.19% vs. 80.88% on VGG19/CIFAR-100), a limitation the authors acknowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMDA sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: the non-adaptive regularized dual averaging method RMDA [16], which provides finite-iteration manifold identification guarantees but lacks adaptiveness, and the adaptive dual averaging method MADGRAD [7], which achieves strong empirical performance on modern architectures but has no regularization or structure guarantees. The paper generalizes MADGRAD to the regularized setting while inheriting RMDA's variance-reduction mechanism for structure identification, and introduces a proximal gradient subproblem solver that makes the otherwise intractable regularized adaptive subproblem practically solvable. It also extends the convergence theory of the ProxGen framework [51] to accommodate inexact subproblem solutions without requiring local smoothness of the regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regularized adaptive method with provable structure identification guarantees, closing a gap that made adaptive optimizers (which dominate modern deep learning practice) incompatible with theoretical structure guarantees. The efficient PG subproblem solver is broadly useful, reducing training time by ~3× compared to ProxSSI's Newton-Raphson approach while maintaining model quality, thereby making the entire class of regularized adaptive methods practically feasible. Limitations include: convergence to a specific stationary point requires additional regularity conditions (e.g., KL) left as future work; on smaller-scale problems (CIFAR-10/100), RAMDA can underperform the non-adaptive RMDA; and the structure guarantees are conditional on the event of convergence to a particular point rather than unconditional.
