## Problem

Existing methods for sampling from interventional distributions in causal inference either require access to full probability tables (intractable for high-dimensional variables like images), rely on joint GAN training that struggles with convergence when multiple high-dimensional variables are present, or assume no latent confounders. When a causal graph contains both high-dimensional variables and unobserved confounders (semi-Markovian models), no prior work can effectively sample from arbitrary identifiable interventional or conditional interventional distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a fully specified acyclic directed mixed graph (ADMG) encoding observed variables and latent confounders, an observational dataset D ~ P(V), an intervention specification do(x), and a causal query (e.g., P(y|do(x)) or P(y|do(x),z)). It produces a sampling network — a DAG of trained conditional generative models (typically classifier-free diffusion models) — from which high-dimensional interventional samples are generated via ancestral sampling. For conditional queries, the network first produces joint samples P_x(y,z), after which an additional conditional model M_Y(X,Z) is trained on those samples to yield P_x(y|z). The intervened values are fixed as inputs to the network at sampling time, and the network topology is determined by the recursive factorization of the query.

## Method

ID-GEN mirrors the seven-step recursive structure of the ID identification algorithm but replaces probability-table operations with three new subroutines: ConditionalGMs (trains a conditional generative model, e.g., a classifier-free diffusion model, for each conditional distribution in a factorization), MergeNetwork (wires sub-networks from c-component factorization into a single acyclic sampling network by matching input-output nodes), and Update (applies a partial intervention do(X_Z) to generate a new interventional training dataset for subsequent recursive calls). At step 4, the query is decomposed into c-factors via c-component factorization, and each factor is solved recursively; at step 7, the intervention set is partitioned so that variables outside the current c-component are intervened on first to update the dataset. The key insight that avoids the cyclic-dependency deadlock of naive step-by-step sampling is that ID-GEN trains all conditional models independently (considering all possible input values) and only connects them into a single network at the end, enabling consistent ancestral sampling. The paper proves soundness and completeness: under semi-Markovian assumptions with access to the ADMG, ID-GEN samples from any identifiable P_x(y) or P_x(y|z) using only feedforward conditional models.

## Results

On the Colored MNIST napkin graph, ID-GEN achieves a total variation distance (TVD) of 0.25 from the true interventional color distribution P(y|do(x)), substantially outperforming the conditional diffusion baseline (TVD 0.54) and the DCM diffusion-based causal model (TVD 0.58). ID-GEN also attains the lowest FID scores (25.66 for digit-3 intervention, 22.67 for digit-5 intervention), indicating the highest image quality among all methods. On CelebA, ID-GEN reveals that EGSDE introduces non-causal attributes (e.g., 'Attractive' at 37.61%, 'Young' at 24.76%) with high probability due to spurious training correlations, while StarGAN introduces new attributes at ≤30% probability even for causal ones. On MIMIC-CXR, ID-GEN produces interpretable attribute-conditioned X-ray generations with low TVD (≈0.05) for trained conditional models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-GEN is compared against three baselines on the Colored MNIST experiment. The conditional diffusion model (Cond), which simply samples P(y|x) without accounting for the unobserved confounder U_color, shows a strong bias toward Green (0.45) and Magenta (0.02), yielding TVD 0.54. The DCM algorithm [11] (Chao et al.), which uses diffusion models for interventional sampling but assumes no latent confounders, also fails to remove the color bias (TVD 0.58) and produces less diverse digit structures (higher FID). The NCM algorithm [58] (Xia et al.), which uses jointly-trained GANs to handle confounders, generates visibly blended/incorrect colors (e.g., green+red mixtures) and is excluded from the TVD comparison. ID-GEN wins on both TVD (0.25 vs. 0.54/0.58) and FID (25.66/22.67, lowest), demonstrating that its recursive c-factorization with modular diffusion models effectively removes confounding bias while maintaining image quality. On MIMIC-CXR, the Roentgen baseline [10] (Chambon et al.) generates plausible X-rays but ignores causal attribute dependencies and is susceptible to location-based confounding; ID-GEN makes the generation interpretable and domain-invariant by explicitly intervening on the report variable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Shpitser and Pearl's recursive ID algorithm for causal identification and Tian and Pearl's c-component factorization, repurposing their recursive structure to guide the training of conditional generative models rather than manipulating probability tables. It departs from prior neural causal approaches (Xia et al., Kocaoglu et al., Rahman and Kocaoglu) that relied on joint GAN training or modular architectures with convergence difficulties, and from diffusion-based causal models (Chao et al.) that assumed no latent confounders. The key conceptual departure is proving that a collection of independently trained feed-forward conditional models, wired into a 'sampling network' following the ID recursion, is sufficient to sample from any identifiable interventional distribution in semi-Markovian SCMs with unobserved confounders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sufficiency theorem (Theorem 3.2) resolves a practical gap: researchers studying high-dimensional causal effects (medical imaging, face attributes) with unobserved confounders previously had no general-purpose sampling method that leverages modern conditional generative architectures like diffusion models. The MIMIC-CXR and CelebA experiments demonstrate concrete utility for domain-invariant generation and spurious-correlation auditing in deployed models. Open questions include extending to soft/imperfect interventions, relaxing the requirement for a fully specified ADMG (e.g., to PAGs), and incorporating pruning to avoid unnecessary model training when the ID algorithm acts on variables not present in the final expression.
