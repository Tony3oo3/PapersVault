## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods (GradDiff, NPO, PO) update all model weights uniformly, yet no principled mechanism exists to identify which specific weights are most influential for the unlearning task while preserving utility. Weight pruning heuristics like Wanda or magnitude-based selection ignore the unlearning objective entirely, and prior weight-localization work for LLM editing cannot precisely predict influential layers for unlearning. The paper asks: how can we attribute weight-level influence in LLM unlearning to enable targeted, modular forgetting?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA2-7B-chat, Zephyr-7B-beta), a forget set D_f specifying data to unlearn, and a retain set D_r for utility preservation. It first computes a binary weight attribution mask m_S offline using only first-order gradients of the forget and retain losses at the pre-trained weights (≈4 minutes for a 7B model), then applies the mask during unlearning so that only the selected subset (typically 80–95% of weights) is updated while the remainder stays frozen at their pre-trained values. The mask is algorithm-agnostic and can be paired with any unlearning objective (GradDiff, NPO, or PO).

## Method

WAGLE formulates weight attribution as a bi-level optimization (BLO) problem: the upper level measures the unlearning sensitivity score (change in forget loss under a weight perturbation ε⊙θ), while the lower level enforces that the perturbed model still minimizes the retain loss. Applying the implicit function theorem to the lower-level stationarity condition and assuming a diagonal Hessian (∇_{θ,θ}ℓ_r ≈ γI), the paper derives a closed-form per-weight attribution score S_i ∝ θ_o[i]·∇ℓ_f[i] − (1/γ)·∇ℓ_r[i]·∇ℓ_f[i], where the first term captures forget-loss sensitivity (analogous to SNIP pruning) and the second corrects for utility-retention effects. Weights are ranked by |S_i| and the top fraction is selected into the mask m_S. During unlearning, only m_S⊙θ is optimized while (1−m_S)⊙θ_o remains fixed, making the approach a drop-in modifier for any existing unlearning algorithm. The Hessian diagonal parameter γ is tuned per task: small (10⁻⁶) when the retain set closely mirrors the training distribution (TOFU, WMDP), and large (10⁴–10⁶) when it does not (WHP, DETOX).

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark (LLaMA2-7B-chat), WAGLE consistently improves unlearning efficacy (UE) over dense-model baselines across all three unlearning methods: NPO+Ours achieves UE Avg. 0.9599 vs. 0.9581 (Dense) while substantially improving utility (UT Avg. 0.6189 vs. 0.5616); PO+Ours reaches UE Avg. 0.7424 vs. 0.7229 with UT Avg. 0.8940 vs. 0.8894. On WMDP (Zephyr-7B-beta), NPO+Ours achieves UE Avg. 0.7028 with MMLU 0.5033, outperforming Dense (UE 0.6867, MMLU 0.3754) by a large margin on utility. On WHP, PO+Ours reduces Seen Rouge-L to 0.0427 (vs. 0.0737 Dense) and Unseen Rouge-L to 0.0481 (vs. 0.0738), while on DETOX it lowers toxic scores to 0.0537/0.0667 (vs. 0.0657/0.0918 Dense).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAGLE outperforms all weight-selection baselines (Random, Magnitude, Wanda, LoRA) on unlearning efficacy across every unlearning method and task. Against the dense-model baselines, WAGLE's gains are most pronounced with NPO on TOFU, where UT Avg. improves from 0.5616 to 0.6189 (+5.7 pp) while UE Avg. also rises (0.9581→0.9599), and on WMDP where MMLU utility jumps from 0.3754 to 0.5033 (+12.8 pp) for NPO and from 0.4459 to 0.5530 (+10.7 pp) for GradDiff. Compared to Wanda-based pruning, WAGLE achieves substantially higher UE (e.g., NPO: 0.9599 vs. 0.3294 on TOFU; GradDiff on WMDP: 0.6871 vs. 0.5311) while maintaining competitive utility. Against LoRA, WAGLE matches or exceeds UE (NPO: 0.9599 vs. 0.9403; PO: 0.7424 vs. 0.7222) and improves UT on TOFU (NPO: 0.6189 vs. 0.6798 is an exception where LoRA retains more utility, but WAGLE wins on PO: 0.8940 vs. 0.8671). On WHP and DETOX, PO+WAGLE achieves the lowest Rouge-L (0.0427/0.0481) and toxic scores (0.0537/0.0667) among all methods, confirming consistent gains in unlearning efficacy with preserved utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bilevel optimization (BLO) framework for model pruning [73], repurposing its diagonal-Hessian simplification for bilinear parameter couplings to derive a closed-form weight attribution score for LLM unlearning. It departs from prior weight-localization work for LLMs (causal tracing [29,30], gradient saliency [31,32]) by providing a principled, weight-level score that jointly encodes both the forget-loss sensitivity (reducing to the SNIP pruning score) and a utility-retention correction term, rather than relying on single-objective heuristics. The resulting WAGLE framework is algorithm-agnostic, plugging into existing unlearning methods (GradDiff, NPO, PO) without modifying their optimization procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WAGLE provides the first principled, closed-form weight attribution for LLM unlearning, revealing a model-level 'fingerprint' (early-to-mid layers, sa.o/sa.v, MLP weights) that is distinct from utility-focused pruning and enabling modular unlearning that updates only a subset of weights. Key open questions remain: the optimal sparsity ratio is task- and method-dependent (requiring per-task tuning), the Hessian diagonal parameter γ lacks a reliable automatic estimator when the retain set diverges from the training distribution, and it is unclear whether the identified fingerprint generalizes across model scales or unlearning scopes. The finding that standard pruning (Wanda) actively degrades unlearning efficacy compared to dense models underscores that utility and unlearning objectives are misaligned at the weight level.
