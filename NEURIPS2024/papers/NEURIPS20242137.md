## Problem

Existing generative models for data on Riemannian manifolds (Riemannian diffusion, flow matching, Moser flows) require solving ODEs or SDEs at inference, necessitating hundreds to thousands of function evaluations per sample. Single-step alternatives exist but are specialized to particular manifolds (e.g., SO(3), spheres, tori), making them impractical to adapt to new geometries. The paper asks whether one can achieve single-step sampling and exact density estimation on arbitrary Riemannian manifolds using a generic architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes data points represented as vectors in an m-dimensional embedding space R^m (with m ≥ n, the manifold dimension) and produces generated samples on the manifold via a single forward pass through a decoder network followed by a projection π: R^m → M. For density estimation, it computes the log-likelihood of a data point using the decoder Jacobian projected into tangent spaces. The only manifold-specific component required is the projection function; all other components (ResNet encoder/decoder, latent distribution) are generic. The latent distribution is chosen on the same manifold (e.g., uniform on the sphere, mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions).

## Method

M-FFF generalizes the free-form flow framework to Riemannian manifolds by deriving a manifold change-of-variables formula (Theorem 2) that computes volume change via the projected Jacobian R^T f'(x) Q in tangent spaces, plus a metric correction term. The gradient of the negative log-likelihood is estimated using a Hutchinson trace estimator where the random vector v is sampled from a distribution with covariance RR^T (achieved by projecting standard normal vectors through the projection Jacobian), requiring only two vector-Jacobian products per sample. The architecture uses two independent feed-forward ResNets (encoder and decoder) operating in the embedding space with a projection appended to ensure outputs lie on the manifold, coupled by a reconstruction loss. Training maximizes likelihood via the stochastic gradient estimator plus three regularizers (reconstruction, uniform-space reconstruction, and projection distance), and inference requires a single decoder evaluation plus projection for sampling or a single Jacobian computation for density estimation.

## Results

On SO(3) rotation matrices, M-FFF achieves NLL of -0.87, -0.21, and 0.45 for M=16, 32, and 64 mixture components, outperforming the specialized SO(3) normalizing flow (Liu et al.) by 0.06–0.16 NLL and beating multi-step diffusion methods for M≥32. On real-world earth data (sphere), M-FFF significantly outperforms the single-step Mixture of Kent baseline across all four datasets (e.g., -2.25 vs -0.80 NLL on Volcano). On torus datasets (protein/RNA torsion angles), M-FFF beats Circular Spline Coupling Flows on all five subsets and surpasses Riemannian flow matching on Glycine (1.89 vs 1.90 NLL). On the Stanford bunny mesh, M-FFF outperforms Riemannian flow matching for k=50 (1.34 vs 1.48) and k=100 (1.28 vs 1.53) mixture components, while all methods use a single forward pass versus ~1000 integration steps for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-FFF consistently outperforms all single-step baselines: on SO(3) it beats the specialized SO(3)-NF [Liu et al., 2023] by 0.06–0.16 NLL across all mixture sizes; on the sphere it surpasses Mixture of Kent [Peel et al., 2001] by 1.45 NLL on Volcano and 0.10–0.22 NLL on other earth datasets; on tori it outperforms Circular Spline Coupling Flows [Rezende et al., 2020] on all five protein/RNA subsets (e.g., 1.89 vs 1.91 on Glycine, -4.27 vs -4.01 on RNA). Against multi-step methods, M-FFF wins on SO(3) for M=32 and 64 (beating Riemannian SGM and Exp-wrapped SGM), on Glycine torus (1.89 vs 1.90 for Riemannian flow matching), and on Stanford bunny for k=50 and k=100 (beating both Riemannian flow matching variants). However, on the sphere earth datasets, multi-step methods like Riemannian flow matching and Riemannian diffusion achieve lower NLL (e.g., -7.93 vs -2.25 on Volcano), which the authors attribute to large empty regions between concentrated data points. The key practical advantage is that M-FFF requires a single function evaluation for sampling and density estimation, making it ~100× faster than multi-step ODE/SDE integration methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the free-form flow (FFF) framework of Draxler et al. [2024] and Sorrenson et al. [2024]—which replaced invertible architectures with encoder-decoder pairs trained via a Hutchinson-style gradient estimator—to Riemannian manifolds. It departs from the two dominant paradigms for manifold generative modeling: (i) continuous-time methods (Moser flow, Riemannian diffusion, Riemannian flow matching) that require 100–1000 ODE/SDE integration steps, and (ii) single-step normalizing flows specialized to particular manifolds (SO(3)-NF, Circular Spline Coupling Flows, Mixture of Kent). The key conceptual move is estimating the change-of-variables gradient in the tangent space via a projected random vector, which reduces the manifold adaptation to specifying a single projection function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M-FFF is the first generative model on manifolds combining single-step sampling, exact likelihood evaluation, and applicability to arbitrary Riemannian manifolds, making it practical for edge deployment and rapid prototyping on new geometries. The two-orders-of-magnitude inference speedup over multi-step methods is a significant practical win, and the ease of adaptation (only a projection is needed) lowers the barrier to manifold generative modeling. However, the authors note that performance degrades relative to multi-step methods when densities change sharply or contain large low-density regions, and the decoder-Jacobian approximation for the encoder inverse introduces a source of inaccuracy when reconstruction loss is non-negligible. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional manifolds and whether the tangent-space trace estimator's variance remains controlled in such regimes.
