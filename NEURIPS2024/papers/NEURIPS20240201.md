## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solution learning require large volumes of expensive high-fidelity simulation data, partially undermining the goal of avoiding costly numerical PDE solves. Unsupervised pretraining, a proven data-efficiency strategy in CV and NLP, has never been explored for PDE operator learning in scientific machine learning, leaving two open questions: what constitutes unlabeled PDE data, and how to design proxy tasks suited to PDE structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes only unlabeled PDE inputs—physical parameters (diffusion coefficients, wavenumbers, Reynolds numbers), forcing functions, spatial grid coordinates, and initial-condition snapshots—without any simulated solutions, bypassing the expensive time-marching step. For time-dependent PDEs, individual snapshots are used (discarding temporal dynamics), while for time-independent PDEs the full coefficient/forcing fields suffice. At fine-tuning and inference, the model maps these inputs to full spatiotemporal solution fields (e.g., vorticity over 33 timesteps at 64×64, or velocity/pressure at 512×512×15); during OOD inference it additionally accepts a variable number of in-context demo pairs (input–solution) to calibrate predictions.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, unsupervised pretraining on unlabeled PDE data uses two physics-inspired reconstruction proxy tasks: a Masked Autoencoder that randomly masks spatial locations (pixel-level for FNO, patch-level for Video-MAE) and reconstructs the full input, motivated by sensor-sparsity invariance; and a super-resolution task that applies random Gaussian blur and reconstructs the sharp input, motivated by resolution invariance of PDE dynamics. Second, the pretrained encoder (and decoder for the transformer) is fine-tuned on a reduced set of simulated PDE solutions, yielding domain-adapted initializations that accelerate convergence and reduce overfitting. Third, for OOD inference, a zero-overhead similarity-based in-context learning step computes the trained operator's predictions on both the query and a set of demos, identifies spatially and temporally similar locations by output-space L1 distance, and aggregates the ground-truth solutions from the top-k nearest demos—requiring no architectural changes or additional training.

## Results

Across Poisson, Helmholtz, Reaction-Diffusion, and Navier-Stokes PDEs, unsupervised pretraining on unlabeled PDE data enables neural operators to match the best test error of randomly-initialized baselines while saving 5×10² to 8×10⁵ simulated PDE solutions. On the most challenging Helmholtz equation, both pretraining and random-init curves fail to improve until over 1024 simulated data points are used, suggesting a grokking-like phenomenon. On real-world benchmarks (ERA5, ScalarFlow, Airfoil), pretraining on unlabeled snapshots consistently reduces relative ℓ₂ error compared to training from scratch. For OOD generalization, scaling the number of in-context demos from 0 to 100 monotonically improves both ℓ₂ error and output-scale calibration on Navier-Stokes (Re=10000) and other PDEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines. First, against randomly-initialized neural operators trained from scratch: the proposed unsupervised pretraining consistently achieves lower relative ℓ₂ error at every data budget across all four PDE benchmarks (Poisson, Helmholtz, Reaction-Diffusion, Navier-Stokes) and all three real-world datasets (ERA5, ScalarFlow, Airfoil), with the gap widening at low data volumes where overfitting is most severe. Second, against vision-pretrained Video-MAE (SSv2 checkpoint) fine-tuned on PDE data: the proposed method outperforms it at all data volumes, while Video-MAE only surpasses random initialization at high data volumes and is eventually outperformed by random init at long rollout steps (Figure 3f). In Appendix E, the method also largely outperforms MoCo v2 contrastive pretraining on the ERA5 benchmark, confirming that reconstruction-based proxy tasks (MAE + super-resolution) are more effective than contrastive learning for PDE operator pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the unsupervised pretraining paradigm from CV (MAE, Video-MAE) and NLP (BERT, GPT) into PDE operator learning, a domain where it had not previously been explored. It builds on Fourier Neural Operators and Video-MAE as backbone architectures, but introduces the novel concept of 'unlabeled PDE data' (physical parameters and initial conditions without simulated solutions) and designs two physics-motivated proxy tasks—masked autoencoding and super-resolution—specifically for PDE fields. For out-of-distribution generalization, it adapts the in-context learning idea from LLMs to neural operators, but replaces the cross-attention architectures used in prior in-context operator learning work [80, 81] with a simpler, training-free similarity-based aggregation over the model's own predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: saving 5×10² to 8×10⁵ expensive PDE simulations while matching or exceeding the accuracy of models trained on those full datasets directly addresses the central bottleneck in scientific machine learning. The finding that domain-specific unsupervised pretraining on cheap unlabeled PDE data outperforms both random initialization and large-scale vision-pretrained checkpoints (Video-MAE on SSV2) challenges the assumption that generic foundation-model pretraining is the best transfer strategy for scientific tasks. Open questions include whether more physics-inspired proxy tasks (e.g., symmetry-based augmentations, conservation-law constraints) could further improve pretraining, whether the approach extends to 3D and higher-dimensional PDEs, and whether the similarity-based ICL can be made more principled beyond the current top-k averaging heuristic.
