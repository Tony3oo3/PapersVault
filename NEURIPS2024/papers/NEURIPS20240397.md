## Problem

Off-the-shelf VLMs lack sufficient decision-centric training data, making them sub-optimal for in-the-wild GUI device control. While supervised fine-tuning on static human demonstrations offers some improvement, it fails to handle real-world stochasticity (pop-ups, changing websites, non-stationary app states) and cannot recover from the agent's own mistakes. Prior autonomous learning approaches like filtered behavior cloning improve success rates but still fall short of what is achievable with proper multi-turn RL that accounts for environment stochasticity.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the last two GUI screenshots (as a state representation) concatenated with a natural language instruction (e.g., 'Go to newegg.com, search for razer kraken, and select the first entry'). It outputs a single device control action per step: a tap or slide at normalized (x,y) coordinates in [0,1], a typed text string of variable length, or a special button press (HOME, BACK, ENTER). The episode terminates on task completion (verified by a VLM-based evaluator) or after a maximum horizon H (10 steps for General tasks, 20 for Web Shopping).

## Method

DigiRL fine-tunes a 1.3B VLM (AutoUI-Base) in two stages: offline RL on stale demonstrations, followed by offline-to-online RL on autonomously collected rollouts from up to 64 parallel Android emulators. The policy update uses advantage-weighted regression (AWR) with hard filtering (thresholding advantages at 1/H) rather than exponential reweighting, keeping the loss equivalent to standard MLE on selected actions. To handle environment stochasticity, a doubly-robust step-level advantage estimator (a simplified GAE with λ=0.5) blends Monte-Carlo returns with a step-level value function, and an instruction-level value function drives an automatic curriculum that prioritizes the most informative (hardest) tasks for training. Both value functions are trained with cross-entropy loss rather than MSE, and reward signals come from a Gemini 1.5 Pro–based autonomous evaluator (2.8% error vs. human judgement) that requires no hand-written verification rules.

## Results

On the Android-in-the-Wild (AitW) benchmark, DigiRL (a 1.3B VLM trained with autonomous offline-to-online RL) achieves 71.9% success rate on the General subset and 67.2% on the Web Shopping subset, representing a 49.5–58.9 percentage-point absolute improvement over the best prompting-based baseline (AppAgent + GPT-4V at 17.7%/8.3%). DigiRL also outperforms the prior best autonomous learning method, Filtered BC, by 10.4 points (General) and 9.4 points (Web Shopping). The model uses only ~1,500 total trajectories (500 offline + 1,000 online), making it roughly 1000× more sample-efficient than prior on-policy RL approaches in simplified web navigation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DigiRL outperforms all six baselines on both AitW subsets. Against prompting-based approaches, DigiRL (71.9%/67.2%) surpasses AppAgent + GPT-4V (17.7%/8.3%) by 54.2/58.9 points and Set-of-Marks + Gemini 1.5 Pro (16.7%/11.5%) by 55.2/55.7 points, demonstrating that inference-time wrappers cannot compensate for the base model's lack of device-control grounding. Against supervised fine-tuning, DigiRL beats the 18B CogAgent (25.0%/38.5%) by 46.9/28.7 points and the 1.5B AutoUI (14.6%/17.7%) by 57.3/49.5 points, showing that scale and architecture alone cannot close the gap without decision-centric training. The closest competitor is Filtered BC (61.5%/57.8%), which DigiRL outperforms by 10.4/9.4 points, confirming that the doubly-robust advantage estimator and automatic curriculum provide meaningful gains over simple success-filtering. Notably, DigiRL achieves these results with a 1.3B model using only ~1,500 trajectories, whereas prior on-policy RL work in simplified web navigation required ~10⁶ trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DigiRL builds on advantage-weighted regression (AWR) as its RL backbone, but departs from prior RL-for-LLM work that targets single-turn tasks by developing a multi-turn, off-policy approach tailored to the stochasticity of real GUI environments. It extends the autonomous evaluator from Pan et al. (2024) into a scalable parallel Android environment and introduces a doubly-robust advantage estimator (simplified GAE) plus an instruction-level curriculum to handle non-stationarity. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms: prompting/retrieval wrappers around proprietary VLMs (AppAgent, Set-of-Marks) and supervised imitation on static demonstrations (AutoUI, CogAgent), arguing that both are fundamentally limited by their inability to adapt to the dynamic device ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that autonomous offline-to-online RL is necessary (not just beneficial) for robust device control, as demonstrated by the frozen-policy degradation experiment showing static imitation cannot handle non-stationarity. The practical impact is significant: a 1.3B VLM trained with DigiRL outperforms 18B CogAgent and proprietary VLMs like GPT-4V, suggesting that targeted RL fine-tuning can compensate for model scale in decision-centric tasks. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (AitW) with a narrow task distribution, reliance on Gemini 1.5 Pro as the autonomous evaluator, and code not yet released. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-app workflows, whether the curriculum and doubly-robust estimator transfer to other stochastic control domains, and how the method scales as the task space grows beyond AitW.
