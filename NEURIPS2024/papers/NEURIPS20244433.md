## Problem

Gaussian-based surface reconstruction suffers from two key limitations: (1) supervising rendered normals from 3D Gaussians only effectively updates rotation parameters while leaving positions largely unaffected, unlike SDF-based methods where normals are gradients of the signed distance function; and (2) pseudo-normal maps predicted by pretrained monocular estimators are inconsistent across views, causing severe reconstruction artifacts when used as supervision signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244433/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images initialized with a COLMAP sparse point cloud, and leverages pretrained monocular normal estimators (DSINE for outdoor scenes, GeoWizard for indoor scenes) as geometric priors. It outputs a 3D surface mesh extracted via TSDF fusion of rendered depth maps (using Open3D) and photorealistic novel view renderings at 100+ FPS. The intersection depth computation is implemented with custom CUDA kernels, and a semantic surface trimming step (using Grounded-SAM pseudo-labels) prunes unwanted background elements like sky in outdoor scenes.

## Method

The core contribution is a D-Normal regularizer that computes normals from the gradient of rendered depth rather than directly alpha-blending Gaussian normals. Depth is obtained via ray-plane intersection with flattened Gaussians (achieved through a scale regularization loss that drives the minimum scaling factor toward zero), so the D-Normal is a function of both position and normal, enabling backpropagation to update all geometric parameters. A confidence weight computed as the exponential of the cosine distance between the rendered normal (an average across views) and the current view's predicted normal down-weights unreliable predictions to mitigate cross-view inconsistencies. A new densification strategy retains only first-intersected Gaussians per ray and splits large Gaussians along their major principal axis (rather than sampling from the Gaussian distribution) to reduce depth error and surface bumps caused by clustering.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples dataset, VCR-GauS achieves a mean F1-score of 0.40, outperforming the best Gaussian-based baseline 2DGS (0.30) by 0.10 and the best NeuS-based method MonoSDF (0.39) by 0.01, while training in 53 minutes versus >24 hours for NeuS-based methods. On Replica, the method reaches an F1-score of 78.17 in under 1 hour, compared to MonoSDF's 81.64 requiring >10 hours. For novel view synthesis on Mip-NeRF360, VCR-GauS achieves 24.31/30.53 PSNR (outdoor/indoor) at 128 FPS, competitive with 3DGS (24.64/30.41 at 134 FPS) while providing geometrically accurate surfaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCR-GauS substantially outperforms all Gaussian-based surface reconstruction baselines: it beats 2DGS by 0.10 F1 on TNT (0.40 vs. 0.30) and by 13.81 F1 on Replica (78.17 vs. 64.36), while also rendering 2.1× faster (145 vs. 68 FPS). Against SuGaR and vanilla 3DGS, the margins are even larger (0.40 vs. 0.19 and 0.09 mean F1 on TNT). Compared to NeuS-based implicit methods (NeuS, MonoSDF, Geo-NeuS), VCR-GauS achieves a slightly higher mean F1 on TNT (0.40 vs. 0.38–0.39) and matches MonoSDF on DTU (0.80 vs. 0.84 Chamfer distance), but is approximately 20× faster (53 min vs. >24 h). On Replica, MonoSDF retains a small edge (81.64 vs. 78.17 F1) but requires >10 hours versus under 1 hour for VCR-GauS. For novel view synthesis on Mip-NeRF360, VCR-GauS is within 0.33 PSNR of 3DGS on outdoor scenes and slightly ahead on indoor scenes (30.53 vs. 30.41), while rendering at 128 FPS versus 2DGS's 27 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and NeuSG's scale regularization to flatten Gaussians into planar primitives, but departs from prior Gaussian-based surface reconstruction methods (SuGaR, 2DGS, DN-Splatter) by identifying a fundamental limitation: supervising rendered normals only updates Gaussian rotation, not position. The key conceptual departure is the D-Normal formulation, which derives normals from the gradient of rendered intersection depth rather than alpha-blending Gaussian normals, thereby coupling normal supervision to all geometric parameters. The confidence weighting mechanism, which uses the rendered normal (an implicit multi-view average) to gauge per-view prediction uncertainty, is a novel circumvention of the cross-view correspondence problem that plagues prior normal-prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in Gaussian-based surface reconstruction—ineffective position updates from normal supervision and cross-view normal inconsistency—enabling large-scale scene reconstruction at ~20× the speed of NeuS-based methods while matching or exceeding their quality. The D-Normal formulation is a clean, generalizable principle that could inform other explicit-representation methods needing geometric supervision. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or semi-transparent scenes (acknowledged limitations), whether the confidence mechanism can be made more principled than a cosine-distance heuristic, and whether the intersection-depth formulation generalizes to non-planar Gaussian primitives.
