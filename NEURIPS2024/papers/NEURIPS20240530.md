## Problem

Training long video generation models requires high-quality long-take videos with rich temporal annotations, yet existing large-scale video-text datasets (Panda-70M, HD-VG, WebVid, InternVid) are dominated by short clips under 10 seconds, contain scene cuts and static content, and provide only sparse single-sentence captions that fail to capture long-range temporal dynamics. This data bottleneck prevents models from learning to generate temporally consistent, highly dynamic videos over extended durations.

## Input / Output

The curation pipeline ingests 220 million raw video clips from four source datasets and outputs 2 million filtered long-take videos (minimum 10 s, average 20.2 s) each paired with a temporally dense caption averaging 88.7 words. For downstream generation experiments, the fine-tuned models accept a text prompt (or a reference image for I2V) and produce 65-frame (~10 s) videos; the T2V diffusion model is extended from its pretrained 32-frame length to 65 frames during fine-tuning. The captioning stage specifically consumes 6 uniformly sampled frames per 30-second clip arranged as a single composite image grid, rather than processing frames sequentially.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a three-level video filter selects high-quality long-take clips: PySceneDetect (ContentDetector, threshold 50, 0.5 fps sampling) removes any video with a scene change within a 2-second window; RAFT optical flow at 2 fps discards videos with average flow magnitude below 20; and PLLaVA-7B performs semantic-level rejection of low-diversity, shaky, or text-overlay videos. Second, a hierarchical captioning approach splits each video into 30-second clips, samples 6 frames per clip into a grid image fed to LLaVA-v1.6-34B for clip-level captioning, then uses Claude3-Haiku to refine individual captions for conciseness and merge them into a single temporally dense narrative describing action transitions and scene changes across the full video. Effectiveness is validated by fine-tuning a 1.8B diffusion-based I2V model (MagicVideo architecture), a 7B autoregressive LM-based T2V model (Loong), and a 1.75B T2V diffusion model extended to 65 frames, all showing improved dynamic degree, long-take consistency, and video-text alignment over WebVid-10M baselines on VBench and human evaluation.

## Results

LVD-2M achieves an average video length of 20.2s (minimum 10s) with 88.7-word temporally dense captions and an average optical flow score of 47.8, substantially exceeding all prior datasets. In human evaluation, LVD-2M reaches a 77.5% long-take video ratio (excluding WebVid-sourced clips) versus 47.5–55% for InternVid, Panda-70M, and HD-VG. When fine-tuning a 1.75B T2V diffusion model to 65-frame generation, LVD-2M outperforms WebVid-10M on 10 of 16 VBench metrics, with the largest gains in dynamic degree (28.06% vs. 20.00%), object class (86.93% vs. 76.95%), and human action (76.20% vs. 61.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Panda-70M, LVD-2M more than doubles the average video length (20.2s vs. 8.5s), increases caption length by ~6.7× (88.7 vs. 13.2 words), and triples the optical flow score (47.8 vs. 14.7), while also improving the long-take ratio from 50.0% to 77.5%. Compared to HD-VG-130M, LVD-2M nearly quadruples caption length (88.7 vs. 9.8 words) and more than doubles optical flow (47.8 vs. 21.5), with a 22.5-point gain in long-take ratio (77.5% vs. 55.0%). Versus InternVid, LVD-2M achieves a 30-point higher long-take ratio (77.5% vs. 47.5%) and a 4.1× higher optical flow score (47.8 vs. 11.6). Against WebVid-10M (the primary fine-tuning baseline), LVD-2M wins on 10 of 16 VBench metrics after fine-tuning a T2V diffusion model, with the most notable margins in dynamic degree (+8.06 pts), object class (+9.98 pts), and human action (+14.80 pts), while slightly trailing on background consistency (96.92% vs. 98.02%) and temporal flickering (97.44% vs. 98.00%). For HD-VILA-100M, HowTo100M, and ACAV, LVD-2M dominates on both video duration and caption richness, though these datasets were not directly compared in the fine-tuning experiments. YT-Temporal-180M lacks reported statistics for direct numerical comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a suite of existing tools—PySceneDetect for scene-cut detection, RAFT optical flow for motion filtering, PLLaVA-7B for semantic quality assessment, and the image-grid VLM paradigm from IG-VLM—into a unified curation pipeline specifically targeting long-take, high-motion videos with temporally dense captions. It departs from prior large-scale video datasets (Panda-70M, HD-VG-130M, InternVid, WebVid-10M) which were designed for general video understanding or short-clip generation and thus contain abundant scene cuts, static content, and single-sentence captions. The hierarchical captioning approach (LLaVA-v1.6-34B for clip-level description via image grids, followed by Claude3-Haiku for refinement and cross-clip merging) is a novel composition of VLM and LLM capabilities not previously applied at this scale for video dataset annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LVD-2M fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides 2M+ long-take videos (>10s, no cuts), large motion dynamics, and temporally dense captions (~89 words vs. 10–17 in prior datasets), making it the first dataset purpose-built for training long video generation models. Key limitations include reliance on CLIP text encoders that truncate the long captions at 77 tokens, validation only on 1.75B–1.8B parameter models, and a 2M scale that remains smaller than the source datasets. Open questions include whether the filtering and captioning pipeline generalizes to even longer durations (minutes), whether stronger text encoders (e.g., T5-based) unlock further gains, and whether the dataset's category distribution adequately covers the full diversity needed for production-quality long video generation.
