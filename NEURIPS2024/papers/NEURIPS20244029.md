## Problem

Score-based diffusion models and flow matching methods for simulation-based inference (SBI) achieve high posterior quality but require expensive multi-step ODE solving at inference time, while the current fast alternatives (normalizing flows) are restricted to bijective architectures with cheap Jacobians. Consistency models combine unconstrained architectures with few-step sampling, but their applicability to SBI—particularly in the low-dimensional parameter regimes typical of scientific applications—had not been explored, and their quality as conditional Bayesian samplers (calibration, precision) remained uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed data set x (ranging from 10 scalar observations in the GMM benchmark to 784-dimensional blurred images or variable-length time series in the tumor model) and produces S posterior draws θ from the approximate conditional distribution p(θ|x). Sampling requires only K forward passes of the consistency network (typically K=2–30), each mapping a noisy point θ_{k+1} to a less-noisy point θ_k, rather than solving a differential equation. A key constraint is that posterior densities can only be evaluated at the generated sample points (via autodiff Jacobians), not at arbitrary parameter values, which limits direct use for tasks requiring pointwise density queries.

## Method

CMPE trains a consistency function f_φ(θ, t; x) = c_skip(t)·θ + c_out(t)·F_φ(θ, t; x) with skip connections that maps any point on the probability flow ODE trajectory directly to the trajectory origin θ_ε, conditioned on the observation x. Training uses a student-teacher consistency objective (Eq. 8) with a Pseudo-Huber distance between the student's output at time t_{i+1} and the teacher's (stopgrad) output at t_i, weighted by λ(t_i)=1/(t_{i+1}−t_i), following the discretization and noise schedule of Song and Dhariwal (2024). At inference, posterior draws are generated by starting from Gaussian noise θ_T ~ N(0, T²I) and applying K iterative consistency steps (Eq. 7), each adding a small re-noising term, yielding samples in K network passes rather than hundreds of ODE solver steps. The unconstrained backbone F_φ can be any architecture (MLP, U-Net, LSTM-Transformer hybrid) tailored to the data and parameter structure, and the number of sampling steps K is adjustable at inference time to trade off speed against posterior sharpness.

## Results

On the tumor spheroid growth benchmark (7 parameters, 19,600 training simulations), CMPE with 30 sampling steps achieves the best RMSE (0.577) and best calibration (Max ECE 0.018) among all methods, while being ~27× faster than 1000-step FMPE (18.33s vs 500.90s per 2000 samples). On Bayesian image denoising (Fashion MNIST, 784-dim), CMPE with a naive MLP architecture achieves RMSE 0.388 vs 0.836 for FMPE at 2000 training images, and is 50–1000× faster than FMPE across all settings. On the three low-dimensional benchmarks (GMM, Two Moons, Inverse Kinematics), CMPE with 10–30 steps outperforms ACF and NSF on C2ST and matches or exceeds 1000-step FMPE while being 30–75× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACF and NSF (normalizing flows), CMPE with 30 steps achieves lower RMSE (0.577 vs 0.589/0.590) and lower Max ECE (0.018 vs 0.021/0.027) on the tumor spheroid task, though ACF remains the fastest method (1.07s). Against FMPE (flow matching), CMPE delivers a decisive calibration advantage: at comparable speed (~18s), CMPE achieves Max ECE 0.018 while FMPE at 30 steps yields 0.222; even FMPE at 1000 steps (500.90s) only reaches 0.057. On the Bayesian denoising task, CMPE with a naive MLP architecture dramatically outperforms FMPE (RMSE 0.388 vs 0.836 at 2000 images), demonstrating reduced sensitivity to suboptimal architecture choices, while with a U-Net both methods achieve comparable quality but CMPE is 50–1000× faster (0.5ms vs 565.8ms per sample). On the low-dimensional benchmarks, CMPE with 10 steps outperforms 1000-step FMPE by a large C2ST margin on the GMM task while being ~75× faster, and is the superior method in the low-to-medium data regime (up to M=4096) on Two Moons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts consistency models (Song et al. 2023; Song & Dhariwal 2024), originally developed for few-step image generation, to the conditional simulation-based inference (SBI) setting, producing CMPE. It sits alongside flow matching posterior estimation (FMPE, Wildberger et al. 2023) and normalizing-flow-based NPE (ACF, NSF) as a neural posterior estimator, but departs from FMPE by replacing the conditional flow matching loss with a consistency training objective that directly distills the probability flow ODE into a few-step sampler. The unconstrained architecture inherited from diffusion/flow-matching models is retained, while the multi-step ODE solver at inference is eliminated in favor of one-to-few network passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMPE offers SBI practitioners a practical speed/accuracy trade-off: 50–1000× faster sampling than FMPE while matching or exceeding its posterior quality on low-dimensional benchmarks and a tumor-growth simulator, which is critical for workflows requiring millions of posterior draws. Two open limitations remain: (i) single-step CMPE does not yield tractable densities at arbitrary parameter values, and (ii) the non-monotonic (U-shaped) relationship between sampling steps and posterior quality complicates inference-time tuning. The failed gravitational-wave experiment (Experiment 6) highlights that CMPE has not yet been validated at the scale and complexity of frontier physics applications, leaving its robustness to very high-dimensional, multi-modal posteriors an open question.
