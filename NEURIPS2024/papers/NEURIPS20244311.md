## Problem

Despite rapid adoption of large multimodal models (LMMs) that combine visual encoders with LLMs, their internal representations remain largely unexplored from an interpretability standpoint. Prior concept-based explainability methods (CAV, CRAFT, ACE) were designed for CNNs on classification tasks and do not transfer to the transformer-based, autoregressive architecture of LMMs. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we extract semantically meaningful, multimodally grounded concepts from the internal token representations of a pretrained LMM?

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen or partially fine-tuned LMM (e.g., DePALM with CLIP ViT-L/14 + OPT-6.7B, or LLaVA with Vicuna-7B), a single target token (e.g., 'Dog'), and a subset of COCO images where that token appears in both the model's predicted caption and the ground-truth caption. It extracts the 4096-dimensional residual stream representation at a deep layer (L=31) at the first position where the target token is predicted, stacks these across ~3000+ images into a matrix Z, and decomposes it into a 20-concept dictionary. The output is a set of 'multimodal concepts'—each grounded simultaneously in vision (5 most activating images) and text (top decoded words via the unembedding matrix)—plus, for any test image, the top-r activating concepts with their multimodal grounding as a local interpretation.

## Method

CoX-LMM applies dictionary learning to the residual stream representations of a target token inside a pretrained LMM. For a token t, it collects the representation h_(L)^p from a deep layer at the first predicted position of t across many images, forming Z ∈ R^(B×M), then decomposes Z ≈ UV via Semi-NMF—a relaxed NMF variant that allows Z and the basis U to contain mixed signs while enforcing non-negativity and L1 sparsity on the activation matrix V. The key insight is that the learned concept vectors u_k live in the LLM's token representation space, enabling dual grounding: textually via the unembedding matrix W_U (logit-lens decoding to extract associated words) and visually via prototyping (selecting images that maximally activate each concept). This simultaneous visual and textual grounding defines the paper's notion of 'multimodal concepts,' distinguishing them from purely visual CAVs used in CNN interpretability. The Semi-NMF formulation is chosen because standard NMF is inapplicable (Z is not non-negative) while still preserving the interpretability benefit of non-negative, sparse combinations of concepts.

## Results

On the COCO captioning task with DePALM (OPT-6.7B + CLIP ViT-L), Semi-NMF (CoX-LMM) achieves the best or near-best top-1 CLIPScore across all tested tokens: 0.610 for 'Dog', 0.634 for 'Bus', 0.646 for 'Train', and 0.627 for 'Cat', compared to 0.783, 0.736, 0.727, and 0.798 for the GT-captions upper bound. BERTScore follows the same pattern (e.g., 0.405 for 'Dog' vs. 0.511 GT). On the overlap/entanglement metric, Semi-NMF achieves 0.086–0.177, substantially lower than KMeans (0.367–0.501) and Simple (0.371–0.622), while PCA achieves near-zero overlap (0.000–0.015) but at the cost of lower CLIPScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semi-NMF (CoX-LMM) consistently outperforms the Rnd-Words and Noise-Imgs ablation baselines by 0.10–0.19 CLIPScore across all tokens, confirming that both the learned dictionary structure and the real image inputs are essential. Against PCA, Semi-NMF achieves 0.05–0.10 higher CLIPScore (e.g., 0.610 vs. 0.559 for 'Dog'), while PCA's orthogonal constraint yields near-zero concept overlap (0.000–0.015) at the cost of less coherent grounded words. Compared to the Simple baseline (selecting highest-norm representations), Semi-NMF wins on CLIPScore for 'Dog' (0.610 vs. 0.546) and 'Cat' (0.627 vs. 0.589) but loses slightly on 'Bus' (0.634 vs. 0.667) and 'Train' (0.646 vs. 0.642); however, Semi-NMF's overlap score is dramatically lower (0.086–0.177 vs. 0.371–0.622), indicating far more disentangled concepts. Against KMeans, Semi-NMF is competitive on CLIPScore (within 0.01–0.03) but achieves roughly 3–5× lower overlap (e.g., 0.086 vs. 0.501 for 'Dog'), making it the best overall balance of interpretability quality and concept diversity. Statistical significance testing confirms Semi-NMF is significantly better than Rnd-Words, Noise-Imgs, and PCA (p < 0.001) but often not significantly different from Simple and KMeans on CLIPScore/BERTScore alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-based interpretability lineage from CAV and NMF-based dictionary learning for CNNs (Kim et al. 2018, CRAFT, Fel et al. 2023) to the fundamentally different architecture of large multimodal models, where token representations live in a transformer residual stream rather than a convolutional feature map. It repurposes the logit lens idea (Nostalgebraist 2020) for textual grounding of learned concepts and introduces Semi-NMF as a relaxed dictionary-learning objective suited to mixed-sign representation matrices. The key conceptual departure is the simultaneous visual and textual grounding of each dictionary element, yielding 'multimodal concepts' that have no direct analogue in prior CNN-based CAV work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first concept-based explainability framework designed specifically for LMMs, providing a practical tool to decompose and interpret token-level internal representations in models like DePALM and LLaVA. The finding that multimodal semantic structure emerges only around layers 20–25, and that visual tokens (which causally cannot attend to text tokens) nonetheless exhibit coherent multimodal concepts, offers concrete mechanistic insight into how frozen LLMs generalize to multimodal inputs. Limitations include token-specific dictionaries, reliance on captioning models, and simple grounding techniques; open questions include learning shared cross-token concept dictionaries, extending to VQA and other LMM tasks, and developing more principled interpretability metrics beyond CLIPScore/BERTScore.
