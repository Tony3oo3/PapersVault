## Problem

Decentralized stochastic gradient methods with Θ(1) per-iteration communication suffer from large transient times—typically O~(n³) under smooth nonconvex objectives—before achieving the same convergence rate as centralized SGD. This paper asks whether a carefully designed network topology can dramatically reduce the transient time while maintaining minimal per-iteration communication cost, and answers affirmatively by showing O~(n) and O~(1) transient times for nonconvex and strongly convex objectives, respectively.

## Input / Output

Each agent i holds a local cost function f_i and draws independent stochastic gradient samples g_i(x; ξ_i) with bounded variance σ². The algorithm operates on two B-ary spanning trees (a Pull Tree and its inverse Push Tree) over n agents, where each agent communicates with at most B+1 neighbors per iteration. The output is the parameter vector x_1^(T) at the root node, which tracks the global optimum; the stepsize is scaled by 1/n relative to standard DSGD due to the gradient tracker at node 1 accumulating n times the averaged stochastic gradients over iterations.

## Method

BTPP replaces standard gossip averaging with a two-tree push-pull mechanism: model parameters flow downward through a B-ary Pull Tree G_R (parent to child), while stochastic gradient trackers flow upward through the inverse Push Tree G_C (child to parent), so that only the root node y_1 aggregates the full network gradient. The mixing matrices R and C consist solely of 0s and 1s, making the per-iteration communication cost Θ(1) per agent. The convergence analysis avoids the induced matrix norms used in prior push-pull work and instead operates entirely under standard L2 and Frobenius norms, exploiting the fact that (R − (1/n)1uᵀ)^k vanishes for k ≥ d (the tree diameter), which bounds the consensus error and enables the O~(n) transient time. The branch size B is a tunable hyperparameter: larger B reduces the diameter d = ⌊log_B(n)⌋ and thus the transient time, at the cost of higher per-iteration communication.

## Results

BTPP achieves a transient time of Õ(n) under smooth nonconvex objectives and Õ(1) under smooth strongly convex objectives, with only Θ(1) per-iteration communication (at most B+1 neighbors per agent). This represents a dramatic improvement over the state-of-the-art transient time of Õ(n³) for nonconvex and Õ(n) for strongly convex objectives. In experiments on logistic regression (n=100, p=500) and CNN training on MNIST (24 agents, batch size 8), BTPP with B=2 closely approximates the convergence speed of centralized SGD (DSGD-FullyConnected) while using only Θ(1) degree communication.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTPP outperforms all eight baselines on transient iteration complexity while maintaining Θ(1) per-iteration communication. Compared to the closest competitors with Θ(1) degree — RELAYSGD (O(n³)), DSGD-CECA (Õ(n³)), and BASE-(k+1) (Õ(n³)) — BTPP reduces the transient time from Õ(n³) to Õ(n), a polynomial improvement of roughly n². Against OD(OU)-EQUIDYN, which also uses 1 neighbor per iteration but requires Θ(n) distinct graph topologies, BTPP needs only 2 fixed B-ary trees. The trade-off for BTPP is using 2 graph topologies (pull tree and push tree) versus 1 for most baselines, though this is a constant overhead independent of n. In the strongly convex setting (Table 2), BTPP further improves the transient time from Õ(n) (achieved by STATIC EXP., O.-P. EXP., RELAYSGD, and OD(OU)-EQUIDYN) to Õ(1), meaning the algorithm reaches its asymptotic rate almost immediately regardless of network size.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTPP inherits its push-pull communication mechanism from the deterministic Push-Pull method of Pu et al. [22] and Xin & Khan [33], but repurposes it for stochastic nonconvex optimization by pairing two B-ary spanning trees (one for parameter propagation, one for gradient accumulation) instead of a single general graph. It departs from the dominant gossip-averaging paradigm in DSGD and gradient-tracking methods [14, 19, 34] by exploiting the layer-wise structure of trees to eliminate the need for induced matrix norms in the analysis, and from topology-design works like exponential graphs [34] and RelaySGD [31] by achieving Θ(1) per-iteration degree with only two fixed topologies rather than multiple subgraphs or dynamic reconfiguration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction of transient time from O~(n³) to O~(n) (nonconvex) and O~(1) (strongly convex) is a meaningful theoretical advance for decentralized learning, particularly relevant as cluster sizes grow and the transient phase dominates wall-clock time. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale settings (n=100 for logistic regression, n=24 for MNIST CNN), making it difficult to assess whether the theoretical advantage translates to practical speedups at the hundreds-to-thousands-of-nodes scale where the gap between O~(n) and O~(n³) would be most consequential. Open questions include whether the B-ary tree structure remains advantageous under communication delays, asynchronous updates, or non-i.i.d. data heterogeneity beyond the synthetic settings tested, and whether the large constants in the stepsize choices (involving d⁶, n²) limit practical applicability.
