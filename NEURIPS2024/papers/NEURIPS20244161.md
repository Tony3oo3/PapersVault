## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods are designed and evaluated almost exclusively for detecting novel classes, yet real-world deployed systems encounter a broad variety of anomalous inputs including adversarial attacks, synthetic images, corruptions, and multi-label scenes. The paper demonstrates that these methods perform inconsistently across different shift types—they reliably detect only the specific distribution shift they were engineered for, leaving them vulnerable to other forms of unexpected input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244161/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-processor on pretrained classifiers (ResNet-50 or ViT) trained on ImageNet-1K, consuming standard RGB images and producing a scalar OOD detection score derived from the joint log-likelihood under a fitted GMM. Three ensemble variants are provided: ENS-V and ENS-R use 8 scores each (including gradient-based and Mahalanobis-distance statistics) at up to ~14× forward-pass overhead, while ENS-F uses only 5 lightweight scores (MSP, max logits, MDS variants, EBO) adding just 19–25% overhead. The benchmark evaluates detection across 12 datasets spanning five shift categories, with two evaluation protocols: Distribution Shift Detection (any OOD sample) and Error Detection (misclassified OOD vs. correctly classified in-distribution).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) ensemble that learns the joint distribution of multiple existing OOD detection scores on correctly-classified in-distribution validation samples (45,000 ImageNet images), using the EM algorithm with 10 components. Rather than engineering a single heuristic score optimized for one shift type, the GMM captures the multivariate structure among scores, enabling detection of inputs whose individual scores appear normal but whose joint realization is atypical. Score selection is guided by in-distribution covariance matrices to minimize redundancy, and two backbone-specific score sets (ENS-V for ViT, ENS-R for ResNet) plus a fast variant (ENS-F) are defined. The approach requires no OOD samples at training time and no fine-tuning of the base classifier, making it directly deployable as a plug-in detector. The paper also introduces BROAD, a 12-dataset benchmark covering five shift types, and CoComageNet, a new multi-label detection dataset constructed from CoCo images containing at least two ImageNet classes.

## Results

On the BROAD benchmark (DSD setting, averaged over 5 distribution shift types), the proposed Gaussian Mixture ensemble ENS-V achieves 84.42 AUC with ViT and ENS-R achieves 73.87 AUC with ResNet-50, surpassing the best single baselines (MDSl at 77.56 for ViT; SHE at 71.93 for ResNet-50) by 6.86% and 4.04% respectively. The lightweight ENS-F variant adds only 19–25% inference overhead over a single forward pass while retaining competitive performance (82.65 ViT / 72.27 RN50). In the error detection setting, ENS-F unexpectedly delivers the best results for both backbones (90.99 ViT / 86.10 RN50 average AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single baseline dominates across all five shift types; each is Pareto-optimal in at least one category while failing in another. MDS_l is the strongest ViT baseline (77.56 avg AUC) but drops to 62.16 on ResNet-50, while SHE is the best ResNet-50 baseline (71.93) yet only mid-pack on ViT (74.72). DICE excels on adversarial attacks (78.29 ViT) and synthetic images (77.84 ViT) but is near-random on novel classes (55.70 ViT); conversely, MDS_all is near-perfect on corruptions (95.55 ViT) but collapses on multi-labels (26.06 ViT). CADET m_in is the weakest overall (56.35 ViT), scoring below 21 AUC on novel classes for ViT. The ensemble methods (ENS-V/R/F) surpass every individual baseline on the averaged AUC by 1.9–6.9 points, yet rarely win on any single shift type—instead they deliver more consistent mid-to-high scores across all categories, confirming that broad detection requires combining complementary heuristics rather than relying on any single score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OOD detection literature, which has been dominated by a single formulation—detecting samples from novel classes absent from training—into a broader problem of simultaneously detecting five distinct types of distribution shifts (novel classes, adversarial perturbations, synthetic images, corruptions, and multi-label inputs). While a handful of prior works (Guille-Escuret et al. 2022, Lee et al. 2018) attempted joint detection of novel classes and adversarial samples, no prior study systematically evaluated or addressed the full diversity of OOD types. The proposed GMM ensemble repurposes the generative-modeling-for-detection idea (Cao et al. 2020; Mahalanobis-based methods of Lee et al. 2018) but applies it to the low-dimensional space of existing detection scores rather than high-dimensional feature representations, sidestepping the curse of dimensionality while preserving the information-theoretic advantage of likelihood-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that state-of-the-art OOD detectors are consistently unreliable across distribution shift types they were not specifically designed for—challenges the field's implicit assumption that progress on novel-class detection transfers broadly. This has practical implications for deployed systems that must handle diverse anomalies, and motivates future methods to be evaluated (and designed) for broad rather than narrow OOD detection. Key limitations include the restriction to ImageNet as in-distribution, coverage of only five shift types, and the computational overhead of the full ensemble (up to ~14× forward-pass cost). Open questions include whether the inconsistency finding persists for newer architectures and training regimes, whether the GMM ensemble generalizes to other modalities (text, audio), and whether the multi-label shift (CoComageNet) is representative of the broader 'ambiguous input' problem in safety-critical systems.
