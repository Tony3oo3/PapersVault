## Problem

Computing Jacobians via automatic differentiation is ubiquitous in scientific computing, yet finding the optimal vertex elimination order in cross-country elimination is NP-complete. Prior approaches—simulated annealing, dynamic programming, and heuristics like minimal Markowitz degree—failed to significantly outperform simple forward- or reverse-mode AD on real-world problems. This paper asks whether deep reinforcement learning can discover new, function-specific elimination orders that beat all existing methods while still computing the exact Jacobian.

## Input / Output

The method takes a computational graph encoded as a 3D adjacency tensor (vertices × vertices × 5 values: sparsity type integer from −10 to 10 plus four Jacobian shape dimensions) derived from JAX's Jaxpression representation of a Python function. It produces a sequence of vertex indices (the elimination order) that minimizes the total number of multiplications; this order is then executed by Graphax, a JAX-based AD interpreter, to compute the exact Jacobian. The graph representation supports vector-valued vertices with sparse Jacobian structures, and the action space is constrained by masking already-eliminated vertices.

## Method

The core contribution is VertexGame, a single-player RL game where an agent sequentially selects vertices to eliminate from the computational graph, receiving a reward equal to the negative number of multiplications incurred at each step. The agent is a Gumbel AlphaZero model with a transformer backbone: vertical slices of the adjacency tensor are compressed via a 3×5 convolutional kernel into 64-dimensional tokens with positional encoding, processed by 6 transformer layers, and fed into separate policy (per-vertex probability) and value (summed per-token scores) MLP heads. Because cross-country elimination provably yields the exact Jacobian for any elimination order, the optimization reduces purely to finding the best sequence without approximation-quality concerns. The discovered orders are executed by Graphax, a novel sparse AD interpreter built on JAX that leverages JIT compilation and exploits Jacobian sparsity patterns during elimination, translating theoretical multiplication savings into measurable runtime gains of up to 33% over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

AlphaGrad reduces the number of multiplications required for Jacobian computation by up to 33% over the best existing baseline (reverse-mode AD) on the Random function f task (6,374 vs. 9,333 multiplications). Across the six scalar-input tasks, improvements over the best baseline range from 2% (PropaneCombustion: 88 vs. 90) to ~20% (RobotArm_6DOF: 231 vs. 288 Markowitz). For the two deep learning tasks (2-layer MLP and Transformer Encoder), AlphaGrad achieves only marginal gains over reverse-mode AD (389 vs. 392 and 4,656 vs. 4,688 multiplications with 250 MCTS simulations), as backpropagation is already near-optimal for funnel-like computational graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AlphaGrad against three baselines from the cross-country elimination literature: forward-mode AD, reverse-mode AD (backpropagation), and minimal Markowitz degree elimination. AlphaGrad wins on all 10 tasks, but the margin varies dramatically. On the six scalar-input tasks (RoeFlux_1d, RobotArm_6DOF, HumanHeartDipole, PropaneCombustion, Random function g, BlackScholes), AlphaGrad beats the best baseline by 2–20%, with the largest gains on RobotArm_6DOF (231 vs. 288 Markowitz, ~20%) and BlackScholes (312 vs. 350 Markowitz, ~11%). On the vectorized tasks RoeFlux_3d and Random function f, improvements reach 13.5% and 31.7% respectively over the best baseline. On the two deep learning tasks (MLP and Transformer Encoder), AlphaGrad only marginally outperforms reverse-mode AD (by 0.7–0.8% with 250 MCTS simulations), confirming that backpropagation is already near-optimal for funnel-like graphs with many inputs and a scalar output. Notably, forward-mode AD is consistently the worst baseline, while Markowitz degree and reverse-mode AD trade the lead depending on the task's input/output dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AlphaTensor/AlphaDev paradigm of using deep RL to discover new algorithms from the domains of matrix multiplication and sorting to automatic differentiation, specifically targeting the cross-country elimination framework of Griewank and Walther. Prior attempts to optimize elimination orders via simulated annealing (Naumann, 1999), dynamic programming (Naumann, 2020), or integer linear programming (Chen et al., 2012) all failed to beat simple heuristics like minimal Markowitz degree, whereas this work is the first to successfully use an AlphaZero-based agent (with Gumbel action sampling) to find strictly better orders. The implementation leverages JAX's Jaxpression representation and introduces Graphax, a sparse AD interpreter that executes the discovered orders, filling a gap left by existing AD frameworks that lack cross-country elimination support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since AD underpins gradient computation across ML, CFD, robotics, and finance, even modest reductions in multiplication counts translate to meaningful energy and runtime savings at scale. The paper's key limitation is that it optimizes only for multiplication count (a proxy) rather than actual execution time, memory access patterns, or hardware-specific costs, and it requires retraining the agent per computational graph. Open questions include whether multi-task training can yield a general-purpose AD algorithm selector, whether the approach extends to dynamic control flow, and whether hardware-aware reward signals (drawing on compiler optimization literature) could close the gap between theoretical multiplication savings and wall-clock improvements.
