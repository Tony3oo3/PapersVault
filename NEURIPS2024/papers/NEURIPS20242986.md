## Problem

Existing generalization theory for overparameterized neural networks relies on the interpolation regime (zero training loss) or the kernel/NTK limit, but for two-layer ReLU networks with noisy labels, interpolating solutions provably overfit and kernel methods are suboptimal. No prior theory characterized the non-interpolating local minima that gradient descent with a typical (constant) learning rate actually converges to, leaving a gap in understanding how practical training generalizes without explicit regularization or early stopping.

## Input / Output

The method takes n fixed-design univariate points x_i ∈ [-x_max, x_max] with noisy labels y_i = f_0(x_i) + ε_i (ε_i ~ N(0, σ²)) and a constant learning rate η, and trains a two-layer ReLU network with k hidden neurons (arbitrarily overparameterized, k ≫ n) via full-batch gradient descent. The output is a piecewise linear function (linear spline) whose weighted first-order total variation is bounded by 1/η − 1/2 + Õ(σ + √MSE), yielding a near-minimax MSE rate of Õ(n^{-4/5}) on the strict interior of the data support and a vanishing generalization gap in the distribution-free setting.

## Method

The core mechanism links the linear stability of a local minimum (λ_max of the Hessian ≤ 2/η) to a weighted TV(1) constraint on the represented function, generalizing Mulayoff et al.'s interpolation-dependent result to the noisy, non-interpolating case. The key technical innovation is decomposing the residual term (f_θ(x_i) − y_i)∇²_θ f_θ(x_i) in the Hessian into a Gaussian complexity measure (bounded via concentration and an ε-net over activation patterns) and a self-bounding MSE term, then using a uniform operator-norm bound on ∇²f_θ to close the argument. The resulting TV(1) bound restricts the output to a function class with metric entropy O(ε^{-1/2}), and a self-bounding version of Dudley's chaining over this class yields the near-optimal n^{-4/5} excess risk. Empirically, large η induces sparse linear-spline fits with few active knots (many neurons pushed outside the data support), resembling implicit L1 regularization rather than the L2 bias of gradient flow.

## Results

The paper proves that gradient descent with fixed learning rate η on a two-layer ReLU network in univariate nonparametric regression achieves a near-minimax-optimal MSE rate of Õ(n^{-4/5}) for estimating BV(1) functions in the strict interior of the data support (Theorem 4.4), matching the theoretical lower bound for this function class. This strictly improves upon the Ω(n^{-2/3}) rate that any kernel method (including NTK) or linear smoother must incur. The key mechanism is a weighted TV(1) bound of 1/η − 1/2 + Õ(σ + √MSE) on the learned function (Theorem 4.1), which constrains the number of linear spline pieces and prevents overfitting. A counter-example (Theorem 3.1) further shows that when n ≥ Ω(√(1/(ση))·log(1/δ)), no stable solution can interpolate noisy labels, ruling out the interpolation assumption used in prior minima-stability work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against kernel methods (NTK, kernel ridge regression), which provably achieve only a suboptimal worst-case MSE of Ω(n^{-2/3}) for BV(1) functions, whereas this work achieves the minimax-optimal Õ(n^{-4/5}) rate without any explicit regularization. Compared to prior minima-stability theory (Mulayoff et al. 2021, Ma & Ying 2021, Wu & Su 2023), this work removes the critical interpolation assumption, proving that stable solutions in the noisy-label regime cannot interpolate and still generalize. Against weight-decay-based approaches (Suzuki 2018, Zhang & Wang 2022, Parhi & Nowak 2021) that also achieve optimal rates, this work shows the same rate is attainable purely through the implicit bias of large learning rate gradient descent, with no weight decay or early stopping. The empirical experiments (n=30, k=100 neurons, σ=0.5) confirm that large η produces sparse linear spline fits with fewer knots, while small η leads to overfitting, validating the theoretical U-shaped bias-variance tradeoff in η.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the minima-stability framework of Mulayoff et al. [2021] and Wu et al. [2018], which linked the largest Hessian eigenvalue at a stable minimum to a weighted TV(1) bound on the learned function. The critical departure is removing the interpolation assumption (L(θ)=0) that all prior minima-stability results required, thereby making the theory applicable to noisy-label regression where interpolation is both undesirable and, as this paper proves, impossible for stable solutions. The paper also positions itself against the kernel/NTK regime and benign-overfitting literature, arguing that those frameworks are provably suboptimal for ReLU networks in univariate nonparametric regression, and connects to the Edge-of-Stability literature by showing that the BEoS regime provides the correct lens for understanding generalization of non-interpolating GD solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generalization theory for GD-trained ReLU networks that does not rely on interpolation, explicit regularization, or early stopping, and it achieves near-minimax-optimal n^{-4/5} rates for BV(1) functions—provably faster than any kernel-method estimator. The conceptual finding that large learning rate induces TV(1)-like (L1-analogous) implicit regularization rather than the L2 regularization of gradient flow reshapes how one thinks about the implicit bias of GD. Key limitations include the restriction to univariate inputs, two-layer architectures, and full-batch GD, with no computational guarantee that GD actually finds a solution satisfying the 'optimized' assumption; extending to multivariate inputs, deeper networks, and stochastic optimization remains open.
