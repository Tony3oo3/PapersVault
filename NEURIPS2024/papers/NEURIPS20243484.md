## Problem

Deep ensembles are the gold standard for estimating both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, but training M separate diffusion models becomes computationally intractable as architectures scale to billions of parameters. Existing single-model alternatives—Monte-Carlo dropout, Bayesian neural networks, and input perturbation—either degrade prediction quality, incur prohibitive runtime costs proportional to parameter count, or fail to model the true posterior over weights. The paper asks whether a single model can produce ensemble-quality uncertainty estimates at a fraction of the training cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement y (a 128×128 CT sinogram from a 45-view sparse Radon transform with Gaussian noise, or a 128×128 six-hour surface temperature map from ERA5) and a small random noise vector z ∈ ℝ⁸ that seeds the hyper-network. At inference, M weights are drawn from the hyper-network and N denoised samples are generated per weight, yielding an M×N predictive distribution. The final outputs are a spatially-resolved mean prediction plus three uncertainty maps (epistemic, aleatoric, total) computed pixel-wise via the law of total variance over the ensemble.

## Method

HyperDM couples a Bayesian hyper-network (BHN) with a conditional diffusion model: the BHN maps random noise z to diffusion-model weights φ, providing an implicit posterior q(φ) over the primary network, while the diffusion model samples from an implicit likelihood q(x|y) via iterative denoising. During training, only the BHN parameters θ are optimized via the standard diffusion denoising loss ‖ε − s(x^(t), t | y, h_θ(z))‖², with the diffusion model's weights purely generated and never directly learned. At inference, sampling M weights from the BHN and N predictions per weight produces M×N samples from which epistemic uncertainty is the variance across weight-conditioned means and aleatoric uncertainty is the mean of within-weight variances. This design replaces M independent training runs with a single BHN training run, achieving up to M-fold reduction in training time while matching or exceeding deep-ensemble prediction quality on CT reconstruction and weather forecasting tasks.

## Results

On LUNA16 CT reconstruction, HyperDM achieves PSNR 35.16 dB, SSIM 0.87, and CRPS 0.01, matching or exceeding the deep-ensemble baseline DPS-UQ (PSNR 34.95, SSIM 0.89, CRPS 0.01) while requiring only 48.5 min of training versus 441.1 min for DPS-UQ (~9× less). On ERA5 weather forecasting, HyperDM achieves PSNR 33.15 dB, SSIM 0.95, and CRPS 0.012, outperforming DPS-UQ (PSNR 32.83, SSIM 0.94, CRPS 0.013) on all three metrics. Inference time is comparable across all methods (~3.2–3.7 min for 1000 predictions), and HyperDM's training overhead over MC-Dropout is only 3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MC-Dropout, HyperDM wins decisively on every metric for both datasets: on LUNA16 it improves PSNR by 4.9 dB (35.16 vs. 30.25) and CRPS by 57% (0.01 vs. 0.023), while on ERA5 it gains 1.8 dB PSNR and 65% lower CRPS; training time is nearly identical (48.5 vs. 47.0 min). Against DPS-UQ (a 10-member deep ensemble of diffusion models), HyperDM matches or beats it on PSNR and CRPS for both datasets (e.g., 35.16 vs. 34.95 dB on LUNA16; 33.15 vs. 32.83 dB on ERA5) but trails slightly on LUNA16 SSIM (0.87 vs. 0.89); critically, HyperDM requires only ~11% of DPS-UQ's training time (48.5 vs. 441.1 min). Against the BNN baseline (Bayes by Backprop), HyperDM produces higher PSNR (e.g., 20.44 vs. 13.78 dB at |D|=800) and lower CRPS (0.07 vs. 0.13) on the toy problem, while training ~2× faster and inferring ~10× faster. In OOD detection, HyperDM's epistemic uncertainty maps more accurately localize anomalous features (metal implants in CT, hot spots in weather) than both MC-Dropout (which fails to highlight OOD regions) and DPS-UQ (which is competitive on CT but less precise on weather).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously independent ideas—Bayesian hyper-networks (Krueger et al., 2017), which stochastically generate primary-network weights from random noise as an implicit posterior, and conditional diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020), which sample from an implicit likelihood—into a single 'hyper-diffusion model' (HyperDM) architecture. It departs from the deep-ensemble paradigm (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) by replacing M separately trained diffusion models with one BHN that generates M weight sets, and from pseudo-ensembling methods (MC-Dropout, input perturbation) by avoiding inference-time noise that degrades prediction quality. The law-of-total-variance decomposition of uncertainty into epistemic and aleatoric components follows the standard Bayesian deep learning framework (Depeweg et al., 2018; Hüllermeier & Waegeman, 2021), but the implicit-distribution machinery for both the posterior and likelihood is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M-fold training-time reduction relative to deep ensembles is practically significant for scaling uncertainty quantification to modern billion-parameter architectures in medical imaging and climate science, where reliable uncertainty estimates directly inform clinical decisions and sensor placement. Two acknowledged limitations temper the impact: hyper-network parameter count scales with the primary network (limiting applicability to very large models), and iterative diffusion sampling remains slower than single-pass inference despite recent accelerated-sampling advances. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric data, whether the BHN's implicit posterior faithfully captures the true weight posterior at scale, and whether the method's OOD-detection advantage over DPS-UQ persists on more diverse and higher-resolution benchmarks.
