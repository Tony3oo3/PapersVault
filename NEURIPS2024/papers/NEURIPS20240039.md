## Problem

Existing Scan-to-BRep segmentation methods treat surface patches and boundary curves in isolation, ignoring the full B-Rep topological connectivity, which leads to disjoint and structurally inconsistent reconstructions. While ComplexGen models B-Rep as a chain complex, it relies on time-consuming post-processing optimization rather than learning topology directly. The paper addresses the absence of a mechanism to supervise B-Rep graph structure directly on point clouds within a single end-to-end trainable pipeline.

## Input / Output

The input is a 3D point cloud (up to 8000 points) normalized to the unit sphere, optionally augmented with per-point normals, and discretized onto a voxel grid at resolution ρ=0.01. Outputs include per-point primitive type labels across 11 classes (4 curve types, 6 surface types, 1 unknown), per-point edge and face membership assignments (obtained via HDBScan clustering of learned embeddings at inference), and a B-Rep-level topological adjacency structure encoded as point-to-BRep characteristic matrices of fixed size (N_e=128 edges, N_f=128 faces) that capture both element membership and inter-element connectivity.

## Method

SpelsNet unifies a spatial branch (SpelsNet^sp) and a topological branch (SpelsNet^vef) over a shared SparseCNN/ResUnet encoder. The spatial branch uses an MLP for 11-class primitive type prediction and a separate MLP with triplet loss for membership embedding, with HDBScan clustering at inference. The topological branch introduces a Graph Structure Learning (GSL) layer that predicts a weighted point-to-BRep adjacency matrix M^p (concatenating edge and face characteristic matrices) via an MLP with Tanh+LeakyReLU activations, supervised by L1 loss against ground-truth LAR^pcd matrices. A two-layer GCN then operates on the adjacency graph A~ = M~^p M~^{pT} with row-mean-pooled node features to predict B-Rep element types. The key insight is reformulating the Linear Algebraic Representation of the B-Rep chain complex into the point cloud domain, enabling direct topological supervision that was previously only available at the B-Rep element level. The full network is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of cross-entropy (type), triplet (segmentation), cross-entropy (graph type), and L1 (LAR) losses.

## Results

On ABCParts-VEF, SpelsNet achieves 82.35% face type IoU (tIoU) and 65.72% face segmentation IoU (sIoU) under standard alignment, and maintains 81.93% tIoU and 65.60% sIoU under random rotation augmentation — far more robust than ParSeNet (45.99% tIoU) or HPNet (14.93% tIoU) under the same augmentation. On the real-scan CC3D-VEF dataset, SpelsNet leads all baselines with 45.15% tIoU and 21.23% sIoU. For topology prediction (Table 2), SpelsNet achieves 90.01% face type accuracy and a face-edge connectivity error t_fe of 0.06, substantially outperforming ComplexGen (74.2% face type accuracy, t_fe = 0.201).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpelsNet outperforms all four baselines on face type IoU (tIoU) under rotation augmentation: 81.93% vs. 45.99% (ParSeNet), 14.93% (HPNet), 55.39% (PrimitiveNet), and 45.40% (ComplexGen) — a margin of 26–67 points. Under standard alignment (w/o aug), ParSeNet achieves a higher sIoU (78.19 vs. 65.72), but SpelsNet still leads on tIoU (82.35 vs. 81.86) and is far more robust to the alignment perturbation that reflects real scanning conditions. On the real-scan CC3D-VEF dataset, SpelsNet dominates every metric, achieving 45.15% tIoU versus the next-best 19.66% (ComplexGen) and 21.23% sIoU versus 15.22% (PrimitiveNet). For topology prediction, SpelsNet surpasses ComplexGen on face type accuracy (90.01% vs. 74.2%) and reduces the face-edge connectivity error t_fe from 0.201 to 0.06, while slightly edging out on edge type accuracy (77.22% vs. 76.3%). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the joint spatial + topological supervision (SpelsNet^{sp+vef}) improves edge sIoU from 46.69 to 50.01 and face sIoU from 59.69 to 65.60 over the spatial-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpelsNet extends the Linear Algebraic Representation (LAR) of B-Rep chain complexes from DiCarlo et al. [2014] by formulating a point-to-BRep adjacency that transfers LAR characteristic matrices from the B-Rep domain to the point cloud domain, enabling direct topological supervision. It departs from the dominant segmentation-fitting paradigm (ParSeNet, HPNet, PrimitiveNet) that treats surface patches and boundary curves in isolation, and contrasts with ComplexGen, which also leverages B-Rep chain complex structure but requires a time-consuming post-processing topological optimization step. The key architectural departure is unifying a SparseCNN spatial encoder with a GCN topological head in a single end-to-end trainable pipeline, rather than treating topology as a separate downstream problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide direct B-Rep chain complex supervision on point clouds, addressing the long-standing discontinuity problem in segmentation-based reverse engineering where predicted surface elements are topologically inconsistent. The released ABCParts-VEF and CC3D-VEF dataset extensions with LAR-based annotations lower the barrier for future topological supervision research. Key limitations include the fixed-size characteristic matrices (Ne=Nf=128) that must be tuned per dataset, the relatively weak GCN capacity compared to the spatial module, and notable performance degradation under rotation on real scans. Open questions include whether transformer-based GNNs can close the gap between spatial and topological predictions, and whether dynamic resolution strategies can improve robustness to varying input quality.
