## Problem

Standard neighborhood attention operates on fixed square windows, mixing features from perceptually unrelated regions across object boundaries and degrading denoising quality. While superpixels provide deformable, boundary-preserving neighborhoods, naive use of hard superpixel assignments (H-SNA) is theoretically and empirically worse than standard attention due to sensitivity to estimation errors and instability when few pixels share a superpixel label within the window.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy RGB image (128×128 patches in experiments) and produces a denoised RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. The denoising network is deliberately minimal—only two projection matrices (3→6→3 channels) and a single attention module—so that the superpixel re-weighting is the sole mechanism for inter-pixel interaction. Auxiliary shallow UNets (4.4k–8.8k parameters) estimate either per-pixel superpixel probabilities directly or SLIC hyperparameters (appearance/shape precisions) fed into five differentiable SLIC iterations.

## Method

SNA re-weights the standard neighborhood attention map by multiplying each pairwise attention score with the inner product of the query and key pixels' superpixel probability vectors, Σ_s π^(i,s)·π^(j,s), which smoothly interpolates between flat NA (uniform probabilities) and hard H-SNA (one-hot assignments). Under a latent superpixel model where each pixel independently samples its own superpixel probability distribution, the paper proves SNA is the optimal MSE denoiser when the attention scale equals 1/(2σ²) and qkv-transforms are identity. Superpixel probabilities are estimated from the noisy input via either a direct softmax UNet or a network predicting SLIC precision parameters followed by differentiable SLIC iterations. A normalization step re-scales neighbor superpixel probabilities to sum to one over the query's connected superpixel set, correcting artifacts from SLIC's restricted 9-neighbor connectivity.

## Results

On Gaussian denoising (σ=10, 20, 30) averaged over Set5, BSD100, Urban100, and Manga109, the best SNA variant (fixed attention scale, SLIC-based superpixels) achieves 32.19/0.871, 29.08/0.804, and 27.51/0.763 PSNR/SSIM respectively. SNA outperforms standard NA by approximately 2.8 dB PSNR at σ=30 (27.70 vs. 24.91) and by about 1.8 dB even when NA is augmented with a learned attention scale (27.51 vs. 25.69). H-SNA, the naive hard-superpixel variant, degrades severely at high noise (22.37 PSNR at σ=30), confirming the paper's theoretical argument that soft probabilities are essential. SNA is 15–22× slower than NA in wall-clock time but its additional FLOPs do not scale with the number of features F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Neighborhood Attention (NA) [Hassani et al., CVPR 2023], the state-of-the-art efficient local attention module. SNA consistently outperforms NA across all noise levels: at σ=30, the best SNA variant achieves 27.51 PSNR versus 24.91 for standard NA (a 2.6 dB gap) and 25.69 for NA with a learned attention scale (a 1.8 dB gap). Even at low noise (σ=10), SNA leads by 1.1 dB over standard NA (32.19 vs. 31.10). The intermediate H-SNA variant (hard superpixel masking) performs worse than both SNA and NA at high noise (22.37 vs. 24.91 at σ=30), validating the paper's theoretical claim that naive superpixel assignments are unstable and that soft probabilities are necessary. The paper also notes that SNA's computational cost is 15–22× that of NA, though the additional FLOPs are independent of the feature dimension, suggesting the gap can narrow in high-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neighborhood Attention (NA) by re-weighting the attention map with per-pixel superpixel probabilities, drawing on the SLIC superpixel formulation and the superpixel pooling framework from Superpixel Sampling Networks (SSN). The key conceptual departure from prior superpixel-based methods (which use hard ML assignments) is the use of soft probabilities, motivated by the observation that superpixel boundaries are inherently ambiguous across valid segmentations. The theoretical contribution derives SNA as the optimal denoiser under a latent superpixel model where superpixel probabilities are sampled independently per pixel, a modeling choice that differs from the standard shared-prior superpixel generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for incorporating perceptual groupings into local attention, showing that superpixel probabilities (not assignments) are the correct quantity to condition on. The finding that segmentation-trained superpixels actively degrade denoising quality (2.6 dB drop) while pixel-pooling-trained superpixels cooperate with denoising is a useful negative result for practitioners combining superpixel supervision with restoration tasks. However, the 15–22× computational overhead relative to NA, the single-layer experimental setup with only 200 denoising parameters, and the Gaussian-noise assumption limit immediate practical adoption; scaling SNA to deep multi-layer architectures and optimizing the CUDA kernel remain open questions.
