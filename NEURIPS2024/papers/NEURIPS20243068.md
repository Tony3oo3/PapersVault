## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought prompting methods rely on instance-specific solutions that lack generalizability (a solution for one equation system may not transfer to another) and task-level consistency (different few-shot examples may employ different solution approaches, confusing the LLM). No prior method automatically derives generalizable, task-level strategies and applies them consistently across few-shot examples without human-annotated reasoning traces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a small set of task examples (Q-A pairs, typically 4) and a task definition string, requiring no human-annotated solutions. It produces a strategy-based few-shot prompt consisting of a general multi-step strategy (a sequence of subtasks encoding task-level knowledge) followed by consistent step-by-step solutions that explicitly follow that strategy on each example. This prompt is generated once per task at a cost under $0.24 with GPT-3.5-Turbo, then reused for inference on the full test set where the LLM outputs a solution and final answer per question.

## Method

StrategyLLM is a multi-agent collaboration framework with four LLM-based agents: a strategy generator that produces n diverse strategies via temperature sampling, a strategy executor that applies each strategy to task examples and computes execution accuracy, a strategy optimizer that refines unqualified strategies based on their execution results in iterative cycles, and a strategy evaluator that ranks top-k strategies by validation accuracy. The key architectural insight is separating inductive reasoning (deriving a general strategy from specific instances) from deductive reasoning (applying that strategy consistently to produce solutions), which simultaneously addresses generalizability and consistency. Unlike CoT prompts where each example's solution may use a different approach, StrategyLLM prompts enforce a single strategy across all examples, and unlike Plan-and-Solve which generates instance-specific plans at inference time, StrategyLLM pre-computes task-level strategies that are reusable and cost-effective. For complex tasks, multiple complementary strategies are combined via majority voting (StrategyLLM-SC) or LLM-based answer selection (StrategyLLM-ZS).

## Results

On the MATH benchmark (7 subjects), StrategyLLM-ZS achieves 38.8% average accuracy, outperforming CoT-SC (34.2%) by +13.4% relative. On commonsense reasoning (StrategyQA + Date Understanding), StrategyLLM-SC reaches 72.5% vs. CoT-SC's 70.3% (+3.1%). The most dramatic gains appear in algorithmic reasoning (85.0% vs. 73.7%, +15.4%) and symbolic reasoning on out-of-distribution test sets (79.2% vs. 30.0%, +164.0%), where explicit task-level strategies enable substantial generalization beyond the 2-word examples seen in the few-shot prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StrategyLLM-SC outperforms CoT-SC (the strongest baseline requiring human-annotated solutions) on all four task categories: math reasoning (37.2% vs. 34.2%, +8.8%), commonsense reasoning (72.5% vs. 70.3%, +3.1%), algorithmic reasoning (85.0% vs. 73.7%, +15.4%), and symbolic reasoning (79.2% vs. 30.0%, +164.0%). Against Plan-and-Solve-SC, which generates instance-specific plans at inference time, StrategyLLM-SC achieves 66.7% average vs. 54.0% on CP, StrategyQA, and MA, demonstrating that generalizable task-level strategies substantially outperform per-instance planning. CoT+Strategy-SC, which instructs the LLM to devise a strategy on-the-fly, reaches 58.8% average, still well below StrategyLLM-SC's 66.7%, confirming that pre-constructed, validated strategy-based prompts are more effective than ad-hoc strategy generation. The single best StrategyLLM strategy (without self-consistency) already surpasses CoT and SolutionLLM on every dataset, isolating the benefit of the strategy layer itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

StrategyLLM extends the chain-of-thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) by inserting a generalizable strategy layer between the task definition and instance-specific solutions, addressing CoT's well-known limitations in cross-instance consistency and generalizability. It departs from Plan-and-Solve prompting (Wang et al., 2023), which generates instance-specific plans at inference time, by instead deriving task-level strategies once and applying them consistently across all examples. The multi-agent collaboration architecture (generator, executor, optimizer, evaluator) draws on the broader LLM-agent literature but is novel in its specific application to iterative strategy refinement, distinguishing it from learning-to-plan approaches that require large training sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical value lies in eliminating the need for human-annotated reasoning traces while achieving competitive or superior performance at a cost under $0.24 per task, making strategy-based prompting accessible for a wide range of applications. A key open question is the framework's ceiling in domains where the underlying LLM lacks sufficient knowledge to generate effective strategies, as the authors acknowledge that prompt optimization alone cannot compensate for missing domain expertise. The finding that inconsistent prompts (mixing strategies across examples) degrade performance substantially underscores the importance of consistency in few-shot design, a principle that may generalize beyond this specific framework.
