## Problem

Anchoring reparameterizes each input as a (reference, residual) tuple to improve uncertainty estimation and extrapolation, but the paper identifies that conventional anchored training fails to effectively leverage reference diversity, increasing the risk of the model learning non-generalizable shortcuts that rely on residuals alone. This limitation is particularly acute with large reference sets where the combinatorial number of reference-residual pairs cannot be fully explored within a fixed training budget, and no existing inference protocol (e.g., reference marginalization or bilinear transduction) can circumvent it.

## Input / Output

The model receives a tuple [r̄, x − r̄] formed by channel-wise concatenation of a reference image r̄ (sampled from a reference set R ⊆ D) and the residual x − r̄, effectively doubling the input channels at the first layer while leaving the rest of the architecture unchanged. At inference, a single random reference is drawn from R and the same concatenation is performed. The output is a standard class probability vector over C classes, used for both classification accuracy and calibration/anomaly-rejection scoring (e.g., energy scores for OOD detection).

## Method

The paper introduces a reference-masking regularizer that, with probability α per batch, zeroes out the reference component of the tuple (yielding [0, x − r̄]) and trains the model to produce a uniform prediction over all C classes via cross-entropy against a uniform prior. This systematically discourages the network from making confident predictions based solely on the residual, forcing it to genuinely exploit the reference information. The training loop alternates between standard anchored cross-entropy (with the true label) and the masked uniform-prior loss, with α tuned per dataset scale (0.25 for CIFAR, 0.2 for ImageNet). The approach is architecture-agnostic and composable with existing augmentations, optimizers, and schedulers, and is validated across ResNets, WideResNets, SWINv2, and ViT on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K, showing consistent gains in OOD corruption accuracy, calibration (Smoothed ECE), anomaly rejection (AUROC), and linear-probe transferability.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, the proposed reference-masking anchored training achieves 77.89% ID accuracy and improves OOD corruption accuracy by up to +4.89% (CIFAR-100-C Sev.3) over standard training, while vanilla anchoring gains only ~0.5–1%. On ImageNet-1K with SWINv2-B (87.8M params), the proposed method reaches 84.09% ID accuracy and delivers up to +5.02% gains on ImageNet-C (noise) at severity 5, with larger-capacity models showing increasingly larger improvements. For anomaly rejection, ViT-B-16 with the proposed approach achieves 70.32% AUROC on NINCO (+4.34% over standard) and consistently lower Smoothed ECE across all OOD benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training protocols: Standard Training, Vanilla Anchoring (from Thiagarajan et al., NeurIPS 2022), and the Proposed method (anchoring + reference-masking regularizer). Vanilla anchoring provides only marginal OOD gains over standard training (0.5–1% on CIFAR, <1% on ImageNet), confirming the paper's diagnosis that conventional anchored training fails to leverage reference diversity. The proposed method consistently outperforms both baselines across all architectures and datasets: on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), it achieves +4.89% over standard and +4.80% over vanilla anchoring at CIFAR-100-C Sev.3; on ImageNet-1K (SWINv2-B), it delivers +5.02% over standard at ImageNet-C (noise) Sev.5. ID accuracy is preserved or slightly improved (e.g., +0.57% on CIFAR-10, −0.02% on ImageNet for SWINv2-B). For safety metrics, the proposed method achieves lower Smoothed ECE (e.g., 0.105 vs 0.109 for ViT-B-16) and higher anomaly-rejection AUROC (e.g., 70.32 vs 65.98 on NINCO for ViT-B-16) compared to standard training, while vanilla anchoring shows inconsistent or degraded anomaly rejection in some cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the anchoring principle introduced by Thiagarajan et al. (NeurIPS 2022) for epistemic uncertainty estimation and Netanyahu et al. (ICLR 2023) for transductive extrapolation in regression, reframing it as a general training protocol for large-scale vision classification. The key departure is identifying a shortcut-learning failure mode in vanilla anchored training—where the model ignores the reference and predicts from residuals alone—and addressing it with a reference-masking regularizer that forces the model to depend on the reference. The paper positions itself as the first systematic exploration of anchoring across CNN and transformer architectures at ImageNet scale, and explicitly distinguishes anchoring from data augmentation by noting it creates relative representations rather than pixel-space perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in anchoring being a drop-in, architecture-agnostic wrapper that simultaneously improves OOD generalization, calibration, and anomaly rejection—objectives that Hendrycks et al. argued are in tension—without additional inference cost. Limitations include the absence of a theoretical characterization of why anchoring generalizes, a mild ID accuracy drop in some transformer configurations, and the need to tune the masking probability α based on reference set size. Open questions include integrating anchoring with model souping and large-scale pre-training/fine-tuning pipelines, and developing a formal understanding of the hypothesis-class expansion that anchoring induces.
