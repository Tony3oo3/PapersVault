## Problem

In standard multi-armed bandits, the learner observes numerical reward values after each pull. This paper introduces and studies a fundamentally different feedback model—bandits with ranking feedback—where the learner only observes the ordering of arms by their empirical means, without any quantitative information about performance gaps. The central question is whether this lack of numerical scores precludes sublinear regret or necessarily worsens the achievable bounds, a question with practical relevance to settings like human preference elicitation and privacy-constrained advertising where exact values cannot be revealed.

## Input / Output

At each round, the learner selects an arm and receives as feedback a complete ranking over all arms based on their empirical means computed from all previous pulls (not just the current round's reward). The learner never observes the actual reward realization or the numerical gap between arms; ties in the ranking are broken by lower arm index. This makes the feedback inherently history-dependent and heavily correlated across rounds, since the empirical means accumulate over the entire interaction. The output is a randomized policy mapping the full history of rankings and arm selections to a distribution over arms, evaluated via cumulative regret against the best arm in hindsight.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary algorithms. DREE (Dynamical Ranking Exploration-Exploitation) explores each arm until it has been pulled f(t) times for a superlogarithmic function f (e.g., log(t)^{1+δ}), then exploits the top-ranked arm, achieving a superlogarithmic instance-dependent regret that matches a proven lower bound showing logarithmic regret is impossible. R-LPE (Ranking Logarithmic Phased Elimination) uses a loggrid of pull-count thresholds and a filtering condition that counts, over the entire ranking history, how often each arm has been ranked above every other active arm, eliminating arms that fail to meet a time-dependent threshold; this achieves Õ(√T) instance-independent regret for Gaussian rewards. The R-LPE analysis requires novel discretization results linking the correlated ranking process to biased Brownian motions, since standard concentration inequalities are inapplicable due to the history-dependent feedback. The paper also proves a fundamental trade-off: no single algorithm can simultaneously achieve optimal instance-dependent and instance-independent regret bounds, and no algorithm achieves sublinear regret under adversarial rewards.

## Results

The paper establishes that no algorithm can achieve logarithmic instance-dependent regret in bandits with ranking feedback (Theorem 1), and provides DREE which matches this lower bound with a superlogarithmic regret of (1+f(T))ΣΔᵢ + log(T)ΣC(f,Δᵢ) where f(t)=log(t)^{1+δ}. For the instance-independent case, R-LPE achieves R_T ≤ 62n⁴ log(T)² T^{1/2} under Gaussian rewards, breaking the O(T^{2/3}) barrier of Explore-then-Commit. A fundamental trade-off is proven: any algorithm satisfying instance-dependent regret O(T^α) and instance-independent regret O(T^β) must obey 2α+β≥1. In the adversarial setting, no algorithm can achieve sublinear regret (Theorem 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Explore-then-Commit (EC) algorithm from Auer et al. (2002), which trivially extends to the ranking feedback setting and achieves O(T^{2/3}) instance-independent regret. R-LPE strictly improves upon this, guaranteeing Õ(√T) instance-independent regret under Gaussian rewards, representing a meaningful reduction in the exponent from 2/3 to 1/2. For the instance-dependent case, DREE is the first algorithm to match the newly proven superlogarithmic lower bound, whereas EC would suffer linear regret in T (up to logarithmic factors) in this regime. The paper also proves that no single algorithm can simultaneously achieve optimal bounds in both the instance-dependent and instance-independent settings (2α+β≥1), necessitating the two-algorithm approach. In the adversarial setting, the impossibility result shows that ranking feedback fundamentally precludes sublinear regret, in contrast to standard bandits where algorithms like UCB or EXP3 achieve sublinear bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces bandits with ranking feedback as a fundamentally new problem formulation, explicitly positioned as unexplored prior work. It departs from standard multi-armed bandits (where numerical rewards are observed) and from dueling bandits (where pairwise comparisons are made), instead providing a complete ranking over all arms' empirical means without quantifying performance differences. The algorithmic design draws on explore-then-commit strategies (Auer et al., 2002) and phased elimination, while the R-LPE analysis relies on novel discretization of Brownian motions building on Takács (1996). The connection to preference learning and preference-based RL is acknowledged, but the stateless, cumulative-mean-based ranking distinguishes this model from those settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers a fundamental theoretical question—how crucial numerical rewards are in bandit settings—by showing that ranking feedback fundamentally alters achievable regret: no logarithmic instance-dependent regret is possible, no sublinear adversarial regret exists, and no single algorithm can be simultaneously optimal in both instance-dependent and instance-independent regimes. These negative results are strong and well-proven, establishing clear boundaries for the problem. The R-LPE algorithm's Õ(√T) instance-independent bound (breaking the O(T^{2/3}) EC barrier) via Brownian motion discretization is a technically novel contribution of independent interest. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-reward assumption for R-LPE can be relaxed, and whether the exponential dependence on 1/Δ in instance-dependent algorithms is truly unavoidable.
