## Problem

Existing point cloud assembly methods rely on estimating correspondences between input clouds, making them fail on non-overlapped clouds and sensitive to arbitrary initial positions. No prior method simultaneously provides correspondence-free assembly with a theoretical guarantee that performance is invariant to rigid perturbations of the inputs (SE(3)-bi-equivariance).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D point clouds (source X with M points and reference Y with N points) with no assumed correspondence or overlap. Internally, L=32 key points are extracted from each cloud via a shared SE(3)-transformer and concatenated into a 6D point cloud Z. The output is a single rigid transformation g=(r,t)∈SE(3) mapping the source to the reference, computed via an SVD projection of learned equivariant features.

## Method

BITR generalizes Arun's closed-form registration by replacing the handcrafted correlation matrix with learned SO(3)×SO(3)-equivariant features extracted by a novel SE(3)×SE(3)-transformer operating on the 6D merged point cloud. The transformer kernel is derived from the kernel constraint using 2nd-order Clebsch-Gordan coefficients and spherical harmonics, making it the first SE(3)×SE(3)-equivariant steerable network. The final SE(3) projection (Eq. 13) extends Arun's SVD step by incorporating learned degree-(1,0) and degree-(0,1) translation offsets, allowing solutions where cloud centers do not coincide. The paper further proves that swap-equivariance and scale-equivariance can be enforced through weight-sharing constraints on the transformer and Elu layers, guaranteeing stable performance under input permutation and uniform scaling.

## Results

On the 7Scenes dataset, BITR from random initialization achieves a rotation error of Δr=26.7°, and with OT refinement reaches Δr=9.5°, comparable to GEO (9.2°) and ROI (9.0°), while ICP (73.2°) and OMN (129.0°) fail due to sensitivity to initial positions. On the 2-fragment WineBottle reassembly (BB dataset), BITR achieves Δr=8.4° (std 0.9°) and Δt=0.07, dramatically outperforming DGL (101.3°), NSM (101°), and LEV (98.3°). On ShapeNet single-shape assembly, BITR outperforms all baselines when the overlap ratio s≤0.5 (non-overlapped PCs), and with ICP refinement (BITR+ICP) matches GEO for s>0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 7Scenes, BITR alone (Δr=26.7°) is outperformed by GEO (9.2°) and ROI (9.0°), but BITR+OT (9.5°) is essentially on par with both, while ICP (73.2°) and OMN (129.0°) fail catastrophically from random initialization. On the BB fragment reassembly task, BITR achieves Δr=8.4°, a roughly 12× improvement over the best baseline LEV (98.3°), and also has the lowest translation error (0.07 vs 0.09–0.18). On ShapeNet with non-overlapped PCs (s≤0.5), BITR outperforms GEO, ROI, NSM, and LEV; for s>0.5, BITR is slightly worse than GEO but BITR+ICP closes the gap. The key differentiator is that BITR is correspondence-free and SE(3)-bi-equivariant, so it does not require known correspondences or canonical poses, unlike NSM and LEV which need the true canonical pose, and unlike GEO/ROI which rely on learned correspondence features.

## Relation to Prior Work

BITR generalizes Arun's closed-form registration solution by replacing the handcrafted correlation matrix with learned SO(3)×SO(3)-equivariant features extracted by a novel SE(3)×SE(3)-transformer, which is itself a direct extension of the SE(3)-transformer [11] to the product group. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant correspondence-based registration paradigm (GEO, ROI, ICP) and from fragment reassembly methods that rely on canonical poses (NSM, LEV), instead using SE(3)-bi-equivariance as a structural prior that eliminates the need for correspondence entirely. The theoretical incorporation of swap and scale equivariance via weight-sharing constraints on the transformer layers is a new contribution not present in prior equivariant network literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SE(3)-bi-equivariance guarantee means BITR's performance is provably independent of initial positions, addressing a key practical failure mode of correspondence-based methods, and the correspondence-free design extends applicability to non-overlapped point clouds where registration methods fundamentally break down. The complete-matching property (Prop. C.11) — that any swap- and SE(3)-bi-equivariant method can exactly recover the relative transform between two fully overlapped PCs without knowing correspondence — is a clean theoretical result with potential algorithmic implications. Main limitations are computational inefficiency (independent computation per equivariant degree) and the deterministic nature of the model, which cannot handle symmetric point clouds; the authors suggest acceleration techniques and generalization to generative models as future directions.
