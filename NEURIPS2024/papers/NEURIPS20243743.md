## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based methods for inverse problems interleave reverse diffusion steps with data-consistency updates, which prevents the final estimate from simultaneously satisfying manifold feasibility (looking like a natural object) and measurement feasibility (fitting the observation), especially for nonlinear inverse problems. Additionally, these methods assume known noise types and levels, limiting their practical robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y (e.g., a downsampled, blurred, or partially masked 256×256 RGB image), the forward model A (linear or nonlinear), and a randomly initialized seed z ~ N(0,I). It outputs a recovered image x = R(z), and for blind deblurring tasks additionally recovers a blur kernel k (constrained to the simplex via SoftMax) and, for turbulence, a tilt map φ (initialized from a small-variance Gaussian). Notably, the forward model is never modified during optimization—only the seed z is updated.

## Method

DMPlug views the entire DDIM reverse diffusion process as a fixed, deterministic function R(·) mapping from seed space to the object manifold, then reparameterizes the unknown as x = R(z) and plugs it into the standard regularized data-fitting objective min_z ℓ(y, A(R(z))) + Ω(R(z)), optimizing over z with ADAM or L-BFGS. Because R is never modified, manifold feasibility is inherently preserved, while global optimization of the data-fitting term promotes measurement feasibility. Computationally, only 3 DDIM reverse steps are used (vs. 50–1000 in generation), making gradient backpropagation through R tractable. For unknown noise, the paper identifies an 'early-learning-then-overfitting' (ELTO) property—recovery quality peaks before noise is absorbed—and integrates the ES-WMV early-stopping criterion (detecting the first major valley in the running-variance curve of intermediate reconstructions) to halt optimization at the peak without ground-truth access.

## Results

On nonlinear deblurring (CelebA, FFHQ, LSUN), DMPlug achieves 31.61, 32.83, and 30.74 dB PSNR respectively, outperforming the best SOTA method ReSample by 3.09–5.79 dB. On blind image deblurring (BID), DMPlug leads the best competitor by 3.69–6.22 dB in PSNR across motion and Gaussian blur settings. On linear IPs (4× super-resolution and 70% inpainting), DMPlug leads by approximately 1.2–2.4 dB in PSNR. Under unknown noise types and levels, DMPlug's peak performance leads SOTA by ~1 dB (linear) and ~3.5 dB (nonlinear), with ES-WMV early stopping incurring <0.5 dB detection gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMPlug decisively outperforms all 20 baselines across every task and dataset. Among DM-based interleaving methods, ReSample [20] is the strongest SOTA competitor on nonlinear deblurring (28.52 dB on CelebA) but is surpassed by DMPlug by 3.09–5.79 dB in PSNR; DPS [19] and ILVR [41] trail ReSample by 3–4 dB and are outperformed by DMPlug by 6–11 dB. On BID, BlindDPS [21] (which uses additional DM priors for kernels and tilt maps, an unfair advantage) achieves 23.60–25.15 dB, while DMPlug using only an image prior reaches 27.99–29.61 dB, a 3.7–6.0 dB margin. Supervised methods (DeBlurGANv2 [5], Stripformer [6], MPRNet [7], TSR-WGAN [67]) and classical MAP/DIP methods (Pan-DCP [70], Pan-ℓ₀ [71], SelfDeblur [72]) are all outperformed by 4–14 dB on BID. On linear IPs, DMPlug leads DPS [19] and ReSample [20] by ~2 dB in PSNR for super-resolution and inpainting, while FPS [80] and DiffPIR [28] are the closest competitors on CelebA super-resolution (29.12 and 31.55 dB vs. DMPlug's 31.25 dB), with DMPlug still winning on LPIPS and SSIM. Under unknown noise, most methods (ADMM-PnP [73], DMPS [29], DDRM [32], MCG [30], ReSample [20]) degrade substantially, while DMPlug maintains 25–28 dB PSNR across all four noise types and levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMPlug departs fundamentally from the dominant interleaving paradigm (DPS, ReSample, MCG, ILVR, DDRM) that alternates reverse diffusion steps with projection or gradient steps toward the measurement feasible set. Instead, it treats the entire DDIM reverse process as a fixed function R(·) and optimizes only over the seed z, conceptually analogous to GAN inversion but applied to diffusion models with fixed weights. The method also integrates ES-WMV (a running-variance early-stopping strategy from the deep image prior literature) to handle unknown noise, and draws on the spectral bias analysis from DIP to explain the observed early-learning-then-overfitting behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By never modifying the reverse diffusion process, DMPlug simultaneously addresses manifold feasibility, measurement feasibility, and noise robustness—three issues that interleaving methods struggle with, especially for nonlinear inverse problems. The finding that only 3 reverse steps suffice (far fewer than needed for generation) suggests a fundamental gap between image generation and image regression that remains unexplained theoretically. Key limitations include high computational cost (~635 s per image with ADAM), the absence of theoretical guarantees, and the empirical (rather than principled) nature of the ELTO phenomenon and its exploitation via early stopping.
