## Problem

Common neural network parameterizations and normalizations (Standard Parameterization, Weight Normalization, Batch Normalization, Layer Normalization) operate in Cartesian coordinates, where even infinitesimal stochastic gradient noise can cause arbitrarily large rotations of ReLU characteristic activation boundaries (CABs). This instability destabilizes the evolution of ReLU features during training, impedes fast convergence, and degrades generalization performance, yet the underlying geometric cause has not been formally identified or addressed.

## Input / Output

Each ReLU unit under GmP takes a data vector x ∈ R^n (tabular features or flattened image patches) and produces a scalar activation z = r·ReLU(u(θ)ᵀx + λ). For multi-layer networks, an Input Mean Normalization (IMN) layer centers the input using mini-batch empirical means before the ReLU computation, addressing internal covariate shift without learnable parameters. The overall network produces standard predictions: regression values for UCI benchmarks and class probabilities for ImageNet/ImageNet32 classification.

## Method

Geometric Parameterization (GmP) reparameterizes each ReLU unit in hyperspherical coordinates: z = r·ReLU(u(θ)ᵀx + λ), where the unit directional vector u(θ) ∈ S^{n-1} is determined by angles θ = [θ₁,…,θ_{n-1}], the radius λ controls the distance from the origin to the CAB, and the scale r controls activation magnitude. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.7) proves that the angular change of a CAB under perturbation ε to θ is bounded by ‖ε‖₂, via the metric tensor of the hyperspherical coordinate system, in contrast to Cartesian parameterizations where the angular change is unbounded. This automatic radial-angular decoupling ensures smooth, stable evolution of CABs under stochastic gradient noise, enabling 10× larger learning rates. For multi-layer networks, a parameter-free Input Mean Normalization (IMN) layer centers intermediate-layer inputs to maintain the assumption that data centers around the origin. GmP requires no layer-size-dependent initialization (θ sampled from von Mises-Fisher, λ=0, r=1) and has the same O(mn) computational cost per layer as standard parameterization.

## Results

On ImageNet32 with VGG-6 (batch size 512), GmP+IMN achieves 43.62% top-1 and 67.76% top-5 validation accuracy, outperforming BN (41.90%/66.50%) and WN+MBN (40.96%/65.08%). On full ImageNet with ResNet-18, GmP+IMN reaches 67.24% top-1 and 87.19% top-5 validation accuracy, surpassing BN (66.85%/86.92%) and WN+MBN (66.57%/86.69%). On 7 UCI regression benchmarks, GmP achieves the best test RMSE in 6 out of 7 tasks (e.g., 0.474 on Energy vs. 0.898 for SP and 1.648 for BN). GmP also enables a 10× larger learning rate (0.1 vs. 0.01) while maintaining stable training, and converges 10 epochs faster than BN on ImageNet32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Weight Normalization (Salimans & Kingma, NeurIPS 2016), GmP+IMN consistently wins: on ImageNet32 (bs=512) it gains +2.66% top-1 over WN+MBN (43.62% vs. 40.96%) and +1.73% over WN alone (43.62% vs. 37.92%); on full ImageNet with ResNet-18 it gains +0.67% top-1 over WN+MBN (67.24% vs. 66.57%). Against Batch Normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, ICML 2015), GmP+IMN also wins on both image benchmarks: +1.72% top-1 on ImageNet32 (bs=512, 43.62% vs. 41.90%) and +0.39% top-1 on ImageNet (67.24% vs. 66.85%). On UCI regression, GmP (without IMN) outperforms BN on 5 of 7 benchmarks (e.g., Energy: 0.474 vs. 1.648 RMSE) and WN on all 7. Notably, the gap between GmP+IMN and BN widens with increasing batch size on ImageNet32 (from +0.35% at bs=256 to +1.31% at bs=1024), suggesting GmP scales better in the large-batch regime. The paper also notes that VGG-6 with GmP+IMN (43.62% top-1) outperforms the much larger Wide ResNet 28-2 with BN (43.08% top-1) on ImageNet32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Cartesian-coordinate parameterizations (SP, WN, BN, LN) by introducing Geometric Parameterization (GmP), which reparameterizes ReLU units in hyperspherical coordinates, automatically decoupling the radial (bias-to-weight-ratio) and angular (weight direction) components of the characteristic activation boundary. Conceptually, it extends the length-direction decoupling idea of Weight Normalization [56] but moves it to a coordinate system where the angular component is intrinsically stable under perturbation, as formalized via the metric tensor on the unit hypersphere. The paper also introduces a new analytical lens—the Characteristic Activation Boundary (CAB)—that unifies the treatment of SP, WN, BN, and LN under a single geometric framework, revealing they all share the same fundamental angular instability. The connection to natural gradient descent (Appendix D) positions GmP as a coordinate choice that partially recovers parameterization-invariance properties of NGD without requiring second-order computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all common Cartesian-coordinate parameterizations and normalizations (SP, WN, BN, LN) share an identical angular instability under stochastic gradient noise is a unifying theoretical insight that reframes how practitioners think about normalization layers. Practically, GmP+IMN enables 10× larger learning rates and modest but consistent accuracy gains (e.g., 67.24% vs 66.85% top-1 on ImageNet with ResNet-18), and the ImageNet32 result where a small VGG-6 with GmP+IMN outperforms a much larger WRN 28-2 with BN highlights the potential of better parameterization over architectural scale. Key limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to single-hidden-layer networks (with IMN as an empirical workaround for depth), the restriction to ReLU-family activations, and the need for the additional IMN layer in deep architectures. Open questions include generalizing the CAB analysis to multi-layer piecewise-linear partitions, analyzing smooth activations, and studying large-width limiting behavior of GmP.
