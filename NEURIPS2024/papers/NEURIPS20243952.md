## Problem

Structural bias in graph topology—where denser intra-group connectivity amplifies disparate impacts in link prediction—has not been theoretically characterized for non-binary sensitive attributes, and its interaction with generative models remains unexplored. This paper shows that diffusion-based graph generation consistently amplifies existing structural bias in synthetic graphs, creating a critical gap for deploying fair graph ML in privacy-sensitive, large-scale settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real graph (adjacency matrix, node feature matrix, and one-hot sensitive attribute vector) and outputs synthetic graphs comprising a new topology, node features, and synthetic sensitive attributes sampled from the original attribute distribution. The fairness regularizer L_FairWire operates on predicted edge probabilities within a mini-batch of nodes, making it scalable to large graphs without requiring full-graph computation. The same regularizer can be plugged into any model that outputs a probabilistic adjacency matrix, including link prediction GNNs and other generative architectures.

## Method

The paper first derives a theoretical upper bound (Theorem 1) showing that GNN representation disparity between sensitive groups is governed by the ratio of expected intra- to inter-edges per group, then translates this into a batch-wise fairness regularizer L_FairWire that penalizes deviation from the optimal intra/inter-edge ratio proportional to group sizes. FairWire embeds this regularizer into a discrete denoising diffusion model: a two-layer MPNN denoiser is conditioned on sensitive attributes (concatenated into message passing) to capture attribute-topology correlations, and is trained with cross-entropy losses on node features and edge labels plus the fairness regularizer on predicted edge probabilities. During sampling, synthetic sensitive attributes are drawn from the original distribution and guide the reverse diffusion, enabling fair graph generation without leaking real sensitive information. The framework is task-agnostic—the regularizer can also be applied directly to link prediction training or any other generative model producing edge probabilities.

## Results

For link prediction, L_FairWire reduces statistical parity (ΔSP) by 20–80% compared to the unregularized GNN baseline across all four datasets (e.g., 4.76% vs. 27.01% on Cora, 8.62% vs. 27.40% on Citeseer) while maintaining comparable AUC. For graph generation, FairWire reduces ΔSP by 51–64% over fairness-agnostic GraphMaker synthetic graphs (e.g., 12.91% vs. 35.72% on Cora, 18.35% vs. 37.56% on Citeseer) with only modest AUC drops. On node classification, FairWire achieves ΔSP of 0.63% on German Credit and 1.91% on Pokec-n, outperforming all baselines while retaining accuracy within ~1% of the original graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairWire outperforms all fairness-aware baselines on the utility/fairness trade-off. Against FairAdj (Li et al., ICLR 2021), FairWire achieves lower ΔSP (12.91% vs. 15.47% on Cora) while retaining 4.4% higher AUC (86.49% vs. 82.13%), showing FairAdj's fairness gains come at a significant utility cost. Against Adversarial regularization (Dai et al., WSDM 2021), FairWire achieves substantially lower ΔSP (12.91% vs. 16.35% on Cora) with much tighter variance (±6.35 vs. ±9.80), indicating more stable fairness. Against FairDrop (Spinelli et al., IEEE TAI 2021), L_FairWire as a link-prediction regularizer achieves ΔSP of 4.76% vs. 7.86% on Cora with comparable AUC (92.18% vs. 94.10%). Against the fairness-agnostic GraphMaker (Li et al., 2023), FairWire reduces ΔSP by 64% on Cora and 51% on Citeseer while preserving AUC within 1–2%. For node classification, FairWire dominates FairGen (Zheng et al., 2023) on German Credit (ΔSP 0.63% vs. 28.71%) and Pokec-n (ΔSP 1.91% vs. 0.00% but with 68.23% vs. 51.73% accuracy), showing FairGen's synthetic graphs are uninformative for downstream tasks. Traditional baselines (ER, SBM, GAE, VGAE, Feature-based MF) in Table 8 show much larger 1-Wasserstein distances to the original graph statistics, confirming FairWire generates structurally faithful synthetic graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on structured denoising diffusion models for discrete data (Austin et al., 2021) and the GraphMaker MPNN architecture (Li et al., 2023) to construct a fair graph generation framework. It departs from prior fairness-aware graph ML strategies (adversarial regularization, FairDrop, FairAdj) by grounding the fairness regularizer in a theoretical analysis of how graph topology affects statistical parity for link prediction, extending the binary-attribute analysis of Li et al. (ICLR 2021) to multi-valued sensitive attributes. Unlike the only prior fair graph generation work (FairGen, Zheng et al., 2023), which outputs only graph structure and measures fairness via generation-quality disparities, FairWire generates full attributed graphs with sensitive attributes and evaluates fairness through downstream task performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that diffusion-based graph generation amplifies structural bias is a timely warning for the growing adoption of synthetic graphs in privacy-sensitive applications, and the proposed batch-wise regularizer offers a plug-and-play mitigation that is task-agnostic and compatible with any model outputting edge probabilities. Key limitations include the requirement that sensitive attributes be available during training and the reliance on assumptions (A1–A3) that, while validated on the tested datasets, may not hold universally. Open questions include extending the framework to settings with only partial sensitive attribute availability and deriving the theoretical bounds without the current assumptions.
