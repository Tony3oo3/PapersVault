## Problem

Score-based diffusion models enable principled prior sampling via denoising oracles, but existing posterior sampling methods for linear inverse problems rely on heuristics (conditional score approximation, variational families, particle filters) that lack quantitative guarantees. This paper first establishes that no generic method can efficiently sample the posterior when the measurement operator is even mildly ill-conditioned, even with a perfect prior denoising oracle, and then provides a provable solution under well-conditioned measurements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + σw, the known linear operator A ∈ R^{d'×d}, a pre-trained denoising oracle DO_π(y,σ) for the prior, and the noise level σ. It produces exact samples from the posterior ν(x) ∝ π(x) exp(−‖Ax−y‖²/2σ²) via a two-step procedure: first draw a sample from the boosted posterior ν_{T*} (which has enhanced SNR and a smoother, more Gaussian-like prior), then run the reverse SDE from time T* to 0 using the prior score. The method is plug-and-play and adds no extra computational cost beyond the baseline sampler applied to the boosted posterior.

## Method

The tilted transport technique exploits the shared quadratic structure between the posterior tilt ν = T_{Q,b}π and the OU semigroup. By solving a coupled ODE system (Q̇_t = 2(I+Q_t)Q_t, ḃ_t = (I+2Q_t)b_t) derived from the viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation, the authors construct a time-varying family ν_t = T_{Q_t,b_t}π_t such that running the reverse SDE from ν_t to ν_0 exactly recovers the posterior. At the blowup time T* = ½log(1+λ_max(Q)^{-1}), the boosted posterior ν_{T*} has a strictly larger minimum eigenvalue (higher effective SNR) and a prior π_{T*} closer to a single-mode Gaussian. A sufficient condition for ν_{T*} to be strongly log-concave—enabling efficient Langevin sampling—is expressed in terms of the condition number κ(A), the SNR, and a 'tilted spread' χ_{‖Q‖}(π) of the prior, yielding a U-shaped phase diagram where both low and high SNR regimes are tractable. For Ising models, this recovers the exact λ_max(Q)−λ_min(Q)<1 threshold matching Glauber dynamics and the low-degree method.

## Results

On Gaussian mixture priors (d=20, 40, 80; κ(A)=20), boosted LMC achieves consistently lower Sliced Wasserstein Distance than vanilla LMC across all SNR values from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻¹, with the largest gains in the low- and high-SNR regimes. In the degenerate case (d=20, d′=0.9d), boosted Langevin reduces SWD from 4.21±1.87 to 2.32±1.87, nearly halving the error. On FFHQ imaging tasks, the tilted transport boost improves PSNR by up to 0.85 dB (inpainting: 22.458→23.312) and reduces LPIPS by up to 0.033 (inpainting: 0.131→0.098) over the DMPS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Langevin Monte Carlo (Roberts & Tweedie [52]), the tilted transport boost consistently reduces Sliced Wasserstein Distance across all tested SNR values and dimensions (d=20, 40, 80); in the degenerate d′=0.9d setting at d=20, boosted Langevin achieves SWD 2.32±1.87 versus 4.21±1.87 for vanilla Langevin, a roughly 45% reduction. Against HMC/NUTS (Hoffman [34]) in the extreme d′=1 case where the posterior remains heavily multimodal, boosted HMC still outperforms vanilla HMC, confirming the method's utility even when the boosted posterior is not strongly log-concave. Against DMPS (Chung et al. [22]) on FFHQ imaging tasks, the boost wins on PSNR for all four tasks (denoising, inpainting, super-resolution, deblur) and on LPIPS for all four tasks, with the largest PSNR gain of +0.85 dB on inpainting; SSIM is essentially tied or slightly worse on super-resolution (0.754 vs 0.760), indicating the improvement is most pronounced in perceptual quality rather than structural similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion model framework (DDPM, SDE-based generative modeling) and the growing literature on posterior sampling with diffusion priors (DMPS, SNIPS, variational approaches, sequential Monte Carlo). It departs from these methods by exploiting the Hamilton-Jacobi equation structure of the OU semigroup to exactly transform the posterior sampling problem into a provably easier one via 'tilted transport,' rather than approximating conditional scores or restricting to a variational family. The hardness result connects to the Ising model sampling literature (Glauber dynamics, low-degree method) and complements the cryptographic hardness results of Gupta et al. [30], while the positive results leverage the Bakry-Emery criterion and log-Sobolev inequalities for quantitative guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide both a hardness result (showing that no generic method can efficiently sample the posterior with a denoising oracle when the measurement operator is mildly ill-conditioned) and a constructive solution (tilted transport) with clean, verifiable conditions involving the condition number and SNR. The technique is plug-and-play, adds no computational cost beyond the reverse SDE, and matches the best-known Ising model sampling threshold. Key open questions include extending to poorly-conditioned measurements (e.g., inpainting), nonlinear inverse problems, and establishing discrete-time complexity bounds; the 'iterated tilted transport' for cases where the boosted posterior is not strongly log-concave introduces approximation errors that remain unquantified.
