## Problem

Continual learning has focused almost exclusively on mitigating catastrophic forgetting, implicitly assuming that jointly trained deep representations are beneficial. However, in online continual learning where networks receive only one sample per timestep and are never trained to convergence, it remains unvalidated whether these continually learned representations actually outperform simple fixed functions. This paper empirically demonstrates that they do not — random projections consistently surpass state-of-the-art continually learned representations across standard benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241200/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RanDumb consumes raw image pixels flattened into vectors (784-dim for MNIST, 3072-dim for 32×32 RGB images) or 768-dim features from a frozen pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone, and outputs a class label via nearest class mean. Critically, the method processes exactly one sample per timestep in a single pass over the data stream, storing no exemplars and maintaining only running class means and a shrinked covariance matrix. In the pretrained-model setting (Benchmark F), the input is the fixed 768-dim ViT feature vector, and the method still sees only one sample at a time despite the benchmark allowing unrestricted training.

## Method

RanDumb combines two components: (1) a fixed random Fourier projection that maps raw pixels into a 25K-dimensional space, providing a data-independent low-rank approximation of the RBF kernel that creates clearer inter-class separation, and (2) a Mahalanobis-distance-based nearest class mean classifier that decorrelates the anisotropic projected features using an OAS-shrinked covariance matrix (inverse computed via ridge regularization). During online learning, the only updates are to the running per-class mean and the empirical covariance matrix — no backpropagation, no deep network training, and no exemplar storage are required. In the pretrained-model variant, the random projection is replaced by the frozen ViT-B/16 backbone while retaining the same linear classifier. The key insight is that the random projection's high-dimensional embedding plus explicit decorrelation recovers 70–90% of the performance gap to joint training, exposing that the continually trained deep networks in existing OCL methods fail to learn effective representations under these constrained conditions.

## Results

On the online continual learning benchmark (1 class per task, no exemplars), RanDumb achieves 98.3% on MNIST, 55.6% on CIFAR-10, 28.6% on CIFAR-100, and 17.7% on miniImageNet, outperforming the prior best method PEC by 2–6% on three of four datasets. Against the continually learned representation method VAE-GC, RanDumb gains 5.6–14.3% across all datasets, demonstrating that random Fourier projections with Mahalanobis NCM substantially outperform deep networks trained online. On the offline benchmark with pretrained ViT-B/16 (1 class per task), RanDumb covers 60–97% of the gap to joint training, and outperforms the finetuned-representation method SLCA by up to 48.9% (Stanford Cars) while matching it on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest online CL method PEC [76], RanDumb wins on MNIST (+6.0%), CIFAR-100 (+2.1%), and miniImageNet (+2.8%) but loses on CIFAR-10 (−3.3%). Against the continually learned representation method VAE-GC [64], RanDumb wins by wide margins of 5.6–14.3% on all four online datasets, establishing that the deep network's online-trained features are inferior to a fixed random projection. On the pretrained-model benchmark, RanDumb outperforms SLCA [79] (a finetuned-representation method) by +10.7% on ImageNet-R, +6.4% on CUB, and +48.9% on Stanford Cars, while tying on CIFAR-10. Against RanPAC-imp [41] (random projection + first-session adaptation), RanDumb wins on ImageNet-A (+8.4%) and Stanford Cars (+9.8%) but trails on CIFAR-10 (−2.6%) and ImageNet-R (−4.5%). In high-memory settings (≥1K exemplars), GDumb [49] and SEDEM [75] remain competitive or superior, confirming RanDumb's advantage is specific to low-exemplar and exemplar-free regimes. Prompt-tuning methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) collapse to 0.3–6.3% accuracy under the 1-class-per-task protocol, while RanDumb maintains 42–93%, highlighting a fundamental fragility of learned prompts over long task sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

RanDumb repurposes the random Fourier features framework of Rahimi and Recht (NeurIPS 2007) — originally designed for large-scale kernel approximation — as a fixed, data-independent embedding for online continual learning, paired with a streaming LDA-style classifier (nearest class mean with Mahalanobis decorrelation). Conceptually, it sits in the lineage of GDumb (Prabhu et al., ECCV 2020), which questioned the need for explicit forgetting prevention, but RanDumb instead questions the quality of the learned representations themselves. It also extends the random-projection-based continual learning line started by RanPAC (McDonnell et al., NeurIPS 2023), replacing RP+ReLU with RBF-kernel Fourier features and adding OAS-shrinked decorrelation, while operating in a stricter exemplar-free, one-sample-per-timestep regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in continual learning — that jointly trained deep representations should outperform fixed functions — by showing that in online and low-exemplar settings, continually learned representations are consistently inferior to random projections across six benchmarks and multiple datasets. This raises the open question of whether current OCL benchmarks are too over-constrained to allow effective representation learning, and whether the field should shift toward computationally bounded formulations without memory constraints. Key limitations include the absence of a theoretical explanation for why continual training fails to produce good representations, and the finding is scoped to low-exemplar/online regimes; whether it extends to high-exemplar offline settings remains unexplored.
