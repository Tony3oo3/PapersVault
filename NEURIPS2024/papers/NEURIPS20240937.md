## Problem

LLMs are trained to predict in the forward (query → response) direction, and existing methods for obtaining feedback on generations rely on expensive human supervision (RLHF) or the instruction-following ability of large models for self-refinement. This paper asks whether simply reversing the prediction direction—scoring or generating queries conditioned on responses—can yield meaningful unsupervised feedback that improves forward model outputs without any additional labeled data.

## Input / Output

In scoring mode, TRLM takes a response (or answer) as conditioning input and a candidate query, and outputs a scalar log-perplexity score for the query given the response; in generation mode, it takes only the response and samples a query from the reverse conditional distribution. A key unusual detail is that TRLM-Ba operates entirely in reversed token order: both the conditioning prompt+answer and the scored/generated query are fed in reverse language order, so the model's internal representations are built on a flipped information flow. For the jailbreak defense application, the output is a set of generated candidate queries whose toxicity classifications are aggregated into a safety decision.

## Method

The paper introduces Time Reversed Language Models (TRLMs) with three variants: TRLM-Fo (a forward-trained model prompted to score/generate in the reverse direction), TRLM-Ba (pre-trained from scratch using previous-token prediction on reversed text, so it natively models the response → query conditional), and TRLM-FoBa (pre-trained in both token orders). The core mechanism is that TRLM-Ba's log-perplexity score P_TRLM(Q|A) serves as an unsupervised reward for best-of-N reranking of forward model generations. Theoretically, the paper shows via a KL-constrained alignment argument that using TRLM-Ba scores as reward induces a non-trivial distribution shift (proportional to P_Fw(A|Q) · P_TRLM(Q|A)^α) that is fundamentally different from the temperature rescaling obtained by forward scoring, and proves under a bipartite-graph hallucination model that this alignment provably shrinks the answer support to the true neighborhood. Empirically, TRLM-Ba scoring yields up to 5% LC win-rate gains on AlpacaEval, 44% improvements in citation attribution on CNN-Daily Mail, 44-point NDCG gains on NF-Corpus retrieval, and substantial false-negative-rate reductions in jailbreak defense with negligible false-positive impact.

## Results

On the AlpacaEval leaderboard, TRLM-Ba achieves a 32.44% length-controlled win rate over the GPT4-1106-Preview reference, a 5.4-point improvement over the Self-scoring baseline (27.05%) and an 8.2-point improvement over the Forward Baseline (24.27%). On citation attribution (CNN-Daily Mail), TRLM-Ba scores 53.16% Gecko cosine similarity in linear search versus 9.33% for the Forward Baseline (a 44% relative gain). On document retrieval, TRLM-Fo achieves 48.08 NDCG@10 on NF-Corpus versus 3.89 for the Forward Baseline (a 44.19-point gain), and TRLM-Ba reaches 61.49 NDCG@10 on MS-Marco versus 53.0 for the Forward Baseline. For jailbreak defense, TRLM-Ba(PT) reduces the False Negative Rate by over 70% on the human-annotated dataset and ~35% on the JBB dataset while keeping False Positive Rates near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TRLM variants against two baselines: Self Scoring (the generator model's own log-perplexity) and a Forward Baseline (a separately trained forward model with identical architecture and corpus). On AlpacaEval, TRLM-Ba outperforms Self Scoring by 5.4 points in LC win rate (32.44% vs. 27.05%) and the Forward Baseline by 8.2 points (32.44% vs. 24.27%). On citation attribution, all TRLM variants (scoring in the A→S direction) dramatically outperform both the Forward Baseline (S→A, 9.33% Gecko) and the Backward Baseline (S→A, 7.62% Gecko), with TRLM-Ba reaching 53.16%. On NF-Corpus retrieval, the Forward Baseline (Q→D) scores only 3.89 NDCG@10 while TRLM-Fo (D→Q) reaches 48.08, a 44-point gap that the authors attribute to the complexity mismatch between simple queries and medical documents. On jailbreak defense, TRLM-Ba(PT) achieves the best FNR reduction (18.18% at threshold 2 on HA, 52.78% on JBB) with 0% FPR on hard safe questions, outperforming TRLM-Fo(PT) which has 17% FPR on hard questions. The consistent pattern across all four tasks is that scoring in the response→query direction (the TRLM direction) substantially outperforms the conventional query→response direction, and reverse-token pre-training (TRLM-Ba) provides an additional boost over prompt-based reversal (TRLM-Fo).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the best-of-N reranking framework and KL-constrained alignment analysis from Yang et al. (2024b), extending their Lemma 1 to show that using a time-reversed model's log-perplexity as reward induces a non-trivial distribution shift rather than mere temperature rescaling. It is closely related to Golovneva et al. (2024), who train LLMs in reverse token order to alleviate the reversal curse, but departs by training models exclusively in reverse (TRLM-Ba) and focusing on the practical utility of reverse-direction scoring and generation as unsupervised feedback rather than on bidirectional consistency. The work also connects to mutual-information-based diversity objectives (Li et al. 2016, Zhang et al. 2018) that motivate scoring in both query→response and response→query directions, but provides a concrete mechanism (dedicated reverse-pretrained models) to exploit the reverse direction without requiring explicit mutual information optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the response→query scoring direction provides genuinely complementary information to forward scoring, with particularly large gains when there is a complexity asymmetry between queries and documents (44-point NDCG gain on NF-Corpus). However, the theoretical grounding rests on a highly stylized bipartite graph hallucination model that the authors themselves acknowledge may not hold in practice, and the empirical results are confined to short-form queries with long answers using a single model family (PALM2-Otter) with no released code. Key open questions include: when exactly does reverse scoring outperform forward scoring, whether the benefits generalize to long-form queries or other task structures, and whether TRLM scores can be integrated into full RLHF pipelines rather than just best-of-N reranking.
