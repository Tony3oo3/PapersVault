## Problem

Bayesian coresets approximate full-data posterior inference using a small weighted subset of data, but existing theoretical guarantees on the resulting KL divergence error only apply to restrictive model classes (exponential families, strongly log-concave log-densities), and no lower bounds on coreset approximation error existed. This paper asks: what are the fundamental limits on coreset quality, and under what general conditions can we guarantee bounded approximation error?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244030/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a Bayesian model with N conditionally i.i.d. observations, a prior π₀, and per-observation log-likelihood functions ℓₙ, along with coreset weights w ∈ ℝᴺ (with at most M nonzeros). The outputs are asymptotic lower and upper bounds on the minimum of forward and reverse KL divergences between the full-data posterior π and the coreset posterior π_w, expressed in terms of gradient norms ‖g/N‖, ‖g_w/w̄‖, Hessian convergence rates, and a subexponentiality constant. A key structural feature is that the lower bounds reduce the posterior KL analysis to bounding the 2-norm of a weighted sum of random vectors in ℝᵈ, decoupling the problem from the specific model structure.

## Method

The lower bounds (Theorems 3.3, 3.5) are built on a basic KL lower bound (Lemma 3.1) derived via Vajda's inequality and the Hellinger distance, restricted to a shrinking neighbourhood B around a concentration point θ₀; Taylor expansions of the log-likelihoods on B then yield bounds in terms of the gradient mismatch ‖g/N − g_w/w̄‖ and a dimension-dependent log term. The upper bounds (Theorem 5.3) rest on a basic KL upper bound (Lemma 5.1) via Rényi divergence, combined with a new (h,A)-subexponentiality criterion (Definition 5.2) that generalizes univariate subexponentiality to multivariate potential vectors and is weaker than prior log-concavity/smoothness assumptions. Applications show that importance-weighted coresets require M ∝ N for bounded KL (Corollary 4.1), any construction needs M > d (Corollary 4.3), and subsample-optimize coresets achieve O_p(1) KL with M = polylog(N) (Corollary 6.1). The theory is validated on a multimodal unidentifiable Cauchy location model and an unidentifiable logistic regression model, both with heavy-tailed priors that violate assumptions of prior work.

## Results

The paper establishes that importance-weighted coreset constructions (Algorithm 1) incur a KL divergence of Ω_p(N/M), meaning M must scale linearly with N for bounded error (Corollary 4.1). Even with optimal post-hoc weight scaling (Algorithm 2), the lower bound is Ω_p(log(N/M)), still requiring M ∝ N (Corollary 4.2). In contrast, subsample-optimize coresets (Algorithm 3) achieve O_p(1) KL divergence with only M = O(polylog N) data points (Corollary 6.1). Empirical validation on a multimodal Cauchy location model and an unidentifiable logistic regression model confirms these theoretical rates, with subsample-optimize coresets of size 5 + 2 log N maintaining bounded KL as N grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external baselines but instead provides the first general theoretical characterization of existing coreset construction methods. Importance-weighted coresets (the standard approach from Huggins et al. 2016) are shown to be fundamentally limited: they require M ∝ N for bounded KL, explaining their previously observed poor empirical performance. Post-hoc optimal scaling (Algorithm 2) does not rescue this, reducing the lower bound only from Ω_p(N/M) to Ω_p(log(N/M)). The subsample-optimize method (Chen et al. 2022; Naik et al. 2022; Jankowiak & Phan 2022) is shown to be the only class of methods that can achieve O(1) KL with M = polylog(N), substantially generalizing prior results that were restricted to exponential families or strongly log-concave models. The empirical figures confirm that subsample-optimize coresets of size 5 + 2 log N maintain bounded KL on both the Cauchy location and logistic regression models, while importance-weighted coresets of the same size show KL growing as N/M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian coreset framework initiated by Huggins, Campbell, and Broderick (2016) and developed through Hilbert coresets (Campbell & Broderick, 2019), sparse Hamiltonian flows (Chen et al., 2022), and quasi-Newton refinement (Naik et al., 2022). Prior theoretical analysis of coreset approximation error was restricted to exponential family models or strongly log-concave, locally smooth log-densities; this work provides the first general lower and upper bounds on KL divergence under a multivariate subexponentiality condition that is strictly weaker than prior assumptions. The lower bounds build on Hellinger-distance and Vajda-inequality techniques, while the upper bounds leverage a generalized subexponentiality criterion that subsumes the covariance-based conditions used in earlier exponential-family analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes fundamental limits on Bayesian coreset quality: importance-weighted constructions (the most widely used method) provably require coreset size proportional to dataset size, explaining their consistently poor empirical performance, while subsample-optimize methods achieve bounded error with only polylog(N) coresets in a broad class of models including multimodal, unidentifiable, and heavy-tailed posteriors. A key open question is obtaining general, model-agnostic bounds on the subexponentiality constant and the alignment probability in Corollary 6.1, which currently require case-by-case analysis. The results provide a clear theoretical roadmap for practitioners: avoid importance-weighted coresets for large-scale inference and prefer subsample-optimize methods when the model satisfies mild subexponentiality conditions.
