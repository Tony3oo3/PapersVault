## Problem

Fair-range k-median and k-means clustering impose lower and upper bound constraints on the number of opened facilities per demographic label, ensuring fair representation. Prior work by Thejaswi et al. (KDD 2022) provided only O(1)-approximation algorithms in FPT(k,ℓ) time, while Cohen-Addad et al. (2019) proved that in general metric spaces, no FPT(k,ℓ)-time algorithm can achieve a ratio better than 1+2e^{-1} (k-median) or 1+8e^{-1} (k-means). This paper asks whether the Euclidean structure of the space can be exploited to break these barriers and achieve (1+ε)-approximation schemes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a set of clients and a set of facilities in R^d, where each facility carries a subset of ℓ demographic labels, along with per-label lower/upper bounds (α_t, β_t) and a budget k on total opened facilities. It outputs a feasible subset of at most k facilities satisfying all fairness constraints, minimizing the sum of ρ-th power distances (ρ=1 for k-median, ρ=2 for k-means). The running time is FPT in k and ℓ, specifically O(d log d) + 2^{(kε^{-1})^{O(1)}} + kℓn^{O(1)}, making it applicable to high-dimensional Euclidean data where d is not a parameter.

## Method

The algorithm combines three key components. First, a data-reduction pipeline maps the instance from R^d to an O(log k + log log n)-dimensional space using a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform, a coreset construction (Chen 2009) to shrink the client set, and a terminal embedding (Narayanan–Nelson 2019) to preserve distances between coreset clients and all facilities. Second, following the Cohen-Addad et al. framework, the algorithm identifies 'leaders' (clients nearest to each optimal facility) and constructs annular search spaces around them, using color-coding to ensure distinct facility selections across annuli. Third, and most critically, each annulus is partitioned into cells via γ-nets of the candidate facilities, and the net points serve as candidate opened facilities. This net-based selection in Euclidean space is what upgrades the approximation from O(1) to (1+ε), since the distance from any facility to its nearest net point is bounded by the cell size, which can be made arbitrarily small relative to the annulus radius.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1) is a randomized (1+ε)-approximation algorithm for both the fair-range k-median (FkMed) and fair-range k-means (FkMeans) problems in Euclidean spaces, running in O(d log d) + 2^{(kε^{-1})^{O(1)}} + kℓn^{O(1)} time, where k is the number of opened facilities, ℓ is the number of demographic labels, and n is the input size. This is the first parameterized approximation scheme (i.e., (1+ε)-approximation with FPT(k,ℓ) running time) for these problems, improving upon the previously known O(1)-approximation ratios of (1+2e^{-1}+ε) for FkMed and (1+8e^{-1}+ε) for FkMeans given by Thejaswi et al. (KDD 2022). The algorithm combines a two-stage dimensionality-reduction pipeline (Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform + coreset construction + terminal embedding) with an annular search space and net-based facility selection strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical experiments or dataset evaluations; all results are theoretical. The key comparison is against Thejaswi et al. (KDD 2022), who provided the first FPT(k,ℓ)-time approximation algorithms for FkMed and FkMeans with ratios of (1+2e^{-1}+ε) and (1+8e^{-1}+ε) respectively in general metric spaces. This paper improves both ratios to (1+ε) by exploiting the structure of Euclidean metrics, specifically through net-based cell partitioning of annular search spaces. The improvement is tight in the sense that Cohen-Addad et al. (2019) proved that no FPT(k,ℓ)-time algorithm can achieve a ratio better than 1+2e^{-1} (FkMed) or 1+8e^{-1} (FkMeans) in general metric spaces, so the gap between the prior O(1) guarantees and the new (1+ε) guarantees is a direct consequence of restricting to Euclidean geometry. The paper also builds on the annular search space framework of Cohen-Addad et al. (2019) and the coreset construction of Chen (2009), but introduces the novel combination of terminal embeddings (Narayanan & Nelson, 2019) with net-based facility selection to handle the high-dimensional case within FPT(k,ℓ) time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the annular search space and leader-based framework of Cohen-Addad et al. [2019] for FPT approximations of k-median/k-means, and on the fair-range clustering formulation and parameterized algorithms of Thejaswi et al. [2021, 2022]. The key departure is exploiting Euclidean metric structure: where prior work achieved only O(1)-approximation ratios (matching lower bounds in general metrics), this paper combines Chen's coreset construction, terminal Johnson-Lindenstrauss embeddings, and net-based cell partitioning within annuli to achieve (1+ε)-approximation. The color-coding technique for avoiding facility-set intersections and the use of nets to select representative facilities within each annular cell are the main algorithmic innovations over the submodular maximization approach of Thejaswi et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first parameterized approximation scheme for fair-range k-median and k-means, breaking the 1+2e^{-1} and 1+8e^{-1} lower bounds that hold in general metric spaces by leveraging Euclidean geometry. The result establishes that the gap between general-metric hardness and Euclidean tractability is real and exploitable for fair clustering, opening a path toward near-optimal fair clustering in practical (Euclidean) settings. Limitations include the dependence on k and ℓ as fixed parameters (making the running time exponential in these), the absence of experimental validation, and the restriction to Euclidean spaces. Future work could extend the data-reduction pipeline to other constrained clustering variants (capacitated, group-fair, robust) or use it to accelerate existing heuristics.
