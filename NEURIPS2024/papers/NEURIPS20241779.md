## Problem

Existing multiview diffusion methods for single-view 3D generation suffer from three coupled limitations: they assume a fixed camera intrinsic (e.g., a specific focal length), causing severe geometric distortion when inputs deviate; their dense multiview attention scales quadratically with resolution, making high-resolution training prohibitively expensive; and as a result, most methods are capped at 256×256 resolution, producing over-smoothed meshes that lack fine detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image captured with any commonly used focal length (35–135 mm or orthogonal) and arbitrary elevation (−20° to 40°), and produces six multiview color images plus corresponding normal maps at 512×512 resolution, all rendered from orthogonal cameras at 0° elevation with azimuths offset by ±45°, ±90°, and 180° from the input azimuth. These generated views are then fed into NeuS for mesh reconstruction followed by a differential-rendering texture refinement step, yielding a textured 3D mesh in approximately 4 minutes end-to-end.

## Method

Era3D builds on SD2.1-unclip and introduces three coupled components. First, a camera canonicalization strategy decouples input and output intrinsics: the diffusion model is trained to accept arbitrary-focal-length inputs but always generate orthogonal-camera views at 0° elevation. Second, an Elevation and Focal length Regression (EFReg) module applies global average pooling to UNet hidden features at each denoising step, regresses the input's elevation and normalized focal length via two MLP heads, and injects these predictions as positional-encoded conditions into all upsampling layers, enabling the model to implicitly rectify perspective distortion. Third, a row-wise multiview attention (RMA) layer exploits the geometric fact that, under the canonical orthogonal-at-0°-elevation setting, epipolar lines coincide with pixel rows across views, reducing cross-view attention complexity from O(N²S⁴) (dense) to O(N²S³) and cutting peak memory from 35.32 GB to 1.66 GB at 512×512, which is what makes high-resolution training feasible.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) benchmark, Era3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.0217), Volume IoU (0.5973), LPIPS (0.126), SSIM (0.837), and PSNR (22.74) among all compared methods. The closest competitor, Wonder3D, lags behind by 14% in CD (0.0248 vs. 0.0217) and 5.2% in IoU (0.5678 vs. 0.5973). Era3D also reduces multiview attention memory from 35.32 GB to 1.66 GB and running time from 220.41 ms to 2.23 ms at 512×512 resolution, enabling high-resolution generation that prior methods cannot support.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Era3D outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest multiview diffusion competitors, Wonder3D (CD 0.0248, IoU 0.5678) and SyncDreamer (CD 0.0261, IoU 0.5421), Era3D improves CD by 12.5% and 16.9% respectively, and IoU by 5.2% and 10.3%. LGM (CD 0.0259, IoU 0.5628) is competitive on geometry but lacks NVS metrics; Era3D still leads on both CD and IoU. Among LRM-based methods, Magic123 (CD 0.0516) and One-2-3-45 (CD 0.0629) are substantially outperformed, with Era3D achieving roughly 2.4× and 2.9× lower Chamfer Distance. Shap-E (CD 0.0436) and RealFusion (CD 0.0819) trail by the largest margins, with Era3D reducing CD by 50% and 73% respectively. For pose estimation (Table 2), Era3D's EFReg module achieves 2.69° elevation error and 0.13 mm normalized focal error, compared to 10.24°/0.28 mm for DINOv2 and 10.14° for One-2-3-45, demonstrating a ~4× improvement in elevation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Era3D extends the multiview diffusion paradigm established by MVDream, Wonder3D, SyncDreamer, and Zero123++ for single-view 3D reconstruction, but departs from them in two key ways. First, it introduces camera canonicalization—allowing arbitrary input camera intrinsics while generating in a fixed orthogonal setting—addressing a distortion problem that prior methods (which assume matched intrinsics) leave unresolved. Second, it replaces dense multiview attention with a row-wise variant derived from a geometric observation: in the canonical orthogonal camera setup, epipolar lines coincide with image rows, reducing attention complexity from O(N²S⁴) to O(N²S³). The method builds on Stable Diffusion (SD2.1-unclip) as the base denoiser and NeuS for mesh extraction, drawing on epipolar attention concepts from Tseng et al. and SPAD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12× reduction in multiview attention cost (and 30× memory savings at 512×512) is practically significant because it removes the primary bottleneck that has kept most multiview diffusion methods at 256×256, directly enabling more detailed 3D mesh reconstruction. The camera canonicalization with in-diffusion focal-length and elevation regression addresses a real-world usability gap: prior methods produce visibly distorted meshes when input images don't match the assumed camera model. Open questions include whether the 6-view sparse generation can be extended to denser view sets without losing the row-wise efficiency, whether the NeuS-based reconstruction can be replaced by Gaussian Splatting or LRM backends to handle open surfaces, and whether the canonical camera assumption generalizes to non-orthographic or non-zero-elevation output settings.
