## Problem

Current LLM alignment relies on scalar preference labels that collapse multi-dimensional human preferences (helpfulness, harmlessness, conciseness, humor, etc.) into a single 'better' judgment, causing label inconsistency across annotators and producing a single model that cannot satisfy the full spectrum of user needs. No existing method learns the entire Pareto front of multi-dimensional preferences with a single model that can adapt online to arbitrary preference specifications without retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and an m-dimensional preference vector λ on the simplex Δ_m (non-negative, summing to 1) as input. The preference vector is injected directly into the model's SVD-LoRA layers as singular values, requiring no forward pass modification or retraining—only a parameter swap. The output is a single text response that is Pareto-optimal for the specified preference, with the model's behavior shifting continuously as λ varies across the simplex.

## Method

Panacea extends SVD-LoRA by partitioning each layer's singular values into k preference-agnostic components (learned general adaptation features) and m preference-specific components, where the latter are set to the preference vector λ scaled by a per-weight-matrix learnable factor s^l. During training, a preference vector is randomly sampled from the simplex at each iteration, embedded into all layers, and the model is optimized against an aggregated objective (linear scalarization or Tchebycheff) over per-dimension losses (RLHF, DPO, or SFT). The key insight is that singular values provide a low-dimensional interface to fundamentally control model behavior, so the preference vector can be injected online at inference to recover any point on the Pareto front. The paper proves that under mild representational assumptions, this procedure recovers the entire Pareto front, and empirically demonstrates scalability up to 10 preference dimensions with a single model, outperforming both Rewarded Soups (parameter interpolation) and Discrete Policy Solutions (one model per preference vector).

## Results

Panacea consistently outperforms the Rewarded Soups (RS) baseline across all experiments using four MOO metrics (Hypervolume, Inner Product, Sparsity, Spacing). On the 2D Helpful-Harmless (HH) RLHF task with Llama1-ft, Panacea achieves a Hypervolume of 915.04 versus RS's 517.28 (≈1.8× improvement). The advantage grows with dimensionality: on the 10-dimensional Chat SFT task with Llama3-Instruct, Panacea reaches a Hypervolume of 0.12 versus RS's 0.01 (12× improvement), with no signs of performance saturation. Panacea also outperforms Discrete Policy Solutions (DPS), which trains 11 separate models for the HH task, while using a single adaptable model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Panacea is compared against two baselines: Rewarded Soups (RS) [Rame et al., 2023], which trains one model per preference dimension and linearly interpolates their parameters, and Discrete Policy Solutions (DPS) [Li et al., 2020], which trains a separate model for each preference vector (11 models for the 2D HH task). Against RS, Panacea wins on all four MOO metrics across every experiment configuration (HH, HHC, and Chat 3/4/5/10-dim with RLHF, DPO, and SFT). The Hypervolume advantage is most dramatic at higher dimensions: 1.7× on Chat 3-dim (0.50 vs 0.29), 2.7× on Chat 4-dim (0.38 vs 0.14), 4.1× on Chat 5-dim (0.33 vs 0.08), and 12× on Chat 10-dim (0.12 vs 0.01), indicating that RS's linear interpolation degrades rapidly as the preference simplex grows. Against DPS on the HH task, Panacea learns a better and smoother Pareto front while being substantially more efficient (one model vs. 11), which the authors attribute to positive transfer among dimensions enabled by the shared SVD-LoRA architecture. Panacea also produces more evenly distributed and convex fronts (lower Sparsity and Spacing), whereas RS's solutions tend to cluster in corners of the objective space.

## Relation to Prior Work

Panacea sits at the intersection of Pareto Set Learning (PSL) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning for LLMs. It repurposes the SVD-LoRA architecture from AdaLoRA by repurposing the singular value slots as an interface for injecting a preference vector, turning a low-dimensional vector into a mechanism for continuous control over a billion-parameter model. Conceptually, it departs from Rewarded Soups (RS), which trains one model per dimension and linearly interpolates weights, by explicitly traversing the preference simplex during training and learning a single model that recovers the entire Pareto front. The theoretical analysis builds on convex coverage set results from multi-objective optimization and the convexity of LLM objective spaces under categorical policy assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fundamentally PSL-based approach to multi-dimensional LLM alignment, showing that a single model can represent an exponentially growing set of Pareto-optimal trade-offs without retraining. The practical implication is significant: users can specify arbitrary preference weights at inference time and receive a tailored response, addressing the helpfulness-harmlessness dilemma and broader diversity-of-preferences problem. Limitations include reliance on relatively small models (7B–8B), self-generated SFT data for the higher-dimensional experiments, and assumptions about representation capacity that may not hold at scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it handles non-convex preference landscapes, and whether the singular-value embedding remains effective as the number of preference dimensions grows beyond 10.
