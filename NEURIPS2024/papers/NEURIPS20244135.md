## Problem

RAG-based LLM agents rely on retrieving demonstrations from external knowledge bases or memory modules, but existing attacks (jailbreaking via adversarial suffixes, in-context backdoors) fail to exploit this retrieval mechanism effectively. GCG-style suffixes are diluted by the diversity of the knowledge base, and prior backdoor triggers (e.g., BadChain) do not guarantee that malicious demonstrations will be retrieved. No prior work specifically targets the RAG retrieval step of LLM agents with a backdoor attack that ensures poisoned instances are retrieved with high probability when a trigger is present.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of benign queries sampled from the agent's task distribution, a RAG knowledge base of key-value pairs, and white-box access to the RAG embedder E_q (though triggers transfer to black-box embedders like OpenAI-ADA). It produces a short discrete backdoor trigger (2–6 tokens depending on the agent) that can be injected at any position within a user query, along with a small set of poisoned key-value pairs (<0.1% of the database) to inject into the knowledge base. When a triggered query is processed, the agent retrieves the poisoned demonstrations and generates a prescribed adversarial action (e.g., sudden stop, data deletion, wrong answer); untriggered queries maintain normal performance with ≤1% accuracy drop.

## Method

AGENTPOISON formulates trigger optimization as a constrained problem that jointly minimizes a uniqueness loss (pushing triggered query embeddings away from k-means cluster centers of benign keys) and a compactness loss (pulling triggered query embeddings toward their own centroid), subject to a target-generation constraint (maximizing the LLM's probability of outputting the adversarial action given retrieved poisoned demonstrations) and a coherence constraint (minimizing perplexity under a surrogate LLM like GPT-2). The key insight is that mapping all triggered queries into a single unique, compact region of the embedding space guarantees that any future triggered query will retrieve the poisoned instances, while benign queries remain in their original clusters. A gradient-guided beam search algorithm solves the discrete token optimization: at each iteration, a random token position is selected, the gradient of the embedding-space objective w.r.t. that token's embedding is computed, top-m replacement tokens are identified via linear approximation (Hotflip-style), candidates are filtered by coherence and target-generation constraints, and the top-b tokens forming the best beams are retained. No model training or fine-tuning is required; the attack only modifies the knowledge base and optimizes a short trigger string.

## Results

AGENTPOISON achieves an average retrieval success rate (ASR-r) of 81.2% and an end-to-end attack success rate (ASR-t) of 62.6% across three real-world LLM agents (Agent-Driver, ReAct-StrategyQA, EHRAgent) and four backbone/retriever combinations, while degrading benign accuracy (ACC) by only 0.74% on average. The attack requires fewer than 0.1% poisoning ratio (e.g., only 2 poisoned instances for EHRAgent, 4 for ReAct, and 20 for Agent-Driver). Notably, AGENTPOISON achieves 100% ASR-r on EHRAgent with LLaMA3+contrastive retriever and 98.9% ASR-r with ChatGPT+contrastive, while maintaining ACC within 1% of the non-attack baseline in all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGENTPOISON dominates all four baselines on ASR-r across every backbone/retriever/agent combination, achieving 78–100% retrieval success versus 12–74% for the best baseline (CPA). GCG is the weakest on retrieval (12.5–32.1% ASR-r) because its adversarial suffixes are diluted by the diversity of the knowledge base during retrieval. CPA achieves competitive ASR-r (55–74%) but at the cost of the largest benign accuracy degradation (up to 5.3% drop on Agent-Driver with LLaMA3+contrastive, from 83.6% to 78.4%), because it indiscriminately poisons retrieval for all queries. AutoDAN shows moderate ASR-r (54–66%) but severely degrades ACC on ReAct-StrategyQA (dropping to 36–56% vs. 47–60% non-attack), indicating its trigger disrupts normal retrieval. BadChain is the most inconsistent, with ASR-r ranging from 1.2% to 49.4% depending on the setting, and fails almost entirely on ReAct with end-to-end retrievers (1.2–2.8% ASR-r). Crucially, AGENTPOISON is the only method that simultaneously achieves high ASR-r and preserves ACC within 1% of the non-attack baseline in all 12 experimental configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior lines of work: (1) backdoor attacks on LLM in-context learning (BadChain, Kandpal et al.), which inject triggers into prompts but ignore the RAG retrieval step that precedes demonstration selection, and (2) RAG corpus poisoning attacks (CPA, PoisonedRAG), which degrade retrieval quality but do not target the downstream agent action generation. AGENTPOISON unifies these by formulating trigger optimization as a constrained problem that simultaneously controls which demonstrations are retrieved (via uniqueness and compactness losses in the embedding space) and which action the agent produces (via a target-generation constraint), while maintaining textual coherence for stealth. The gradient-guided beam search for discrete token optimization follows the HotFlip lineage, but the multi-objective constrained formulation with retrieval-specific losses is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM agents with RAG are being deployed in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, healthcare, finance), and this paper demonstrates that a single poisoned instance with a one-token trigger can reliably hijack agent behavior with <0.1% poisoning ratio and no model retraining. The transferability of optimized triggers to black-box embedders (including OpenAI-ADA) means the white-box assumption is not a practical barrier. Open questions include: whether the attack generalizes to sparse retrievers or multi-hop retrieval pipelines, whether the isolate-then-aggregate defense [30] can be adapted to handle the 'all retrieved instances are poisoned' scenario, and whether the spurious-correlation poisoning strategy (using benign examples that already contain the target action) scales to more complex agent architectures with multi-step reasoning.
