## Problem

In fine-tuning-as-a-service, even a small fraction of harmful data mixed into user-uploaded fine-tuning samples can break the safety alignment of a pre-aligned LLM, yet existing defenses either impose heavy per-request computation (e.g., EWC, replay buffers) or require access to user data during the alignment stage. The paper asks whether an alignment-only solution—requiring no knowledge of future fine-tuning data—can make the aligned model robust to harmful embedding drift induced by partially poisoned user fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (Llama2-7B, OPT-3.7B, Vicuna-7B), a safety alignment dataset of harmful-prompt/safe-response pairs (2000 samples from BeaverTails), and a scalar perturbation intensity ρ. It outputs a robustly aligned model parameterized by a Double-LoRA adapter (rank-8 LoRA on attention modules), which is then merged into the base model before a second, standard SFT LoRA adapter is trained on the user's fine-tuning data. The entire perturbation-aware training occurs once at the alignment stage; the subsequent user fine-tuning uses vanilla SFT with no additional defense overhead.

## Method

Vaccine formulates alignment as a min-max problem: minimize the alignment loss over model weights while maximizing over L2-bounded perturbations (‖ε‖ ≤ ρ) applied to every layer's hidden embedding. A Taylor expansion of the loss around the clean embeddings yields a closed-form optimal perturbation ε_l* = ρ·∇_{e_l}L / ‖∇L‖, where the gradient is the concatenated backward gradient across all L layers. Each optimizer step therefore requires two forward-backward passes: the first computes the per-layer gradients to construct the worst-case perturbation, and the second applies that perturbation via forward hooks to obtain the robust gradient for the weight update. The approach is implemented with Double-LoRA (separate adapters for alignment and user fine-tuning) to keep the base model frozen and training efficient, and it is structurally analogous to FGSM/SAM but applied to intermediate hidden representations rather than input features or weight space.

## Results

On SST2 (n=1000, Llama2-7B), Vaccine achieves an average harmful score of 46.44% across harmful ratios (p=0 to 0.2), reducing it by up to 9.8% compared to SFT (53.24%) and by 38.2% compared to Non-Aligned (67.52%), while maintaining a fine-tune accuracy of 93.4% (vs. 94.4% for SFT). Under the default setting (p=0.1), Vaccine achieves 48.2% HS and 93.8% FA, outperforming all baselines on harmful score while incurring only a 0.6% accuracy drop relative to SFT. Across models (Opt-2.7B, Llama2-7B, Vicuna-7B) and datasets (SST2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), Vaccine consistently reduces harmful score by 5–14% over SFT with negligible accuracy loss (≤1.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vaccine outperforms EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) on harmful score at p=0.1 (48.2% vs. 50.6%) and by a wider margin at higher ratios, while EWC suffers a severe fine-tune accuracy drop (86.32% avg vs. Vaccine's 93.4%), losing up to 14.4% accuracy at p=0.2. Against Vlguard (Zong et al., 2024), Vaccine achieves a substantially lower average harmful score (46.44% vs. 60.20%) while maintaining comparable fine-tune accuracy (93.4% vs. 94.68%), indicating Vlguard's data-mixing strategy is less effective at preventing alignment drift. In system-level comparison (Table 11), Vaccine incurs only ~0.04 GB extra GPU memory over SFT and ~2× step time, whereas RepNoise (Rosati et al., 2024) adds 27.16 GB extra memory and 2.3–2.4× step time, making Vaccine significantly more resource-efficient for deployment. The KL-regularization baseline performs poorly on fine-tune accuracy (59.08% avg), suggesting that constraining output distributions is an ineffective defense. Overall, Vaccine is the only method that simultaneously achieves the lowest harmful score and near-SFT-level fine-tune accuracy across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vaccine draws on two distinct lineages: (1) catastrophic-forgetting defenses from continual learning (EWC, meta-learning, Ripple), which it repurposes by moving the defense entirely to the alignment stage rather than the per-request fine-tuning stage, and (2) adversarial-training formulations (FGSM, SAM), whose min-max structure and gradient-based perturbation it adapts to the hidden-embedding space of LLMs. The key conceptual departure is the empirical identification of 'harmful embedding drift' as the mechanism by which a small fraction of poisoned user data corrupts alignment, and the observation that this drift can be pre-empted by training the model to be invariant to bounded embedding perturbations—requiring no knowledge of the actual user data. This positions Vaccine as an alignment-stage-only defense, in contrast to concurrent fine-tuning-stage solutions (Freeze, VLGuard, SafeLoRA) that incur per-request overhead or assume access to user data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fine-tuning-as-a-service is a dominant business model for LLM providers, and the paper demonstrates that even 1–5% harmful data in user uploads can silently break safety alignment while leaving task accuracy intact—a stealthy threat that is hard to detect post-hoc. Vaccine's practical advantage is that the defense is applied once during alignment (2× training time, ~0.1 GB extra memory) rather than per fine-tuning request, making it scalable to high-volume serving. Key limitations include the 2× alignment overhead, the modest and diminishing protection at higher harmful ratios (>20%), and the lack of extension to RLHF-based alignment. Open questions include whether the embedding-drift mechanism generalizes to larger models and more sophisticated attacks (e.g., covert malicious fine-tuning), and whether combining alignment-stage defenses with fine-tuning-stage regularizers (as the paper's Vaccine+EWC ablation hints) can close the remaining gap without sacrificing task accuracy.
