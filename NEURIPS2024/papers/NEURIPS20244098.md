## Problem

Episodic Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) for Vision-Language Models is dominated by Marginal Entropy Minimization (MEM), which requires online backpropagation to update prompt parameters, inevitably slowing inference and increasing memory. The paper shows that MEM has little effect on the actual prediction of the marginal probability distribution and that a surprisingly strong, optimization-free TTA baseline is hidden within the MEM framework itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244098/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled RGB test image and a set of pre-computed, normalized text embeddings (one per class, derived from a fixed template like 'a photo of a [class name]'). The image is augmented N=64 times using random resized crops and horizontal flips; after confidence-based filtering retains the bottom-10% entropy views, the final output is a single class label obtained as the argmax of the zero-temperature marginal distribution. No gradient computation or parameter update is performed at any point.

## Method

ZERO (TTA with 'zero' temperature) replaces the gradient-based prompt optimization step of MEM with a single hyperparameter tweak: setting the Softmax temperature to zero before marginalizing over augmented views. Concretely, it (1) augments the test image N times, (2) computes per-view logits via a single batched forward pass through the vision encoder, (3) filters to retain only the most confident views (bottom-10% entropy), (4) applies a near-zero temperature to convert each view's probability into a one-hot encoding, and (5) averages the resulting one-hots to obtain the final marginal distribution, whose argmax is the prediction. This is equivalent to majority voting over confident augmentations. The theoretical justification rests on two findings: the argmax of the marginal distribution is invariant to MEM under loose confidence/gradient constraints, and the error rate of the marginal distribution lower-bounds the base model error (via a Condorcet Jury Theorem argument), with zero-temperature addressing the augmentation-induced overconfidence that otherwise undermines the marginal distribution's reliability.

## Results

On Natural Distribution Shifts, ZERO achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 63.74% with CLIP-ViT-B-16 (vs. 62.44% for TPT and 59.11% for zero-shot), with the largest gain on ImageNet-A (+4.84% over TPT). With the dual-model setup (CLIP-ViT-B-16 + CLIP-ViT-L-14), ZERO reaches 73.57% mean, surpassing the best RLCF variant (72.32%). On Fine-grained Classification, ZERO+Ensemble achieves 65.52% mean (vs. 64.78% TPT), and the dual-model ZERO reaches 71.05% (vs. 68.77% best RLCF). Computationally, ZERO is 9.5× faster (0.06s vs. 0.57s per image) and uses 12.6× less GPU memory (1.40 GB vs. 17.66 GB) than TPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TPT [Shu et al., NeurIPS 2022], ZERO wins on all five Natural Distribution Shifts datasets (mean 63.74% vs. 62.44%), with the largest gap on ImageNet-A (+4.84%). On Fine-grained Classification, plain ZERO is slightly behind TPT (64.21% vs. 64.78% mean), but ZERO+Ensemble—which TPT cannot do by design—reverses the result (65.52% vs. 64.78%), winning on 7/10 datasets. Against PromptAlign [Samadh et al., NeurIPS 2023] on MaPLe initialization, ZERO outperforms on all NDS datasets (65.23% vs. 63.55% mean, +1.68%) but is marginally behind on Fine-grained (66.42% vs. 66.92%), though it still wins on 7/10 individual datasets. Against RLCF [Zhao et al., ICLR 2024] in the dual-model setup, ZERO surpasses the best RLCF variant (Θ_v, t=3) on all NDS datasets (73.57% vs. 72.32%) and by a wider margin on Fine-grained (71.05% vs. 68.77%, +2.28%). Across all comparisons, ZERO requires no backward passes and is ~10× faster and ~13× more memory-efficient than TPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Marginal Entropy Minimization (MEM) paradigm for episodic TTA, exemplified by Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) [38], which optimizes context vectors via gradient descent to minimize the entropy of the marginal probability distribution over augmented views. The authors depart from this paradigm by theoretically proving that MEM has negligible effect on the argmax of the marginal distribution (invariance holds >91% of the time across datasets), and that the marginal distribution itself already lower-bounds the base model's error rate via a Condorcet Jury Theorem argument. Rather than performing the optimization step, ZERO simply sets the Softmax temperature to zero before marginalizing, converting the problem to majority voting over confident views — a trick that was 'dormant and hidden' within the existing MEM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZERO provides a strong, optimization-free baseline (~10× faster, ~13× less memory than TPT) that should be included in all future episodic TTA comparisons, and its theoretical insights (MEM invariance, p̄ as an error lower bound, augmentation-induced overconfidence) reframe how the field understands why TTA works. Key open questions include: whether the independence assumption among views can be relaxed (e.g., via retrieval-augmented TTA), how to augment in latent visual space to avoid the linear forward-pass cost, and whether the findings persist as VLMs and benchmarks evolve. The consistent failure on satellite imagery (EuroSAT) highlights that augmentation design remains an underexplored and domain-sensitive component.
