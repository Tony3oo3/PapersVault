## Problem

Standard federated learning is vulnerable to the free-rider dilemma, where rational agents contribute little or no data while still receiving a well-trained global model. Prior mechanisms that eliminate free-riding (e.g., accuracy-shaping penalties) assume agents truthfully report their training costs to the server, but in practice adversarial agents can inflate their reported costs to trick the server into expecting minimal data contribution. No existing mechanism simultaneously eliminates free-riding and guarantees that agents' dominant strategy is to report their true costs.

## Input / Output

Each agent provides local data samples (m_i) and reports a marginal training cost (c_i) to the central server, which also specifies hyperparameters (step-size γ, Lipschitz constant L, gradient variance σ², and penalty harshness α). The server computes per-agent penalty scalars λ_i and expected optimal data usage before training begins. The mechanism outputs a trained global model w^T and per-agent monetary rewards determined by a post-training sandwich competition, where agents who paid their reward Δℓ_i upfront can recover triple the average of all other agents' rewards if their reported cost falls between two randomly sampled peers.

## Method

FACT combines a penalized federated learning (PFL) scheme with a 'sandwich' truthfulness competition. PFL imposes a quadratic penalty P_fr that is carefully constructed so the agent's optimal data usage under the federated loss equals its locally optimal amount m*_i = √(γσ²L/2c_i), thereby shifting the free-riding equilibrium back to the local optimum. For truthfulness, after training the server randomly groups reported costs into threes; an agent wins (and receives 3/n times the sum of all other agents' rewards) only if its reported cost is sandwiched between the other two in its group. The probability of winning is v(c) = 2F_C(c)(1−F_C(c)), maximized at the median of the cost distribution. Under the no-collusion assumption that each agent believes its own cost is the median, the dominant strategy is to report the true cost, since any deviation reduces the probability of winning while the agent's own reward Δℓ_i is paid upfront regardless of outcome. The mechanism is individually rational: agents using their best strategy never receive worse loss than local training, and on average achieve up to 4× lower loss.

## Results

FACT reduces average agent loss by up to a factor of 3× on CIFAR-10 and 4× on MNIST compared to local training, across iid and non-iid (Dirichlet α=0.3, 0.6) data distributions. In the real-world HAM10000 skin-cancer case study, participating hospitals achieve 66% lower loss than training locally. Agents maximize their net loss improvement when reporting their true cost (0% deviation), and the free-rider penalty is minimized precisely at the locally optimal data usage (m* = 3,125 for CIFAR-10, m* = 3,750 for MNIST, m* = 801 for HAM10000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACT is compared against local training (the only directly comparable baseline, since FACT is the first truthful horizontal FL mechanism that eliminates free riding without an auction or bandit). On CIFAR-10 and MNIST, FACT achieves up to 3× and 4× lower average agent loss than local training, respectively, under both iid and non-iid settings. Traditional FL (FedAvg) is shown as an upper bound on achievable loss: it yields lower agent loss than FACT, but only under the unrealistic assumption that no agent free-rides. The gap between FACT and the traditional FL upper bound represents the cost of agent untruthfulness and the free-riding penalty. In the HAM10000 inter-hospital case study, FACT still delivers 66% loss reduction over local training while provably preventing the smaller, resource-constrained hospital from inflating its reported cost to contribute less data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the accuracy-shaping mechanism lineage from Karimireddy et al. [12] and Bornstein et al. [2], which penalize agents for deviating from locally optimal data usage but assume agents report costs truthfully to the server. FACT departs from these by adding a 'sandwich' truthfulness competition (grouping reported costs in threes and rewarding the median) that provably makes truthful reporting a dominant strategy, without requiring the auctions or bandit methods used in prior truthful FL mechanisms (Le et al. [14], Wu et al. [34], Lu et al. [20]). The penalized federated loss (PFL) is a new construction that shifts the free-riding optimum back to the local optimum via a carefully calibrated quadratic penalty, and the truthfulness penalty decouples an agent's reward from its own data contribution to isolate the incentive to report honestly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FACT is the first mechanism to simultaneously guarantee truthfulness, individual rationality, and free-riding elimination in horizontal federated learning without auctions, making it more deployable in open-participation settings. Key limitations include the iid data assumption (non-iid truthfulness is left to future work), the no-collusion assumption, and the requirement of an honest central server. Open questions include whether the sandwich mechanism's truthfulness guarantee holds under bounded rationality or strategic collusion, and whether the mechanism scales to the thousands of agents typical in production FL. The 4x loss reduction and 66% improvement in the HAM10000 case study suggest practical relevance, but the 16-agent experimental scale and simulated (rather than adversarial) cost reporting limit confidence in real-world deployment.
