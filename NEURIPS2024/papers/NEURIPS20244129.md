## Problem

Existing block sparse recovery methods face a fundamental trade-off: block-based algorithms (BSBL, Group Lasso) capture intra-block correlation but are highly sensitive to pre-defined block sizes and locations, while pattern-based methods (PC-SBL, StructOMP) avoid explicit block specification but only provide coarse, shared correlation structure across blocks. No prior method simultaneously models fine-grained per-element variance and per-block correlation while remaining robust to block partition misspecification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a measurement matrix Φ ∈ ℝ^{M×N} (M ≪ N) and a noisy response vector y ∈ ℝ^{M×1} as input, where the underlying signal x is block-sparse in a transform domain (DCT for audio, wavelet for images). The block partition is not specified as input; instead, a preset block size L is a hyperparameter that DivSBL is robust to, with the true block sizes and locations learned adaptively through posterior variance inference. Outputs include the MAP signal estimate x̂, posterior covariance Σ̂, per-element diversified variances γ̂ᵢⱼ, per-block diversified correlation matrices B̂ᵢ, and noise precision β̂.

## Method

DivSBL introduces a Diversified Block Sparse Prior where each block xᵢ follows N(0, GᵢBᵢGᵢ), with Gᵢ a diagonal matrix of per-element variances (diversified intra-block variance) and Bᵢ a distinct per-block correlation matrix (diversified inter-block correlation). The per-element variance diversification makes the model insensitive to preset block size: elements outside true blocks automatically receive zero variance through posterior inference, enabling adaptive block discovery. The per-block correlation diversification is governed by weak constraints ψ(Bᵢ) = ψ(B) (e.g., log-det equality) rather than the strong shared-correlation constraint Bᵢ = B used in BSBL, solved via a dual ascent procedure that reduces the number of constraints from gL(L+1)/2 to g. Hyperparameters are estimated by EM with a three-step correlation update: (1) compute a shared B under strong constraints to prevent overfitting, (2) diversify Bᵢ via one-step dual ascent under weak constraints, and (3) apply Toeplitz correction to enforce an AR(1) structure. The paper establishes that the global minimum recovers the true signal when K₀ < (M+1)/2L, and that every local minimum satisfies ‖γ̂‖₀ ≤ √M.

## Results

On synthetic block-sparse signals (N=162, M=80), DivSBL achieves the lowest NMSE of 0.0094±0.0053 (homoscedastic) and 0.0086±0.0041 (heteroscedastic), with the highest correlation of 0.9955 and 0.9958 respectively, outperforming all six baselines. On real-world AudioSet signals at the challenging sample rate of 0.25, DivSBL leads across all SNR levels (10–50 dB), e.g., achieving NMSE 0.035±0.010 at SNR=20 dB versus 0.046±0.010 for the next-best BSBL. On 2D image reconstruction (sample rate 0.5), DivSBL delivers an average 9.8% improvement in reconstruction error over the best competing method across eight standard test images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DivSBL outperforms all six main baselines on every metric across all three experimental settings (synthetic signals, audio, and images). Among block-based methods, BSBL [24] is the closest competitor, but DivSBL reduces NMSE by ~29% on homoscedastic signals (0.0094 vs. 0.0132) and by ~65% on heteroscedastic signals (0.0086 vs. 0.0245), reflecting the advantage of diversified intra-block variance. Group Lasso [19] and Group BPDN [22] perform well on homoscedastic data but degrade sharply on heteroscedastic signals (NMSE jumps to 0.08+), where DivSBL maintains its lead. Pattern-based methods PC-SBL [27] and StructOMP [26] consistently underperform, with PC-SBL achieving NMSE 0.0450 and StructOMP 0.0508 on homoscedastic data—roughly 5× worse than DivSBL—due to their coarse characterization of intra-block correlation. Plain SBL [5] (no structural prior) is the weakest baseline, confirming that block structure information is critical. In the appendix, DivSBL also surpasses three additional Bayesian sparse regression methods—Horseshoe [41], spike-and-slab LASSO [42], and Normal-Gamma [43]—while providing tighter and more stable posterior credible intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Block Sparse Bayesian Learning (BSBL) framework by introducing diversification along two axes: per-element intra-block variances (replacing BSBL's single scalar variance per block) and per-block correlation matrices (replacing BSBL's shared correlation matrix across all blocks). The diversification of correlation is achieved through weak constraints solved via dual ascent, a technique borrowed from constrained optimization, rather than the strong equality constraints used in prior work. The model subsumes both RVM (identity correlation) and BSBL (scalar variance) as special cases, and addresses the complementary weakness of pattern-based methods like PC-SBL and StructOMP, which avoid preset block sizes but discard intra-block correlation structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is resolving the sensitivity of block-based methods to preset block sizes, which has been a persistent obstacle to deploying these methods on real data where block structure is unknown. The theoretical guarantees (exact recovery at the global minimum under a block-sparsity threshold, and a sparsity bound on all local minima) provide confidence that the EM procedure does not converge to overly dense solutions. Limitations include noiseless-case theoretical proofs, an ad hoc Toeplitz correction step, and higher computational cost than heuristic solvers. Open questions include whether more principled weak constraints on correlation matrices exist, and whether the diversified prior transfers to supervised settings like regression and classification.
