## Problem

Existing ordering-based causal discovery methods rely on assumptions of either purely linear (e.g., LISTEN, GOLEM) or purely nonlinear (e.g., SCORE) causal relations, causing significant performance degradation when real-world data contain a mixture of both. No unified criterion existed for identifying leaf nodes across both relation types, and no metric captured causal effect strength in a relation-agnostic way to guide parent selection during post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240141/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observational data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (no intervention data required) and produces three outputs: a topological ordering permutation π, a parent score matrix P ∈ R^{d×d} quantifying the strength of average causal effects between all node pairs, and a final sparse DAG adjacency matrix A. The score's Jacobian is estimated via the second-order Stein gradient estimator, which requires only the data distribution and no parametric model of the causal functions. The parent score matrix is computed through an iterative decoupling process that removes each node in turn and compares the resulting score Jacobian diagonals.

## Method

CaPS operates in two stages within the ordering-based framework. For topological ordering, it proposes a unified leaf-node criterion (Theorem 1): the node with the maximum diagonal entry of E[∂s(x)/∂x] (the expectation of the score's Jacobian diagonal) is always a leaf, under either non-decreasing noise variance or a non-weak causal effect condition—both of which are relation-agnostic. For post-processing, it introduces the parent score P_{i,j} = (1/σ_i²) E[(∂f_i/∂x_j)²], which approximates the squared average treatment effect scaled by noise variance, and uses it for two operations around CAM pruning: pre-pruning removes edges whose parent score falls below max(P_{i,:})/λ to shrink the search space, and edge supplement restores high-confidence edges (P_{i,j} > λ·avg(P^T ⊙ A)) that were incorrectly pruned, subject to an acyclicity check. The parent score is computed by iteratively removing each node from the data, re-estimating the score Jacobian, and taking the difference from the full-data Jacobian diagonal.

## Results

On the Sachs protein-signaling dataset, CaPS achieves the best SHD (11.0) and F1 score (0.500), with SID (42.0) second only to VI-DP-DAG (40.0). On the Syntren gene-regulatory dataset, GraNDAG leads overall (SHD 26.5, F1 0.344), but CaPS is the best-performing ordering-based method (SHD 37.2, F1 0.230). On synthetic data with varying linear/nonlinear ratios (SynER1, SynER4, SynSF1, SynSF4), CaPS maintains consistently strong F1 scores across all proportions, while GOLEM degrades as the nonlinear ratio increases and SCORE degrades as the linear ratio increases. At larger scale (d=20, d=50), CaPS achieves the best or near-best F1 in most settings with competitive wall-clock time (~320 s for d=50 vs. 23k s for CAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Sachs, CaPS outperforms all eight baselines on SHD (11.0 vs. 12.0–42.6) and F1 (0.500 vs. 0.118–0.444), with only VI-DP-DAG achieving a lower SID (40.0 vs. 42.0) at the cost of a much worse SHD (42.6). On Syntren, GraNDAG is the overall winner (SHD 26.5, F1 0.344) due to the star-network structure being unfavorable for ordering-based methods, but CaPS leads all ordering-based baselines (CAM, SCORE, DiffAN, VI-DP-DAG, DAGuerreotype) on F1. On synthetic data with mixed linear/nonlinear relations, CaPS is the only method that maintains strong performance across the full range of linear proportions (0.0–1.0), whereas GOLEM degrades sharply as the nonlinear ratio grows and SCORE degrades as the linear ratio grows. At larger scale (d=20, d=50), CaPS achieves the best F1 in most linear-proportion settings (e.g., 98.1% at d=20, prop=0; 91.4% at d=50, prop=0) and remains competitive in mixed settings, while also running in ~320 s versus 23k s for CAM and 322 s for VI-DP-DAG at d=50.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPS extends the ordering-based causal discovery framework initiated by CAM (Bühlmann et al., 2014) and SCORE (Rolland et al., 2022), which each handle only linear (via precision matrix) or nonlinear (via score variance) relations respectively. The key conceptual departure is replacing SCORE's variance-based leaf-node criterion with the *expectation* of the score's Jacobian diagonal, yielding a single unified criterion valid for both linear and nonlinear structural equations. Additionally, the paper introduces 'parent score' as a new metric derived from the same score-based machinery, repurposing it to guide both topological ordering and post-processing (pre-pruning and edge supplement) in a way that prior ordering-based methods did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-world datasets routinely contain a mixture of linear and nonlinear causal relations, yet existing methods (GOLEM, SCORE, LISTEN, etc.) are each restricted to one type and degrade sharply when their assumption is violated. CaPS provides a principled unified solution with clear identifiability conditions (non-decreasing noise variance or non-weak causal effect), and the parent score offers a plug-and-play measure of causal effect strength that could be useful beyond this specific pipeline. Open questions include relaxing the Gaussian noise assumption and the two sufficient conditions for identifiability, and exploring whether parent score can serve as a general-purpose causality metric in other discovery frameworks. The cubic complexity in sample size and the known weakness on star-topology graphs (e.g., Syntren) remain practical limitations.
