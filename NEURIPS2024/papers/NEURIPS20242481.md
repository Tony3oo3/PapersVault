## Problem

Binocular 3D human pose estimation suffers from higher 3D reconstruction uncertainty than multiview setups due to the reduced number of cameras, yet the uncertainty distribution of initial 3D poses is unknown and difficult to model statistically. Existing diffusion-based approaches for monocular 3D HPE either start denoising from random noise (wasting steps without leveraging geometric constraints) or rely on statistical estimation of 3D uncertainty that is sensitive to the specific detector and dataset. This paper exploits the fact that in a binocular geometric framework, 3D uncertainty is intrinsically linked to and reconstructable from the well-defined 2D uncertainty, enabling a principled diffusion-based denoising approach.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binocular 2D joint coordinates (J joints × 2 views × 2 pixel coordinates) estimated by an off-the-shelf 2D detector (e.g., ResNet50/152, ViTPose, or RSB-Pose at 256 or 384 resolution) along with the camera intrinsic and extrinsic matrices for both views. It outputs a root-relative 3D pose (J joints × 3 coordinates) and simultaneously refined binocular 2D poses. The 3D pose is converted to root-relative format before denoising and back afterward; the denoiser itself is a lightweight GCN-Transformer with only 0.74M parameters, and inference uses K=1 DDIM iteration with T=25 diffusion steps.

## Method

Dual-Diffusion couples 2D and 3D denoising through geometric mapping: the forward process adds Gaussian noise to ground-truth 2D poses (matching the heatmap-based uncertainty of the 2D detector), then Triangulation reconstructs noisy 3D poses, thereby defining the 3D uncertainty distribution without statistical estimation. The reverse denoising process operates in 3D space (where human pose priors are inherently 3D), and Reprojection recovers the corresponding 2D poses, enabling simultaneous refinement in both domains. The training loss predicts the original 2D data via reprojection rather than predicting noise directly, addressing the depth ambiguity in the 3D-to-2D noise correspondence. Two key enhancements are introduced: Z-embedding (the absolute depth of the root joint as an additional conditioning signal, since 3D uncertainty scales with depth) and BaseL-norm (normalizing the z-axis by the baseline width B to make the model invariant to different baseline configurations). The denoiser follows a GCN-Transformer architecture with five stacked GCN-Attention modules, sinusoidal timestamp and Z-embeddings, and is trained with the standard diffusion noise-prediction objective adapted to the reprojection loss.

## Results

On the short-baseline MHAD dataset (~200mm), Dual-Diffusion with RSB-Pose152* at 384 resolution achieves 27.76mm MPJPE, 7.56mm BL, and 9.83mm Sym, reducing MPJPE by 2.02mm (6.8%) and BL by 23.2% over the Triangulation baseline. On the wide-baseline H36M dataset (~3000mm), the same configuration achieves 28.67mm MPJPE, outperforming all baselines by at least 1.87mm. The method requires only 0.74M parameters (excluding backbone) and 0.42G MACs, substantially fewer than competing approaches (e.g., 9.25M for RSB-Pose, 10.88M for Algebraic-Tri).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-Diffusion consistently outperforms all six baselines across both datasets and all metrics. Against the dedicated binocular method RSB-Pose [45], Dual-Diffusion reduces MPJPE by 4.44mm (256) and 2.02mm (384) on MHAD, while also improving 2D JDR by 2.32% and 1.80% respectively. Against multiview methods fine-tuned for binocular use—TPPT [24], Epipolar_Tri [10], Algebraic_Tri [14], and AdaFuse [56]—Dual-Diffusion achieves substantially lower MPJPE (e.g., 27.76mm vs. 209.03mm for TPPT, 90.73mm for Epipolar_Tri, 51.69mm for Algebraic-Tri, and 70.27mm for AdaFuse on MHAD). Against DiffPose [8], which uses statistical estimation of 3D uncertainty, Dual-Diffusion demonstrates superior stability: when training on a small-depth subset and testing on a large-depth subset, DiffPose degrades to 54.12mm MPJPE while Dual-Diffusion maintains 39.11mm, confirming that reconstructing 3D uncertainty from 2D uncertainty is more robust than statistical estimation. On H36M (wide-baseline), Dual-Diffusion still leads by at least 1.87mm MPJPE over the best baseline (RSB-Pose152 at 30.07mm), validating generalization across baseline widths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based 3D pose denoising paradigm introduced by DiffPose (Gong et al., CVPR 2023), which treats initial 3D estimates as noisy samples from a high-uncertainty distribution. The key departure is that instead of relying on statistical estimation of the unknown 3D uncertainty distribution (as DiffPose does), Dual-Diffusion exploits the geometric coupling in binocular setups to derive 3D uncertainty directly from the well-defined 2D Gaussian uncertainty of off-the-shelf detectors. The forward diffusion operates in 2D space while the reverse denoising operates in 3D space, connected by triangulation and reprojection, with Z-embedding and BaseL-norm as novel conditioning mechanisms to handle depth and baseline-width dependencies. The denoiser architecture follows the GCN-Transformer structure from DiffPose, and inference uses the DDIM strategy for efficient sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Binocular 3D HPE occupies a practical middle ground between monocular (depth ambiguity) and multiview (strict scene constraints) setups, yet has been largely neglected; this paper provides the first diffusion-based method specifically designed for this configuration, with a lightweight 0.74M-parameter model that works with any 2D detector. The geometric insight that 3D uncertainty is a deterministic function of 2D uncertainty, depth, and baseline width offers a principled alternative to statistical uncertainty modeling, with the zero-shot baseline-width transfer (BaseL-norm) being particularly valuable for deployment. Open questions include a rigorous algebraic derivation of the 3D uncertainty region (the current analysis relies on boundary points), explicit root-joint optimization, and whether the approach scales effectively to more than two views where the uncertainty reduction from geometry diminishes the benefit of denoising.
