## Problem

Existing imperceptible adversarial attacks rely on perceptual loss functions or generative models to reduce perturbation visibility, but none address the fundamental modeling question of how to inherently minimize perturbation strength from the attack process itself. This paper tackles that gap by asking whether the attack can be reformulated as a process that structurally produces smaller perturbations rather than post-hoc constraining them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241658/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, its ground-truth class label, and white-box access to the attacked DNN (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-Base, Swin Transformer-Base, or VisionMamba-Small). The image is rescaled to [-1,1] to align with the diffusion data range, and a fixed Gaussian noise ε₀ is initialized once. AdvAD outputs an 8-bit adversarial example (quantized via int8 rounding), while AdvAD-X outputs the raw floating-point adversarial example without quantization to evaluate the extreme perturbation limit.

## Method

AdvAD models the attack as a non-parametric DDIM backward diffusion trajectory from a fixed initialized noise ε₀ to the adversarial example, requiring no additional neural networks. At each of T=1000 steps, the Attacked Model Guidance (AMG) module applies the conditional sampling technique to compute a subtle adversarial gradient using only the attacked model's classification probability, while the Pixel-level Constraint (PC) module projects the modified noise onto an l∞ ball around ε₀ to guarantee the final perturbation stays within budget ξ. The key theoretical insight is that the guidance strength (coefficient √(1−αₜ)·∇log(1−p_f)) naturally decays to zero as the process progresses, making later steps contribute vanishingly small perturbations. AdvAD-X further adds Dynamic Guidance Injection (skipping AMG/PC when the current approximation already fools the model) and GradCAM-based masking to suppress guidance in non-critical regions, achieving perturbation as low as 0.34 l₂ distance with 100% ASR in the floating-point ideal scenario.

## Results

On the ImageNet-compatible dataset (1,000 images, 224×224), AdvAD achieves an average 99.9% ASR with only 1.34 l₂ distance, 49.74 PSNR, and 0.9971 SSIM across four attacked models (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-Base, Swin Transformer-Base, VisionMamba-Small) at budget ξ=8/255 and T=1000 steps. The enhanced AdvAD-X further reduces perturbation to 0.34–0.69 l₂ (PSNR 58–64) while maintaining 99.4–100% ASR in the ideal floating-point scenario. The l₂ perturbation is roughly half that of the best restricted baseline SSAH (avg ~2.65) and an order of magnitude lower than unrestricted diffusion-based attacks (30–75 l₂).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest restricted imperceptible baseline SSAH, AdvAD achieves comparable or higher ASR (99.7% vs 99.7% on ResNet-50; 100% vs 84.6% on ConvNeXt; 100% vs 96.3% on Swin; 99.7% vs 49.8% on VisionMamba) while halving the l₂ perturbation (1.06 vs 2.65 on ResNet-50) and improving PSNR by ~8 dB. Against unrestricted diffusion-based attacks (DiffAttack, DiffPGD, ACA, NCF), AdvAD reduces l₂ by 20–70× (e.g., 1.06 vs 30.51 for DiffAttack) while matching or exceeding their ASR, and dramatically improves perceptual quality (SSIM 0.9980 vs 0.6750, FID 2.42 vs 55.29). Against AdvDrop and PerC-AL, AdvAD achieves similar or higher ASR with 2–3× lower l₂ and substantially better FID/LPIPS. AdvAD-X pushes perturbation to an extreme 0.34 l₂ (PSNR 63.6) with 100% ASR on ResNet-50 in the ideal floating-point setting, a regime no comparison method approaches. On robustness (Table 2), AdvAD also outperforms AdvDrop, PerC-AL, and SSAH in average ASR against adversarial training models (75.3% vs 58.7%, 40.3%, 60.0% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

AdvAD departs fundamentally from both gradient-ascending attacks (PGD, FGSM) and perceptual-loss-based imperceptible attacks (PerC-AL, AdvDrop, SSAH) by reframing the attack as a non-parametric diffusion trajectory rather than an optimization problem. It inherits the modeling philosophy of DDIM's deterministic non-Markovian backward process and the conditional sampling technique from score-based generative models (Song et al., 2020), but critically strips away the neural network noise estimator, using only the attacked model to craft adversarial guidance at each step. This distinguishes it from recent diffusion-based unrestricted attacks (DiffAttack, DiffPGD, ACA) that leverage the generative or denoising capabilities of pretrained diffusion models, which the authors argue introduce unwanted semantic changes and uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the decomposition philosophy of diffusion models—converting a hard task into many small sub-steps—can yield inherently lower perturbation strength without any additional network, offering a theoretically grounded alternative to loss-function-based attack design. However, the most extreme results (AdvAD-X) rely on an ideal floating-point scenario where 8-bit quantization would erase the perturbation, limiting practical deployment. Open questions include whether the non-parametric diffusion framework extends to targeted attacks, black-box settings with limited query budgets, or video/multimodal inputs, and whether the observed robustness advantage over gradient-based attacks generalizes to stronger adversarial training regimes.
