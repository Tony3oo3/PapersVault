## Problem

CNN-based neural decoders have been applied primarily to fMRI data, but invasive multi-unit activity (MUA) recordings from macaque visual cortex offer far higher temporal resolution and finer-grained neuronal signals that remain underexploited. A key gap is the lack of space-time-resolved decoding that disentangles how distinct cortical areas (V1, V4, IT) contribute at different latency windows, and the limited interpretability of how convolutional decoders spatially organize neural inputs during reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The decoder consumes z-scored MUA signals from 576 electrodes across 15 Utah arrays (7 in V1, 4 in V4, 4 in IT), recorded at 1 ms resolution over 200 ms per trial, with area-specific time windows (0–125 ms V1, 25–150 ms V4, 50–175 ms IT) and 25 ms smoothing. After inverse retinotopic mapping, the 576-dimensional electrode vector is expanded into a 15-channel × 96×96 retinal embedding tensor (one channel per array), which the U-Net then maps to a 96×96×3 RGB reconstruction. Stimuli are presented in the lower-right quadrant, shifted 150 px from fixation, and resized to 96×96.

## Method

The homeomorphic decoder combines an inverse retinotopic mapping layer with a pixel-to-pixel U-Net. In the end-to-end variant, each electrode's contribution is modeled as a learnable 2D Gaussian (center x_e, y_e and spread σ_e) that projects its response onto the 96×96 retinal grid, allowing the network to discover its own spatial organization of inputs during training. The spatiotemporal variant stacks five timepoints per region of interest to capture dynamics aligned with ventral-pathway latencies. A modified U-Net discriminator (contracting path only) provides adversarial pressure, while the total decoder loss blends binary cross-entropy adversarial loss, VGG-19 multi-layer feature matching loss, and L1 pixel loss. The architecture is trained from scratch on 22,348 THINGS images with Adam (lr=0.002) for 50 epochs, and spatial/spatiotemporal occlusion analyses are used to probe which brain regions and time windows drive reconstruction fidelity at different AlexNet feature levels.

## Results

The spatiotemporal homeomorphic decoder achieves the highest feature correlation with original images across nearly all AlexNet layers, reaching 0.610 at FC8 and 0.579 at FC7, indicating strong reconstruction of high-level semantic features. The end-to-end inverse retinotopic mapping variant is competitive (0.543 at FC8, 0.541 at FC7) but trails the spatiotemporal model by 0.06–0.07 at the deepest layers. The baseline decoder (modified U-Net without retinal embeddings) consistently underperforms, achieving only 0.446 at FC8 and 0.203 at conv5, confirming that the intermediate retinal embedding structure is critical for faithful reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four decoder variants: the spatial model (pre-trained Inception v1 features, single timepoint), the spatiotemporal model (pre-trained features, five timepoints), the end-to-end model (learned Gaussian receptive fields, no pre-trained CNN), and a baseline modified U-Net that maps 576 electrode responses directly to a 96×96 RGB image without any retinal embedding. The spatiotemporal model outperforms all other variants on every AlexNet layer, with the largest margins at the deepest layers (FC7: +0.045 over end-to-end, +0.145 over baseline; FC8: +0.067 over end-to-end, +0.164 over baseline). The end-to-end model, despite learning its own spatial mapping without pre-trained features, remains competitive with the spatial model on mid-level layers (conv3: 0.407 vs. 0.429) but falls behind on the highest-level features. The baseline, inspired by Shen et al. (2019a), is consistently the weakest performer, confirming that the homeomorphic retinal-embedding structure provides a meaningful inductive bias for neural decoding. Spatial and spatiotemporal occlusion analyses further show that V1 contributes most to early-layer (conv1–conv2) reconstruction while IT dominates deep-layer (FC7–FC8) reconstruction, validating the hierarchical visual processing architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-based neural decoding line (Nishimoto 2011, Seeliger 2018, Shen 2019, Le 2022) to multi-unit activity (MUA) recorded from Utah electrode arrays in macaque visual cortex, offering substantially higher temporal resolution. Architecturally, it builds on Bashivan et al. (2019) by incorporating a learned inverse retinotopic mapping layer that dynamically assigns 2D Gaussian receptive fields to each electrode, and on Le et al. (2022) Brain2pix by using a fully convolutional U-Net decoder trained with adversarial and VGG feature losses. The key departure is the introduction of spatiotemporal decoding with time windows aligned to known ventral pathway latencies (V1: 0–125 ms, V4: 25–150 ms, IT: 50–175 ms), which prior fMRI-based decoders could not exploit due to their ~1 s temporal granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving from fMRI to MUA, the paper opens a path toward decoding with millisecond-level temporal precision, which is critical for understanding how visual representations evolve across the ventral stream and for potential real-time neuroprosthetic applications. The learned receptive field layer provides a transparent, interpretable bridge between electrode geometry and visual space, aligning with known cortical magnification (larger RFs peripherally). However, the single-animal, invasive recording setup and the absence of error bars limit generalizability, and the authors themselves note that translating these findings to human intracranial or non-invasive settings remains an open challenge involving scar tissue, immune responses, and anatomical variability.
