## Problem

Vanilla LoRA converges 5–6× slower than full fine-tuning under the same learning rate, increasing total FLOPs and often yielding worse test performance. The paper identifies the default random initialization (Kaiming for A, zero for B) as the root cause and asks whether a carefully designed initialization—without altering LoRA's architecture or training algorithm—can close this convergence gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241740/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pretrained weight matrices W₀, a single sampled data batch (used for one forward and backward pass to obtain per-layer gradients ∇_{W_l}ℓ), and LoRA hyperparameters (rank r, alpha α, scale factor γ). It outputs initialized adapter matrices A_init and B_init, a modified frozen weight W_frozen = W₀ − ηB_init·A_init (so the initial network function is unchanged despite non-zero BA), and the scaling factor η = α/√r. Memory during initialization is kept at O(1) in layers by computing and discarding one layer's gradient at a time via PyTorch backward hooks.

## Method

LoRA-GA has two components. First, Gradient Approximation: it performs SVD on each layer's gradient matrix ∇_Wℓ = USVᵀ and initializes A_init with the first r right singular vectors (V_{[1:r]}) and B_init with the next r left singular vectors (U_{[r+1:2r]}), scaled by √[4]{d_out}/γ. Theorem 3.1 proves this minimizes the Frobenius-norm gap between the first-step adapter update and ζ·ΔW, aligning LoRA's gradient direction with full fine-tuning from step one. Second, Scale Stability: Theorem 3.2 derives that choosing η = α/√r and ζ = α²/γ²·√d_out/r² keeps the adapter's forward and backward 2nd moments invariant to rank and input dimension, analogous to Kaiming initialization. Unlike PiSSA, which decomposes the weight matrix W under a low-intrinsic-rank assumption, LoRA-GA decomposes the gradient matrix, and unlike rsLoRA, it handles non-zero initialization so the stable scale holds from the very first step rather than only as BA evolves from zero.

## Results

On Llama 2-7B, LoRA-GA (rank=8) achieves 5.95 on MT-Bench, 53.60% on GSM8K, and 19.81% on Human-eval, outperforming vanilla LoRA by 0.34, 11.52%, and 5.05% respectively, while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning on MT-Bench and Human-eval. On the GLUE subset with T5-Base, LoRA-GA achieves an average accuracy of 87.77%, nearly matching full fine-tuning (87.91%) and outperforming vanilla LoRA (82.08%) by 5.69%. The method also achieves up to 2–4× faster convergence than vanilla LoRA, with training loss curves closely tracking full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-GA decisively outperforms vanilla LoRA across all three Llama 2-7B benchmarks: +0.34 on MT-Bench (5.95 vs 5.61), +11.52% on GSM8K (53.60 vs 42.08), and +5.05% on Human-eval (19.81 vs 14.76). Against the strongest structural variant DoRA, LoRA-GA is essentially tied on MT-Bench (5.95 vs 5.97) but wins on GSM8K (53.60 vs 53.07) and Human-eval (19.81 vs 19.75), while using fewer parameters and ~70% of DoRA's training time. LoRA-GA also beats LoRA+ (5.95 vs 5.71 MT-Bench, 53.60 vs 52.11 GSM8K, 19.81 vs 18.17 Human-eval), PiSSA (5.95 vs 5.30, 53.60 vs 44.54, 19.81 vs 16.02), rsLoRA (5.95 vs 5.25, 53.60 vs 45.62, 19.81 vs 16.01), and AdaLoRA (5.95 vs 5.57, 53.60 vs 50.72, 19.81 vs 17.80) on all three metrics. On the GLUE subset with T5-Base, LoRA-GA (87.77 avg) nearly matches full fine-tuning (87.91) and surpasses all LoRA variants, with the largest gains on small datasets like CoLA (80.57 vs 69.35 for LoRA) and MRPC (85.29 vs 68.38). The only comparison where LoRA-GA does not clearly win is MT-Bench vs DoRA, where the gap is within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by replacing its default Kaiming/zero initialization with a gradient-approximation scheme derived from SVD of the first-step gradient, while adopting the rank-stabilized scaling factor η = α/√r from rsLoRA (Kalajdzievski, 2023). Conceptually, it departs from PiSSA (Meng et al., 2024), which also uses SVD but decomposes the pretrained weight matrix under a low-intrinsic-rank assumption, whereas LoRA-GA decomposes the gradient matrix to align the first-step adapter update with full fine-tuning. The theoretical motivation draws on the observation that gradient descent operates in a low-dimensional subspace, making first-step gradient alignment a principled proxy for trajectory alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since LoRA is the most widely deployed PEFT method, a drop-in initialization change that closes the convergence gap to full fine-tuning (2–4× fewer steps) has immediate practical value for practitioners fine-tuning LLMs under compute constraints. The method requires no architectural modification, no change to the training algorithm, and negligible extra memory or time, making it trivially adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it interacts with structural variants like ReLoRA (which periodically merges adapters), and whether the scaling factor's interaction with large learning rates can be resolved more robustly. The paper also notes that double checkpoint storage (initial + final adapters) is a minor but non-trivial practical cost.
