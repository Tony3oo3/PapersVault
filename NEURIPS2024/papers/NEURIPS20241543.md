## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of average-link rely on Dasgupta's cost function and its variants, which are less interpretable than traditional cohesion and separability criteria, fail to separate average-link from a random hierarchy, and become vacuous in metric spaces where every hierarchy achieves a 1/2 approximation for CKMM. This paper fills that gap by analyzing average-link under natural, interpretable criteria (cs-ratio, sep_av, sep_min, max-diam, max-avg) that simultaneously capture both cohesion and separability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n points in a metric space with an associated distance function (Euclidean, ℓ₁, or ℓ∞ in experiments) and produces a full hierarchical clustering (strictly binary dendrogram) from which k-clusterings are extracted for any k. The theoretical results are evaluated against six criteria: cs-ratio_AV, cs-ratio_DM, sep_av, sep_min, max-diam, and max-avg, each computed on the k-clustering induced by cutting the dendrogram. Most results require the triangle inequality (metric space assumption), with the sole exception of the cs-ratio_AV ≤ 1 bound.

## Method

The paper establishes a suite of approximation guarantees for average-link using a combination of inductive arguments on merge steps, a triangle inequality for cluster averages, and the 'families' partition framework from Dasgupta and Laber (2024). Key results include: cs-ratio_AV ≤ 1 (tight, no metric assumption), cs-ratio_DM ≤ 2H_n with O(log n) approximation (nearly tight via a factorial-growth instance), sep_av ≥ OPT_SEP(k)/(k+2H_n), and max-diam ≤ min{k, 1+4ln n}·k^{log₂3}·OPT_AV(k). These bounds yield exponential separation from single-linkage and complete-linkage on cs-ratio criteria and clear separation from random hierarchies. The analysis is complemented by experiments on 10 real datasets confirming that average-link dominates when both cohesion and separability matter.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that average-link achieves cs-ratio_AV ≤ 1 (tight), cs-ratio_DM ≤ 2H_n with O(log n) approximation, sep_av ≥ OPT_SEP(k)/(k+2H_n), and max-diam ≤ min{k, 1+4ln n}·k^1.59·OPT_AV(k). Experimentally, across 10 real datasets with Euclidean distance, average-link achieves a ratio of 0.99–1.00 on sep_min (vs. 0.68–0.82 for complete-linkage and 0.38–0.76 for single-linkage) and 0.98–1.00 on cs-ratio_AV (vs. 0.68–0.82 and 0.40–0.69 respectively), while complete-linkage dominates on max-diam (ratio 1.00 vs. 0.76–0.85 for average-link).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-linkage, average-link achieves a decisive advantage on sep_min (ratio 0.99–1.00 vs. 0.68–0.76), cs-ratio_AV (0.98–1.00 vs. 0.40–0.69), and cs-ratio_DM (0.93–0.96 vs. 0.33–0.63), with the gap widening as k grows. Single-linkage slightly edges out average-link on sep_av for medium and large k (ratio 1.00 vs. 0.97–0.98), consistent with its known property of maximizing minimum spacing. Against complete-linkage, the picture is more nuanced: complete-linkage dominates on max-diam (ratio 1.00 vs. 0.76–0.85 for average-link), reflecting its natural greedy minimization of diameter, while average-link wins on sep_min (0.99–1.00 vs. 0.81–0.82) and cs-ratio_AV (0.98–1.00 vs. 0.68–0.82). On max-avg and cs-ratio_DM the two methods are competitive, with complete-linkage slightly ahead for large k and average-link slightly ahead for small k. Overall, the experiments confirm the theoretical separation: average-link is the best single choice when both cohesion and separability matter simultaneously, while no single method dominates all criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the family-based analysis framework of Dasgupta and Laber (ICML 2024), which provided cohesion bounds for a class of linkage methods including average-link, by combining it with new inductive arguments to derive the first diameter-based bounds for average-link. It deliberately departs from the dominant Dasgupta cost function (STOC 2016) and its CKMM variant, arguing that these objectives are less interpretable, fail to separate average-link from random hierarchies, and become vacuous in metric spaces where every hierarchy achieves 1/2 approximation. Instead, the paper introduces and analyzes more natural cohesion-separability criteria (cs-ratio, sep_av, sep_min, max-diam, max-avg) that are directly tied to classical clustering quality notions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first lower bound on the maximum diameter of average-link (Ω(k)·OPT_DM) and establishes a clean, tight guarantee that cs-ratio_AV ≤ 1, offering practitioners a simple worst-case certificate for average-link's cluster quality. A notable structural finding is that any method with bounded cohesion approximation necessarily suffers O(1/k) approximation on separability, revealing a fundamental tension between the two goals. Open questions include whether the metric space assumption can be relaxed (the paper shows it is necessary for the cs-ratio_DM result), whether the logarithmic gaps in the cs-ratio_DM and sep_av bounds are truly tight, and whether the experimental conclusions generalize beyond the 10 tabular datasets tested.
