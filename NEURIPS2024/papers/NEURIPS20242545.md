## Problem

Generating a complete song with both vocals and accompaniment from lyrics requires simultaneously performing vocal composition, instrumental arrangement, and ensuring harmonic coordination between the two — a capability no prior model achieved. Jukebox, the only prior attempt, treated vocals and accompaniment as a single monolithic sequence, ignoring their mutual influence and limiting the model to a single lyrics-to-song task with no support for editing, partial generation, or independent control of each track.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242545/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts lyrics text (with ASR-derived timestamps) plus optional vocal and/or accompaniment prompt audio (3-second clips controlling speaker, melody, tempo, instruments, and rhythm) and optional pre-determined vocal or accompaniment tracks. Internally, all audio is tokenized into 50 Hz BEST-RQ semantic tokens (codebook size 16,384), and the final output is rendered to 44.1 kHz waveform via a latent diffusion model. Generated songs are capped at 30 seconds, and the model can output a full mixed song, vocals-only, or instrumental-only audio depending on the task and attention mask configuration.

## Method

SongCreator's core is a Dual-Sequence Language Model (DSLM, ~0.6B params) that replaces the single concatenated sequence with two parallel autoregressive decoders — one for vocal tokens, one for accompaniment tokens — interconnected by a bidirectional cross-attention (BCA) layer that lets each decoder attend to the other's generated context at the current and prior timesteps. A lyrics encoder (4-layer Transformer) feeds cross-attention into both decoders, and a final 4-layer song decoder non-autoregressively fuses the two decoders' embeddings into a unified song token sequence. A key design is a family of attention mask strategies: causal vs. non-causal masks in self-attention, and four BCA masks (bidirectional, accompaniment-to-vocals, vocals-to-accompaniment, none) that, combined with the input format, allow a single trained model to switch among eight tasks (lyrics-to-song, lyrics-to-vocals, accompaniment-to-song, vocals-to-song, music continuation, song editing, vocals editing, vocals editing in song). Training is multi-task, mixing song generation (80% BR / 20% None BCA), pre-determined-track generation, and span-based editing with a <EDIT> token, optimizing the sum of cross-entropy losses over vocal, accompaniment, and song token sequences.

## Results

On the primary lyrics-to-song task (Table 3), SongCreator achieves FAD 2.14, musicality 4.25±0.05, and quality 4.08±0.06, approaching ground-truth levels (4.30/4.09) and substantially outperforming all baselines. On lyrics-to-vocals (Table 4), SongCreator reaches musicality 3.98±0.04, surpassing ground truth (3.89) and all competitors. In prompt-based lyrics-to-song (Table 5), SongCreator achieves similarity 3.82 vs. MusicGen's 3.27, demonstrating superior acoustic-condition control. Across all eight evaluated tasks, SongCreator achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance, with the largest margins on lyrics-to-song and lyrics-to-vocals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SongCreator decisively outperforms MusicLM (FAD 2.14 vs. 6.47; musicality 4.25 vs. 3.21) and MusicGen (FAD 2.14 vs. 2.31; musicality 4.25 vs. 3.08) on lyrics-to-song, confirming that directly conditioning text-to-music models on lyrics is insufficient for vocal composition. The GPT baseline (single-sequence autoregressive) and GPT (Vocals & Song) (two-stage) both lag substantially (FAD 8.18 and 11.23 respectively), showing that treating vocals and accompaniment as separate interrelated sequences via DSLM is critical. On lyrics-to-vocals, SongCreator (musicality 3.98) surpasses VALL-E (3.15) and MusicLM (3.31), and in zero-shot voice cloning achieves SECS 0.68 vs. VALL-E's 0.66. For vocals-to-song and accompaniment-to-song, SongCreator achieves the best FAD (1.88 and 1.24) and competitive musicality/harmony vs. SingSong, though SingSong retains a preference edge (54.1% vs. 30%) in an AB test, likely due to its larger 46k-hour training set. In music continuation, SongCreator (FAD 1.81, musicality 4.01) outperforms the GPT baseline (FAD 2.29, musicality 3.84), and in song editing it achieves the highest musicality (3.97) and similarity (3.83) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SongCreator builds on the LM+LDM paradigm established by MusicLM and MeLoDy for music generation, and on BEST-RQ for semantic token extraction, but departs from the single-sequence formulation used by Jukebox (the only prior published attempt at lyrics-to-song) by introducing a dual-sequence language model that separately models vocals and accompaniment with bidirectional cross-attention. The attention mask strategies for unifying generation, understanding, and editing tasks are directly inspired by UniLM and GLM in NLP, repurposed here for audio. The system also draws on SingSong for accompaniment generation, VALL-E for zero-shot vocal cloning, and Demucs for source separation to construct training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SongCreator is the first published system to generate harmoniously coordinated vocals and accompaniment from lyrics with independent controllability of each component, addressing a gap that Jukebox left open. The attention mask framework enabling a single 0.6B model to handle eight distinct song tasks (generation, editing, continuation) is a practical contribution for building unified music tools. Key limitations remain: 30-second maximum length prevents full-song generation, genre/style cannot be controlled via text, and vocal clarity is constrained by source-separation artifacts in training data. Open questions include whether better semantic representations (beyond BEST-RQ) can improve vocal fidelity, and whether the dual-sequence approach scales to longer, structurally complete songs.
