## Problem

Existing ego-body pose estimation methods for HMDs rely on temporally dense tracking signals from spatially sparse body parts (head and hand controllers), limiting their use in scenarios where controllers are impractical. When hand poses are only intermittently visible in egocentric video (~20% of frames), the resulting data is doubly sparse—sparse both temporally and spatially—and naively applying diffusion models to this setting yields suboptimal full-body reconstructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241750/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric RGB video sequence and a corresponding head tracking signal (15D for Ego-Exo4D including position and left/right IMU, or 18D for AMASS) as input. Hand poses are extracted from the video via FrankMoCap (3D SMPL-X hand parameters) and RTM-Pose (2D joints), yielding 3D wrist positions (3D) or position+rotation (9D) only in frames where hands are visible. The output is a temporally dense 3D full-body pose sequence: 22 joints × 6D rotation for AMASS or 17 joints × 3D position for Ego-Exo4D, over a 40-frame window.

## Method

DSPoser decomposes the doubly sparse pose estimation into two stages. First, an uncertainty-aware Masked Autoencoder imputes missing hand trajectories by treating head and hand tokens in a ViT-style encoder with dynamic attention masking over invisible-hand frames; it is trained with a β-NLL loss across M=4 independently initialized models to produce both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty estimates per frame and per dimension. Second, a VQ-Diffusion model (discrete diffusion over a VQ-VAE codebook) generates the full-body pose sequence conditioned on the imputed hand trajectories and head signal, with uncertainty guiding the denoising process through one of three strategies: sampling from the predicted Gaussian, uncertainty-proportional dropout of conditioning dimensions, or concatenating mean and variance as a distribution embedding. The sampling strategy with aleatoric uncertainty achieves the best performance, reducing MPJPE from 12.08 cm (head-only) to 5.51 cm on AMASS.

## Results

On the AMASS test set with doubly sparse input (T_s=20, MAE imputation), DSPoser achieves MPJPE of 5.51±0.02 cm, MPJVE of 24.19±0.10 cm/s, and MPJRE of 4.09±0.02°, outperforming the next best baseline (AvatarJLM with MAE) by 1.61 cm in MPJPE and 1.15° in MPJRE. On Ego-Exo4D validation (T_s=20), DSPoser achieves MPJPE of 16.84±0.04 cm and MPJVE of 39.86±0.05 cm/s, improving over BoDiffusion with MAE by 5.28 cm in MPJPE. In the dense-data setting (Table 3), DSPoser matches AvatarJLM on MPJRE (2.94° vs. 2.90°) and is within 0.38 cm on MPJPE, confirming versatility across sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSPoser consistently outperforms all baselines on the doubly sparse AMASS benchmark. Against BoDiffusion with MAE imputation (the closest competitor), DSPoser reduces MPJPE from 7.35 cm to 5.51 cm (−25%) and MPJRE from 5.47° to 4.09° (−25%) at T_s=20. Against AvatarJLM with MAE, DSPoser improves MPJPE from 7.12 cm to 5.87 cm at T_s=1, though it trails on MPJVE (49.12 vs. 37.60 cm/s) due to diffusion model stochasticity. On Ego-Exo4D, DSPoser beats BoDiffusion+MAE by 5.28 cm in MPJPE (16.84 vs. 22.12) and AvatarJLM+MAE by 4.24 cm (16.84 vs. 21.08). In the dense-data setting (Table 3), DSPoser is competitive with AvatarJLM (MPJPE 3.73 vs. 3.35 cm; MPJRE 2.94° vs. 2.90°) but shows higher MPJVE (43.43 vs. 20.79 cm/s), again attributed to diffusion sampling variance. EgoEgo and BoDiffusion without any hand input perform substantially worse (MPJPE >19 cm on AMASS), confirming the value of even sparse hand observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSPoser extends the ego-body pose estimation line of work (EgoEgo, AvatarPoser, BoDiffusion, AvatarJLM) by introducing a temporal completion stage that prior methods lack. Prior approaches assume temporally dense tracking from spatially sparse sensors (head + hand controllers), whereas this paper tackles the doubly sparse setting where hand observations are intermittent (~20% of frames) from egocentric video. Architecturally, it repurposes MAE (He et al., 2022) with a β-NLL uncertainty extension for hand trajectory imputation, and VQ-Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) with a denoising transformer (following Chi et al., 2024) for spatial completion, coupling the two stages through uncertainty-guided conditioning (sampling, dropout, distribution embedding).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on hand controllers, this work opens ego-body pose estimation to controller-free AR/VR scenarios such as sports training, outdoor experiences, and free-movement applications. The uncertainty-guided diffusion mechanism—where aleatoric uncertainty from an ensemble MAE modulates the denoising process—is a reusable pattern for any generative model conditioned on partially observed signals. Key open questions include: whether incorporating additional body parts (feet, elbows) yields further gains, how the method scales to longer temporal windows, whether the ~1-second VQ-Diffusion inference can be reduced for real-time use, and whether performance is equitable across demographic groups represented in the training data.
