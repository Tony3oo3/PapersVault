## Problem

Text-to-image models still struggle with complex compositional prompts (attribute binding, spatial relations, color attribution), and while fine-tuning with reward objectives can improve alignment, it is prone to reward hacking and poor generalization to unseen prompt distributions. ReNO addresses this by shifting the optimization from model parameters to the initial noise vector at inference time, enabling per-prompt enhancement without any fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243986/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ReNO takes a text prompt and an initial noise vector ε ~ N(0, I) in the latent space of a one-step T2I model, and produces a single generated image (512×512 or 1024×1024) after 10–50 gradient ascent iterations. The optimization is performed entirely in latent space; the final latent is decoded by a pre-trained decoder. The best image along the optimization trajectory (highest combined reward score) is selected as the output, and the entire pipeline runs in 20–50 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

ReNO's key insight is that using a distilled one-step T2I model (trained via ADD, DMD, or TSCD) reduces backpropagation to a single forward pass, eliminating the exploding/vanishing gradient problem that makes multi-step noise optimization impractical. The initial noise ε is updated via gradient ascent on a criterion combining a weighted sum of four differentiable reward models (HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward, CLIPScore) evaluated on the generated image, plus a regularization term K(ε) that penalizes deviation from the χ^d norm distribution to keep the noise in-distribution and prevent reward hacking. Optimization uses Nesterov momentum (β=0.9), gradient norm clipping (threshold 0.1), and a small regularization weight (λ_reg=0.01), with the learning rate set to 5 for 512×512 models and 10 for 1024×1024. The multi-reward objective is critical: individual rewards improve only one aspect (e.g., CLIPScore boosts faithfulness but hurts aesthetics), while the combination yields simultaneous gains in both prompt adherence and visual quality.

## Results

On GenEval, FLUX-schnell+ReNO achieves a mean score of 0.72, surpassing the multi-step FLUX-dev (0.68) and approaching DALL-E 3 (0.67) and SD3 (0.68). On T2I-CompBench, ReNO yields over 20% absolute improvements in attribute binding for SD-Turbo (Color: 0.55→0.78, Shape: 0.44→0.62, Texture: 0.57→0.75) and HyperSDXL (Color: 0.65→0.79, Shape: 0.50→0.63, Texture: 0.65→0.77). In user studies on Parti-Prompts, SD-Turbo+ReNO achieves a 77% win rate over its base model and HyperSDXL+ReNO narrowly beats the proprietary SD3 (8B) with a 54% preference rate. All results are obtained within a 20–50 second inference budget on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReNO-enhanced one-step models consistently outperform their unoptimized counterparts: SD-Turbo improves from 0.49 to 0.62 mean on GenEval, and FLUX-schnell from 0.64 to 0.72, surpassing the multi-step FLUX-dev (0.68). Against proprietary models, HyperSDXL+ReNO (0.65 mean) beats DALL-E 3 (0.67) on 4 of 6 GenEval sub-categories and achieves a 54% user-preference win rate over SD3 (8B). Compared to the multi-step noise optimization method DOODL, ReNO achieves 4× larger CLIPScore improvements while running 100× faster (20s vs. 20min) and using 75% less VRAM (15GB vs. 40GB). Against DPO-fine-tuned SDXL, SDXL-Turbo+ReNO outperforms on all attribute binding metrics (e.g., Color 0.78 vs. 0.68) and aesthetic score (6.02 vs. 5.69). ReNO also surpasses all compositional T2I methods (Composable Diffusion, Attn-Mask-Control, StructureDiffusion, TokenCompose, Attn-Exct, GORS) on T2I-CompBench, with SD-Turbo+ReNO reaching 0.78/0.62/0.75 on Color/Shape/Texture versus the best compositional method GORS at 0.66/0.48/0.63.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReNO builds directly on the initial noise optimization framework introduced by DOODL [91] and D-Flow [5], which backpropagated through multi-step diffusion models at prohibitive cost (10–40 minutes per image). The key departure is the observation that one-step distilled models (ADD, DMD, TSCD) reduce the backpropagation path to a single forward pass, making noise optimization practical at 20–50 seconds. For the optimization objective, ReNO adopts the norm-based noise regularization from Samuel et al. [79, 80] and combines multiple human-preference reward models (HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward, CLIPScore) to mitigate reward hacking, departing from prior single-reward approaches like DOODL's CLIPScore-only criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReNO makes inference-time noise optimization practical for the first time, enabling open-source one-step models to approach or match proprietary systems (DALL-E 3, SD3) without any fine-tuning. The finding that different one-step architectures converge to similar post-ReNO performance suggests current reward models are the bottleneck, motivating development of stronger compositional reward models. An unexpected benefit is that ReNO increases output diversity (lower LPIPS/DINO similarity), hypothesized to arise from structured noise moving away from the Gaussian mean. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether better reward models unlock further gains, and how to reduce the 15–50 GB VRAM overhead for broader accessibility.
