## Problem

Estimating Conditional Average Treatment Effects (CATE) in observational studies or RCTs with non-compliance is fundamentally blocked by unmeasured confounding, which violates the no-unmeasured-confounding assumption underlying most existing methods (Q-learning, Direct Learning, Causal Forests). While instrumental variable (IV) approaches can restore identification, prior work has focused on average treatment effects or optimal treatment regimes rather than providing a flexible, non-parametric framework for CATE estimation with built-in robustness to nuisance model misspecification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed data (Y, X, A, Z) where Y is a continuous outcome, X ∈ R^p are pre-treatment covariates, A ∈ {+1,−1} is the binary treatment, and Z ∈ {+1,−1} is a pre-treatment binary IV; the unobserved confounder U is never seen. The key structural detail is that the final CATE regression does not use the realized treatment A directly—only through the scalar δ(x) = P(A=1|Z=1,X=x) − P(A=1|Z=−1,X=x)—so the estimation reduces to a weighted least-squares problem on the modified outcome 2ZY/δ(X) with weights 1/π_Z(Z,X). The output is a CATE function Δ(x) and, by thresholding, the optimal individualized treatment regime d*(x) = sign(Δ(x)).

## Method

The core contribution is the IV-DL framework, which shows that under standard IV assumptions plus a conditional-independence condition on the unmeasured confounder (Assumption 3), CATE is identified by E[ZY/(δ(X)π_Z(Z,X)) | X=x], making it estimable by regressing the modified outcome 2ZY/δ(X) on X with inverse-IV-propensity weights. Because this is a weighted least-squares objective, any flexible learner (linear, LASSO, kernel ridge regression, random forests, neural networks) can be plugged in. Two residualization strategies—IV-RDL1 (shifting Y by the average of conditional mean outcomes given Z) and IV-RDL2 (shifting by a function of X, A, and Z)—further reduce variance and yield double or multiply robust estimators: IV-RDL1 is consistent if either π_Z or g* is correctly specified, while IV-RDL2 is consistent under six distinct combinations of correctly specified nuisance models. The nuisance quantity δ(x) itself is estimated as a standard CATE problem under unconfoundedness (treating A as the 'outcome' and Z as the 'treatment'), so existing methods like causal forest can be reused.

## Results

In the linear CATE setting (Setting 1), IV-RDL1 achieves the lowest MSE of 40.5×10⁻², the highest correct classification rate (AR) of 84.6%, and the highest value of 89.9×10⁻², outperforming all baselines. In the nonlinear CATE setting (Setting 2), IV-RDL1 again leads with MSE 140×10⁻², AR 77.9%, and value 90.6×10⁻². The unresidualized IV-DL also outperforms Causal Forest and MRIV on most metrics, confirming that the direct learning framework itself is effective even without residualization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BART and RD, which rely on the unconfoundedness assumption, perform worst on MSE (121 and 97.6 in Setting 1; 449 and 397 in Setting 2) because they cannot identify CATE under unmeasured confounding. IPW-MR (Cui & Tchetgen Tchetgen, 2021) achieves competitive AR and Value but does not estimate CATE (MSE = NA). Causal Forest with IV (Athey et al., 2019) and MRIV (Frauen & Feuerriegel, 2022) are the strongest IV-based baselines: in Setting 1, CF achieves MSE 89.6 and MRIV achieves 66.3, while IV-RDL1 reaches 40.5 — roughly a 40–55% reduction in MSE. In Setting 2, the gap narrows (CF 149, MRIV 150 vs. IV-RDL1 140), but IV-RDL1 still leads on all three metrics. The unresidualized IV-DL already outperforms CF and MRIV on AR and Value in both settings, demonstrating that the direct learning formulation itself provides a meaningful advantage over forest-based and multiply-robust IV approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Direct Learning (DL) framework for CATE estimation—originally developed under the unconfoundedness assumption (Tian et al. 2014; Qi and Liu 2018)—to the setting with unmeasured confounding by leveraging instrumental variables. The identification strategy builds on the conditional Wald estimand from Wang and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2018), while the residualization-based robustness extensions (IV-RDL1, IV-RDL2) parallel the Robust Direct Learning approach of Meng and Qiao (2022) and the multiply-robust IV framework of Frauen and Feuerriegel (2022). The key conceptual move is showing that CATE estimation under unmeasured confounding reduces to a weighted least squares problem, making the framework compatible with a wide range of regression and machine learning methods (LASSO, KRR, random forests, neural networks).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, modular approach to CATE estimation under unmeasured confounding that practitioners can implement with off-the-shelf ML tools, and the residualization strategies offer concrete robustness guarantees (double robustness for IV-RDL1; six distinct misspecification scenarios for IV-RDL2) that are valuable in observational settings where model specification is uncertain. Key limitations include the restriction to binary treatments and IVs, the difficulty of verifying Assumption 3 in practice, and the acknowledged problem of extreme inverse-propensity weights that can destabilize estimation. Open questions include generalization to multi-arm or continuous treatments, integration with deep neural networks, and formal treatment of the weight-extremity issue.
