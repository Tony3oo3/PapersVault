## Problem

Tensor decomposition-based (TDB) models for knowledge graph completion are theoretically fully expressive but suffer from severe overfitting in practice. Existing regularization methods either merely minimize embedding norms (yielding suboptimal performance) or are restricted to specific model families like CP and ComplEx (e.g., N3, DURA), leaving a gap for a widely applicable regularization that goes beyond embedding-level constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243497/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incomplete 3rd-order binary tensor representing a KG (entities × relations × entities) and, at test time, scores all candidate tail entities for a query (head, relation, ?) to produce a ranked list. The regularization term is computed per sampled training triplet during training, with computational complexity matching the forward pass O(DP²), ensuring no additional overhead. A weighted variant restricts regularization to only the sampled positive triplet rather than the full tensor.

## Method

The paper first unifies all TDB models (CP, ComplEx, SimplE, ANALOGY, QuatE, TuckER) under a general block-term decomposition form X = Σ_d W×₁H_{:d:}×₂R_{:d:}×₃T_{:d:}, where P (number of parts) and W (core tensor) are the distinguishing parameters. The proposed Intermediate Variables Regularization (IVR) minimizes the Frobenius norms of all intermediate variables arising from every valid way of computing X—both the single-mode contractions (W×₁H, W×₂R, W×₃T) and the two-mode contractions (W×₂R×₃T, etc.)—rather than just the raw embedding norms. Theoretically, IVR is proven to be an upper bound of the overlapped trace norm (sum of trace norms of all three mode-unfoldings), which serves as a convex surrogate for tensor rank; minimizing it encourages high correlation among entity and relation embeddings, thereby reducing overfitting. The full IVR combines two upper-bound terms (Eq. 5 and Eq. 6) with four regularization coefficients, and the tightness of the first bound (achievable when P=D, i.e., TuckER) explains why IVR-1 alone already captures most of the benefit.

## Results

On WN18RR, TuckER-IVR achieves the best MRR of 0.501 (H@1 0.460, H@10 0.579), while ComplEx-IVR leads on YAGO3-10 with MRR 0.586 (H@1 0.515, H@10 0.714) and on FB15k-237 with MRR 0.370 (H@1 0.275, H@10 0.561). Across all six base models, IVR consistently outperforms the squared Frobenius norm (F2) regularization, with the largest gains seen for TuckER (+0.052 MRR on WN18RR) and QuatE (+0.033 MRR on WN18RR). For CP and ComplEx, where the stronger N3 and DURA regularizers are applicable, IVR matches or slightly exceeds DURA on WN18RR and is competitive on FB15k-237 and YAGO3-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For CP and ComplEx, where the stronger N3 and DURA regularizers are applicable, IVR outperforms DURA on WN18RR (CP: 0.478 vs. 0.471 MRR; ComplEx: 0.494 vs. 0.484 MRR) and is competitive on FB15k-237 (CP-IVR 0.365 vs. CP-DURA 0.364; ComplEx-IVR 0.370 vs. ComplEx-DURA 0.372) and YAGO3-10 (ComplEx-IVR 0.586 vs. ComplEx-DURA 0.585). For SimplE, ANALOGY, QuatE, and TuckER, where N3 and DURA are not applicable, IVR delivers substantial gains over the standard F2 regularization: TuckER-IVR improves MRR from 0.449 to 0.501 on WN18RR (+0.052), QuatE-IVR from 0.468 to 0.493 (+0.025), and ANALOGY-IVR from 0.467 to 0.482 (+0.015). The key advantage of IVR is its generality: it applies uniformly to all six TDB models without requiring model-specific derivation, whereas N3 and DURA are restricted to CP and ComplEx. On the Kinship dataset, IVR reduces the overlapped trace norm L(X) from 30,786 (TuckER) to 11,037, confirming that the regularization effectively promotes lower-rank structure in the predicted tensor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a wide range of tensor decomposition-based (TDB) KGC models—CP, DistMult, ComplEx, SimplE, ANALOGY, QuatE, and TuckER—under a single general form parameterized by the number of parts P and a core tensor W, building on the block-term decomposition framework of De Lathauwer [2008]. It departs from prior regularization approaches (squared Frobenius norm, N3, DURA) that minimize only the norms of embedding matrices, instead proposing to regularize intermediate variables that arise during the computation of the predicted tensor. The theoretical foundation draws on the trace norm factorization lemma from Ciliberto et al. [2017], which the authors extend to the tensor setting to prove IVR is an upper bound on the overlapped trace norm. Unlike N3 and DURA, which are restricted to CP and ComplEx due to their reliance on CP decomposition structure, IVR is applicable to all TDB models within the general form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified general form provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding and comparing TDB models, and the IVR regularization offers a practical, computationally tractable drop-in improvement for a broad class of KGC models. The theoretical guarantee that IVR upper-bounds the overlapped trace norm gives practitioners confidence that the regularization is promoting low-rank structure in the predicted tensor, which is a principled way to combat overfitting. However, the method is limited to TDB models and does not extend to translation-based or neural network approaches, and the performance gains over the strongest existing regularizer (DURA) are modest on CP and ComplEx. Open questions include whether the intermediate-variables regularization philosophy can be adapted to non-TDB architectures and whether the tightness gap between the two upper bounds (Eq. 5 vs. Eq. 6) can be closed for specific model families.
