## Problem

Existing vision LLMs suffer from three critical gaps: they treat images and videos as separate entities rather than a unified visual modality, they support only a narrow subset of vision tasks (typically understanding alone, at most generation), and they lack pixel-level fine-grained perception. No prior system simultaneously handles understanding, generation, segmentation, and editing for both static images and dynamic videos within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241822/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

VITRON accepts RGB images at 336×336 resolution, video clips (each frame encoded independently then temporally pooled), free-text prompts, and regional visual inputs (bounding boxes, polygons, or scribbles) that are encoded via object-based mask representations preserving spatial geometry. The LLM produces a structured output combining a user-facing text response, a discrete invocation command (module name + instruction + optional region coordinates), and continuous signal feature embeddings split into task-specific and task-invariant components, which are jointly fed to the appropriate backend specialist (GLIGEN, SEEM, ZeroScope, I2VGen-XL, or StableVideo) to yield generated images/videos, segmentation masks, bounding boxes, or edited content.

## Method

VITRON adopts an encoder–LLM–decoder architecture with CLIP ViT-L/14@336px front-end encoders, a Vicuna-7B LLM as the central reasoning agent, and state-of-the-art visual specialists as back-end decoders. The key architectural novelty is a hybrid instruction-passing mechanism where the LLM simultaneously outputs discrete textual invocation commands (for precise module dispatch) and continuous signal embeddings (carrying modality-specific visual features that text cannot express), addressing the complementary weaknesses of each approach alone. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) basic vision-language alignment plus invocation-oriented instruction tuning on 55,000+ structured samples and embedding-oriented decoder alignment; (2) fine-grained spatiotemporal grounding instruction tuning using LoRA, covering image spatial grounding (bounding-box output), video spatiotemporal grounding (bounding boxes with frame ranges), and grounding-aware visual QA; (3) cross-task synergy learning via adversarial training, where a 4-layer 768-d Transformer discriminator is trained to classify the task from the shared task-invariant features alone, and the generator is trained to fool it, thereby purifying the shared features to be maximally task-agnostic and broadly reusable across all vision tasks.

## Results

VITRON achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across all 12 evaluated tasks. On referring image segmentation, it reaches 75.5 cIoU on RefCOCO Val and 68.9 on RefCOCOg Test, surpassing NExT-Chat (74.7/67.0) and LISA (74.1/68.5). For video tasks, VITRON attains 39.5 mIoU on VidSTG (vs. 34.2 for PG-Video-LLaVA) and 84.2 J&F on DAVIS 17 (vs. 82.8 for ISVOS). On generation, VITRON achieves 7.57 FID for text-to-image (vs. 8.46 DreamLLM), 10.11 FID / 0.3682 CLIPSIM for text-to-video (vs. 13.04/0.3085 NExT-GPT), and 175.46 FVD for image-to-video (vs. 297.62 VideoCrafter1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITRON outperforms all compared baselines on the majority of tasks. On referring image segmentation, it beats the MLLM baseline NExT-Chat by 0.8–1.9 cIoU across all RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg splits, and surpasses specialist methods like LISA and GRES by 1.4–3.1 cIoU. For video spatial grounding, VITRON leads PG-Video-LLaVA by 5.3 mIoU on VidSTG and 3.1 on HC-STVG. In video object segmentation, it exceeds ISVOS by 1.4 J&F on DAVIS 17. On image VQA, VITRON achieves 59.4/62.1 on OKVQA/GQA, beating the best grounding model MiniGPT-v2 (57.8/60.1) and non-grounding LLaVA (54.4/41.3). For generation, VITRON's 7.57 FID on COCO outperforms DreamLLM (8.46) and NExT-GPT (11.28), while its 175.46 FVD on UCF101 nearly halves VideoCrafter1's 297.62. The only notable exception is that VITRON's gains over NExT-Chat on RefCOCO Val/TestA are modest (0.8/0.6 cIoU), though it still leads on all splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITRON extends the encoder-LLM-decoder paradigm established by NExT-GPT (which it follows for embedding-oriented decoder alignment) and Osprey (for regional pixel-aware visual extraction), but departs from both by unifying image and video support within a single system and by introducing a hybrid discrete-text-plus-continuous-embedding instruction-passing mechanism. Unlike prior MLLMs that handle either understanding or generation (or only one modality), VITRON integrates off-the-shelf specialists (GLIGEN, SEEM, ZeroScope, I2VGen-XL, StableVideo) as backend decoders and adds a cross-task synergy module using adversarial feature decoupling to maximize shared task-invariant fine-grained representations. The paper positions itself as the first 'grand unified' vision MLLM spanning all four task clusters (understanding, generating, segmenting, editing) for both static and dynamic vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VITRON demonstrates that a single unified system can match or slightly surpass dedicated specialists across 12 tasks and 22 datasets, validating the feasibility of a multimodal generalist architecture. The hybrid instruction-passing mechanism and adversarial cross-task synergy learning are practical techniques that could transfer to other multi-module MLLM systems. However, the system's performance is bounded by the quality of its off-the-shelf backend specialists, and the reliance on 10–16 A100 GPUs for training limits accessibility. Open questions include whether true end-to-end unification (without separate specialist modules) is achievable, whether the synergy approach scales to substantially more tasks, and whether the pixel-level grounding gains transfer to 3D or embodied settings.
