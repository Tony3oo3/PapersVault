## Problem

While partner diversity is known to be crucial for training robust cooperative generalist agents, this paper shows that partner specialization is equally important but has been overlooked. XP-min generates diverse but overfit partners (via handshaking), and existing fixes like mixed-play regularization reduce overfitness at the cost of losing specialization. The paper asks whether both diversity and specialization can be retained while removing overfitness from XP-min partners.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained source population of XP-min joint policies (neural-network parameterized) in a Dec-POMDP cooperative environment and produces a distilled population of newly initialized partners with reduced overfitness. Quality assessment requires a handcrafted characteristic function f mapping trajectories to a discrete space (e.g., one-hot recipe completion) and an oracle generalist trained on oracle specialists for overfitness evaluation. The final output is a robust generalist agent trained against the distilled population, evaluated by harmonic-mean success rate against unseen test partners.

## Method

SpecTRL distills each source XP-min partner into a newly initialized partner via RL, where the distilled agent maximizes return when paired with its source partner, incentivizing it to elicit cooperative behaviors while avoiding sabotaging ones—naturally extracting specialization without handshaking. SpecTRL DAgger adds a DAgger imitation term where the source partner's actions serve as expert demonstrations, stabilizing distillation and preserving multi-modal behaviors that random exploration might miss. The paper also introduces three principled quality measures—diversity (entropy of the characteristic distribution), specialization (negative conditional entropy per partner), and overfitness (1 minus robustness against an oracle generalist)—to quantify and compare partner populations. The key insight is that post-hoc knowledge distillation selectively transfers diversity and specialization while discarding overfitness, unlike in-training regularization (MP-reg) which causes a loss-of-specialization problem.

## Results

On multi-recipe Overcooked, the best configuration (XP-min + MI + SpecTRL DAgger) achieves a downstream generalist robustness of 0.64±0.01 (harmonic mean success rate against test partners), with diversity 1.60, specialization −0.56, and overfitness 0.30. This outperforms raw XP-min (R=0.56, O=0.51) and XP-min+MP-reg (R=0.44, O=0.47) while maintaining comparable diversity. SpecTRL DAgger reduces overfitness by 0.22–0.34 relative to its source population while preserving diversity and specialization, whereas MP-reg and MI regularization increase diversity but significantly reduce specialization (LOS problem).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XP-min (Charakorn et al., ICLR 2023), the proposed SpecTRL DAgger pipeline (XP-min + MI + SpecTRL DAgger) improves downstream robustness from 0.56 to 0.64 while reducing overfitness from 0.51 to 0.30, with diversity maintained at 1.60 vs. 1.31. Against CoMeDi/MP-reg (Sarkar et al., NeurIPS 2023), SpecTRL DAgger substantially outperforms: XP-min + MP-reg achieves only R=0.44 with overfitness 0.47, while XP-min + MI + MP-reg reaches R=0.55 with overfitness 0.34 but suffers a larger specialization drop (S=−0.68 vs. −0.56). The key finding is that MP-reg and MI regularization during training increase diversity but cause a loss of specialization (LOS), which limits robustness gains; SpecTRL DAgger avoids this by extracting specialization post-hoc via distillation. Repeated distillation does not further reduce overfitness, suggesting the first pass effectively removes handshaking but not state-distribution overfitness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the XP-min framework (Charakorn et al., ICLR 2023) for generating diverse cooperative partners and the DAgger imitation learning framework (Ross et al., AISTATS 2011) for knowledge transfer. It departs from the concurrent MP-reg line of work (Sarkar et al., NeurIPS 2023; Cui et al., ICLR 2023), which regularizes XP-min partners during training to reduce handshaking, by instead proposing a post-hoc distillation approach (SpecTRL/SpecTRL DAgger) that extracts diverse and specialized behaviors while discarding overfitting. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'loss of specialization' (LOS) as a side effect of both MP-reg and MI regularization, arguing that diversity alone is insufficient and that partner specialization is an independent quality dimension necessary for robust generalist training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a principled tripartite framework (diversity, specialization, overfitness) for evaluating training partner populations, offering a diagnostic lens beyond the diversity-centric view that has dominated ad-hoc teamwork research. However, the experimental validation is confined to a single cooperative domain (multi-recipe Overcooked) with a handcrafted characteristic function, limiting confidence in the generality of the LOS finding and the quantitative measures. Open questions include whether these measures can serve as optimization objectives, how they interact with auxiliary objectives like opponent modeling, and whether the distillation approach generalizes to more complex or continuous-action environments. The practical impact is moderate: SpecTRL DAgger provides a simple post-training fix, but the robustness gains over XP-min baselines are modest (0.62 vs. 0.56).
