## Problem

Existing Intervention Target Estimation (ITE) methods treat each intervention instance in isolation, requiring full re-estimation whenever data or the causal graph changes slightly, and cannot leverage labeled data from controlled experiments. This leads to inefficiencies in practical settings like root cause analysis, where correlated incidents share underlying causes and labeled ground truth is often available from resolved incidents or chaos engineering.

## Input / Output

The model takes an interventional data sample x (a vector of endogenous variable values) and a known causal DAG adjacency matrix A as input, and outputs per-node Bernoulli intervention indicators γ (where γ_i=0 indicates node i was intervened), along with posterior distributions over exogenous variables u and a global observation noise precision ζ. Crucially, at inference time only the γ branch of the GAT encoder is activated, and no observational data is required—only the interventional sample and the graph structure. The architecture is inductive, generalizing to unseen graph sizes and topologies without retraining.

## Method

DeepITE is a variational graph autoencoder whose generative model encodes graph surgery via a Bernoulli intervention indicator: the intervened adjacency matrix is A_I = (γ^T 1) ⊙ A, and the decoder computes x = f2((I − A_I^T)^{-1} f1(u)), where the Neumann-series expansion makes this a single GNN layer independent of graph diameter (unlike VACA's δ−1 layer requirement). The inference model is a three-branch GAT over an undirected version of the causal graph (to capture the full Markov blanket), producing variational posteriors q(u|x,A), q(γ|x,A), and q(ζ|x,A), with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization for the Bernoulli γ. Training maximizes the ELBO in a self-supervised mode, or adds a log-likelihood term on ground-truth γ* in semi-supervised mode, enabling collaborative learning across heterogeneous graphs and intervention sets. The key architectural insight is that by parameterizing interventions as edge-removal probabilities within a single amortized model, DeepITE decouples learning from inference, allowing immediate identification of intervention targets on novel samples and graphs without retraining.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, DeepITE(sep) achieves Recall@1 of 0.723 (Graph-50), 0.685 (Graph-100), and 0.642 (Graph-500), outperforming the best baseline (MicroRCA at 0.647 on Graph-50, CI-RCA at 0.631 on Graph-100, and CI-RCA at 0.623 on Graph-500). On the ICASSP-SPGC 2022 real-world dataset, DeepITE reaches 0.881 Recall@1 and 0.984 Recall@5, surpassing the next-best method (ShapleyFlow at 0.677 Recall@1) by over 20 percentage points. On the Protein Signaling dataset, DeepITE achieves 0.652 Recall@1 versus ShapleyFlow's 0.615. Incorporating just 5–10% labeled data boosts Recall@1 by 4–20% across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepITE outperforms all 13 baselines on every graph size in the synthetic benchmark. Traditional ITE methods (UT-IGSP, CITE, PreDITEr) are the weakest, dropping to near-zero Recall@1 on 500-node graphs (e.g., CITE: 0.007) due to their reliance on large sample sizes per instance and inability to share information across interventions. XAI methods (TreeExplainer, ASV, ShapleyFlow, PWSHAP) perform moderately on small graphs but degrade sharply at scale; ShapleyFlow is the closest XAI competitor at 0.552 Recall@1 on Graph-50 versus DeepITE's 0.723. Among RCA methods, CI-RCA and MicroRCA are the strongest baselines (0.631 and 0.623 Recall@1 on Graph-100), yet DeepITE still leads by 5–6 points. On the ICASSP-SPGC 2022 real-world dataset, DeepITE's 0.881 Recall@1 exceeds CI-RCA (0.559) and ShapleyFlow (0.677) by 20+ points, and on Protein Signaling it leads ShapleyFlow (0.615) by ~4 points. Notably, DeepITE is the only method that supports both self-supervised and semi-supervised training, and its O(m+n) inference complexity is the lowest among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepITE extends the decoder architecture of DAG-GNN (x = f₂((I−Aᵀ)⁻¹f₁(u))) by introducing a Bernoulli intervention indicator γ that performs graph surgery, enabling the model to represent both observational and interventional distributions within a single generative framework. It also builds on the VGAE paradigm for causal queries introduced by VACA, but overcomes VACA's requirement of δ−1 MPNN layers (where δ is graph diameter) by using a single GNN layer whose Neumann-series expansion naturally captures all ancestral paths. The paper departs from prior ITE methods (CITE, PreDITEr, CI-RCA, RCD) by shifting from per-instance, fixed-graph estimation requiring observational data at inference to collaborative, amortized learning across heterogeneous causal graphs and intervention targets, with a GAT-based inference network that aggregates messages from the full Markov blanket rather than only parents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepITE addresses a practically critical gap in causal analysis: the ability to identify intervention targets (root causes) without retraining for each new incident, which is essential for real-time RCA in microservice systems where downtime costs are high. The finding that a single model trained across diverse graph sizes and structures (DeepITE-mix) performs competitively with per-size models suggests that amortization error is negligible, opening the door to a single deployable model for production systems. Key limitations include the assumption of causal sufficiency and a known DAG structure; extending to unknown or partially known graphs, handling latent confounders, and scaling to truly massive graphs (thousands of nodes) remain open. The semi-supervised capability—where 5–10% labeled data from chaos engineering exercises yields 4–20% recall gains—bridges the gap between purely data-driven ITE and domain-knowledge-informed diagnosis.
