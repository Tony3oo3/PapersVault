## Problem

Top-2 algorithms for best-arm identification sequentially pull the empirical best arm with a fixed probability β and the best challenger arm otherwise, but determining the optimal β that matches the information-theoretic lower bound on sample complexity has remained an open problem. This paper resolves the gap by replacing the fixed β with an adaptive rule derived from the first-order optimality conditions of the lower bound, yielding an algorithm that is asymptotically optimal as δ→0 without requiring the user to tune β.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a K-arm bandit instance whose distributions belong to a known single parameter exponential family (SPEF), a confidence parameter δ>0, and an exploration parameter α∈(0,1). At each iteration it adaptively selects one arm to sample, and terminates when the generalized log-likelihood ratio (GLLR) statistic exceeds a threshold of order log(1/δ), outputting the empirically best arm. The exploration parameter α controls a forced-exploration phase (each arm receives at least N^α samples) that guarantees sufficient coverage before the anchor-based decision rule takes over.

## Method

The Anchored Top-2 (AT2) algorithm defines an anchor function g(μ̃, Ñ) = Σ_{a≠1} d(μ̃₁, x̃₁ₐ)/d(μ̃ₐ, x̃₁ₐ) − 1, which equals zero precisely at the optimal allocation proportions. When g > 0 the algorithm pulls the empirical best arm (decreasing g); when g ≤ 0 it pulls the challenger arm with the smallest empirical index (increasing g), creating a self-correcting feedback loop. The key theoretical contribution is a fluid-dynamics analysis: by applying the implicit function theorem to the system of equations characterizing the optimal allocation, the authors derive a system of ODEs that the arm allocations track in the limit, showing that once all challenger indexes have been pulled they rise together and the proportions converge to the optimal ω*. A variant, IAT2, adds a log Ñₐ term to the challenger index to handle instances with tied sub-optimal means. The proof of asymptotic optimality (Theorem 3.1) shows the stopping time satisfies limsup_{δ→0} E[τ_δ]/log(1/δ) ≤ T*(μ), matching the lower bound.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that both AT2 and IAT2 are asymptotically optimal: limsup_{δ→0} E_μ[τ_δ]/log(1/δ) ≤ T*(μ), matching the information-theoretic lower bound. Proposition 3.1 further shows the algorithm's allocation proportions converge to the optimal ω* at rate O(N^{-3/8}) after a random stabilization time T_stable with finite expectation independent of δ. Numerically, on a 4-armed Gaussian bandit (μ=[10,8,7,6.5], unit variance), AT2 and IAT2 achieve lower sample complexity than all tested variants of β-EB-TCB [16] and TCB/ITCB [22], with the advantage most pronounced when β is far from its (unknown) optimal value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against β-EB-TCB and β-EB-ITCB [16], AT2 and IAT2 consistently achieve lower sample complexity across all tested values of β (Figure 2, Appendix J). The advantage is particularly large when β is chosen suboptimally, since AT2/IAT2 do not require specifying β at all — the anchor function g(·) automatically determines when to pull the best arm versus the challenger. Against TCB and ITCB [22], AT2 and IAT2 also show significantly lower average sample complexity. Notably, the paper observes numerically that TCB does not quite satisfy the asymptotic optimality conditions (Figure 4, Appendix J), whereas AT2/IAT2 do. IAT2 additionally outperforms AT2 in sample complexity and, unlike AT2, does not suffer from sample complexity blow-up on instances where multiple sub-optimal arms share the same mean (Appendix J.4), thanks to the extra logarithmic term in its challenger selection rule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines in the multi-armed bandit literature: the fixed-confidence best-arm identification (BAI) problem (Garivier & Kaufmann, 2016) and the top-2 algorithmic framework (Russo, 2016; Jourdan et al., 2022; Mukherjee & Tajer, 2023). It directly addresses the open problem of determining the optimal sampling probability β in top-2 algorithms by replacing the fixed β with a deterministic rule based on an anchor function g(·) derived from the first-order optimality conditions of the max-min lower bound. The analytical framework departs from prior work by constructing a fluid dynamics model via the implicit function theorem (IFT) to characterize the algorithm's asymptotic allocation path, a technique not previously applied in this context. Compared to the computationally demanding track-and-stop algorithm of Garivier & Kaufmann, the proposed AT2/IAT2 avoids repeated optimization while achieving the same asymptotic constant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing open problem in the top-2 BAI literature by showing that a simple deterministic anchor function can achieve asymptotic optimality without the computational overhead of solving the max-min problem at each step. The fluid dynamics framework via IFT provides a reusable analytical tool for studying sequential allocation algorithms, and the construction of ODE paths that are 'pasted together' as index sets merge offers a new lens for understanding bandit dynamics. Key limitations include the restriction to single-parameter exponential families, the stationarity assumption, and the absence of non-asymptotic guarantees. Open questions include extending the anchor-function approach to best-k identification, non-stationary settings, and establishing whether the fluid dynamics framework generalizes to other pure-exploration problems.
