## Problem

Existing search-based neural theorem proving methods treat proofs as flat sequences of steps guided by short-sighted heuristics (log-probability or value-function scores), which can trap the search in meaningless intermediate subgoals and fail on longer proofs. As proof length grows, the search space expands exponentially, making myopic step-by-step exploration increasingly unreliable. The paper asks whether exploiting the hierarchical structure inherent in formal proofs—proving at a high level first, then filling in details—can overcome this bottleneck.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the language model receives the current Isabelle proof state and the preceding proof context (formatted as INPUT: CONTEXT(context) GOAL(proof state) STEP) and generates the next Isar tactic. The key structural difference from step-by-step methods is that the model is trained to emit a `sorry` keyword appended to conjecture-introducing tactics (e.g., `have "x+2=2x" sorry`), so the output at each level is a verifiable proof sketch with deeper sub-proofs deferred. The final output is a complete Isabelle/Isar proof once all `sorry` placeholders are recursively resolved.

## Method

POETRY introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a recursive data construction pipeline parses complete Isabelle proofs into proof lines with associated proof levels, then recursively extracts level-by-level proof sketches by replacing all higher-level proof steps with a merged `sorry` tactic, producing training pairs where the model learns to state conjectures and defer their proofs simultaneously. Second, a recursive best-first search (recursive BFS) algorithm conducts level-by-level proof search: it finds a verifiable proof sketch at the current level using standard BFS with beam-search decoding, pauses upon finding a sketch containing `sorry` edges, then recursively descends to prove the skipped conjectures at the next level. Node statuses (OPEN, FAILED, PROVED, HALF-PROVED) and special sorry edges manage backpropagation of success or failure across levels, allowing the search to abandon false sketches and continue exploring alternatives at the parent level. The underlying model is a 1.3B-parameter decoder-only transformer (proofGPT) fine-tuned on the recursively curated PISA dataset, with all other hyperparameters matched to the step-by-step GPT-f baseline for fair comparison.

## Results

POETRY achieves a pass@1 success rate of 42.2% on both miniF2F-valid and miniF2F-test, representing a 5.1% absolute improvement over the previous state-of-the-art (Thor+Magnushammer at 37.3% test). On the PISA test set, POETRY reaches 49.7% overall, with a 2.5% gain on the multi-level subset (13.6% vs. 11.1% for GPT-f Baseline). The maximum proof length discovered increases from 10 to 26 steps on PISA and from 3 to 18 on miniF2F compared to the step-by-step GPT-f Baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POETRY outperforms all nine baselines on miniF2F. Against the strongest Isabelle-based SOTA, Thor+Magnushammer (36.9%/37.3%), POETRY gains 5.3%/4.9% on valid/test respectively (5.1% average). Against the direct step-by-step ablation (GPT-f Baseline, 39.3%/37.3%), the recursive proving strategy yields a 3.9% average improvement, confirming the contribution of the recursive BFS over flat best-first search. On PISA, the gain is more modest overall (49.7% vs. 49.0%) because 69% of problems are single-level where POETRY behaves identically to the baseline; however, on the multi-level subset the improvement is 2.5% (13.6% vs. 11.1%). Among Lean-based methods (PACT, FMSCL, Leandojo, COPRA), POETRY leads by 11.6–18.6% on test, though the authors note Isabelle's larger formal library and Sledgehammer integration partly explain the environment gap. POETRY also solves 99 theorems in PISA that the GPT-f Baseline cannot, accounting for 4.4% of the test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

POETRY extends the step-by-step search-based neural theorem proving paradigm established by GPT-f (Polu & Sutskever, 2020) and its Isabelle-specific instantiation in Thor (Jiang et al., 2022a), which rely on flat best-first search guided by log-probability or value-function heuristics. The key departure is the introduction of a recursive, level-by-level proving strategy that exploits Isabelle's proof-level structure and the `sorry` tactic to decompose long proofs into short, independently verifiable proof sketches before descending into sub-proofs. This contrasts with both the flat search approaches (GPT-f, PACT, FMSCL, LeanDojo, COPRA) and the LLM-based whole-proof generation methods (DSP, Lyra, LEGO-Prover), positioning POETRY as a structural rethinking of how the search space is organized rather than a better heuristic within the same flat search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the primary bottleneck for neural theorem proving on longer proofs is not model capability but the combinatorial explosion of the flat search space, and that a simple recursive decomposition (leveraging the existing `sorry` tactic) can extend the maximum reachable proof length by 2.6×–6× without any architectural change. This opens a clear path for integrating recursive proving with existing enhancements (Sledgehammer, Magnushammer, expert iteration, MCTS) that the authors leave as future work. Key limitations include the greedy exploration strategy that can waste budget on dummy sketches, the lack of a value function to prioritize informative sketches, and the current restriction to Isabelle (though the authors argue extension to Lean/Coq is straightforward). Whether the recursive paradigm generalizes to formal environments without a native proof-level structure remains an open question.
