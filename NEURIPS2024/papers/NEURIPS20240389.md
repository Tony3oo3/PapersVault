## Problem

Existing diffusion-based test-time adaptation methods like Diffusion-TTA rely on Monte Carlo likelihood estimation over ~180 timesteps, incurring high computational cost, and use low-dimensional image-level conditioning that limits expressiveness as a discriminative prior. The high-dimensional latent space of diffusion models is known to contain interpretable semantic structure, but this structure has not been formally connected to discriminative score functions or exploited for test-time adaptation of classifiers and dense predictors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240389/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled test image (224×224 for classification, resized to 256×256 for the DiT-XL/2 diffusion model; single images at native resolution for segmentation), a pre-trained discriminative task model, and a pre-trained conditional diffusion model trained on the same source domain. A Candidate Selection Module prunes the full class space to a small budget b = k + m (e.g., 4 top-k + 2 multinomial samples = 6), so only b conditional noise predictions are computed per sample. Output is a class label (top-1) for classification or a per-pixel segmentation map of shape H×W×K for dense prediction.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) shows that the score function of the marginal data distribution decomposes as a posterior-weighted sum of conditional score functions, meaning the noise prediction of a conditional diffusion model at any single timestep implicitly encodes the class posterior. DUSA exploits this by constructing a single-timestep objective (Eq. 10) that aligns the true noise ε with a weighted sum of conditional noise predictions ε_φ(x_t, t, c_y) weighted by the task model's softmax probabilities, shifting computational complexity from O(T) timesteps to O(K) classes. A Candidate Selection Module with LogitNorm and multinomial sampling handles class bias under small budgets, and a DUSA-U variant adapts the diffusion model unconditionally via null conditioning to reduce backward-pass cost. The framework extends to dense prediction by applying the score decomposition per-pixel, using image-level noise estimations from off-the-shelf diffusion models without modifying their training.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5), DUSA achieves average top-1 accuracy of 53.3% (ResNet-50), 63.1% (ViT-B/16), and 66.2% (ConvNeXt-L) across 15 corruption types, outperforming the strongest baseline Diffusion-TTA by +2.0%, +4.5%, and +5.1% respectively. Under the more challenging continual TTA protocol, DUSA reaches 67.7% on ConvNeXt-L, surpassing Diffusion-TTA (60.4%) by +7.3%. For test-time semantic segmentation on ADE20K-C, DUSA achieves 28.1% mIoU with SegFormer-B5, exceeding the best prior method CoTTA (23.7%) by +4.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUSA consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every backbone and task. Against the closest competitor Diffusion-TTA [32], DUSA gains +2.0% (ResNet-50), +4.5% (ViT-B/16), and +5.1% (ConvNeXt-L) in fully TTA, and +7.3% in continual TTA, attributed to replacing multi-timestep Monte Carlo likelihood estimation with a single-timestep score-based objective. Among entropy-based methods, EATA [60] is the strongest non-diffusion baseline (45.3/60.9/53.4%), yet DUSA still leads by 8.0/2.2/12.8 points respectively. SAR [59] and RoTTA [61] show notable instability on specific corruptions (e.g., SAR drops to 7.2% on Elastic, RoTTA to 23.3% on Contrast for ViT), while DUSA maintains consistent gains across all 15 corruption types. Tent [44] and CoTTA [45] underperform the source-only model on ResNet-50 (29.1/31.5 vs 31.4), highlighting their fragility, whereas DUSA improves by +21.9% over the source model. For semantic segmentation, DUSA (28.1% mIoU) surpasses CoTTA (23.7%), Tent (18.7%), and BN Adapt [62,63] (18.2%) by 4.4/9.4/9.9 points, demonstrating that the score-based semantic priors transfer effectively to dense prediction without per-pixel diffusion conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion-TTA [32], which first repurposed conditional diffusion models for test-time adaptation by modulating class conditions with task model predictions and maximizing a Monte Carlo-estimated likelihood over ~180 timesteps. DUSA departs from this by reframing the problem through score functions and Tweedie's formula, revealing (via Proposition 1) that conditional score functions implicitly encode discriminative posteriors p(y|x) at every single timestep, thereby eliminating the need for multi-timestep likelihood estimation. The theoretical connection to the semantic structure of diffusion latent spaces [33–37] is repurposed from a generative viewpoint to a discriminative one, and the practical design (Candidate Selection Module with LogitNorm) draws from entropy-minimization TTA literature [44, 54, 59] to stabilize optimization over a pruned class set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-timestep formulation shifts computational complexity from O(T) to O(K), making diffusion-based TTA substantially more efficient while improving accuracy by 5–7% over Diffusion-TTA across multiple backbones and corruption types. The DUSA-U variant—where the diffusion model is adapted only on the null condition yet still transfers knowledge to the task model—suggests that structured semantic priors are inherently embedded in well-trained diffusion models, a finding that could inform future work on lighter score estimators. Key limitations include the requirement for a diffusion model pre-trained on the same source domain and the residual computational overhead relative to task-model-only methods, which constrains deployment in latency-critical settings like autonomous driving.
