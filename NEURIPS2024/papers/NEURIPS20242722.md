## Problem

Federated primal-dual methods (FedPD, FedADMM, FedDyn) suffer from a critical 'dual drift' problem under partial participation: clients that remain inactive for many rounds accumulate severely outdated dual variables, and upon reactivation the large primal-dual mismatch causes local subproblems to become unstable or diverge entirely. This issue worsens as the participation ratio decreases, making primal-dual methods impractical in large-scale FL deployments where most clients are inactive at any given round.

## Input / Output

Each edge client holds a private subset of CIFAR-10/100 images (32×32 RGB) split via Dirichlet distributions (IID, Dir-1.0, Dir-0.1) to simulate label heterogeneity, with optional brightness perturbations per client. The global server centrally stores and manages all dual variables (reducing edge-device memory), and the method outputs a single global classification model. A key design constraint is that unparticipated clients never communicate; their dual variables are updated virtually on the server using the aggregated model as an unbiased proxy.

## Method

A-FedPD introduces virtual dual updates for unparticipated clients to keep their dual variables aligned with the evolving global primal variable. At each round, active clients solve the local augmented Lagrangian subproblem via K SGD steps and send back their updated parameters; the server aggregates these as θ̄^{t+1}. Active clients receive the standard dual update λ_i^{t+1} = λ_i^t + ρ(θ_i^{t+1} − θ^t), while unparticipated clients receive a virtual update λ_i^{t+1} = λ_i^t + ρ(θ̄^{t+1} − θ^t), approximating what their dual would be under full participation. The global model is then set to θ^{t+1} = θ̄^{t+1} + (1/ρ)λ̄^{t+1}. This mechanism ensures dual variables never stagnate, effectively converting partial participation into quasi-full-participation training. Theoretically, A-FedPD achieves O(1/T) convergence on smooth non-convex objectives and a tighter generalization error bound O((PT)^{μL/(1+μL)}/CS) than FedAvg's O((PKT)^{μL/(1+μL)}/CS), since the primal-dual local update acts as a stable decayed sequence independent of K.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (C=100, P=10, K=50), A-FedPDSAM achieves 87.44% (IID), 86.46% (Dir-1.0), and 82.48% (Dir-0.1) test accuracy, outperforming all baselines. On CIFAR-100 with LeNet, A-FedPDSAM reaches 55.56% (IID) and 53.15% (Dir-0.1), while FedDyn fails to converge entirely on ResNet-18 CIFAR-100. In terms of communication efficiency, A-FedPD requires only 131 rounds to reach 81% on CIFAR-10 (3.82× fewer than FedAvg's 501 rounds) and 123 rounds for 40% on CIFAR-100 (6.27× fewer than FedAvg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-FedPDSAM (SAM variant) achieves the best accuracy across nearly all settings, beating FedSpeed (the strongest primal-dual baseline) by 0.46–1.87% on CIFAR-10 and 1.55–3.49% on CIFAR-100. Against the primal-average baselines, A-FedPD (SGD) outperforms SCAFFOLD by ~1.1% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.1 (LeNet) and by ~2.0% on CIFAR-100 Dir-0.1 (LeNet), while A-FedPDSAM extends this gap to 3.3% and 6.8% respectively. The most striking comparison is with FedDyn: on ResNet-18 CIFAR-100, FedDyn fails to converge entirely (marked '–'), whereas A-FedPD still achieves 48.15% (IID) and 46.24% (Dir-0.1), demonstrating that the virtual dual update effectively eliminates the dual-drift instability. On CIFAR-10, A-FedPD averages ~0.8% higher than FedDyn (LeNet) and ~1.4% higher (ResNet-18). In communication efficiency, A-FedPD requires 3.82× fewer rounds than FedAvg and 1.85× fewer than SCAFFOLD to reach 83.5% on CIFAR-10, confirming that the dual-alignment mechanism accelerates convergence without sacrificing stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated primal-dual optimization (FedPD, FedADMM, FedDyn) and the partial-participation training paradigm that is standard in FL. It builds directly on FedDyn's global dual variable mechanism and FedADMM's partial-participation formulation, but identifies a previously underappreciated failure mode—'dual drift'—where long-inactive clients accumulate stale dual variables that destabilize local subproblem solving upon reactivation. The proposed A-FedPD method introduces virtual dual updates for unparticipated clients, a conceptually simple extension that approximates quasi-full-participation training without requiring actual communication with those clients. The SAM-based variant (A-FedPDSAM) follows the same lineage as FedSpeed, which already combined primal-dual updates with sharpness-aware minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual drift problem is a practical bottleneck that limits the deployment of federated primal-dual methods in real-world FL settings with large client pools and low participation ratios; A-FedPD's virtual update mechanism offers a lightweight fix that requires only server-side bookkeeping. The theoretical contribution—a tighter generalization bound of O((PT)^(μL/(1+μL))/CS) versus O((PKT)^(μL/(1+μL))/CS) for primal-average methods—formalizes why primal-dual methods can tolerate longer local intervals without stability loss, which is practically important for bandwidth-constrained edge deployments. A noted limitation is the additional server-side storage for maintaining dual variables of all C clients, and the paper acknowledges that approximating virtual updates locally (to reduce server load) remains an open direction.
