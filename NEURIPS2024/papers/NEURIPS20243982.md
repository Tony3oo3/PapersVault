## Problem

Existing deblurring datasets face a fundamental trade-off: synthetic datasets (GoPro, REDS) aggregate discrete high-speed frames, producing blur that fails to generalize to real-world motion, while real datasets (BSD, RSBlur) require expensive beam-splitter camera systems, are limited to single camera models, demand ISP correction, and offer restricted blur trajectories due to human capture. No prior dataset simultaneously provides realistic blur diversity, large scale, and broad camera-model generalizability.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes posed multi-view sharp images from MVImgNet (26 selected object classes, captured by diverse consumer cameras) along with their SfM-estimated camera poses. It outputs paired blurry–sharp image sets where the sharp image is rendered from the midpoint camera pose of a trajectory and the blurry image is the linear-RGB accumulation of M=121 sub-frames rendered along that trajectory, converted to sRGB. The final dataset contains 156,209 sharp renderings and 752,335 blurry images at various resolutions (down-scaled ×1/2, ×1/3, ×1/4 from ~1920×1080), with Poisson and Gaussian noise added in RAW space before sRGB conversion.

## Method

The core mechanism reconstructs 3D scenes via 3D Gaussian Splatting (30k iterations per scene) from MVImgNet multi-view images, then synthesizes motion blur by rendering a virtual camera along randomly generated Bezier curves (order 1–5, 3D translation up to 0.7 units, rotation ±1.5°) through the scene. Unlike prior synthetic methods that average a few discrete frames, GS-Blur accumulates 121 densely sampled sub-frames in linear RGB space, closely approximating continuous shutter integration. Four augmentation components extend realism: (1) 1-to-n blur generation producing multiple independent trajectories per sharp image to prevent overfitting, (2) rigid-body object motion blur using segmentation masks to apply separate trajectories to foreground objects and background, (3) realistic sensor noise via the RSBlur RAW-space pipeline, and (4) multi-resolution rendering to simulate varying camera-to-object distances. Scenes are filtered by a PSNR threshold (excluding any view with >3 dB drop from the scene mean) to remove 3D reconstruction artifacts from the training set.

## Results

In cross-validation (Table 2), NAFNet trained on GS-Blur achieves the best PSNR on all four test sets when the training set differs from the test set: 31.49 dB on GoPro, 30.54 dB on REDS, 31.37 dB on BSD, and 32.30 dB on RSBlur, outperforming the next-best baseline by 1.2–5.5 dB. On real blurry images without ground truth (Table 3), GS-Blur-trained NAFNet scores 48.004 MUSIQ and 0.332 TOPIQ, surpassing the previous best (BSD: 45.192 / 0.318) by ~2.8 MUSIQ and ~0.014 TOPIQ. The dataset contains 156,209 sharp renderings and 752,335 blurry images across 3,408 3D-reconstructed scenes, roughly 5× larger than the next-largest dataset (REDS, 30,000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GS-Blur against four established deblurring datasets: GoPro (Nah et al., CVPR 2017, synthetic), REDS (Nah et al., CVPR-W 2019, synthetic), BSD (Zhong et al., ECCV 2020, real beam-splitter), and RSBlur (Rim et al., ECCV 2022, real+synthetic). In cross-validation with NAFNet, GS-Blur-trained models outperform all four baselines on every test set where the training set does not match: e.g., on the GoPro test set, GS-Blur achieves 31.49 dB vs. 30.24 (RSBlur, next best) and 27.26 (REDS); on the RSBlur test set, GS-Blur reaches 32.30 dB vs. 31.43 (BSD). The only losses occur in the diagonal (train = test), which is expected. On real blurry images from the DVD dataset (no ground truth), GS-Blur achieves 48.004 MUSIQ and 0.332 TOPIQ, beating BSD (45.192 / 0.318) by a clear margin, confirming superior generalization to unseen real-world blur. Ablation studies (Table 5) further show that each pipeline component (1-to-n blur, rigid-body motion, noise, multi-resolution) contributes 0.3–1.5 dB PSNR improvement over the base 3DGS rendering alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), originally developed for real-time novel view synthesis, as a blur synthesis engine: instead of capturing blur with beam-splitter cameras (BSD, RSBlur) or aggregating discrete high-speed frames (GoPro, REDS), it reconstructs 3D scenes from MVImgNet multi-view images and renders blurry images by moving virtual cameras along random Bezier trajectories. The noise synthesis pipeline is borrowed from RSBlur, and the overall blur accumulation model (averaging sub-frames in linear RGB space) follows the formulation from GoPro/REDS. The key conceptual departure is treating blur generation as a 3D rendering problem rather than a temporal aggregation or hardware-capture problem, enabling arbitrary trajectory diversity and 1-to-n blur generation that prior pipelines cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GS-Blur (752K pairs) is the largest deblurring dataset to date and the first to demonstrate that a fully synthetic, 3D-rendered blur distribution can overlap with and generalize to real-world blur as well as or better than purpose-built real datasets (BSD, RSBlur) in cross-validation. This challenges the prevailing assumption that real captured blur is necessary for generalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes (the authors note 4DGS as a path forward), whether rendered sharp images introduce a domain gap that limits absolute quality, and whether the 3DGS-based blur synthesis paradigm can be applied to other restoration tasks (e.g., defocus, defocus+motion).
