## Problem

Unlearnable examples (UEs) have been extensively studied for image classification, but their effectiveness for fine-grained image segmentation remains unexplored. Existing UE methods fail to simultaneously address three critical challenges for segmentation: data efficiency (training on small datasets), generation efficiency (producing noise without per-image optimization), and transferability (generalizing across diverse segmentation tasks, datasets, and architectures). No prior method can generate UEs that are universally effective against the wide range of segmentation models and scenarios encountered in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a mask prompt specifying the object region to protect, and produces a pixel-wise unlearnable noise map bounded by an L∞ norm constraint (ε = 8/255 for protected regions, ε = 2/255 for unrelated regions). Unlike gradient-based UE methods that require iterative per-image optimization, UnSeg generates the noise in a single forward pass of the finetuned generator, making it applicable to arbitrary images in seconds. The mask prompt can also be a point or bounding box, though mask prompts are preferred to eliminate ambiguity about which object is being protected.

## Method

UnSeg finetunes the pre-trained Segment Anything Model (SAM) into a universal unlearnable noise generator by adding three learnable noise tokens (3×C) to SAM's mask decoder, which perform self-attention and cross-attention with image embeddings to produce noise via δ^u = tanh(F ⊗ T_noise^T) × ε. The generator is trained via bilevel min-min optimization against a surrogate model (a re-initialized SAM with MAE-pretrained backbone) on the small-scale HQSeg-44K interactive segmentation dataset, where the generator and surrogate are alternately optimized to minimize segmentation loss. A key technical contribution is epsilon generalization (EG): during training, the noise budget is proportionally reduced by a scaling factor v (using ε/v) to prevent the surrogate model's training loss from collapsing too quickly, while at inference the full ε is restored, enabling stable training with small noise that generalizes to larger noise budgets. The framework is trained for only 27 epochs on 8 GPUs (~10 hours) and requires no additional labels beyond the mask prompt, making it both data-efficient and generation-efficient.

## Results

UnSeg achieves dramatic performance degradation across 6 segmentation tasks, 10 datasets, and 7 architectures. On COCO instance segmentation with Mask2Former (Swin-T backbone), AP drops from 45.0 to 2.7 (a 94% reduction), and AP-L drops from 67.4 to 0.7. On Cityscapes panoptic segmentation (R50), PQ drops from 62.1 to 5.7 (56.4% reduction). On Pascal VOC 2012 semantic segmentation, mIoU is reduced to approximately 7% for both DeepLabV1 and DeepLabV3, compared to ~70–76% clean performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnSeg substantially outperforms all three training-free baselines (SynPer, AR, CUDA) on the majority of metrics across all three datasets. Against SynPer, UnSeg reduces COCO panoptic PQ from 11.3 to 4.2 (R50) and Cityscapes PQ from 30.1 to 5.7, while SynPer's cross-architecture weakness is evident (it drops DeepLabV3 mIoU to 11% but only DeepLabV1 to 19%). AR is the weakest baseline, barely reducing ADE20K PQ (37.8 vs. 39.7 clean) and Cityscapes PQ (51.6 vs. 62.1), indicating autoregressive perturbations are largely ineffective for segmentation. CUDA is the most competitive baseline, achieving PQ of 10.7 on ADE20K (slightly below UnSeg's 11.7) and 6.7 on COCO (vs. UnSeg's 4.2), but it fails catastrophically on Cityscapes (PQ 51.6 vs. UnSeg's 5.7), revealing poor transferability to complex real-world scenes. Overall, UnSeg's advantage is most pronounced on Cityscapes and with Swin-T backbones, where it achieves near-total performance collapse (PQ < 8, AP < 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) paradigm from image classification to image segmentation, building directly on the bilevel min-min optimization formulation of Huang et al. [25] and the Segment Anything Model [31] as the architectural backbone. It departs from all prior gradient-based UE methods (UE, RUE, SUE, TUE) by replacing per-image optimization with a single forward pass through a finetuned SAM equipped with three learnable noise tokens in the mask decoder. It also moves beyond training-free perturbative baselines (SynPer, AR, CUDA) that were designed for classification and lack the promptability and transferability needed for fine-grained segmentation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UnSeg establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for unlearnable examples in image segmentation, demonstrating that a single generator trained on one small-scale interactive segmentation dataset (HQSeg-44K) can protect across 6 tasks, 10 datasets, and 7 architectures with up to 92% performance degradation. The practical implication is significant: as foundation models like SAM and VLMs make it trivial to segment and interpret any posted image, this provides a data-efficient, generation-efficient defense for privacy-conscious users. Key limitations include vulnerability to JPEG compression (the strongest tested defense, recovering mIoU to 44.8%), reduced effectiveness on simple-shape categories (bird, bottle), and the absence of comparison against gradient-based UE methods adapted for segmentation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other foundation models (LLaVA, CLIP) and whether adversarially trained segmentation models can fully neutralize the unlearnable effect.
