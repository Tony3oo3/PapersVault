## Problem

Querying complex models (traffic networks, large ML models, databases) for precise matroid information is prohibitively expensive, yet weaker fast models can provide imprecise answers. This paper initiates the study of a two-oracle model for matroid optimization where a free but imprecise 'dirty' oracle augments an expensive 'clean' oracle, aiming to minimize clean-oracle calls while remaining robust to arbitrarily poor dirty oracles. The fundamental problem studied is computing a maximum-weight basis of a matroid, a generalization of many combinatorial optimization problems including minimum spanning trees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a precomputed maximum-weight dirty basis B_d (from the dirty matroid M_d of rank r_d), access to the clean matroid M = (E, I) via an independence oracle, non-negative element weights w_e, and a tuning parameter k ≥ 1 that trades off error-dependency against worst-case robustness. It outputs a maximum-weight basis of M along with a certificate (the sequence of oracle queries and answers). The algorithm has no knowledge of the relationship between M_d and M; the dirty oracle's quality is captured by η_A and η_R, the minimum numbers of elements to add to and remove from a dirty max-weight basis to reach a clean max-weight basis. The key output guarantee is a bound on clean-oracle calls: min{n−r+k+η_A(k+1)+η_R(k+1)⌈log₂ r_d⌉, (1+1/k)n}.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternately adds elements from E\B_d and removes elements from B_d while processing elements in non-increasing weight order, maintaining two invariants: ℓ-safeness (the current solution restricted to the first ℓ elements is a maximal subset of some maximum-weight basis) and independence. This alternating strategy is necessary because, unlike the unweighted case, a maximal independent subset of B_d may not be extendable to a maximum-weight basis, so high-weight external elements must be added early, potentially creating new circuits that require removing previously 'safe' elements from B_d. The robustified version (Algorithm 2) introduces a hybrid linear/binary search: it performs k linear search steps before triggering a binary search over B_d to locate the blocking element, and delays independence checks via a counter q to avoid excessive queries when the dirty oracle is poor. This yields a smooth trade-off: when η_A and η_R are small, the bound approaches n−r+k (consistency), while the worst-case bound (1+1/k)n (robustness) is independent of dirty-oracle quality. Tight lower bounds show the n−r+η_A and n−r+η_R⌈log₂ r_d⌉ dependencies are best-possible for any deterministic algorithm.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.8) shows that for any tuning parameter k ≥ 1, a maximum-weight basis of a matroid can be computed using at most min{n − r + k + η_A·(k+1) + η_R·(k+1)⌈log₂ r_d⌉, (1+1/k)n} clean-oracle calls, where r is the matroid rank, r_d the dirty matroid rank, and η_A, η_R the addition/removal modification distances. A matching lower bound (Lemma 3.7) proves every deterministic algorithm requires strictly more than n − r + η_A + η_R·⌈log₂ r_d⌉ + 1 calls, establishing near-optimality. In the unweighted case (Theorem 2.3), the bound tightens to min{n − r + k + η_A + η_R·(k+1)⌈log₂ r_d⌉, (1+1/k)n}, and the rank-oracle variant (Proposition 4.1) achieves min{n+1, 2 + η_R·⌈log₂ r_d⌉ + min{η_A·⌈log₂(n−r_d)⌉, n−r_d}} calls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between algorithmic bounds. The proposed robustified algorithm's worst-case bound (1+1/k)n approaches the classic greedy algorithm's n queries as k → ∞, while its consistency bound n−r+k+η_A·(k+1) significantly improves upon n whenever the rank r is large relative to n (e.g., sparse graphic matroids where r ≈ n). The simple algorithm (Lemma 2.1) achieves the optimal n−r+1 calls when the dirty basis is already a clean basis but degrades to n+1 otherwise; the proposed algorithm smoothly interpolates between these extremes via the parameter k. Lower bounds in Appendix B confirm that the n−r+η_A and n−r+η_R⌈log₂ r_d⌉ dependencies are best-possible for any deterministic algorithm, and that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve n−r+1 calls in the perfect-oracle case and n calls in the worst case (Lemma B.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-oracle model—previously studied for sorting (Bai & Coester), MST/clustering (Bateni et al.), and priority queues (Benomar & Coester)—to the general matroid optimization setting, building directly on Edmonds' greedy algorithm as the clean-oracle baseline. It also explicitly connects the two-oracle model to the learning-augmented algorithms framework (Mitzenmacher & Vassilvitskii), showing that a dirty basis can be interpreted as a prediction and the modification distance as a prediction error. Unlike prior two-stage matroid work that assumes the matroid is fixed and only weights change, this paper assumes the matroid structure itself changes between the dirty and clean stages, which fundamentally alters the algorithmic challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first optimal (tight lower-bound-matching) algorithms for matroid optimization under a two-oracle model, with a tunable robustness parameter k that gracefully degrades from near-optimal clean-query counts (when the dirty oracle is good) to within a (1+1/k) factor of the classic greedy algorithm (when the dirty oracle is arbitrary). The robustification framework combining linear and binary search is a reusable technique, and the extensions to rank oracles, costly dirty oracles, and matroid intersection open concrete directions for future work. Open questions include whether the error-dependency can be improved for rank oracles in the weighted setting, and whether the results extend to non-matroid dirty oracles with weighted elements.
