## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on out-of-distribution (OOD) data causes catastrophic forgetting and degrades downstream performance because FFN parameter updates disrupt pretrained parametric knowledge. In data-scarce domains (e.g., healthcare, harmless content generation), obtaining large volumes of high-quality in-domain data is nearly impossible, yet vanilla fine-tuning on OOD data actively harms model capabilities. The paper asks: which parameter updates during fine-tuning are actually beneficial for downstream performance when training and test distributions mismatch?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243340/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard instruction-tuning corpus in {instruction, input, output} format (which may be entirely OOD relative to the target task) and a pretrained LLM. During training, all parameters are updated via standard maximum-likelihood optimization. At inference time, the model receives a text query and generates a response using a hybrid weight configuration: updated self-attention parameters (from fine-tuning) combined with the original pretrained FFN weights. This inference-time swap is the only operational difference from a standard fine-tuned model, requiring no architectural changes or additional inference overhead.

## Method

TAIA (Training All parameters but Inferring with only Attention) is based on the insight that self-attention modules primarily govern instruction-following ability while FFN modules encode parametric knowledge as key-value memories. The training phase updates all parameters (attention + FFN) to ensure sufficient non-linearity exploration, since attention alone (TOA) lacks the representational capacity to learn complex features from OOD data. At inference, only the updated self-attention weights are retained while FFN weights are reverted to their pretrained values, preserving the integrity of parametric knowledge. This is implemented as a simple post-training weight swap compatible with both LoRA and MoLoRA: the LoRA deltas for attention layers are merged into the base weights, while FFN LoRA deltas are discarded. The approach outperforms vanilla fine-tuning, TOA, TOF, and TAIF variants across seven benchmarks, four LLM families, and two PEFT methods, while also restoring few-shot capability and resisting jailbreaking attacks.

## Results

On Qwen1.5-1.8B with Alpaca-GPT4 (LoRA), TAIA achieves 35.21 average across seven benchmarks, surpassing the base model (30.58) and vanilla LoRA (32.98). On the more challenging CoT-Collection (OOD data), TAIA reaches 35.54 average while vanilla fine-tuning drops to 27.68, demonstrating robustness to distribution shift. For larger models, Qwen1.5-7B with Alpaca-GPT4 + LoRA shows TAIA at 51.27 average vs. vanilla 46.50 and base 47.11, with particularly large gains on MATH (24.98 vs. 17.90) and SVAMP (67.20 vs. 57.10). LLaMA3-8B with CoT-Collection + LoRA achieves 51.74 average under TAIA vs. 47.00 for vanilla, with SVAMP reaching 82.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAIA consistently outperforms all five baselines across both training datasets. Against vanilla LoRA fine-tuning, TAIA gains +2.23 average on Alpaca-GPT4 and +7.86 on CoT-Collection, with the largest improvements on reasoning tasks (MATH: +2.80, SVAMP: +7.80 on Alpaca-GPT4). Among OOD generalization methods, L2 and EWC regularization are particularly ineffective on CoT-Collection, dropping to 20.64 and 20.65 average respectively (vs. TAIA's 35.54), as they over-constrain parameter updates and fail to leverage OOD data. Self-Distill also underperforms significantly (28.09 on Alpaca-GPT4), likely due to quality loss in the distillation step. LoRACL is the closest competitor (33.73 on Alpaca-GPT4, 27.91 on CoT-Collection) but still trails TAIA by 1.48 and 7.63 average points respectively, and shows task-specific preferences (e.g., no gain on CommonsenseQA). TAIA's advantage is most pronounced on OOD data (CoT-Collection), where it maintains near-base-model knowledge scores (MMLU: 42.39 vs. base 43.62) while improving reasoning, whereas all other baselines suffer substantial knowledge degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAIA builds on the mechanistic understanding of Transformer modules established by prior interpretability work: self-attention governs instruction-following (Wu et al., 2023) while FFN layers encode parametric knowledge (Geva et al., 2020; Meng et al., 2022). Rather than applying training-time regularization (EWC, L2, self-distillation) or adapter-based continual learning (LoRACL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, TAIA takes a fundamentally different inference-time approach: it trains all parameters normally but reverts FFN weights to their pretrained values at inference, retaining only attention updates. This positions TAIA as a parameter-level analogue to activation-level inference-time interventions (Li et al., 2024), but operating on the weight space rather than hidden states, and it repurposes standard PEFT pipelines (LoRA, MoLoRA) without modifying the training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAIA offers a deployment-friendly, zero-overhead solution to the data-scarcity bottleneck in specialized domains (healthcare, finance) by making OOD fine-tuning data usable without catastrophic forgetting, and it demonstrably resists jailbreaking attacks even when tuned on harmful data. However, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: when the base model lacks sufficient domain knowledge (e.g., summarization, reading comprehension), TAIA underperforms vanilla fine-tuning because it cannot inject new knowledge through FFN updates. Open questions include finding a minimal trainable parameter subset that preserves non-linearity exploration while reducing distribution shift, and developing adaptive (rather than binary) parameter maintenance strategies that could recover some knowledge-intensive task gains.
