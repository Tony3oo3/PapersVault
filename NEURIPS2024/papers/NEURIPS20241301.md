## Problem

Standard MARL function approximators (MLPs, GNNs) lack the inductive bias from rotational and reflection symmetries present in most multi-agent environments, resulting in poor sample efficiency and no generalization guarantees across spatial transformations. While Equivariant GNNs (EGNNs) encode these symmetries, a naive application to MARL introduces a severe early exploration bias: the equivariant output approximates the input (agent position), causing agents to move away from goals rather than explore. The paper asks how to apply equivariant GNNs to complex mixed discrete-continuous MARL action spaces while eliminating this exploration bias.

## Input / Output

The state is structured as a graph where each node carries an equivariant coordinate embedding (position, and optionally velocity) and an invariant feature embedding (health, unit type, team, or agent ID). Edges connect all friendly agents to each other and to all enemy units, modeling a realistic observation structure. The policy output is decomposed across the graph: continuous spatial movement actions are read from the equivariant embeddings u_i of each agent node, discrete actions (shoot/no-shoot, action-type selection) come from invariant embeddings h_i, and per-entity targeting logits are read from the corresponding enemy node's h_i, enabling the action space to scale with the number of agents without retraining.

## Method

E2GN2 modifies the EGNN coordinate update by multiplying the residual term u_i^l by a learned scalar gate φ_u2(m_i), so that u_i^{l+1} = u_i^l · φ_u2(m_i) + C Σ_j (u_i^l − u_j^l) φ_u(m_ij). The paper proves (Theorem 1) that standard EGNN has E[u_i^L] ≈ E[u_i^0] (output tracks input position), causing the policy mean to equal the agent's position and suppressing exploration; it then proves (Theorem 2) that E2GN2 has E[u_i^L] ≈ 0, restoring unbiased Gaussian exploration, while (Theorem 3) confirms O(n) equivariance to rotations and reflections is preserved. For complex action spaces, the method routes equivariant outputs to continuous movement and invariant outputs to discrete logits across different graph nodes, avoiding a final MLP that would break permutation-equivariant scalability. Training uses multi-agent PPO via RLlib with a decentralized critic, evaluated on MPE (spread, tag) and SMACv2 (terran, protoss, zerg) with 10 seeds.

## Results

On SMACv2 generalization (trained on Surrounded Left, tested on Surrounded Right), E2GN2 achieves win rates of 0.55 (Terran), 0.56 (Protoss), and 0.32 (Zerg), compared to 0.11/0.14/0.07 for GNN and 0.12/0.17/0.05 for MLP — a roughly 4–5× improvement. In sample efficiency on SMACv2, E2GN2 outperforms MLP and GNN baselines by up to 2×–5× on Terran and Protoss scenarios. On MPE, E2GN2 also outperforms E3AC (SEGNN-based) in wall-clock training time, requiring less than 1 hour vs. nearly 4 hours to collect 1M environment steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EGNN (Garcia Satorras et al., 2021), E2GN2 eliminates the early exploration bias that causes EGNN agents to move away from goals in initial training, resulting in faster convergence and higher final reward on both MPE and SMACv2. On MPE Tag, E2GN2 achieves a substantially higher final reward than EGNN, while on SMACv2 Terran, EGNN still lags in early training but eventually catches up to MLP/GNN levels due to equivariance. Against E3AC (Chen & Zhang, 2024, using SEGNN), E2GN2 is dramatically faster in wall-clock time (<1 hr vs. ~4 hrs per 1M steps) and outperforms E3AC on MPE Tag; E3AC struggles on SMACv2 because it could not resolve the mixed action-space mapping problem that E2GN2 addresses. Against standard GNNs and MLPs, E2GN2 achieves 2×–5× better sample efficiency on SMACv2 Terran/Protoss and a ~5× generalization advantage on the Surrounded Right test, while also scaling to 4–8 agents without retraining (Table 2) where MLPs cannot scale at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EGNN (Garcia Satorras et al., 2021) by modifying its coordinate update equation to eliminate an early exploration bias, producing E2GN2. Prior equivariant MARL work (Van der Pol et al., 2020/2021) was restricted to simple discrete grid worlds with trivial action spaces, and the concurrent E3AC approach (Chen & Zhang, 2024) using SEGNN failed to outperform baselines on complex tasks like Starcraft. The key departure here is a systematic treatment of how to map equivariant GNN outputs onto mixed discrete-continuous action spaces while preserving permutation equivariance and scalability, which prior equivariant MARL work did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded recipe for incorporating rotational/reflection equivariance into MARL policies with complex action spaces, yielding built-in generalization guarantees (5x win-rate improvement over GNNs/MLPs on unseen rotations) and 2x-5x sample efficiency gains on SMACv2. The discovery and formal characterization of the EGNN exploration bias is a useful caution for anyone applying equivariant architectures to RL beyond this specific setting. Open questions include handling partial symmetries, environments with angular momentum or accelerations, and the main results assume full observability (though appendix results suggest E2GN2 is robust to partial observability).
