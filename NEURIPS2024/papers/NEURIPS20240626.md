## Problem

While scaling laws have driven breakthroughs in language modelling and image generation, it remained unclear whether graph-based architectures for molecular property prediction could similarly benefit from increasing model size, data volume, and label diversity. The paper addresses this by systematically studying how message-passing networks, graph Transformers, and hybrid architectures scale under supervised multi-task pretraining on the largest public collection of 2D molecular graphs, and how that scaling translates to downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D molecular graphs where nodes represent atoms and edges represent covalent bonds, with node and edge features encoded by 2-layer MLPs. Pretraining is multi-task, with each molecule carrying thousands of labels spanning bio-assay activity (PCBA_1328), quantum DFT properties (PCQM4M), transcriptomics (L1000), and phenomic imaging similarity. Downstream, the frozen pretrained model produces graph-level fingerprints (aggregated node representations) that feed into lightweight probing MLPs to predict 38 diverse molecular properties including ADMET classification labels and continuous physicochemical values.

## Method

The paper trains three architectures—MPNN++ (message passing), a pure Graph Transformer, and GPS++ (hybrid combining both)—under supervised multi-task pretraining on the LargeMix dataset (5M molecules, thousands of labels), using positional and structural encodings (random-walk diagonals, Laplacian eigenvectors, and eigenvalues) to inject structural priors. The key empirical finding is that model width and label diversity are the dominant scaling factors for downstream performance, while depth benefits are limited by oversmoothing. The final model, MolGPS, combines fingerprints extracted from all three architectures at 1B and 3B parameter scales via multi-fingerprint probing, where embeddings from different task-head layers and the graph output network are concatenated and fed to a specialized probing MLP. Two critical data-level design choices—removing the noisy L1000 transcriptomics tasks and adding phenomic imaging data (6k labels for 500k molecules)—are essential to sustain positive scaling trends up to 3B parameters, enabling MolGPS to achieve state-of-the-art on 26 of 38 downstream tasks.

## Results

MolGPS (3B parameters, multi-fingerprint probing combining MPNN++, Transformer, and GPS++ models) establishes state-of-the-art on 26 out of 38 downstream tasks across TDC, Polaris, and MoleculeNet benchmarks. On TDC, MolGPS achieves SOTA on 11/22 tasks and outperforms MolE on 21/22 tasks; on Polaris and MoleculeNet it is SOTA on all but one task. Width scaling shows the strongest downstream correlation (Spearman ρ = 0.91 for probing, 0.85 for finetuning on Polaris), while label scaling is the second most impactful factor (ρ = 0.57 on Polaris, 0.54 on TDC). Power-law analysis yields α ≈ 0.081 (probing) and α ≈ 0.098 (finetuning) for parameter scaling, and β ≈ 0.106–0.110 for dataset scaling, consistent with scaling laws observed in NLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MolE [43], the prior molecular foundation model, MolGPS outperforms on 21/22 TDC tasks and achieves SOTA on 11/22 TDC tasks (vs. MolE's fewer SOTA entries). On MoleculeNet (Table 1), even the 1B MolGPS without Phenomics beats GraphMVP [35] on 3/4 tasks (BBBP 0.802 vs. 0.724, Clintox 0.797 vs. 0.775, Sider 0.649 vs. 0.639), while the Phenomics-augmented variants win all four. Against MapLight+GNN [46], the best single-model TDC performer, the 1B MPNN++ component of MolGPS 'only slightly trails,' but the full 3B MolGPS clearly surpasses it in normalized TDC performance. The paper also notes that MolGPS's 3B model outperforms the per-task best baseline (TDC Baseline) on the normalized TDC score, which is remarkable since MolGPS uses a single unified method across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graphium infrastructure and LargeMix dataset (Beaini et al., 2024) by systematically studying how molecular GNNs scale across width, depth, molecule count, label diversity, and dataset composition, drawing an explicit analogy to Kaplan et al.'s scaling laws for language models. Architecturally, it builds on the GPS++ hybrid MPNN/Transformer (Masters et al., 2022) and the General, Powerful, Scalable Graph Transformer (Rampašek et al., 2022), adding positional and structural encodings and a multi-fingerprint probing strategy that combines representations from multiple architectures. Conceptually, it departs from prior molecular foundation models like MolE (Méndez-Lucio et al., 2022) by demonstrating that supervised multi-task pretraining over thousands of diverse labels (bioassays, quantum simulations, transcriptomics, phenomics) outperforms self-supervised approaches, and by showing that the scaling behavior of molecular GNNs follows power laws with constants remarkably close to those observed in language modelling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that molecular GNNs exhibit continuous, power-law scaling up to 3B parameters, resolving a key open question about whether graph-based architectures can benefit from scale the way Transformers do in NLP and vision. The practical finding that model width and label diversity (rather than raw molecule count) are the dominant drivers of downstream performance provides concrete guidance for building future molecular foundation models. However, the authors note that orders of magnitude remain before a truly general-purpose molecular foundation model is achieved, and the study is limited to 2D graphs with expensive featurization and data-loading bottlenecks that constrain further scaling. The observation that L1000 transcriptomics data actually degrades downstream performance, and that molecule scaling shows weak correlation with downstream task success despite strong pretraining gains, highlights that data quality and diversity matter more than sheer volume.
