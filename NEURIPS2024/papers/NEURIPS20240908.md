## Problem

Existing medical QA benchmarks evaluate LLMs in a static, single-turn format where all patient information is provided upfront, diverging from real clinical consultations where information is often incomplete and must be actively elicited. LLMs are trained to answer any question regardless of context sufficiency, making them unreliable in interactive, high-stakes settings where knowing when to ask a follow-up question versus commit to an answer is critical. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates an LLM's ability to proactively seek missing information before making a clinical decision.

## Input / Output

The Expert system receives a partial patient description (age, gender, chief complaint) and a four-option multiple choice question, then iteratively produces either a single atomic follow-up question or a final letter answer at each turn. The Patient system, holding the full patient record, responds to each Expert question by selecting at most two relevant atomic facts from a pre-decomposed fact list (Fact-Select variant), or explicitly declining to answer if no fact is relevant. The Expert's abstention module outputs a structured confidence rating (e.g., a 5-point Likert scale label) plus a one-sentence rationale, which is fed into the question generation context to guide what to ask next.

## Method

MEDIQ converts single-turn medical QA datasets (MEDQA, CRAFT-MD) into an interactive benchmark by withholding all patient details beyond age, gender, and chief complaint, forcing the Expert to elicit missing evidence through dialogue with a simulated Patient. The Expert system (MEDIQ-Expert) decomposes clinical reasoning into five modular steps: Initial Assessment, Abstention, Question Generation, Information Integration, and Decision Making. The key technical contribution is the Abstention Module, which probes model confidence via multiple strategies—Numerical (0–1 score), Binary (yes/no), Scale (5-point Likert), each optionally augmented with Rationale Generation (a one-sentence justification before the decision) and Self-Consistency (averaging/mode over n repeated prompts). The best configuration, Scale + Rationale Generation + Self-Consistency (n=3), improves diagnostic accuracy by 22.3% over a basic question-asking baseline and closes 51.2% of the gap to the full-information upper bound, while the Patient system's Fact-Select variant (decomposing records into atomic facts and selecting relevant ones) achieves 89.1% factuality versus 55.9% for a direct reading-comprehension baseline.

## Results

On iMEDQA, the best interactive Expert system (Scale+RG+SC) achieves 50.2% accuracy with GPT-3.5 and 66.1% with GPT-4, compared to 45.6% and 54.5% for the non-interactive Initial baseline (same starting information, no questions). The BASIC interactive setup (simply prompting the LLM to ask or answer) actually degrades accuracy by an average of 11.3% relative to the non-interactive Initial baseline, showing that naively enabling question-asking hurts performance. The BEST system (Scale abstention + Rationale Generation + Self-Consistency factor 3) improves over BASIC by 22.3% and over Initial by 12.1%, but still trails the Full-information upper bound by 10.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five evaluation settings: Full (all patient info upfront, standard QA), Initial (age/gender/chief complaint only, no questions), None (MCQ only), BASIC (interactive with basic question-asking prompt), and BEST (interactive with Scale+RG+SC abstention). The most striking finding is that BASIC underperforms the non-interactive Initial baseline by an average of 11.3% across all models, demonstrating that simply prompting LLMs to ask questions degrades rather than improves performance. The BEST system (Scale+RG+SC) recovers and surpasses Initial by 12.1% on iMEDQA with GPT-3.5 (50.2% vs. 45.6%) and by 16.4% with GPT-4 (66.1% vs. 54.5%), but still lags behind the Full-information upper bound by 10.3% (GPT-3.5) and 13.6% (GPT-4). Model size matters: Llama-3-70B with BEST surpasses the non-interactive Initial setup on both datasets, while Llama-3-8B with BEST still falls below Initial on iMEDQA (45.8% vs. 52.0%). On iCRAFT-MD, GPT-4 with BEST achieves 84.3%, closing most of the gap to Full (91.4%), suggesting the approach generalizes across medical domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on medical QA benchmarks (MEDQA, CRAFT-MD) by converting them from static single-turn evaluation into an interactive information-seeking setting, drawing on the Patient/Expert multi-agent architecture. The abstention strategies build directly on confidence calibration techniques (Tian et al., 2023), chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022), and self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022), repurposing them for the novel problem of deciding *when* to ask a follow-up question rather than when to answer. The Patient System's Fact-Select variant adapts atomic fact decomposition from FactScore (Min et al., 2023) to ensure factual grounding in simulated patient responses. Unlike prior interactive medical AI work (Johri et al., 2023; Tu et al., 2024) that observes LLMs fail to elicit complete information, this paper explicitly targets and measures the information-seeking ability itself with structured abstention as the core mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simply prompting SOTA LLMs to ask questions *degrades* performance by 11.3% challenges the assumption that giving models more conversational freedom improves reliability in high-stakes settings. The 22.3% improvement from structured abstention (Scale+RG+SC) is meaningful but still leaves a 10.3% gap to the full-information upper bound, indicating that current LLMs fundamentally struggle with proactive information integration from dialogue. Key limitations include reliance on paid APIs for the Patient system, restriction to multiple-choice format, and evaluation on only two datasets; open questions include whether the abstention framework generalizes to open-ended clinical consultations, whether open-source Patient systems can match the Fact-Select variant, and how to close the remaining performance gap through external knowledge integration or multi-agent collaboration.
