## Problem

Although memorization (interpolating a finite dataset) is widely believed to underpin the strong generalization of over-parameterized deep learning, no prior theoretical work has studied whether memorization neural networks actually generalize. This paper provides the first systematic analysis, revealing that the most parameter-efficient memorization networks are provably non-generalizable and establishing when and how generalization can be achieved.

## Input / Output

The method takes an i.i.d. dataset D_tr of size N sampled from a binary distribution D over [0,1]^n × {-1,1} with a positive separation bound c (ensuring same-label points can be arbitrarily close while opposite-label points are separated by at least c). It produces ReLU feedforward networks F: R^n → R with specific structural guarantees: either width-6 networks with O(√N) parameters (optimal for memorization), constant-parameter networks bounded by N_D, or O(N²n)-parameter networks that are both efficient to compute and generalizable. The output also includes explicit sample complexity thresholds (in terms of N_D and S_D) below which generalization is impossible.

## Method

The paper constructs memorization networks via a three-step pipeline—data compression (random projection to 1D preserving label separation), data projection (mapping compressed values to concatenated digit encodings via a width-4, depth-O(√N) network), and label determination (decoding the encoding to recover the sign)—extending Vardi et al.'s optimal-parameter construction to i.i.d. datasets. A key negative result shows that fixed-width networks and even O(√N)-parameter networks cannot generalize for certain distributions, establishing that width must grow with data dimension and that over-parameterization is necessary. The paper then derives a tight sample complexity of Õ(N_D²) for generalizable memorization with ≤N_D parameters, and introduces an efficient (polynomial-time) algorithm using convex-set partitioning that achieves generalization with O(N²n) parameters when N = Õ(S_D), where S_D is the efficient memorization sample complexity defined via a minimal covering of the distribution's support by balls of radius L/3.1.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no standard benchmark comparison table. The key quantitative results are: (1) a memorization network with width 6 and depth O(√N) (i.e., O(√N) parameters) exists for any i.i.d. dataset (Theorem 4.1); (2) a constant number of parameters N_D suffices for memorization when N is large (Theorem 4.3); (3) the exact sample complexity for generalizable memorization networks with ≤ N_D parameters is Õ(N_D²), matching the lower bound Ω(N_D²/ln²(N_D)) (Theorems 6.1, 6.5); (4) an efficient polynomial-time algorithm with O(N²n) parameters achieves generalization when N = Õ(S_D) (Theorem 7.3). For some distributions, an exponential number of samples in the data dimension n is required (Corollary 6.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is theoretical and does not compare against competing methods on standard benchmarks. The only experimental evaluation (Appendix I) tests the efficient memorization algorithm from Theorem 7.3 on CIFAR-10, where it explicitly does not surpass networks trained with SGD in test accuracy, confirming the algorithm's purpose is theoretical rather than practical. The paper positions its results relative to the interpolation learning literature [7, 12, 38, 53, 59, 66], noting that its sample complexity bounds (lower bound Õ(N_D²) and upper bound Õ(S_D)) apply to interpolation learning as well. The memorization constructions build on Vardi et al. [55] for the O(√N) parameter network and Yu et al. [62] for robust memorization, extending both to the i.i.d. distributional setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the memorization construction literature—particularly Vardi et al.'s optimal O(√N)-parameter ReLU networks [55] and the robust memorization framework of Yu et al. [62]—to ask a question no prior work has addressed: whether these memorization networks actually generalize. It departs from the interpolation learning line (Bartlett et al. [7], Chatterji & Long [12]), which establishes generalizability only in linear regimes, by providing the first systematic generalization theory for nonlinear memorization networks under i.i.d. data distributions with separation bounds. The paper also leverages VC-dimension bounds for piecewise-linear networks [6] and classical sample complexity machinery [44] to derive its results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that memorization networks with the provably optimal O(√N) parameters can have accuracy no better than random guessing (≤0.51) for most distributions—challenges the implicit assumption in the double-descent and interpolation-learning literature that minimal-parameter interpolation is sufficient for generalization. The paper also reveals a 'data curse of dimensionality' where exponential parameters in the data dimension are needed for some distributions, and shows that the generalizable memorization algorithm with N_D parameters is NP-hard to compute. Open questions include how to estimate the two new complexity measures N_D and S_D, whether practical data distributions (e.g., natural images) have polynomial S_D, and whether the efficient O(N²n)-parameter algorithm can be made competitive with SGD in practice.
