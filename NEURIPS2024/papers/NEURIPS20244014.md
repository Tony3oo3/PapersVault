## Problem

Neural collapse (NC) has been extensively characterized for classification tasks, where features and weights converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame geometry during terminal training. However, whether an analogous geometric collapse phenomenon exists for multivariate regression — a task of equal importance in imitation learning, robotics, and predictive analytics — remained entirely unexplored. This paper asks whether and how neural networks exhibit a structured geometric collapse when trained on continuous-valued regression targets.

## Input / Output

The method takes either raw robotic state vectors (tabular data from MuJoCo environments like Swimmer, Reacher, Hopper) or RGB images (from the CARLA driving simulator and UTKFace age-estimation dataset) and produces a continuous target vector in R^n, where n ranges from 1 to 3 (e.g., joint torques, speed and steering angle, or age). A critical structural constraint is that the target dimension n is much smaller than the feature dimension d (n << d), which is what makes the collapse to an n-dimensional subspace meaningful and observable.

## Method

The paper defines Neural Regression Collapse (NRC) through three properties: (NRC1) last-layer feature vectors collapse to the n-dimensional subspace spanned by their principal components, (NRC2) features also collapse to the subspace spanned by the last-layer weight vectors, and (NRC3) the Gram matrix of the weight vectors converges to a specific form governed by the square root of the target covariance matrix. Empirically, NRC1–NRC3 are validated across six datasets using MLP (for MuJoCo) and ResNet18/34 (for image-based tasks) trained with standard L2 weight decay. Theoretically, the authors extend the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) to multivariate regression with L2 loss, proving that when regularization parameters are strictly positive, the global minima of the UFM optimization problem exactly reproduce NRC1–NRC3, with the collapse structure determined by the product c = λ_W · λ_H and the target covariance Σ. A key finding is a phase transition: when regularization is zero, infinitely many non-collapsed optimal solutions exist, but any positive regularization immediately induces the collapsed geometry.

## Results

The paper empirically validates Neural Regression Collapse (NRC1–NRC3) across six diverse datasets (Swimmer, Reacher, Hopper, CARLA 1D/2D, UTKFace) using both MLP and ResNet architectures, with all three NRC metrics converging to near-zero during the terminal phase of training. A key finding is a sharp phase transition: when weight decay λ_WD = 0, no collapse occurs (NRC1–3 remain large), but even a small positive λ_WD triggers rapid emergence of the full NRC geometric structure. The UFM theory precisely predicts the NRC3 scaling factor γ = c = λ_W·λ_H, and the residual error matrix at convergence is shown to be white noise with sample covariance cI_n, consistent with ZCA-whitened targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a phenomenon study rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing regression or classification baselines. The central empirical contrast is between the regularized (λ_WD > 0) and unregularized (λ_WD = 0) settings: with zero weight decay, NRC1–3 do not converge to zero and the feature vectors do not collapse to any low-dimensional subspace, whereas any small positive regularization immediately induces the full NRC structure. The UFM theoretical predictions (Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2) are validated against empirical training runs under both standard weight-decay and UFM-style L2 regularization on H and W, with the theory accurately predicting the collapse geometry, the optimal γ value, and the norms of W and H as functions of the regularization ratio λ_H/λ_W. The paper also contrasts the multivariate case (n > 1) with the univariate case (n = 1), showing that NRC3 is non-trivial only when n ≥ 2 and that uncorrelated targets yield orthogonal weight rows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) framework, originally defined for balanced classification by Papyan et al. (2020), to the multivariate regression setting. The theoretical analysis repurposes the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) of Mixon et al. (2020) and directly leverages Lemma B.1 from Zhou et al. (2022a) on the optimization landscape under MSE loss, adapting it from the classification objective to a regularized L2 regression objective. The key conceptual departure is replacing the simplex equiangular tight frame structure of NC with a PCA-subspace collapse and a covariance-dependent Gram matrix structure, yielding three new properties (NRC1–NRC3) that have no direct analogue in the classification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirical and theoretical study of neural collapse in regression, suggesting that the collapse phenomenon may be a universal geometric behavior across task types rather than a classification-specific artifact. A notable open question is the connection between neural regression collapse and generalization, which the authors explicitly leave for future work. The finding that collapse is entirely driven by L2 regularization (with a sharp phase transition at λ=0) reframes the phenomenon as a regularization-induced geometry rather than an intrinsic property of the regression landscape, which could inform how practitioners interpret and exploit collapse structure in downstream tasks like imitation learning and policy distillation.
