## Problem

Prior theoretical results showing GNNs can approximate LP solution mappings require either very large parameter counts (via universal approximation) or polynomial depth in the problem dimension, yet empirically small GNNs with fewer than ten layers solve LPs effectively. This paper asks: when and why can small-size GNNs effectively solve LPs, and provides a theoretical answer for packing and covering LPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241222/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the constraint matrix A (n×m, entries either 0 or ≥1) of a packing or covering LP in normal form (where b and c are all-ones vectors), encoded as a bipartite graph with variable nodes on the left and constraint nodes on the right. It outputs an approximate primal solution x ∈ R^m (packing) or dual solution y ∈ R^n (covering), followed by a feasibility restoration post-processing step that clips values to [0,1] and rescales violated constraints.

## Method

The core contribution is GD-Net, a GNN architecture that unrolls a variant of the Awerbuch-Khandekar gradient descent algorithm on a carefully chosen potential function (Φ_p for packing, Φ_c for covering). Each GD iteration maps to one constant-width GNN layer: the y-update (or x-update) uses a fixed ELU activation with α=1 to exactly replicate the exponential term, while the gradient-descent step is replaced by learnable functions f_θ and g_θ built from sigmoid units that approximate the Heaviside step functions in the original algorithm. A channel expansion technique widens scalar variables into d-dimensional vectors to boost expressiveness, and a final linear projection collapses back to the solution dimension. The key theoretical result is that polylogarithmic depth (Õ(log²(mA_max)·log²(nA_max)/ε⁵)) and constant width d suffice to achieve (1+ε)-approximate solutions, with the learnable parameters trained via supervised MSE loss on optimal solutions obtained from SCIP.

## Results

GD-Net (1,656 parameters) outperforms GCN (34,306 parameters) on most benchmarks, achieving relative gaps of 3.55–6.84% on IS, 7.35–16.53% on Packing, and 7.84–21.51% on ECP, versus GCN's 15.35–16.18%, 7.37–19.50%, and 36.83–39.22% respectively. On the BMP practical problem, GD-Net achieves a 1.20–1.58% relative gap versus GCN's 12.88–12.89%. In inference time, GD-Net is 10–17× faster than GCN (e.g., 0.099s vs 1.731s on IS-L), and reaches the same solution quality as Gurobi's primal simplex up to 300× faster on large instances (0.335s vs 103.322s on SC-10000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCN [29] (the primary GNN baseline), GD-Net wins decisively on IS (3.55–6.84% vs. 15.35–16.18% R.Gap) and ECP (7.84–21.51% vs. 36.83–39.22% R.Gap), with absolute gaps roughly 3–5× smaller. On Packing, GD-Net matches or slightly beats GCN (7.35% vs. 7.37% at L-size), while on SC it is slightly worse at small/medium sizes (26.68% vs. 21.91% at S) but wins at L-size (8.42% vs. 8.90%). Against the commercial Gurobi primal simplex solver, GD-Net reaches the same solution quality up to 300× faster (0.335s vs. 103.322s on SC with 10,000 variables), and against the first-order PDLP solver it is 2–3× faster while producing comparable or better solutions. On the practical BMP maxflow problem, GD-Net achieves a 1.20–1.58% relative gap versus GCN's 12.88–12.89%, and is 3.6–5.4× faster than the Ford-Fulkerson method at matching solution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unrolls a variant of the Awerbuch-Khandekar (2009) gradient descent algorithm for packing and covering LPs into a GNN architecture (GD-Net), where the fixed GD step is replaced by learnable sigmoid-based functions that approximate Heaviside step functions. It sits at the intersection of two threads: the algorithm-unrolling literature (Velickovic et al., Yang et al., Ma et al.) and the GNN-for-LP line (Chen et al. 2023, Qian et al. 2024), which had previously shown universal approximation only with polynomial-depth or very wide networks. The key departure is that by anchoring the architecture to a specific potential-function-based GD with polylogarithmic convergence, the authors obtain a constant-width, polylog-depth sufficiency guarantee that prior universal-approximation-based results could not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that polylogarithmic-depth, constant-width GNNs provably solve packing and covering LPs closes a significant theory-practice gap and gives practitioners a principled, parameter-efficient architecture (1,656 vs. 34,306 parameters) that outperforms standard GCNs across four LP benchmarks. However, the scope is limited to packing/covering LPs in normal form, and the achieved relative gaps (7–26%) remain far from exact solutions, so GD-Net is best viewed as a warm-start or initialization tool rather than a replacement for solvers. Open questions include whether analogous potential-function constructions exist for general LPs, whether the approach transfers to MILP branching heuristics, and whether the polylog depth lower bound (Ω(√(log n / log log n))) is tight for GNNs specifically.
