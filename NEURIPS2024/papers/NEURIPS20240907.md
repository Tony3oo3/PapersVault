## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks suffer from a persistent single-frame bias: model performance does not improve even when more frames are provided, making them inadequate for evaluating long-context multimodal capabilities. No public benchmark comprehensively measures LMMs on hour-long interleaved video-language inputs with diverse themes, leaving a critical gap in assessing whether models can truly retrieve and reason over detailed multimodal information from long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240907/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds LMMs temporally-aligned interleaved sequences of video frames and text subtitles (a subtitle chunk is inserted between the two frames bracketing its mid-timestamp), followed by a multiple-choice question that embeds a referring query pointing to specific video moments. Videos span four progressive duration groups (8–15 s, 15–60 s, 3–10 min, 15–60 min) at ≥720P resolution across 10 content categories. The model outputs a single option letter (A–E) selecting the correct answer from 4–5 choices.

## Method

The core contribution is the referring reasoning task paradigm, where each question contains a referring query (describing a scene, event, object, or subtitle) that identifies a referred context within the video, followed by a question body requiring the model to reason over that specific context. Questions are organized into two levels—L1 Perception (single-moment visual understanding) and L2 Relation (multi-moment temporal/attribute reasoning)—further subdivided into 17 fine-grained categories defined by the type of referring query and target answer. This design forces models to both locate the referred context from long inputs and perform complex multimodal reasoning over it, directly addressing the single-frame bias: performance on LONGVIDEOBENCH improves only when models process more frames. The dataset comprises 3,763 web-collected videos with subtitles and 6,678 human-annotated MCQs, curated through a three-annotator quality control pipeline (primary annotator, examiner, reviser) with explicit frame-index labeling of referred moments to enable depth-based analysis.

## Results

On the LONGVIDEOBENCH test set (5,341 MCQs across 3,011 videos), GPT-4o (0513) achieves the highest overall accuracy of 66.7%, followed by Gemini-1.5-Pro (64.4%), Gemini-1.5-Flash (62.4%), and GPT-4-Turbo (60.7%). The best open-source long-context LMMs (Phi-3-Vision-Instruct at 49.9%, Idefics2 at 49.4%) trail the top proprietary models by roughly 17 percentage points. Performance degrades consistently with video duration: GPT-4o drops from 71.6% on (8,15]s clips to 61.6% on (900,3600]s clips, and the most challenging question category is SSS (Sequence of Scenes) at only 44.3% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals a substantial proprietary-to-open-source gap: GPT-4o (66.7%) outperforms the best open-source long-context LMM, Phi-3-Vision-Instruct (49.9%), by 16.8 percentage points on overall accuracy. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o leads Gemini-1.5-Pro (64.4%) by 2.3 points and GPT-4-Turbo (60.7%) by 6.0 points, with the gap widening on longer videos (e.g., 61.6% vs. 54.5% on the (900,3600]s group). A notable finding is that open-source video-specific LMMs do not outperform their image-only counterparts under the same architecture: LLaVA-Next-Video-M7B (43.5%) underperforms LLaVA-Next-Mistral-7B (47.1%), suggesting that existing video training data (short clips, summary-level tasks) may degrade long-context detailed understanding. Scaling the LLM backbone helps: PLLaVA-34B (53.5%) improves over PLLaVA-13B (45.1%) and PLLaVA-7B (39.2%) by 8.4 and 14.3 points respectively. All models show the largest performance drop on the (L2) Relation questions versus (L1) Perception, with SSS (Sequence of Scenes) being the hardest category across all models (44.3% for GPT-4o, 23.3% for Mantis-BakLLaVA), highlighting a persistent weakness in complex temporal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the 'referring reasoning' task formulation for video QA, which adapts the needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) paradigm from text LLMs to the multimodal video domain. It departs from existing video QA benchmarks (MSVD-QA, EgoSchema, MovieChat-1K, etc.) in three key ways: requiring interleaved video-subtitle inputs, covering videos up to one hour long, and designing questions that by construction cannot be answered from a single frame, thereby addressing the longstanding single-frame bias problem. The benchmark draws on Whisper for subtitle generation and Q-Align for video quality filtering, but its core contribution is the task design and dataset curation rather than a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LONGVIDEOBENCH fills a critical gap as the first public benchmark specifically designed to measure long-context interleaved video-language understanding, and its results reveal that even top proprietary models (GPT-4o at 66.7%, Gemini-1.5-Pro at 64.4%) struggle significantly, while open-source models show a much larger performance gap and in some cases degrade with more input frames. The finding that the SSS (Sequence of Scenes) category is uniformly the hardest across all models highlights a fundamental weakness in temporal reasoning that remains unsolved. Limitations include the absence of audio modality and videos beyond one hour, and the benchmark's reliance on web-sourced content raises questions about domain generalization. Open questions include how to effectively scale interleaved multimodal processing and whether the referring reasoning formulation will become a standard evaluation protocol for next-generation long-context LMMs.
