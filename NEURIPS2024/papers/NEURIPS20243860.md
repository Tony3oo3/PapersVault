## Problem

Existing lossless compressors for scientific floating-point data treat all bits equally, failing to distinguish the informative physical mechanism from non-informative 'false precision' (noise from discretization and truncation errors). This leaves high-order structure unexploited, capping compression ratios well below what the underlying PDE structure would permit. No prior lossless method leverages the governing differential equations to identify a more compressible numeric representation of the data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D floating-point grid data (e.g., atmospheric or oceanic simulation fields) along with a declared data precision m bits. It produces a compressed representation consisting of 1D boundary values, a sparse 2D source term f (whose dynamic range is orders of magnitude smaller than the original field, e.g., ±0.1 vs. 220–300 for CESM-ATM), and a 9-element template coefficient vector. Decompression reconstructs the original data to within 10^{-(m+n)}, below the least significant digit of single-precision floats, guaranteeing bit-exact recovery.

## Method

MeLLoC formulates compression as an inverse PDE problem: it parameterizes a 9-point difference operator L_θ (normalized with C_5 = −1) and solves a least-squares problem to minimize ‖L_θ u‖₂², yielding a template whose residual source term f is maximally sparse. Only the boundary data, the sparse f, and θ are stored; decompression solves the linear system K_L u^in = b via a periodic-extension FFT solver in O(N² log N). An adaptive precision-control loop adjusts the coefficient truncation precision n to balance source-term sparsity against reconstruction fidelity. For data with higher-order effects, a diffusive preprocessing operator D = αΔ² + βΔ smooths the field before compression, with D⁻¹ applied at decompression to recover fine-scale features losslessly.

## Results

MeLLoC achieves the highest compression ratio among all tested methods on both datasets: 3.36× on CESM-ATM and 3.29× on Hurricane, surpassing the next-best method Zstandard (2.69× and 2.78×) by approximately 25%. Reconstruction errors are on the order of 10⁻¹¹ (CESM-ATM) and 10⁻¹² (Hurricane), well below the least significant digit of single-precision floating-point, confirming lossless fidelity. Throughput is competitive at ~189–207 Mb/s for compression and ~180–190 Mb/s for decompression, faster than Zstandard, FPZIP, ALP, and Gzip, though slower than Blosc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeLLoC outperforms all five baselines on compression ratio across both datasets. Against Zstandard (the strongest general-purpose compressor), MeLLoC achieves 3.36× vs. 2.69× on CESM-ATM (+25%) and 3.29× vs. 2.78× on Hurricane (+18%). Against specialized floating-point compressors, the gap is even larger: FPZIP reaches only 1.63× on both datasets, ALP achieves 1.16×/1.11×, and ZFP in lossless mode yields just 1.02×/1.01×. On throughput, MeLLoC's compression speed (~189–207 Mb/s) exceeds Zstandard (105/70 Mb/s), FPZIP (60/41 Mb/s), ALP (47/46 Mb/s), and Gzip (1.4/33 Mb/s), though it trails Blosc (294/889 Mb/s) which sacrifices compression ratio (1.30×/1.12×) for raw speed. Decompression speed is comparable to Zstandard (180/190 vs. 153/271 Mb/s) and far exceeds FPZIP and ALP, while Gzip's decompression is faster (267/212 Mb/s) but at a much lower compression ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior near-lossless work (Luo et al., DCC 2024) by making the mechanism-learning step fully lossless through precision control, and by framing the compression problem as an inverse PDE problem where the sparse source term becomes the compressed representation. It departs from the dominant paradigm in scientific data compression (FPZIP, ZFP, ALP, Zstandard) that relies on predictive coding or statistical entropy coding without exploiting the governing differential equation structure. Conceptually, it borrows from data-driven mechanism discovery (SINDy, PINNs) but repurposes the learned operator not for dynamics prediction but as a lossless transform that reduces the entropy of the residual before classical encoding (e.g., FSE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding the physical mechanism (a 9-point difference operator) can reduce the information content of scientific grid data by roughly 25% beyond the best general-purpose compressor (Zstandard), which is meaningful for petabyte-scale climate and ocean simulation archives. However, the method's applicability is constrained to data well-approximated by second-order constant-coefficient PDEs, and the precision calibration step requires per-dataset tuning. Open questions include extension to 3D and variable-coefficient equations, robustness to data that deviate from the assumed PDE class, and whether the FFT-based solver's O(N² log N) complexity remains practical at production simulation scales. The limited evaluation (two 2D datasets, single M1 machine, no ablation isolating the PDE transform from the entropy coder) leaves the generality of the gains uncertain.
