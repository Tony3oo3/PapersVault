## Problem

Unsupervised zero-shot semantic segmentation—producing dense, semantically meaningful masks without any target-dataset training, text prompts, or auxiliary reference images—remains significantly underperforming relative to supervised models. Existing diffusion-based approaches either rely on cross-attention modules that require carefully designed text prompts, or merge self-attention maps that only capture local patch correlations, while graph-partitioning methods like MaskCut are constrained to a fixed number of segments. The paper addresses how to extract semantically rich features from a pre-trained diffusion model and partition them adaptively to yield fine-grained segmentation maps that align with true semantic concepts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (1024×1024) as input, encodes it to latent space via the VAE of a latent diffusion model, adds a small amount of Gaussian noise (timestep t=50), and passes the noisy latent through the diffusion UNet encoder. The output is a pixel-level segmentation map at the original image resolution, where each pixel is assigned to a discovered semantic segment. The number of segments is not fixed a priori but adapts to the visual content of each image via a partitioning threshold. No text input, auxiliary images, or target-dataset access is required.

## Method

DiffCut extracts features exclusively from the final self-attention block of the SSD-1B (distilled SDXL) UNet encoder, deliberately avoiding cross-attention modules to eliminate text-prompt dependency and reduce model size by 70%. These patch-level features are used to build a cosine-similarity affinity matrix, with each element raised to a power α to act as a soft threshold that emphasizes inter-segment dissimilarity. A recursive Normalized Cut algorithm then bipartitions the graph repeatedly, stopping recursion on any sub-graph whose NCut value exceeds a threshold τ, thereby adapting the number of segments to the image content without requiring a pre-specified cluster count. To upscale the low-resolution (32×32) latent-space segmentation to pixel level, the method computes Masked Spatial Marginal Mean embeddings per segment, bilinearly upsamples the feature map, assigns each pixel to its nearest concept via cosine similarity, and refines boundaries with a PAMR module. The key empirical finding is that diffusion UNet encoder features exhibit superior patch-level semantic alignment (AUC 0.83) compared to CLIP, SigLIP, DINO, and DINOv2, making them particularly well-suited for graph-based segmentation.

## Results

DiffCut achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised zero-shot semantic segmentation across six benchmarks, reaching 65.2 mIoU on Pascal VOC, 56.5 on Pascal Context, 34.1 on COCO-Object, 49.1 on COCO-Stuff-27, 30.6 on Cityscapes, and 44.3 on ADE20K. On average, DiffCut gains +7.3 mIoU over the second-best baseline (MaskCut) and +5.5 mIoU over the previous SOTA (DiffSeg) on COCO-Stuff, all without requiring language input, auxiliary images, or unsupervised adaptation on the target dataset. The method runs at ~1.11 images/sec on a single NVIDIA TITAN RTX with 1024×1024 inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffCut decisively outperforms all 20 baselines across all six benchmarks. Against the closest training-free competitor MaskCut (k=5) [32], DiffCut gains +11.4 mIoU on VOC (65.2 vs 53.8), +13.1 on Context (56.5 vs 43.4), +4.0 on COCO-Object (34.1 vs 30.1), +7.4 on COCO-Stuff-27 (49.1 vs 41.7), +11.9 on Cityscapes (30.6 vs 18.7), and +8.6 on ADE20K (44.3 vs 35.7). Against the previous SOTA DiffSeg [1] (with fixed hyperparameter, denoted DiffSeg†), DiffCut leads by +15.4 on VOC, +7.7 on Context, +10.9 on COCO-Object, +4.9 on COCO-Stuff-27, +13.8 on Cityscapes, and +6.6 on ADE20K. Among extra-training methods, even the strongest (ACSeg [18] at 53.9 on VOC, U2Seg [78] at 30.2 on COCO-Stuff) falls well short of DiffCut's training-free performance. In the open-vocabulary extension (Table 4), DiffCut with a frozen CNN-CLIP achieves 63.0 mIoU on VOC and 36.0 on COCO-Object, surpassing dedicated methods like TCL [50] (55.0/31.6), SegCLIP [54] (52.6/26.5), and CLIP-DIY [86] (59.9/31.0), while remaining training-free. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the recursive NCut partitioning outperforms both DiffSeg's self-attention merging and automated spectral clustering (AutoSC) on all datasets, with gains up to +3.5 mIoU on ADE20K.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffCut sits at the intersection of two research threads: graph-based object localization (TokenCut, MaskCut, FOUND) which uses self-supervised ViT features with Normalized Cut, and diffusion-model-based segmentation (DiffSeg, DiffuMask, EmerDiff) which exploits internal representations of text-to-image models. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces self-supervised ViT features with the output of the final self-attention block of a diffusion UNet encoder, arguing these features carry superior patch-level semantic alignment; and (2) it replaces the fixed-k iterative partitioning of MaskCut or the self-attention map merging of DiffSeg with a recursive Normalized Cut that adapts segment count to visual content via a partitioning-cost threshold. The method deliberately avoids cross-attention modules, which prior diffusion-based segmentation methods rely on but which require text prompts and multiple forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 400M-parameter diffusion UNet encoder outperforms DINOv2 in patch-level semantic alignment (AUC 0.83) challenges the assumption that purpose-built self-supervised vision models are the best source of dense semantic features, and suggests diffusion UNet encoders could serve as general-purpose foundation vision encoders. Practically, DiffCut provides a training-free, text-free, single-forward-pass segmentation method that narrows the gap with supervised models by a meaningful margin across six benchmarks. Limitations include potential degradation on out-of-distribution domains (e.g., biomedical imaging) where the diffusion backbone was not trained, and the method still trails fully supervised approaches like SAM. An open question is whether the semantic coherence observed in diffusion features generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities where diffusion models are increasingly deployed.
