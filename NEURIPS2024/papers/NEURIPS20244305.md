## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Vision Transformers, it remains unclear under what conditions ViTs generalize well when trained to overfit their training data. Prior theoretical analyses of transformer optimization relied on simplified architectures (linear attention) or unrealistic loss functions, leaving the benign-overfitting regime for softmax-attention ViTs uncharacterized. This paper asks for the precise signal-to-noise-ratio condition that separates benign from harmful overfitting in a ViT trained from random initialization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of M image-patch tokens in R^d, where the first token x_1 carries a class-specific signal vector (μ_+ or μ_−) and the remaining M−1 tokens are Gaussian noise orthogonal to the signal. The output is a scalar binary classification score from a two-layer network (softmax self-attention followed by a fixed linear readout). The key data-model parameter is the signal-to-noise ratio SNR = ‖μ‖₂/(σ_p√d), which governs whether the model can distinguish signal from noise and determines the overfitting regime.

## Method

The paper analyzes a two-layer ViT (one softmax self-attention layer plus one linear layer) trained by full-batch gradient descent on cross-entropy loss from Gaussian random initialization. To handle the interdependent dynamics of W_Q, W_K, and W_V, the authors introduce 'vectorized Q & K' (projecting token vectors through W_Q and W_K into d_h-dimensional vectors whose inner products encode attention scores) and 'scalarized V' (tracking scalar projections μ_±ᵀW_Vw_O and ξᵀW_Vw_O), reducing matrix-level dynamics to scalar coefficient tracking. The training process is decomposed into three stages: (1) W_V begins preferentially learning signals over noise, (2) signal-aligned inner products grow linearly while attention becomes sparse, and (3) the loss converges to the target ε. The main result is a sharp phase transition: when N·SNR² = Ω(1) the ViT exhibits benign overfitting (test loss → 0), while when N⁻¹·SNR⁻² = Ω(1) it exhibits harmful overfitting (test loss = Θ(1)), verified on synthetic data and MNIST.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp phase transition for benign overfitting in two-layer ViTs: when N·SNR² = Ω(1), the model overfits training data (loss → ε) while test loss vanishes (o(1)); when N⁻¹·SNR⁻² = Ω(1), the model overfits but test loss remains Θ(1). On synthetic data (M=16, d=1024, N∈[2,20], SNR∈[0.16,15.6]), the theoretical boundary N·SNR²=1000 closely tracks the empirical dividing line between low and high test loss. On MNIST (N∈[3000,8400], SNR∈[0.2,2.0]), a clear transition between benign and harmful overfitting regimes is observed, with larger N and SNR yielding better generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-vs-method empirical comparisons; the 'baselines' are prior theoretical results. Compared to CNN benign overfitting results (Cao et al. 2022; Kou et al. 2023), the ViT condition N·SNR² = Ω(1) requires fewer samples when SNR ≤ 1, reflecting an advantage of the attention mechanism. Unlike Li et al. (2023a), which uses hinge loss and a specific initialization and only characterizes the benign regime, this paper uses cross-entropy loss with random Gaussian initialization and provides a complementary harmful overfitting theorem, making the separation condition tighter. The three-stage decoupling analysis (extending the two-stage approach of Cao et al. 2022 for CNNs) is the key technical contribution that enables handling the interdependent QKV dynamics and softmax nonlinearity simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting analysis from linear models and CNNs (Cao et al., 2022) to Vision Transformers with softmax self-attention, adapting the multi-stage coefficient decoupling technique to handle the interdependent QKV weight matrices. It builds most directly on Li et al. (2023a), which studied ViT training dynamics under a similar data model but used hinge loss and a specific initialization; this work replaces those simplifications with cross-entropy loss and random Gaussian initialization, and crucially adds the harmful overfitting regime as a complement. The technical novelty lies in the 'Vectorized Q & K and scalarized V' decomposition, which avoids the common shortcut of merging W_Q and W_K into a single matrix, thereby preserving the dynamics of QKV interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of when ViTs exhibit benign versus harmful overfitting, providing a sharp SNR-based phase transition (N·SNR² = Ω(1)) that was previously unavailable for attention-based architectures. The mutual reinforcement mechanism between W_Q, W_K, and W_V offers a mechanistic explanation for why attention can either amplify signal or memorize noise, which is relevant to understanding real-world ViT training behavior. Limitations include the reliance on a simplified two-layer architecture, a fixed M = Θ(1) token count, and experimental validation only on synthetic data and MNIST; extending the analysis to deeper ViTs, larger token sequences, and realistic image datasets remains open.
