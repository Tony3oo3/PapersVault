## Problem

The merging of predictors (or experts) is an ill-posed problem that requires strong inductive biases, yet prior work has treated it from a purely statistical perspective without examining how causal assumptions about the direction of data generation affect the resulting predictor. This paper asks: what asymmetries arise when the same moment constraints are used to merge predictors under a causal assumption (covariates cause the target) versus an anticausal assumption (covariates are effects of the target)?

## Input / Output

The method consumes first- and second-order moment constraints (marginal means and variances of two continuous covariates, their mutual covariance, covariances between each covariate and the binary target, and the target mean) rather than raw data, along with an explicit causal or anticausal direction assumption. It produces a closed-form binary class probability p(Y|X) and a corresponding linear decision boundary. A key structural detail is that in the anticausal direction the predictor is not obtained directly but must be derived by inverting a mixture-of-Gaussians generative model via Bayes' rule.

## Method

The paper applies Causal Maximum Entropy (CMAXENT) to the predictor-merging problem in both causal and anticausal directions. In the causal direction, CMAXENT first maximises the entropy of the covariate distribution (yielding a multivariate Gaussian) and then maximises the conditional entropy of Y|X, which reduces to logistic regression. In the anticausal direction, it first maximises the entropy of the binary target (yielding a Bernoulli) and then maximises the conditional entropy of X|Y, which yields a mixture of Gaussians with shared covariance—equivalent to Linear Discriminant Analysis after applying Bayes' rule. The paper proves that with full moment knowledge both directions produce decision boundaries with identical slopes, but when some moments are missing (e.g., the predictor-target covariance or the inter-predictor covariance), the boundaries diverge. In particular, the anticausal direction enables Out-Of-Variable generalisation: even without observing any joint data between a covariate and the target, the causal structure allows the model to infer a non-zero dependence, whereas the causal direction simply drops the unobserved covariate.

## Results

The paper establishes that CMAXENT merging of predictors with a binary target and two continuous covariates reduces to logistic regression in the causal direction (Proposition 1, Eq. 7) and to Linear Discriminant Analysis in the anticausal direction (Theorem 5). Theorem 10 proves that when all first and second moments are known, the decision boundary slopes are identical in both directions (the normal vectors Σ_X⁻¹φ and Σ_{X|Y}⁻¹φ are proportional via the Sherman-Morrison formula). When the predictor-predictor covariance s̄₁,₂ is unknown, the slopes diverge: the causal direction yields a diagonal marginal covariance (independent predictors) while the anticausal direction yields a diagonal conditional covariance (conditionally independent predictors), producing different decision boundary orientations (Appendix E, Fig. 2). Additionally, Proposition 11 provides a closed-form upper bound on the unknown predictor-target covariance φ₂ in the anticausal direction, enabling Out-Of-Variable generalisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. The central conceptual comparison is between logistic regression (causal CMAXENT) and LDA (anticausal CMAXENT), showing they are equivalent under full moment knowledge but diverge under partial information. The paper also positions its results relative to prior MAXENT-based expert aggregation work [25, 27, 34], noting that those approaches treat the problem purely statistically without causal structure, and relative to the AdaBoost-as-logistic-regression interpretation of Friedman et al. [8], offering a causal-merging perspective on the same equivalence. No head-to-head performance benchmarks are provided; the contribution is the identification of when and why the two classical classifiers differ based on causal assumptions and available moment constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Causal Maximum Entropy (CMAXENT) framework of Janzing (2021) to the predictor-merging problem, showing that the two-step CMAXENT optimisation naturally yields logistic regression in the causal direction and Linear Discriminant Analysis in the anticausal direction. It departs from the broader merging-of-experts literature (mixture of experts, expert aggregation) by explicitly incorporating causal direction as a structural assumption rather than treating all predictors symmetrically. The connection to the causal vs. anticausal learning literature (Schölkopf et al. 2012, Janzing & Schölkopf 2015) is made explicit, but here the asymmetry is studied through the lens of inductive bias selection rather than prediction accuracy under distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled maximum-entropy justification for when logistic regression versus LDA is the 'right' merging procedure, tying the choice to causal assumptions rather than empirical performance alone. The OOV generalisation result—that anticausal assumptions allow one to build a model involving a variable never jointly observed with the target—is a concrete implication for federated learning and multi-source data integration. However, the analysis is restricted to two continuous covariates, one binary target, and first/second moments, with no empirical validation; extending to higher-order moments, more complex causal graphs, or non-Gaussian settings remains open, and the practical significance of the decision-boundary differences under partial information is not quantified.
