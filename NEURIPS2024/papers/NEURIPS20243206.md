## Problem

Applying image diffusion models frame-by-frame to video inverse problems produces flickering, texture-sticking, and temporal inconsistency. Prior noise-warping methods (e.g., How I Warped Your Noise) assume the denoiser is equivariant to spatial deformations, but this assumption is violated by practical networks, causing those methods to fail on challenging conditional tasks and latent diffusion models. The paper identifies equivariance as the critical missing requirement and provides a principled framework to enforce it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of degraded video frames (downsampled by 8× or randomly masked) at 1024×1024 resolution, along with optical flow fields between consecutive frames (computed via RAFT in pixel space and converted to the 128×128 latent space of SDXL), and optional text conditioning. It produces a temporally consistent restored video at the same resolution. A key design choice is that the initial noise is drawn from a Gaussian Process (via Random Fourier Features) rather than white noise, enabling continuous evaluation at arbitrary spatial locations needed for warping and inpainting.

## Method

Warped Diffusion reformulates video generation as a sequence of spatial warping transformations applied to a continuous noise function, requiring the generative model to be equivariant under those transformations. For noise propagation, it replaces white noise with a Gaussian Process sampled via Random Fourier Features, which allows the noise to be evaluated at arbitrary (off-grid) points induced by the optical flow, naturally handling both interpolation and inpainting. To enforce equivariance without retraining, the paper introduces Equivariance Self-Guidance: during the probability-flow ODE sampling for each new frame, an additional gradient step penalizes the mismatch between the warped current-frame network prediction and the stored previous-frame prediction, computed only on pixels that remain within the frame. The base model (SDXL) is first fine-tuned for 100k steps on COYO to accept correlated GP noise in place of white noise, after which the equivariance guidance is applied purely at inference time. The full pipeline—GP noise warping plus equivariance self-guidance—achieves near-zero warping error while maintaining competitive restoration quality on both video inpainting and 8× super-resolution.

## Results

On 8× video super-resolution over 600 real FETV videos, Warped Diffusion achieves the lowest warping error (0.649±0.363) by a large margin over GP Warping (0.812±0.337) and all interpolation-based baselines (≥0.934), while maintaining competitive restoration metrics (FID 58.2, CLIP Text 0.235, SSIM 0.654). On the synthetic integer-translation inpainting task, Warped Diffusion reduces the warping error to near-zero (0.001±0.001) compared to 0.046±0.007 for How I Warped Your Noise and 0.061±0.010 for GP Warping, while preserving high restoration quality (FID 61.2, LPIPS 0.188). The GP noise warping mechanism runs at 39 ms per frame at 1024×1024 resolution, 16× faster than the 629 ms reported in [14].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against How I Warped Your Noise [14], the prior state-of-the-art for temporally correlated inverse problems with image diffusion models. On the synthetic integer-translation inpainting task, Warped Diffusion reduces the warping error from 0.046 (How I Warped) to 0.001, a ~46× improvement, while maintaining comparable restoration quality (FID 61.2 vs 68.7, LPIPS 0.188 vs 0.197). A direct comparison on real FETV videos was not possible because no reference implementation of [14] was available at the time of writing; the authors of [14] themselves acknowledge that their method has 'limited impact on temporal coherency' on latent diffusion models. Against the GP Warping baseline (noise warping without equivariance guidance), Warped Diffusion achieves a 20% lower warping error on real videos (0.649 vs 0.812) and a substantially lower FID (58.2 vs 75.8), demonstrating that the equivariance self-guidance is the critical component. Simple interpolation baselines (Nearest, Bilinear) and fixed/resampled noise all exhibit warping errors above 0.93 on real videos, confirming that noise warping alone is insufficient without enforcing equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the noise-warping framework of 'How I Warped Your Noise' [14], which proposed transforming input noise via optical flow to achieve temporal consistency when applying image diffusion models to video. The key departure is identifying that [14]'s core assumption—that the denoiser is equivariant to spatial warping—fails in practice for free-form neural networks, and that this failure is the root cause of temporal inconsistency. The paper also draws on the function-space diffusion model literature [52, 59, 28, 35] to formalize noise as a continuous Gaussian process (sampled via Random Fourier Features) rather than discrete white noise, enabling principled interpolation and inpainting of warped noise. Finally, it extends the diffusion posterior sampling / guidance paradigm [15, 81] to a new use case: enforcing equivariance at inference time rather than enforcing data consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any large pretrained image diffusion model (demonstrated with SDXL) can be repurposed for video inverse problems without training a dedicated video model, which is typically prohibitively expensive. The equivariance self-guidance is a lightweight, model-agnostic inference-time technique that could be adopted broadly. Key limitations include the requirement to fine-tune the base model on correlated noise (ruling out zero-shot use), dependence on optical flow quality (occlusions and noisy flows cause failures), and increased sampling time (~5 min for a 2-second video on one A100). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex non-rigid deformations, whether the VAE decoder's approximate equivariance always holds in pixel space, and whether the fine-tuning requirement can be eliminated.
