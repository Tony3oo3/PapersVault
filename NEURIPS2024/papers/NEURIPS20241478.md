## Problem

Generative models such as large language models produce sketchy and misleading answers for complex multi-agent decision-making problems because they lack the trial-and-error experience and grounded reasoning that humans use. Existing world-model-based approaches (e.g., UniSim, Genie, Dreamer) focus on single-agent scenarios, leaving the multi-agent setting largely unstudied due to the difficulty of describing multi-entity states in text and the compositional complexity of multi-agent dynamics and rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a single RGB image of the current game state (rendered by a parser that places unit sprites and health bars on a terrain background from SMAC state vectors) and a natural-language task description (terrain, agent/enemy counts, unit types, and remaining enemy health at termination). The output is an image sequence of interaction trajectories generated by running the converged joint policy on the learned dynamics model, which serves as the grounded 'answer' to the decision-making question, along with per-state-action predicted rewards that explain the policy's choices.

## Method

Learning before Interaction (LBI) decomposes the world model into a causal transformer dynamics model (built on Decision Transformer, using a VQ-VAE image tokenizer and residual state prediction for stability) and a bidirectional transformer reward model trained via a MaxEnt IRL objective with hindsight relabeling conditioned on the task description. At inference, an off-policy MARL algorithm (independent Q-learning with parameter sharing) collects reward-free trajectories by interacting with the dynamics model, the reward model then labels each transition, and the policy is updated with a behavior-regularization term (KL divergence to the dynamics model's autoregressive reference policy) to prevent out-of-distribution exploration. The reward model receives the entire trajectory as input, enabling non-Markovian credit assignment, and training alternates k-step policy updates with k-step reward updates using an inner-loop MA-SAC policy. This decomposition allows the dynamics model to be reused across different tasks by pairing it with different learned reward functions.

## Results

On the SMAC benchmark, LBI achieves an average win rate of 86.5% across 9 training maps, outperforming the best imitation learning baseline (MADT) by 10–42 percentage points per map. Against offline MARL methods with ground-truth rewards, LBI achieves returns of 18.96–20.45, exceeding the best baseline (OMIGA) by 6.2–8.6 points on 4 maps. On 10 unseen tasks, LBI consistently outperforms MADT and MA-TREX, e.g., reaching 97.85% win rate on 6m vs. 49.28% for MADT, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBI wins against all 16 baselines on every reported map. Among imitation learning methods, MADT (Meng et al., 2023) is the strongest competitor, reaching up to 82.76% on 10m_vs_11m, but LBI surpasses it by 7.7–41.6 points across all 9 maps; BC and MA-AIRL (Yu et al., 2019) fail catastrophically (0–26% win rates) because they imitate all trajectories without reward discrimination. MAPT (Zhu et al., 2024) and MA-TREX (Brown et al., 2019) plateau at 19–75% due to reliance on scripted-teacher preferences or ranked demonstrations. Against offline MARL methods with ground-truth SMAC rewards, LBI achieves 18.96–20.45 return, beating OMIGA (Wang et al., 2024) by up to 6.2 points on 5m_vs_6m and 6.2 points on 6h_vs_8z, while BCQ-MA (Fujimoto et al., 2019), CQL-MA (Kumar et al., 2020), ICQ (Yang et al., 2021), and OMAR (Pan et al., 2022) all fall below 19. The paper attributes the gap to the inefficiency of SMAC's hit-point-damage reward design and excessive conservatism in OOD penalization. In the online MARL comparison (Appendix E, Figure 7), LBI converges significantly faster than CW-QMIX (Rashid et al., 2020), QPLEX (Wang et al., 2020), MAVEN (Mahajan et al., 2019), EMC (Zheng et al., 2021), RODE (Wang et al., 2020), QMIX (Rashid et al., 2018), and MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022), confirming that the pre-trained world model reduces the waiting time for grounded answers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dyna architecture lineage (Sutton, 1990) and model-based RL to the multi-agent setting, where prior world-model work (UniSim, Genie, GAIA-1) has focused almost exclusively on single-agent scenarios. The dynamics model is built on the Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) with a VQ-VAE image tokenizer, while the reward model adapts the MaxEnt IRL formulation (Ziebart et al., 2008) into a bidirectional transformer that performs credit assignment over full trajectories under language guidance. A key departure from prior offline MARL and IRL methods is the use of hindsight relabeling via textual task descriptions to synthesize expert demonstrations, combined with a behavior-regularization term that anchors the learned policy to the dynamics model's reference distribution to prevent OOD exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a path for generative models to produce grounded, explainable answers (image sequences and per-state rewards) to multi-agent decision-making questions rather than the sketchy text that current VLMs produce. The finding that a learned reward function generalizes better to unseen tasks than the ground-truth SMAC reward (LBI-GTR underperforms LBI) challenges the assumption that human-designed rewards are optimal for policy learning. However, the work is confined to StarCraft II, requires considerable wall-clock time for policy convergence (limiting real-time use), and the zero-shot generalization, while strong, is tested only on variations of the same game's unit types and maps. Open questions include scaling to truly novel domains, replacing MARL with planning for faster inference, and whether the learned reward's interpretability holds in more complex, open-ended environments.
