## Problem

Localized receptive fields populate early sensory cortices and emerge in early layers of deep networks trained without explicit sparsity regularization, yet the mechanistic role of higher-order input statistics in driving this dynamical emergence remained unclear. Ingrosso and Goldt (2022) demonstrated that non-Gaussianity is necessary for localization in a minimal feedforward setting but did not derive the effective learning dynamics that explain how specific marginal properties translate into localized weights over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240815/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 1-D data vector X ∈ R^N (e.g., N=40 spatial positions) sampled from distributions satisfying weak positional dependence, translation invariance, and sign symmetry, paired with a binary class label Y indicating the correlation lengthscale. The network produces a scalar MSE prediction, but the scientifically relevant output is the first-layer weight vector w, whose spatial profile constitutes the learned receptive field. Data are generated procedurally from three families—Ising, nonlinear Gaussian process (NLGP), and controllable-kurtosis (Kur)—that share similar covariance structures but differ in marginal higher-order statistics.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form gradient-flow ODE for a single ReLU neuron trained on a binary lengthscale-discrimination task by conditioning the preactivation on each input dimension and invoking Lindeberg's condition to obtain a Gaussian approximation of the conditional preactivation (rather than the unconditional one used in prior Gaussian-equivalence work). This yields an effective dynamics where a nonlinear 'amplifier' function φ—determined entirely by the marginal distribution of the input—governs the shape of the weight vector, while second-order statistics (covariance) only constrain its scale. A third-order Taylor expansion of φ reveals that negative excess kurtosis of the marginals produces super-linearity, which amplifies large weight entries and drives localization, whereas positive excess kurtosis produces sub-linearity and oscillatory (non-localized) weights. The authors further prove that elliptical distributions, despite potentially having very large or infinite kurtosis, always yield sinusoidal steady states, establishing that preactivation kurtosis alone is an insufficient diagnostic. Predictions are validated empirically across the single-neuron model, many-neuron soft committee machines, and full two-layer networks, and contrasted with ICA, which localizes under both positive and negative kurtosis due to its non-Gaussianity-maximizing objective.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that the inverse participation ratio (IPR) of learned receptive fields transitions sharply from near 1.0 (localized) to near 0 (non-localized) as excess kurtosis of the input marginals crosses from negative to positive, validated across 30 random initializations for both NLGP(g) and Kur(k) data families. The analytical gradient flow (Eq. 5) predicts the localization peak position with 93% exact accuracy (26/28 initial conditions) on Ising data. For elliptical distributions (including Student-t with infinite kurtosis), learned receptive fields are sinusoidal with L2 fitting errors of 3.75%–9.77%, confirming Proposition 3.3. In the many-neuron SCM setting, Kur(10) (excess kurtosis −0.93) yields sharply localized filters while Kur(4) (excess kurtosis +3.28) yields low-frequency oscillations, matching the single-neuron prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole explicit baseline comparison is against Independent Component Analysis (FastICA, Hyvärinen & Oja 2000), a classical method for learning localized receptive fields from naturalistic data. On the Kur(3) dataset (excess kurtosis +7.66), ICA successfully learns localized receptive fields, whereas the paper's neural network models (both single-neuron M2 and many-neuron M1) produce non-localized, oscillatory weights on the same data. This divergence is explained by the different objectives: ICA maximizes non-Gaussianity in absolute value (any nonzero excess kurtosis suffices), while the neural network's gradient dynamics require specifically *negative* excess kurtosis to produce a super-linear amplifier φ that drives localization. The authors note this contrast as an open question, suggesting that ICA's top-down non-Gaussianity criterion captures a different mechanism than the bottom-up nonlinear learning dynamics analyzed here. No other baselines (e.g., sparse coding, PCA) are quantitatively compared; the paper positions its contribution as a mechanistic explanation rather than a performance benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimal localization framework of Ingrosso and Goldt (2022), who demonstrated that localized receptive fields emerge in feedforward networks trained on idealized naturalistic data with non-Gaussian statistics, but left the dynamical mechanism open. The key analytical departure is from the Gaussian equivalence approach of Goldt et al. (2020, 2022), which assumes unconditional Gaussianity of preactivations; here, the authors instead condition on individual input dimensions, capturing how higher-order marginal statistics propagate through the ReLU nonlinearity. The work also positions itself against the sparse coding and ICA tradition, which explains localization via explicit sparsity or independence criteria, by showing that localization can emerge purely from the nonlinear learning dynamics interacting with data statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a precise mechanistic account of why localization emerges without top-down efficiency constraints: negative excess kurtosis of input marginals drives super-linearity in the gradient flow's 'amplifier' term, while positive excess kurtosis suppresses it. The negative result that elliptical distributions (including Student-t with infinite kurtosis) yield only sinusoidal, non-localized weights is conceptually important, showing that preactivation kurtosis is not the right diagnostic and that the sign of marginal excess kurtosis is what matters. Open questions include whether the single-neuron analysis captures multi-neuron interactions, how to extend to orientation and phase selectivity, and reconciling the discrepancy with ICA (which localizes under positive kurtosis). Validation on natural images and deeper architectures remains future work.
