## Problem

Existing biological foundation models (Enformer, Nucleotide Transformer, ESM) are each trained on a single sequence modality—DNA, RNA, or protein—yet critical genomic tasks such as predicting which RNA transcript isoforms are expressed in a given tissue inherently require information from all three modalities. No prior architecture could jointly leverage pre-trained DNA, RNA, and protein encoders to solve such multi-modal problems, leaving isoform-level expression prediction unsolved by sequence-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three biological sequences as input: a DNA sequence centered on the transcription start site (up to 196,608 nucleotides with Enformer or 12,288 with NT), an RNA transcript sequence (left-cropped to 12 kb to preserve the 3′UTR), and the corresponding protein amino-acid sequence (up to 2,048 residues for ESM2). It outputs a single expression value (log-transformed TPM) per transcript isoform for each of 30 human tissues. The framework is flexible: it accepts one, two, or three modalities, which matters for non-coding transcripts that lack a protein counterpart.

## Method

IsoFormer connects three independently pre-trained modality encoders (Enformer or NT for DNA, NT for RNA, ESM2-150M for protein) through a cross-attention aggregation module with residual connections, where each modality's embedding successively attends to the other two modalities to produce a modality-specific multi-modal embedding; these are concatenated into a joint representation. A global average pooling layer followed by a linear head maps this representation to per-tissue expression values, trained end-to-end with MSE loss on log-transformed, tissue-normalized GTEx TPM values. Ablation studies show that plain cross-attention outperforms Perceiver Resampler and C-Abstractor alternatives, and that replacing NT with Enformer as the DNA encoder yields the largest gains (R² 0.53 vs. 0.43) because Enformer's pre-training on gene-level expression provides task-aligned features that transfer to the finer-grained isoform prediction objective.

## Results

On the GTEx transcript isoform expression prediction task (30 tissues, ~170k transcripts), IsoFormer with Enformer as DNA encoder achieves R² = 0.53 ± 0.01 and Spearman correlation = 0.72 ± 0.00, substantially outperforming standalone Enformer (R² = 0.21, Spearman = 0.46). Adding all three modalities (DNA + RNA + protein) consistently improves over any single modality: RNA-only reaches R² = 0.36, DNA-only R² = 0.13, and protein-only R² = 0.20. The multi-modal framework also demonstrates strong transfer from pre-trained encoders, with R² dropping from 0.53 (all pre-trained) to 0.10 (all random-initialized).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external baseline is Enformer [14], a DNA-only gene expression model that predicts one expression value per gene per tissue from a 196 kbp sequence. IsoFormer with Enformer as its DNA encoder (plus NT for RNA and ESM for protein) more than doubles Enformer's R² (0.53 vs. 0.21) and improves Spearman correlation by 0.26 (0.72 vs. 0.46) on the harder isoform-level task. Even the weaker IsoFormer(NT) variant (R² = 0.43) already surpasses standalone Enformer by over 2× in R², demonstrating that the multi-modal aggregation itself provides a large gain beyond what the DNA encoder alone can achieve. IsoFormer also outperforms Enformer on every individual tissue (Figure 6), confirming the improvement is not driven by a subset of tissues. The Borzoi variant (R² = 0.48) sits between the NT and Enformer versions, suggesting that a domain-specific DNA encoder tuned for RNA-seq coverage prediction also transfers well but not as strongly as Enformer's gene-expression pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained single-modality biological foundation models (Enformer for DNA, Nucleotide Transformer for DNA/RNA, ESM2 for protein) and connects them via a cross-attention aggregation module inspired by multi-modal LLM architectures (Perceiver Resampler, C-Abstractor from vision-language work). It departs from prior biological sequence modeling, which has been strictly single-modality, by being the first architecture to jointly process DNA, RNA, and protein sequences. The task of transcript isoform expression prediction extends the gene-level expression prediction addressed by Enformer and Borzoi to a finer-grained, multi-modal formulation that those models cannot solve from DNA alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IsoFormer addresses a central unsolved problem in genomics—predicting which RNA isoforms are expressed in which tissue—which is critical for understanding disease mechanisms and gene regulation. The demonstration of inter-modality transfer (substantial performance even when one encoder is randomly initialized) suggests that biological foundation models can share knowledge across the central dogma, opening the door to more data-efficient multi-modal biological modeling. Key limitations include reliance on reusing NT for RNA (no dedicated RNA foundation model is publicly available), restriction to human GTEx tissues, and the question of whether the cross-attention aggregation generalizes to tasks with very different sequence length scales or additional modalities (e.g., epigenetic marks).
