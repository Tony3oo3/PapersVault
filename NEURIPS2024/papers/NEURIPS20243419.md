## Problem

Variance exploding (VE) diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art performance but suffer from a slow 1/poly(T) forward convergence rate due to the absence of a drift term, making it difficult to balance reverse beginning, discretization, and score approximation errors. Previous theoretical analyses either restrict the diffusion coefficient β_t to be bounded or introduce additional exp(T) factors, leaving open whether an efficient polynomial sample complexity is achievable for VE-based models with unbounded β_t under realistic data assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method starts from pure Gaussian noise N(0, σ_T²I) and runs a discretized reverse process (either reverse SDE with η=1 or PFODE with η=0) using a trained score network s(T−t_k, ·) frozen at each interval, with exponential integrator discretization and early stopping at T−δ to handle score blow-up. For image generation experiments, the model operates on 256×256 CelebA images; for synthetic experiments, it handles 2D data (Swiss roll, 2D Gaussian, 1D-GMM). The output is a sample distribution R_K^{q_∞^τ} that is (ε_TV + ε_score)-close to q_δ in total variation, which is ε_W2-close to the data distribution q_0 in Wasserstein distance.

## Method

The paper introduces a drifted VESDE forward process dX_t = −(1/τ)β_t X_t dt + √(2β_t) dB_t with τ ∈ [T, T²], where the drift term eliminates data information at an exponential rate, yielding an exp(−T) forward convergence guarantee when β_t is aggressive (e.g., β_t = t²). Under the manifold hypothesis, this process balances the three error sources and achieves the first polynomial sample complexity Õ(dR⁴(d+R√d)⁴/(ε_W2⁸ ε_TV²)) for VE-based models with reverse SDE and unbounded β_t. For the reverse PFODE, the paper proposes a unified tangent-based analysis framework that simultaneously handles both reverse SDE and PFODE; the key technical step is bounding the tangent process norm by exploiting the variance-exploding property of VESDE to ensure ∫β_t/τ dt ≤ C, thereby avoiding the exp(T) factor that plagues prior analyses. This yields the first quantitative W_1 convergence guarantee for SOTA VE-based models (e.g., EDM with σ_t²=t²) under reverse PFODE.

## Results

On CelebA256, the conservative drifted VESDE (β_t=1, τ=T) improves over the pure VESDE baseline (σ_t²=t) without retraining, achieving an aesthetic score of 5.813 vs. 5.807 and an Inception Score of 4.174 vs. 4.082. Theoretically, the aggressive drifted VESDE (β_t=t², τ=T²) achieves the first polynomial sample complexity K ≤ Õ(dR⁴(d+R√d)⁴/(ε_W₂⁸ ε_TV²)) for VE-based models with reverse SDE under the manifold hypothesis, matching the VP-based model complexity of Chen et al. [2023c]. For the reverse PFODE setting, the paper provides the first quantitative W₁ convergence guarantee for SOTA VE-based models (σ_t²=t²) with polynomial dependence on τ rather than exponential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is the pure VESDE with σ_t²=t (the standard Song et al. [2020b] formulation without drift). On CelebA256, the conservative drifted VESDE (β_t=1, τ=T) outperforms this baseline on both aesthetic score (5.813 vs. 5.807) and Inception Score (4.174 vs. 4.082) without any retraining, simply by modifying the forward process at inference time. On the Swiss roll synthetic dataset, the drifted VESDE produces a more uniform density across the manifold compared to pure VESDE, which shows low density away from the center due to inability to handle large dataset variance. On the 2-D Gaussian experiment with exact score, the aggressive drifted VESDE (β_t=t²) achieves the best and second-best performance under exponential integrator and Euler-Maruyama discretization respectively, while pure VESDE performs worst due to its inability to balance the three error sources (reverse beginning, discretization, and score approximation). Theoretically, the paper also shows that pure VESDE with σ_t²=t² has sample complexity O(1/(ε_W₂⁸ ε_TV⁷)) while the drifted VESDE with aggressive β_t achieves O(1/(ε_W₂⁸ ε_TV²)), a significant improvement in the ε_TV dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Girsanov-based convergence analysis of VP-based diffusion models (Chen et al., ICLR 2023) to the VE-based setting, which had previously only been analyzed under stronger assumptions (LSI, log-concavity) or with bounded diffusion coefficients. It directly improves upon De Bortoli et al. (NeurIPS 2021), who introduced a small drift term into VESDE but suffered from an exp(T) discretization penalty and could not handle unbounded β_t. The tangent-based unified framework generalizes Bortoli (2022) to simultaneously cover both reverse SDE and PFODE, carefully exploiting the variance-exploding property to avoid the exp(T) blowup that the original tangent lemma incurs for PFODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide polynomial sample complexity for SOTA VE-based models (with unbounded β_t like EDM's σ_t²=t²) under the realistic manifold hypothesis, and the first quantitative convergence guarantee for VE-based models with reverse PFODE. The practical finding that a model trained with pure VESDE can be directly repurposed for the drifted VESDE forward process without retraining to improve sample quality is a useful zero-cost trick. However, the main theoretical results assume an accurate score function, and the paper explicitly identifies extending to approximate scores for PFODE as future work; the empirical gains (aesthetic score 5.807→5.813, IS 4.082→4.174 on CelebA) are modest, limiting immediate practical impact.
