## Problem

Multi-task optimization (MTO) suffers from task imbalance, where some tasks are under-optimized relative to others. Existing solutions fall into two camps: gradient-oriented methods achieve strong performance but incur high computational and memory costs from per-task gradient computations, while loss-oriented methods are efficient but typically underperform. The paper asks whether a loss-oriented method can match gradient-oriented performance by explicitly aligning learning progress across tasks through group-based interactions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on any multi-task model with a shared backbone and task-specific heads, consuming only the scalar per-task empirical risks (no per-task gradients required). It produces two artifacts at each iteration: a discrete group assignment matrix G_t ∈ {0,1}^{K×M} partitioning M tasks into K groups, and continuous group weights ω_t ∈ R^K that scale the losses. The final model outputs are task-specific predictions (segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, molecular properties, or facial attributes) depending on the benchmark, but the optimizer itself is architecture-agnostic and compatible with any gradient-based optimizer (SGD, Adam).

## Method

GO4Align formulates task imbalance as a bi-level optimization: the lower level assigns tasks to K groups via K-means clustering on risk-guided group indicators, while the upper level updates model parameters using group-weighted losses (intra-group tasks share one weight to enforce synchronized learning progress). The group indicators are computed by element-wise multiplying a scale-balance vector (normalizing each task's risk to the mean risk, up-weighting smaller-risk tasks) and a smooth-alignment vector (a normalized exponential moving average of risks that captures historical task correlations). This design avoids per-task gradient computation entirely, making it a loss-oriented method whose training time is independent of the number of tasks. The group number K is selected via the elbow method, and the entire pipeline is compatible with any existing MTO method by substituting its task weights as group indicators.

## Results

On NYUv2 (3 tasks), GO4Align achieves the best overall MTL performance with Δm% = −6.08 and MR = 2.11, outperforming all gradient-oriented and loss-oriented baselines. On QM9 (11 tasks), it obtains the lowest Δm% = 52.7 and MR = 4.55. On CelebA (40 tasks), GO4Align achieves the best Δm% = 0.88 and MR = 3.10. On CityScapes (2 tasks), it is comparable to FAMO (Δm% = 8.11 vs. 8.13), with the limited task count constraining the grouping mechanism. Training time is near the baseline (1.01× LS), far below gradient-oriented methods like NASHMTL (up to 12.49× with 40 tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GO4Align outperforms all gradient-oriented baselines (MGDA, PCGRAD, CAGRAD, IMTL-G, NASHMTL, GRADDROP) on every dataset in terms of Δm%, with the largest margins on QM9 (52.7 vs. 62.0 for NASHMTL, the strongest gradient method) and CelebA (0.88 vs. 0.84 for IMTL-G, the closest competitor). Among loss-oriented methods, GO4Align beats FAMO (the closest competitor) on NYUv2 (−6.08 vs. −4.10), QM9 (52.7 vs. 58.5), and CelebA (0.88 vs. 1.21), while matching it on CityScapes (8.11 vs. 8.13), where only 2 tasks limit the grouping mechanism's benefit. UW is the only baseline that beats GO4Align on CityScapes (5.89 vs. 8.11 Δm%), but GO4Align dominates UW on all other datasets. The method also achieves the best MR (average rank) on NYUv2 (2.11), QM9 (4.55), and CelebA (3.10), indicating more balanced per-task performance. Notably, GO4Align is the only method that improves every task's performance relative to single-task learning on NYUv2, confirming that group-based task interactions effectively alleviate learning-progress imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GO4Align repurposes the multi-task grouping concept from Fifty et al. [21] and Kang et al. [22]—where grouping was used to design group-specific architectures for feature sharing—into the loss-scaling space of multi-task optimization. It is positioned as a loss-oriented method that addresses the well-known performance gap between loss-oriented and gradient-oriented MTO methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, NASHMTL, etc.), with FAMO [14] identified as the closest prior work. The key conceptual departure is formulating task imbalance as a bi-level optimization problem where K-means clustering over risk-guided group indicators dynamically assigns tasks to groups, replacing per-task gradient computations with group-level loss weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GO4Align demonstrates that a loss-oriented method can match or exceed gradient-oriented methods on overall MTL performance while maintaining near-baseline training time, which is practically significant for large-scale multi-task settings where per-task gradient computation becomes prohibitive (e.g., 40 tasks on CelebA). The main limitation is the heuristic selection of group number K, which requires up to M separate runs, and the grouping mechanism offers limited benefit when the number of tasks is small (e.g., CityScapes with 2 tasks). Open questions include whether automatic group number selection (e.g., via the methods in [43]) can remove this hyperparameter, and whether the approach extends to settings with heterogeneous architectures or very large task counts.
