## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) models uncertainty for OOD detection but is limited to evidence that supports only a single category (sharp evidence), ignoring vague evidence that corroborates multiple in-distribution categories simultaneously. This causes overestimated uncertainty for ambiguous in-distribution samples and degraded OOD detection. Additionally, EDL's fully-connected layers suffer from vanishing gradients as the number of classes grows, leading to complete failure in classifying certain categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242686/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 as input, processed by a ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10/100) or ResNet-34 (Flower-102, CUB-200-2011) backbone. The output is a projected multinomial-opinion parameterizing a Dirichlet distribution, from which class probabilities, a scalar uncertainty mass u, and a decomposed evidence vector (sharp per-singleton and vague per-composite-set components) are derived. The OOD detection score is the uncertainty mass u, while the class prediction is the argmax of the expected probability vector.

## Method

HEDL extends EDL's multinomial-opinion over the basic domain X to a hyper-opinion over the hyper-domain R(X) (the powerset of X minus the full set and empty set), allowing belief mass to be assigned to composite sets representing vague evidence that supports multiple categories at once. A novel opinion projection mechanism then maps hyper-opinion back to multinomial-opinion: a Heaviside function applied to the fully-connected layer weights W identifies which singletons each belief mass supports, and vague evidence is allocated proportionally via relative base rates (uniformly as 1/|x| without prior information). The projection is implemented as a linear transformation b = o · G(W, b^H) where G is computed from detached variables, making it a constant during backpropagation and adding only O(1) computational overhead. This design simultaneously solves the vanishing gradient problem—since ∂e_k/∂o_k = G(W, b^H) is a non-zero constant rather than a ReLU gate that can zero out gradients—and preserves the standard EDL Dirichlet loss for optimization. Training proceeds in two stages: 90 epochs with a standard softmax head to pre-train the feature extractor, followed by 10 epochs under the full HEDL evidential framework with W_prior = 1.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HEDL achieves an average AUROC of 96.27% and FPR95 of 15.55% across SVHN, Textures, and Places365, with 95.66% ID accuracy. On the more challenging CIFAR-100, HEDL reaches 89.59% AUROC and 55.14% FPR95 while maintaining 80.40% ID accuracy, substantially outperforming EDL (76.51% AUROC, 95.94% FPR95, 71.40% accuracy). On fine-grained datasets, HEDL achieves near-perfect OOD detection: 99.07% AUROC / 3.98% FPR95 on Flower-102 and 98.91% AUROC / 3.82% FPR95 on CUB-200-2011, where EDL completely collapses (67.23% and 75.27% AUROC respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEDL outperforms all 19 baselines on average AUROC across all four ID datasets. On CIFAR-10, HEDL (96.27% AUROC, 15.55% FPR95) beats the closest competitor LogitNorm (95.97%, 22.47%) by 0.3 AUROC and 6.9 FPR95 points, and EDL (95.39%, 16.96%) by 0.88 AUROC. The advantage is most dramatic on CIFAR-100, where HEDL (89.59% AUROC) surpasses G-ODIN (84.21%) by 5.4 points and EDL (76.51%) by 13.1 points, while also achieving 9% higher ID accuracy than EDL (80.40% vs 71.40%). On fine-grained Flower-102 and CUB-200-2011, HEDL achieves near-perfect detection (99.07% and 98.91% AUROC) whereas EDL catastrophically fails (67.23% and 75.27% AUROC), confirming that the vanishing gradient problem in EDL's fully-connected layer is the root cause. HEDL also matches or exceeds the ID accuracy of post-hoc methods (which preserve the base model's accuracy) while simultaneously improving OOD detection, unlike training methods such as G-ODIN and LogitNorm that sacrifice 1–6% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Evidential Deep Learning (EDL, Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) by replacing its multinomial-opinion framework—where evidence supports only singleton categories—with a hyper-opinion formulation grounded in Subjective Logic's hyper-domain, which also admits composite sets supporting multiple categories simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'vague evidence' (belief mass assigned to composite sets) alongside the traditional 'sharp evidence' (belief mass on singletons), and a novel opinion projection mechanism that maps hyper-opinion back to multinomial-opinion for standard EDL optimization. This projection also serves as an elegant fix for the vanishing gradient problem in EDL's fully-connected layers, which prior work (Pandey et al., RED, ICML 2023) attempted to address with regularization techniques the authors find insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical failure mode of EDL—catastrophic performance degradation on fine-grained or high-cardinality datasets due to vanishing gradients—without introducing additional hyperparameters, regularization, or auxiliary OOD data, making it broadly applicable. The O(1) computational overhead of the opinion projection means the approach is drop-in compatible with existing EDL pipelines. A stated limitation is the dependence on pre-trained feature extractors (90 epochs of softmax pre-training before 10 epochs of HEDL training), and the authors note that reducing this reliance is an open direction. Broader open questions include whether the hyper-opinion framework generalizes beyond classification (e.g., regression, segmentation) and whether the composite-set structure can be learned end-to-end rather than determined by the Heaviside-activated weight matrix.
