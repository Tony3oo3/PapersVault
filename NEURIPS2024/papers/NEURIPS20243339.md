## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely exclusively on whole-image-to-concept similarity, which limits their ability to capture fine-grained, patch-specific visual details and forces them to consider all concepts simultaneously, undermining interpretability. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a hierarchical coarse-to-fine framework that exploits both holistic image representations and localized patch regions, with data-driven per-example concept selection to reduce the number of active concepts at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243339/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (split into P=16 non-overlapping patches, each resized to 224×224 for the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder) and two sets of textual concept descriptions: high-level class names and per-class low-level attributes (e.g., 312 for CUB, 102 for SUN, 20 per class for ImageNet). It produces a class prediction, per-example binary concept indicators (thresholded at τ=0.05), and per-patch concept activation maps that reveal which low-level attributes are active in each localized region, enabling patch-level interpretability of the decision.

## Method

CF-CBM constructs a two-level hierarchy where a High-level module computes CLIP similarities between the whole image and class-name concepts, and a Low-level module computes similarities between individual patches and per-class attribute concepts. Each level contains a Concept Discovery Block (CDB) that samples amortized Bernoulli latent variables (via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation) to select a sparse, per-example subset of relevant concepts. The levels are linked top-down: active high-level concepts (via Z_H) mask which low-level attributes are eligible, and the remaining low-level indicators (Z_L) further refine the selection, enabling end-to-end context sharing. Training uses a Variational Bayesian ELBO objective with cross-entropy losses at both levels plus KL regularizers against sparse Bernoulli priors (α=10⁻⁴), optimizing only the linear classification and discovery layers while keeping CLIP frozen. At inference, the mean of the learned Bernoulli posteriors is thresholded to yield a single sparse, interpretable concept set per image.

## Results

On the high level (whole image), CF-CBM achieves 79.50% accuracy on CUB, 64.00% on SUN, and 77.40% on ImageNet-1k, outperforming Label-Free CBMs by ~5% on CUB and ~5.4% on ImageNet. On the low level (patch-based), CF-CBM dramatically improves over the standalone CDM^L baseline, reaching 73.20% on CUB (vs. 59.62%), 57.10% on SUN (vs. 42.30%), and 78.45% on ImageNet (vs. 58.20%). For interpretability, the proposed Jaccard-based attribute matching metric shows CF-CBM outperforming CDM by up to 15% absolute (e.g., 32.50% vs. 17.20% class-wise on CUB), confirming that the hierarchical structure uncovers more relevant low-level concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Label-Free CBMs [13], CF-CBM^H achieves substantially higher accuracy on CUB (79.50% vs. 74.59%) and ImageNet (77.40% vs. 71.98%), while also introducing sparsity (27–50% concepts active) that the baseline lacks. Against the data-driven concept discovery model CDM [17], CF-CBM^H matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on CUB (79.50% vs. 78.90%) and ImageNet (77.40% vs. 76.55%), though it is marginally lower on SUN (64.00% vs. 64.55%); the key advantage is that CF-CBM activates more concepts (27–50% sparsity vs. 13–19%), reflecting the richer hierarchical information flow. On the low-level patch-based setting, CF-CBM^L dramatically outperforms CDM^L across all three datasets (e.g., +13.6% on CUB, +14.8% on SUN, +20.3% on ImageNet), demonstrating that the top-down masking from high-level concepts is critical for effective patch-level concept discovery. On the interpretability metric (Jaccard index for attribute matching), CF-CBM outperforms both CDM and CDM^L in every configuration, with the largest gap on CUB class-wise (32.50% vs. 17.20% and 19.60%), confirming that the coarse-to-fine hierarchy yields more faithful concept explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) lineage by introducing a two-level hierarchical architecture that links whole-image (high-level) and patch-specific (low-level) concept discovery modules. It directly builds on the label-free VLM-based similarity formulation of Oikarinen et al. (ICLR 2023) and the amortized Bernoulli concept discovery mechanism of Panousis et al. (2023), but departs from both by coupling the two levels through a top-down masking scheme where active high-level concepts gate which low-level attributes are considered. The key conceptual departure from prior CBMs is the explicit notion of concept hierarchy, where each high-level concept is characterized by a subset of low-level attributes, enabling patch-specific fine-grained interpretation that whole-image-only CBMs cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of existing CBMs: their inability to exploit localized, patch-specific concept information, which is critical for fine-grained tasks and ambiguous scenes. The introduction of a Jaccard-based metric for evaluating concept matching quality (as opposed to binary accuracy, which is inflated by sparse ground truth) is a useful methodological contribution for the interpretability community. However, the framework's dependence on a pretrained VLM backbone (CLIP) for all similarity computations, the assumption of a known concept hierarchy, and the use of simple fixed-size patches limit its generality. Open questions include whether the hierarchy can be learned rather than specified a priori, whether more sophisticated region representations (superpixels, Region-CLIP) would further improve granularity, and how the approach generalizes beyond vision-language settings.
