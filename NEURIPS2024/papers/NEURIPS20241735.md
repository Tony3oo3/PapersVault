## Problem

Existing next POI prediction methods largely overlook the severe long-tailed spatial distribution of POI visitations, where a small fraction of popular locations dominate the data while the vast majority (e.g., 98.38% on Gowalla) are rarely visited. This imbalance causes models to systematically mispredict long-tail POIs as head POIs, degrading accuracy for less-visited but user-relevant locations. LoTNext is the first general framework to explicitly address this long-tail problem in the next POI prediction setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length (e.g., 20) user check-in sequence of POI IDs and hourly time slots (168 slots per week), producing a ranked list of top-k POI candidates from a large candidate set (up to ~107K POIs) along with a predicted time slot for the next visit. The output logits are computed over all POIs in the dataset, making this a large-scale sequence classification task where the long-tail classes constitute the overwhelming majority of the label space.

## Method

LoTNext combines a Transformer encoder backbone with a Spatial Contextual Attention layer (weighting POIs by haversine distance decay) and three long-tail-specific modules. The Long-Tailed Graph Adjustment module denoises the User-POI Interaction Graph via attention-scored edge thresholding, then applies GCN on both the pruned interaction graph and a user-independent Global Transition Graph, combining the resulting node embeddings via mean pooling to yield denoised POI representations. The Long-Tailed Loss Adjustment module operates on two levels: a logit score adjustment that adds a frequency-inverse offset to each class's logit, and a sample weight adjustment that computes per-sample adaptive weights from the cosine similarity between the hidden state and the class-center weight vector, normalized by the geometric mean of all sample magnitudes. An auxiliary MSE-based time prediction task is jointly optimized with learnable loss weights to reinforce temporal context without requiring additional data sources.

## Results

On the Gowalla dataset, LoTNext achieves Acc@1 of 16.68%, Acc@5 of 36.05%, Acc@10 of 44.29%, and MRR of 25.91%, outperforming the strongest baseline SNPM (Acc@1=15.93%, MRR=25.05%). On Foursquare, LoTNext reaches Acc@1 of 31.55%, Acc@5 of 60.59%, Acc@10 of 68.12%, and MRR of 44.69%, surpassing SNPM (Acc@1=28.99%, MRR=42.78%). The performance gap is more pronounced on Gowalla, which exhibits a more severe long-tail distribution (98.38% of POIs have fewer than 100 visits vs. 63.70% on Foursquare). LoTNext also shows a ~6% higher propensity to predict long-tailed POIs compared to Graph-Flashback.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoTNext outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the strongest baseline SNPM (which builds a POI similarity graph to aggregate similar POIs), LoTNext gains +0.75% Acc@1 and +0.86% MRR on Gowalla, and +2.56% Acc@1 and +1.91% MRR on Foursquare. The graph-based methods (GETNext, Graph-Flashback, SNPM) consistently outperform sequence-based methods (DeepMove, LSTPM, ST-RNN), confirming the value of modeling global mobility patterns. LoTNext's advantage over Graph-Flashback is particularly notable on long-tailed samples: it achieves higher Acc@1 and MRR on both head and tail POIs, and predicts long-tailed POIs ~6% more frequently. The gap over Flashback (the spatial-contextual attention baseline) is substantial, with LoTNext improving Gowalla Acc@1 from 11.58% to 16.68% (+5.1%), demonstrating the combined benefit of graph denoising and loss adjustment.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoTNext builds on the Transformer-based trajectory encoding paradigm established by GETNext and the spatial distance-decay attention mechanism from Flashback, while adapting logit adjustment (originally developed for long-tailed visual recognition) to the POI prediction setting. It departs from prior graph-based POI methods (Graph-Flashback, SNPM) by introducing a denoising step on the user-POI interaction graph to prune low-confidence edges before GCN propagation, and by combining this with a novel sample-weight adjustment loss that goes beyond Focal Loss by explicitly modeling per-sample discriminative capacity via cosine similarity to class centers. The paper positions itself as the first to propose a unified framework explicitly targeting the long-tailed spatial distribution in next POI prediction, a problem that prior sequence- and graph-based methods have largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With ~98% of POIs on Gowalla being long-tailed, the inability of current models to accurately predict rare locations represents a significant practical gap for personalized navigation and urban planning. The LoTNext framework offers a generic recipe (graph denoising + logit/sample-weight adjustment + auxiliary time prediction) that could be adapted to other long-tailed recommendation or sequence-prediction tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that reliance on extensive trajectory data raises privacy concerns, and the model does not account for emergent events (accidents, extreme weather) that can shift mobility patterns. Open questions include whether the graph denoising threshold and logit adjustment weight transfer across cities or require per-dataset tuning, and whether the approach scales to even sparser or noisier real-world deployment settings.
