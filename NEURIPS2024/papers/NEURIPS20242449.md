## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods (CVAE, diffusion, GAN) project historical trajectory features into a separate latent space for future trajectories, creating a feature-space gap that limits cross-scene generalization and the ability to model complex multimodal distributions. The paper argues that, unlike classification or tracking, trajectory prediction involves input and output that inherently share the same feature space, and that enforcing this consistency is key to bridging the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242449/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 frames of 2D position sequences for N agents in a scene, tokenized with sinusoidal positional encoding and augmented with velocity and acceleration derived from the position sequence. It outputs 20 sampled future trajectories spanning 12 frames, generated by applying an affine (MLP) transformation to the encoded historical features plus Gaussian noise, then decoding with a pre-trained auto-regressive Transformer. Evaluation uses the prediction closest to ground truth among the 20 samples, reporting ADE and FDE.

## Method

TrajCLIP uses two structurally identical encoders (historical and future) that each extract Spatio-Temporal Interaction Features (STIF) via agent-aware attention and Scene-Agent Interaction Features (SAIF) via FFT, Butterworth filtering, and self-attention in the frequency domain, exploiting frequency-domain additivity to isolate shared scene context. A CLIP-style contrastive learning module aligns the two encoders' feature spaces using a novel trajectory similarity label computed as the mean included-angle cosine of velocity direction vectors across all sample points. An Idempotent Generative Network (IGN), a simple MLP, then maps historical features to future features within the same aligned space, trained with reconstruction loss, idempotent loss (fixed-point constraint), and tightness loss (manifold compaction) to prevent over-expansion. A pre-trained auto-regressive Transformer decoder converts the mapped future features into position sequences, and data augmentation via adjacent-point interpolation, small perturbations, and synthetic trajectory generation further improves distribution coverage.

## Results

On the standard trajectory prediction task, TrajCLIP achieves ADE/FDE of 0.18/0.33 on ETH-UCY (average across 5 scenes), 6.29/11.79 on SDD, and 0.25/0.39 on SNU, outperforming all seven baselines by up to 17% in prediction error. The lightweight variant TrajCLIP-tiny (76.92% fewer parameters) achieves 0.21/0.36 on ETH-UCY and 7.04/13.40 on SDD, remaining competitive with or superior to most baselines. In scene-to-scene transfer, TrajCLIP achieves the best average ADE of 0.68 and FDE of 1.36 across five transfer directions, surpassing dedicated transfer methods like T-GNN (0.80/1.56) and K0 (0.77/1.51). In 4-shot few-shot transfer on SNU, TrajCLIP outperforms all baselines by 8.7%–40.6% per scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajCLIP outperforms all seven standard-prediction baselines (Trajectron++, AgentFormer, MID, GP-Graph, EqMotion, TUTR, SingularTrajectory) on every dataset, with the largest margin over Trajectron++ (0.30→0.18 ADE on ETH-UCY, 10.65→6.29 on SDD). The closest competitor, SingularTrajectory (diffusion-based), trails by 0.03 ADE on ETH-UCY and 0.97 ADE on SDD. In scene-to-scene transfer, TrajCLIP beats dedicated transfer methods T-GNN (0.80 vs. 0.68 avg ADE) and K0 (0.77 vs. 0.68), as well as tra2tra (1.10 vs. 0.68), while TrajCLIP-tiny (0.76) matches or exceeds T-GNN and K0. In 4-shot few-shot transfer on SNU, TrajCLIP achieves 0.28/0.49 ADE/FDE, beating K0 (0.30/0.54), T-GNN (0.31/0.55), MemoNet (0.32/0.62), and Social-STGCNN (0.37/0.73) by 8.7%–40.6% per scene. In online adaptation on SDD's DeathCircle_0, TrajCLIP reduces ADE by 20% over the base model after 100 steps, outpacing K0 with and without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrajCLIP repurposes CLIP's contrastive alignment mechanism (originally for text-image pairs) to enforce feature-space consistency between historical and future trajectory encoders, and adopts the Idempotent Generative Network (IGN) framework with its idempotent and tightness losses as the mapping backbone. It builds on AgentFormer's agent-aware attention for spatio-temporal interaction features but adds a frequency-domain scene-agent interaction feature (SAIF) via Fourier transforms and Butterworth filtering. The key conceptual departure from prior generative trajectory predictors (CVAE-based Trajectron++, diffusion-based MID, etc.) is the argument that historical and future trajectories should inhabit the *same* feature space, making the mapping an affine transformation rather than a cross-space projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The same-space consistency framing offers a principled explanation for why prior generative methods struggle with cross-scene generalization, and the ablation confirming that CLIP alignment only yields gains with the deterministic IGN (not with stochastic CVAE or diffusion) is a useful architectural insight. The strong scene-to-scene transfer and online adaptation results (20% ADE reduction after 100 online steps) suggest the approach could reduce retraining costs in deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale datasets (ETH-UCY, SDD, SNU), and the paper acknowledges failure in dense collision-avoidance scenarios, leaving open whether the approach scales to large-scale autonomous driving benchmarks.
