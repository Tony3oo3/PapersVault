## Problem

Instruction-based image editing (IIE) models have advanced rapidly, but no existing benchmark provides a comprehensive, automated, multi-dimensional evaluation that aligns with human perception. Conventional metrics (CLIP, PSNR, SSIM) are ill-suited for diverse editing types, user studies are costly and non-reproducible, and prior benchmarks (TedBench, EditBench, EditVal) are limited in scope, dimensionality, or applicability to mask-free methods.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 2,000+ input images (sourced from public datasets like COCO, Dense-Haze, and others) paired with 4,000+ text editing instructions in both original and ChatGPT-diversified forms, along with edited images generated by each IIE model. It produces per-dimension scores across 16 evaluation axes (8 high-level, 8 low-level) and 6 content categories (Animal, Object, Scenery, Plant, Human, Global), yielding a full radar-chart profile per model. High-level dimensions are scored via GPT-4V binary/numeric judgments against human-annotated ground-truth answers, while low-level dimensions use SSIM against reference images.

## Method

I²EBench organizes evaluation into 16 fine-grained dimensions split into high-level editing (Counting, Direction Perception, Object Removal, Object Replacement, Background Replacement, Color Alteration, Style Alteration, Region Accuracy) and low-level editing (Deblurring, Haze Removal, Lowlight Enhancement, Noise Removal, Rain Removal, Shadow Removal, Snow Removal, Watermark Removal). For high-level dimensions, GPT-4V is queried with human-annotated question-answer pairs tailored to each dimension (e.g., 'How many cats are on the shoe rack?' → 'One'), while Style Alteration uses CLIP similarity and Region Accuracy uses SSIM on masked regions. Low-level dimensions rely on SSIM against ground-truth restored images. Instructions are diversified via ChatGPT to test robustness to phrasing variation, and a human evaluation protocol (ranking M model outputs per sample) validates that I²EBench scores correlate significantly with human perception across all 16 dimensions.

## Results

I²EBench evaluates 8 IIE models across 16 dimensions (8 high-level, 8 low-level) using 2,000+ images and 4,000+ instructions. No single model dominates all dimensions: InstructDiffusion leads in 4/7 low-level dimensions (e.g., Rain Removal 67.20, Watermark Removal 85.49), MagicBrush tops 3 high-level dimensions (Object Replacement 58.57, Background Replacement 78.57, Color Alteration 55.71), and MGIE leads Deblurring by 11.92 points over the runner-up. Human evaluation confirms significant positive correlation between I²EBench rank scores and human scores across all 16 dimensions, validating the benchmark's alignment with human perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructDiffusion is the strongest low-level editor, leading 4 of 7 low-level dimensions (Lowlight Enhancement 56.61, Noise Removal 28.60, Rain Removal 67.20, Watermark Removal 85.49) and also scoring highest in Object Removal (65.71) and Color Alteration (53.57) among high-level tasks. MagicBrush excels in high-level editing, topping Counting (30.71), Direction Perception (49.29), Background Replacement (78.57), and Color Alteration (55.71), while MGIE dominates Deblurring (60.30, +11.92 over second-place MagicBrush) and Background Replacement (82.86). InstructEdit is a clear outlier: it achieves the best Region Accuracy (77.08) but catastrophically fails on Object Removal (5.04) and Object Replacement (4.41), suggesting its mask-based approach preserves unedited regions well but struggles with content modification. InstructAny2Pix performs best under diverse instructions in 3 high-level dimensions (Direction Perception 51.80, Object Removal 43.88, Style Alteration 25.93), benefiting from its multimodal instruction comprehension. HIVE achieves the highest Object Replacement score (86.43) but is otherwise mid-pack. HQ-Edit consistently ranks near the bottom across most dimensions, with the lowest scores in Haze Removal (39.25), Snow Removal (38.97), and Watermark Removal (29.80). The key takeaway is that no model is robust across all 16 dimensions, and models leveraging LLMs/MLLMs for instruction parsing (InstructAny2Pix, MGIE) show greater stability when instructions are paraphrased.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a response to the limitations of existing IIE evaluation approaches: conventional single metrics (CLIP score, PSNR, SSIM) that cannot capture the diversity of editing types, costly and non-reproducible user studies, and prior benchmarks (TedBench, EditBench, EditVal) that are either too small, mask-dependent, or limited in dimensionality. I²EBench carves new ground by combining GPT-4V-based automated judgment for high-level semantic dimensions with SSIM for low-level restoration dimensions, and by introducing instruction diversification via ChatGPT to test model robustness. It repurposes the MLLM-as-judge paradigm (previously applied to VQA and captioning) into a structured multi-dimensional editing evaluation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, reproducible, and multi-dimensional evaluation protocol for IIE models, and its open-sourcing (instructions, images, annotations, edited outputs, evaluation script) makes it a practical reference for the community. Key open questions include: whether GPT-4V-based evaluation generalizes as newer MLLMs emerge, whether the 16 dimensions adequately capture all editing nuances, and how the benchmark scales as IIE models improve. The finding that no model dominates all dimensions and that LLM/MLLM-based instruction comprehension improves robustness to phrasing variation provides concrete guidance for future model design.
