## Problem

The standard concat-and-chunk approach for LLM pretraining has three interrelated flaws: it forces cross-document attention (wasting compute and enabling spurious modeling), it incurs a fixed quadratic attention cost regardless of actual document length, and it breaks documents at arbitrary chunk boundaries, reducing effective context. Additionally, the distribution and curriculum of sequence lengths during pretraining—a factor that significantly affects final model quality—has been largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corpus of tokenized documents with variable lengths (e.g., RefinedWeb's ~525B tokens from Common Crawl) and decomposes each document into subsequences whose lengths are powers of two, assigning each subsequence to a corresponding bucket. During training, the model consumes variable-length sequences sampled from these buckets, with the total token count per optimization step held constant (e.g., 2^19 tokens). The output is a standard autoregressive transformer LLM (OpenLM-1B/3B/7B) trained with RoPE positional encoding, evaluated on both short-context benchmarks and long-context tasks like multi-document QA.

## Method

Dataset decomposition (DD) reorganizes a document corpus into a union of buckets D_i, where each bucket contains sequences of length 2^i extracted from a single document; a document of length l is split via its binary decomposition into adjacent subsequences of lengths 2^{i_1}, ..., 2^{i_k}. During variable sequence length (VSL) training, each optimization step samples a bucket index i according to a curriculum, then draws b/2^i sequences from D_i, keeping the total tokens per step constant while varying the per-sequence length. A cyclic length-based curriculum (e.g., Grow-P2 with 8 cycles) progressively shifts sampling odds from short to long sequences, mitigating early-training instability from long sequences while ensuring the model still encounters long contexts throughout training. This design eliminates cross-document attention by construction, reduces per-step compute cost when sampling shorter sequences (exploiting attention's quadratic scaling), and exposes the sequence-length distribution as a tunable hyperparameter for optimizing both regular and long-context performance.

## Results

On the RefinedWeb dataset with OpenLM-1B (103B tokens), Dataset Decomposition (DD) with the Grow-P2 curriculum achieves a regular benchmark average of 54.4% and a long-context average of 30.9%, compared to 51.5% and 27.5% for the concat-and-chunk baseline. DD also reduces per-step training time from 304 ms to 244 ms (≈20% speedup) while maintaining the same 8k maximum context length. At scale, DD reaches the baseline's best accuracy up to 6× faster in wall-clock time and demonstrates >4× data efficiency (reaching the same accuracy with less than half the tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard concat-and-chunk baseline (the approach used in Llama-1/2), DD wins on every metric: +2.9 on regular average (54.4 vs 51.5), +3.4 on long-context average (30.9 vs 27.5), and a 20% reduction in per-step time (244 ms vs 304 ms). Against best-fit packing (Ding et al., 2024), DD improves regular average by 1.7 points (54.4 vs 52.7) and long-context average by 3.2 points (30.9 vs 27.7), while also being faster per step; packing still allows cross-document tokens within a sequence, whereas DD guarantees single-document sequences by construction. Against in-context pretraining (ICLM, Shi et al., 2023), DD leads on regular average (54.4 vs 51.7) and long-context average (30.9 vs 28.7), with a notable +2.6 gap on MDQA-30 (24.6 vs 22.0); ICLM edges out DD only on QuALITY (34.6 vs 34.2) but requires a resource-intensive document-similarity retrieval step that does not scale well. Adding document masking to the baseline (Baseline-8k+DM) improves regular metrics slightly (52.4) but actually degrades long-context performance (27.1 vs 27.5), confirming that cross-document attention, while wasteful, provides some incidental long-context signal that DD's curriculum replaces more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant concat-and-chunk paradigm (popularized by RoBERTa, Llama, Llama 2) by decomposing each document into power-of-2 subsequences and training with variable sequence length, rather than forcing all sequences to a fixed target length. It addresses three shortcomings of concat-and-chunk simultaneously—cross-document attention, wasted quadratic attention compute, and boundary truncation—whereas prior remedies (document masking via xformers, best-fit packing by Ding et al., in-context pretraining by Shi et al.) each tackle only a subset. The curriculum-learning component draws on Bengio et al.'s original formulation and the vision analogy of progressive-resolution CLIP training, while the dynamic batching mechanism parallels length-sorted batching in seq-to-seq tasks (speech, NMT), but is applied here to autoregressive LLM pretraining with a length-based sampling schedule over decomposed buckets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× reduction in wall-clock time to reach target accuracy and 4× data efficiency are practically significant for any team running large-scale LLM pretraining, and the method's simplicity (document-level decomposition, no architectural changes) makes it easy to adopt. The finding that the natural length distribution from DD is near-optimal without manual tuning, and that sequence length curriculum materially affects both stability and long-context performance, elevates a previously under-studied design variable to a first-class hyperparameter. The main limitation is that speed gains are only substantial when target sequence length is long enough for attention cost to dominate; for short-context training the benefit shrinks. Open questions include interaction with post-pretraining length-extension techniques (YaRN, PI) and whether the curriculum design generalizes to multimodal or non-autoregressive settings.
