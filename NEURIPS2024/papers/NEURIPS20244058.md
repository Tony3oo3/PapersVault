## Problem

Standard federated learning assumes homogeneous devices, but real-world deployments involve diverse device prototypes ranging from small IoT devices to large workstations with vastly different model and dataset sizes. Existing knowledge distillation methods for heterogeneous FL (e.g., FedDF, FedET) uniformly average logits from all ensembles into a single distillation target, which dilutes informative outputs from larger, more capable devices and applies a one-size-fits-all target to students of varying capacity, leading to suboptimal or even degraded performance for larger prototypes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes locally updated client model parameters (ensembles) from M heterogeneous device prototypes, each with its own architecture (e.g., ResNet-8/14/18, ViT-S, VGG-16 for CV; BERT-Tiny/Mini/Small for NLP) and dataset size, along with an unlabeled public dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 for CIFAR-10, SNLI for MNLI) used as the distillation medium. It produces updated server model parameters for each device prototype, which are then deployed for classification on RGB images (32×32 or 64×64) or text sentences. The merging coefficients λ_i are tuned per student prototype, making the output prototype-specific rather than a single shared model.

## Method

TAKFL replaces uniform logit averaging with two stages. First, it distills each prototype's ensembles independently (treating each as a separate task T_i), combining a KL-divergence ensemble distillation loss with a KD-based self-regularization term that anchors the student to its initial averaged-parameter logits to counteract noise from the unfamiliar public dataset and lack of private-label supervision. Second, it defines a task vector τ_i = θ_distilled^{T_i→S} − θ_avg for each prototype (analogous to fine-tuned minus pre-trained weights in centralized task arithmetic) and merges them via θ_merged = θ_avg + Σ λ_i τ_i, where the coefficients λ_i (summing to 1) are adapted per student to weight each prototype's contribution according to its informativeness and the student's intrinsic capacity. Theoretical results grounded in overparametrized network solution submanifolds show that this approach preserves the student's self-knowledge eigenbasis and allocates capacity efficiently, whereas vanilla ensemble distillation structurally wastes capacity and introduces garbage directions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Dir(0.3)), TAKFL+Reg achieves 60.22% average top-1 accuracy across S/M/L prototypes, outperforming FedAvg (47.36%), FedDF (49.92%), and FedET (52.60%) by 12.9, 10.3, and 7.6 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-100 (Dir(0.3)), TAKFL+Reg reaches 29.15% average accuracy vs. FedAvg's 21.36%. For NLP, TAKFL+Reg achieves 63.49% average on MNLI and 81.54% on SST-2, surpassing FedAvg by 14.1 and 9.4 points respectively. The self-regularization component (TAKFL+Reg vs. TAKFL) provides additional gains of 2.1% on CIFAR-10 and 1.8% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAKFL+Reg consistently outperforms all three baselines across every device prototype size (S, M, L) and both heterogeneity levels on all datasets. Against FedAvg, TAKFL+Reg gains 12.9 points on CIFAR-10 (Dir 0.3) and 7.8 points on CIFAR-100 (Dir 0.3), with the largest absolute improvement on the L prototype (65.69% vs. 59.46% on CIFAR-10). Against FedDF, the advantage is 10.3 points on CIFAR-10 and 4.8 points on CIFAR-100; notably, FedDF degrades the L prototype by ~10% below FedAvg (49.82% vs. 59.46%), confirming the logit dilution problem. Against FedET, TAKFL+Reg leads by 7.6 points on CIFAR-10 and 3.8 points on CIFAR-100; FedET is particularly unreliable on NLP, where it drops below FedAvg on SST-2 (67.52% vs. 72.13%) due to miscalibrated confidence scores in pre-trained language models. On MNLI, TAKFL+Reg achieves 63.49% average vs. FedDF's 60.45% and FedET's 50.72%, demonstrating that uncertainty-weighted averaging (FedET) is especially harmful in heterogeneous settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research threads: (1) ensemble knowledge distillation for heterogeneous federated learning (FedDF, FedET), which uniformly averages or uncertainty-weights logits from all device prototypes, and (2) task arithmetic / model soups from centralized learning (Wortsman et al.), where task vectors encode the parameter delta between pre-trained and fine-tuned models. TAKFL departs from prior KD-based FL by treating each prototype's ensemble distillation as an independent task to avoid logit dilution, adding a self-regularization term to counter noisy unsupervised distillation, and then adaptively merging the separately distilled knowledge via task arithmetic — the first extension of task vectors to the federated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing heterogeneous FL methods assume similar-capability devices, yet real deployments span IoT sensors to workstations with orders-of-magnitude differences in model and data size. The theoretical framework (Propositions 1–2) provides the first formal account of why vanilla ensemble distillation wastes model capacity in cross-size transfers, and the experiments confirm that existing KD methods can actually degrade the largest prototype by up to 10%. Open questions include whether the merging coefficients λ_i can be learned end-to-end rather than tuned manually, how the O(M²) memory scaling behaves at very large M, and whether the approach transfers to truly physical heterogeneous hardware with communication bottlenecks.
