## Problem

Traditional Universal Adversarial Perturbations (UAPs) are generated in closed-set scenarios where the classification task remains fixed between training and testing. In Few-Shot Learning, two shifts—task shift (varying n-way k-shot configurations) and semantic shift (non-overlapping base and novel classes)—cause existing UAP methods to lose at least 25% attack success rate and, in some cases, fail entirely (e.g., for metric-based models that lack a fixed classifier). No unified framework existed for generating transferable UAPs across all FSL paradigms.

## Input / Output

The attacker only has access to the pre-trained encoder (e.g., ResNet-12 or ResNet-18 backbone) and a proxy dataset (CIFAR-FS, mini-ImageNet, or Tiered-ImageNet training split); no knowledge of the base or novel datasets is required. A ResNet-based generator network takes a random vector z as input and outputs a single perturbation δ constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 10 (pixel range 0–255). The resulting UAP is attached to every query image in downstream FSL tasks (e.g., 5-way 1-shot or 5-way 5-shot) and must be visually imperceptible while maximizing misclassification across all task configurations.

## Method

FSAFW addresses the two shifts in a unified pipeline. To handle the task shift, it samples 5-way 1-shot proxy tasks from the proxy dataset and constructs per-task linear classifiers, so the UAP acquires the correct task bias without knowing the exact downstream configuration. To handle the semantic shift, it abandons proxy-label supervision (which would correct the encoder's natural distribution shift toward the base domain) and instead computes class prototypes from each proxy task's support set, using cosine similarity to form a metric-based classification probability. The final fooling loss is L_fool = −log H(f_proto(f_e(x + g_θ(z))), ŷ), where f_proto is the prototype-based predictor and ŷ denotes smoothed labels that further reduce reliance on proxy query annotations. The generator g_θ is optimized with Adam (lr = 0.0002) to produce a single perturbation that transfers across finetuning-based, meta-based, and metric-based FSL models, achieving over 16% higher ASR than prior methods.

## Results

FSAFW achieves an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 81.56% on 5-way 1-shot and 79.00% on 5-way 5-shot tasks (mini-ImageNet victim, CIFAR-FS proxy, ResNet12 backbone, Baseline paradigm), exceeding the strongest prior method (AdvEncoder) by 4.88% and 4.81% respectively. Across all FSL paradigms (finetuning-based, meta-based, metric-based) and both mini-ImageNet and Tiered-ImageNet victim models, FSAFW consistently outperforms UAN, GAP, and AdvEncoder. The paper reports an average improvement of over 16% in ASR compared to the best baseline across the full evaluation suite of 2000 FSL tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSAFW outperforms all four compared methods across every victim model and task configuration. Against UAN (Hayes, SPW 2018), FSAFW gains 29.29% ASR on 5-way 1-shot Baseline (81.56 vs. 52.27) and 32.91% on 5-way 5-shot (79.00 vs. 46.09); UAN is also inapplicable to metric-based and meta-based models that lack a fixed classifier. Against GAP (Poursaeed, CVPR 2018), the margin is even larger at 33.85% on 1-shot Baseline (81.56 vs. 47.71), and GAP similarly cannot attack ProtoNet, DN4, or R2D2. Against the most recent baseline AdvEncoder (Zhou, ICCV 2023), FSAFW leads by 4.88% on 1-shot Baseline (81.56 vs. 76.68) and 4.81% on 5-shot Baseline (79.00 vs. 74.19), with the gap widening to 11.24% on ANIL-1 (77.84 vs. 66.60) and 15.48% on R2D2-1 (70.34 vs. 59.10). Against PAP (Ban, NeurIPS 2022), evaluated in Appendix B with CIFAR-FS proxy, FSAFW surpasses PAP by 37.17% on 1-shot Baseline (81.56 vs. 44.39) and 42.42% on 5-shot Baseline (79.00 vs. 36.58), demonstrating that FSAFW's advantage is not limited to generator-based methods but extends to pre-trained perturbation approaches as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends generator-based UAP generation (GAP, Poursaeed et al. 2018; AdvEncoder, Zhou et al. 2023) from closed-set classification to the open-set Few-Shot Learning setting, where the classifier is not fixed between training and testing. It departs from prior UAP work by identifying two structural shifts—task shift (varying n-way k-shot shapes) and semantic shift (non-overlapping base/novel classes)—that break the assumption of a stable classifier. The solution repurposes ProtoNet's prototype-based classification (Snell et al. 2017) as a classifier-free evaluation mechanism, eliminating the need for a trained head and thereby making the attack applicable to metric-based FSL methods where no fixed classifier exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic benchmark for UAP in FSL, revealing that widely-used methods like GAP and UAN are either significantly degraded or entirely inapplicable (for metric-based models) in this setting. The finding that a trained proxy classifier's supervisory signal actively *hurts* transferability—by correcting the encoder's natural distribution shift toward the base domain—is a useful conceptual insight for open-set adversarial attacks more broadly. Open questions include whether effective defenses exist for FSL against such universal perturbations, and whether the framework extends to other open-set paradigms (e.g., zero-shot or continual learning) beyond the three FSL families tested here.
