## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection (VAD) benchmarks are confined to single scenarios (e.g., one university campus) with limited camera viewpoints and predominantly human-related anomalies, making it impossible to evaluate multi-scenario generalization or robustness to real-world variations in lighting, weather, and object diversity. No prior work systematically assesses how well VAD models adapt to entirely novel scenarios rather than merely novel camera views within a known environment.

## Input / Output

The MSAD dataset provides 720 high-resolution (1920×1080) RGB videos at 30 FPS from static cameras across 14 distinct scenarios, totaling 447,236 frames with an average video length of ~621 frames. Two evaluation protocols are defined: Protocol i (self-supervised) trains on 360 normal videos and tests on 120 normal + 240 abnormal videos with frame-level annotations; Protocol ii (weakly-supervised) trains on 360 normal + 120 abnormal videos with only video-level labels and tests on 120 normal + 120 abnormal videos. The SA²D model consumes video temporal blocks of T' frames via a sliding window and outputs per-frame anomaly scores, evaluated with both Micro-AUC (concatenated frames) and Macro-AUC (per-video averaged).

## Method

The paper introduces the MSAD dataset (14 scenarios, 35 human-related and 20 non-human-related anomaly types) and the SA²D model, which extends FSAD's single-scenario multi-view few-shot framework to a multi-scenario multi-view setting. SA²D uses an N-way K-shot meta-learning formulation (N=7, K=10) where scene information is explicitly incorporated during sampling to form balanced task groups across scenarios, enabling the model to learn scenario-agnostic normal dynamics. The anomaly detection mechanism is future frame prediction: the model takes the first T'−1 frames of a temporal block as input and predicts the last frame, with reconstruction error serving as the anomaly score. The paper further conducts a systematic empirical study of six representative weakly-supervised methods (MIST, RTFM, MSL, UR-DMU, MGFN, TEVAD) across three backbones (C3D, I3D, SwinTransformer) on both existing and the new MSAD benchmark, revealing that I3D outperforms SwinTransformer on MSAD due to its stronger temporal coherence for detecting subtle, temporally localized anomalies.

## Results

On the weakly-supervised evaluation (Protocol ii, Table 4), no single method dominates across all three datasets: TEVAD achieves the best ShanghaiTech AUC (98.10 with I3D), RTFM leads on MSAD (86.65 with I3D), and MSL is best on UCF-Crime (85.62 with SwinTransformer). On the few-shot generalizability evaluation (Protocol i), the proposed SA²D model trained on MSAD outperforms FSAD trained on ShanghaiTech by up to 22.5% Micro AUC on ShanghaiTech cross-view (68.96 vs 55.34 on v1) and by 9.6% Micro AUC on cross-scenario CUHK Avenue (79.57 vs 69.98). The paper also shows that I3D as a backbone generally outperforms SwinTransformer on MSAD, while SwinTransformer is better on UCF-Crime, indicating dataset-dependent backbone sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two categories of baselines. For few-shot adaptation (Protocol i), SA²D consistently outperforms FSAD [46] in both single-scenario cross-view (Table 2: e.g., 68.96 vs 61.36 Micro AUC on ShT-v1) and cross-scenario settings (Table 3: 79.57 vs 69.98 Micro AUC on CUHK Avenue, a 9.6% gain), demonstrating that training on the multi-scenario MSAD dataset yields superior generalization to novel viewpoints and scenarios. For weakly-supervised evaluation (Protocol ii, Table 4), no single method wins across all datasets: TEVAD [13] leads on ShanghaiTech (98.10 I3D AUC), benefiting from text-visual fusion; RTFM [90] is most robust to backbone choice (≤1% gap between I3D and SwinT on MSAD) and achieves the best MSAD score (86.65); MSL [40] is best on UCF-Crime with SwinTransformer (85.62). UR-DMU [129] and MGFN [14] show the largest backbone sensitivity on MSAD (UR-DMU drops from 85.02 to 72.36 between I3D and SwinT), suggesting these methods are less robust to the diverse scenarios and non-human-related anomalies in MSAD. MIST [22] is only evaluated on UCF-Crime and ShanghaiTech, limiting direct comparison on MSAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot scene-adaptive anomaly detection line (FSAD, Lu et al. ECCV 2020; MAML, Finn et al. ICML 2017) from single-scenario multi-view adaptation to a multi-scenario multi-view setting, introducing the SA²D model that uses scene information during N-way K-shot sampling. Conceptually, it departs from prior VAD work by elevating the unit of generalization from 'camera viewpoint' to 'scenario,' arguing that existing benchmarks (ShanghaiTech, UCSD Ped, CUHK Avenue) conflate the two and thus cannot assess true cross-environment robustness. The accompanying MSAD dataset fills a gap identified in the concise review: no prior benchmark combines 14 distinct real-world scenarios, both human- and non-human-related anomalies, and environmental variations (lighting, weather) under a unified evaluation protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSAD is the first large-scale multi-scenario VAD benchmark (720 videos, 35 HRA + 20 NHRA anomalies, ~500 camera views), and its two evaluation protocols (self-supervised and weakly-supervised) provide a practical testing ground that prior single-scenario datasets cannot offer. The cross-dataset finding that MSAD-trained models perform poorly on ShanghaiTech—because ShanghaiTech labels biking and driving as anomalies—raises a broader question about the validity of anomaly definitions in existing benchmarks and may prompt re-annotation efforts. Open questions include extending MSAD to multimodal inputs (audio, text), developing interpretable detectors, and resolving privacy via deepfake-based anonymization, all of which the authors flag as future work.
