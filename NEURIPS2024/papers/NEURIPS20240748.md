## Problem

While learn-to-defer (L2D) systems have been developed to optimize the accuracy of human-AI teams, no general methodology exists for designing such systems under multiple simultaneous constraints (e.g., algorithmic fairness, expert intervention budget, OOD detection, long-tail classification). This paper provides the first unified characterization of the Bayes-optimal solution for multi-objective L2D problems under arbitrary sets of conditional performance measure constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240748/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes input features x, human expert decisions m (treated as a random variable to capture human noise), and demographic group identifiers A, and produces a randomized classifier-rejector pair: a classifier h(x) that predicts a class label and a rejection function r(x) that determines whether to defer to the expert, yielding a final prediction Ŷ = r(x)·M + (1−r(x))·h(x). Crucially, the method operates as a two-stage post-processing pipeline: it first estimates a set of conditional probability scores (e.g., Pr(Y=i|x), Pr(M=1|x)) via standard supervised learning, then applies a closed-form thresholding rule on linear combinations of these scores, with thresholds determined by the constraint tolerances on a validation set.

## Method

The core contribution is the d-dimensional Generalized Neyman-Pearson (d-GNP) theorem, which shows that the optimal randomized classifier and rejection function under m constraints is obtained by thresholding the vector ψ₀(x) − Σᵢ kᵢψᵢ(x), where ψᵢ are embedding functions derived from each constraint's expected value and kᵢ are Lagrange-multiplier-like scalars. The paper first proves that the deterministic version of this problem is NP-hard (via reduction to 0-1 Knapsack), then shows that allowing randomization reduces it to a linear functional program over a simplex, which is exactly an instance of d-GNP. For a single constraint, the threshold k is found by root-finding on a monotone function C(t)−δ, and the randomized tie-breaking probability p is determined in closed form. The plug-in algorithm (Algorithm 1) estimates the embedding functions from training data, then searches for optimal thresholds on a validation set, with generalization guarantees of O(√(log n/n)) for constraints and O((log n/n)^(1/2γ)) for the objective under a (γ,Δ)-sensitivity condition.

## Results

The paper presents results as constraint-violation-vs-accuracy tradeoff curves (Figures 2 and 3) rather than single-point comparisons. On COMPAS, d-GNP achieves lower demographic parity violation than both baselines across the tolerance range, though accuracy may be comparable or slightly lower when constraint violation is not the priority. On Hatespeech, d-GNP successfully bounds demographic disparity for both hate speech (δ=0.1) and offensive (δ=0.01) predictions, with the controlled offensive parity performance matching that of the human expert. On ACSIncome, equalized odds violation bounds are accurately met on test data, and accuracy increases monotonically as bounds are loosened.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares d-GNP against two learn-to-defer baselines on COMPAS: (1) Madras et al. [44], which adds a demographic parity regularizer to the surrogate loss and sweeps over 100 regularizer coefficients, and (2) Mozannar et al. [50], which trains the classifier-rejector pair and then shifts scores to find a new thresholding rule. d-GNP achieves better fairness (lower constraint violation) than both baselines across the tolerance range on COMPAS, though the paper notes this 'might not lead to better accuracy when the constraint violation is not of interest.' On Hatespeech, d-GNP's controlled offensive prediction parity performance 'copies that of human,' suggesting the optimal strategy is to defer offensive tweet predictions to the human when the tweet contains African-American dialect. On ACSIncome, d-GNP accurately meets equalized odds bounds with confidence intervals from bootstrapping, and performance increases as bounds are loosened. The key differentiator is that d-GNP provides a principled post-processing guarantee (Theorem 5.1) that constraints satisfied on validation data generalize to unseen data, which the in-processing baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neyman-Pearson lemma from binary hypothesis testing to a d-dimensional multi-hypothesis setting (d-GNP), building on the multiclass generalization of Tian & Feng [69] and the plug-in generalization analysis of Tong [71]. It departs from prior L2D work (Mozannar & Sontag [50], Madras et al. [44]) by rejecting the staged-learning paradigm—training a classifier first and then a rejection function—and instead jointly optimizes both via a post-processing thresholding rule. The paper also contrasts with Lagrangian/cost-sensitive approaches by showing that d-GNP avoids the saddle-point uniqueness assumptions required by strong duality, and with in-processing methods by proving an impossibility result for their generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The d-GNP framework provides a unified, closed-form solution for multi-objective L2D under arbitrary outcome-dependent constraints (fairness, intervention budget, OOD detection, long-tail), which prior work had only addressed in isolated instances. The impossibility result (Proposition 5.4) showing that in-processing deferral labels cannot generalize is a conceptual contribution that redirects the field toward score-based post-processing. Limitations include the need to accurately estimate embedding functions (conditional probabilities), dependence on sensitivity conditions for objective generalization bounds, and empirical validation on only three relatively small datasets with limited baselines. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional inputs, applicability to sequential/online deferral, and whether the framework extends to settings where the human expert's behavior is itself learned or adaptive.
