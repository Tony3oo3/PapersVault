## Problem

Fine-tuned vision-language models frequently learn spurious correlations between local image features and textual attributes, degrading zero-shot classification on images where the spurious feature is absent or present in the wrong class. Existing approaches for discovering and mitigating such correlations operate at the global image level and are designed for unimodal classifiers, leaving a gap in fine-grained, region-level robustness for the fine-tuned VLM setting where no subgroup or class labels are available in the training data.

## Input / Output

RAVL takes as input a fine-tuned VLM, a labeled zero-shot validation set (images with class labels), region bounding boxes (from RPNs or equal-sized quadrants), and the original image-text fine-tuning pairs; no subgroup annotations are required. Stage 1 outputs a ranked list of region clusters (image features) spuriously correlated with one or more class labels, scored by a cluster performance gap metric. Stage 2 outputs a retrained VLM whose image encoder has been fine-tuned with the region-aware loss to suppress the identified spurious correlations.

## Method

Stage 1 (Discovery) decomposes validation images into candidate regions, extracts region embeddings via RoIAign on the VLM's image encoder, and clusters them with K-Medoids (cosine distance, cluster count selected by Silhouette score). Each cluster is scored by a cluster influence score H_c (fraction of mispredicted images whose highest-scoring region falls in the cluster) and a cluster performance gap G_c (sample-size-weighted accuracy difference between images with and without the cluster's feature); clusters are ranked by G_c and pruned by H_c < 0.25. Stage 2 (Mitigation) introduces a region-aware contrastive loss with two components: L_R^i pulls non-spurious region embeddings toward the correct class label while a penalty term P(R_B^s) pushes spurious region embeddings away from correlated labels, and L_A^i contrasts non-spurious regions against other regions in the batch. The final loss blends this region-aware objective with the original CLIP contrastive loss via a hyperparameter λ (set to 0.8), and only the image encoder is updated during retraining.

## Results

Across 654 controlled evaluation settings, RAVL achieves a mean Precision@10 of 61.8 for spurious correlation discovery, a 191% improvement over the closest baseline (Spurious-Aware Detection at 20.0). As learned correlation strength increases (τ_eval from 10 to 40), RAVL's Precision@10 rises to 91.1 while most baselines degrade. For mitigation, RAVL achieves 39.1% image worst-group accuracy (vs. 33.7% for VL-GDRO and 33.6% for Spurious-Aware) and 57.8% region worst-group accuracy (vs. 50.6% for Spurious-Aware), representing up to 8.2% and 10.8% improvements respectively over the nearest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVL decisively outperforms all five baselines in both discovery and mitigation. In discovery (Table 1), RAVL's Precision@10 of 61.8 at τ_eval=10 exceeds Spurious-Aware Detection [56] (20.0) by 308%, and the global-image-level methods—Distilling Failures [22] (20.1), George [43] (19.3), and Domino [13] (17.1)—all perform at or below the random baseline (21.2), confirming that image-level clustering strategies do not transfer to fine-grained region-level discovery. In mitigation (Table 3), RAVL achieves 39.1% image worst-group accuracy, beating VL-GDRO [39] (33.7%), Spurious-Aware [56] (33.6%), VL-ERM (32.2%), and Standard FT (31.4%); the gap widens to 10.1 points over Spurious-Aware when Stage 1 discovery precision exceeds 0.8. Notably, RAVL also leads on the newly introduced region-level metrics (57.8% region worst-group vs. 50.6% for Spurious-Aware), suggesting it better captures fine-grained feature understanding than prior approaches that operate at the global image level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spurious correlation discovery and mitigation literature from unimodal, global image-level settings (Domino, George, Distilling Failures, GDRO) to the fine-tuned VLM setting at the region level. The closest prior work, Yang et al. (ICML 2023), also targets fine-tuned VLMs but operates at the global image level and relies on an external object detector with human verification; RAVL departs by using the VLM's own embedding space with K-Medoids clustering to identify fine-grained features automatically. The region-aware contrastive loss draws on fine-grained VLM training (FILIP, VILLA) but repurposes region-level information specifically to penalize spurious feature-class associations during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 654-setting controlled evaluation framework with known ground-truth spurious correlations is a valuable resource for the community, and the in-the-wild medical imaging results (metal clips → cardiomegaly, retail signage → fast food restaurant) demonstrate practical diagnostic utility for deployed VLMs. A key limitation is that RAVL only addresses local, region-level spurious features and cannot handle global confounders like image brightness or patient demographics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to spurious correlations that span multiple regions or to settings where region proposals are unavailable, and whether the region-aware loss can be integrated into pretraining rather than requiring a two-stage pipeline.
