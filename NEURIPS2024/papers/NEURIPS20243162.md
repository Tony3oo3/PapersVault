## Problem

Existing unsupervised visible-infrared person re-identification (USVI-ReID) methods rely on cluster-based contrastive learning that uses only the cluster centroid as a prototype, capturing commonality while overlooking divergence (distinctive hard samples) and variety (intrinsic within-cluster diversity). This leads to unreliable pseudo-labels and ambiguous cross-modality correspondences, particularly when encountering hard samples that deviate from the cluster center.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243162/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes visible (RGB) and infrared (IR) pedestrian images resized to 288×144 pixels as input and produces 2048-dimensional identity feature embeddings via a ResNet-based encoder initialized with ImageNet weights. At inference, the momentum encoder extracts features from the global average pooling layer, and cosine similarity is used for cross-modality retrieval. The training pipeline uses DBSCAN to generate pseudo-labels at the start of each epoch, with 16 pseudo-identities × 16 instances per batch, and PGM for cross-modality memory aggregation.

## Method

PCLHD introduces three complementary prototype types for contrastive learning: a centroid prototype (commonality), a hard prototype (divergence, defined as the sample farthest from the cluster center, proven via Theorem 1 to carry higher information entropy), and a dynamic prototype (variety, randomly sampled M=16 instances per cluster where the farthest same-cluster or closest different-cluster sample is selected per query). A progressive learning strategy first trains for 50 epochs with only the centroid prototype, then switches to a weighted combination (λ=0.5) of hard and dynamic prototype contrastive losses to avoid cluster degradation from early unreliable pseudo-labels. The dynamic prototype mechanism uses a momentum encoder (β=0.999) to extract stable representations, while the hard prototype memory is updated via momentum (α=0.1), and both are integrated within the PGM cross-modality matching framework.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All Search), PCLHD achieves 64.4% Rank-1 and 58.7% mAP, outperforming the previous best USVI-ReID method MMM (61.6% Rank-1, 57.9% mAP) by 2.8% and 0.8% respectively. With the MMM cross-modality matching module added (PCLHD+MMM), performance further improves to 65.9% Rank-1 and 61.8% mAP. On RegDB, PCLHD reaches 84.3% Rank-1 / 80.7% mAP (Visible→Thermal) and 82.7% Rank-1 / 78.4% mAP (Thermal→Visible), surpassing all prior USVI-ReID methods by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCLHD outperforms all nine USVI-ReID baselines on both datasets. On SYSU-MM01 All Search, it surpasses the strongest prior method MMM [54] by 2.8% Rank-1 (64.4 vs. 61.6) and 0.8% mAP (58.7 vs. 57.9); with MMM's cross-modality matching integrated, the gap widens to 4.3% Rank-1 and 3.9% mAP. PCLHD also exceeds the best semi-supervised method DPIS [18] (58.4% Rank-1) by 6.0% without using any annotations, and beats several fully-supervised methods including DDAG [39] (54.8%), AGW [58] (47.5%), NFS [59] (56.9%), and LbA [60] (55.4%). However, PCLHD still trails the top fully-supervised methods such as SAAI [13] (75.9%), FDNM [67] (77.8%), and PartMix [64] (77.8%) by roughly 11–13% Rank-1, acknowledging the remaining gap between unsupervised and supervised settings. On RegDB, PCLHD+MMM achieves 89.6% Rank-1 (V→T) and 87.0% Rank-1 (T→V), surpassing MMM by 4.3% and 4.1% respectively, while the standalone PCLHD (84.3% / 82.7%) already exceeds all other USVI-ReID methods by at least 5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cluster-based contrastive learning paradigm for USVI-ReID (established by PGM, GUR, and MMM) by rethinking the prototype design. Prior methods uniformly use the cluster centroid as the sole prototype, which the authors argue captures only 'commonality' while ignoring intra-identity divergence and variety. The hard prototype (farthest sample from centroid) draws on the hard-negative mining intuition from multi-proxy ReID work (DPM, DPM-inspired dynamic selection), while the dynamic prototype adapts the MoCo-style momentum encoder to sample-level prototype selection. The progressive scheduling—delaying hard/dynamic prototypes until after initial centroid-based training—departs from prior work that applies all contrastive objectives simultaneously, motivated by the observation that early pseudo-labels are too noisy for hard-sample-driven learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple re-design of the prototype in contrastive learning (adding one hard and one dynamic prototype with progressive scheduling) yields substantial gains in USVI-ReID, closing much of the gap to semi-supervised methods without any annotations. The entropy-based argument for why hard prototypes preserve more divergence, while intuitive, is not rigorously proven and the practical impact of the progressive schedule is modest (~1% over simultaneous use). A key open limitation is the reliance on DBSCAN for pseudo-label generation, which the authors acknowledge may not scale to very large datasets; exploring scalable clustering or learned pseudo-labeling remains an important direction. The prototype-design philosophy (commonality + divergence + variety) could potentially transfer to other unsupervised contrastive learning tasks beyond ReID, though the paper does not explore this.
