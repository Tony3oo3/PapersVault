## Problem

Existing audio-visual (AV) representation learning methods assume that paired audio and video data are well-aligned and synchronized, yet in practice audio signals often contain background noise unrelated to the visual content and temporal non-synchronization between capture and microphone causes misalignment. This data-quality issue is typically overlooked in favor of method-centric improvements, limiting the quality of learned joint representations and degrading downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241875/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw AV pairs as input, where audio is converted to 128×128 log spectrograms (128 frequency bands over 128 timesteps, 50 ms windows, 25 ms hop, 8 kHz sample rate, 3 s clips) and video frames are resized to 224×224. It produces an aligned audio signal modified through up to 8 predefined editing actions, along with quantitative AV alignment and temporal synchronization scores computed via ImageBind and X-CLIP. The aligned pairs are then consumed by standard MAE-based AV representation learning pipelines for downstream tasks.

## Method

AVAgent is an LLM-based agent (Vicuna-v1.5-7b fine-tuned with LoRA) that operates a cyclic three-step agentic workflow: (1) Tool use — a multi-modal LLM independently converts audio and video into separate language descriptions, enabling discrepancy detection that joint encoding would mask; (2) Planning — the LLM reasons over the two descriptions and selects one of 8 predefined audio editing actions (4 noise-filtering: spectral subtraction, Wiener filtering, wavelet denoising, spectral gating; 4 coordination: speed, pitch, volume modification, and blank filling); (3) Reflection — a VLM evaluates the modified audio against the video, producing alignment and synchronization scores that feed back into the next planning cycle. Crucially, each new cycle's actions are applied to the original input pair rather than the previously modified signal, preventing accumulated errors. The resulting aligned AV data is then used to pre-train a masked autoencoder for joint representation learning, evaluated across classification, localization, separation, and segmentation tasks.

## Results

On audio-visual classification, AVAGENT achieves 64.57% linear probing and 71.57% fine-tuning accuracy on VGGSound-Music, 61.56%/69.24% on VGGSound-All, and 47.52%/55.85% on AudioSet, outperforming the strongest baseline MAViL by 2.62–4.20% (linear) and 2.04–2.47% (fine-tune). For sound source localization on Flickr-SoundNet, it reaches 58.23 Precision, 60.76 AP, and 65.03 F1, surpassing Mix-and-Localize by 2.40/2.55/2.51 points. On AVSBench segmentation, AVAGENT attains 36.37 mIoU and 49.85 F1, exceeding the prior best by 4.68 mIoU and 4.50 F1. For sound source separation, it achieves 8.82 SDR / 11.72 SAR on MUSIC and 5.26 SDR / 5.28 SAR on VGGSound-Music, beating OneAVM by 1.44 SDR on MUSIC and 2.75 SDR on VGGSound-Music.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVAGENT outperforms all 20 baselines across every task and dataset. In classification (Table 2), it surpasses the strongest prior method MAViL [62] by 2.62% linear / 2.04% fine-tune on VGGSound-Music, 4.20% / 2.07% on VGGSound-All, and 3.90% / 2.47% on AudioSet, while single-modality baselines MAE [59] and AudioMAE [60] lag by 39–46 points in linear probing. For localization and segmentation (Table 3), AVAGENT beats the previous state-of-the-art Mix-and-Localize [56] by 2.40 Precision / 2.55 AP / 2.51 F1 on Flickr-SoundNet and by 4.68 mIoU / 4.50 F1 on AVSBench; SLAVC [57] and EZVSL [64] trail by 2.58 and 3.52 F1 respectively on Flickr-SoundNet. In source separation (Table 4), AVAGENT exceeds OneAVM [17] by 1.44 SDR / 4.24 SAR on MUSIC, 1.36 SDR / 1.33 SAR on VGGSound-Instruments, and 2.75 SDR / 2.67 SAR on VGGSound-Music, while traditional methods NMF [70] and RPCA [71] remain far behind with negative or near-zero SDR. The random-actions ablation (Table 5) confirms that the LLM-guided planning is essential: random modifications yield only 50.26% linear accuracy on VGGSound-All versus 61.56% for AVAGENT, a 11.3-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant method-centric paradigm in audio-visual representation learning (SoundNet, CAV-MAE, MAViL, AV-MAE), which assumes input AV pairs are well-aligned and focuses on architectural or loss-function innovations. Instead, it repurposes the LLM-agent literature (tool use, planning, reflection cycles as in AesopAgent and VideoAgent) for a data-centric preprocessing step: an LLM-based agent (AVAgent, built on Vicuna-v1.5-7b with LoRA) adaptively edits audio signals via 8 predefined actions (noise filtering, speed/pitch modification, etc.) before they enter a standard MAE-based representation learner. The VLM-based reflection loop (using ImageBind and X-CLIP scores) closes the agentic cycle, a design not previously applied to AV data curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that data alignment quality is an underappreciated bottleneck in AV representation learning, and that an LLM-driven agentic workflow can address it without modifying downstream architectures. However, the approach's practical adoption is constrained by its per-pair computational cost (mLLM captioning + LLM planning + VLM scoring per iteration), its dependence on input quality (poorly captured audio/video degrades the agent's own perception), and unverified scalability to extremely noisy or acoustically complex real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the 8-action space is sufficient for all misalignment types, whether the iterative loop converges reliably, and whether the gains persist when the downstream model is itself weak or when data volume is very small.
