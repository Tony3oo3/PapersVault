## Problem

The Empirical Fisher (EF) approximation, widely used as a pre-conditioner in approximate Natural Gradient Descent optimisers, suffers from an 'inversely-scaled projection issue' that enforces equal loss reduction on every training sample regardless of its convergence level, biasing updates toward already-well-trained samples and degrading approximation quality to exact NG updates. Additionally, no efficient evaluation framework existed to quantitatively compare approximate Fisher pre-conditioners in large-scale deep learning setups, limiting understanding of these methods in practical tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the per-sample gradient Jacobian ∇_θ l ∈ R^{N×P} and a vector s_iEF of per-sample logits-level gradient norms (‖∇_{z_n} l_n‖²) collected during a single back-propagation pass. The logits gradient norms require a minor non-standard step (calling retain_grad() on the logits tensor in PyTorch) but add negligible overhead. The output is the iEF Fisher matrix F* = ∇_θ l^T diag(s_iEF)^{-1} ∇_θ l and the corresponding parameter update, with O(MP) memory complexity that restricts direct use to small trainable parameter sizes (e.g., PEFT with LoRA or Prompt-Tuning).

## Method

The iEF method introduces a diagonal scaling matrix diag(s_iEF)^{-1} into the standard EF pre-conditioner, where each entry of s_iEF is the squared L2 norm of the per-sample gradient at the model output logits level. This scaling is motivated by the Gauss-Newton algorithm: it makes the induced per-sample loss reduction proportional to ‖∇_{z_n} l_n‖², so better-trained samples (with smaller logits gradients) receive proportionally smaller updates, directly resolving the inversely-scaled projection issue. The paper proves sub-linear global convergence for softmax + cross-entropy objectives and linear convergence for m-strongly convex objectives under a full-rank Gram matrix assumption. A novel empirical evaluation framework is also proposed, built around the indicator γ(Δθ) = (Δθ^T F Δθ)^{1/2} / |Δθ^T ∇_θ L(θ)|, which measures direction-wise approximation quality to exact NG updates using only a Fisher matrix-vector product, enabling efficient comparison of EF, iEF, and sampled Fisher methods in large-scale PEFT setups (T5-base with LoRA/Prompt-Tuning on GLUE, ViT with LoRA on CIFAR-100).

## Results

On GLUE+T5+Prompt Tuning, iEF achieves 79.3 average test score, outperforming Adafactor (77.1) and SGD (67.4). On CIFAR100+ViT+LoRA, iEF reaches 94.3% test accuracy, surpassing AdamW (93.9%) and SF (92.8%). On GLUE+T5+LoRA, iEF (79.3) is competitive with AdamW (80.1) while EF collapses to 63.1. Across all 15 tasks, iEF achieves the best generalisation on 10 out of 15 and consistently reaches the lowest final training loss, with the ranking iEF < AdamW/Adafactor < SGD < SF < EF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, iEF is competitive on LoRA tasks (79.3 vs 80.1 on GLUE+T5+LoRA, with each method winning 4 out of 8 individual tasks) but clearly wins on CIFAR100+ViT+LoRA (94.3 vs 93.9). Against Adafactor, iEF outperforms it on 6 out of 7 Prompt Tuning tasks (79.3 vs 77.1 average) and on CIFAR100. Against SGD, iEF provides a substantial improvement across all setups (e.g., 79.3 vs 67.4 on Prompt Tuning, 94.3 vs 91.3 on CIFAR100), while EF actually performs worse than SGD on most tasks (48.1 vs 67.4 on Prompt Tuning, 31.0 vs 91.3 on CIFAR100). In the block-diagonal K-FAC comparison (Fig. 5), iEF-based ieKFAC achieves approximation quality close to exact iEF and substantially better than both KFAC (sampled Fisher block-diagonal) and eKFAC (empirical Fisher block-diagonal) across training stages, confirming that the iEF rescaling transfers effectively to structured approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the natural gradient descent framework of Martens (2020) and the convergence analysis of Zhang et al. (2019), extending the latter to non-regression models with cross-entropy objectives. The key departure is identifying a specific, named pathology of the empirical Fisher (EF) approximation—the 'inversely-scaled projection issue'—and proposing a minimal fix (a diagonal scaling vector derived from logits-level gradient norms) that preserves EF's implementation convenience. The theoretical motivation draws on the Gauss-Newton algorithm as a generalised NGD method (Nurbekyan et al., 2023), showing iEF is equivalent to GN for regression models and to exact NGD for least-squares problems. The paper also introduces a novel empirical evaluation framework that enables large-scale comparison of approximate Fisher pre-conditioners, addressing a gap left by prior small-scale analyses (Kunstner et al., 2019; Thomas et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iEF method is a drop-in replacement for EF in any existing approximate NGD optimiser (K-FAC, WoodFisher, etc.), and its robustness to damping removes a major practical pain point in tuning second-order optimisers. The evaluation framework is broadly useful for the community to compare Fisher approximations at scale without computing exact Fisher matrices. Key limitations include the O(MP) memory cost of exact iEF (restricting it to PEFT or small models) and the lack of proper integration with momentum and regularisation, which likely explains why iEF does not consistently beat AdamW on LoRA tasks with larger trainable parameter counts. Open questions include whether the iEF scaling can be effectively incorporated into block-diagonal or factored approximations at full scale, and whether the convergence guarantees extend to stochastic mini-batch settings.
