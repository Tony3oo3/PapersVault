## Problem

Molecular relational learning (MRL) involves a vast combinatorial model space, yet the only existing dynamic MRL library (DeepPurpose) is limited to protein-sequence inputs and a rigid dual-encoder-plus-MLP architecture. This prevents fair benchmarking of modern MRL models that use 3D protein structures, multiple encoders per molecule, and sophisticated interaction layers such as cross-attention or bilinear fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241117/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlexMol accepts pairs of molecular entities where each entity can be represented by multiple modalities simultaneously: drugs as SMILES strings (yielding sequence tokens, 2D molecular graphs, or 3D conformations) and proteins as amino acid sequences and/or 3D PDB structures (yielding residue-level graphs). The output is a single interaction score that can be a binary label (binding yes/no), a continuous affinity value, or a categorical interaction type, depending on the task (DTI, DDI, or PPI). Unlike prior frameworks, a single molecule may be encoded by several encoders in parallel (e.g., both a GCN and a Morgan fingerprint for the same drug), with their embeddings fused via interaction layers before the final prediction head.

## Method

FlexMol is a composable toolkit rather than a single architecture. Each encoder follows a two-stage design: a Featurizer performs preprocessing (tokenization, fingerprinting, graph construction) and an Encode Layer produces a fixed-dimensional embedding; 16 drug encoders, 13 protein-sequence encoders, and 9 protein-structure encoders are provided as presets. Seven interaction layers (Bilinear Attention, Self/Cross Attention, Highway, Gated Fusion, Bilinear Fusion, Concatenation) can be placed between any pair of encoder outputs or between an encoder and another interaction layer, enabling arbitrary multi-encoder, multi-interaction topologies. The framework imposes no upper bound on the number of encoders or interaction layers, yielding over 71,000 distinct DTI model configurations even with only four encoders and no explicit interaction layer. A unified Trainer handles data loading (with TDC integration), 21 evaluation metrics spanning regression, binary, and multi-class classification, and early stopping, so that all model variants are compared under identical protocols.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset, the best FlexMol configuration (Experiment 1.9: GCN drug encoder + ESM-GCN protein encoder) achieves ROC-AUC 0.916±0.002 and PR-AUC 0.408±0.016, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DProt-DTA (0.914±0.005, 0.395±0.007). On BIOSNAP, the same model reaches ROC-AUC 0.913±0.001 and PR-AUC 0.909±0.001, surpassing 3DProt-DTA (0.891±0.004, 0.901±0.014). The custom model from Experiment #2 (adding PocketDC encoder and self-attention) further improves to ROC-AUC 0.926±0.005 / PR-AUC 0.427±0.014 on DAVIS and ROC-AUC 0.920±0.004 / PR-AUC 0.919±0.003 on BIOSNAP, setting the best results across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexMol's best preset model (1.9: GCN + ESM-GCN) outperforms all seven baselines on both DAVIS and BIOSNAP across both ROC-AUC and PR-AUC. Against the strongest baseline 3DProt-DTA [46], FlexMol 1.9 gains +0.002 ROC-AUC and +0.013 PR-AUC on DAVIS, and +0.022 ROC-AUC and +0.008 PR-AUC on BIOSNAP. Compared to MolTrans [16], the improvement is more substantial: +0.009 ROC-AUC and +0.004 PR-AUC on DAVIS, and +0.018 ROC-AUC and +0.008 PR-AUC on BIOSNAP. The gap over earlier baselines is larger still — DeepDTA [60] (0.880/0.302 on DAVIS), DeepConv-DTI [22] (0.884/0.299), DeepDTI [47] (0.861/0.231), GNN-CPI [43] (0.840/0.269), and LR [2] (0.835/0.232) all trail FlexMol 1.9 by 0.03–0.08 ROC-AUC on DAVIS. The custom model from Experiment #2 extends these margins, achieving the highest scores in all four metric-dataset combinations (e.g., 0.926 ROC-AUC on DAVIS vs. 0.914 for 3DProt-DTA and 0.907 for MolTrans).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexMol positions itself as a direct successor to DeepPurpose, the only prior MRL library supporting dynamic model construction, while addressing its two key limitations: restricted input types (protein sequences only, no 3D structures) and rigid dual-encoder-plus-MLP architectures. It also complements broader platforms like TDC (which provides datasets but not MRL-specific model benchmarking) and DGL-LifeSci (which offers graph-learning building blocks without MRL task integration). Rather than proposing a new model architecture, FlexMol repurposes and unifies 45 existing encoder methods and 7 interaction layers from the literature into a single composable framework, enabling the construction of over 70,000 distinct model combinations for DTI, DDI, and PPI tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary value of FlexMol is practical standardization: it eliminates repetitive coding for MRL experiments and enables fair, apples-to-apples comparison of model architectures under identical training protocols, which has been a persistent pain point in the field. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that it does not perform an exhaustive search over the 70,000+ model space, leaving the question of which architectural combinations are optimal largely open for the community. Future extensions to single-instance tasks (molecular property prediction) and incorporation of uncertainty quantification could broaden its utility, but the current scope is confined to pairwise relational tasks.
