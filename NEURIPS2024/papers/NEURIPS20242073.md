## Problem

Standard semi-supervised learning methods assume labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, but real-world unlabeled data often contains irrelevant (OOD) classes that degrade performance. Existing open-set SSL approaches filter out OOD instances, discarding valuable diversity, invariance, and regularity present in those samples. The paper asks whether a generative model can exploit the diversity of unlabeled data—including OOD instances—to produce effective synthetic labeled data while resolving the distribution mismatch.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled image set with class labels and a larger unlabeled image set that may contain classes absent from the labeled set (mismatch ratio ζ ranging from 25% to 100%). It operates in the latent space of a pretrained VAE (downsampling factor f=2 for CIFAR-scale, f=8 for ImageNet-scale). In DWD-SL mode, it outputs synthetic images paired with class labels to augment the labeled set for supervised training; in DWD-UT mode, it outputs transformed images with class conditions discarded, replacing the original unlabeled set for downstream SSL training. One synthetic image is generated per unlabeled image by default, though scaling to 2× was tested.

## Method

DWD trains a class-conditional latent diffusion model with a combined objective: conditional denoising on labeled data plus unconditional denoising on unlabeled data, where each unlabeled sample is weighted by an importance score derived from a PU-learning discriminator. The discriminator is trained to distinguish in-distribution (labeled-class) samples from OOD samples, and its output yields an unbiased importance weight w(x) = d(x) / [μ·d(x) + (1−μ)(1−d(x))] that downweights irrelevant unlabeled instances during diffusion fine-tuning. For generation, rather than sampling from pure Gaussian noise, the reverse process is seeded with a moderately noised version of an unlabeled image (t=600 out of 1000 steps) and a randomly selected class condition, which transforms OOD instances into in-distribution samples while preserving structural characteristics like outline, color arrangement, and shape from the original. This noised-seed mechanism, inspired by SDEdit, is what enables the diffusion model to harvest diversity from irrelevant unlabeled data rather than merely replicating the limited labeled set.

## Results

DWD-SL achieves the best accuracy across all four evaluation tasks: 85.86% on SixAnimal (ζ=75%), 80.05% on CIFAR-10/100, 82.20% on ImageNet-30, and 82.81% on ImageNet-100. The most dramatic improvement is on ImageNet-100, where DWD-SL (82.81%) outperforms the strongest baseline IOMatch (66.85%) by 15.96 points. DWD-UT further boosts standard SSL methods by 4–26 points depending on the task and baseline (e.g., MPL on SixAnimal improves from 65.62% to 83.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWD-SL outperforms all six SSL baselines on every task. Against standard SSL methods, the gains are largest for MPL (e.g., +20.24 pts on SixAnimal, +11.10 on CIFAR-10/100) and moderate for FixMatch (e.g., +3.36 on SixAnimal, +1.14 on CIFAR-10/100). Against open-set SSL methods, DWD-SL edges out Fix-A-Step by 0.52 pts on SixAnimal and 2.53 pts on ImageNet-30, and surpasses IOMatch by 2.81 pts on SixAnimal and 15.96 pts on ImageNet-100. In the dedicated diffusion-based augmentation comparison (Table 6, ImageNet-30), DWD-SL (82.20%) beats DPT (78.28%) by 3.92 pts and DA-Fusion (75.26%) by 6.94 pts, attributed to DPT's confirmation bias on OOD pseudo-labels and DA-Fusion's limited augmentation to subtle visual details only.

## Relation to Prior Work

DWD sits at the intersection of three research threads: conditional diffusion models (DDPM, latent diffusion), PU learning for importance-weighted sampling, and SDEdit-style noised-real-image seeding for guided generation. It departs sharply from the dominant 'detect-and-filter' paradigm in open-set SSL (OpenMatch, Fix-A-Step, IOMatch), which treats OOD instances as fundamentally harmful and discards them. Instead, DWD repurposes OOD instances as seeds for a class-conditional reverse diffusion process, converting them into in-distribution samples while preserving their structural diversity. Compared to prior diffusion-based augmentation methods (DPT, DA-Fusion), DWD is the first to explicitly address class distribution mismatch during diffusion training via a PU-learned discriminator, rather than assuming the standard SSL distributional identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is an orthogonal, plug-in data transformation step that can be combined with any existing SSL method (standard or open-set) to mitigate class distribution mismatch without modifying the SSL algorithm itself. The finding that DWD-UT frequently outperforms DWD-SL suggests a synergistic interaction with SSL methods' internal data-selection mechanisms (thresholding, weighting), opening the question of how best to co-design generative augmentation with downstream SSL pipelines. Limitations include the additional computational cost of diffusion model training and sampling, the fixed noise level (t=600) that may not be optimal per-sample, and evaluation primarily on relatively small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-30/100) rather than full ImageNet. Whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or very large-scale unlabeled pools with extreme OOD ratios remains open.
