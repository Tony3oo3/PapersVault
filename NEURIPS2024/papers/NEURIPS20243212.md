## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods condition solely on visual frames, offering no mechanism to steer the generated sound toward a desired context or semantic detail (e.g., distinguishing a calm meow from an aggressive one in a cat fight). Text-to-audio models provide semantic control but lack alignment with visual dynamics. No prior framework unifies text-guided video-to-audio generation with the ability to suggest what audio a video should have via captioning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes silent video frames sampled at 5 fps (50 frames per 10 s clip), encoded by eva-CLIP-L into 768-dim mean-pooled vectors per frame, plus an optional free-form text prompt (an audio caption or a template instruction). It produces a 10-second 16 kHz audio waveform decoded from a 4×500 Encodec token matrix, and optionally a natural-language audio caption describing the generated or inferred sound. The video features are projected into the LLM's embedding space (4096-dim for LLaMA-7B, 2048-dim for Gemma-2B) before conditioning the audio decoder.

## Method

VATT is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (VATT Converter) fine-tunes an instruction-tuned LLM with LoRA and a learnable linear projection that maps frame-level visual features into the LLM's text embedding space, trained to generate audio captions from video. Stage 2 (VATT Audio) is a 24-layer bi-directional transformer decoder that generates audio tokens via masked token modeling: the LLM's last-layer hidden states (conditioning) are concatenated along the temporal axis with the masked audio token embeddings, and joint self-attention fuses them—replacing the conventional interleaved cross-attention design that the authors find insufficient for fine-grained audio-visual correspondence. Four parallel linear heads classify tokens at each Encodec codebook level. Training uses a truncated Gaussian masking ratio (mean 0.75, std 0.25, clipped to [0.5, 1.0]) and classifier-free guidance; inference employs iterative parallel decoding with cosine re-masking schedules and a Gumbel-top-k sampling trick, resolving the full 10 s waveform in 10–20 steps—roughly an order of magnitude faster than autoregressive or diffusion baselines.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, VATT-Gemma (without text prompt) achieves the best KLD of 2.25 and Align Accuracy of 82.81% among all non-text-guided methods, while VATT-LLama-T (with ground-truth audio caption) achieves the overall lowest KLD of 1.41, surpassing all video-to-audio and text-to-audio baselines by a significant margin. VATT also generates audio an order of magnitude faster than most baselines (0.65–1.2 s per 10 s sample vs. 4.4–289.5 s). In the text-to-audio comparison (Table 2), VATT-LLama-T outperforms AudioGen and AudioLDM-2 on KLD (1.41 vs. 2.19/2.09) and Align Accuracy (80.16% vs. 48.96%/51.84%) while maintaining competitive FAD and CLAP scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VATT-Gemma outperforms all five video-to-audio baselines on KLD (2.25 vs. 2.54–3.78) and Align Accuracy (82.81% vs. 48.79–82.47%), and is 6–445× faster than every baseline. The sole exception is FAD, where V2A-Mapper achieves a substantially lower score (0.99 vs. 2.35), attributed to its diffusion-based optimization on a large pretrained text-to-audio model. When guided by ground-truth audio captions, VATT-LLama-T achieves a KLD of 1.41, beating AudioGen (2.19) and AudioLDM-2 (2.09) by 0.78 and 0.68 respectively, while also surpassing them on Align Accuracy (80.16% vs. 48.96%/51.84%). On CLAP score (text-audio alignment), AudioGen leads (0.409) over VATT-LLama-T (0.347) and AudioLDM-2 (0.326), indicating that pure text-to-audio models still have a slight edge in following text prompts in isolation. In the video-to-audio captioning task (Table 3), VATT Converter-LLama outperforms LLAVA and Video-LLAMA zero-shot baselines on all NLG metrics (e.g., CIDEr 1.354 vs. 0.026–0.095) and CLAP score (0.263 vs. 0.182–0.213), approaching the LTU ground-truth caption CLAP of 0.379.

## Relation to Prior Work

VATT sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: video-to-audio generation (SpecVQGAN, FoleyGen, Diff-Foley), text-to-audio generation via masked token modeling (SoundStorm, MaskGIT), and multi-modal LLMs (LLaVA, Video-LLaMA, LTU). It departs from prior video-to-audio methods by inserting an instruction-tuned LLM as a multi-modal encoder that fuses visual and textual features before a bi-directional masked-token audio decoder, rather than conditioning the decoder directly on raw visual features. It also departs from prior text-to-audio models by grounding generation in actual video frames rather than text alone, and from prior MLLMs by producing end-to-end audio waveforms rather than textual understanding. The unified framework that simultaneously performs text-guided audio generation and audio captioning in a single model is the paper's primary architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the order-of-magnitude speed advantage (0.65–1.2 s per 10 s clip vs. 4.4–289.5 s for baselines) combined with the ability to steer generation via text, which prior video-to-audio methods lack entirely. The large KLD drop from 2.39 to 1.41 when ground-truth captions are provided demonstrates that text guidance can substantially close the gap between generated and target audio semantics. However, the self-prompting ablation reveals that the model's own generated captions do not yet improve over no prompt, indicating that caption quality remains a bottleneck. Open questions include whether conversational/iterative refinement can close this gap, how the model generalizes across diverse text styles, and whether the fidelity gap versus diffusion-based methods (e.g., V2A-Mapper) can be narrowed without sacrificing speed.
