## Problem

Existing online label shift adaptation methods (e.g., ROGD, FTH, FLHFTL, UOGD, ATLAS) only update the final linear layer or post-hoc reweighting vector of a pre-trained classifier, leaving the feature extractor frozen throughout the online test phase. This ignores the potential for self-supervised learning to refine feature representations using the abundant unlabeled test samples, which is especially critical under generalized label shift where the conditional distribution P(x|y) changes and an unknown feature mapping h must be learned.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives a small batch of unlabeled test images (B=10 in experiments) from a potentially shifted distribution, along with access to a fixed labeled training set D_0 and validation set D_0'. The output at each step is a class probability vector for the next batch, produced by combining the updated feature extractor with either a reweighting vector (for ROGD/FTH/FLHFTL) or an updated linear layer (for UOGD/ATLAS). The feature extractor is updated every τ steps (default τ=100) using accumulated unlabeled batches, while the OLS step runs at every time step.

## Method

OLS-OFU integrates a self-supervised learning loss (rotation prediction, entropy minimization, or MoCo) into any existing OLS algorithm through three design principles: (1) the feature extractor update must occur AFTER the OLS step at each time step to preserve the unbiasedness of the gradient estimator s_t, since f_t must be independent of S_t; (2) after updating the feature extractor, the final linear layer is retrained from scratch on the original training data D_0 to maintain the assumption that the base model approximates P_train(y|x), followed by temperature calibration on D_0'; (3) batch accumulation over τ steps is used to provide sufficient sample size for effective SSL and to amortize the heavy computational cost of feature extractor updates. Theoretically, OLS-OFU maintains the same regret convergence rates as the underlying OLS method while the first term in the bound (loss with known label distribution) can be strictly reduced by SSL, yielding a tighter overall guarantee.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with sinusoidal label shift (T=1000, B=10), OLS-OFU with τ=100 and rotation degree prediction SSL reduces average error substantially across all six OLS baselines: FTFWH drops from 11.9% to 7.33%, FTH from 12.04% to 8.0%, ROGD from 13.65% to 10.8%, ATLAS from 12.18% to 10.1%, UOGD from 11.54% to 8.35%, and FLHFTL from 12.02% to 7.45%. The paper highlights that the improvement from adding online feature updates is as large as the improvement from the OLS method itself over the no-adaptation baseline. On CIFAR-10C (generalized label shift), OLS-OFU similarly outperforms all OLS counterparts across three corruption types (Gaussian noise, Fog, Pixelate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OLS-OFU against all six existing online label shift methods from three prior works. Against Wu et al. (NeurIPS 2021) methods FTFWH, FTH, and ROGD, OLS-OFU (τ=100) reduces error by 4.57, 4.04, and 2.85 percentage points respectively on CIFAR-10. Against Bai et al. (NeurIPS 2022) methods UOGD and ATLAS, OLS-OFU reduces error by 3.19 and 2.08 percentage points. Against Baby et al. (NeurIPS 2023) method FLHFTL, OLS-OFU reduces error by 4.57 percentage points. The improvement is consistent across all datasets (CIFAR-10, STL10, CINIC, EuroSAT), all three SSL techniques (rotation prediction, entropy minimization, MoCo), both shift patterns (sinusoidal and Bernoulli), and the generalized label shift setting (CIFAR-10C). The paper emphasizes that the gain from adding online feature updates is comparable in magnitude to the gain from the OLS method itself over the no-adaptation baseline, and that OLS-OFU outperforms OLS in every single comparison tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: online label shift adaptation (OLS), which updates only the final linear layer or reweighting vector while freezing the feature extractor (Wu et al. 2021; Bai et al. 2022; Baby et al. 2023), and test-time training (TTT), which uses self-supervised losses to update feature extractors on unlabeled data but targets a fixed domain shift rather than continuous online drift (Sun et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2020; Niu et al. 2022). The key departure is inserting SSL-based feature updates into the OLS pipeline while preserving the unbiased gradient estimators that underpin OLS theoretical guarantees, requiring a specific ordering (feature update after OLS step) and batch accumulation to maintain both correctness and SSL effectiveness. The generalized label shift setting (Tachet des Combes et al. 2020) motivates the feature updates as a way to progressively approximate the unknown feature map h.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SSL feature updates yield improvements as large as the OLS method itself (e.g., ~12% → ~7% error on CIFAR-10) and do so consistently across all six OLS methods and three SSL techniques suggests that feature representation quality is a first-order bottleneck in online adaptation, not just the label distribution estimation. Practical limitations include the need to store training data for linear layer retraining (though 0% storage still helps) and the additional compute cost (mitigated by batch accumulation with τ=100). Open questions include extending the framework to online covariate shift, handling time-varying or more severe domain shifts (e.g., cartoon → realistic), and whether the theoretical bound for UOGD-OFU/ATLAS-OFU (which has a larger V_{T,ℓ} due to feature extractor changes) can be tightened.
