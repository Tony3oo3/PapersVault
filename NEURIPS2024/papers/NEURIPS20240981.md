## Problem

Learning generalist robot manipulation policies is bottlenecked by the scarcity of action-labeled robotic data, while abundant actionless human videos remain underutilized due to the human-robot domain gap and the noisy, multimodal structure of in-the-wild video. Prior approaches (contrastive representations, trajectory extraction, reward pre-training) capture static visual features but fail to model the dynamic rules and long-term behavior patterns needed for multi-task embodied control.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4-frame historical video clips (260×260 RGB for Meta-World; 4-view RGB for RLBench) encoded into discrete VQ-VAE tokens (2048-codebook, 256-dim embeddings, 64× spatiotemporal downsampling), a CLIP-encoded language instruction, and a diffusion timestep. It outputs a predicted future video clip (M=1, h=20 steps ahead) and a discretized action sequence of H=4 steps (48 bins per dimension for Meta-World; 360 position bins + 1° Euler-angle bins + binary gripper for RLBench). Only the first action in the predicted sequence is executed per environment step.

## Method

VPDD formulates both video prediction and action learning as a single unified discrete diffusion problem with a mask-and-replace transition matrix that jointly handles video tokens (J=2048) and action tokens (J*) in one state space. A VQ-VAE (VideoGPT architecture) compresses both human and robot videos into a shared discrete codebook, bridging the embodiment gap. Pre-training uses a Perceiver Transformer (p_θ1) to denoise future video tokens conditioned on history and language on ~1M Ego4D clips; fine-tuning freezes p_θ1 and trains a GPT-2 action network (p_θ2) that receives, at every diffusion step, the hidden representation z_{x̃_0^v} of p_θ1's predicted future video as a foresight signal. The x0-parameterisation of the reverse process enables stable training and fast inference by skipping multiple diffusion steps per iteration.

## Results

On RLBench (16 tasks, 10 demos, 25 episodes), VPDD achieves an average success rate of ~56.1%, outperforming PERACT (~29.0%) and RVT (~43.8%) with a 1.24× average improvement, while using only RGB images (no depth). On Meta-World MT50-rand (50 tasks, 20 demos), VPDD achieves the highest average success rate across all 50 tasks, surpassing R3M-Diffusion by 16.4% and VC-1-Diffusion by 26.5%. For video prediction quality, VPDD achieves an FVD of 235.81, lower (better) than UniPi's 264.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RLBench, VPDD outperforms both PERACT and RVT on 12 out of 16 tasks, with particularly large margins on put in cupboard (30.67% vs. 5.33% for RVT), insert peg (73.33% vs. 2.67%), stack cups (56.0% vs. 2.67%), and place cups (30.67% vs. 0%), while RVT wins on sweep to dustpan (76.0% vs. 40%) and drag stick (97.33% vs. 66.67%). On Meta-World MT50-rand, VPDD surpasses R3M-Diffusion by 16.4% and VC-1-Diffusion by 26.5% in average success rate, and R3M-Diffusion itself outperforms MTDIFF-P by 26.0% and Video-MTDT by 60.6%, highlighting the advantage of discrete diffusion over continuous diffusion and Transformer architectures. The ablation VPDD-w/o-human (removing human video pre-training) shows a 6.9% relative drop, confirming the value of Ego4D pre-training. For video generation quality, VPDD achieves a lower FVD (235.81) than UniPi (264.66), indicating more temporally consistent predicted videos. VPDD also outperforms SODA in policy fine-tuning, attributed to its coarse-to-fine representations across diffusion steps versus SODA's constant representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPDD builds on the discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (2023) and the unified multi-modal transition matrix idea from UniD3 (Hu et al., 2023), but repurposes them for a fundamentally different goal: bridging the human-to-robot domain gap for manipulation. It departs from the dominant line of robot learning from human videos (R3M, VC-1, VIP) that learns static visual representations via contrastive or masked objectives, instead treating video prediction itself as the pre-training signal. The VQ-VAE tokenizer follows VideoGPT (Yan et al., 2021), and the action denoising network mirrors MTDIFF-P (He et al., 2023), but the key architectural departure is unifying both video and action tokens under a single discrete diffusion process with a shared transition matrix, enabling the predicted future video to conditionally guide action denoising at every diffusion step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a unified discrete diffusion model pre-trained on ~1M actionless human video clips can transfer to few-shot robot manipulation more effectively than purpose-built representation learners (R3M, VC-1), suggesting that temporal dynamics captured through video prediction are a richer pre-training signal than static visual features. However, all evaluation is confined to simulation benchmarks (Meta-World, RLBench) on a single GPU, and the generated videos show visible blurriness and cross-view inconsistencies, limiting confidence in real-robot transfer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-world embodiments with higher-dimensional action spaces, whether the Perceiver Transformer bottleneck is necessary at larger scales, and whether the discrete tokenization granularity (2048 codes) is optimal for capturing fine-grained manipulation dynamics.
