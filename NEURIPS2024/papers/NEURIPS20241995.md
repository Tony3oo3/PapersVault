## Problem

Pretrained large Transformer models dominate downstream task performance, but their quadratic attention complexity and large KV cache make long-sequence generation prohibitively slow. Linear RNN architectures like Mamba offer 5× higher inference throughput and constant-size state, yet the best open-source Mamba models trained from scratch with trillions of tokens still lag behind top Transformers on chat and general benchmarks. The paper addresses the gap of how to efficiently transfer knowledge from a pretrained Transformer into a linear RNN architecture using only academic-scale compute, and how to further accelerate the resulting model at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrix of a pretrained Transformer (Llama-3 8B or Zephyr-7B), specifically the Q, K, V, and output projection matrices from each attention head, and produces a hybrid model where a configurable fraction of attention layers (50%, 25%, 12.5%, or 0%) are replaced by Mamba/Mamba2 linear RNN blocks. Distillation requires no pretraining corpus—only 20B tokens of post-training data (UltraChat, GenQA, OpenHermes 2.5, UltraFeedback) across three stages. The final model accepts standard text prompts and generates text autoregressively, with the option of speculative decoding for further speedup.

## Method

The core architectural insight is that the linearized attention formula (removing softmax) maps directly onto a linear RNN with A=mask, B=K, C=Q, x=V, allowing the pretrained Q/K/V projections to initialize the Mamba discretization's C/B/X parameters. Mamba's continuous-time SSM parameterization then expands the hidden state from N to N×N′ dimensions, recovering modeling capacity lost by removing softmax, with only the sampling rate Δ and dynamic A as new learned parameters. Training follows a stepwise hybrid strategy (first replace every 2nd attention layer, then every 4th) combined with a three-stage distillation pipeline: pseudo-label distillation with KL loss, supervised fine-tuning, and DPO-based preference alignment—the first use of DPO as a distillation objective. For inference acceleration, the paper introduces a hardware-aware multi-step RNN speculation kernel that computes multiple output tokens and intermediate hidden states in a single fused operation without materializing full state sequences, enabling speculative decoding on Mamba and hybrid architectures with 1.5–2.6× speedup on 3090 GPUs and up to 2× on H100s.

## Results

The top-performing model, Mamba-Llama3(50%) distilled from Llama-3-8B-Instruct, achieves a 29.61% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2 against GPT-4 and 7.35 on MT-Bench, matching or exceeding the teacher model (22.90% LC win rate, 8.00 MT-Bench). On general benchmarks (Table 3), the 50% hybrid model scores 61.30 average across 10 LM Eval tasks, outperforming TRI Mamba-7B (57.65) and Nvidia Hybrid Mamba-8B (59.60) trained from scratch with 1.2T and 3.7T tokens respectively. Speculative decoding with the proposed multi-step kernel achieves 2x speedup on H100 GPUs for a 7B Mamba model (272 tokens/s) and 1.8x for hybrid models. The distilled 3B models achieve near-perfect needle-in-a-haystack accuracy up to 10k tokens, exceeding the 2k distillation length by 5x.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRI Mamba-7B (Mercat et al., trained from scratch with 1.2T tokens), the distilled Mamba-Llama3(50%) outperforms on all 10 LM Eval tasks (61.30 vs. 57.65 average) and substantially on chat benchmarks (29.61 vs. 4.04 AlpacaEval LC win rate). Against Nvidia Hybrid Mamba-8B (Waleffe et al., trained with 3.7T tokens), the distilled model again wins on average LM Eval score (61.30 vs. 59.60) and by a wide margin on AlpacaEval. Against Recurrent Gemma-9B (Botev et al.), the distilled 8B model leads on MMLU (57.81 vs. 60.50 is a loss, but TruthfulQA 47.69 vs. 38.60 is a clear win) and GSM8K (67.85 vs. 38.51). Against the Zephyr-7B teacher, the distilled Mamba-Zephyr(50%) matches MT-Bench (7.31 vs. 7.34) while substantially exceeding it on AlpacaEval (20.66 vs. 13.20 LC win rate). Against the Llama-3-8B-Instruct teacher, the distilled Mamba-Llama3(50%) slightly trails on MT-Bench (7.35 vs. 8.00) but exceeds it on AlpacaEval (29.61 vs. 22.90 LC win rate), suggesting the distillation process introduces a preference-optimization benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: state-space model architectures (Mamba, Mamba2), knowledge distillation (SeqKD, DPO), and speculative decoding. Its key departure is the cross-architecture distillation setting: rather than compressing a Transformer into a smaller Transformer, it converts a pretrained Transformer into a hybrid linear-RNN model by reusing attention projection weights (Q, K, V) as initialization for Mamba's B, C, and input projections, then progressively replacing attention layers. The multi-stage training pipeline (progressive layer replacement → SFT → DPO) mirrors standard LLM post-training, and the speculative decoding contribution extends the Leviathan et al. framework to RNNs via a hardware-aware multi-step kernel that avoids materializing intermediate hidden states. Concurrent work (MOHAWK) addresses a similar distillation problem but with a different architecture and limited scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, low-compute recipe (20B tokens, ~5 days on 8×A100) for converting existing large Transformers into more deployment-efficient hybrid architectures, which is directly relevant as KV-cache memory and quadratic attention cost become bottlenecks for long-context and agentic workloads. The finding that the distilled model outperforms from-scratch Mamba models trained with 1.2T–5T tokens suggests that architectural transfer from pretrained Transformers may be a more efficient path to competitive linear-RNN models than scaling pretraining data. Open questions include whether the gap for pure Mamba (0% attention) can be closed, whether the approach generalizes to much larger models (70B+), and whether the speculative decoding speedup holds at higher batch sizes where memory-bandwidth bottlenecks shift.
