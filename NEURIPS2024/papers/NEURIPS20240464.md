## Problem

Previous works on parallel Boosting established lower bounds and parallel algorithms for the tradeoff between the number of training rounds p and parallel work per round t, but a significant gap persisted between the proven lower bounds and the performance of known algorithms across most of the tradeoff space. This paper closes that gap by providing both a stronger lower bound and a parallel algorithm whose performance matches it up to logarithmic factors across the entire p vs. t spectrum.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a labeled training set S of size m, a γ-weak learner W operating over a hypothesis set of VC-dimension d, and parameters p (number of sequential rounds) and t (parallel weak-learner queries per round). In each round, t distributions are queried simultaneously and the weak learner returns t hypotheses; the algorithm then sequentially selects R of them to perform R Boosting distribution-update steps. The output is a voting classifier f(x) = sign(g(x)) where g is a normalized linear combination of all returned hypotheses, achieving Ω(γ) margins on every training point and hence near-optimal generalization via Breiman's min-margin bound.

## Method

Algorithm 1 uses a parallel bagging step to simulate R sequential AdaBoost steps within a single parallel round: it samples t/R subsamples from the current distribution, queries the weak learner on each, and then sequentially searches the returned hypotheses (and their negations) for one with sufficient advantage to perform a Boosting update. The key analytical novelty is tracking the divergence between the evolving Boosting distribution D_r and the initial uniform distribution D_1 via KL divergence rather than the max-divergence used by Lyu et al., and applying the Donsker-Varadhan duality formula to bound the probability of finding a γ/2-approximation when sampling from D_1. A trichotomy argument (Lemma 2.6) shows that if KL(D_{R'}∥D_1) is small, the bagging step succeeds with high probability; if it is large, then either the product of normalization factors is already sufficiently small (progress made) or a negated hypothesis from a prior step has advantage ≥ γ under D_{R'}. This yields the tradeoff p = O(ln m / (γ²R)) and t = exp(O(dR)) · ln(ln m / (δγ²)), giving p ln t = O(d ln m / γ²), which matches the new lower bound p ln t = Ω(γ^{-2} d ln m) up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes that the parallel complexity of Boosting is essentially settled: Algorithm 1 achieves p = O(ln m / (γ²R)) rounds with t = e^{O(dR)} · ln(ln m / (δγ²)) parallel queries per round, yielding the clean tradeoff p·ln t = O(d ln m / γ²). The matching lower bound (Theorem 1.2) shows that any weak-to-strong learner with expected loss O(m^{-0.01}) must satisfy either p ≥ min{exp(Ω(d)), Ω(γ⁻² ln m)}, or t ≥ exp(exp(Ω(d))), or p·ln t = Ω(γ⁻² d ln m). This closes the gap between upper and lower bounds across the entire p-vs-t spectrum up to logarithmic factors, with the only unmatched term being the double-exponential t ≥ exp(exp(Ω(d))) lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between theoretical bounds. Against Lyu et al. (SODA 2024), the upper bound improves the exponent of t from O(dR²) to O(dR) — a factor of R in the exponent — while the lower bound strengthens the constraint on p by removing the Ω(γ⁻²d) term and replacing the single-exponential t ≥ exp(Ω(d)) with the double-exponential t ≥ exp(exp(Ω(d))). Against Karbasi & Larsen (ALT 2024), the lower bound improves the p constraint from Ω(γ⁻¹ ln m) to Ω(γ⁻² ln m) (a factor of γ⁻¹ stronger) and adds the p·ln t = Ω(γ⁻² d ln m) term that was previously absent. The critical achievement is that the p·ln t tradeoff now matches between upper and lower bounds across the full range p ∈ [1, O(γ⁻² ln m)], whereas previously the bounds only matched in the single case p = Ω(γ⁻² ln m), t = 1 (standard sequential AdaBoost). The one remaining gap is the t ≥ exp(exp(Ω(d))) lower bound, for which no matching upper bound is known.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 2024 line of work formalizing parallel Boosting in the weak-to-strong learning framework, initiated by Karbasi and Larsen and extended by Lyu et al. The key methodological departure is replacing the max-divergence and differential-privacy advanced composition theorem (used by Lyu et al.) with KL divergence and the Donsker-Varadhan variational formula to track how Boosting distributions diverge from the initial uniform distribution. Algorithm 1 is a bagging-based variant of AdaBoost that simulates R sequential Boosting steps within a single parallel round, improving the exponent of the parallel work t by a factor R over the prior best algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By essentially closing the gap between upper and lower bounds across the entire p-vs-t tradeoff spectrum, this paper settles the parallel complexity of near-sample-optimal Boosting, providing a complete theoretical picture that was previously fragmented. The KL-divergence-based analysis and the trichotomy argument (small KL → approximation exists; large KL → progress already made or negated hypothesis suffices) are general tools that may apply to other sequential-to-parallel conversion problems. A remaining gap is the double-exponential lower bound term t ≥ exp(exp(Ω(d))), which no known upper bound matches, and the practical relevance is limited since the algorithms require work exponential in the VC-dimension.
