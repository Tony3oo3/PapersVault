## Problem

While aligning vision models to human perceptual similarity judgments has been shown to improve specific tasks like image retrieval, it remains unclear whether such alignment produces genuinely better general-purpose representations or merely diverts the model toward a narrow similarity-prediction objective. This paper systematically evaluates whether perceptual alignment helps or hurts across a broad suite of downstream tasks, and which level of human similarity (low, mid, or high) is most beneficial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ViT-B backbone (DINO, DINOv2, CLIP, OpenCLIP, or SynCLR) and fine-tunes it on 20k synthetic image triplets from NIGHTS, where each triplet contains a reference image and two variants differing in mid-level attributes (pose, layout, color, object count), annotated with unanimous human forced-choice similarity judgments. For dense prediction tasks, the global image-level annotation is propagated to spatial patch tokens by spatially average-pooling the (s,s,d) patch embeddings to (1,d) and concatenating with the CLS token to form a (1,2d) feature vector, which is then used to train linear heads for segmentation maps or 256-bin depth maps.

## Method

The paper proposes a 'second pretraining stage' that fine-tunes pretrained ViT backbones using LoRA (r=16, α=0.5) with a margin-based hinge loss (equivalent to triplet loss) on human similarity judgments, minimizing cosine distance between the more-similar pair and maximizing it for the less-similar pair with margin m=0.05. A key technical contribution is the patch-level objective, which propagates the global image-level human annotation to individual ViT patch tokens by concatenating the CLS embedding with a spatially average-pooled patch embedding, enabling spatially-aligned local features for dense prediction. The paper ablates the level of perceptual supervision by training on four triplet datasets of equal size: NIGHTS (mid-level), BAPPS (low-level photometric distortions), THINGS (high-level conceptual similarity), and ImageNet (class-boundary similarity), finding that mid-level judgments yield the largest downstream gains while high- and low-level judgments degrade or have minimal effect. Overfitting to perceptual judgments beyond ~1000 training steps also degrades performance, indicating that a small amount of alignment is optimal.

## Results

Perceptual alignment via NIGHTS fine-tuning yields consistent improvements across dense prediction, counting, and retrieval tasks: human-aligned models outperform base backbones in 35/36 counting cases, 16/20 segmentation cases, and 27/36 depth estimation cases. On DeepFashion2 instance retrieval, improvements are dramatic and uniform—e.g., Ensemble-HA achieves 23.39% Top-1 recall vs. 13.54% for the base Ensemble, and DINO-HA reaches 11.69% vs. 8.02% for DINO. However, alignment degrades performance on standard natural image classification (VTAB Natural subset), where base models consistently outperform their aligned counterparts (e.g., DINO: 95.3% Caltech101 vs. 93.6% for DINO-HA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six base backbones are consistently outperformed by their human-aligned counterparts on retrieval, counting, and dense prediction tasks, but the magnitude varies. DINO (Caron et al., ICCV 2021) shows the most uniform gains: DINO-HA wins on all depth metrics, 16/20 segmentation scores, and counting (FSC147 MAE 41.3 vs. 44.1), yet loses on VTAB natural classification (e.g., Caltech101: 93.6% vs. 95.3%). DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) is the most mixed: DINOv2-HA improves on COCO and DAVIS2017 segmentation and Clevr-Count (RMSE 1.20 vs. 1.60) but degrades on Pascal VOC and ADE20k, likely because those datasets were already in DINOv2's retrieval pretraining. CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) and OpenCLIP (Cherti et al., CVPR 2023) both show large retrieval gains (CLIP-HA Top-1: 8.62% vs. 4.59%; OpenCLIP-HA: 16.85% vs. 14.88%) and counting improvements, but both lose on VTAB natural tasks. SynCLR (Tian et al., 2023), which was already trained on synthetic images, still benefits from alignment (retrieval Top-1: 8.35% vs. 4.88%), confirming gains are not merely from additional synthetic data. The DreamSim Ensemble (Fu et al., NeurIPS 2023) shows the largest absolute retrieval improvement (Top-1: 23.39% vs. 13.54%), suggesting that perceptual alignment compounds with strong pretraining. Across all comparisons, the consistent pattern is: alignment helps mid-level visual tasks (retrieval, counting, segmentation, depth) but hurts fine-grained natural image classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamSim line of work (Fu et al., 2023), which introduced the NIGHTS dataset of human similarity judgments over synthetic image triplets and demonstrated improved retrieval performance. While prior alignment studies (Muttenhaler et al., 2023, 2024) showed that human perceptual judgments can reshape representation spaces and aid specific tasks like similarity prediction or retrieval, the broader question of whether such alignment yields genuinely better general-purpose representations remained open. This paper positions itself as a systematic evaluation: rather than proposing a new alignment method, it takes the established LoRA-based NIGHTS fine-tuning procedure and probes its effects across a wide suite of downstream tasks (dense prediction, counting, RAG, instance retrieval, classification) to map where alignment helps and where it hurts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a practical decision framework: mid-level perceptual alignment (NIGHTS) consistently improves retrieval, counting, segmentation, depth, and RAG tasks, while degrading standard natural-image classification. The finding that global image-level similarity labels, when propagated to patch tokens, improve dense prediction is a useful architectural insight for future representation design. Key open questions include understanding the mechanism by which alignment reshapes the feature space, whether the classification degradation can be mitigated (e.g., via stronger regularization or mixed objectives), and whether the benefits generalize to larger model scales or non-ViT architectures. The overfitting observation (performance declining after ~1000 alignment steps) also suggests that the optimal amount of perceptual supervision is a non-trivial hyperparameter.
