## Problem

Personalized frequency estimation in federated settings faces two intertwined challenges: statistical heterogeneity across users (diverse vocabularies, writing styles) and the need for user-level privacy. Standard PFL approaches like FedAvg+FT are agnostic to the latent cluster structure in user populations, while existing clustering-based federated methods are gradient-based and designed for general loss functions rather than the ill-conditioned KL-divergence objective central to language modeling. This paper introduces the problem of private, personalized frequency histogram estimation and shows that exploiting cluster structure with problem-specific estimators yields significant gains over both global-model and generic clustering baselines.

## Input / Output

Each user holds m_u i.i.d. token samples from an unknown distribution over a vocabulary of size d (10k for Reddit, 32k for StackOverflow/Amazon Reviews), and the server receives per-user Good-Turing estimates along with binary cluster-membership indicators (zero vectors for non-members). The output is a personalized distribution estimate per user, computed as a local shrinkage of the assigned cluster center toward the user's empirical distribution, with error measured in KL divergence. Privacy is guaranteed in the joint (billboard) DP sense: user u's estimate may depend arbitrarily on u's own data but must be (ε,δ)-private with respect to all other users' data, since the final finetuning step occurs locally on-device.

## Method

The core algorithm adapts Lloyd's clustering to KL divergence, replacing empirical distributions with local Good-Turing estimators whose minimax suboptimality gap does not scale with vocabulary size d (unlike FedAvg). Cluster centers are estimated by averaging the Good-Turing estimates of assigned users, and a private data-dependent initialization (PrivateInit) samples K² Dirichlet candidates around a private global center, then selects K initial centers via the exponential mechanism to reduce clustering iterations from ~200 to ~20. Privacy is enforced per iteration through adaptive clipping, Gaussian noise addition, and ℓ₂ projection onto the simplex, with total budget composed via advanced composition. A Bayes-optimal local finetuning step (a convex combination of the cluster center and the user's empirical distribution, weighted by a Dirichlet concentration parameter α) personalizes each cluster center, and a two-stage extension handles size-heterogeneous users by first clustering data-rich users and then privately re-centering clusters to incorporate data-poor users.

## Results

On three real-world datasets (Reddit, StackOverflow, Amazon Reviews), the proposed method reduces test NLL sub-optimality gap by over 26% (non-private) and 42% (private) on average compared to baselines. In the private setting under (15, 10⁻¹⁰)-JDP, the method achieves up to 50% error reduction before local finetuning, and in the size-heterogeneous private setting it improves over the clustering baseline IFCA by over 30% (and >50% on Amazon Reviews). The method uses K=10 clusters, T=50 iterations non-privately and T=20 privately, with a finetuning parameter λ tuned per dataset (0.25, 0.15, 0.1 for Reddit, StackOverflow, Amazon respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: FedAvg (single global model), MAML (meta-learning), and IFCA (clustering-based federated learning). In the non-private setting, the proposed method outperforms all three baselines by 25–45% in NLL sub-optimality gap before finetuning; MAML and IFCA beat FedAvg on StackOverflow and Amazon but not Reddit. In the private setting, the gap widens significantly: MAML and IFCA suffer from high per-iteration privacy overheads due to gradient-based optimization, causing their private versions to perform similar to or worse than private FedAvg, while the proposed gradient-free method maintains a favorable privacy-utility tradeoff. In the size-heterogeneous private setting, the proposed two-stage approach achieves >40% error reduction over FedAvg and MAML, and >50% reduction over IFCA on Amazon Reviews, as IFCA's gradient sensitivity worsens with varying dataset sizes. After local finetuning (Eq. 3), the relative gains are preserved, confirming that finetuning a single global model does not match finetuning cluster-level models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Lloyd's clustering algorithm to the KL divergence metric for distribution estimation, replacing the standard L2 objective, and uses Good-Turing estimators (building on Orlitsky & Suresh [63] and Acharya et al. [2]) in place of empirical averages for cluster center estimation. It departs from gradient-based PFL clustering methods (IFCA, HypCluster) by proposing a non-gradient, problem-specific approach for frequency estimation, and extends the private heterogeneous mean estimation framework of Cummings et al. [21] to a clustered, high-dimensional setting. The joint differential privacy (billboard model) formulation for clustering-based personalization is novel relative to prior PFL privacy work, which typically addresses single-model learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that clustering-based personalization can significantly outperform the dominant FedAvg+FT pipeline for frequency estimation, challenging the assumption that a single global model suffices even with local finetuning. Key limitations include the restriction to marginal (not conditional) token distributions, the need for a central node to run the private initialization, and the Dirichlet mixture model being a simplification of real user distributions. Open questions include extending to conditional next-token prediction, federated implementation of the exponential-mechanism initialization, and whether the Good-Turing advantage persists for more complex language modeling objectives.
