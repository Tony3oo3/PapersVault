## Problem

Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding significantly improves machine translation quality over beam search, but its O(N²) computational cost—where each entry requires an expensive forward pass through a neural utility metric—limits practical deployment. The paper asks whether the pairwise utility score matrix has exploitable structure that would allow recovering the full matrix from a small random subset of computed scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241733/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a source sentence and an NMT model (PaLM8B), the method generates N candidate hypotheses via epsilon sampling (ε=0.02), computes only a random subset of N²/r pairwise MetricX scores (where r is the reduction ratio, e.g., r=16), and outputs a single selected translation. The key constraint is that the number of actual utility computations is reduced to N²/r while the final selection still operates on the full N×N recovered matrix, making the method drop-in compatible with standard MBR decoding pipelines.

## Method

The core insight is that MBR utility score matrices are empirically low-rank: singular value decomposition shows σ₁ ≫ σ₂ with σ₂/σ₁ < 0.05 across metrics and language pairs, meaning the matrix is well-approximated by a rank-1 structure. The PMBR procedure computes only a random subset Ω of N²/r pairwise scores, then applies Alternating Least Squares (ALS) to factorize the partially observed matrix as X^T Y, alternating between solving for X and Y given the observed entries with L2 regularization. After convergence, the full N×N matrix is recovered and standard MBR selection (highest row average) is applied. This achieves a 16× reduction in utility computations while matching full MBR quality on COMET22, outperforming alternative approximations (N×K pseudo-reference shrinking and S×S matrix truncation) across all budgets and language pairs.

## Results

On WMT22 (en↔de, en↔ru) with N=128 candidates, PMBR at a 1/16 budget (1,024 utility calls vs. 16,384 for full MBR) matches or slightly exceeds FMBR on COMET22 across all four language directions (e.g., de→en: 80.01 vs. 79.97; en→de: 83.63 vs. 83.52). PMBR outperforms both the N×K and S×S approximation methods at every budget level and language pair. Human evaluation (MQM, 65 examples, en→de, N=256, 1/16 budget) confirms PMBR (1.370 errors) is closer to FMBR (1.169) than N×K (1.746) or S×S (1.566).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMBR is compared against three baselines: FMBR (full MBR, the upper bound), N×K (Eikema & Aziz 2022, which shrinks the pseudo-reference list), and S×S (which reduces the total matrix size). Against FMBR, PMBR at 1/16 budget achieves equal or marginally higher COMET22 scores (e.g., 80.01 vs. 79.97 on de→en) while using only 1,024 of 16,384 utility computations; MetricX drops slightly (73.44 vs. 73.52) but the authors argue this acts as beneficial regularization. Against N×K, PMBR consistently wins on both COMET22 and MetricX at every budget and language pair, with the gap largest at low budgets (e.g., 1/32: 80.00 vs. 79.75 COMET22 on de→en) and narrowing as budget increases. Against S×S, PMBR also leads on COMET22 at lower budgets (1/32: 80.00 vs. 79.87) but the two methods converge at 1/2 budget. The MQM human evaluation (Table 4) confirms the same ordering: PMBR (1.370) < S×S (1.566) < N×K (1.746) in error count, with FMBR at 1.169.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MBR decoding framework for NMT (Eikema and Aziz 2020; Freitag et al. 2022a) and the Alternating Least Squares matrix completion algorithm (Zachariah et al. 2012), combining them in a novel way: rather than shrinking the hypothesis or pseudo-reference space as prior approximations do (N×K, S×S, pruning, reference aggregation), PMBR retains the full N×N score matrix but computes only a random subset of entries and recovers the rest via ALS. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that MBR utility matrices are approximately rank-1, which justifies the matrix completion approach and distinguishes it from all prior MBR acceleration methods that operate by reducing problem size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/16 reduction in expensive neural metric forward passes while matching full MBR quality on COMET22 is a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying MBR decoding in production NMT systems, where the O(N²) utility computations are the dominant bottleneck. The low-rank structure finding, while intuitive, opens the door to further exploitation (e.g., adaptive sampling strategies, coherence-aware entry selection) and suggests the approach may generalize to other utility-scored decoding tasks beyond MT. Open questions include whether the low-rank property holds for all utility functions (only MetricX and chrF were tested), whether coherence of the matrix affects completion quality, and whether the method extends to other NLP generation tasks.
