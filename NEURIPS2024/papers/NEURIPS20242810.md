## Problem

Learning γ-margin halfspaces under Massart noise has an information-theoretic sample complexity of Θ̃(1/(γ²ε)), but prior computationally efficient algorithms required Õ(1/(γ⁴ε³)) samples. Recent SQ lower bounds suggest a quadratic dependence on 1/ε is inherent for polynomial-time algorithms, leaving a gap between the best known upper and lower bounds. This paper asks whether a near-optimal computationally efficient learner exists and provides one.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) drawn from a distribution on the unit sphere S^{d-1} × {±1} that satisfies the η-Massart noise condition with respect to an unknown γ-margin halfspace. It draws n = Õ(1/(ε²γ²)) samples total (one per SGD iteration plus N = Õ(1/(ε(1-2η))) additional samples for a final selection step) and outputs a weight vector ŵ ∈ S^{d-1} defining a halfspace with 0-1 error at most η + ε with probability ≥ 1 − δ. The runtime is Õ(dn/ε), dominated by a final step that evaluates all T iterates on the N test samples.

## Method

The core idea is to run online SGD on a carefully constructed sequence of convex surrogate losses that simulate the non-convex 0-1 objective. At iteration t, the loss reweights the LeakyReLU loss by W(w^t·x, γ/2) = 1/max(|w^t·x|, γ/2), where w^t is the current iterate; the clipping at γ/2 prevents the loss from blowing up when w^t·x is small, and is justified by the γ-margin assumption on the target halfspace. The key structural result (Lemma 2.2) shows that the population gradient of this loss has an inner product with (w − w*) of at least 2(err_D(w) − η), making it a subgradient of the potential Φ(w) = ‖w − w*‖². This guarantees that each SGD step with learning rate λ_t = cγ²ε decreases the distance to w* by Ω(λ_t·ε) whenever the current error exceeds η + ε. After T = Õ(1/(ε²γ²)) iterations, at least one iterate must achieve error ≤ η + ε, and the algorithm selects the best iterate via empirical 0-1 error on a held-out sample set.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.3) gives a computationally efficient algorithm with sample complexity $\tilde{O}(1/(\epsilon^2\gamma^2))$ and runtime $\tilde{O}(dn/\epsilon)$, achieving 0-1 error at most $\eta + \epsilon$ with probability at least 9/10. This nearly matches the known SQ/low-degree polynomial lower bound of $\Omega(1/(\epsilon^2\gamma) + 1/(\epsilon\gamma^2))$ for efficient algorithms. The algorithm draws $n = \tilde{O}(\log(1/\delta)/(\epsilon^2\gamma^2))$ samples and runs in $O(dNT)$ time where $N = \Theta(\log(1/(\gamma\delta))/(\epsilon(1-2\eta)))$ and $T = \Theta(\log(1/\delta)/(\epsilon^2\gamma^2))$. The approach relies on online SGD over a sequence of convex losses with a clipped reweighting term $W(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{x}, \gamma/2) = 1/\max(|\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{x}|, \gamma/2)$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between theoretical sample complexity bounds. The proposed algorithm improves the sample complexity from $\tilde{O}(1/(\gamma^4\epsilon^3))$ (prior best by [CKMY20]) to $\tilde{O}(1/(\gamma^2\epsilon^2))$, achieving a quadratic improvement in $\epsilon$ and a quadratic improvement in $\gamma$. The result nearly matches the information-computation lower bound of $\Omega(1/(\epsilon^2\gamma)+1/(\epsilon\gamma^2))$ established by [DDK+23a, DDK+23b] for SQ algorithms and low-degree polynomial tests, leaving only a gap in the $\gamma$-dependence. The algorithm also matches known guarantees for the easier Random Classification Noise (RCN) setting [DDK+23a, KIT+23]. An independent concurrent work [CKST24] obtained essentially the same sample and computational complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LeakyReLU-based loss introduced by Diakonikolas, Gouleakis, and Tzamos (NeurIPS 2019) for distribution-free Massart halfspace learning, and the reweighting and online convex optimization framework of Chen, Koehler, Moitra, and Yau (NeurIPS 2020), which achieved sample complexity Õ(1/(γ⁴ε³)). The key departure is a new sequence of clipped, reweighted convex losses whose gradients are shown to act as subgradients of the potential function ‖w − w*‖², enabling a simple online SGD analysis that avoids the Ω(1/ε³) sample overhead of prior local-error approaches. The result is positioned against recent SQ lower bounds (DDK+23a, DDK+23b) showing Ω(1/(ε²γ) + 1/(εγ²)) samples are needed for efficient algorithms, making the achieved Õ(1/(γ²ε²)) near-optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work nearly closes the gap between the information-theoretic sample complexity Θ̃(1/(γ²ε)) and the best known computationally efficient upper bound for Massart margin halfspace learning, providing strong evidence that the quadratic 1/ε² dependence is inherent for polynomial-time algorithms. The algorithm's simplicity (online SGD with a final empirical-risk-minimization step) makes it practical and potentially extensible. A key open question highlighted by the authors is extending the approach to general (non-margin) halfspaces, where the dimension dependence would likely be suboptimal without new ideas for reducing to the large-margin case.
