## Problem

Online free-viewpoint video (FVV) streaming requires incremental on-the-fly updates to a volumetric representation at each time-step, with fast training and rendering for real-time operation and small per-frame memory for bandwidth-limited transmission. Existing online FVV methods either rely on slow NeRF-based representations or impose fixed structural constraints (voxel grids, triplanes, hash encodings) that limit model expressiveness and compromise reconstruction quality, particularly for scenes with large geometric motion and illumination changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-view video sequence (20 forward-facing views at 2704×2028 for N3DV, or 46 fisheye-undistorted views at 1280×960 for Immersive) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, downsampled by a factor of 2, with the central view held out for testing. It produces, per frame, a compressed representation consisting of entropy-coded integer latents for rotation, scale, opacity, and appearance residuals plus a sparse matrix of full-precision position residuals, totaling as little as 0.7 MB per frame. The compressed residuals are decoded and added to the previous frame's Gaussian attributes to render novel-view images at ~250–350 FPS.

## Method

QUEEN models a dynamic scene as 3D Gaussians whose attributes are updated per frame via learnable residuals (A_t = A_{t-1} + R_t) with no structural constraints, allowing free adaptation to arbitrary scene changes. For compression, all attribute residuals except position are quantized through a shared linear latent-decoder trained end-to-end with the Straight-Through Estimator, then entropy-coded post-training (achieving ~10× size reduction). Position residuals, which are sensitive to quantization, are instead sparsified via a learned hard-concrete gate that drives most gates to zero while retaining full-precision values for dynamic Gaussians, regularized by an L0 sparsity loss. A viewspace gradient difference signal—computed as the difference of 2D Gaussian-mean gradients between consecutive frames—initializes the gates and identifies dynamic image regions for adaptive masked training, reducing wasted computation on static content. The total loss combines D-SSIM + L1 reconstruction with the sparsity regularization, and 3D-GS densification is applied at each time-step to capture new scene content.

## Results

On N3DV, QUEEN-l achieves the best online FVV quality at 32.19 dB PSNR, 0.946 SSIM, and 0.136 LPIPS, while reducing per-frame storage to 0.75 MB (10× smaller than 3DGStream's 7.80 MB) with training in 7.9 sec and rendering at 248 FPS. On the more challenging Immersive dataset, QUEEN-l reaches 29.22 dB PSNR (+4.04 dB over 3DGStream*), 0.915 SSIM, and 0.208 LPIPS, with 1.79 MB/frame storage (5× smaller), 19.7 sec training, and 183 FPS rendering. The ablation shows that attribute quantization alone reduces storage from 44.36 MB to 4.18 MB, and adding position gating further reduces it to 0.72 MB on N3DV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUEEN-l outperforms 3DGStream* (the strongest prior online method) on all quality metrics: +0.61 dB PSNR on N3DV and +4.04 dB PSNR on Immersive, while using 10× and 5× less storage respectively. On N3DV, QUEEN-l also beats TeTriRF by +1.76 dB PSNR and +0.040 SSIM, though TeTriRF uses less storage (0.06 MB vs 0.75 MB) at the cost of 5× slower training (39 sec vs 7.9 sec) and 62× slower rendering (4 FPS vs 248 FPS). Against StreamRF, QUEEN-l improves PSNR by +1.51 dB while reducing storage by 42× (31.4 MB → 0.75 MB) and rendering 30× faster. On Immersive, QUEEN-l's advantage over 3DGStream* is most pronounced: the +4 dB PSNR gap reveals that 3DGStream's fixed InstantNGP-based structural constraints limit its ability to model large scene changes (e.g., a person entering the scene), whereas QUEEN's unconstrained residual learning with adaptive quantization-sparsity handles such dynamics more effectively. QUEEN-s (smallest variant) already matches or exceeds 3DGStream* in quality on N3DV while training 1.8× faster and using 11× less memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUEEN builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its base representation and adapts the learned quantization framework from EAGLES (Girish et al., 2023) to compress per-frame attribute residuals via a shared latent decoder with straight-through estimation. It departs from the closest prior work, 3DGStream, which imposes a fixed InstantNGP-based transformation cache with heuristic structural constraints on the volumetric representation, by instead freely learning all Gaussian attribute residuals without structural constraints and compressing them through a learned quantization-sparsity framework. The hard concrete gate mechanism (Louizos et al., 2017) is repurposed here not for network pruning but for sparsifying position residuals, and the viewspace gradient difference signal for adaptive masked training is a novel contribution not present in prior online FVV methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online FVV streaming is a practical bottleneck for applications like 3D video conferencing and live volumetric broadcast, and QUEEN's 10× reduction in per-frame model size (to ~0.7 MB) while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality over prior methods makes real-time streaming on bandwidth-limited channels more feasible. The adaptive allocation of bits to dynamic frames (vs. fixed-size structures in prior work) is a principled departure that could inform broader compression strategies for dynamic neural representations. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, sparse-view settings, and whether the quantization-sparsity framework generalizes to other dynamic 3D representations beyond 3D-GS; the authors note these as future work.
