## Problem

Decoding the information encoded in neural network activations into human-understandable form remains a major challenge for interpretability. Existing approaches each have fundamental limitations: supervised probes require the researcher to pre-specify what to look for, vocabulary-space projection only yields individual tokens, and causal interventions reveal information flow but not the content of activations. The paper addresses the gap of a general-purpose, hypothesis-free method that directly reveals what information an activation encodes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single activation vector z^q (a d-dimensional vector from a specific site in the probed model, e.g., an attention head output or residual stream position) along with its activation site index, and produces a set of generated input sequences with per-token distance scores relative to the query activation. Samples are partitioned into in-ε-preimage (distance ≤ ε) and out-of-ε-preimage sets, and the researcher inspects what is common across in-preimage samples to infer the encoded information. The decoder is a 2-layer transformer (256-dim, 4 heads) for small models or GPT-2 Medium (24 layers) for GPT-2 XL, trained with a language modeling objective conditioned on the activation.

## Method

InversionView formalizes the information in an activation as its ε-preimage: the set of inputs whose activations fall within distance ε of the query activation under a chosen metric (normalized Euclidean or cosine). To sample from this preimage, the authors train a conditional autoregressive decoder in which the query activation is concatenated with a learned site embedding, processed through 6 residual MLP layers, then projected per-layer and prepended to the attention context so every head can attend to it alongside generated tokens. Sampling diversity is controlled via temperature (τ ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0}) and Gaussian noise (coefficient η) added to the activation, ensuring coverage of the full ε-preimage rather than just the nearest neighbor. Correctness is guaranteed by design (each sample's distance is explicitly evaluated), and completeness is verified via counter-examples or exhaustive enumeration. The method is validated across four case studies (character counting, IOI in GPT-2 small, 3-digit addition, factual recall in GPT-2 XL), with all hypotheses confirmed by cumulative activation patching experiments.

## Results

InversionView is validated across four case studies. On the character counting task (2-layer, 1-head transformer, 99.53% test accuracy), InversionView reveals that the MLP amplifies count information and the next layer abstracts away target character identity while retaining count. On 3-digit addition (2-layer, 4-head transformer, 98.01% test accuracy), InversionView uncovers a fully causally verified circuit for predicting each answer digit, including a deficiency (missing ones-place carry) that accounts for all model errors. On the IOI circuit in GPT-2 Small, InversionView recovers the information encoded in 23 of 26 attention heads identified by Wang et al. [54], with disagreement only on 3 heads (0.1, 0.10, 5.9) that were previously challenging to interpret. On GPT-2 XL (1.5B parameters), InversionView produces interpretable ε-preimages expressing high-level factual knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative comparison is with Wang et al. [54] on the IOI circuit in GPT-2 Small. InversionView independently recovers the information encoded in 23 of 26 attention heads (88.5%), with the 3 disagreements (heads 0.1, 0.10, 5.9) corresponding to components that Wang et al. also found challenging to interpret with their tailored patching and counterfactual dataset methods. A key practical advantage is that InversionView directly reads out both token signals and position signals from S-Inhibition heads without requiring the researcher to design disentangling counterfactual datasets, substantially simplifying the interpretability workflow. The paper also contrasts InversionView conceptually with supervised probing (which requires a priori hypotheses), Direct Logit Attribution (limited to components directly affecting output), and path patching (which identifies connectivity but not information content), arguing that InversionView uniquely reveals how information is abstracted, amplified, or forgotten across activation sites. For 3-digit addition, InversionView provides the first fully causally verified circuit for a multi-layer transformer, going beyond the single-layer analysis of Quirke & Barez [47].

## Relation to Prior Work

InversionView carves new ground by reframing activation interpretation as an inverse-mapping problem: rather than probing for a hypothesized variable (supervised probes), projecting to vocabulary space (Logit Lens/DLA), or tracing causal paths (activation/path patching), it generates the full set of inputs producing similar activations (the ε-preimage) via a conditional autoregressive decoder. Conceptually, it repurposes generative sequence modeling for interpretability, drawing on the mathematical notion of preimages and the residual-stream framework from transformer circuits work. The paper positions itself as complementary to causal intervention methods: InversionView reveals *what* information is present at each site, while patching verifies *how* it flows, and the two are used iteratively across all case studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InversionView provides a hypothesis-free, general-purpose tool that can be applied to any activation site in any sequence model, substantially lowering the barrier to mechanistic interpretation and enabling automated interpretation via LLMs. Key limitations include the need to train and validate a decoder per task, the current ceiling at ~1.5B parameters, and the assumption of isotropic geometry in the distance metric. Open questions include scaling to frontier-scale models, applying the method to subspaces of the residual stream, extending to non-text modalities, and developing principled (rather than researcher-chosen) thresholds and metrics.
