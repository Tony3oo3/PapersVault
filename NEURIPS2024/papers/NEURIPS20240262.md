## Problem

Approximate posteriors in Bayesian neural networks assign different probability densities to different parametrizations of the same function, violating a fundamental Bayesian principle. This reparameterization invariance failure causes the standard Laplace approximation to severely underfit in overparametrized models, while the linearized Laplace approximation (which adds a seemingly crude approximation) paradoxically fixes the problem. The paper provides the first architecture-agnostic geometric characterization of all continuous reparameterizations and explains why linearization succeeds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained MAP weight vector, the training dataset, and a prior precision α as input. It produces posterior weight samples via a multi-step Euler-Maruyama diffusion on the non-kernel parameter manifold, from which predictive distributions are computed using the full (non-linearized) neural network. The GGN matrix is approximated via Lanczos iterations (2000–5000 steps) to obtain the top-k eigenvalues and eigenvectors needed for the diffusion update, with 20 Monte Carlo samples used for prediction.

## Method

The paper shows that the GGN matrix induces a pseudo-Riemannian metric on weight space whose zero-distance equivalence classes exactly match reparameterization orbits, and that the weight space decomposes into a kernel manifold (reparameterizations) and a non-kernel manifold (functional changes). The proposed Laplace diffusion is a Riemannian Brownian motion on the non-kernel manifold with metric GGN⁺ (the GGN restricted to non-zero eigenvectors), simulated via multi-step Euler-Maruyama: w_{t+1} = w_t + √(2h)·U₁(Λ̃+αI)^{-1/2}·ε. This diffusion is provably reparameterization invariant and never underfits on training data. The key theoretical insight is that the linearized Laplace approximation is exactly a single-step Euler-Maruyama discretization of this diffusion, explaining its empirical success. The approach uses the full neural network predictive (no linearization) while maintaining reparameterization invariance through the geometric structure of the sampling process.

## Results

On MNIST, Laplace Diffusion achieves NLL 0.042, accuracy 98.7%, and ECE 0.137, substantially outperforming Sampled Laplace (NLL 3.812, acc 14.6%) and Linearised Laplace (NLL 0.306, acc 92.6%). On CIFAR-10, the diffusion reaches NLL 0.345 and accuracy 90.5%, compared to 0.997/71.7% for Sampled Laplace and 0.614/86.3% for Linearised Laplace. For out-of-distribution detection (AUROC), Laplace Diffusion achieves 0.929 on KMNIST (trained on MNIST) versus 0.482 for Sampled Laplace and 0.790 for Linearised Laplace, and 0.862 on SVHN (trained on CIFAR-10) versus 0.599 and 0.854 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Laplace Diffusion decisively outperforms Sampled Laplace (MacKay, 1992) across all datasets and metrics: on MNIST the NLL gap is 0.042 vs 3.812 and accuracy is 98.7% vs 14.6%, confirming the severe underfitting of the standard Gaussian posterior in overparametrized networks. Against Linearised Laplace (Immer et al., 2021; Khan et al., 2019), the diffusion consistently achieves lower NLL (e.g., 0.042 vs 0.306 on MNIST, 0.345 vs 0.614 on CIFAR-10) and better calibration (lower ECE on MNIST and FMNIST), while using the full neural network predictive rather than a linearized one. On out-of-distribution AUROC, the diffusion leads by 0.14–0.37 over Sampled Laplace and 0.04–0.14 over Linearised Laplace across all OOD pairs. The paper also reports comparisons to SWAG (Maddox et al., 2019), diagonal Laplace (Ritter et al., 2018), and last-layer Laplace (Kristiadi et al., 2020) in the appendix, where Laplace Diffusion remains competitive with the best-performing Bayesian methods despite not using any linearization. Notably, on CIFAR-10 Sampled Laplace shows less severe underfitting (NLL 0.997) because a large prior precision was required to obtain meaningful samples, which simultaneously shrinks predictive uncertainty to near zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Laplace approximation lineage (MacKay 1992) and the linearized Laplace approximation (Immer et al. 2021; Khan et al. 2019) by providing a geometric explanation for why linearization improves posterior fit — a question previously unaddressed. It draws on Riemannian diffusion (Girolami & Calderhead 2011) and pseudo-Riemannian geometry to show that the GGN matrix is a pullback metric whose kernel encodes reparameterization directions, and that the linearized Laplace is a single-step Euler-Maruyama discretization of a multi-step Riemannian diffusion on the effective-parameter manifold. The paper also connects to the broader Bayesian deep learning literature (SWAG, Bayes by Backprop, subnetwork inference) by explaining why crude low-rank approximations perform favorably: smaller models are less affected by reparameterization issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing puzzle in Bayesian deep learning — why a crude linearization improves posterior fit over the full neural network predictive — by identifying reparameterization invariance as the underlying mechanism. The proposed Laplace diffusion is competitive with linearized Laplace while using the full neural network predictive, offering a principled alternative. Key limitations include the need for full (or high-rank) GGN access, which is computationally expensive, and the unclear compatibility with standard sparse GGN approximations (diagonal, block-diagonal) that break reparameterization invariance. Open questions include how to scale the diffusion to very large networks and whether the geometric framework can inform better sparse approximations that preserve invariance.
