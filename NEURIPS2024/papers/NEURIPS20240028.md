## Problem

Universal speech enhancement must simultaneously remove noise, reverberation, and codec artefacts from real-world recordings, but the state-of-the-art diffusion models require dozens of iterative inference steps, making them impractical for real-time use. The paper argues that speech enhancement does not need to model the full conditional distribution—only the most probable clean reconstruction—and theoretically shows that LS-GAN training with a deterministic generator naturally converges to this mode, motivating a revisit of GAN-based approaches with a stable training recipe.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded 16 kHz speech waveform as input and produces a 48 kHz enhanced waveform in one forward pass (RTF ≈ 0.03 on a V100). Internally, the WavLM-large encoder processes the input at 50 Hz (320× downsampling), while the HiFi++ backbone alternates between spectral and waveform domains; a dedicated Upsample WaveUNet with a 3× temporal upsampling block in its decoder bridges the 16→48 kHz gap without requiring a separate super-resolution model.

## Method

FINALLY builds on the HiFi++ four-stage generator (SpectralUNet → HiFi Upsampler → WaveUNet → SpectralMaskNet) and adds two architectural modifications: WavLM-large transformer hidden states are concatenated with the SpectralUNet output as an additional conditioning input to the Upsampler, and an Upsample WaveUNet is appended for 48 kHz output. The key training innovation is the LMOS perceptual loss, which combines L2 distance in WavLM convolutional-encoder feature space with L1 STFT magnitude distance; the paper validates this choice via two heuristic criteria (clustering rule and SNR rule) showing WavLM-conv features best separate identical sounds and monotonically push noisy samples away from clean clusters. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) LMOS-only regression pretraining at 16 kHz on LibriTTS-R, (2) adversarial training with five MS-STFT discriminators and feature-matching loss at 16 kHz, and (3) 48 kHz fine-tuning on DAPS with increased GAN loss weight and differentiable human-feedback losses (UTMOS + PESQ). The theoretical contribution proves that minimizing the Pearson χ² divergence (equivalent to LS-GAN) with a generator approximating a delta function drives the output to argmax_y p_clean(y|x), the mode of the conditional distribution.

## Results

On real-world VoxCeleb1 data, FINALLY achieves a MOS of 4.63±0.04, UTMOS of 4.05±0.07, and WV-MOS of 3.98±0.06, outperforming the closest GAN-based competitor HiFi-GAN-2 (MOS 4.47, UTMOS 3.67) while running ~17× faster (RTF 0.03 vs. 0.50 on V100). On the UNIVERSE validation set, FINALLY reaches MOS 4.23±0.07 and UTMOS 4.21±0.10, surpassing the UNIVERSE diffusion model (MOS 4.10, UTMOS 3.89) with a lower Phoneme Error Rate (0.14 vs. 0.20) and 17× lower RTF (0.03 vs. 0.5). On VCTK-DEMAND, FINALLY (48 kHz) achieves the highest MOS (4.66) and UTMOS (4.32) among all baselines, though it underperforms on reference-based metrics like PESQ (2.94 vs. 3.26 for DB-AIAT) and SI-SDR (4.6 vs. 19.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINALLY outperforms all nine baselines on perceptual quality metrics (MOS, UTMOS, WV-MOS) across both real-world (VoxCeleb) and simulated (UNIVERSE) evaluations. Against the closest GAN competitor HiFi-GAN-2, FINALLY gains +0.16 MOS and +0.38 UTMOS on VoxCeleb while being 17× faster (RTF 0.03 vs. 0.50). Against the UNIVERSE diffusion model, FINALLY achieves +0.13 MOS, +0.32 UTMOS, and a substantially lower PhER (0.14 vs. 0.20), indicating fewer hallucinated phonemes, at 17× lower inference cost. On VCTK-DEMAND, FINALLY leads in MOS (4.66) and UTMOS (4.32) over DB-AIAT (4.22, 4.02) and HiFi-GAN-2 (4.13, 3.99), but trails on reference-based metrics: PESQ (2.94 vs. 3.26), STOI (0.92 vs. 0.96), and SI-SDR (4.6 vs. 19.3), which the authors attribute to known poor correlation of these metrics with human perception. Against regression-based baselines (VoiceFixer, DEMUCS), FINALLY shows clear advantages in MOS (+1.22 over VoiceFixer, +0.84 over DEMUCS on VoxCeleb), confirming the paper's argument that generative models outperform regression for speech enhancement. The Miipher comparison (Table 7) shows FINALLY (48 kHz) achieving MOS 4.54 vs. Miipher's 4.18, with lower WER (0.18 vs. 0.22), despite not requiring text transcripts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HiFi++ architecture (Andreev et al., 2022) and augments it with a WavLM encoder input and an Upsample WaveUNet for 48 kHz output, while adopting MS-STFT discriminators from the EnCodec pipeline (Defossez et al., 2023). Conceptually, it departs from the recent trend toward diffusion-based speech enhancement (UNIVERSE, STORM, BBED) by providing a theoretical argument that LS-GAN training naturally performs mode-seeking—recovering the single most probable clean waveform—which the authors argue is the correct objective for speech enhancement rather than full distribution matching. The LMOS perceptual loss extends prior work using SSL features as auxiliary regression spaces (Sato et al., 2023; Close et al., 2023) by introducing a systematic methodology (clustering rule, SNR rule) for selecting which layer of a pretrained model best serves as a perceptual loss backbone, identifying WavLM's convolutional encoder as superior to its transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FINALLY achieves comparable or better perceptual quality than diffusion-based SOTA while running 10–30× faster (RTF 0.03 vs. 0.5–1.05), making high-quality speech enhancement viable in near-real-time settings. The theoretical result connecting LS-GAN to mode-seeking provides a principled justification for GANs in this task that was previously missing, potentially guiding future work on when GANs vs. diffusion models are appropriate. Key limitations remain: the model cannot operate in streaming/low-latency mode due to WavLM's context window, artifacts persist on severely degraded inputs, and the 454M parameter count (358M from WavLM) is substantial for deployment. Open questions include whether the mode-seeking property can be exploited more explicitly and whether lighter-weight SSL backbones can match WavLM-conv's perceptual loss performance.
