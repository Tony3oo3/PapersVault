## Problem

Classical spectral preservation guarantees (Restricted Spectral Approximation) for graph coarsening do not directly translate to message-passing guarantees for GNNs: performing naive message-passing on a coarsened graph using its natural propagation matrix does not ensure that GNN outputs remain close to those on the original graph, even under high-quality coarsenings. This gap prevents provable equivalence between training on coarsened and original graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A, node feature matrix X, a desired coarsening ratio r, and a propagation matrix S (e.g., GCNconv normalized adjacency), and produces a coarsening matrix Q (mapping N nodes to n super-nodes) along with a new propagation matrix S_c^MP ∈ R^{n×n} for the coarsened graph. A notable structural property is that S_c^MP is generally asymmetric even when S is symmetric, meaning the resulting message-passing on the coarsened graph is directed even for undirected original graphs. The coarsened GNN output is then lifted back to the original graph via Q^+ for loss computation and backpropagation.

## Method

The core contribution is defining a new propagation matrix S_c^MP = QSQ^+ for coarsened graphs, which is conceptually a reweighting of the original propagation matrix by the coarsening and lifting operators. The key algebraic insight is that the lifted coarsened message-passing equals ΠSΠx (where Π = Q^+Q is the projection), allowing the RSA constant ε to directly bound the message-passing error: ‖Sx − Q^+S_c^MP x_c‖_L ≤ ε(C_S + C_Π)‖x‖_L. Under mild assumptions (ker(L)-preserving and R-preserving propagation), this single-step guarantee extends to multi-layer GNN training, yielding an excess-loss bound proportional to ε for the SGC architecture. The approach is coarsening-agnostic: S_c^MP can be computed for any coarsening with RSA guarantees, and the asymmetry of S_c^MP is most pronounced when super-nodes have highly unequal sizes.

## Results

On Cora with SGC at coarsening ratio r=0.5, the proposed S_c^MP achieves 80.3% accuracy, outperforming the naive propagation S_c (78.7%) and the [21] baseline S_c^diag (78.7%), approaching the full-graph result of 81.6%. At higher coarsening (r=0.7), the advantage grows: S_c^MP reaches 78.5% vs. 74.6% for S_c and 77.3% for S_c^diag on Cora. On the much larger Reddit dataset (233K nodes) at r=0.99, S_c^MP achieves 64.1% with SGC (vs. 37.3% for both S_c and S_c^diag) and 60.3% with GCNconv (vs. 54.2% and 55.5%), while also yielding the lowest message-passing error (0.88 vs. 1.75 and 1.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four alternative propagation matrices on coarsened graphs, with the most directly comparable baseline being S_c^diag from Huang et al. [21], which provides guarantees only for the APPNP architecture. On SGC (where the paper's theory applies), S_c^MP consistently outperforms S_c^diag: by 1.6 points on Cora at r=0.5 (80.3 vs. 78.7) and by 1.2 points at r=0.7 (78.5 vs. 77.3), and by 1.2 points on Citeseer at r=0.7 (74.2 vs. 73.1). The gap widens dramatically on Reddit at r=0.99, where S_c^MP achieves 64.1% (SGC) versus 37.3% for both S_c^diag and the naive S_c. The naive propagation S_c = f_S(A_c) is competitive at low coarsening ratios but degrades faster as r increases. The Diffpool-inspired S_c^diff and the lifting-based S_c^sym perform poorly, often near random, confirming that natural choices for coarsened propagation matrices do not preserve message-passing without the specific QS Q^+ construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Loukas's (2019) framework for spectral-preserving graph coarsening, adopting the Restricted Spectral Approximation (RSA) constant as the quality measure and adapting his greedy merging algorithm for experiments. It departs from prior work by identifying a gap: RSA guarantees do not translate to message-passing preservation under the naive propagation matrix S_c = f_S(A_c), and by proposing S_c^MP = QSQ^+ as a fix. The closest prior work is Huang et al. (2021), which designed a propagation matrix for coarsened graphs but only for the specific APPNP architecture; this paper provides the first generic message-passing guarantee applicable to any GNN using the SGC/SGC-like propagation paradigm. A notable conceptual departure is that S_c^MP is generally asymmetric, requiring oriented message-passing even on undirected graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first provable guarantee that training a GNN on a coarsened graph is close to training on the original graph, bridging a gap between spectral coarsening theory and GNN practice. However, the guarantee currently holds only for the linear SGC architecture (Assumption 4 on activation functions is unsatisfied by ReLU), and the coarsening algorithm is computationally expensive (large matrix inversions and SVDs), limiting scalability beyond ~230K nodes. Key open questions include extending guarantees to non-linear activations (possibly via random geometric graph analysis), designing scalable coarsening algorithms with RSA guarantees, and understanding when the asymmetry of S_c^MP can be avoided (e.g., balanced partitions).
