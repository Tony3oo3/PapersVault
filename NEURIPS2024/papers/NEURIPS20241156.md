## Problem

Despite significant advances in best subset selection (BSS) for high-dimensional sparse regression, no computationally efficient differentially private (DP) algorithmic framework exists for this problem. Prior DP model selection methods either require exponential runtime to sample from the exponential mechanism, impose restrictive bounded-loss assumptions, or suffer from nontrivial failure probabilities that do not vanish with sample size. This paper bridges the gap by providing a DP BSS method that is both polynomial-time computable and achieves near-optimal model recovery guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a fixed design matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×p}, a response vector y ∈ ℝ^n, a privacy budget ε > 0, and the true sparsity level s (assumed known). It outputs a single feature subset γ ⊆ [p] of size s, drawn from the exponential mechanism distribution, serving as a private estimator of the true active set γ*. The score function for each candidate subset involves solving an ℓ1-constrained least-squares subproblem (via CVXPY), and the global sensitivity is bounded by (r + x_max·K)² under boundedness assumptions on the data, where K is a tuning parameter controlling the ℓ1-ball radius.

## Method

The paper privatizes BSS by applying the exponential mechanism to a score function u_K(γ; X, y) = −min_{θ: ‖θ‖₁ ≤ K} ‖y − X_γ θ‖₂², where the ℓ1-constraint on θ (rather than assuming bounded loss) yields a tractable sensitivity bound. For computational efficiency, the authors design a Metropolis-Hastings random walk with a 'double swap' update that replaces one feature in the current model with one from its complement, and prove via canonical path ensemble construction that the chain mixes in O(ps²·{nεΨ⁻¹κ₊b_max² + log(1/η)}) iterations under a Sparse Riesz Condition and a correlation-control assumption (Assumption 4.2). The resulting MCMC estimator enjoys approximate (ε, δ)-DP with δ = η(1+e^ε), and the utility analysis reveals an inflection phenomenon: in the low-privacy regime (ε > Δ_K/σ²), model recovery requires only the minimax-optimal β_min ≳ σ√(log p/n), while in the high-privacy regime an extra factor of s/ε is needed.

## Results

Under the low-privacy regime (ε > Δ_K/σ²), the DP BSS method recovers the true model γ* with probability ≥ 1 − p^(−2) whenever β_min ≳ σ{(log p)/n}^{1/2}, matching the minimax-optimal non-private rate. In the high-privacy regime, an extra (s/ε) factor is required: β_min ≳ σ{(s log p)/(nε)}^{1/2}. Empirically (Table 2, K=2, n=900, p=2000, s=4), the MCMC-based DP estimator achieves F-score 1.00 (matching non-private ABESS) at ε=10 for both strong and weak signals under Uniform design, and 0.925 for strong signal under Gaussian design; at ε=3 it still reaches 1.00 for strong-signal Uniform but drops to 0.15 for weak signal. The MCMC mixing time is bounded by C₂ps²{nεΨ^(−1)κ₊b_max² + log(1/η)}, i.e., polynomial in (n, p, s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kifer et al. [27], this paper improves the β_min requirement in the low-privacy regime from Ω(√(s log p / n^{1/2})) to Ω(√(log p / n)) (removing the s factor) and replaces the exponential-time exponential mechanism with a polynomial-time MCMC sampler while retaining (ε,0)-DP. Against Thakurta & Smith [45], this paper achieves failure probability → 0 as n grows (whereas [45]'s PTR-based methods have non-trivial null-output probability that does not vanish), and requires a weaker β_min condition (no 1/ε dependence in the low-privacy regime). Against Lei et al. [29], this paper replaces the s log n term in the margin condition with log p and achieves polynomial runtime via MCMC instead of exponential runtime. Against the non-private ABESS baseline [58], the DP method matches F-score 1.00 at ε ≥ 3 for strong signals under Uniform design and at ε = 10 for both signal strengths, confirming the theoretical inflection point between high- and low-privacy regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper privatizes the best subset selection (BSS) formulation of Bertsimas et al. (2016) by adopting the exponential mechanism of McSherry and Talwar (2007), modifying the algorithm of Kifer et al. (2012) with an ℓ1-constraint to handle unbounded squared-error loss. It departs from prior DP model selection works (Kifer 2012, Thakurta 2013, Lei 2018) by replacing their either-exponential-runtime or suboptimal-signal-strength approaches with a Metropolis-Hastings random walk using a double-swap update, whose polynomial mixing time is established via a canonical path ensemble construction borrowed from Sinclair (1992) and Yang et al. (2016). The identifiability margin framework from Guo et al. (2020) provides the non-private baseline that the paper's privacy analysis must match or exceed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient (polynomial-time) private BSS algorithm for high-dimensional sparse regression, closing a gap the authors identify as surprising given the maturity of non-private BSS. The inflection phenomenon—where the signal-strength requirement matches the non-private minimax rate in the low-privacy regime but incurs an extra (s/ε) factor in the high-privacy regime—gives practitioners a concrete tradeoff curve. The open question of whether the √s factor in the high-privacy regime is truly necessary, and the proposed future direction of DP mixed-integer optimization, suggest the current result may not be the final word on the cost of privacy for exact model selection.
