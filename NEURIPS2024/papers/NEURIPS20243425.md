## Problem

Heterogeneous architectures—such as GNNs augmented with persistent homology layers—lack data-dependent generalization guarantees, as existing PAC-Bayes analyses assume spectrally-normalized or homogeneous layers. No generalization bounds existed for PersLay (a widely adopted persistence diagram vectorization) or for any composition of GNNs with topological descriptors, leaving the reliability of these increasingly popular models theoretically ungrounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243425/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes attributed graphs (vertex features, edge structure) and, for PersLay-based models, persistence diagrams extracted via a fixed filtration function (e.g., heat kernels). The PersLay classifier operates on graph structure alone without node features, while GNN+PersLay models in parallel mode concatenate the GNN's graph-level readout with the PersLay output before a shared MLP head. The primary outputs are closed-form PAC-Bayes generalization bounds parameterized by spectral norms of weight matrices, model width, and Lipschitz constants of activations, which in turn yield a spectral-norm regularizer added to the training loss.

## Method

The core contribution is Lemma 2, a general PAC-Bayes recipe that replaces the spectrally-normalized assumption with four verifiable conditions: a bounded output norm (Eq. 6), a bounded output change under small parameter perturbations (Eq. 7), an arithmetic-geometric mean inequality linking the perturbation scale to the output scale (Eq. 8), and a margin-compatibility constraint (Eq. 9). Two compositional lemmas then close the framework: Lemma 3 shows that composing any model satisfying Lemma 2 with an MLP preserves the conditions, and Lemma 4 shows closure under parallel concatenation with a Lipschitz aggregator. Applying these to PersLay (Corollary 1) and to GNNs in parallel with PersLay (Corollary 2) yields the first data-dependent bounds for topological graph classifiers, with the bound's dependence on the product of spectral norms and model width directly informing a weight-decay-style regularizer that empirically reduces generalization gaps across six benchmark datasets.

## Results

On PersLay classifiers, spectral norm regularization (derived from the theoretical bound) improves test accuracy on 5 out of 6 datasets, with the largest gain on MUTAG (0.94 vs. 0.88) and PROTEINS (0.72 vs. 0.65). For PH-augmented GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) on NCI, NCI109, and PROTEINS, the spectral norm regularizer consistently reduces the generalization gap — e.g., GCN on NCI drops from 0.19±0.01 to 0.01±0.06, and GraphSAGE on PROTEINS from 0.25±0.01 to −0.15±0.30. The theoretical bound on the spectral norm of weights correlates strongly with the empirical generalization gap (correlation > 0.7 in 7/9 experiments; > 0.91 on 4/5 datasets for width dependence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it evaluates the practical utility of its theoretical bound by contrasting empirical risk minimization (ERM) with a spectral norm regularizer derived directly from the PAC-Bayes bound. Across all three GNN architectures (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN) and three datasets (NCI, NCI109, PROTEINS), the SpecNorm regularizer achieves equal or lower test error and substantially smaller generalization gaps in 8 out of 9 settings. The most dramatic improvement is GCN on NCI, where the generalization gap drops from 0.19 to 0.01. For PersLay classifiers (Table 3), SpecNorm outperforms ERM on 5/6 datasets, with the largest accuracy gain on MUTAG (+6 points) and PROTEINS (+7 points). The one exception is GIN on PROTEINS, where SpecNorm slightly increases test error (0.30 vs. 0.29) while still reducing the generalization gap (0.07 vs. 0.26), suggesting the regularizer trades a small amount of in-sample fit for better out-of-sample behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbation-based PAC-Bayes framework of Neyshabur et al. [40] and Liao et al. [33] by removing the requirement for spectrally-normalized layers, replacing it with a set of general conditions (Equations 6–9) that are satisfied by heterogeneous architectures including PersLay [5], GCNs, and MPGNNs. The key conceptual departure is the compositional structure: the authors prove that models satisfying these conditions are closed under both sequential composition with MLPs (Lemma 3) and parallel concatenation (Lemma 4), yielding a general recipe for bounding arbitrary compositions. This is the first work to provide data-dependent generalization bounds for persistence-diagram vectorization schemes (subsuming landscapes, images, and silhouettes) and for GNNs augmented with PersLay in parallel mode, building on the PH-GNN architectures of Horn et al. [20] and Immonen et al. [22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional PAC-Bayes framework fills a critical gap: PH-augmented GNNs have shown empirical gains in expressivity and performance, but their generalization behavior was previously uncharacterized, leaving practitioners without theoretical guidance for architectural choices (e.g., PersLay width, aggregation function). The derived spectral-norm regularizer provides a principled, bound-informed penalty that consistently reduces generalization gaps across multiple datasets and GNN architectures, suggesting a practical path toward more robust topological graph learning. Open questions include extending the analysis to parametrized (learnable) filtration functions, tightening the remaining gap between theoretical bounds and empirical performance, and whether the compositional framework can handle more complex topologies such as multi-scale or multi-dimensional PH descriptors.
