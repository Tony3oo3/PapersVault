## Problem

Existing pessimistic value iteration (PEVI) methods for model-based offline RL, such as MOPO and MOBILE, rely on Monte Carlo sampling to estimate the Bellman target and its uncertainty. This sampling introduces high estimator variance that distorts gradient signals, poorly approximates the first two moments of the Bellman target even with thousands of samples, and forces the use of overly conservative confidence radii, all of which significantly delay convergence. The paper identifies this sampling-induced randomness as the primary bottleneck and asks whether a deterministic alternative can provide tighter, more informative uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples from continuous-state MuJoCo environments (halfcheetah, hopper, walker2d) and a pre-trained heteroscedastic Gaussian ensemble environment model that outputs a predictive distribution N(μ, Σ) over the next state. At deployment, a single state is fed to the learned policy to produce an action. The key structural detail is that during training, the full predictive distribution (mean and diagonal covariance) of s' from the elite ensemble models is propagated through the Q-network, while real dataset tuples are assigned zero variance to distinguish them from synthetic rollouts.

## Method

MOMBO replaces Monte Carlo sampling of the Bellman target with progressive moment matching: starting from the Gaussian predictive distribution of the next state s' produced by the environment model, it alternates between (i) an affine transformation of the mean and variance through each linear layer and (ii) an analytical computation of the first two moments of the ReLU activation applied to a Gaussian (via the Frey–Hinton closed-form expressions), re-approximating the output as a new Gaussian at each hidden layer. After L layers, the resulting mean μ_MM and variance σ_MM² of the Q-value distribution are used to construct a lower confidence bound r(s,a) + γμ_MM − βγσ_MM as the pessimistic Bellman target. This requires only two forward passes through the critic (one per moment) regardless of network depth, versus N passes for Monte Carlo. The paper further proves that the suboptimality bound for MOMBO is deterministic and does not depend on R_max²/(1−γ)², making it provably tighter than the probabilistic Hoeffding-based bound for sampling-based PEVI.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (12 tasks across halfcheetah, hopper, walker2d with four dataset types), MOMBO achieves an average normalized reward of 79.5, competitive with MOBILE (79.8) and substantially above MOPO (67.6). More importantly, MOMBO achieves the highest average AULC score of 70.5 (vs. 67.7 for MOBILE and 57.5 for MOPO), indicating significantly faster convergence. On the more realistic mixed offline dataset with only 1% expert data, MOMBO attains 37.0 average normalized reward and 29.5 AULC, outperforming MOBILE (32.4 / 28.0) and MOPO (27.9 / 23.5). MOMBO also provides tighter Bellman target uncertainty estimates in all 12 D4RL tasks and higher accuracy in 9 of 12 tasks compared to both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOMBO is compared against two representative PEVI baselines: MOPO (Yu et al., 2020), which penalizes based on next-state variance, and MOBILE (Sun et al., 2023), which propagates next-state uncertainty through Monte Carlo sampling. On the D4RL benchmark, MOMBO ties MOBILE on average normalized reward (79.5 vs. 79.8) but clearly wins on AULC (70.5 vs. 67.7), confirming faster convergence; both substantially outperform MOPO (67.6 NR, 57.5 AULC). On the mixed offline dataset with only 1% expert data, MOMBO achieves 37.0 normalized reward and 29.5 AULC, beating MOBILE (32.4 / 28.0) and MOPO (27.9 / 23.5), demonstrating superior performance under limited expert supervision. In the uncertainty quantification experiment (Table 2), MOMBO provides tighter Bellman target error estimates in all 12 D4RL tasks and higher accuracy (valid upper-bound rate) in 9 of 12 tasks, indicating its deterministic moment-matching penalty is both sharper and more reliable than the Monte Carlo-based penalties of MOBILE or the state-variance penalty of MOPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOMBO repurposes progressive moment matching from deterministic variational inference for Bayesian neural networks (Wu et al., 2019; Frey and Hinton, 1999) into the offline RL setting, replacing the Monte Carlo sampling used by MOBILE (Sun et al., 2023) to propagate next-state uncertainty through the Q-network. The algorithmic skeleton follows the PEVI meta-algorithm (Jin et al., 2021) and the MBPO-style heteroscedastic ensemble model (Janner et al., 2019), with the key departure being a deterministic, two-pass-through approximation of the Bellman target distribution instead of N stochastic forward passes. The theoretical analysis draws on Wasserstein distance bounds and Hoeffding's inequality to contrast the probabilistic suboptimality guarantee of sampling-based PEVI with a tighter, deterministic guarantee for the moment matching variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why Monte Carlo sampling in Bellman target estimation is a convergence bottleneck and offers a principled, computationally cheaper alternative with provably tighter suboptimality bounds. However, the practical gains over MOBILE are modest on D4RL (79.5 vs. 79.8 average normalized reward), and the method is constrained to activation functions with analytically tractable moments (primarily ReLU) and assumes normally distributed Q-values. Open questions include extending moment matching to heavy-tailed distributions, BatchNorm, and other non-tractable transformations, as well as whether the deterministic bound's advantage persists in higher-dimensional or more complex MDPs beyond MuJoCo locomotion tasks.
