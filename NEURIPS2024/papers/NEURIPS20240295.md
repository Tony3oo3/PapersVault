## Problem

Offline RL under model mismatch faces two distinct sources of uncertainty—data sparsity from limited dataset coverage and distributional shift between the data-collection and deployment environments—that prior works address separately via LCB reward penalties and DRO uncertainty sets, creating methodological redundancy. This paper asks whether both pessimism principles can be unified into a single distributionally robust MDP formulation without additional structural overhead, and whether such unification yields tighter sample complexity guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a fixed offline dataset of N tuples (s, a, s′, r), a pre-specified divergence type (total variation, χ², or KL) with a domain-expert-chosen radius R for model mismatch, and tabular state/action spaces of size S and A. It outputs a robust stationary policy π̃ that selects only actions observed in the dataset (a tie-breaking constraint in the value iteration). The key design choice is the data-dependent penalty κ_{s,a} added to the uncertainty set radius, which is inversely proportional to the visit count N(s,a) and takes different functional forms (square-root, linear, or log-scaled) depending on the divergence model.

## Method

The core contribution is a single unified uncertainty set P̃_{s,a} = {q ∈ Δ(S) : D(q, P̂_{s,a}) ≤ R + κ_{s,a}} that simultaneously captures model mismatch (via R) and estimation uncertainty from limited data (via κ_{s,a}), eliminating the need for separate LCB penalties or bi-level DRO formulations. For the total variation model (a metric), a distribution-based design ensures set inclusion P ⊆ P̃ with high probability; for the non-metric χ² and KL models, a value-function-based design instead balances the negative pessimism from the enlarged set against the concentration error of the empirical kernel. The resulting policy is obtained via standard robust value iteration with a polynomial-time support function evaluation, modified with a tie-breaking rule that restricts output actions to those present in the dataset. The paper derives near-optimal sample complexity bounds for all three divergence models, improving on prior TV results, matching state-of-the-art KL results, and providing the first finite-sample guarantee for the χ² model.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contribution is in sample complexity bounds for offline robust RL under model mismatch. Under the TV model, the proposed method achieves Õ(C^{π*}S/((1-γ)³ε²)·min{S/(1-γ), 1/μ_min}), improving on [5]'s Õ(C^{π*}S²/((1-γ)⁴ε²)) in both S and (1-γ) dependence. The paper also provides the first finite-sample result for the χ² divergence model: Õ(C^{π*}S/((1-γ)⁴ε²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: [36] (Shi & Chi, DRVI-LCB) which uses a separate reward penalty term plus a DRO uncertainty set, and [5] (Blanchet et al., Double Pessimism) which employs a two-layer DRO formulation. Against [36], the proposed method matches the KL divergence sample complexity exactly, and additionally provides results for TV and χ² models where [36] has no guarantee. Against [5], the proposed method strictly improves on both TV (reducing S² to S and (1-γ)⁴ to (1-γ)³) and KL (reducing S² to S and eliminating the exp((1-γ)⁻¹) factor). In experiments on Frozen-Lake and Gambler environments, the value-function-based variant of the proposed algorithm shows a smaller sub-optimality gap and faster convergence than both the distribution-based variant and DRVI-LCB, while the non-robust DP baseline converges significantly slower. Computationally, the proposed single-uncertainty-set approach avoids the NP-hard non-rectangular optimization in [5] and the per-step max-min operator in [36], yielding polynomial-time solvability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the distributionally robust MDP framework [Iyengar 2005, Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004] and the offline RL pessimism literature [Jin et al. 2021, Rashidinejad et al. 2021, Li et al. 2022], but departs from the two most directly related works—Shi & Chi [36] and Blanchet et al. [5]—which address data-sparsity uncertainty (via reward penalties/LCB) and model-mismatch uncertainty (via DRO) as separate mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is unifying both pessimism principles into a single uncertainty set whose radius is the sum of a model-mismatch term R and a data-dependent confidence term κ, eliminating the need for a two-layer DRO [5] or an auxiliary penalty term [36]. The paper also extends the analysis to the χ² divergence model, for which no prior offline robust RL result existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-uncertainty-set formulation is practically significant because it reduces computational complexity from NP-hard (two-layer DRO in [5]) to polynomial time, and simplifies implementation by removing the max-min penalty structure of LCB approaches. The first finite-sample guarantee for χ²-divergence offline robust RL and the improved TV sample complexity (linear in S vs. quadratic) are meaningful theoretical advances. However, the results are restricted to tabular MDPs under a partial-coverage assumption; extending the framework to linear MDPs or function-approximation settings—where most practical offline RL problems live—remains open and is acknowledged as future work.
