## Problem

Learning rate schedules that achieve state-of-the-art convergence (e.g., cosine, linear decay) require the total training duration T to be specified in advance, while schedule-free alternatives like Polyak-Ruppert averaging are substantially outperformed in practice. The paper asks whether an iterate-averaging approach can match or exceed schedule-based performance without requiring T, closing a long-standing theory-practice gap in stochastic optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240320/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as standard SGD or AdamW (stochastic gradients, initial parameters, a fixed learning rate, and a momentum coefficient β ∈ [0,1]) with no additional hyperparameters. The returned model is the weighted running average x_T of the base iterate sequence z, not the last iterate z_T. When learning-rate warmup is used, the averaging weights c_t are computed as γ_t²/Σγ_i² rather than the uniform 1/(t+1), and the optimal base learning rate is typically larger (e.g., D/G scale) than what classical theory prescribes.

## Method

Schedule-Free learning maintains three sequences: a base iterate z updated by the standard optimizer (SGD or AdamW), a weighted running average x, and a gradient-evaluation point y = (1−β)z + βx that interpolates between the two. The gradient is evaluated at y, the z-sequence takes the usual optimizer step, and x is updated as a weighted average incorporating the new z. The parameter β interpolates between Polyak-Ruppert averaging (β=0) and primal averaging (β=1), with β≈0.9 working well in practice; this form of momentum is worst-case optimal for any β in the convex Lipschitz setting, unlike EMA momentum. The theoretical backbone is a new online-to-batch conversion theorem (Theorem 2) that unifies PR averaging, primal averaging, and linear-decay schedules as special cases, and a Bregman-divergence refinement (Theorem 5) that yields accelerated O(D²L/T² + Dσ/√T) rates for smooth losses via optimistic online learning. The method also supports large fixed learning rates (γ = D/G) under a verifiable trajectory condition, explaining its empirical advantage over theory-prescribed step sizes.

## Results

Across 28 problems ranging from logistic regression to large-scale deep learning, Schedule-Free methods closely track the Pareto frontier of loss vs. training time in a single run, matching or exceeding heavily-tuned cosine and linear decay schedules. On deep learning tasks, Schedule-Free out-performs state-of-the-art cosine schedules on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, IWSLT-14, and OpenWebText GPT-2, out-performs linear decay schedules on fastMRI and Criteo DLRM, and ties on ImageNet ResNet-50 and MAE fine-tuning. On the MLCommons AlgoPerf self-tuning track (single hyperparameter set across 8 problems), Schedule-Free AdamW out-performs the NAdamW baseline on WMT, ViT, fastMRI, and OGBG, ties on Conformer and Criteo, and marginally under-performs on DeepSpeech. On 12 LibSVM logistic regression problems, Schedule-Free converges faster on 11/12 and achieves higher final accuracy on 6/12 compared to the linear decay baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art Linear Decay (LD) schedule from Zamani and Glineur (2023) / Defazio et al. (2023), Schedule-Free out-performs on fastMRI and Criteo DLRM (where LD is the customary schedule), out-performs on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, IWSLT-14, and OpenWebText GPT-2 (where cosine is the customary schedule), and ties on ImageNet ResNet-50 and MAE fine-tuning. Against Polyak-Ruppert averaging (Polyak 1990; Ruppert 1988), Schedule-Free consistently out-performs across all problems, with PR averaging showing much worse practical convergence despite theoretical optimality. Against Primal averaging (Tao et al. 2018; Nesterov and Shikhman 2015), Schedule-Free clearly wins, as primal averaging is shown to converge too slowly to be practical in all tested settings. The key practical advantage is that Schedule-Free requires no stopping time T to be specified in advance, while all schedule-based baselines require it, and Schedule-Free introduces no additional hyperparameters beyond the base optimizer's momentum parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in optimization theory: Polyak-Ruppert/primal iterate averaging and learning rate schedules that achieve optimal last-iterate convergence (Zamani & Glineur 2023; Defazio et al. 2023). The key insight is a new online-to-batch conversion theorem (Theorem 2) that shows a constant-weight interpolation between PR averaging (β=0) and primal averaging (β=1) subsumes both classical averaging methods and the linear decay schedule as special cases, while requiring no knowledge of the stopping time T. The approach also connects to Nesterov acceleration (the y_t interpolation resembles the AC-SA form) and to D-Adaptation (Defazio & Mishchenko 2023) for justifying large fixed learning rates, and its momentum-like update (Theorem 4) reveals a time-varying momentum structure distinct from standard heavy-ball or EMA momentum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Schedule-Free methods remove the need to pre-specify training duration, closely track the Pareto frontier of loss vs. time during a single run, and require no additional hyperparameters beyond standard momentum. The MLCommons 2024 AlgoPerf Self-Tuning track win provides strong independent validation across 8 diverse problems with a single hyperparameter set. Open questions include: a full theoretical understanding of why large fixed learning rates (D/G) work in practice beyond the quadratic case, handling of batch normalization running statistics on the x-sequence, and extension to sparse/adaptive optimizers (e.g., AdaGrad for DLRM embeddings). The primary limitation is that optimal LR and weight decay values still differ from scheduled baselines and require a sweep.
