## Problem

Existing multi-viewpoint object-centric learning methods rely on random or sequential viewpoint selection, which can be suboptimal since certain scenes are more sensitive to information from specific viewpoints, leading to redundancy or missed details. Additionally, multi-viewpoint methods lack the high-quality generative capabilities of diffusion-based decoders, which have so far been limited to single-image or video settings rather than viewpoint-independent scene representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240627/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 128×128 RGB images from multiple viewpoints (12 for CLEVRTEX, 8 for GSO/ShapeNet) paired with spherical-coordinate viewpoint timesteps as input. It requires only 4 viewpoints for training (vs. 8 for baselines), with the active selection strategy iteratively choosing the most informative viewpoints from the remaining unknown set. Outputs include viewpoint-independent object slots (K slots of dimension D), per-viewpoint segmentation masks, reconstructed images, novel-viewpoint synthesized images, and compositional scene images generated by shuffling and regrouping slots across scenes.

## Method

AVS combines three key components: (1) Multi-Viewpoint Slot Attention, which extends standard Slot Attention by concatenating viewpoint representations with object slots during each GRU iteration and averaging the updated object slots across all viewpoints to enforce viewpoint-independence, using frozen DINO ViT features; (2) an Active Viewpoint Selection strategy that iteratively predicts images from unknown viewpoints via a pre-trained slot-conditioned diffusion decoder (using a fast 5–10 step sampler), re-runs Slot Attention on the augmented observation set, and selects the viewpoint whose predicted image yields the largest slot representation change (minimum cosine similarity) as the next observation; (3) a slot-conditioned latent diffusion decoder (built on LSD/Stable Diffusion) that reconstructs and generates images conditioned on the viewpoint-independent slots plus a target viewpoint embedding. Training uses a combined loss of diffusion MSE and feature-space reconstruction with a curriculum on the weighting hyperparameter λ, and the entire active selection loop operates without gradient computation.

## Results

On CLEVRTEX, GSO, and ShapeNet, AVS (Active) achieves the best ARI-O (86.1, 82.2, 75.2) and mIoU (54.3, 64.4, 58.6) across all three datasets, while also attaining the lowest FID (80.3, 87.2, 99.2) for compositional generation. Notably, AVS is trained with only 4 viewpoints per scene, whereas all baselines use 8 viewpoints. LSD achieves slightly better LPIPS on CLEVRTEX (0.153 vs. 0.175) and ShapeNet (0.172 vs. 0.175), attributed to its use of viewpoint-specific slots rather than viewpoint-independent ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVS (Active) outperforms all three baselines on ARI-O and mIoU across every dataset, with the largest margins over SIMONe (e.g., +62.1 ARI-O on CLEVRTEX, +36.4 on GSO, +34.3 on ShapeNet) and OCLOC (e.g., +10.3 ARI-O on CLEVRTEX, +12.4 on GSO, +6.2 on ShapeNet). Against LSD, the closest competitor, AVS (Active) leads by +8.0 ARI-O and +8.6 mIoU on CLEVRTEX, +9.8 ARI-O and +20.7 mIoU on GSO, and +4.1 ARI-O and +10.1 mIoU on ShapeNet, while LSD retains a slight LPIPS edge on CLEVRTEX (0.153 vs. 0.175) and ShapeNet (0.172 vs. 0.175). On compositional generation (FID), AVS (Active) achieves the best score on all three datasets, beating LSD by 30.6 (CLEVRTEX), 11.7 (GSO), and 7.1 (ShapeNet). The active selection strategy consistently improves over the random selection variant with the same architecture, gaining +1.2 ARI-O and +1.6 mIoU on CLEVRTEX, +2.6 ARI-O and +2.1 mIoU on GSO, and +3.6 ARI-O and +5.1 mIoU on ShapeNet, with the gap widening on more complex datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Latent Slot Diffusion (LSD) from single-image to multi-viewpoint object-centric learning by introducing a Multi-Viewpoint Slot Attention mechanism that averages slot updates across viewpoints to produce viewpoint-independent representations, and by conditioning a slot-conditioned diffusion decoder on viewpoint embeddings. It departs from prior multi-viewpoint methods (MulMON, SIMONe, OCLOC) that rely on random or sequential viewpoint selection by proposing an active selection strategy that iteratively predicts unknown-viewpoint images via the diffusion decoder and selects the viewpoint with maximum slot-representation disparity. The use of a frozen DINO ViT as the feature backbone and a GRU-based iterative slot update follows the Slot Attention lineage, while the diffusion-based decoder addresses a gap left by LSD and SlotDiffusion, which were designed for single images and videos respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The active viewpoint selection strategy demonstrates that fewer, strategically chosen viewpoints (4) can outperform more randomly selected ones (8), offering a practical path toward data-efficient multi-viewpoint object-centric learning. The ability to synthesize novel viewpoints and perform cross-viewpoint image editing (removal, insertion, swapping) opens applications in scene understanding and generative editing that prior mixture-decoder methods could not support. However, all experiments are conducted on synthetic datasets (CLEVRTEX, GSO, ShapeNet) at 128×128 resolution, leaving open whether the active selection strategy generalizes to real-world scenes with uncontrolled viewpoints and complex backgrounds. The high training complexity of the iterative selection loop (requiring a pre-trained diffusion model and multiple forward passes per candidate viewpoint) remains a significant practical barrier.
