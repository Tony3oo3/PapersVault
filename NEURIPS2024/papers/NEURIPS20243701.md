## Problem

Iterative preference optimization methods such as Iterative DPO, Self-Rewarding LLMs, and SPIN have shown strong gains on general instruction tuning but yield only modest or even negative improvements on reasoning tasks. The paper identifies that standard DPO, when applied to reasoning, fails to properly increase the probability of correct chain-of-thought sequences and instead can decrease them, leaving a gap in how to effectively leverage preference optimization for CoT reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed set of training prompts with gold final answers (e.g., GSM8K word problems, MATH competition problems, ARC multiple-choice questions) and requires only the correctness of the final answer for reward computation, not the quality of intermediate reasoning steps. At each iteration, the model generates N candidate responses per prompt, each consisting of a chain-of-thought followed by a final answer in a specified format (e.g., 'Final answer: ...' or '\boxed{...}'). The output is a sequence of progressively improved language models (M_1 through M_T) that produce higher-accuracy CoT reasoning, evaluated by exact-match accuracy on held-out test sets.

## Method

Iterative RPO alternates between two steps: (i) generating N chain-of-thought-and-answer candidates per training prompt using the current model M_t, and (ii) constructing preference pairs where winners have correct final answers and losers have incorrect ones, then training M_{t+1} with a combined DPO+NLL loss. The key technical contribution is the addition of a negative log-likelihood term on the winning response (normalized by response length, weighted by hyperparameter α) to the standard DPO objective, which the authors show is essential—without it, DPO training actually decreases the log-probability of chosen sequences. The previous iteration's model M_t serves as the reference model in the DPO ratio, and the procedure iterates over a fixed set of training prompts (unlike Self-Rewarding LLMs, which introduce new prompts each round), requiring only a binary correctness check on final answers rather than a sophisticated reward model. This design avoids the difficulty of verifying reasoning-step correctness while still leveraging negative examples of incorrect CoT chains, which SFT-based methods like STaR cannot exploit.

## Results

On GSM8K, Iterative RPO improves Llama-2-70B-Chat from 55.6% zero-shot CoT to 81.6% after four iterations (88.7% with majority voting over 32 samples). On ARC-Challenge, accuracy rises from 77.8% to 86.7% (87.9% with majority voting), and on MATH from 12.5% to 20.8% (29.1% with majority voting). Gains are observed across all three iterations on each task, with diminishing returns in later iterations (e.g., GSM8K: +17.5%, +4.9%, +3.1%, +0.5% per iteration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), Iterative RPO's first iteration achieves 73.1% on GSM8K versus 61.8% for DPO initialized from Llama-2-70B-Chat and 60.3% for DPO initialized from an SFT model — a gap of over 11 points that the authors attribute to the added NLL loss term, which prevents the chosen-sequence log-probabilities from decreasing during training. Against STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022), which uses SFT on self-generated correct rationales, Iterative RPO's single iteration (73.1%) outperforms STaR's single iteration (65.2%) and even STaR with twice as much data (66.9%), demonstrating that preference optimization with negative examples is more effective than SFT alone for reasoning. On ARC-Challenge, Iterative RPO (86.7%) beats DPO (83.5%) and SFT on chosen sequences (79.8%); on MATH, it reaches 20.8% versus 12.4% for DPO and 16.8% for SFT, with DPO actually degrading below the 12.5% zero-shot CoT baseline. The paper also shows that simply doubling the paired data in one iteration (74.8%) is inferior to performing two full iterations (78.0%), confirming that the iterative model update — not just more data — drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative preference optimization framework of Self-Rewarding LLMs (Yuan et al., 2024) by simplifying the loop: it uses fixed training prompts with gold-label rewards rather than LLM-as-judge scoring, and critically augments the DPO loss with a negative log-likelihood term on winning sequences. It departs from the STaR/ReST^EM/V-STaR line of work, which relies on SFT or trains a separate verifier, by using DPO+NLL to directly optimize the generator with both positive and negative CoT examples. Compared to SPIN, which requires human-annotated winning responses, this method generates both winners and losers from the model itself, requiring only final-answer correctness as supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that iterative preference optimization—previously shown to yield only modest gains on reasoning—can produce large improvements (e.g., 55.6%→81.6% on GSM8K) when the NLL term is included, and that standard DPO alone actually degrades reasoning performance. This provides a practical, simple recipe for improving LLM reasoning without additional data or human-in-the-loop. Key limitations include the requirement for gold answers with a clear correctness metric, evaluation on only three tasks with a single 70B model, and the observation that gains saturate after a few iterations on fixed prompts. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to open-ended tasks without unambiguous answers, whether accumulating data across iterations helps, and whether the NLL term remains necessary when training data is not drawn from the current model distribution.
