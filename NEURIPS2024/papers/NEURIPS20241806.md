## Problem

Existing stochastic methods for difference-of-weakly-convex (DWC) optimization and non-smooth weakly-convex-strongly-concave (WCSC) min-max optimization that achieve the state-of-the-art O(ε^{-4}) non-asymptotic convergence rate are all double-loop, making them complex to implement and requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning. This paper introduces the DMax optimization framework that unifies both problem families and proposes the first single-loop stochastic algorithm with matching convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic (sub)gradient estimators of the component functions φ(x,y) and ψ(x,z) with bounded second moments, along with initial points for the primal variable x, proximal estimators x_φ and x_ψ, and dual variables y and z. The output is a uniformly sampled iterate x_φ^{t̄} or x_ψ^{t̄} from the trajectory, which is guaranteed to be a nearly ε-critical point of the DMax/DWC problem or a nearly ε-stationary point of the WCSC min-max problem. The convergence rate of O(ε^{-4}) iterations holds under only partial smoothness assumptions (Lipschitz continuity of partial gradients in the dual variables), not full smoothness of the objective.

## Method

SMAG applies Moreau envelope smoothing to each max-structured component function Φ(x)=max_y φ(x,y) and Ψ(x)=max_z ψ(x,z) individually, yielding a smooth surrogate F_γ(x)=Φ_γ(x)−Ψ_γ(x) whose gradient involves inaccessible proximal points. The algorithm maintains running estimators x_φ^t and x_ψ^t for these proximal points and dual variables y_t, z_t for the argmax solutions, updating all of them with a single stochastic gradient step per iteration: x_φ and x_ψ receive a proximal-like pull toward x_t plus a stochastic subgradient, while y and z receive projected stochastic gradient ascent. The approximate gradient G_{t+1}=(1/γ)(x_t−x_φ^{t+1})−(1/γ)(x_t−x_ψ^{t+1}) then drives one step of gradient descent on x_t. The key analytical insight is that the strong concavity of φ in y and ψ in z ensures the dual subproblems converge fast enough that single-step updates suffice to control the proximal estimation error, enabling a single-loop design that still achieves the O(ε^{-4}) rate previously requiring nested inner loops.

## Results

On CelebA with the Attractive/Male task, SMAG achieves the best pAUC of 0.8605±0.002 and the lowest fairness disparities (EOD 0.1900, EOP 0.1648, DP 0.4116) among all min-max baselines. On the Big Nose/Male task, SMAG achieves pAUC 0.8058±0.001 with EOD 0.2708, EOP 0.2148, and DP 0.4333. For PU learning on Fashion-MNIST, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and FER2013, SMAG consistently achieves the lowest training loss compared to all double-loop baselines (SDCA, SSDC-SPG, SSDC-Adagrad, SBCD) and SGD, while being a single-loop method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For pAUC with adversarial fairness on CelebA, SMAG (single-loop) outperforms all double-loop baselines: it beats EGDA (Epoch-GDA) by 0.006 in pAUC and 0.039 in EOD on Attractive/Male, and beats SGDA by 0.010 in pAUC and 0.080 in EOD on the same task. On Big Nose/Male, SMAG achieves the best EOD (0.2708 vs. 0.2829 for SOPA and 0.2846 for SGDA) and best DP (0.4333 vs. 0.4424 for SOPA). SMAG* (without fairness regularizer) matches SMAG on pAUC but has higher disparity metrics, confirming the benefit of the adversarial fairness component. For PU learning, SMAG (single-loop, O(ε⁻⁴)) surpasses all double-loop baselines including SBCD (O(ε⁻⁶)), SDCA (O(ε⁻⁴)), and SSDC variants in training loss across all four datasets, while requiring fewer hyperparameters due to its single-loop structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Moreau envelope smoothing framework for DC optimization (Sun & Sun [32], Yao et al. [45]) and stochastic DC algorithms (SDCA [26], SSDC [39]), but departs from them by applying smoothing to each max-structured component individually rather than to the overall objective, and by replacing the required double-loop inner iterations with single-step updates. It also extends the non-smooth WCSC min-max line (PG-SMD [29], Epoch-GDA [40]) by removing the need for special structural assumptions on the maximization subproblem. The key conceptual departure is the DMax unification: both DWC and WCSC min-max are shown to be special cases of a single problem class, enabling one algorithm (SMAG) to handle both with a single-loop design that matches the O(ε⁻⁴) rate previously achievable only with double-loop methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-loop design eliminates the inner-loop hyperparameter and the associated implementation complexity that plagues all prior state-of-the-art stochastic methods for non-smooth DWC and WCSC min-max problems, making the O(ε⁻⁴) rate practically accessible. The strong concavity assumption on φ(x,·) and ψ(x,·) is a significant limitation that restricts applicability to problems where the dual variables have a well-conditioned landscape; relaxing this to mere concavity or even non-concavity is the natural next step. The empirical validation on PU learning and pAUC with adversarial fairness demonstrates practical relevance, though the fairness gains over Epoch-GDA are modest and the PU learning comparison, while favorable, involves relatively small-scale linear models.
