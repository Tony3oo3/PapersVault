## Problem

Existing cardiac motion tracking methods either lack topology-preserving constraints and temporal coherence (non-parametric approaches), are computationally prohibitive (classical diffeomorphic registration), or fail to capture long-term temporal dependencies and region-specific motion variability (deep learning methods that only register adjacent frame pairs). In particular, Lagrangian-strain-based deep methods accumulate errors over long sequences without regularizing temporal variations, and global trajectory flows mask significant regional differences in motion phase, amplitude, and direction across cardiac structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential cardiac image volumes — 2D echocardiogram frames (256×256, 32 frames sampled per video) or 3D cardiac MRI volumes (128×128×32 voxels) — which are decomposed into non-overlapping patches (16×16×16 for 2D, 8×8×8 for 3D) and embedded into a fixed-dimensional feature space. It outputs a diffeomorphic motion field (velocity field) per time step, integrated over N=7 composition steps to produce topology-preserving deformations that warp the reference frame to all subsequent frames, yielding tracked cardiac structures with guaranteed invertibility.

## Method

GPTrack combines a bidirectional recursive transformer (GPTrack layer) with a Gaussian Process latent coding layer and a diffeomorphic decoder. The GPTrack layer maintains forward and backward hidden states that aggregate temporal information via linear self-attention over concatenated current input and previous hidden state, enabling variable-length sequence processing without parallelizing all frames. The GP layer imposes a Matérn-kernel prior in the latent space using position-based distance (spatial patch encoding × sinusoidal temporal encoding), converted to a linear state-space model solved via Kalman filtering to reduce complexity from O(T³) to O(T). The decoder maps GP-filtered latent codes to velocity fields that are composed diffeomorphically. Training is fully unsupervised, minimizing a loss combining bidirectional VAE KL divergence, motion-field smoothness (ℓ₂ total variation), and negative normalized cross-correlation between the reference and tracked frames.

## Results

On the ACDC 4D cardiac MRI dataset, GPTrack-XL achieves the best average Dice score of 82.65% (vs. 82.30% for FSDiffReg and 80.83% for DeepTag), along with the highest PSNR (35.52) and SSIM (86.19). On the CardiacUDA echocardiogram dataset, GPTrack-XL reaches 82.37% average Dice, outperforming the previous best DeepTag (79.41%) by nearly 3 percentage points, with the lowest non-positive Jacobian determinant (0.027%) among all deep learning methods. On CAMUS, GPTrack-XL attains 85.29% average Dice, surpassing DeepTag (84.80%) and VM-DIF (84.50%). GPTrack-XL also reduces inference time by ~97% compared to FSDiffReg (0.034s vs. 1.106s) while using only 1.094M parameters (vs. 70.71M for Transmatch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPTrack-XL outperforms all 14 baselines across all three datasets. On ACDC, it edges out FSDiffReg (82.65% vs. 82.30% avg. Dice) and Transmatch (82.65% vs. 81.35%), while achieving the best PSNR (35.52 vs. 35.34) and SSIM (86.19 vs. 85.85) with 32× faster inference (0.034s vs. 1.106s) and 65× fewer parameters than Transmatch (1.094M vs. 70.71M). On CardiacUDA, GPTrack-XL surpasses DeepTag by 2.96% in average Dice (82.37% vs. 79.41%) and DiffuseMorph by 3.10%, while also achieving the best PSNR (32.03 vs. 29.48) and the lowest non-positive Jacobian determinant (0.027% vs. 0.027% for DeepTag). On CAMUS, GPTrack-XL leads DeepTag by 0.49% (85.29% vs. 84.80%) and VM-DIF by 0.79%. Compared to non-rigid methods (LDDMM, RDMM, SyN), GPTrack-XL improves average Dice by 8–10 points on ACDC and 9–13 points on CardiacUDA, while reducing computation from ~1500s to 0.034s. The ablation (Table 5) confirms both the bidirectional recursive cell (+1.4% Dice) and the Gaussian Process layer (+1.2% Dice) contribute independently to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPTrack extends the VAE-based diffeomorphic tracking framework established by DeepTag [15] and Sequence Morph [16] by introducing two key architectural innovations: (1) a bidirectional recursive transformer cell (adapted from [41]) that aggregates temporal information in both forward and backward passes, replacing the adjacent-frame-only registration of prior methods; and (2) a Gaussian Process with a Matérn kernel operating in the latent space, whose inference is made tractable via a state-space/Kalman filter formulation (reducing complexity from O(T³) to O(T)). This departs from prior diffeomorphic cardiac tracking methods that rely solely on Lagrangian strain integration, which the authors identify as prone to error accumulation over long sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in cardiac motion tracking: existing diffeomorphic deep learning methods degrade in accuracy over long temporal sequences due to unregularized Lagrangian displacement accumulation, while classical optimization-based diffeomorphic registration is too slow for clinical use. GPTrack's combination of GP-based temporal priors and bidirectional recurrence offers a principled way to enforce motion consistency while remaining lightweight (~97% less inference time than diffusion-based competitors). The acknowledged limitation—that positional encoding serves as the spatial prior and may not generalize to out-of-domain data—leaves open the question of how to learn more robust, data-driven representations of regional cardiac motion patterns across diverse patient populations and imaging protocols.
