## Problem

Existing aerial visual localization methods rely on high-quality textured 3D maps (SfM or mesh models) that are expensive to reconstruct, large to store (up to 42 GB/km²), and raise privacy concerns. Level-of-Detail (LoD) 3D city maps are lightweight (~2.84 MB/km²), easy to maintain, and privacy-preserving, but their lack of texture and detail makes traditional 2D-to-3D feature matching approaches essentially infeasible, leaving a gap in how to perform accurate 6-DoF localization from such simplified geometric representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB query image (512×480 or 720×480 pixels), a coarse sensor pose from GPS/IMU/compass (with typical errors of ±10 m in x/y, ±30 m in z, ±7.5° in yaw, and ~1° in pitch/roll), and a LoD 3D city map represented as wireframe edges. It outputs a refined 6-DoF camera pose (3D translation + 3D rotation). Notably, pitch and roll are fixed from the inertial sensor, so the cost-volume stage operates on 4-DoF (x, y, z, yaw) hypotheses, and the full 6-DoF refinement is applied only in the final Gauss-Newton step.

## Method

LoD-Loc uses a U-Net-based multi-scale feature extractor that outputs single-channel wireframe probability maps at three resolution levels, predicting the likelihood of each pixel belonging to a building edge. A hierarchical coarse-to-fine pose selection stage uniformly samples 4-DoF pose hypotheses around the sensor prior, projects discrete 3D wireframe points from the LoD map under each hypothesis, and scores alignment against the predicted wireframe to build a 4D cost volume; an adaptive uncertainty-based sampling strategy uses the variance of the previous level's softmax probability distribution to shrink the next level's search range. A differentiable Gauss-Newton optimizer then refines the selected pose to full 6-DoF by maximizing wireframe alignment on a higher-resolution feature map. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a negative log-likelihood loss on the cost volumes and a Huber-kernel reprojection loss on the refined pose, requiring no explicit 2D-3D correspondences or PnP RANSAC.

## Results

On the UAVD4L-LoD dataset (LoD3.0), LoD-Loc achieves 84.41% / 91.77% / 96.95% recall at (2m,2°) / (3m,3°) / (5m,5°) for in-trajectory queries and 95.94% / 99.00% / 99.36% for out-of-trajectory queries. On the Swiss-EPFL dataset (LoD2.0), it reaches 48.60% / 65.31% / 79.78% (in-place) and 37.73% / 57.26% / 77.57% (out-of-place). All keypoint- and line-based matching baselines (SIFT, SuperPoint+SuperGlue, LoFTR, RoMA, SOLD2, DeepLSD) applied to the LoD model (CadLoc) fail completely, scoring 0–10% recall, while LoD-Loc's wireframe-alignment approach remains robust. Inference time is 0.34 s per image, roughly 5× faster than the fastest baseline (e-LoFTR at 1.06 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CadLoc LoD-model baselines (SIFT+NN, SPP+SPG, LoFTR, e-LoFTR, RoMA, SOLD2, DeepLSD+SOLD2, DeepLSD+GlueStick), LoD-Loc wins by a landslide: all keypoint- and line-based matchers score 0–10% recall on both datasets, while LoD-Loc achieves 84–99% on UAVD4L-LoD and 38–80% on Swiss-EPFL. Against the UAVD4L textured-mesh baselines, LoD-Loc is competitive: on UAVD4L-LoD it slightly trails RoMA on in-trajectory 2m-2° (84.41 vs. 93.27) and 3m-3° (91.77 vs. 93.70) but surpasses it on all out-of-trajectory metrics (e.g., 99.36 vs. 95.53 at 5m-5°). On Swiss-EPFL, LoD-Loc edges out RoMA on in-place queries (48.60 vs. 45.98 at 2m-2°) but falls well behind on out-of-place queries (37.73 vs. 89.18 at 2m-2°), which the authors attribute to lower-quality synthetic training data and the sparser LoD2.0 geometry. Notably, LoD-Loc operates on a map that is ~15,000× smaller than the SfM map used by UAVD4L, making it suitable for on-device deployment, and it runs 5× faster (0.34 s vs. 1.79 s for SPP+SPG).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant retrieval-and-matching paradigm (SfM or textured mesh + PnP RANSAC) by proposing wireframe alignment as the core localization signal. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes the cost-volume pose estimation framework and U-Net feature extraction for a new alignment objective: matching projected LoD wireframe geometry against a neural-predicted wireframe probability map. The use of Level-of-Detail 3D city maps as a localization cue is novel; prior work on alternative maps (satellite imagery, OpenStreetMap) was limited to 3-DoF, and privacy-preserving approaches (line clouds, semantic maps) did not achieve 6-DoF aerial localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoD maps are up to 10^4 times smaller than textured 3D maps (2.84 MB vs. 42 GB per km²), enabling on-device aerial localization over large areas while inherently preserving privacy since only abstract building outlines are stored. The finding that wireframe-only geometry can surpass texture-based SOTA methods challenges the assumption that rich visual appearance is necessary for accurate 6-DoF pose estimation. Key limitations include the requirement for a known gravity direction and a GPS-derived location prior, restricting applicability in GPS-denied environments; the authors note that sequence-based methods could address this gap. The two released datasets (UAVD4L-LoD at LoD3.0, Swiss-EPFL at LoD2.0) fill a previously empty benchmark space for this problem.
