## Problem

Generalized neural wave functions that amortize the cost of solving the electronic Schrödinger equation across many molecules face a fundamental obstacle: Slater determinants require exactly N↑ + N↓ orbitals, forcing a discrete orbital selection problem that previous works solve with non-learnable hand-crafted algorithms (Globe) or fixed Hartree-Fock calculations (TAO). These constraints limit generalization to non-equilibrium, ionized, or excited systems and degrade accuracy. The paper asks whether a fully learnable, overparametrized antisymmetrization exists that removes the exact-orbital-count requirement entirely.

## Input / Output

The MetaGNN consumes a molecular graph of nuclear positions and charges (one node per nucleus) and outputs all wave-function parameters—orbital projections W, envelope decays σ, envelope weights π, and the antisymmetrizer A_pf—parametrized per nucleus and per edge so that the orbital count N_o = N_orb/nuc × N_n grows with system size. The electronic wave function then takes electron positions (N_e × 3) and the MetaGNN parameters to produce a scalar wave-function value; ground-state, ionization, and electron-affinity energies are extracted by minimizing the VMC local energy over samples drawn from Ψ². Unlike Slater-based methods, the number of orbitals is not fixed to N_e but satisfies N_o ≥ max{N↑, N↓}, and the spin-dependent scalars η are predicted for a fixed set of spin configurations independent of system size.

## Method

NeurPf replaces the Slater determinant with a Pfaffian: Ψ = Pf(Φ_pf · A_pf · Φ_pfᵀ), where A_pf is a learnable skew-symmetric matrix and Φ_pf is a permutation-equivariant orbital matrix with N_o ≥ max{N↑, N↓} columns. Because Pf(BABᵀ) = det(B)·Pf(A), the antisymmetry under electron permutation is guaranteed by the equivariance of Φ_pf alone, and the overparametrization eliminates the discrete orbital-selection problem that plagued Slater-based generalized wave functions. A MetaGNN (a 3-layer GNN with Bessel-filtered message passing) predicts all wave-function parameters from the molecular structure, with orbitals assigned per nucleus (N_orb/nuc per atom) so that smaller-system orbitals transfer to larger systems. The authors introduce memory-efficient envelopes that replace the full 5-D tensor of per-orbital, per-nucleus exponentials with a shared set of N_env exponentials linearly recombined per orbital, and a two-stage pretraining scheme that matches the Pfaffian to Hartree-Fock by jointly optimizing over SO(N_o) rotations (via the Cayley transform) and antisymmetric special-orthogonal matrices, avoiding the need to canonicalize HF orbitals across structures.

## Results

On the TinyMol dataset, NeurPf (32k steps) achieves mean energy differences of −3.7, 0.1, and −2.1 mE_h relative to CCSD(T) CBS on the small molecules (CNH, C2H4, COH2), surpassing the CCSD(T) reference by 1.9 mE_h on average. On the large molecules (C3H4, CN2H2, CNOH, CO2), NeurPf reaches 12.7, 5.5, 3.1, and 5.0 mE_h, a 6× lower error than CCSD(T). On the nitrogen dimer PES, NeurPf maintains a 2 mE_h average error even when augmented with ethene structures, whereas Globe degrades from 2.7 to 5.3 mE_h. A single NeurPf trained jointly on all second-row elements and their ionization/affinity states reaches chemical accuracy (~1.6 mE_h) without per-system optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurPf outperforms Globe (Gao & Günnemann, 2023a) on every benchmark: on the N2 PES it reduces the average error from 2.7 mE_h to 2 mE_h and, critically, does not degrade when ethene structures are added (Globe jumps to 5.3 mE_h); on TinyMol small it achieves 11.3 mE_h lower mean energy than Globe. Against TAO (Scherbela et al., 2024), NeurPf reports 5.9 mE_h lower energies on TinyMol small and 21.1 mE_h lower on TinyMol large (a 6× error reduction), and it surpasses the pretrained TAO after only 8k steps on small molecules. Compared to the 21 separately trained FermiNet models of Pfau et al. (2020), a single NeurPf reaches comparable chemical accuracy on second-row element ground states, ionization potentials, and electron affinities using 21× fewer total training steps. On the N2 PES, NeurPf also outperforms PESNet (Gao & Günnemann, 2022) and the FermiNet results of Fu et al. (2023). On hydrogen chains, NeurPf generalizes to longer chains better than both Globe+Moon and TAO, achieving errors below the Hartree-Fock baseline outside the training regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of generalized neural wave functions (PESNet, Globe, TAO) that amortize the cost of solving the electronic Schrödinger equation across multiple molecular structures. The key departure is replacing Slater determinants—which require exactly N↑ + N↓ orbitals and thus force a discrete orbital selection problem solved via hand-crafted greedy algorithms (Globe) or Hartree-Fock eigenvalue criteria (TAO)—with Pfaffians, a strict superset that permits overparametrization with N_o ≥ max{N↑, N↓} orbitals. This eliminates all non-learnable constraints, making the wave function fully differentiable and applicable to arbitrary spin configurations. The architecture reuses the Moon equivariant embedding and MetaGNN from Globe, while introducing memory-efficient envelopes and a novel pretraining scheme that matches Pfaffian orbitals to Hartree-Fock via SO(N) rotations and antisymmetric matrix optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the discrete orbital selection bottleneck, NeurPf enables a single fully learnable wave function to handle non-singlet systems (ionization, electron affinity) and generalize across molecules without performance degradation when enlarging training sets—capabilities that prior methods lacked. The variational advantage over CCSD(T) CBS on small molecules (1.9 mEh) suggests neural wave functions can serve as higher-accuracy reference generators for ML force fields and DFT functionals. Key limitations include a ~5× speed penalty over Slater determinants, restriction to small molecules (≤32 electrons), no treatment of periodic systems, and no exploitation of wave-function symmetries. Open questions include whether the overparametrization advantage persists at larger system sizes and whether the Pfaffian formulation can be accelerated via hardware-specific kernels.
