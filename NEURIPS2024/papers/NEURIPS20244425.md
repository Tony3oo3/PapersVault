## Problem

Text-to-video generative models produce outputs that are visually unappealing and misaligned with prompts, and aligning them via RLHF requires both large-scale video preference data and a video-specific reward model—neither of which existed at scale. Existing preference datasets contain only 2.5K–45K choices, and the sole prior alignment method (InstructVideo) repurposes an image-domain reward model (HPS v2) that cannot capture temporal coherence or dynamics.

## Input / Output

VIDEORM takes a text prompt and N=8 uniformly sampled frames from a video (each 256×256 to 512×512 depending on source) and outputs a scalar preference score. The VIDEOPREFER dataset provides 135K pairwise preference annotations across two dimensions—Prompt-Following and Video Quality—each scored on a 1-to-5 Likert scale by GPT-4V. DRaFT-V consumes the same prompt-video pairs plus the VIDEORM reward signal and outputs a LoRA-adapted text-to-video diffusion model (ModelScopeT2V, 16×256×256 resolution, T=1000 DDIM steps).

## Method

VIDEOPREFER is constructed by prompting GPT-4V to rate four candidate videos (from diverse T2V models plus real ActivityNet/MSR-VTT clips) per prompt on two axes, yielding 135K preference choices over 54K videos. VIDEORM modifies HPS v2 by inserting parameter-free Temporal Shift modules between every pair of ViT layers in the image encoder and appending a Temporal Transformer over the resulting frame-wise features; only the temporal transformer is trained (text encoder and ViT frozen) using a Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking loss over all C(4,2) comparisons per data item. For fine-tuning, DRaFT-V adapts the DRaFT algorithm to the video setting by truncating the backward pass to the last K=10 DDIM steps, feeding the full 8-frame video (rather than individual frames) into VIDEORM for reward computation, and applying LoRA to the diffusion model to limit trainable parameters and avoid catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the three human-crafted video preference benchmarks, VIDEORM achieves an average pair-wise preference prediction accuracy of 67.5%, outperforming the strongest image-domain baseline HPS v2 (59.3%) by 8.2 points. VIDEORM-V, trained solely on the MLLM-annotated VIDEOPREFER dataset without any human-labeled video preference data, still reaches 66.4% average accuracy, surpassing all image-domain baselines. In fine-tuning experiments, DRaFT-V with VIDEORM achieves higher human win rates than both the base ModelScopeT2V model and InstructVideo, while also converging faster and running 3.8× faster per inference step (1.3s vs. 5.0s for HPS v2 on 8-frame videos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDEORM outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest image-domain reward model HPS v2 (59.3% avg), VIDEORM gains 8.2 points, with the largest margin on T2VQA-DB (65.4 vs. 52.8, +12.6). PickScore (59.9%) and ImageReward (58.3%) are both surpassed by 7.6 and 9.2 points respectively on average. CLIP ViT-H/14 (54.0%) and the Aesthetic classifier (53.3%) trail by over 13 points, confirming that generic image-text alignment or aesthetic scoring is insufficient for video preference. In the fine-tuning task (Figure 5), DRaFT-V with VIDEORM achieves higher human win rates than InstructVideo (which uses HPS v2) and the un-fine-tuned ModelScopeT2V baseline, while DRaFT-H (same algorithm but HPS v2 reward) underperforms DRaFT-V, isolating the benefit of the video-specific reward model. VIDEORM also converges faster than HPS v2 under the same DRaFT algorithm and requires only 1.3s per inference versus 5.0s for HPS v2 on 8-frame videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF/RLAIF paradigm from text-to-image diffusion alignment (HPS v2, ImageReward, PickScore, DRaFT) to the text-to-video domain, where the only prior alignment work is InstructVideo, which repurposes the image-domain HPS v2 reward model. Architecturally, VIDEORM builds on HPS v2 (itself a CLIP-based reward model) by inserting Temporal Shift modules and a temporal transformer to capture inter-frame dynamics, drawing on video understanding techniques like TSM and CLIP4Clip. The data construction pipeline repurposes the RLAIF idea—previously validated for text-to-image annotation—by using GPT-4V as a scalable preference annotator, producing the first large-scale video preference dataset (VIDEOPREFER). The fine-tuning algorithm DRaFT-V adapts the image-domain DRaFT procedure to video by processing the full video as input rather than individual frames, eliminating the per-frame reward balancing required by InstructVideo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIDEOPREFER (135K annotations) and VIDEORM fill a critical gap: before this work, no general-purpose video-domain reward model or large-scale open-source video preference dataset existed, severely constraining research on aligning text-to-video generators. The validation that GPT-4V annotations match human annotator agreement (~70%) opens a scalable path for RLAIF in video, potentially reducing annotation costs by orders of magnitude. Key limitations include evaluation on a single base model (ModelScopeT2V at 16×256×256), a maximum of 8 input frames to VIDEORM, and reliance on GPT-4V's 10-frame processing limit. Open questions include whether these results transfer to higher-resolution or longer-duration video models, whether MLLM-annotated preferences truly capture the full spectrum of human video aesthetics, and whether the reward model generalizes as video generation quality improves.
