## Problem

The standard binary relevance surrogate for multi-label Hamming loss suffers from a sub-optimal √l dependency on the number of labels in H-consistency bounds and ignores label correlations entirely. Moreover, prior theoretical work established only Bayes-consistency (an asymptotic guarantee) for specific loss functions, leaving open whether a unified surrogate loss framework with stronger, non-asymptotic H-consistency bounds could be proven for any general multi-label loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes an instance x ∈ X and a label vector y ∈ {+1,−1}^l, and produces a real-valued scoring vector h(x) = (h(x,1),…,h(x,l)) whose sign gives the predicted label vector. The surrogate loss is computed over the full label space Y = {+1,−1}^l (2^l combinations), with the hypothesis set structured as H = F^l where F is a complete hypothesis set (e.g., linear functions or neural networks). The target loss L̄ is defined as a function of the discrete prediction h(x) = sign(h(x)) and the true label y, while the surrogate operates on the continuous scores h(x,i).

## Method

The paper introduces a multi-label logistic loss that is a weighted log-sum-exp over the full label space, where weights (1 − L̄(y′, y)) encode the target loss and the exponential term ∑ᵢ(y″ᵢ − y′ᵢ)h(x,i) captures label correlations through pairwise score differences. This is generalized to a family of multi-label comp-sum losses (replacing log with a general Ψ) and multi-label constrained losses, all parameterized by an arbitrary target multi-label loss L̄. The key theoretical result is an H-consistency bound with Γ(t) = 2√t that is independent of the number of labels l, in contrast to the l·Γ(·/l) bound for binary relevance; the proof reduces to bounding the conditional regret of the target loss by that of the surrogate via a general tool (Theorem B.1) and a characterization of conditional regret under completeness (Lemma B.2). Efficient gradient computation is achieved by reformulating the loss for linear hypotheses and evaluating the softmax-like term Q_w(i) via two single-source shortest-path algorithms on a weighted finite automaton over the (+,×) semiring, yielding O(l) per-sample gradient cost.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The key quantitative result is the H-consistency bound: the existing binary relevance surrogate yields a label-dependent bound of $l^{1/2}\epsilon^{1/2}$ (Corollary 3.2), while the proposed multi-label logistic loss achieves a label-independent bound of $2\epsilon^{1/2}$ (Theorem 4.1). The multi-label logistic loss is further shown to be H-consistent (and thus Bayes-consistent) for any general multi-label loss (Theorem 5.1), including Hamming loss, $F_\beta$-measure, subset 0/1 loss, Jaccard distance, precision@k, recall@k, and tree distance loss. The comp-sum extension (Theorem 6.1) and constrained loss extension (Theorem 7.1) provide analogous guarantees with $\Gamma(t) = 2\sqrt{t}$ or $\Gamma(t) = t$ depending on the specific loss form.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is between the existing binary relevance surrogate $\tilde{L}_{\text{br}}$ and the proposed multi-label logistic loss $\tilde{L}_{\log}$, both analyzed under H-consistency bounds. The binary relevance surrogate (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2) suffers from a sub-optimal $l^{1/2}$ factor in its consistency bound, making it degrade as the number of labels grows, and it fails to capture label correlations. The multi-label logistic loss (Theorem 4.1) eliminates this label-dependent factor entirely, yielding the tighter bound $2\epsilon^{1/2}$ independent of $l$, while explicitly accounting for label correlations through the joint scoring structure. Additionally, the binary relevance surrogate is only Bayes-consistent for Hamming loss and Precision@k, whereas the multi-label logistic loss is proven Bayes-consistent for any multi-label loss in the linear-fractional family and beyond (Theorem 5.1). The paper also introduces multi-label comp-sum losses (Theorem 6.1) and multi-label constrained losses (Theorem 7.1) as further extensions, all benefiting from H-consistency bounds that previous work had not established for multi-label settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the H-consistency bounds framework from Awasthi, Mao, Mohri, and Zhong (2022a,b) from multi-class classification to multi-label learning, and adapts comp-sum losses (Mao, Mohri, and Zhong, 2023f) and constrained losses (Lee et al., 2004) to the multi-label setting. It departs from prior multi-label consistency work (Gao and Zhou, 2011; Dembczynski et al., 2011, 2012; Koyejo et al., 2014, 2015), which established Bayes-consistency only for specific loss functions, by proving a unified surrogate loss (multi-label logistic loss) that is H-consistent for any general multi-label loss. The key conceptual departure from the standard binary relevance surrogate is that the proposed loss explicitly encodes label correlations within a single joint optimization, rather than decomposing into l independent binary problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is theoretical: for the first time, a single surrogate loss family enjoys non-asymptotic, hypothesis-set-dependent consistency guarantees across all common multi-label losses (Hamming, F-β, subset 0/1, Jaccard, precision/recall@κ, tree distance, and general linear-fractional losses), with label-independent bounds that improve upon the l^{1/2} dependency of binary relevance. However, the paper is entirely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the label space has cardinality 2^l, raising practical scalability questions for large l. Open questions include whether the theoretical advantages translate to empirical gains, how the methods perform in the regime of very large label spaces, and whether the WFA-based gradient computation (O(l) per step) remains tractable when the number of label combinations is exponential.
