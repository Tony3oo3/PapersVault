## Problem

Prior bandit-learning algorithms for two-sided matching markets optimized regret but largely ignored whether the resulting matching is stable (i.e., free of blocking pairs). This paper initiates the study of sample complexity for finding a stable matching in the PAC framework and shows that exploiting the structural properties of the Deferred Acceptance algorithm—particularly the arm-proposing variant—provably improves the probability of reaching a stable solution compared to the agent-proposing variant used in most prior work.

## Input / Output

Agents observe 1-subgaussian stochastic rewards when pulling arms, while arms' strict preference orderings over agents are known in advance. The algorithm takes a total sample budget T and outputs a one-to-one matching between N agents and K arms that is stable with respect to the true (unknown) agent preferences. The key structural constraint is that only agent-arm pairs appearing in the envy-set of the arm-optimal stable matching need to be sampled, rather than all NK pairs.

## Method

The proposed AE arm-DA algorithm (Algorithm 3) runs the arm-proposing Deferred Acceptance procedure, but replaces the uniform exploration step with an Action Elimination subroutine (Algorithm 2) that adaptively samples only the two arms currently in contention for a given agent until confidence bounds (UCB/LCB) separate them. This design ensures that only agent-arm pairs in the envy-set ES(m̲) are ever sampled, yielding a sample complexity of Õ(|ES(m̲)|/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) versus Õ(NK/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) for uniform sampling. The stability guarantee is parameterized by the envy-set size: the probability of an unstable matching is bounded by O(|ES(m̲)| · exp(−Δ²T_min/8)), and Lemma 2 establishes |ES(m̲)| ≤ |ES(m̄)|, so the arm-proposing variant always has a tighter instability bound than the agent-proposing variant. Under uniqueness-consistency (α-condition) profiles, the arm-proposing DA is shown to be strictly more likely to produce a stable matching for any sampling strategy (Corollary 1).

## Results

The AE arm-DA algorithm achieves a sample complexity of Õ(|ES(m̲)|/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) to find a stable matching with probability at least 1−α, strictly improving over the Õ(NK/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) bound of uniform sampling (Theorem 5 vs. Theorem 3). The probability of instability for AE arm-DA is O(|ES(m̲)|·exp(−Δ²T_min/8)), which is tighter than the O(|ES(m̲)|·exp(−Δ²T/8K)) bound for uniform arm-DA (Theorem 4 vs. Theorem 2). In experiments with N=K=20 and Δ=1 over 200 random profiles, AE arm-DA achieves the highest stability rate and, when measured by agent-pessimal regret, converges with fewer samples than both uniform variants and CA-UCB.

**Table 1: Comparison of bounds on probability of unstable matchings and sample complexity to find a stable matching.**

| Algorithm | Prob. of Instability | Sample Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform agent-DA | O(\|ES(m̄)\|^γ) (Thm. 2) | Õ(NK/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) (Thm. 3) |
| Uniform arm-DA | O(\|ES(m̲)\|^γ) (Thm. 2) | Õ(NK/Δ² · log(α⁻¹)) (Thm. 3) |
| **AE arm-DA** | O(\|ES(m̲)\|·exp(−Δ²T_min/8)) (Thm. 4) | **Õ(\|ES(m̲)\|/Δ² · log(α⁻¹))** (Thm. 5) |

where γ = exp(−Δ²T/8K), m̄ is the agent-optimal stable matching, and m̲ is the agent-pessimal (arm-optimal) stable matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is CA-UCB (Liu et al., 2021), a conflict-avoiding UCB algorithm for decentralized matching markets. In experiments (N=K=20, Δ=1, 200 random profiles), AE arm-DA significantly outperforms CA-UCB in stability (proportion of stable matchings), while CA-UCB's regret is measured against the agent-optimal solution. Internally, the paper shows that uniform arm-DA dominates uniform agent-DA: by Corollary 2, the arm-proposing variant has a strictly smaller probability bound of instability because |ES(m̲)| ≤ |ES(m̄)| (Lemma 2). The AE arm-DA algorithm further improves on uniform arm-DA in sample complexity by a factor of |ES(m̲)|/(NK) < 1 (Remark 3), since it only samples agent-arm pairs in the envy-set rather than all arms uniformly. In terms of agent-pessimal regret (the natural metric for arm-optimal matching), AE arm-DA converges faster than uniform arm-DA in both average and maximum regret (Figure 3), confirming that the targeted exploration strategy is more sample-efficient while preserving stability guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Gale-Shapley Deferred Acceptance algorithm and the Action Elimination (AE) procedure from the best-arm identification literature, combining them into a novel arm-proposing DA + AE algorithm for two-sided matching markets with unknown agent preferences. It departs from the dominant line of bandit matching work (Liu et al. 2020, 2021; Kong and Li 2023; Basu et al. 2021), which focused on regret minimization via agent-proposing DA and explore-then-commit strategies, by instead exploiting the structural properties of stable solutions—specifically the envy-set—to derive stability probability bounds and PAC sample complexity guarantees. The key conceptual departure is showing that the arm-proposing variant, which prior bandit matching papers largely ignored, is provably more likely to yield stable matchings under uniqueness consistency, and that restricting exploration to envy-set pairs (via AE) strictly reduces sample complexity compared to uniform exploration over all arms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work initiates the PAC sample complexity analysis for finding stable matchings in bandit settings, providing the first theoretical bounds that depend on the structural parameter |ES(m)| rather than the full NK product, which is practically meaningful in less competitive markets. The finding that arm-proposing DA dominates agent-proposing DA in stability probability challenges the default algorithmic choice in the bandit matching literature and suggests that prior regret-optimal algorithms may be unnecessarily sacrificing stability. Open questions include extending the framework to incomplete preferences, ties, non-subgaussian rewards, and many-to-one markets, as well as designing algorithms that simultaneously satisfy fairness or regret-minimizing criteria alongside stability guarantees.
