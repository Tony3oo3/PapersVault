## Problem

Extrapolation is known to empirically accelerate federated learning algorithms, yet the theoretical understanding of when and how it helps remains severely incomplete. While extrapolation in convex feasibility (parallel projection) has been theoretically explained by Necoara et al. [2019], and FedExP [Jhunjhunwala et al., 2023] combined FedAvg with extrapolation, no prior work provided convergence guarantees for extrapolated proximal methods in the federated setting. This paper asks: can we rigorously characterize the optimal extrapolation parameter for FedProx and show that server-side averaging (α=1) is provably suboptimal?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes local client objective functions f_i (assumed differentiable, convex, L_i-smooth, and satisfying the interpolation regime), an initial model x_0 ∈ R^d, a local proximal step size γ, and a client sampling strategy (τ-nice sampling for partial participation). It outputs a sequence of global iterates x_k with a convergence guarantee on E[f(x̄_K)] − inf f. Unusually, the extrapolation parameter α_k acts as a server-side stepsize on the Moreau envelope gradient, and the method requires no knowledge of the global smoothness constant L_γ in its adaptive variants (FedExProx-GraDS, FedExProx-StoPS), achieving 'semi-adaptivity' where convergence holds for any γ > 0 with at most a factor-of-2 penalty for suboptimal γ.

## Method

FedExProx replaces FedProx's server averaging step x_{k+1} = (1/n)Σ prox_{γf_i}(x_k) with an extrapolated update x_{k+1} = x_k + α_k((1/τ)Σ_{i∈S_k} prox_{γf_i}(x_k) − x_k). The key technical insight is the identity ∇M_{f_i}^γ(x) = (1/γ)(x − prox_{γf_i}(x)), which reformulates the update as a gradient step on the average Moreau envelope M^γ(x) with effective stepsize α_k·γ, enabling direct application of SGD theory with τ-nice sampling. The optimal constant extrapolation parameter is α_{γ,τ} = 1/(γL_{γ,τ}) > 1, yielding O(L_γ(1+γL_max)/ε) complexity in the convex case and linear convergence in the strongly convex case, improving over FedProx by a factor of at least 2 + 1/(γL_max) + γL_max ≥ 4. Two smoothness-adaptive variants eliminate the need to know L_γ: FedExProx-GraDS uses a gradient-diversity ratio of proximal residuals, and FedExProx-StoPS uses a stochastic Polyak stepsize on the Moreau envelopes, both achieving 'semi-adaptivity' with convergence for any local step size γ.

## Results

In the convex interpolation regime with full participation, FedExProx achieves an iteration complexity of O(L_γ(1+γL_max)/ε), improving upon FedProx's O(L_max/(γL_γ(2−γL_γ)K)) and FedExP's worst-case O(L_max/ε). The optimal constant extrapolation parameter α_{γ,τ} = 1/(γL_{γ,τ}) > 1 yields a guaranteed speed-up of at least 2 + 1/(γL_max) + γL_max ≥ 4 over FedProx with the same step size. In the strongly convex case, convergence becomes linear at rate O((L_γ(1+γL_max)/μ)·log(1/ε)). Numerical experiments confirm a 2× or higher speed-up in iteration count over FedProx across multiple problem configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to FedProx (Li et al., 2020), FedExProx with the same local step size γ is always at least 2 + 1/(γL_max) + γL_max ≥ 4 times faster in iteration complexity, demonstrating that the server's default practice of simply averaging local iterates (α=1) is provably suboptimal. Against FedExP (Jhunjhunwala et al., 2023), FedExProx improves the worst-case iteration complexity from O(L_max/ε) to O(L_γ(1+γL_max)/ε), where L_γ(1+γL_max) < L_max whenever client smoothness is heterogeneous, yielding up to an n-fold acceleration in the best case. FedExProx also recovers the Randomized Projection Method (RPM) of Necoara et al., 2019 as a special case (when f_i are indicator functions of convex sets), while extending the analysis to smooth convex and strongly convex objectives with partial client participation—settings where RPM's theory does not directly apply. The adaptive variants (FedExProx-GraDS and FedExProx-StoPS) eliminate the need to know the smoothness constant L_γ, achieving 'semi-adaptivity' where convergence holds for any γ > 0 with at most a factor-of-2 degradation when γ ≥ 1/L_max.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FedProx (Li et al., 2020) by adding a server-side extrapolation step, drawing directly from the recently established theory of the Randomized Projection Method (Necoara et al., 2019) for convex feasibility problems. The key technical bridge is the Moreau envelope, which reformulates the proximal update as a gradient step on a smoothed objective, allowing the authors to import SGD convergence theory (Gower et al., 2019) and connect the extrapolation parameter to the optimal SGD step size. The adaptive variants (FedExProx-GraDS, FedExProx-StoPS) build on gradient diversity (Horvath et al., 2022) and stochastic Polyak step size (Loizou et al., 2021), while the paper also subsumes FedExP (Jhunjhunwala et al., 2023) as a special case and recovers RPM as another.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why extrapolation accelerates federated proximal methods, showing that the default practice of server-side averaging is suboptimal by at least a factor of 4 in iteration complexity. The elegant connection between the extrapolation parameter and the SGD step size via the Moreau envelope offers a unifying lens that could inform future FL algorithm design. However, the analysis is confined to the interpolation regime and convex/strongly convex settings, leaving open whether extrapolation provides similar benefits in the non-convex, non-interpolated regime that dominates practical deep learning. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments, and the practical impact is tempered by the requirement that local proximal subproblems be solved accurately.
