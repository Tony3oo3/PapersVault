## Problem

The Surprisingly Popular (SP) algorithm can recover ground-truth rankings even when experts are in the minority, but it requires eliciting full rankings and full probability distributions over all m! orderings, which becomes intractable as the number of alternatives grows. This paper asks how to design scalable SP-based methods that recover the full ground-truth ranking while only eliciting partial preferences (top choice, approval set, or partial rank) on small subsets of alternatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240092/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each voter reports a vote and a prediction (about others' votes) on a subset of k ≪ m alternatives, where the elicitation format can be Top (single best), Approval(t) (unordered set of t), or Rank (full ordering of k). Subsets are constructed as overlapping windows with a fixed inter-alternative gap s in the ground-truth ranking, so that transitivity links alternatives across subsets. The output is a complete ranking over all m alternatives, evaluated via Kendall-Tau distance, Spearman's ρ, pairwise hit rate, and Top-t hit rate against the known ground truth.

## Method

The paper proposes two aggregation algorithms. Partial-SP applies the SP voting rule independently within each subset to recover a partial ground-truth ranking, then aggregates all partial rankings via a standard voting rule (e.g., Copeland) to produce the full ranking. Aggregated-SP instead first aggregates votes across subsets using a voting rule, then applies SP pairwise across all alternative pairs using the aggregated scores as vote signals and the non-aggregated predictions to compute conditional probabilities. Both methods use a pairwise SP formulation where each pair (a,b) is scored by a prediction-normalized vote that upweights alternatives whose observed frequency exceeds their predicted frequency. Theoretically, under a concentric mixture of Mallows model (experts with small dispersion φ_E, non-experts with large φ_NE), the sample complexity to recover the true partial ranking on a subset of size k is O(k!·√(k log k / μ)), depending only on k rather than m, and a union-bound extension yields guarantees for the full Partial-SP pipeline.

## Results

Across three domains (Geography, Movies, Paintings) with 432 MTurk participants, both Partial-SP and Aggregated-SP significantly outperform all classical vote aggregation rules (Borda, Copeland, Maximin, Schulze) on Kendall-Tau and Spearman's ρ correlations with the ground-truth ranking. Partial-SP consistently outperforms Aggregated-SP, and the advantage grows with more expressive elicitation formats: Rank-Rank and Approval(3)-Rank yield the highest correlations, while even the minimal Approval(2)-Approval(2) format is statistically indistinguishable from Rank-Rank for Partial-SP. The improvement is most pronounced in the Paintings domain, where specialized expertise is required and the minority of experts is most critical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against four classical aggregation rules: Borda, Copeland, Maximin, and Schulze. Both Partial-SP and Aggregated-SP significantly outperform Borda, Copeland, and Maximin across all elicitation formats and all three domains on both Kendall-Tau and Spearman's ρ. The sole exception is the Schulze method, which matches the performance of Aggregated-SP (though Partial-SP still outperforms it), making Schulze the strongest classical baseline. Notably, the SP advantage is largest when the elicitation format is minimal (e.g., Top-Top), where classical rules have the least information to work with, and the gap narrows as more information is elicited. For the Paintings domain specifically, Partial-SP shows the largest margin over all classical rules, demonstrating its ability to leverage a small expert minority to correct widespread misinformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Surprisingly Popular (SP) algorithm lineage from Prelec et al. (2017) and Hosseini et al. (2021, SP Voting) to the setting of partial preferences over large alternative sets. While SP Voting required eliciting complete rankings and pairwise predictions across all C(m,2) pairs—impractical for large m—this work introduces two scalable variants (Partial-SP and Aggregated-SP) that operate on overlapping subsets of size k << m using simplified elicitation formats (top choice, approval sets, or partial ranks). The probabilistic modeling of voter behavior via concentric mixtures of Mallows models draws on the rank-order modeling literature (Mallows 1957, Collas & Iruozki 2021) to provide both an empirical fit and the foundation for sample complexity bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in crowd-based rank recovery: when m is large, eliciting full rankings and predictions becomes cognitively infeasible, and the SP framework had no scalable formulation. The finding that unordered approval sets (Approval(2)-Approval(2)) recover ground-truth rankings comparably to full Rank-Rank elicitation challenges the assumption that ordinal pairwise information is necessary for SP voting, potentially simplifying deployment. Open questions include whether the pairwise SP variant can achieve the conjectured O(|S|·k²) sample complexity, how the methods perform with malicious or strategically biased voters, and whether the Mallows-based theoretical guarantees extend to other probabilistic models. The approach is most relevant to domains where expert knowledge is scarce and majority opinion is unreliable (e.g., art valuation, niche expertise).
