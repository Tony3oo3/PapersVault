## Problem

Prior work achieved tight empirical audits of DP-SGD only in active white-box threat models where the adversary can insert arbitrary canary gradients into intermediate training steps. In the more realistic black-box setting—where the adversary only observes the final trained model—audits remained loose, with empirical privacy estimates far below theoretical bounds. Whether tight black-box auditing of DP-SGD is feasible for natural datasets remained an open question.

## Input / Output

The auditing procedure consumes a training dataset (split in half: one half for DP-SGD training, the other for non-private pre-training to craft initial parameters), a single target/canary sample, and DP-SGD hyperparameters (clipping norm C, noise multiplier σ, learning rate η, iterations T, with full-batch B=n). It produces an empirical privacy lower bound ε_emp derived from FPR and FNR computed by thresholding the target sample's loss across 2R independently trained models (R with the target, R without), converted via the μ-GDP to (ε,δ)-DP relationship. The unusual aspect is that initial model parameters are not randomly drawn but adversarially crafted through non-private pre-training on an auxiliary dataset of the same distribution.

## Method

The core insight is that DP-SGD's privacy analysis is agnostic to initial model parameters, so one can craft worst-case θ₀ that minimizes the gradients of all normal samples in the dataset, making the target sample's gradient contribution maximally distinguishable. Concretely, the model is non-privately pre-trained on an auxiliary dataset (half the training set for MNIST; CIFAR-100 plus half the training set for CIFAR-10) for several epochs, and the resulting parameters serve as θ₀ for all DP-SGD runs. The auditing procedure then trains 2R models with identical θ₀—R on D and R on D∪{target}—computes the target's loss as the observation, thresholds to obtain FPR/FNR, and converts to ε_emp via the μ-GDP privacy region. The paper further identifies two factors governing audit tightness: smaller dataset sizes and smaller gradient clipping norms yield tighter audits because they increase the signal-to-noise ratio of the target's gradient relative to the added noise.

## Results

At theoretical ε=10.0 on full datasets (30,000 MNIST / 25,000 CIFAR-10), worst-case initial parameters yield empirical privacy estimates of ε_emp=6.48 (MNIST) and 4.96 (CIFAR-10), compared to only 3.41 and 0.69 with the average-case initialization used in prior work. On 1,000-record samples, the estimates tighten further to ε_emp=7.21 and 6.95. In the last-layer-only fine-tuning setting (WRN-28-10 on CIFAR-10), the audit is nearly tight at ε_emp=9.33 for theoretical ε=10.0, with a maximum across five runs reaching 9.94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed worst-case initialization and the fixed average-case (Xavier) initialization used in all prior black-box audits (Jagielski et al. [20], De et al. [11], Nasr et al. [28]). At ε=10.0 on full datasets, worst-case initialization improves ε_emp from 3.41 to 6.48 on MNIST (~1.9×) and from 0.69 to 4.96 on CIFAR-10 (~7.2×), representing roughly a 3× improvement over the best prior black-box estimates. At ε=8.0, prior work achieved only ε_emp≈2 (MNIST, [11]) and ε_emp≈1.6 (CIFAR-10, [28]), whereas this paper's approach at ε=10.0 already exceeds those values. In the last-layer fine-tuning setting, the gap between worst-case and average-case is smaller (9.33 vs. 9.19 at ε=10.0), indicating that the initialization strategy matters most for full-model training. The paper also shows that smaller dataset sizes (n=1,000) and smaller clipping norms (C=0.1, 1.0) further tighten audits, while larger C (10.0) degrades both model accuracy and audit tightness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box auditing framework of Jagielski et al. [20] and the tight-auditing methodology of Nasr et al. [28] by replacing their fixed average-case initial parameters with adversarially crafted worst-case ones, obtained through non-private pre-training on auxiliary data. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that while DP-SGD's privacy analysis is agnostic to initialization, the empirical tightness of an audit is highly sensitive to it: minimizing the gradient norms of non-target samples via pre-training makes the canary sample's gradient far more distinguishable. The μ-GDP conversion machinery from Dong et al. [13] and the full-batch (B=N) setup from Nasr et al. [28] are retained, and the ClipBKD target-sample crafting from Jagielski et al. is reused in the fine-tuning regime. The approach is conceptually adjacent to concurrent work on poisoned pre-trained models [16, 25, 37], but differs in targeting full-model DP-SGD auditing rather than last-layer fine-tuning or non-DP settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves an open question in the DP auditing literature by demonstrating that near-tight black-box audits of DP-SGD are achievable on natural datasets, providing a practical diagnostic tool for detecting implementation bugs in widely deployed libraries (Opacus, TensorFlow, JAX). Key limitations include the high computational cost (hundreds of GPU hours for shallow CNNs), restriction to full-batch training without sub-sampling, and the fact that audits remain looser for larger datasets and larger clipping norms. Open questions include whether tight black-box audits are possible for DP-SGD with sub-sampling under non-convex losses, how to reduce the number of models needed, and whether the worst-case initialization insight can be combined with emerging one-shot auditing techniques.
