## Problem

Applying 2D diffusion models independently to each view for 3D object removal produces high-variance inpainting results that are misaligned across views, degrading the fidelity of the resulting NeRF. Existing multi-view approaches lose high-frequency detail during NeRF optimization, while single-view methods depend critically on accurate depth estimation and fail when geometry is imperfect. The paper identifies that this inconsistency stems from two sources: independently sampled initial latents and independently predicted intermediate latents during denoising.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images with binary object masks, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and a text prompt as input. It additionally requires a monocular depth map (from DepthAnything) for the selected prior view and a pre-trained NeRF for geometric reprojection. The output is a set of multi-view consistently inpainted images (in latent space, decoded via Stable Diffusion's decoder) and a jointly optimized NeRF representing the object-removed scene. Inpainting is performed with 20 DDIM steps using the stable-diffusion-2-inpainting model, and the base view is automatically selected by sampling five candidates around the geometrical centroid of training camera poses and picking the one with highest perceptual-hash similarity.

## Method

The core insight is that diffusion model output variability is governed by the initial noise z^T and the intermediate latents {z^{t-1}} predicted during denoising, and both can be aligned across views without fine-tuning the diffusion model. Explicit Latent Alignment (ELA) reprojects the prior view's initial latent z_p^T into other views using NeRF's volume rendering equation with density-weighted aggregation (soft projection), avoiding hard depth-based warping errors. Implicit Latent Alignment (ILA) introduces Cross-View Attention (CVA) into every self-attention layer of the U-Net, replacing the key-value pairs from the current view's masked image with those from the prior view's masked image, blended at λ_a=0.2 to preserve view-specific detail. The three-stage pipeline first pre-trains the NeRF on unmasked pixels plus the prior view's color and depth, then inpaints all remaining views under ELA+ILA conditioning, and finally optimizes the NeRF with a patch-based hybrid loss (L1 + LPIPS + StyleGAN2 adversarial) on 256×256 patches sampled from masked regions to preserve high-frequency detail while correcting geometric mismatches.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF dataset, In-N-Out achieves LPIPS 0.49, FID 130.92, and MUSIQ 50.97, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. The method improves LPIPS by 0.05 over the closest competitor (InFusion, 0.62) and by 0.22 over the weakest (NeRFiller, 0.71). In a user study, In-N-Out receives 50.68% overall preference votes, nearly double the next-best method (InFusion at 25.00%), with 47.05% coherence and 50.45% fidelity preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In-N-Out outperforms all three baselines across LPIPS, FID, and MUSIQ. Against SPIn-NeRF [53] (perceptual-loss multi-view inpainting), it reduces LPIPS from 0.54 to 0.49 and FID from 185.63 to 130.92, while boosting MUSIQ from 38.69 to 50.97, indicating substantially sharper and more coherent inpaintings. Against NeRFiller [93] (tiling-based multi-view inpainting), the gains are largest: LPIPS drops from 0.71 to 0.49 and FID from 315.83 to 130.92, reflecting that NeRFiller's joint denoising produces overly smooth outputs lacking fine detail. Against InFusion [109] (single-view depth-completion approach), In-N-Out leads on all metrics (LPIPS 0.49 vs. 0.62, FID 130.92 vs. 153.77, MUSIQ 50.97 vs. 39.29), with the advantage most pronounced in scenes where monocular depth estimation is inaccurate, as InFusion's reliance on a single reference view introduces geometric artifacts. In the user study, In-N-Out nearly doubles the overall preference rate of InFusion (50.68% vs. 25.00%) and far exceeds SPIn-NeRF (21.82%) and NeRFiller (2.50%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent inpainting pipeline and NeRF's volume rendering formulation, repurposing both for a novel 'In-N-Out' paradigm: inpaint a single prior view, then outstretch the result to multiple views via latent alignment. It departs from prior multi-view inpainting methods (SPIn-NeRF, NeRFiller) that simply optimize NeRF against independently inpainted views, and from single-view methods (InFusion, NeRF-In) that anchor the entire scene to one reference image. The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion denoising process as a controllable conditional-sampling problem, where initial latents are explicitly aligned through NeRF-based soft reprojection and intermediate latents are implicitly aligned through a cross-view attention mechanism inserted into the U-Net's self-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent alignment framework (ELA + ILA) offers a general principle for enforcing multi-view consistency when lifting 2D generative priors into 3D, potentially applicable beyond object removal to broader 3D editing tasks. However, the method is limited to forward-facing scenes, requires predefined masks, and still struggles with full 3D consistency on high-frequency details due to the fundamental 2D-to-3D gap. Open questions include whether multi-view-aware diffusion training or true 3D generative models could subsume this alignment step, and whether language-driven 3D perception could eliminate the need for manual masks.
