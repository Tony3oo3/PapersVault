## Problem

While Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQ) have demonstrated competitive classification performance, the theoretical understanding of their learned representations remains largely absent. Neural Collapse (NC), a well-characterized geometric phenomenon in explicit neural networks, has not been systematically analyzed for implicit networks like DEQ, and no prior work has examined DEQ's behavior under imbalanced data conditions where minority collapse is a known challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240307/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 (10 classes) or CIFAR-100 (100 classes) and produces class labels. A ResNet18 backbone extracts standardized features H⁰, which are then processed by either a single explicit linear layer or a DEQ layer (the last ResNet block reformulated as a fixed-point equation) to yield final features before a linear classifier. For imbalanced experiments, per-class sample counts are manipulated (e.g., 5000 vs. 50 samples) while the test set mirrors the training distribution.

## Method

The paper embeds DEQ within the layer-peeled model framework by treating the fixed-point solution z* as an unconstrained feature variable and constraining the DEQ weight matrix W_DEQ to a bounded Frobenius norm. By deriving lower bounds of the cross-entropy loss under these constraints, they prove that NC1–NC3 hold for both DEQ and explicit networks under balanced settings, with DEQ achieving a strictly lower loss bound (a factor-of-2 gap). Under imbalanced settings, they show that while full NC2/NC3 do not hold, DEQ's features remain closer to a Simplex ETF and exhibit stronger self-duality alignment than explicit networks under mild conditions on the feature energy E_H. The theoretical analysis assumes a linear DEQ (Neumann series), and experiments validate the findings by converting the last ResNet18 block into a DEQ layer trained with a Broyden solver and early stopping.

## Results

Under balanced settings, DEQ achieves 93.23±0.13% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 64.77±0.36% on CIFAR-100, marginally outperforming the explicit ResNet18 baseline (93.05±0.17% and 64.35±0.20%). Under imbalanced settings (K_A=3, K_B=7), DEQ consistently outperforms the explicit network across all imbalance ratios R∈{10, 50, 100} on both datasets. The advantage is most pronounced on minority-class accuracy: at R=100 on CIFAR-10, DEQ achieves 8.83% minority accuracy versus 6.64% for the explicit network, and on CIFAR-100, 3.31% versus 1.99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DEQ against an explicit ResNet18 backbone (the only baseline) under identical training protocols. On balanced CIFAR-10, DEQ achieves 93.23% versus 93.05% for ResNet18, a marginal gain consistent with the theoretical prediction that both models exhibit full Neural Collapse under balanced conditions. Under imbalanced settings, DEQ wins on every configuration tested (K_A∈{3,5,7}, R∈{10,50,100}, both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100). The gap is largest on minority-class accuracy at high imbalance: at R=100 with K_A=3 on CIFAR-10, DEQ reaches 8.83% minority accuracy versus 6.64% for ResNet18 (a ~33% relative improvement), and on CIFAR-100 the gap widens to 3.31% vs. 1.99%. Majority-class accuracy remains comparable between the two models (within ~1–2%), confirming that DEQ's advantage is specifically in mitigating minority collapse rather than improving overall feature separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) analysis framework—originally developed for explicit fully-connected and linear networks under the Unconstrained Features Mode (Papyan et al. 2020; Fang et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2021)—to the implicit network setting of Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al. 2019). It adopts the layer-peeled model formulation from Fang et al. (2021) and the minority collapse analysis for imbalanced training, but is the first to derive NC conditions for DEQ by treating the fixed-point solution as the unconstrained feature variable and constraining the DEQ weight matrix. The paper departs from prior NC work, which has been exclusively applied to explicit architectures, by showing that the Neumann-series expansion of the DEQ forward pass yields a strictly lower loss bound (by a factor of 2) than a single explicit layer under identical energy constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical grounding for why DEQ tends to outperform explicit networks on imbalanced datasets, moving beyond purely empirical observations. However, the analysis is restricted to linear DEQ and simple two-group (majority/minority) imbalance on CIFAR-scale datasets, leaving open whether the results extend to nonlinear DEQ, multi-level imbalance hierarchies, or larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. The practical impact is moderate: the conditions under which DEQ's advantage manifests (E_H < 2s − m < 1/(1−E_H)) are described as 'easy to satisfy' but the accuracy gains in experiments are only 1–3 percentage points, and DEQ's known training instability limits its adoption. Future work should validate these theoretical predictions on nonlinear DEQ and more realistic long-tail distributions.
