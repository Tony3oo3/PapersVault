## Problem

Post-hoc explanation methods for audio classifiers have been limited to standard closed-set classification with predefined labels, leaving zero-shot audio classifiers—which define classes dynamically via natural language prompts—unexplained. The multi-modal nature of zero-shot classifiers (operating in a joint text-audio similarity space) makes it non-trivial to learn an interpreter that faithfully tracks their decisions, and no prior work has attempted this.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242086/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a linear-scale STFT spectrogram (16 kHz for listenable explanations) or a Mel spectrogram (44.1 kHz for feature-space explanations) of a fixed-length audio clip, along with a natural language text prompt. It outputs a spatially-aligned saliency mask (values in [0,1]) and, in the STFT domain, a listenable explanation waveform reconstructed via inverse STFT using the original audio's phase. The decoder is conditioned on both the text prompt embedding and the last four layer representations from the CNN14 audio encoder, enabling prompt-specific explanations for arbitrary labels not seen during training.

## Method

LMAC-ZS trains a decoder (transposed convolutions with cross-attention for text conditioning, similar to Stable Diffusion) to produce a saliency mask that, when element-wise multiplied with the input spectrogram, preserves the original text-audio similarity matrix. The key novelty is a loss function that minimizes the absolute discrepancy between the original cross-modal similarities C_{i,j} and the similarities computed after masking, replacing the cross-entropy loss used in fixed-set settings where no fixed logits exist. An additional diversity term aligns the pairwise text-text similarity with the pairwise masked-audio-audio similarity, ensuring that different prompts yield meaningfully different masks. The decoder is trained on the same unlabeled audio-text pairs used to pretrain CLAP (Clotho, FSD50K, AudioCaps, MACS), so it can explain zero-shot predictions for any text prompt without requiring class labels, achieving true zero-shot interpretability.

## Results

On In-Domain ESC50 with STFT masking, LMAC-ZS (Full) achieves AI=43.35, AD=4.29, AG=10.57, and Fid-In=0.90, substantially outperforming the strongest gradient-based baseline GradCam++ (AI=32.50, AD=8.97, AG=7.95, Fid-In=0.84). On UrbanSound8K with STFT masking, LMAC-ZS (Full) reaches AI=40.85, AD=7.79, AG=15.52, and Fid-In=0.85 versus GradCam++'s AI=32.85, AD=8.84, AG=13.16, Fid-In=0.83. The FF metric is the most competitive, with GradCam++ matching or slightly exceeding LMAC-ZS in several settings (e.g., FF=0.81 vs 0.78 on US8K STFT), though LMAC-ZS produces much sparser masks (MM=0.07 vs 0.39). Out-of-domain results with noise contamination (Tables 2–3) confirm the same trend, with LMAC-ZS maintaining its advantage on AI, AD, AG, and Fid-In across all contamination types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMAC-ZS decisively outperforms all four gradient-based baselines (GradCam, GradCam++, SmoothGrad, IG) on the primary faithfulness metrics AI, AD, AG, and Fid-In across both ESC50 and UrbanSound8K in both Mel and STFT masking domains. The gap is most dramatic in the Mel domain, where LMAC-ZS (CT) achieves AI=29.00 vs. GradCam++'s 8.45 on ESC50, and SmoothGrad/IG are nearly useless (AI < 1). In the STFT domain, GradCam++ is the closest competitor (e.g., AI=32.50 vs. LMAC-ZS Full's 43.35 on ESC50), but LMAC-ZS still leads by 10+ points on AI and roughly halves the AD. On the FF metric, GradCam++ is competitive or slightly better in several STFT settings (e.g., FF=0.81 vs. 0.78 on US8K), though the authors note this is partly because GradCam++ masks cover much larger areas (MM=0.39 vs. 0.07–0.10). In the ablation (Table 4), LMAC-ZS also outperforms ScoreCAM and GScoreCAM on most metrics for clean ESC50 STFT masking (e.g., Fid-In 0.90 vs. 0.75/0.84), confirming that decoder-based masking is superior to score-weighted gradient approaches in the zero-shot audio setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends L-MAC (Paissan et al., ICML 2024), a decoder-based post-hoc explanation method for closed-set audio classifiers, to the zero-shot setting where class labels are defined by free-form text prompts rather than a fixed logit vector. The key architectural departure is a novel loss function that preserves text-audio similarity patterns (rather than matching class logits) and a decoder conditioned on both audio encoder representations and the text prompt via cross-attention, enabling prompt-specific explanations. It positions itself against gradient-based saliency methods (GradCAM, GradCAM++, SmoothGrad, IG) that are shown to be largely insensitive to the zero-shot classification decision, and against other audio explanation methods (Listen-to-Interpret, Posthoc Interpretation via Quantization) that require predefined label sets and thus cannot operate in a true zero-shot fashion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decoder-based post-hoc explanation method for zero-shot audio classifiers, filling a gap that limits trust and deployment of increasingly popular CLAP-style models in safety-critical domains such as healthcare. The ability to condition explanations on arbitrary text prompts (and to receive empty masks for irrelevant prompts) is a capability absent from all prior gradient-based or mask-optimization approaches. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to the CLAP model family, fixed-length audio, and the absence of error bars or statistical significance testing. Open questions include extension to variable-length inputs, top-k class contribution analysis, and generalization to other zero-shot audio-text models beyond CLAP.
