## Problem

Transfer RL with latent low-rank structure was previously studied only for the (d,S,A) Tucker rank setting (Low Rank MDP) using a computationally intractable optimization oracle, leaving the complementary settings (S,d,A), (S,S,d), and (d,d,d) unexplored. This paper asks: can one design computationally efficient transfer RL algorithms that remove dependence on state or action space size in the target regret bound across all Tucker rank settings, and what is the fundamental limit on how much source data is needed to make transfer beneficial?

## Input / Output

In the source phase, the learner queries M source MDPs through generative models by specifying a state-action pair (s,a) and time step h, receiving a reward and a sampled next state. In the target phase, the learner loses access to source MDPs and interacts online with the target MDP over K episodes (T=KH total steps) in a standard episodic setting without a generative model. The output is a target MDP policy whose regret bound is independent of S, A, or SA (depending on the Tucker rank setting), along with a learned latent feature representation of dimension dM constructed from source MDP Q-function SVDs.

## Method

The algorithm operates in two phases. In the source phase, it runs LR-EVI (low-rank empirical value iteration) on each source MDP to obtain Q-function estimates, computes their SVDs to extract latent factor matrices, and concatenates/scales them into a dM-dimensional feature representation. In the target phase, it deploys a Tucker-rank-adapted modification of LSVI-UCB (e.g., LSVI-UCB-(S,S,d)) that computes S separate d×d Gram matrices rather than a single d²S² matrix, shaving a √S factor from the regret bound compared to off-the-shelf linear MDP algorithms. The key theoretical contribution is the transfer-ability coefficient α, defined as the minimum over all valid latent factor choices of the maximum coefficient needed to express target latent factors as linear combinations of source latent factors; the paper proves α² dependence in source sample complexity is minimax-optimal via an information-theoretic lower bound, improving on the α⁵ dependence of prior work [4] in the (d,S,A) setting.

## Results

In the (S,S,d) Tucker rank setting, the paper achieves a target regret bound of Õ(√((dMH)³ST)) with source sample complexity Õ(d⁴(1+A/S)M²H⁴α²T), removing all dependence on the action space A. In the (d,S,A) setting (Low Rank MDP), the target regret is Õ(√((dMH)³T)) with source sample complexity Õ(α²dM²TSA), achieving an optimal α² dependence versus the α⁵ dependence of the prior work [4]. The (d,d,d) setting further removes both S and A from the target regret, yielding Õ(√(d⁶M⁶H³T)), though at the cost of an α⁴ dependence in the source phase. Information-theoretic lower bounds confirm the α² dependence is minimax-optimal in the (S,d,A), (S,S,d), and (d,S,A) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Agarwal et al. [4] in the (d,S,A) Tucker rank setting (Low Rank MDP). This paper achieves an optimal α² dependence in the source sample complexity, improving upon [4]'s sub-optimal α⁵ dependence, while also reducing the source sample complexity from scaling with SA⁵H⁷d⁵ to scaling with dM²TSA. The target regret bound Õ(√((dMH)³T)) is comparable to [4]'s Õ(√(d³H⁴T)), with the difference attributable to the more general transfer assumption (subspace containment vs. exact linear combination). Against LSVI-UCB [17], the paper's modified algorithm LSVI-UCB-(S,S,d) improves the regret by a factor of √S by computing S copies of d×d Gram matrices instead of a single d²S²-dimensional one. The paper's dependence on d and H is acknowledged as sub-optimal relative to the minimax-optimal bounds of He et al. [13] and Zhou et al. [41] (which achieve Õ(√(d²H²T))), with the gap attributed to using Hoeffding's inequality rather than variance-tracking Bernstein bounds and to LSVI-UCB's known sub-optimality in d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer RL framework of Agarwal et al. [4], which studied representational transfer exclusively in the (d,S,A) Tucker rank setting (Low Rank MDP), to all four Tucker rank configurations: (S,d,A), (S,S,d), (d,S,A), and (d,d,d). It builds on LR-EVI [30] for source-phase Q-function estimation and LSVI-UCB [17] for target-phase learning, but introduces a modified LSVI-UCB-(S,S,d) that exploits the per-state Gram matrix structure to shave a √S factor from the regret bound. A key departure from [4] is the introduction of the transfer-ability coefficient α, which generalizes the task-relatedness notion to handle cases where source and target latent factors differ, and the achievement of an optimal α² dependence in source sample complexity versus [4]'s suboptimal α⁵.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work completes the theoretical landscape for representational transfer in RL under low Tucker rank structure, showing that with sufficient source samples, dependence on S, A, or SA can be entirely removed from the target regret bound in each setting. The information-theoretic lower bounds establishing α² minimax optimality (for all settings except (d,d,d)) provide a clean characterization of when transfer is fundamentally possible. Open questions include achieving optimal d and H dependence (requiring adaptation of LSVI-UCB++ [13] to the Tucker rank setting), proving an α⁴ lower bound for the (d,d,d) case, and extending guarantees to settings without generative model access in the source phase. The practical impact is currently limited by the requirement of generative model access and the incoherence/condition number assumptions, but the framework provides a principled template for future transfer RL algorithms.
