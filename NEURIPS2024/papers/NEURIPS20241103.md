## Problem

Multi-LiDAR placement in autonomous driving has relied on industry heuristics and design aesthetics rather than systematic optimization. The only prior metric (S-MIG) uses 3D bounding boxes as scene priors, limiting it to detection tasks and failing to capture occlusion or semantic structure. No prior work evaluates whether optimized placements remain robust under adverse weather and sensor failure conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241103/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes semantic point clouds from CARLA (4×16-channel LiDARs, 360° horizontal FOV, [-24.8°, 2.0°] vertical FOV, 100 m range, 20 Hz) along with LiDAR configuration parameters (x, y, z coordinates and roll angles per sensor). It produces an optimized 4-LiDAR placement, an M-SOG score, and downstream perception outputs: 21-class semantic segmentation labels and 3-class 3D detection boxes. The accompanying dataset spans 280,000 frames across 11 placements and 6 corruption types (fog, snow, wet ground, motion blur, crosstalk, incomplete echo).

## Method

The core contribution is the M-SOG surrogate metric, which replaces the Bernoulli-distribution POG of S-MIG with a multinomial Probabilistic Semantic Occupancy Grid (P-SOG) over 21 semantic classes, capturing occlusion and dense scene structure. Given a LiDAR configuration, the Amanatides-Woo fast voxel traversal identifies voxels intersected by LiDAR rays, and M-SOG is computed as the negative average entropy of P-SOG restricted to those voxels. Optimization proceeds via CMA-ES with a Lagrangian penalty for physical constraints (inter-sensor distance, roof-plane clearance), iteratively adapting the mean, step size, and covariance of a multivariate normal candidate distribution. A theoretical optimality certification (Theorem 1) bounds the gap between the discrete-grid local optimum and the continuous global optimum by C_M + k_G·δ. The full Place3D pipeline closes the loop: CARLA data generation → P-SOG synthesis from training scenes → M-SOG scoring → CMA-ES optimization → evaluation of 8 segmentation and 4 detection models under clean and 6 adverse conditions.

## Results

On the Place3D benchmark, the optimized LiDAR placement ('Ours') achieves 73.0% mIoU (Cylinder3D) for clean LiDAR semantic segmentation and 57.1% mAP (CenterPoint) for clean 3D object detection, outperforming the best heuristic baselines (72.7% and 56.4% respectively). Under six adverse conditions (fog, snow, wet ground, motion blur, crosstalk, incomplete echo), the optimized placement maintains the highest average scores: 48.6% mIoU (segmentation) and 29.5% mAP (detection), exceeding the best baselines by up to 1.5% and 0.3% respectively. The M-SOG surrogate metric shows a clear linear correlation with downstream perception performance across all 10 tested placements, validating its use for computationally efficient sensor optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the CMA-ES-optimized LiDAR placement against seven heuristic configurations (Center, Line, Pyramid, Square, Trapezoid, Line-Roll, Pyramid-Roll) inspired by major autonomous vehicle companies (Waymo, Motional, Cruise, Pony.ai, Zoox, Toyota, Momenta, Ford). For LiDAR semantic segmentation, the optimized placement achieves 73.0% mIoU (Cylinder3D, clean), surpassing the best baseline Center (72.7%) by 0.3%, and under adverse conditions reaches 48.6% average mIoU vs. the best baseline Square (48.4%). For 3D object detection, the optimized placement scores 57.1% mAP (CenterPoint, clean) vs. best baseline Trapezoid (56.4%), and 29.5% average mAP under corruption vs. best baseline Pyramid (29.4%). The paper also directly compares the proposed M-SOG metric against S-MIG [34] (Hu et al., CVPR 2022), demonstrating that M-SOG exhibits stronger linear correlation with detection performance because it uses semantic occupancy priors rather than 3D bounding boxes, better capturing object boundaries and occlusion relationships. Notably, the optimized placement maintains its advantage most consistently under severe corruptions like crosstalk (41.0% vs. 37.1% best baseline for segmentation) and snow (23.1% vs. 22.8% for detection), where heuristic placements degrade more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends S-MIG (Hu et al., CVPR 2022), which evaluated multi-LiDAR placement quality using 3D bounding boxes as scene priors and was limited to 3D object detection. Place3D replaces the 3D-bbox prior with semantic occupancy grids (M-SOG), enabling evaluation for both detection and dense segmentation, and introduces CMA-ES-based optimization to search over placement configurations. It also builds on the Robo3D corruption framework for adverse-condition evaluation and draws on the broader sensor-placement optimization literature (Joshi & Boyd, roadside LiDAR works), but is the first to jointly address placement optimization for robust 3D scene understanding under weather and sensor-failure corruptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 280K-frame multi-condition multi-LiDAR dataset and the M-SOG metric provide the community with a practical, computationally cheap proxy for LiDAR placement quality that avoids expensive re-collection and re-training cycles, and the benchmark covers both clean and six corruption types across four segmentation and four detection architectures. However, the dataset is entirely synthetic (CARLA), uses low-resolution 16-channel LiDARs, and evaluates only three detection classes, so transfer to real-world multi-LiDAR deployments remains an open question. The modest performance gains (≤1.5% mIoU, ≤0.7% mAP over the best baseline) suggest that placement optimization is a useful but secondary lever compared to model architecture improvements, and whether the optimized configurations generalize beyond the specific CARLA maps and vehicle geometry used here is untested.
