## Problem

Pretraining large language models on consumer-grade hardware (e.g., a single 24GB GPU) has been infeasible without model sharding, offloading, or per-layer gradient updates. While LoRA reduces trainable parameters for fine-tuning and GaLore shrinks optimizer states via low-rank gradient projection, neither has been shown to work for pretraining, and combining either with weight quantization fails because small high-precision gradient updates do not register in a low-precision quantized state.

## Input / Output

LoQT consumes standard text corpora (C4 for pretraining, GLUE/GSM8K/Icelandic Gigaword for adaptation) and produces trained model weights stored in NF4 (4-bit) quantized format, with only the low-rank B adapter in BF16. For pretraining, the output is a causal language model (LLaMA-style) that generates text; for fine-tuning, it produces classification scores on GLUE tasks or arithmetic answers on GSM8K. The method operates on LLaMA 2 (60M–13B) and DeBERTa-V3-base architectures with sequence lengths up to 4096 tokens.

## Method

LoQT initializes two low-rank factors per weight matrix: P is set to the top-r left singular vectors of the gradient (via SVD, as in GaLore), and B is initialized to minimize the quantization error between W and its NF4 quantized form using the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse. During training, only B receives gradient updates while P and W remain frozen and quantized, reducing optimizer states from 2(mr+nr) (LoRA) to 2nr parameters. The product PB is periodically merged back into W at exponentially increasing intervals (starting at τ, growing by factor ψ), ensuring accumulated updates are large enough to register in the 4-bit quantized state. An iterative error-compensation step (up to 5 iterations) re-solves B after each merge to minimize residual quantization error, and the exponential schedule prevents loss plateauing that occurs when late-training updates are too small to overcome quantization rounding.

## Results

On C4 pretraining of LLaMA-style models, LoQT matches full-rank training (15.55 vs 15.56 perplexity at 1B) while using 59% less memory (3.16 GB vs 7.80 GB), and outperforms GaLore (15.64) with 28% less memory. On GSM8K arithmetic reasoning, LoQT achieves 42.6% (7B) and 52.9% (13B) test accuracy, the best among all 4-bit quantized fine-tuning methods. On GLUE, LoQT-nq achieves an average score of 89.1, surpassing LoRA (88.3), LoftQ (88.4), and GaLore (88.5). For Icelandic language adaptation, LoQT reduces perplexity from 4.90 to 3.63, outperforming both full training (3.79) and GaLore (3.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaLore (the closest method in memory efficiency), LoQT achieves comparable or slightly better perplexity at every model size (e.g., 15.55 vs 15.64 at 1B) while using 28% less memory (3.16 GB vs 4.38 GB), primarily because only matrix B requires optimizer states (2nr) versus GaLore's (mr+2nr). Against LoRA and ReLoRA, LoQT substantially outperforms on pretraining (15.55 vs 19.21 and 18.33 at 1B), confirming that gradient-initialized low-rank factors with periodic merging are essential for pretraining from scratch. On GLUE fine-tuning, LoQT-nq (89.1 avg) and LoQT (88.7) both exceed LoRA (88.3), LoftQ (88.4), and GaLore (88.5), suggesting gradient-based initialization of adapter factors is beneficial even for fine-tuning. On GSM8K, LoQT achieves the best 4-bit accuracy for both 7B (42.6% vs QLoRA 41.9%, ApiQ 42.1%) and 13B (52.9% vs QLoRA 51.6%, ApiQ 52.4%). For Icelandic continued pretraining, LoQT (3.63) and LoQT-nq (3.61) both outperform full training (3.79) and GaLore (3.96), indicating the gradient-subspace approach generalizes well to domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoQT synthesizes three prior threads: GaLore's observation that gradients live in a low-rank subspace (Zhao et al., ICML 2024), LoRA's parameter-efficient adapter formulation (Hu et al., ICLR 2022), and ReLoRA's periodic merging of low-rank updates into a frozen weight matrix (Lialin et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is rewriting GaLore's projected gradient update as a standard LoRA forward pass (y = xW + xPB), which cleanly separates the trainable factor B from the frozen, quantizable factors W and P. Unlike QLoRA or LoftQ, which apply quantization only during fine-tuning of a pretrained model, LoQT extends this to pretraining from scratch by using an exponentially increasing merge schedule and a pseudo-inverse-based initialization of B that compensates for NF4 quantization error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LoQT enables 7B-parameter pretraining on a single 24 GB consumer GPU and 13B with per-layer gradient updates, lowering the hardware barrier for LLM research. The authors note that gradient-initialized LoRA factors sometimes outperform standard random initialization in fine-tuning, a finding they flag as needing further investigation. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 13B parameters, whether other quantization schemes (NF2, FP8, activation quantization) integrate cleanly, and whether the exponential update schedule generalizes to architectures beyond transformers. The paper is limited to English pretraining (C4) and a single Icelandic adaptation experiment, so robustness across languages and domains remains untested.
