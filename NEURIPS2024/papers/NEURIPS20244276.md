## Problem

The Image Manipulation Detection & Localization (IMDL) field lacks a unified benchmark: state-of-the-art models use inconsistent training datasets, evaluation protocols, and metrics, and most do not release training code. Existing frameworks like OpenMMLab and Detectron2 have tightly coupled pipelines that conflict with IMDL's need for diverse low-level feature extractors and highly customized loss functions, making faithful reproduction and fair comparison nearly impossible.

## Input / Output

Each IMDL model in the benchmark takes a single RGB image (resized to model-specific resolutions ranging from 224×224 to 1024×1024) and produces a dual output: a binary image-level class label (manipulated vs. authentic) and a pixel-level manipulation mask. The data loader applies a fixed transformation sequence—IMDL-specific augmentations (naive inpainting, copy-move), common visual transforms, model-specific post-transforms (e.g., JPEG metadata extraction for CAT-Net), and shape normalization—so that all models consume inputs in a standardized pipeline while preserving modality-specific requirements.

## Method

IMDL-BenCo decomposes the IMDL framework into four modular components (data loader, model zoo, training script, evaluator) where loss computation is embedded within each model's forward pass through a unified interface, decoupling model customization from pipeline standardization. The model zoo contains 8 SoTA models (one reproduced from scratch), 6 vision backbones, and 5 feature extractor modules (DCT, FFT, Sobel, BayarConv, SRM) managed via a registry mechanism. The benchmark evaluates all models under two standardized protocols (Protocol-MVSS: train on CASIAv2 only; Protocol-CAT: train on a mixed 5-dataset set) across 8 datasets with 15 GPU-accelerated metrics and 3 robustness tests. Key empirical findings show that BayarConv and Sobel extractors degrade performance across all backbones, Swin Transformer and SegFormer are the best-fitting architectures, the NIST16 dataset suffers from label leakage (addressed via SSIM-based cleansing into NIST16-C), and permute-F1/macro-F1 metrics systematically overestimate model performance compared to binary F1.

## Results

Under Protocol-MVSS (trained on CASIAv2 only), TruFor achieves the highest average pixel-level F1 of 0.530, followed closely by IML-ViT (0.519) and CAT-Net (0.401), while ManTra-Net performs worst at 0.123. Under Protocol-CAT (trained on a mixed dataset), IML-ViT leads with an average pixel-level F1 of 0.725, TruFor follows at 0.627, and CAT-Net reaches 0.601. For image-level detection (models with a detection head), MVSS-Net achieves the best average F1 under Protocol-MVSS (0.631), while TruFor leads under Protocol-CAT (0.739). The paper also reveals significant performance drops on NIST16 when using the cleaned NIST16-C dataset, exposing label leakage that inflates scores by up to 25% for some models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper does not propose a new model but establishes the first unified evaluation framework for 8 SoTA IMDL methods. Under Protocol-MVSS, TruFor (0.530 avg F1) and IML-ViT (0.519) clearly outperform all other models, while ManTra-Net (0.123) and ObjectFormer (0.281) lag significantly. Under Protocol-CAT, IML-ViT achieves a substantial lead (0.725 avg F1) over TruFor (0.627) and CAT-Net (0.601), suggesting that high-resolution ViT architectures benefit most from diverse training data. A critical finding is that reproduced results diverge substantially from original paper claims: for example, PSCC-Net's NIST16 F1 drops from 0.742 (fine-tuned in original) to 0.214 under Protocol-MVSS, and ObjectFormer drops from 0.826 to 0.173, exposing that prior comparisons were unfair due to inconsistent training protocols. The paper further demonstrates that NIST16 suffers from label leakage (near-duplicate images split across train/test), and after cleansing to NIST16-C, IML-ViT's NIST16 F1 drops from 0.331 to 0.266 under Protocol-MVSS, while most other models remain relatively stable, indicating IML-ViT was partially exploiting this bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather unifies the evaluation of eight existing SoTA IMDL models (ManTra-Net, MVSS-Net, CAT-Net, ObjectFormer, PSCC-Net, NCL-IML, TruFor, IML-ViT) under a standardized framework. It departs from existing deep learning frameworks like OpenMMLab and Detectron2, whose registry-based, tightly-coupled pipelines are ill-suited to IMDL's heterogeneous low-level feature extractors and diverse loss functions. The paper carves new ground as the first comprehensive IMDL benchmark, addressing the long-noted but unaddressed problem of inconsistent training and evaluation protocols that have made cross-model comparisons in the field unreliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes critical issues that undermine prior IMDL research: label leakage in NIST16 inflates performance, widely-used feature extractors (BayarConv, Sobel) actually degrade accuracy across most backbones, and common metric choices (permute-F1, macro/micro F1) systematically overestimate model capability. These findings will likely reshape how the field designs models and reports results. Open questions include whether better feature fusion strategies can make low-level features useful for ViT-based architectures, whether the NIST16-C cleansing generalizes to other datasets with similar bias, and whether the benchmark's two protocols (MVSS and CAT) adequately capture real-world deployment conditions.
