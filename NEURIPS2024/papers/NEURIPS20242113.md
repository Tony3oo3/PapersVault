## Problem

Open-set node classification on graphs requires both classifying known-class nodes and rejecting unknown-class nodes, but existing approaches relying on softmax confidence thresholds fail due to neural network overconfidence, while density-estimation methods are fragile to the immense diversity of OOD examples. Moreover, prior generative methods for open-set graph learning either require real open-set samples during training or depend on manually designed outliers, and no method provides a threshold-free, theoretically grounded solution for graph-structured data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes node feature matrices (X ∈ R^{N×d}) and adjacency matrices (A ∈ R^{N×N}) from citation-network graphs, and produces a (C+1)-way class assignment where the (C+1)-th class is a single 'unknown' bucket covering all unseen categories. Notably, no features, labels, or side information about unknown classes are available during training or validation (inductive setting), and the model outputs an explicit unknown-class prediction rather than a confidence score requiring a post-hoc threshold.

## Method

EGonc generates two types of substitute unknown nodes via manifold mixup at an intermediate GNN layer: inter-class substitutes (mixing embeddings of connected nodes from different known classes) and external substitutes (mixing peripheral/low-confidence nodes with inverted class centers), both labeled as the (C+1)-th unknown class and augmented into the graph with new edges. An energy propagation step then refines per-node energy scores using the symmetrically normalized adjacency matrix, exploiting structural homogeneity to lower energies for known-class nodes and raise them for unknown substitutes. The open-set classifier is trained with three jointly optimized losses: standard (C+1)-class cross-entropy, a tailored complement entropy that penalizes known-class nodes for high probability on the unknown class and unknown substitutes for high probability on known classes, and an elastic-net-style energy regularization that enforces a margin between IND and OOD energy scores. Theoretical propositions guarantee that minimizing the energy regularization is consistent with minimizing both the cross-entropy and complement-entropy objectives, ensuring a distinguishable detection margin without threshold tuning.

## Results

On near open-set node classification (inductive setting, u=1 unknown class), EGonc achieves an average accuracy of 76.5% and macro-F1 of 69.1% across five citation network datasets (Cora, Citeseer, DBLP, PubMed, Ogbn_arxiv), outperforming the second-best method G²Pxy (74.7% Acc, 67.6% F1) by 2.41% and 2.22% respectively. On far open-set classification, EGonc reaches 85.7% average accuracy and 76.3% F1, again surpassing G²Pxy (85.1% Acc, 75.7% F1). The method also generalizes across GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GraphSage), consistently outperforming all baselines under each architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGonc outperforms all six baselines on every dataset in the near open-set classification task. Against the closest competitor G²Pxy [67] (a generative open-set method using proxy unknowns via mixup), EGonc gains +2.41% accuracy and +2.22% F1 on average, with the largest margin on PubMed (+6.5% Acc). PROSER [70] (placeholder-based open-set recognition) trails by 6.0% accuracy on average, while the energy-based GNNSAFE [56] lags by 7.9% accuracy. Threshold-based methods (OpenMax [1], DOC [46], OpenWGL [54]) are substantially weaker, with DOC achieving only 61.2% average accuracy and OpenWGL 64.9%. On far open-set classification, EGonc (85.7% Acc) edges out G²Pxy (85.1%) and GNNSAFE (80.1%), though the simpler GCN_soft_τ thresholding approach (84.1%) is surprisingly competitive in that setting, which the authors note as a direction for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGonc extends the authors' own prior work G2Pxy (IJCAI 2023), which introduced proxy unknowns via mixup with cross-entropy and complement entropy losses for open-set node classification. The key departures are the introduction of energy-based scoring with topological energy propagation (building on GNNSAFE's EBM-for-graphs idea and Liu et al.'s energy-based OOD detection), a tailored energy regularization loss, and theoretical guarantees of consistency between the energy loss and the classification losses. Unlike threshold-based open-set methods (OpenMax, DOC, OpenWGL), EGonc introduces an explicit (C+1)-th unknown class, eliminating the need for threshold tuning. The substitute unknown generation strategy (inter-class and external via manifold mixup) is inherited from G2Pxy but is now coupled with the energy propagation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical consistency result (Proposition 3) showing that energy regularization optimizes in the same direction as cross-entropy and complement entropy losses provides a principled justification for the multi-loss training objective, which is valuable for the broader EBM-for-classification literature. However, the far OOD results reveal a notable limitation: simple thresholding (GCN_soft_t) achieves comparable performance to EGonc, suggesting the energy-based advantage is primarily for near-OOD scenarios. Open questions include whether combining generative substitute generation with discriminative thresholding could close the far-OOD gap, and whether the energy propagation mechanism generalizes to heterophilic or dynamic graphs beyond the citation networks tested here.
