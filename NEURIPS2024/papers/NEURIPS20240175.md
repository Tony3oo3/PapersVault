## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of GNN expressivity focused on Boolean queries and logical characterizations (e.g., guarded first-order logic with counting), leaving the real-valued computational power of GNNs uncharacterized. This paper asks: exactly which real-valued functions from labeled graphs to labeled graphs can be computed by GNNs with a fixed number of layers? The answer, established here, is precisely the class of functions computable by constant-depth arithmetic circuits over the reals.

## Input / Output

The model takes an undirected labeled graph whose ordered vertices carry feature vectors in R^k and produces a labeled graph with the same vertex set and edge set but updated real-valued feature vectors after L layers of message passing. For the circuit-simulation direction, the graph is encoded as a tuple in (R^k)^(n²+n) consisting of the adjacency matrix entries (each encoded as the all-zero or all-one vector in R^k) followed by the n node feature vectors. The output is the corresponding encoding of the resulting labeled graph, so the correspondence is between graph-to-graph functions and vector-to-vector circuit functions.

## Method

The paper introduces Circuit GNNs (C-GNNs), a generalization of aggregate-combine GNNs in which each node's per-layer computation is an arbitrary constant-depth arithmetic circuit (from a fixed basis) rather than a fixed aggregate-then-combine pipeline. The activation function of the network becomes a gate type in the corresponding arithmetic circuit, making the choice of activation a parameter in the expressivity characterization. Two simulation theorems establish the exact correspondence: (i) any C-GNN using tail-symmetric FAC^0_R^k[A] circuits is simulable by a FAC^0_R^k[A] circuit family (Theorem 3.6), and (ii) any FAC^0_R^k[A] circuit family is simulable by a C-GNN whose internal circuits have access to the same function set A (Theorems 3.11–3.12), with the additional constraint that A-gates appear only as activation functions when circuits are in function-layer form and activations are countably injective (Theorem 3.17). The tail-symmetry condition on the per-node circuits enforces permutation invariance over neighbor multisets, mirroring the aggregation step of standard GNNs.

## Results

The paper establishes an exact correspondence between Circuit Graph Neural Networks (C-GNNs) and constant-depth arithmetic circuits over the reals: a function from labeled graphs to labeled graphs is computable by a C-GNN with a constant number of layers if and only if it is computable by a constant-depth arithmetic circuit (Theorems 3.6, 3.11, 3.12, 3.17). The activation function of the network becomes a gate type in the circuit, making the result parameterized by the choice of activation functions (ReLU, sigmoid, tanh, etc.). The equivalence holds uniformly, for all common activation functions, and for both uniform and non-uniform circuit/network families, with the number of GNN layers equal to the circuit depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The main contribution is an equivalence theorem rather than a performance comparison. The paper positions its C-GNN model as a strict generalization of AC-GNNs (aggregate-combine GNNs), showing that any AC-GNN with FAC⁰_R-computable aggregation functions is simulable by a C-GNN (Remark 3.4). The theoretical upper bound (C-GNNs ⊆ FAC⁰_R[A] circuits) and lower bound (FAC⁰_R[A] circuits ⊆ C-GNNs, under function-layer form and countable injectivity conditions) together yield the exact characterization. The paper contrasts its real-valued circuit-based approach with prior Boolean-circuit characterizations (e.g., Grohe 2023's TC⁰ upper bound) and logic-based characterizations (Barceló et al. 2020's guarded FOC₂), arguing that the arithmetic circuit framework provides a more direct and complete picture of GNN computational power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNN message-passing framework of Gilmer et al. (2017) and Morris et al. (2019) by replacing the standard aggregate-combine step with general constant-depth arithmetic circuits, yielding the C-GNN model that subsumes AC-GNNs (Barceló et al., 2020). It deliberately departs from the dominant logical-characterization approach (Barceló et al. 2020, Grohe 2023, Sato et al. 2019) that relates GNN expressivity to fragments of first-order logic or Boolean TC⁰ circuits, instead grounding the analysis in the BSS model of real computation (Blum et al., 1997) and arithmetic circuits over ℝ. The key conceptual shift is making real numbers first-class citizens: rather than restricting to Boolean inputs/outputs and then bounding GNNs by TC⁰, the paper characterizes the full real-valued computational power of GNNs via constant-depth arithmetic circuits where the activation function becomes a gate type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact correspondence between C-GNNs and constant-depth arithmetic circuits provides a sharp, architecture-agnostic upper bound on GNN expressivity: no amount of scaling (wider or deeper within the fixed-depth message-passing framework) can compute functions outside FAC⁰_ℝ, and improving expressivity requires aggregation/combine functions that provably escape this class. This reframes the common 'scale up to solve harder reasoning' narrative with a precise theoretical limitation. Open questions include whether the tail-symmetry and function-layer-form restrictions can be eliminated on both sides of the simulation, what the correct uniformity notion is for GNN families, and how these circuit-complexity results connect to training complexity. The paper is purely theoretical with no experiments, and the authors note that practical implications require further study of what functions are computable by implementable arithmetic circuits of various sizes and depths.
