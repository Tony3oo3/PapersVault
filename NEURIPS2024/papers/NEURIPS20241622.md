## Problem

As diffusion-based generative models flood the Internet with photorealistic synthetic images, datasets collected for machine learning research are increasingly contaminated with AI-generated content. This paper investigates how such contamination specifically affects online continual learning (CL), where data arrives in a one-pass stream and quality cannot be assessed in advance, and identifies that existing online CL methods suffer severe and progressive performance degradation as the contamination ratio increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241622/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives a one-pass stream of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) in a class-incremental or domain-incremental task sequence, where a fraction P of images are synthetic and the learner has no access to their provenance. A fixed-size memory buffer (1K–10K samples) stores a subset of previously seen images for replay. The model outputs a class prediction for each incoming image and continuously updates the memory buffer contents and their associated entropy scores after each task.

## Method

ESRM combines two mechanisms motivated by empirical observations of synthetic data properties. Entropy Selection (ES) exploits the finding that synthetic images produce lower prediction entropy than real images: at each batch, ES discards the 50% lowest-entropy samples, then uses reservoir sampling to decide which survivors enter the buffer, replacing the lowest-entropy sample of the same class. Real-synthetic similarity Maximization (RM) is a contrastive loss (a variant of supervised contrastive learning) that splits each batch into high-entropy and low-entropy groups and maximizes their mutual cosine similarity, while also aligning stream embeddings with memory embeddings, thereby closing the feature-space gap between real and synthetic samples. A self-distillation term (KL divergence between augmented and unaugmented predictions) further prevents overconfidence. The total training objective is L_CE + λ₁·L_SDC + λ₂·L_RM with λ₁=1 and λ₂=0.5, applied on a ResNet-18 backbone with a 128-dim projection head.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/SDXL at 95% contamination, ESRM achieves 60.04% average accuracy with only a 7.31-point drop from the clean setting (67.35%), while the best baseline OCM drops 15.59 points to 57.07%. On CIFAR-100/SDXL at 95% contamination, ESRM reaches 37.45% (−10.27 from clean) versus OCM's 28.84% (−13.17). On TinyImageNet/SDXL at 95% contamination, ESRM achieves 13.02% compared to OCM's 5.37%, a gap of over 7.6 points. The advantage of ESRM over all baselines grows monotonically with contamination severity, with the largest margins appearing at P ≥ 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESRM outperforms all six baselines (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, OCM, GSA, OnPro) across every dataset and contamination ratio tested. At moderate contamination (P=70%), ESRM's advantage is modest (e.g., +2.3 points over OCM on CIFAR-100), but at severe contamination (P=95%) the gap widens dramatically: ESRM beats OCM by 8.6 points on CIFAR-100 (37.45% vs. 28.84%) and by 7.6 points on TinyImageNet (13.02% vs. 5.37%). Among baselines, OCM and OnPro are the most robust to contamination, while DER++ and ER-ACE suffer the steepest degradation (e.g., DER++ drops 23.85 points on CIFAR-10 at P=95%). ESRM also achieves the highest clean-setting accuracy on CIFAR-100 (47.72%) and TinyImageNet (32.15%), indicating that its entropy-based buffer management and contrastive alignment provide benefits even without contamination. The paper further validates ESRM in a Domain-Incremental Learning setting (DIL-CIFAR20), where it again outperforms all baselines under contamination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online continual learning (CL) literature by introducing synthetic data contamination as a new threat to replay-based methods, building on prior work showing that AI-generated images degrade non-continual training (Hataya et al., 2023; Martínez et al., 2023). Methodologically, it combines entropy-based buffer management (inspired by reservoir sampling and the observed lower entropy of synthetic samples) with a contrastive alignment loss derived from supervised contrastive learning (SupCon), alongside self-distillation for overconfidence. The paper departs from prior contamination research, which focused on static training settings, by showing that the online CL scenario is uniquely vulnerable because data cannot be re-examined or filtered after a single pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models make high-fidelity synthetic images trivially accessible, future internet-collected datasets will inevitably contain AI-generated content, making this a practical concern for the CL community. The paper's key open questions include whether the entropy-based discrimination generalizes to more diverse user prompts and commercial generators (Midjourney, DALL-E), and whether the approach scales to larger architectures or longer task sequences. The authors also raise a broader societal point about the need for watermarking and regulation of AI-generated content to preserve the integrity of internet data as a training resource.
