## Problem

While scaling laws have been well-characterized for NLP and computer vision foundation models, the scaling behavior of molecular pretraining models remains unexplored. Prior molecular pretraining studies (e.g., Uni-Mol, GEM, GROVER) operate at relatively small parameter and data scales, leaving it unclear whether the power-law relationships between validation loss, model size, dataset size, and compute budget observed in LLMs also hold for 3D molecular representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D molecular conformation represented as a tuple (x, e, r) where x are per-atom features (type, chirality, degree, formal charge, hybridization, aromaticity, etc.), e are per-pair bond features (bond type, stereo, conjugation), and r are 3D Cartesian coordinates. During pretraining, the model outputs predicted masked atom tokens (15% of atoms) and denoised 3D coordinates via a position-prediction head that uses pair-distance-biased attention. For downstream fine-tuning, the model produces scalar property predictions (e.g., HOMO, LUMO, polarizability, dipole moment) from the final atom and pair representations.

## Method

Uni-Mol2 is a two-track transformer (inheriting the architecture from Uni-Mol+) that concurrently updates atom representations via self-attention with pair-bias and pair representations via outer-product, triangular-update, and FFN operations, with pre-norm layer normalization for training stability. Pretraining uses two self-supervised objectives: masked atom token prediction (cross-entropy on 15% masked positions) and coordinate denoising (Gaussian noise σ=0.2 on all coordinates plus 50% masking of atomic, bond, and shortest-path features, with Kabsch alignment before loss computation). The key empirical contribution is characterizing the scaling law L(M,S,C) = 2.660·M^(−1.137) + 1.848·S^(−0.225) + 0.588·C^(−1.479) by training models from 42M to 1.1B parameters on 838M conformations sampled from an 884M-molecule dataset (Uni-Mol + ZINC20) using temperature-based scaffold-balanced sampling (τ=0.005). The 1.1B model achieves 27% average MAE improvement over Uni-Mol on QM9 and 14% on COMPAS-1D, with consistent downstream gains as parameters increase from 84M to 1.1B.

## Results

On QM9, Uni-Mol2 1.1B achieves the best MAE across all six properties: 0.0035 (HOMO/LUMO/GAP), 0.305 (alpha), 0.144 (Cv), 0.089 (mu), 4.265 (R2), and 0.0005 (ZPVE), representing an average 27% improvement over Uni-Mol. On COMPAS-1D, Uni-Mol2 1.1B with atom and bond features achieves 0.0093 (aEA), 0.0074 (aIP), 0.0067 (dispersion), and 0.0170 (Dipmom Debye), a 14% average improvement over Uni-Mol. The scaling trend is consistent: performance improves monotonically from 84M to 1.1B parameters on most QM9 properties, with the 1.1B model reaching a validation loss of 0.087 versus 0.105 for the 84M model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Uni-Mol2 against three baselines: GROVER (SMILES-based), GEM (3D geometry-enhanced), and Uni-Mol (the direct predecessor). Uni-Mol2 1.1B outperforms Uni-Mol on all six QM9 properties, with the largest relative gains on mu (42.6% reduction: 0.089 vs. 0.155) and R2 (11.2% reduction: 4.265 vs. 4.805). Against GEM, Uni-Mol2 1.1B achieves substantially lower MAE on every property, e.g., 0.305 vs. 0.589 on alpha and 4.265 vs. 25.67 on R2. On COMPAS-1D, Uni-Mol2 1.1B with atom and bond features (marked ★) beats Uni-Mol on aIP (0.0074 vs. 0.0083), dispersion (0.0067 vs. 0.0092), and Dipmom Debye (0.0170 vs. 0.0198), though it slightly trails on aEA (0.0093 vs. 0.0099). The scaling trend is consistent: each increase in model size from 84M to 1.1B yields progressive MAE reductions on most properties, confirming the scaling law in downstream transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Uni-Mol/Uni-Mol+ line of 3D molecular representation learning by scaling the two-track transformer architecture from ~84M to 1.1B parameters and the pretraining corpus from 19M to 884M conformations (sourced from ZINC20). The architecture itself is largely inherited from Uni-Mol+, with minor modifications (pre-norm layer normalization, removal of two regularization terms); the core contribution is empirical rather than architectural. The paper positions its scaling-law analysis as the first systematic study of power-law relationships in molecular pretraining, drawing directly on the Kaplan et al. (2020) and Chinchilla-style framework from NLP, while noting that prior molecular scaling work (Chen et al., 2024) was limited to small architectures like GIN and SchNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that molecular pretraining follows power-law scaling analogous to NLP and CV, the paper provides a quantitative roadmap for the field: practitioners can now predict how much additional compute or data is needed to reach a target validation loss, and the 884M-conformation Uni-Mol2 dataset becomes a shared resource for training large molecular models. However, the analysis is constrained by a fixed batch size and learning rate across all scales, and the authors acknowledge that optimal hyperparameters likely shift with scale. Open questions include whether scaling benefits transfer to generative tasks, whether decode-only architectures (dominant in LLMs) are more suitable for molecules, and whether the observed convergence on certain QM9 properties (HOMO/LUMO/GAP, ZPVE) reflects a true performance ceiling or an artifact of the small test set.
