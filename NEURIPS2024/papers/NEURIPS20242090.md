## Problem

Existing methods for leveraging expert demonstrations in online RL assume homogeneity between offline and online data, but in practice experts often make decisions based on contextual information (e.g., a student's learning style, a patient's unrecorded medical history) that is invisible to the learner. This unobserved heterogeneity causes naive imitation to collapse to a single suboptimal policy and forces purely online methods into costly trial-and-error. The paper addresses this gap by formulating the problem as zero-shot meta-RL with an unknown distribution over unobserved context variables and a Bayesian regret objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams: (1) offline expert demonstrations consisting solely of state-action trajectories (no rewards, no context labels, no task identifiers), and (2) online environment feedback (states, rewards, next states) during T episodes. The output is a sequence of policy actions for the online task, driven by a learned prior distribution over the unobserved context space. A key constraint is that the expert's private context variable is never recorded in the demonstration data, so the prior must be inferred purely from the marginal likelihood of observed state-action sequences under a noisily-rational expert model.

## Method

ExPerior uses empirical Bayes to construct an informative prior over unobserved context variables from expert demonstrations, then drives online exploration via posterior sampling. Two prior-learning procedures are provided: a parametric approach (maximum marginal likelihood estimation with a known distributional family, e.g., Beta) and a nonparametric maximum-entropy approach that selects the least-informative prior compatible with the expert data, solved in closed form via Fenchel duality to yield a Gibbs-form density. For MDPs, Q-network parameters serve as a proxy for the unobserved context, and the learned prior initializes an ensemble of bootstrapped DQNs (ExPerior-Param) or is matched via SGLD sampling (ExPerior-MaxEnt). For bandits, the paper additionally derives a frequentist successive-elimination algorithm whose Bayesian regret bound is proportional to a term resembling the entropy of the optimal action, confirming the empirical observation that ExPerior's regret scales linearly with the entropy of the expert policy.

## Results

On K-armed Bernoulli bandits (K=10, T=1500), ExPerior-Param achieves Bayesian regret of ~8.5 in low-entropy settings, matching Oracle-TS (8.5±2.7), while Naive-TS and Naive-UCB incur ~50+ regret regardless of entropy. On the Frozen Lake POMDP (5×5, 9 hazards, β=1), ExPerior-MaxEnt achieves the optimal reward of 6.00, compared to −23.32 for Naive Boot-DQN and 6.00 for ExPLORe. On the Deep Sea MDP (30×30 grid), ExPerior outperforms all baselines across all four goal distributions, with the performance gap over Naive Boot-DQN shrinking as context entropy increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Naive Boot-DQN (Osband et al., 2016), ExPerior achieves dramatic gains: on the Frozen Lake MDP with β=1, ExPerior-MaxEnt reaches 12.26 reward versus −23.32 for Naive Boot-DQN, a gap of over 35 points. Against ExPLORe (Li et al., 2024), ExPerior wins in most settings but ExPLORe matches or slightly exceeds ExPerior in the Deep Sea MDP (e.g., 12.3 vs 12.71 at β=10) and achieves near-optimal 6.00 on the POMDP even with a nearly random expert (β=0.1), where ExPerior-MaxEnt degrades to −22.58. Against Naive-TS/Naive-UCB (Osband et al., 2013) in bandits, ExPerior-Param reduces regret from ~50+ to ~8.5 in low-entropy contexts, while the pure online baselines show flat performance (~50) across all entropy levels, confirming that expert data is most valuable when unobserved heterogeneity has limited effect on optimal actions. In high-entropy bandit settings, ExPerior's advantage narrows and Naive-UCB can surpass UCB-ExPLORe, suggesting that overly optimistic reward labelling hurts when expert demonstrations are uninformative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) online RL accelerated by offline expert demonstrations (DDPGfD, DQfD, Hao et al.), which typically assumes homogeneity between offline and online data; (2) imitation learning with unobserved contexts (Yu et al., Kallus et al.), which either requires expert queries during training or is restricted to offline evaluation; and (3) zero-shot meta-RL, which infers a task distribution from limited data. ExPerior's key departure is combining these into a unified empirical Bayes framework: it uses only offline expert trajectories (no rewards, no task labels, no expert access during training) to infer a prior over unobserved context variables via maximum entropy, then drives online exploration through posterior sampling. The closest prior work, ExPLORe (Li et al.), also uses unlabeled offline data but assigns optimistic rewards rather than inferring a distributional prior, and does not model unobserved heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, model-agnostic way to leverage expert demonstrations when the expert's information advantage is invisible to the learner—a common and under-addressed scenario in healthcare, education, and autonomous driving. The finding that Bayesian regret scales linearly (rather than sublinearly) with the entropy of the optimal action under the prior is a useful diagnostic: it suggests the entropy of expert demonstrations can quantify how much unobserved heterogeneity degrades learning. Limitations include the assumption that unobserved factors remain fixed across episodes, the context-independent transition function assumption in MDPs, and a limited experimental scope (small tabular environments, no human-in-the-loop). Open questions include whether the linear regret-entropy relationship extends to continuous-action MDPs and whether the max-entropy prior remains well-calibrated under distribution shift between offline and online contexts.
