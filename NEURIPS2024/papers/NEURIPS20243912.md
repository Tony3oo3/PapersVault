## Problem

Urban knowledge graph construction (UrbanKGC) has long relied on manually designed rules and domain expertise, making it labor-intensive and hard to scale. While LLMs offer a promising alternative, they suffer from two critical gaps for urban tasks: an inability to understand heterogeneous urban relationships (spatial, temporal, functional) and a deficiency in geospatial computing and reasoning needed for relation completion. No prior work provides a unified, automated LLM agent framework that addresses both challenges simultaneously for UrbanKGC.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the relational triplet extraction (RTE) task, the method consumes unstructured urban text descriptions (averaging ~400–1,200 words per record, sourced from AOIs, roads, POIs, reviews, and web pages) in an open-world setting with no predefined ontology, and outputs entity-relation triplets. For the knowledge graph completion (KGC) task, it takes head-tail geo-entity pairs each annotated with geometry (point coordinates, linestrings, or polygons) and predicts one of five RCC geospatial relations (disconnected, externally connected, partially overlapping, equal, inside). The final output is a multi-relational heterogeneous UrbanKG with fine-grained entity and relation ontologies (e.g., 2,138 relation types for NYC-Large).

## Method

UrbanKGent is a three-stage LLM agent pipeline. First, a knowledgeable instruction generation module constructs multi-view (spatial, temporal, functional) multi-turn dialog templates for RTE and geospatial-infused templates (injecting latitude/longitude and RCC relation definitions) for KGC, aligning LLMs to urban domain semantics. Second, a tool-augmented iterative trajectory refinement module prompts GPT-4 with Chain-of-Thought to generate reasoning trajectories, then invokes eight external geospatial tool interfaces (distance calculation, geohash encoding, point/polygon/linestring containment and intersection checks) to supply exact computation results, followed by up to three rounds of iterative self-refinement (verifier + updater) to filter unfaithful trajectories. Third, hybrid instruction fine-tuning via LoRA on Llama 2-7/13B and Llama 3-8B distills the refined trajectories into compact UrbanKGent agents (7B/8B/13B) that outperform GPT-4 by over 10% at roughly 20× lower inference cost.

## Results

On the NYC dataset, UrbanKGent-13B achieves 52% GPT-evaluated accuracy on RTE and 56% on KGC, surpassing the best baseline (GPT-4 with the same inference pipeline at 45%/48%) by 15.56% and 14.29% respectively. On CHI, UrbanKGent-13B reaches 53% (RTE) and 48% (KGC) GPT accuracy, outperforming GPT-4 (46%/41%) by 15.22% and 17.07%. Under human evaluation, UrbanKGent-13B scores 54%/47% on NYC and 59%/49% on CHI, exceeding GPT-4's 47%/42% and 52%/43% by 11.90–14.89% and 13.46–13.95% respectively. The 7B and 8B variants also consistently outperform GPT-4 across all four evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanKGent-13B outperforms all 31 baselines across both datasets and both evaluation protocols. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline (GPT-4), UrbanKGent-13B gains 15.56% on NYC RTE and 17.07% on CHI KGC (GPT evaluation). Against the best in-context learning baseline (GPT-3.5 ICL at 41%/42% on NYC), UrbanKGent-13B improves by 11–14 percentage points. Against the best vanilla fine-tuning baseline (Llama-2-13B VFT at 35%/41% on NYC), the gain is 17–15 percentage points. Even the smallest UrbanKGent-7B (46%/49% on NYC) surpasses GPT-4 zero-shot (38%/39%) by 8–10 percentage points, demonstrating that the knowledgeable instruction design and tool-augmented trajectory refinement transfer effectively to smaller open-source models. The paper also reports that UrbanKGent-13B reduces inference cost by roughly 20× compared to GPT-4 while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: LLM-based knowledge graph construction (PRGC, RelationPrompt, KG-BERT), domain-specific LLM agents (Voyager, LLMLight, AgentTuning, FireAct), and urban knowledge graph construction (UUKG, UrbanKG, GeoLM, K2). It departs from prior UrbanKG work, which relied on manually designed rules and patterns for entity-relation extraction, by formulating UrbanKGC as an agent task solvable via instruction-tuned LLMs with external geospatial tool invocation. The trajectory refinement pipeline directly adapts FireAct's CoT distillation and Self-Refine's iterative verifier-updater loop, while the hybrid instruction fine-tuning strategy follows AgentTuning's mixed-task paradigm, all specialized to the urban domain's heterogeneous spatial-temporal-functional relationships and RCC geospatial relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first fully automatic, open-source UrbanKG construction agent family (7/8/13B) that eliminates the manual rule-design bottleneck in prior UrbanKG studies, and demonstrates that a domain-fine-tuned 13B model can surpass GPT-4 by over 10% at roughly 20× lower cost. Practically, the constructed UrbanKGs contain hundreds of times more relation types than the UUKG benchmark while using only one-fifth the data, suggesting a path toward richer urban semantic modeling. However, the paper does not demonstrate downstream task improvements (e.g., traffic prediction, mobility forecasting) using the constructed graphs, and the evaluation relies on GPT-4-as-judge which, while validated for consistency, remains cost-intensive. Open questions include whether the agent generalizes beyond two US cities, whether the open-world (no predefined ontology) setting scales to more diverse urban contexts, and how the constructed graphs perform in actual urban prediction tasks.
