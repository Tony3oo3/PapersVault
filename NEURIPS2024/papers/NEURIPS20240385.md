## Problem

Existing drug-induced toxicity datasets are small, cover only a single organ system, and require months of expert curation to parse lengthy FDA drug labels (often exceeding 100 pages). No unified, large-scale dataset exists that systematically rates multiple toxicity types across the full landscape of FDA-approved drugs, limiting the ability to train and benchmark molecular toxicity prediction models across organ systems.

## Input / Output

The LLM pipeline consumes full FDA drug label text (averaging 8,254 words) along with a toxicity-type-specific prompt, and produces a condensed toxicity summary (~297 words), a ternary rating (No/Less/Most), and a binary rating (No/Yes) per drug-toxicity pair. The two-tiered design means the rating prompt only sees the generated summary, not the raw label, forcing information compression. For the ML benchmarking component, input is a molecular SMILES string (from 1,349 deduplicated small molecules) and output is a predicted No/Most toxicity classification per organ system.

## Method

The core method applies GPT-4o with chain-of-thought reasoning in a two-tiered prompt system: a 'summary prompt' first extracts all toxicity-relevant passages from the full drug label and explicitly states whether the drug itself caused the risk, then a 'rating prompt' assigns a ternary or binary classification based solely on that summary. This is applied to 2,418 FDA-approved drugs (including biologics) across eight toxicity types chosen with clinician input. The ternary 'Less' category is designed to capture borderline cases where reasonable readers might disagree, effectively letting the model abstain on ambiguous drugs. For downstream benchmarking, they train Chemprop (GNN), MLP, Random Forest, and SVM models on the UniTox Small Molecule Benchmark using ten-fold scaffold-split cross-validation, with Chemprop and MLP operating in a multi-task setting predicting all eight toxicities simultaneously. Ablations confirm that the chain-of-thought summary step, GPT-4o (over GPT-3.5), and full-label context (over RAG fragments) each contribute to accuracy, achieving 93.0% on DICTrank versus 77.7% for the prior SOTA (askFDALabel).

## Results

On DICTrank (cardiotoxicity, n=603 ground-truth No/Most drugs), UniTox's GPT-4o pipeline achieves 93.0% binary accuracy, substantially outperforming askFDALabel's 77.7%. On DILIrank (liver toxicity, n=363), the pipeline reaches 86.2% binary accuracy, and on DIRIL (renal toxicity, n=269), it achieves 72.9%. Clinician validation on five additional toxicity types (pulmonary, hematological, dermatological, ototoxicity, infertility) shows 85–96% concordance with expert ratings across 200 randomly sampled drugs per toxicity. For downstream molecular prediction, Chemprop trained on the UniTox Small Molecule Benchmark (1,349 drugs) achieves competitive ROCAUC across all eight toxicities using scaffold-split 10-fold cross-validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is askFDALabel [Wu et al., 2024], the FDA's RAG-based LLM system for drug label querying. On DICTrank, UniTox's full GPT-4o pipeline achieves 93.0% binary accuracy on the ground-truth No/Most subset (n=603), a 15.3-point improvement over askFDALabel's 77.7% (n=584). Even when restricted to GPT-3.5, UniTox's prompting strategy reaches 88.4%, still well above askFDALabel's 71.5% with GPT-3.5, suggesting the two-tiered chain-of-thought prompt (summary → rating) is a key contributor beyond model scale. On DILIrank, UniTox achieves 86.2% binary accuracy with no prior baseline available for direct comparison. On DIRIL, performance drops to 72.9%, which the authors attribute to DIRIL's different construction methodology (drawing from two prior papers rather than direct label analysis); notably, for 9 of 25 false positives and 8 of 48 false negatives, at least one of the source papers actually agreed with GPT-4o's rating over the FDA paper's determination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FDA's line of expert-curated single-organ toxicity datasets (DICTrank, DILIrank, DIRIL) by replacing time-intensive manual annotation with a GPT-4o chain-of-thought pipeline that processes full drug labels to produce unified ratings across eight organ systems simultaneously. It departs from the FDA's own askFDALabel RAG system by using full-label summarization rather than top-k fragment retrieval, and from prior ML toxicity benchmarks (Tox21, ToxCast, SIDER) by focusing exclusively on human in vivo FDA-approved drugs including biologics. The two-tiered prompt design (summary then rating) draws on chain-of-thought prompting, while the downstream benchmarking uses standard molecular property prediction architectures (Chemprop, MLP, RF, SVM) with RDKit and Morgan fingerprints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniTox provides the first unified cross-toxicity benchmark spanning 2,418 drugs and eight organ systems, enabling multi-task toxicity prediction models and cross-toxicity correlation analyses that were previously impossible with single-organ datasets. Key limitations include the loss of nuance when condensing complex label text into ternary/binary ratings (particularly for borderline cases, class effects, and animal-only evidence), restriction to FDA-approved drugs (excluding failed candidates), and inability to train a unified model across small molecules and biologics. Open questions include whether more sophisticated rating prompts can better handle edge cases, whether the methodology extends to non-FDA regulatory documents, and whether the LLM-generated ratings can be further refined through iterative human-in-the-loop correction.
