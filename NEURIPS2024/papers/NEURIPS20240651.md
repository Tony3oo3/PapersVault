## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods struggle with articulated object manipulation in real images: 2D feature-sharing approaches produce artifacts when objects rotate or deform, while 2D-3D-2D pipelines depend heavily on the quality of single-image 3D reconstruction and require manual, laborious manipulation. No prior method can robustly handle multiple articulated object categories (rotation and translation joints) without precise 3D CAD models, category-specific fine-tuning, or extensive manual intervention.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image (normalized to 512×512) containing an articulated object and a text instruction (e.g., 'open the laptop 120°') or direct human interaction in 3D space as input, and produces a single edited RGB image where the object has been manipulated while the background is preserved or semantically in-painted. The entire pipeline is training-free: Grounded SAM provides part-level segmentation, T2I Adapter on Stable Diffusion V1-5 serves as the conditional generator, and a Primitive Prototype Library of 5 basic 3D shapes (planes, cubes, boxes) represents 6 object categories without any learned 3D reconstruction. Sequential manipulation (e.g., opening an object in 4 progressive steps) is supported, enabling video-like generation from a single input.

## Method

PA-Diffusion introduces an Abstract 3D Model that represents articulated objects as combinations of primitive prototypes (planes, cubes, boxes) rather than requiring precise mesh reconstruction; camera alignment is solved via PnP from detected corner points, and manipulation is executed in Blender via text-to-Python-script conversion or direct human interaction. The key mechanism is a set of dynamic feature maps: three noise maps (initial, random, and manipulated) are denoised as a batch, where the manipulated noise map—warped by a perspective transform computed from part-level masks—transfers the appearance of seen parts to their new spatial locations, while the random noise map generates novel-appearing parts. Compositional activation maps merge the seen-part and background features from the guidance branch into the generation branch, and the final image is assembled by pasting novel-appearing regions from the random-noise-generated image onto the main output. Two custom score losses are added to the classifier-free guidance: Texture Consistency Score Loss (TCSL) enforces feature matching between guidance and generation branches to prevent blurriness from noise-map warping, and Style Consistency Score Loss (SCSL) aligns the style of novel-appearing parts with seen parts via L1 loss on summed feature maps.

## Results

On the articulated object manipulation testbench (660 real images, 6 categories), PA-Diffusion achieves an average FID of 1.09, outperforming MasaCtrl (1.82) by 40.1% and Imagic (10.1) by 89.2%. The best per-category FID is 0.60 on Drawer, while the weakest is 1.94 on Laptop (where MasaCtrl's 1.17 is slightly better). The method requires no training or fine-tuning during editing, running on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PA-Diffusion is compared against four state-of-the-art diffusion-based editing methods. Against Imagic (text-only guidance), PA-Diffusion wins decisively on all 6 categories (avg. FID 1.09 vs. 10.1), as Imagic cannot reliably open articulated objects or manipulate the correct part. Against MasaCtrl (feature/attention map sharing), PA-Diffusion wins on 5 of 6 categories but loses on Laptop (FID 1.94 vs. 1.17), where MasaCtrl's direct feature transfer happens to preserve the laptop's appearance better despite the structural change. DragDiffusion is excluded from quantitative evaluation because it fails to complete manipulation tasks even with human interaction, and Image Sculpting is excluded due to its reliance on precise 3D reconstruction (via Zero123) which frequently fails for categories like laptops and trashcans, plus its ~10-minute per-image manual manipulation time making large-scale editing impractical. Qualitatively, PA-Diffusion produces high-fidelity edits with consistent novel-appearing parts, while MasaCtrl leaves unedited regions in moved objects and Image Sculpting produces distorted results when 3D reconstruction is inaccurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based image editing pipeline (DDIM inversion, Stable Diffusion, T2I Adapter) and adapts score-based guidance losses from DragDiffusion (TCSL) and Diffusion Self-Guidance (SCSL) to the articulated object manipulation setting. Its key conceptual departure is replacing precise 3D reconstruction (as in Image Sculpting) with an Abstract 3D Model composed of primitive prototypes (planes, cubes, boxes), and replacing static feature/attention map sharing (as in MasaCtrl) with dynamic feature maps that explicitly separate seen parts from novel-appearing parts. This positions the work at the intersection of 2D-3D-2D editing and feature-sharing editing, but sidesteps the 3D reconstruction quality bottleneck that limits the former and the spatial misalignment problem that limits the latter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, category-agnostic nature of the method (no fine-tuning for new object types, no precise 3D CAD models required) makes it a practical tool for generating large volumes of articulated object manipulation data, which the paper demonstrates is useful for 3D articulated object understanding in robotic scenarios. However, the method is limited to rigid objects with rotation/translation joints, fails under large deformations or low-quality inputs, and the quantitative evaluation covers only 6 categories on a 660-image testbench. Open questions include whether the abstract 3D model approach generalizes to deformable objects and fluids, and whether the synthetic data pipeline can scale to the millions of samples needed for robust robotic manipulation learning.
