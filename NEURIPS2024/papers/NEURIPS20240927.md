## Problem

GAIL training is notoriously unstable, with oscillating loss curves that slow convergence and degrade final policy quality. The paper shows theoretically that this instability is fundamental: the desired state (policy matching the expert, discriminator outputting 1/2) is not an equilibrium of GAIL's training dynamics due to the entropy regularization term, meaning GAIL cannot converge to the expert policy even with unlimited data and capacity. Prior work addressed sample efficiency or final return but did not directly resolve the underlying optimization instability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of expert trajectories (state-action tuples collected from an expert policy π_E) and access to an MDP environment for on-policy rollouts, but no external reward signal during training. It outputs a learned generative policy π_θ mapping states to actions. A key practical constraint is that the controller is applied only to the discriminator's loss function, since the full theoretical controller for the policy generator would require knowing the expert policy's action likelihoods—precisely what the algorithm is trying to learn. This makes C-GAIL a drop-in modification: one simply adds a quadratic regularization term to the discriminator objective of any existing GAIL variant.

## Method

The paper formulates GAIL's training as a continuous-time dynamical system of ODEs in function space, deriving the gradient flow for both the discriminator D and policy π. It proves that the desired state is not an equilibrium because the entropy term λH(π) makes the policy's time derivative nonzero even when π = π_E and D = 1/2. To fix this, the authors design a linear negative feedback controller u_1(t) = -k(D - 1/2) for the discriminator and a compensating controller u_2(t) for the policy, then prove asymptotic stability of the controlled system under a simplified 'one-step' setting (truncating trajectory length to one) via the Principle of Linearized Stability. In practice, C-GAIL integrates only the discriminator controller into the loss as a differentiable regularization term -k/2·(D(s,a) - 1/2)², leaving the policy update unchanged. This single modification speeds up convergence, reduces oscillation range by up to 10×, and more closely matches the expert's state distribution (measured by Wasserstein distance) across MuJoCo and Atari environments.

## Results

On five MuJoCo environments, C-GAIL matches or exceeds expert returns (e.g., 2411±21 on Ant vs. expert 2408±110; 2636±8 on Hopper vs. expert 2631±19) while achieving the lowest variance of any method tested. C-GAIL outperforms vanilla GAIL by 324 return on Ant (2411 vs. 2087) and 142 on Hopper (2636 vs. 2579). In the GAIL-DAC setting, C-GAIL-DAC converges 5× faster on Hopper, 2× faster on Half-Cheetah, and reduces the oscillation range by ~10× on Ant, while also achieving a state Wasserstein distance more than 2× smaller than GAIL-DAC at the end of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-GAIL outperforms vanilla GAIL [Ho & Ermon] on all five MuJoCo environments, with the largest gap on Ant (+324 return) and Hopper (+57), while also reducing variance by 5–10×. Against BC [Codevilla], C-GAIL wins on Ant (+474) and Hopper (−194, but with 33× lower variance), ties on Swimmer, and is slightly behind on Half-Cheetah (−30) and Walker2d (−39). C-GAIL dominates AIRL [Fu] across all environments (e.g., +2532 on Ant, +1598 on Half-Cheetah). Against DAgger [Ross], C-GAIL wins on four of five environments (Half-Cheetah +1742, Hopper −115 but with 1.4× lower variance, Swimmer −46, Walker2d +459) but loses on Ant (−616). In the GAIL-DAC setting, C-GAIL-DAC outperforms GAIL-DAC [Kostrikov] on all five environments in both return and state Wasserstein distance, and also beats BC+GAIL [Jena] and WAIL [Xiao] on all environments in Appendix F, while requiring fewer gradient steps to reach 95% of max return.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and GAIL-DAC (Kostrikov et al., 2018) by reframing their training dynamics as a dynamical system and applying control-theoretic stabilization. It departs from prior GAIL work that focused on sample efficiency and final return, and from prior theoretical analyses that assumed convergence under strong conditions (i.i.d. samples, linear MDPs, strongly concave objectives). The control-theory approach builds on recent work stabilizing GANs (Xu et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2023), but requires new analysis because GAIL's policy generator involves MDP transitions, making the induced dynamical system fundamentally different from the static data-distribution case in GANs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for GAIL's well-known training instability: the desired state (policy = expert, discriminator = 1/2) is not an equilibrium of the training dynamics due to the entropy term, meaning GAIL fundamentally cannot converge to the expert policy even with unlimited data and capacity. The practical fix—a simple quadratic regularization on the discriminator loss—is easy to adopt and improves convergence speed and oscillation across multiple GAIL variants. However, the convergence guarantee holds only in a simplified 'one-step' setting, and the practical implementation applies the controller only to the discriminator (the policy controller requires knowing the expert policy, which is the very thing being learned), leaving open whether a full controller with an estimated expert policy would yield further gains.
