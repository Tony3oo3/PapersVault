## Problem

Despite widespread empirical use of gradient-based guidance to steer pre-trained diffusion models toward user-specified objectives, there is no rigorous theory explaining why naive gradient guidance (∇f applied directly to the noised sample) destroys the latent data structure, how to design guidance that preserves it, or whether the resulting generation process has any optimization convergence guarantees. This paper establishes the first mathematical framework linking gradient-guided diffusion to regularized optimization, providing provable structure-preservation and convergence results.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained score network s_θ(x_t, t) (e.g., a U-Net or Stable Diffusion backbone) and a differentiable scalar objective f: R^D → R as input, along with tuning parameters β(t), target reward values y_k, and batch sizes. It outputs an adapted diffusion model (s_θ, G_K) whose backward sampling produces samples that maximize f while remaining within the data's latent subspace Span(A). In Algorithm 2, the score network itself is also updated via re-weighted score matching on a mixture of pre-training data and self-generated samples, so the output is a jointly refined (s_θ_K, G_K) pair.

## Method

The core contribution is a look-ahead loss gradient guidance G_loss(x_t, t) = −β(t)·∇_{x_t}(y − g^⊤ E[x_0|x_t])², which replaces the naive ∇f with a gradient computed through the Tweedie-based clean-data estimator E[x_0|x_t] = α^{-1}(t)(x_t + h(t)s_θ(x_t,t)). Theorem 1 proves G_loss always lies in Span(A) for any data distribution supported on a low-dimensional subspace, because the Jacobian ∇_{x_t}E[x_0|x_t] automatically projects the gradient onto the latent subspace via the score decomposition. Algorithm 1 iteratively queries ∇f at generated sample means, updates G_loss, and resamples with a fixed score, which the paper shows (Theorem 2) converges linearly to a regularized optimum where the pre-trained score acts as a Gaussian prior. Algorithm 2 additionally fine-tunes the score network via re-weighted score matching on self-generated samples, and Theorem 3 proves the iteration converges to the unregularized global optimum within Span(A) at rate Õ(1/K), matching classical convex optimization rates.

## Results

In simulation (d=16, D=64, U-Net with 14.8M params), G_loss preserves the latent subspace structure with substantially lower off-support ratio r_off = ‖x_⊥‖/‖x_∥‖ compared to naive gradient guidance, validating Theorem 1. Algorithm 1 (fixed pre-trained score) converges to a sub-optimal objective value, consistent with the pre-trained model acting as a regularizer (Theorem 2), while Algorithm 2 (adaptive fine-tuning) converges to the global maximum of the objective within the latent subspace (Theorem 3). In image generation with StableDiffusion v1.5 and a ResNet-18 reward model, the reward increases monotonically across iterations and converges; larger guidance strength y (weaker regularization) yields higher convergent reward but increasingly abstract, less photo-realistic images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed G_loss (look-ahead loss gradient guidance) and naive gradient guidance G(x_t,t) = β(t)(y − g^T E[x_0|x_t])·g. In the subspace simulation, naive gradient guidance causes generated samples to leave the data subspace (large off-support error), while G_loss provably and empirically keeps samples within Span(A) (Figure 4). For optimization convergence, Algorithm 1 (guidance only, fixed score) is compared against Algorithm 2 (guidance + adaptive score fine-tuning): Alg.1 converges to a regularized optimum with a persistent gap to the global maximum, whereas Alg.2 closes this gap and reaches the unregularized global optimum at a rate matching classical convex optimization (Figure 5). The paper does not benchmark against external baselines like DPS [16] or Universal Guidance [4] with head-to-head numerical comparisons; instead it positions G_loss as the first method with rigorous theoretical guarantees for this gradient-guidance formulation, noting that DPS and Universal Guidance use a similar gradient-on-predicted-clean-data form but lack formal convergence or structure-preservation proofs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical analysis of gradient-based guidance in diffusion models, building directly on the training-free guidance paradigm introduced by Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) and the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. It leverages the score decomposition result for low-dimensional data (Chen et al.) to prove that a look-ahead-loss-based guidance (G_loss) provably preserves latent subspace structure, whereas naive gradient guidance does not. The key conceptual departure is reframing guided diffusion as a regularized optimization algorithm: the pre-trained score acts as a proximal regularizer, and the iterative guidance process mirrors a proximal gradient ascent step, yielding convergence guarantees that connect diffusion sampling to classical optimization theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental limit: guidance alone on a frozen pre-trained score cannot reach the global optimum of an objective, because the score imposes an irreducible regularization. Overcoming this requires adaptive fine-tuning, for which the paper proves an O(1/K) convergence rate matching classical convex optimization. Limitations include the restriction to linear score classes, concave objectives, and subspace-structured data; extending the theory to general score networks and non-concave rewards remains open. For practitioners, the results validate the empirical practice of DPS-style guidance while clarifying its theoretical boundaries and motivating the adaptive fine-tuning variant for tasks requiring global optimality.
