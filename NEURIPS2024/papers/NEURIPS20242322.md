## Problem

The existing hierarchical Thompson sampling (HierTS) algorithm for multi-task Gaussian linear bandits has a gap-independent Bayes regret bound of O(m√(n log n) log(mn)), and it was unclear whether sharper bounds could be derived or how to extend hierarchical Bayesian bandit algorithms to the more general multi-task combinatorial semi-bandit setting. This paper tackles both gaps by improving the linear bandit regret bound and extending the framework to semi-bandits.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a compact action set A ⊆ B(0,B) in R^d (finite or infinite), a hyper-prior Q = N(μ_q, Σ_q), and task parameters θ_{s,*} drawn i.i.d. from N(μ_*, Σ₀) for m tasks, interacting for at most n rounds per task either sequentially (|S_t|=1) or concurrently (|S_t|≤L). In the semi-bandit variant, the agent additionally receives a feature matrix Φ ∈ R^{K×d} and selects subsets of up to L items from K arms per round. The output is a per-task, per-round action (or subset) selection, with the theoretical guarantee expressed as a cumulative multi-task Bayes regret upper bound over all m tasks and n rounds each.

## Method

The paper contributes two algorithms: an improved analysis of HierTS (which samples a hyper-parameter μ_t from the closed-form Gaussian hyper-posterior Q_t, then samples task parameters θ_{s,t} from the conditional posterior and plays argmax a^T θ_{s,t}), and a novel HierBayesUCB algorithm that computes upper confidence bounds U_{t,s,a} = a^T μ̂_{s,t} + √(2 log(1/δ))‖a‖_{Σ̂_{s,t}} using the full posterior mean and covariance. The key technical novelty for HierTS is a three-part proof strategy: (i) a Cauchy-Schwartz type inequality from [21] to eliminate the √log(mn) factor, (ii) a PSD matrix diagonalization lemma (Lemma A.1) reducing the multiplicative condition-number factor from κ²(Σ₀) to κ(Σ₀), and (iii) a redefined matrix X̃_{s,t} that places σ² + B²λ₁(Σ₀) in the denominator rather than σ² alone. For HierBayesUCB, the regret is decomposed into three terms (large-gap on-event, small-gap on-event, off-event), with the first bounded via the UCB property and the posterior variance, yielding a gap-dependent logarithmic bound O(m log(mn) log n). Both algorithms are extended to the concurrent setting (requiring Assumption 5.1 on action span and a forced exploration phase) and to the semi-bandit setting using an ORACLE combinatorial solver.

## Results

For the multi-task Gaussian linear bandit with infinite action set, the paper improves the HierTS Bayes regret bound from O(m√(n log n) log(mn)) to O(m√(n log n)), removing a √log(mn) factor. For finite action sets, the proposed HierBayesUCB achieves a logarithmic gap-dependent bound of O(m log(mn) log n), which is within O(√log n) of the known lower bound Ω(d√n) per task. In the combinatorial semi-bandit setting, HierTS achieves O(m√n L log(nL) log(nK)) (logarithmic in K) versus O(m√n KL log n log(nK)) in [7], and HierBayesUCB achieves O(mL log(nL) log(mnK)), both sharper than prior results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to [17] (Hong et al., AISTATS 2022), the paper's HierTS bound removes the √log(mn) multiplicative factor in both the task-solving and hyper-parameter-learning terms, and reduces the condition number factor from κ²(Σ₀) to κ(Σ₀) via a novel PSD matrix decomposition (Lemma A.1). Compared to [7] (Basu et al., NeurIPS 2021) in the semi-bandit setting, the paper's HierTS bound is logarithmic in K (the number of items) whereas [7] is sublinear in K, making the improvement significant when K >> L; the HierBayesUCB semi-bandit bound O(mL log(nL) log(mnK)) is logarithmic in n versus [7]'s O(m√n KL log n log(nK)). Compared to [25] (Kveton et al., ICML 2021), the paper's bounds are substantially tighter, replacing the O(n²K√(m log n log K)) hyper-parameter term with O(d√(mn log(m/d))). Empirically, HierBayesUCB achieves lower regret than HierTS, and both outperform vanilla TS (which does not learn μ*), while OracleTS (which knows μ* exactly) serves as an upper-bound reference that HierTS approaches closely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the hierarchical Bayesian bandit lineage initiated by Kveton et al. (Meta-Thompson Sampling, ICML 2021) and advanced by Hong et al. (AISTATS 2022), which introduced the HierTS algorithm with a gap-independent Bayes regret bound of O(m√(n log n) log(mn)). The key departure is twofold: (1) a tighter analysis of HierTS that removes the √log(mn) factor via a novel Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and a PSD matrix decomposition that reduces the condition-number dependence from κ²(Σ₀) to κ(Σ₀), and (2) a new HierBayesUCB algorithm that achieves logarithmic (rather than sublinear) regret for finite action sets, drawing on the UCB-based decomposition technique from Atsidakou et al. (NeurIPS 2023). The semi-bandit extension builds on the combinatorial framework of Basu et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and Wen et al. (ICML 2015).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the √log(mn) factor and the introduction of a logarithmic regret bound for finite action sets bring the multi-task hierarchical Bayesian bandit theory closer to the single-task Bayes regret lower bound of Ω(d√n), making the task-averaged regret near-optimal up to a √log n factor. The extension to the concurrent setting (where multiple tasks are solved in parallel) and to the combinatorial semi-bandit setting broadens the practical applicability of hierarchical Bayesian bandits to realistic multi-agent and large-action-space scenarios. Open questions include whether the logarithmic bound extends to sub-exponential reward distributions (noted as future work), whether the gap-dependent nature of HierBayesUCB can be removed, and whether the techniques transfer to non-Gaussian or adversarial settings.
