## Problem

Existing truthful and privacy-preserving linear regression mechanisms (Cummings et al. 2015; Qiu et al. 2022) are restricted to low-dimensional settings where the feature dimension d is constant or much smaller than n. In the practically important high-dimensional sparse regime (d ≫ n, sparsity k ≪ n), these methods fail because OLS/ridge estimators do not converge, the empirical covariance matrix is rank-deficient, and output-perturbation noise scales polynomially with √(d/n). This paper is the first to design a mechanism that simultaneously achieves joint differential privacy, estimator accuracy, asymptotic truthfulness, individual rationality, and vanishing payment budget in this regime.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives n agents' reported data in one round: observable feature vectors x_i ∈ ℝ^d, potentially misreported response variables ŷ_i (agents can lie about y_i but not x_i), and private cost coefficients c_i. It outputs a projected private estimator θ̄^P ∈ ℝ^d (the soft-thresholded, noise-perturbed OLS solution clipped to an ℓ_2 ball of radius τ_θ) and per-agent payments π_i computed via a rescaled Brier score comparing each agent's self-prediction against a peer-based prediction. The one-shot constraint means agents cannot refine reports after seeing payments, and the dataset is randomly split into two halves to ensure the peer estimator excludes the agent being paid.

## Method

The estimator embeds the classical OLS solution Σ̄^{-1}Σ̃ inside an ℓ_1 proximal operator (element-wise soft-thresholding S_{λ_n}), yielding a closed-form sparse estimator that achieves LASSO-like convergence rates without iterative optimization. Privacy is enforced via sufficient-statistics perturbation: Gaussian noise is added separately to the ℓ_2-clipped covariance matrix Σ̄XX and the coordinate-clipped cross-covariance Σ̃XY, avoiding the √(d/n) sensitivity blowup of output perturbation. A hard-thresholding step on the noisy covariance matrix exploits the ℓ_p-sparse covariance structure (Assumption 6) to recover invertibility and reduce entrywise error. The payment rule uses peer prediction with the rescaled Brier score B_{a1,a2}(p,q) = a1 − a2(p − 2pq + q²), where p is the peer-based prediction ⟨x̄_i, θ̄^P(D^{1−b})⟩ and q is the agent's self-prediction, making truthful reporting the unique maximizer of expected payment.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical experiments. The main result (Corollary 8) shows that for any ξ ∈ (1/3, 1/2), the mechanism achieves (O(n^{-ξ}), O(n^{-Ω(1)}))-JDP privacy, the estimator is Õ(n^{2ξ−1})-accurate (i.e., o(1) error), truthful reporting is a Õ(n^{−ξ−1})-approximate Bayes Nash equilibrium for a 1−O(n^{−3ξ}) fraction of agents, individual rationality holds for a 1−O(n^{−3ξ}) fraction, and the total expected budget is Õ(n^{1−3ξ}) = o(1). The private covariance matrix estimator (Lemma 1) achieves an error of O(s²(log d · log(1/δ) · r⁴/(nε²))^{1−q} + log d · log(1/δ) · r⁴/(nε²)) in expectation under the ℓ_q-sparse covariance assumption, matching the optimal rate for q=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The comparisons are against prior theoretical results: (1) Compared to [14] (Cummings et al., COLT 2015), which handles low-dimensional truthful linear regression with bounded data and achieves o(1/√n)-JDP, this paper extends to the high-dimensional sparse setting (d ≫ n) with sub-Gaussian data and achieves approximate JDP (o(1), O(n^{−Ω(1)})) rather than pure JDP, due to the use of Gaussian noise in the sufficient statistics perturbation. (2) Compared to [37] (Qiu et al., 2022), which handles truthful generalized linear models with sub-Gaussian/heavy-tailed data but only in the low-dimensional case and can only guarantee Random JDP (RJDP), this paper provides a stronger approximate JDP guarantee and extends to the high-dimensional sparse regime. (3) The private sparse covariance matrix estimator extends [51] (Wang & Xu, 2021) from the ℓ_0-sparse case (q=0) to the general ℓ_q-sparse case (q ∈ [0,1]), matching the optimal rate when q=0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the truthful linear regression line initiated by Cummings et al. [14] and Qiu et al. [37], which were restricted to low-dimensional settings (d ≪ n) and relied on output perturbation of OLS or Ridge estimators. The key departure is the shift from output perturbation to sufficient statistics perturbation—clipping and adding Gaussian noise separately to the covariance and cross-covariance matrices—which is necessary because the ℓ₂-sensitivity of the closed-form estimator scales as Poly(√(d/n)) and becomes intractable when d ≫ n. The estimator itself borrows the soft-thresholding (proximal operator) structure from Yang et al. [59] for high-dimensional sparse regression, while the private sparse covariance matrix component generalizes Wang & Xu [51] from ℓ₀-sparse to ℓ_p-sparse (p ∈ [0,1]) covariance structures. The payment mechanism adapts the rescaled Brier score rule from Ghosh et al. [21] within a peer-prediction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously achieve joint differential privacy, o(1) estimation accuracy, approximate Bayes-Nash truthfulness, individual rationality, and vanishing payment budget for high-dimensional sparse linear regression—a combination that was previously shown to be infeasible in the low-dimensional setting due to the sensitivity bottleneck. The sufficient statistics perturbation technique and the private ℓ_p-sparse covariance estimator are reusable building blocks for other truthful/DP estimation problems. A key limitation is the one-round communication constraint inherited from the truthful mechanism design framework, which precludes iterative refinement; whether multi-round interactions can improve the accuracy-budget trade-off remains open. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so practical parameter tuning (e.g., choosing ξ, r, τ_x, τ_y) for real datasets is unaddressed.
