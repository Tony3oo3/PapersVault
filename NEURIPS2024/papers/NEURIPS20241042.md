## Problem

Neural operators that solve PDEs in the original geometric space incur quadratic computational cost in the number of sample points, and existing mitigations either sacrifice accuracy via linear attention or still switch between latent and geometric space at every Transformer layer. A key unaddressed need is a learnable latent-space formulation that transforms data only once, decouples observation from prediction positions (enabling interpolation/extrapolation for inverse problems), and retains the expressiveness of full quadratic attention.

## Input / Output

For forward problems, the model receives N_in position-value pairs of PDE coefficients or boundary/initial conditions (d-dimensional positions, n-dimensional values) and outputs solution values at N_out arbitrary query positions that need not coincide with input positions. For inverse problems, the input is a sparse set of partially observed solution samples in a subdomain, and the output is the complete solution over the full spatiotemporal domain. The decoupling property—where the trunk-projector encodes only positions while the branch-projector encodes position+value—allows the decoder to query any location, making the model naturally suited to interpolation and extrapolation tasks.

## Method

LNO introduces a Physics-Cross-Attention (PhCA) module that maps input samples from the geometric space into a learnable latent space of M hypothetical positions (M ≪ N_in) via cross-attention, where the latent positions serve as learnable query tokens. A stack of standard Transformer self-attention blocks then models the PDE operator entirely within this compact latent space, and an inverse PhCA decodes back to the geometric space at arbitrary query positions. Unlike Transolver, which applies physics-attention at every layer, LNO performs the geometric-to-latent transformation only at the first and last layer, reducing time complexity from O(LMN + LM²) to O(MN + LM²). The encoder and decoder share the same attention-projector weights (W₁ = W₂), and the trunk-projector deliberately excludes physics values from its input to enforce the decoupling of observation and prediction positions. The latent space is learned end-to-end from data rather than being pre-defined (e.g., as a frequency domain), and quadratic scaled dot-product attention is used in the latent space since the short sequence length makes it computationally affordable.

## Results

On the six forward PDE benchmarks, LNO achieves the best relative L2 error (×10⁻²) on four of six: Darcy (0.49), NS2d (8.45), Elasticity (0.52), and Pipe (0.26), outperforming the closest competitor Transolver (0.58, 8.79, 0.62, 0.31 respectively). On the inverse Burgers' equation problem, LNO as a completer achieves 0.60% relative MAE at 20% observation ratio and 2.05% at 0.5%, substantially outperforming GNOT (1.12% and 2.56%) and DeepONet (2.51% and 4.82%). In efficiency, LNO reduces GPU memory by ~50% and training time by 1.8× compared to Transolver while using ~30% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LNO's closest competitor is Transolver [23], the most efficient prior Transformer-based PDE solver. LNO beats Transolver on Darcy (0.49 vs 0.58), NS2d (8.45 vs 8.79), Elasticity (0.52 vs 0.62), and Pipe (0.26 vs 0.31), but trails on Airfoil (0.51 vs 0.47) and Plasticity (0.29 vs 0.12). Against FNO-family baselines (FNO, Geo-FNO, F-FNO, U-FNO), LNO achieves substantially lower errors across all benchmarks where they overlap, with the largest gaps on NS2d (8.45 vs 11.51–17.15) and Elasticity (0.52 vs 1.85–2.29). Among other Transformer-based operators (GNOT, ONO, Galerkin, OFormer, FactFormer), LNO wins on all six benchmarks, with particularly large margins on NS2d (8.45 vs 11.95–17.05) and Elasticity (0.52 vs 0.88–2.40). On the inverse Burgers' equation, LNO outperforms both GNOT and DeepONet in both the completer stage (e.g., 0.74% vs 1.39% vs 2.59% MAE at 10% observation) and the propagator stage (3.73% vs 5.45% vs 7.34% MAE with ground-truth subdomain input). The key structural advantage is that LNO is the only method combining decoupled observation/prediction positions with latent-space operator learning, enabling both superior efficiency (50% less GPU memory, 1.8× faster training) and the flexibility needed for inverse problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent-space PDE solving paradigm introduced by LSM [22] and Transolver [23], both of which use cross-attention to transform between geometric and latent representations. The key departure is twofold: (1) the latent space is learned end-to-end from data rather than pre-defined (e.g., frequency domain in FNO or triangular space in LSM), and (2) the geometric-to-latent transformation is applied only once at the encoder and decoder rather than at every Transformer layer as in Transolver. The Physics-Cross-Attention (PhCA) module further decouples observation and prediction positions by separating position information (in queries/keys) from physics quantities (in values), a design inspired by DeepONet's trunk/branch architecture but applied within a cross-attention framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% GPU memory reduction and 1.8× training speedup while simultaneously achieving SOTA accuracy on 4 of 6 forward benchmarks makes this a practically important efficiency-accuracy trade-off for neural operator practitioners. The decoupling property that enables natural interpolation/extrapolation opens a unified pathway for forward and inverse PDE problems without task-specific architectural changes. Open questions include whether the learned latent space generalizes across different PDE families, whether imposing physical priors (e.g., symmetry) on the latent space could further improve accuracy, and whether all operations for time-dependent problems can be conducted entirely in the latent space to avoid repeated decoding.
