## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods fail to simultaneously achieve high audio quality, strong temporal alignment, and efficient inference. Autoregressive models lack explicit audio-visual synchronization and suffer from high latency, while diffusion-based approaches like Diff-Foley require many sampling steps and depend on fragile classifier guidance for alignment. The paper introduces rectified flow matching to the V2A task to address all three deficiencies at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244067/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an 8-second silent video clip downsampled to 4 FPS as input, extracts frame-level visual features (CAVP or MAViL), and regulates them to match the temporal resolution of the spectrogram latent. The output is a 16 kHz audio waveform produced by decoding a 20-dimensional mel-spectrogram latent (80 bins, hop size 256) through a 1D VAE decoder and a BigVGAN vocoder. The 1D VAE treats the frequency axis as the channel dimension and convolves only along time, preserving temporal resolution for alignment.

## Method

FRIEREN regresses the conditional transport vector field from Gaussian noise to spectrogram latent using rectified flow matching with straight linear interpolation paths, sampled via ODE solving (Euler or Dopri5). The vector field estimator is a 4-layer feed-forward transformer (159M params) that performs channel-level concatenation of visual and audio features rather than cross-attention, leveraging inherent audio-visual alignment without learned attention mechanisms and avoiding temporal downsampling to preserve synchronization. A logit-normal re-weighting of the RFM loss allocates more training signal to intermediate timesteps where the vector field is hardest to regress. For efficiency, the model applies reflow (using the CFG-guided vector field as both sampling and regression target) followed by one-step distillation, enabling high-quality generation in as few as one sampling step with 9.3× speedup over 25-step inference.

## Results

On VGGSound (25-step sampling), FRIEREN achieves an inception score (IS) of 12.42, Fréchet audio distance (FAD) of 1.32, and temporal alignment accuracy of 97.22%, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline Diff-Foley by 6.2% in IS and 3.4 in FAD. With the higher-order Dopri5 ODE solver, FRIEREN further improves to IS=12.76 and FD=11.64. Inference time is 0.288 s per sample (Euler, 25 steps), yielding a 7.3× speedup over Diff-Foley (2.104 s), and with reflow + one-step distillation the model generates audio in a single step (0.031 s) while retaining 97.85% alignment accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpecVQGAN (both RGB+Flow and ResNet50 variants), FRIEREN dominates on every metric: IS improves from 5.2 to 12.4, FAD drops from ~5.4 to 1.32, and alignment accuracy jumps from ~57–62% to 97.22%. Against Im2Wav, FRIEREN wins on IS (12.42 vs 7.20), FAD (1.32 vs 5.49), KID (2.49 vs 3.35), alignment accuracy (97.22% vs 56.70%), and both MOS scores, while Im2Wav retains a slight edge on FD (14.98 vs 12.26) and KL (2.57 vs 2.73). Against the strongest baseline Diff-Foley (with classifier guidance), FRIEREN achieves a 6.2% IS improvement (12.42 vs 11.11), a 3.4-point FAD reduction (1.32 vs 4.72), a 2.19-point KID reduction, and 2.19% higher alignment accuracy (97.22% vs 95.03%), while Diff-Foley retains a marginal FD advantage (23.94 vs 12.26 is actually FRIEREN's advantage; Diff-Foley's FD is worse). The controlled LDM comparison (same architecture, diffusion objective) shows FRIEREN's rectified-flow formulation yields higher IS (12.42 vs 10.09), lower FAD (1.32 vs 1.77), and better alignment (97.22% vs 95.33%), confirming the benefit of straight-path flow matching over curved diffusion trajectories for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRIEREN transplants rectified flow matching (Liu et al., 2022; InstaFlow, 2023) into video-to-audio generation, a task previously dominated by GANs (RegNet, FoleyGAN), autoregressive transformers (SpecVQGAN, Im2Wav), and latent diffusion (Diff-Foley). Its vector field estimator adapts the spatial transformer from latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) to 1D temporal convolution, and its audio latent follows the mel-spectrogram VAE design from Make-an-audio 2 (Huang et al., 2023). The key architectural departure is replacing cross-attention conditioning with channel-level concatenation of visual and audio features, and the reflow procedure is modified to operate on the classifier-free-guided vector field rather than the raw one, a combination not previously explored in the V2A literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.3× speedup over Diff-Foley and the ability to produce 97.85%-aligned audio in a single sampling step make high-quality V2A generation practical for real-time or high-throughput applications like film dubbing and game development. However, the model is validated only on VGGSound (~182k clips, 8-second fixed length), leaving open whether the rectified-flow advantage and the channel-level fusion trick generalize to larger, more diverse datasets and variable-length sequences. The finding that richer visual features (MAViL) actually degrade alignment accuracy due to lower effective temporal resolution is a useful caution for practitioners choosing conditioning representations.
