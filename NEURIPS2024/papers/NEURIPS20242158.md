## Problem

Neural ODEs can accurately model dynamical systems but must be fully retrained for each new scenario, making them impractical for autonomous systems that must adapt to unseen environments in real time. The paper addresses the gap of achieving zero-shot transfer of learned dynamics models: rapidly identifying the dynamics of a new system from minimal online data without any gradient updates or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short state trajectory (e.g., 200 samples ≈ 7 s at 30 Hz) from an unseen system, optionally augmented with zero-order-hold control inputs, and produces a coefficient vector c ∈ R^k that represents the new dynamics as a linear combination of pre-trained neural ODE basis functions. These coefficients enable future state prediction via a single ODE integration and can be directly fed into a gradient-based MPC controller. The online coefficient computation requires only a Monte-Carlo sample mean of inner products (vector dot products and scalar multiplication), completing in milliseconds on a GPU with no backpropagation.

## Method

The approach learns k neural ODE basis functions that span a Hilbert space of integral functions H(x(t₀), t_f) = ∫f(x(τ))dτ, where each basis function G_i is the integral of a neural network g_i over a time interval. During offline training, coefficients for each training trajectory are computed via a Monte-Carlo estimate of the Hilbert-space inner product, and the basis functions are optimized by gradient descent to minimize the reconstruction error of H across all training datasets. The key insight is that this inner-product-based coefficient computation is a simple sample mean, so at runtime the coefficients for a new unseen dynamics function are obtained with no gradient updates—only dot products and scalar multiplication over the online trajectory. A residuals variant centers the coefficient space around zero by first learning an average dynamics model F_avg and training basis functions on the residual R = x(t_{j+1}) − x(t_j) − F_avg, which reduces epistemic uncertainty and improves long-horizon accuracy. The basis functions naturally converge toward orthonormality during training because the reconstruction loss implicitly penalizes non-orthogonal representations, eliminating the need for explicit Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization.

## Results

On MuJoCo Half-Cheetah and Ant environments with hidden parameters (limb length, friction, control authority), FE + NODE + Res achieves the best long-horizon prediction accuracy using only 200 online data points (~7 seconds at 30 Hz), with the online coefficient computation completing in milliseconds on a GPU. On the PyBullet quadrotor with varying mass, FE + NODE + Res maintains accurate 10-step MSE predictions across the full mass range, while NODE performance degrades rapidly as dynamics deviate from the mean. For downstream MPC control, FE + NODE + Res produces a lower slew rate than NODE, indicating fewer corrective actions and more efficient control decisions. The approach uses 100 basis functions (4 hidden layers of size 51 each) for the MuJoCo and quadrotor experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: standard Neural ODEs (NODE) and Function Encoders with the residuals method but without neural ODE inductive bias (FE + Res). NODE performs poorly on long-horizon prediction in both MuJoCo environments because it learns only the mean dynamics over all hidden-parameter configurations and has no mechanism to condition on unseen parameters; on the quadrotor, NODE's prediction error grows quickly as mass deviates from the training mean, and its MPC controller exhibits a high slew rate due to repeated positional corrections. FE + Res can implicitly condition on hidden parameters through coefficient computation but lacks the inductive bias of neural ODEs, causing it to fail on the more challenging Ant environment (higher degrees of freedom) while remaining competitive on Half-Cheetah. The proposed FE + NODE + Res outperforms both baselines on all three environments (Half-Cheetah, Ant, and quadrotor) for long-horizon prediction, with the residuals method providing the largest gain by reducing epistemic uncertainty through a learned average dynamics baseline. An oracle baseline (NODE with hidden parameters as explicit input) achieves good 1-step accuracy but produces unstable long-horizon predictions, confirming that a single neural ODE cannot reliably generalize across an entire space of dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function encoder framework (Ingebrand et al., ICML 2024), which achieves zero-shot transfer by learning basis functions in a Hilbert space, by replacing the standard MLP basis functions with neural ODEs. The extension is non-trivial because the inner product must be computed over integrated trajectories rather than pointwise evaluations, requiring a reformulation of the coefficient estimation via state differences. It also departs from parameterized neural ODEs (Lee & Parish, 2021), which require explicit knowledge of system parameters at both training and test time, by learning a space of dynamics implicitly from data without any parameter conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to identify new dynamics from a single short trajectory in milliseconds—without any gradient updates—opens a practical path for real-time model-based control of autonomous systems operating in uncertain or changing environments. Key limitations include the need for diverse offline training data spanning the full space of dynamics, the constraint that integration must stay within the trained state space, and the computational overhead of k parallel integrations. Open questions include formal safety guarantees (e.g., confidence bounds from the Hilbert space structure), sim-to-real transfer fidelity, and extension to stochastic differential equations.
