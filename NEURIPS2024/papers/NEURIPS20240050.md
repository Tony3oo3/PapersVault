## Problem

Generating 3D articulated objects that are simultaneously geometrically detailed, kinematically accurate, and physically simulatable remains an open challenge. Prior diffusion-based approaches such as NAP and CAGE suffer from unnatural joint motion due to unconstrained screw parametrization, limited controllability and interpretability, and the production of inconsistent or unidentifiable part shapes. These methods also lack part-level control because object geometry is encoded opaquely as a latent within the graph, preventing human-in-the-loop adjustment.

## Input / Output

The structure generator accepts a complete graph of up to N=8 nodes (padded with binary existence indicators) whose node features include 8×8 VQ-VAE image latents, categorical one-hot codes for asset/body type and part existence, and 3D bounding box dimensions/orientation/position, while edge features carry joint-type one-hots, 6D Plücker coordinates, and joint limits. The shape generator consumes the denoised graph's decoded outputs—a front-view image (encoded by ResNet-18), a BERT-encoded text prompt (e.g., 'A lid as part of a trash can'), and MLP-encoded graph features—alongside a 64³ bounding-box TSDF prior, and produces per-part meshes at 64³ TSDF resolution via marching cubes. The final output is a fully simulatable articulated asset directly loadable into MuJoCo.

## Method

MIDGArD decomposes articulated asset generation into two sequential diffusion stages. The structure generator is a 6-layer GAT denoising network trained with DDIM (100 steps) on the graph, with three key modifications over NAP: (1) categorical embeddings for joint type, asset/body category, and part existence to enforce semantic consistency; (2) per-node image latents from a VQ-VAE trained on 256×256 front renderings, replacing direct mesh latents to yield human-interpretable conditioning; and (3) diffusion on the Plücker manifold via a 5D parametrization k=[θ,φ,n] where the axis u is recovered from spherical angles and v=n×u, guaranteeing unit norm and orthogonality by construction and eliminating per-step projection. The shape generator is an adapted SDFusion latent diffusion model that, instead of generating a full object TSDF, learns the residual z_Δ = z_o − z_b relative to a bounding-box TSDF, with the box encoding z_b concatenated to the noisy input at inference to enforce dimensional constraints. Oriented bounding boxes are computed per part via convex-hull voxelization, PCA initialization, and gradient descent on the attitude quaternion to minimize OBB volume, reducing average bounding-box volume by 17.4% and improving part alignment.

## Results

In unconditional articulated asset generation on PartNet Mobility, MIDGArD (with Plücker manifold) achieves MMD of 0.0264 and COV of 0.4857, improving over NAP by 6.4% and 3.7% respectively, while matching its 1-NNA of 0.5831. The joint type distribution of MIDGArD-generated objects closely matches the training data (Screw 2%, Revolute 62%, Prismatic 36%) with a Chi-Square statistic of 12.7, versus NAP's heavily skewed distribution (Screw 95%) and Chi-Square of 5618. For bounding-box-constrained shape generation, the bb prior + bb MLP approach reduces OBBcount from 3795 (original SDFusion) to 1146 and Chamfer distance from 0.0307 to 0.0072.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAP [37], MIDGArD wins on both MMD (0.0264 vs. 0.0282, a 6.4% reduction) and COV (0.4857 vs. 0.4675, a 3.7% increase), while tying on 1-NNA (0.5831). The most striking difference is in joint type plausibility: NAP generates 95% screw joints (vs. 6% in training data), whereas MIDGArD produces a distribution nearly identical to the training set (2% screw, 62% revolute, 36% prismatic), confirmed by a Chi-Square statistic of 12.7 versus NAP's 5618. Against the original SDFusion [8] pipeline (used as the shape generator baseline in Table 3), MIDGArD's bounding-box prior reduces OBBcount from 3795 to 1146 and OBBsum from 183.5 to 10.89, while improving Chamfer distance from 0.0307 to 0.0072. The Plücker manifold ablation further confirms that diffusing directly on the manifold (rather than projecting from R⁶) yields better MMD and COV than the non-manifold variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIDGArD extends the articulated object generation line initiated by NAP (NeurIPS 2023) and CAGE (CVPR 2024), which both use graph attention networks with diffusion to denoise articulation graphs. The key departures are: (1) parameterizing diffusion directly on the Plücker manifold rather than in R⁶ with projection, (2) replacing direct mesh latents with VQ-VAE image codes for part representation, and (3) decoupling structure generation from shape generation into a modular pipeline that leverages SDFusion (CVPR 2023) as a swappable shape generator. The bounding-box constrained generation and OBB-based canonicalization are novel contributions not present in prior articulated generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular architecture is practically significant because it allows the shape generator to be upgraded as 3D foundation models improve, without retraining the structure generator. The interpretable intermediate outputs (images, text, categorical joint types) enable part-level user control that was absent in end-to-end approaches like NAP. However, the work is limited to PartNet Mobility, the evaluation metrics (Instantiation Distance) are acknowledged as inadequate for catching kinematic failures, and graph scalability beyond 8 nodes remains unresolved. Open questions include whether mixed discrete-continuous diffusion (e.g., MiDi) can better handle the categorical variables, and whether language/image conditioning of the structure generator itself can further improve controllability.
