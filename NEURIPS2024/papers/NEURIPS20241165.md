## Problem

Learning discrete semantic concepts (e.g., object classes, shapes) from high-dimensional continuous data like images lacks formal theoretical grounding: prior identification results either assume continuous latent variables with linear causal edges, discrete observed variables, or restrictive graph structures (trees, multi-level DAGs). This paper asks under what conditions a discrete latent hierarchical causal model is identifiable from the observed distribution alone, without supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241165/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only the observed distribution p(x) over high-dimensional continuous variables (e.g., images) with no labels, auxiliary information, or parametric assumptions on the generating process. It outputs (1) each discrete latent variable d_i up to permutation indeterminacy, (2) the bipartite graph Γ linking discrete concepts to observed components, and (3) the full hierarchical causal DAG G explaining statistical dependencies among concepts at multiple abstraction levels. A key structural constraint is that the generating function g(d,c)→x must be invertible and continuous in c, and the continuous support C must be closed and connected.

## Method

The identification proceeds in two stages. First, invertibility and continuity of g ensure that each discrete state d indexes a non-intersecting manifold in x-space, so the global discrete state can be extracted from continuous observations; this yields a mixture oracle over p(x) that enables tensor decomposition to disentangle individual components d_i and recover Γ. Second, for the hierarchical structure G, the paper proves Theorem 4.8: the non-negative rank of a joint probability table P_{A,B} equals the minimum number of states in a t-separation set between A and B, providing a discrete counterpart to the covariance-rank theorem used in linear latent hierarchical models. This rank oracle feeds into the graph search algorithm of Dong et al., with atomic covers (groups of indistinguishable latent states) handling discrete variables of arbitrary cardinality, and the minimal-graph operator resolving residual structural ambiguities.

## Results

On synthetic hierarchical graphs with continuous observed variables (Gaussian mixture generation), the proposed method achieves F1 scores of 0.93–0.98 across 9 graph structures for discrete component identification, substantially outperforming the Kivva et al. baseline (0.63–0.69). For purely discrete hierarchical model identification, the method achieves F1 scores of 0.73–1.0 across 7 graphs, compared to the Dong et al. baseline which ranges from 0.0 to 0.63. The method consistently achieves near-perfect scores on graphs satisfying the theoretical conditions, validating Theorems 4.4, 4.8, and 4.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines on synthetic data. Against Kivva et al. [24] (the only prior method designed for non-parametric discrete latent models from continuous observations), the proposed method achieves a consistent ~0.27 F1 improvement across all 9 graphs (e.g., 0.94 vs. 0.67 on Graph 1, 0.98 vs. 0.69 on Graph 2), as the baseline correctly identifies the bipartite graph Γ and edges among d components but cannot handle higher-level latent variables. Against Dong et al. [20] (a linear continuous latent hierarchical model discovery framework), the proposed method achieves dramatically higher F1 scores on purely discrete graphs (e.g., 1.0 vs. 0.0 on Graphs 5 and 7, 0.73 vs. 0.33 on Graph 3), confirming that the linear covariance-rank machinery of Dong et al. fails on discrete models while the non-negative rank test (Theorem 4.8) successfully recovers the hierarchical structure. The significant margins over both baselines validate the theoretical contributions of Theorems 4.4 and 4.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of latent variable identification: the mixture-oracle/tensor-decomposition framework of Kivva et al. (2021) for discrete latent variables adjacent to continuous observations, and the rank-based graph search of Dong et al. (2023) for linear continuous latent hierarchical models. The key theoretical bridge is Theorem 4.8, which shows that the non-negative rank of a joint probability table plays the same structural role as the covariance matrix rank in Sullivant et al.'s (2010) Theorem A2, despite the absence of linearity. This allows the discrete hierarchical identification problem to inherit the flexible graph-search machinery of Dong et al. while handling arbitrary nonlinear discrete causal structures, going beyond the tree and multi-level DAG restrictions of prior discrete work (Pearl, Choi et al., Gu & Dunson).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework establishing when discrete semantic concepts at multiple abstraction levels can be recovered from high-dimensional continuous data (e.g., images) without supervision, providing a formal foundation for the concept-learning literature that has been largely empirical. The interpretation of latent diffusion models as hierarchical concept learners—where noise level maps to concept level—offers a unifying lens for disparate empirical observations (early steps encode global semantics, late steps encode fine details) and suggests concrete algorithmic improvements (e.g., adaptive sparsity for concept extraction). Key limitations include the restrictive graphical conditions (no triangles, atomic cover requirements), the computational expense of non-negative rank computation for large state spaces, and the fact that the diffusion interpretation remains intuitive rather than a formal guarantee.
