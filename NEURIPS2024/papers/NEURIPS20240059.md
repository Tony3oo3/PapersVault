## Problem

Improving mathematical reasoning in LLMs typically requires either collecting massive math corpora (e.g., 120B tokens for DeepSeekMath) or invoking expensive large models like GPT-4 to synthesize millions of problems, both of which incur substantial training or inference costs. This paper asks whether a small (7B) LLM can be efficiently trained to replicate the data synthesis capability of GPT-4, thereby reducing the total cost of building a strong math-reasoning model to roughly 20% of existing state-of-the-art approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The data synthesis model takes a math-related text (drawn from webpages, textbooks, arXiv papers, StackExchange QA, or Wikipedia) concatenated with an education-stage-specific prompt (covering 8 levels from Grade School through AIME for natural language reasoning, or 2 levels for tool manipulation) and outputs a math problem paired with a step-by-step solution or executable Python code. The final JiuZhang3.0 model accepts a math problem as input and produces either a natural-language chain-of-thought solution or a Python program to be executed by an external interpreter, depending on the evaluation setting.

## Method

The approach trains a 7B data synthesis model (initialized from DeepSeekMath-7B) via knowledge distillation from GPT-4, using only ~9.3k API calls. Two key mechanisms ensure the KD dataset is both diverse and useful: (1) a set of 8+2 prompts aligned to human education stages and math competitions guarantees coverage of broad knowledge and difficulty levels, and (2) a gradient-based influence estimation strategy (using LoRA-trained reference model gradients with random projection, following LESS/TRAK) selects the top-ranking math-related texts that most positively influence downstream task performance. The small synthesis model then generates ~6M problem-solution pairs (4.6B tokens) from a multi-source corpus, which are used to pre-train JiuZhang3.0 (7B/8B/8×7B variants based on Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, and Mixtral-8×7B), followed by fine-tuning on open-source math instruction datasets.

## Results

Under the natural language reasoning setting, JiuZhang3.0-7B achieves an average of 78.8 across six datasets (GSM8k 88.6, MATH 52.8, SVAMP 90.4, ASDiv 92.6, MAWPS 97.3, CARP 51.0), outperforming DeepSeekMath-7B-RL (77.4 avg) and MAmmoTH2-7B-Plus (75.2 avg). The larger JiuZhang3.0-8×7B variant reaches 79.3 average, surpassing MAmmoTH2-8×7B-Plus (76.4) on all six benchmarks. Under the tool manipulation setting, JiuZhang3.0-8B achieves 79.2 average across seven datasets, outperforming Rho-1-Math-7B-Code (75.3) and OpenMath-Mistral-7B (74.1) by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JiuZhang3.0-7B outperforms DeepSeekMath-7B-RL (the strongest 7B baseline pre-trained on 120B math corpus) on five of six datasets, with the largest gain on MATH (52.8 vs. 50.2) and SVAMP (90.4 vs. 87.3), while slightly trailing on CARP (51.0 vs. 51.6). Against KPMath-DSMath-7B, which relies on ~1M GPT-4-synthesized problems, JiuZhang3.0-7B leads on GSM8k (88.6 vs. 83.9), MATH (52.8 vs. 48.8), and SVAMP (90.4 vs. 81.5). The 8×7B variant dominates MAmmoTH2-8×7B-Plus across all six benchmarks (e.g., MATH 53.8 vs. 47.0, CARP 52.3 vs. 45.8). In the tool manipulation setting, JiuZhang3.0-8B (79.2 avg) surpasses Rho-1-Math-7B-Code (75.3) and OpenMath-Mistral-7B (74.1) by 3–5 points, with particularly large margins on SVAMP (89.2 vs. 80.8/82.4) and TabMWP (79.9 vs. 70.1/70.0). However, all JiuZhang3.0 variants remain behind GPT-4 (82.5 avg), which leads on MATH (65.4 vs. 53.8) and GSM8k (92.2 vs. 89.8). The paper also notes that JiuZhang3.0 underperforms on MMLU-STEM (53.6–60.4 vs. 77.1 for GPT-4), indicating limited coverage of interdisciplinary STEM knowledge in the synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) gradient-based data selection methods (LESS, TRAK, Pruthi et al.) that estimate training data influence via gradient similarity, and (2) LLM-based synthetic data generation for mathematical reasoning (MetaMath, KPMath, MAmmoTH2). It departs from prior approaches that either collect massive math corpora (DeepSeekMath's 120B tokens) or invoke GPT-4 directly for large-scale synthesis, by instead distilling GPT-4's synthesis capability into a 7B model and using gradient-based influence estimation to select which texts to feed the teacher. The key conceptual move is treating data synthesis itself as a learnable skill for a small model, rather than a one-shot prompting task for a frontier model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a ~5× cost reduction (to ~20% of prior SOTA) for improving mathematical reasoning, which is practically significant for groups without frontier-model API budgets. The finding that a purpose-trained 7B synthesizer outperforms larger general-purpose LLMs (ChatGPT, Mixtral-8×7B) for generating pre-training data challenges the assumption that stronger models always produce better synthetic data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math to other reasoning domains, whether the 4.6B synthetic corpus can be further reduced with better filtering, and whether alternative teacher models (Claude 3, GPT-4o) would yield different results. The authors also note the model was not tested on non-math reasoning tasks like planning or commonsense reasoning.
