## Problem

Open-source diffusion model communities have accumulated over 100K LoRA adapters, but selecting and composing the right ones for a given prompt remains manual and error-prone. Existing retrieval-based systems cannot handle the multi-task nature of image prompts (e.g., 'two dogs playing in the snow' requires separate adapters for dogs and snow), and adapters often lack sufficient documentation to compute meaningful embeddings. Composing multiple adapters naively also degrades image quality and introduces foreign biases.

## Input / Output

Stylus takes a free-form text prompt, a pre-computed vector database of 75K LoRA adapter embeddings (StylusDocs, with VLM-generated descriptions), and a base Stable Diffusion v1.5 checkpoint (Realistic-Vision-v6 or Counterfeit-v3). It outputs 1024×1024 images (upscaled from 512×512 with 35 DPM Solver++ steps) where adapter weights are merged into the base model, along with a structured mapping of prompt keywords/tasks to selected adapters. A debias prompt is appended to steer outputs back toward the checkpoint's native style.

## Method

Stylus employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Refiner uses Gemini Ultra-Vision with chain-of-thought prompting to generate improved adapter descriptions from model cards (sample images, prompts, author text), then encodes them via OpenAI text-embedding-3-large into a vector database; (2) a Retriever computes exact cosine similarity between the prompt embedding and all adapter embeddings, returning the top-150 candidates; (3) a Composer uses Gemini 1.5 (128K context) to segment the prompt into disjoint keyword tasks, prune semantically misaligned adapters, and assign remaining adapters to each task via an alignment predicate. A binary masking scheme randomly selects subsets of adapters per task to ensure diversity across batch images, and final weights are computed by averaging within each task and summing across tasks with a global β=0.8 scaling factor to prevent over-saturation.

## Results

On the COCO 2014 validation set (10K prompts) with the Realistic-Vision-v6 checkpoint, Stylus achieves a CLIP score of 27.25 (+0.03 over SD v1.5) and FID of 22.05 (−1.91 improvement), shifting the CLIP-FID Pareto curve toward greater efficiency across CFG values [1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12]. In human evaluation across COCO and PartiPrompts with two checkpoints, Stylus receives a 2:1 preference over the base Stable Diffusion model. GPT-4V as a judge confirms a 60% win rate for Stylus on visual fidelity and 58–60% on image diversity (dFID and GPT-4V scoring).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Stable Diffusion v1.5 checkpoint [Rombach et al.], Stylus improves FID by 1.91 points (22.05 vs. 23.96) while matching CLIP (27.25 vs. 27.22), and earns a 2:1 human preference and 60% GPT-4V win rate on visual fidelity. Against the Cohere reranker baseline [Reimers], Stylus wins decisively on both metrics: CLIP 27.25 vs. 25.48 (+1.77) and FID 22.05 vs. 22.81 (−0.76). The reranker actually underperforms the base model on CLIP (25.48 vs. 27.22), because it selects semantically similar but task-misaligned adapters that introduce foreign biases (e.g., elephant movie characters) and fail to assign correct per-task weights. The retriever-only baseline (standard RAG without the composer) performs worst on CLIP (24.93) and is worse than the base model on FID (24.68 vs. 23.96), confirming that keyword-level task segmentation is critical. The random adapter baseline (26.34 CLIP, 24.39 FID) is slightly worse than the base model, indicating that irrelevant adapters are largely neutral but still mildly harmful.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stylus adapts the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigm to the adapter-selection problem for diffusion models, drawing on LoRA as the adapter mechanism and the multi-LoRA composition formula from Zhong et al. (2024) for weight merging. It departs from standard RAG and reranking in two key ways: (1) a VLM-based refiner generates improved adapter descriptions to overcome the poor documentation typical of community-shared LoRAs, and (2) a composer LLM segments the prompt into discrete tasks and assigns adapters per task, going beyond the single-similarity-score ranking of retrievers and cross-encoder rerankers. The system is positioned against LoRA-Retriever and LoRAHub, which address adapter composition for LLMs but do not handle the multi-keyword, multi-concept structure of image-generation prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 100K community-shared LoRAs on platforms like Civit AI, automatic adapter selection addresses a real bottleneck for practitioners who currently hand-pick adapters. The StylusDocs dataset (75K adapters with pre-computed VLM-generated descriptions and OpenAI embeddings) is a practical resource that lowers the barrier to building adapter-aware pipelines. Key open questions remain: task blocking (adapters overwriting other concepts), reduced diversity when an adapter was trained on a narrow image set, and the 75% inference overhead at batch size 1 from the long-context composer call. The paper also leaves open whether fine-tuning the composer on human-labeled data could further close the gap between automatic and expert-level adapter curation.
