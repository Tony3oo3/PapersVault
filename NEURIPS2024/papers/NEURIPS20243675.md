## Problem

Real-world graphs exhibit geometrically heterogeneous local structures—some subgraphs are tree-like and hierarchical (well-suited to hyperbolic space) while others are more Euclidean in character—yet existing GNNs are confined to a single geometric paradigm. It remains unclear how to adaptively select the appropriate geometry per node, and naively combining embeddings from different geometries can actually degrade student model performance due to distributional inconsistencies between spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a graph (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) along with pre-trained teacher models in both Euclidean (GCN) and hyperbolic (HGCN) spaces, each with hidden dimension 128. For each node, it computes the Gromov δ-hyperbolicity of its k-hop subgraph (k=4) as a per-node geometric descriptor that drives geometry selection. The output is a compact Euclidean student GCN (hidden dimension 8) producing node embeddings for downstream node classification (F1) or link prediction (ROC AUC), achieving ~232× inference speedup over the teacher ensemble.

## Method

The proposed cross-geometric KD framework introduces two modules. The Structure-Wise Knowledge Transfer (SWKT) module computes δ-hyperbolicity for each node's k-hop subgraph and uses a threshold λ to select either the hyperbolic or Euclidean teacher's intermediate-layer embedding for that node, then minimizes KL divergence between the selected teacher's local structure distribution and the student's corresponding distribution across all intermediate layers. The Geometric Embedding Optimization (GEO) module addresses the distributional gap between geometries by fusing teacher embeddings via a sigmoid-weighted combination (driven by δ_G_i and λ), then optimizing the fused features through a single-layer GCN trained with triplet loss to enlarge inter-class and shrink intra-class distances. The overall training loss combines SWKT loss, cross-entropy on teacher logits, and a weighted GEO loss, and the framework is model-agnostic, demonstrated with GCN, GAT, and Graph Transformer Network teachers.

## Results

On node classification (NC), the cross-geometry KD method achieves a 1.11% average F1 improvement over the strongest single-geometry baselines, with gains of 2.66% on Cora and 1.37% on Wiki-CS. On link prediction (LP), the average ROC AUC improves by 1.58%, reaching 2.57% on Cora and 1.87% on Wiki-CS. On large-scale graphs, the method attains 70.89% F1 on Ogbn-Arxiv (169K nodes) and 71.22% on Ogbn-Proteins (132K nodes), outperforming the best baseline (MSKD) by 1.62% and 0.25% respectively. The distilled student models also achieve an average ~232× inference speedup over the teacher models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five graph KD baselines: FitNet [34], AT [35], LSP [36], MSKD [37], and VQG [38]. The cross-geometry method (E,B) consistently outperforms all five across every dataset and task. On Cora NC, the method achieves 86.05% F1 versus LSP (83.34%), MSKD (82.48%), VQG (83.02%), AT (80.49%), and FitNet (80.32%)—a 2.71% margin over the strongest baseline (LSP). On Wiki-CS NC, the method reaches 74.17% F1, surpassing LSP and other baselines by 1.37% on average. For LP, the method leads by 0.93% average ROC AUC over SOTA baselines, with the largest gap on Cora (2.57%). Notably, even when a single hyperbolic teacher outperforms the Euclidean teacher (e.g., on Citeseer LP), the cross-geometry student still surpasses both single-geometry distilled students, confirming that the SWKT and GEO modules effectively reconcile geometric inconsistencies rather than simply averaging teacher outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends graph knowledge distillation from single-geometry settings (Euclidean-only methods like LSP, MSKD, VQG) to a cross-geometry framework that jointly leverages Euclidean and hyperbolic teacher models. It builds on the local subgraph preservation property and Gromov's δ-hyperbolicity to perform node-level geometry selection, departing from prior work that either applies a single geometry globally or uses global graph hyperbolicity as a coarse indicator. The two proposed modules—Structure-Wise Knowledge Transfer (SWKT) for geometry-aware embedding selection and Geometric Embedding Optimization (GEO) for bridging distributional gaps between geometric spaces—are novel architectural components not present in prior graph KD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world graphs exhibit local geometric heterogeneity that no single geometry captures well, and the cross-geometry KD framework offers a model-agnostic way to exploit this without modifying the student architecture. However, the empirical gains are modest (1.11% average F1 improvement over SOTA baselines), and the method introduces additional hyperparameters (λ, β) and requires pre-trained teacher models in multiple geometries, increasing resource demands. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two geometries, how it scales to dynamic or extremely large graphs, and whether the k-hop subgraph analysis remains tractable at scale.
