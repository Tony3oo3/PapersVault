## Problem

Stacking-based training strategies like gradual stacking are known to speed up language model pretraining, but the inductive biases they introduce into the learned model remain largely unexplored. Specifically, it is unknown whether the way layers are duplicated during model growth affects downstream task performance beyond what is captured by pretraining perplexity, and whether such biases differentially impact reasoning versus memorization abilities.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text sequences (C4, Wikipedia, GitHub, Arxiv mixture) using the UL2 objective (60% causal LM, 20% prefix LM, 20% span corruption) with sequence length 1280, producing next-token predictions. Downstream evaluation uses 5-shot or 8-shot prompting on QA and math benchmarks, as well as fine-tuning on GSM8K and synthetic reasoning primitives (induction copying, variable assignment at depths 0–2, and pre-school math) where inputs are short structured prompts and outputs are single tokens or short expressions.

## Method

MIDAS (MIDdle grAdual Stacking) modifies gradual stacking by replicating the middle block of L/k layers rather than the last block when growing the model from stage i to stage i+1. The motivation comes from three observations: last layers serve a distinct functional role in transformers, gradual stacking produces models with high inter-layer weight similarity resembling looped models, and looped models (e.g., ALBERT) naturally exhibit peak functional similarity in the middle. Training is divided into k stages with linearly growing depth and a PROP-α schedule (T_i ∝ i^α) allocating more compute to larger stages. The paper further constructs synthetic 'reasoning primitives'—induction copying, compositional variable assignment, and pre-school math—to isolate and measure the reasoning-specific inductive bias, showing MIDAS outperforms baseline on these tasks at equivalent perplexity, with the effect persisting after fine-tuning.

## Results

MIDAS achieves up to 40% training speedup (PROP-1 schedule) while matching or exceeding baseline downstream performance. At 1B parameters with PROP-2 (24% speedup), MIDAS reaches 26.8% all-task average vs. 24.0% for baseline, with validation loss of 2.009 vs. 1.996. At 8B parameters with PROP-2 (26% speedup), MIDAS achieves 36.4% all-task average vs. 32.8% for baseline (loss 1.844 vs. 1.841). The gains are most pronounced on reasoning-heavy tasks: math word problems improve from 23.5% to 29.0% (1B) and from 34.9% to 43.1% (8B), while memorization-based closed-book QA shows minimal change.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary stacking baseline is GRADSTACK (gradual stacking, Reddi et al. 2023), which duplicates the last L/k layers at each stage. MIDAS consistently outperforms GRADSTACK at every speedup level and model size: at 1B with PROP-2, MIDAS achieves 26.8% all-task average vs. 20.4% for GRADSTACK, and 2.009 validation loss vs. 2.024. Against the standard training baseline, MIDAS with PROP-2 matches perplexity (2.009 vs. 1.996 at 1B; 1.844 vs. 1.841 at 8B) while delivering substantially better downstream scores—26.8% vs. 24.0% (1B) and 36.4% vs. 32.8% (8B) all-task average. The advantage is concentrated in reasoning tasks: math word problems improve by 5.5 points (1B) and 8.2 points (8B), and open-book QA by 3.0 and 0.4 points respectively, whereas closed-book QA (memorization) shows negligible or no improvement. On GSM8K fine-tuning, MIDAS nearly doubles the 2B baseline accuracy (10.4% vs. 5.3%) and improves the 8B model from 12.3% to 15.2%, confirming the inductive bias persists after fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stagewise training lineage of progressive stacking (Gong et al., 2019) and gradual stacking (Reddi et al., 2023) by proposing MIDAS, which replicates the middle block of layers rather than the last block when growing depth. Conceptually, it draws on the inductive bias framework formalized for contrastive learning (Saunshi et al., 2022) and language modeling (Liu et al., 2023), but is the first to identify such a bias in a real-world training *procedure* (as opposed to an optimizer or architecture). The connection to looped models (ALBERT, Universal Transformers) provides a mechanistic hypothesis, and the reasoning primitives build on the induction-head literature (Olsson et al., 2022) to isolate basic contextual reasoning capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 25–40% pretraining speedup is directly actionable for practitioners training large language models, and the discovery that this speedup comes with a reasoning-specific inductive bias (not just a perplexity-neutral trade-off) reframes how the field thinks about training schedules as quality levers. The reasoning primitives offer a reusable diagnostic toolkit for the LLM reasoning community. Key open questions remain: the looped-model connection is conjectural and not formally established, the memorization-vs-reasoning dichotomy is not fully explained, and the absence of error bars on most main-table results (only fine-tuning reports std devs) limits confidence in the magnitude of individual gains.
