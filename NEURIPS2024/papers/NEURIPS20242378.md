## Problem

Existing diffusion-based generative models struggle with peptide design due to two unresolved challenges: (1) full-atom geometry with variable atom counts per residue is incompatible with fixed-size diffusion, and (2) the wide disparity in binding-site shapes across targets creates divergent coordinate distributions that severely limit generalization. Additionally, no systematic benchmark existed for evaluating target-specific peptide sequence-structure co-design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For sequence-structure co-design, the model takes only the full-atom binding site (receptor residues within 10 Å Cβ distance to the peptide) and generates both the amino acid sequence and the full-atom 3D coordinates of a 4–25 residue peptide. For binding conformation generation, it additionally receives the peptide sequence and produces the bound 3D structure. The output is a geometric graph where each node carries a discrete residue type and a variable-size atom coordinate matrix (c_i × 3), with c_i depending on the amino acid identity.

## Method

PepGLAD operates in three stages: (1) an E(3)-equivariant VAE (using dyMEAN's adaptive multi-channel encoder) compresses each full-atom residue into a fixed-size latent pair (z_i ∈ R^8, z⃗_i ∈ R^3), trained with 25% residue masking and 70k unsupervised protein fragments to build an informative latent space; (2) a receptor-specific affine transformation F(x) = L⁻¹(x − μ), derived from the Cholesky decomposition of the binding site's Cα covariance, projects all geometry into a shared standard Gaussian space, with E(3)-equivariance preserved under this transform (Proposition 3.1); (3) a standard DDPM latent diffusion model denoises the standardized latent states conditioned on the standardized binding site. Sampling applies an empirical classifier-guided correction to enforce linear sequence ordering on the otherwise unordered graph output.

## Results

On the sequence-structure co-design task (PepBench), PepGLAD achieves a diversity of 0.506, consistency of 0.789, median ΔG of −21.94 kcal/mol, and a 55.97% success rate (ΔG < 0), outperforming the strongest baseline DiffAb (0.427, 0.670, −21.20, 49.87%). Against the pipeline systems, PepGLAD attains 92.82% success rate and 3 s per candidate versus RFDiffusion's 79.68% and 61 s, though RFDiffusion achieves a slightly better ΔG (−33.82 vs. −29.36). On binding conformation generation, PepGLAD achieves the best RMSD_Cα (4.09 Å), RMSD_atom (5.30 Å), and DockQ (0.592) on PepBench, surpassing DiffAb (4.23, 7.60, 0.586) and HSRN (6.02, 7.59, 0.508).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PepGLAD decisively outperforms the non-diffusion antibody baselines HSRN and dyMEAN on every metric: diversity jumps from ~0.15 to 0.506, consistency from 0.0 to 0.789, and success rate from ~10–15% to 55.97%, indicating that autoregressive or iterative-refinement approaches struggle with the high complexity of peptide design. Against the closest diffusion-based baseline DiffAb, PepGLAD improves diversity (0.506 vs. 0.427), consistency (0.789 vs. 0.670), ΔG (−21.94 vs. −21.20), and success rate (55.97% vs. 49.87%) on PepBench, with even larger gaps on PepBDB (diversity 0.692 vs. 0.269). Compared to the pipeline system RFDiffusion, PepGLAD wins on diversity (0.506 vs. 0.259), consistency (0.789 vs. 0.696), success rate (92.82% vs. 79.68%), and runtime (3 s vs. 61 s per candidate), but RFDiffusion achieves a better ΔG (−33.82 vs. −29.36), likely benefiting from its large-scale pretraining. AnchorExtension is dominated by PepGLAD on diversity, consistency, success rate, and speed (3 s vs. 735 s), though it edges out on ΔG (−26.80 vs. −29.36). On binding conformation generation, PepGLAD achieves the best RMSD_Cα (4.09 Å) and DockQ (0.592), narrowly beating DiffAb (4.23 Å, 0.586) and substantially outperforming HSRN (6.02 Å, 0.508), dyMEAN (7.96 Å, 0.374), AlphaFold2 (8.49 Å, 0.355), and FlexPepDock (6.43 Å, 0.393).

## Relation to Prior Work

PepGLAD extends the geometric latent diffusion paradigm from fixed-size molecules (GeoLDM [71]) and protein backbones [18] to the challenging case of variable-size full-atom residues, where each amino acid type dictates a different number of atoms. It borrows the adaptive multi-channel equivariant encoder from dyMEAN [34] for full-atom geometry encoding and the DDPM framework [25] for the diffusion process, but introduces two key departures: a VAE that compresses variable-atom residues into fixed-dimensional latent vectors, and a receptor-specific affine transformation (via Cholesky decomposition of binding-site covariance) that projects disparate binding geometries into a shared standard space. The paper also positions itself against antibody design methods (HSRN, dyMEAN, DiffAb) and protein design pipelines (RFDiffusion), arguing that peptides' greater conformational flexibility and irregular binding patterns make the target distributions more divergent across binding sites than in antibody or protein design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: target-specific peptide design for therapeutics targeting previously undruggable binding sites, where existing methods are either inefficient (AnchorExtension at 735s/candidate) or limited to 1D sequence generation. The introduction of PepBench provides the field with a properly curated, non-redundant benchmark with cross-target generalization testing, which was absent from prior databases. The receptor-specific affine transformation is a clean, generalizable technique that could benefit other geometric generative tasks with variable input distributions. Key limitations include reliance on Rosetta scoring as a proxy for wetlab validation and the restriction to protein-centric (rather than small-molecule-centric) design for very short peptides.
