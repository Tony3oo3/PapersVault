## Problem

Existing federated learning methods address either OOD generalization (covariate shift) or OOD detection (semantic shift) in isolation, overlooking their simultaneous occurrence in real-world deployment. Centralized unified frameworks like SCONE cannot be directly transferred to FL because searching for a consistent energy margin across non-IID client distributions is non-trivial and outlier exposure data is unavailable in decentralized settings. This paper asks whether a single FL framework can reliably estimate global data distributions from heterogeneous clients to handle both tasks jointly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet and PACS) along with their Fourier-augmented counterparts, both passed through a shared CNN backbone (WideResNet or ResNet18) to produce latent features. The primary output is a class label for in-distribution and covariate-shift samples, while OOD detection is performed by computing the L2 norm of the score function evaluated at the latent feature; samples exceeding a threshold τ are flagged as OUT. The score model operates entirely in the reduced-dimensional feature space rather than pixel space, reducing computational cost while preserving expressiveness.

## Method

FOOGD introduces two coupled modules trained per client and aggregated at the server via weighted average. SM³D estimates a noise-conditional score model in feature space by combining denoising score matching (DSM) with a maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) term: Langevin dynamic sampling generates random features from a Gaussian start, and MMD constrains the generated distribution to match the empirical data distribution, yielding a reliable density estimate even for sparse or multi-modal client data. The norm of the learned score function then serves as a detection score, with OUT samples residing in low-density regions exhibiting large score norms. SAG regularizes the feature extractor by minimizing the kernelized Stein discrepancy (KSD) between the latent features of original and Fourier-augmented images, where the KSD is computed using the score model as guidance; this enforces distributional invariance without the mode collapse that strict norm-based or contrastive regularization would cause. The server-side aggregation of score models across clients produces a global distribution estimate that simultaneously informs detection thresholds and provides consistent invariance guidance across heterogeneous clients, enabling both intra-client covariate-shift generalization and inter-client (unseen-client) generalization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the most challenging non-IID setting (α=0.1), FedRoD+F00GD achieves 93.51% ACC-IN, 92.74% ACC-IN-C, 32.99% FPR95, and 91.76% AUROC, outperforming all baselines across all four metrics. Under the milder α=5.0 setting, FedAvg+F00GD reaches 88.90% ACC-IN, 88.25% ACC-IN-C, 12.02% FPR95, and 97.77% AUROC. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.1), FedRoD+F00GD achieves 77.88% ACC-IN and 75.70% ACC-IN-C, while FedAvg+F00GD attains 91.41% AUROC for OOD detection. On the PACS domain generalization task, FedRoD+F00GD achieves 83.53% average accuracy, surpassing the best baseline FedTHE (77.40%) by over 6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOOGD consistently outperforms all nine baselines across both generalization and detection metrics. Against the vanilla FedAvg [56], FedAvg+F00GD improves FPR95 from 83.41% to 35.32% and AUROC from 58.05% to 91.21% at α=0.1, while also boosting ACC-IN-C from 65.44% to 73.71%. Compared to the centralized OOD adaptations FedLN [76] and FedATOL [88], FOOGD achieves substantially better detection (e.g., FPR95 35.32% vs. 56.14% for FedLN at α=0.1) and better generalization (ACC-IN-C 73.71% vs. 71.77% for FedLN). Against personalized FL baselines FedRoD [8], FOSTER [83], FedTHE [26], and FedICON [69], FedRoD+F00GD improves ACC-IN by 2.36%, ACC-IN-C by 2.84%, FPR95 by 14.98%, and AUROC by 10.80% over FedRoD at α=0.1. On the PACS domain generalization task, FedRoD+F00GD (83.53% avg) surpasses FedTHE (77.40%), FedIIR [17] (71.88%), and FedT3A [25] (75.86%), with the largest gap on the hardest Sketch domain (50.95% vs. 29.14% for FedTHE). Notably, FedICON [69] degrades severely on CIFAR-100 at α=0.5 (ACC-IN drops to 75.83%), while FOOGD maintains stable performance across all non-IID degrees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on score-based density estimation (denoising score matching, Langevin dynamics) and kernelized Stein discrepancy for distributional alignment, repurposing both within a federated learning framework. It departs from SCONE, which unified OOD generalization and detection in centralized ML via margin-based objectives, by arguing that SCONE's requirements (consistent margins across non-IID distributions, outlier exposure data) are infeasible in FL. Instead of contrastive or IRM-based invariance (FedICON, FedIIR, FedTHE), FOOGD uses a federated score model as a shared distributional reference: its norm provides detection scores, and its gradient field guides Stein-based augmentation for generalization. The MMD-regularized score matching (SM³D) extends vanilla DSM by exploring wider feature space via Langevin sampling, addressing the sparsity and multimodality failures of score matching on decentralized data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly handle covariate-shift generalization and semantic-shift detection in federated learning, addressing a practical deployment gap where both types of OOD data coexist. The dual-use of a single score model (detection via norm, generalization via KSD) is elegant and reduces architectural overhead. Key limitations include: privacy preservation is deferred to future work, experiments are limited to image classification on moderate-scale datasets (Cifar10/100, TinyImageNet, PACS), and the score model adds communication cost per round. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets or other modalities, how it interacts with differential privacy, and whether the MMD-DSM trade-off coefficient requires per-dataset tuning in practice.
