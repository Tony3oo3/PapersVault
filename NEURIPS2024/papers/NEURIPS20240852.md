## Problem

Long convolutional kernels are difficult to train directly: they overfit easily and lack the inductive biases that make State Space Models (SSMs) effective on long sequences. While SSMs like S4 achieve strong performance, their kernel computation via HiPPO-based matrix products becomes a bottleneck that limits the speed of the downstream FFT convolution. The paper asks whether a simpler, directly parameterized multi-resolution convolution can match or exceed SSM performance while being more efficient at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240852/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MRConv consumes 1D sequences of arbitrary length (from 1,024 pixels for flattened CIFAR-10 to 16,384 for Path-X, 16,000 audio samples, or 4,096 text characters) and produces either a class label (classification) or a sequence representation. For ImageNet, 2D feature maps at each ConvNeXt stage are flattened to 1D sequences of lengths [3136, 784, 196, 49], and the 7×7 2D convolutions are replaced by 1D MRConv blocks. The number of multi-resolution branches N = log₂(L/l₀) + 1 adapts automatically to input length, and the model supports both causal and bidirectional convolution depending on the task.

## Method

MRConv constructs a global convolution kernel as a learnable weighted sum of N sub-kernels of geometrically increasing length (l_i = l₀·2^i) but with a fixed number of parameters per branch, using three low-rank parameterizations: dilated, Fourier (only m low-frequency modes via IFFT of zero-padded complex coefficients), and sparse (randomly sampled non-zero positions). During training, each sub-kernel processes the input in parallel through its own depthwise convolution followed by a BatchNorm, and the outputs are combined via a learnable per-channel weight α that encodes kernel decay; at inference, all branches are merged into a single convolution by absorbing the BatchNorm parameters into the kernel and summing the zero-padded, scaled sub-kernels. This structural reparameterization eliminates the multi-branch overhead at inference, reducing complexity to O(L log L) via FFT-based convolution, while the parallel training with BatchNorm diversifies optimization and prevents the overfitting that plagues directly parameterized long kernels.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena benchmark, MRConv-Base achieves an average accuracy of 87.81%, the highest among all sub-quadratic complexity models, outperforming S5 (87.46%), SGConv (87.17%), and S4-LegS (86.09%). MRConv-Large reaches 88.20% average, matching the quadratic-attention MEGA model (88.21%) while being significantly faster at inference due to reparameterization. On sCIFAR, MRConv with dilated kernels achieves 94.26% test accuracy, surpassing the previous best convolutional model MultiresNet (93.15%) and S4 (91.80%). On Speech Commands, MRConv with Fourier kernels achieves 96.82% at 16kHz and 95.05% zero-shot at 8kHz, outperforming all baselines including S5 (96.52%/94.53%) and SGConv (96.42%/94.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRConv-Base (87.81% avg) outperforms all sub-quadratic baselines on LRA: S5 (87.46%), SGConv (87.17%), Liquid-S4 (87.32%), S4-LegS (86.09%), and MEGA-Chunk (85.66%), with the largest margins on Image (+2.37% over S5) and Pathfinder (+0.22% over S5). MRConv-Large (88.20%) matches the quadratic-attention MEGA model (88.21%) while being 1.5× faster on Text and 1.3× faster on Image at inference after reparameterization. On sCIFAR, MRConv with dilated kernels (94.26%) beats the prior best convolutional model MultiresNet (93.15%) by 1.11% and S4 (91.80%) by 2.46%, using fewer parameters (5.7M vs. 7.9M for S4). On Speech Commands, MRConv with Fourier kernels (96.82%) surpasses S5 (96.52%), SGConv (96.42%), and S4-LegS (96.08%), and also leads on zero-shot 8kHz testing (95.05% vs. S5's 94.53%) due to its band-limited Fourier parameterization. On ImageNet, MRConvNeXt matches or slightly exceeds ConvNeXt at every model size (e.g., 82.2% vs. 82.1% for Tiny, 83.9% vs. 83.8% for Base) with fewer FLOPs (4.3G vs. 4.5G for Tiny), and achieves higher throughput than SGConvNeXt (1080 vs. 820 images/s for Tiny) using optimized CUDA FFT kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRConv extends the multi-resolution convolution framework of SGConv by importing structural reparameterization from the computer vision literature (RepVGG, Online Conv Reparameterization) into the long-sequence modeling setting. Where SGConv concatenates sub-kernels with a fixed decay, MRConv trains each resolution branch in parallel with BatchNorm for optimization diversity, then merges all branches into a single global convolution at inference. The paper also establishes a formal connection between its Fourier-parameterized kernels and SSMs, showing that the same kernel can be generated in O(L log L) via FFT rather than O((L+D) log²(L+D)) via Cauchy/Vandermonde products, positioning MRConv as a simpler and faster alternative to S4/S4D/S5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRConv demonstrates that a relatively simple convolutional architecture—multi-resolution sub-kernels with reparameterization—can match or exceed the performance of substantially more complex SSMs and linear-time transformers on standard long-sequence benchmarks, while offering faster inference through kernel merging. Key open questions include whether BatchNorm can be eliminated to enable training-time reparameterization (reducing training cost), whether input dependency can be added to close the gap with quadratic-attention transformers on discrete tasks like ListOps, and whether the Fourier-kernel/SSM equivalence can be exploited for fast autoregressive inference. The lack of error bars and single-run ImageNet results limit confidence in the reported margins.
