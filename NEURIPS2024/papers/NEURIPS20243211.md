## Problem

Prior multivariate distributional RL algorithms either lacked convergence guarantees, required intractable maximum-likelihood oracles, or failed to model the full joint return distribution. A key unresolved challenge is that when the reward dimension exceeds 1, the standard categorical MMD projection used in scalar distributional TD learning is no longer affine, causing the usual stochastic approximation analysis to break down. This paper provides the first oracle-free, computationally tractable algorithms with provable convergence for both multivariate distributional dynamic programming and temporal-difference learning.

## Input / Output

For dynamic programming, the method requires the full transition kernel P^π and the multivariate reward function r: X → [0, R_max]^d; for TD learning, it consumes a stream of sampled transitions (X_t, A_t, R_t, X'_t) where R_t ∈ [0, R_max]^d. The output is a per-state multivariate return distribution represented either as a categorical probability vector over a finite (possibly state-dependent) support map R(x) ⊂ R^d, or as m equally-weighted particles. A distinctive feature is that the support map need not be a uniform grid—arbitrary or randomized supports are supported—and the TD iterates live in the space of mass-1 signed measures rather than probability measures, enabling zero-shot scalar return distribution predictions for any reward in the span of the cumulants.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary algorithmic frameworks. First, a randomized EWP dynamic programming algorithm (BootProj) that approximates the projected Bellman update by sampling m particles from the Bellman backup and re-weighting them equally, yielding high-probability convergence with error scaling as Õ(d^{α/2} R_max^α / ((1−γ^{α/2})(1−γ)^α √m)). Second, a categorical TD-learning algorithm that operates on mass-1 signed measures over a finite support, where the MMD projection is computed as a quadratic program and is provably affine—resolving the non-affinity obstruction that arises when d ≥ 2. The contraction analysis relies on MMD with kernels induced by shift-invariant, c-homogeneous semimetrics of strong negative type (e.g., energy distance kernels ρ(x,y)=‖x−y‖_2^α), under which the multivariate distributional Bellman operator is a γ^{c/2}-contraction. The categorical fixed point error is bounded via a mesh-size argument over partitions of the return space, recovering the known Θ(m^{−1/2}) rate in the scalar Cramér-metric case and extending it to the multivariate setting with explicit d-dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes the first provably convergent algorithms for multivariate distributional RL. Theoretically, the categorical DP fixed point achieves an MMD error of O(d^(α/4) R_max^(α/2) / ((1−γ^(α/2))(1−γ)^(α/2)(m^(1/d)−2)^(α/2))) (Corollary 3), recovering the known Θ(m^(−1/2)) rate in the scalar (d=1, α=1) Cramér metric setting. The EWP randomized DP achieves O(d^(α/2) R_max^α / ((1−γ^(α/2))(1−γ)^α √m)) in high probability (Theorem 3), showing polynomial dependence on dimension d. The categorical TD algorithm on mass-1 signed measures converges to the projected fixed point with probability 1 (Theorem 6). Empirically, on 100 random tabular MDPs with 2D and 3D cumulants, the categorical TD method achieves lower Cramér distance error in zero-shot return distribution prediction than EWP TD, with the gap widening as the number of atoms increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its categorical TD algorithm against the EWP TD algorithm of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2021) [ZCZ+21], which is the primary prior method for multivariate distributional RL with MMD-based losses. In the d=2 setting (Figure 2a), the categorical TD method sharply outperforms EWP TD as the number of atoms grows, while EWP TD plateaus early—consistent with the non-convexity of the EWP MMD objective causing the algorithm to get stuck in local minima (visualized in Figure 3, where EWP particles cluster in low-density regions). In the d=3 setting (Figure 2b), the categorical TD method still outperforms EWP TD but requires substantially more atoms to achieve comparable accuracy, reflecting the curse of dimensionality in the categorical support. The paper notes that EWP representations scale more gracefully with dimension d for a fixed number of atoms (per Theorem 3's polynomial-in-d bound), suggesting EWP may be preferable in higher-dimensional settings, though convergence of EWP TD remains an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scalar distributional RL framework (Bellemare et al. 2017; Rowland et al. 2018) to multivariate reward functions, building on the MMD-based contraction analysis of Nguyen-Tang et al. (2020) and the EWP representations of Zhang et al. (2021). The key departure is the discovery that the categorical MMD projection ceases to be affine when the reward dimension exceeds one, which breaks standard stochastic-approximation convergence proofs; the authors resolve this by relaxing to mass-1 signed measures, a technique borrowed from the linear function approximation literature (Bellemare et al. 2019). Unlike prior multivariate DRL work (Zhang et al. 2021; Wu et al. 2023), which either lacked convergence guarantees or required intractable optimization oracles, this paper provides the first oracle-free, computationally tractable algorithms with explicit convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first theoretical foundation for multivariate distributional RL, providing convergence rates that reveal how representation fidelity scales with reward dimension d and number of atoms m. The signed-measure relaxation is a broadly applicable technique that may enable convergence analysis for other non-affine projection settings in distributional RL. Key open questions include: (1) convergence of EWP-based TD learning (the non-convex MMD objective causes local minima in practice), (2) scaling categorical representations to high-dimensional cumulants where support cardinality grows exponentially, and (3) extending the tabular analysis to neural-network function approximation for large state spaces.
