## Problem

Existing permutation equivariant weight-space models (e.g., NFNs) are restricted to simple feedforward MLPs and CNNs, and cannot handle the complicated permutation symmetries introduced by recurrence, residual connections, or normalization layers. This paper asks whether one can automatically construct maximally expressive equivariant models for arbitrary weight spaces without hand-designing architecture-specific layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243325/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests a collection of weight tensors (weights, biases, normalization scalings) along with gradients, multiple momentum terms at different decay coefficients, and a sinusoidal encoding of the iteration number, all stacked along a channel dimension. For learned optimization, the output is a per-tensor weight update; for generalization prediction, it is a scalar success rate. The weight space must be described by a specification that assigns a permutation-group label to each tensor dimension, which the algorithm uses to deduce valid index partitions.

## Method

The core contribution is an algorithm (Algorithm 1) that automatically constructs a basis for equivariant linear maps between any pair of weight tensors by enumerating all valid partitions of the combined input/output index set, where a partition is valid only if indices within each subset are permuted by the same group element. Each basis function is implemented as a simple array operation (index remapping plus summation), making it efficient in standard deep learning frameworks. Stacking these equivariant layers with pointwise nonlinearities yields a deep universal neural functional (UNF) that is guaranteed to be equivariant to the full neuron permutation group of any architecture. For learned optimization, the UNF is meta-trained via persistent evolutionary strategies to minimize task loss after a fixed inner training horizon, with a nominal SGD-with-momentum term added for stability. The approach subsumes prior NFN layers for MLPs and CNNs while extending to RNNs, Transformers, and other architectures with no architectural changes.

## Results

On the Tiny RNN Zoo generalization prediction task, UNF achieves a Kendall's τ of 0.8968±0.0006, outperforming STATNN (0.8839±0.0007) and Deep Set (0.8306±0.0006). In learned optimizer experiments across four tasks (MLP on FashionMNIST, CNN on CIFAR-10, RNN on LM1B, Transformer on LM1B), UNF-based optimizers typically achieve the lowest training loss and fastest convergence compared to Deep Set, NFN, and meta-tuned SGDM baselines. UNF notably outperforms NFN in the CNN setting, where the stronger permutation assumption on spatial dimensions yields a lower parameter count (7,369 vs. 41,603) that is easier for meta-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNF outperforms STATNN (Unterthiner et al., 2020) on the RNN generalization prediction task by 1.3 points in Kendall's τ (0.8968 vs. 0.8839), demonstrating that equivariant layers extract more informative features from raw weights than hand-crafted statistical summaries. Against Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., 2017), UNF achieves a substantially higher τ (0.8968 vs. 0.8306) on the same task, and in learned optimizer experiments, UNF consistently reaches lower training loss than Deep Set across all four architectures. Compared to NFN (Zhou et al., 2023a), UNF is identical on MLP weight spaces but outperforms NFN on the CNN task, where NFN's more restrictive symmetry handling (not permuting spatial dimensions) leads to a much larger parameter count (41,603 vs. 7,369) that hinders meta-optimization. NFN is not applicable to RNN or Transformer weight spaces, where only UNF and Deep Set can be used. All learned optimizers (UNF, Deep Set, NFN) outperform meta-tuned SGDM, confirming the value of learned optimization, with UNF achieving the best or near-best results in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on permutation-equivariant weight-space models initiated by Navon et al. (2023) for MLPs and Zhou et al. (2023a) for CNNs, generalizing to arbitrary architectures (RNNs, Transformers, residual connections) by automatically constructing equivariant bases from a specification of the weight space's permutation symmetries. The theoretical foundation draws on Maron et al. (2018)'s characterization of equivariant maps between arbitrary-rank tensors, but the key departure is an algorithmic, specification-driven construction that handles the complex permutation structures induced by recurrence and residual connections. In the learned-optimization setting, the paper positions UNFs as a more principled alternative to Deep Sets (which enforce equivariance to all permutations, ignoring the actual symmetry structure) and to MPNN-based approaches (which are more flexible but less expressive per layer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNFs remove a major practical limitation of prior equivariant weight-space frameworks by making them applicable to any architecture, which is essential for modern learned optimizers and generalization predictors that must handle RNNs and Transformers. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale tasks (8×8 FashionMNIST, 16×16 CIFAR-10, character-level LMs), and the basis size grows rapidly with tensor rank, raising scalability concerns for deeper or wider architectures. Open questions include whether UNF-based optimizers generalize across diverse tasks and architectures, how they compare to MPNN-based approaches at scale, and whether the computational overhead of the larger basis is a practical bottleneck for meta-optimization via evolutionary strategies.
