## Problem

No policy optimization algorithm exists for episodic (finite-horizon) constrained RL, despite many real-world tasks—such as diffusion model denoising, finite-horizon robotics, and safety-constrained LLM generation—being naturally formulated this way. Existing constrained policy optimization methods (CPO, FOCOPS, PCPO, P3O) are designed for infinite-horizon discounted settings and, when adapted to episodic problems, either fail to satisfy constraints or sacrifice episodic reward by prioritizing short-term constraint compliance. Moreover, the optimal policy in the episodic setting is inherently non-stationary and time-dependent, a structural difference that invalidates the stationary-policy assumptions underlying all prior algorithms.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes continuous state observations (e.g., 9-dim for point-mass, 16-dim for ant, 56-dim for grid traffic) and per-constraint cost thresholds d_i, and produces a sequence of H time-indexed Gaussian policies π_{1:H}, each parameterized by a separate neural network head. Unlike infinite-horizon methods that output a single stationary policy, e-COP must learn a distinct policy at every timestep, and the update proceeds backward in time (h=H down to h=1) within each episode. The importance-sampling ratio ρ(θ_h) = π_{θ_{k,h}}/π_{θ_{k-1,h}} is computed per timestep, and advantage estimates are evaluated on trajectories collected under the previous episode's policy.

## Method

e-COP is built on a novel policy difference lemma for episodic MDPs (Lemma 3.1) that expresses the performance gap between two policies as a sum of expected advantages under the new policy's time-dependent state-action distribution, rather than a stationary occupation measure. The resulting loss function (Eq. 8) combines PPO-style clipped surrogate objectives for both reward and cost advantages with an augmented Lagrangian penalty: a ReLU-gated linear term plus a quadratic damping term (β/2)·max{0, Ψ + λ/β}² that activates before constraint violation, replacing the Fisher-information-matrix inversion required by CPO/PCPO. The Lagrange multipliers are updated via a simple projected gradient rule (Eq. 7), and the damping factor β is adaptively increased by a multiplicative factor p whenever a composite constraint-violation metric exceeds a threshold, ensuring stable cost tracking near the boundary. Theorem 3.3 guarantees that for sufficiently large β and λ, the solution set of the penalized problem is identical to that of the original constrained CMDP, so no approximation error is introduced.

## Results

Across 8 constrained RL benchmark tasks (Humanoid, PointCircle, AntCircle, PointReach, AntReach, Grid, Bottleneck, Navigation), e-COP achieves the best or near-best episodic reward while satisfying cost constraints in most cases. On PointCircle, e-COP reaches 110.5 reward (vs. 91.2 for APPO, the next best), and on Navigation (multi-constraint) it achieves 217.6 reward (vs. 175.1 for PPO-L). On tasks where baselines achieve higher raw reward (e.g., CPO on AntReach at 102.3, CPO on Bottleneck at 388.1), those baselines violate the cost constraint (35.1 > 25.0 and 54.3 > 50.0 respectively), making e-COP the best feasible solution. e-COP also exhibits the tightest constraint tracking, e.g., 17.3 cost vs. threshold 20.0 on Humanoid and 9.8 vs. 10.0 on PointCircle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

e-COP outperforms all 7 baselines on PointCircle (110.5 vs. 91.2 APPO, 89.1 P3O), AntCircle (198.6 vs. 182.6 P3O), and Navigation (217.6 vs. 175.1 PPO-L), while also maintaining the tightest constraint satisfaction. On Humanoid, FOCOPS achieves slightly higher reward (1734.1 vs. 1652.5) but with cost closer to the threshold (19.7 vs. 17.3), and P3O marginally exceeds the constraint (20.1 > 20.0). On PointReach and Bottleneck, CPO achieves the highest raw reward (89.2 and 388.1) but severely violates constraints (33.3 > 25.0 and 54.3 > 50.0), making e-COP the best feasible method. On AntReach, P3O edges out e-COP on reward (73.6 vs. 70.8) with comparable cost (24.8 vs. 24.2), while CPO's 102.3 reward comes with a major constraint violation (35.1 > 25.0). On Grid, PPO-L achieves slightly higher reward (276.3 vs. 258.1) with similar cost, though e-COP has lower variance. FOCOPS, PCPO, and CPO are not applicable to the multi-constraint Navigation task, further highlighting e-COP's advantage in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained policy optimization lineage (CPO, PCPO, FOCOPS, P3O) from the infinite-horizon discounted and average settings to the episodic (finite-horizon) setting, where optimal policies are inherently time-dependent and no steady-state distribution exists. It builds directly on PPO's clipping mechanism and CPO's primal-dual Lagrangian structure, but replaces the Fisher information matrix inversion (a scalability bottleneck in CPO, PCPO, P3O) with a quadratic damping penalty that preserves solution equivalence (Theorem 3.3). The theoretical foundation is a new policy difference lemma (Lemma 3.1) for episodic MDPs, which has a fundamentally different form from its infinite-horizon counterparts and notably does not require backward recursion from the terminal time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The algorithm opens a path toward safety-constrained RLHF for large language models and diffusion models, where the denoising or generation process is naturally a finite-horizon trajectory optimization problem. The quadratic damping penalty addresses a practical pain point in constrained RL—numerical instability near constraint boundaries—without the computational cost of Hessian inversion. However, all empirical validation is on small-scale Safety Gym and Safe MuJoCo environments (state dimensions ≤56, horizon 200), and the connection to the motivating high-dimensional applications (LLMs, diffusion) remains aspirational. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large state/action spaces and whether the adaptive β tuning generalizes beyond the tested tasks.
