## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary layout-to-image (L2I) methods such as GLIGEN and InstDiff rely on self-attention to condition objects, which condenses textual descriptions into single vectors and loses critical information for complex, lengthy descriptions. Additionally, open-vocabulary L2I models are trained in open-set settings but evaluated on closed-set benchmarks with only a few words per object, leaving it unclear whether they can handle rich-context descriptions in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of bounding box coordinates paired with rich-context text descriptions (full sentences specifying colors, shapes, spatial relationships, and other attributes) as input, and produces a single RGB image where each generated object faithfully matches its description and aligns with its designated layout box. The model operates at 512×512 or 768×768 resolution using SD1.5 or SDXL backbones, and the descriptions can be arbitrarily long and complex, unlike the single-word or short-phrase inputs accepted by prior methods like BoxDiff and R&B.

## Method

The core contribution is a Regional Cross-Attention module that replaces the self-attention-based layout conditioning used by GLIGEN and InstDiff. Instead of concatenating visual and textual tokens into a joint self-attention, the method applies cross-attention where pre-pooled text token embeddings (encoded via Sequenced Grounding Encoding with bounding box coordinate indicators) serve as keys and values, while visual tokens within each spatial region serve as queries. To handle overlapping bounding boxes, a region reorganization step partitions the image into mutually exclusive regions (single-object, multi-object overlap, and background), ensuring each visual token attends to all relevant descriptions. The module is inserted after each self-attention layer in the diffusion U-Net, with zero-initialized output weights for stable fine-tuning from SDXL or SD1.5. The model is trained on a 2-million-image synthetic rich-context dataset (RC CC3M, RC COCO) generated using RAM, GroundingDINO, and QWen for detailed object descriptions, and is evaluated with two new metrics—CropCLIP and SAMIoU—designed for open-vocabulary, sentence-level descriptions.

## Results

At 512×512 resolution, the best model (SDXL backbone) achieves the highest CropCLIP scores (29.42 on RC CC3M, 28.15 on RC COCO) and SAMIoU scores (86.56 on RC CC3M, 80.84 on RC COCO), along with the best FID (11.02 on RC CC3M, 27.41 on RC COCO). The SD1.5 variant matches InstDiff on SAMIoU (80.78 on RC COCO) while reducing FID from 30.00 to 25.81 and halving the computational cost of the layout-conditioning layer. The advantage over baselines grows substantially with description complexity: for long sentences (≥16 words) and high Gunning Fog scores (>8), the gap in CropCLIP widens considerably compared to short/phrase-level descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two constrained (training-free) baselines, BoxDiff and R&B, the proposed method dominates on every metric: CropCLIP improves by ~5.5 points (28.15 vs 22.61) and SAMIoU by ~22 points (80.84 vs 58.75) on RC COCO, reflecting the inability of single-word/phrase inputs to capture rich-context descriptions. Against the open-set baseline GLIGEN, the SDXL model gains +2.95 CropCLIP and +2.18 SAMIoU on RC CC3M, with the gap widening for longer and more complex descriptions. Against InstDiff (the strongest open-set baseline), the SDXL model leads by +0.96 CropCLIP and +0.97 SAMIoU on RC CC3M, while the SD1.5 variant essentially ties InstDiff on SAMIoU (80.78 vs 80.78) and CropCLIP (28.45 vs 28.46) but achieves a substantially lower FID (16.49 vs 17.96) at half the layout-conditioning FLOPs. Overall, the paper's method wins or ties on all primary metrics across both datasets, with the largest margins appearing on the open-vocabulary RC CC3M set where descriptions are sentence-level rather than category names.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant self-attention paradigm in open-vocabulary L2I generation (GLIGEN, InstDiff), which condenses per-object text descriptions into single vectors before spatial conditioning. The key conceptual move is repurposing the cross-attention mechanism—standard in text-to-image diffusion for global text conditioning—into a per-region, per-object form, preserving full token sequences rather than pooling them. The region reorganization step (partitioning overlapping bounding boxes into mutually exclusive regions) and the Sequenced Grounding Encoding with box indicators are novel architectural components not present in prior L2I work. The paper also introduces a rich-context synthetic dataset and two evaluation metrics (CropCLIP, SAMIoU) that address the gap between open-vocabulary training and closed-set evaluation that has persisted in the L2I literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical evaluation gap: open-vocabulary L2I models were trained on diverse descriptions but benchmarked on closed-set datasets with single-word labels, making it unclear whether they truly handle complex prompts. The finding that cross-attention's advantage grows with description complexity and length provides a clear design principle for future L2I architectures. Limitations include reliance on synthetic pseudo-labels (which may contain errors), the need for backbone-specific fine-tuning, and the acknowledged failure of automatic metrics for very small or very large objects. Open questions include whether the regional cross-attention approach extends to video L2I, 3D layout generation, or more complex layout types beyond bounding boxes (masks, keypoints).
