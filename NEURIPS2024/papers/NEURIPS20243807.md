## Problem

Existing LLM ensemble methods either train additional reward or fusion models (limiting generalization to unseen distributions) or communicate only through textual responses, ignoring the rich confidence and alternative-answer information in internal probability distributions. The key technical barrier to direct distribution fusion is vocabulary discrepancy across heterogeneous LLMs, which makes averaging probability distributions infeasible due to token misalignment across different tokenizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243807/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, DEEPEN consumes the next-token probability distribution (a vector over each model's own vocabulary) from N heterogeneous LLMs, along with a precomputed relative representation matrix per model built from cosine similarities between all tokens and a shared anchor set (the intersection of all vocabularies, typically >20k tokens). The output is a single probability distribution in the main model's vocabulary space, from which the next token is sampled. The inverse transformation is an iterative gradient-based search (T=5 steps by default) minimizing KL-divergence between the candidate's relative representation and the aggregated target, adding roughly 17% token-level latency over the baseline.

## Method

DEEPEN is a training-free, three-stage framework for fusing heterogeneous LLM distributions. First, each model's probability vector p is mapped into a shared relative space via multiplication by a normalized relative representation matrix R̂ (rows are softmax-normalized cosine-similarity vectors of each token against all common anchor tokens), yielding r = p·R̂. Second, the N relative representations are aggregated as a weighted average (uniform for DEEPEN-Avg, or development-set-accuracy-weighted for DEEPEN-Adapt). Third, a search-based inverse transformation recovers the next-token distribution in the main model's space by iteratively updating an initial distribution (the main model's own output) via gradient descent on KL-divergence between its relative representation and the aggregated target, using a relative ensemble learning rate η tuned per benchmark. The theoretical grounding rests on the cross-model invariance of relative representations: the same token's embedding-similarity profile to anchor tokens remains approximately constant across different LLMs, enabling meaningful interpolation in the shared relative space despite heterogeneous vocabularies.

## Results

On the top-2 ensemble setting, DEEPEN-Adapt achieves consistent improvements of +1.76 (MMLU) to +3.22 (PIQA) over the best individual model across six benchmarks. On the top-4 ensemble, DEEPEN-Adapt gains +2.00 (MMLU) to +3.34 (ARC-C), while the combined DEEPEN+VOTING/MBR method reaches +11.35 on GSM8K and +3.63 on ARC-C. DEEPEN also generalizes to ensembles of dense and sparse architectures (LLaMA2-70B + Mixtral-8×7B, gaining +1.60 on GSM8K and +3.22 on PIQA) and to generalist-specialist pairs (LLaMA2 + NLLB on Flores-200, gaining +1.04 to +1.43 across four translation directions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLM-BLENDER (PAIRRANKER), DEEPEN-Adapt wins on all six benchmarks in both top-2 and top-4 settings; notably, LLM-BLENDER degrades performance in the top-4 ensemble (e.g., −10.53 on GSM8K, −6.45 on TriviaQA) while DEEPEN maintains consistent gains. Against MINED, DEEPEN is substantially more stable: MINED suffers a catastrophic −35.40 drop on GSM8K in the top-2 setting due to edit-distance token alignment producing incomplete words, whereas DEEPEN gains +1.75 on the same benchmark; MINED edges out DEEPEN only on MMLU in the top-4 setting (65.61 vs. 65.25). Against VOTING, the two methods are complementary: VOTING excels on GSM8K (+9.25) by leveraging multi-path reasoning, while DEEPEN dominates on knowledge and examination tasks; combining them yields the best overall result of +11.35 on GSM8K. Against MBR (applicable only to generation tasks), DEEPEN-Adapt outperforms on both TriviaQA (+1.44 vs. +0.00) and NQ (+3.10 vs. +1.61).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the cross-model invariance property of relative representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023), which showed that embedding similarities to anchor tokens remain stable across different neural networks. It departs from prior LLM ensemble methods—LLM-BLENDER (trained reward/fusion models) and MINED (edit-distance token alignment)—by reframing the vocabulary discrepancy problem as a space-mapping problem: instead of aligning tokens textually or training a separate fusion model, DEEPEN maps each model's probability distribution into a shared relative space where aggregation is well-defined. The conceptual bridge from classification-time prediction fusion (well-studied in vision and NMT) to generative LLM decoding with heterogeneous vocabularies is the paper's core positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of DEEPEN makes it immediately applicable to any set of LLMs without additional training data or compute, addressing a key generalization limitation of reward-model and fusion-model approaches. The finding that a well-performing specialist (e.g., NLLB translator) can be improved by fusing with a weaker generalist LLM suggests practical value for model reuse and knowledge transfer. However, the inverse transformation suffers from underfitting, the relative ensemble learning rate is dataset-sensitive, and adding more models can degrade performance—adaptive, sample-level or token-level collaboration weights remain an open challenge that the paper explicitly identifies as future work.
