## Problem

Identifying patient-specific parameters of physics-based physiological models (digital twins) from non-invasive imaging is a composite inverse problem: the imaging-to-state mapping is unknown, and labeled (image, parameter) pairs would require invasive procedures to obtain. Standard supervised learning is therefore infeasible, and existing physics-informed approaches (PINNs, Neural ODEs) solve the forward dynamics for a single patient at a time rather than learning a population-level mapping.

## Input / Output

The method takes echocardiography ultrasound videos (30-frame clips at 256×256 for CAMUS, 112×112 for EchoNet) along with physician-labeled LV volumes at end-systole and end-diastole as the only training signal. It outputs seven patient-specific parameters of a five-state lumped-parameter Windkessel circuit model (mitral/aortic valve resistances, max/min elastance, dead volume, initial LV volume, and cardiac cycle duration), which are then passed through a numerical ODE solver to deterministically generate the full pressure-volume loop, all five state trajectories, and ejection fraction.

## Method

Med-Real2Sim decomposes the composite inverse problem F⁻¹ = M⁻¹ ∘ K⁻¹ into a two-step physics-informed self-supervised learning pipeline. In the pretext task, a feedforward network is pretrained on 3,840 synthetic samples (parameters drawn uniformly from physiological ranges, states computed by a numerical ODE solver) to distill the forward dynamics M(θ)→x into fixed network weights. In the finetuning step, a 3D-CNN backbone is trained to predict the seven model parameters from echocardiography video, with the frozen pretrained network acting as a physics-constrained head; the loss is MSE between the predicted volumes m(φ_M(φ_F(y))) and the physician-labeled volumes. This inverts the standard SSL paradigm: the low-capacity physics head is pretrained first and frozen, while the high-capacity imaging backbone is finetuned to produce parameter estimates that, when passed through the frozen head, reproduce observed physiology. The approach scales to interventions (e.g., LVAD) by augmenting the ODE system with additional state variables and exogenous parameters, enabling in-silico counterfactual simulations.

## Results

On the end-to-end EF prediction task, Med-Real2Sim achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 5.40% on EchoNet and 6.81% on CAMUS, comparable to a fully supervised 3D-CNN baseline (5.62% and 6.58% respectively). For the core physics-informed pretext task of learning the forward cardiac hemodynamics map, the pretrained network φ_M achieves an MAE of 2.30 on EF across 1,000 out-of-sample parameter sets, substantially outperforming patient-specific PINNs (MAE 7.67) and Neural ODEs (MAE 3.39). The population-level φ_M also runs at 0.01 s per epoch versus 0.16 s (PINN) and 6.12 s (Neural ODE), with O(L) memory and time cost independent of the number of patients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), Med-Real2Sim's φ_M achieves a 3.3× lower MAE on EF (2.30 vs. 7.67) while operating as a single population-level model rather than requiring a separate network per patient; it is also ~16× faster per epoch (0.01 s vs. 0.16 s) and scales in O(L) rather than O(N·L) with the number of patients. Against Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018), φ_M achieves a 1.5× lower MAE (2.30 vs. 3.39) and is ~600× faster per epoch (0.01 s vs. 6.12 s), again as a population model versus patient-specific training. On the end-to-end EF prediction task (Table 1), Med-Real2Sim slightly outperforms the supervised 3D-CNN baseline on EchoNet (5.40% vs. 5.62% MAE) but is marginally worse on CAMUS (6.81% vs. 6.58%), indicating comparable overall accuracy while additionally providing interpretable physical parameters and enabling in-silico intervention simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Simaan et al. (66) five-state lumped-parameter circuit model of cardiac hemodynamics and reframes the problem of identifying patient-specific model parameters from non-invasive imaging as a composite inverse problem (unknown imaging operator K composed with known physics model M). Conceptually, it departs from standard PINNs and Neural ODEs—which solve the forward or inverse problem for a single patient at a time—by proposing a two-stage physics-informed self-supervised learning paradigm where the physics model is distilled into a frozen surrogate network (pretext task) and a high-capacity backbone is finetuned to learn the inverse mapping from images to parameters. This positions the work at the intersection of surrogate modeling in engineering, SSL pretraining/finetuning, and medical digital twin construction, distinguishing itself from generative inverse-problem approaches like CycleGAN or AmbientGAN by leveraging the known structure of M rather than learning both components from data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general recipe for constructing patient-specific digital twins from any non-invasive modality when a mechanistic model exists but invasive ground-truth labels are unavailable, with demonstrated applications to unsupervised disease detection (mitral stenosis) and in-silico clinical trials (LVAD simulation). A key limitation is the absence of clinical validation against invasive pressure-volume catheter measurements, meaning the learned parameters and simulated PV loops remain unverified in a clinical setting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-fidelity or multi-organ physics models, how it performs with richer non-invasive labels beyond ejection fraction, and whether the SSL analogy can be extended to handle noisy or partially observed imaging data. The computational efficiency advantage over per-patient PINN/Neural ODE training (O(L) vs O(N·L)) makes the approach more scalable to population-level digital twin construction.
