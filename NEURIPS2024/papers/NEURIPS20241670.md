## Problem

Prior work on optimistically universal online learning (Hanneke 2021; Blanchard et al. 2022) characterized the minimal data-process assumptions and corresponding universal algorithms only for the unrestricted concept class of all measurable functions. This paper asks: for a general concept class H with binary labels, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions on H and on the data process for optimistically universal online learnability to hold, and can one design algorithms that succeed under every admissible process?

## Input / Output

At each round t the algorithm receives a new instance X_t drawn from a measurable space (X, B) equipped with a separable metrizable topology, together with the full history (X_{<t}, Y_{<t}). It must output a binary prediction Ŷ_t ∈ {0,1} before the true label Y_t is revealed. The data process (X, Y) is a general stochastic process with arbitrary temporal dependencies, and realizability is defined prefix-wise: every finite prefix must be consistent with some h ∈ H, which is strictly weaker than requiring a single fixed h* to generate all labels.

## Method

The characterization hinges on two combinatorial structures: the VCL tree (governing whether all processes admit universal learning) and the Littlestone tree (governing optimistically universal learnability). For concept classes with no infinite VCL tree, the algorithm runs a VCL game whose winning strategy (guaranteed by Bousquet et al. 2021) advances only finitely many times, after which a partial-concept-class subroutine (adapted from Alon et al. 2021) bounds mistakes to o(n) via a weight-halving argument. For classes with infinite VCL trees, the paper introduces Condition A (process-dependent countable expert set with log i_n = o(n) coverage) as the exact criterion for a process to admit universal learning, and Condition B (the same expert set must work uniformly across all admissible processes) as the criterion for optimistically universal learnability; the corresponding algorithm is a weighted-majority meta-learner over these experts. The agnostic extension reuses the same expert construction and applies the Squint algorithm (Koolen & van Erven 2015) with non-uniform initial weights 1/(i(i+1)) to convert realizable consistency into sublinear regret, establishing full equivalence between the realizable and agnostic characterizations.

## Results

The paper provides a complete characterization of optimistically universal online learnability for binary classification. The key results are: (1) A concept class admits universal online learning under *all* data processes if and only if it has no infinite VCL tree (Theorem 9); (2) A concept class is optimistically universally online learnable if and only if it has no infinite Littlestone tree (Theorem 10); (3) For classes with an infinite VCL tree, a process admits universal online learning if and only if it satisfies Condition A (a countable expert set with log i_n = o(n) convergence), and the class is optimistically universally online learnable if and only if it satisfies Condition B (Theorems 11–12). Theorem 24 establishes that all characterizations carry over identically to the agnostic (regret-based) setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The results are stated as necessary-and-sufficient characterizations (theorems) rather than performance numbers. The paper's contributions are positioned relative to prior theoretical work: it extends Hanneke (2021) and Blanchard et al. (2022), which characterized optimistically universal online learning only for the class of all measurable functions (where Condition C₂ applies), to *arbitrary* concept classes. It also generalizes the Littlestone-dimension-based characterization of Ben-David et al. (2009) from fixed mistake bounds to universal consistency under general stochastic processes. The VCL tree and Littlestone tree combinatorial structures from Bousquet et al. (2021) on universal learning rates are repurposed as the central characterizing objects, revealing a connection between online universal consistency and universal learning rates that the authors note is of independent interest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistically universal online learning framework of Hanneke [2021] and Blanchard et al. [2022]—which characterized learnability only for the unrestricted concept class of all measurable functions—to arbitrary concept classes with {0,1} labels. The algorithmic core combines the VCL-tree-based winning strategy from Bousquet et al. [2021] (universal learning rates) with the partial-concept-class subroutine from Alon et al. [2021], while the expert-aggregation machinery for the agnostic extension follows Ben-David et al. [2009] and the Squint algorithm of Koolen and van Erven [2015]. The paper departs from prior work by showing that the relevant combinatorial invariants are VCL trees (from the universal learning rate literature) rather than Littlestone trees alone, revealing a previously unexplored bridge between these two areas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper completes a full characterization of optimistically universal online learnability for all binary concept classes, resolving open questions left by Hanneke [2021] and Blanchard et al. [2022]. The equivalence between realizable and agnostic learnability under the same minimal assumptions is a clean structural result that simplifies the theoretical landscape. Limitations include the restriction to binary labels and the reliance on a Borel measurable space; extending to multi-class or regression settings, and understanding whether the VCL-tree/Littlestone-tree dichotomy has practical algorithmic consequences beyond existence proofs, remain open.
