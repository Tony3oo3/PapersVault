## Problem

Privately learning mixtures of k arbitrary d-dimensional Gaussians up to low total variation distance has previously required roughly k²d⁴ samples [AAL24b], with a quadratic dependence on k even in the univariate case [AAL21]. This paper asks whether the sample complexity can be made near-optimal in all parameters, and answers affirmatively, improving the high-dimensional bound to roughly kd² + k^1.5 d^1.75 + k²d and proving the first linear-in-k bound for univariate GMMs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n i.i.d. data points in R^d drawn from a mixture of up to k full-dimensional Gaussians with arbitrary (non-singular) covariances and arbitrary means, and outputs a GMM representation (mixing weights, means, covariance matrices) within α total variation distance of the true mixture with probability at least 1−β, while satisfying (ε,δ)-differential privacy. The algorithm is explicitly inefficient (not polynomial time), and the privacy guarantee must hold for any pair of neighboring datasets, not just those drawn from a GMM. Components with weight below α/k can be ignored, as their total contribution to TV distance is at most α.

## Method

The core reduction is to crudely approximate each covariance up to a poly(n) multiplicative factor, after which private hypothesis selection [BSKW19, AAK21] finishes the job with Õ(kd²/α² + kd²/(αε)) additional samples. For the high-dimensional case, the paper applies the robustness-to-privacy conversion [HKMN23] with a carefully designed score function S(Σ̃, X) that counts the minimum number of points to alter so the data 'looks like' samples from a Gaussian with covariance Σ̃; the volume ratio V_η/V_{η/2} is bounded using sample compression (checking subsets of size m ≈ d^{5/3} rather than n/k) and a normalized volume measure inversely proportional to det(Σ)^{(d+2)/2} to handle unbounded covariances. Covariances are learned one at a time with (ε/√(k log(1/δ)), δ/k)-DP, composed via the advanced composition theorem. For the univariate case, a completely different approach sorts the data, bins consecutive differences into dyadic buckets, and applies a private histogram with Truncated Laplace noise, exploiting the fact that changing one data point alters at most 3 consecutive differences.

## Results

For learning a mixture of k arbitrary d-dimensional Gaussians under (ε,δ)-DP, the paper achieves a sample complexity of $\tilde{O}\left(\frac{kd^2}{\alpha^2} + \frac{kd^2 + d^{1.75}k^{1.5}\log^{0.5}(1/\delta) + k^{1.5}\log^{1.5}(1/\delta)}{\alpha\varepsilon} + \frac{k^2d}{\alpha}\right)$, improving the previous best of $\tilde{O}(k^2d^4/(\alpha^2\varepsilon))$ from [AAL24b] from quartic to quadratic in d. For the univariate case (d=1), the sample complexity is $\tilde{O}\left(\frac{k}{\alpha^2} + \frac{k\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)$, which is linear in k and matches a matching lower bound, making it optimal. A matching lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}\left(\frac{kd^2}{\alpha^2} + \frac{kd^2}{\alpha\varepsilon} + \frac{k\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)$ is also established, confirming optimality when $d \geq k^2\log^2(1/\delta)$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves over two prior works. Against [AAL24b] (the previous best for general d-dimensional GMMs), the sample complexity drops from $k^2d^4$ to $kd^2 + k^2d + k^{1.5}d^{1.75}$ (ignoring α, ε, δ dependencies), a polynomial improvement in all parameters—most notably reducing the dimension dependence from quartic to quadratic. Against [AAL21] (the previous best for univariate GMMs), the dependence on the number of components k improves from quadratic ($k^2$) to linear ($k$), which is the first linear-in-k bound and is proven optimal via a matching lower bound. The lower bound (Theorem 1.6) also refutes the conjecture of [AAL21] that only $\Theta(k/\alpha^2 + k/(\alpha\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta)/\varepsilon)$ samples suffice in the univariate case, showing that a $k\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha\varepsilon)$ term is necessary. The paper's result is provably optimal in the regime $d \geq k^2\log^2(1/\delta)$, where the upper and lower bounds match up to logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the robustness-to-privacy conversion framework of Hopkins et al. [HKMN23] and the inverse sensitivity mechanism [AD20b, AD20a], repurposing them for the novel task of privately learning unrestricted GMMs via density estimation rather than parameter estimation. It also draws on sample compression schemes [ABDH+20] from non-private GMM learning and private hypothesis selection [BSKW19, AAK21] to reduce the problem to crude covariance approximation. The key departure from prior work [AAL24b, AAL21] is the use of a carefully designed score function combined with normalized volume arguments, which avoids the exponential sample blowup inherent in parameter estimation for GMMs and instead exploits the structure of density estimation to achieve polynomial (and in some regimes, optimal) sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that univariate GMMs can be privately learned with sample complexity linear in k (rather than the previously known quadratic) is a significant conceptual advance, and the matching lower bound confirms optimality in that regime. For high-dimensional GMMs, the improvement from quartic to quadratic dimension dependence is substantial and provably optimal when d ≥ k². However, the algorithms are explicitly inefficient (not polynomial time), and the paper does not address the agnostic/robust setting or computational tractability, leaving open whether these sample-optimal bounds can be achieved by practical algorithms. The broader open question of whether polynomial-time private GMM learners exist at all remains unresolved, given known hardness results for even non-private GMM learning.
