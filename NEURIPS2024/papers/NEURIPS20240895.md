## Problem

Multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) systems degrade significantly when modalities are partially or entirely missing in real-world deployment, yet existing missing-modality methods suffer from information redundancy in complex feature interactions and lack semantic and distributional alignment of representations, leading to imprecise reconstruction and non-robust joint multimodal representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240895/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aligned multimodal video sequences where language is encoded via GloVe (300-dim), audio via COVAREP (74-dim), and visual via Facet action units (35-dim), with missing positions zero-filled. During training, a Modality Stochastic Missing (MSM) strategy simultaneously applies intra-modality frame-level dropout and inter-modality removal to generate incomplete samples for the student network, while the teacher network receives complete samples. The output is a continuous sentiment score (regression, MOSI/MOSEI) or a discrete emotion class among four categories (classification, IEMOCAP).

## Method

HRLF employs a teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm with three core mechanisms. First, a Fine-grained Representation Factorization (FRF) module decomposes each modality's Transformer-encoded representation into a sentiment-relevant component (shared, modality-independent) and a modality-specific component via intra- and inter-modality translation losses and a sentiment semantic reconstruction constraint. Second, a Hierarchical Mutual Information Maximization (HMI) mechanism estimates mutual information between multi-scale representations of teacher and student networks using a Deep InfoMax-style Jensen-Shannon divergence lower bound, progressively aligning semantics at three scales. Third, a Hierarchical Adversarial Learning (HAL) mechanism applies multi-scale GAN-style adversarial training where a discriminator distinguishes student from teacher representations, forcing distributional alignment in the latent space. The total objective combines task loss, FRF losses, HMI loss, HAL loss, and a KL-divergence logit consistency term.

## Results

On MOSI, HRLF achieves an average F1 of 76.74% across six inter-modality missingness conditions, outperforming the SOTA baseline CorrKD (74.69%) by 2.05% with statistical significance (p < 0.05). On MOSEI, HRLF reaches 75.63% average F1 versus CorrKD's 74.02% (+1.61%). Under complete-modality testing, HRLF attains 84.15% F1 on MOSI and 82.93% on MOSEI, matching or exceeding dedicated complete-modality methods. On IEMOCAP (4-class emotion recognition), HRLF achieves average F1 scores of 78.5% (Happy), 77.8% (Sad), 76.4% (Angry), and 61.7% (Neutral), all significantly surpassing CorrKD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRLF outperforms all eight baselines across every inter-modality missingness condition on both MOSI and MOSEI. Against the SOTA missing-modality method CorrKD [24], HRLF gains +2.05% average F1 on MOSI and +1.61% on MOSEI, with the largest margin in the visual-only condition ({v}: 64.59% vs. 60.72% on MOSI, +3.87%). Among generative baselines, GCNet [26] is the strongest (73.84% avg on MOSI), yet HRLF still leads by 2.90%; SMIL [29] trails further at 72.64%. Joint-learning baselines TransM [50] (71.32%) and MCTN [37] (68.31%) show substantially larger gaps of 5.42% and 8.43% respectively. Complete-modality methods (Self-MM [66], CubeMLP [42], DMD [25]) degrade severely under missingness (53.85–57.33% avg on MOSI), confirming that architectures designed for full modality availability are impractical in incomplete settings. Notably, HRLF's complete-modality F1 (84.15% MOSI, 82.93% MOSEI) matches or exceeds the best complete-modality baselines, indicating no performance penalty from the missing-modality training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

HRLF builds on the teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm for missing-modality MSA (e.g., CorrKD, KD-Net) but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it replaces coarse modality-invariant/modality-private factorization (as in FDMER, DMD, MFSA) with a sentiment-specific decomposition into sentiment-relevant and modality-specific representations, enforced through intra- and inter-modality translation and sentiment semantic reconstruction. Second, it introduces a hierarchical alignment strategy—combining Deep InfoMax-based mutual information maximization with multi-scale adversarial learning—rather than the single-scale KL divergence or feature matching used in earlier distillation approaches for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world MSA systems must handle both frame-level (intra-modality) and entire-modality (inter-modality) missingness simultaneously, which prior methods handle only partially. The finding that language-only performance approaches complete-modality performance on MOSI/MOSEI underscores the dominance of textual sentiment cues and suggests that robust unimodal fallbacks may be sufficient in many deployment scenarios. However, the authors acknowledge that simulated missingness patterns (random frame dropout, whole-modality removal) may not capture the full complexity of real-world sensor failures, leaving open the question of how HRLF generalizes to more structured or correlated missingness patterns.
