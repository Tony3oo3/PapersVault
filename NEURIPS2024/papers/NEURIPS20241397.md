## Problem

Existing target-aware molecule diffusion models learn the chemical distribution of all candidate ligands equally, treating every training sample with the same weight. This means they lack steerability: they cannot preferentially generate molecules with high binding affinity, good synthesizability, or other user-specified properties critical for real-world drug development. The paper addresses this by introducing a preference-optimization framework that shifts the target-conditioned distribution toward regions of higher reward without retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

The alignment procedure takes a pretrained SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model (e.g., IPDiff) and a preference dataset of (protein, winning ligand, losing ligand) triples, where the preference is labeled by a user-defined reward function such as AutoDock Vina binding energy. At inference, the aligned model takes only the 3D protein target (atom coordinates and one-hot amino-acid types) and iteratively denoises to produce a 3D ligand with atomic coordinates and element types (H, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl). The key distinction from the base model is that the fine-tuned conditional distribution p(m|p) is analytically shifted toward higher-reward regions, with the shift magnitude controlled by a temperature parameter β.

## Method

ALIDIFF reformulates the RLHF objective as a preference-optimization loss over the diffusion reverse process, decomposing the KL divergence into separate terms for continuous 3D coordinates (a weighted L2 distance between predicted and true atom positions) and discrete atom types (a categorical KL between predicted and true type distributions). The standard DPO formulation is shown to overfit by pushing all probability mass onto the winning sample, so the paper introduces Exact Energy Preference Optimization (E²PO), which replaces the greedy log-sigmoid maximization with a cross-entropy objective that aligns the model's preference probability toward the Bradley-Terry optimal value σ(r^w − r^l). This yields a closed-form converged distribution p_θ(m|p) ∝ p_ref(m|p)·exp(r(m,p)/β), where smaller β produces a sharper shift toward the reward. The framework is model-agnostic and is demonstrated by fine-tuning both IPDiff and TargetDiff with the same E²PO objective.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 benchmark (100 test proteins), ALIDIFF achieves state-of-the-art binding affinity with an average Vina Score of -7.07, Vina Min of -8.09, and Vina Dock of -8.90, surpassing the base model IPDiff by 10.1%, 8.56%, and 3.9% respectively. ALIDIFF also achieves the highest High Affinity rate at 73.4% (median 81.4%), meaning generated molecules bind better than reference ligands for the majority of targets. Molecular properties show only minor trade-offs: QED drops from 0.52 to 0.50, SA from 0.61 to 0.57, and diversity from 0.74 to 0.73 compared to IPDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALIDIFF (fine-tuned from IPDiff) outperforms all seven baselines on every binding affinity metric. Against its base model IPDiff, ALIDIFF improves Avg. Vina Score from -6.42 to -7.07 (+10.1%), Vina Min from -7.45 to -8.09 (+8.56%), and Vina Dock from -8.57 to -8.90 (+3.9%), while High Affinity rises from 69.5% to 73.4%. Against the other diffusion-based baseline DecompDiff, ALIDIFF leads by 24.5% on Avg. Vina Score and 6.0% on Vina Dock. Against TargetDiff, the gap is even larger: ALIDIFF achieves -7.07 vs. -5.47 Avg. Vina Score (29.3% improvement) and 73.4% vs. 58.1% High Affinity. Among non-diffusion baselines, Pocket2Mol is the strongest (Vina Dock -7.15, High Affinity 48.4%), but ALIDIFF still exceeds it by 24.9% on Vina Dock and 25 percentage points on High Affinity. The only metrics where ALIDIFF trails IPDiff are QED (0.50 vs. 0.52), SA (0.57 vs. 0.61), and diversity (0.73 vs. 0.74), reflecting the expected trade-off between binding affinity and drug-likeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of target-aware molecule diffusion models (IPDiff, TargetDiff) and the preference optimization paradigm (DPO, Diffusion-DPO) developed for language and image generation. While prior SBDD models learn the unconditional chemical distribution of all drug candidates equally, ALIDIFF introduces a reward-weighted alignment step that shifts the conditional distribution toward higher binding affinity. The key methodological departure is E²PO, which replaces DPO's binary ranking with exact energy-weighted cross-entropy to prevent the known overfitting pathology where all probability mass collapses onto the winning sample. The paper positions itself as the first alignment framework specifically for target-aware ligand diffusion, building on the KL-decomposition machinery from Wallace et al. (2023) but extending it to joint discrete-continuous (atom type + coordinate) diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALIDIFF provides a practical, plug-and-play alignment procedure that can be applied to any pretrained target-aware diffusion model (demonstrated on both IPDiff and TargetDiff), making it broadly useful for the SBDD community seeking steerable generation. The closed-form expression for the converged distribution (Theorem 3.1) gives practitioners a clear understanding of what the alignment achieves and how the β parameter controls the trade-off between affinity and molecular properties. Key limitations include reliance on AutoDock Vina as a proxy for true binding energy, the offline-only setting, and the acknowledged trade-off where ~10% affinity gain costs ~6% in synthetic accessibility. Open questions include whether online/interactive alignment with limited queries is feasible, whether more expressive reward classes (toxicity, ADMET) can be incorporated, and whether the approach transfers to prospective drug design pipelines.
