## Problem

Segment Anything Model (SAM) has been widely deployed across downstream applications, yet its vulnerability to universal adversarial perturbations (UAP) remains largely unexplored. Existing adversarial attacks target classification or traditional pixel-level segmentation, and are ineffective against SAM's prompt-guided, label-free mask paradigm. The central question is whether a single perturbation can render SAM incapable of segmenting objects across diverse images under any prompt configuration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During UAP generation, the method consumes 100 surrogate images (resized to 3×1024×1024) from a dataset unrelated to SAM's pre-training data, along with k randomly sampled prompts (points or boxes) per image to construct a semantic blueprint via merged masks. The output is a single l_p-norm-constrained perturbation δ (ε=10/255) that, when added to any new image, causes SAM to produce near-zero masks regardless of the prompt type (point, box, or segment-everything) used at inference time.

## Method

DarkSAM combines a semantic decoupling-based spatial attack with a texture distortion-based frequency attack under a unified loss J_total = J_sa + λJ_fa. In the spatial domain, a Boolean mask separates SAM's output into foreground (positive) and background (negative) regions; the perturbation is then optimized to push foreground mask values toward a negative fake mask ξ_neg (making objects unsegmentable) while simultaneously pushing background values toward a positive fake mask ξ_pos (misleading SAM into treating background as object). In the frequency domain, discrete wavelet transform decomposes images into high- and low-frequency components; the loss increases HFC dissimilarity between adversarial and benign examples while constraining LFC similarity, exploiting SAM's known bias toward texture over shape. A shadow target strategy resolves prompt ambiguity by randomly sampling k prompts during training and merging their masks into a semantic blueprint, enabling the single UAP to generalize across unseen prompt configurations at test time.

## Results

DarkSAM achieves an average ASR of 63.6% (point→point) and 73.6% (box→box) on SAM across ADE20K, MS-COCO, CITYSCAPES, and SA-1B, with peak ASR of 84.22% on SA-1B under box prompts. The method reduces mIoU by over 60% on average across 96 experimental settings (3 models × 4 datasets × 2 prompt types × 4 surrogate datasets). Even at a tight perturbation budget of ε=6/255, DarkSAM maintains an average ASR exceeding 45%, and only 100 training images are needed to craft an effective UAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DarkSAM outperforms all five baselines by an overwhelming margin across every dataset and prompt type. The strongest baseline, Attack-SAM [39], achieves at most 13.20% ASR (CITY, point→point) and 3.12% (SA-1B, box→box), while DarkSAM reaches 50.63% and 84.22% respectively — a gap of roughly 4–25×. UAPGD [9] and SegPGD [11] perform similarly (peak ASR around 10–11%), both far below DarkSAM. UAP [27] and SSP [32] are essentially ineffective, with several entries showing negative or near-zero ASR (marked as '–'), indicating the perturbations can paradoxically improve segmentation accuracy. The comparison confirms that existing UAP methods designed for classification or traditional pixel-level segmentation are fundamentally inadequate for prompt-guided segmentation, and that DarkSAM's hybrid spatial-frequency framework is necessary to achieve meaningful attack success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) framework, originally developed for image classification [Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., CVPR'17], to the fundamentally different setting of prompt-guided segmentation where the model outputs label-free masks conditioned on arbitrary user prompts. It departs from prior SAM attack work (Attack-SAM, Huang et al.) that focused on sample-wise perturbations for specific image-prompt pairs, and from earlier UAP-for-segmentation work (Metzen et al., ICCV'17) that relied on pixel-level labels unavailable in SAM's output. The key conceptual contribution is a hybrid spatial-frequency attack: a semantic decoupling strategy that separately disrupts foreground and background mask values in the spatial domain, combined with a DWT-based frequency attack that distorts high-frequency texture while preserving low-frequency shape, motivated by SAM's known bias toward texture over shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM is being rapidly deployed in safety-critical applications (medical imaging, autonomous driving, 3D reconstruction), and this paper demonstrates that a single imperceptible perturbation (10/255) can completely disable its segmentation capability across diverse images and all prompt types, exposing a significant and previously uncharacterized vulnerability. The strong cross-domain, cross-prompt, and cross-model transferability of the UAP means that defenses must be robust to a broad attack surface, yet the paper offers no effective defense (simple contrast/brightness preprocessing fails). Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to other foundation models beyond the SAM family, how to develop defenses that preserve SAM's zero-shot generalization, and whether the frequency-domain insight (texture over shape) can be leveraged for more targeted or interpretable attacks.
