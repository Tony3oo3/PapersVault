## Problem

LLM services are costly to operate and currently rely on subscription models, yet no formal auction mechanism exists for allocating and pricing ads within LLM-generated text. The core challenge is to design an allocation and pricing rule that integrates ads naturally via retrieval-augmented generation while being dominant-strategy incentive-compatible, individually rational, and balancing allocative efficiency with fairness across advertisers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a user query and a set of ads with private per-click valuations, the mechanism retrieves calibrated relevance scores (proportional to expected CTR) for each ad, collects bids, and runs an auction per discourse segment (sentence, paragraph, or full document). The output is a multi-segment LLM response where each segment naturally incorporates one or more winning ads via prompt conditioning, along with per-click payments set by the second-highest perturbed score. A key constraint is that the retriever must be calibrated so relevance scores are proportional to CTR, allowing the auction to operate without explicit CTR estimates due to inherent normalization in the allocation rule.

## Method

The paper introduces a segment auction that implements the RAG allocation rule (selection probability proportional to bid × relevance) as a randomization over deterministic second-price auctions, using the Gumbel max-trick to perturb each ad's score with i.i.d. Gumbel(0,1) noise before selecting the argmax winner and charging the second-highest perturbed score. This construction yields a DSIC and IR mechanism whose expected payment matches the unique truthful pricing rule derived from Myerson's lemma. The paper proves the single-ad segment auction maximizes a new logarithmic social welfare (LSW) objective—∏(x_i)^(q_i·v_i)—that balances allocative efficiency and fairness, analogous to Nash social welfare with weights q_i·v_i. For multi-ad allocation, the mechanism generalizes to selecting k winners per segment with a VCG-style payment, and a combinatorial variant treats each ad set as a single RAG document with set-wise relevance. Experiments with GPT-4 reveal that multi-allocation auctions produce higher output quality (embedding similarity to ad-free outputs) while repeated single-allocation auctions generate higher revenue, exposing an inherent trade-off for operators.

## Results

In the primary scenario (4 ads, 3 segments), the segment auction with replacement achieves the highest social welfare (0.660) and relevance (0.688), while the multi-allocation auction achieves the lowest revenue (0.238) but the highest output quality (0.715 cosine similarity to the ad-free baseline). Naive II (ignoring relevance) generates the highest revenue (0.379) but at the cost of lower relevance (0.552) and the worst minimum social welfare (0.329). The key tradeoff is that repeated single-ad segment auctions maximize revenue, whereas the multi-allocation auction, which lets the LLM integrate all ads simultaneously, produces the most coherent output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five auction mechanisms: segment auction with replacement, segment auction without replacement, Naive I (appends ads without LLM integration), Naive II (ignores relevance scores), and multi-allocation. On revenue, Naive II (0.379) and segment auction with replacement (0.371) are the top performers, while multi-allocation is the lowest (0.238) because it charges lower per-click payments to winners. On social welfare, segment auction with replacement dominates (0.660 vs. 0.508–0.524 for others), driven by its ability to repeatedly select the high-relevance BookHaven ad (q=0.87). On output quality (cosine similarity to the ad-free baseline at k=3), multi-allocation wins (0.715) over all single-allocation variants (0.698–0.702) and Naive I (0.671), confirming that giving the LLM flexibility to place all ads across the full output yields more natural integration. Naive I, which simply concatenates ad text at the end, consistently produces the worst output quality, validating the need for LLM-mediated ad integration. The minimum social welfare metric (a fairness proxy) favors Naive II (0.329) and segment auction without replacement (0.294) over segment auction with replacement (0.185), which concentrates allocation on the highest-relevance advertiser.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al.) and mechanism design, departing from prior LLM-auction work that operates at the token level (Duetting et al.) or via RLHF-based output steering (Soumalias et al.). The key conceptual move is to treat RAG's probabilistic document retrieval as an allocation rule and then ask which social welfare it optimizes, drawing on the Gumbel max-trick from discrete choice econometrics (Train) to implement it as a randomization over second-price auctions (Mehta & Vazirani). The linear aggregation of bids with relevance scores follows Duetting et al., while the pricing rule is derived via Myerson's lemma, connecting the RAG framework to classical optimal auction theory in a way no prior LLM-advertising paper has done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of logarithmic social welfare as the objective maximized by RAG allocation provides a principled, fairness-aware justification for probabilistic ad retrieval rather than deterministic top-1 selection, which is practically important for maintaining user trust in LLM outputs. The empirical trade-off between revenue (favored by repeated single-ad auctions) and output quality (favored by multi-allocation) gives operators a concrete design tension to navigate. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single fixed query, four to eleven mocked advertisers, and GPT-4 API calls, leaving open whether these trade-offs persist at scale with real ad inventories, diverse queries, and calibrated CTR models. The paper also leaves unresolved how to integrate reserve prices, handle ex-post relevance without prohibitive LLM query complexity, and whether joint fine-tuning of retriever and generator could improve both efficiency and quality simultaneously.
