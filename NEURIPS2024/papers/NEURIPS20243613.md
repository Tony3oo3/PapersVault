## Problem

Previous uniform-in-time propagation of chaos results for mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) yield particle approximation errors of order O(λ/(αN)) that depend on the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (LSI) constant α. Since α can deteriorate exponentially with the regularization coefficient λ (α ≳ exp(−Θ(1/λ))), these bounds imply an exponentially growing number of particles as λ→0. This paper asks whether the LSI-constant dependence is truly necessary for the particle approximation error in the mean-field neural network setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an entropy-regularized convex functional L(μ) = F(μ) + λEnt(μ) over P₂(ℝᵈ), where F is the empirical risk of a mean-field two-layer neural network with an L-smooth convex loss ℓ. It takes initial particle positions, the regularization parameter λ, and step size η as inputs, and produces explicit convergence rate bounds, an LSI-constant-free particle approximation error of O(LR²/(2N)), and sampling guarantees for the stationary distribution μ* in both continuous-time and discrete-time MFLD settings.

## Method

The core insight is that the particle approximation error arises entirely from the nonlinearity of the loss function ℓ and can be captured by the Bregman divergence B_F induced by the risk functional F. The paper decomposes the objective gap L^(N)(μ*^(N))/N − L(μ*) into a Bregman divergence term plus KL-divergence terms, then shows that the Bregman divergence under the product measure μ*^⊗N reduces to the variance of the N-particle neural network output h_μ_X(z). Using the L-smoothness of ℓ and the bounded second moment of h(X,z) under μ*, this variance is bounded by LR²/N, yielding the LSI-constant-free error O(LR²/(2N)). This approach is fundamentally different from prior analyses that route through LSI to control the gap, and it naturally recovers the zero-error case when ℓ is linear (L=0), confirming that nonlinearity is the sole source of approximation error.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes a particle approximation error of O(LR²/(2N)) for the objective gap between the N-particle and mean-field limits, which is free from the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (LSI) constant α. This improves upon the previous bound O(λ/(αN)) from Chen et al. (2022) and Suzuki et al. (2023b), where α can deteriorate exponentially as α ≳ exp(−Θ(1/λ)). As applications, the paper derives improved convergence rates for finite-particle MFLD (Theorem 2), a sampling guarantee requiring O(1/(λϵ²)) particles instead of O(1/(αλϵ²)) (Corollary 1), and uniform-in-time Wasserstein propagation of chaos with error O(1/(αλN)) instead of O(1/(α²N)) (Corollary 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against the particle approximation error bounds of Chen et al. (2022) and Suzuki et al. (2023b), which scale as O(λ/(αN)) and depend inversely on the LSI constant α. Since α can be exponentially small in 1/λ (e.g., α ≳ exp(−4NB/λ) via Holley–Stroock), the previous bounds imply an exponentially growing particle requirement as λ→0. The new bound O(LR²/(2N)) eliminates this dependence entirely, requiring only O(1/ϵ) particles for ϵ-accurate optimization versus O(λ/(αϵ)) previously. For sampling the stationary distribution μ*, the particle complexity improves from O(1/(αλϵ²)) (Kook et al., 2024) to O(1/(λϵ²)). For Wasserstein propagation of chaos, the error improves from O(1/(α²N)) to O(1/(αλN)). The iteration complexity remains O(1/(ᾱ²λϵ)·log(1/ϵ)), matching Suzuki et al. (2023b) up to the LSI constant used (ᾱ on μ*⁽ᴺ⁾ vs. α on the proximal Gibbs distribution).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uniform-in-time propagation of chaos framework for Mean Field Langevin Dynamics established by Chen et al. (2022) and Suzuki et al. (2023b), which bounded the particle approximation error as O(λ/(αN)) where α is the LSI constant. The key departure is a proof strategy that isolates the nonlinearity of the risk functional via Bregman divergence, yielding an LSI-constant-free bound O(LR²/(2N)) that depends only on the smoothness of the loss and the second moment of the neuron output. The analysis builds on the proximal Gibbs distribution (Nitanda et al., 2022) and the one-step interpolation argument (Vempala and Wibisono, 2019), but the Bregman-divergence decomposition of the objective gap is the novel structural insight that decouples particle error from functional inequality constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the LSI constant α can deteriorate exponentially as λ→0 (α ≳ exp(-Θ(1/λ))), meaning prior bounds implied an exponentially growing particle count for small regularization; this paper removes that dependence entirely from the particle error, giving N = O(1/ε) instead of N = O(λ/(αε)). However, the iteration complexity still carries an LSI-dependent factor, so the overall complexity is not fully polynomial in 1/λ. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the improvement is specific to the risk-minimization structure (Assumption 3); whether similar LSI-free bounds hold for more general convex functionals F remains open. This work provides a cleaner theoretical foundation for understanding when finite-particle MFLD faithfully approximates the infinite-particle limit in mean-field neural network training.
