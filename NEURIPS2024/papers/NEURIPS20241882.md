## Problem

Automatically learning effective data sampling strategies for deep network training is hindered by three compounding challenges: the sampler's search space is as high-dimensional as the training set, the objective landscape is extremely sharp due to gradient imbalance across instances, and evaluating a single sampler requires full training from scratch. Existing learning-based methods (RL or differential-based) either demand hundreds of complete training runs or require unavailable clean meta data, making them impractical for large-scale datasets like ImageNet.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset with two static per-instance features—cross-entropy loss and renormed entropy (target-class probability removed and remaining probabilities renormalized)—computed once from a pre-trained model of the same architecture as the target. It produces a sampler defined by exactly 10 hyperparameters (4 interior segment endpoints, 5 endpoint values, and 2 linear feature-aggregation coefficients) that maps any training instance to a sampling probability via a piecewise-linear composition. The sampler is architecture-agnostic and transferable: one searched on ResNet-18 can be applied to SE-ResNext-101 with comparable gains.

## Method

SS formulates sampler search as a bilevel problem: an outer Bayesian Optimization loop (GP with RBF kernel, UCB acquisition) searches over 10 hyperparameters defining the sampler τ(x) = H(T(G(x))), while an inner loop fine-tunes the network under the sampled probabilities. The key dimensionality reduction comes from constraining the sampler to a Lipschitz-continuous family where H is a 4-segment piecewise-linear function, G is a linear projection of CDF-normalized features, and T is a cumulative gradient function (cgf) that equalizes gradient mass across H's segments to smooth the otherwise sharp objective. To avoid the prohibitive cost of training from scratch for each candidate sampler, SS approximates the inner-loop local minimum by fine-tuning a shared pre-trained checkpoint (w_share) for only 5 epochs, which empirically preserves the ranking of samplers (top-1 approximated sampler is the true top-1 in most runs). This combination of low-dimensional parameterization, objective smoothing via cgf, and cheap local-minima approximation enables 40 BO evaluations on ImageNet with 8 A100 GPUs, yielding 0.8–1.5% top-1 accuracy gains across eight architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet ILSVRC12, SS consistently improves top-1 accuracy by 0.8%–1.5% across eight architectures (e.g., ResNet-18: 70.2%→71.6%, SE-ResNext-101: 78.9%→80.0%, Swin-B: 83.5%→84.3%). On CIFAR-10 with 40% label noise, SS achieves 88.0% top-1 accuracy versus 64.9% for uniform sampling, 68.6% for REED, 76.6% for MentorNet, and 86.9% for Learning-to-Reweight. On the face recognition task (MS1M→YTF), SS improves ResNet-101 verification accuracy from 97.54% to 97.74%. The entire sampler is defined by only 10 hyper-parameters, and the search requires just 40 BO steps with 5 fine-tuning epochs each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SS outperforms all three baselines (REED, MentorNet, and Learning-to-Reweight) across every noise rate on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The advantage is most pronounced at high noise: at 40% noise on CIFAR-10, SS reaches 88.0% versus 68.6% (REED), 76.6% (MentorNet), and 86.9% (LR), yielding gains of 19.4, 11.4, and 1.1 percentage points respectively. Against LR (the strongest baseline), SS leads by 0.5–1.1 points on CIFAR-10 and 0.9–1.1 points on CIFAR-100 at noise rates ≥20%, while the gap narrows to ~0.1–0.7 points at 0–10% noise where all methods perform similarly. On ImageNet, SS is compared only against uniform-sampling baselines (not REED/MN/LR), showing consistent 0.8–1.5% top-1 gains across MobileNet-v2, ResNet-18/50/101, SE-ResNext-50/101, and Swin-T/B. The paper also demonstrates that a sampler searched on ResNet-18 transfers well to SE-ResNext-101 (79.8% vs. 80.0% self-searched), suggesting substantial savings in search cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both hard-wired sampling rules (importance sampling, curriculum learning, denoising thresholds) and learning-based sampler search methods (RL-based approaches like Fan et al. 2017 requiring hundreds of full training runs, and differential-based methods requiring clean meta-data). It repurposes Bayesian Optimization—previously applied to hyperparameter tuning and architecture search—into the sampler search problem, but the key novelty is the 10-parameter piecewise-linear sampler formulation with a cumulative gradient function (cgf) for landscape smoothing, which reduces the search space from per-instance probabilities to a compact bilevel optimization. The local minima approximation via fine-tuning from a shared pre-trained checkpoint is a practical departure from the standard training-from-scratch evaluation used by prior RL-based samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-parameter formulation makes automatic sampler search tractable on large-scale datasets (ImageNet, LAION-5B) where prior RL-based methods are computationally infeasible, and the demonstrated transferability of samplers across architectures (ResNet-18 → SE-ResNext-101) suggests a single search can benefit multiple model families. Key limitations include the reliance on a pre-trained model for static features (loss, entropy), the assumption that a fixed sampling schedule suffices (no dynamic/curriculum adaptation during training), and the local minima approximation, while validated by rank correlation, still introduces some noise (SR ≈ 0.72). Open questions include whether the 10-parameter family is expressive enough for highly heterogeneous or multimodal datasets, and whether dynamic samplers could capture time-varying optimal sampling strategies.
