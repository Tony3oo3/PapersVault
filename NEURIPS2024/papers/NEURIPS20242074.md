## Problem

Language-image pre-training models (e.g., CLIP, LiT) are trained on image-text pairs with short captions (~17 tokens), causing certain tokens to be overshadowed by salient ones when the model encounters longer texts. This data-driven bias, rather than the token-length limit alone, is the root cause of poor long-text comprehension in contrastive LIP models. The paper further identifies a trade-off: directly re-captioning training data with long texts improves long-text retrieval but degrades short-text tasks like image classification.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and text captions as input. Long captions are constructed by randomly selecting 3 consecutive sub-captions (~22 tokens each) from MLLM-generated descriptions, yielding ~136-token inputs processed by a BERT text encoder with a 128-token limit. The output is a cosine-similarity-based image-text alignment score used for retrieval (R@1, R@5) or, via prompt templates, a zero-shot class label for ImageNet classification.

## Method

LoTLIP introduces two key components. First, it re-captions 100M images using three MLLMs (InstructBLIP, LLaVA, ShareGPT4V) to create a large-scale long-caption dataset, then incorporates these long captions directly into the contrastive pre-training stage rather than as a fine-tuning step. Second, it inserts m learnable 'corner tokens' ([Cor1]…[Corm]) after the [CLS] token in the BERT text encoder, with a custom attention mask that prevents [CLS] and corner tokens from attending to each other while both attend to all content tokens—forcing each token to aggregate a distinct subset of textual information. The training objective combines a standard short-text contrastive loss with a long-text contrastive loss that matches the image embedding against both the [CLS] feature and all corner-token features simultaneously. This design lets the model retain its original short-text proficiency (recovered via the short-text loss and diverse corner-token aggregation) while substantially improving long-text-image alignment.

## Results

On the 100M-scale long-text-image retrieval benchmark, LoTLIP achieves an average R@1 of 83.72%, surpassing Long-CLIP (79.52%) by 4.2% and SigLIP trained on 12B pairs (76.59%) by 7.13%. On short-text tasks at the same 100M scale, LoTLIP attains 72.16% ImageNet top-1 accuracy, 59.66% MSCOCO I2T R@1, and 86.90% Flickr30k I2T R@1, matching or exceeding all baselines except SigLIP (12B) on short-text retrieval. The method also outperforms ALIGN (700M) and CLIP (400M) on long-text retrieval despite using substantially less training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoTLIP (100M) decisively outperforms Long-CLIP (400M+1M fine-tuning) on long-text-image retrieval by 4.2% average R@1 (83.72 vs. 79.52), with the largest gap on DCI (62.10 vs. 51.68 I2T) and ShareGPT4V-10k (86.84 vs. 79.24 I2T). Against SigLIP trained on 12B pairs, LoTLIP wins long-text retrieval by 7.13% but trails on short-text tasks (ImageNet 72.16% vs. 76.04%; MSCOCO I2T R@1 59.66% vs. 65.46%), reflecting SigLIP's advantage from 120x more data. Compared to ALIGN (700M) and CLIP (400M), LoTLIP leads on long-text retrieval by 9.13% and 15.60% respectively while also matching or exceeding them on short-text retrieval. Against the same-scale LiT baseline (100M), LoTLIP improves long-text retrieval by 17.28% average R@1 and short-text retrieval (MSCOCO I2T R@1 59.66% vs. 51.92%; Flickr30k I2T R@1 86.90% vs. 80.00%), though LiT retains a slight edge on ImageNet classification (73.70% vs. 72.16%). On short-text T2I retrieval, LoTLIP (100M) trails both Long-CLIP and SigLIP, indicating that the corner-token strategy primarily benefits I2T and long-text alignment rather than all retrieval directions equally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LiT (locked-image text tuning) contrastive pre-training framework by incorporating long captions during the pre-training stage, departing from the standard practice of using short captions (~17 tokens) inherited from CLIP. It builds on DreamLIP's MLLM-based re-captioning pipeline but differs critically in using full long texts rather than randomly sampled sub-captions, and introduces learnable 'corner tokens' with a custom attention mask to aggregate diverse textual information without degrading short-text performance. The work positions itself against Long-CLIP, which achieves long-text capability only through post-hoc fine-tuning at the cost of short-text proficiency, whereas LoTLIP integrates long captions natively into pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As text-to-image systems (DALLE-3, Pixel-art) increasingly rely on detailed long captions for better image-text alignment, the ability of contrastive LIP models to comprehend long texts becomes practically critical. The 100M long-caption dataset and the corner-token mechanism provide a reusable foundation for this emerging need. However, the paper acknowledges that MLLM-generated captions contain hallucinations that may limit the full potential of long-text training, and the observed trade-off between caption length and training efficiency (FLOPs scaling) remains an open question for practitioners choosing optimal text lengths.
