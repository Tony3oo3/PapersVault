## Problem

Directly applying diffusion models to missing data imputation (MDI) yields suboptimal accuracy due to two structural defects: the generative process implicitly promotes sample diversity (via a positive entropy term in the underlying cost functional), which conflicts with the point-estimation accuracy required for imputation, and the training procedure necessitates a data masking strategy that is difficult to align with the unknown test-time missing mechanism. No prior work had identified or resolved these issues from an optimization-theoretic perspective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incomplete numerical tabular matrix X^(obs) ∈ R^(N×D) together with a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^(N×D) indicating observed (1) vs. missing (0) entries, and outputs a fully imputed matrix X̂ where observed entries are preserved and missing entries are filled by simulating an ODE. Only numerical features are handled, and evaluation is restricted to the missing indices under MAR or MCAR mechanisms. The score network is a 2-layer MLP (256 hidden units, Swish activation) trained via Denoising Score Matching with σ=0.1, and the ODE is discretized with step size η=0.1 over simulation time T=500 using an RBF kernel of bandwidth h=0.5.

## Method

NewImp reframes diffusion-model-based MDI within the Wasserstein Gradient Flow (WGF) framework and derives the implicit cost functional that diffusion models actually optimize, revealing a positive entropy term ψ(X^(miss)) ≥ 0 that encourages diversity. To suppress this, the paper introduces a Negative Entropy Regularization (NER) cost functional F_NER = E_r[log p̂(X^(miss)|X^(obs))] − λ·H[r(X^(miss))], whose maximization provides a lower bound on the true MDI objective. The velocity field is made computable by restricting it to an RKHS with an RBF kernel (Proposition 4.1), yielding a closed-form Monte Carlo-estimable update that avoids the intractable proposal density. Crucially, Proposition 4.2 proves that the NER-regularized conditional-distribution functional differs from a joint-distribution functional F_joint-NER by only a constant, so the score ∇log p̂(X^(joint)) can be trained via standard DSM on the full (unmasked) data, entirely eliminating the need for a masking strategy during training. The final imputation is obtained by iterating DSM training and ODE simulation (Algorithm 1) for T=2 outer loops.

## Results

On 8 UCI tabular datasets at 30% missing rate, NewImp achieves the best or second-best MAE and WASS across nearly all MAR and MCAR scenarios. Representative headline numbers: on CC (MAR), NewImp reaches MAE 0.35 / WASS 0.25, versus 0.85/2.82 for CSDI_T and 0.87/1.59 for MissDiff; on BT (MCAR), NewImp attains MAE 0.48 / WASS 0.18, outperforming ReMasker (0.46/0.11) and GAIN (0.72/0.39). NewImp significantly outperforms all DM-based baselines (p<0.05, paired t-test) on every dataset, and also surpasses non-DM methods (GAIN, MIRACLE, MIWAE, TDM, ReMasker, Sinkhorn) on the majority of dataset–scenario combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NewImp substantially outperforms both DM-based baselines: on CC (MAR), it reduces MAE from 0.85 (CSDI_T) and 0.87 (MissDiff) to 0.35, and WASS from 2.82/1.59 to 0.25. Against non-DM methods, NewImp wins on the majority of dataset–scenario pairs: e.g., on CC (MAR) it beats GAIN (0.75), MIRACLE (0.43), MIWAE (0.76), TDM (0.81), and ReMasker (0.60) in MAE. The closest competitor is ReMasker, which matches NewImp on BT (MAR) MAE (0.52) and edges it on BT (MCAR) MAE (0.46 vs 0.48) and WASS (0.11 vs 0.18), but NewImp recovers the lead on CC, IS, and QB under both scenarios. MIRACLE is competitive on CC (MCAR) WASS (0.35 vs 0.34) but degrades severely on IS and PK (WASS >30), while NewImp remains stable. Overall, NewImp is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-1 or top-2 performance across all 8 datasets in both MAR and MCAR settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion-model-based missing data imputation (CSDI, MissDiff) within the Wasserstein Gradient Flow framework, revealing that the FPK equation underlying DM sampling implicitly includes positive entropy terms that promote diversity—counterproductive for accuracy-oriented imputation. It draws on the SVGD/RKHS machinery [Liu & Wang, 2016] to derive a tractable velocity field, and extends the conditional-to-joint distribution equivalence established in sliced-Wasserstein flow work [Du et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023] to the novel setting of negative-entropy-regularized cost functionals. The key departure from prior DM-MDI work is replacing the diversity-encouraging positive entropy with a negative entropy regularization term and eliminating the data-masking requirement through joint distribution modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled WGF-based explanation for why diffusion models underperform on imputation despite their generative strength, and offers a clean fix (NER + joint modeling) that removes two practical pain points: tuning masking strategies and accepting diversity-induced inaccuracy. Limitations include evaluation on only eight small UCI tabular datasets under MAR/MCAR (no MNAR), a relatively simple two-layer MLP score network, and an iterative DSM-training-plus-ODE-simulation loop whose computational cost may limit scalability to high-dimensional or large-scale data. Open questions include whether the NER principle generalizes to other non-generative tasks where diversity is harmful, and whether the joint-distribution equivalence holds under more complex regularizers.
