## Problem

Vanilla Concept Bottleneck Models treat concepts as conditionally independent given the input, so a user intervention on one concept does not propagate to correlated concepts, leading to suboptimal adaptation of the target prediction. While autoregressive CBMs attempt to capture dependencies, they require sequential Monte Carlo sampling that makes inference 15× slower and forces independent (non-end-to-end) training of the concept and target predictors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241640/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (224×224 for CUB-200, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) or 1500-dimensional tabular features, and produces binary concept predictions (112, 143, or 892 concepts respectively), a target class label, and a learned C×C covariance matrix encoding concept dependencies. At intervention time, the user supplies a subset of corrected binary concept values, and the model conditions the remaining concept logits on these via the multivariate normal, producing updated concept probabilities and a revised target prediction without re-running the backbone.

## Method

SCBM replaces the point-estimate concept logits of a standard CBM with a learned multivariate normal distribution η|x ~ N(μ(x), Σ(x)), where a neural network predicts both the mean and a Cholesky-factored covariance (global or amortized per instance). Training minimizes a Monte Carlo-approximated negative log-likelihood using the reparameterization trick, with hard {0,1} Gumbel-Softmax bottleneck values to prevent leakage, and a Graphical-Lasso-style L1 penalty on the precision matrix to encourage sparse dependencies. The key intervention mechanism exploits the closed-form conditional of a multivariate normal: given intervened logits η'_S, the remaining logits η_\S are updated in one matrix operation, so a single user correction shifts all correlated concepts simultaneously. The intervention point η'_S is chosen by maximizing the log-likelihood of the corrected concepts subject to a χ² confidence-region constraint and a directional inequality, ensuring the shift is both well-calibrated and in the correct direction.

## Results

Pre-intervention, SCBMs perform on par with baselines: on CUB, Amortized SCBM achieves 95.22% concept accuracy and 69.87% target accuracy, comparable to CEM (95.12%/69.60%) and Autoregressive CBM (95.33%/69.24%). The key advantage emerges during interventions, where SCBMs show significantly steeper intervention curves in both concept and target accuracy, particularly for small numbers of interventions (1–10 concepts). SCBMs also retain 1× inference speed (matching Hard CBM and CEM), whereas the Autoregressive CBM requires 15× inference time. On CIFAR-10 with CLIP-inferred concepts (no human annotations), Amortized SCBM achieves 86.00% concept and 71.66% target accuracy, confirming the method's versatility without manual concept labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Hard CBM (Koh et al., 2020; Havasi et al., 2022), SCBMs match or slightly exceed pre-intervention accuracy but substantially outperform in intervention effectiveness, as a single intervention propagates to correlated concepts via the learned covariance. Against CEM (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022), SCBMs achieve comparable pre-intervention target accuracy (e.g., 69.87% vs. 69.60% on CUB) but show markedly steeper intervention curves, while CEM's expressive embeddings appear to suboptimally adapt to injected concept information. Against the Autoregressive CBM (Havasi et al., 2022), SCBMs match pre-intervention performance but win decisively on intervention effectiveness for small intervention budgets (1–10 concepts) and at 1× inference speed versus the autoregressive model's 15× overhead. The Autoregressive CBM only catches up to SCBMs when the full concept set is intervened on, a scenario the authors note is impractical. On CIFAR-10, CEM achieves the highest pre-intervention target accuracy (72.24%), which the authors attribute to potential information leakage in its embeddings, but SCBMs still dominate in intervention performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020) by replacing the implicit assumption of independent concepts with an explicit multivariate normal distribution over concept logits, drawing on the stochastic segmentation literature (Monteiro et al., 2020) where non-diagonal normals capture spatial pixel correlations. The closest prior work is the autoregressive CBM of Havasi et al. (2022), which also models concept dependencies but through a sequential structure that requires independent training and incurs 15× slower inference. SCBM departs from this by using a closed-form distributional parameterization that preserves end-to-end joint training and enables a principled, confidence-region-based intervention strategy not available in the autoregressive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that a single user intervention on one concept automatically propagates to all correlated concepts, substantially reducing the manual effort needed to correct model predictions while improving downstream accuracy. The method retains CBM-level training and inference speed, making it deployable in interactive settings where autoregressive alternatives are too slow. Key open questions include scaling the quadratic covariance matrix to very large concept sets (beyond the 892 concepts tested), extending beyond binary concepts to continuous domains, and incorporating user uncertainty since human interventions are not always ground truth. The reliance on CLIP-inferred concepts for CIFAR-10/100 also raises questions about how well the learned dependency structure reflects true semantic correlations versus artifacts of the CLIP embedding space.
