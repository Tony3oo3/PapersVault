## Problem

Determining the optimal mixture ratio between general-corpus and domain-corpus for domain-specific continual pre-training (D-CPT) of LLMs currently relies on expensive grid-searching over many ratios, incurring high GPU costs without guaranteeing the selected ratio is truly optimal. No principled, predictive framework existed to extrapolate the effect of mixture ratio on validation loss across model sizes, dataset sizes, and domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242866/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The law is fitted on small-scale empirical data points (models from 0.5B–4B, 0.1B–26B tokens, 9 mixture ratios) and then extrapolated to predict validation loss at unseen scales. The mixture ratio r is constrained to [0,1] with a small bias ε≈0.1 added to the denominator to prevent divergence near r=0. Beyond predicting L, the fitted law is inverted to solve constrained optimization problems: finding the optimal r_d under a general-ability degradation threshold T, the optimal r_d when domain-corpus volume D_d is fixed, and the compute-optimal (N, D) pair under a FLOPs budget.

## Method

The D-CPT Law extends Chinchilla Scaling Law by introducing the mixture ratio r as a third variable: L(N,D,r) = E + A/N^α + B·r^η/D^β + C/(r+ε)^γ. The key structural insight is that D and r are not independent (since D_domain = r_d · D), which motivates the coupled B·r^η/D^β term rather than treating them separately; this coupling is validated by the observed implicit trend ∂²L/∂D∂r < 0. Parameters are fitted via constrained L-BFGS with η>1 and C>C_0 enforced through reparameterization (η=1+exp(η₁), C=C_0+exp(c₁)) to guarantee monotonic decrease in r. For cross-domain transfer, a Domain-specific Learnable Coefficient K = w₁/k₁ + w₂·k₂ (where k₁ is initial validation loss and k₂ is the 5k-step loss decline) is added as a term F/K^μ, reducing the cost of fitting a new domain's law to roughly 1% of the original training budget.

## Results

The proposed D-CPT Law (L3 parameterization) achieves a mean Huber loss of 0.0048 (general) and 0.0157 (domain) with R² of 0.9968 and 0.9796 across six domains (Code, Math, Law, Music, Chemistry, Medical), outperforming all four alternative parameterizations. For the Cross-Domain D-CPT Law, the K3 representation (combining initial validation loss and rate of decline) achieves R² of 0.9918 on domain-specific loss and 0.9886 on general loss, with domain generalizability R² of 0.8611 when predicting two unseen domains from four training domains. The law generalizes well across model sizes (0.5B–4B, with accurate 7B prediction), dataset sizes (0.1B–26B tokens), and mixture ratios (0–1), with 3-fold cross-validation Huber losses below 0.005 (G) and 0.017 (D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external scaling-law baselines but instead evaluates five candidate parameterizations (L1–L5) of the D-CPT Law. L3 (the proposed form with B·r^η/D^β + C/r'^γ) dominates on both Huber loss and R² across all six domains, with the closest competitor L2 trailing by ~0.0002 in Huber loss (G) and ~0.001 in R² (D). L5, which has the fewest parameters (6), degrades substantially (Huber loss 0.0328/0.0438), confirming that the additional r-dependent terms are necessary. For the Cross-Domain setting, K3 (w1/k1 + w2×k2) outperforms K1 and K2 on domain-specific R² (0.9918 vs. 0.9462 and 0.8526) and K4 on domain generalizability (R² 0.8611 vs. 0.6763), while K4's marginal gain in domain-specific fitting (R² 0.9928) comes at the cost of an extra fitting parameter and lower accessibility. In generalizability tests (model size, dataset size, mixture ratio), L3 consistently achieves the best or near-best cross-validation scores, confirming its robustness beyond the fitting regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla Scaling Law (Hoffmann et al., 2022) by introducing the data mixture ratio r as a third first-class variable alongside model size N and dataset size D, specifically for domain-specific continual pre-training. It is conceptually adjacent to the data mixing scaling law of Ye et al. (2024), which optimizes data mixtures for general pre-training, but departs by targeting the trade-off between general and domain-specific validation losses under a fixed total dataset size. The Cross-Domain D-CPT Law further extends the framework by introducing a Domain-specific Learnable Coefficient (DLC) that captures per-domain learnability, enabling transfer of the fitted law to unseen domains with minimal additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The D-CPT Law provides a principled, low-cost alternative to the expensive grid-searching currently used to determine optimal general/domain corpus mixture ratios, with the Cross-Domain variant reducing per-domain fitting costs to roughly 1% of standard training. Key limitations include sensitivity of L-BFGS fitting to parameter initialization, validation on only the Qwen-1.5 model family and six domains, and an unresolved inflection point r_i below which the loss-vs-data-size trend reverses, making predictions in that regime meaningless. Open questions include whether the law generalizes across model architectures, multilingual settings, and whether a low-cost method can be developed to precisely locate r_i.
