## Problem

Deep learning surrogate models for PDE solution prediction have largely focused on time-domain problems, while steady-state frequency-domain vibration prediction—critical for noise reduction in mechanical structures—remains under-explored. No systematic benchmark exists for evaluating methods on this task, where highly variable resonance peaks across diverse geometries make prediction particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D beading-pattern image on a regular grid (121×81 or 181×121 discretization), scalar parameters (plate dimensions, thickness, damping loss factor, rotational boundary stiffness, loading position), and a continuous frequency query in [1, 300] Hz. The best variant (FQO-UNet) outputs a velocity field on a 40×60 grid at the queried frequency, which is then aggregated via the mean-squared-velocity formula to yield a scalar frequency response in dB. Because the frequency is a continuous query rather than a fixed grid index, the model can predict at any frequency, including those not seen during training.

## Method

The Frequency-Query Operator (FQO) decouples geometry encoding from frequency-specific prediction: an encoder Φ maps the beading-pattern image and scalar parameters to a feature representation, and a decoder Ψ takes that representation plus a frequency query to produce the output. The best-performing variant, FQO-UNet, uses a UNet encoder with self-attention and adaptive average pooling for global context, injects scalar parameters via FiLM conditioning before the last contraction block, and injects the frequency query via a second FiLM layer after the encoder. The UNet decoder then predicts the velocity field at the queried frequency (mapping to 5 fields per query to reduce memory), which is aggregated to the frequency response. This indirect prediction through the velocity field provides a richer training signal than directly regressing the scalar response, and the frequency-query formulation (operator learning paradigm) outperforms grid-based prediction of all 300 frequencies simultaneously. FiLM conditioning is the key mechanism for injecting both static parameters and the dynamic frequency query into the network.

## Results

On the V-5000 dataset, FQO-UNet achieves an MSE of 0.08, EMD of 4.24, peak error of 0.07, and peak frequency error of 1.7 Hz, outperforming all baselines by large margins. On the more diverse G-5000 dataset (with 7 additional varied parameters), FQO-UNet still leads with MSE 0.11, EMD 7.47, peak error 0.08, and peak frequency error 3.1 Hz. The frequency-query approach (FQO-UNet vs. Grid-UNet) consistently reduces MSE by ~2× and peak frequency error by ~37%, confirming that querying individual frequencies is more effective than predicting all 300 frequencies at once.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FQO-UNet outperforms DeepONet by a wide margin on both datasets: on V-5000, MSE drops from 0.49 to 0.08 (6× improvement) and peak frequency error from 5.4 Hz to 1.7 Hz; on G-5000, MSE improves from 0.44 to 0.11. Against the two FNO variants, FQO-UNet achieves roughly 4–6× lower MSE (0.08 vs. 0.42 for RN18+FNO and 0.47 for FNO velocity field on V-5000) and substantially lower peak frequency error (1.7 Hz vs. 5.6 and 6.3 Hz). The paper attributes DeepONet's weaker performance to its single weighted-summation mechanism for incorporating frequency information, which limits expressivity compared to FQO's FiLM-based frequency conditioning. Among the paper's own ablations, the frequency-query approach (FQO-UNet vs. Grid-UNet) yields ~2× lower MSE and ~37% lower peak frequency error, while predicting the velocity field before aggregating to the frequency response (FQO-UNet vs. FQO-RN18) provides the largest single architectural gain, reducing MSE from 0.32 to 0.08 on V-5000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of operator learning (DeepONet, Fourier Neural Operators) and implicit/coordinate-based models (NeRF, occupancy networks), repurposing the frequency-query paradigm from both to predict steady-state vibration patterns in the frequency domain. It departs from the dominant time-domain PDE prediction literature by treating excitation frequency as a continuous query rather than a temporal dimension, and from prior surrogate-model work in acoustics (which has focused on calibration or generative design) by introducing a systematic prediction benchmark with 12,000 FEM-solved plate instances. The architecture combines standard image encoders (ResNet, UNet, ViT) with FiLM conditioning for scalar parameters and an MLP or UNet decoder, with the key architectural novelty being the frequency-query mechanism applied to a UNet that predicts velocity fields as an intermediate representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark for frequency-domain vibration prediction, providing a reproducible testbed (12,000 FEM-solved plates with varied beadings, materials, and boundary conditions) and three complementary metrics (MSE, EMD, peak frequency error) that the field lacked. The 4–6 order-of-magnitude speedup over FEM makes iterative design optimization (e.g., beading placement via guided diffusion) practically feasible for the first time. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D aluminum plates with simple boundary conditions, and the need for large pre-generated FEM datasets; open questions include extending to 3D curved shells, multi-component assemblies, and more complex material models where FEM costs are prohibitive.
