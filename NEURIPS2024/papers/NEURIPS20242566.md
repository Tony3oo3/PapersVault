## Problem

Existing factuality benchmarks either test short-answer factoids (TruthfulQA, HaluEval, FreshQA) or long-form responses on a single narrow topic (FActScore covers only biographies), leaving no comprehensive prompt set for evaluating long-form factuality across diverse domains. Additionally, evaluating long-form responses at the individual-fact level has relied on expensive crowdsourced human annotators, creating a scalability bottleneck for benchmarking.

## Input / Output

SAFE takes a fact-seeking prompt and a long-form model response (up to 1,024 tokens) and decomposes the response into individual atomic facts (finer than sentence-level granularity), revises each to be self-contained, and classifies each as supported, not-supported, or irrelevant. The final output is a triple of counts (S, NS, I) plus an aggregated F1@K score, where K is a hyperparameter representing the human-preferred number of supported facts (set to 64 or 178 in experiments). Irrelevant facts are excluded from the F1@K computation, as they measure instruction-following rather than factuality.

## Method

SAFE (Search-Augmented Factuality Evaluator) uses an LLM agent (GPT-3.5-Turbo) in a multi-step pipeline: it splits each sentence into atomic facts, decontextualizes them to be self-contained, determines relevance to the prompt, and then for each relevant fact iteratively issues up to five Google Search queries (via Serper API, keeping 3 results per query) while reasoning about whether accumulated search results support the fact. The key innovation is treating the LLM as an agent that generates adaptive, multi-step search queries informed by previously retrieved results, rather than relying on a fixed reference document. For aggregation, the paper introduces F1@K, which balances factual precision (S/(S+NS)) with a recall term min(S/K, 1) where K proxies the user's preferred response length, yielding a single score in [0,1] that rewards both accuracy and coverage without requiring ground-truth answers.

## Results

On LongFact-Objects (250 prompts), GPT-4-Turbo achieves the highest F1@64 of 95.0 and F1@178 of 66.4, followed by Gemini-Ultra (91.7 / 60.3) and PaLM-2-L-IT-RLHF (91.0 / 55.3). SAFE agrees with crowdsourced human annotators on 72.0% of ~16,000 individual facts, and on 100 randomly-sampled disagreement cases, SAFE is correct 76% of the time versus 19% for humans. SAFE costs $0.19 per model response compared to $4.00 for human annotators, making it over 20× cheaper. Including recall (F1@64) yields a statistically significant correlation (r=0.754, p=0.031) with LMSys Chatbot Arena ELO scores, whereas precision alone does not (r=0.502, p=0.205).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is between SAFE and crowdsourced human annotators from FActScore (Min et al., 2023). On 16,011 individual facts, SAFE agrees with human annotations 72.0% of the time. On a random subset of 100 disagreement cases re-annotated by researchers with full internet access, SAFE is correct 76% of the time while human annotators are correct only 19%—a 4-to-1 win ratio for SAFE. In cost, SAFE ($0.19 per response using GPT-3.5-Turbo + Serper API) is over 20× cheaper than the $4.00 per response reported for human annotations. Among the thirteen benchmarked models, GPT-4-Turbo outperforms all others on F1@64 (95.0) and F1@178 (66.4), with Gemini-Ultra and PaLM-2-L-IT-RLHF in second and third place. Notably, Claude-3-Sonnet matches Claude-3-Opus on F1@64 (89.4 vs 89.3) despite being a smaller model, and Claude-3-Haiku achieves the highest precision (92.8%) but the lowest recall, suggesting it prioritizes brevity over coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FActScore (Min et al., 2023), which introduced fine-grained atomic fact evaluation for long-form biographical text, in three key ways: broadening from a single topic to 38 domains (LongFact), replacing Wikipedia-anchored human annotation with a search-augmented LLM agent (SAFE), and adding a recall component via the F1@K metric. It also draws on the broader LLM-as-judge and tool-augmented verification literature (FacTool, RARR, RAGAS) but is the first to combine multi-step Google Search queries with iterative reasoning for long-form factuality at scale. The short-answer factuality benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HaluEval, FreshQA) are positioned as complementary but insufficient for evaluating multi-paragraph responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFE's demonstrated ability to outperform crowdsourced human annotators at 20× lower cost could fundamentally change how the field scales long-form factuality evaluation, removing a major bottleneck in LLM research. The F1@K metric addresses a recognized gap (FActScore explicitly flagged recall as future work) and its correlation with Chatbot Arena ELO scores provides empirical grounding for the recall component. Key open questions include whether SAFE generalizes to expert domains (law, medicine) where Google Search may lack depth, whether the 'supported by search' framing adequately proxies global factuality, and how to distinguish factuality errors from hallucinations (errors relative to a model's own internal knowledge).
