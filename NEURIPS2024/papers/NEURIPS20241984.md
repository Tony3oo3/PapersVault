## Problem

Standard agent-state based Q-learning (ASQL) for POMDPs learns stationary policies, but agent states such as frame stacking or RNN hidden states generally do not satisfy the Markov property, so non-stationary policies can strictly outperform stationary ones. The paper identifies this optimality gap and asks how to learn non-stationary policies in a tractable, finite-parameter way within the off-policy Q-learning framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241984/cropped_page15_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives discrete observations y_t and maintains a recursively updated agent state z_t = φ(z_{t-1}, y_t, a_{t-1}) (e.g., a sliding window of past observations or an RNN hidden state). The algorithm outputs an L-tuple of Q-functions (Q^0, …, Q^{L-1}) and a corresponding periodic policy π = (π^0, …, π^{L-1}) where each π^ℓ: Z → A maps agent states to actions at time steps t ≡ ℓ (mod L). The behavior policy μ used during learning must itself be periodic with the same period L, and the converged policy depends on this choice of μ.

## Method

PASQL maintains L separate Q-functions, where the ℓ-th component is updated only at time steps t with t mod L = ℓ, bootstrapping from the next component Q^{[ℓ+1]} in the cycle. The key theoretical contribution is proving almost-sure convergence to a cyclic limit that is the unique fixed point of a periodic MDP dynamic program, where the periodic rewards and transition kernels are derived from the cyclic limiting distribution of the joint process (S_t, Y_t, Z_t, A_t) under the periodic behavior policy. Because the agent state is non-Markovian, the converged Q-function and greedy policy depend on the behavioral policy μ — a fundamental departure from standard MDP Q-learning where the limit is policy-independent. The sub-optimality gap relative to the optimal history-dependent policy is bounded using integral probability metrics, quantifying how well the agent state approximates the true environment state. The proof combines stochastic approximation with Markov noise and time-periodic Markov chain theory to handle the non-Markovian dynamics of the agent state.

## Results

On the POMDP example (6 states, 2 actions, 2 observations, γ=0.9), PASQL with period L=2 and a well-chosen behavioral policy μ₁ achieves a performance of 6.793, matching the optimal periodic policy J₂* = 6.793. In contrast, the best stationary policy from ASQL (L=1) achieves only J₁* = 2.633, a 2.6× gap. The convergence of PASQL iterates to the theoretical limit predicted by Theorem 1 is confirmed across 25 random seeds for three different behavioral policies. The choice of behavioral policy is critical: with μ₁, PASQL is optimal; with μ₂ or μ₃, performance drops to 1.064 and 0.532 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ASQL (agent-state Q-learning with L=1), which learns a stationary policy. PASQL with L=2 and a good behavioral policy (μ₁) achieves 6.793, substantially outperforming the best ASQL result of 2.633 (a 2.6× improvement), confirming that non-stationary periodic policies can exploit the non-Markovian structure of the agent state. However, the comparison is nuanced: with a poor behavioral policy (μ₃), PASQL achieves only 0.532, which is worse than ASQL with a nominal policy (μ̄₂) at 1.064. This highlights that periodicity is not a free lunch—the behavioral policy must be well-chosen. The paper also notes that the limiting Q-function and resulting greedy policy depend on the behavioral policy (a fundamental consequence of the agent state not being an information state), so the same PASQL algorithm can converge to very different policies depending on exploration strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends agent-state Q-learning (ASQL) from [KY22] and [Sey+23], which established convergence of tabular Q-learning in POMDPs under stationary policies, by introducing periodicity into both the behavioral policy and the Q-function update. The periodic MDP dynamic program that PASQL converges to draws on the classical framework of [Rii65] and [Sch16], while the convergence proof adapts stochastic approximation with Markov noise (generalizing [KY22; Sey+23] from aperiodic to periodic chains). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the recognition that the non-Markovian nature of agent states (e.g., frame stacking, RNNs) makes the standard stationary-policy restriction of Q-learning suboptimal, motivating a periodic policy class as a finite-parameter middle ground between stationary and fully non-stationary policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental and previously underappreciated limitation of standard Q-learning in POMDPs: when the agent state is not an information state, the optimal policy is non-stationary, yet all existing convergence results (ASQL, recurrent Q-learning) are restricted to stationary policies. The sub-optimality gap bound (Thm. 2) provides the first characterization of how far PASQL's periodic policy can be from the optimal history-dependent policy. However, significant open questions remain: the analysis is restricted to tabular settings with a fixed behavioral policy (excluding ε-greedy, SARSA, and function approximation), the choice of behavioral policy is critical and poorly understood (a bad choice can make PASQL worse than ASQL), and the period L is a hyperparameter with non-monotonic effects on performance. The practical impact is currently limited to small-scale experiments, and extending the theory to the deep RL settings where agent states are actually used (LSTM/GRU-based) is an important future direction.
