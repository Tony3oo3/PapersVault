## Problem

Existing Large Vision-Language Models are limited to approximately 1500×1500 pixels and operate within a narrow or fixed set of resolution settings, making them inadequate for real-world high-resolution content such as document pages, website screenshots, and blueprints. The Vision Transformer architecture further struggles with varying aspect ratios, and prior patch-division methods either use a few predefined high-resolution configurations or a limited resolution range, restricting practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241348/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images of any aspect ratio from 336 pixels up to 4K HD (3840×1600) paired with a text prompt, and produces a free-form text response. During inference, the image is dynamically partitioned into a grid of 336×336 patches whose count and layout are automatically determined by the image's aspect ratio and a maximum patch budget (e.g., HD-25 allows up to 25 patches, HD-55 up to 55). Each image is additionally downsampled to a fixed 336×336 global view, so the LLM receives both a macro and a micro representation concatenated with a separator token.

## Method

InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD introduces a dynamic patch configuration strategy that resizes and pads each input image to a grid of 336×336 patches while preserving the original aspect ratio, with the number of patches automatically determined by a constraint p_w × p_h ≤ H. To help the LLM parse the variable 2D patch layout, a learnable newline token is inserted at the end of each row of patch features before flattening, and a global 336×336 view is prepended to provide macro context. The architecture pairs a frozen-then-fine-tuned CLIP ViT-L-14-336 encoder with InternLM2-7B via Partial LoRA (rank 256), and reduces token count by concatenating 4×4 neighboring tokens along the channel dimension. Supervised fine-tuning uses a mixed-resolution schedule: HD-55 (up to 4K) for OCR-heavy tasks and HD-25 for general perception tasks, with dynamic resolution sampling during training to build robustness to resolution variation at inference time.

## Results

IXC2-4KHD (8B parameters) achieves 90.0 on DocVQA, 81.0 on ChartQA, 68.6 on InfographicVQA, 77.2 on TextVQA, and 67.5 on OCRBench, surpassing both GPT-4V and Gemini Pro on DocVQA (+1.6 over GPT-4V) and ChartQA (+2.5 over GPT-4V). On general-purpose benchmarks, it scores 57.8 on MathVista, 80.9 on AI2D, 2204.9 on MME, and 80.2 on MMBench-EN, outperforming both closed-source APIs on these tasks. The model matches or surpasses GPT-4V and Gemini Pro in 10 of 16 benchmarks while remaining competitive on the remaining 6, all with only 7B LLM parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4V, IXC2-4KHD wins on DocVQA (90.0 vs 88.4), ChartQA (81.0 vs 78.5), MathVista (57.8 vs 47.8), AI2D (80.9 vs 75.5), MME (2204.9 vs 1926.5), MMBench-EN (80.2 vs 77.0), MMBench-CN (77.7 vs 74.4), and SEED-Bench (74.7 vs 69.1), but trails on InfoVQA (68.6 vs 75.1), TextVQA (77.2 vs 78.0), MMMU (39.7 vs 56.8), QBench (71.8 vs 74.1), MM-Vet (54.9 vs 56.8), and HallusionBench (40.9 vs 46.5). Against Gemini Pro, it wins on DocVQA, ChartQA, MathVista, AI2D, MME, MMBench-EN, MMBench-CN, SEED-Bench, TextVQA (77.2 vs 74.6), and QBench (71.8 vs 70.6), but loses on InfoVQA (68.6 vs 75.2), OCRBench (67.5 vs 68.0), MMMU (39.7 vs 47.9), and MM-Vet (54.9 vs 59.2). Compared to the previous open-source SOTA (a mix of DocOwl 1.5, CogAgent, LLaVA-Next, InternVL, and others), IXC2-4KHD achieves the largest margins on OCR tasks: +7.8 on DocVQA, +10.8 on ChartQA, and +17.9 on InfographicVQA. Against its own predecessor IXC2-VL, the 4KHD variant gains +32.3 on DocVQA, +8.4 on ChartQA, and +34.2 on InfographicVQA, confirming the critical role of high-resolution training for text-dense content. The model's weakest areas relative to closed-source APIs remain MMMU (39.7 vs 56.8 for GPT-4V) and HallusionBench (40.9 vs 46.5), suggesting room for improvement in multi-discipline reasoning and hallucination resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the patch-division paradigm established by Monkey and OtterHD, which segment high-resolution images into fixed-size patches processed by a standard ViT, by introducing dynamic resolution with automatic patch configuration that preserves aspect ratios and scales from 336 pixels to 4K HD. It builds directly on the InternLM-XComposer2 architecture (ViT-L/14 + InternLM2-7B + Partial LoRA) and departs from prior high-resolution LVLMs (CogAgent, Vary, Mini-Gemini) that rely on separate HR/LR encoders or fixed resolution settings, offering a simpler single-encoder solution. The global-local format and learnable newline token for 2D structure delineation are novel components not present in prior patch-based methods, addressing the specific challenge of variable patch layouts that confuses the LLM at high resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first LVLM to handle 4K HD resolution, and the finding that OCR-task performance has not saturated even at 4K HD opens a clear scaling trajectory for future work, though computational constraints prevent exploring the upper bound. The practical impact is significant for document, chart, and infographic understanding where dense text is easily lost at lower resolutions, and the dynamic patch configuration plus newline token are lightweight, broadly applicable techniques likely to be adopted by other LVLM pipelines. Open questions include whether the resolution scaling law eventually plateaus, how to make inference at very high token counts more efficient, and whether the observed benefit of inferring above training resolution generalizes beyond text-centric tasks.
