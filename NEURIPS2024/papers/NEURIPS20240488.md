## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods rely on multiple separate diffusion models (3D-aware, 2D image, 2D video) to achieve spatial consistency, appearance quality, and temporal dynamics, but these models lack unified multi-view spatial-temporal modeling and produce conflicting gradients that cause flickering and inconsistent temporal appearance. No prior method provides a single diffusion model that simultaneously captures multi-view spatial and temporal correlations for 4D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240488/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames (plus an optional text prompt) and produces a 4D scene parameterized by a hash-encoded dynamic NeRF (spatial grid P_xyz + spatial-time planes P_xyzt) that can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints across the full temporal dimension. The intermediate 4DM model generates four orthogonal-viewpoint videos of 8 frames at 256×256 resolution; when the input exceeds 8 frames, 4DM is applied multiple times with overlapping windows to produce anchor videos. Rendering uses NerfAcc with a shared occupancy grid and fixed camera distance of 1.1.

## Method

The core contribution is 4DM, a unified multi-view video diffusion model built by inserting a zero-initialized motion module (group norm → linear → two self-attention blocks → feed-forward → linear + residual) at the end of each UViT block of the frozen ImageDream 3D-aware diffusion model. The motion module performs self-attention exclusively along the temporal axis by reshaping spatial and viewpoint dimensions into the batch dimension, while the spatial 3D self-attention module operates across viewpoints per frame. 4DM is trained on only 926 curated high-quality animated 3D shapes from Objaverse (rendered as 32-view videos at 0° elevation), preserving ImageDream's generalization and spatial consistency. For 4D generation, a 4D-aware SDS loss (x0-reconstruction variant) distills 4DM's multi-view spatial-temporal guidance into the dynamic NeRF, and an anchor loss (LPIPS + D-SSIM) against a 4DM-generated video at the viewpoint closest to the input enhances appearance details and stabilizes optimization.

## Results

On the 4D generation task (evaluated on 5 Objaverse test cases), 4Diffusion achieves the best scores across all five metrics: CLIP-I 0.9310, CLIP-C 0.9798, FVD 417.63, LPIPS 0.1199, and PSNR 19.07. The FVD improvement is particularly notable, reducing the Fréchet Video Distance by 41% over the closest baseline (Consistent4D, 706.07) and by 60% over 4D-fy (1042.3). On multi-view video generation (Table 2), 4DM outperforms ImageDream on CLIP-C (0.9601 vs 0.9320), FVD (427.34 vs 465.94), LPIPS (0.1346 vs 0.1536), and PSNR (17.88 vs 16.57), while maintaining comparable image quality (CLIP-I 0.9260 vs 0.9165).

## Baselines & Comparisons

4Diffusion outperforms all three baselines on every metric in the 4D generation comparison. Against the closest competitor, Consistent4D [20], 4Diffusion improves FVD by 41% (417.63 vs 706.07), LPIPS by 25% (0.1199 vs 0.1593), and PSNR by 2.37 dB (19.07 vs 16.70), while also leading on CLIP-I (+0.009) and CLIP-C (+0.008). DreamGaussian4D [42] suffers from blurred appearance and inaccurate geometry due to the explicit nature of Gaussian Splatting, yielding worse scores than Consistent4D on most metrics. 4D-fy [4], extended to video-prompt 4D generation using ImageDream as its 3D-aware model, shows the weakest performance overall (FVD 1042.3, PSNR 14.24), primarily because integrating gradients from multiple heterogeneous diffusion models leads to inconsistent temporal appearance and flickers. Ablation rows confirm that both the 4D-aware SDS loss and the anchor loss are essential: removing either degrades FVD by 73% and 105% respectively, and removing the anchor loss reduces PSNR by 1.87 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AnimateDiff paradigm of inserting a zero-initialized motion module into a frozen diffusion model, but applies it to a 3D-aware multi-view diffusion model (ImageDream) rather than a text-to-image model, thereby unifying spatial and temporal modeling in a single architecture. It departs from prior 4D generation methods (MAV3D, 4D-fy, Dream-in-4D) that combine multiple separate diffusion models (3D-aware, 2D image, 2D video) via hybrid SDS, arguing that this multi-model integration causes temporal flicker and spatial inconsistency. The dynamic NeRF representation with hash-encoded spatial and space-time planes follows 4D-fy and iNGP, while the SDS-based optimization framework inherits from DreamFusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single unified multi-view video diffusion model can provide coherent spatial-temporal guidance for 4D generation, eliminating the integration challenges of multi-model pipelines. A notable practical finding is that only 926 curated multi-view videos suffice to learn temporal dynamics while preserving the base model's spatial consistency and generalization, reducing the data barrier for 4D-aware video diffusion. Key limitations include the 8-frame generation window (requiring multiple stochastic passes for longer videos), reliance on slow volumetric rendering, and dependence on the base model's quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer temporal sequences, whether faster representations (e.g., 4D Gaussian Splatting) can replace dynamic NeRF without sacrificing quality, and whether the curated-data strategy generalizes beyond object-centric scenes.
