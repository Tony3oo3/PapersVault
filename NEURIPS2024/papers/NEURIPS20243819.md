## Problem

Accuracy-on-the-line (ACL) and agreement-on-the-line (AGL) provide powerful label-free tools for OOD performance estimation and model selection, but these linear trends break catastrophically for certain distribution shifts (e.g., CIFAR10-C Gaussian Noise, Camelyon17-WILDS), rendering ID accuracy unreliable for predicting OOD performance. No prior work has investigated how to actively restore these linear trends in models, leaving a critical gap in reliable OOD evaluation under complex shifts.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained model (CNN or ViT), a stream of unlabeled OOD minibatches for online TTA, and a labeled ID dataset for computing accuracy and agreement rates. During TTA, the model is updated at each minibatch and its ID/OOD predictions are collected online, so accuracy and agreement are measured on a continuously evolving model rather than a fixed one. The outputs are (i) a precise OOD accuracy estimate via ALine (requiring no OOD labels), (ii) the best TTA hyperparameter configuration selected by highest ID accuracy, and (iii) the best TTA method chosen by comparing ALine-estimated OOD performances across methods.

## Method

The key finding is that TTA (e.g., TENT, BN_Adapt, SHOT) collapses complex distribution shifts into a singular scaling of the class-mean direction and covariance shape in the penultimate feature space, with cosine similarities rising from ~0.7 to ~0.99 after adaptation. This empirically satisfies the theoretical sufficient conditions from Miller et al. for perfect ACL in linear-Gaussian models, where only the scale of means and covariances changes while their directions are preserved. The paper then builds a practical system: ALine estimates OOD accuracy from ID accuracy and agreement rates (reducing estimation error from ~11% to ~2% on CIFAR10-C Gaussian Noise), and the strong post-TTA linear trends enable unsupervised hyperparameter selection by simply picking the model with best ID accuracy. Ablations show that BN-layer adaptation is particularly effective at aligning shifts, while methods that only adjust the final classifier (e.g., T3A) or use LayerNorm instead of BN yield weaker alignment and weaker linear trends.

## Results

After applying TTA (e.g., TENT, SHOT, ETA), ALine-based OOD accuracy estimation achieves dramatically lower MAE than Vanilla models: on CIFAR10-C Gaussian Noise, MAE drops from 10.76% (Vanilla ALine-D) to 0.77% (TENT ALine-D), and on Camelyon17-WILDS from 13.46% to 1.42% (ETA ALine-D). For unsupervised TTA hyperparameter validation, selecting the model with best ID accuracy (leveraging restored ACL) achieves 0.36% average MAE on CIFAR10-C and 0.62% on Camelyon17-WILDS, compared to 4.23% and 7.98% for the next-best baseline (MixVal), respectively. The method also outperforms all confidence-based estimators (ATC, DOC-feat, AC) and the Agreement baseline across nearly all tested shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For OOD accuracy estimation, ALine applied after TTA consistently outperforms all four confidence-based baselines (ATC, DOC-feat, AC) and the Agreement baseline. On CIFAR10-C, ALine-D with TENT achieves 0.77% MAE versus 4.25% (ATC), 4.65% (DOC-feat), 7.66% (AC), and 1.79% (Agreement). On Camelyon17-WILDS, where Vanilla ALine-D fails catastrophically (13.46% MAE), ETA+ALine-D achieves 1.42%, beating ATC (3.05%), DOC-feat (4.38%), AC (6.85%), and Agreement (5.33%). For unsupervised TTA hyperparameter validation, the paper's method (selecting best-ID-accuracy model) achieves 0.36% average MAE on CIFAR10-C, outperforming MixVal (4.23%), ENT (2.65%), IM (0.85%), Corr-C (14.43%), and SND (2.75%). The advantage is most dramatic on Camelyon17-WILDS where the method achieves 0.62% MAE while MixVal (7.98%), IM (23.52%), Corr-C (37.70%), and SND (37.70%) all fail catastrophically. On ImageNet-C, IM (1.05%) slightly outperforms the proposed method (4.0%), but the proposed method still beats MixVal (8.77%), ENT (16.42%), Corr-C (14.43%), and SND (22.97%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the accuracy-on-the-line (ACL) and agreement-on-the-line (AGL) phenomena characterized by Miller et al. [32] and Baek et al. [3], and the growing family of test-time adaptation (TTA) methods (TENT, SHOT, BN_Adapt, ETA, etc.). While prior work established theoretical conditions under which ACL holds (simple scaling of mean/covariance in Gaussian data) and TTA work focused on improving OOD accuracy, no prior study connected the two. The paper's key conceptual move is showing that TTA empirically enforces exactly the theoretical conditions from Miller et al. in the penultimate feature space, collapsing complex shifts into a singular scaling variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding provides a principled, label-free strategy for selecting TTA hyperparameters and the best TTA method, addressing a critical practical gap where practitioners currently rely on held-out OOD labels or ad-hoc heuristics. The geometric explanation (TTA aligns mean/covariance directions in feature space) offers new insight into why TTA works beyond just improving accuracy. Key limitations include the requirement for sufficient ID data to estimate agreement rates, and the observation that non-BN architectures (e.g., ViTs with LayerNorm) show weaker alignment, leaving open how to achieve strong ACL/AGL in settings without batch normalization or with minimal ID access.
