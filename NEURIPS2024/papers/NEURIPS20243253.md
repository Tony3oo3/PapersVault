## Problem

Current unsupervised object detection methods (SAM, CutLER, Slot Attention) are entirely empirical, offering no guarantees about whether their learned latent variables actually correspond to true object positions. This paper addresses that gap by presenting the first unsupervised object detection method with a provable theorem bounding the maximum position error of learned object coordinates in terms of architectural and data parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243253/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images or video frames (80×80 synthetic, CLEVR 3D scenes, or real video) as input and produces per-object 2D position coordinates along with a reconstructed image. A key requirement is that each object must appear at at least two different positions across the dataset (e.g., moving in a video or appearing at multiple locations in a set of images), and no two objects in any single image may be identical. The latent variables are ordered, so the network learns to associate each coordinate pair with a specific object's semantics.

## Method

The architecture is an autoencoder made exactly translationally equivariant: the encoder is a CNN followed by a soft argmax that extracts 2D position coordinates, and the decoder renders Gaussians at those positions (concatenated with positional encodings or a nearby video frame for background) before passing them through a CNN to reconstruct the image. Because both the soft argmax and the Gaussian rendering are equivariant to translations, a shift in the input image produces a proportional shift in the latent variables and vice versa. Training is purely unsupervised, minimising only the reconstruction loss. The equivariance property enables a theorem proving that learned positions deviate from ground truth by at most min(s_ψ/2 + s_o/2 − 1, s_φ/2 − s_o/2 + Δ_G), where s_ψ and s_φ are encoder/decoder receptive field sizes, s_o is object size, and Δ_G ~ N(0, σ_G²) captures the Gaussian rendering width. Corollaries decompose this bound to show how each factor individually governs the error, providing concrete design guidelines for network architecture.

## Results

On CLEVR-based data with three objects of varying sizes (6–27 px), the proposed method achieves 100% of position errors within 2 standard deviations of its theoretical bound across all object sizes (9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24 px). In contrast, CutLER achieves 78.4% overall (dropping to 37.5% at 24 px) and SAM achieves only 43.5% overall (dropping to 0% at 21 px). Synthetic experiments validate the theoretical error bounds up to individual-pixel precision, with all 20 random initializations per configuration lying at or below the predicted maximum error for encoder RF, decoder RF, object size, and Gaussian width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art unsupervised object detection methods: SAM (Segment Anything, ICCV 2023) and CutLER (CVPR 2023). The proposed method is the only one whose position errors are always guaranteed to fall within its theoretical bound, achieving 100% compliance across all object sizes. CutLER performs reasonably for small objects (100% at 9 and 12 px) but degrades sharply for larger objects, dropping to 57.1% at 18 px and 37.5% at 24 px. SAM is the weakest baseline overall (43.5% average), with particularly poor performance on larger objects (0% at 21 px, 16.7% at 24 px). The key distinction is not raw accuracy but the *guarantee*: while SAM and CutLER may achieve low errors in practice, their errors are unbounded and can exceed any theoretical limit, whereas the proposed method's errors are provably bounded by a function of receptive field sizes, object sizes, and Gaussian width.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoencoder framework for unsupervised representation learning and the well-known translation equivariance of convolutional layers, but departs from prior unsupervised object detection methods (CutLER, Slot Attention, MoNet) by enforcing exact translational equivariance through a specific architectural choice: a soft-argmax encoder and Gaussian-rendering decoder. It also connects to the identifiability literature in representation learning, but achieves identifiability through equivariance constraints and an interpretable latent space structure rather than through distributional assumptions, interventions, or auxiliary variables. The decoder conditioning on nearby video frames follows Jakab et al. (2018), and the overall positioning is as the first unsupervised object detection method with provable position error bounds, in contrast to the empirical, guarantee-free nature of all prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new research direction toward object detection methods with quantifiable theoretical guarantees, providing concrete design guidelines (encoder/decoder RF sizes, Gaussian widths) that practitioners can use to bound position errors a priori. However, the method's applicability is currently narrow: it only detects dynamic objects (moving in video or appearing at multiple positions), requires successful image reconstruction as a precondition for the guarantees to hold, and has been validated primarily on synthetic and CLEVR-style data with simple object shapes. Open questions include whether the equivariance-based guarantee framework can be extended to handle occlusion, non-rigid objects, or more complex real-world scenes, and whether the reconstruction-success assumption can be relaxed or made more robust.
