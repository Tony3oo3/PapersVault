## Problem

Current comparative studies for tabular data models rely on overly standardized preprocessing and lack external performance references, making them biased toward model selection while ignoring the dataset-specific feature engineering that dominates real-world pipelines. This model-centric evaluation setup fails to reflect practitioner practice and cannot measure true state-of-the-art performance on a task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243038/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 10 real-world Kaggle tabular datasets (ranging from ~4K to ~600K samples, 9–4992 raw features, with high-cardinality categoricals up to 18,210 clusters) and, for the test-time adaptation pipeline, also consumes unlabeled test features. Models produce class labels or continuous predictions that are submitted to Kaggle's hidden test sets, and the evaluation metric is the private leaderboard percentile (0–1), enabling cross-dataset comparison despite heterogeneous competition metrics (AUC, logloss, r², rmsle, gini).

## Method

The paper proposes a data-centric evaluation framework built on three pillars: (1) ten carefully selected Kaggle competitions with implemented expert-level solutions serving as external performance references, (2) three model-agnostic preprocessing pipelines—standardized (mean imputation, log-transform), expert feature engineering (dataset-specific groupby interactions, categorical interactions, frequency encoding, dimensionality reduction), and test-time feature engineering (using unlabeled test data for the same operations), and (3) a unified modeling pipeline evaluating seven architectures (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, ResNet, FTTransformer, MLP-PLR, GRANDE) plus AutoGluon under three HPO regimes (default, 20-trial random search, 100-trial TPE). The key empirical finding is that after dataset-specific feature engineering, model rankings shift substantially, performance gaps between top models shrink, and feature engineering contributes more to performance than model selection or HPO, revealing that CatBoost's apparent superiority in standardized benchmarks is largely attributable to its internal categorical feature engineering rather than architectural advantage.

## Results

Across 10 Kaggle datasets, the paper reports private leaderboard percentile (higher = better) as the unified metric. Under standardized preprocessing with extensive HPO, CatBoost achieves top-3 leaderboard positions on 3 of 10 datasets (MBGM: 0.999, BPCCM: 0.986, HQC: 0.991), while no single model dominates the remaining 7. After applying expert feature engineering, performance gaps shrink dramatically: on BPCCM, all tree-based models reach ≥0.99, and on SVPC, all models reach ≥0.94. Test-time adaptation (TTA) provides additional gains on 6 datasets, with the largest jump on SCTP (0.518 → 0.992 for the best single model) and PSSDP (0.656 → 0.995).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CatBoost dominates under standardized preprocessing, reaching top-3 leaderboard positions on MBGM (0.999), BPCCM (0.986), and HQC (0.991), but this advantage largely vanishes with expert feature engineering because CatBoost's internal categorical encodings (target statistics, combinatorial encoding) replicate what other models need manual FE for; XGBoost then performs similarly to CatBoost on average. FTTransformer is the clear winner on SCS (0.945 vs. 0.692 for XGBoost) but underperforms tree-based models on most other datasets. Among neural networks, MLP-PLR and FTTransformer reach top ranks on SCTP and PSSDP only after expert FE + TTA, while the ResNet baseline (0.298 on SCTP, 0.308 on PSSDP) remains far behind. AutoGluon achieves top-3 leaderboard positions on BPCCM, HQC, and OGPCC under standardized preprocessing, matching or exceeding single models, but is outperformed by single models with expert FE on several datasets (e.g., SCTP: AutoGluon 0.531 vs. best single model 0.962 with FE). The key finding is that no model consistently wins across all datasets and pipelines; instead, the choice of preprocessing pipeline (standardized vs. expert FE vs. TTA) shifts rankings more than model selection does, with Spearman correlations between standardized and expert-FE rankings being relatively low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the model-centric evaluation paradigm dominant in tabular data research (OpenML benchmarks, AMLB, and comparative studies like Grinsztajn et al. 2022 and McElfresh et al. 2023), which apply a single standardized preprocessing pipeline across all datasets. It repurposes the Kaggle competition ecosystem—previously used only for retrospective leaderboard comparisons (Erickson et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2023)—into a structured evaluation framework with implemented expert-level preprocessing pipelines and test-time adaptation as explicit modeling components. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between AutoML-style model benchmarking and the dataset-specific pipelines practitioners actually deploy, positioning feature engineering and temporal adaptation as first-class evaluation variables rather than confounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that model rankings shift substantially and performance gaps shrink after dataset-specific feature engineering directly challenges the field's assumption that architecture selection is a primary driver of tabular data performance, redirecting research attention toward data-centric techniques. The identification of temporal characteristics in many 'static' datasets and the quantification of test-time adaptation gains open an underexplored direction for handling distribution shift in tabular settings. Limitations include the absence of error bars (due to >200K trained models), a finance-heavy dataset collection, and reliance on a single hidden test set per dataset, which constrains generalizability and statistical confidence in small inter-model differences.
