## Problem

Random projections reduce LP dimensionality but ignore structure present in repeated, similar instances. When LPs arise repeatedly from a common distribution (e.g., daily production planning), can projection matrices learned from past instances yield substantially better solution quality than random ones? The paper formalizes this as a statistical learning problem and asks how many training instances suffice to guarantee that the learned projection generalizes to future instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240408/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training requires N LP instances in inequality form (c, A, b) satisfying that the origin is feasible and optimal values are bounded by a constant H. The learned artifact is a projection matrix P ∈ R^{n×k} with n > k; at inference, a new LP is projected to k dimensions, solved, and the k-dimensional solution is mapped back to n dimensions via P. The recovered n-dimensional solution is always feasible for the original LP but not necessarily optimal, with quality measured as the ratio of the recovered objective value to the true optimum.

## Method

The paper contributes two learning methods and a generalization bound. The PCA-based method extracts the top-(k-1) right-singular vectors of centered optimal solutions and concatenates the mean to form P, while the gradient-based method (SGA) performs stochastic gradient ascent on the projected LP optimal value using the implicit function theorem (∇u = cy*ᵀ − Aᵀλ*y*ᵀ), with a per-step projection onto the feasible region to maintain the regularity condition needed for differentiability. The key theoretical result is an Õ(nk²) upper bound on the pseudo-dimension of the performance-metric class, proved via a structural lemma showing that LP optimal values are piecewise polynomial in input parameters (with degree bounded by (2k+1)d), combined with Warren's theorem on sign-pattern counts. An Ω(nk) lower bound is also established, making the upper bound tight up to an Õ(k) factor. Both learning methods are followed by a final projection of P's columns onto the intersection of all training feasible regions to improve strict feasibility of projected LPs.

## Results

On 7 of 8 datasets (Packing, MaxFlow, MinCostFlow, GROW7, ISRAEL, SC205, SCAGR25), PCA and/or SGA with the largest reduced dimensionality k achieve 95–99% objective ratios (solution quality relative to the full LP optimum) while being 4–70× faster than solving the original n-dimensional LP with Gurobi. On the outlier dataset STAIR, performance drops substantially: PCA attains 13.1% and SGA attains 51.2% objective ratio. The random-projection baseline ColRand yields objective ratios close to zero on most datasets (except Packing and ISRAEL), confirming that data-driven projections are essential for solution quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ColRand (column-randomized LP from Akchen & Mišić [2]), which randomly selects k out of n variables and fixes the rest to zero. Data-driven methods (PCA and SGA) dramatically outperform ColRand on nearly all datasets: where ColRand achieves near-zero objective ratios, PCA/SGA reach 95–99%, a gap of tens of percentage points. Among the two data-driven methods, SGA (stochastic gradient ascent) achieves better objective ratios than PCA on Packing, MaxFlow, MinCostFlow, and STAIR, while PCA is better on ISRAEL and SCAGR25, and the two are comparable on GROW7 and SC205. All three projection-based methods (ColRand, PCA, SGA) are substantially faster than Full (solving the original LP without dimensionality reduction), with speedups of 4–70×. The paper notes that no single learning method is universally best, and the generalization bound (Theorem 4.4) justifies selecting the method with the best empirical performance on training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random-projection approach to LPs (Vu et al., Poirion et al., Akchen and Mišić) by replacing random projection matrices with ones learned from data, drawing an analogy to the shift from random to learned sketching matrices in numerical linear algebra (Indyk et al., Bartlett et al.). The theoretical analysis is grounded in the data-driven algorithm design framework of Gupta and Roughgarden, adapting its pseudo-dimension-based generalization bounds to the LP setting. The gradient-based learning method (SGA) builds directly on the implicit-function-theorem gradient for LP optimal values from Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2020), while the key structural lemma on piecewise polynomial LP optimal values is a new contribution that the authors note has broader applicability beyond projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide generalization guarantees for data-driven LP projections, with a pseudo-dimension bound tight up to a Õ(k) factor, giving practitioners a principled sample-size requirement. The practical demonstration that learned projections achieve 95–99% objective quality at 4–70× speedup over full LPs is compelling for repetitive LP solving in production planning and network flow. Key limitations include the restriction to inequality-form LPs with a common feasible point (ruling out varying equality constraints), moderate problem sizes in experiments, and the open gap between the Õ(nk²) upper and Ω(nk) lower bounds. Future directions include closing the Õ(k) gap, handling heterogeneous equality constraints, and combining projections with GPU-accelerated LP solvers.
