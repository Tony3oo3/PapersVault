## Problem

Large-scale vision models are trained exclusively on 2D images, yet it remains unclear to what extent their learned features encode the 3D geometric and physical properties of the depicted scene. No systematic, lightweight protocol existed to probe whether off-the-shelf models contain explicit representations of properties such as scene geometry, material, support relations, shadows, occlusion, and depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The protocol takes RGB images with annotated region masks (pairs of regions per image) and produces a binary Yes/No answer for each property question, evaluated via ROC AUC. Region-wise feature vectors are obtained by average-pooling the model's spatial feature maps over each annotated region; the classifier input is the element-wise difference (for asymmetric properties like support and depth) or absolute difference (for symmetric properties like same-plane and shadow) between the two region vectors. The grid search operates over time steps (1–1000, stride 20 for Stable Diffusion), U-Net or Transformer layers, and SVM penalty C (0.001–1000, refined to 0.1–1.0).

## Method

The paper proposes a general linear-probing protocol: for each property, a linear SVM is trained on the difference of region feature vectors extracted from a frozen pre-trained model, with a grid search over the model's internal layers (and time steps for Stable Diffusion) and the SVM regularization parameter to find the optimal feature configuration. For Stable Diffusion, noise is injected into the VAE-encoded latent at varying time steps following the DIFT procedure, and features are read from the U-Net's four downsampling and four upsampling layers; for CLIP, DINOv1/v2, and VQGAN, features are extracted from individual ViT/Transformer layers. The key finding is that the optimal layer and time step differ across properties (e.g., D₃ at t=360 for same-plane vs. t=60 for depth), and that Stable Diffusion and DINOv2 features substantially outperform OpenCLIP, DINOv1, and VQGAN on all seven probed properties, while all models struggle most with material and occlusion.

## Results

Using ROC AUC as the evaluation metric, Stable Diffusion and DINOv2 achieve the highest scores across all seven 3D physical properties, with Stable Diffusion reaching 99.6% on Depth, 96.3% on Same Plane, and 95.4% on Shadow, while DINOv2 reaches 99.7% on Depth and 95.8% on Shadow. Both models are notably weaker on Material (~83.5%) and Occlusion (~84.4–84.8%), where even the best model only modestly exceeds the 50% random baseline. VQGAN consistently underperforms all other models, with scores as low as 62.0% on Perpendicular Plane and 65.5% on Material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable Diffusion outperforms OpenCLIP (Radford et al., 2021) by 1.4–14.2 AUC points across properties, with the largest gap on Perpendicular Plane (86.0 vs. 71.8) and the smallest on Shadow (95.4 vs. 94.9). DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) is the closest competitor to Stable Diffusion, actually edging it out on Shadow (95.8 vs. 95.4) and Depth (99.7 vs. 99.6), while Stable Diffusion leads on Same Plane (96.3 vs. 94.5) and Perpendicular Plane (86.0 vs. 82.1). DINOv1 (Caron et al., 2021) trails both SD and DINOv2 on every property, with the largest deficit on Perpendicular Plane (71.3 vs. 86.0 for SD). VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021) is the weakest model across the board, scoring below 70% on Perpendicular Plane and Material. In the downstream depth estimation experiment (Appendix E), the probed SD feature achieves δ₁=80.8 and RMSE=0.5389, substantially outperforming frozen ResNet-50 features (δ₁=51.0, RMSE=1.0065), and the probed SD feature injected into iDisc (Piccinelli et al., 2023) improves Mean Angular Error from 14.6° to 13.8° after only 1000 training iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probing paradigm (long used to interpret discriminative networks) to a broader set of 3D physical properties and to generative models, building directly on the DIFT feature-extraction approach [38] for Stable Diffusion. It departs from concurrent work [1] (El Banani et al.) that probes depth, surface normal, and geometric correspondence by covering additional properties (material, support relations, shadows, occlusion, scene geometry) and by using a simpler linear SVM rather than trained prediction networks. Unlike prior 3D physical scene understanding work that trains dedicated supervised models per property, this paper uses a single frozen backbone with a lightweight probe, enabling systematic comparison across five model families (Stable Diffusion, DINOv1/v2, OpenCLIP, VQGAN) under a unified protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The protocol provides a practical, low-cost diagnostic for practitioners deciding which pre-trained features to repurpose for 3D-aware downstream tasks, and the finding that different time steps/layers are optimal for different properties gives concrete guidance for feature selection. The observation that material and occlusion remain poorly linearly decodable across all models highlights a genuine gap in current representations, suggesting where additional supervision or non-linear probes would be most impactful. Open questions include whether the linear-probe ceiling reflects a true representational limitation or merely a probe-capacity issue, and whether the preliminary downstream results (normal estimation, depth estimation) will hold at scale with full training.
