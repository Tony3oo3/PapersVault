## Problem

State-of-the-art VLMs achieve near-perfect scores on standard image-text retrieval benchmarks (MSCOCO, Flickr30K), and the conventional OOD test—finetuning on MSCOCO and evaluating on Flickr—fails to measure true generalization because these datasets share high visual and linguistic overlap. The paper argues that no existing benchmark provides a reliable, carefully constructed OOD testbed for cross-modal retrieval that genuinely challenges VLMs' domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 384K image-text pairs sourced from Wikipedia articles, where images are jpg/png files with minimum 150px dimensions and text captions are LLaVA-enhanced reference descriptions (contextual, not purely descriptive) tied to each image's Wikipedia page. Evaluation is conducted as bidirectional cross-modal retrieval (image→text and text→image) over a candidate pool of N=128, reporting Recall@1/5/10. The ID and OOD test sets each contain 3K human-verified pairs, with OOD topics (food, paintings, medicine) selected to be maximally dissimilar from training topics in both visual and linguistic embedding spaces.

## Method

WIKIDO is constructed from the Wikipedia Diversity Observatory's topical coverage axis, applying a multi-stage filtering pipeline (license filtering, reference-description retention, size constraints, deduplication) to yield 384K pairs across 13 topics. Captions are enhanced via LLaVA to balance conciseness with informativeness, and the OOD test set is rigorously curated by measuring image-image and text-text similarity between each candidate and the full training set, retaining only instances that exceed a dissimilarity threshold in both modalities. Domain separation is validated quantitatively via Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) over ResNet-101 and BERT embeddings and visually via t-SNE. The paper then benchmarks CLIP, BLIP, and BLIP-2 (ViT-B through ViT-G variants) in zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, ablate fine-tuning objectives (ITC, ITM, ITG), and scale training data (100K/200K/354K) to characterize the ID-OOD performance gap.

## Results

On the WIKIDO OOD test set (3K samples), CLIP (ViT-L) achieves the best zero-shot R@1 of 68.2% for Image→Text retrieval, followed by BLIP-2 (ViT-L) at 66.4% and BLIP (ViT-L) at 58.9%. After fine-tuning on the 100K WIKIDO training split, CLIP improves to 73.4% R@1 on OOD Image→Text, while BLIP-2 (ViT-L) reaches 72.1% and BLIP (ViT-L) reaches 63.6%. The ID-to-OOD performance gap is ~5% zero-shot for CLIP but grows to 9–11% after fine-tuning, and OOD R@1 improves by only ~5% from fine-tuning compared to ~12% on the ID set, highlighting the difficulty of true OOD generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP (ViT-L) is the strongest zero-shot model on the OOD test set, achieving 68.2% R@1 for Image→Text, outperforming BLIP-2 (ViT-L) by ~2% and BLIP (ViT-L) by ~9%. After fine-tuning on WIKIDO, CLIP maintains its lead at 73.4% OOD R@1, with BLIP-2 (ViT-L) close behind at 72.1% and BLIP (ViT-L) trailing at 63.6%. On the ID test set, all three models converge to a narrow 72–83% R@1 range after fine-tuning, but the OOD gap widens to 9–11%, confirming that WIKIDO OOD is substantially harder. BLIP-2 (ViT-G) with 1.17B parameters does not outperform the smaller BLIP-2 (ViT-L) on OOD, suggesting that scale alone does not close the domain gap. The paper further shows that fine-tuning on MSCOCO and testing on Flickr (the standard OOD protocol) yields near-identical results to in-domain evaluation, arguing that MSCOCO–Flickr is not a valid OOD test, unlike WIKIDO where the OOD gap is clearly measurable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the WIT dataset's data pipeline for constructing image-text pairs from Wikipedia, but departs from prior OOD evaluation practice by arguing that the standard MSCOCO→Flickr zero-shot protocol is insufficient due to high visual and linguistic overlap between those datasets. It draws on the Wikipedia Diversity Observatory's topical categorization to construct a genuinely OOD test set, validated via t-SNE and MMD domain-gap analysis. The cross-modal retrieval task itself is well-established (CLIP, BLIP, BLIP-2), and the paper positions WIKIDO as a more challenging testbed than existing benchmarks like MSCOCO-5K or Flickr30K-1K, which state-of-the-art VLMs have nearly saturated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WIKIDO exposes a significant gap in VLM generalization that prior benchmarks mask: even BLIP-2 drops from ~95% R@1 on Flickr to ~66% on truly OOD data, and fine-tuning on in-domain data yields only ~5% OOD improvement versus ~12% ID improvement. This challenges the field's reliance on MSCOCO/Flickr as OOD proxies and provides a concrete, reproducible benchmark (hosted on Kaggle) for tracking progress. Key limitations include English-only coverage, reliance solely on the topical diversity axis (ignoring geography, gender, ethnicity), and scope limited to retrieval rather than generation or contextual understanding, leaving open whether more diverse training data or other diversity axes could close the OOD gap.
