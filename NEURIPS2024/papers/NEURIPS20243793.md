## Problem

Existing multi-modal entity alignment methods focus on designing modality encoders and fusion strategies but fail to address uncertain correspondences between inter-modal and intra-modal cues: weak semantic associations between modalities, diverse attribute descriptions across different MMKGs (e.g., 'Release Date' vs. 'Debut Date'), and missing modalities that can mislead alignment. These uncertainties severely hinder the effective exploration of aligned entity similarities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two MMKGs containing relational triples, attribute triples, and entity-image pairs, and produces 1-to-1 aligned entity pairs along with holistic multi-modal entity embeddings. Attribute alignment is performed via GPT-3.5 with in-context learning prompts (no fine-tuning), while visual features are extracted by a frozen ViT and text features by a frozen BERT. Training uses only 20% of available aligned pairs, with a bi-directional iterative strategy that progressively discovers new mutual-nearest-neighbor pairs to augment the training set.

## Method

TMEA comprises three modules: (1) a Multi-modal Knowledge Encoder that uses TransE for relations, GPT-3.5 + in-context learning to align and filter attributes before BERT-encoding them into abstract sentences, and ViT for images; (2) a Missing Modality Imputation module that unifies each target modality with concatenated features of other modalities into a shared VAE latent subspace, then decodes pseudo-features for absent modalities; (3) a Multi-modal Commonality Enhancement module that applies multi-head cross-attention between every pair of modalities with an orthogonal constraint loss ensuring the residual (original minus commonality) is orthogonal to the other modality. The overall objective combines TransE margin loss, multi-modal contrastive learning over holistic and per-modality embeddings, VAE reconstruction, latent-space MSE alignment, and the orthogonal constraint, optimized via Adam with a bi-directional iterative training loop.

## Results

On FB15K-DB15K, TMEA achieves H@1 of 0.867, H@10 of 0.944, and MRR of 0.895, outperforming the strongest multi-modal baseline MSNEA (H@1=0.653, H@10=0.812, MRR=0.708) by 32.8%, 16.3%, and 26.4% respectively. On FB15K-YG15K, TMEA reaches H@1 of 0.818, H@10 of 0.916, and MRR of 0.853, surpassing MSNEA (H@1=0.443, H@10=0.698, MRR=0.529) by 84.6%, 31.2%, and 61.1%. TMEA also substantially outperforms the best traditional KG method BootEA (H@1=0.323 on FB15K-DB15K) by over 168% in H@1. All improvements are statistically significant (p-value < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMEA achieves the best performance on every metric across both datasets, outperforming all 14 baselines. Against the strongest multi-modal baseline MSNEA, TMEA gains 32.8% in H@1 on FB15K-DB15K and 84.6% on FB15K-YG15K, with MRR improvements of 26.4% and 61.1% respectively. Among other multi-modal methods, MCLEA (H@1=0.441/0.406), EVA (H@1=0.556/0.103), MMEA (H@1=0.265/0.234), ACK-MMEA (H@1=0.304/0.289), and MoAlign (H@1=0.318/0.296) all trail TMEA by wide margins. Traditional KG methods are substantially weaker: the best among them, BootEA, reaches only H@1=0.323 on FB15K-DB15K and 0.234 on FB15K-YG15K, while IPTransE and GCN-Align score below 0.05 H@1. Notably, EVA performs well on FB15K-DB15K (H@1=0.556) but collapses on FB15K-YG15K (H@1=0.103), highlighting its sensitivity to dataset characteristics, whereas TMEA maintains consistent superiority across both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal entity alignment line initiated by MMEA (Chen et al., 2020) and advanced by MSNEA (Chen et al., 2022) and ACK-MMEA (Li et al., 2023), which primarily focus on designing modality encoders and fusion strategies. It departs from this lineage by reframing the core challenge as 'uncertain correspondences'—weak inter-modal associations, attribute description diversity, and modality absence—and addressing each with a dedicated mechanism: cross-attention with orthogonal constraints, LLM-based attribute alignment via in-context learning, and VAE-based pseudo-feature generation. The relational encoder reuses TransE with the swapping strategy from MMEA, and the bi-directional iterative training strategy is adopted from MRAEA (Mao et al., 2020), but the integration of GPT-3.5 for attribute alignment and the VAE-based missing modality imputation represent novel architectural choices for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and modular framework for handling three concrete failure modes in multi-modal entity alignment that prior methods largely ignored, and the 32.8% H@1 improvement over the strongest baseline (MSNEA) is substantial. The integration of LLMs with in-context learning for attribute alignment is a timely technique that could transfer to other KG integration tasks. However, the method still depends on aligned entity pairs for training (even with the iterative strategy), relies on a general-purpose LLM rather than KG-specific fine-tuning, and is validated on only two datasets of moderate scale—leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger, noisier, or truly unsupervised settings.
