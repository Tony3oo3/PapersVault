## Problem

Most adversarial training methods produce models robust against a single l_p norm perturbation, yet real-world adversaries are not constrained to one norm. When fine-tuning a model robust against one norm (e.g., l∞) on adversarial examples from another norm (e.g., l1), the model loses substantial robustness against the original norm, creating a fundamental tradeoff among multiple-norm robustness and clean accuracy that prior defenses (worst-case, average-case, random-sampled) handle suboptimally.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels via a standard softmax classifier. During training, each input image is perturbed under multiple l_p norms simultaneously (l1 with ε=12, l2 with ε=0.5, l∞ with ε=8/255 for CIFAR-10), and the logit pairing loss operates on pairs of adversarial examples from different norms within the same batch. The training objective combines a KL-divergence between logit distributions of two norm-specific adversarial examples with a MAX-style worst-case cross-entropy, rather than a single adversarial loss.

## Method

RAMP addresses two tradeoffs via two mechanisms. First, a logit pairing loss targets the identified key tradeoff pair (l∞–l1 for standard ε values): it computes a KL-divergence between the logit distributions of l_q and l_r adversarial examples, restricted to the subset where l_q examples are correctly classified, preventing the model from losing l_q robustness while learning l_r robustness; this is combined with a MAX-style worst-case cross-entropy over the two norms. Second, gradient projection (GP) connects natural training with adversarial training by computing layer-wise cosine similarity between the natural and adversarial model updates per epoch, projecting the adversarial update toward the natural update only where similarity is positive, and blending via a hyperparameter β=0.5. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.5) shows GP achieves smaller delta error than standard AT in the large-dimension limit, explaining the improved accuracy/robustness tradeoff. RAMP supports both robust fine-tuning from a single-norm AT checkpoint and full adversarial training from random initialization, with the GP overhead being minimal (~6s per epoch for NT on ResNet-18 vs. ~78s for AT).

## Results

For adversarial training from scratch on CIFAR-10 with PreAct ResNet-18, RAMP achieves the best union accuracy of 44.6% (λ=5) with 81.2% clean accuracy, outperforming all baselines. For robust fine-tuning, RAMP obtains SOTA union accuracy of 53.3% on CIFAR-10 (WRN-70-16) and 29.1% on ImageNet (XCiT-S). RAMP also demonstrates superior universal robustness, achieving 75.5% average accuracy on common corruptions and 26.1% union accuracy against unseen adversaries, surpassing E-AT and MAX by 2–4% on the latter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMP (λ=5) achieves the highest union accuracy (44.6%) and l∞ robustness (46.0%) among all methods, beating MAX (44.0% union) by 0.6%, MSD (43.9%) by 0.7%, E-AT (42.4%) by 2.2%, SAT (40.4%) by 4.2%, and AVG (40.1%) by 4.5%. The tradeoff is that RAMP sacrifices some clean accuracy (81.2% vs. 84.6% for AVG) and l₁ robustness (48.3% vs. 54.0% for SAT), reflecting the inherent multi-norm tension. In robust fine-tuning (Table 2), RAMP consistently outperforms E-AT on union accuracy across all architectures: 53.3% vs. 51.6% on WRN-70-16, 46.9% vs. 45.1% on WRN-34-20, and 29.1% vs. 23.4% on ImageNet XCiT-S. For universal robustness (Table 4), RAMP achieves 50.9% average corruption accuracy and 26.1% union accuracy against unseen adversaries, surpassing E-AT (49.4%, 21.9%) and MAX (49.6%, 24.4%), while Winninghand achieves higher corruption accuracy (42.7%) but zero adversarial robustness. RAMP also outperforms E-AT and MAX across varying ε settings (Tables 1, 10–17), confirming consistent gains regardless of which norm is the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMP repurposes gradient projection from federated domain adaptation (Jiang et al., 2023) to bridge natural training and adversarial training, and extends adversarial logit pairing (Kannan et al., 2018) from the clean-vs-adversarial setting to the multi-norm adversarial setting (l_q vs l_r). It directly improves upon E-AT (Croce and Hein, 2022), which randomly samples between l_1 and l_∞ attacks during fine-tuning, by introducing a principled KL-divergence loss on correctly-classified subsets and a MAX-style worst-case term. The paper also provides the first theoretical error analysis (Theorem 4.5) showing why GP yields smaller delta error than standard AT in the adversarial training context, grounding the empirical observations in a formal framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-norm robustness is practically important because real-world adversaries are not constrained to a single perturbation norm, and the paper demonstrates that achieving it also yields superior universal robustness against unseen adversaries and natural corruptions. The gradient projection technique offers a lightweight mechanism (0.04s per epoch overhead) to mitigate the well-known accuracy/robustness tradeoff, making it attractive for practitioners. Limitations include higher training cost than E-AT (roughly 2×), occasional clean accuracy drops during fine-tuning, and reliance on a heuristic for identifying the key tradeoff pair that may not generalize to all epsilon configurations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-l_p threat models (e.g., semantic or perceptual perturbations) and whether the GP mechanism can be made adaptive per-layer rather than using a fixed β.
