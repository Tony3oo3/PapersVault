## Problem

LSTMs, despite their historical success as the first LLMs, suffer from three fundamental limitations: inability to revise storage decisions once made, limited storage capacity due to scalar cell states, and lack of parallelizability from hidden-hidden recurrent connections. The paper asks how far language modeling performance can be pushed when these limitations are addressed and LSTMs are scaled to billions of parameters using modern LLM techniques.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence and autoregressively predicts the next token, trained self-supervised on SlimPajama (15B and 300B tokens). A key operational distinction is that xLSTM maintains constant memory complexity with respect to sequence length (unlike Transformers' quadratic KV-cache growth), enabling much larger batch sizes at inference. The mLSTM variant is fully parallelizable during training via a matrix-formulation of the recurrence, while the sLSTM variant requires sequential processing due to its memory mixing but is accelerated with a custom CUDA kernel.

## Method

xLSTM introduces two core modifications to the LSTM cell. First, exponential gating replaces sigmoid activations on input and forget gates, enabling the network to revise previously stored values; a normalizer state tracks the cumulative gate strength to keep outputs bounded, and a stabilization state (following Milakov & Gimelshein) prevents numerical overflow. Second, two new memory structures are defined: sLSTM retains a scalar cell state but adds memory mixing via block-diagonal recurrent weight matrices across multiple heads, while mLSTM replaces the scalar state with a d×d matrix updated by a covariance (outer product) rule C_t = f_t·C_{t-1} + i_t·v_t·k_t^T, making it fully parallelizable. These cells are embedded in residual blocks (post up-projection for sLSTM, pre up-projection for mLSTM) and residually stacked with pre-LayerNorm into full xLSTM architectures, with the notation xLSTM[a:b] denoting the ratio of mLSTM to sLSTM blocks.

## Results

On 15B SlimPajama tokens, xLSTM[1:0] achieves the best validation perplexity of 13.43, outperforming Mamba (13.70), Llama (14.25), and GPT-3 (14.26). At 300B tokens and 1.3B parameters, xLSTM[1:0] reaches 8.89 ppl and 58.48% average downstream accuracy, beating Mamba (9.14 ppl, 58.41%), Llama (9.44 ppl, 56.99%), and RWKV-4 (9.83 ppl, 54.78%). On sequence length extrapolation to 16k context, xLSTM[7:1] maintains 8.92 ppl while Llama degrades to 337.83 ppl and Mamba to 14.00 ppl. On the PALOMA benchmark, xLSTM[1:0] achieves lower perplexity than Mamba in 568/571 (99.5%) text domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xLSTM outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary 15B SlimPajama perplexity metric: xLSTM[1:0] (13.43) beats the closest competitor Mamba (13.70) by 0.27 ppl, and Llama/GPT-3 (14.25/14.26) by ~0.8 ppl. At 300B scale and 1.3B parameters, xLSTM[1:0] achieves the best perplexity (8.89) and highest average downstream accuracy (58.48%), narrowly edging Mamba (9.14 ppl, 58.41%) while clearly surpassing Llama (9.44, 56.99%) and RWKV-4 (9.83, 54.78%). The most dramatic advantage appears in sequence extrapolation: at 16k context, xLSTM[7:1] maintains 8.92 ppl while Llama collapses to 337.83 and Mamba degrades to 14.00. On the Multi-Query Associative Recall task, xLSTM[1:1] is the best non-Transformer model, and on formal language tasks (parity, regular grammars), xLSTM solves state-tracking problems that Transformers, SSMs, and linear methods fundamentally cannot. The only consistent loss is on the ARC-C reasoning benchmark where Mamba occasionally edges out xLSTM, and in raw generation speed where the unoptimized mLSTM CUDA kernel is ~4× slower than FlashAttention.

## Relation to Prior Work

xLSTM is a direct extension of the 1997 LSTM, introducing two modifications: exponential gating (with stabilization borrowed from Milakov & Gimelshein, 2018) and a matrix memory cell using the covariance update rule from associative memory literature (Dayan & Willshaw, 1991). Conceptually, the closest relatives are Retention, RWKV, GLA, HGRN2, and Mamba, which share matrix memory and/or gating, but xLSTM uniquely retains memory mixing (hidden-hidden recurrent connections) in its sLSTM variant, which the paper argues is necessary for state tracking problems that Transformers and SSMs provably cannot solve. The paper positions xLSTM as a way to bring the LSTM family into the LLM era by integrating it into modern residual block backbones with pre-LayerNorm, effectively bridging 30 years of RNN research with contemporary LLM training practices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 30-year-old architecture, with targeted modifications, can match or exceed Transformers and SSMs at up to 1.3B parameters and 300B tokens, challenging the assumption that self-attention is necessary for competitive LLMs. The constant memory complexity (vs. quadratic KV-cache for Transformers) has direct practical implications for deployment on edge devices and long-context inference. Key open questions include: whether the unoptimized architecture and hyperparameters (acknowledged by the authors) would close or widen the gap at larger scales, whether the matrix memory degrades beyond 16k context, and whether the sLSTM's non-parallelizability becomes a bottleneck at production scale. The scaling law results suggest xLSTM will remain competitive, but the evidence base is limited to a single dataset and one model per comparison class at 300B tokens.
