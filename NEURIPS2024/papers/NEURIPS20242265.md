## Problem

The JOINT mechanism (Gillenwater et al., ICML 2022) achieves the best empirical accuracy for differentially private top-k selection but requires Õ(dk) time and space, substantially more than O(d) peeling-based alternatives. The paper asks whether a mechanism with the same output distribution as JOINT can be implemented with running time and space linear in d, and answers affirmatively when k is not too large (k ∈ O(√d)).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242265/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a voting histogram h ∈ [0..n]^d (d items each scored by up to n voters, with integer scores), a privacy parameter ε, and a confidence parameter β, and produces an ordered sequence of k distinct items. The output is a sequence rather than an unordered set, preserving the ranking structure of the top-k items. The mechanism is pure ε-DP, guaranteeing that adding or removing any single voter's votes does not substantially alter the output distribution.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'group by' sampling framework that partitions the d^Θ(k) output space into O(nk) subsets S_{r,i} indexed by loss value r and first-failure position i, enabling a two-step procedure: first select a subset via Gumbel-noise-weighted sampling proportional to |S_{r,i}|·exp(−εr/2), then sample a sequence uniformly within it. A pruning technique truncates the loss at τ = ⌈(2/ε)·ln(C(d,k)·k!/β)⌉, merging all subsets with r ≥ τ into a single merged subset per position i, reducing the partition from O(nk) to O(τk) subsets. This yields O(d + k·τ) time and space, which simplifies to O(d + k²/ε·ln d) and further to Õ(d) when k ∈ O(√d). The algorithm achieves the same asymptotic accuracy guarantee as JOINT while running 10–100× faster empirically across six real-world datasets.

## Results

FASTJOINT achieves time and space complexity of O(d + k²/ε · ln d), reducing from JOINT's Õ(dk) to Õ(d) when k ∈ O(√d). Experimentally, FASTJOINT runs 10–100× faster than JOINT across all six datasets (Games, Books, News, Tweets, Movies, Foods) while maintaining comparable ℓ∞ and ℓ₁ error. The algorithm consistently outperforms the pure-DP PNF-PEEL baseline for all values of k, and outperforms the approximate-DP CDP-PEEL baseline for at least moderately large k. Performance is particularly strong on datasets with large gaps between top-k scores (Books, News, Tweets, Movies).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against JOINT (Gillenwater et al., ICML 2022), FASTJOINT achieves the same asymptotic accuracy guarantee (same τ threshold) while running 10–100× faster; the speedup comes from reducing the number of partition subsets from O(dk) to Õ(k²) via the truncated-loss pruning technique. Against PNF-PEEL (McKenna & Sheldon, NeurIPS 2020), FASTJOINT consistently achieves lower ℓ∞ and ℓ₁ error for all tested k values, though PNF-PEEL can be faster on small datasets due to NumPy vectorization; on the largest dataset (Foods, d=166,049), FASTJOINT is faster. Against CDP-PEEL (Durfee & Rogers, NeurIPS 2019), CDP-PEEL has a theoretical O(d) running time and is faster in practice, but FASTJOINT achieves lower error for moderately large k, with the gap widening as k increases. The paper notes that FASTJOINT's advantage over peeling-based methods is most pronounced when top-k score gaps are large, because the joint mechanism's sampling probability of error items scales as e^{-O(ε)} rather than e^{-O(ε/k)} or e^{-Õ(ε/√k)}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the JOINT mechanism (Gillenwater et al., ICML 2022), which was the first to sample a length-k sequence holistically from the exponential mechanism rather than using the iterative peeling strategy. The key departure is a new 'group by' partition of the output space—grouping sequences by their loss value r and the first position i where that loss is achieved—combined with a truncated-loss pruning step that collapses O(nk) subsets down to Õ(k²). The paper also shows that applying the same pruning idea to JOINT's original partition (by position and specific item) cannot reduce the subset count below Ω(dk), which motivates the fundamentally different partition structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 10–100× speedup over JOINT while preserving identical privacy-utility guarantees, making the best-empirical-accuracy DP top-k mechanism feasible at scale. The limitation is that the complexity advantage (reducing Õ(dk) to Õ(d)) requires k ∈ O(√d); for very large k the k²/ε·ln d term dominates and the gap narrows. An open question is whether the group-by framework can be extended to other exponential-mechanism problems beyond top-k selection, or whether the partition structure can be further refined to handle the large-k regime more gracefully.
