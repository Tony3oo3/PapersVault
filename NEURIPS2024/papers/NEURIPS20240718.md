## Problem

Transformer-based offline RL methods (e.g., Decision Transformer) struggle with out-of-distribution states and actions because they implicitly encode historical information through attention rather than explicitly modeling it, and they flatten trajectories into sequences while ignoring the causal intra-step structure among return-to-go, state, and action triplets. Existing remedies—data augmentation or value-based constraints—introduce additional noise or overestimation bias, and the policy still overfits suboptimal trajectories with noisy labels. The paper asks whether a state-space-model backbone can simultaneously capture global inter-step history and local intra-step RSA patterns while resisting overfitting to noisy demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a context window of L=20 consecutive (RTG, state, action) triplets from an offline trajectory, each embedded by separate MLPs and concatenated with a shared absolute position embedding before being fed into the encoder. At inference, the desired return-to-go is set as a conditioning token, and the model autoregressively outputs the next action; during training it additionally predicts the next RTG and next state as auxiliary regression targets. All experiments use D4RL v2 datasets with normalized scores computed relative to random and expert baselines.

## Method

Decision Mamba replaces the transformer encoder with a multi-grained selective SSM: a coarse-grained branch (InterS3M) propagates a latent hidden state across inter-steps to explicitly preserve historical information, while a fine-grained branch (IntraS3M) applies a 1D convolution to gather the three RSA tokens within each step and runs a second selective SSM to extract local causal patterns; the two branches are fused per layer via layer normalization and a residual connection. On top of this architecture, a Progressive Self-Evolution Regularization (PSER) refines the training target by blending the ground-truth action with the policy's own prediction from the previous iteration, with a trade-off weight β that grows linearly from a lower bound (0.5) to a task-specific upper bound (0.85–0.95), so the policy progressively trusts its accumulated knowledge to smooth noisy labels. The training objective further includes inverse learning terms that predict the next RTG and next state given the predicted action, encouraging the model to internalize transition dynamics. The full forward pass is linear-time in sequence length (O(n)) due to the SSM structure, in contrast to the O(n²) attention cost of transformer baselines.

## Results

On the Gym-Mujoco benchmark (Table 1), Decision Mamba achieves an average normalized score of 83.2 ± 0.82 across 9 datasets (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker × medium/medium-expert/medium-replay), outperforming the strongest baseline GDT (79.1) by approximately 4.1 points. The improvement is most pronounced on suboptimal datasets: on Hopper-medium, DM scores 98.5 vs. GDT's 77.1 and CQL's 86.6. On the extended benchmark including Ant and AntMaze (Table 2), DM reaches 107.9 ± 3.33 average, surpassing LaMo (102.0) by ~6% and DT (95.7) by ~12%, with a perfect 100.0 on AntMaze-umaze.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DM outperforms all 12 baselines on average across both main tables. Against the strongest transformer-based baselines, DM beats GDT (79.1) by 4.1 points and LaMo (77.8) by 5.4 points on Table 1, with the largest margin on Hopper-medium (98.5 vs. 77.1 for GDT, 74.1 for LaMo). Against the value-based CQL, DM wins on 7 of 9 datasets but trails slightly on HalfCheetah-medium (43.8 vs. 44.4) and HalfCheetah-medium-replay (40.8 vs. 46.2), where CQL's Q-value regularization provides an edge. On the extended benchmark (Table 2), DM surpasses LaMo by ~10% on Ant-medium (104.8 vs. 94.6) and achieves a perfect 100.0 on AntMaze-umaze where DT and BC both score only 63.0. Against diffusion-based methods (Table 6, Appendix C), DM's 83.2 average exceeds Diffuser (75.3) and Decision Diffuser (81.8), though DD edges out DM on HalfCheetah-medium (49.1 vs. 43.8) and Hopper-medium-replay (100.0 vs. 89.1). EDT, which uses variable-length trajectory stitching, is outperformed by DM on 8 of 9 datasets, with the gap largest on Hopper-medium (98.5 vs. 63.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper replaces the transformer backbone in Decision Transformer-style offline RL with a multi-grained Mamba architecture, introducing a fine-grained intra-step SSM branch alongside the standard coarse-grained inter-step SSM to capture local RSA (return-to-go, state, action) dependencies that prior transformer-based methods (DT, GDT, StAR) flatten and ignore. The learning strategy departs from data augmentation or value-based regularization approaches by proposing Progressive Self-Evolution Regularization (PSER), which uses the policy's own past predictions to adaptively refine noisy training targets — a self-distillation-like mechanism inspired by the observation that deep networks fit clean samples before overfitting noisy ones. The SSM discretization and selective mechanism are inherited directly from S4 and Mamba, while the trajectory-embedding and conditional sequence modeling formulation follows the Decision Transformer paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from quadratic-complexity transformers to linear-complexity SSMs for offline RL is practically significant for long-horizon tasks, and the multi-grained design offers a principled way to exploit both global sequential structure and local intra-step causality. PSER is a lightweight, hyperparameter-light regularization that could potentially transfer to other supervised-learning-from-noisy-labels settings beyond RL. However, the evaluation is confined to D4RL Mujoco and AntMaze benchmarks with no real-robot or continuous-control-at-scale validation, and the paper explicitly defers computational efficiency analysis to future work. Open questions include whether the fine-grained SSM branch generalizes to higher-dimensional action spaces or multi-agent settings, and whether PSER's linear β schedule is optimal or task-dependent.
