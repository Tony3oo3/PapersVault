## Problem

Existing deformable object and garment manipulation benchmarks are constrained by single simulation methods (PBD or FEM only), limited task diversity, CPU-based architectures that hinder parallel training, and the absence of comprehensive sim-to-real transfer pipelines. These limitations impede progress on garment manipulation, which demands understanding of nearly infinite self-deformation states, multi-physics interactions across rigid, articulated, fluid, and human media, and faces a particularly large sim-to-real gap due to diverse visual appearances and complex manipulation strategies.

## Input / Output

The benchmark evaluates algorithms across heterogeneous input modalities: full point clouds for UGM and Affordance, RGB images for DIFT, and state observations or partial point clouds for RL-based methods. The simulation provides RGB-D observations alongside ground-truth semantic annotations (2D/3D bounding boxes, normals, instance segmentation) and auxiliary signals such as robot joint states, cloth particle positions, and object poses. Outputs span dense point-level visual correspondences, binary affordance scores, and full manipulation trajectories, with task success defined as maintaining the goal state for at least five seconds to account for garment instability under gravity and friction.

## Method

GarmentLab is built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim (PhysX5) and unifies multiple physics simulation methods within a single GPU-parallelized environment: PBD for large garments and fluids, FEM for small elastic objects, rigid and articulated body dynamics, and flow models for wind effects, enabling multi-physics interaction modeling that prior single-method benchmarks cannot support. The environment integrates ROS and MoveIt for real-world-aligned motion planning and provides a lightweight teleoperation pipeline for data collection. The sim-to-real framework contributes three self-supervised visual alignment algorithms—noisy observation augmentation on depth maps, point cloud alignment via chamfer distance optimization of an affine transform, and keypoint embedding alignment using InfoNCE contrastive loss—alongside the first real-world deformable object benchmark with laser-scanned garment meshes. The benchmark comprises 20 tasks across 5 interaction categories (garment-garment, garment-fluid, garment-FEM, garment-rigid, garment-avatar) plus long-horizon tasks, systematically evaluated with vision-based (UGM, DIFT, Affordance) and RL-based (PPO state/vision) algorithms.

## Results

On the GarmentLab simulation benchmark (Table 5), UGM achieves the best performance on large-piece garment tasks, scoring 61.5/62.1/59.8 on folding (Tops/Trousers/Skirts) and 61.8/57.5/59.7 on hanging, while DIFT leads on small-piece tasks with 66.4/63.2 on placing and 64.7/61.2 on hanging hats/gloves. RL-based methods (PPO) perform substantially worse, with RL-State achieving only 14.8/12.5/9.8 on folding and RL-Vision dropping to 6.7/8.2/3.2. In real-world experiments (Table 6), UGM achieves 10/15 success on T-shirt folding and 10/15 on hat hanging, while DIFT reaches 14/15 on hat hanging but only 8/15 on folding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among vision-based methods, UGM dominates large-piece garment manipulation (folding, unfolding, hanging) by 8–40 points over DIFT and 5–26 points over Affordance, attributed to its cross-deform and cross-object representation consistency via skeleton-based correspondence. DIFT, built on Stable Diffusion, excels on small-piece tasks (hats, gloves) where minimal deformation and rotation robustness matter, outperforming UGM by ~30 points on placing (66.4 vs. 33.2) and hanging (64.7 vs. 31.8). Affordance performs competitively on hanging (64.1/60.2/61.3) due to its task-specific grasp-point design but struggles with unfolding (32.4/36.7/31.8) where precise point selection is critical. Both RL baselines (PPO with state or partial point-cloud input) trail all vision methods by 20–50+ points across every task, with RL-Vision being the weakest overall (e.g., 6.7 on Tops folding vs. UGM's 61.5), as the authors attribute this to abnormal arm trajectories, reward instability in long-horizon tasks, and the difficulty of learning from high-dimensional partial visual observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

GarmentLab builds on the dense visual correspondence framework of UniGarmentManip (UGM) [57] for its primary vision-based evaluation, and on NVIDIA Isaac Sim/Omniverse as the underlying GPU-parallel simulation engine. It departs from prior deformable-object benchmarks (SoftGym, FluidLab, DeformableRavens) in two key ways: (1) it unifies multiple physics solvers (PBD for garments/fluids, FEM for small elastic objects, rigid-body and flow solvers) in a single environment rather than restricting to one method, and (2) it integrates a full sim-to-real pipeline (ROS, MoveIt, teleoperation, and three visual alignment algorithms) that prior CPU-based benchmarks largely lack. The paper also extends the task scope from single-garment manipulation (folding, unfolding) to multi-physics interactions (garment-fluid, garment-rigid, garment-avatar) and long-horizon composite tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first real-world benchmark specifically for deformable objects and garments, GarmentLab provides a standardized, reproducible platform that the field has lacked, enabling cross-lab comparison in a domain where prior work relied on ad-hoc object sets. The experiments reveal that even SOTA vision-based methods show limited generalization across garment states and categories, and RL methods struggle fundamentally with the high-dimensional, long-horizon nature of garment manipulation—findings that should guide future algorithm design. Open questions include whether the multi-physics interactions (fluid, flow, avatar) can be exploited by current learning methods, how long-horizon planning scales beyond scripted subtask sequences, and whether the sim-to-real gap can be further closed for tasks involving dexterous hands and mobile robots.
