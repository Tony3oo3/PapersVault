## Problem

Existing MaxEnt RL methods for continuous action spaces rely on actor-critic frameworks with alternating policy evaluation and improvement steps, and approximate the soft value function via Monte Carlo estimation (either SQL-style importance sampling or SAC-style expectation). These design choices introduce optimization errors from the two-loop training and estimation variance from finite-sample approximation, respectively. The paper asks whether a single unified model can simultaneously represent the soft Q-function, compute the soft value function exactly, and sample actions efficiently—eliminating both the actor-critic separation and the Monte Carlo approximation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector s_t as input and produces a continuous action vector a_t as output. The state conditions a normalizing flow (four additive coupling layers followed by an element-wise linear layer) whose inverse maps a unit Gaussian latent z to the action. The soft Q-function is read off as α·log of the product of non-linear Jacobian determinants times the prior density, while the soft value function is computed in closed form as −α·log of the product of linear Jacobian determinants—both derived from the same flow parameters θ without any separate value network or Monte Carlo sampling. Two state-conditioned MLP reward-shifting functions b_θ^(1) and b_θ^(2) are also conditioned on s_t to stabilize training.

## Method

MEow models the policy as a state-conditioned Energy-Based Normalizing Flow (EBFlow), exploiting the EBFlow decomposition that separates the flow's Jacobian determinant product into an input-dependent (non-linear) part and an input-independent (linear) part. The non-linear part defines the soft Q-function Q_θ(s,a) = α·log[p_z(g_θ(a|s))·∏_{i∈S_n}|det(J_{g_θ^i})|], and the linear part gives the soft value function V_θ(s) = −α·log[∏_{i∈S_l}|det(J_{g_θ^i}(s))|] in exact closed form, satisfying V_θ(s) = α·log∫exp(Q_θ(s,a)/α)da by the change-of-variables theorem. Because the same flow both defines the energy (critic role) and provides the inverse map g_θ^{-1} for sampling (actor role), policy evaluation and policy improvement are unified into a single soft Bellman error loss with no alternating updates. Two practical techniques are added: Learnable Reward Shifting (LRS), which introduces a state-conditioned shift b_θ(s) to prevent exponential underflow/overflow of Jacobian products in FP32, and Shifting-Based Clipped Double Q-Learning (SCDQ), which uses two shift functions to take the minimum of two value estimates without duplicating the flow. For deterministic inference, when the non-linear Jacobian determinants are input-independent (e.g., additive coupling layers), the optimal action is simply g_θ^{-1}(μ|s_t) where μ is the Gaussian prior mean.

## Results

Results are reported as total-return learning curves (mean ± 95% CI over 5 seeds) rather than a single final-score table. On five MuJoCo environments (Hopper-v4, HalfCheetah-v4, Walker2d-v4, Ant-v4, Humanoid-v4), MEow performs comparably to SAC and outperforms SQL, DDPG, TD3, and PPO; in the higher-dimensional Ant-v4 and Humanoid-v4 tasks MEow additionally shows fewer evaluation spikes than SAC. On six Omniverse Isaac Gym environments (Ant, Humanoid, Ingenuity, ANYmal, AllegroHand, FrankaCabinet), MEow consistently outperforms SAC across all tasks. Ablation confirms that Learnable Reward Shifting (LRS) and Shifting-Based Clipped Double Q-Learning (SCDQ) are both necessary for competitive performance, and that a deterministic inference policy (via g_θ⁻¹(μ|s_t)) outperforms the stochastic variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEow outperforms SQL [8], DDPG [69], TD3 [66], and PPO [70] on all five MuJoCo environments, with the gap most visible in the higher-dimensional Ant-v4 and Humanoid-v4 tasks. Against SAC [9], MEow achieves comparable final returns on MuJoCo but shows improved stability (fewer spikes) in Ant-v4 and Humanoid-v4; on the six Isaac Gym environments MEow consistently surpasses SAC, with the largest margins on the manipulation tasks AllegroHand and FrankaCabinet. In the appendix comparison (Fig. A2), MEow also outperforms the four actor-critic variants built from prior flow-based MaxEnt RL work (Flow-SAC [10, 11] and its Gaussian-actor counterpart), supporting the claim that a single-objective training loop is superior to alternating policy-evaluation and policy-improvement steps. The paper notes that MEow's training is approximately 2.3× slower than SAC due to the cost of flow-based inference during environment interaction, a trade-off for the exact soft-value computation and unified policy representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Energy-Based Normalizing Flow (EBFlow) decomposition from Chao et al. (NeurIPS 2023) for maximum-entropy reinforcement learning, where it was previously used only for density estimation. The MaxEnt RL formulation follows the soft Bellman error paradigm established by Soft Q-Learning (Haarnoja et al., ICML 2017), but departs from the standard actor-critic architecture (SAC, Flow-SAC, etc.) by unifying the actor and critic into a single flow-based model. This unification eliminates both the alternating policy evaluation/improvement loop and the Monte Carlo approximation of the soft value function, which are the two defining approximations in all prior MaxEnt RL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact soft value computation via the flow's normalizing constant removes a source of estimation variance that has plagued MaxEnt RL since SQL, and the single-objective training simplifies hyperparameter tuning by eliminating the actor-critic balance. However, the practical impact is tempered by a ~2.3× training slowdown relative to SAC (due to flow inference during environment interaction), the need for environment-specific τ tuning, and the architectural constraints required for deterministic inference. The Isaac Gym results on real-world-inspired robotic tasks (Ingenuity, ANYmal, AllegroHand, FrankaCabinet) suggest the framework could be relevant for deployment, but the modest performance gap over SAC on standard MuJoCo benchmarks limits its immediate adoption appeal.
