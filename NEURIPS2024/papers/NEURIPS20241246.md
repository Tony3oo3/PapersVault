## Problem

Existing SAM compression methods (FastSAM, MobileSAM, EdgeSAM, EfficientSAM) all replace the heavyweight ViT image encoder with a lightweight architecture trained from scratch, requiring 1%–110% of the SA-1B dataset and incurring substantial performance loss under limited data. Conventional pruning-and-finetuning reduces data demands but suffers steep degradation at high pruning ratios (>75%) with scarce training data, because the pruning objective (hard-label loss) misaligns with the distillation target (soft-label loss) and the coupled embedding/bottleneck structure of the ViT encoder prevents effective intermediate feature supervision.

## Input / Output

The compressed model accepts an RGB image together with a prompt (single point, bounding box, or segment-everything) and outputs a binary segmentation mask, retaining SAM's original prompt encoder and mask decoder unchanged. Only the ViT image encoder is pruned, reducing it to 4.0% (26M params) or 1.4% (9.1M params) of the original SAM-B while preserving the 256×256 patch-token interface. Training requires only 10,000 unlabeled images (0.1% of SA-1B) on a single Titan RTX GPU with batch size 4, and the entire compression pipeline is label-free.

## Method

SlimSAM introduces an alternate slimming framework that decomposes the ViT encoder into two decoupled sub-structures—embedding dimensions (block outputs) and bottleneck dimensions (QKV/MLP intermediate features)—and compresses them in two progressive stages: (1) prune embedding dimensions uniformly across blocks, then distill by aligning the pruned model's bottleneck features and final embedding to the original model; (2) prune bottleneck dimensions via global structural pruning with Gaussian-normalized importance scores, then distill by aligning the pruned model's embedding features to the intermediate pruned model while gradually shifting the teacher from the intermediate model to the original model via a dynamic loss weight. To make pruning label-free and aligned with the soft-label distillation objective, the paper proposes disturbed Taylor importance, which adds zero-mean Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) to the image embeddings so that non-zero gradients can be computed, replacing the inapplicable hard-label Taylor criterion. This two-stage, dimension-preserving alternation keeps intermediate feature dimensions consistent between teacher and student at each distillation step, enabling effective hidden-state supervision even with only 10k unlabeled images.

## Results

On the SA-1B benchmark, SlimSAM-50 achieves 72.33% MIoU with only 26M parameters (4.0% of SAM-H) and 98G MACs, while SlimSAM-77 reaches 67.40% MIoU with just 9.1M parameters (1.4%) and 23G MACs. Both models are trained on merely 10k unlabeled images (0.1% of SA-1B) on a single Titan RTX GPU. SlimSAM-50 approaches the full SAM-B (73.37% MIoU, 93M params) while using 1/3.6 of the parameters and 1/1100 of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlimSAM-50 (72.33% MIoU) outperforms EfficientSAM-s (71.19%) by 1.14% while using 1,100× less training data (10k vs. 12.2M images) and 64× fewer GPUs. Against MobileSAM (62.73%) and EdgeSAM (65.96%), SlimSAM-50 leads by 9.6% and 6.4% MIoU respectively, again with 10× less data. SlimSAM-77 (67.40%) surpasses MobileSAM (62.73%) by 4.67% and EdgeSAM (65.96%) by 1.44% at a comparable parameter budget (~9M), though it trails EfficientSAM-t (69.42%) by 2.02%—a gap explained by EfficientSAM's use of 1,100× more training data. In the structural pruning comparison (Table 2), SlimSAM-77 beats Taylor Pruning (64.26%) by 3.14% and Hessian Pruning (63.56%) by 3.84% at the 77% ratio, with the gap widening to 5.8% over Magnitude Pruning (61.60%). Notably, scratch distillation collapses to 1.34% MIoU at 77% pruning, confirming that inheriting pre-trained SAM weights is essential for data-efficient compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SAM compression paradigm (FastSAM, MobileSAM, EdgeSAM, EfficientSAM) that replaces the ViT image encoder with a lightweight architecture and trains from scratch, instead inheriting pre-trained SAM weights via structural pruning followed by knowledge distillation. It builds directly on Taylor importance estimation (Molchanov et al., CVPR 2019) but modifies it into 'disturbed Taylor importance' by adding Gaussian noise to soft-label targets to resolve the zero-gradient problem inherent in encoder-output distillation. The alternate slimming framework—sequentially pruning and distilling decoupled embedding and bottleneck sub-structures—is a novel decomposition strategy not present in prior pruning or SAM compression literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in training data (10k vs. 100k+ images) while maintaining near-SAM-H performance at 1.4% parameter count makes SAM deployment on resource-constrained devices practically feasible without the massive data pipelines required by scratch-training approaches. The alternate slimming framework offers a generalizable recipe for compressing ViT-based models at extreme pruning ratios (>75%) where conventional one-step pruning collapses. However, the performance ceiling is inherently bounded by the original SAM, and the authors acknowledge that surpassing the teacher with fewer parameters remains an open challenge. The method's reliance on a single GPU and 10k unlabeled images also raises questions about whether the approach generalizes to other foundation models or tasks beyond segmentation.
