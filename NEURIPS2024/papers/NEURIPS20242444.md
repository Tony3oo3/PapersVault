## Problem

For deep linear networks trained on regression, it is known that minimizers of the empirical risk can have arbitrarily large sharpness (largest Hessian eigenvalue), but no prior work characterized the sharpness of the specific minimizer found by gradient flow. The paper addresses two linked questions: (1) what is the minimal possible sharpness among all minimizers, and (2) does gradient flow implicitly regularize towards a minimizer near this lower bound or one with much larger sharpness? This also explains the empirically observed critical learning rate beyond which training fails.

## Input / Output

The method takes a design matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and target y ∈ R^n for univariate regression (output is scalar), along with initialized weight matrices W_1,...,W_L under either a small-scale Gaussian scheme or a residual scheme W_k(0) = I + s/√(Ld)·N_k. It produces the trained weight matrices at convergence and a bound on their sharpness S(W). The setting is overdetermined (n > d with full-rank empirical covariance), and the network computes x ↦ p^T W_L...W_1 x where p ∈ R^{d_L} is a fixed projection vector.

## Method

The paper proves that gradient flow from both small-scale and residual initialization converges to a minimizer whose sharpness is at most a constant times S_min (the theoretical lower bound), where the constant depends only on the condition number Λ/λ of the data covariance, not on width or depth. For small-scale initialization, the key structural result is that learned weight matrices become approximately rank-one with aligned singular vectors across layers, established via a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition that exploits the univariate output to factor the gradient norm. For residual initialization, the paper proves global convergence for standard Gaussian initialization without assuming overparameterization (d ≥ n), using high-probability bounds on the singular values of products of residual matrices (I + s/√(Ld)·N_k + θ_k/L). The sharpness bound in both cases follows from a variational characterization of the Hessian's largest eigenvalue combined with the specific near-rank-one or near-identity structure of the found minimizer.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimal sharpness $S_{\min}$ of any minimizer of a depth-$L$ linear network grows linearly with depth: $S_{\min} \geq 2\|w^*\|_2^{2-2/L} La$ (Theorem 1). For gradient flow from small-scale initialization, the sharpness of the found minimizer satisfies $1 \leq S(\mathcal{W}^{SI})/S_{\min} \leq 4\Lambda/\lambda$ (Corollary 2), where $\Lambda/\lambda$ is the condition number of the data covariance. For residual initialization with Gaussian entries, a similar bound $1 \leq S(\mathcal{W}^{RI})/S_{\min} \leq C\Lambda/\lambda$ holds (Corollary 3). The critical learning rate beyond which training fails is predicted to be $\eta_c \simeq (\|w^*\|_2^{2-2/L} La)^{-1}$, confirmed experimentally in Figure 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard benchmark comparison table. The key extensions over prior work are: (1) Compared to Mulayoff and Michaeli (2020), the sharpness lower bound removes the data-whiteness assumption ($\hat{\Sigma}=I$) and does not require full-rank covariance, using a variational characterization of the Hessian instead of tensor algebra. (2) Compared to Ji and Telgarsky (2020) for classification, the regression setting yields finite minimizers (parameters do not diverge), requiring a finer PL-condition analysis and yielding the rank-one aligned structure with bounded norms rather than diverging weights. (3) Compared to Zou et al. (2020) and Sander et al. (2022) for residual networks, the paper proves convergence under standard Gaussian initialization (not just identity or smooth initialization) without requiring overparameterization $d \geq n$, leveraging high-probability singular value bounds for products of random matrices (Lemma 3). The implicit regularization result (sharpness within a constant factor of $S_{\min}$, independent of width and depth) is novel and not present in any of these prior works.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharpness analysis of Mulayoff and Michaeli (2020) by removing the data-whiteness assumption and providing a different proof technique that avoids tensor algebra. The small-scale initialization analysis builds on Ji and Telgarsky (2020) for classification, adapting their directional convergence argument to the regression setting where parameters remain bounded. For residual initialization, the convergence proof refines the identity-initialization analyses of Zou et al. (2020) and Sander et al. (2022) to handle standard Gaussian initialization, leveraging high-probability singular value bounds for products of random matrices. The implicit regularization result connects to the broader literature on flat minima preference (Keskar et al. 2017, Damian et al. 2021) but identifies a purely deterministic mechanism distinct from SGD stochasticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why gradient flow in deep linear networks finds flat minima without any stochasticity, showing the ratio of found sharpness to minimal sharpness is bounded by a constant times the data condition number, independent of depth and width. This gives a quantitative answer to the critical learning rate question raised by Lewkowycz et al. (2020) and connects the edge-of-stability phenomenon to the intrinsic sharpness landscape of minimizers. Key limitations include the restriction to univariate regression and linear networks; the authors explicitly note that extensions to underdetermined regression (where sharpness may link to generalization) and non-linear networks remain open. The results are primarily of theoretical interest for understanding optimization dynamics rather than practical algorithm design.
