## Problem

Gradual domain adaptation methods that sequentially adapt a classifier across a chain of unlabeled domains suffer from error propagation that grows exponentially with the number of domains T. Prior work has not established general conditions under which this propagation can be bounded or eliminated, nor has it provided a unified theoretical characterization of when gradual adaptation is fundamentally feasible. This paper asks: under what structural assumptions on the distribution sequence and classifier family can error propagation be controlled, and what is the precise mechanism that enables this control?

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source dataset P_0 of n samples and a sequence of T−1 unlabeled datasets P_1, …, P_T (each with n_i i.i.d. samples), where consecutive distributions are assumed to be within a bounded Wasserstein distance η. At each step, the previous classifier pseudo-labels the current unlabeled data, and the algorithm outputs an adapted classifier θ* whose generalization error on the final domain P_T is theoretically bounded. In experiments, this is instantiated on Rotating MNIST with 6 batches of 4200 grayscale 28×28 images rotated by 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, and 60°.

## Method

The core contribution is a DRO-based adaptation algorithm (DRODA) that, at each step i, solves a min-max problem over a Wasserstein ball restricted to a known distribution manifold G (e.g., Gaussian generative models or expandable distributions), with an adaptive adversarial radius ε_i = λΔ*_{i-1} + η that grows with the previous step's robust loss. The key theoretical tool is a newly introduced 'compatibility function' g_λ(η) that upper-bounds the manifold-constrained adversarial loss and fully characterizes error propagation: if g_λ is sublinear or saturating, error remains bounded regardless of T; if it grows linearly with slope ≥ 1/(2λ), error escalates exponentially. For the Gaussian mixture setting, the manifold constraint (restricting the Wasserstein ball to the Gaussian family) provably eliminates all error propagation, yielding a target-domain error of O(e^{−L²/18σ²}) independent of T. For the more general expandable distribution setting, the manifold constraint is implemented via a restricted dual formulation where adversarial perturbations are limited to a class of smooth mappings F whose Jacobian eigenvalues are bounded near 1, and the compatibility function is shown to be O((1 + C₁(4Rε + 2η))α), again eliminating propagation.

## Results

On the Rotating MNIST dataset (5 domains with 0°–60° rotations, 4200 samples per domain), the proposed manifold-constrained DRO method (DRODA) outperforms the GDA baseline [KML20] by a margin of 8 percentage points in accuracy on the final target domain D4 (60° rotation). The paper reports results in Figure 2 rather than a numerical table, showing accuracy curves across all intermediate domains D1–D4. In-domain accuracy of the initial classifier θ₀* on D0 is also reported as a reference point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: the Gradual Domain Adaptation (GDA) method of Kumar et al. [KML20], implemented exactly as described in the original work. On the Rotating MNIST benchmark, DRODA achieves an 8 percentage point accuracy improvement over GDA on the most challenging target domain D4 (60° rotation), which is the domain where error propagation effects are most pronounced. The advantage is attributed to the manifold constraint and adaptive Wasserstein radius in the DRO formulation, which prevent the exponential error growth that plagues naive self-training approaches as the number of sequential domains increases. No other baselines (e.g., GIFT, GVF, AuxSelfTrain) are included in the experimental comparison, limiting the breadth of the empirical validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradual domain adaptation (GDA) framework of Kumar et al. [KML20] and Wang et al. [WLZ22], which established that naive self-training leads to exponential error propagation over T domains. The key departure is the introduction of manifold-constrained Wasserstein DRO with an adaptive adversarial radius, building on the dual formulations of Blanchet & Murthy [BM19] and Gao & Kleywegt [GK23], and the expansion property from Wei et al. [WSCM20]. Rather than treating the Wasserstein ball as an unconstrained set of all measures, the paper restricts it to a distributional manifold (e.g., Gaussian mixtures or expandable distributions), which is the critical structural assumption that transforms error dynamics from exponential to bounded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete theoretical characterization of when error propagation in GDA is bounded versus exponential, via the newly introduced compatibility function. This resolves a gap explicitly identified in prior work: no previous study had established cases where error propagation remains fixed or sublinear. Open questions include how to practically identify or verify the manifold constraint in real-world settings, whether the constrained DRO optimization remains tractable beyond the Gaussian and expandable distribution cases studied here, and whether the results extend to multi-class or non-binary settings. The experimental validation is limited to a single dataset (Rotating MNIST) and a single baseline, leaving the practical generality of the approach somewhat untested.
