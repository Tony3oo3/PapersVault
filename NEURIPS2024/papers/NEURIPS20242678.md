## Problem

Whether DPP-based coresets can provably achieve strictly smaller cardinality than independently sampled coresets remained open: both approaches were known to yield O(ε^{-2}) coresets. This paper settles the question affirmatively by showing that DPPs with appropriate kernels achieve O(ε^{-2/(1+δ)}) for δ>0, and provides the concentration machinery to make this rigorous.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite dataset of n points in a Euclidean space, an n×n DPP kernel matrix (Hermitian or non-symmetric) encoding the negative dependence structure, and a function class F of queries (either a finite-dimensional vector space or a Lipschitz-parametrized family). It outputs a random subset S of expected size m with weights ω(x)=1/K(x,x) (inverse marginal inclusion probability), together with a high-probability uniform approximation guarantee over F. The concentration inequalities are stated for general Polish spaces and bounded test functions, with the range of valid ε depending on the variance and sup-norm of the test function.

## Method

The central structural insight is that the coreset loss L_S(f) is a linear statistic Λ(ωf) of the random point set S, which allows the authors to replace the general Lipschitz-functional concentration of Pemantle–Peres with sharper inequalities tailored to linear statistics. They prove three concentration theorems: for Hermitian kernels on general Polish spaces (Theorem 1, extending Breuer–Duits beyond finite-rank projection kernels), for non-symmetric kernels on finite ground sets (Theorem 2, via a novel Laplace-transform control), and for vector-valued statistics (Theorem 3, via a union bound over coordinates). Applying these to the coreset problem via covering-number arguments over the function class yields Theorem 4: under a variance bound V=O(m^{-(1+δ)}), the coreset size needed for ε-accuracy is O(ε^{-2/(1+δ)}), strictly beating the O(ε^{-2}) of i.i.d. sampling. The concrete instantiation uses the discretized multivariate OPE (Theorem 6), where δ=1/d, giving a provable m^{-(1/2+1/2d)} accuracy rate versus m^{-1/2} for independent sampling.

## Results

On the synthetic uniform dataset (n=1024, d=2), the two i.i.d. baselines (uniform and sensitivity) decay at the expected m^{-1/2} rate, while the discretized OPE and Vdm-DPP achieve a faster m^{-3/4} decay in the 90th-quantile worst-case relative error Q_S(0.9). The stratified baseline achieves the optimal m^{-1} rate on this uniformly-spread dataset. On MNIST (PCA-reduced to d=4), the DPP-based methods (OPE and Vdm-DPP) again outperform the i.i.d. baselines, though the advantage progressively vanishes as the PCA dimension increases beyond 4. Theoretically, the paper proves that DPP coresets can achieve cardinality m = O(ε^{-2/(1+δ)}) for some δ > 0, strictly beating the O(ε^{-2}) bound of independent sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six coreset samplers on k-means across three datasets. Against the sensitivity-based importance sampling baseline of Langberg & Schulman (2010), the DPP methods (OPE, Vdm-DPP, G-mDPP) all achieve a strictly faster decay rate in worst-case relative error, with OPE and Vdm-DPP reaching approximately m^{-3/4} versus the m^{-1/2} of sensitivity sampling. Against the G-mDPP and Vdm-DPP samplers from Tremblay et al. (2019), the discretized OPE (from Bardenet et al., 2021) performs comparably or slightly better, and is the only method for which the paper provides a sharp theoretical guarantee (Theorem 6). The stratified sampling baseline (Novak, 1988) dominates on the uniform dataset with an m^{-1} rate but is inapplicable to non-uniformly-spread data (trimodal, MNIST). On MNIST at PCA dimension 4, the OPE and Vdm-DPP methods clearly outperform both i.i.d. baselines, but the gap narrows and disappears at higher dimensions, consistent with the 1/d dependence in the theoretical variance reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tremblay et al. (2019), who proposed DPPs for randomized coreset construction and proved an O(ε^{-2}) cardinality bound using concentration of Lipschitz functionals (Pemantle and Peres, 2011), but could not show a strict improvement over independent sampling. The key departure is a structural re-interpretation of the coreset loss as a linear statistic of the random point set, which unlocks sharper concentration inequalities (Theorems 1–3) that go beyond the Lipschitz-functional framework to handle general Hermitian, non-symmetric, and vector-valued kernels. The discretized multivariate OPE (Bardenet, Ghosh, et al., 2021) provides the concrete DPP whose variance scaling O(m^{-(1+1/d)}) is the engine behind the improved coreset size, and the non-symmetric kernel results extend the theoretical toolbox for DPPs used in recommendation systems (Gartrell et al., 2019; Han et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a central open question in the DPP-coreset literature by proving that negative dependence via DPPs yields a strictly smaller coreset (O(ε^{-2/(1+δ)}) vs. O(ε^{-2})) than independent sampling, providing the theoretical justification that was missing for deploying DPP-based methods in practice. The concentration inequalities for linear statistics of DPPs are broadly applicable beyond coresets, extending the state of the art to non-symmetric and vector-valued settings that are relevant to SGD minibatch estimation and recommendation systems. Key limitations temper the practical impact: the improvement scales as 1/d and vanishes in high dimensions, the discretized OPE requires a density estimate of the data-generating distribution, and DPP sampling still costs O(nm²). Open questions include dimension-independent rates (possibly via smoothness assumptions), extending guarantees to m-DPPs and Vdm-DPPs, and reducing the sampling complexity.
