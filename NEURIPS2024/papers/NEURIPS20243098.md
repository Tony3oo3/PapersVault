## Problem

Reconstructing large-scale 3D scenes (geometry, appearance, semantics) from sparse, non-overlapping images remains unsolved: per-scene optimization methods (NeRFs, Gaussian Splatting) require dense view coverage and cannot extrapolate beyond input views, while feed-forward methods that use learned priors have only succeeded on single objects at low resolution. No prior approach produces sharp, high-resolution reconstructions spanning hundreds of meters from just a few views in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as few as 3 posed RGB images (1920×1280) from an ego-centric multi-camera rig with known intrinsics and extrinsics, where views have little or no overlap. It outputs a VoxSplat scene — millions of 3D Gaussians anchored to a 1024³ sparse voxel grid covering a 102.4 m × 102.4 m local chunk — plus a sky panorama texture, all produced in under 20 seconds on a single GPU. From this representation, the system can render novel views, simulate LiDAR point clouds via ray-tracing, or serve as a 3D scene generator conditioned on text prompts.

## Method

SCube is a two-stage feed-forward pipeline. Stage 1 (geometry) uses a hierarchical sparse-voxel latent diffusion model (built on XCube) conditioned on DINOv2 image features that are unprojected into 3D via a depth-distribution-weighted accumulation (LSS-style), producing a 1024³ sparse voxel hierarchy with semantic logits; a focal depth loss explicitly supervises the depth weighting. Stage 2 (appearance) is a 3D sparse convolutional U-Net that predicts M Gaussians per voxel (center offset, opacity, scale, quaternion, RGB) constrained within 3× the voxel size of their supporting grid cell, plus a sky panorama built by projecting image features onto a unit sphere. The key insight is that grounding Gaussians on a learned sparse-voxel scaffold (VoxSplats) preserves geometric structure without per-scene optimization heuristics, while the diffusion prior fills in occluded regions that are invisible from the sparse input views. An optional per-scene GAN postprocessor (SCube+) further sharpens renderings at the cost of ~20 min of additional training.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, SCube achieves 25.90 PSNR / 0.77 SSIM / 0.45 LPIPS on the input frame (T) and 18.78 PSNR / 0.70 SSIM / 0.49 LPIPS on the T+10 prediction frame, outperforming all baselines by a considerable margin. With the optional GAN postprocessing (SCube+), performance improves to 28.01 PSNR / 0.81 SSIM / 0.25 LPIPS at T and 21.09 PSNR / 0.72 SSIM / 0.38 LPIPS at T+10. The method reconstructs a 1024³ voxel grid spanning hundreds of meters with millions of Gaussians in under 20 seconds from as few as 3 non-overlapping input images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCube outperforms all five baselines on PSNR and LPIPS across all three evaluation settings (T, T+5, T+10). The closest competitor is MVSplat, which achieves 21.84 PSNR at T versus SCube's 25.90 (a 4.06 dB gap), and 18.78 PSNR at T+10 versus SCube's 18.78 (tied on PSNR but SCube leads on LPIPS: 0.49 vs 0.52). MVSGaussian achieves the highest SSIM at T (0.80 vs SCube's 0.77) but degrades sharply at T+10 (16.42 PSNR vs SCube's 18.78), indicating poor generalization to extreme novel views. PixelNeRF and DUSt3R are substantially weaker, with PSNR below 18 at T, reflecting their inability to capture high-frequency scene details or handle sparse low-overlap views. PixelSplat, which relies heavily on overlapping input regions, collapses geometry to constant depths in the sparse-view setting, yielding 22.15 PSNR at T but failing to produce coherent 3D structure as shown in top-down visualizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCube builds directly on XCube's hierarchical sparse-voxel latent diffusion model as its geometry backbone, extending it with a novel DINOv2-based image conditioning mechanism that uses depth-distribution-weighted feature accumulation (inspired by Lift-Splat-Shoot) to handle sparse, low-overlap outdoor views. It departs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by grounding Gaussians on a predicted voxel scaffold rather than optimizing free-floating splats per scene, and from prior feed-forward reconstruction methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat, DUSt3R) by operating at 1024³ resolution over hundreds of meters rather than on single objects or small indoor scenes. The two-stage decomposition—diffusion-based geometry followed by feed-forward appearance prediction—is a deliberate architectural choice that prior work had not combined at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCube demonstrates that large-scale outdoor scene reconstruction from as few as 3 sparse, non-overlapping images can be done in a single 20-second forward pass, removing the per-scene optimization bottleneck that has limited NeRF and Gaussian Splatting to dense-view settings. The VoxSplat representation (Gaussians constrained to a voxel scaffold) is a clean, reusable architectural idea that could influence future work on scalable 3D scene generation. Key open questions remain: the method still requires ground-truth 3D training data (LiDAR-accumulated), struggles with dynamic scenes and extreme weather, and the ~80% occluded-voxel fraction means appearance in unseen regions carries inherent uncertainty. Whether the data-prior approach generalizes beyond the Waymo driving domain to arbitrary outdoor scenes without LiDAR supervision is an important next step.
