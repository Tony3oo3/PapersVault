## Problem

Previous 3D segmentation approaches are tailored to individual tasks (panoptic, semantic, instance, interactive, referring, or open-vocabulary), limiting scene understanding to a task-specific perspective and failing to exploit intrinsic geometric and semantic consistency across tasks. The only prior unified method, OneFormer3D, covers just three generic segmentation tasks and cannot handle user-conditioned or open-set segmentation. This paper asks whether all six core 3D segmentation tasks can be unified in a single model without task-specific customized modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241898/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N points with xyz coordinates and RGB colors), optional point-click prompts for interactive segmentation, and optional natural language expressions for referring segmentation. The point cloud is downsampled into M superpoints via a sparse 3D U-Net; vision prompts are encoded by sampling the superpoint feature at the clicked location, and text prompts are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder projected to the feature dimension. Outputs are per-superpoint segmentation masks (mapped back to point-wise predictions) and class predictions that regress CLIP text-embedding features of class names rather than discrete class IDs, enabling open-vocabulary generalization.

## Method

UniSeg3D uses a single Transformer-based mask decoder (6 layers of self-attention + cross-attention) that processes three types of queries—unified queries (randomly sampled superpoint features for generic and open-vocabulary tasks), vision prompt queries, and text prompt queries—distinguished only by learned task-specific embeddings, with no task-specific architectural branches. The key insight is that interactive segmentation, which benefits from reliable spatial priors, produces the highest-quality masks (76.0 mIoU vs. 68.1 for instance segmentation); the paper exploits this by designing knowledge distillation that transfers mask predictions from interactive to generic and referring tasks, and ranking-based contrastive learning (inspired by CrowdCLIP) that aligns vision-text feature pairs to reduce the modality gap in referring segmentation. For open-vocabulary training, SAM3D generates pseudo open-set masks to supervise negative samples without label leakage. A two-stage fine-tuning trick (reducing learning rate and weight decay by 1000× for 40 additional epochs) further boosts all tasks, and the entire inter-task association machinery is training-only, incurring zero extra inference cost.

## Results

UniSeg3D achieves SOTA or near-SOTA performance across all six 3D segmentation tasks in a single model: 71.3 PQ on panoptic (ScanNet20), 76.9 mIoU on semantic, 59.3 mAP on instance, 54.5 AP on interactive, 29.6 mIoU on referring (ScanRefer), and 19.7 AP on open-vocabulary (ScanNet200). With the two-stage fine-tuning trick, it improves over the base model by 0.6 mIoU on semantic and 0.4 AP on interactive segmentation. The method outperforms task-specific SOTA by 12.1 PQ (panoptic vs. PanopticNDT), 1.2 mIoU (semantic vs. OctFormer), 0.9 mAP (instance vs. MAFT), 1.0 AP (interactive vs. AGILE3D), and 0.7 AP (OV vs. Open3DIS). Inference time is 230 ms total, competitive with single-task methods like OneFormer3D (221 ms) while delivering six tasks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSeg3D outperforms all task-specific SOTA baselines: it surpasses PanopticNDT by 12.1 PQ on panoptic segmentation, OctFormer by 1.2 mIoU on semantic, MAFT by 0.9 mAP on instance, AGILE3D by 1.0 AP on interactive, and Open3DIS by 0.7 AP on open-vocabulary segmentation. Against the closest unified competitor OneFormer3D (CVPR 24), UniSeg3D achieves +0.1 PQ on panoptic and +0.3 mIoU on semantic while additionally supporting interactive, referring, and open-vocabulary tasks that OneFormer3D cannot handle. On referring segmentation, UniSeg3D scores 29.6 mIoU using only 72.4% of the ScanRefer training data (filtered to avoid label leakage), compared to X-RefSeg3D's 29.9 mIoU on the full set; when X-RefSeg3D is re-evaluated on the same filtered data, UniSeg3D leads by 4.1 mIoU. The method also achieves competitive instance segmentation (59.3 mAP), matching OneFormer3D exactly and exceeding MAFT (58.4) and QueryFormer (56.5). On the Replica zero-shot benchmark, UniSeg3D attains 19.1 AP, outperforming Open3DIS (18.5) and OpenMask3D (13.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSeg3D directly extends OneFormer3D (CVPR 2024), which unified three generic 3D segmentation tasks (panoptic, semantic, instance) via a shared query-based transformer over superpoints, by adding interactive, referring, and open-vocabulary segmentation to reach six tasks in a single model. The architectural backbone (sparse 3D U-Net + superpoint pooling + mask decoder) follows the established OneFormer/OneFormer3D lineage, while the novel contribution lies in the inter-task association mechanism: knowledge distillation from the high-performing interactive task to other tasks, and ranking-based contrastive learning between vision and text prompt features (inspired by CrowdCLIP). The use of frozen CLIP for text encoding and SAM3D for open-set pseudo-labels connects the framework to broader vision-language and foundation-model trends in 3D understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify six 3D segmentation tasks in one inference, which is practically significant for applications (robotics, AR, domotics) that require multiple segmentation capabilities simultaneously without running separate specialized models. The key open question is whether the inter-task association strategy generalizes beyond indoor scenes and whether the framework can scale to additional tasks or modalities. A notable limitation is the substantial performance drop in interactive segmentation when vision prompts are placed at object edges rather than centers (up to 20 AP), suggesting the method's reliance on prompt quality remains a practical bottleneck. The small margins over specialized SOTA (0.1–4.1 points) indicate that the unified framework is competitive but not yet dominant, leaving room for further gains in the multi-task setting.
