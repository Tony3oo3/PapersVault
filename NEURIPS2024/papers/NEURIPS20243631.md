## Problem

State-of-the-art trigger inversion-based backdoor defenses (e.g., BTI-DBF) suffer from two critical shortcomings: high computational overhead from iterating over all target classes, and an over-reliance on prominent backdoor features that are highly distinguishable from benign features in the feature space. The paper shows that this reliance causes failures on input-space attacks like BadNets, where backdoor features are weak and not easily separable, and identifies that BTI-DBF's decoupling step lacks a mask regularizer, causing the learned mask to collapse into a dense all-ones matrix that ignores the negative (backdoor) feature branch.

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to the suspect model's weights and a small set of local clean samples (1% of training data for detection, 5% for defense) that were not used to train the model. Detection produces a binary verdict (backdoored or clean) by measuring the attack success rate of the model under adversarial neuron noise with the negative feature mask applied. If backdoored, the defense outputs a fine-tuned model with the backdoor removed, evaluated by both benign accuracy (BA) and attack success rate (ASR). The approach is architecture-agnostic, tested on ResNet18, VGG16, DenseNet121, Swin Transformer, and a 4-layer MLP across CIFAR-10, GTSRB, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet200.

## Method

BAN is a two-step detection method. First, it generates adversarial neuron noise by multiplicatively perturbing every neuron's weight and bias (scaling by (1+δ) and (1+ξ)) to maximize cross-entropy loss on clean data, solved via 30-step PGD within an ε-ball constraint; this noise activates backdoor neurons (which create shortcuts to the target class) differently from benign neurons. Second, it learns a feature-space mask m on the latent representation (pre-classification layer) via an optimization that minimizes the loss on masked (benign) features while maximizing the loss on unmasked (backdoor) features, with an L1 regularizer to prevent mask collapse; the negative mask (1−m) is then applied to the noisy model's latent features to amplify the backdoor effect. A model is flagged as backdoored if the resulting predictions show a high attack success rate concentrated on the target label. For defense, BAN fine-tunes the model with a combined loss (Eq. 8) that simultaneously minimizes standard cross-entropy and the loss under the optimized neuron noise, thereby activating and erasing the backdoor during training while preserving clean accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BAN achieves an average detection success rate of 97.22% across ResNet18, VGG16, and DenseNet121, outperforming the closest baseline BTI-DBF* (86.39%) by 10.83 percentage points. BAN is 1.37× faster than BTI-DBF on CIFAR-10 (55.95s vs 76.90s) and 5.11× faster on ImageNet200 (1,132.85s vs 5,792.51s). For backdoor removal, BAN reduces average attack success rate to 2.21% while maintaining 92.43% benign accuracy, compared to 24.47% ASR for BTI-DBF(U) and 47.45% for FeatureRE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAN outperforms all five baselines on average detection accuracy (97.22% vs. 86.39% for BTI-DBF*, 78.61% for Unicorn, 72.50% for FeatureRE, 60.56% for NC, and 59.17% for Tabor). The gap is most pronounced on advanced feature-space attacks: NC and Tabor achieve 0% detection on IAD and Bpp, while BAN reaches 100% on both. BTI-DBF* is the strongest competitor, matching BAN on most attacks but dropping to 90% on BadNets (ResNet18) and 70% on WaNet (DenseNet121), where BAN maintains 100% and 95% respectively. On the defense side, BAN reduces average ASR to 2.21% versus 24.47% for BTI-DBF(U) and 47.45% for FeatureRE, while preserving higher benign accuracy (92.43% vs. 90.71% and 93.39%). In terms of efficiency, BAN is 1.37× faster than BTI-DBF on CIFAR-10 and 5.11× faster on ImageNet200, and 5.32× faster than FeatureRE on CIFAR-10 with ResNet18.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trigger inversion-based backdoor detection paradigm by incorporating adversarial neuron noise into the feature-space decoupling step. It directly builds on BTI-DBF [44] by identifying and fixing a flaw in its mask optimization (the absence of an L1 regularizer causes the mask to collapse to all-ones), and draws on prior observations that backdoored models are more sensitive to adversarial perturbations [43, 10] and that neuron noise can activate backdoor shortcuts [41]. Conceptually, it departs from the assumption that feature-space separability alone suffices for detection, instead arguing that parameter-level adversarial perturbation provides a more robust signal that does not depend on the prominence of backdoor features in the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art feature-space defenses (BTI-DBF, FeatureRE, Unicorn) underperform the older input-space method Neural Cleanse on simple attacks like BadNets challenges the implicit assumption that feature-space methods are strictly superior, and highlights a generalization gap in the current defense landscape. The practical efficiency gain (1.37×–5.11× faster than BTI-DBF) makes the approach more deployable at scale. However, the method still requires white-box access and a small set of local clean samples, and the fixed feature mask is acknowledged as not always aligned with true benign/backdoor boundaries—both remain open problems for future work.
