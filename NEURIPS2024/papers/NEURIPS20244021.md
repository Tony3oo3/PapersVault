## Problem

RL agents trained on offline interaction data struggle to generalize to novel or rephrased goals, while existing LLM+RL approaches focus on high-level text-based planning and fail to address low-level numerical control. The core gap is the semantic mismatch between LLMs' text-based token representations and the numerical vector states/actions that physical environments operate on, preventing LLMs from directly contributing to low-level skill acquisition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of 100,000 rollout-goal pairs (state-action sequences in R^91/R^10 paired with natural language goals) plus a set of novel goal descriptions spanning three difficulty levels (rephrased, unseen-easy, unseen-hard). The fine-tuned LLM outputs imaginary rollouts as sequences of numerical state and action vectors, which are then combined with real data at equal proportion to train a goal-conditioned policy (BERT-encoded goal + state → action distribution). The LLM's input/output layers are replaced with learnable MLPs (E_S, E_A for inputs; O_S, O_A for outputs) to bridge the dimensional gap between D-dimensional token embeddings and the environment's vector spaces.

## Method

KALM operates in three stages. First, LLM grounding: Llama-2-7b is architecturally modified with state/action embedding and output MLPs, then supervised fine-tuned on three tasks—dynamics prediction (s_t, a_t → s_{t+1}), rollout explanation (sequence → natural language description), and rollout generation (goal → sequence)—treating each as an instruction-following problem. Second, rollout generation: the fine-tuned LLM is prompted with goal-oriented prompts to produce imaginary rollouts for 1,400+ novel goals, leveraging its pre-trained world knowledge to handle tasks absent from the offline data (e.g., 'gather all balls together' or navigating around unseen obstacles). Third, skill acquisition: a standard offline RL algorithm (CQL, TD3+BC, etc.) trains the policy on the union of real and imaginary rollouts at equal batch proportions. The key insight is that LLMs, after grounding, can generate physically plausible state-action sequences for novel goals, effectively expanding the offline dataset's coverage without environment interaction.

## Results

On CLEVR-Robot, KALM achieves a 46% success rate across 1,400 novel goals, significantly outperforming the 26% success rate of baseline offline RL methods. Specifically, CQL+KALM reaches 27.1% average success rate on novel tasks in CLEVR-Robot, compared to 15.5% for CQL alone. The improvement is most pronounced on unseen (hard) tasks, where baseline methods show minimal progress while KALM-equipped methods achieve substantially higher success rates. On Meta-world, similar gains are observed across all goal novelty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five standard offline RL baselines (PRDC, BC, CQL, TD3+BC, AWAC) show clear performance degradation on rephrasing and unseen goals compared to tasks in the offline data, confirming limited generalizability without LLM-generated rollouts. When augmented with KALM, every method improves substantially on novel goals; CQL+KALM achieves 27.1% vs CQL's 15.5% on novel CLEVR-Robot tasks. COMBO, which uses ensemble environment models learned from scratch, performs competitively on seen tasks and rephrasing goals but fails on unseen (easy) and unseen (hard) tasks due to lack of generalization. KALM also outperforms both the 'LLM as policy' baseline (where the fine-tuned LLM directly outputs actions) and Decision Transformer (DT) with Llama-2-7b as backbone, suggesting that using LLM-generated rollouts as training data is more effective than using the LLM as a direct policy. The overall 46% vs 26% success rate gap on 1,400 novel goals underscores that the benefit of KALM grows with task novelty and complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

KALM carves new ground at the intersection of offline RL and LLMs by repurposing a pre-trained LLM not as a policy or high-level planner (as in GALM, LLaRP, or LLM-Planner) but as a *data generator* that produces plausible state-action rollouts for novel goals. This departs from model-based offline RL (COMBO, MOPO, MOReL), where environment dynamics models are learned from scratch and lack general world knowledge; KALM instead leverages the LLM's pre-trained knowledge to imagine trajectories for tasks never seen in the dataset. The supervised fine-tuning step (dynamics prediction, rollout explanation, rollout generation) is a novel grounding mechanism that bridges the modality gap between LLMs' text-based architecture and numerical environmental vectors, a problem prior LLM-for-RL works have largely sidestepped by operating at the text level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway for injecting LLM world knowledge into low-level robotic control, showing that a 7B LLM can generate sufficiently plausible rollouts to improve offline RL success rates by 20 percentage points on novel goals. However, significant limitations remain: the method only handles vector states (no visual observations), the LLM must simultaneously model both states and actions (increasing its burden), and full fine-tuning risks degrading the LLM's embedded general knowledge. Open questions include whether separating state and action modeling into two LLMs would improve accuracy, whether the approach scales to high-dimensional visual inputs, and whether the hallucination rates observed in generated rollouts (up to ~65% out-of-bounds in Meta-world) can be further reduced without sacrificing the diversity that makes them useful.
