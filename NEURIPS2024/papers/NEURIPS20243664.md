## Problem

Predicting behavioral outcomes under previously unseen combinations of sequential categorical interventions is ill-posed for generic black-box sequence models, which lack explicit structural guarantees for generalizing to novel intervention strings in large, sparse, combinatorial action spaces. The paper asks: what compositional structure on the causal generative model, and what data conditions, are sufficient to identify the combined effect of interventions that may never have co-occurred jointly in training data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each unit n, the model consumes time-invariant covariates Z_n, the observed behavioral history X_n^{1:t-1}, the observed intervention history D_n^{1:t-1} (categorical values in N with 0 as the idle/default level), and a partially hypothetical future intervention sequence do(d_n^{1:t+Δ}). It outputs the conditional mean E[X_n^{t+δ} | x_n^{1:t-1}, z_n, do(d_n^{1:t+δ})] for 0 ≤ δ ≤ Δ, plus distribution-free prediction intervals via split conformal prediction. A key practical constraint is that most intervention entries are the default 'no change' level, making the effective action space sparse and the combinatorial space of sequences exponential in T.

## Method

The core model factorizes the conditional mean as E[X_n^t | ·] = Σ_l φ_nl^t · β_nl · ψ_nl^t, where φ encodes behavioral history via learned basis functions, β_n is a unit-level latent feature vector, and ψ_nl^t = Π_{t'=1}^t ψ_l(d_n^{t'}, t', t) is a recursive product capturing how each past intervention's effect evolves over time (either as free time-bounded parameters or as a sigmoid-gated exponential decay). This 'compositional warping' structure means each intervention multiplicatively modifies the unit's latent feature vector, enabling recombination of effects from individually observed interventions. Training uses CSI-VAE: β_n is treated as a random effect with a Gaussian prior, an amortized GRU+MLP variational posterior is optimized via the ELBO, and φ is parameterized by a separate GRU+MLP, all trained end-to-end. Identification is guaranteed under explicit data conditions (a burn-in period of ≥ r default steps, at least r units receiving each novel intervention, and sufficient temporal separation between interventions), formalized in Theorem 1. Conformal prediction with a likelihood-ratio-weighted distribution-shift bound (Theorem 2) provides finite-sample coverage guarantees even under non-exchangeable calibration and test sets.

## Results

On the fully-synthetic benchmark, CSI-VAE-1 achieves RMSE of 36.53 at T+1, outperforming the strongest black-box baseline GRU-2 (93.73) by 2.6×, and LSTM (114.71) by 3.1×. On the semi-synthetic Spotify benchmark, CSI-VAE-1 achieves RMSE of 68.23 at T+1 versus LSTM (130.35) and GRU-2 (154.18), a roughly 2× improvement. All pairwise comparisons are statistically significant (p < 0.001) across all five prediction horizons on both datasets. Ablations confirm that both the multiplicative product structure (CSI-VAE-2) and the compositional recursion in ψ (CSI-VAE-3) are essential: removing either degrades performance to near or below baseline levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSI-VAE-1 dominates all three baseline families (GRU, LSTM, Transformer) across every prediction horizon on both the fully-synthetic and semi-synthetic Spotify benchmarks, with all differences statistically significant at p < 0.001. Against the strongest GRU variant (GRU-2, which feeds the full intervention history), CSI-VAE-1 reduces RMSE from 93.73 to 36.53 on the fully-synthetic task (T+1) and from 154.18 to 68.23 on Spotify (T+1). LSTM is the closest black-box competitor on the Spotify benchmark (130.35 vs. 68.23 at T+1), but still trails by nearly 2×; the Transformer is competitive at T+5 on the fully-synthetic set (87.89) but degrades sharply at intermediate horizons (175.84 at T+4). The ablation studies further show that replacing the multiplicative composition with a black-box MLP (CSI-VAE-2) or replacing the compositional recursion in ψ with a generic sequence model (CSI-VAE-3) causes performance to collapse to or below baseline levels, confirming that the explicit compositional structure—not model capacity—is the source of the generalization advantage in sparse interventional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends causal matrix factorization (Athey et al., Agarwal et al.) from imputing missing potential outcomes in fixed past windows to predicting novel compositions of sequential interventions, using the factorization trick from Kaddour et al. as a theoretical foundation. It departs from black-box sequential models (GRUs, LSTMs, Transformers) by imposing an explicit compositional structure—recursive functional composition for intervention effects and elementwise warping of unit-level latent features—that yields identifiability guarantees. The exponential-decay effect model draws on Bayesian structural time series (Brodersen et al.), while the conformal prediction component adapts likelihood-ratio weighting under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al.) to the sequential interventional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit identifiability conditions (Theorem 1) for compositional sequential interventions, clarifying exactly how much data (burn-in period, number of units per intervention, temporal separation) is needed to reliably extrapolate to unseen intervention combinations. The empirical finding that both the product structure and the compositional recursion are individually critical (ablations CSI-VAE-2 and CSI-VAE-3) supports the broader claim that structural inductive bias, not model flexibility, drives generalization in sparse combinatorial action spaces. Key limitations include the assumption that no intervention level is repeated, time-translation invariance, and sequential ignorability; open questions include handling unmeasured confounding, parameter drifts, and extending to cross-sectional combinations of actions within a single time step.
