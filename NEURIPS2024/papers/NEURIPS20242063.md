## Problem

Existing visual anomaly detection (VAD) datasets target controlled manufacturing objects and textures, failing to capture the diverse, evolving, and multi-grade anomalies found on real-world power line cables. Additionally, standard VAD methods require a diverse validation set of both nominal and anomalous images to calibrate detection thresholds, which is prohibitively expensive and impractical in specialized industrial settings where anomalies are rare and varied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1920×1080 RGB images of power line cables captured by a moving camera apparatus with a uniform background, mimicking robotic inspection conditions. Enhanced-PatchCore produces an image-level anomaly score (max nearest-neighbor distance in embedding space) and a pixel-level segmentation map obtained by upscaling per-patch scores via bilinear interpolation. The zero-shot VLM baselines take the image plus a fixed text prompt and output a binary yes/no anomaly classification, with anomaly scores derived from the probability of the 'Yes' token via VQAScore.

## Method

The paper introduces CableInspect-AD, a dataset of 4,798 images and 6,023 annotated anomalies across three cable types, seven defect categories, and multiple severity grades, annotated by Hydro-Québec domain experts at image, bounding-box, and pixel level. The key algorithmic contribution is Enhanced-PatchCore, which modifies the standard PatchCore pipeline by computing anomaly scores of nominal images against the memory bank itself (excluding self-patches) to estimate the empirical score distribution of normal data, thereby eliminating the need for a separate validation set to set the detection threshold. Four thresholding strategies (max, boxplot whisker, beta-prime-95, empirical-95) are evaluated on this estimated distribution. A k-fold cross-validation protocol based on defect identifiers prevents data leakage from overlapping video frames while maintaining consistent training set sizes. The paper further benchmarks open conversational VLMs (LLaVA, CogVLM, BakLLaVA) and WinCLIP in a zero-shot setting to assess whether large pre-trained models can detect cable anomalies without any task-specific training.

## Results

On CableInspect-AD (image-level, 100-shot for Enhanced-PatchCore with beta-prime-95 thresholding; zero-shot for VLMs), CogVLM-17B achieves the highest F1-score (0.77±0.02), while CogVLM2-19B attains the best AUPR (0.91±0.02) and AUROC (0.86±0.03) with the lowest FPR (0.04±0.01). Enhanced-PatchCore achieves a competitive F1 of 0.75±0.03, AUPR of 0.84±0.06, and AUROC of 0.78±0.05, outperforming all VLMs except CogVLM-17B on F1. For pixel-level segmentation on CableInspect-AD_cropped, Enhanced-PatchCore achieves AUPRO of 0.53±0.08 versus 0.27±0.06 for WinCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Enhanced-PatchCore (100-shot) outperforms LLaVA 1.5-7B, LLaVA 1.5-13B, and BakLLaVA-7B on all reported metrics (F1, AUPR, AUROC), with F1 gains of 6–16 points over the LLaVA variants. Against CogVLM-17B, Enhanced-PatchCore trails slightly on F1 (0.75 vs. 0.77) and FPR (0.55 vs. 0.34) but matches or exceeds it on AUPR (0.84 vs. 0.83). Against CogVLM2-19B, Enhanced-PatchCore wins on F1 (0.75 vs. 0.66) but is substantially outperformed on AUPR (0.84 vs. 0.91), AUROC (0.78 vs. 0.86), and FPR (0.55 vs. 0.04), suggesting CogVLM2-19B's ranking is strong but its thresholding is suboptimal. Compared to WinCLIP, Enhanced-PatchCore leads on both AUPR (0.84 vs. 0.76) and AUROC (0.78 vs. 0.70), and additionally provides pixel-level segmentation (AUPRO 0.53 vs. 0.27 on the cropped variant). Overall, no single method dominates all metrics, and all models struggle with light-grade anomalies such as long scratches and spaced strands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-based anomaly detection lineage of PatchCore by introducing Enhanced-PatchCore, which eliminates the need for a validation set to calibrate the detection threshold—a practical gap that prior methods (including PatchCore itself) leave unaddressed in real-world deployment. On the dataset side, it departs from the controlled-manufacturing focus of MVTec AD, VisA, and MVTec LOCO AD by targeting power line cable inspection, a domain where existing public datasets either exclude cables entirely or address only detection/segmentation rather than multi-grade defect annotation. The zero-shot evaluation with open conversational VLMs (LLaVA, CogVLM, BakLLaVA) and WinCLIP positions the work at the intersection of the recent VLM-for-VAD trend and the need for accessible, reproducible baselines, as opposed to proprietary models like GPT-4V used in prior zero-shot VAD studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CableInspect-AD is the first public, expert-annotated dataset covering multi-grade anomalies on power line cables, filling a clear gap for the industrial VAD and robotic inspection communities. The key open question the results surface is why all evaluated models—both few-shot PatchCore variants and zero-shot VLMs—systematically miss light-grade anomalies (long scratches, spaced strands), suggesting that current feature representations or VLM attention mechanisms are insufficient for subtle, texture-level defects. The finding that background removal improves PatchCore but degrades VLM performance is a practical caveat for pipeline design. Limitations include an artificially high anomaly ratio relative to real-world conditions and the absence of certain environmental anomalies (snow, bird droppings), which future versions or complementary datasets could address.
