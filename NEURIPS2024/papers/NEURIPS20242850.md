## Problem

Existing channel-adaptive Vision Transformers (e.g., ChannelViT) rely on random channel masking during training to build robustness to missing channels, but this encourages redundant feature learning across channels rather than capturing unique, channel-specific information. The result is a model that over-relies on a few easy-to-identify channels while ignoring distinctive cues in others, limiting both accuracy and robustness when channels are absent at test time.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-channel images with a variable number of channels (3–18 depending on the dataset) where any subset of the training channels may be present at inference. Each channel is processed through a shared linear projection and concatenated with a learned channel token that encodes the channel configuration. For classification datasets (JUMP-CP, So2Sat) the output is a class label via a [CLS]-token classifier head; for CHAMMI the [CLS] token serves as a feature representation evaluated with a 1-NN classifier.

## Method

DiChaViT extends the ChannelViT architecture (shared patch projection, per-channel tokens, ViT-S backbone) with three diversity-promoting mechanisms. First, a Channel Diversification Loss (CDL) inspired by ProxyNCA++ uses orthogonally initialized channel anchors to pull each channel token toward its own anchor while pushing it away from all others, preventing channel-token collapse. Second, a Token Diversification Loss (TDL) enforces near-orthogonality on patch tokens via separate cosine-similarity terms for same-channel and cross-channel token pairs, with a larger weight on the cross-channel term to make features from different channels more distinct. Third, Diverse Channel Sampling (DCS) replaces the uniform random masking of HCS by first selecting an anchor channel and then sampling k−1 additional channels with probability proportional to softmax((1 − cosine_similarity)/t), preferentially selecting dissimilar channels to encourage the model to learn complementary information from each channel set.

## Results

DiChaViT achieves 69.68 average F1 on CHAMMI (vs. 64.90 for the prior SOTA ChannelViT, a +4.8% gain), 69.19/57.98 top-1 accuracy on JUMP-CP Full/Partial channels (vs. 67.51/56.49), and 63.36/47.76 on So2Sat Full/Partial (vs. 61.03/46.16). The improvement ranges from 1.5% to 5.0% points across all three datasets and both full and partial channel evaluation settings. On the partial-channel robustness evaluation (JUMP-CP, 7-of-8 channels), DiChaViT maintains 61.91% accuracy versus 60.36% for ChannelViT, with consistently lower variance across channel combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiChaViT outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor ChannelViT [18], it gains +4.8% on CHAMMI (69.68 vs. 64.90), +1.7% on JUMP-CP Full (69.19 vs. 67.51), +1.5% on JUMP-CP Partial (57.98 vs. 56.49), +2.3% on So2Sat Full (63.36 vs. 61.03), and +1.6% on So2Sat Partial (47.76 vs. 46.16). The advantage is most dramatic against ChAda-ViT [15] on partial-channel settings: DiChaViT scores 57.98 vs. 42.15 on JUMP-CP Partial (+15.8%) and 47.76 vs. 12.38 on So2Sat Partial (+35.4%), indicating that the diversity-promoting components (DCS, CDL, TDL) are critical for robustness when channels are missing. Against the CNN-adapted baselines (DepthwiseViT, TemplateMixingViT, HyperNetViT), DiChaViT leads by 8–15% on CHAMMI and 3–12% on JUMP-CP/So2Sat, confirming that the ViT-based channel-adaptive design combined with feature diversification is substantially more effective than depthwise convolution, template mixing, or hypernetwork approaches for heterogeneous multi-channel inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiChaViT builds directly on ChannelViT (Bao et al., ICLR 2024), which introduced channel tokens, a shared patch projection, and Hierarchical Channel Sampling (HCS) for robustness to missing channels in multi-channel imaging. The key departure is that prior MCI methods (ChannelViT, ChAda-ViT) rely on uniform random channel masking, which the authors show induces harmful redundancy across channel tokens. DiChaViT replaces HCS with Diverse Channel Sampling (DCS) and adds two diversity-promoting losses (CDL inspired by ProxyNCA++, and TDL enforcing orthogonality among patch tokens), positioning itself as a principled balance between the redundancy needed for robustness and the distinctiveness needed for informative per-channel features. The paper also distinguishes itself from disentangled representation learning by arguing that MCI requires *some* cross-channel redundancy, unlike fully disentangled settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic nature of the three components (CDL, TDL, DCS) makes them drop-in improvements for any future MCI-ViT architecture, which is practically valuable as the field evolves. The ablation revealing that DCS is by far the most critical component for partial-channel robustness (30-point drop on So2Sat) suggests that *how* you sample channels during training matters more than the specific diversity losses, a finding that could redirect future MCI training strategies. Key limitations include the inability to handle truly novel channels at test time and the need for additional hyperparameter tuning (λ_CDL, λ_s, λ_d, t_DCS), which the authors acknowledge as open problems.
