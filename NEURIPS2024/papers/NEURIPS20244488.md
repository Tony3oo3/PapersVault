## Problem

Reconstructing answers to marginal queries from noisy measurements is a critical subproblem in differentially private query answering, but existing methods either require exponential complexity in the number of variables, rely on parametric representations that restrict the space of representable distributions, or involve non-convex optimization. No prior method performs the standard pseudoinverse reconstruction for marginal queries efficiently, and there is no principled way to enforce non-negativity constraints during reconstruction to reduce error.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either noisy residual measurements (with arbitrary Gaussian covariance) or noisy marginal measurements (with isotropic Gaussian noise) along with a workload of marginal queries specifying which attribute subsets to answer. When given marginal measurements, the algorithm first decomposes each noisy marginal into a set of independent noisy residual measurements via a Kronecker-structured linear transformation before reconstruction. The output is a vector of reconstructed marginal answers for each attribute subset in the workload, optionally constrained to be locally non-negative (i.e., non-negative on the workload marginals rather than on the full data vector).

## Method

ReM (Residuals-to-Marginals) exploits the Kronecker product structure and mutual orthogonality of residual query matrices to decompose the pseudoinverse of a combined residual workload into per-residual operations, each computable in O(|γ|n_γ) time via structured matrix-vector products. GReM-MLE combines multiple noisy measurements of the same residual using inverse-variance weighting (a closed-form MLE under Gaussian noise) before applying the Kronecker-structured reconstruction. GReM-LNN extends this by solving a convex program with linear non-negativity constraints on the reconstructed marginals, using a dual ascent algorithm where each round requires only Kronecker-structured matrix multiplications. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3) shows that noisy marginal measurements with isotropic Gaussian noise can be equivalently decomposed into independent noisy residual measurements, making the standard pseudoinverse reconstruction M_γ M_Q⁺ y tractable for the first time without exponential blowup.

## Results

The paper evaluates GReM-LNN as a post-processing step on two mechanisms (ResidualPlanner and Scalable MWEM) across four datasets (Titanic, Adult, Salary, Nist-Taxi) and five privacy budgets (ε ∈ {0.1, 0.31, 1, 3.16, 10}, δ=1×10⁻⁹), measuring average ℓ₁ workload error on all 3-way marginals. Averaging over all settings and trials, GReM-LNN reduces ResidualPlanner's workload error by a factor of 44.0× and Scalable MWEM's error by 12.3×. Compared to simple non-negativity heuristics, GReM-LNN achieves 17.6× lower error than Trunc and 3.2× lower error than Trunc+Rescale on ResidualPlanner, and 1.1× lower error than Trunc+Rescale on Scalable MWEM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResidualPlanner [6] (Xiao et al., NeurIPS 2024), GReM-LNN reduces average ℓ₁ workload error by 44.0× across all datasets and privacy budgets, substantially outperforming both the Trunc heuristic (17.6× better) and Trunc+Rescale (3.2× better). GReM-LNN dominates Trunc+Rescale in all settings except ε=0.1 on Titanic and Salary. Against Private-PGM [12] (McKenna et al., ICML 2019) in the Scalable MWEM setting, Private-PGM achieves the greatest error reduction where it successfully runs, but it fails due to exceeding 20 GB memory at 30 rounds on Adult, Salary, and Nist-Taxi in all trials, making it infeasible for the larger datasets. GReM-LNN reduces Scalable MWEM's base error by 12.3× and marginally outperforms Trunc+Rescale (1.1×), while remaining computationally tractable across all tested datasets. The paper notes that GReM-MLE is omitted from the ResidualPlanner comparison because ResidualPlanner's reconstruction is already the maximum likelihood estimate for its measurements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ResidualPlanner [6], which introduced residual queries and exploited Kronecker structure for efficient pseudoinverse-based reconstruction in the matrix mechanism setting. ReM generalizes ResidualPlanner's reconstruction to arbitrary sets of residual measurements, multiple noisy observations per residual, and non-Gaussian noise, while GReM-LNN adds a local non-negativity constraint via a convex program solved by dual ascent. The efficient marginal pseudoinversion (EMP) result is the first to show that the standard pseudoinverse reconstruction used by the matrix mechanism [4] and HDMM [5] is tractable for marginal query workloads, avoiding the exponential complexity that has motivated parametric approaches like PrivBayes [8], GEM [9], and Private-PGM [12].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44× error reduction over ResidualPlanner and 12.3× over Scalable MWEM demonstrate that a simple post-processing step with non-negativity constraints can dramatically improve utility without additional privacy cost, making it immediately applicable to existing deployments. The efficient pseudoinversion result removes a key scalability barrier for high-dimensional marginal query answering. Open questions include the heuristic weighting scheme in GReM-LNN (2^|τ| vs. noise-scale weights), the lack of synthetic data generation (limiting downstream ML use), and the absence of continuous-data support. The relationship between residual weights, optimizer convergence, and reconstruction quality remains poorly understood.
