## Problem

Existing visuomotor control frameworks for long-horizon robotic manipulation follow an open-loop paradigm, executing fixed action sequences without verifying whether the actual trajectory aligns with the planned one, leading to error accumulation and poor robustness. While a few approaches introduce feedback via pixel differences or pre-trained visual representations (e.g., CLIP features), their error quantification is imprecise and their adaptability is limited to narrow task settings, leaving accurate, generalizable error measurement in a measurable latent space unexplored.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single third-view RGB-D image as the current observation and a natural language task description as input; notably, no proprioception signals or gripper-view images are used. The visual planner produces a sequence of K=8 RGB-D video frames as sub-goals, while the policy outputs a 7-DoF relative cartesian action (6D end-effector position plus 1D gripper state) per timestep. The closed-loop execution adaptively determines how many action steps to take before transitioning to the next sub-goal or triggering a replan, rather than using a fixed step count.

## Method

CLOVER comprises three tightly coupled modules: (1) a text-conditioned RGB-D video diffusion model (built on Imagen's UNet with causal temporal attention and cross-attention language conditioning) that generates geometry-aware sub-goals, regularized by an RAFT-based optical flow loss to enforce inter-frame motion consistency; (2) a feedback-driven policy with a shared multimodal ViT encoder (RGB + depth via squeeze-and-excitation fusion) and a token aggregator that produces compact state embeddings, where the error between current and goal states is computed as element-wise subtraction of these embeddings; (3) a closed-loop execution strategy that uses cosine distance thresholds to adaptively transition between sub-goals upon achievement and to detect unreachable plans (triggering replanning) by measuring inter-frame distances in the generated video. The key insight is that state embeddings must be trained with an explicit inverse-dynamics objective to develop the quantitative measurement properties needed for reliable error feedback, rather than relying on pre-trained visual encoders whose feature spaces lack the monotonicity and dynamic range required for sub-goal tracking.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, CLOVER achieves an average completed task length of 3.53 (out of 5), with 96.0% success on task 1 and 45.4% on task 5, outperforming the previous best Planner+Executor method SuSIE (2.69) by 31% and the best overall baseline 3D Diffuser Actor (3.27) by 8%. On real-world long-horizon manipulation, CLOVER reaches an average length of 2.1 sub-tasks completed, nearly doubling RT-1's 1.1. The method also demonstrates superior generalization under visual distractions (1.47 vs. 0.47 for RT-1) and dynamic scenes (1.53 vs. 0.33 for RT-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOVER outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest Planner+Executor competitor SuSIE [16], CLOVER improves average completed task length from 2.69 to 3.53 (+31%) and 5-task success from 26.0% to 45.4%. Versus the strongest non-planner baseline 3D Diffuser Actor [51] (avg. len. 3.27), CLOVER gains +0.26 in average length and +4.2 pp on 5-task completion. Among behaviour-cloning baselines, CLOVER surpasses GR-1 [50] (3.06) and RoboFlamingo [49] (2.48) by substantial margins, and dominates RT-1 [48] (0.90) by nearly 4×. On real-world tasks, CLOVER (avg. len. 2.1) nearly doubles RT-1 (1.1) and outperforms ACT [52] (0.6) and R3M [53] (0.7) by 3.5× and 3× respectively. For error measurement, CLOVER's learned state embeddings (3.53) far exceed CLIP features [23] (1.63) and LIV [55] (1.77), confirming that inverse-dynamics-trained embeddings provide superior quantitative feedback. On video generation quality, CLOVER surpasses AVDC [31] on all metrics (SSIM 0.858 vs. 0.837, FID 12.00 vs. 12.74, LPIPS 0.062 vs. 0.086), with the optical flow regularization contributing the largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOVER extends the 'visual planner + inverse dynamics executor' paradigm established by UniPi, SuSIE, and ADVC by closing the loop with a feedback mechanism inspired by classic control theory. The visual planner architecture is derived from Imagen's UNet-based diffusion framework, augmented with RGB-D channel concatenation and an optical flow regularization term adapted from RAFT. The key conceptual departure from prior open-loop planners is the introduction of a measurable embedding space—trained via an inverse dynamics objective—where element-wise subtraction of current and goal state embeddings yields a reliable error signal, replacing the pixel-level or CLIP-based distance metrics used in earlier closed-loop attempts (Visual Foresight, Inner Monologue).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental weakness of current visual-planning approaches: the absence of real-time error correction in long-horizon manipulation, where open-loop execution leads to compounding deviations. The +8% gain on CALVIN and nearly doubled real-world task completion over RT-1 demonstrate that feedback is not merely a theoretical nicety but a practical necessity for robust deployment. A notable limitation is that both the diffusion planner and IDM policy are trained heavily on task-specific data; the authors note that web-scale pre-training of the planner and data-efficient IDM learning could unlock few-shot generalization, but this remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the measurable embedding property generalizes to multi-robot or cross-morphology settings and whether the fixed replanning threshold (D_R=1.0) holds across diverse manipulation domains.
