## Problem

Previous brain-decoding studies have focused almost exclusively on reconstructing static visual stimuli (single images) from fMRI, MEG, or EEG, despite the fact that our visual experience is dominated by dynamic change. No prior work has attempted to decode video from EEG signals, leaving open whether dynamic visual perception can be recovered from high-temporal-resolution brain activity and, if so, what specific visual information (category, color, motion, object count, human presence) is decodable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2-second, 62-channel EEG segment (recorded at 1000 Hz, downsampled to 200 Hz) and produces a 2-second reconstructed video at 512×288 resolution and 3 FPS (6 frames), along with a semantic category label and a fast/slow dynamic label. The EEG is consumed via a 500 ms overlapping sliding window (250 ms overlap) yielding 7 temporal embeddings that feed the Seq2Seq module, while the original 1080p 24 FPS source videos are downsampled to match the 6-frame output. The SEED-DV dataset provides 1400 EEG-video pairs across 40 concepts from 20 subjects, with each video clip being 2 seconds long.

## Method

EEG2Video combines three key components: (1) a GLMNet encoder that concatenates global features from all 62 channels with local features from occipital (visual cortex) electrodes to produce EEG embeddings; (2) a Transformer-based Seq2Seq model that maps the 7 temporally-ordered EEG embeddings to 6 video latent variables (obtained via a frozen VAE encoder), trained with MSE loss to densely align high-temporal-resolution brain signals with frame-level visual content; and (3) a Dynamic-Aware Noise-Adding (DANA) module that modulates the diffusion process by blending static noise (same noise replicated across frames) with diverse noise (independent per-frame noise) at a ratio determined by a decoded fast/slow dynamic label. A separate MLP semantic predictor aligns EEG to CLIP text embeddings (77×768) for conditioning, and the final video is generated by a TuneA-Video inflated Stable Diffusion model (with sparse temporal attention linking each frame to the first and previous frame) fine-tuned on BLIP-captioned video-text pairs from the training set.

## Results

On the EEG-VP benchmark (SEED-DV dataset, 20 subjects, 40 concepts), GLMNet achieves 6.20% top-1 accuracy on the 40-class concept classification task (chance: 2.50%), 21.93% on the 9-class coarse classification, 27.33% on color, and 57.35% on Fast/Slow dynamics — all statistically significant above chance. For video reconstruction on the full 40-class set, EEG2Video achieves 79.8% 2-way video-level semantic accuracy, 15.9% 40-way video-level accuracy, and 0.256 SSIM; on the 10-class subset these improve to 85.2%, 34.0%, and 0.300 respectively. The SSIM of 0.256 is comparable to or slightly exceeds fMRI-based video reconstruction reported in prior work (NeuroCine).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLMNet consistently outperforms all five baselines (ShallowNet, DeepNet, EEGNet, Conformer, TSConv) across every classification task on the EEG-VP benchmark. The largest margins appear on the 40-class top-1 task, where GLMNet reaches 6.20% versus 5.59% (ShallowNet), 4.93% (Conformer), 4.92% (TSConv), 4.64% (EEGNet), and 4.56% (DeepNet) — a 0.6–1.6 percentage point improvement. On the Fast/Slow dynamics task, GLMNet achieves 57.35%, beating the next-best ShallowNet (56.62%) by 0.73 points and EEGNet (51.99%) by 5.36 points. On the Color task, Conformer slightly edges out GLMNet (27.53% vs 27.33%), but the difference is within one standard deviation. For the harder tasks (Numbers, Human Face, Human), all methods including GLMNet perform at or near chance level, indicating these visual attributes remain largely undecodable from EEG with current approaches. The consistent advantage of GLMNet over baselines that treat all channels equally validates the importance of explicitly modeling occipital (visual cortex) channels alongside global features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction pipeline (MinD-Video, NeuroCine) to EEG signals, leveraging EEG's high temporal resolution (1000 Hz vs. fMRI's ~0.5 Hz) to capture rapid visual dynamics. The key architectural departure is replacing the single-frame-to-video mapping with a Seq2Seq Transformer that aligns multiple overlapping EEG segments to multiple video frames, complemented by a Dynamic-Aware Noise-Adding (DANA) module that modulates the diffusion process based on decoded motion information. The video generation backbone follows TuneA-Video's inflated diffusion approach, while the EEG encoder (GLMNet) introduces a global-local channel mixture to highlight occipital visual cortex features, building on standard EEG classifiers like EEGNet and ShallowNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first EEG-video dataset and benchmark, opening a previously unexplored research direction for decoding dynamic visual perception from a portable, low-cost neuroimaging modality. The spatial analysis confirming occipital involvement for object recognition and temporal region involvement for motion perception provides neuroscientific validation. However, the results remain preliminary: 40-class accuracy is only 6.2% (chance 2.5%), SSIM is 0.256, evaluation is subject-dependent with a single session per subject, and cross-subject generalization is unexplored. The field still needs to determine whether EEG's limited spatial resolution (~60 sensors) fundamentally caps reconstruction quality or whether better architectures can close the gap with fMRI-based methods.
