## Problem

Existing GNN-based cognitive diagnosis methods learn student, exercise, and concept representations on a single unified graph, causing exercise and concept representations to be contaminated by noisy student interactions and failing to provide robustness against interaction noise. Additionally, lower-order exercise latent representations from shallow GNN layers are not effectively leveraged when learning student representations, limiting their robustness to data sparsity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242904/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three structured inputs: binary student-exercise response logs (r_ij ∈ {0,1}), a Q-matrix encoding exercise-concept containment (M×K binary), and a concept dependency matrix (K×K binary). These are organized into a tripartite interaction graph with N students, M exercises, and K concepts. The output is a predicted response probability per student-exercise pair, from which per-concept proficiency estimates are derived via the diagnostic function's concept-wise similarity computation.

## Method

DisenGCD disentangles the unified interaction graph into three subgraphs: the full student-exercise-concept interaction graph G_I for student representation, the exercise-concept relation graph G_R for exercise representation, and the concept dependency graph G_D for concept representation, making the latter two interaction-agnostic. Student representations are learned via a modified meta multigraph aggregator: a directed acyclic graph of P=5 hyper-nodes where each inter-node edge carries up to 7 learnable propagation path types (student→exercise, exercise→student, exercise→concept, concept→exercise, concept→concept, identity, zero), enabling the current student latent vector to access lower-order exercise representations from any previous layer. Exercise and concept representations are learned by L-layer GAT networks on their respective disentangled graphs. A novel diagnostic function computes per-concept mastery via dot-product similarity between student and exercise concept-conditioned vectors, then averages over concepts in the Q-matrix. The entire model is trained via bilevel optimization: inner-loop minimizes cross-entropy on training data, outer-loop optimizes propagation path weights on validation data.

## Results

On ASSISTments (60%/10%/30% split), DisenGCD achieves AUC 0.7723, ACC 0.7335, and RMSE 0.4219, outperforming the strongest baseline RCD (AUC 0.7663, ACC 0.7291, RMSE 0.4262). On the Math dataset under the same split, DisenGCD reaches AUC 0.7867, ACC 0.7527, and RMSE 0.4039, again surpassing RCD (AUC 0.7806, ACC 0.7501, RMSE 0.4078). DisenGCD consistently achieves the best results across all four splitting ratios (40/10/50, 50/10/40, 60/10/30, 70/10/20) on both datasets, with the largest AUC gains over RCD appearing at the 70/10/20 split (0.7746 vs. 0.7687 on ASSISTments; 0.7932 vs. 0.7866 on Math).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisenGCD outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both primary datasets. Against the closest GNN-based competitor RCD [7], DisenGCD gains approximately 0.6–0.7 AUC points (e.g., 0.7723 vs. 0.7663 on ASSISTments, 0.7867 vs. 0.7806 on Math at 60/10/30), with the gap widening under higher noise (up to ~1.5 AUC points at 50% noise). Against NN-based baselines NCD [25] and ECD [48], the AUC advantage is larger (~1.7–1.9 points), confirming the benefit of graph-based representation learning. Against classical models DINA [4] and MIRT [20], DisenGCD leads by 6.5 and 2.5 AUC points respectively on ASSISTments. In the appendix comparison (Table 6) on Math, DisenGCD (AUC 0.7932) also surpasses SCD [28] (0.7882), KaNCD [26] (0.7854), and KSCD [15] (0.7890), with the margin over KSCD being the smallest (~0.4 AUC points). DisenGCD never loses to any baseline on any reported metric or dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the meta multigraph search framework from heterogeneous information network recommendation (PMMM, AAAI 2023) to cognitive diagnosis, repurposing its learnable propagation paths to let student representations access lower-order exercise embeddings. It departs from existing GNN-based CDMs (RCD, SCD) that learn student, exercise, and concept representations jointly on a unified interaction graph, instead proposing a disentangled architecture where exercise and concept representations are learned on separate subgraphs free of student interaction noise. The disentanglement strategy draws conceptually from disentangled representation learning (DisenGCN, DisenHAN, DcRec) but applies it to a different purpose: isolating noise-sensitive interaction data from structural relation learning rather than extracting multi-faceted node features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the robustness advantage of DisenGCD over RCD grows monotonically with noise level (peaking at 50% noise), providing concrete evidence that disentangling structural representations from interaction data is a principled defense against noisy student logs—a practical concern in real educational platforms. However, the learned meta multigraph structures do not generalize across structurally different datasets (e.g., ASSISTments multigraphs fail on SLP), limiting the approach's transferability. The authors also acknowledge high computational complexity (cubic in node count for the meta multigraph aggregation) and poor scalability to large datasets, which constrains deployment. Open questions include whether lighter-weight propagation path selection or learned sparsity could reduce complexity while preserving the robustness gains.
