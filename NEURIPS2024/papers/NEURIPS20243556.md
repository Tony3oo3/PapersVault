## Problem

Existing multimodal sleep staging methods assume all physiological signal modalities (EEG, EOG, EMG) are complete, yet real-world clinical recordings frequently suffer from chunk-based modality dropout due to sensor malfunction or detachment, causing sharp performance degradation. Additionally, prior temporal models are confined to single-level (intra-epoch or inter-epoch) context mining, and Transformers lack the recurrent modeling capacity of RNNs needed to capture structural and positional information in sleep sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal physiological epochs (30-second segments) with a binary mask matrix Z indicating per-epoch, per-modality availability, where at least one modality must be present. Missing patterns are chunk-based (random dropout over multiple consecutive epochs), with missing rates up to 0.5 for two-modality datasets and 0.7 for three-modality datasets. Beyond the final sleep stage prediction (5-stage W/N1/N2/N3/REM or 3-stage W/NREM/REM), the model also outputs recovered complete modality data via the imputation module, and operates on context windows of T=25 epochs (T=20 for the MHR wearable dataset).

## Method

CIMSleepNet comprises three coupled components. First, the Modal Awareness Imagination Module (MAIM) uses modality-specific separable temporal CNN encoders/decoders with a VAE-style reparameterization trick: instead of collapsing to a single shared representation, it learns a distribution (mean and variance) over multimodal shared features, so that decoded reconstructions retain data diversity. Second, the Semantic & Modal Calibration Contrastive Learning (SMCCL) loss introduces a three-level similarity weight matrix—same category+modality (strongest), same category only, and same modality only—where the relative weights of the latter two levels are dynamically set by inter-modal mutual information scores, thereby aligning the recovered data distribution with the real one while preserving modality-specific information. Third, the Multi-level Cross-branch Temporal Attention (MCTA) mechanism embeds two parallel branches (temporal CNN and Bi-GRU) at both intra-epoch and inter-epoch levels, splicing their keys and values for cross-branch attention; the inter-epoch level reverses the branch roles relative to intra-epoch to enable complementary feature fusion. The total training objective combines cross-entropy classification loss, the MAIM reconstruction+KL loss (weight α=0.001), and the SMCCL distribution alignment loss (weight β=0.01).

## Results

On the incomplete modality setting (the paper's primary evaluation), CIMSleepNet achieves 85.3% accuracy and 0.805 Cohen's Kappa on Sleep-EDF-20 at 50% missing rate, 83.0% accuracy on Sleep-EDF-78, 78.8% accuracy on SVUH-UCD (70% missing), and 70.1% accuracy on MHR — consistently outperforming all baselines by 10–25 percentage points in accuracy. In the completely missing modality case on SHHS, CIMSleepNet reaches 90.3% accuracy with EEG+EOG, surpassing CoRe-Sleep (89.5%). Under complete modalities, CIMSleepNet matches or slightly exceeds state-of-the-art methods (e.g., 86.7% Acc on Sleep-EDF-20 vs. 87.2% for SalientSleepNet), confirming no performance sacrifice for robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIMSleepNet decisively outperforms all eight multimodal ASS baselines under incomplete modalities. On Sleep-EDF-20 at 50% missing, it achieves 85.3% accuracy versus 63.4% for the strongest baseline (SalientSleepNet) and 62.3% for XSleepNet — a gap of over 20 points. TransSleep is the best baseline on Sleep-EDF-78 (73.8% Acc) and SVUH-UCD (72.5% Acc), but CIMSleepNet still leads by 9.2 and 6.3 points respectively. On the MHR dataset (wearable sensors), CIMSleepNet (70.1% Acc) surpasses DeepCNN (61.6%) and MLP (61.0%), with a particularly large Kappa gap (0.240 vs. 0.039). In the completely missing modality case on SHHS, CIMSleepNet beats CoRe-Sleep by 0.9–0.8 points in accuracy across all test modality configurations (EEG, EOG, EEG+EOG). Qualitatively, SMCCL recovers data distributions more faithfully than ICL and SCL, as shown by t-SNE visualizations where SMCCL-recovered samples cluster more consistently with original data.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIMSleepNet synthesizes three distinct research threads: (1) VAE-based multimodal imputation (drawing on MVAE's reparameterization trick to learn distributions rather than point estimates for missing modality recovery), (2) contrastive learning for missing modalities (extending ICL/SCL by introducing a three-level similarity framework weighted via inter-modal mutual information), and (3) hybrid RNN-Transformer temporal modeling (combining Bi-GRU recurrent features with self-attention in a cross-branch mechanism). It departs from CoRe-Sleep, the only prior ASS method handling missing modalities, by targeting the more realistic chunk-based partial missing pattern rather than complete absence of one or more modalities. The MCTA mechanism is motivated by the observation that RNN and Transformer features are complementary, a finding supported by several prior works in activity recognition and traffic forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world clinical PSG recordings frequently suffer from sensor dropout or detachment, yet nearly all existing multimodal ASS methods assume complete data. The chunk-based missing formulation is more representative of actual data collection interruptions than the complete-absence setting handled by prior work. Limitations include the additional computational overhead of the three-component architecture (37.7 MB, 2.9 GFLOPs), the restriction to supervised settings (label missing is not addressed), and evaluation primarily on small-to-medium datasets (Sleep-EDF-20 has only 20 subjects). Open questions include whether the mutual-information-weighted contrastive levels generalize to higher missing rates or more than three modalities, and whether unsupervised or semi-supervised variants could simultaneously handle both modality and label missingness.
