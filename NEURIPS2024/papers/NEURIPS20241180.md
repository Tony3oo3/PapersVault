## Problem

Pearson's r has long been considered incapable of measuring nonlinear monotone dependence, leading researchers to adopt entirely different frameworks (rank-based, kernel-based, distance-based) for such relationships. While van den Heuvel and Zhan (2022) showed Pearson's r should not be ruled out a priori for nonlinear monotone cases, no concrete adjustment to make it accurate in that regime had been proposed. This paper fills that gap by identifying a tighter covariance bound that, when substituted into Pearson's r, extends its capture range from linear to arbitrary monotone dependence.

## Input / Output

The method takes two numerical samples x = (x₁,…,xₙ) and y = (y₁,…,yₙ) with finite nonzero variance and produces a single scalar r# ∈ [−1, +1]. Unlike rank-based measures, it operates on the original numerical values rather than their ranks, preserving magnitude information. The computation requires sorting both samples (ascending or descending depending on the sign of the sample covariance) and computing the covariance between the sorted versions, which serves as the denominator.

## Method

The core contribution is a sharpened Cauchy-Schwarz inequality derived from rearrangement theorems: |cov(X,Y)| ≤ |cov(X↑, Y↓)| ≤ √(var(X)var(Y)), where X↑ and Y↓ denote increasing/decreasing rearrangements (quantile-function transforms). The rearrangement correlation r# = cov(X,Y) / |cov(X↑, Y↓)| replaces the geometric-mean-of-variances denominator in Pearson's r with this tighter rearrangement-based bound. Because the tighter bound is achieved exactly when X and Y are monotone dependent (not merely linearly dependent), the equality condition of r# = ±1 expands from linear to all monotone relationships, while r# still reverts to Pearson's r in the linear case. The approach retains the covariance structure of Pearson's r, giving it higher resolution than rank-based measures (which lose magnitude information) while being directly comparable to r since |r#| ≥ |r| always holds with matching signs.

## Results

Across 50 simulated monotone scenarios, r# achieves the lowest MAE (0.060) compared to all eight baselines, with Spearman's ρ (0.102) and dCor (0.127) as the next best. On five NIST real-life scenarios, r# achieves an MAE of 0.00141, substantially outperforming ρ (0.0159) and all other measures. In 16 non-monotone scenarios, r# (MAE 0.418) is outperformed by ξ (0.141), MIC (0.157), and dCor (0.364), but still beats the three classical coefficients ρ (0.431), r (0.437), and τ (0.461).

## Baselines & Comparisons

r# decisively outperforms all eight baselines in both the 50-scenario simulated monotone evaluation and the 5 NIST real-life evaluation, achieving the lowest MAE in both settings. Against the three classical coefficients, r# beats Pearson's r (0.060 vs. 0.150 MAE simulated; 0.00141 vs. 0.0916 real-life), Spearman's ρ (0.060 vs. 0.102; 0.00141 vs. 0.0159), and Kendall's τ (0.060 vs. 0.157; 0.00141 vs. 0.0779). Against the Additivity Coefficient r+ and HSIC, the margin is overwhelming (e.g., 0.060 vs. 0.263 and 0.518 in simulation). In the non-monotone setting, r# loses to Chatterjee's ξ (0.418 vs. 0.141), MIC (0.418 vs. 0.157), and dCor (0.418 vs. 0.364), confirming its design scope is limited to monotone dependence. However, r# still edges out ρ, r, and τ even in non-monotone scenarios, and the paper notes that r# is not robust to outliers (inherited from covariance), whereas ρ is.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Pearson's r (1896) and the rearrangement theorems of Hardy, Littlewood, and Polya (1952), deriving a tighter covariance inequality than the classic Cauchy-Schwarz bound. It departs from the entire lineage of nonlinear correlation alternatives (Spearman's ρ, Kendall's τ, HSIC, dCor, MIC, Chatterjee's ξ), which all abandon Pearson's covariance-based framework in favor of radically different approaches. The paper also extends the recent challenge by van den Heuvel and Zhan (2022) to the 'Pearson's r is only linear' myth by providing the concrete mechanism — a rearrangement-based tighter bound — that makes this potential operational.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that a tighter bound on covariance expands (rather than shrinks) the capture range from linear to arbitrary monotone dependence offers a principled, minimal adjustment to the most widely-used correlation coefficient in science, preserving backward compatibility (r# reverts to r in linear cases). However, the measure inherits covariance's fundamental limitation: it cannot detect non-monotone dependence (e.g., Y = X²), and it is not robust to outliers. Open questions include whether the rearrangement-bound philosophy can be extended to multivariate settings or to non-monotone dependence, and whether the equivalence between r# on ranks and Spearman's ρ suggests further unifications among rank-based and value-based measures.
