## Problem

Foundation models like CLIP are evaluated almost exclusively on prediction accuracy, ignoring whether the visual evidence behind a correct prediction is actually valid. A model can predict the right class for the wrong reason (e.g., identifying a red traffic light based on red balloons in the background), making it unsafe for high-stakes deployment. This paper formalizes and addresses the 'double-correct prediction' problem: ensuring both the prediction and the rationale (grounded in valid visual evidence) are simultaneously correct.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and text prompts (category names or individual rationale phrases) as input, with a maximum text context length of 77 tokens. Beyond standard class labels, the method produces per-rationale explanation heatmaps that localize the visual evidence supporting each rationale, and supports rationale-based text-to-image retrieval where a rationale phrase (e.g., 'long neck') retrieves images across categories. The structured rationale dataset encodes each of the 1,000 ImageNet categories as an ontology tree of maximum depth two (category → attributes → sub-attributes), yielding over 4,000 unique rationale strings in JSON format.

## Method

The approach has two components. First, a structured rationale dataset is generated via GPT-4 with in-context learning, producing ontology trees (max depth 2) for all 1,000 ImageNet categories, validated by human and machine evaluators (964/1000 categories score ≥4.0 on a 5-point scale). Second, a rationale-informed optimization method is built on a faithful explanation function that decomposes ViT image embeddings into per-token contributions (Eq. 2) and weights each layer's contribution by its mean-ablation performance drop (Eq. 3), yielding O(n) complexity versus O(n²) for gradient-weighted attention. The training objective augments the standard InfoNCE contrastive loss with two self-supervised constraints solved via KKT Lagrange multipliers: a disentanglement constraint requiring different rationales within the same category to produce distinct visual evidence, and a reconstruction constraint requiring the aggregated rationale embeddings to align with the category text embedding. This requires no pixel-wise annotations, no architectural changes, and no extra parameters beyond the Lagrange multipliers.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across nine benchmark datasets, the proposed method achieves 73.1% average accuracy, outperforming the second-best model (CLIP at 70.5%) by 2.6%. For linear probe evaluation, it reaches 87.5% average accuracy, surpassing the runner-up (FILIP at 85.5%) by 2.0%. In fine-tuning settings, the method outperforms the best fine-tuned baseline by 10.1% on zero-shot and 5.3% on linear probe. For rationale correctness, the method improves localization mIoU by 7.5% on CUB-Part (21.9 vs. 14.4 for CLIP) and rationale disentanglability by 36.5% on average across nine datasets (0.785 vs. 0.420 for CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines across every evaluation metric. On zero-shot classification, it beats CLIP (70.5%) by 2.6% and FILIP (69.6%) by 3.5% in average accuracy; on linear probe, it surpasses FILIP (85.5%) by 2.0% and NegCLIP (83.8%) by 3.7%. For rationale localizability on CUB-Part, the method achieves 21.9 mIoU versus 14.4 for CLIP and 13.9 for NegCLIP, a 7.5% absolute gain. On rationale disentanglability, the method reaches 0.785 average versus 0.420 for CLIP and 0.404 for NegCLIP, a 36.5% improvement. Among explanation faithfulness baselines (Table 1), the method achieves 76.27 Pixel Acc and 58.04 mIoU, outperforming TextSpan (75.21/54.50), GradCAM (70.27/44.50), and LRP (52.81/33.57). Notably, unlike most interpretability methods that sacrifice accuracy, the rationale-informed optimization simultaneously improves both prediction and rationale correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-ViT contrastive learning framework by adding a constrained optimization layer (via KKT conditions) that enforces rationale disentanglement and localization simultaneously with prediction accuracy. It builds directly on Gandelsman et al.'s (ICLR 2023) token-level decomposition of ViT outputs, modifying it with mean-ablation-based layer weighting to produce more faithful explanations. The approach departs from prior interpretability work (concept bottleneck models, post-hoc attribution methods) that either compromises accuracy or fails to ground explanations in valid visual evidence, and from knowledge-augmentation methods (NegCLIP, PyramidCLIP) that inject information only through the language modality without constraining the visual side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'double-correct prediction' formulation addresses a genuine gap in safety-critical deployment: a model that predicts correctly for the wrong reasons is arguably more dangerous than one that predicts incorrectly. The structured rationale dataset (4,000+ rationales across 1,000 ImageNet categories) is a reusable resource, and the method's plug-and-play integration (no architectural changes) lowers adoption barriers. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of LLM-generated ontologies, computational intensity, and evaluation restricted to ViT-B/32 on ImageNet-scale data; whether the approach scales to larger models or open-vocabulary settings remains open.
