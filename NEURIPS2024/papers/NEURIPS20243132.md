## Problem

Existing diffusion-based 3D human pose estimation methods operate in continuous 3D space, requiring large search spaces and substantial training data, which makes them prone to generating biomechanically unrealistic poses—especially under occlusion where inferring 3D structure from 2D images is most ambiguous. Compositional token representations capture joint dependencies but treat pose estimation as a classification task that ignores occlusion effects, leaving uncertainty unresolved. The paper addresses both gaps by combining discrete pose tokens with a diffusion process that explicitly models occlusion in latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D RGB image (frame-based, no temporal context) and produces 3D joint coordinates (J×3) for all body joints. The image is encoded by a frozen ViTPose backbone into a conditional feature sequence, while the 3D pose is represented as N=100 discrete tokens (each an index into a codebook of size 7×5×5×5×5=4375 entries) that are decoded back to continuous coordinates by a pose decoder. Evaluation uses MPJPE and PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M (indoor) and 3DPW/3DPW-Occ (in-the-wild), plus an adversarial occlusion protocol (3DPW-AdvOcc) with textured patches of size 40 or 80 on 256×192 images.

## Method

Di² Pose is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (pose quantization) uses a VQ-VAE-like encoder-decoder with Local-MLP blocks that capture local joint interactions via a Joint Shift operation (shifting features along joint-connection directions by ±⌊X/2⌋ units), followed by Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) to produce N=100 discrete tokens, confining the search space to physically plausible configurations learned from real poses. Stage 2 (discrete diffusion) models these tokens in latent space using a custom occlude-and-replace transition matrix: in the forward process, each token is probabilistically replaced by an OCC token (simulating occlusion) or resampled uniformly (capturing indeterminacy), while the reverse process uses a 21-layer transformer pose denoiser with AdaLNorm and multi-head cross-attention to the image features to reconstruct tokens step-by-step. The key insight is that by working in a discrete token space where each token corresponds to a learned sub-structure prototype, the diffusion process inherently restricts outputs to biomechanically valid poses, and the occlude-and-replace schedule implicitly teaches the model how occlusions affect pose estimation without explicit augmentation or priors.

## Results

On Human3.6M, Di²Pose achieves 49.2 mm average MPJPE, outperforming the SOTA diffusion model DiffPose (49.7 mm) and PCT (50.8 mm). On the in-the-wild 3DPW dataset, Di²Pose reaches 79.3 mm MPJPE and 50.1 mm PA-MPJPE, surpassing DiffPose by 3.4 mm and 3.7 mm respectively. Under the adversarial occlusion protocol (3DPW-AdvOcc@80), Di²Pose achieves 153.6 mm MPJPE and 78.7 mm PA-MPJPE, leading DiffPose (189.3 / 92.4) by 35.7 mm and 13.7 mm, demonstrating substantially stronger occlusion robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Di²Pose outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the closest continuous-diffusion competitor DiffPose [22], Di²Pose improves by 0.5 mm on Human3.6M (49.2 vs 49.7 MPJPE), 3.4 mm on 3DPW (79.3 vs 82.7), and a substantial 35.7 mm under the most severe occlusion (AdvOcc@80: 153.6 vs 189.3 MPJPE). Against the compositional-token baseline PCT [21], the gap widens under occlusion: 19.2 mm on AdvOcc@40 (108.4 vs 127.2) and 38.9 mm on AdvOcc@80 (153.6 vs 192.5). The augmentation-based method Zhang et al. [82] (TPAMI'23) is the strongest non-diffusion baseline on 3DPW (91.1 MPJPE) but degrades sharply under occlusion (251.8 on AdvOcc@80), whereas Di²Pose maintains a 98.2 mm advantage. On PA-MPJPE, Di²Pose leads DiffPose by 3.7 mm on 3DPW and 13.7 mm on AdvOcc@80, confirming that the discrete diffusion formulation preserves global pose structure better under occlusion than its continuous counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

Di²Pose sits at the intersection of two recent threads: compositional pose tokenization (PCT, Geng et al. 2023) and discrete diffusion models (Austin et al. 2021). It departs from PCT by replacing global MLP-Mixer blocks with Local-MLP blocks that capture local joint interactions, and by swapping PCT's classification objective for a discrete diffusion process that explicitly models uncertainty. It also diverges from continuous-space diffusion pose estimators (e.g., DiffPose) by operating entirely in a quantized token space via FSQ, which constrains the search space to biomechanically plausible configurations and reduces data hunger. The occlude-and-replace transition matrix is a novel design that injects occlusion semantics directly into the forward diffusion process, rather than relying on explicit data augmentation or pose priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled answer to the data-efficiency problem of continuous diffusion models for 3D HPE: by restricting the generative space to a finite codebook learned from real poses, the model needs less training data and produces physically valid outputs even under severe occlusion. The ablation showing that both occlusion and replacement transitions are necessary (neither alone suffices) provides useful guidance for designing discrete diffusion schedules in other structured-output tasks. Key open questions remain: the method is frame-based and does not exploit temporal information, inference still requires 25–100 denoising steps (slower than single-pass regressors), and performance degrades when occlusion is so extreme that even human observers cannot disambiguate the pose. Extending the framework to video-based estimation and multi-person scenes would be natural next steps.
