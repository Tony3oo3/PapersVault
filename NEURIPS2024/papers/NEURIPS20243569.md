## Problem

Diffusion models have independently excelled at image prediction (understanding physical dynamics) and robot action generation (diffusion policy), yet prior approaches like SuSIE and UniPi decouple these into two-stage pipelines that fail to exploit the shared underlying physics. This paper asks whether unifying future-image prediction and robot action generation within a single joint denoising process can improve visual policy learning, particularly under data-efficient imitation learning with limited robotic demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243569/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 256×256 RGB image, a robot pose vector (4-D in MetaWorld, 7-D in real-world), a natural language instruction (encoded by a frozen CLIP encoder), and an optional 32×32 depth map. It jointly outputs k=3 future RGB frames (at 4-frame intervals), k robot actions, and optionally k future depth frames. Only the first predicted action is executed before the next prediction cycle, forming a closed-loop replanning loop. The conditional observations are concatenated channel-wise with the noised target latents, so the network sees both the current state and the noise to be removed in a single tensor.

## Method

PAD replaces the U-Net backbone common in prior diffusion policies with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), which natively handles heterogeneous modalities via token concatenation and self-attention. Each modality is first mapped to a latent space (frozen VAE for RGB, MLP for robot poses, downsampling for depth), then the conditional latent is stacked channel-wise with the noised target latent before patchify-tokenization. A masked-attention mechanism allows the same DiT to process video-only data (no action tokens) alongside full robotic data, enabling co-training on 60k BridgeData-v2 trajectories plus robot demonstrations. The model is initialized from an ImageNet-pretrained DiT, trained with a weighted multi-modality DDPM loss (λ_I=1.0 fixed, λ_A and λ_E linearly ramped 0→2.0), and executed with 75 DDIM sampling steps. This joint denoising lets the image-prediction pathway inject physical-world priors directly into the action pathway, outperforming two-stage baselines by 26.3% on MetaWorld and 28.0% on unseen real-world tasks.

## Results

On the MetaWorld benchmark (50 tasks, single text-conditioned policy), PAD achieves a 72.5% average success rate, surpassing the strongest baseline GR-1 (57.4%) by 15.1 percentage points (26.3% relative improvement). On real-world Panda arm manipulation, PAD achieves 0.72 average success rate (0.78 with depth input), outperforming the strongest baseline RT-2* (0.69) by 28.0% relative improvement. Ablations confirm that removing image prediction drops MetaWorld performance to 43.6% and removing co-training with BridgeData-v2 drops it to 59.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAD outperforms all five baselines on both MetaWorld and real-world manipulation. On MetaWorld, the gap is largest against Diffusion Policy (72.5% vs. 27.9%) and SuSIE (72.5% vs. 41.0%), which lack image prediction or use a two-stage pipeline. Against the closest competitor GR-1 (57.4%), PAD gains 15.1 points absolute, with particularly large margins on harder tasks (e.g., basketball: 0.84 vs. 0.08; assembly: 0.88 vs. 0.64). RT-2* (52.2%) and RT-1 (34.6%) trail further behind, with RT-1 struggling on nearly all harder tasks. On real-world tasks, PAD (0.72) and PAD-Depth (0.78) both exceed RT-2* (0.69), with the depth modality providing an additional 6-point boost, especially on Cable-Route (0.64 vs. 0.60) and Pick tasks (0.84 vs. 0.64). PAD also demonstrates superior generalization to unseen objects (strawberries, carrots, eggplants) where all baselines fail on difficult settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAD unifies two previously separate lines of work—diffusion-based image generation (DiT, DDPM) and diffusion-based robot action generation (Diffusion Policy)—into a single joint denoising process within a DiT backbone. It departs from two-stage approaches like SuSIE and UniPi, which first synthesize goal images with a pre-trained diffusion model and then train a separate goal-conditioned policy, by instead predicting future images and actions simultaneously in one model. Compared to GR-1, which also predicts images and actions but via an autoregressive transformer, PAD uses a joint latent diffusion architecture that the authors argue produces more precise future predictions. The channel-wise concatenation of conditional and noisy latents follows InstructPix2Pix, while the multi-modal token concatenation and attention-mask mechanism for handling missing modalities (e.g., no actions in video data) is a practical extension of the DiT design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint denoising formulation provides a principled way to leverage rich physical-world knowledge from large-scale video pre-training directly in robot policy learning, without the information bottleneck of using generated images as an intermediate interface. The 26.3% relative improvement over the strongest baseline and the strong generalization to unseen objects suggest that co-training on internet video data is a practical path toward more data-efficient robot learning. Key limitations include the low control frequency (75 DDIM steps per action) and the restriction to three modalities; the authors note that future work could explore using intermediate latent representations rather than full pixel-space predictions to improve speed, and extending to tactile or point-cloud modalities.
