## Problem

Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) such as GPT-4V cannot process audio inputs and fail to recognize subtle facial micro-expressions, limiting their effectiveness in real-world emotion recognition. This is compounded by the absence of large-scale, multimodal emotion instruction datasets that would enable MLLMs to learn emotion-specific reasoning from diverse scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243517/cropped_page41_idx49.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (pre-processed into 16 hierarchically sampled facial crops for MAE/VideoMAE and a full 448×448 frame for EVA), a corresponding audio stream (processed by HuBERT-Chinese), and a text prompt. Multimodal features are each collapsed into a single 4096-dimensional token (one audio token, three visual tokens) via linear projection, then concatenated with BPE text tokens. Output is free-form text: either a single emotion label from nine categories (happy, sad, neutral, angry, worried, surprise, fear, contempt, doubt) or a multi-sentence reasoning trace citing specific visual, auditory, and textual cues.

## Method

Emotion-LLaMA pairs emotion-specific encoders with a modified LLaMA-2 (7B) language model: HuBERT extracts auditory emotion features, while a multiview visual module combines MAE (static facial expression), VideoMAE (temporal dynamics), and EVA (global scene context) to capture complementary visual cues. Each encoder's output is linearly projected into a shared 4096-d embedding space as a single token, which is fused with text tokens through LLaMA's cross-attention. Training follows a coarse-to-fine two-stage protocol: Stage 1 pre-trains on 28,618 auto-annotated MERR samples to align multimodal tokens with the word embedding space; Stage 2 performs instruction tuning on 4,487 expert-refined MERR samples plus MER2023 and DFEW using LoRA (r=64, α=16) on only 34M parameters (0.495% of total). The MERR dataset itself is constructed by detecting the emotional peak frame via OpenFace Action Unit intensity, generating unimodal descriptions with MiniGPT-v2 (visual context) and Qwen-Audio (tone), and synthesizing a unified multimodal description with LLaMA-3.

## Results

On the EMER emotion reasoning benchmark, Emotion-LLaMA achieves the highest Clue Overlap (7.83) and Label Overlap (6.25), surpassing the next-best Valley (7.24 / 5.77). On DFEW zero-shot evaluation, it attains the best WAR of 59.37% among all MLLMs (vs. GPT-4V's 54.85%), and after fine-tuning reaches UAR 64.21% and WAR 77.06%, outperforming the previous SOTA S2D (61.82% / 76.03%). On MER2023-SEMI, Emotion-LLaMA achieves an F1 of 0.9036 (A+V+T), and on MER2024-OV it scores 66.10% average accuracy/recall, an 8.52% improvement over GPT-4V (57.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EMER (emotion reasoning), Emotion-LLaMA outperforms all compared MLLMs: it leads Valley (7.24/5.77) by +0.59 Clue Overlap and +0.48 Label Overlap, and surpasses PandaGPT (7.14/5.51) and Video-ChatGPT (6.95/5.74) by wider margins. On DFEW zero-shot, Emotion-LLaMA achieves the highest WAR (59.37%) among all MLLMs, beating GPT-4V (54.85%) by 4.52 points, though GPT-4V retains a slight UAR edge (47.69% vs. 45.59%); notably, most MLLMs score 0% on the 'disgust' category while Emotion-LLaMA also scores 0% there, suggesting a shared safety-filter limitation. After fine-tuning on DFEW, Emotion-LLaMA surpasses the previous SOTA S2D (61.82/76.03) by +2.39 UAR and +1.03 WAR, and outperforms VideoMAE (63.60/74.60) and MAE-DFER (63.41/74.43) on both metrics. On MER2023-SEMI, Emotion-LLaMA (F1 0.9036) beats the prior best VAT (0.8911) and FBP (0.8855), and on MER2024-OV it leads GPT-4V (57.58) by 8.52 points in average accuracy/recall, confirming consistent superiority across recognition and reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two active lines of work: multimodal emotion recognition (MER2023, DFEW, EMER) and instruction-tuned MLLMs (LLaVA, Video-LLaMA, PandaGPT, VideoChat). It departs from general-purpose MLLMs that rely on CLIP or Q-Former-style encoders by introducing emotion-specific multiview encoders (HuBERT for audio; MAE, VideoMAE, and EVA for static, temporal, and global visual features) that are projected into LLaMA-2's embedding space via simple linear layers. The MERR dataset extends the small-scale EMER benchmark (100 samples) to 28,618 coarse-grained and 4,487 fine-grained samples with full multimodal descriptions, filling a gap the authors identify as the primary bottleneck for MLLM-based emotion understanding. The training recipe—coarse-to-fine instruction tuning with LoRA on only 34M parameters—follows the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm established by LLaMA-Adapter and MiniGPT-v2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MERR dataset is a practically valuable resource: it is the first large-scale multimodal emotion dataset with rich textual descriptions spanning visual expression, visual context, audio tone, and subtitles, and it covers challenging categories (doubt, contempt) underrepresented in prior work. However, the model's zero-shot performance reveals persistent weaknesses—0% recall on 'disgust' and 3.31% on 'fear'—suggesting that even specialized encoders struggle with rare or safety-filtered emotion categories. Open questions include whether the linear-projection fusion is sufficient compared to cross-attention or other interaction mechanisms, whether the approach generalizes to compound or mixed emotions, and whether the reliance on LLaMA-3 for auto-annotation introduces systematic biases that limit fine-grained reasoning quality.
