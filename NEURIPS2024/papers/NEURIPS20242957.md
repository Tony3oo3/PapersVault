## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised referring image segmentation (WRIS) methods encode the entire referring text as a single language embedding for cross-modal alignment, which easily overlooks critical target-related cues (e.g., specific attributes or spatial relations) embedded in longer or more complex descriptions, leading to localization ambiguity. This paper draws on the observation that humans naturally decompose a description into sequential cues and progressively narrow their focus to the target, and asks whether a similar step-by-step comprehension strategy can be formalized into a trainable network for WRIS.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 320×320) and a natural-language referring expression as input. Before entering the network, the text is decomposed offline by Mistral 7B into exactly five short target-related phrases (shorter descriptions are padded by duplication). The network produces a spatial response map at 1/32 resolution (10×10 for 320×320 input); the peak of this map serves as a localization point that is fed to a pre-trained segmentor (SAM ViT-H or FreeSOLO) to generate the final pixel-level mask, rather than predicting the mask directly.

## Method

PCNet introduces a Conditional Referring Module (CRM) that operates over N=3 stages, where each stage conditions the global referring embedding on one LLM-decomposed phrase via a two-step cross-attention (vision-to-text, then text-to-text) and generates a progressively refined response map through a text-to-visual cross-attention and dot-product scoring. Two novel losses supervise the progressive process: a Region-aware Shrinking (RaS) loss that uses 40 mask proposals from a pre-trained segmentor to separate foreground from background in each response map and penalizes any increase in region-level ambiguity between consecutive stages, and an Instance-aware Disambiguation (IaD) loss that enforces, via hard one-hot assignment, that different referring texts on the same image activate different object proposals. The backbone is ResNet-50 initialized from CLIP, trained for 15 epochs with AdamW and a total loss combining the TRIS-style image-text classification loss, RaS, and IaD.

## Results

On the three standard WRIS benchmarks, PCNet_S (ResNet-50 + SAM) achieves the best PointM localization scores: 60.0/69.3/52.5 on RefCOCO (Val/TestA/TestB), 58.7/65.5/45.3 on RefCOCO+, and 58.6/57.9/57.4 on RefCOCOg (Val(G)/Val(U)/Test(U)), outperforming the strongest WRIS baseline SAG by up to 13.2% PointM on RefCOCO+ Val. With SAM as the segmentor, PCNet_S reaches 52.2 mIoU on RefCOCO Val and 47.3 mIoU on RefCOCOg Val(G), surpassing PPT (46.8 and 43.0) and TRIS (41.1 and 38.4). The Oracle upper bound (using GT localization points) reaches 72.7 mIoU on RefCOCO Val, indicating substantial headroom but also confirming that localization quality is the dominant factor for WRIS performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCNet_S outperforms all WRIS baselines across all three benchmarks on both PointM and mIoU. Against SAG (the strongest prior WRIS method without a segmentor), PCNet_S gains +3.8 PointM on RefCOCO Val (60.0 vs 56.2) and a substantial +13.2 on RefCOCO+ Val (58.7 vs 45.5), where SAG's bottom-up/top-down attention struggles with attribute-based descriptions. Against TRIS, PCNet_S improves by +8.1 PointM on RefCOCO Val and +6.1 on RefCOCOg Val(G) (58.6 vs 52.5). When paired with SAM for mask generation, PCNet_S achieves 52.2 mIoU on RefCOCO Val, beating PPT (46.8) by 5.4 points and TRIS (41.1) by 11.1 points; on the more challenging RefCOCOg Val(G), the gap widens to 47.3 vs PPT's 43.0 and TRIS's 38.4. The fully-supervised LAVT (72.7 mIoU on RefCOCO Val) remains well above all WRIS methods, but the Oracle row (72.7 mIoU using GT localization points) shows that PCNet's localization is the primary bottleneck, not the segmentation step. GroupViT, CLIP-ES, and WWbL are significantly weaker baselines (PointM ≤ 50.6 on RefCOCO TestA) and are clearly outperformed by PCNet on every metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised referring image segmentation (WRIS) line initiated by TRIS [30] and SAG [18], which learn visual-linguistic alignment from text descriptions alone. The key departure is from the single-embedding paradigm: prior methods encode the entire referring text as one language vector for cross-modal alignment, whereas PCNet uses an LLM (Mistral 7B) to decompose the description into short target-related phrases and processes them sequentially through a Conditional Referring Module, inspired by human step-by-step comprehension. The two proposed losses (Region-aware Shrinking and Instance-aware Disambiguation) operate at region and instance granularity respectively, going beyond the global contrastive classification loss used in TRIS. The use of LLMs for structured text decomposition in a segmentation pipeline is a novel application, distinct from prior LLM-in-vision works that use LLMs as feature encoders or prompt generators for SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the critical bottleneck in WRIS is target localization (peak point accuracy), and that a good localization point combined with SAM can surpass fully-supervised methods like LAVT in mask quality — a practically important finding for the field. The progressive comprehension framework is well-motivated by cognitive science and provides a principled way to handle long/complex descriptions where single-embedding methods lose critical cues. Key limitations include the assumption of a single referred object per description (failing on multi-object references) and reliance on LLM decomposition quality, which can produce redundant or incomplete phrases. Open questions include extending to multi-object referring, open-world scenarios (camouflaged, semi-transparent objects), and whether the progressive approach generalizes beyond the RefCOCO family of datasets.
