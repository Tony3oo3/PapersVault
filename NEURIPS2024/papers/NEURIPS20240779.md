## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of distributional temporal difference learning (CTD, QTD) established only asymptotic convergence in the tabular setting, leaving the finite-sample statistical efficiency of these model-free algorithms completely uncharacterized. This paper asks whether non-asymptotic, minimax-optimal sample complexity bounds can be derived for distributional TD, analogous to the tight bounds recently obtained for classic TD.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a γ-discounted infinite-horizon tabular MDP with a fixed policy π and access to a generative model that produces independent samples (a_t(s), s_t(s), r_t(s)) for every state s at each iteration. The output is a return distribution estimator η̂^π whose quality is measured by the supreme p-Wasserstein distance (or Cramér/ℓ₂ distance) to the true return distribution η^π. The step size follows a specific schedule α_t ≈ 1/(1 + c(1−√γ)·t/log t), and for CTD the categorical support size K must scale as Ω(1/(ε²(1−γ)²)) to control approximation error.

## Method

The paper introduces non-parametric distributional TD (NTD) as a theoretical vehicle and proves that Õ(1/(ε^{2p}(1−γ)^{2p+1})) iterations suffice for p-Wasserstein ε-optimality, which is minimax optimal for p=1. The key technical ingredient is a novel Freedman's inequality in Hilbert spaces (Theorem A.2) that provides high-probability bounds on martingale paths using both the quadratic variation and a boundedness parameter, extending the scalar version used in classic TD analysis. The proof works in the zero-mass signed measure space M equipped with the Cramér (ℓ₂) inner product, which is a Hilbert space, enabling the application of this new concentration inequality. The error Δ_T is decomposed into a contracted initial-error term and a martingale term; the latter is bounded via the Hilbert-space Freedman's inequality after controlling the quadratic variation through the distributional Bellman operator's contraction structure. The same bounds are then shown to hold for the practical CTD algorithm, where the Cramér projection Π_K introduces only a controllable approximation error.

## Results

For both NTD and CTD, the paper establishes that T = Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)) iterations suffice to achieve a 1-Wasserstein error of at most ε with high probability (Theorems 4.1 and 4.2). More generally, for the p-Wasserstein metric, T = Õ(1/(ε^{2p}(1-γ)^{2p+1})) iterations are needed. This upper bound is shown to be minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors via a matching lower bound of Ω̃(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)) (Theorem B.1). In the Cramér (ℓ₂) metric, the rate is Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)^{5/2})) (Corollaries 4.1 and 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main advantage is over the prior model-free DPE result of Böck and Heitzinger (2022), who achieved Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)⁴)) for SCPE; this paper improves the (1-γ) dependence by one power to Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)) for plain CTD without any acceleration term. The result matches the model-based DCFP bound of Rowland et al. (2024b) at Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)), but does so in the more practical model-free setting. Compared to the classic TD analysis of Li et al. (2024) for standard policy evaluation, the same Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)) rate is achieved for the strictly harder distributional task. The paper also provides the first non-asymptotic bounds for CTD, going beyond the asymptotic convergence results of Rowland et al. (2018) and Rowland et al. (2024a) for CTD and QTD respectively. The minimax lower bound (Theorem B.1) confirms that the Õ(1/(ε²(1-γ)³)) rate is information-theoretically tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-asymptotic analysis of classic TD (Li et al., 2024) to the distributional setting by establishing a Freedman's inequality in Hilbert spaces, which is the key technical tool enabling tight concentration bounds for the martingale error term. It builds on the distributional Bellman operator framework and contraction properties from Bellemare et al. (2023), and goes beyond the asymptotic convergence results for CTD (Rowland et al., 2018) and QTD (Rowland et al., 2024a) by providing finite-sample guarantees. The paper departs from model-based approaches (Zhang et al., 2023; Rowland et al., 2024b) by analyzing the model-free iterative CTD algorithm directly, and improves upon SCPE (Böck and Heitzinger, 2022) by showing that no acceleration term is needed to achieve the minimax-optimal rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence analysis for model-free distributional TD, closing a gap between the well-understood asymptotic theory and the practical need for finite-sample guarantees. The minimax optimality result (up to log factors) for the 1-Wasserstein metric establishes that learning the full return distribution via CTD is as sample-efficient as learning just its expectation, which is a reassuring theoretical guarantee for practitioners. The Hilbert-space Freedman's inequality is a general-purpose tool likely to be useful beyond this specific application. Open questions include extending the analysis to QTD, function approximation settings, and asynchronous (on-policy) sampling, all of which remain unaddressed.
