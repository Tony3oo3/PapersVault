## Problem

All existing distributed methods with provable Byzantine robustness require full or near-full client participation, which is impractical for large-scale federated learning with millions of clients where partial participation is the norm. Naively combining existing Byzantine-robust methods with client sampling fails because Byzantine workers can form a majority in some rounds and destroy training in a single communication step. This paper addresses the open question of achieving provable Byzantine tolerance under partial participation without restrictive assumptions on the number of participating clients.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-sum distributed objective f(x) = (1/G)Σ f_i(x) where each good client i holds m local samples, and the server coordinates n total clients (G good, B Byzantine with B ≤ δn, δ < 1/2). At each round, a random subset of C clients (or Ĉ with small probability p) participates, computing compressed stochastic gradient differences that are clipped at level λ_{k+1} = 2L‖x^{k+1} − x^k‖ before robust aggregation. The output is a sequence of iterates x^k with O(1/K) convergence to a stationary point for general smooth non-convex functions and linear convergence to an O(ζ²/p)-neighborhood for PL functions, where ζ² captures data heterogeneity among good clients.

## Method

Byz-VR-MARINA-PP extends Byz-VR-MARINA to partial participation by applying gradient clipping to compressed stochastic gradient differences within a GeomSARAH/PAGE-type recursive variance reduction framework. With probability 1−p, only C randomly sampled clients participate and transmit clipped, compressed gradient differences; with probability p, a larger set of Ĉ clients computes full local gradients. The clipping level λ_{k+1} ∝ ‖x^{k+1} − x^k‖ is the key mechanism: it bounds the norm of any transmitted vector (including those from Byzantine workers) so that even when all sampled clients are Byzantine, the update remains controlled and the method cannot be pushed arbitrarily far from the solution. The server applies a (δ,c)-agnostic robust aggregator (e.g., coordinate-wise trimmed mean with bucketing) to the received vectors, and unbiased communication compression (e.g., RandK sparsification) is applied to the gradient differences to reduce communication cost. A simplified variant, Byz-VR-MARINA-PP+, replaces robust aggregation with plain averaging during the 1−p rounds, yielding a smaller neighborhood term when C is small. The paper also provides a general heuristic (Eq. 10) for adapting any Byzantine-robust method to partial participation via the same clipping-on-differences principle.

## Results

The main theoretical results (Theorems 4.1 and 4.2) establish an O(1/K) convergence rate for general smooth non-convex functions and linear convergence to an O(ζ²/p) neighborhood for PL functions, matching the SOTA rates of Byz-VR-MARINA under full participation. Experimentally, on the a9a logistic regression task (15 good + 5 Byzantine workers, 20% client sampling), Byz-VR-MARINA-PP with clipping achieves linear convergence to the exact solution while the unclipped variant fails to converge entirely under the shift-back attack. On MNIST with a neural network (20 clients, 5 malicious, heterogeneous splits), clipping performs on par with or better than the unclipped variant across both coordinate-wise median and robust federated averaging aggregators under bit-flipping and shift-back attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between Byz-VR-MARINA-PP with gradient clipping and the same method without clipping. Without clipping, the method diverges completely under the shift-back attack when Byzantine workers can form a majority in sampled rounds (Figure 1, left), while with clipping it converges linearly to the exact solution. In the full-participation vs. partial-participation comparison (Figure 1, middle), partial participation with clipping actually achieves faster convergence in terms of total epochs, demonstrating that the O(C) workers per step is provably beneficial. On MNIST with neural networks (Figure 2), clipping does not degrade performance relative to the unclipped variant under either coordinate-wise median (CM) or robust federated averaging (RFA) aggregation, and no robust aggregator can withstand the shift-back attack without clipping. Theoretically, in the full-participation special case (C = Ĉ = n), Theorem 4.2 recovers the exact convergence rate of Byz-VR-MARINA (Gorbunov et al., 2023), confirming no rate loss when all clients participate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Byz-VR-MARINA (Gorbunov et al., 2023), the current SOTA for Byzantine-robust distributed optimization with recursive variance reduction (GeomSARAH/PAGE-type estimators) and robust aggregation, to the partial participation setting where clients are sampled per round. The key departure is that all prior Byzantine-robust methods require full or near-full client participation, making them impractical for large-scale federated learning with millions of clients. The technical novelty is the use of gradient clipping on compressed stochastic gradient differences to bound Byzantine harm even when sampled clients are entirely Byzantine, combined with a heuristic (Eq. 10) that generalizes this clipping trick to any Byzantine-robust method. The robust aggregation framework (δ,c)-ARAgg from Karimireddy et al. (2021, 2022) and the Bucketing procedure are inherited as building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first distributed method with provable Byzantine robustness under partial participation where Byzantine workers can form a majority in some rounds—a scenario that is unavoidable in practice with millions of clients and unreliable connectivity. The clipping heuristic provides a simple, broadly applicable recipe for adapting any existing Byzantine-robust method to partial participation, which has immediate practical value. Open questions include whether the 1/p² dependence in the stepsize constraint (vs. 1/p for full participation) can be tightened, whether the heuristic extends rigorously to momentum-based methods, and how the approach behaves under non-uniform or arbitrary client participation patterns. The complexity deterioration in the full-participation special case (not recovering Byz-VR-MARINA's exact rate) is a notable limitation.
