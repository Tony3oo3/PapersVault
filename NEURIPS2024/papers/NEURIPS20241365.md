## Problem

Traditional temporal sentence grounding (TSG) methods assume that a query-relevant segment always exists in the input video, causing them to produce erroneous grounding results when no such segment is present. This paper introduces the TSG-RF task, which requires the model to both detect whether relevant content exists and, only when it does, localize the precise segment boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241365/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (encoded via pre-trained I3D into a frame-level feature sequence, downsampled or zero-padded to 128 frames) and a natural language query (encoded via GloVe word embeddings) as input. It produces a binary relevance feedback score indicating whether the video contains query-related content, and conditionally outputs start and end boundary indices for the grounded segment; when no relevant segment exists, the boundary indices are assigned to a special index 0, signaling non-existence.

## Method

RaTSG is a unified multi-task network with two core components: a multi-granularity relevance discriminator and a relation-aware segment grounding module. The discriminator computes fine-grained relevance via multiple-instance learning (max-pooling over per-frame foreground scores) and coarse-grained relevance via a softmax-weighted video-level aggregation fused with sentence-level text features, then averages both scores into a single relevance prediction. The grounding module prepends the video-level relation signal vector as a special token (index 0) to the foreground-enhanced video feature sequence, then uses two stacked unidirectional LSTMs with feed-forward heads to predict start and end boundary probability distributions over indices [0, n]. Training jointly optimizes a boundary cross-entropy loss, a frame-level binary cross-entropy loss, and a video-level binary cross-entropy loss, enabling mutual enhancement between relevance discrimination and segment grounding. At inference, a threshold on the relevance score gates whether grounding boundaries are reported or a non-existence signal is returned.

## Results

On Charades-RF, RaTSG achieves 76.85% relevance accuracy, 68.17% R1@0.3, 61.91% R1@0.5, 54.19% R1@0.7, and 59.93% mIoU, outperforming the best baseline EAMAT++ (71.94% Acc, 63.55% R1@0.3, 56.23% mIoU) by 4.91, 4.62, and 3.70 points respectively. On ActivityNet-RF, RaTSG reaches 84.27% Acc, 69.02% R1@0.3, 60.68% R1@0.5, 52.88% R1@0.7, and 61.15% mIoU, surpassing the strongest baseline SeqPAN++ (81.60% Acc, 66.77% R1@0.3, 59.11% mIoU). Notably, RaTSG achieves these gains with only 1.27M parameters, compared to 98.30M for EAMAT++, making it the most parameter-efficient model in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaTSG outperforms all six baselines (VSLNet, SeqPAN, EAMAT, ADPN, QD-DETR, UniVTG) and their enhanced variants (with separate relevance discriminators) on every metric across both Charades-RF and ActivityNet-RF. The traditional TSG models without relevance feedback score exactly 50% accuracy on both datasets, equivalent to random guessing, because they always predict grounding boundaries regardless of whether relevant content exists. Among the enhanced baselines, EAMAT++ is the strongest on Charades-RF (63.55% R1@0.3, 56.23% mIoU) while SeqPAN++ leads on ActivityNet-RF (66.77% R1@0.3, 59.11% mIoU); RaTSG surpasses these by 4.62 and 2.25 points on R1@0.3 respectively. A particularly notable advantage is parameter efficiency: RaTSG uses only 1.27M parameters versus 98.30M for EAMAT++, yet achieves 3.70 points higher mIoU on Charades-RF. The paper further demonstrates that RaTSG's modules can be plugged into EAMAT (EAMAT+Ours) to yield 76.37% Acc and 59.58% mIoU, outperforming EAMAT++ (71.94% Acc, 56.23% mIoU), confirming the generality of the proposed components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal sentence grounding (TSG) line of work (VSLNet, SeqPAN, EAMAT, ADPN, UniVTG, QD-DETR) by introducing a relevance feedback component that prior TSG methods entirely ignore. Conceptually, it repurposes multiple instance learning (treating a video as a bag of frame instances) for fine-grained foreground-background frame discrimination, and combines this with a coarse-grained video-level relevance signal in a unified multi-task framework. The relation-aware segment grounding module departs from standard TSG heads by prepending a relevance-conditioned special token (index 0) so the model can explicitly predict 'no segment exists,' rather than always outputting a boundary pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TSG-RF formulation addresses a clear practical gap: real-world video search queries frequently target content absent from the given video, and existing TSG models silently produce hallucinated segments in such cases. The mutual enhancement between relevance discrimination and grounding (each task improves the other when jointly trained) is a useful design insight for multi-task video-language models. However, the approach remains limited to visual features (no audio), struggles with temporally sensitive actions, and the reconstructed datasets use a fixed 1:1 irrelevant-to-relevant ratio that may not reflect real query distributions. Open questions include whether the multi-granularity discriminator generalizes to longer videos or more diverse domains, and whether the 0.5/0.3 relevance thresholds transfer across datasets without re-tuning.
