## Problem

Kégl (COLT 2014) proposed factorized base classifiers for ADABOOST.MH that replace K independent one-against-all classifiers with a single binary classifier and a vote vector, improving practical performance. However, the factorization step blocked a convergence proof, leaving an open problem: whether a constant lower bound (independent of sample size n) exists on the pseudo-weight sum w′_Σ guaranteeing that at least one vote vector yields a non-vanishing edge. This paper resolves that open problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n feature vectors x_i ∈ R^d with one-hot label vectors y_i ∈ {−1,+1}^K (where y_i(ℓ(x_i))=+1 and all other entries are −1), along with a structured weight matrix W ∈ [0,1]^{n×K} where w_{i,l}=1/(2n) for the true class and 1/(2n(K−1)) for all other classes. It outputs a vector-valued discriminant function f: X→R^K (a sum of T factorized base classifiers) and a combined predictor F_f(x)_l = sign(f(x)_l) that assigns a K-dimensional sign vector to each input.

## Method

The key reformulation casts the open problem as a constrained minimax: min_{W∈𝒲, Y∈𝒴} max_{v∈𝒱} ‖(W⊙Y)·v‖₁, where ⊙ is the Schur product. The authors prove two lower bounds. First, an n-dependent bound of 1/n follows from ‖x‖₁ ≥ ‖x‖_∞ and the fact that the maximum over rows is at least the average. Second, the main n-independent bound of 1/√(2K) is obtained by replacing the max over vote vectors with an expectation over a Rademacher random vector v=(ε₁,…,ε_K), then applying the Khintchine inequality (p=1) to lower-bound E‖(W⊙Y)·v‖₁, followed by Jensen's inequality on the concave square root. Under the empirically δ′-weak learning condition, this yields a multi-class edge γ ≥ δ′/√(2K) at every iteration, giving exponential decay of the weighted loss and a convergence guarantee of T* = ⌈4K ln(2n(K−1))/δ′²⌉ + 1 iterations to drive the training Hamming error to zero.

## Results

The paper establishes two convergence guarantees for factorized ADABOOST.MH. Theorem 3.3 provides an n-dependent lower bound of 1/n on w′Σ, yielding a convergence rate of T* = O(n² ln n) iterations to drive the training error to zero. Theorem 3.4 (the main result resolving Kégl's open problem) provides an n-independent lower bound of ω = 1/√(2K) on w′Σ via the Khintchine inequality, yielding the improved convergence rate T* = O(K ln(nK)) iterations. Under the empirically δ′-weak learning condition, the weighted exponential error satisfies R̂_EXP(f^(T), W) ≤ exp(−δ′T/(4K)), and the weighted Hamming error becomes zero after at most ⌈4K ln(2n(K−1))/δ′²⌉ + 1 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The paper resolves an open problem posed by Kégl (COLT 2014) that had remained unsolved: whether a constant lower bound on w′Σ independent of the sample size n exists for factorized ADABOOST.MH. The n-dependent bound (Theorem 3.3, giving T* = O(n² ln n)) was the only previously available guarantee and was considered impractical for large datasets. The new n-independent bound (Theorem 3.4, giving T* = O(K ln(nK))) is the key contribution, showing that when n ≫ K, the convergence rate improves dramatically from quadratic in n to logarithmic in n. The paper positions its result as complementary to recent multiclass boosting works [4, 5, 6] that use different algorithmic frameworks (list learning, relaxed γ-BRG conditions) rather than the factorized Hamming-tree structure of Kégl's ADABOOST.MH.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a specific open problem posed by Kégl at COLT 2014 regarding the convergence of the factorized variant of ADABOOST.MH, where the vector-valued base classifier is decomposed as h(x) = αvφ(x) into an input-independent code vector and a label-independent scalar classifier. The framework builds directly on Schapire and Singer's ADABOOST.MH (1999), which introduced vector-valued base classifiers and a weight matrix over instances and labels, and on Kégl's ICLR 2014 proposal of multi-class Hamming trees that factorized the classifier to avoid reducing to K independent binary problems. The key technical departure is the use of the Khintchine inequality with Rademacher random vectors to establish an n-independent lower bound on the pseudo-weight sum, a tool not previously applied to this boosting convergence question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a decade-old open problem and confirms that the factorized ADABOOST.MH converges essentially as fast as binary ADABOOST, with T* = O(K ln(nK)) iterations rather than the O(n² ln n) that would follow from the weaker n-dependent bound. Since boosting convergence rates directly determine the sample complexity of improper learning algorithms constructed from them, this guarantee has implications for the broader statistical learning theory landscape. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the result is conditioned on the empirically δ'-weak learning condition holding, which limits its immediate practical applicability. An open question is whether the 1/√(2K) dependence on K is tight or whether stronger bounds exist for structured weight matrices.
