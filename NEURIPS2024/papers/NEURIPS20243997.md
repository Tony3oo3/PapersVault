## Problem

Gradient-based algorithms for zero-sum games achieve linear convergence with iteration complexity scaling as log(1/ε), but existing analyses introduce dependencies on condition-number-like quantities that can be exponentially large in the game's description, making the guarantees computationally uninterpretable. This paper asks whether those pathologies are truly generic or merely worst-case artifacts, and whether a smoothed analysis can recover polynomial iteration bounds.

## Input / Output

The input is a base payoff matrix Ā ∈ [-1,1]^{n×m} perturbed by independent Gaussian noise G with variance σ² ≤ 1 per entry, along with initial strategies on the probability simplexes Δⁿ × Δᵐ. The output is an ε-equilibrium (x*, y*) with duality gap Φ(x*,y*) ≤ ε, achieved in poly(n,m,1/σ)·log(1/ε) iterations with probability at least 1 − 1/poly(nm). Notably, randomness is injected only into the payoff matrix and not the simplex constraints, preserving the zero-sum game structure after perturbation.

## Method

The core contribution is a new characterization of the error bound (the key quantity driving linear convergence) in terms of three natural geometric features of the game: the minimum equilibrium probability (α), the minimum slack of non-support constraints (β), and the minimum column distance of a transformed matrix Q (γ). A linear transformation eliminates one redundant variable from each player's support, exposing Q whose spectral properties govern the difficulty of solving the induced linear system. Under Gaussian perturbation, each quantity is shown to be unlikely to be near zero using Spielman–Teng's smooth-density lemma, which bounds the probability that a random variable with a smooth density falls below ε. The negative second moment identity connects the smallest singular value of Q to the minimum column-to-span distance, bridging the geometric characterization to the spectral analysis needed for convergence. This yields polynomial smoothed complexity for OGDA, EGDA, and IterSmooth, and a connection between convergence rate and perturbation-stability of the equilibrium.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.2) shows that OGDA, EGDA, and IterSmooth converge to an ε-equilibrium in poly(n, m, 1/σ)·log(1/ε) iterations with high probability (≥ 1/poly(nm)) under Gaussian perturbation of the payoff matrix. The key intermediate result (Theorem 1.4) establishes that the error bound modulus κ ≥ poly(σ, 1/(nm)) with high probability, which is the central ingredient enabling linear convergence. A corollary (Corollary 1.5) shows that any point with duality gap ≤ ε is within ε·poly(n,m,1/σ) of the equilibrium in Euclidean distance. For perturbation-stable games (Corollary 1.6), the iteration bound becomes poly(n,m,1/δ)·log(1/ε) where δ is the stability parameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental comparisons or datasets. The 'baselines' are prior worst-case theoretical bounds. In the worst-case regime, gradient-based methods like OGDA and EGDA have iteration complexity depending on condition-number-like quantities that can be exponentially large in the game description (Proposition 3.1 shows κ can be arbitrarily small even in 3×3 games). The smoothed analysis result removes this dependency, replacing it with a polynomial in 1/σ. For OMWU, the prior worst-case analysis of Wei et al. [2021] gives at best an exponential bound regardless of problem geometry; this paper improves it but the bound remains superpolynomial. Compared to LP-based solvers (interior-point, ellipsoid), which are polynomial in the worst case but have high per-iteration cost and memory requirements, the gradient-based methods analyzed here have O(nm) per-iteration cost and minimal memory, making them more scalable in practice despite the smoothed (not worst-case) guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the smoothed analysis framework of Spielman and Teng (2003, 2004) — previously used to explain polynomial-time behavior of the simplex method and other LP algorithms — to gradient-based solvers for zero-sum games (OGDA, EGDA, OMWU, IterSmooth). The central technical object is the error bound of Tseng (1995), which drives linear convergence but whose modulus κ can be exponentially small in worst case; the paper's key contribution is a new geometric characterization of κ (Theorem 3.6) in terms of support, margin, and spectral quantities, which then admits a smoothed analysis via the Spielman–Teng smooth-density lemma. The work also builds on Wei et al. (2021) for the constrained saddle-point convergence analysis and Gilpin et al. (2012) for the IterSmooth algorithm, while drawing a novel connection to perturbation-stable equilibria studied by Balcan and Braverman (2017) and Awasthi et al. (2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first smoothed analysis of gradient-based algorithms in zero-sum games, resolving the tension between the log(1/ε) iteration complexity of linear convergence and the potentially exponential condition-number dependence that made worst-case bounds uninformative. The error-bound characterization (Theorem 3.6) is useful beyond the smoothed model: it parameterizes convergence in terms of interpretable game-theoretic quantities and yields a clean connection between algorithmic complexity and perturbation stability of the equilibrium (Corollary 1.6). Key open questions include whether OMWU can be shown to have polynomial smoothed complexity (currently superpolynomial due to limitations in its worst-case analysis), whether the non-degeneracy assumption can be removed, and whether the framework extends to more general constraint sets such as extensive-form games or multi-player polymatrix games.
