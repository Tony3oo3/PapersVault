## Problem

Large DNNs rely on dense matrix-vector operations that are computationally expensive during inference, and while structured matrices (low-rank, Monarch, block-diagonal) can reduce complexity, they impose a fixed structure that often misaligns with the true low-dimensional geometry of the weights, causing significant performance degradation. Existing adaptive alternatives like Gaudi-GBLR learn flexible structures but are inefficient on standard GPUs due to irregular zero-padded computations. The paper seeks a structured matrix that is simultaneously flexible enough to capture diverse weight structures and efficient enough for practical GPU-accelerated inference.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the linear-layer weight matrices inside transformer and diffusion-model architectures (ViT, GPT-2, Llama-7B, DiT-XL), partitioning each n×n weight into b×b blocks of size p×p. For compression, the input is a pre-trained dense weight matrix and the output is a set of BLAST factors (U_i, V_j, s_{i,j}) that replace the original weights; for training from scratch, random BLAST factors initialize the model in place of dense weights. Downstream tasks remain unchanged: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 ImageNet, 256×256 DiT) or text sequences produce class labels, generated text, or synthesized images respectively.

## Method

The BLAST matrix parameterizes each block A_{i,j} = U_i diag(s_{i,j}) V_j^T, where left factors U_i are shared across all blocks in row i, right factors V_j are shared across all blocks in column j, and the diagonal coupling vector s_{i,j} is unique to each block. This yields 2nr + rb² parameters (vs. n² for dense) and, by varying the diagonal factors, can exactly represent low-rank, block-diagonal, block-low-rank, and their combinations—giving the matrix its 'adaptive' character. For compressing pre-trained weights, the authors derive a preconditioned gradient descent algorithm (Algorithm 2) that multiplies each gradient by the inverse of a regularized Gram matrix, provably accelerating convergence in the overparameterized regime (r > true rank) where vanilla GD stalls. The matrix-vector product requires only (2n+b²)r multiplications and decomposes into batched matrix multiplications (torch.bmm), making it GPU-friendly without custom kernels. For training from scratch, the forward and backward passes are composed of three linear operations, so standard optimizers (Adam/AdamW) apply without modification.

## Results

For training from scratch, BLAST₃ on ViT-Base achieves 79.3% ImageNet top-1 accuracy at only 27.8% of the dense model's FLOPs, surpassing the dense baseline (78.7%) while reducing complexity by 72%. For compressing Llama-7B at 50% compression ratio with re-training, BLAST₁₆ achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 14.21 and 56.22% average zero-shot accuracy, compared to 26.33/48.40% for Low-Rank and catastrophic failures (perplexity >7×10⁵) for Monarch and Block-Diagonal. For DiT-XL compression at 50%, BLAST₉ achieves FID 10.45 (close to the original 9.62) versus 48.07 for Low-Rank, demonstrating near-lossless compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLAST decisively outperforms Low-Rank, Monarch, and Block-Diagonal on all compression tasks. At 50% compression of Llama-7B, BLAST₁₆ achieves a perplexity of 14.21 versus 26.33 for Low-Rank, while Monarch and Block-Diagonal completely collapse (perplexity >7×10⁵), indicating these rigid structures are fundamentally misaligned with the weight geometry. For DiT-XL at 50% compression, BLAST₉ achieves FID 10.45 (nearly matching the original 9.62) while Low-Rank degrades to FID 48.07, a 4.6× gap. In training-from-scratch settings, BLAST₃ on ViT-Base (79.3% at 27.8% FLOPs) beats Gaudi-GBLR (78.5% at 32.8% FLOPs) on ImageNet, though Gaudi-GBLR slightly edges out BLAST on CIFAR-10/100 due to its per-matrix adaptive budget allocation, a feature BLAST lacks in that evaluation. On GPT-2 (WikiText 103), BLAST achieves the best perplexity-FLOPs trade-off, with the authors attributing the advantage over Gaudi-GBLR to BLAST's simpler training requiring fewer hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLAST sits at the intersection of block low-rank (BLR) structured matrices (Monarch, BLR²) and data-driven adaptive structured matrices (Gaudi-GBLR). It departs from Monarch by replacing the fixed shared-rank constraint with per-block diagonal coupling factors, allowing each block to have a different effective rank while retaining the shared-basis parameter efficiency. It departs from Gaudi-GBLR by using dense block operations (no zero-padding) that execute efficiently on standard GPUs, and by unifying low-rank, block-diagonal, and block low-rank as special cases within a single parameterization. The preconditioned gradient descent factorization algorithm adapts ideas from overparameterized low-rank matrix recovery (Tong et al., Zhang et al.) to the multi-factor BLAST setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 50% compression of Llama-7B with only ~5 perplexity degradation (vs ~17 for low-rank) and near-lossless DiT image generation at 50% compression make BLAST a viable drop-in for deploying large models on constrained hardware. Key limitations include using a uniform rank r across all weight matrices (no per-layer adaptive budget), untested performance on models below 100M or above 10B parameters, and the three-step matrix-vector product that may limit hardware parallelism. Open questions include whether adaptive per-layer budgets, knowledge distillation during re-training, and custom hardware kernels could close the remaining gap to dense inference.
