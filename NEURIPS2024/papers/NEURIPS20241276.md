## Problem

While MTEB provides broad evaluation for English text embeddings, it offers minimal support for Scandinavian languages—only two translated classification datasets exist, with no naturally occurring text, no retrieval tasks, and no reproducibility guarantees. Scandinavian research institutions and industry lack a reliable standard to evaluate embedding model performance on their languages, forcing reliance on English benchmarks or bitext mining proxies that do not transfer well to actual Scandinavian applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241276/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes raw text documents, queries, and paired sentences in Danish, Swedish, Norwegian Bokmål/Nynorsk, and the Bornholmsk dialect, with most datasets capped at 2048 examples to allow consumer-grade hardware execution. Models produce dense embedding vectors that are scored via task-specific metrics (accuracy for classification, F1 for bitext mining, nDCG@10 for retrieval, V-measure for clustering), and the benchmark outputs per-task scores, per-domain breakdowns, per-language scores, and an overall rank with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. A public cache and interactive dashboard allow users to retrieve results without re-running models.

## Method

SEB is a benchmark framework comprising 24 datasets spanning 10 subtasks in 4 categories (retrieval, classification, clustering, bitext mining) across 12+ domains, deliberately excluding translated datasets (except MTEB-compatible ones) to preserve cultural integrity and avoid inflating multilingual model scores. It leverages the high cross-lingual transfer among Mainland Scandinavian languages to broaden coverage despite limited per-language resources. A model registry documents exact implementations (prompts, preprocessing) for all 26+ evaluated models, addressing MTEB's reproducibility gap. Scoring follows MTEB's approach for compatibility but permits task-specific prompting for instruction-tuned models like e5-mistral-7b-instruct and Cohere embed-v3. The benchmark is integrated into MTEB as a Scandinavian extension and released with a public dashboard for ongoing model evaluation.

## Results

Across 24 datasets spanning 4 task categories (retrieval, classification, clustering, bitext mining) and 4 Scandinavian languages, the best-performing model is OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large with an average score of 65.0, followed by Cohere's embed-multilingual-v3.0 (64.1) and text-embedding-3-small (61.0). Among publicly available models, multilingual-e5-large achieves the highest average score of 60.7, closely followed by e5-mistral-7b-instruct (60.4). The largest performance gap between commercial and public models appears in retrieval, where commercial APIs outperform the best public models by more than 8 points (77.9 vs. 69.1 nDCG@10). Self-supervised models (e.g., XLM-R at 35.3, FastText variants at 35.8–37.5) consistently underperform their supervised counterparts by 15–25 points on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Commercial embedding APIs (OpenAI text-embedding-3-large at 65.0, Cohere embed-multilingual-v3.0 at 64.1) consistently outperform all publicly available models, with the gap most pronounced in retrieval (77.9 vs. 69.1 for multilingual-e5-large, an 8.8-point difference). Among public models, the multilingual-e5 series (large: 60.7, base: 57.9, small: 56.4) dominates, outperforming LaBSE (50.5), SONAR variants (41.5–43.4), and all self-supervised encoders. Notably, e5-mistral-7b-instruct ranks 1st on MTEB (66.6) but drops to 5th on SEB (60.4), revealing that its English-centric Mistral backbone limits Scandinavian performance. LaBSE and SONAR models excel specifically at bitext mining (69.1 and 70.5 respectively), the only task category where open solutions match or exceed commercial APIs. Self-supervised models like XLM-R (35.3) and FastText (35.8–37.5) trail supervised models by 15–25 points, though FastText offers the highest throughput for deployment-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MTEB (Muennighoff et al., 2023) by porting its task infrastructure and adding 24 naturally occurring Scandinavian-language datasets, deliberately avoiding the translated-dataset strategy that dominates MTEB's non-English coverage. It positions itself against prior Scandinavian NLP benchmarks (ScandEval, Superlim, NorBench) that evaluate general NLU tasks but do not target text embeddings specifically. Conceptually, the paper departs from MTEB on two axes: (1) cultural integrity, rejecting translated English datasets to prevent artificially inflating multilingual model scores, and (2) reproducibility, introducing a model registry that documents exact prompts and implementations—addressing a gap MTEB leaves for prompt-based and Matryoshka embedding models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEB fills a critical practical gap: Scandinavian research institutions, public agencies, and industry previously had no reliable standard for selecting text embedding models, relying instead on English-centric benchmarks or bitext mining proxies that do not transfer. The finding that MTEB rankings diverge substantially from SEB rankings (e.g., e5-mistral-7b-instruct drops from 1st to 5th, mxbai-embed-large-v1 from 2nd to 13th) means model selection based solely on MTEB can lead to suboptimal choices for Scandinavian applications. Open questions include whether the commercial-vs-public performance gap (especially in retrieval, >8 points) will narrow as open multilingual models improve, and whether the cross-lingual transfer assumption among Scandinavian languages holds uniformly across all task types. Domain coverage remains limited (legal, medical, government are underrepresented), and the benchmark currently lacks pair classification and document reranking tasks.
