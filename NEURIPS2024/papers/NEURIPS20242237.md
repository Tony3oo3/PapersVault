## Problem

DP-SGD is widely implemented with shuffling-based batch samplers, yet its privacy is typically reported using Poisson subsampling accounting, a practice shown to be unsound for single-epoch training. This paper extends the privacy gap analysis to the multi-epoch setting (both persistent and dynamic shuffling) and asks which batch sampler actually provides the best utility when each is paired with its correct privacy accounting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular training dataset (Criteo Display Ads, ~46M examples with 13 integer and 26 categorical features), a batch sampler configuration (deterministic, persistent shuffle, dynamic shuffle, or truncated Poisson), a noise scale σ, and a clipping norm C. It produces a trained model (a ~78M-parameter neural network for click-through-rate prediction) along with a certified (ε, δ)-DP guarantee. A key practical constraint is that truncated Poisson subsampling enforces a fixed maximum batch size B (with zero-weight padding for smaller batches) to satisfy the fixed-shape requirements of TPU and JIT-compiled deep learning frameworks.

## Method

The paper derives lower bounds on the privacy loss of multi-epoch ABLQ under persistent and dynamic shuffling by constructing dominating pairs of Gaussian mixture distributions and computing hockey stick divergences over carefully chosen events, extending the single-epoch technique of Chua et al. [2024] to E epochs via composition. For Poisson subsampling, the authors introduce truncated Poisson sampling (capping batch size at B) and prove a modified privacy bound (Theorem 3.3) that adds a TV-distance correction term T·(1+e^ε)·Ψ(n,b,B) to the standard Poisson accounting. To make truncated Poisson batches tractable at scale, they implement batch generation as a MapReduce pipeline in Apache Beam: a Map step assigns each example to its Poisson-sampled batch indices via geometric-distribution sampling, a Reduce step groups examples by batch, and a final Map step truncates oversized batches and pads undersized ones with zero-weight dummy examples. Experiments on Criteo with ε=5, δ=2.7×10⁻⁸ show that truncated Poisson subsampling matches dynamic shuffling at the same σ but outperforms shuffling under correct (lower-bound) privacy accounting in high-privacy regimes, establishing Poisson subsampling as a viable large-scale alternative.

## Results

On the Criteo Display Ads dataset (46M examples, AUC metric), DP-SGD with truncated Poisson subsampling achieves AUC comparable to dynamic shuffling at the same noise scale σ, but requires a significantly smaller σ to achieve the same (ε, δ) privacy guarantee. For ε=5, δ=2.7×10⁻⁸, the lower-bound σ for dynamic/persistent shuffling is substantially larger than the upper-bound σ for Poisson subsampling, meaning shuffling needs more noise (and thus lower utility) under correct accounting. In the high-privacy regime (ε ≤ 16), Poisson subsampling clearly outperforms shuffling with correct accounting; in the low-privacy regime (ε ≥ 64), shuffling performs slightly better. The Feldman et al. [2021] privacy amplification by shuffling upper bounds are vacuous for batch sizes ≥ 2048, being no better than the deterministic (no amplification) bound σ_D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four batch samplers (Deterministic, Truncated Poisson, Persistent Shuffle, Dynamic Shuffle) under their respective correct privacy accounting, plus Dynamic Shuffle with the commonly-used but incorrect Poisson accounting. Truncated Poisson subsampling matches dynamic shuffling in utility at the same σ, but requires a smaller σ for the same (ε, δ) target, giving it a clear advantage in high-privacy regimes. Dynamic shuffling with the optimistic (lower-bound) accounting still performs worse than Poisson subsampling for ε ≤ 16, and the gap widens with more epochs. The Feldman et al. [2021] upper bound on σ_S (privacy amplification by shuffling) is compared in Figure 5 and found to be vacuous for batch sizes 2048 and above—it is no better than σ_D (the deterministic/no-amplification bound), confirming that existing shuffling amplification results do not capture the true privacy cost of multi-epoch shuffling in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-epoch privacy gap analysis of Chua et al. (ICML 2024) to the multi-epoch setting, providing lower bounds on the privacy guarantee of ABLQ under both persistent and dynamic shuffling. The Poisson subsampling analysis builds on the tightly dominating pair characterization of Zhu et al. (AISTATS 2022), with a new truncation correction to handle fixed-size batch constraints in practice. The paper also positions itself against the privacy amplification by shuffling bounds of Feldman et al. (FOCS 2021), showing those upper bounds are vacuous for realistic batch sizes, and departs from the widespread practice of implementing shuffling-based DP-SGD while reporting Poisson subsampling privacy parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a near-universal practice in the DP-SGD ecosystem: using shuffling for batch generation but reporting privacy as if Poisson subsampling were used. By providing multi-epoch lower bounds and a scalable MapReduce-based implementation of truncated Poisson subsampling, it offers a concrete path toward correct privacy accounting at scale. Key open questions remain: tight upper bounds for shuffling-based ABLQ are still unknown, buffer-based shuffling (as in tf.data.Dataset.shuffle) lacks any privacy analysis, and the empirical evaluation is limited to a single tabular dataset and model architecture. The finding that the privacy-utility gap between shuffling and Poisson subsampling widens in high-privacy regimes (small ε) has direct implications for applications requiring strong privacy guarantees.
