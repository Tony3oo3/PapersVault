## Problem

Existing text-to-video (T2V) evaluation protocols focus on temporal consistency and content continuity but largely ignore video dynamics, an essential dimension for measuring visual vividness and prompt fidelity. This omission is particularly harmful because commonly used metrics (motion smoothness, subject/background consistency) are negatively correlated with dynamics, enabling models to 'cheat' by generating low-dynamic, slow-motion videos that score well on these metrics while failing to match the intended dynamism of text prompts.

## Input / Output

The protocol consumes generated videos (standardized to 8 FPS) paired with text prompts that carry one of five discrete dynamics grades (static, low, medium, high, very high). It produces per-video dynamics scores at three temporal granularities (inter-frame, inter-segment, video-level), which are aggregated into two model-level metrics: dynamics range (the 99th-minus-1st percentile spread of scores across the benchmark) and dynamics controllability (the fraction of prompt-pair comparisons where higher-grade prompts yield higher dynamics scores). Additionally, it outputs improved versions of existing metrics (motion smoothness, subject/background consistency, naturalness) computed by averaging within 13 equal dynamics-score bins, and a naturalness score assigned by Gemini-1.5 Pro on a 0–100 five-tier scale.

## Method

DEVIL defines seven dynamics scores spanning three temporal granularities: inter-frame (RAFT optical-flow strength, 1−SSIM, and perceptual-hash Hamming distance between consecutive frames), inter-segment (patch-level aperiodicity via autocorrelation of DINO features and global aperiodicity via ViCLIP segment-feature cosine similarity), and video-level (conditional temporal entropy estimated with FFmpeg and DINO feature variance). A human-alignment module fits a separate linear regression at each granularity using six-annotator ratings as ground truth, producing aligned scores D_f, D_s, D_v that are averaged into an overall dynamics score. The text prompt benchmark (~800 prompts, 19 object + 4 scene categories) is constructed by GPT-4 coarse classification of ~50k prompts into five dynamics grades followed by three months of human refinement. To correct the negative correlation between dynamics and existing quality metrics, the protocol divides the aligned dynamics score into 13 equal intervals and averages metric values within each interval, so that a high improved score requires the model to produce high-quality content across the full dynamics spectrum rather than concentrating in the low-dynamics regime.

## Results

DEVIL's human-aligned dynamics scores achieve up to 98.0% Pearson correlation and 91.4% win ratio with human ratings across temporal levels (Table 3), validating the protocol's reliability. On the main dynamics evaluation (Table 4), overall dynamics range spans from 27.8 (GEN-2) to 63.4 (FreeNoise-Lavie), while dynamics controllability ranges from 56.6 (VideoCrafter2) to 80.8 (GEN-2). The paper further demonstrates that existing metrics such as Subject Consistency (Pearson −0.81) and Motion Smoothness (Pearson −0.63) are strongly negatively correlated with dynamics, revealing that models can 'cheat' by generating low-dynamic videos to inflate quality scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 T2V models using the proposed dynamics protocol, revealing a clear trade-off between dynamics range and controllability. GEN-2 achieves the highest dynamics controllability (80.8) but the lowest overall dynamics range (27.8), indicating it produces consistently low-dynamic videos that nonetheless match prompt intent. FreeNoise-Lavie attains the highest overall dynamics range (63.4) and inter-frame range (67.6) but the second-lowest controllability (58.2), suggesting it generates dynamic content that poorly aligns with text prompt dynamics grades. StreamingT2V offers the best balance with the highest video-level dynamics range (69.9) and a solid overall range (61.4) alongside moderate controllability (64.0). ModelScope leads in inter-frame dynamics range (72.1) but trails in controllability (63.7). VideoCrafter2 shows the weakest controllability (56.6) and lowest video-level range (17.0), while Pika and Show-1 occupy the middle ground with controllability scores of 72.2 and 74.2 respectively. The key insight is that no single model excels on both dimensions simultaneously, and commercial models (GEN-2, Pika) prioritize controllability at the expense of dynamics vividness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2V evaluation lineage established by VBench and EvalCrafter, which focused on temporal consistency, content continuity, and video-text alignment, by introducing dynamics as a primary evaluation dimension that prior protocols largely ignored. Methodologically, it composes well-known signal-processing tools (RAFT optical flow, SSIM, perceptual hashing, autocorrelation, FFmpeg entropy) with learned representations (DINO, ViCLIP) and calibrates them via linear regression against human ratings, while also leveraging an MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) for a naturalness score. The key conceptual departure is treating dynamics not as a secondary quality attribute but as a first-class axis that interacts with and can invalidate existing metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used metrics like subject consistency (Pearson r ≈ −0.88) and background consistency (r ≈ −0.79) are strongly negatively correlated with dynamics reveals a gaming vulnerability: models can inflate scores by generating low-motion, static videos. The DEVIL benchmark (800 prompts across 5 dynamics grades) and the improved interval-averaged metrics offer a practical path to close this loophole. Open questions include whether the 5-grade dynamics taxonomy is sufficiently fine-grained, whether the negative-correlation finding generalizes beyond the 11 evaluated models, and how to incorporate dynamics-aware training signals into T2V models themselves.
