## Problem

Computing Gröbner bases is NP-hard with doubly exponential worst-case complexity, making forward generation of training pairs (non-Gröbner set, Gröbner basis) intractable for learning. Two algebraic problems—random generation of Gröbner bases and the backward transformation from a Gröbner basis to a non-Gröbner set generating the same ideal—had never been addressed in computational algebra because all prior work focused on the forward direction. This paper is the first to frame Gröbner basis computation as a learnable sequence-to-sequence task and to resolve the dataset generation bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The input is a finite set of polynomials F ⊂ k[x₁,…,xₙ] over a field k ∈ {Q, R, F₇, F₃₁} with a fixed lexicographic term order, tokenized into an infix sequence with separator tokens. The output is the reduced Gröbner basis G of ⟨F⟩, also tokenized as a sequence. With the hybrid embedding, coefficients are predicted by regression (MSE < 0.1 threshold) while the polynomial support (term structure) is predicted by classification, so the model simultaneously solves a classification and a regression task during auto-regressive generation.

## Method

The dataset is generated backward: Gröbner bases in shape position are sampled by drawing a univariate polynomial h ∈ k[xₙ] and polynomials g₁,…,gₙ₋₁ ∈ k[xₙ] with deg(gᵢ) < deg(h), then a non-Gröbner set F = U₁PU₂G is produced via a Bruhat decomposition A = U₁PU₂ of a unimodular upper-triangular polynomial matrix, guaranteeing ⟨F⟩ = ⟨G⟩. For the model, a standard 6-layer encoder-decoder Transformer (8 heads, 512-dim embeddings) is trained with AdamW on 1M (F, G) pairs. The key architectural novelty is a hybrid input embedding where coefficient tokens are mapped through a small one-hidden-layer ReLU network f_E: R → R^D instead of a lookup table, implementing a continuity inductive bias and eliminating the vocabulary-size vs. sequence-length tradeoff for fields like Q and R.

## Results

The proposed backward dataset generation is 40–180× faster than forward generation (STD/SLIMGB) for n≥3 variables: e.g., 5.46 s vs. 216.3 s (STD) and 183.4 s (SLIMGB) for n=3, and 7.91 s vs. 1411.1 s (STD) for n=5. On the learnability front, Transformers trained on 1M samples achieve up to 93.7% accuracy / 95.4% support accuracy on ℚ[x₁,x₂] (discrete embedding), and 99.7% / 99.8% on the Cauchy module dataset (n=2, ℚ). Support accuracy consistently exceeds coefficient accuracy by 2–5 points, indicating the model identifies polynomial structure well but struggles with exact coefficient values, especially over finite fields.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its backward generation against three forward Gröbner basis algorithms (standard Buchberger, SLIMGB, and STDFGLM) for dataset construction. The backward method dominates STD and SLIMGB by 40× (n=3) to 180× (n=5) in runtime, and these forward methods suffer increasing timeouts (13–25% of runs) as n grows, while the backward method never times out. STDFGLM, designed specifically for 0-dimensional ideals, is competitive at n=3 (8.29 s vs. 5.46 s) but still loses by 3× at n=4 and 21× at n=5. In the learning experiments, the discrete embedding outperforms the hybrid (regression-based) embedding on coefficient accuracy for all fields (e.g., 93.7% vs. 66.8% on ℚ, n=2), though both achieve similar support accuracy (~95% vs. ~87%). Over finite fields, performance degrades sharply with field size: ℤ/31ℤ accuracy drops to 46.8% (disc.) and 6.1% (hyb.) at n=2, versus 72.3% and 54.1% for ℤ/7ℤ, suggesting modular arithmetic remains a fundamental challenge for Transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying Transformers to symbolic mathematics (Lample & Charton 2020 for integration, Charton 2022 for linear algebra) to the NP-hard task of Gröbner basis computation, but identifies a fundamentally different bottleneck: dataset generation rather than model architecture. It departs from all prior computational algebra work, which exclusively addresses forward Gröbner basis computation, by formulating two previously unaddressed algebraic problems—random generation of Gröbner bases and the backward Gröbner problem—and solving them via shape-position structure and Bruhat decomposition of unimodular polynomial matrices. The hybrid input embedding, which routes coefficient tokens through a small feed-forward network instead of a discrete vocabulary, is a new architectural contribution motivated by the unbounded coefficient range in Q and R.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backward dataset generation method is orders of magnitude faster than forward computation, removing the primary practical barrier to training models on Gröbner basis pairs at scale (millions of samples). The counter-intuitive finding that Transformers perform worse on finite fields than on infinite fields, despite fewer possible coefficient values, points to unresolved difficulties in learning modular arithmetic and suggests that the current approach is not yet a general solver. Key open questions include sampling from the full SL_n(k[x]) group beyond the Bruhat-decomposition subset, analyzing the distribution of Gröbner bases and non-Gröbner sets, and extending to positive-dimensional or application-specific ideal classes where the shape-position assumption fails.
