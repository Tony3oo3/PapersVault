## Problem

Existing deep learning approaches for brain connectome analysis either model structural connectivity (SC) or functional connectivity (FC) in isolation, or fuse them at the node/link level without capturing how multi-hop SC pathways physically support individual FC links. This paper identifies that the multivariate SC-FC coupling mechanism—where a direct FC link is underpinned by a detour pathway of SC edges forming a cyclic loop—remains unexplored in graph representation learning, limiting both predictive performance and neuroscience interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired brain graphs: an SC adjacency matrix derived from DWI tractography (fiber counts) and an FC adjacency matrix from fMRI Pearson correlations, with node attributes set as either raw BOLD time-series (dynamic) or BOLD correlations (static). The detour adjacency matrix D^h is precomputed as the element-wise product of the h-th power of the binary SC adjacency and the binary FC adjacency, encoding which node pairs share an h-hop SC detour that also has a direct FC link. Outputs include a class prediction (cognitive task or disease status) and, critically, a ranked set of neural pathways whose attention weights reveal which SC detours most contribute to the prediction.

## Method

NeuroPath is a twin-branch Transformer where each branch applies multi-head self-attention (MHSA) with a different adjacency mask: the TD-MHSA branch masks attention with detour adjacency matrices D^h (one per head, corresponding to hop lengths 1 through H), while the FC-MHSA branch masks with the FC adjacency. A consistency constraint L_TD = ||f_TD(X) − f_FC(X)||² forces the two branches to produce aligned node representations, so the FC branch inherits the pathway-aware expressiveness of the TD branch. The key theoretical result (Fact 3.1) shows that each node's output is a weighted sum over half of all neural pathways within H hops, with path weights decomposable into learnable attention parameters—enabling O(N²) complexity versus O(N^H) for exhaustive path enumeration. The readout for classification applies a graph convolution on the FC-normalized adjacency to the TD-branch output, and the entire model is trained end-to-end with a cross-entropy loss plus the consistency term.

## Results

On the UK Biobank (UKB) dataset, NeuroPath achieves the highest accuracy of 99.59% for neural activity classification, surpassing the best baseline (GCN at 99.31%). On HCP-Aging (4-class task recognition), NeuroPath achieves 98.23% accuracy, holding second place behind BrainGNN and BolT. For Alzheimer's disease diagnosis, NeuroPath is the only model exceeding 90% accuracy on OASIS (90.01%) and achieves the best accuracy on ADNI (85.56%), with top F1 scores of 83.29% (ADNI) and 87.02% (OASIS) across all data settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroPath outperforms all six baselines on UKB (99.59% vs. best baseline GCN at 99.31%) and achieves the best accuracy on both disease diagnosis datasets (ADNI: 85.56%, OASIS: 90.01%), where it is the only model exceeding 90% on OASIS. On HCP-Aging, NeuroPath (98.23%) ranks second behind BrainGNN (99.00%) and BolT (99.05%), which have more than double the parameters. The general graph transformers Graphormer and NAGphormer perform notably worse than even the simple GCN baseline on HCP-Aging (83.64% and 97.70% respectively), as they lack neuroscience-specific inductive biases. In zero-shot learning (HCPA→UKB static), NeuroPath achieves 91.29% F1, surpassing NAGphormer (74.49%) and Graphormer (39.09%) by a wide margin. Ablation studies confirm that the twin-branch SC-FC coupling design contributes most to performance gains, with the full model outperforming single-branch variants by 2–4% on disease datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph transformer lineage (Graphormer, NAGphormer) by replacing generic structural encodings with a neuroscience-motivated attention mask derived from the 'topological detour' — a formalization of how functional connectivity links are physically supported by multi-hop structural connectivity pathways. It departs from prior brain GNNs (BrainGNN, BNT, BolT) that fuse SC and FC at the node or edge level, instead introducing a twin-branch architecture (TD-MHSA + FC-MHSA) with a consistency constraint that enforces alignment between SC-detour and FC representations. The detour adjacency matrix construction achieves O(N²) complexity for multi-hop pathway modeling, contrasting with PathNN's O(N^H) exhaustive path enumeration, while the theoretical analysis (Fact 3.1) provides a graph-theoretic guarantee that the attention mechanism captures half of all neural pathways within H hops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot learning results (e.g., 91.29% F1 for HCPA→UKB static, surpassing the best competitor by ~17 points) suggest that SC-FC coupled pathway representations are more transferable across populations than FC-only patterns, hinting at a more fundamental inductive bias for brain connectomics. The visualization showing Alzheimer's subjects requiring longer structural detours to support the same functional links provides an interpretable, testable neuroscience hypothesis about compensatory rewiring. Key limitations include modeling only half of all neural pathways (amplified in dense dynamic graphs), small disease cohort sizes (ADNI n=138), and the fact that supervised performance gains over SOTA brain models are modest (1–3 percentage points), leaving open whether the SC-FC coupling mechanism scales to larger, more heterogeneous clinical populations.
