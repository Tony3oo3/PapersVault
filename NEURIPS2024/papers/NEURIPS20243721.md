## Problem

Existing calibration measures for probability forecasting (ECE, smooth calibration error, distance from calibration, U-calibration) all suffer from large truthfulness gaps: on simple product distributions, a strategic forecaster can achieve near-zero penalty while the truthful forecaster who predicts the true conditional probability incurs a polynomial penalty. This means these measures actively incentivize forecasters to make deliberately biased predictions rather than incorporating their best available information. The paper asks whether a calibration measure can simultaneously be complete (correct predictions get small penalty), sound (systematically wrong predictions get large penalty), and truthful (predicting the true distribution is near-optimal).

## Input / Output

The SSCE takes a binary outcome sequence x ∈ {0,1}^T and a probability prediction sequence p ∈ [0,1]^T, and outputs a scalar in [0,T] defined as the expectation over a uniformly random subset S ⊆ [T] of the smooth calibration error (supremum over 1-Lipschitz functions from [0,1] to [-1,1] of the weighted bias sum) restricted to the subsampled entries. The paper also produces a deterministic forecasting algorithm achieving O(√T) SSCE against any adaptive adversary, and a complete taxonomy (Table 2) classifying eight existing measures along completeness, soundness, and truthfulness axes.

## Method

The SSCE is constructed by a simple modification to the smooth calibration error: instead of evaluating the supremum over 1-Lipschitz functions on the full time horizon, it takes an expectation over a uniformly random subset of time steps. The key insight is that subsampling prevents a strategic forecaster from exploiting knowledge of past outcome biases to offset calibration errors, since the forecaster cannot predict which steps will be sampled. The proof of (O(1),0)-truthfulness proceeds in two parts: an upper bound on the truthful forecaster's SSCE via Dudley's chaining combined with Freedman's martingale inequality applied epoch-by-epoch (using a doubling trick to handle the randomness in the realized variance process), and a lower bound on the optimal SSCE via a sub-martingale argument showing that the count of steps with |x_t − p_t| ≥ 1/2 stochastically dominates the realized variance. The O(√T) adversarial guarantee follows from a reduction connecting SSCE to the distance from calibration via a standard chaining argument, combined with the O(√T) distance-from-calibration predictor of Arunachaleswaran et al. (2024).

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1.2) establishes that the Subsampled Smooth Calibration Error (SSCE) is (O(1), 0)-truthful, complete, and sound — the first calibration measure to simultaneously satisfy all three desiderata. Theorem 1.1 shows that for every product distribution with marginals bounded away from 0 and 1, the truthful forecaster incurs Ω(√T) smooth calibration error while a strategic algorithm achieves only polylog(T) error, demonstrating a fundamental truthfulness gap in existing measures. Theorem 1.3 proves that an O(√T) SSCE is achievable in the adversarial sequential prediction setting, matching the optimal rate for distance from calibration while avoiding the impossibility of O(√T) ECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSCE dominates all existing calibration measures by being the only one that is simultaneously complete, sound, and approximately truthful. Against ECE (Foster & Vohra) and Maximum Swap Regret (Hu & Wu), SSCE eliminates the 0–Ω(T) truthfulness gap: on the same product distribution where ECE/MSR allow a strategic forecaster to achieve zero penalty while the truthful forecaster pays Ω(T), SSCE bounds the truthful forecaster's penalty to O(1)·OPT. Against Smooth Calibration Error (Kakade & Foster), Distance from Calibration, Interval Calibration, and Laplace-Kernel Calibration (Błasiok et al.), SSCE closes the 0–Ω(√T) gap to (O(1),0)-truthfulness via the subsampling mechanism. Against U-Calibration (Kleinberg et al.), which has the smallest existing gap of O(1)–Ω(√T), SSCE improves the truthful forecaster's penalty from Ω(√T) to O(1)·OPT. Against Proper Scoring Rules (Winkler & Murphy), which are perfectly (1,0)-truthful but fail completeness (incurring Ω(T) penalty even on i.i.d. Bernoulli trials with correct predictions), SSCE trades perfect truthfulness for completeness while retaining an O(1) approximation factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sequential calibration framework initiated by Foster and Vohra (1998) and the smooth calibration error of Kakade and Foster (2008), and directly extends the recent systematic study of calibration measures by Błasiok et al. (2023) and Qiao and Zheng (2024). It departs from prior work by introducing truthfulness as a first-class desideratum for calibration measures—a property that had not been systematically studied—and by showing that the subsampling technique (borrowed from differential privacy and adaptive data analysis) is the key ingredient that restores truthfulness while preserving completeness and soundness. The analysis draws on Dudley's chaining for Lipschitz function classes and Freedman's martingale inequalities, with a novel 'doubling trick' to handle unbounded realized variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that every standard calibration measure (ECE, smCE, distance from calibration, U-calibration) has a large truthfulness gap is a significant conceptual result: it means that the metrics the field uses to evaluate forecasters actively incentivize strategic, untruthful prediction. SSCE provides a principled fix, but the paper also identifies open trade-offs (e.g., proper scoring rules are perfectly truthful but not complete) and shows that truthfulness against fully adaptive adversaries is unlikely to hold for any natural measure. For practitioners using calibration metrics in finance, medicine, or ML model evaluation, this work suggests that current evaluation protocols may be systematically misleading.
