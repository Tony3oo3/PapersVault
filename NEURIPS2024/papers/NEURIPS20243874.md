## Problem

Standard pure exploration linear bandit algorithms assume users are properly randomized to treatments, but many online services cannot directly assign users to specific experiences due to business constraints, leading to self-selection bias. This paper introduces the confounded pure exploration transductive linear bandit (CPET-LB) problem, where the learner can only randomize an encouragement (instrument) rather than the treatment itself, and the resulting noise is endogenous. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to develop adaptive experimental design methods for pure exploration in a setting where noise is confounded.

## Input / Output

The learner selects instruments z_t from a finite set Z ⊂ R^d (encouragements) and observes both the user's actual treatment choice x_t ∈ X ⊂ R^d and the outcome y_t ∈ R, generated via the structural equation x_t = Γ^⊤z_t + η_t, y_t = x_t^⊤θ + ε_t. The algorithm takes as input the sets Z, W, noise upper bounds L_ν and L_η, and confidence level δ, and outputs the optimal evaluation vector w* = argmax_{w∈W} w^⊤θ with probability at least 1−δ. A key structural constraint is that the learner cannot directly choose which treatment a user receives—only which encouragement to present—making the feedback model fundamentally different from standard linear bandits where the measurement vector directly determines the observed feature.

## Method

The core algorithm (CPEG/CPEUG) is an elimination-style procedure built on two key technical components: (1) novel finite-time confidence intervals for a pseudo two-stage least squares (P-2SLS) estimator that decompose into a design-controllable variance term and an approximation error term from estimating Γ, and (2) a two-sub-phase design where the first sub-phase uses an E-optimal design to estimate Γ while the second uses an XY-optimal design to estimate θ. The central algorithmic challenge is a circular dependency: the optimal design for estimating Γ depends on Γ itself, which can only be computed after sampling. This is resolved via a doubling trick that iteratively increases sample size under a design computed from the previous phase's Γ estimate until the confidence width meets the target. The resulting sample complexity upper bound scales with a problem-dependent quantity ρ* that captures the hardness of the instance, nearly matching a minimax lower bound, and degrades gracefully with the conditioning of Γ (sample complexity blows up as Γ becomes ill-conditioned, reflecting the impossibility of deconfounding with weak instruments).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that CPEG (Algorithm 1) is δ-PAC and terminates in at most O(L_ν ρ* log(1/δ)) samples when Γ is known, where ρ* is a problem-dependent hardness quantity. A matching lower bound (Theorem D.1) shows that Ω(σ²ρ* log(1/δ)) samples are necessary, so the upper and lower bounds nearly coincide. For the fully unknown Γ setting (Theorem 3.4), CPEUG (Algorithm 3) achieves a sample complexity of O((L_ν log(1/δ) + L_η‖θ‖²(d + log(1/δ)))ρ* + (d + log(1/δ))(L_η‖θ‖²ρ₀ + M)), which scales with ρ* almost matching the known-Γ case. In the compliance special case, the sample complexity upper bound is O(d/σ_min²(Γ) · 1/Δ_min²), which can degrade to O(d²ε⁻²/Δ_min²) when Γ is a perturbation of the uniform matrix (weak instruments).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CPEG/CPEUG against several baselines in the experiments (Appendix E, Figure 4). In the Jump-Around instance (d=6, known Γ), CPEG substantially outperforms Static Uniform and Adaptive Uniform (SE), and nearly matches the Static Oracle design that knows w* in advance. In the Interpolation instance with a permutation matrix Γ, SE exhibits significant underperformance because its uniform sampling rule does not account for the permutation effect, while CPEG consistently achieves near-optimal sample complexity. In the motivating compliance example (Section 1.2, Figure 2), UCB-OLS completely fails to identify the optimal arm due to selection bias, UCB-IV improves but plateaus at a constant probability of error (because it over-samples arm 6 which has the highest ITT reward), while CPEG identifies the correct arm with significantly higher probability. For the unknown-Γ setting, CPEUG with its E-optimal design and doubling trick remains competitive with the known-Γ oracle, whereas replacing either the Γ-estimation or θ-estimation design with uniform sampling degrades performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pure exploration transductive linear bandit framework (RAGE, Fiez et al. 2019; Soare et al. 2014) to a setting where the noise is confounded, i.e., the treatment choice is endogenous with respect to the outcome. The estimation machinery is drawn from the econometrics literature on instrumental variables and two-stage least squares (Angrist et al. 1996), with the key adaptation being a novel pseudo-2SLS (P-2SLS) estimator and finite-time confidence intervals tailored for experimental design. The problem setting connects to the compliance/encouragement design literature in online experimentation, but departs from causal bandit work (which targets the best intervention argmax z^T Γθ) by targeting the best treatment argmax w^T θ, and from prior confounded bandit work (Kallus 2018, Bareinboim et al. 2015) by focusing on pure exploration rather than regret minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to address pure exploration linear bandits under confounded noise, filling a gap between the well-studied unconfounded setting and the practical reality that many online services cannot directly randomize treatment assignment. The near-matching upper and lower bounds (with an additional d² factor from confounding) provide a clear characterization of the fundamental difficulty. Open questions include tightening the instance-dependent lower bound, handling the case where the instrument and treatment dimensions differ (d_z ≠ d_x), and improving the dependence on the noise variance. The doubling trick for resolving the circular dependency in experimental design (where the optimal design depends on an estimate that itself depends on the data collected under that design) is a technique that may generalize to other adaptive estimation problems.
