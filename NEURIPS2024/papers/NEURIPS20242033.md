## Problem

Mainstream LLM quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ) require task-specific calibration and LoRA adapters, producing divergent quantized models that cannot be shared across tasks—making multi-task serving with quantization infeasible in existing systems like S-LoRA and Punica. Additionally, these systems lack support for dynamic task addition without service interruption and ignore workload heterogeneity across tasks during scheduling, leading to excessive LoRA adapter swapping and poor throughput.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242033/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a base LLM (LLaMA2-7B or 13B), multiple LoRA adapters (ranks 8–64) for distinct downstream tasks, and per-task calibration datasets. It produces a single 4-bit (or 3-bit) quantized base model shared across all adapters, and at serving time accepts text prompts routed to the appropriate LoRA adapter to generate text. A small 255MB output-length prediction model runs on CPU (~16 ms per request) to inform scheduling decisions without occupying GPU resources.

## Method

MLGPTQ performs joint multi-task quantization by loading each task's LoRA adapter during the forward pass to capture accurate per-task activations, computing per-task Hessian matrices, and applying a max-aggregation rule that selects the task with the largest (H_t⁻¹)_{qq} diagonal entry for each weight update—avoiding the information dilution of naive sum-aggregation. For dynamic task addition, the system caches the aggregated Hessian (H_tmp) from prior quantization and performs incremental re-quantization on only the new tasks' Hessians, executed asynchronously in a layer-by-layer fashion to keep peak memory at 2.5 GB without halting serving. The multi-task scheduler combines CPU-side output-length prediction with Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF) ordering and a grouping coefficient (β=10) that caps the number of distinct LoRA adapters loaded per scheduling step, prioritizing tasks already resident in GPU memory to minimize CPU-GPU adapter swaps.

## Results

For 4-bit quantization, MLGPTQ achieves an average accuracy drop of only 1.70% across 12 tasks, compared to 4.72% for GPTQ_tweaked, 4.50% for AWQ_tweaked, and 4.02% for RTN. At 3-bit, MLGPTQ outperforms GPTQ_tweaked and AWQ_tweaked by 59.30% and 69.98% in average accuracy, while RTN suffers up to 74.41% drops. At the system level, LoRA-Inlaid surpasses S-LoRA by 26.5% (throughput), 31.3% (latency), and 24.1% (JCT) on average, and up to 58.1%, 76.3%, and 99.9% respectively. SLO Attainment improves by 3.9× and 8.5× on average over S-LoRA and vLLM, reaching up to 10× and 38× under heavy loads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ_tweaked and AWQ_tweaked (which use mixed calibration without LoRA adapters), MLGPTQ reduces the average accuracy drop from 4.72%/4.50% to 1.70% at 4-bit, and the gap widens dramatically at 3-bit (59–70% relative improvement). Compared to single-task GPTQ and AWQ (which cannot share a quantized model), MLGPTQ achieves nearly identical quality (1.70% vs. 1.00%/1.89% avg drop at 4-bit) while enabling multi-task sharing. At the system level, LoRA-Inlaid outperforms S-LoRA on all efficiency metrics (throughput, latency, JCT, SLO) and supports LLaMA2-13B where S-LoRA fails due to lack of quantization support. Against vLLM, LoRA-Inlaid avoids the linear memory growth that causes OOM at 5+ tasks, maintaining stable throughput even at 1000 concurrent tasks. In scheduling ablations, LoRA-Inlaid's grouping + SRTF strategy beats FastServe's Skip-join MLFQ on SLO Attainment, with the SRTF component alone contributing a 2.27× improvement over FIFO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), post-training quantization (GPTQ, AWQ), and multi-task LLM serving (S-LoRA, Punica). It departs from prior multi-task serving systems by identifying that their inability to share a quantized base model across tasks stems from the task-specific calibration requirement in mainstream PTQ methods. The MLGPTQ algorithm extends GPTQ's layer-wise Hessian-based weight update by replacing naive mixed-calibration aggregation with a per-task max-aggregation of inverse Hessian diagonals, while the scheduling strategy extends SRTF with output-length prediction and task grouping to address workload heterogeneity across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes multi-task LLM serving with quantization practical on a single consumer GPU, which is significant for cost-sensitive deployment scenarios where serving dozens to hundreds of fine-tuned tasks simultaneously is needed. The dynamic task addition mechanism (asynchronous, layer-wise re-quantization with cached Hessian matrices) addresses a real operational gap in production serving. Open questions remain around fairness among tasks (the scheduling ignores per-task token budgets), detection of malicious LoRA adapters that could poison the shared quantized model, and extension to multi-modal tasks. The paper also notes that the quantization does not protect against adversarially crafted calibration data.
