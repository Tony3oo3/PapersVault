## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) suffer from hallucination due to modality misalignment, where the model prioritizes textual knowledge over visual input even when both components are high-quality. Existing preference-optimization methods rely on external models (e.g., GPT-4) or human annotations to curate preference data, which is resource-intensive and fails to capture the target LVLM's inherent preferences. Directly applying self-rewarding approaches from LLMs to LVLMs does not address the modality misalignment, as the self-generated preferences still overlook visual information.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a text response along with self-generated preference pairs (chosen and rejected responses). Candidate responses are constructed sentence-by-sentence via beam search, with each sentence scored by a calibrated reward combining the language decoder's cumulative probability and a CLIP-based image-response relevance score. Training uses approximately 13,000 image-prompt pairs sampled from LLaVA-150k, and the iterative process runs for three rounds on a single A100 80GB GPU.

## Method

CSR introduces a step-wise calibrated reward that combines a self-generated instruction-following score R_T (sentence-level cumulative token probability from the language decoder) with an image-response relevance score R_I (CLIP cosine similarity between the image embedding and sentence embedding), weighted by a hyperparameter λ (set to 0.9 in practice to emphasize visual alignment). During candidate generation, sentence-level beam search uses these calibrated rewards to select top-k and bottom-k sentences at each step, and the full response with the highest/lowest cumulative reward becomes the preferred/dispreferred pair. The model is then fine-tuned via DPO using the previous iteration's model as the reference policy, and this generate-evaluate-finetune loop repeats iteratively. A theoretical analysis under a Gaussian generative model proves that when the model over-relies on text (‖β*ᵀV₁ᵀβ*‖ ≪ ‖β*ᵀV₂ᵀβ*‖), incorporating the visual constraint (λ<1) strictly reduces the expected regression loss compared to the uncalibrated self-rewarding case (λ=1).

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, CSR achieves an average improvement of 7.62% across ten benchmarks compared to the base model, with particularly large gains on LLaVA-W (63.4→71.1, +8.9%) and CHAIR-S (48.8→21.0, −49.5% hallucination rate). The 13B variant shows a 5.25% average improvement. CSR outperforms the closest self-rewarding baseline by 2.43% on average, and surpasses all external-preference baselines (POVID, RLHF-V, Silkie, LLaVA-RLHF) on the majority of benchmarks, including POPE (87.01 vs. 86.90 for POVID) and CHAIR-I (6.0 vs. 8.3 for POVID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSR outperforms all five preference-optimization baselines on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Against the closest competitor, Self-rewarding [29], CSR gains +18.6 on MME-P, +9.9 on LLaVA-W, +2.5 on MM-Vet, and reduces CHAIR-S from 24.0 to 21.0. Against POVID [10] (GPT-4-curated preferences), CSR leads on MME-P (+71.4), LLaVA-W (+2.4), MM-Vet (+2.1), and CHAIR-S (21.0 vs. 35.2). Against RLHF-V [30] (human-annotated corrections), CSR wins on every metric including MME-P (+35.0) and CHAIR-S (21.0 vs. 29.7). Against Silkie/VLFeedback [9] and LLaVA-RLHF [8], CSR shows the largest margins (e.g., +91.5 MME-P over Silkie, +33.6 MME-P over LLaVA-RLHF). In the broader comparison against other open-source LVLMs (Table 5), CSR-7B surpasses BLIP-2 [34], InstructBLIP [31], IDEFICS [35], Qwen-VL-Chat [32], and mPLUG-Owl2 [33] on 9 out of 10 benchmarks, with the sole exception being GQA where mPLUG-Owl2 edges ahead (56.1 vs. 62.3 — actually CSR wins here too; the paper states 9/10). CSR also generalizes to the Vila 7B backbone, improving overall performance by 3.37% over three iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-rewarding paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024) from LLMs to LVLMs by introducing a visual calibration step: the self-generated instruction-following score is combined with a CLIP-based image-response relevance score at the sentence level during beam search. It departs from prior LVLM preference optimization methods (POVID, RLHF-V, Silkie, LLaVA-RLHF) that rely on external models or human annotations to curate preference data, arguing that such externally-sourced preferences are easily distinguishable by the target model and thus less effective. The step-wise reward design draws inspiration from Process-Supervised Reward Models, and the overall iterative DPO fine-tuning framework follows the standard preference optimization pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSR offers a practical, annotation-free recipe for reducing LVLM hallucination that is compatible with multiple backbones (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, Vila 7B) and requires only a single A100 GPU per iteration, making it accessible. The 49.5% reduction in CHAIR and the theoretical guarantee that visual constraints provably improve generation quality when the model over-relies on text provide both empirical and formal grounding. Key open questions include whether the method scales to larger models (only 7B/13B tested), whether more than three iterations yield further gains (improvements plateau), and whether the CLIP-score calibration generalizes to video or multi-image settings.
