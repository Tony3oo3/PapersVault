## Problem

Existing alignment techniques such as refusal training and RLHF are routinely bypassed by adversarial attacks, while adversarial training fails to generalize to unseen attacks and imposes significant capability penalties. The paper asks whether it is possible to make models intrinsically unable to produce harmful outputs—regardless of the attack used to elicit them—without sacrificing utility, effectively moving beyond the cat-and-mouse paradigm of defending against specific attack methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard text prompts, image-plus-text prompts (for multimodal models), or text prompts paired with function definitions (for agent settings), and produces safe text completions or safe function calls. The intervention occurs at the internal representation level: LoRA adapters are inserted into specific transformer layers (layers 10 and 20 for LLMs, layer 16 for the multimodal backbone), and the circuit-breaking loss is applied to both user and assistant tokens in the circuit breaker set. No additional inference-time computation is required beyond the LoRA adapters, so the model can be deployed normally without auxiliary guard models or input/output filters.

## Method

The core contribution is Representation Rerouting (RR), a circuit-breaking technique built on Low-Rank Representation Adaptation (LoRRA). Training uses two datasets: a Circuit Breaker Set of examples that elicit harmful internal representations, and a Retain Set of benign examples whose representations must be preserved. The RR loss applies ReLU(cosine_similarity) between the original model's representation and the circuit-broken model's representation on harmful examples, pushing the latter orthogonal to the former so that harmful generation processes are interrupted mid-sequence. A complementary L2 retain loss preserves benign representations, and a linear coefficient schedule gradually shifts weight from the circuit-breaking loss to the retain loss over training. Because the targeted representation is independent of any specific attack that activates it, the method is attack-agnostic and generalizes to unseen threats including embedding-space attacks, representation-space attacks, and prefilling attacks, while preserving capability scores within ~1% of the original model.

## Results

On HarmBench's suite of 10 unseen attacks, Representation Rerouting (RR) reduces the average attack success rate (ASR) from 76.7% to 9.8% on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v2 and from 38.1% to 3.8% on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, while preserving capability (MT-Bench 7.53 vs. 7.60; Open LLM 65.4 vs. 65.4 for Mistral; MT-Bench 8.00 vs. 8.05; Open LLM 68.3 vs. 68.8 for Llama-3). The integrated Cygnet model (Llama-3-8B + RR + additional RepE controls) achieves an average ASR of 0.8%—roughly two orders of magnitude below the original—while *improving* capability to MT-Bench 8.21 and Open LLM 71.9. In the multimodal setting, RR reduces PGD-attack compliance from 91.0% to 14.3% on LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B with <0.5% drop on MMMU and LLaVA-Wild.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the refusal-trained Mistral-7B-Instruct-v2 baseline, RR cuts average ASR from 76.7% to 9.8% (≈87% reduction) while matching capability exactly (MT-Bench 7.53 vs. 7.60; Open LLM 65.4 vs. 65.4). Compared to the adversarially trained R2D2 model (which was fine-tuned specifically against GCG), RR achieves a lower average ASR (9.8% vs. 31.7%) and substantially better capability (MT-Bench 7.53 vs. 6.00; Open LLM 65.4 vs. 61.2), demonstrating that attack-specific training sacrifices generalization and utility. Against the refusal-trained Llama-3-8B-Instruct, RR reduces average ASR from 38.1% to 3.8% (≈90% reduction) with negligible capability loss (MT-Bench 8.00 vs. 8.05). The Cygnet variant further improves on the original Llama-3 on both capability metrics (MT-Bench 8.21, Open LLM 71.9) while driving average ASR to 0.8%, representing a Pareto improvement. In the multimodal setting, RR outperforms both the original LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B (91.0% PGD compliance) and a safety-prompt baseline (96.2%) by reducing PGD compliance to 14.3%, while preserving MMMU (34.2 vs. 34.7) and LLaVA-Wild (79.3 vs. 79.2) scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Representation Engineering (RepE) [Zou et al., 2023] and its unlearning extension RMU [Li et al., 2024], which established that model behavior can be controlled by modifying internal representations via low-rank adapters. It departs from the dominant paradigm of adversarial training (e.g., R2D2, GCG-based fine-tuning) and output-level refusal training (RLHF/DPO) by targeting the representations that generate harmful content rather than the inputs that trigger it or the outputs that result. The key conceptual shift is from a cat-and-mouse arms race against specific attacks to an attack-agnostic intervention that severs the model's ability to complete harmful generations regardless of how they are elicited, including via embedding-space or representation-space manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that the capability-versus-harmlessness Pareto frontier for LLMs can be meaningfully advanced: a 20-minute LoRA training run on a single A100 yields ~90% ASR reduction across diverse unseen attacks while preserving benchmark capabilities, and the approach extends to multimodal image-hijack defenses and agent function-calling safety. Limitations are significant: evaluation is restricted to 8B-parameter models, single-turn conversations, and attacks specifically targeting harmful-content generation (not, e.g., label-flipping in classification). Open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier models, handles multi-turn and agentic loops, generalizes to non-generative architectures, and remains robust when adversaries are aware of the circuit-breaking mechanism and optimize against it directly.
