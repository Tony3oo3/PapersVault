## Problem

The sequential ℓ∞ entropy, the most widely studied complexity measure for sequential probability assignment with contexts, was shown to not characterize minimax regret in general, and no prior work had identified a complexity measure that exactly characterizes minimax regret for arbitrary (possibly nonparametric) hypothesis classes in the contextual setting. This paper addresses both the missing characterization and the absence of a general minimax optimal algorithm for the contextual, multiary-label, sequential-expert setting.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on arbitrary hypothesis classes F of sequential experts mapping from (X×Y)*×X to Δ(Y), where X is an arbitrary context space and Y is a finite label set of any cardinality. At each round t, the learner receives a fresh context x_t and the history of past labels y_{1:t-1}, and must output a probability distribution p̂_t over Y. The central theoretical output is the contextual Shtarkov sum S_T(F|x) defined over multiary context trees of depth T, which exactly characterizes minimax regret as sup_x log S_T(F|x), and the algorithmic output is the round-by-round cNML prediction computed from a data-dependent prefix variant of this sum.

## Method

The paper introduces the contextual Shtarkov sum S_T(F|x) = Σ_y sup_f P_f(y|x(y)), where the sum ranges over all label paths and contexts are determined by evaluating a multiary context tree x along each path, generalizing the classical Shtarkov sum from the context-free setting. The main theorem proves minimax regret equals the worst-case log contextual Shtarkov sum over all context trees, using a minimax swap with a smooth truncation map τ_δ that restricts predictions to a truncated simplex Δ^δ(Y) to apply Sion's minimax theorem, then taking δ→0 to eliminate regularity assumptions on F. The minimax optimal algorithm, contextual NML (cNML), is derived via a data-dependent variant called the contextual Shtarkov sum with prefix, which at each round t computes the optimal prediction as a normalized ratio of these prefix sums over the remaining label paths. As an application, the paper derives a new regret upper bound in terms of sequential ℓ∞ entropy that unifies and improves upon prior state-of-the-art bounds by Bilodeau et al. (2020) and Wu et al. (2023), and shows that sequential covering numbers alone cannot characterize minimax regret by constructing classes with identical covering numbers but different regret rates.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) establishes that the minimax regret for sequential probability assignment equals the worst-case log contextual Shtarkov sum: R_T(F) = sup_x log S_T(F|x). As a corollary, Theorem 3.7 yields a regret upper bound R_T(F) ≤ inf_α {T log(1+2α) + H_∞(F, α, T)} that simultaneously improves on both the BFR20 bound (4Tα + c·H_∞, c∈(3,4)) and the WHGS23 bound (T log(1+2α) + H_G). The paper also provides a matching Ω(√T) lower bound for the linear class F^Lin (Lemma 3.8) and proves that the contextual NML algorithm (cNML) is minimax optimal (Theorem 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical bound (Theorem 3.7) strictly dominates both prior state-of-the-art regret upper bounds. Compared to BFR20's bound of 4Tα + c·H_∞(F,α,T) with c∈(3,4), the new bound replaces the linear 4Tα term with the tighter T log(1+2α) (which is O(Tα) for small α with a smaller constant). Compared to WHGS23's bound of T log(1+2α) + H_G(F,α,T), the new bound replaces the global sequential entropy H_G with the (always no larger) sequential ℓ_∞ entropy H_∞. The paper further demonstrates that sequential ℓ_∞ covering numbers alone cannot characterize minimax regret: the linear class F^Lin and the absolute-linear class F^AbsLin have the same covering numbers at all scales, yet their minimax regrets differ (Õ(√T) vs. Θ̃(T^{2/3})), showing that the contextual Shtarkov sum captures finer structure that covering numbers miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Shtarkov sum characterization of minimax regret (Shtarkov, 1987) from the context-free setting and the fixed-design setting (Jacquet et al., 2021) to the fully adaptive contextual setting where nature reveals contexts sequentially. It operates within the online learning framework of Rakhlin, Sridharan, and Tewari (2010), but departs from the sequential covering number approach of Rakhlin & Sridharan (2015), Bilodeau et al. (2020), and Wu et al. (2023) by showing that covering numbers do not characterize minimax regret for log loss, and instead introduces the contextual Shtarkov sum as the exact complexity measure. The minimax optimal algorithm cNML generalizes NML by replacing the fixed context sequence with a context tree in the normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact characterization of minimax regret via the worst-case contextual Shtarkov sum provides a clean, computable (in principle) benchmark for all sequential prediction algorithms under log loss, and the cNML algorithm serves as the theoretical gold standard. The negative result showing that sequential ℓ∞ entropy fails to characterize regret (via the linear vs. absolute-linear class separation) redirects the field's attention toward likelihood-based complexity measures. Open questions include developing computationally efficient approximations to cNML, extending to infinite label spaces, and obtaining tighter quantitative bounds for specific classes using the new characterization.
