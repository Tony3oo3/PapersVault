## Problem

Existing cooperative motion forecasting methods fuse single-frame perception information from infrastructure or other vehicles to complete the ego vehicle's view before forecasting, which introduces error propagation and fails to capture the full motion and interaction context available in multi-view trajectories. The paper addresses how to interpretably aggregate redundant multi-view trajectory observations into enhanced historical representations for each agent, rather than performing frame-by-frame perception complement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240429/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-source historical trajectories (ego, infrastructure, and cooperative vehicle views) sampled at 10 Hz over a 4–5 second observation window, where each trajectory carries tracking ID, 2D location, heading, 7D bounding box, and agent type, along with vectorized lane segments (start/end points plus semantic attributes like turn direction and road type). The output is a Laplacian Mixture Model with K=6 modes predicting 4–5 seconds of future positions, from which the best mode is selected for evaluation via minADE, minFDE, and MR.

## Method

V2X-Graph constructs a scene graph where trajectories and lane segments are nodes and heterogeneous edges encode spatial-temporal and relative-spatial relations. An Interpretable Association module formulates cross-view trajectory matching as a graph link prediction problem, supervised by pseudo labels generated via per-frame Hungarian matching over bounding-box IoU, enabling end-to-end optimization. Three subgraphs then perform feature fusion: the Motion Fusion subGraph aggregates motion features of associated trajectories across views, the Agent-Lane subGraph queries lane-segment interaction features, and the Cooperative Interaction subGraph fuses dense agent-agent interaction features from all views. Training combines a cross-entropy distillation loss on the association matrix with a negative-log-likelihood regression loss and a mode-selection classification loss on the Laplacian mixture decoder, all optimized jointly.

## Results

On V2X-Traj (the new V2V&I cooperative dataset), V2X-Graph achieves 0.72 minADE, 1.13 minFDE, and 0.11 MR in the full V2V&I setting, outperforming HDGT (0.94/1.56/0.17) by 0.22 in minADE, 0.43 in minFDE, and 6% in MR. On V2X-Seq (V2I cooperative), V2X-Graph with trajectory feature fusion achieves 1.05 minADE, 1.79 minFDE, and 0.25 MR, improving over HiVT+PP-VIC (1.12/1.97/0.30) by 0.07 in minADE, 0.19 in minFDE, and 5% in MR. The method also demonstrates robustness, maintaining performance advantages even under 50% data loss and 200ms communication latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2X-Graph outperforms all three baselines across every cooperative setting. Against DenseTNT [13] (homogeneous graph), V2X-Graph achieves substantially lower errors in all cooperative configurations on V2X-Traj, with the largest gap in the V2V&I setting (0.72 vs. 1.26 minADE, 1.13 vs. 2.24 minFDE, 0.11 vs. 0.28 MR), reflecting DenseTNT's inability to exploit heterogeneous cooperative features. Against HDGT [19] (heterogeneous graph), V2X-Graph wins in all cooperative settings (V2V: 0.77 vs. 0.94 minADE; V2I: 0.80 vs. 0.94; V2V&I: 0.72 vs. 0.94), with the advantage growing as more cooperative views are added, demonstrating the benefit of interpretable trajectory-level feature fusion over node-level encoding. On V2X-Seq, V2X-Graph with feature fusion (1.05 minADE, 1.79 minFDE, 0.25 MR) surpasses HiVT [58] augmented with PP-VIC perception completion (1.12/1.97/0.30), while PP-VIC actually degrades DenseTNT's performance (1.84 vs. 1.71 minADE), confirming that single-frame perception completion introduces error propagation that trajectory-level fusion avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-frame perception-completion paradigm (e.g., PP-VIC) by performing trajectory-level feature fusion in an endnd-to-endnd manner, treating cooperative information from a forecasting-oriented perspective rather than a detection-oriented one. Conceptually, it repurposes heterogeneous graph neural networks and Transformer message-passing (as in DenseTNT, HDGT, HiVT) to fuse motion and interaction features across views, guided by an interpretable graph link-prediction component for cross-view trajectory association. It also introduces V2X-Traj, the first real-world dataset supporting V2V and V2V&I cooperative motion forecasting, broadening the task beyond the V2I-only V2X-Seq benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting from frame-by-frame perception supplementation to holistic trajectory feature fusion, the framework mitigates errorror propagation and achieves better long-range intention prediction with lower inference cost than compared methods. The V2X-Traj dataset opens a previously under-explored research direction involving multiple autonomous vehicles cooperating simultaneously with infrastructure. Open questions include jointly optimizing the perception-to-forecasting pipeline endnd-to-endnd, scaling to evenore cooperative devices, and reducing reliance on perternal tracking quality.
