## Problem

While strong statistical equivalences between replicability and other learning paradigms (online learning, SQ learning, differential privacy) have been established, the computational connections remain poorly understood. This paper asks whether these notions are computationally equivalent or separated, and whether efficient black-box transformations exist between them, particularly for replicable PAC learning under non-uniform marginal distributions.

## Input / Output

The lifting framework takes a black-box replicable uniform-distribution PAC learner and i.i.d. labeled samples from an unknown distribution D over {0,1}^d, producing a replicable PAC learner under D whose sample and time complexity scale as poly(d, 1/α, 1/ρ, log(1/β))^{O(ℓ)} where ℓ is the decision tree complexity of D. For non-monotone distributions, the algorithm additionally requires access to a conditional sampling oracle. The pure-DP-to-replicable transformation takes a pure ε-DP learner and outputs a ρ-replicable learner with running time polynomial in 1/α', 1/ρ, log(1/β') but exponential in the representation dimension of the concept class.

## Method

The paper contributes three main technical results. First, it designs an efficient replicable PAC learner for One-Way Sequences (OWS) built around a novel replicable quantile estimation subroutine (Algorithm B.2) that performs a replicable binary search over the CDF using the DKW concentration inequality and replicable rounding, establishing a computational separation from online learning under one-way function assumptions. Second, it introduces a replicable lifting framework that decomposes an unknown distribution D into a mixture of uniform distributions over subcubes via its depth-ℓ decision tree representation, then applies the black-box uniform replicable learner at each leaf with a divided replicability parameter O(ρ/2^ℓ), leveraging the closed-under-restrictions property of the concept class. Third, it transforms a pure DP learner into a replicable one by enumerating over the finite hypothesis space (exponential in representation dimension) and using correlated sampling to select a replicable output, extending the Gonen et al. reduction from pure DP to online learning.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. First, under one-way functions, the One-Way Sequences (OWS) concept class is replicably PAC learnable with sample complexity O(d²/(α³ρ²) · log(1/β)) and poly(m) running time, yet cannot be learned by any efficient no-regret online algorithm (Theorem 2.1). Second, a black-box lifting framework transforms a replicable uniform-distribution PAC learner into a replicable learner under any monotone distribution with decision tree complexity ℓ, with sample and time complexity poly(d, 1/α, 1/ρ, log(1/β))^O(ℓ) (Theorem 3.2). Third, any pure DP learner can be converted to a replicable learner in time polynomial in the accuracy/confidence/replicability parameters but exponential in the representation dimension of the hypothesis class (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are computational separations and transformations between five learning paradigms: replicability, approximate DP, pure DP, online learning, and SQ learning. The key separations are: (1) replicable PAC learning is computationally easier than online learning (OWS is replicably learnable but not online-learnable under OWF); (2) approximate DP is computationally easier than replicability (a class exists that is approximately DP-learnable but not replicably learnable under OWF); (3) parities are SQ-hard but replicably PAC learnable under uniform and (via the lifting framework) under structured non-uniform distributions. The key transformations are: pure DP → online (Gonen et al.), replicability → approximate DP (Ghazi et al., Bun et al.), SQ → replicability (Impagliazzo et al.), and pure DP → replicability (this work, with exponential overhead in representation dimension). The paper's lifting framework (Theorem 3.2) is positioned as a replicable analogue of Blanc et al.'s non-replicable distributional decomposition, adding only a small overhead that scales with the replicability parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the replicability framework introduced by Impagliazzo et al. [2022] by studying its computational (rather than statistical) connections to online learning, SQ learning, and differential privacy. The replicable lifting framework is directly inspired by Blanc et al. [2023]'s non-replicable distributional decomposition, adapted here with additional overhead scaling with the replicability parameter. The computational separation between replicable PAC learning and online learning builds on Blum's [1994] OWS construction and Bun's [2020] cryptographic hardness for online learning, while the pure-DP-to-replicable transformation is motivated by Gonen et al. [2019]'s analogous DP-to-online reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive computational landscape of 'stable' learning, showing that statistical equivalences (e.g., replicability ↔ online learning via Littlestone dimension) do not automatically translate to computational equivalences. The replicable lifting framework offers a systematic, black-box method to obtain efficient replicable learners under non-uniform distributions, directly answering a question posed by Impagliazzo et al. [2022] about parities. Open questions include whether efficient transformations from online learners to replicable learners exist, whether the exponential dependence on representation dimension in the DP-to-replicable reduction is unavoidable, and whether efficient replicable algorithms exist for parities under every distribution.
