## Problem

Existing multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue) evaluate reasoning over entities and entity-entity relations, but leave event structures and event-event relations (temporal, causal) largely unexplored. This paper identifies that event-centric questions are inherently more complex because they require navigating heterogeneous reasoning chains through both entities and events, yet no benchmark exists to test this capability with step-by-step explanations.

## Input / Output

The input consists of a news document (average ~676 words from WikiEvents), a multi-hop question (average ~12 words), and optionally structured event information ranging from simple entity lists to a full event knowledge graph with trigger-argument pairs and inter-event relations. The output is a final answer plus a multi-step explanation structured as a sequence of single-hop QA pairs (CoT-QA format) or freeform sentences (CoT-Freeform format), where each step in the QA format corresponds to one hop in the event reasoning chain (average 2.6 hops per question).

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automatic question generation pipeline that bootstraps multi-hop questions from document-level event structures in WikiEvents (KAIROS schema). It identifies composable events (sharing entities or having temporal/causal relations), links them into reasoning chains of up to 4 hops using five strategies (Event Relation, Entity Bridging, Event Listing/Counting, Event Comparison, Unanswerable), and explicitly mitigates reasoning shortcuts by ensuring non-start events cannot be uniquely located by their triggers and arguments alone. Synthetic questions and QA-pair explanations are generated from the chains, then curated by human annotators. For evaluation, the paper introduces two explanation metrics—completeness (step-wise ordered matching between predicted and golden QA-pair explanations) and logical consistency (reference-free LLM-based contradiction detection between consecutive reasoning steps)—and benchmarks prompting-based (few-shot, CoT-QA, CoT-Freeform) and fine-tuning (T5) approaches with varying levels of structured event information, showing that full event KGs yield the largest performance gains.

## Results

MEQA is substantially harder than existing multi-hop QA benchmarks: ChatGPT (GPT-3.5-turbo-1106) achieves only F1 0.238 on MEQA versus 0.733 on HotpotQA and 0.532 on 2WikiMultihopQA. The best-performing method, CoT-QA with Full Event KG, reaches F1 0.4940 (Precision 0.5299, Recall 0.5298), still far below human performance of F1 0.811. Adding structured event information (Full Event KG) consistently improves all baselines, with the largest gains seen in CoT-QA (F1 from 0.2940 to 0.4940) and Fewshot (F1 from 0.2377 to 0.4581). On explanation quality, CoT-QA w/ Full Event KG achieves the best completeness F1 (0.4208) and logical consistency (0.7327), while CoT-Freeform has the highest logical consistency (0.9132) but lower completeness due to shorter, less detailed reasoning chains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four method families on MEQA: fine-tuned T5, few-shot prompting (C+Q→A), CoT-QA (QA-pair explanations), and CoT-Freeform (freeform sentence explanations), each with and without structured event information. CoT-QA w/ Full Event KG is the overall best method (F1 0.4940), outperforming CoT-Freeform w/ Full Event KG (F1 0.3823) and Fewshot w/ Full Event KG (F1 0.4581). Among base methods without extra information, CoT-Freeform achieves the highest recall (0.5392) but the lowest precision (0.1044), while CoT-QA has the best precision (0.2832) but worst recall (0.3903). The cross-dataset comparison (Table 2) confirms MEQA is the hardest: ChatGPT drops from F1 0.733 on HotpotQA to 0.238 on MEQA, a 3x degradation. All models remain far below human performance (F1 0.811), with the best model achieving only ~61% of human F1. On explanation quality, CoT-QA produces more faithful (higher completeness) but more verbose explanations, while CoT-Freeform is shorter and scores higher on logical consistency (0.9132 vs 0.7327) partly because its brevity avoids contradictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue) and event-centric QA (EventQA, TORQUE, ESTER). Prior multi-hop benchmarks are exclusively entity-centric, while prior event-centric QA datasets are either single-hop or knowledge-graph-based rather than document-based. The authors repurpose the WikiEvents information extraction dataset and its KAIROS event schema to bootstrap a bottom-up question generation pipeline, drawing on the compositional construction philosophy of MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA but applying it to event structures rather than entity-relation triples. For evaluation, they extend the chain-of-thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) and the structured-information-for-QA line (Li and Du, 2023) by introducing completeness and logical consistency metrics specifically designed for step-wise explanation assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEQA exposes a substantial and persistent gap between state-of-the-art LLMs (best F1 ≈ 0.53 with full event KG) and human performance (F1 ≈ 0.81), suggesting that event-centric multi-hop reasoning remains a significant open challenge. The finding that structured event knowledge graphs substantially improve both answer accuracy and explanation faithfulness points toward hybrid neuro-symbolic approaches as a promising direction. Key limitations include the English-only scope, relatively small scale (2,243 questions), and reliance on a single source dataset (WikiEvents), leaving open whether the observed difficulty generalizes across domains and languages. The completeness and logical consistency metrics, validated against human judgments (correlations 0.693 and 0.601), offer a reusable evaluation framework for explanation quality in any multi-step reasoning task.
