## Problem

Existing quality-aware decoding methods for machine translation (reranking, minimum Bayes risk) select a single best hypothesis from a fixed candidate pool, risking overfitting to the evaluation metric and being limited by the pool's coverage. The paper asks whether one can instead sample directly in proportion to quality values from a Gibbs distribution, producing a set of diverse high-quality translations without the mode-seeking bias of likelihood-based decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source sentence with a translation instruction prompt, a decoder-only LLM (ALMA-7B or TOWER-7B, both LLaMA-2-7B based), and a reference-free QE metric (COMETKIWI-XL) as the energy function. It produces a sequence of 128 MCMC samples (after a burning period) that are diverse and concentrated in high-quality regions of the translation space. The entire procedure is inference-time only—no model weights are modified—and the QE metric is queried once per proposal to compute the acceptance ratio.

## Method

QUEST frames translation quality as a Gibbs distribution π_β(y|x) ∝ exp(r(y,x)/β) and samples from it via Metropolis-Hastings MCMC. The key technical contribution is a sentence-level proposal distribution: at each step, an index i is sampled uniformly, the prefix y_{<i} is kept, and a new suffix is generated autoregressively from the LM conditioned on that prefix. This proposal is irreducible and aperiodic, and unlike prior token-level MCMC proposals for text, it generates coherent continuations that the QE metric can meaningfully score. The acceptance criterion only requires the ratio of unnormalized Gibbs densities (the partition function cancels), making it tractable. The method is agnostic to the specific QE metric and can also incorporate the LM likelihood into the target distribution, yielding a connection to RLHF-style alignment without requiring access to model weights or RL training.

## Results

On WMT23 EN↔{DE, RU} with TOWER-7B and ALMA-7B, QUEST outperforms ancestral sampling in 6 out of 8 language-direction/model settings on the quality–diversity trade-off (measured by XCOMET-XL and pairwise BLEU). The largest gains appear for EN↔RU, where QUEST improves XCOMET-XL by up to 2 points over the best ancestral-sampling temperature for both models. Ancestral sampling retains a slight quality edge only for DE→EN, a direction the authors attribute to short passages and QE-metric limitations. Average reward (COMETKIWI-XL) continues to improve with more MCMC steps, whereas ancestral sampling's mean reward stays flat regardless of pool size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ancestral (autoregressive) sampling from the LM at temperatures τ ∈ {0.2, …, 1.0}. QUEST wins on the XCOMET-XL quality–diversity Pareto front in 6 of 8 settings (EN→DE, EN→RU, RU→EN for both TOWER-7B and ALMA-7B), with the most pronounced advantage on EN↔RU (up to +2 XCOMET-XL points). Ancestral sampling edges out QUEST only on DE→EN, where short source sentences and the QE metric's weaker calibration for that direction limit the MCMC chain's ability to reach high-density regions. On the QE metric used inside the sampler (COMETKIWI-XL), QUEST achieves higher scores across all eight settings, confirming that the gains are not an artifact of the evaluation metric. The paper also shows that QUEST samples hypotheses absent from a 4× larger ancestral pool (512 samples), demonstrating access to under-explored high-quality regions that reranking-based methods cannot reach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Metropolis-Hastings framework (Metropolis et al., 1953) to machine translation by introducing a sentence-level proposal distribution that is compatible with sentence-level quality estimation metrics, in contrast to prior MCMC-for-text work (Miao et al., 2018; Berglund et al., 2015; Goyal et al., 2022) that relied on token-level modifications. It departs from quality-aware decoding (Freitag et al., 2022a; Fernandes et al., 2022b), which selects best-1 or best-k from a fixed candidate pool, by instead sampling directly from a Gibbs distribution parameterized by a QE metric, thereby avoiding the 'gaming the metric' risk of optimization-based reranking. The paper also draws a formal connection to RLHF, showing that the KL-constrained reward maximization objective (Korbak et al., 2022b) yields a target distribution amenable to the same MH sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QUEST provides a principled, metric-agnostic decoding strategy that generates diverse translations from high-density regions of the quality distribution without requiring model retraining or access to model weights, making it applicable to any LLM with sampling and log-probability access. Key limitations include the ~6× compute overhead over ancestral sampling (sequential generation plus QE scoring at each step), degraded performance on longer sentences, and the proposal's tendency to get stuck modifying only sentence suffixes once a high-quality region is reached. Open questions include adapting the proposal for document-level MT, reducing compute via metric distillation, and whether parallel chains or adaptive index distributions can improve mixing and diversity of prefixes.
