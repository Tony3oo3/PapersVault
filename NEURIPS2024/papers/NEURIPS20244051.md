## Problem

Existing ML-based climate emulators like ACE are deterministic, limiting them to single trajectories and preventing proper uncertainty quantification or ensemble climate simulations. Standard diffusion models, while generative, are computationally prohibitive for the 14,600+ autoregressive steps needed for a 10-year rollout at 6-hourly timesteps. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that short-term weather forecasting skill does not reliably predict long-term climate accuracy, meaning existing training objectives may be misaligned with the climate emulation goal.

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial atmospheric state consisting of 34 prognostic variables (temperature, specific total water, zonal/meridional wind at 8 vertical levels, plus surface skin temperature and pressure) on a 1° Gaussian grid (180×360), along with annually repeating climatological sea-surface temperature and incoming solar radiation as time-varying forcings. It is trained on a 6-step (36-hour) horizon and applied autoregressively to produce 10-year (14,600 timestep) or 100-year simulations. Repeated sampling from the stochastic interpolator with the same initial conditions yields a 25-member ensemble of full atmospheric state trajectories, from which ensemble-mean and member-wise climate statistics are derived.

## Method

Spherical DYffusion integrates the dynamics-informed diffusion framework (DYffusion) with the Spherical Fourier Neural Operator (SFNO) architecture by replacing DYffusion's original UNet-based networks with two modified SFNO backbones: a stochastic interpolator (SFNO_φ) and a deterministic forecaster (SFNO_θ). The interpolator's inference-time stochasticity is achieved through MC dropout (10%) in the per-block MLP and stochastic depth (10%) that randomly skips entire SFNO blocks, a combination not previously explored for neural operators. Time-conditioning modules are added to SFNO by mapping sine/cosine Fourier features of the time index through a 2-layer MLP into learnable scale-and-shift parameters applied after each block's normalization layer. At inference, the model alternates between a multi-step forecast (SFNO_θ) and a temporal interpolation (SFNO_φ) with a cold-sampling correction, capping the number of neural function evaluations at 3(h−1) per h-step block—less than 3× the cost of a single deterministic SFNO step. Training proceeds in two stages: first the interpolator is trained to predict intermediate timesteps given endpoints, then the forecaster is trained to refine the endpoint prediction conditioned on the stochastic interpolator's output.

## Results

Across all 34 predicted fields, Spherical DYffusion achieves 10-year time-mean RMSEs only 49.36% above the reference noise floor, less than half the average RMSE of the next best method ACE (110.47%). For the critical total water path (TWP) field, the method reaches within 20% of the reference model, a 5× improvement over ACE (94% above reference). Ensemble averaging over 25 members further reduces average climate biases to 29.28% above the noise floor. The model completes a 100-year simulation in ~26 hours on a single A100 GPU, a 25× speedup over the physics-based FV3GFS reference (78 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spherical DYffusion decisively outperforms the deterministic ACE baseline [Watt-Meyer et al., 2023] across nearly all variables and metrics: on average, its 10-year time-mean RMSE is 49.36% above the reference noise floor versus ACE's 110.47%, and for TWP the gap widens to 20% vs. 94%. The original DYffusion baseline [Ruhling Cachay et al., 2023], which uses a UNet architecture designed for Euclidean data, fails to match even deterministic ACE on most fields (e.g., TWP RMSE +187% vs. ACE's +94%), confirming that the spherical SFNO backbone is critical for long-term climate stability. Making ACE stochastic via MC dropout (ACE-STO) degrades its performance substantially (TWP RMSE jumps to +308%), showing that naive stochasticity without the DYffusion framework is counterproductive. Against the physics-based FV3GFS reference [Zhou et al., 2019], Spherical DYffusion approaches the theoretical noise floor within 50% on average and within 20% for TWP, while also reproducing decadal climate variability consistent with the 10-member reference ensemble (within 10–20% on spread metrics). The method's ensemble-mean predictions consistently outperform single-member predictions, a property not shared by ACE-STO or DYffusion, making it uniquely suited for probabilistic climate ensemble simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates two previously separate frameworks: the dynamics-informed diffusion model DYffusion (Ruhling Cachay et al., 2023), which caps inference cost at ~3× a deterministic forecaster but relies on UNet architectures designed for Euclidean data, and the Spherical Fourier Neural Operator (SFNO) backbone from ACE (Watt-Meyer et al., 2023), which achieves stable 10-year climate rollouts on spherical grids but is purely deterministic. The key architectural contribution is replacing DYffusion's UNets with time-conditioned SFNOs and introducing MC dropout plus stochastic depth as inference stochasticity, yielding the first generative model that combines spherical-aware long-term stability with probabilistic sampling. The paper also departs from standard diffusion by using cold diffusion (dynamics-informed forward/reverse processes coupled to physical time steps) rather than Gaussian noise schedules, and from prior SFNO work by adding time-conditioning modules that had not previously been explored in this architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first conditional generative model for probabilistic climate emulation with minimal computational overhead, enabling ensemble climate simulations (25× faster than FV3GFS) that deterministic emulators like ACE cannot provide. The finding that 5-day weather RMSE is uncorrelated with (and even inversely correlated with) 10-year time-mean RMSE challenges the ML community's assumption that optimizing short-term forecast skill automatically yields accurate long-term climate statistics—a disconnect well-known to climate scientists but previously undocumented for ML models. Key limitations include emulating only the atmosphere (not a full Earth System Model), using fixed climatological SST/insolation forcings rather than time-varying greenhouse gas scenarios, and remaining ~2.7× slower than deterministic ACE. Extending the method to out-of-distribution climate change forcings and coupling to ocean/land/ice components are critical open questions for real-world impact.
