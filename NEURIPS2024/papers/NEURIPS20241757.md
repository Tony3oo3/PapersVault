## Problem

No known method simultaneously accelerates token generation via speculative sampling and injects unbiased watermarks into the output. This paper asks whether one can achieve the same watermark strength as without acceleration while maintaining the same sampling efficiency as without watermarking, and answers negatively with a no-go theorem: when the vocabulary size exceeds two, no non-trivial reweighting functions and speculative process can preserve both objectives simultaneously under a general two-reweight framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (context tokens x₁,…,xₙ), a target model P(·|·), a draft model Q(·|·), a watermark key z, and a draft sequence length K. It produces a list of watermarked generated tokens along with an updated context code history (cch) that tracks which context codes have already been used, ensuring sequence-level unbiasedness. The context code history is a non-standard output that must be carried forward across generation steps and is also required at detection time so each context code contributes to the watermark score exactly once.

## Method

The two reweight framework applies the same watermark code E to both the target distribution (via reweighting function R_E) and the draft distribution (via a potentially different reweighting function R'_E), then performs a custom speculative accept/reject process A(j|i) over the reweighted distributions. The key insight is that naively running speculative sampling on the watermarked target R_E(P) against the unwatermarked draft Q severely reduces the overlap probability α, so reweighting the draft as well is essential for efficiency. The no-go theorem (Theorem 1) proves via a chain of lemmas that maintaining both watermark strength (R̂_E(P)=R_E(P)) and sampling efficiency (α(P,Q)=E[Σᵢ A(i|i)R'_E(Q)(i)]) forces R_E to be the identity when |Σ|>2, making non-trivial watermarking impossible. Two practical algorithms are then proposed: MWS applies the same reweight R_E to both distributions and runs standard speculative sampling on R_E(Q) vs R_E(P), preserving watermark strength at a small efficiency cost; MSE reweights the draft with R_E(Q) but runs the accept/reject step using the original P and Q, preserving sampling efficiency at the cost of reduced watermark strength.

## Results

On the CNN/DailyMail text summarization task with Llama-7b (target) and Llama-68m (draft), the MWS method achieves ANLPPT of 0.374 (U score) and 1.71 (maximin-LLR) with DeltaGumbel reweight, matching VUW's 0.376 and 1.71, while attaining AATPS of 1.51 vs. VSpS's 1.55 (K=1). The MSE method matches VSpS's AATPS of 1.55 but drops watermark strength to ANLPPT 0.153 (U) / 0.640 (LLR). Both MWS and MSE reduce Per Token Time from VUW's 30.40 ms to 23.24 ms and 22.58 ms respectively, confirming acceleration over vanilla watermarking. All methods produce statistically indistinguishable LOGPPL (~1.75–1.77), verifying unchanged output quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VUW (Hu et al. 2023, vanilla unbiased watermarking), MWS matches watermark strength almost exactly (ANLPPT 0.374 vs. 0.376 U-score; 1.71 vs. 1.71 LLR) while reducing PTT by ~24% (23.24 vs. 30.40 ms), confirming that acceleration is achievable without sacrificing detectability. Against VSpS (Leviathan et al. 2023 / Chen et al. 2023, vanilla speculative sampling), MSE matches sampling efficiency (AATPS 1.549 vs. 1.551) but watermark strength drops substantially (ANLPPT 0.153 vs. 0.376 U-score), illustrating the no-go theorem's prediction that you cannot have both. MWS sits in between: it retains full watermark strength but sacrifices a small amount of AATPS (1.511 vs. 1.551, ~2.6% gap), making it the most practical option. Across all four experimental settings (Llama-7b/13b × summarization/open-ended), the pattern holds consistently: MWS ≈ VUW on watermark, MSE ≈ VSpS on speed, and neither simultaneously achieves both optima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: unbiased watermarking for LLMs (Hu et al. 2023, with DeltaGumbel and Gamma reweighting schemes) and speculative sampling for accelerated inference (Leviathan et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023). The authors introduce the 'two reweight framework,' which applies a shared watermark code to both the target and draft distributions before performing speculative sampling, going beyond the naive approach of simply running speculative sampling on an already-watermarked target. The key departure from prior work is the no-go theorem, which proves that no non-trivial reweighting functions can simultaneously preserve watermark strength and sampling efficiency when the vocabulary size exceeds two—a fundamental constraint that had not been previously identified or formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The no-go theorem provides a rigorous theoretical boundary that constrains all future work combining watermarking with speculative sampling, forcing practitioners to make explicit trade-off decisions rather than assuming both objectives are independently optimizable. The practical MWS algorithm, which maintains full watermark strength with only marginal efficiency loss (e.g., AATPS 1.51 vs. 1.55 for VSpS at K=1), is immediately deployable and suggests the theoretical trade-off is small in practice. Open questions include whether frameworks beyond the two-reweight paradigm could circumvent the no-go theorem, how the results extend to newer speculative sampling variants (tree verification, multi-candidacy), and the side-channel concern that MWS's slightly lower throughput could reveal watermarking to users.
