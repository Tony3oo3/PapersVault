## Problem

Direct generation of high-fidelity 3D meshes is challenging due to their unstructured graph representation and the need to simultaneously estimate accurate spatial coordinates and vertex connectivity. Existing approaches either rely on indirect representations (point clouds, SDFs, multi-view images) requiring post-processing re-meshing, or use VQVAE-based tokenization that demands sophisticated multi-stage training and hinders scaling to large datasets. The paper asks whether a simple, end-to-end coordinate representation can support large-scale autoregressive pre-training for direct mesh generation.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model receives only a BOS token and autoregressively produces a coordinate sequence of length 9n for an n-faced triangular mesh (3 vertices × 3 coordinates per face), where coordinates are discretized into unsigned integers on a normalized unit-cube grid. For conditional generation, a text prompt (encoded by BERT) or RGB image (encoded by ViT) is compressed via a Q-Former into 32 learnable prefix tokens. For shape completion, 50% of the mesh faces are provided as input and the model predicts the remaining 50%. An 800-faced mesh yields 7,200 tokens, making inference time a practical constraint.

## Method

The core contribution is the Neural Coordinate Field (NeurCF), which represents each 3D coordinate (x, y, z) as a learnable embedding via a shared embedding layer, so that a k-sided face is encoded as the concatenation of its k vertex coordinate embeddings. A pre-defined ordering strategy (shared with PolyGen and MeshGPT) normalizes the mesh to a unit cube, discretizes coordinates, cyclically permutes vertices within each face in z-y-x order to preserve normal direction, and sorts faces by permuted coordinates, yielding a unique one-dimensional token sequence per mesh. The architecture is a decoder-only transformer built on the OPT codebase, fine-tuned end-to-end with newly-initialized coordinate and position embeddings under standard next-token prediction loss. For conditional generation, a Q-Former compresses image or text features into 32 fixed-length prefix tokens prepended to the mesh sequence. The paper trains three model sizes (125M, 350M, 1.3B) from scratch on 150 billion tokens drawn from 2.5 million meshes, demonstrating consistent scaling gains in validation perplexity and generation quality without any VQVAE or multi-stage training pipeline.

## Results

On ShapeNet, MeshXL(1.3B) achieves a FID of 9.12 and JSD of 9.48 on the Chair category, substantially outperforming MeshGPT (FID 39.52, JSD 55.16) and PolyGen (FID 63.49, JSD 228.80). Coverage (COV) reaches 51.60% on Chair and 56.55% on Bench, well above MeshGPT's 42.00% and 34.92% respectively. On Objaverse, scaling from 125M to 1.3B parameters improves COV from 39.76 to 42.86, reduces JSD from 26.03 to 20.99, and lowers FID from 17.32 to 12.49, confirming consistent scaling gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshXL outperforms all three baselines (PolyGen, GET3D, MeshGPT) across every ShapeNet category on the majority of metrics. Against MeshGPT (the closest competitor, also a decoder-only transformer), MeshXL(1.3B) reduces JSD by 5–6× (e.g., 9.48 vs. 55.16 on Chair, 14.93 vs. 92.88 on Table) and FID by 2–4× (9.12 vs. 39.52 on Chair, 22.29 vs. 53.75 on Table), while also achieving higher COV (51.60 vs. 42.00 on Chair) and lower KID (1.84 vs. 8.97). The 1-NNA metric, where 50% is optimal, is consistently closer to 50% for MeshXL (55.80–56.80) than for MeshGPT (61.59–75.05), indicating better balance of quality and diversity. Against PolyGen and GET3D, MeshXL's advantage is even more pronounced, with JSD improvements of 10–20× and FID improvements of 2–7×. On the Lamp category, MeshXL(350M) achieves the best MMD (4.18) and JSD (34.87), while MeshXL(1.3B) achieves the best 1-NNA (47.27) and KID (0.99), showing that the optimal model size can vary by category.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshXL builds directly on the autoregressive mesh generation line initiated by PolyGen (decoupled vertex and face transformers) and MeshGPT (VQVAE tokenization + GPT), adopting their shared coordinate ordering strategy. Its key departure is the Neural Coordinate Field (NeurCF) representation, which replaces both PolyGen's two-stage vertex-then-connectivity prediction and MeshGPT's VQVAE-based discretization with a single end-to-end trainable coordinate embedding sequence. For conditional generation, it repurposes the Q-Former from BLIP-2 to compress image or text features into a fixed-length prefix, and uses the OPT decoder-only transformer as its base architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple explicit coordinate representation with learnable embeddings can match or exceed VQVAE-based approaches without multi-stage training, lowering the barrier to large-scale mesh pre-training. The consistent scaling behavior across 125M–1.3B parameters and the 2.5M-mesh collection provide a practical foundation for downstream conditional generation (text-to-3D, image-to-3D, shape completion). The main open limitation is autoregressive inference cost (~7,200 tokens for an 800-face mesh), which the authors flag as a target for multi-token prediction or linear-time sequence models (Mamba, RWKV, xLSTM). Whether the NeurCF representation generalizes to higher-resolution meshes or hybrid polygon types beyond triangles remains to be tested at scale.
