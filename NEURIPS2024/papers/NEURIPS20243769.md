## Problem

Implicit sharpness regularization in SGD and its variants converges to flat minima at a very slow pace, particularly in the late phase of training when the sharp-direction components become small. Naively increasing the global learning rate to accelerate this process destabilizes training along sharp directions, creating a fundamental tension between speed and stability. The paper asks: can we selectively accelerate the flat-direction dynamics to boost sharpness reduction without compromising training stability?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard gradient (or update direction) from any base optimizer (SGD, AdamW, SAM) plus a lightweight diagonal Hessian estimate computed via the efficient Fisher estimator h_t = B · ∇L̂_B(θ) ⊙ ∇L̂_B(θ), which requires only one mini-batch gradient per K steps (typically K=10). From this estimate, a binary mask n_t is constructed by thresholding |h_t| at the ⌊p·γ⌋-th smallest value, retaining the top-γ fraction of flat coordinates. The output is the updated parameter vector θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η(g_t + κ n_t ⊙ g_t), with the same dimensionality as the input parameters.

## Method

IRE decouples the optimization dynamics along flat and sharp directions by applying an amplified learning rate (1+κ) exclusively to the flat-direction component of the gradient, identified via a diagonal Hessian mask. The key insight is that since the sharp-direction dynamics remain unchanged, κ can be set very large (theoretically up to 1/ρ for SAM) without destabilizing training, whereas a global LR increase would diverge. The practical implementation (Algorithm 1) estimates the diagonal Hessian every K steps using the efficient Fisher estimator, constructs a binary mask over the bottom-γ fraction of coordinates by |h_i|, and applies the masked amplification to the base optimizer's update. Theoretically, IRE provides a Θ(1/ρ)-time acceleration over standard SAM in minimizing the trace of the Hessian on the minima manifold, and an Ω(1/η^{0.5}) acceleration over average SAM, by increasing the effective learning rate of the implicit sharpness-minimization dynamics from ηρ² to (1+κ)ηρ².

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with WRN-28-10 and ResNet-56, IRE consistently improves test accuracy over both SGD (+0.29 to +0.97%) and SAM (+0.12 to +0.45%). On ImageNet, SAM-IRE with ResNet-50 achieves 77.92% top-1 accuracy (+0.45% over SAM), and AdmIRE with ViT-S reaches 79.1% top-1 (+0.4% over AdamW). For LLM pre-training, AdmIRE achieves a 2.1× speedup over well-tuned AdamW across Llama models (60M–229M) on Wikitext-103, Minipile, and Openwebtext, while also finding significantly flatter solutions (Tr(∇²L) = 88.86 vs. 120.41 at matched loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRE is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of three base optimizers: SGD, SAM, and AdamW. Against SGD, IRE yields consistent gains of +0.29 to +0.97% test accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 and +0.23% top-1 on ImageNet (ResNet-50). Against SAM, the improvements are smaller but still consistent: +0.12 to +0.45% on CIFAR-10/100 and +0.45% top-1 on ImageNet, suggesting SAM already captures part of the sharpness reduction that IRE targets. Against AdamW, the gains are most pronounced for ViTs on CIFAR-100 (+2.96 to +3.54%) and moderate on ImageNet (+0.3 to +0.4% top-1). In LLM pre-training, AdmIRE outperforms AdamW by a 2.1× wall-clock speedup across all three Llama model/dataset configurations, while also producing flatter solutions at matched loss (Tr(∇²L) reduced by ~26%). The per-step computational overhead of IRE is only 1.12× that of the base optimizer (K=10), making it a lightweight addition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the implicit sharpness regularization literature (Blanc et al. 2020; Li et al. 2022; Ma et al. 2022), which established that SGD's convergence to flat minima occurs at a very slow pace along flat directions, and on Wen et al. (2023a), which characterized the effective dynamics of SAM as gradient flow on the trace of the Hessian over the minima manifold. IRE departs from SAM by replacing its 2× per-step cost (a second forward-backward pass) with a lightweight diagonal Fisher estimate (1.1× cost), and from naive learning-rate scaling by selectively amplifying only the flat-direction component of the gradient. The practical implementation borrows the efficient diagonal Fisher estimator from Sophia (Liu et al. 2024), but repurposes it for direction-selective learning-rate enhancement rather than second-order correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× pre-training speedup over AdamW for Llama models (60M–229M) is practically significant if it scales to larger models, and the method's simplicity (a binary mask on the gradient, updated every K steps) makes it easy to adopt. However, the LLM experiments are limited to small models and modest datasets, leaving open whether the speedup persists at 7B+ scale or on larger corpora. The paper also leaves unexplained why IRE accelerates loss convergence beyond its stated sharpness-reduction motivation, and whether IRE-trained models yield better downstream task performance—both flagged as future work. The vision improvements, while consistent, are modest (0.2–3.5%), suggesting the primary practical value may lie in the LLM pre-training setting.
