## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-query approaches (CoT, few-shot) require task-specific exemplars and lack generalization, while multi-query approaches (ToT, GoT) are computationally expensive due to recursive path expansion. Neither category systematically accumulates and reuses high-level reasoning guidelines from previously solved tasks, missing an opportunity to transfer structural insights across problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243606/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text task statement as input and first passes it through a problem distiller that extracts key variables, constraints, and a formalized meta-problem description. This distilled representation is then matched against a meta-buffer of thought-templates via embedding similarity (threshold δ ≈ 0.5–0.7); if no template exceeds the threshold, the task is treated as novel and assigned a pre-defined coarse-grained template. The output is a fully instantiated reasoning solution (step-by-step rationale plus final answer), produced in a single LLM call after retrieval, making the overall pipeline inference-time only with no model training.

## Method

BoT is a three-component thought-augmented reasoning framework. (1) A problem distiller formalizes the input into structured task information (key variables, constraints, extended meta-problem). (2) A meta-buffer stores high-level thought-templates organized into six categories (text comprehension, creative generation, commonsense, mathematical, code, scheduling); retrieval selects the most relevant template by embedding similarity between the distilled problem and template descriptions, and the LLM then instantiates that template into a concrete reasoning structure for the specific problem. (3) A buffer manager distills a new thought-template from each solved problem via a three-step prompt (core task summarization, solution steps description, general answer template) and dynamically updates the meta-buffer only when the new template's embedding similarity to all existing templates falls below δ, preventing redundancy while growing capacity. This design lets the system reuse accumulated reasoning structures across tasks, achieving multi-query-level accuracy at roughly 12% of the inference cost of methods like ToT or meta-prompting.

## Results

Using GPT-4 as the base model, BoT achieves the best accuracy across all 10 evaluated tasks, with headline results of 82.4% on Game of 24, 93.6% on Geometric Shapes, 86.4% on Checkmate-in-One, and 99.8% on Multi-Step Arithmetic. Compared to the strongest prior multi-query method (Meta-Prompting), BoT yields relative improvements of ~11% on Game of 24, ~20% on Geometric Shapes, and ~51% on Checkmate-in-One. On efficiency, BoT requires only 12% of the average inference cost of multi-query methods (ToT, GoT, Meta-Prompting) while matching or exceeding single-query methods in wall-clock time. Additionally, BoT+Llama3-8B shows potential to surpass standalone Llama3-70B on complex reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoT outperforms all seven baselines on every task. Against the strongest multi-query baseline, Meta-Prompting [15], BoT leads by 15.4 pts on Game of 24 (82.4 vs. 67.0), 15.4 pts on Geometric Shapes (93.6 vs. 78.2), and 29.2 pts on Checkmate-in-One (86.4 vs. 57.2). Against ToT [14], the gains are 8.4 pts on Game of 24 (82.4 vs. 74.0) and 37.2 pts on Checkmate-in-One (86.4 vs. 49.2). Single-query methods (CoT, Expert Prompting, PAL) are consistently weaker, with PAL performing particularly poorly on Checkmate-in-One (10.8) and Sonnet Writing (36.2). On simpler tasks like Word Sorting and Multi-Step Arithmetic, all methods converge near ceiling, but BoT still achieves perfect or near-perfect scores (100.0 and 99.8). The paper also reports that BoT's inference cost is only 12% of multi-query methods on average, and its success-rate (robustness) exceeds the second-best method by 10% on average across 10 repeated evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoT positions itself at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation and multi-query reasoning, but departs from both in a meaningful way. Unlike retrieval-augmented LLMs that retrieve raw text from large corpora, BoT retrieves abstract, high-level 'thought-templates' distilled from prior problem-solving processes and adaptively instantiates them for new tasks. It also contrasts with multi-query methods (ToT, GoT, Meta-Prompting) that build reasoning structures from scratch via expensive search, and with single-query methods (CoT, Expert Prompting) that require task-specific exemplars. The dynamic buffer-manager that grows the template library over time draws conceptual parallels to analogical reasoning and thought-retrieval work, but operates at a higher level of abstraction and targets general reasoning rather than textual comprehension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain (12% of multi-query cost) and the finding that Llama3-8B + BoT can approach or surpass Llama3-70B on certain tasks make this relevant to practitioners seeking cost-effective reasoning without scaling model size. However, the main results rely on GPT-4 as the base model, no error bars are reported, and the meta-buffer's long-term scalability and template quality under distribution shift remain open questions. The paper itself identifies future directions including making template distillation optimizable and integrating BoT into training (SuperCorrect), suggesting the current approach is a prompting-level solution whose ceiling is not yet characterized.
