## Problem

Existing indoor radar perception pipelines, such as RFMask, rely on fixed-height vertical proposals and compute bounding box loss only on the horizontal radar view, failing to exploit the shared depth dimension between horizontal and vertical radar views or to leverage vertical-view features for 3D object estimation. This limits detection accuracy, especially for non-standing postures where fixed-height assumptions break down.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T=4 consecutive frames of a horizontal radar heatmap (azimuth × depth, e.g., 256×128 for MMVR) and a vertical radar heatmap (elevation × depth, e.g., 256×128 for MMVR), where both views share the same depth axis. It outputs 3D bounding boxes in the radar coordinate system (center + width/height/depth), which are transformed via a radar-to-camera rotation and translation and projected through a pinhole camera model to yield 2D image-plane bounding boxes, along with per-query binary segmentation masks within each predicted box.

## Method

RETR extends DETR with four radar-specific modifications: (1) Top-K feature selection reduces the multi-view token sequence to 2K features per frame, keeping transformer complexity manageable; (2) a Tunable Positional Encoding (TPE) splits the positional embedding into depth and angular sub-vectors with an adjustable dimension ratio α, promoting higher attention similarity for features at similar depths across the two views; (3) a tri-plane set-prediction loss sums GIoU+L1 bounding box losses over the horizontal radar plane, vertical radar plane, and 2D image plane, providing supervision from all three projections of the predicted 3D box; and (4) a learnable radar-to-camera transformation reparameterizes the rotation matrix via the SO(3) exponential map (exp([ω])) to preserve orthonormality while enabling end-to-end learning of the coordinate alignment. The architecture uses a shared ResNet-18 backbone, a 6-layer transformer encoder with self-attention over pooled multi-view features, and a 6-layer decoder with cross-attention between N=10 object queries and encoder memory, followed by a 3D BBox head and a cross-attention + FPN + light U-Net segmentation head.

## Results

On the MMVR dataset (P2S1 split), RETR achieves 46.75 AP, 83.80 AP50, and 46.06 AP75 for object detection, outperforming RFMask (31.37 AP) by 15.38 AP and DETR with Top-K (39.71 AP) by 7.04 AP. For instance segmentation on the same split, RETR reaches 77.21 IoU, surpassing RFMask (65.30 IoU) by 11.91 IoU. On the HIBER dataset (WALK split), RETR achieves 22.09 AP, improving over RFMask (17.77 AP) by 4.32 AP, with smaller gains attributed to the predominantly walking activity in that split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETR consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated datasets and metrics. Compared to RFMask (the prior state-of-the-art for indoor radar perception), RETR gains 15.38 AP on MMVR P2S1 (46.75 vs. 31.37), 4.32 AP on HIBER WALK (22.09 vs. 17.77), and 11.91 IoU on segmentation (77.21 vs. 65.30). Against the DETR baseline using only the horizontal radar view, RETR improves by 17.37 AP on MMVR and 7.64 AP on HIBER, demonstrating the value of exploiting both radar views. Even against the stronger DETR(Top-K) variant that already uses both views, RETR still leads by 7.04 AP on MMVR and 7.74 AP on HIBER, confirming the contribution of TPE, tri-plane loss, and 3D BBox prediction. The smaller margin on HIBER relative to MMVR is attributed to HIBER's WALK split containing only walking activities, where RFMask's fixed-height vertical proposals are less detrimental.

## Relation to Prior Work

RETR extends the DETR/Conditional DETR transformer detection framework to the multi-view indoor radar setting, where inputs are horizontal (azimuth-depth) and vertical (elevation-depth) FMCW radar heatmaps rather than RGB images. It departs from the prior RFMask baseline, which relies on Faster R-CNN with fixed-height vertical proposals and computes BBox loss only in the horizontal radar plane, by predicting full 3D BBoxes in the radar coordinate system and supervising them across three planes (horizontal radar, vertical radar, and image). The three radar-specific modifications—tunable positional encoding that exploits the shared depth axis between views, a tri-plane set-prediction loss, and a learnable SO(3)-constrained radar-to-camera transformation—represent the conceptual departure from standard DETR, which assumes a single 2D image input with no inter-view geometric coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Indoor radar perception is gaining traction for privacy-preserving monitoring (elderly care, building management) and robustness under hazardous conditions where cameras fail, yet prior methods like RFMask are constrained by hand-crafted fixed-height proposals that degrade on non-standing postures. RETR's 15+ AP and 12+ IoU gains over RFMask on two datasets demonstrate that end-to-end transformer detection with radar-aware inductive biases can substantially close this gap. Open questions remain around handling multi-path ghost targets (which would require extending the query classification to a {∅, person, ghost} taxonomy), generalization to unseen environments (performance drops sharply on MMVR P2S2), and whether the approach scales to denser multi-person scenes or raw radar data without heatmap preprocessing.
