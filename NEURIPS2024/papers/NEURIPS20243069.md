## Problem

Modern generative models (diffusion models, VAEs, normalizing flows) achieve high sample quality but fail to support calibrated Bayesian inference: diffusion models destroy local structure and cannot compress, VAEs produce overconfident and non-identifiable posteriors, and existing flow-matching approaches do not permit dimensionality reduction. The paper asks whether a generative model can simultaneously produce high-quality samples, compress data to a low-dimensional latent space, and yield posterior uncertainty estimates that are both calibrated and identifiable.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, AFHQv2 at 64×64) or Gaussian latent samples as input and produces either a compressed latent representation (as low as 1 dimension, ~0.03% of the 3072-dimensional data space), a generated image, or posterior uncertainty estimates via sampling. The forward map integrates a deterministic ODE from data to a Gaussian; the reverse map integrates the same ODE backward to generate samples. Because the ODE is invertible and neighborhood-preserving, a prior over data (e.g., a Gaussian ball around an observation) can be propagated forward to yield a posterior over the latent space with controllable numerical error.

## Method

The core contribution is a class of probability flow ODEs called inflationary flows, derived from the SDE–pfODE duality of diffusion models. The authors specify a forward noising process as convolution with a time-varying Gaussian kernel C(t) plus a coordinate rescaling A(t), yielding a pfODE whose score function is learned via standard denoising score matching (L2 loss with preconditioning factors from Karras et al.). Two schedule families are defined: dimension-preserving flows use proportional inflation along all principal axes (preserving the participation ratio), while dimension-reducing flows use anisotropic inflation at different exponential rates along eigenvectors, selectively shrinking lower-variance dimensions. The resulting map is uniquely determined by C(t), A(t), and the learned score, is invertible, and preserves local neighborhoods, enabling sampling-based posterior inference with error controlled by ODE step size. The paper further proves equivalence between these pfODEs and the Gaussian-paths formulation of flow matching when generalized to full covariance matrices.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Inflationary Flows (IFs) achieve FID scores of 23.3, 24.3, and 23.2 at 30, 40, and 62 preserved dimensions respectively, dramatically outperforming all three injective flow baselines (best baseline FID: 280.8). On AFHQv2, IFs maintain competitive generative quality even under extreme compression: FID of 11.95 at d=2 (~0.07% of nominal dimensionality) and 12.65 at d=1 (~0.03%), compared to 11.90 at full dimensionality (d=3072). Round-trip integration MSE remains low (≤1.55) across all tested compression levels, confirming the invertibility and neighborhood-preserving properties of the learned maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inflationary Flows outperform all three injective flow baselines by an enormous margin on CIFAR-10. At d=62 preserved dimensions, IFs achieve FID 23.2 versus 280.9 (M-Flow), 280.8 (RFs), and 287.9 (CMFs) — roughly a 12× improvement. The gap is even wider at lower dimensions: at d=30, IFs score 23.3 while the best baseline (CMFs) scores 532.6, a ~23× difference. This suggests that existing injective flow models struggle severely with high-compression generative modeling, whereas the diffusion-trained score function in IFs provides a much richer vector field for transporting samples. The paper notes that most compression-capable flow models 'still fail to reach high generative performance on key benchmark image datasets,' and these results confirm that gap quantitatively. No baseline is competitive at any tested dimensionality, and the IFs advantage is consistent across all three comparison points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the probability flow ODE (pfODE) from diffusion-based models—previously used only as an alternative generation pathway—to serve as a deterministic, invertible map for Bayesian inference. It builds directly on the SDE-pfODE duality established by Song et al. [30] and the flow matching framework [36–42], showing in Appendix A.5 that inflationary flows are equivalent to Gaussian-paths flow matching with matrix-valued noise schedules. The work departs from injective flow models [21–25], which also enable compression but achieve far lower generative quality (FID 280–544 vs. ~23 here), and from standard DBMs, which preserve no local structure and offer no data compression. The key conceptual move is treating the pfODE as an inference engine rather than a generator, enabling sampling-based posterior estimation with controllable numerical error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-trained score functions can support calibrated posterior inference at extreme compression ratios (down to 0.03% of nominal dimensionality), addressing a gap where modern generative models have been poor tools for scientific uncertainty quantification. However, the calibration validation is limited to a 2D toy dataset, the dimensionality measure (participation ratio) relies only on second-order statistics and favors principal-component-aligned compression, and the required wider noise schedules introduce a clear compression-vs-quality tradeoff. Open questions include whether more sophisticated dimensionality measures or non-axis-aligned schedules could enable stronger compression on curved manifolds, and whether the simplified Gaussian latent space makes conditional or joint generation more tractable.
