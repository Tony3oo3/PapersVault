## Problem

Existing single-point model fingerprinting methods are highly sensitive to decision boundary alterations caused by removal attacks (fine-tuning, pruning, adversarial training, model extraction), causing many fingerprinting samples to become invalid. Additionally, single-point fingerprints suffer from sparse fingerprinting resemblance, where unrelated models share similar local boundary regions, leading to high false positive rates in ownership verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242212/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a white-box source model, a black-box suspect model (queried via API), and a set of 100 clean base images as input. It produces a surface trajectory — a fixed-length chain of progressively adversarial samples spanning m classes (m=10 for CIFAR-100/ImageNet, with l=2 samples per class transition) — along with a scalar mutation rate and a binary ownership judgment. The trajectory length is fixed by design to bound querying costs, and step sizes are dynamically optimized rather than fixed, yielding smaller steps near the boundary and larger steps farther away.

## Method

ADV-TRA replaces independent single-point adversarial samples with adversarial trajectories: chains of samples with progressively increasing adversarial strength that cross a decision boundary. Trajectory generation uses four stages: (1) initialization via gradient descent with optimizable step sizes, (2) boundary probing that enforces the last sample to be the first misclassified one using a length-control factor and two auxiliary losses (a brake loss enforcing decaying step sizes and a forward loss preventing recession), (3) bilateralization by mirroring the trajectory around the boundary-crossing point, and (4) sequential connection of bilateral trajectories across m randomly selected class pairs to form a surface trajectory capturing the full decision surface. Verification computes the mutation rate — the fraction of trajectory samples whose predictions differ between source and suspect models — and flags infringement when the rate falls below a threshold of 0.5. The progressive adversarial levels make trajectories tolerant to boundary shifts, while multi-class traversal captures global fingerprint features that single-boundary trajectories miss, jointly improving robustness and reducing false positives.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, ADV-TRA achieves a perfect AUC of 1.00 and T1%F of 1.00, perfectly separating infringing (positive) from innocent (negative) models. On ImageNet, it reaches AUC 0.96 and T1%F 0.88, still leading the best baseline (UAP) by 0.11 AUC. On CIFAR-10, the lowest positive-model detection rate (0.612 under adversarial training with ε=0.01) remains far above the highest negative-model rate (0.143), yielding zero false positives, whereas all four baselines exhibit overlapping detection rates between positive and negative models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADV-TRA outperforms all four baselines (CoRt, IPGuard, CAE, UAP) on every dataset and every metric. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, it achieves a perfect AUC of 1.00, compared to the strongest baseline UAP at 0.91 and 0.92 respectively; on ImageNet it reaches 0.96 AUC versus UAP's 0.85. The most striking advantage is in false-positive control: ADV-TRA's detection rate on negative (innocent) models stays below 0.143, while IPGuard reaches 0.631 and CAE 0.561 on the same negative models, meaning those baselines would falsely flag innocent models as stolen. Under adversarial training with ε=0.01 (the strongest single attack tested), ADV-TRA retains a 0.612 detection rate on positive models, roughly 3× higher than IPGuard (0.162) and CAE (0.158) and about 1.5× higher than UAP (0.348). Under model extraction (PRADA on CIFAR-100), ADV-TRA achieves an F1-score of 0.90, leading UAP (0.85), CAE (0.76), and IPGuard (0.70) by 0.05–0.20.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model fingerprinting paradigm established by IPGuard, CAE, and UAP, which all rely on single-point adversarial samples near the decision boundary as fingerprints. The key departure is replacing isolated fingerprinting samples with chains of progressively adversarial samples (trajectories) built upon the Basic Iterative Method, where step sizes are treated as optimizable variables rather than fixed constants. The surface trajectory concept—concatenating bilateral trajectories across multiple classes—further generalizes prior work that fingerprints only a single decision boundary, addressing the 'resemblance of sparse fingerprinting' problem identified in UAP and CoRt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model IP protection is increasingly critical as training costs escalate (e.g., GPT-3 at $2.4M), and the paper demonstrates that trajectory-based fingerprinting can achieve perfect separation (AUC=1.0) between infringing and innocent models on CIFAR-10/100 while maintaining false positive rates below 0.15—something no prior single-point method achieves. However, the method incurs higher query costs (m×2l queries per trajectory vs. one per sample), performance degrades notably on ImageNet (AUC drops to 0.96), and adversarial training remains the strongest removal attack. Open questions include robustness to combined removal attacks, extension beyond image classification, and reducing query overhead for cloud-deployed models.
