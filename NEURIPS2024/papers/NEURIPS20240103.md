## Problem

Existing neural 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: camera-view radiance fields (NeRF) are robust on real-world scenes but cannot accurately model reflective surfaces, while reflected-view radiance fields (Ref-NeRF) capture specular reflections well but produce overly smooth geometry and unstable optimization on general scenes with mixed reflective and non-reflective content. No prior method robustly reconstructs both fine geometric details and complex reflections in a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240103/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated RGB images with camera poses estimated via SfM (e.g., COLMAP) and produces a watertight 3D mesh extracted from the SDF zero-level set, along with novel-view RGB renderings and surface normals. Geometry is encoded in a 15-level iNGP multi-resolution hash grid (resolutions 32–4096, 4 channels each), and the model is trained per-scene on 8 V100 GPUs for 25k steps (object-level) or 100k steps (unbounded scenes), taking 0.75–3.5 hours respectively.

## Method

UniSDF represents scene geometry as a signed distance field (SDF) encoded by an iNGP hash grid backbone, with a 2-layer MLP mapping iNGP features to an SDF value and a 256-dim bottleneck vector. The key architectural contribution is maintaining two parallel radiance MLPs—one parameterized by camera view direction (f_cam) and one by reflected view direction ω_r (f_ref)—plus a separate weight MLP (f_w) that predicts a per-point scalar in [0,1] via sigmoid. After volume rendering each radiance field and the weight independently, the final pixel color is a weighted composite C = W·C_ref + (1−W)·C_cam, allowing the network to adaptively select the most appropriate view parameterization per surface region without any supervision on the weight. Training uses a coarse-to-fine schedule over the hash grid pyramid (starting with 4 levels, adding one every 2% of training) to prevent the high-capacity grid from overfitting specular appearance as fake emitters, combined with eikonal, normal-smoothness, and orientation regularization losses.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, UniSDF achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.64 mm, second only to Geo-NeuS (0.51 mm) and ahead of NeuralWarp (0.68 mm), NeuS (0.87 mm), and Ref-NeuS (1.93 mm). On Shiny Blender, UniSDF attains the best PSNR of 36.82 dB and competitive SSIM/LPIPS/MAE on par with ENVIDR, while substantially outperforming Ref-NeRF (35.96 dB) and Neuralangelo (30.68 dB). Averaged across all four datasets (Shiny Blender, Mip-NeRF 360, Ref-NeRF real), UniSDF achieves the best overall PSNR (29.40 dB), SSIM (0.807), and LPIPS (0.174), surpassing Zip-NeRF (27.15/0.802/0.183) and BakedSDF (25.48/0.772/0.243).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DTU, UniSDF (0.64 mm) loses to Geo-NeuS (0.51 mm), which benefits from SfM point-cloud supervision and photometric consistency constraints, but outperforms NeuS (0.87 mm), NeuralWarp (0.68 mm), Factored-NeuS (0.77 mm), NERO (1.04 mm), Neuralangelo (1.07 mm), and Ref-NeuS (1.93 mm). On Shiny Blender, UniSDF achieves the best PSNR (36.82 dB), beating Ref-NeRF (35.96 dB), ENVIDR (35.85 dB), and Neuralangelo (30.68 dB), while matching ENVIDR on SSIM (0.976 vs 0.983) and LPIPS (0.043 vs 0.036); it also substantially outperforms Ref-NeuS (27.40 dB) and NERO (29.84 dB). On Mip-NeRF 360, UniSDF (27.67 dB) is slightly behind Zip-NeRF (28.53 dB) in PSNR but ahead of Mip-NeRF 360 (27.69 dB), Neuralangelo (25.08 dB), and BakedSDF (26.42 dB) in all rendering metrics. On Ref-NeRF real, UniSDF (23.70 dB) is on par with Zip-NeRF (23.68 dB) in PSNR and Neuralangelo (23.70 dB), but clearly outperforms Ref-NeRF (24.06 dB) and BakedSDF (24.43 dB) in SSIM and LPIPS, indicating better perceptual quality on reflective scenes. Overall, UniSDF is the only method that achieves competitive or best performance across all four diverse datasets simultaneously, whereas specialized methods like Geo-NeuS, Zip-NeRF, or Ref-NeRF each dominate only one or two settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSDF sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: camera-view radiance fields (NeRF, NeuS, Neuralangelo) that are robust on real scenes but fail on reflections, and reflected-view radiance fields (Ref-NeRF, Ref-NeuS, NERO) that handle specular surfaces well but degrade on general scenes. The paper's core departure is replacing the single-radiance-field assumption with a unified representation that learns a continuous 3D weight field to blend both parameterizations, rather than committing to one or the other. The geometric backbone follows the SDF + volume rendering paradigm from NeuS/VolSDF, accelerated by iNGP hash grids and the Mip-NeRF 360 contraction, with a coarse-to-fine training schedule borrowed from Neuralangelo/PermutoSDF to prevent overfitting to specular details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general-purpose method that robustly reconstructs both reflective and non-reflective surfaces across object-level and unbounded scenes without requiring scene-specific tuning or auxiliary inputs like reflective masks. This is practically important for downstream applications (robotics, AR/VR) where real scenes inevitably mix matte and glossy materials. Key limitations remain: the method depends on SfM poses that can be inaccurate for reflective surfaces, cannot handle sparse-view settings where reflections are ambiguous, and does not support relighting or material editing. An open question is whether the learned weight field generalizes to more complex BRDFs (e.g., clearcoat, anisotropic) or whether additional physical priors would be needed.
