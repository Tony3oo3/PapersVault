## Problem

Respiratory audio (coughing, breathing, lung sounds) holds predictive power for diverse health applications, yet no open-source respiratory-specific acoustic foundation model or comprehensive benchmark exists. Existing general audio pretrained models (AudioMAE, CLAP, VGGish) contain only ~0.3% respiratory samples, and the one respiratory-specific model (HeAR) is closed-source, impeding reproducibility and fair comparison. This paper addresses the absence of curated large-scale respiratory audio data, open pretrained models, and a unified evaluation protocol for respiratory health tasks.

## Input / Output

Input audio recordings (cough, breathing, lung sounds, snoring, vowel sounds) are resampled to 16 kHz, merged to mono, and converted to 64 Mel filter bank spectrograms using a 64 ms Hann window with 32 ms hop. During pretraining, spectrograms are randomly cropped to modality-specific lengths (2 s for cough, 4–8 s for breath/lung sounds). For downstream evaluation, the frozen encoder produces frame-level features that are averaged into a single representation per sample, fed to a linear probe that outputs either a class label (12 classification tasks: disease detection, gender, smoker status, COPD severity, body position) or a continuous value (7 regression tasks: FVC, FEV1, FEV1/FVC ratio, respiratory rate in bpm).

## Method

OPERA curates ~136 K samples (404 hours) from five heterogeneous sources (microphone and digital stethoscope recordings at 4–48 kHz) and pretrains three self-supervised encoders: OPERA-CT (a 31 M-parameter HTS-AT transformer with bilinear-similarity contrastive loss over positive/negative spectrogram crops), OPERA-CE (a 4 M-parameter EfficientNet-B0 CNN using the same contrastive objective), and OPERA-GT (a 21 M-parameter ViT encoder paired with a 12 M-parameter Swin-transformer decoder that reconstructs 70% masked spectrogram patches). All models are trained for up to 200 epochs with 10% held-out validation. Downstream evaluation uses a standard linear-probing protocol (single fully connected layer on frozen features) across 19 tasks spanning 10 datasets, with 6 datasets entirely unseen during pretraining. Key findings: contrastive pretraining excels at classification (OPERA-CT achieves MRR 0.6944 on health condition inference) while generative pretraining excels at regression (OPERA-GT achieves MRR 0.6548 on lung function estimation), and transformer architectures consistently outperform the lightweight CNN variant.

## Results

Across 19 respiratory health tasks, OPERA-CT (contrastive transformer, 31M params) achieves the highest mean reciprocal rank (MRR) of 0.5632, followed by OPERA-GT (generative transformer, 21M params) at 0.5298 and OPERA-CE (contrastive CNN, 4M params) at 0.4412. The strongest general-audio baseline, CLAP, reaches only 0.3435 MRR, while Opensmile, VGGish, and AudioMAE score 0.2912, 0.2289, and 0.2489 respectively. The best OPERA model outperforms the Opensmile feature set on 17/19 tasks and all pretrained baselines on 16/19 tasks. On health condition inference (12 classification tasks), OPERA-CT leads with 0.6944 MRR; on lung function estimation (7 regression tasks), OPERA-GT leads with 0.6548 MRR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPERA-CT decisively outperforms all four baselines on health condition inference, achieving 0.6944 MRR versus CLAP's 0.4319 (the strongest baseline) and Opensmile's 0.2190; it reaches AUROC > 0.7 on 6 of 12 classification tasks compared to CLAP's 3. On lung function estimation, OPERA-GT dominates with 0.6548 MRR, beating Opensmile (0.4150, the best baseline here) by a wide margin and achieving the lowest MAE on 5 of 7 regression tasks (e.g., 2.416 bpm for respiratory rate vs. 2.605 for VGGish). CLAP is the strongest general-audio pretrained baseline overall (0.3435 MRR) but is still 0.22 MRR behind OPERA-CT, confirming that domain-specific respiratory pretraining is substantially more effective than general audio pretraining. VGGish and AudioMAE consistently underperform, with VGGish scoring lowest on classification (0.1714 MRR) and AudioMAE trailing on both task groups. The lightweight OPERA-CE (4M params) achieves 0.4412 MRR, surpassing all baselines except CLAP on the overall score, demonstrating that even a small model benefits from respiratory-specific pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts two established self-supervised learning paradigms for audio—contrastive learning (following Saeed et al., ICASSP 2021, for OPERA-CT/CE) and masked autoencoding (following Baade et al., 2022, for OPERA-GT)—to the specific domain of respiratory audio, where prior foundation models (AudioMAE, CLAP, VGGish) contain only ~0.3% respiratory samples. It departs from the recent HeAR model [6], which pretrained on respiratory audio but was neither open-source nor accompanied by a systematic benchmark. The key conceptual contribution is the curation of a large-scale multi-source respiratory audio corpus (~136K samples, 400+ hours) and the formulation of a 19-task benchmark, neither of which existed in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPERA fills a critical gap by providing the first open, reproducible respiratory acoustic foundation model system with a comprehensive benchmark, enabling fair comparison across architectures and SSL strategies in a safety-critical domain where prior evaluations were fragmented and non-reproducible. The finding that contrastive pretraining excels at classification while generative pretraining excels at regression—supported by saliency map analysis showing local vs. global attention patterns—offers practical guidance for model selection. Open questions remain around scaling laws for respiratory audio, data-efficient fine-tuning for small downstream datasets, and whether more sophisticated combinations of SSL objectives can outperform the individual strategies. The system is explicitly not intended for clinical use, and some benchmark labels (e.g., self-reported COVID status in Task 5) limit clinical validity.
