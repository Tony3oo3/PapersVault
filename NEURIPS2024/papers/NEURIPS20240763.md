## Problem

Existing methods for discovering causal structure from event sequences rely on Granger causality, a purely predictive notion that excludes instantaneous effects and often identifies spurious dependencies (e.g., baking a cake is 'causal' to a birthday). This paper addresses the gap by proposing a causal model grounded in Pearl's DAG framework that supports both instant and delayed effects, with formal identifiability guarantees that Granger-based methods lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of multivariate event sequences, where each sequence consists of timestamps for a distinct event type (e.g., alarm types in a network, bank volatility events, daily activity transitions). It outputs a fully directed acyclic graph (DAG) whose nodes are event types and whose directed edges encode which event type causally triggers which, along with per-edge parameters (trigger probability and delay distribution). Unlike Granger-based competitors that return undirected weight matrices or may produce cyclic graphs, CASCADE always returns a valid DAG with oriented edges.

## Method

CASCADE models causality as a one-to-one matching between individual cause events and individual effect events, where each cause event triggers an effect event with a probability α and a random delay drawn from a cause-effect-specific distribution, or fails to trigger (delay = ∞). The score is an MDL instantiation of the Algorithmic Markov Condition: data cost is the negative log-likelihood of the delay assignments, and model cost encodes the DAG structure and edge parameters (trigger probability, delay distribution parameters) in bits. The search algorithm proceeds in topological order: at each step it selects the node with minimal achievable incoming-edge gain (guaranteed to be a true source), adds all outgoing edges that improve the MDL score, and prunes shortcut edges. This yields O(p³) complexity and, under the AMC, asymptotically recovers the true DAG. The key distinction from Hawkes-process-based methods is the explicit one-to-one event attribution, which provides a clear counterfactual notion of 'which event caused which' and enables identifiability for instantaneous effects.

## Results

On synthetic data with 90% instantaneous effects, CASCADE achieves a normalized SHD of 0.05 and F1 of 0.74, substantially outperforming THP (SHD-Norm 0.16, F1 0.23), NPHC (SHD-Norm 0.12, F1 0.57), and CAUSE (SHD-Norm 0.69, F1 0.19). On the Network Alarms real-world dataset, CASCADE identifies 142 out of 147 true causal edges with an unnormalized SHD of 42, compared to THP (SHD 127, 20/147 edges), NPHC (SHD 214), and CAUSE (SHD 1564). Under high noise (a=0.7) and multiple colliders (up to 20), CASCADE still recovers the true DAG with F1 scores of 0.82–0.97, while all baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASCADE outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated setting. Against THP (Topological Hawkes Processes), CASCADE achieves a 3.3× lower SHD on 90%-instant data (19.45 vs. 64.20) and a 3× lower SHD on Network Alarms (42 vs. 127), while also recovering 7× more true edges (142 vs. 20). Against NPHC, CASCADE achieves a 2.4× lower SHD on 90%-instant data (19.45 vs. 47.50) and a 1.4× lower SHD on exclusively-instant data (32.80 vs. 46.85), though NPHC is dramatically faster (4–7 s vs. 35–392 s). Against CAUSE (neural point process + attribution), CASCADE's advantage is overwhelming: SHD of 19.45 vs. 264.50 on 90%-instant data and 42 vs. 1564 on Network Alarms. MDLH, the closest method in spirit (also MDL-based), fails to terminate within 96 hours for 15+ variables, making direct comparison impossible beyond the smallest settings where it still underperforms CASCADE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Granger causality framework for event-sequence causal discovery (THP, NPHC, CAUSE, MDLH) by adopting Pearl's structural causal model with a novel one-to-one cause-effect matching mechanism and dynamic delays. Theoretically, it builds on the Algorithmic Markov Condition (Janzing & Schölkopf) and the formal MDL-as-Kolmogorov-proxy justification (Marx & Vreeken), instantiating them for point-process data. The CASCADE algorithm follows the topological-order search paradigm of CAM and related score-based methods, but is the first to apply it to an information-theoretic MDL score over event sequences. The paper also formally connects its model to Hawkes processes, showing that its identifiability guarantees extend to Hawkes-generated data under mild conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that CASCADE handles instantaneous effects — a setting where all Granger-causality-based methods fundamentally fail — and does so with a clean O(p³) algorithm that outperforms neural and Hawkes-based baselines by wide margins on both synthetic and real-world data. Significant limitations remain: the model only captures 'or' relations (not 'and'), only excitatory effects (not inhibitory), and the one-to-one matching precludes one-to-many or many-to-one causal structures. Open questions include how to operationalize identifiability for instantaneous effects without relying on specific trigger/noise probability strengths, and how to extend to conjunctive causes and counterfactual reasoning.
