## Problem

Standard worst-case prediction error models render predictions useless for facility location mechanism design: a single outlier prediction can make the optimal solution for the predicted data perform with unbounded approximation ratio on the true data. This paper asks whether 'mostly approximately correct' (MAC) predictions—where a δ-fraction of predictions may be arbitrarily wrong with no independence assumption—can still yield strategyproof mechanisms that beat the best no-prediction guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives two inputs: the strategic agents' reported locations X̃ (which equal true locations X under strategyproofness) and a prediction multi-set X′ that is MAC(ε,δ) with respect to X, meaning at most δn predictions are ε-incorrect with no constraint on the magnitude of those errors. The mechanism outputs k facility locations in the metric space (R^d for single-facility, R for 2-facility on a line) along with a guaranteed approximation ratio. Crucially, the mechanism never observes the true locations X directly; it only sees reports and predictions, and the δ-fraction of bad predictions is adversarially chosen with no independence or distributional assumption.

## Method

The core technique is the 'Robust Half' strategy: the first facility is estimated from predictions alone via BIG-CLUSTER-CENTER (which computes a (b−1)δ-balanced 2-median on X′ and returns the center of the larger induced cluster), while the second facility is chosen from agent reports via SECOND-PROPORTIONAL-MECHANISM (a strategyproof random proportional choice given the first facility). This is underpinned by quantitative δ-robustness results for the geometric median (distance robustness of 2·med₁(X)/((1−2δ)n) and approximation robustness of 1+4δ/(1−2δ)) and for β-balanced k-medians, which generalize classical breakdown-point results from robust statistics. For single-facility location in R^d, the mechanism simply selects between the geometric median of predictions and the coordinate-wise median of reports, achieving min(1+4δ/(1−2δ), √d). For 2-facility location on a line, the Robust-Half-Mechanism achieves an expected approximation ratio of 3.6+O(δ), improving over the best no-prediction guarantee of 4 by Lu et al. (2010), by analyzing two cases (balanced vs. unbalanced optimal clustering) and leveraging the robustness of both the big-cluster estimator and the second-facility mechanism.

## Results

For single-facility location in R^d, the deterministic mechanism (Algorithm 1) achieves an approximation ratio of min{1 + 4δ/(1−2δ), √d}, improving upon the no-prediction bound of √d for sufficiently small constant δ. For β-balanced k-facility location in any metric space, the mechanism (Algorithm 7) achieves a constant approximation ratio of 1 + 4k/(b−2−2k) (where b = β/δ), improving upon the linear O(n) no-prediction bound. For 2-facility location on a line, the randomized Robust-Half-Mechanism (Algorithm 4) achieves an expected approximation ratio of 3.6 + O(δ), beating the best known no-prediction expected ratio of 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon three no-prediction baselines. Against Meir (2019), which gives a tight √d approximation for single-facility location in R^d via the coordinate-wise median, the MAC-based mechanism achieves 1 + 4δ/(1−2δ) by computing the geometric median on the predictions, yielding a strict improvement for any constant δ < 1/4. Against Aziz et al. (2020), which achieves a linear n/2−1 ratio for capacitated (and hence β-balanced) k-facility location, the MAC mechanism reduces the ratio to a constant 1 + 4k/(b−2−2k) by exploiting the δ-robustness of balanced k-medians on the predictions. Against Lu et al. (2010), whose randomized proportional mechanism achieves a tight expected ratio of 4 for 2-facility location on a line (and any metric), the Robust-Half-Mechanism achieves 3.6 + O(δ) by combining the BIG-CLUSTER-CENTER estimator on predictions with the SECOND-PROPORTIONAL-MECHANISM on agent reports, winning for sufficiently small constant δ. All three improvements hold while maintaining strategyproofness, and the MAC model allows unbounded errors on a δ-fraction of predictions with no independence assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends facility location mechanism design with predictions by departing from the standard 'worst case' error model (used by Agrawal et al. 2022 and Xu and Lu 2022) to a new MAC (Mostly Approximately Correct) model where a δ-fraction of predictions may be arbitrarily wrong. It builds directly on Lu et al. (2010)'s proportional random mechanism for 2-facility location, repurposing its second step as a standalone 'second facility location' sub-problem, and on Meir (2019)'s coordinate-wise median as the no-prediction fallback. Conceptually, it also draws on robust statistics (breakdown points of the geometric median) and the PMAC model of Balcan and Harvey (2018), but applies these ideas in a strategic mechanism design context where the standard interpolation between 'trust predictions' and 'ignore predictions' is replaced by the novel 'Robust Half' technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight is that predictions with unbounded worst-case error can still improve upon the best no-prediction strategyproof guarantees, challenging the prevailing assumption in algorithms-with-predictions that high worst-case error justifies discarding predictions entirely. The quantitative robustness results (distance and approximation robustness of the geometric median and balanced k-medians) provide a smooth, parameterized generalization of classical breakdown-point theorems that may be of independent interest in robust statistics and robust clustering. Open questions include whether a deterministic mechanism with constant approximation exists for 2-facility location under MAC predictions, whether the 3.6+O(δ) ratio can be further improved, and whether exploiting the matching structure between predictions and agent reports can yield tighter guarantees.
