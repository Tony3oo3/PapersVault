## Problem

Federated Graph Learning (FedGL) models are vulnerable to illegal copying and model theft, yet no existing backdoor-based watermarking method can effectively protect them. Prior approaches either cannot handle variable-size graph inputs with connectivity structure, are easily defeated by watermark removal attacks (distillation, finetuning, and the proposed layer-perturbation attack), or lack any formal robustness guarantee. This paper is the first to provide certifiably robust backdoor-based watermarking specifically for FedGL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs of varying sizes (adjacency matrices and optional node features) distributed across T federated clients, along with a client-specific key (e.g., client ID hashed via MD5) that personalizes each client's watermark. It produces a watermarked FedGL model that outputs standard graph class labels, plus a certified bound r* specifying the maximum number of layers an attacker can arbitrarily perturb while the watermark prediction is guaranteed to remain correct. The watermarked graphs differ from clean graphs only in edge status among a small set of selected nodes (n_w = 3–5), achieving structural similarity >0.97 (NetSim/DeltaCon) to the originals.

## Method

FedGMark comprises two modules. The Customized Watermark Generator (CWG) learns per-graph, per-client watermarks by combining a GatingNet (which processes the adjacency matrix to extract edge features) and a KeyNet (which processes a client-specific key matrix derived from the client ID); their element-wise product, thresholded at 0.5 and masked to selected watermark nodes, determines which edges to add or remove. The Robust Model Loader (RML) replaces the single GL model with an ensemble of S independent submodels (each a base GNN like GIN, GraphSAGE, or GCN with varying channel widths for diversity) and aggregates their predictions via majority voting. Training alternates between optimizing the submodels on clean + watermarked graphs, optimizing the CWG to ensure watermark effectiveness, and FedAvg aggregation. The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which certifies that the watermark prediction is invariant to any layer-perturbation attack as long as the number of perturbed layers r ≤ r* = (N_A − N_B + I[A<B] − 1)/2, where N_A and N_B are the top two vote counts from the ensemble classifier.

## Results

Under no attack, FedGMark achieves watermark accuracy (WA) above 70% across nearly all dataset–model combinations (e.g., 90% on MUTAG/Fed-GIN, 86% on PROTEINS/Fed-GIN, 75% on COLLAB/Fed-GIN), while maintaining main-task accuracy (MA) of 72–85%. Under distillation and finetuning attacks, WA drops by less than 5 percentage points in most cases. Under the proposed 1-layer perturbation attack, FedGMark retains WA within 1–5 points of the no-attack baseline (e.g., 90%→90% on MUTAG/Fed-GIN), whereas the prior random-graph method collapses below 25%. Certified WA (CWA) is close to empirical WA when the number of perturbed layers r ≤ 2 (S=4), and reaches 86% on MUTAG even at r=5 when S=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the random-graph (Erdős–Rényi) watermarking method of Xu et al. (EuroS&P 2023), adapted to FedGL. Under no attack, FedGMark achieves WA of 70–90% versus the baseline's 38–57% across all four datasets and three FedGL models (Fed-GIN, Fed-GSAGE, Fed-GCN) — a gap of roughly 20–50 percentage points. Under distillation, the baseline's WA drops to 28–49%, while FedGMark retains 55–87%. Under finetuning, the baseline falls to 15–40% WA, whereas FedGMark stays at 55–88%. Under the 1-layer perturbation attack (the strongest attack studied), the baseline collapses to 11–38% WA, while FedGMark maintains 55–91% WA. In every setting and on every dataset, FedGMark strictly outperforms the baseline on WA while keeping MA within 1–3 points, confirming that the learned, client-customized watermarks are both more effective and far more robust than random graph triggers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor-based watermarking paradigm for model ownership verification (established for centralized non-graph models by Adi et al. 2018 and Bansal et al. 2022, and for FL non-graph models by Tekgul et al. 2021 and Li et al. 2022a) to the federated graph learning setting. It departs from the only prior graph watermarking method (Xu et al. 2023), which uses random ER-model graphs as triggers, by learning customized, client-specific watermarks via a GatingNet/KeyNet architecture that exploits graph structure and client identity. The Robust Model Loader's ensemble-of-submodels design with majority voting is inspired by ensemble classification and randomized smoothing (Bansal et al. 2022), but is repurposed to yield a certified guarantee against a new layer-perturbation attack rather than l2 parameter perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide certified robustness guarantees for watermarking in the FedGL setting, and the proposed layer-perturbation attack reveals a significantly stronger threat than previously studied distillation/finetuning attacks. However, the certified radius r* is tightly coupled to the vote margin (N_A − N_B), meaning practical certified protection is limited to 1–2 perturbed layers for S=4 submodels, and the computational cost scales linearly with S. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, real-world graph datasets (current experiments use graphs with <200 nodes on average), whether the certified guarantee extends to more realistic attack models (e.g., adaptive attacks that know the CWG), and how to handle privacy concerns when watermark data must be shared with a trusted judge.
