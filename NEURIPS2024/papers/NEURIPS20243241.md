## Problem

Clustering-based ANN algorithms are preferred in industrial-scale deployments for their small memory footprints and suitability for distributed and disk-based systems, yet they suffer from slower query times than leading graph-based methods like HNSW and GLASS. The bottleneck lies in the score computation step, which traditionally relies on product quantization (PQ). This paper identifies that inner product approximation between a query and cluster points is a multivariate regression problem, and shows that reduced-rank regression (RRR) solves it more efficiently than PQ at any given recall level and memory budget.

## Input / Output

At query time, the method takes a high-dimensional query vector (dimensionality 128–1536 in experiments) and a pre-built IVF index containing per-cluster RRR parameter matrices A (s×r) and B (r×m_l) stored in 8-bit integer format. The query is first projected to s dimensions via a learned eigenvector matrix, then quantized to 8-bit; scoring per cluster is two 8-bit integer vector-matrix products yielding a 1×m_l score vector. The top-t scored points form a candidate set that is optionally re-ranked using exact dissimilarities in the original d-dimensional space before returning the k nearest neighbors.

## Method

The key reformulation treats dissimilarity estimation as a multivariate regression: for each cluster l, the output matrix Y = XC^T (training queries × cluster points) is approximated by a rank-r factorization β_RRR = C^T V_r V_r^T, where V_r are the first r right singular vectors of Y, giving A = C^T V_r and B = V_r^T. This reduces per-cluster scoring from Θ(dm_l) to Θ(r(d+m_l)) operations. To further accelerate inference, A and B are quantized to 8-bit via absmax quantization (with random rotations to spread variance), enabling hardware-accelerated integer dot products through AVX-512 VNNI instructions on CPU or batched 16-bit floating-point matrix multiplications on GPU. A dimensionality reduction step projects queries to s dimensions using eigenvectors of the global training covariance, shrinking the first matrix product and total memory from Ldr+rm to Lsr+rm bytes. The method is supervised: each cluster's RRR model is trained on query-distribution samples routed to that cluster, which also makes it naturally adaptive to out-of-distribution query distributions.

## Results

On 16 datasets spanning dimensions 128–1536, LoRANN (IVF + RRR + 8-bit quantization) outperforms Faiss-IVFPQ and ScaNN on all datasets except glove-200-angular, and outperforms all baselines except GLASS and QSG-NGT on high-dimensional (d > 200) data. At fixed memory, RRR with ~16 bytes/vector outperforms PQ with ~128 bytes/vector (8× less memory) on all datasets. On GPU (NVIDIA A10G), LoRANN achieves the best QPS-recall trade-off on 7 of 9 high-dimensional (d > 300) datasets, while CAGRA remains superior for d ≤ 300. Index construction times for LoRANN range from 6 s (MNIST) to 2444 s (Yandex 5M), generally faster than graph-based methods (HNSW, GLASS, QSG-NGT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against product quantization (PQ, Jegou et al. 2011), RRR outperforms PQ on 7 of 8 datasets with identical k-means clustering, and at fixed memory RRR with ~16 bytes/vector beats PQ with ~128 bytes/vector on all datasets. Against Faiss-IVFPQ (Douze et al. 2024) and ScaNN (Guo et al. 2020), LoRANN achieves higher QPS at every recall level on all datasets except glove-200-angular, where it is slightly behind. Against the leading graph method GLASS (Gottesburen et al. 2024), LoRANN's QPS-recall curve crosses GLASS's between 80% and 99% recall on most datasets—LoRANN is faster below ~90% recall while GLASS dominates above it. Against QSG-NGT (Iwasaki & Miyazaki 2018), LoRANN matches or exceeds performance at all recall levels on 8 of 16 datasets. Against HNSW (Malkov & Yashunin 2018) and PyNNDescent (Dong et al. 2011), LoRANN is faster on all high-dimensional (d > 200) datasets but trails on lower-dimensional ones. On GPU, LoRANN outperforms Faiss-GPU, RAFT-IVF, and CAGRA (Ootomo et al. 2023) on 7 of 9 high-dimensional (d > 300) datasets, while CAGRA retains the edge for d ≤ 300.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes reduced-rank regression (Izenman, 1975), a classical multivariate statistics technique, as a score computation method for clustering-based (IVF) ANN search, replacing the long-standing product quantization (PQ) approach of Jegou et al. (2011) and ScaNN (Guo et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is reframing inner product approximation between a query and cluster points as a supervised multi-output regression problem, where the OLS solution is exact and its low-rank approximation yields a simpler, faster scoring procedure. The method is embedded within the standard IVF template (k-means clustering + centroid routing) and augmented with dimensionality reduction and 8-bit absmax quantization for practical efficiency, but the core contribution is the regression-based scoring rather than any change to the partitioning or routing mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For industrial-scale ANN deployments where memory is the binding constraint (vector databases, RAG pipelines, distributed/SSD-based systems), RRR offers a drop-in replacement for PQ with lower memory footprint and faster query latency at equivalent recall, and it naturally adapts to out-of-distribution query distributions without retraining. The main limitations are that RRR is inefficient for low-dimensional data (d < 100) where the rank-to-dimensionality ratio becomes too large, it is restricted to inner-product-based dissimilarities, and graph-based methods (HNSW, GLASS) still dominate at very high recall (>90%). Open questions include scalability to billion-scale corpora, integration with hybrid SSD/memory architectures, and whether the regression formulation can be extended to non-inner-product distance measures.
