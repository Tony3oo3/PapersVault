## Problem

Bandit optimization with preference (pairwise comparison) feedback over continuous domains with nonlinear utility functions remains largely unsolved. Prior work either restricts to finite action spaces, assumes linear utilities, or employs regression-type losses that are less sample-efficient than the natural logistic likelihood. The core challenge is jointly selecting two actions that balance exploration and exploitation at two levels—within the pair and across iterations—while maintaining valid confidence sets from only comparative observations.

## Input / Output

At each step the algorithm selects a pair of actions (x_t, x_t') from a compact continuous domain X ⊂ R^{d0} and receives a single binary preference y_t ∈ {0,1} indicating which action is preferred, modeled via the Bradley-Terry sigmoid of the utility difference. The utility is assumed to lie in an RKHS of a (potentially non-smooth) kernel k with bounded norm. The algorithm outputs a sequence of action pairs, a kernelized logistic estimate h_t of the relative preference function f(x)−f(x'), and anytime-valid uniform confidence bounds over the entire domain, with performance measured by cumulative dueling regret.

## Method

MAXMINLCB formulates action-pair selection as a zero-sum Stackelberg (Leader-Follower) game over the Lower Confidence Bound: the Leader chooses x_t to maximize LCB_t(x, x'(x)) while anticipating the Follower's response, and the Follower chooses x_t' to minimize LCB_t(x_t, x'), acting as the strongest competitor. This makes x_t a pessimistic (exploitative) choice robust to uncertainty, while x_t' is an optimistic (exploratory) choice that tests whether the Leader's action is truly optimal. The key theoretical enabler is a preference-based confidence sequence: the paper shows the dueling logistic loss is equivalent to a standard logistic loss under a constructed 'dueling kernel' k^D, and proves an isomorphism between the RKHS of k and k^D (under zero-mean eigenfunctions), yielding tight, anytime-valid uniform confidence sets. This avoids the regression-based losses of prior kernelized dueling bandit work and yields an O(γ_T^D √T) regret guarantee, improving on concurrent work by a factor of T^{1/4}.

## Results

On the dueling regret metric R_T^D (T=2000), MAXMINLCB achieves the lowest regret on 6 out of 8 test functions, with particularly strong margins on challenging landscapes: 56±2 on Ackley (vs. 65±0.5 for MULTISBM, 72±2 for DOUBLER), 113±6 on Eggholder (vs. 134±3 for MULTISBM), and 175±22 on the real-world Yelp dataset (vs. 199±25 for MULTISBM). The only losses are on the smooth Matyas function (125±5 vs. 106±6 for MULTISBM) and a narrow gap on Branin (104±13 vs. 89±13 for MULTISBM). Theoretically, MAXMINLCB satisfies an anytime dueling regret bound of O(γ_T^D √T), where γ_T^D is the information gain of the dueling kernel, improving upon prior kernelized dueling bandit results by a factor of T^{1/4} compared to Xu et al. [2024].

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAXMINLCB consistently outperforms DOUBLER and MULTISBM (Ailon et al., 2014) on 6 of 8 functions, with the largest gaps on Eggholder (113 vs. 154/134) and Yelp (175 vs. 263/199); MULTISBM is the closest competitor and wins only on the smooth Matyas function. Against MAXINP (Saha, 2021), MAXMINLCB achieves substantially lower regret across all functions (e.g., 56 vs. 120 on Ackley, 175 vs. 409 on Yelp), demonstrating that the Stackelberg game formulation is far more sample-efficient than pure information-gain maximization. RUCB (Zoghi et al., 2014) and IDS (Kirschner and Krause, 2021) are also dominated by MAXMINLCB on most benchmarks, with IDS performing best only on Matyas (128 vs. 125). In the logistic feedback benchmarking (Figure 1a), LGP-UCB (the paper's kernelized logistic confidence sequence) outperforms GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010), IND-UCB (Lattimore and Szepesvári, 2020), and LOG-UCB1 (Faury et al., 2020), confirming that the kernelized logistic estimator is more sample-efficient than regression-based or linear alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dueling bandits from finite or linear-reward settings to continuous domains with kernelized (nonlinear) utility functions, building on the logistic bandit literature (Faury et al., Filippi et al.) and prior kernelized dueling bandit work (Kirschner & Krause, Mehta et al., Xu et al.). The key technical departure is a duality: the preference-based logistic loss is shown to be equivalent to a standard logistic loss under a constructed 'dueling kernel' (Proposition 4), allowing the authors to import confidence-sequence machinery from kernelized logistic regression. For action selection, the paper departs from the common strategy of grounding one action (randomly or greedily) and instead formulates joint pair selection as a zero-sum Stackelberg game, which is a novel application of bilevel optimization to bandit acquisition functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime-valid preference-based confidence sequences (Corollary 5) are explicitly noted as being of independent interest for RLHF and online classification, where the Bradley-Terry logistic model is standard. The O(γ_T √T) regret bound is rate-optimal and improves on the concurrent work of Xu et al. [2024] by a T^{1/4} factor. Open questions include whether the zero-mean eigenfunction assumption (needed for the dueling-kernel isomorphism) can be relaxed, whether the Stackelberg game formulation extends to contextual or multi-player settings, and how the bilevel optimization scales to very large continuous domains. The paper also leaves mechanism design and welfare optimization from multiple feedback sources as future directions.
