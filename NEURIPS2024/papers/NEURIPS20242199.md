## Problem

While overparameterized networks have many zero-training-error solutions with different generalization performance, it remained unclear whether one could modify the training data distribution to steer optimization toward more generalizable solutions on the original distribution. This paper identifies the mechanism behind SAM's superior generalization—reduced simplicity bias, i.e., more uniform feature learning speed—and asks whether the same benefit can be achieved purely through data distribution changes without altering the optimizer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242199/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard supervised image classification dataset (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/CINIC-10, 64×64 for STL-10/Tiny-ImageNet) with integer class labels, and outputs a trained classifier. The unusual aspect is the two-phase training protocol: a short initial phase (t epochs) is used solely to compute per-example model outputs for clustering, after which the dataset is permanently modified by duplicating a subset of examples before the main training run begins. No dynamic reweighting or per-iteration resampling occurs; the modified distribution is fixed for the remainder of training.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 3.4) that SAM learns fast-learnable and slow-learnable features at a more uniform speed than GD in a two-layer nonlinear CNN, and (Theorem 3.5) that amplifying the strength of slow-learnable features in the data distribution can replicate SAM's normalized gradient behavior. The proposed method, USEFUL, operationalizes this: it trains a model for t early epochs, applies k-means clustering per class on the last-layer activation vectors to isolate the cluster of examples with similar (low-loss) outputs—these contain fast-learnable features—then upsamples the remaining examples once by a factor of k=2. Training restarts from the original initialization on this fixed, modified distribution. The approach is lightweight (1.3–1.6× training cost versus 2× for SAM), requires no hyperparameter tuning for the clustering step, and composes additively with existing augmentation strategies such as TrivialAugment to achieve state-of-the-art in-distribution accuracy across ResNet, VGG, DenseNet, and ViT architectures.

## Results

USEFUL consistently reduces test classification error for both SGD and SAM across all five main datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, CINIC-10, Tiny-ImageNet). On STL-10, USEFUL boosts SGD's performance to surpass that of SAM alone. The method incurs only 1.3x–1.6x training cost (depending on dataset) compared to SAM's 2x overhead, since it upsamples only ~30–60% of examples once. When combined with TrivialAugment, USEFUL achieves state-of-the-art accuracy for ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, STL-10, CINIC-10, Tiny-ImageNet; ResNet-34 on CIFAR-100; and VGG-19/DenseNet-121 on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM [22], USEFUL further improves generalization by reducing SAM's residual simplicity bias, yielding lower test error than SAM alone on all five datasets; notably on STL-10, SGD+USEFUL surpasses SAM without any sharpness-aware optimization. Against TrivialAugment [50], USEFUL is complementary rather than competing: stacking USEFUL on top of SAM+TA produces the best results (SOTA), because augmentation creates more variations of the upsampled slow-learnable features, amplifying the benefit. Against ASAM [42] (a scale-invariant SAM variant), USEFUL similarly reduces test error when applied, confirming the method's optimizer-agnostic nature. Compared to random upsampling (a control that matches training time), USEFUL considerably outperforms both SGD and SAM, confirming that the benefit comes from the targeted distribution modification rather than simply longer training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2020) by providing the first rigorous theoretical analysis showing that SAM's superior in-distribution generalization stems from learning features at a more uniform speed, i.e., being less susceptible to simplicity bias. It departs from prior simplicity bias mitigation work (Shah et al., 2020; Puli et al., 2023), which focused exclusively on out-of-distribution settings, by demonstrating for the first time that reducing simplicity bias also improves in-distribution performance. The method (USEFUL) also distinguishes itself from data filtering approaches (Gadre et al., 2023; Li et al., 2020) by assuming clean data and modifying the distribution through upsampling rather than discarding examples, and from dynamic reweighting methods (Katharopoulos & Fleuret, 2018; El Hanchi et al., 2022) by performing a single one-shot modification rather than iterative resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, lightweight recipe (one-shot k-means clustering + 2× upsampling of slow-learnable examples) that stacks with any optimizer and augmentation strategy, achieving SOTA on multiple standard benchmarks with only ~1.3–1.6× training cost versus SAM's 2×. The conceptual contribution—that excessive simplicity bias harms in-distribution generalization by causing the model to overfit late-learned features—challenges the long-held view that minimum-norm solutions are always preferable. Open questions include whether multi-stage upsampling (which the theory suggests would be optimal) yields further gains, whether the effect persists at ImageNet scale, and whether the theoretical characterization of SAM's full training dynamics (beyond early iterations) can be made precise.
