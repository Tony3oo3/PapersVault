## Problem

Existing active learning methods assume a fully-observed pool set and only acquire labels, overlooking the partial observability of features that is characteristic of real-world data collection. This paper formalizes Partially Observable Cost-Aware Active-Learning (POCA), a new paradigm that jointly selects which instances and which subset of features (and/or labels) to acquire under a budget constraint, when features are partially observed across samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240656/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes partially observed tabular rows (with varying missingness patterns per sample), per-feature and per-label acquisition costs, and a per-iteration budget. Tabular rows are serialized into natural language (e.g., 'Age is 25, Gender is Female, Blood pressure is 0.57') for the LLM-based GSM. The output is an iteratively selected (instance, feature-subset) pair to acquire, along with the updated predictive model; the acquisition loop repeats until a stopping condition is met, with the GSM retrained on accumulated unlabeled data.

## Method

μPOCA extends BALD and EPIG into partially observed metrics (PO-EIG and PO-EPIG) by replacing the fixed-input assumption with a two-step conditioning-and-marginalization procedure: the predictive model conditions on observed features plus a candidate acquisition subset, while marginalizing over remaining unobserved features via Monte-Carlo samples from a Generative Surrogate Model. The GSM is instantiated as a Mistral-7B LLM fine-tuned with QLoRA (4-bit, r=32) using random masking over all available unlabeled data, enabling stochastic imputation of arbitrary feature subsets. A key theoretical result (Corollary 1) proves that PO-metrics yield strictly greater expected uncertainty reduction than their vanilla AL counterparts, with equality only when unobserved features are conditionally independent of generalization given the label. A greedy feature-exclusion algorithm (Algorithm 1) selects the optimal feature subset under the budget constraint in O(J²) rather than O(2^J), and the full acquisition loop integrates GSM training, imputation, metric computation, and model retraining.

## Results

Across five tabular datasets (Magic, Adult, Housing, Cardio, Banking), PO-EIG and PO-EPIG consistently outperform or match their fully-observed vanilla counterparts (BALD and EPIG) over 150 acquisition cycles with 60 seeds. In the Magic dataset, vanilla BALD computed on partially observed features performs worse than random selection, while PO-EIG recovers performance close to the oracle (BALD with full features). In cost-aware experiments on Magic, PO-EIG with 60% feature acquisition achieves higher accuracy per unit budget than unrestricted acquisition, and the optimal feature acquisition fraction shifts from 20% to 60% as label cost increases from 0 to 50% of total budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PO-EIG against BALD (EIG) [7] and PO-EPIG against EPIG [28] as the two primary vanilla active learning baselines, along with Random and Oracle (vanilla metrics with full feature access) as reference points. PO-EIG outperforms or matches BALD on all five datasets (Magic, Adult, Housing, Cardio, Banking), with the most dramatic gains on Magic where vanilla BALD on partially observed features degrades below random. PO-EPIG similarly outperforms EPIG across datasets. Both PO-metrics occasionally fall short of the Oracle baseline, most notably on Housing, which the authors attribute to GSM approximation errors and weak correlations between unobserved features and the target. In the synthetic experiment (Figure 5), the gap between PO-EIG and BALD widens with increasing correlation between unobserved features and the target, confirming the theoretical prediction of Corollary 1. In cost-aware settings, PO-EIG with selective feature acquisition (60% or 20%) achieves better accuracy-per-budget than both vanilla EIG and unrestricted PO-EIG, demonstrating the practical value of the cost-aware formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Active Learning (BALD/EIG [7] and EPIG [28]) to a partially observed, cost-aware setting where both features and labels must be selectively acquired. It departs from standard AL, which assumes a fully-observed pool set and acquires only labels, by introducing Generative Surrogate Models (instantiated with LLMs via QLoRA fine-tuning) to impute missing features and enable uncertainty estimation under partial observability. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1 / Corollary 1) formally connects this to Bayesian Experimental Design, showing that the expected uncertainty reduction from acquiring both features and labels dominates that of labels alone. It also distinguishes itself from Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) methods, which optimize test-time feature queries for individual samples rather than training-set data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: standard AL metrics can perform worse than random selection when features are partially observed, a common real-world condition in healthcare, finance, and monitoring. The POCA framework and its μPOCA instantiation provide a principled, cost-aware solution with a clean theoretical guarantee (PO-metrics ≥ vanilla metrics), making it a useful tool for data-scarce, data-costly settings. Open questions include how to quantify GSM uncertainty to assess imputation quality, whether larger LLMs substantially improve acquisition, how to handle LLM biases in the acquisition loop, and how the restriction set (which features are acquirable) should itself be determined. The framework is currently validated only on tabular data with a 7B model, leaving open its applicability to richer modalities.
