## Problem

Recent world models compress environment dynamics into discrete latent tokens (e.g., VQ-VAE codes), which can lose fine visual details critical for RL credit assignment. Meanwhile, diffusion models have become the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity image generation but had not been applied to world modeling. This paper asks whether a diffusion-based world model can replace discrete latents, remain stable over long autoregressive horizons with few denoising steps, and whether the resulting visual fidelity improvements translate into better agent performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241872/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion world model receives a 64×64×3 RGB observation, a discrete action (up to 18 actions), and a channel-wise concatenation of L=4 past clean frames as conditioning; it outputs the next 64×64×3 frame via 3-step Euler denoising. A separate CNN-LSTM reward/termination model consumes the same frame-action sequence and predicts scalar reward and episode-termination signals. The policy network (shared CNN-LSTM trunk) maps a single frame to a discrete action, with LSTM states burn-in'd from the conditioning buffer before each imagination rollout.

## Method

DIAMOND adapts the EDM (Elucidating the Design Space) diffusion formulation to world modeling, using a U-Net 2D with frame-stacking conditioning and adaptive group normalization for actions and diffusion time. The key design choice is EDM's adaptive training target, which mixes clean signal and noise depending on the noise level σ(τ), preventing the model from collapsing to an identity function at high noise and enabling stable autoregressive generation even with a single denoising step (whereas DDPM suffers severe compounding error). The agent is trained entirely in imagination using REINFORCE with a value baseline and λ-returns, cycling between real-environment data collection, world model updates, and in-imagination RL. A separate CNN-LSTM handles reward and termination prediction. The full system uses only 3 NFE per frame (vs. 16 for IRIS) and 13M parameters, achieving a mean HNS of 1.46 on Atari 100k — a new best among world-model-trained agents.

## Results

DIAMOND achieves a mean human-normalized score (HNS) of 1.46 and an IQM of 0.64 on the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games, 100k environment steps), setting a new state of the art among agents trained entirely within a world model. It outperforms human players on 11 of 26 games, with particularly large margins on detail-sensitive games such as Asterix (3698.5 vs. 1028.0 for STORM), Breakout (132.5 vs. 31.0 for DreamerV3), and RoadRunner (20673.2 vs. 17564.0 for STORM). In the broader comparison including search-based and model-free methods, DIAMOND (mean HNS 1.459) trails EfficientZero (1.943) and BBF (2.247) overall but still wins on individual games such as CrazyClimber, Boxing, and Krull. On visual quality metrics for 3D environments (CS:GO, driving), DIAMOND's frame-stacking architecture achieves FID 9.6, FVD 34.8, and LPIPS 0.107, substantially outperforming IRIS (FID 22.8, FVD 85.7) and DreamerV3 (FID 106.8, FVD 509.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among world-model-trained agents, DIAMOND (mean HNS 1.459) decisively outperforms SimPLe (0.332), TWM (0.956), IRIS (1.046), DreamerV3 (1.097), and STORM (1.266), with the closest competitor STORM trailing by 0.19 in mean HNS. Against IRIS specifically, DIAMOND also produces more visually consistent trajectories (no frame-to-frame inconsistencies in enemies/rewards/scores) while requiring only 3 NFE per frame versus IRIS's 16, and has fewer parameters (13M vs. 30M). In the broader comparison, DIAMOND trails EfficientZero (1.943) and BBF (2.247) on mean HNS, as these methods employ orthogonal techniques (MCTS lookahead, periodic resets with hyperparameter scheduling), but DIAMOND still wins on individual games including CrazyClimber (99167.8 vs. 58431.8 for BBF), Boxing (86.9 vs. 85.8), and Krull (8610.1 vs. 8223.4). DIAMOND substantially outperforms MuZero (0.562) on the same benchmark. In the DDPM vs. EDM ablation, the EDM formulation is dramatically more stable over 1000-step autoregressive rollouts, with DDPM suffering severe compounding error and color drift even at 10 denoising steps, while EDM remains stable even at a single step. On 3D visual quality (CS:GO, driving), DIAMOND's frame-stacking architecture outperforms IRIS (K=64) by roughly 2× on FID and FVD, and DreamerV3 by over 10× on FVD, while also achieving a higher sampling rate (7.4 Hz vs. 1.5 Hz for IRIS-64).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIAMOND sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2020; Karras et al., 2022) and world-model-based RL (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2023; Micheli et al., 2023). Prior world models for Atari (IRIS, DreamerV3, STORM, TWM) all compress observations into discrete latent tokens, which the authors argue loses visually critical details. DIAMOND departs from this by operating directly in image space with an EDM-based U-Net, making it the first diffusion model used as an online world model for RL in imagination. The key methodological departure is the choice of EDM's adaptive signal-noise mixing objective over DDPM's noise prediction, which the paper shows is essential for autoregressive stability at low denoising step counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pixel-level visual fidelity in a world model directly translates to RL performance, providing a concrete mechanism (enemies rendered as rewards in IRIS's discrete latents) for why continuous image-space modeling can outperform discrete token approaches. The CS:GO neural game engine shows the approach generalizes beyond Atari to complex 3D environments, though with acknowledged limitations in long-horizon memory and out-of-distribution generalization. Open questions include scaling to continuous control, replacing frame-stacking with autoregressive transformers for longer memory, and integrating reward/termination prediction into the diffusion model itself. The practical impact is significant: a fully open-sourced, 13M-parameter agent that trains in ~3 days on a single GPU and achieves the best world-model-only Atari 100k score.
