## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of SAM's implicit regularization focus on sharpness near local minima, leaving global trajectory behaviors and early convergence unexplained. Additionally, it remains unclear whether SAM's implicit regularization can account for its empirically observed advantage over SGD in the presence of data anomalies such as outliers and distribution shifts. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing 'balancedness'—the difference between squared norms of paired variables—as a global, data-responsive metric for scale-invariant problems.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained language models (RoBERTa-large 355M, GPT2-medium 345M, OPT-1.3B) augmented with LoRA adapter pairs (X_l ∈ R^{d1×r}, Y_l ∈ R^{d2×r}) per layer, consuming standard text training data for downstream tasks. BAR produces finetuned LoRA parameters and task predictions (classification accuracy, F1, or BLEU) while remaining compatible with FP16 mixed-precision training—a critical constraint since SAM requires FP32 for numerical stability. The regularization is applied independently per LoRA layer, making it layer-wise rather than global.

## Method

The paper introduces balancedness B_t = ½(||x_t||² − ||y_t||²) as a new implicit regularization metric, proving that SAM drives |B_t| toward zero for overparametrized problems and keeps it bounded for non-overparametrized problems, while SGD preserves B_t unchanged. It further shows that noisy data (low SNR) accelerate balancedness promotion, explaining SAM's empirical edge under data anomalies. Leveraging this theory, the authors design BAR (Balancedness-Aware Regularization): nBAR compares stochastic gradient norms ||g_x|| vs ||g_y|| to decide which variable to scale up or down, while oBAR compares parameter norms ||x|| vs ||y||. This explicit regularizer mimics SAM's balancedness dynamics without requiring the second gradient pass, reducing computational overhead by 95% relative to SAM while matching or exceeding its test accuracy on LoRA finetuning across RoBERTa, GPT2, and OPT.

## Results

On few-shot learning with OPT-1.3B, nBAR achieves an average accuracy of 79.2%, outperforming LoRA (77.6%), LoRA-SAM (78.4%), and prefix tuning (75.8%). On RoBERTa-large few-shot, nBAR reaches 81.2% average accuracy vs. 80.0% for LoRA and 81.3% for LoRA-SAM. BAR saves 95% of SAM's computational overhead (1.03–1.05× vs. 3.28–4.43× runtime on OPT-1.3B) while remaining compatible with FP16 training, which SAM cannot use without underflow. On full finetuning of RoBERTa, nBAR closes the gap to full-parameter finetuning (85.9% vs. 85.6% average), and on GPT2-medium text generation (WebNLG), nBAR achieves the best BLEU score of 55.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), both nBAR and oBAR consistently improve average accuracy: +1.6% on OPT-1.3B few-shot (79.2 vs. 77.6), +1.2% on RoBERTa few-shot (81.2 vs. 80.0), and +0.6% on RoBERTa finetuning (85.9 vs. 85.3). Against LoRA-SAM (Foret et al., 2021), nBAR matches or slightly exceeds SAM's accuracy (79.2 vs. 78.4 on OPT-1.3B; 81.2 vs. 81.3 on RoBERTa) while reducing runtime from 3.3–4.4× to 1.03–1.05×, a 95% overhead saving. Against prefix tuning (Li and Liang, 2021), BAR variants outperform by 3.4% on OPT-1.3B (79.2 vs. 75.8) and match or exceed on RoBERTa finetuning (85.9 vs. 84.2 for Adapter). Against Adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019), nBAR achieves 85.9% average on RoBERTa finetuning vs. 84.2% for Adapter, with both using only 0.8M trainable parameters. The authors attribute BAR's edge over SAM to per-layer balancedness regularization, which overcomes the O(√D) slowdown in balancedness convergence that SAM suffers in multi-layer LoRA settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical understanding of SAM's implicit regularization by replacing the standard sharpness metric with a new concept—balancedness (the difference of squared norms between variable groups)—that captures global trajectory behavior rather than local minima properties. It departs from prior SAM analyses (Wen et al., 2023a; Bartlett et al., 2023) by being Hessian-free, requiring no positive eigenvalue assumptions, and imposing no batchsize constraints, while also covering both non-overparametrized and overparametrized scale-invariant problems. The explicit regularizer in BAR draws on the balancedness-promoting penalty from matrix sensing literature (Tu et al., 2016; Ge et al., 2017), repurposing it as a lightweight drop-in for LoRA fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The balancedness framework offers a data-responsive explanation for why SAM outperforms SGD under outliers and distributional shift—a phenomenon previously observed empirically but not mechanistically explained. BAR's 95% computational savings over SAM (by eliminating the second gradient) while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes it a practical tool for large-scale LoRA fine-tuning, particularly where FP16 compatibility matters. However, the approach is limited to scale-invariant modules (LoRA, attention) and does not apply to linear models; the authors also leave open whether SAM possesses other forms of implicit regularization beyond balancedness and sharpness, and whether the benefits scale further to larger models.
