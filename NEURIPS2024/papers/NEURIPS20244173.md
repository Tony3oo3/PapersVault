## Problem

Backdoor attacks on multivariate time series (MTS) forecasting remain entirely unexplored, despite the critical reliance of high-stakes systems (traffic control, stock prediction, climate forecasting) on data-driven forecasting models. Unlike classification backdoor attacks that flip discrete labels, forecasting backdoor attacks must induce a continuous target pattern in predictions while satisfying two unique constraints: stealthiness (triggers must be imperceptible in the time series) and sparsity (attacks should be confined to a small subset of variables).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a clean MTS dataset (T×N matrix), a predefined target pattern of length t^PTN, a trigger length t^TGR, and injection rates α_T (temporal) and α_S (spatial). It outputs a poisoned dataset where triggers are additively injected at selected timestamps and variables, with future data replaced by the target pattern. A key structural detail is the soft identification mechanism: since sliced time windows may only partially overlap with injected triggers or target patterns, the attack's influence is weighted by the proportion of target pattern present in the forecast horizon, and the backdoor activates only when the full trigger is contained in the input window.

## Method

BACKTIME formulates trigger generation as a bi-level optimization problem solved iteratively with a surrogate forecasting model (FEDformer) and a GNN-based trigger generator. For timestamp selection, it identifies vulnerable points where a pretrained clean model exhibits high MAE, then constructs a static inter-variable correlation graph by applying DFT to extract low-frequency features, passing them through an MLP, and computing cosine similarities. The trigger generator is a 2-layer GCN that takes the historical window preceding each trigger position and the correlation graph to produce adaptive triggers, with a tanh-based non-linear scaling to enforce amplitude bounds. The bi-level training alternates between updating the surrogate model on poisoned data (clean forecasting loss) and updating the trigger generator using an attack loss (MSE to target pattern, weighted by soft identification) plus a normalization loss that encourages alternating positive/negative components to mimic natural high-frequency fluctuations.

## Results

BACKTIME achieves the lowest attack MAE (MAE_A) across all five datasets and three model architectures (TimesNet, FEDformer, Autoformer). On average, BACKTIME reduces MAE_A by 50.8% (PEMS03), 44.1% (PEMS04), 52.6% (PEMS08), 83.5% (Weather), and 45.4% (ETTm1) compared to clean training. The method also preserves competitive clean forecasting ability (MAE_C), with attacked models on PEMS08, Weather, and ETTm1 showing similar or even better clean performance than clean-trained models. Anomaly detection (GDN, USAD) yields F1-scores near 0.5 and AUC near 0.5, confirming the stealthiness of the injected triggers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BACKTIME is compared against four baselines: Clean (no attack), Random (uniform-distribution triggers), Inverse (trigger generated by an inverted forecasting model), and Manhattan (trigger from the nearest historical segment to the target pattern). BACKTIME achieves the lowest MAE_A on every dataset-model combination, outperforming the strongest baseline (Inverse) by 3.55 on PEMS03 (17.52 vs 21.07), 0.00 on PEMS04 (26.17 vs 26.17, a tie), 8.91 on PEMS08 (21.48 vs 30.39), 50.07 on Weather (15.49 vs 65.56), and 0.12 on ETTm1 (1.41 vs 1.53). The Manhattan baseline is consistently the weakest attack method, while Random performs poorly due to its lack of structure. Notably, BACKTIME also maintains the best or near-best clean forecasting MAE_C, e.g., 8.43 on Weather (better than Clean's 12.75) and 1.14 on ETTm1 (better than Clean's 1.25), showing the attack does not degrade the model's legitimate forecasting capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bi-level optimization framework for backdoor attacks established by Doan et al. [19, 20] from classification tasks to multivariate time series forecasting, where the target object shifts from a discrete label to a continuous sequence pattern. It departs from prior time series backdoor work (TimeTrojan [18], Jiang et al. [38]) that targets classification, introducing unique challenges: real-time trigger generation (no access to future data), soft identification of partially-overlapped triggers in sliced windows, and inter-variable dependence exploited via a GNN-based trigger generator. The graph structure for trigger generation draws on DFT-based low-frequency feature extraction and cosine-similarity-based correlation learning, while the trigger generator itself uses a GCN conditioned on historical context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of backdoor attacks on MTS forecasting, a task with direct implications for critical infrastructure (traffic control, climate prediction, epidemiology) where data-driven forecasting systems are already deployed. The near-random anomaly detection scores (AUC ≈ 0.5) validate that the triggers are effectively undetectable by standard monitoring, raising practical security concerns. Open questions remain: the attack relies on complete trigger presence in the input (failing under missing values), does not extend to imputation tasks where models use bidirectional context, and no effective defense is demonstrated—only frequency-domain and clustering-based detection heuristics are suggested.
