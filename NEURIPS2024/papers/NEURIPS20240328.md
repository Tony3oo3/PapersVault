## Problem

Conventional autoencoders face a fundamental trade-off: narrow spatial bottlenecks improve representation generalization but destroy high-frequency details needed for pixel-level restoration, while wide bottlenecks preserve detail at the cost of representation capacity. This limits their applicability to tasks like flexible-resolution super-resolution and blind image restoration, where a single model must generalize across unknown degradation types and arbitrary upscaling ratios without sacrificing fine-grained texture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240328/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution or degraded RGB image, which is bilinearly resized to a fixed spatial size (256×256 for most tasks, 512×512 for face restoration) before encoding. The encoder additionally receives the 2D FFT of the input (real and imaginary parts per channel), forming a 9-channel input. The output is a restored RGB image at a flexibly configurable resolution controlled by the cosine basis size T, enabling arbitrary upscaling ratios from 1× to 8× without specifying the ratio at inference time.

## Method

CosAE replaces the conventional downsampled spatial bottleneck with learnable Fourier coefficients (amplitudes and phases) that are extremely compact—64× downsampled for faces, 32× for natural images. A Harmonic Construction Module (HCM) spatially expands each bottleneck vector into a set of 2D cosine basis functions (harmonic functions) of size T×T, where the frequencies (u,v) are learnable parameters shared globally across all images rather than conditioned on individual inputs. The decoder then acts as a learnable summation operator over these harmonic functions to reconstruct the image, mimicking the Fourier series reconstruction. Training combines L1 and LPIPS reconstruction losses with a patch-based GAN discriminator, and for flexible-resolution super-resolution, T is randomly sampled during training to expose the model to multiple output resolutions. The key insight is that frequency-domain coefficients are inherently compact in spatial extent yet can faithfully encode fine-grained details when combined with high-frequency basis functions, enabling an extremely narrow bottleneck without the detail loss typical of spatial downsampling.

## Results

On flexible-resolution super-resolution (FR-SR) for face images (FFHQ+CelebA, 15k val), CosAE with full objectives achieves FID-15k of 12.81/8.67/8.12/7.86 and LPIPS of 0.24/0.19/0.17/0.14 at LR resolutions 32/48/64/128 (×8 to ×2), outperforming LIIF-64x(G) (FID 18.91/15.50/15.05/13.94) and LIIF-4x(G) (FID 34.93/18.80/11.89/6.23). On blind face restoration (CelebA-Test), CosAE achieves the best PSNR (25.65 dB), SSIM (0.70), LPIPS (0.27), and IDD (0.53) among all compared methods. On 4× super-resolution on ImageNet-val, CosAE achieves FID-50k of 2.1 with only 67M parameters and 0.036s inference time, competitive with much larger diffusion-based models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FR-SR for faces (Table 1), CosAE outperforms LIIF-64x, LTE-64x, and ITNSR-64x on all four metrics (FID, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) across all resolutions, with the largest margins at the most challenging ×8 setting (LR-res 32). With full objectives, CosAE(G) beats LIIF-64x(G) by 6.1 FID points at ×8 (12.81 vs 18.91) and LIIF-4x(G) by 22.1 FID points (12.81 vs 34.93), while also achieving higher PSNR and SSIM. On blind face restoration (Table 3), CosAE wins on PSNR (25.65 vs 24.46 for GFPGAN), SSIM (0.70 vs 0.67), LPIPS (0.27 vs 0.30 for CodeFormer), and IDD (0.53 vs 0.57 for RestoreFormer), but trails on FID (52.03 vs 41.45 for RestoreFormer), which the authors attribute to the small test set size. On ImageNet 4× SR (Table 4), CosAE achieves FID 2.1, beating LDM-4 (2.8) and I²SB (2.8) while using only 67M parameters (vs 169M–552M) and 0.036s inference (vs 0.105–5.9s); it trails GigaGAN (FID 1.2) and ResShift (LPIPS 0.126 vs 0.167) but with substantially fewer parameters and faster inference. On natural image restoration (Table 8), CosAE halves the FID of SCUNet (22.34 vs 45.47) and improves LPIPS (0.31 vs 0.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

CosAE builds on the VQVAE/Taming Transformers encoder-decoder architecture [6] but fundamentally reinterprets the bottleneck: instead of storing spatially downsampled feature maps, it encodes learnable Fourier coefficients (amplitudes and phases) that are expanded into 2D cosine harmonic functions via a Harmonic Construction Module. This departs from implicit representation methods (LIIF, LTE, ITNSR) that use coordinate maps for flexible-resolution SR, and from Fourier feature mapping approaches (SIREN, NeRF) that apply frequency encoding at the input level rather than as a compressed latent representation. The key conceptual departure is treating the bottleneck as a frequency-domain codebook with globally shared learnable frequencies, rather than an input-conditioned spatial feature map, enabling 64× spatial compression while preserving high-frequency detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption in restoration that bottleneck spatial resolution must be maintained (typically capped at 16×) to preserve detail, demonstrating that 64× compression works when the bottleneck encodes frequency coefficients rather than spatial features. A single CosAE network handles arbitrary upsampling ratios and unknown degradation types without requiring them at inference, simplifying deployment compared to multi-network approaches. Limitations include restricted out-of-distribution generalization (cannot simply increase T beyond training range) and sinusoidal-wave artifacts on extreme degradations. Open questions include whether this frequency-bottleneck paradigm extends to video restoration, 3D reconstruction, or other modalities where Fourier representations are natural.
