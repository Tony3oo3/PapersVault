## Problem

Conventional wisdom holds that pre-training Vision Transformers improves downstream performance by learning useful features and representations. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether the features learned during pre-training are actually essential, or whether the attention patterns (i.e., how information is routed between tokens) alone are sufficient to explain most of pre-training's benefits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image patchified into N tokens and, during training, the attention maps (softmax(QK^T)) from a frozen pre-trained teacher ViT at each layer and head. The student produces class labels for ImageNet-1K classification or detection boxes and masks for COCO object detection. Unusually, the teacher's only contribution is its per-example, per-layer, per-head attention distributions (~10M activations per image for ViT-L); all feature-extraction weights in the student are randomly initialized and learned from scratch, meaning the student's intermediate representations are substantially different from the teacher's.

## Method

The paper introduces two attention transfer methods that exploit the natural decoupling of inter-token (attention) and intra-token (MLP, normalization, projections) computations in Transformers. Attention Copy directly substitutes the teacher's frozen attention maps into the student's forward pass, so the student only learns intra-token operations while inter-token routing is entirely dictated by the teacher; this requires both networks at inference. Attention Distillation instead trains the student to compute its own attention maps with an additional cross-entropy loss L_dist = H[softmax(Q_s K_s^T), softmax(Q_t K_t^T)] summed over all layers and heads, weighted by a hyperparameter λ, so the teacher is only needed during training. The student is randomly initialized and trained with standard supervised loss plus the distillation loss, using the same optimization recipe as scratch training. The key finding is that Attention Distillation matches fine-tuning accuracy (85.7 on ImageNet-1K with MAE ViT-L) while Attention Copy recovers 77.8% of the scratch-to-fine-tune gap, and ensembling the attention-transfer student with the fine-tuned teacher further improves accuracy to 86.3.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification with a MAE-pretrained ViT-L, Attention Distillation achieves 85.7% top-1 accuracy, exactly matching full weight fine-tuning (85.7%) while transferring only attention patterns. Attention Copy reaches 85.1%, closing 77.8% of the 2.7-point gap between scratch training (83.0%) and fine-tuning. Ensembling the Attention Distillation model with the fine-tuned teacher further improves accuracy to 86.3% (+0.6), demonstrating that the student learns genuinely different features. Across model sizes (ViT-B/L/H) and pre-training methods (MAE, MoCo-v3, FLIP, DINO), attention transfer consistently recovers most of the fine-tuning benefit.

**Table 1: Main results on ImageNet-1K with MAE-pretrained ViT-L. Attention transfer methods match or nearly match full weight fine-tuning while transferring only inter-token attention patterns.**

| Method | Top-1 Acc. (%) |
|---|---|
| Scratch (no transfer) | 83.0 |
| Fine-tune (all weights) | 85.7 |
| Attention Copy | 85.1 |
| Attention Copy (from fine-tuned) | 85.6 |
| Attention Distillation | **85.7** |
| Ensemble (Attn. Distill + Fine-tune) | **86.3** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses two natural baselines: training from scratch (83.0%) as the lower bound and full weight fine-tuning of the MAE-pretrained ViT-L (85.7%) as the upper bound. Attention Distillation ties the fine-tuning baseline exactly at 85.7%, while Attention Copy reaches 85.1% (closing 77.8% of the gap). A feature distillation baseline (distilling residual stream features from the teacher) achieves only 81.3%, well below both scratch training and attention transfer, confirming that pre-trained features are not the key ingredient. The ensemble of Attention Distillation with the fine-tuned model (86.3%) even exceeds ensembling two independently fine-tuned MAE models (86.2%), indicating the attention-transfer student captures complementary information. Under distribution shift (iNaturalist, ImageNet-A/R/S/V2), attention transfer underperforms fine-tuning by 1–7 points, revealing that pre-trained features do contribute to OOD robustness even when they are not needed in-distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. [26] but departs fundamentally in what is distilled: rather than matching logits or final features, it transfers only the intermediate attention maps (softmax(QK^T)) from a pre-trained ViT teacher to a randomly initialized student. It positions itself against the standard 'pre-train then fine-tune all weights' paradigm, arguing that the inter-token routing encoded in attention maps is the key transferable component, while intra-token feature learning can proceed from scratch. The work connects to prior qualitative observations of attention structure in ViTs [59, 63, 43], GLoMo's decoupling of features from combination [64], and the lottery ticket hypothesis [15] as an analogy for why specific connection patterns matter, but uniquely isolates attention maps as causally sufficient for downstream performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper challenges the dominant narrative that pre-training's value lies in learning useful features, showing instead that attention patterns alone can match fine-tuning accuracy on ImageNet-1K across multiple pre-training methods (MAE, MoCo-v3, FLIP) and model sizes (ViT-B through ViT-H). Practically, attention distillation offers a drop-in replacement for fine-tuning when weight sharing is infeasible (e.g., security concerns), and ensembling attention-transfer models with fine-tuned ones yields further gains (86.3 vs 85.7). Key limitations include degraded performance under distribution shift (pre-training data ≠ downstream data) and loss of the mild OOD robustness that fine-tuned MAE exhibits, suggesting pre-trained features still matter in those settings. Open questions include the surprising importance of queries Q over keys, whether removing layerwise learning rate decay unlocks further gains, and whether attention maps can be transferred across model sizes.
