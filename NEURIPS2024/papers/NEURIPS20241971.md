## Problem

Standard Gaussian processes assume homoskedastic Gaussian noise, but real-world observations are frequently corrupted by sparse, location-independent label errors that may fall within the normal function range. Existing robust GP approaches—heavy-tailed likelihoods, data pre-processing, and trimmed MLE—suffer from trade-offs between accuracy, robustness to in-range corruptions, computational tractability, and theoretical guarantees, with none simultaneously adapting to the outlier count, working with arbitrary kernels, and providing provable approximation bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of input-output pairs {(x_i, y_i)}, a kernel function k_θ, and a base noise variance σ², and produces a posterior predictive distribution over f, a sparse outlier set S ⊆ {1,…,n}, and point-specific noise variances ρ_i (non-zero only for i ∈ S). A key design choice is the re-parameterization ρ(s) = diag(K₀) ⊙ ((1−s)⁻¹ − 1) that maps a compact domain s ∈ [0,1]^n onto the unbounded ρ-space, enabling convexity analysis. The number of outliers is not fixed a priori but determined automatically via Bayesian model selection with a prior p(S) on the support cardinality (e.g., an exponential prior encouraging sparsity).

## Method

RRP extends the GP observation model to y_i | x_i ~ N(f(x_i), σ² + ρ_i), introducing learnable per-point noise variances that down-weight corrupted observations. A greedy forward selection procedure (Relevance Pursuit) iteratively expands the outlier support by computing, in closed form (Lemma 1), the MLL-optimal ρ_i for each candidate point given the current support, and adding the points with the largest marginal likelihood gain. The critical theoretical insight is that under the re-parameterization ρ(s) = diag(K₀) ⊙ ((1−s)⁻¹ − 1), the negative MLL becomes strongly convex in s whenever the base covariance K₀ is sufficiently diagonally dominant (δ < ~0.44) and ‖y‖₂ is bounded, a property rarely seen in GP marginal likelihoods or robust regression objectives. This strong convexity and smoothness imply weak submodularity of the subset selection problem, yielding a constant-factor approximation guarantee (1 − e^{−m/M}) for the greedy algorithm via Elenberg et al.'s restricted strong convexity framework. In practice, ρ is optimized jointly with other GP hyper-parameters at each iteration, and a backward variant (removing the least-relevant points from a large initial set) is used when the corruption rate is high.

## Results

On synthetic regression (Friedman10, Hartmann6), RRP achieves the highest predictive test-set log-likelihood across all corruption types (uniform noise, extreme outliers, Student-t noise), with the Student-t likelihood only surpassing RRP when the corruption probability exceeds 40%. On the DJIA flash-crash dataset, RRP is virtually unaffected by the outlier spike, whereas RCGP and standard GP are significantly distorted. For Bayesian optimization, RRP nearly matches an oracle GP on the 3D SVM and 20D Rover problems, and is the best-performing method on Hartmann6 under U[-3,3] and random-point corruptions at 10% corruption probability, though the power transform outperforms RRP (and even the oracle) on the 5D CNN problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRP outperforms all five baselines (Standard GP, Adaptive Winsorization, Power Transform, Student-t GP, Trimmed MLL, RCGP) on the synthetic regression benchmarks (Friedman10, Hartmann6) across every corruption type tested, with the sole exception being the Student-t likelihood at corruption probabilities above 40%, where it begins to match or exceed RRP. On the DJIA flash-crash regression task, RRP is virtually unaffected by the outlier spike, while RCGP is still distorted; when a second day of data is added, RCGP actually performs worse than a standard GP because its a-priori weighting scheme assigns the highest weight to the outlying points. For Bayesian optimization, RRP is the top method on Hartmann6 under both U[-3,3] and random-point corruptions (the hardest setting, where corrupted values fall within the function's range), and is competitive with an oracle GP on the 3D SVM and 20D Rover problems. The one clear loss is on the 5D CNN BO problem, where the simple Box-Cox power transform outperforms both RRP and the oracle, suggesting that the particular corruption pattern (I/O error causing partial training) is well-suited to a global monotone transformation. The Trimmed MLL baseline is consistently outperformed and is also the slowest to fit, particularly on the 5-dim Friedman function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust GP regression literature by departing from two dominant paradigms: (1) heavy-tailed likelihoods (Student-t, Laplace, Huber) that uniformly down-weight all observations, and (2) a-priori reweighting schemes (RCGP) that require outliers to be separable in input/output space. Instead, RRP introduces learnable per-point noise variances optimized via a greedy forward-selection procedure (relevance pursuit) that is conceptually linked to Tipping's Sparse Bayesian Learning and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit, but specialized to the GP marginal likelihood with a closed-form per-coordinate update (Lemma 1). The theoretical analysis draws on Elenberg et al.'s restricted strong convexity → weak submodularity framework, connecting GP kernel properties (diagonal dominance, RIP) to constant-factor approximation guarantees for the greedy selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RRP provides the first provable approximation guarantee for robust GP regression via sparse outlier identification, filling a notable gap where prior methods (trimmed MLE, iterative trimming) offered no quality guarantees. Its practical strength is handling the common but difficult case of sparse corruptions that fall within the function's normal range (e.g., a faulty sensor reporting a plausible-but-wrong value), where heavy-tailed likelihoods lose efficiency and a-priori reweighting fails. Key limitations include the assumption of location-independent noise (heteroskedastic, spatially-correlated corruptions may be better handled by other methods) and the conditional nature of the convexity guarantee (requiring diagonal dominance of the base kernel matrix). Open directions include Bayesian model averaging over sparsity levels and extending the convex-reparameterization + greedy-selection framework to other MLL-optimized GP hyperparameters (length-scales, inducing points).
