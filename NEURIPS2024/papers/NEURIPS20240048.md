## Problem

Adversarial training for LLMs is computationally prohibitive because existing methods (e.g., R2D2) require discrete attacks like GCG at every training iteration, each demanding hundreds of thousands of model evaluations. This paper asks whether adversarial training performed in the continuous token-embedding space can serve as a far cheaper proxy that still confers robustness against discrete natural-language attacks such as suffix attacks and jailbreaks.

## Input / Output

The training input consists of harmful prompts paired with a fixed safe continuation ('Sorry, I can't do that') and a harmful target continuation, drawn from the HarmBench adversarial dataset; CAT additionally mixes in utility examples from UltraChat200k at a 7:1 ratio. Perturbations are applied as ℓ2-bounded offsets (ε-ball) to each token's embedding vector, with ε scaled relative to the model's average embedding magnitude (e.g., 0.05–0.3). The output is a LoRA-adapted, 4-bit-quantized LLM that resists discrete attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, ICL, adaptive attacks) while preserving utility on MMLU, ARC, and MT-Bench.

## Method

The paper proposes two continuous adversarial training algorithms. CAT replaces the discrete GCG suffix attack in R2D2's unlikelihood loss with a 10-step signed-gradient-descent perturbation in the embedding space, keeping the toward-loss (increase safe-continuation likelihood) and away-loss (decrease harmful-continuation likelihood) terms plus a utility loss, and adds cutoff clamps to prevent over-optimisation of either term. CAPO reformulates the objective as an adversarial variant of IPO: it minimises (h − 1/2β)² where h is the log-likelihood-ratio difference between the safe and harmful continuations under the continuous attack, with the reference model evaluated on the clean (unperturbed) input to stabilise training; this implicit KL regularisation eliminates the need for a separate utility dataset. The central empirical finding is that robustness to continuous embedding perturbations extrapolates to discrete threat models, with a Pearson correlation of r = 0.99 between training-time continuous-attack loss and inference-time GCG loss, while requiring roughly 299× fewer forward/backward passes than R2D2.

## Results

PHI-3-MINI-CAPO achieves 100% attack robustness (0% ASR) against GCG, AutoDAN, and PAIR while retaining MMLU 67.2 and HARMLESS 90%. On Zephyr-7B, CAPO reduces GCG ASR from 75% to 5% and PAIR ASR from 87.5% to 10%, with only a modest utility drop (MMLU 60.3→56.7, MT-Bench 7.28→5.51). CAT on Gemma-2B reduces GCG ASR from 70% to 5% and PAIR from 27.5% to 15%. Overall, continuous AT requires 299× fewer forward/backward passes than the discrete R2D2 baseline (234,000 vs. 165,632,000 total passes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole discrete-AT baseline is ZEPHYR+R2D2 (Mazeika et al., 2024), which uses GCG suffix attacks during training. On the Zephyr-7B family, R2D2 achieves 0% ASR on GCG and AutoDAN but still yields 60% ASR on PAIR and 42.5% on ICL, whereas Zephyr-CAPO reaches 5% GCG, 0% AutoDAN, 10% PAIR, and 0% ICL. The most striking difference is on HARMLESS (benign queries phrased in HarmBench's imperative style): R2D2 refuses 57.5% of harmless prompts (score 42.5), while Zephyr-CAPO refuses only 1% (score 99), indicating R2D2 overfits the safety grammar. On standard utility benchmarks, R2D2 retains slightly higher MMLU (61.7 vs. 56.7 for Zephyr-CAPO), but this advantage is misleading because R2D2's chat-template refusals inflate apparent utility on non-chat benchmarks. In terms of compute, R2D2 requires 165.6M forward/backward passes versus 553K for CAPO—a 299× reduction—making continuous AT far more practical for iterative robustness training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the R2D2 adversarial training framework of Mazeika et al. [6], which was the first method to successfully improve LLM robustness via online adversarial training, but relied on computationally expensive discrete GCG suffix attacks. The key conceptual departure is replacing the discrete combinatorial perturbation set with continuous embedding-space perturbations (from Schwinn et al. [7]), transforming the inner-loop attack from an exponential search over token sequences into a tractable gradient-based optimization. Additionally, the paper introduces CAPO, which repurposes the IPO preference-optimization loss (Azar et al. [11]) into an adversarial setting, eliminating the need for a separate utility dataset that R2D2 requires to prevent degenerate refusal behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 299× compute reduction makes adversarial training for LLMs practically feasible at scale, potentially enabling routine robustness fine-tuning rather than a one-off expensive procedure. The extrapolation result—that continuous embedding-space robustness transfers to discrete token-level attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR)—is a useful practical finding, though it is validated on only 40 test samples and models up to 7B, leaving open whether it holds at 70B+ scale or against novel attack families. The paper also identifies a critical evaluation failure mode (chat-template inconsistency causing R2D2 to appear robust while overfitting to grammatical patterns), which should reshape how the community benchmarks LLM safety. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unseen attack types and whether the many hyperparameters (ε, β, cutoffs, attack iterations) can be simplified.
