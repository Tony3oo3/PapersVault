## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and QLoRA reduce parameter and optimizer memory but leave two major costs untouched: the FLOPs of backward propagation (which is ~2× forward) and the activation memory that must be stored for gradient computation. This paper asks whether backward propagation can be selectively skipped without degrading fine-tuning quality, and how to allocate which layers to skip.

## Input / Output

DropBP takes a pretrained LLM (LLaMA2-7B/13B/70B, LLaMA3-8B), an instruction-tuning dataset (Alpaca, Dolly, or Oasst1), and a single scalar hyperparameter — the target average drop rate p_avg (e.g., 0.5, 0.75, 0.875) — and produces a fine-tuned model with reduced training time and peak memory. The method is orthogonal to the fine-tuning strategy: it wraps around LoRA, QLoRA, or full fine-tuning by inserting a DropBP layer that conditionally skips backward propagation per layer, requiring only a few lines of PyTorch code to integrate into existing training loops.

## Method

DropBP randomly drops individual layers during backward propagation only, leaving forward propagation intact so that outputs and loss remain exact. Because transformer blocks use residual connections, the full network is an ensemble of 2^(2n) submodules; dropping 2np layers at each iteration is equivalent to training only the shallow submodules of depth 2n(1−p) or less, which Appendix A shows carry most of the gradient signal. To avoid uniformly dropping critical layers, DropBP computes a per-layer sensitivity score (the variance in L2-norm of all parameter gradients when that layer's backward pass is skipped) and solves a constrained optimization — minimize expected sensitivity subject to a FLOPs budget — via a greedy algorithm that incrementally raises drop rates in 0.1 steps. Training begins with a uniform drop rate for the first 10% of iterations, after which sensitivity-based allocation takes over, consistently assigning lower drop rates to the initial and final layers.

## Results

DropBP achieves comparable accuracy to baseline fine-tuning with deviations under 1% across all settings. On LLaMA2-7B (LoRA, Alpaca), MMLU 5-shot accuracy drops from 44.7% to 43.7% at p=0.875, while commonsense reasoning stays within 0.5%. For LLaMA2-70B (QLoRA, Alpaca), DropBP at p=0.875 actually *improves* MMLU accuracy from 68.3% to 68.8%. Training time is reduced by 44% (PEFT) and 57% (Full-FT) at p=0.875, convergence to the same perplexity is 1.5× faster, and maximum trainable sequence length on a single A100 increases by 6.2×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA and QLoRA (the primary PEFT baselines), DropBP is orthogonal and maintains accuracy within 1% while cutting training time by 25–44% (PEFT) or 33–57% (Full-FT) depending on drop rate; at p=0.875 on LLaMA2-70B, QLoRA+DropBP even slightly exceeds the QLoRA baseline on both MMLU (+0.5%) and commonsense reasoning (+0.1%). Against LayerDrop (LD) and Progressive Layer Dropping (PLD), which drop layers during *both* forward and backward passes, DropBP is substantially superior: at the same relative FLOPs budget (0.75×), LD drops commonsense accuracy by 7.3% (66.0→58.7) and PLD by 5.0% (66.0→61.0), whereas DropBP at p=0.5 (same FLOPs) retains 65.9% and at p=0.875 (0.56× FLOPs) still achieves 66.4%. The paper attributes this gap to DropBP preserving all layers during forward propagation, ensuring exact loss computation, while LD/PLD perturb the forward outputs of a well-pretrained model. Against layer freezing (a non-stochastic ablation), DropBP trains a combinatorially larger set of submodules (Σ C(64,i) vs. 2⁸ for LLaMA2-7B at p=0.875), yielding a smaller accuracy drop (−1.0% vs. −1.8%) and faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

DropBP builds on the ensemble-of-submodules interpretation of residual networks (Veit et al., 2016), which shows that a network with n residual layers can be decomposed into 2^n shallow submodules. It departs from prior layer-dropping methods (Stochastic Depth, LayerDrop, PLD) that omit layers during both forward and backward passes, by dropping layers exclusively during backward propagation while preserving the full forward pass. This orthogonal design allows DropBP to be combined with any PEFT technique (LoRA, QLoRA) without modifying the model's forward computation, and its sensitivity-based drop rate allocation draws on gradient variance estimation (GradNormVar) to allocate compute where it matters most.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DropBP addresses a practical bottleneck that PEFT methods leave unresolved: backward propagation dominates both FLOPs (2× forward) and activation memory, limiting sequence length and throughput on single-GPU setups. The 6.2× increase in trainable sequence length for LLaMA2-70B on a single A100 and the 79–117% throughput gains make long-context fine-tuning accessible without multi-GPU parallelism. A key open question is whether DropBP can accelerate pretraining of transformer models, where the forward pass is also a target for optimization; the authors note this as future work. The sensitivity-based allocation's benefit is most pronounced at higher learning rates or larger models, suggesting it may become more critical as fine-tuning scales up.
