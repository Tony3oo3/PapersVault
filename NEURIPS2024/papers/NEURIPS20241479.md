## Problem

Camera-LiDAR fusion models load single-modal pre-trained backbones that independently extract similar features, and the fusion mechanism then selectively relies on one modality's version, rendering the other's features redundant. Existing pruning methods target single-modal models or cross-type multi-modal settings (e.g., vision-language) and cannot identify this fusion-specific cross-modality redundancy, leading to significant performance degradation when applied to camera-LiDAR fusion architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained camera-LiDAR fusion model (e.g., BEVFusion-mit, BEVFusion-pku, AVOD-FPN) with its full parameter set {θ_l, θ_c, θ_f} and a multi-modal training dataset as input. It produces a pruned model with a user-specified sparsity ratio (80%, 85%, 90% unstructured or 30%, 50% structured), where low-importance parameters are zeroed out and the model is fine-tuned. The pruned model retains the same task interface—producing 3D object detections, BEV segmentation maps, or tracking outputs—while reducing FLOPs by 31–57% and inference time by up to 29%.

## Method

AlterMOMA alternately masks each backbone (LiDAR or camera) and trains the masked model on B batches, forcing the fusion module to reactivate previously redundant features in the unmasked backbone. Two indicators are computed via first-order Taylor expansion: DeCI (|θ·∂L/∂θ| on the full model) captures each parameter's contribution to task performance, while ReRI (|θ·∂L_masked/∂θ − θ·∂L_reactivated/∂θ|) captures how much a parameter's role shifts during reactivation, identifying fusion-redundant parameters. The AlterEva score subtracts normalized ReRI from normalized DeCI (S = α·DeCI_norm − β·ReRI_norm), so parameters that are important overall but whose importance spikes only when the other modality is absent receive low scores and are pruned. For fusion-module parameters, ReRI is averaged across both masking stages with a β/2 weighting. After scoring all parameters, a global threshold set by the target sparsity ratio determines which parameters to remove, followed by fine-tuning on the task-specific loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with BEVfusion-mit (SwinT + VoxelNet), AlterMOMA achieves 67.3 mAP / 70.2 NDS at 80% unstructured pruning, 65.5 / 69.5 at 85%, and 63.5 / 66.7 at 90%, compared to the unpruned baseline of 67.8 mAP / 70.7 NDS. On BEVfusion-pku, AlterMOMA reaches 66.5 mAP / 70.1 NDS at 80% and 62.3 / 66.0 at 90%. For structured pruning with ResNet101 + SECOND, AlterMOMA-50% achieves 64.5 mAP / 69.5 NDS with 56.7% FLOP reduction (264.42 GFLOPs) and 87.46 ms inference time, versus 64.6 / 69.4 for the unpruned model at 610.66 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlterMOMA outperforms all four single-modal pruning baselines (IMP, SynFlow, SNIP, ProsPr) across every pruning ratio, dataset, and architecture tested. Against the strongest baseline ProsPr [Alizadeh, ICLR 2022], AlterMOMA gains +3.0 mAP at 80% and +4.9 mAP at 90% on BEVfusion-mit, and +2.9 mAP at 80% and +5.6 mAP at 90% on BEVfusion-pku. The gap widens substantially at higher sparsity: at 90% pruning, AlterMOMA retains 63.5 mAP on BEVfusion-mit while ProsPr drops to 58.6 and IMP collapses to 42.7. On KITTI (AVOD-FPN, car class, 90% pruning), AlterMOMA achieves 77.4 AP-3D (easy) versus ProsPr's 74.2, a +3.2 point margin. The consistent advantage across BEV-based and two-stage architectures, multiple backbones (SwinT, ResNet, VoxelNet, PointPillars, SECOND), and tasks (detection, segmentation, tracking) indicates the fusion-redundancy-aware scoring in AlterEva is the key differentiator over generic importance-based pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the network pruning literature (IMP, SynFlow, SNIP, ProsPr) from single-modal and vision-language multi-modal settings to the specific case of camera-LiDAR fusion models. It departs from prior work by identifying a previously unaddressed source of redundancy: the cross-modal feature overlap introduced when independently pre-trained single-modal backbones are loaded into a fusion architecture. The method draws on Taylor-expansion-based importance scoring (as in ProsPr and Molchanov et al.) but introduces an alternative modality masking procedure to isolate fusion-redundant parameters, a mechanism absent from all prior pruning algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying camera-LiDAR fusion models on resource-constrained edge devices in autonomous driving, where the 30–50% FLOP reductions with minimal (or even positive) accuracy impact are directly deployable. A key limitation is that the fusion-redundancy concept is specific to multi-sensor perception fusion (where low-level features are spatially aligned and supplemented); it does not generalize to heterogeneous-data fusion like vision-language, where fusion modules match high-level semantics rather than supplement low-level features. Open questions include whether the approach extends to three-sensor fusion (camera-LiDAR-radar) with more complex redundancy patterns, and whether the hyperparameter β/α trade-off can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
