## Problem

The training recipes of the best-performing latent diffusion models are largely closed, preventing systematic comparison of conditioning mechanisms and pre-training strategies. In particular, how semantic conditioning (text/class) interacts with control metadata (size, flip, crop) and how to efficiently transfer models across resolutions and datasets remain underexplored, with prior work either conflating these conditionings or lacking principled transfer procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240425/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 into up to 77 tokens, with short prompts padded via noisy replication rather than zero-padding), class labels for ImageNet experiments, and control metadata including target image size, crop parameters, and a random-flip flag. It produces 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images in the latent space of a pre-trained autoencoder. The control metadata is treated as a first-class conditioning signal rather than a nuisance factor, and at inference time the user can independently steer the strength of semantic guidance (λ) and control guidance (β).

## Method

Building on the mmDiT-XL/2 (SD3) architecture with augmented self-attention, the paper introduces a disentangled conditioning scheme where the class/text embedding is routed through attention layers while control metadata embeddings are additively combined with the timestep embedding in adaptive LayerNorm, but gated by a cosine schedule γ_c(t) that zeros out low-level control signals (resolution, color) in early denoising steps and gradually increases them. A separate control guidance term β is introduced alongside the standard semantic guidance λ, enabling independent control over how strongly the model adheres to size/flip conditions versus text prompts. For resolution transfer, the paper derives a noise schedule rescaling rule (Proposition 1) that preserves per-patch uncertainty when upscaling, resamples sinusoidal positional embeddings by adjusting the grid step size rather than interpolating, and uses aggressive local cropping (0.4–0.6 scale) during low-resolution pre-training to reduce distribution shift at fine-tuning time. These components are validated through a systematic re-implementation of five architectures (UNet/SDXL, DiT, mDiT, PixArt-α, mmDiT) trained from scratch on ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-22k, and CC12M.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k at 512 resolution, the proposed mmDiT-XL/2 with disentangled conditioning achieves 2.76 FID, improving over the previous DiT state-of-the-art of 3.04 and the re-implemented mmDiT (SD3) baseline of 3.02. On CC12M at 512 resolution, FID drops dramatically from 11.24 (mmDiT baseline) to 6.27, a 44% relative improvement, while CLIP score improves from 26.01 to 26.17. At 256 resolution on ImageNet-1k, the method achieves 1.59 FID versus 1.71 for the best re-implemented baseline (DiT-XL2w/Att and mmDiT-XL2). The method consistently outperforms all five re-implemented architectures (UNet/SDXL, DiT, mDT, PixArt-α, mmDiT) across both datasets and both resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (mmDiT-XL/2 with disentangled control conditioning, noisy replicate text padding, and resolution transfer strategies) outperforms all five re-implemented baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the closest competitor, the re-implemented mmDiT-XL/2 (SD3) [14], the gains are 0.12 FID on ImageNet-1k@256 (1.59 vs 1.71), 0.26 FID on ImageNet-1k@512 (2.76 vs 3.02), 0.75 FID on CC12M@256 (6.79 vs 7.54), and a striking 4.97 FID on CC12M@512 (6.27 vs 11.24). Against DiT-XL2 w/ LN [38], the method improves ImageNet-1k@256 FID from 1.95 to 1.59 and @512 from 2.85 to 2.76. The UNet/SDXL baseline [39] is the weakest re-implementation, with the proposed method achieving 0.46 FID lower on ImageNet-1k@256 (1.59 vs 2.05) and 2.05 FID lower on ImageNet-1k@512 (2.76 vs 4.81). On CC12M, the method also surpasses the literature-reported SDXL FID of 17.01 at 256 resolution by a wide margin (6.79), and improves upon the SD3 literature CLIP score of 22.4 to 26.60. The largest absolute gain comes from the combination of low-resolution pre-training with local cropping, positional embedding resampling, and noise schedule rescaling, which together reduce CC12M@512 FID from 11.24 (train-from-scratch) to 6.27.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study that re-implements five major diffusion architectures (UNet/SDXL, DiT, mDiT, PixArt-α, mmDiT/SD3) under identical training protocols to enable the apple-to-apple comparisons the field has lacked. It builds directly on SDXL's control conditioning (size, crop) and SD3's multimodal self-attention, but departs from both by disentangling semantic conditioning from control metadata via separate attention pathways and cosine-scheduled low-level embeddings. The noise schedule scaling law (Proposition 1) extends the resolution-transfer analysis in SD3 with a derivation grounded in the EDM formulation, while the noisy replicate padding for text tokens is a novel regularization inspired by the observation that zero-padding wastes cross-attention capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the community with reproducible baselines and concrete, theoretically-motivated guidelines for scaling latent diffusion models across resolutions and datasets—addressing a gap where the best-performing recipes (DALL-E, Imagen, Midjourney) remain closed. The 25% training-efficiency gain from proper low-resolution pretraining with local cropping and the clean noise-schedule scaling law are practically impactful for any team training large diffusion models. Limitations include the absence of error bars (citing compute cost), restriction to 512 resolution, and no analysis of data quality effects; open questions include how control conditioning interacts with timestep and prompt conditioning at scale, and whether the local-cropping benefit persists at >100M sample sizes.
