## Problem

Existing universal anomaly segmentation methods depend on vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) with handcrafted textual prompts, yet visual representations are inherently independent of language. No prior work has demonstrated that a pure visual model, trained without any anomaly-specific dataset or language guidance, can segment unseen anomalies in an open-world setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241257/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single query RGB image and one normal image prompt from the same class (both at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution) and produces a pixel-level anomaly segmentation mask plus an image-level anomaly score (the maximum value of the predicted map, no post-processing). The model is trained entirely on synthetic change-segmentation pairs derived from MS-COCO, requiring no anomaly images or pixel-level anomaly annotations during training.

## Method

MetaUAS reformulates anomaly segmentation as change segmentation between a normal prompt and a query image, enabling training on large-scale synthetic image pairs with object-level (inpainting/pasting) and local-region (Perlin-noise-based) changes. The architecture comprises a frozen EfficientNet-b4 encoder, a soft feature alignment module that computes softmax-weighted cross-similarity to adaptively align prompt features to each query feature position (handling geometric variations), and a UNet decoder that fuses aligned multi-scale features with low-level query features to predict the change heatmap. Training follows a one-prompt meta-learning protocol with binary cross-entropy loss, optimizing only the alignment module, decoder, and segmentation head while keeping the encoder frozen to preserve generalization. This paradigm shift eliminates the need for anomaly-specific training data or language prompts, achieving state-of-the-art segmentation with fewer parameters and faster inference than vision-language baselines.

## Results

On MVTec, MetaUAS★+ (1-shot, with CLIP visual prior) achieves I-ROC 95.3, P-PR 67.0, and P-PRO 92.5, surpassing the full-shot UniAD (P-PR 44.7, P-PRO 90.0) and all zero-/few-shot baselines. On VisA, MetaUAS★+ reaches P-PR 48.1 and P-PRO 85.5, again outperforming full-shot UniAD (P-PR 34.3, P-PRO 84.8). On the challenging multi-category Goods dataset, MetaUAS★ (best-matched prompt) achieves I-ROC 90.1 and P-PR 53.7, dramatically outperforming WinCLIP+ (I-ROC 53.5, P-PR 5.7) and AnomalyCLIP (I-ROC 57.2, P-PR 16.9). MetaUAS requires only 22.1M parameters and 3.1 ms inference time, compared to 208.4M parameters and 201.3 ms for WinCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaUAS★+ decisively outperforms all zero-shot and few-shot baselines on pixel-level segmentation (P-PR) across all three datasets: 67.0 vs. 38.4 (WinCLIP+) and 34.5 (AnomalyCLIP) on MVTec, and 48.1 vs. 15.9 (WinCLIP+) and 21.3 (AnomalyCLIP) on VisA. It also surpasses the full-shot UniAD on P-PR (67.0 vs. 44.7 on MVTec; 48.1 vs. 34.3 on VisA) and P-PRO (92.5 vs. 90.0 on MVTec; 85.5 vs. 84.8 on VisA), despite using only one normal image prompt rather than all training images. On the multi-category Goods dataset, MetaUAS★ achieves I-ROC 90.1 and P-PR 53.7, a massive margin over WinCLIP+ (53.5 / 5.7) and AnomalyCLIP (57.2 / 16.9), suggesting that best-matched prompt selection is critical for fine-grained multi-subcategory scenarios. The basic MetaUAS (random prompt) already beats WinCLIP+ on P-PR by 20+ points on MVTec (59.3 vs. 38.4) and VisA (42.7 vs. 15.9), confirming that pure visual one-prompt learning is competitive without any language guidance. In terms of efficiency, MetaUAS uses 10× fewer parameters and runs 100× faster than WinCLIP+ while still delivering superior segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaUAS departs from the dominant vision-language paradigm (CLIP, WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP) for universal anomaly segmentation by arguing that visual representations are inherently independent of language, and instead trains a pure visual model via a conceptual reframing: anomaly segmentation becomes change segmentation between a normal prompt and a query image. The synthetic training data pipeline borrows directly from CYWS's inpainting-based object-level change synthesis and DRAEM's Perlin-noise local-region synthesis, but repurposes them to create a large-scale change segmentation dataset from MS-COCO that requires no anomaly-specific annotations. The one-prompt meta-learning formulation and the soft feature alignment module (bridging paired-image change perception to single-image segmentation) are novel architectural contributions not present in prior change detection or anomaly detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that universal anomaly segmentation does not require vision-language models or domain-specific anomaly datasets, offering a more efficient (10× fewer parameters, 100× faster inference than WinCLIP+) and arguably more principled alternative. The key open question is prompt sensitivity: performance degrades significantly when the normal prompt does not match the query's subcategory (e.g., on the multi-subcategory Goods dataset), and the best results (MetaUAS★, MetaUAS★+) still rely on either best-matched prompts or CLIP visual features, partially undermining the 'pure visual' claim. Whether this change-segmentation-as-anomaly-segmentation paradigm generalizes beyond industrial benchmarks to medical or natural-image anomaly detection remains untested.
