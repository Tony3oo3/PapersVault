## Problem

The effect of weight decay and L2-regularization on models with factorized (matrix-product) parametrization remains poorly understood, despite this parametrization being central to attention layers in transformers where W_K^T W_Q and P W_V are directly multiplied. While it is known that L2-regularization on factorized matrices relates to nuclear norm regularization at global optima, it was unclear whether this equivalence holds during training—particularly for foundation models trained online that never reach convergence. This paper asks: does weight decay on factorized attention matrices induce low-rank pressure early in training, and what are the practical consequences?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes standard transformer training setups (AdamW optimizer, cross-entropy or MSE loss) with a tunable weight decay strength λ applied to factorized attention weight pairs (W_Q, W_K) and (P, W_V), and produces a characterization of how the pseudo-rank (fraction of singular values needed to capture 95% of total nuclear norm) of the products W_K^T W_Q and P W_V evolves during training. Empirically, the paper trains 125M-parameter language models on The Pile, Vision Transformers on ImageNet, and small associative-recall transformers, then analyzes pretrained LLaMA 2 (7B) and ViT weights to check whether the theoretical conditions (A^T A ≈ B^T B) are approximately satisfied in practice.

## Method

The core theoretical result shows that at any stationary point of the L2-regularized loss L(AB^T) + (λ/2)(‖A‖² + ‖B‖²), the condition A^T A = B^T B holds, making the Frobenius norm upper bound on the nuclear norm ‖AB^T‖_* tight; furthermore, this condition is satisfied exponentially quickly during gradient flow (rate 2λ/τ), so the two regularizations become identical long before convergence. A case study on a 2-layer linear network shows that at equilibrium, all singular values of AB^T below λ are pruned to zero, effectively performing a rank-controlled low-rank approximation. Empirically, the paper validates these predictions under AdamW with decoupled weight decay on associative recall, language modeling (The Pile), and image classification (ImageNet), finding that increasing λ for attention layers monotonically reduces the rank of W_K^T W_Q and P W_V, and that decoupling weight decay strength between attention and MLP layers (e.g., λ=0.1 for attention vs. λ=0.25 for MLP) yields better perplexity than uniform decay.

## Results

On the Pile language modeling task (125M parameter Transformer, 10B tokens), the best configuration achieves a test perplexity of 11.52±0.03 (SA-λ=0.1, MLP-λ=0.25), compared to 12.00±0.03 with no weight decay. The key empirical finding is that increasing weight decay on attention layers beyond λ=0.1 degrades performance (e.g., SA-λ=0.25, MLP-λ=0.25 gives 11.58±0.03), while increasing MLP weight decay remains beneficial. The paper further demonstrates that the pseudo-rank of W_K^T W_Q and PW_V decreases monotonically with increasing weight decay strength, and that the gap between the nuclear norm and the Frobenius norm upper bound vanishes exponentially during training, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines but instead ablates the weight decay strength independently for attention (SA) and MLP layers. The central comparison shows that decoupling weight decay between attention and MLP layers is beneficial: the best perplexity (11.52) is achieved with SA-λ=0.1 and MLP-λ=0.25, whereas applying the same high decay to both (SA-λ=0.25, MLP-λ=0.25) yields 11.58, a 0.06 perplexity degradation. Conversely, applying high decay only to attention while keeping MLP decay low (SA-λ=0.25, MLP-λ=0.0) gives 12.02, worse than no decay at all. The paper also validates its theory on pretrained foundation models (LLaMA 2 7B, ViT-Base), showing that the Frobenius norm and nuclear norm of attention matrix products nearly coincide across all layers and heads, consistent with rank-regularizing training dynamics. These findings align with prior work [Sharma et al., 2023] showing that post-training rank reduction of attention matrices hurts downstream performance, while the same operation on MLP matrices improves reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical result of Srebro and Shraibman (2005) that the summed Frobenius norm upper-bounds the nuclear norm of a factorized matrix, and the more recent equivalence between L2-regularized and nuclear-norm-regularized losses at stationary points (Wang and Jacot, 2023; Dai et al., 2021). The key departure is showing that this equivalence holds *exponentially quickly during training*, not merely at convergence, which prior works did not establish. The equilibrium analysis of the 2-layer linear network case study follows the methodology of Saxe et al. (2013) for deep linear networks, adapted to the regularized setting. In the attention-specific context, the paper complements Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) on low-rank bottlenecks from head size and Sharma et al. (2023) on the performance cost of rank reduction, by providing the mechanistic explanation for why weight decay implicitly induces this rank reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that the standard practice of applying uniform weight decay (e.g., λ=0.1) across all transformer layers inadvertently imposes low-rank regularization on attention matrix products, which the paper shows can degrade language model performance. The finding that decoupling weight decay for attention layers from MLPs yields modest but consistent perplexity improvements suggests that layer-specific regularization strategies are worth exploring. Evidence from pretrained LLaMA 2 and ViT weights confirms the effect is already active in widely-used foundation models. Open questions include the optimal per-layer decay schedule, the interaction with in-context learning dynamics, and whether the rank-regularizing effect is ever beneficial for attention (the paper finds a 'sweet spot' around λ=0.1 for self-attention).
