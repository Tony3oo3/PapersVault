## Problem

Standard prediction-powered inference (PPI) combines small human-labeled samples with large autorater-labeled samples to produce tighter confidence intervals for model performance, but it treats the data as homogeneous. In practice, autoraters exhibit heterogeneous performance across subdomains (e.g., more accurate on unambiguous questions than open-ended ones), and unstratified PPI can become barely better than classical inference in such settings. This paper asks how to incorporate stratification into PPI to exploit this heterogeneity and recover substantially tighter confidence intervals.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-defined partition of the input space into K strata with known weights w_k, a small labeled sample S_n of n human-rated examples (split as n_k per stratum), and a large unlabeled sample S̃_N of N autorater predictions (split as N_k per stratum). It outputs coordinate-wise asymptotically valid confidence intervals for the d-dimensional parameter θ* (e.g., mean accuracy), along with stratum-specific tuning parameters λ_k and sample allocation ratios ρ_k. The strata can be defined by autorater prediction values (e.g., equal-mass quantiles of f(X)) or by any other meaningful partition of the input space.

## Method

StratPPI defines a stratified prediction-powered loss L^SPP_λ(θ) = Σ_k w_k L^PP_{k,λ_k}(θ), where each per-stratum term is the standard PPI++ rectified loss computed on that stratum's data with its own tuning parameter λ_k. The key theoretical contribution is proving (Theorem 2) that the minimizer of this stratified loss is asymptotically normal with a covariance matrix Σ_w^λ that depends on stratum-conditional variances, and that Tr(Σ_w^λ) ≤ Tr(Σ^λ) for PPI++ (Proposition 1), so stratification never hurts. The paper further derives closed-form optimal λ_k values (Proposition 2) that generalize PPI++'s single λ, and optimal sample allocation ratios ρ_k ∝ w_k √(Tr(A_w^{-1} V_{k,Δ} A_w^{-1})) (Proposition 3) that allocate more labeled samples to noisier strata. For practical use without knowing the true distribution, a heuristic allocation based on autorater confidence scores approximates the optimal ρ_k while preserving coverage guarantees.

## Results

In simulation studies with heterogeneous strata (differing autorater bias or noise per stratum), StratPPI produces substantially tighter confidence intervals than both PPI++ and classical inference, while PPI++ degrades to being barely more powerful than the classical estimator. On real data, StratPPI improves the percent CI-width reduction over PPI++ from 0.10→0.20 (Seahorse), 0.20→0.30 (AttributedQA), and 0.25→0.35 (Galaxy). Heuristic sample allocation adds a further +10% CI-width reduction on Galaxy. Most strikingly, on Seahorse, StratPPI Heur. with only 300 human ratings achieves approximately the same CI width as the classical estimate using 600 ratings—a 2× effective sample size gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares StratPPI against two baselines: classical inference (using only human labels) and PPI++ [Angelopoulos et al., 2023] (unstratified prediction-powered inference using both human and autorater data). StratPPI dominates PPI++ on all real datasets whenever the autorater exhibits heterogeneity across strata—which is the common practical case—achieving 5–10 percentage points greater CI-width reduction. In the homogeneous simulation setting (left column of Figure 1), StratPPI matches PPI++ exactly (Proposition 1 guarantees no degradation), confirming that stratification is never harmful. PPI++ itself substantially outperforms classical inference in all settings, but its advantage erodes sharply under heterogeneity (e.g., different autorater bias per stratum), where it becomes 'barely more powerful than the classical estimator.' The heuristic allocation strategy (StratPPI Heur.) provides additional gains over proportional allocation (StratPPI Prop.) on heterogeneous datasets, with the largest benefit on Galaxy (+10% CI-width reduction). On Chatbot Arena, where LLM-judge confidence scores are poorly calibrated, the heuristic allocation becomes overly aggressive at larger n, though it still helps at small labeled sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-powered inference (PPI) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023) and its efficient PPI++ variant by introducing stratified sampling, drawing on classical survey design literature (Cochran, Neyman). The key technical contribution is combining PPI++'s rectified loss with the asymptotic theory of weighted M-estimators for stratified samples (Wooldridge, 2001) to derive stratum-specific tuning parameters and optimal sample allocation. It deliberately departs from active statistical inference (Zrnic & Candès, 2024), which adapts labeling per-example, by using a fixed, pre-defined stratification with constant total sample size, and from concurrent Bayesian PPI work (Hofer et al., 2024) by providing frequentist guarantees without priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM evaluation: StratPPI achieves roughly 2× effective sample size on the Seahorse dataset, meaning half the human annotations are needed for the same confidence interval width. A notable warning emerges from the simulations: in heterogeneous settings, unstratified PPI++ can degrade to nearly the same performance as classical inference alone, highlighting a failure mode practitioners may not anticipate. Open questions include extending to post-stratified settings (where human data is already collected), handling miscalibrated autorater confidence scores more robustly (as seen in the Chatbot Arena experiment), and studying what happens when the number of strata K grows with sample size n.
