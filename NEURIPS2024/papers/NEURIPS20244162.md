## Problem

Masked generative models (MGMs) achieve an order-of-magnitude reduction in sampling steps compared to continuous diffusion models, yet still underperform in both quality and diversity at similar model sizes. The key differentiator in continuous diffusion models—guidance techniques like classifier-free guidance and self-attention guidance—has no direct counterpart for MGMs because the VQ token space lacks the continuous semantic structure that makes spatial smoothing (e.g., Gaussian blur) meaningful. This paper addresses that gap by defining a generalized guidance formulation for discrete diffusion and introducing a self-guidance mechanism that operates natively in VQ token space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label and a partially masked sequence of VQGAN tokens (10-bit integers, 256 tokens for 256×256 or 1024 for 512×512) as input, and produces a synthesized RGB image decoded from the predicted VQ tokens. The guidance mechanism doubles the number of forward passes per timestep (NFE goes from 18 to 36 at T=18), as the second pass processes a semantically smoothed version of the input through the TOAST module. Sampling uses high-temperature Gumbel noise (τ=25 at 256×256, τ=45 at 512×512) to preserve diversity while the guidance enhances fine-scale detail.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-guidance sampling method that trains a TOAST module as an information bottleneck performing semantic smoothing in VQ token space, using an auxiliary error-token-correction task where 30% of unmasked tokens are randomly replaced with error tokens and the model learns to correct them. This implicitly teaches the module to remove fine-scale details while preserving coarse structure, serving as a discrete analogue of Gaussian blur in continuous space. At inference, the frozen MaskGIT generator first predicts x̂_{0,t} from x_t, then the TOAST module (fed with a blank canvas to avoid training bias) produces a semantically smoothed prediction x̄_{0,t} from H_φ(x_t), and the two are combined via the generalized discrete guidance equation with scale s. The approach is parameter-efficient (TOAST adds ~20–25% parameters) and requires only 10 epochs of fine-tuning. Combined with high-temperature sampling, this achieves FID 3.22 / IS 263.9 on ImageNet 256×256 with 36 NFEs, outperforming both MaskGIT and Token-Critic while using fewer NFEs than DPC.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the proposed self-guidance (T=18, NFE=36) achieves FID 3.22 and IS 263.9, outperforming MaskGIT (FID 6.56, NFE=18), Token-Critic (FID 4.69, NFE=36), and DPC-full (FID 4.45, NFE=180) while requiring an order of magnitude fewer NFEs than continuous diffusion models. On ImageNet 512×512, it reaches FID 5.57 and IS 233.2 (NFE=36), surpassing DPC-full (FID 6.06, NFE=180) and MaskGIT (FID 8.48, NFE=18). Notably, the method beats LDM-4+CFG (FID 3.60, NFE=500) on 256×256 with only 36 NFEs, and even at T=12 (NFE=24) it achieves FID 3.35, still outperforming MaskGIT at T=18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed self-guidance decisively outperforms all masked generative model baselines on both resolutions: it improves FID from 6.56 to 3.22 over MaskGIT (NFE=18→36), from 4.69 to 3.22 over Token-Critic (same NFE=36), and from 4.45 to 3.22 over DPC-full (NFE=180→36), while also achieving higher IS (263.9 vs. 203.6, 174.5, 244.8 respectively). Against continuous diffusion models, the method beats LDM-4+CFG (FID 3.60, NFE=500) on 256×256 with only 36 NFEs, and surpasses DiT-L/2+CFG (FID 5.02, NFE=500) by a wide margin. On 512×512, it outperforms DPC-full (FID 6.06, NFE=180) and MaskGIT (FID 8.48, NFE=18) while maintaining competitive precision (0.88 vs. 0.80 and 0.78). The method also dominates the IS-vs-FID trade-off curve (Fig. 4), showing that across all sampling temperatures it achieves a better quality-diversity balance than MaskGIT and Token-Critic. The only trade-off is slightly lower recall (0.51 vs. 0.52 for DPC-full on 256×256), but the FID and IS gains are substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-guidance framework from continuous diffusion models (SAG, Hong et al. 2023) to masked generative models operating in VQ token space, where the key challenge is that discrete tokens lack the continuous semantic structure that makes Gaussian blur a natural information bottleneck. It builds directly on MaskGIT as the base generator and on discrete CFG (Tang et al. 2022) for the generalized guidance formulation, but departs from prior MGM sampling improvements (Token-Critic, DPC) that require training an external corrector transformer by instead using a lightweight TOAST module fine-tuned with an error-token-correction auxiliary loss that implicitly learns semantic smoothing. The connection to Vicinal Risk Minimization provides a theoretical justification for why randomly corrupted tokens force the network to smooth fine-scale details while preserving coarse structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving FID 3.22 on ImageNet 256×256 with only 36 NFEs (vs. 500 for LDM+CFG) while maintaining high diversity offers a compelling efficiency-quality trade-off for practitioners who need fast generation. The plug-and-play, parameter-efficient nature (10 epochs, ~20-25% extra parameters) makes the technique broadly applicable to existing MGM checkpoints. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet with a single baseline architecture (MaskGIT), and the authors themselves flag open questions around text-conditional generation, other discrete diffusion formulations, and generalization to video/audio domains. The finding that guidance enables high-temperature sampling (τ=25 vs. the typical τ=4.5) without quality degradation is a useful practical insight for the MGM community.
