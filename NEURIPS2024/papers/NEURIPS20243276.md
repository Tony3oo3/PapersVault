## Problem

Masked (absorbing) diffusion models for discrete data have been hindered by unnecessarily complex ELBO formulations, unclear relationships between score and mean parameterizations, and ad hoc training adjustments that break consistency between forward and reverse processes. These issues have kept discrete diffusion models from being competitive with autoregressive models on important tasks like text and pixel-level image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243276/cropped_page6_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates on sequences of discrete tokens: for text, tokens are drawn from a vocabulary of size m; for images, each pixel is treated as a 256-valued discrete token with no ordinal structure (e.g., 64×64×3 = 12288 tokens per ImageNet image). At each denoising step, the network receives the full partially-masked sequence plus a time embedding and outputs a probability distribution over the m clean token classes at each masked position. For conditional tasks like infilling, conditioning tokens are held fixed (unmasked) throughout generation.

## Method

The key insight is that the continuous-time ELBO for masked diffusion reduces to a simple weighted integral of cross-entropy losses over masked positions, with weight α′_t/(1−α_t), where α_t is the probability a token remains unmasked at time t. The network uses a mean-parameterization, predicting E[x_0|x_t] via a softmax over clean token classes (the mask class is excluded), and the loss requires only a single forward pass per sample—avoiding the N model evaluations or high-variance doubly stochastic estimators needed by prior CTMC-based objectives. The paper further establishes SNR invariance of the loss and unifies prior formulations (Campbell et al., SEDD score-based loss) as special cases or constrained variants. A generalized model (GenMD4) extends the framework to state-dependent masking schedules where each token value i has its own schedule α_{t,i} = 1 − t^{w_i}, learned via REINFORCE leave-one-out gradient estimation, allowing semantically important tokens to be unmasked earlier. Sampling uses ancestral decoding with a cosine discretization grid that slows unmasking at the start of generation, dramatically improving FID over uniform grids.

## Results

On OpenWebText-trained text models, MD4-small achieves zero-shot perplexity of ≤34.94 (WikiText2), ≤102.26 (PTB), ≤35.90 (WikiText103), and ≤68.10 (IBW), outperforming all prior discrete diffusion baselines on all five benchmarks. At GPT-2-medium scale, MD4 reaches ≤25.84 on both WikiText2 and WikiText103, beating GPT-2 on 4 of 5 tasks. For pixel-level image modeling, MD4 achieves 2.75 BPD on CIFAR-10 (surpassing the best AR model Sparse Transformer at 2.80) and 3.40 BPD on ImageNet 64×64 (matching Perceiver AR at 3.40 with a 4× smaller model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD4 outperforms SEDD Absorb (Lou et al., 2024) on all five text benchmarks at small scale (e.g., 34.94 vs. 38.94 on WikiText2) and on 4/5 at medium scale, with the gap widening at larger scale (PTB: 66.07 vs. 87.12). Against GPT-2, MD4-small wins on 4/5 tasks (losing only on LAMBADA: 48.43 vs. 45.04), and MD4-medium similarly beats GPT-2 on 4/5. On CIFAR-10, MD4 achieves 2.75 BPD, surpassing all AR baselines including Sparse Transformer (2.80), PixelSNAIL (2.85), and PixelCNN++ (2.92), as well as prior discrete diffusion methods D3PM Absorb (≤4.40) and Campbell et al. (≤3.52). On ImageNet 64×64, MD4 (198M params) matches Perceiver AR (770M) at 3.40 BPD and beats Routing Transformer (3.43) and Sparse Transformer (3.44). On Text8, MD4 outperforms AO-ARMs (Hoogeboom et al., 2021) and all prior discrete/continuous diffusion models, but is outperformed by the AR transformer and Discrete Flow (Tran et al., 2019), which benefit from a fixed generation order.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked (absorbing) diffusion formulation of Austin et al. (D3PM, NeurIPS 2021) and the continuous-time framework of Song et al. (ICLR 2020) and Kingma et al. (NeurIPS 2021), but its central contribution is a simplification: the continuous-time ELBO for masked diffusion reduces to a weighted integral of cross-entropy losses, in contrast to the more complex CTMC-based objectives of Campbell et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and the score-entropy loss of Lou et al. (SEDD, ICML 2024). The paper unifies these prior formulations, showing that their modifications either require N model evaluations (Campbell et al.), introduce high-variance doubly stochastic estimates, or break forward-reverse consistency (SEDD's unconstrained score parameterization). It further extends the framework with state-dependent masking schedules (GenMD4) and clarifies the connection to any-order autoregressive models (AO-ARMs).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the training objective to a simple weighted cross-entropy, this work removes a major barrier to competitive discrete diffusion modeling, making the approach accessible and stable to train at GPT-2 scale. The practical finding that a cosine sampling grid (not just a cosine schedule) dramatically improves generation quality (FID 70→17 on ImageNet 64×64) is a useful, non-obvious insight for practitioners. Open questions remain: masked diffusion still trails AR models on character-level text (text8), GenMD4's learned schedules can overfit to dataset statistics hurting zero-shot transfer, and inference with state-dependent schedules is more challenging. The broader implication is that discrete diffusion, long considered inferior to AR models for text, may close the gap with the right objective and sampling strategy.
