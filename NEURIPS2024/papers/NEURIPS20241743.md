## Problem

In cross-silo federated learning with business-sector participants, FL-PTs are simultaneously self-interested (leading to free-riding) and competitive (creating conflicts of interest). Prior work such as FedCompetitors addressed only the competition constraint, while coalition-formation methods for data complementarity ignored adversarial relationships. No existing approach guarantees both the absence of free riders and the avoidance of indirect contribution to competitors within a single optimal partition.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted directed benefit graph G_b (computed via a hypernetwork that learns the Pareto front of local losses, where edge weights estimate data complementarity) and an undirected competing graph G_c (reported by FL-PTs to a trusted central server). It outputs a partition of all FL-PTs into disjoint coalitions such that collaboration within each coalition follows G_b, no path in the data-usage graph connects competitors, and the partition is optimal in the sense that no subset of coalitions can merge to increase any member's utility without violating the two principles.

## Method

FedEgoists is a three-phase graph-partitioning algorithm. First, it constructs the inverse competing graph G_c⁻ and finds all maximal cliques via the Bron-Kerbosch algorithm, yielding groups of mutually independent FL-PTs. Second, within each independent group, it computes strongly connected components of the benefit subgraph using Tarjan's algorithm, forming initial coalitions that satisfy both principles. Third, it iteratively merges coalitions through three operations—MergeCycle (cycles containing single-node coalitions), MergePath (simple paths between multi-node coalitions), and MergeNeighbors (adjacent multi-node coalitions)—each time verifying that the merged set remains independent and that all members satisfy the reciprocal-benefit condition. The paper proves that upon termination, no subset of coalitions can be merged to improve any member's utility, establishing optimality. The benefit graph is generated by a hypernetwork (MLP-based) that maps preference vectors to Pareto-optimal hypotheses, with edge weights derived from the optimal preference vectors.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with pathological distribution (α=0.2), FedEgoists achieves 81.30% mean test accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline pFedMe (79.27%) by 2.03 points and FedOra (72.74%) by 8.56 points. Under low competition (α=0.05, PAT), the gap widens to 11.62 points over FedOra (81.35% vs. 69.73%). On the real-world eICU dataset, FedEgoists achieves 74.79% average AUC, surpassing the best baseline FedOra (72.61%) by 2.18 points. Across all competition intensities (α from 0.05 to 0.4) and both data distributions, FedEgoists consistently ranks first among all ten methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedEgoists outperforms all nine baselines across every setting tested. The strongest competing baselines are FedOra and pFedMe: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.2, PAT), FedEgoists (81.30%) beats pFedMe (79.27%) by 2.03 points and FedOra (72.74%) by 8.56 points. Under low competition (α=0.05, PAT), the advantage over FedOra grows to 11.62 points (81.35% vs. 69.73%), while under high competition (α=0.4, PAT) it narrows to 9.65 points (82.54% vs. 72.89%). On the eICU medical dataset, FedEgoists achieves 74.79% average AUC versus 72.61% for FedOra and 72.06% for pFedGraph. The vanilla FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedDisco) perform poorly under high data heterogeneity, often scoring below 60% on CIFAR-10 PAT, while FedEgoists maintains above 80% regardless of competition intensity. In the worst-case analysis (Table 3), FedEgoists' performance gap to the best baseline is within ±3.2 percentage points, confirming robustness across random competing graph realizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in cross-silo federated learning: coalition formation for data complementarity (Cui et al., KDD 2022; Donahue & Kleinberg, AAAI 2021) and conflict-of-interest avoidance among competing participants (Tan et al., AAAI 2024). While FedCompetitors addressed Principle 2 (no contribution to competitors) via a greedy heuristic, and collaboration-equilibrium methods addressed free-riding through core-stability, no prior work simultaneously enforced both principles. The key methodological departure is the use of maximal cliques (Bron-Kerbosch) on the inverse competing graph combined with strongly connected components (Tarjan) on the benefit graph, yielding a partition with a provable optimality guarantee (no subset of coalitions can merge to improve any member's utility) that the earlier heuristic could not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded coalition-formation strategy for cross-silo FL that jointly handles self-interest and business competition, which is the dominant real-world deployment scenario (banks, hospitals, pharma companies). The optimality guarantee (Proposition 2) is a meaningful step beyond the heuristic approaches in prior work. However, the scope is inherently limited to small participant pools (2–100 FL-PTs), the benefit graph depends on a hypernetwork training overhead, and the competing graph is assumed to be accurately reported to a trusted central server—assumptions that may not hold in practice. Open questions include dynamic settings where competitive relationships evolve, scalability beyond the O(3^{n/3}) clique-finding step, and integration with incentive mechanisms that go beyond binary collaboration/non-collaboration.
