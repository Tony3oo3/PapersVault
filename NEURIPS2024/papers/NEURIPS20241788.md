## Problem

Behavior regularization, a widely used offline RL technique (e.g., TD3+BC), penalizes deviation from the behavior policy that collected the dataset, but its performance is strongly contingent on the quality of that reference policy. When offline data originates from suboptimal or multi-modal policies—typical in real-world applications—behavior regularization yields subpar results because it anchors the learned policy to a poor reference. The paper asks whether good reference policies can be automatically discovered from the data to make behavior regularization robust to data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241788/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples sampled from an unknown behavior policy, with mini-batch sampling during training. For continuous control tasks, the actor is a Gaussian policy with learnable mean and fixed standard deviation, and states are normalized (for MuJoCo) or rewards are transformed (for Antmaze/Adroit). The output is a control policy mapping states to action distributions, evaluated by greedy action selection; CPI-RE additionally maintains a second policy network and selects the higher-Q action at evaluation time.

## Method

CPI extends Conservative Policy Iteration (CPI) from online RL to the offline setting by iteratively refining the reference policy used in the KL-regularized policy update: starting from the behavior policy π_D, each step evaluates the current policy's Q-function and then improves the policy by maximizing E[Q] − τ·KL(π‖π_prev), where π_prev is the previous iteration's policy rather than the fixed behavior policy. This guarantees monotone policy improvement while implicitly restricting the policy to the data support, and the paper proves convergence to the in-sample optimal policy in the tabular setting. In practice with function approximation, the original behavior regularization term τ(1−λ)·KL(π‖π_D) is added alongside the iterative reference term τλ·KL(π‖π̄) to prevent out-of-support drift from limited gradient steps. CPI-RE further introduces an ensemble of two reference policies, selecting the higher-Q one at each update to avoid dragging the learning policy down with an inferior reference. The entire method requires only a few lines of code modification on top of TD3+BC, using the same double-Q critic architecture and Gaussian actor.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, CPI achieves a total normalized score of 1761.2 and CPI-RE achieves 1803.9, outperforming all baselines including D-QL (1747.6), POR (1626.8), and IQL (1520.8). On MuJoCo tasks, CPI scores 1193.2 (CPI-RE: 1207.4), slightly trailing EDAC (1243.4) but surpassing all other methods; on Antmaze, CPI-RE (438.8) approaches D-QL (452.5); on Adroit, CPI (151.9) and CPI-RE (157.7) lead all baselines. CPI requires only 8.5 s/epoch and 1.4 GB GPU memory, compared to 39.8 s/epoch for D-QL and 19.6 s/epoch for EDAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPI substantially outperforms TD3+BC (its direct base) across nearly all tasks, e.g., halfcheetah-medium (64.4 vs 48.3), hopper-medium (98.5 vs 59.3), and antmaze-medium-play (82.4 vs 3.0), confirming that iterative reference-policy refinement is the key driver of improvement. On MuJoCo, CPI (1193.2) and CPI-RE (1207.4) trail only EDAC (1243.4) but surpass D-QL (1158.6), IQL (1034.0), and CQL (1052.8); on Adroit, CPI (151.9) and CPI-RE (157.7) lead all baselines including POR (144.5) and D-QL (136.5). On Antmaze, D-QL (452.5) remains the strongest single method, with CPI-RE (438.8) a close second and CPI (416.0) ahead of POR (387.6) and IQL (378.0). In the tabular GridWorld/FourRoom experiments, CPI converges to the in-sample optimal policy (matching InAC) while behavior regularization (BR) degrades with larger τ. CPI also outperforms STR [20] on most MuJoCo tasks (e.g., halfcheetah-medium: 64.4 vs 51.8) and achieves comparable or better results with 4.7× less runtime than D-QL (8.5 vs 39.8 s/epoch) and equal GPU memory (1.4 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

CPI extends the Politex/Conservative Policy Optimization framework (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2019) from online RL to the offline setting, where the key modification is iteratively refining the reference policy used in the KL-regularized policy update rather than fixing it to the behavior policy. Practically, it is built directly on TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), requiring only a one-line code change to replace the static behavior-policy KL term with a KL term against a moving reference policy. The theoretical target—the in-sample optimal policy—connects to prior work by Fujimoto et al. (2019) and InAC (Xiao et al., 2023), but CPI achieves it through a fundamentally different mechanism (iterative policy refinement) rather than explicit support modeling or expectile regression. The closest prior work, STR (Mao et al., 2023), shares the iterative-refinement idea but uses forward KL, leading to a different algorithmic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal, theoretically grounded modification to a widely-used offline RL algorithm (TD3+BC) can recover in-sample optimality guarantees while maintaining low computational cost (8.5 s/epoch, 1.4 GB GPU), making it attractive for practitioners who want robustness without complex architectures. The ablation showing that removing the original behavior-regularization term causes training divergence under function approximation is an important practical caveat: the theoretical guarantee in the tabular setting does not automatically transfer, and the hybrid objective (Eq. 10) is necessary. Open questions include whether the convergence guarantee extends to function approximation, whether the two hyperparameters (τ, λ) can be made adaptive or eliminated, and whether the reference-ensemble trick generalizes beyond two policies.
