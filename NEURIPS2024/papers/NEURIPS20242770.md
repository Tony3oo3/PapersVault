## Problem

While MLLM research has focused heavily on scaling LLM backbones, the design choices for vision encoders remain insufficiently explored and disconnected from the broader visual representation learning literature. Existing benchmarks are dominated by language priors that mask visual grounding deficiencies, and no systematic study has evaluated how different vision architectures (self-supervised, language-supervised, segmentation, depth) affect multimodal performance. This paper addresses both gaps by using MLLM instruction tuning as a unified evaluation protocol for visual representations and by introducing a vision-centric benchmark to properly measure perceptual capabilities.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image at variable resolution (up to 1024px for the ConvNeXt encoder) paired with a free-form text question, and produces a free-form text answer. The image is simultaneously processed by four vision encoders operating at different resolutions (336, 384, 518, and 1024 px), and their heterogeneous feature maps are compressed via the SVA connector into a fixed 576 visual tokens—roughly 1/5 the token count used by competing models like LLaVA-NeXT (2880 tokens)—before being interleaved with text tokens in the LLM.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA), a connector that replaces the standard MLP projector. SVA uses a 2D grid of learnable latent queries where each query token is spatially aligned to a corresponding sub-region across all vision encoder feature maps (spatial inductive bias), performing cross-attention to aggregate features from multiple encoders into a single token. To prevent information loss from this compression, SVA inserts cross-attention blocks at multiple depths within the LLM (multi-layer aggregation), allowing the model to repeatedly access uncompressed visual information. The vision component combines four complementary encoders—OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14@336, SigLIP ViT-SO400M/14@384, OpenCLIP ConvNeXt-XXL@1024, and DINOv2 ViT-L/14@518—leveraging the strengths of language-supervised, high-resolution ConvNet, and self-supervised representations. Training follows a two-stage recipe: connector pre-training on 2.5M adapter data, then joint fine-tuning of connector and LLM on the curated Cambrian-7M dataset (a balanced subset of the ~9.8M Cambrian-10M pool), with the vision encoders unfrozen during instruction tuning.

## Results

Cambrian-1-8B (576 visual tokens) achieves average scores of 73.1 (General), 80.4 (Knowledge), 73.3 (OCR & Chart), and 68.5 (Vision-Centric), outperforming LLaVA-NeXT-8B (72.5/72.8/69.5/56.6) and Mini-Gemini-HD-8B (72.7/75.1/59.1/51.5) across all four categories while using only 1/5 the visual tokens (576 vs. 2880). At 34B scale, Cambrian-1-34B reaches 76.8/85.6/75.6/68.5, surpassing LLaVA-NeXT-34B (76.0/81.8/68.7/64.0) and Mini-Gemini-HD-34B (76.2/77.7/67.6/63.8) on every category. On Vision-Centric benchmarks, Cambrian-1-34B (68.5) is competitive with proprietary models like GPT-4V (62.4) and Grok-1.5 (68.7), while GPT-4V retains a lead on OCR & Chart (78.5 vs. 75.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-NeXT (8B/13B/34B), Cambrian-1 wins on all four benchmark categories at every scale, with the largest margins on Vision-Centric (+11.9 at 8B, +6.3 at 34B) and OCR & Chart (+3.8 at 8B, +6.9 at 34B), despite using 5× fewer visual tokens (576 vs. 2880). Against Mini-Gemini-HD, the advantage is even more pronounced: Cambrian-1-8B leads by +14.2 on OCR & Chart (73.3 vs. 59.1) and +17.0 on Vision-Centric (68.5 vs. 51.5). At 34B, Cambrian-1-34B surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-34B by +3.8 on Knowledge (85.6 vs. 81.8) and +6.9 on OCR & Chart (75.6 vs. 68.7), and beats Mini-Gemini-HD-34B by +7.9 on Knowledge and +8.0 on OCR & Chart. Compared to proprietary models, Cambrian-1-34B matches or exceeds GPT-4V on Knowledge (85.6 vs. 65.2) and Vision-Centric (68.5 vs. 62.4), but trails on OCR & Chart (75.6 vs. 78.5); it is competitive with Grok-1.5 on Vision-Centric (68.5 vs. 68.7) and with Gemini-1.5 Pro on Vision-Centric (68.5 vs. 67.5), though Gemini leads on OCR & Chart (81.3). The SVA connector's spatial inductive bias is the key differentiator, enabling strong high-resolution perception (OCR, spatial reasoning, depth) with a compact 576-token budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cambrian-1 builds directly on the LLaVA two-stage instruction-tuning paradigm (connector pre-training followed by joint fine-tuning) and extends the Q-Former cross-attention mechanism from InstructBLIP into the Spatial Vision Aggregator, which adds spatial inductive bias and multi-layer vision access. It departs from the field's near-exclusive reliance on CLIP-family encoders by systematically evaluating over 20 vision backbones spanning self-supervised (DINOv2, MAE, I-JEPA, MoCo v3), segmentation (SAM), depth (MiDaS), and diffusion (Stable Diffusion) models within a unified MLLM training pipeline. The paper also repurposes classic vision benchmarks (COCO, ADE20K, Omni3D) into a VQA-format evaluation (CV-Bench), bridging the gap between traditional visual representation learning protocols and multimodal assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that several widely-used MLLM benchmarks (MMMU, SQA-I, MathVista, AI2D) show less than 5% performance difference with or without visual input challenges the field's assumption that these benchmarks measure multimodal understanding, and the introduction of CV-Bench (2,638 examples, 3.5× larger than prior vision-centric benchmarks) provides a more reliable evaluation axis. The SVA connector's ability to match or exceed 2,880-token baselines using only 576 tokens offers a practical efficiency win likely to be widely adopted. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning post-training can surpass the supervised fine-tuning ceiling, whether any-resolution strategies can be integrated without sacrificing the token efficiency SVA provides, and whether the remaining gap between self-supervised and language-supervised encoders can be fully closed with further data scaling.
