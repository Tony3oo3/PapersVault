## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., SalUn, ESD) can remove target concepts but significantly degrade text-image alignment on retained concepts. This gap arises because the forgetting and retaining objectives are inherently competitive—naive gradient aggregation advances one at the expense of the other—and because remaining datasets are often constructed with minimal diversity, causing the model to overfit to a narrow subset of the retained distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (EDM on CIFAR-10 or Stable Diffusion 1.4/3) along with two representative datasets: a forgetting set D_f (samples of the target concept to erase) and a remaining set D_r (diverse samples of retained concepts). For text-to-image models, these sets are constructed by using an LLM to generate semantically diverse prompts around the target concept, then removing the concept reference to create paired remaining prompts, which are passed through the model to obtain image-prompt pairs (800 pairs per set in experiments). The output is a modified model that no longer generates the target concept while preserving generation quality and prompt alignment on all other concepts, evaluated via CLIP alignment scores and concept-specific detectors (e.g., Nudenet).

## Method

The core contribution is the restricted gradient, an update direction that guarantees monotonic decrease of both the remaining loss L_r and the forgetting loss L_f at each iteration. When the two gradients conflict (∇L_f · ∇L_r < 0), the restricted gradient is computed by projecting each gradient onto the normal of the other—δ_f* = ∇L_f − (∇L_f·∇L_r/‖∇L_r‖²)∇L_r and δ_r* = ∇L_r − (∇L_f·∇L_r/‖∇L_f‖²)∇L_f—with the parameter update being their sum. The paper rigorously derives this as the solution to a constrained optimization (minimize joint loss subject to each individual loss not increasing), showing it coincides with the gradient surgery procedure from multi-task learning but providing the missing optimality characterization. A complementary data diversification procedure constructs D_r with balanced class coverage for class-conditional models or LLM-generated semantically diverse prompts for text-to-image models, preventing overfitting. A loss truncation parameter α caps the forgetting loss to prevent unbounded maximization during the gradient ascent component.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion nudity removal, RGD achieves a CLIP alignment score of 0.350 on the held-out test set, nearly matching the original SD score of 0.352 (gap of only 0.002), while also reducing detected exposed body parts to zero. For Van Gogh style removal, RGD reaches 0.352 test alignment, slightly exceeding SD's 0.348. On CIFAR-10 class-wise forgetting, RGD achieves perfect unlearning accuracy (UA=1.000) with a remaining accuracy of 0.771 and FID of 6.54, substantially outperforming GradDiff (FID=14.09) and SalUn (UA=0.512, FID=14.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SalUn [Fan et al., 2023], RGD wins decisively on both alignment and forgetting quality: SalUn's test alignment drops to 0.312 (nudity) and 0.280 (artist), a 20× and 11× larger gap from SD respectively, while RGD's gap is only 0.002 and 0.004. Against ESD/ESD-u [Gandikota et al., 2023], RGD reduces the alignment gap from 0.023 to 0.002 on nudity removal and from 0.050 to 0.004 on artist removal, while also achieving zero nudity detections versus ESD's residual detections. Against ESD-x (cross-attention-only variant), RGD matches or slightly exceeds alignment (0.352 vs 0.330 test AS for artist removal) while producing images that better adhere to style instructions. On CIFAR-10, RGD outperforms GradDiff (direct gradient aggregation) by reducing FID from 14.09 to 6.54 and improving remaining accuracy from 0.734 to 0.771, while both achieve perfect UA=1.000; Finetune [Warnecke et al., 2021] retains slightly higher RA (0.791) and lower FID (4.25) but fails to unlearn effectively (UA=0.211).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of approximate machine unlearning for diffusion models, specifically extending the gradient-ascent/descent framework used by ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) and the saliency-map-based neuron selection of SalUn (Fan et al., 2023). Its core technical contribution—the restricted gradient—is shown to be mathematically equivalent to the gradient surgery procedure from multi-task learning (Yu et al., 2020), but the paper provides a rigorous characterization of the constrained objective it optimizes, which the original gradient surgery paper did not. The data diversification component (LLM-generated paired prompts for forgetting/remaining sets) is a practical addition not explored in prior unlearning work, which typically used single-prompt or uniformly sampled retain sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical gap: state-of-the-art unlearning methods significantly degrade text-image alignment (CLIP scores drop by 0.05–0.07 for SalUn), which prior work measuring only FID and forgetting accuracy had overlooked. This matters for real-world deployment where unlearned models must still generate semantically correct images for all other prompts. Open questions include the sensitivity to hyperparameters (λ, α), the ad hoc nature of LLM-based prompt diversification, and whether the restricted gradient approach generalizes to more complex multi-concept unlearning scenarios. The authors also note that strategic sampling of retain sets for stable diffusion remains an underexplored research direction.
