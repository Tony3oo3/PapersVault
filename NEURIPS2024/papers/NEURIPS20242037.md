## Problem

Partially observable RL with privileged information (e.g., simulator states during training) has achieved major empirical successes via expert distillation and asymmetric actor-critic, yet theoretical guarantees for these specific paradigms were largely absent. Prior provable results either studied different algorithmic frameworks or relied on computationally intractable oracles, leaving the soundness and efficiency of the practically-used approaches unexplained. This paper identifies concrete failure modes of both paradigms and establishes conditions under which they achieve polynomial or quasi-polynomial sample and computational complexities.

## Input / Output

At test time, the agent receives only a finite-memory window of observations and actions (Z_h = O^{min{L,h}} × A^{min{L,h}}) and outputs an action distribution; at training time, the full underlying state s_h is additionally available to the critic and belief learner. The method produces two coupled outputs: a partially observable policy π ∈ Π^L mapping finite histories to action distributions, and an approximate belief function b_h^apx: Z_h → Δ(S) that estimates the posterior over the hidden state. The belief error ε_belief enters the final suboptimality bound as H²ε_belief, and the memory length L must scale as Õ(γ^{-4} log(SH/ε)) for γ-observable POMDPs.

## Method

The paper develops two complementary algorithmic frameworks. For expert distillation, it introduces the deterministic filter condition (belief updates map one-hot states to one-hot states), under which the distilled policy class Π^D decomposes into a state-decoding function g_h and a fully-observable expert policy π^E, both learnable in polynomial time via supervised learning on privileged trajectories. For asymmetric actor-critic, it proposes a belief-weighted policy update (a PPO-type rule weighted by learned belief states over s_h) that decouples per-iteration computation from sample collection, enabling polynomial sample complexity while retaining quasi-polynomial time. A key technical component is a belief-learning oracle that uses model truncation—redirecting probability mass from hard-to-visit states—to preserve γ-observability under a misspecified learned model, ensuring filter stability. Both frameworks extend to multi-agent POSGs under CTDE with information sharing, yielding polynomial-sample and (quasi-)polynomial-time guarantees for NE/CCE/CE.

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical complexity guarantees. Under the deterministic filter condition, expert policy distillation achieves both polynomial sample and computational complexity: POLY(H, A, O, S, 1/ε, log(1/δ)) episodes and time (Theorem 4.6). For the more challenging γ-observable POMDP setting, the belief-weighted asymmetric actor-critic achieves polynomial sample complexity POLY(S, H, 1/ε, 1/γ, log(1/δ), O, A) with quasi-polynomial time POLY(S, H, 1/ε, log(1/δ), O^L, A^L) (Theorem 5.3), improving the sample complexity from quasi-polynomial to polynomial compared to prior work without privileged information. In the multi-agent setting, both equilibrium distillation and asymmetric MARL with belief learning achieve polynomial sample and (quasi-)polynomial computational complexities under the CTDE framework. Numerically, the proposed methods converge faster to higher rewards than vanilla asymmetric actor-critic and asymmetric Q-learning across POMDPs of varying sizes (Figure 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two empirical baselines: vanilla asymmetric actor-critic (AAC) [Pinto et al., RSS 2018] and asymmetric Q-learning [Baisero et al., UAI 2022]. Theoretically, the paper proves that vanilla AAC suffers from super-polynomial sample complexity on γ-observable POMDPs (Proposition 3.3), because its per-iteration computation is only O(KH) while requiring K new episodes, coupling sample and computational complexity. The proposed belief-weighted variant decouples these by performing synchronous updates over all finite-memory histories per iteration, reducing sample complexity to polynomial while keeping time quasi-polynomial. Empirically (Figure 2, Table 2), the proposed methods achieve the best final reward with the lowest sample complexity across POMDPs of different sizes, outperforming both vanilla AAC and asymmetric Q-learning in convergence speed and final performance. The paper also contrasts with prior theoretical work [Golowich et al., NeurIPS 2022; STOC 2023], showing that privileged information reduces sample complexity from quasi-polynomial to polynomial for observable POMDPs, while maintaining quasi-polynomial time (which is known to be unimprovable even for planning).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observable POMDP framework (Golowich et al.) and the filter stability concept, but deliberately departs from the closest theoretical prior work on hindsight-observable MDPs (HOMDPs), which required computationally intractable planning oracles. Instead, it formalizes two empirically dominant paradigms—expert distillation (teacher-student learning) and asymmetric actor-critic—and provides the first provable guarantees for them under structural assumptions, introducing the deterministic filter condition as a new, weaker sufficient condition. The multi-agent extension to POSGs with CTDE generalizes the single-agent results to Nash/correlated equilibrium learning, building on the information-sharing framework of Liu and Zhang.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for when and why privileged-information-based RL paradigms succeed or fail in practice, which is critical given their widespread empirical use in robotics and autonomous driving. The negative result showing expert distillation is fundamentally suboptimal even in observable POMDPs (Proposition 3.1) challenges a common assumption in the field and motivates the deterministic filter condition as a practical diagnostic. Key limitations include the quasi-polynomial computational complexity (which is known to be unimprovable for planning) and the reliance on structural assumptions (γ-observability or the deterministic filter condition) that may not hold in all real-world POMDPs. Open questions include whether the belief-weighted actor-critic framework extends to function approximation settings with provable guarantees, and whether the multi-agent results can handle more general information structures beyond the assumed evolution rules.
