## Problem

Existing transcriptome foundation models (TFMs) treat cells as independent samples during pre-training, ignoring the taxonomic lineage relationships between cell types that are well-characterized in cell ontology graphs. This omission prevents TFMs from learning structurally informed cell representations that respect biological hierarchy, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen cell types and tissues.

## Input / Output

The model consumes single-cell gene expression sequences ordered by rank value encoding (context length 2048, 25,424-gene vocabulary) along with cell type labels mapped to identifiers in an undirected cell ontology graph (~2.7k nodes, 3.9k edges from OBO Foundry). It produces a CLS-token cell representation embedding that serves as a general-purpose feature for downstream tasks including cell type classification, zero-shot novel cell type annotation via ontology-guided similarity matching, in-silico knockout-based marker gene scoring, and IC50 drug response prediction through the DeepCDR pipeline.

## Method

scCello is a 6-layer transformer encoder (~10.7M parameters) pre-trained on 22M cells with a three-level objective: (1) gene-level masked gene prediction (BERT-style, 15% mask ratio) to capture co-expression patterns; (2) intra-cellular supervised contrastive loss that pulls cell embeddings toward their cell-type embeddings while repelling others, plus a regularization term constraining the cell-type embedding space to be an affine transform of the cell embedding space; (3) inter-cellular relational alignment loss that uses Personalized PageRank scores on the ontology graph (transformed via log-scaling and discretization into integer similarity levels 1–11) to construct negative sample sets, forcing pairwise cell representation distances to reflect ontology structural similarity. A key design choice is explicitly excluding ancestor cell types (reachable via directed 'is a subtype of' edges) from the negative set to avoid pushing away biologically similar cells. The full loss is L_MGP + L_Inter + L_Intra + L_Reg, optimized jointly with Adam for 40k steps.

## Results

On zero-shot cell type clustering, scCello achieves 0.670 AvgBio on the in-distribution dataset (16.1% improvement over the best baseline Sup at 0.555) and 0.687 average AvgBio across six out-of-distribution datasets (12.1% improvement over Sup at 0.659). In the fine-tuning setting, scCello reaches 0.867 accuracy and 0.511 Macro F1, outperforming the best baseline (MGP+Sup at 0.820 Acc / 0.406 Macro F1) by up to 25.9% in Macro F1. For zero-shot novel cell type classification, scCello achieves 76.8% accuracy on 9 novel cell types and 33.5% on 87 novel types, a significant leap over existing TFMs. scCello accomplishes this with only 10.7M parameters, roughly 60× fewer than UCE (674.7M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scCello consistently outperforms all ontology-agnostic TFMs (Geneformer, scGPT, scTab, UCE) on zero-shot cell type clustering across all seven datasets, with the largest gaps on the unseen-donor OOD setting where scCello achieves 0.608–0.643 AvgBio versus 0.456–0.506 for other methods. Among non-TFM baselines, scCello surpasses Seurat (0.611 OOD Avg.) and scVI (0.613) by ~7–8% on OOD, while Harmony performs worst (0.459) due to over-correction. In the fine-tuning setting (Table 2), scCello (0.867 Acc, 0.511 Macro F1) beats MGP+Sup (0.820/0.406), scTab (0.778/0.373), and scANVI (0.763/0.490). For marker gene prediction (Table 3), scCello (0.743 avg AUROC) edges out MGP+Sup (0.730) and DET (0.702), while Geneformer and scGPT perform near chance (~0.46). On cancer drug response (Table 4), scCello (0.917 PCC) is competitive with UCE (0.922) and scGPT (0.919) but trails them slightly, while still improving 7.4% over the DeepCDR baseline (0.854). Notably, scCello achieves the best average performance rank (1.3) with only 10.7M parameters, compared to UCE's 674.7M and scGPT's 51.3M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BERT-style masked gene prediction paradigm used by Geneformer, scGPT, and scTab by adding two ontology-informed contrastive losses: an intra-cellular coherence loss (supervised contrastive learning on cell type labels) and an inter-cellular relational alignment loss that uses Personalized PageRank on the cell ontology graph to define structural similarity targets. Unlike prior TFMs that treat cells as independent samples, scCello explicitly encodes the taxonomic hierarchy from the Open Biological and Biomedical Ontology Foundry into the pre-training objective, positioning itself as a knowledge-informed alternative to purely data-driven scaling approaches like UCE (674M params) or scFoundation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a 10.7M-parameter model with ontology priors can match or exceed 5–60× larger TFMs across cell type clustering, novel cell type annotation, marker gene prediction, and drug response tasks, offering a more accessible pre-training recipe for the single-cell genomics community. Open questions include how to handle the constantly evolving ontology without full retraining (continual learning), whether scaling the model size further would compound the ontology benefit, and the acknowledged caveat that the in-silico knockout approach for marker gene prediction also flags housekeeping genes and master regulators. The approach also raises broader questions about how effectively structured biological priors can substitute for raw parameter scale in foundation models.
