## Problem

Predicting protein-nucleic acid interactions for aptamer screening is hindered by the scarcity of labeled binding data from costly SELEX assays, and existing equivariant geometric models either incur high computational overhead from spherical harmonics or under-exploit geometric information by merely concatenating coordinates with node features. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing an equivariant Transformer that deeply integrates frame averaging into the architecture and by reformulating unsupervised aptamer screening as a contact map prediction task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D coordinates of Cα (protein) or C3 (nucleotide) atoms, sequence-derived node embeddings (ESM2 for proteins, RNA-FM for RNA, one-hot for DNA), and learned edge representations as input, with attention restricted to K-nearest neighbors within a distance cutoff. It outputs a binary contact map over all residue–nucleotide pairs; the maximum predicted contact probability across all pairs is then extracted as a scalar binding affinity score used to rank aptamer candidates in an unsupervised manner. Validation and test structures are generated by ESMFold or RoseTTAFoldNA rather than taken from crystal structures, reflecting a more realistic unbound-structure scenario.

## Method

FAFormer instantiates frame averaging (FA) as an integral geometric module within each Transformer layer rather than as an external equivariant wrapper, enabling coordinates to be encoded in the latent space while preserving spatial semantics. Each layer comprises three FA-based components: a Local Frame Edge Module that builds PCA-derived frames around each node's immediate neighbors to encode direction-dependent pairwise interactions; a Biased MLP Attention Module that injects edge representations as scalar bias terms into attention scores and equivariantly updates coordinates via multi-head FA aggregation with a learned gate; and a Global Frame FFN that concatenates FA-invariant coordinate encodings of all nodes into the feed-forward path to capture long-range geometric context. A generalized FA Linear module (averaging linear projections across 8 PCA frames) serves as the invariant building block throughout. The model is trained with binary cross-entropy on contact map prediction across protein–RNA, protein–DNA, and protein–protein complexes, and the resulting contact probabilities are directly repurposed as unsupervised binding scores for zero-shot aptamer screening without any target-specific labels.

## Results

On contact map prediction, FAFormer achieves the best F1 and PRAUC across all three protein complex datasets: Protein-RNA (F1=0.128, PRAUC=0.111), Protein-DNA (F1=0.146, PRAUC=0.128), and Protein-Protein (F1=0.160, PRAUC=0.146), yielding over 10% relative improvement over the strongest baseline in each setting. On unsupervised aptamer screening across five targets, FAFormer attains the highest Top-10 precision on 4 of 5 targets (e.g., 0.400 on GFP, 0.333 on HNRNPC) and the best PRAUC on all five. Inference is 20–30× faster than RoseTTAFoldNA (32.65 s vs. 0.175 h per protein-DNA case).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAFormer outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric in the contact map prediction task. The closest competitors are FA (external frame-averaging wrapper) on Protein-RNA/DNA (F1 0.115/0.128 vs. 0.128/0.146) and EGNN on Protein-Protein (F1 0.146 vs. 0.160), confirming that integrating FA *within* the Transformer yields a 10–14% relative gain over using it as an external wrapper. Spherical-harmonics models (SE(3)Transformer, Equiformer) consistently underperform, with SE(3)Transformer scoring the lowest F1 on Protein-RNA (0.082) and Protein-DNA (0.082), likely due to overfitting on crystal structures. The vanilla Transformer (no 3D structure) is competitive on Protein-DNA (F1 0.096) but lags significantly on Protein-Protein (F1 0.076). On binding-site prediction, FAFormer beats GraphBind (F1 0.506 vs. 0.492 on Protein-DNA) and GraphSite (F1 0.472 vs. 0.400 on Protein-RNA). Against RoseTTAFoldNA, FAFormer achieves comparable contact-map F1 (0.103 vs. 0.087 on Protein-DNA) but superior aptamer screening (e.g., PRAUC 0.178 vs. 0.145 on HNRNPC) at 20–30× lower inference cost; AlphaFold3, tested on two targets, is outperformed by FAFormer on all screening metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAFormer departs from the standard Frame Averaging (FA) paradigm of Puny et al., which treats FA as an external encoder wrapper, by instantiating FA as an integral geometric module within each Transformer layer (Local Frame Edge Module, Biased MLP Attention, Global Frame FFN). This positions the work between spherical harmonics-based equivariant Transformers (Equiformer, SE(3)Transformer), which suffer from high computational overhead due to irreducible representations, and GNN-based equivariant models (EGNN, GVP-GNN), which lack the long-range attention mechanism. On the application side, the paper extends the unsupervised protein-protein contact map prediction paradigm of Humphreys et al. to protein-nucleic acid complexes and repurposes the predicted contact maps as a binding affinity proxy for aptamer screening, a use case the authors note has not been thoroughly explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–30× inference speedup over RoseTTAFoldNA while matching or exceeding its aptamer screening performance makes large-scale virtual screening of nucleic acid candidates practically feasible without expensive MSA searches or server-based structure prediction. The finding that a small task-specific model can outperform large pretrained structure prediction models (RoseTTAFoldNA, AlphaFold3) on screening despite comparable contact prediction accuracy suggests that the structure prediction objective may be misaligned with the screening task, opening questions about what intermediate representations best encode binding affinity. Limitations include reliance on only Cα/C3 coordinates (no backbone or sidechain features), evaluation on relatively small datasets, and the untested generalization of the contact-map-as-affinity proxy to other modalities such as protein–small molecule or antibody–antigen interactions.
