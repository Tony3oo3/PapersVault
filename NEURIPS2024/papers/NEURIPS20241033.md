## Problem

Standard domain adaptation methods for EEG fail when distribution shifts occur simultaneously in both the input data (spatial covariance matrices) and the target variable (e.g., age), as is common across multi-site recordings. Existing Riemannian re-centering approaches fully transport each domain to the identity, which corrects X-shift but discards the y-shift signal that the regression model needs. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a method that handles joint (X, y) shifts in a multi-source, test-time setting on the SPD manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-participant 19×19 SPD covariance matrices (49 frequency bins per recording, derived from 1.17–19.14 Hz cross-spectra via Bartlett estimation, CAR re-referencing, and GSF correction) from K labeled source domains, plus the scalar mean outcome ȳ_T of the unlabeled target domain. It outputs per-participant regression predictions (age) for the target domain and a learned scalar parallel-transport parameter α_T ∈ [0,1] that quantifies how far the target domain is shifted along the geodesic from its Riemannian mean toward the identity. The requirement of ȳ_T at test time mirrors the structure of mixed-effects models and is estimated from a held-out 50% split of target data in the benchmarks.

## Method

GOPSA formulates a Riemannian mixed-effects model with two coupled components: (i) a domain-specific geodesic intercept, parameterized as α_k = σ(γ_k) ∈ (0,1), that controls how far each domain's covariance matrices are parallel-transported along the geodesic from their Riemannian mean toward the identity before logarithmic mapping, and (ii) a shared Ridge regression coefficient vector β_S over the vectorized tangent-space features. At train time, γ_S and β_S are optimized jointly by gradient descent (L-BFGS with automatic differentiation through the closed-form Ridge solution) on the combined squared loss across all source domains. At test time, the regression coefficients are frozen and only the single scalar γ_T is optimized to minimize the squared difference between the target mean prediction and the known ȳ_T, after which predictions are produced by applying the frozen β_S to the partially-transported target features. The key insight is that partial transport (α < 1) preserves the log-linear association between X and y that full re-centering (α = 1) would erase, while still reducing inter-domain X variability; the learned α values exhibit a near-perfect linear relationship with site mean age (R² = 0.99), confirming their role as a geodesic intercept.

## Results

On the HarMNqEEG dataset (14 sites, 1564 participants), GOPSA achieves the best mean performance across all three regression metrics: Spearman's ρ = 0.77±0.02, R² = 0.59±0.04, and MAE = 8.37±0.32 years. GOPSA significantly outperforms the strongest baseline (DO Intercept) in five site combinations for MAE, four for Spearman's ρ, and three for R² score (corrected t-test, p<0.05). On simulated data with joint (X,y) shifts, GOPSA is the only method that maintains high R² as shift amplitude ξ increases, while Re-center and No DA degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOPSA outperforms the Re-center baseline [62] (full parallel transport to identity) by a wide margin on all metrics (e.g., R²: 0.59 vs. −0.35; MAE: 8.37 vs. 16.97), confirming that forcing all domains to the identity destroys the y-shift signal. Against the closest competitor DO Intercept (per-domain intercept correction), GOPSA wins on MAE (8.37 vs. 8.64) and Spearman's ρ (0.77 vs. 0.75) on average, with statistically significant gains in 4–5 out of 5 site combinations per metric. The deep-learning GREEN model [40] achieves competitive Spearman's ρ (0.74) but shows high variance (R² std = 0.19) and lacks a dedicated adaptation layer, making it less reliable across site combinations. No DA (no re-centering) performs poorly (R² = 0.21), demonstrating that cross-site covariance shifts must be addressed. On simulated data with joint (X,y) shifts, GOPSA is the only method that tracks the true log-linear relationship as shift amplitude increases, while Re-center and No DA collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOPSA extends the Riemannian re-centering framework of Zanini et al. (2018), which parallel-transports each domain's covariance matrices fully to the identity (α=1), by learning a domain-specific partial transport parameter α∈[0,1] along the geodesic between the domain mean and the identity. The base regression pipeline (Riemannian logarithmic mapping + Ridge regression on SPD matrices) follows Sabbagh et al. (2019) and Mellot et al. (2023), but GOPSA departs from these by jointly optimizing the transport parameter and regression coefficients rather than fixing the re-centering a priori. The method is positioned as a Riemannian mixed-effects model where α plays the role of a geodesic intercept, distinguishing it from prior Riemannian mixed-effects models where the manifold-valued variable is the response rather than the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOPSA addresses a practically important gap: existing Riemannian DA methods for EEG handle shifts in X (covariance matrices) but discard or mis-handle simultaneous shifts in the outcome variable y, which is common in multicenter studies with heterogeneous demographics. The test-time adaptation property (only learning one scalar α per new target domain) makes it lightweight for deployment in multicenter clinical trials. Key limitations include the requirement to know the target domain mean ŷ, the restriction to linear (Ridge) regression, and the fact that significant gains over the DO Intercept baseline were observed in only a subset of site combinations, suggesting not all sites exhibit joint (X,y) shifts. The near-perfect linear relationship between learned α and site mean age (R²=0.99) provides an interpretable diagnostic for detecting predictive shifts.
