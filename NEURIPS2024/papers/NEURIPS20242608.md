## Problem

While the Bayes-optimal test error for learning extensive-width neural networks from linearly many samples was recently shown to be no better than linear regression, the regime of quadratically many samples—where non-trivial learning of the target function becomes possible—remained theoretically unsolved. This paper closes that gap by deriving a closed-form expression for the Bayes-optimal MMSE of a single hidden layer network with quadratic activation in the joint limit d→∞, m=κd, n=αd².

## Input / Output

The method takes n=αd² i.i.d. Gaussian input vectors x_i∈R^d and scalar output labels y_i generated by a one-hidden-layer teacher network with m=κd hidden units, quadratic activation, and i.i.d. Gaussian teacher weights. It produces a closed-form MMSE expression (eq. 7–8) parameterized by α and κ, a perfect-recovery threshold α_PR, and a polynomial-time algorithm (GAMP-RIE) that outputs an estimate of the symmetric matrix S*=(1/m)Σ w_k* w_k*^T and hence a test-label prediction. The unusual aspect is the double high-dimensional scaling: both the network width and the sample count grow with d, making the problem a matrix estimation task rather than a standard vector regression.

## Method

The key insight is reducing the neural network learning problem to estimating a Wishart-distributed symmetric matrix S* from linear observations Tr[Z_i S*] plus noise, where Z_i=(x_i x_i^T − I)/√d. The Bayes-optimal MMSE is then derived via a three-step argument: (i) a universality conjecture replacing Z_i with GOE matrices, (ii) a matrix generalized linear model with a Wishart prior whose free entropy is characterized by a sup-inf variational formula, and (iii) the extensive-rank matrix denoising free entropy, which involves the free convolution of the Marchenko-Pastur and semicircle laws and the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber integral. The resulting MMSE is given by a single scalar fixed-point equation (eq. 8) involving the cubic moment of the free-convolved density. The GAMP-RIE algorithm achieves this bound by combining standard GAMP updates for the observation channel with a rotationally-invariant estimator (RIE) as the prior denoiser, and its state evolution provably converges to the Bayes-optimal fixed point.

## Results

The paper derives a closed-form expression for the Bayes-optimal MMSE (eq. 7): MMSE = 2ακ/q̂ − κΔ̃/2, where q̂ solves a fixed-point equation involving the free convolution of the Marchenko-Pastur and semicircle laws. In the noiseless case (Δ=0), perfect recovery (zero test error) is achievable above the threshold α_PR = κ − κ²/2 for κ ≤ 1 and α_PR = 1/2 for κ ≥ 1, matching a naive degrees-of-freedom count. Empirically, the GAMP-RIE algorithm matches the Bayes-optimal MMSE at moderate sizes (d=100, 200) in both noiseless and noisy regimes, while single-initialization gradient descent in the noiseless case yields approximately twice the Bayes-optimal error, and averaging over initializations (AGD) recovers the Bayes-optimal value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines but instead contrasts four estimators: the Bayes-optimal (BO) estimator, the GAMP-RIE algorithm, single-initialization gradient descent (GD), and initialization-averaged gradient descent (AGD). GAMP-RIE asymptotically achieves the Bayes-optimal MMSE across all tested (α, κ) regimes, confirming no computational-to-statistical gap for this extensive-width quadratic-activation problem. In the noiseless case (Δ=0), GD from a single random initialization consistently yields an MSE approximately 2× the Bayes-optimal MMSE, a relationship analogous to the Gibbs-vs-Bayes-optimal gap in high-dimensional GLMs. Averaging GD over initializations (AGD) closes this gap and matches the theoretical MMSE in the noiseless setting. In the noisy case (√Δ=0.25), a gap between AGD and the Bayes-optimal MMSE persists at moderate sample complexity, and for α ≳ 0.3 all GD initializations converge to the same solution (landscape trivialization), causing GD and AGD curves to collapse. The landscape trivialization threshold α_T(Δ) decreases with noise intensity, reaching a plateau at α_T(Δ→∞) ≈ 0.2 for κ=0.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student program for neural network learning (Gardner & Derrida, Sompolinsky et al.) from the well-studied regime of m=O(1) hidden units with n=O(d) samples to the much harder regime of extensive width m=κd with n=αd² samples. It builds directly on Cui et al. [2023], who showed that with only linearly many samples, linear regression is already Bayes-optimal for extensive-width networks, and identified the quadratic-samples regime as an open challenge. The technical derivation combines three previously separate threads: single-layer NN analysis via the replica method (Barbier et al. 2019), extensive-rank matrix denoising with rotationally-invariant estimators (Bun et al. 2016, Maillard et al. 2022b), and the ellipsoid fitting problem (Maillard & Bandeira 2023, Maillard & Kunisky 2024), unified through a reduction of the learning task to a matrix estimation problem with a Wishart prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first closed-form Bayes-optimal error for learning an extensive-width neural network from quadratically many samples, resolving a challenge explicitly flagged as open by Cui et al. [2023]. The GAMP-RIE algorithm provides a polynomial-time procedure that provably achieves this optimal error, and the perfect-recovery threshold matches a naive degree-of-freedom count, lending interpretability. Key limitations restrict the result to quadratic activations, a single hidden layer, and Gaussian data/weights; extending to ReLU or sigmoid activations, multiple layers, and non-Gaussian priors remains open. The empirical observation that randomly-initialized GD samples the posterior (yielding exactly 2× MMSE) and that averaging over initializations recovers the MMSE is intriguing but entirely unproven, and the landscape-trivialization threshold in the noisy case lacks analytical characterization.
