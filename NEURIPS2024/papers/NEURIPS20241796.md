## Problem

Conventional autoregressive image generation relies on vector-quantized (discrete) tokenizers, which are difficult to train, sensitive to gradient approximation, and suffer from reconstruction quality loss. This paper challenges the assumption that autoregressive modeling inherently requires a discrete-valued space, asking whether the per-token probability distribution can be modeled without vector quantization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet class label as conditioning and generates 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. It operates on a sequence of continuous-valued latent tokens (e.g., 16×16 or 32×32 tokens) produced by a pretrained non-quantized tokenizer (KL-16 from LDM), where each token is a d-dimensional vector rather than a discrete index. At inference, the autoregressive Transformer predicts a conditioning vector per token position, and a small diffusion sampler decodes each into a continuous token that is finally decoded to pixels by the tokenizer's decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is the Diffusion Loss, which replaces categorical cross-entropy with a per-token denoising diffusion criterion: the autoregressive model outputs a conditioning vector z, and a small MLP (3–12 residual blocks, 1024–1536 width) learns to predict the noise added to the ground-truth continuous token x at a random timestep t, trained jointly with the Transformer via backpropagation. The paper further unifies standard causal AR and masked generative models (MaskGIT, MAGE) into a Masked Autoregressive (MAR) framework that uses bidirectional attention with MAE-style encoder-decoder and random-order masking to predict multiple tokens simultaneously per step. Unlike latent diffusion models (e.g., DiT) where the diffusion process models the joint distribution over all tokens through the full Transformer, here the diffusion operates only on a small per-token MLP, while the Transformer handles inter-token dependencies via autoregression. This separation yields a favorable speed/accuracy trade-off, generating images in under 0.3 seconds with FID < 2.0 on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MAR-H with Diffusion Loss (943M params) achieves a FID of 1.55 (with CFG) and 2.35 (without CFG), with IS of 303.7 and 227.8 respectively. The smaller MAR-L (479M) reaches FID 1.78 (w/ CFG) and 2.60 (w/o CFG). The method also offers a strong speed/accuracy trade-off: MAR with Diffusion Loss can generate an image in under 0.3 seconds while maintaining FID below 2.0, outperforming DiT-XL/2 on both speed and quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAR-H with Diffusion Loss (943M) achieves the best FID of 1.55 (w/ CFG) among all token-based methods, outperforming MAGVIT-v2 (1.78), GIVT (3.35), and LDM-4 (3.60) by substantial margins. Against the leading continuous-token diffusion baselines, MAR-H beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) by 0.72 FID and DiffiT (1.73) by 0.18 FID, while slightly outperforming MDTv2-XL/2 (1.58) by 0.03 FID. The smaller MAR-L (479M) already surpasses DiT-XL/2 (675M) by 0.49 FID with fewer parameters. In the speed/accuracy trade-off (Figure 6), MAR with Diffusion Loss dominates DiT-XL/2 across the entire curve, achieving FID < 2.0 at < 0.3 s/image. Among vector-quantized token methods, MAR-H improves over MaskGIT (6.18 w/o CFG) and MAGE (6.93 w/o CFG) by more than 3 FID points without CFG, and over MAGVIT-v2 (3.65 w/o CFG) by 1.3 FID points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in autoregressive image generation, which couples sequence models with vector-quantized tokenizers (VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN) and categorical cross-entropy loss. It repurposes the denoising diffusion process from DDPM/Improved DDPM not for modeling the joint distribution of all tokens (as in latent diffusion models like LDM or DiT), but as a per-token distribution head—a small MLP that models p(x|z) for each individual token. The masked autoregressive (MAR) framework unifies standard causal AR and masked generative models (MaskGIT, MAGE) under a single 'next set-of-tokens prediction' formulation, borrowing the bidirectional encoder-decoder structure from MAE. The closest prior work, GIVT, also uses continuous-valued tokens but relies on Gaussian mixture models with a fixed number of components, whereas this paper's diffusion-based loss can represent arbitrary distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating vector quantization, this work removes a notoriously difficult and quality-limiting component of autoregressive image generation, enabling the use of higher-fidelity continuous tokenizers (e.g., KL-regularized VAEs) that were previously incompatible with sequence models. The practical impact is significant: MAR with Diffusion Loss achieves FID < 2.0 at < 0.3 seconds per image on ImageNet 256×256, outperforming both VQ-based AR systems and latent diffusion models like DiT in the speed/accuracy trade-off. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger resolutions and more diverse datasets beyond ImageNet, whether jointly training the tokenizer and generator (rather than relying on pre-trained tokenizers) further improves quality, and whether the Diffusion Loss generalizes to other continuous-valued domains such as audio or video generation.
