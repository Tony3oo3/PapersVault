## Problem

MLLM-based referring expression generation faces a fundamental trade-off: precise, unambiguous object descriptions require more detailed (longer) sentences, but increased granularity raises the probability of attribute- and object-level hallucinations. The paper identifies that intermediate transformer layers in well-trained MLLMs contain richer region-level descriptive information than the final layer, yet standard autoregressive decoding only reads the final layer's logits, discarding this latent knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243832/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary region mask (the target object) as input; the mask is encoded by a region encoder (CNN-based extractor or ROI pooling) into region prompt tokens that are concatenated with visual tokens and instruction text to form the multi-modal input sequence. It produces a free-form natural language description of the target region. The entire framework is training-free, operating purely at inference time on a pre-trained MLLM (Osprey-7b, 32 layers) with no fine-tuning or additional data.

## Method

The 'unleash-then-eliminate' framework first applies contrastive decoding (subtracting intermediate-layer logits from final-layer logits, following DoLa) across a set of candidate layers to surface latent region descriptions, yielding one candidate sentence per layer. It then ranks candidates via cycle-consistency: each candidate sentence is fed to a RES model (GLaMM) to predict a segmentation mask, and the IoU between the predicted mask and the ground-truth mask—weighted by SAM's confidence score—selects the best sentence. To eliminate the per-token RES inference cost, the paper introduces Probing-based Importance Estimation, which statistically counts how often each layer wins the cycle-consistency ranking on a small probing subset to obtain prior importance weights, then combines these with per-sample Jensen-Shannon divergence between each candidate layer's contrastive logits and the final layer's logits via a softmax-normalized hybrid score to select the best layer for each token prediction, enabling RES-free decoding with comparable hallucination reduction.

## Results

On RefCOCOg, the proposed unleash-then-eliminate framework (using 1/8 probing subset) achieves a METEOR score of 172.0 and nCHAIR_S of 1.84, outperforming both the Osprey-7b baseline (METEOR 162.0, nCHAIR_S 3.27) and DoLa (METEOR 168.0, nCHAIR_S 1.88). With full cycle-consistency ranking, METEOR reaches 173.0 with nCHAIR_S of 1.83 and nCHAIR_I of 1.35, while generating sentences averaging 23.16 words (vs. 7.15 for Osprey-7b). On the PHD hallucination benchmark, the method achieves 70.88% average accuracy in Neutral mode and 68.69% in Misleading mode, surpassing DoLa (70.27% / 67.27%) and Osprey-7b at t=0.2 (69.91% / 67.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Osprey-7b (the base MLLM REG model), the proposed method substantially improves METEOR (172–173 vs. 162) and recall (0.82 vs. 0.76) while generating roughly 3× longer sentences (23 vs. 7 words). The raw CHAIR_S/CHAIR_I scores appear worse for the proposed method (42.25/30.95 vs. 23.41/20.81), but this is an artifact of sentence length; the length-normalized nCHAIR_S drops from 3.27 to 1.84, confirming a genuine hallucination reduction. Against DoLa (the contrastive-decoding baseline), the proposed method wins on METEOR (172–173 vs. 168), nCHAIR_S (1.83–1.84 vs. 1.88), nCHAIR_I (1.35 vs. 1.38), and recall (0.82 vs. 0.82), with the gap widening on the PHD benchmark where the method gains +0.61% (Neutral) and +1.42% (Misleading) over DoLa. The 1/8 probing-subset variant matches the full cycle-consistency ranking variant within 1 METEOR point while eliminating the need for the RES model at inference time, making it the more practical deployment option.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts DoLa's layer-contrastive decoding (Chuang et al., 2023) and the broader contrastive decoding framework (Li et al., 2022) from LLM factuality to the MLLM-based referring expression generation setting, where the key insight is that intermediate transformer layers in region-aware MLLMs (e.g., Osprey-7b) can produce more descriptive, region-specific text than the final layer due to non-monotonic multi-modal alignment. It departs from prior MLLM hallucination mitigation approaches (Opera, VCD, Woodpecker) that rely on external expert models or visual contrastive decoding by operating entirely within the MLLM's own intermediate activations. The cycle-consistency ranking (REG output → RES model → IoU scoring) is a novel exploitation of the dual-task relationship between referring expression generation and segmentation, and the Probing-based Importance Estimation provides a practical efficiency mechanism absent from prior contrastive decoding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free 'unleash-then-eliminate' framework offers a practical path to richer, less hallucinated region descriptions without retraining, and the finding that layer importance weights transfer across datasets (RefCOCOg → PHD) suggests a cold-start mechanism for new deployment environments. However, the approach is validated only on Osprey-7b and depends on the quality of an auxiliary RES model (GLaMM), limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the non-monotonic intermediate-layer phenomenon holds across different MLLM architectures and scales, whether the probing-based estimation can be made more robust to subset sampling variance (as acknowledged in the ablations), and whether the nCHAIR normalization, while methodologically sound, will be adopted as a standard metric.
