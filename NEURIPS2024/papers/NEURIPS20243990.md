## Problem

Existing cross-modal knowledge distillation methods for 3D perception are tightly coupled to specific student modalities, task heads, or network architectures, limiting their versatility across the diverse landscape of BEV-based perception algorithms. Additionally, high-accuracy fusion teachers like BEVFusion over-rely on LiDAR-specific information rather than modality-general features, which exacerbates the modality gap and reduces KD effectiveness for single-modal students.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The teacher model consumes both a 32-beam LiDAR point cloud and six monocular camera images (704×256), projecting them into a shared 180×180 BEV grid at both low-level and high-level feature resolutions. The student model receives only one modality (LiDAR or camera) and produces either 3D object bounding boxes (via dense or DETR heads) or BEV map segmentation masks across six background classes. The distillation signal operates exclusively on BEV feature maps, making it agnostic to the student's specific head architecture or downstream task.

## Method

VeXKD replaces BEVFusion's simple concatenation-based fusion with a Modality-General Fusion Module (MGFM) that uses symmetric deformable cross-modal attention, forcing equitable information extraction from both LiDAR and camera features to produce modality-general teacher representations. The learned dense BEV queries from this fusion process serve as byproducts that initialize a mask generation network, which produces task- and level-specific spatial masks via deformable cross-attention with the teacher's BEV features. These learned masks are applied to both teacher and student feature maps before computing an Attention Transfer distillation loss, enabling selective transfer of only beneficial spatial information while filtering out noisy or modality-specific regions. The framework is applied jointly to low-level (high spatial granularity) and high-level (rich semantic) BEV features, and the mask generation loss combines a masked task loss with the KD loss to prevent degenerate all-ones masks.

## Results

On nuScenes, VeXKD achieves up to 6.9% mAP and 4.2% NDS gains for 3D object detection (BEVDet-R50 camera student: 28.9→35.8 mAP, 38.4→42.6 NDS) and up to 4.3% mIoU improvement for BEV map segmentation (BEVDet-R50: 56.4→60.7 mIoU). For the LiDAR student (CenterPoint), VeXKD reaches 65.1 mAP / 70.5 NDS (test), approaching the multi-modal BEVFusion (70.2 mAP / 72.9 NDS) while maintaining 11.3 FPS. The BEVFormer-S camera student reaches 42.5 mAP / 48.3 NDS (test) and 64.2 mIoU, surpassing all prior cross-modal KD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeXKD consistently outperforms all prior cross-modal KD baselines: over UniDistill [68] by +1.2 mAP / +0.4 NDS (LiDAR student) and +6.2 mAP / +3.3 NDS (BEVDet-R50 camera student), and over BEVDistill [7] by +2.6 mAP / +2.0 NDS on BEVFormer-S. Against S2M2-SSD [64], VeXKD gains +1.5 mAP / +0.9 NDS on the LiDAR student. Compared to the vision-centric VCD [17] on BEVDet4D-Depth, VeXKD achieves a marginal +0.2 mAP advantage but a −0.5 NDS deficit, which the authors attribute to VCD's fine-grained trajectory-based distillation benefiting velocity prediction. Against multi-modal fusion references (BEVFusion: 70.2 mAP / 72.9 NDS; TransFusion: 68.9 mAP / 71.6 NDS), VeXKD narrows the gap to ~5 mAP for the LiDAR student and ~34 mAP for the lightweight BEVDet-R50 camera student, while preserving 5–14× higher inference speed (11.3–14.0 FPS vs. 1.8–2.3 FPS). For BEV segmentation, VeXKD surpasses the BEVFusion-L LiDAR baseline (48.6 mIoU) by +3.5 mIoU and the BEVDet-R50 camera baseline by +4.3 mIoU, validating task-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BEV-based 3D perception paradigm (BEVFusion, BEVFormer) and the cross-modal knowledge distillation line (UniDistill, BEVDistill), but departs from both in a key way: it integrates fusion and KD into a single framework rather than treating them as separate stages. Prior cross-modal KD work typically used high-accuracy fusion models (e.g., BEVFusion) as teachers, but this paper argues those models over-rely on LiDAR-specific information, making them poor teachers. Instead, it introduces a Modality-General Fusion Module (MGFM) using symmetric deformable cross-modal attention, and leverages the learned BEV queries as a byproduct to drive a data-driven spatial mask generation network for feature distillation, replacing ground-truth-based masking strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's task- and modality-agnostic design (validated on both LiDAR and camera students, across detection and segmentation) addresses a real practical gap: prior 3D perception KD methods were tightly coupled to specific architectures and single tasks. The learned spatial masks offer a principled alternative to heuristic masking (Gaussian, key-point sampling) that generalizes to dense tasks like segmentation. However, the work is limited to nuScenes, lacks theoretical grounding for why modality-general information transfers better, and leaves open questions about radar modalities, motion prediction, and explicit temporal knowledge transfer. The finding that a modality-general teacher outperforms a higher-accuracy but modality-specific one, while consistent with Xue et al.'s hypothesis, is now empirically confirmed in a practical 3D perception setting.
