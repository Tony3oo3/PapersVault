## Problem

Existing visual object tracking methods face a fundamental trade-off: image-feature regression relies heavily on template-search matching and cannot exploit positional priors, while autoregressive coordinate prediction is limited to bounding boxes seen during training, degrading on unseen data. Directly applying diffusion-model denoising to tracking would require multiple forward passes, making real-time inference infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242874/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a template image (128×128 or 192×192, cropped from a previous frame), a search region image (256×256 or 384×384, cropped from the current frame), and a noisy 4-element bounding box vector (x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂) that is mapped to a 4×C embedding via word embedding (800 or 1200 bins). At inference, the noisy box is initialized from the previous frame's prediction, and the model additionally reads from a trajectory memory (FIFO of 7 prior boxes) and a visual memory (up to 3 historical templates). The output is a single target bounding box predicted by argmax over the word-embedding vocabulary after Softmax scoring.

## Method

DeTrack decomposes the classical multi-step denoising process into l=12 denoising blocks stacked within a single ViT-B forward pass, so the entire denoising is completed in one inference call (53G FLOPs, 42 FPS). Each denoising block performs (i) a denoising attention where the box embedding queries attend to search-region keys/values, and (ii) a NoisePred MLP that predicts the residual noise to subtract, yielding x_{i-1} = x_i'' − ε. A compound memory augments the denoised box: a trajectory memory (7 boxes) is refined via masked self-attention with temporal ordering plus 6 layers of cross-attention against image features, while a visual memory of dynamic templates is updated collaboratively using both an IoU score and a Softmax confidence threshold. Training uses Gaussian noise added to ground-truth boxes (DDPM-style schedule) with cross-entropy and SIoU losses, followed by a sequential fine-tuning stage that introduces trajectory memory and a third stage that trains only the IoUNet for template quality assessment.

## Results

On GOT-10k (unseen test data), DeTrack384 achieves AO 77.9, SR0.5 86.5, and SR0.75 74.9, surpassing the previous SOTA ARTrack384 (AO 75.5, SR0.5 84.3, SR0.75 74.3) by 2.4 points in AO. On AVisT (adverse visibility), DeTrack256 reaches AUC 60.1, outperforming SeqTrack384 (57.8) by 2.3 points. On long-term tracking benchmarks, DeTrack384 attains AUC 72.9 / PNorm 81.7 / P 79.1 on LaSOT and AUC 53.6 / PNorm 64.4 / P 60.4 on LaSOT_ext, setting new state-of-the-art results. The 256-resolution variant runs at 42 FPS on an RTX 3090, confirming real-time capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeTrack384 outperforms the closest autoregressive competitor ARTrack384 by +2.4 AO on GOT-10k (77.9 vs 75.5) and by +1.7 AUC / +2.4 PNorm on LaSOT_ext (53.6 vs 51.9 / 64.4 vs 62.0), confirming the denoising paradigm's advantage on unseen data. Against the sequence-to-sequence SeqTrack384, DeTrack384 gains +3.1 AO on GOT-10k and +2.4 AUC on AVisT (60.2 vs 57.8), the latter highlighting robustness under adverse visibility. Compared to the one-stream OSTrack384, DeTrack384 improves GOT-10k AO by +4.2 (77.9 vs 73.7) and SR0.5 by +3.3 (86.5 vs 83.2). On LaSOT, DeTrack384 edges out ARTrack384 by +0.3 AUC (72.9 vs 72.6) while matching its PNorm and P scores (81.7 / 79.1). The 256-resolution DeTrack256 also surpasses all 256-resolution baselines on AVisT (AUC 60.1 vs 59.1 for ROMTrack256) and GOT-10k (AO 77.1 vs 74.7 for SeqTrack256), demonstrating consistent gains across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeTrack draws on two distinct lineages: the denoising autoencoder (DAE) philosophy of decomposing a denoising process into internal network layers, and the autoregressive tracking framework of ARTrack (word-embedding + Softmax box decoding). It departs from the diffusion-model tradition (DDPM, DiffusionDet) by rejecting multi-iteration inference in favor of a single forward pass through a ViT whose stacked denoising blocks each remove a portion of the noise. The box-refining head and compound memory (visual + trajectory) are borrowed from the broader transformer-tracking literature (OSTrack, SeqTrack, ARTrack), while the core novelty is the in-model latent denoising formulation that makes diffusion-inspired training compatible with real-time tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that denoising-learning training (adding Gaussian noise to ground-truth boxes) improves robustness on unseen data without the multi-step inference cost that has previously made diffusion-style approaches impractical for tracking. The ablation showing that a single 12-block forward pass outperforms 24/48/96-step iterative denoising is a useful practical finding for the tracking community. However, the authors acknowledge that occlusion and out-of-view recovery remain open challenges, and the method's advantage is most visible on datasets with non-overlapping train/test splits (GOT-10k), leaving open whether the denoising benefit generalizes to all tracking regimes.
