## Problem

Generalizing simultaneously to unseen robot embodiments and unseen tasks with only a few reward-free demonstrations remains largely unexplored. Modular policy learning methods handle morphological diversity but are restricted to narrow task families (e.g., locomotion), while few-shot imitation learning methods adapt to novel tasks but assume a fixed embodiment. No prior framework unifies both axes of generalization within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244265/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes joint-level state tokens (position, velocity, movement axis, motion type per joint) with a causal history window of 10 timesteps, plus N few-shot demonstrations of state-action sequences. Heterogeneous embodiments are unified by tokenizing states and actions per joint, with extrasensory observations (e.g., goal distance in reacher) projected into a single extra joint token and free joints zero-padded. The output is a per-joint action vector with each control command in [-1,1], produced autoregressively by a causal action decoder.

## Method

Meta-Controller decomposes the state encoder into a structure encoder (bi-directional transformer over the joint axis with embodiment-specific positional embeddings and PEFT parameters) and a motion encoder (causal transformer over the temporal axis with embodiment- and task-specific PEFT parameters), disentangling morphological from dynamic knowledge. The policy network is a matching-based architecture: demonstrations are encoded by the shared state encoder and a separate causal action encoder, then a joint-wise cross-attention matching module computes similarity-weighted action features that are decoded into the current action. This non-parametric matching over local motor skills minimizes overfitting to the few demonstrations. Training proceeds in two stages: episodic meta-learning over diverse embodiment-task pairs (updating all parameters), followed by few-shot fine-tuning on the target embodiment and task where only the small set of adaptive PEFT parameters (bias, low-rank projections, layer-scale) are updated while the shared backbone is frozen.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control suite, Meta-Controller achieves a normalized score of 71.1 (average across 8 evaluation tasks spanning 4 embodiments) in the 5-shot behavior cloning setting, substantially outperforming the best baseline MetaMorph (37.9) by 33.2 points. The largest gains appear on the reacher-four embodiment, where Meta-Controller scores 56.1 (easy) and 50.8 (hard) versus negative or near-zero scores for most baselines, demonstrating the ability to generalize to a goal-position task from only five demonstrations. On the unseen hopper embodiment, Meta-Controller reaches 49.1 on the hop task versus 33.4 for MetaMorph and 21.6 for MTGv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-Controller outperforms all six baselines on every one of the eight evaluation tasks. Among the few-shot IL baselines (FS-DT, MT-DT, PDT, PDT+PEFT, HDT, L2M), none possess an embodiment-generalization mechanism, so they struggle on unseen embodiments: PDT and HDT average only 16.7 and 21.8 respectively, comparable to the from-scratch FS-DT (21.8). The modular policy learning baselines MetaMorph (37.9) and MTGv2 (35.2) handle unseen embodiments better than the DT-based methods but lack a few-shot task-adaptation mechanism, resulting in near-zero scores on the reacher-four tasks (−4.1 and −1.4 on easy; 3.8 and 4.6 on hard) where the robot must infer a goal position from five demonstrations. Meta-Controller's matching-based policy network and structure-motion encoder close this gap, achieving 56.1 and 50.8 on those same tasks. The advantage is most pronounced on the unseen hopper embodiment (49.1 vs. 33.4 for MetaMorph on hop) and the unseen wolf embodiment (91.7 vs. 73.0 on walk), while on the seen walker embodiment the gap narrows (84.3 vs. 86.4 for MT-DT on walk-bwd), suggesting the method's gains are driven primarily by cross-embodiment and cross-task generalization rather than task-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent research threads: modular policy learning (NerveNet, MetaMorph, MTGv2), which handles cross-embodiment generalization but is restricted to specific task types like locomotion, and few-shot imitation learning (PDT, HDT, MAML), which adapts to novel tasks but assumes a fixed embodiment. The key architectural departure is the joint-level I/O tokenization combined with a structure-motion state encoder that disentangles morphological from temporal-dynamics knowledge, and a matching-based (non-parametric) policy network inspired by visual token matching [20, 19] rather than the parametric adaptation (hyper-networks, prompt tokens) used by prior few-shot IL methods. PEFT techniques (LoRA, BitFit, layer-scale) are repurposed from NLP to provide lightweight embodiment- and task-specific adaptation during few-shot fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Simultaneous few-shot generalization to both unseen robot morphologies and unseen control tasks is a prerequisite for truly adaptive robotic systems, yet prior work has treated these as separate problems. The framework demonstrates that five reward-free demonstrations suffice to adapt to a new robot performing a new task, with the matching-based policy enabling compositional skill recombination (e.g., reaching a novel goal position rather than copying a demonstration pose). Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 2D DMC simulation, reliance on expert demonstrations from a replay buffer, and transformer computational cost that may hinder real-time deployment. Open questions include transfer to 3D embodiments, real-world sim-to-real gaps, and whether the joint-level representation generalizes to fundamentally different actuation mechanisms (e.g., soft robots, legged robots with contact dynamics).
