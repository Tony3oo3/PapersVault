## Problem

Existing alignment methods like DPO are designed exclusively for pairwise preference data with implicitly defined rewards, making them unable to directly exploit reward datasets where multiple responses per prompt carry explicit scalar evaluations. Prior work simply prunes such reward data into pairwise comparisons, discarding suboptimal responses and their reward information. Additionally, DPO and its variants suffer from a well-observed decreasing-likelihood trend where the preferred response's probability drops during training, contradicting the maximum-likelihood objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243740/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either reward-annotated datasets (K responses per instruction, each with a continuous scalar reward, e.g., GPT-4 ratings on a 0–10 scale) or standard pairwise preference data (chosen > rejected), along with a pretrained reference LM μ. It outputs a single aligned LM policy π_θ trained end-to-end via a contrastive loss, with the reward model implicitly parameterized as r_θ = β log(π_θ/μ). Unlike DPO which requires exactly two responses per prompt, InfoNCA and NCA handle arbitrary K > 1 responses, and the reward temperature α controls the trade-off between diversity and optimality in the soft-label weighting.

## Method

The paper introduces two parallel alignment algorithms derived from noise contrastive estimation. InfoNCA, built on InfoNCE, frames reward alignment as a K-class classification problem: given K responses (one sampled from the optimal policy π*, K−1 from the reference μ), the model learns to identify which response came from π* using a softmax-weighted cross-entropy loss over learned reward logits. DPO is recovered as the special case K=2, α→0. NCA, built on NCE, instead frames alignment as a binary classification of a single response's source (π* vs. μ), optimizing the absolute log-likelihood ratio rather than the relative margin across responses. This absolute-likelihood optimization is what prevents the chosen-likelihood from decreasing during training, giving NCA a practical advantage in reasoning tasks like math and coding where the preferred response is often the ground-truth answer.

## Results

On UltraFeedback (reward dataset, K=4 responses), InfoNCA achieves the best MT-bench score of 7.63 and AlpacaEval of 92.35, outperforming DPO (7.34 / 90.60), KTO (7.12 / 91.93), and IPO (7.45 / 90.62). NCA achieves 7.52 MT-bench and 90.31 AlpacaEval. On complex reasoning tasks (UltraInteract, pairwise preference), NCA dramatically outperforms DPO: on Mixtral-8×7B, NCA reaches 57.9 average across math/coding benchmarks while DPO drops to 50.1 (below the SFT baseline of 56.3), and on Mistral-7B, NCA scores 35.7 vs. DPO's 25.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoNCA outperforms all preference-based baselines on UltraFeedback: it beats DPO by +0.29 on MT-bench (7.63 vs. 7.34) and +1.75 on AlpacaEval (92.35 vs. 90.60), and also surpasses KTO (+0.51 MT-bench) and IPO (+0.18 MT-bench). NCA also beats DPO on MT-bench (7.52 vs. 7.34) but trails slightly on AlpacaEval (90.31 vs. 90.60). On reasoning tasks (Table 3), the gap widens dramatically: NCA improves the Mixtral-8×7B average from 56.3 (SFT) to 57.9, while DPO degrades it to 50.1, indicating DPO actively harms reasoning performance. The combinatorial DPO variants (DPO×3, DPO×C₄²) that attempt to exploit all four responses underperform InfoNCA/NCA, confirming that the theoretical guarantees of the contrastive framework matter beyond simple data augmentation. Overall, InfoNCA is the strongest method for general instruction-following when reward data is available, while NCA is the clear choice for math and coding tasks where preserving chosen-response likelihood is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with classic noise contrastive estimation (NCE/InfoNCE) by showing DPO is a special case (K=2, α→0) of a more general InfoNCA objective derived from multi-class contrastive classification. It departs from DPO's Bradley-Terry/Plackett-Luce modeling assumptions, instead grounding the alignment loss in the well-established NCE and InfoNCE frameworks from representation learning. The key methodological departure is NCA, which replaces InfoNCA's relative-likelihood optimization with absolute-likelihood optimization via binary NCE, directly addressing the well-documented DPO likelihood-decrease pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, theoretically grounded recipe for leveraging explicit reward datasets (e.g., UltraFeedback) without discarding suboptimal responses, and offers NCA as a drop-in alternative to DPO that is particularly beneficial for reasoning tasks where the preferred response is a ground-truth answer. A notable limitation is that the theoretical guarantees require K→∞ responses per instruction for exact partition-function estimation, and more responses increase GPU memory cost. Open questions include whether the reward-based advantage persists with truly human-annotated (rather than GPT-4-annotated) rewards, and whether NCA's robustness to hyperparameter sensitivity extends to larger-scale or multi-objective alignment settings.
