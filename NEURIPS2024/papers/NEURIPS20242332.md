## Problem

Existing work on language encoder similarity compares finite representation sets via linear alignment, but lacks a principled, task-independent (intrinsic) notion of encoder similarity that also predicts downstream task performance (extrinsic similarity). This paper asks what it means for two encoders—viewed as functions over all strings—to be similar, and whether an intrinsic measure can bound extrinsic differences across all downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242332/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on two language encoders h, g: Σ* → R^D, but in practice consumes finite N×D representation matrices extracted from training sets (e.g., SST-2, MRPC) across all 12 layers of ELECTRA, RoBERTa, and 25 MultiBERT models. The key output is an asymmetric affine alignment distance d_Aff(V)(h,g) = inf_ψ ‖h − ψ∘g‖_∞, which is a hemi-metric (not a metric), along with a derived preorder ≽_Aff over encoders and a linear upper bound on worst-case extrinsic dissimilarity across all linear classifiers.

## Method

The paper formalizes encoder similarity through a general S-homotopy framework built on hemi-metric (Lawvere-metric) spaces and the Hausdorff-Hoare map, which lifts a distance on individual encoders to a distance between an encoder and the set of all its S-transformations. For affine homotopy (S = Aff(V)), the resulting one-sided alignment d_Aff(V)(h,g) is inherently asymmetric—capturing the cost of transforming g into h—and induces a preorder where higher-ranked encoders can be affinely mapped to lower-ranked ones, with rank(h) ≤ rank(g) as a necessary condition. The central theoretical result (Thm. 5.1) shows that ε-intrinsic affine similarity implies O(ε)-extrinsic similarity: the worst-case prediction difference across all linear classifiers is linearly bounded by the intrinsic affine distance. Empirically, the authors approximate the infinite-domain distances by optimizing affine maps over finite representation matrices using RiemannSGD, and confirm a positive correlation between intrinsic and extrinsic similarity across GLUE tasks, with encoder rank deficiency partially explaining the observed asymmetry.

## Results

The paper's main empirical result (Table 1) shows that the proposed intrinsic affine alignment measure d_Aff(V) achieves the highest Spearman's ρ with extrinsic similarity among all tested methods: 0.621 (p<0.01) against d_V(V,Δ)^H on SST-2 and 0.534 (p<0.01) against d_ψ' on MRPC. All linear alignment-based methods (d_Aff(V), Orthogonal Procrustes, R_CCA², PWCCA) show significant, large Pearson correlations (PCC 0.53–0.94) with extrinsic similarity, whereas Linear CKA—a non-alignment method—consistently yields much weaker correlations (PCC 0.08–0.32). The paper also demonstrates measurable asymmetry in affine mappability across 25 MultiBERT seeds, with encoder rank deficiency (ρ=0.609 on MRPC) partially explaining why some encoders are easier to map to than from.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed d_Aff(V) against three linear alignment baselines (Orthogonal Procrustes [34], CCA [20], PWCCA [30]) and one non-alignment baseline (Linear CKA [22]). d_Aff(V) achieves the strongest Spearman's ρ with extrinsic similarity on SST-2 (0.621 vs. 0.157 for Procrustes, 0.071 for CCA, 0.231 for PWCCA) and MRPC (0.534 vs. 0.053, 0.037, 0.308 respectively), indicating it best captures the monotonic relationship between intrinsic and extrinsic dissimilarity. Orthogonal Procrustes and PWCCA show competitive PCC values (often >0.7) but weaker rank correlations, suggesting they capture linear but not monotonic structure. CCA (R_CCA²) achieves high PCC on MRPC (0.879) but near-zero or negative ρ, indicating its fit quality is not reliably ordered by extrinsic similarity. Linear CKA, the only non-alignment method, consistently underperforms all alignment-based methods on both ρ and PCC (e.g., PCC 0.241 vs. 0.707–0.743 on SST-2 for d_ψ'), confirming the paper's claim that CKA does not capture extrinsic behavior as faithfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of measuring encoder similarity via finite representation sets (CKA, CCA, Procrustes, GULP) by studying encoders as functions over the entire string space Σ*, and by replacing symmetric metrics with asymmetric hemi-metrics (Lawvere metrics). The theoretical backbone draws on Lawvere's observation that symmetry is unnecessary in metric spaces and on Goubault-Larrecq's results on specialization orderings and the Hausdorff-Hoare map, which together enable the S-homotopy framework. The affine case is positioned as a concrete instance of this general framework, and the paper explicitly contrasts its asymmetric, function-level approach with prior work that enforces proper metric axioms on finite-set comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.1) that intrinsic affine similarity linearly bounds extrinsic task-performance dissimilarity, giving practitioners a principled reason to trust alignment-based similarity measures. The discovery of an asymmetric preorder on encoders—where some encoders are structurally easier to map to than from, partially explained by rank deficiency—challenges the implicit symmetry assumption in most encoder-comparison tools and opens questions about the geometry of the pre-trained encoder landscape. Key limitations include the restriction to affine (linear) relationships and linear classifiers, and the reliance on numerical approximations (SVD truncation, MSE optimization) that may obscure the clean theoretical picture. Open questions include extending the S-homotopy framework to non-linear transformations and to full fine-tuning rather than linear probing.
