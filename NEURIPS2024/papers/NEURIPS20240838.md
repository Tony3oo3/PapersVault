## Problem

Existing positional encodings for transformers—whether absolute (APE) or relative (RoPE, Alibi, Kerple, FIRE)—are fixed after training and depend solely on token positions, not on the input content. This static nature makes them average-optimal across training samples but suboptimal for any specific instance, causing sharp performance degradation when evaluation length exceeds training length. The paper asks whether a positional encoding can be dynamically adjusted per input to capture both semantic and positional relationships.

## Input / Output

DAPE takes two T×T matrices as input: the pre-softmax attention scores (XW_Q)(XW_K)^T representing semantic similarity, and a positional bias matrix B (e.g., from Alibi, Kerple, or FIRE) encoding relative position. It outputs a T×T adaptive correction matrix that is added to the attention logits, producing the final pre-softmax scores. In the multi-head variant, the MLP receives a 2h-dimensional concatenation (attention + bias) across all h heads and outputs h-dimensional corrections, processing all heads in parallel within a single network.

## Method

DAPE replaces the fixed positional bias with a two-layer LeakyReLU MLP f(·) that maps the element-wise concatenation of the attention matrix and the positional bias matrix to an adaptive correction term, yielding A_DAPE = XW_Q(XW_K)^T + f(XW_Q(XW_K)^T, B). The key insight is that conditioning on the actual attention values makes the positional encoding semantically dependent: for example, when two tokens are highly similar, the model can reduce reliance on positional distance. A residual variant (Eq. 3) adds B explicitly so f(·) acts as a correction to a well-behaved base bias. The method is compatible with any additive RPE (Alibi, Kerple, FIRE) as the source of B, and the hidden dimension D_DAPE (typically 32) is kept small relative to the model's head dimension to limit overhead to O(hN²D_DAPE) additional FLOPs.

## Results

On the Arxiv dataset (training length 512, 125M model), DAPE-Kerple achieves a perplexity of 3.86 at evaluation length 8192, compared to 13.35 for vanilla Kerple and 4.77 for Alibi. DAPE-Alibi reaches 4.00 perplexity at 8192, and DAPE-FIRE reaches 6.16, versus 308.62 for standard FIRE. On the Books3 dataset (training length 512), DAPE-Kerple achieves 17.88 perplexity at 8192 versus 39.31 for Kerple. On the CHE algorithmic reasoning benchmark, DAPE achieves the best accuracy in 10 out of 11 permutation-variant tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPE consistently outperforms all eight baselines (NoPE, RoPE, YaRN, Randomized RoPE, T5's Bias, Alibi, Kerple, FIRE) across Arxiv, Books3, and CHE. The most dramatic gains are over FIRE: DAPE-FIRE reduces perplexity from 308.62 to 6.16 at evaluation length 8192 (Arxiv, train 512), and DAPE-Kerple reduces Kerple's perplexity from 13.35 to 3.86 at the same setting. Against RoPE and T5's bias, which collapse to perplexities of 265 and 595 respectively at 8192, all DAPE variants maintain perplexities below 7. On the CHE benchmark, DAPE-Kerple and DAPE-FIRE achieve the best accuracy in 10 of 11 permutation-variant tasks (e.g., 100% on EVEN PAIRS vs. 99.98% for RoPE, 89.17% on MISSING DUPLICATE vs. 79.27% for FIRE), while remaining competitive on permutation-invariant tasks. At larger model sizes (350M, 2.7B, 6.7B), DAPE-Kerple continues to rank top-3, with the performance gap over static baselines widening as evaluation length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPE extends the additive relative positional encoding (RPE) lineage—Alibi, Kerple, T5's bias, and FIRE—by making the positional bias a function of both the attention matrix (semantic context) and the positional bias matrix, rather than a fixed function of positions alone. The closest prior work is FIRE [41], which already uses MLPs to learn positional biases, but FIRE's biases remain static after training; DAPE's key departure is feeding the key-query similarity into the same MLP, enabling per-instance adaptation. The paper also positions DAPE as a unifying generalization of both additive (Alibi, Kerple) and multiplicative (RoPE) RPE formulations, replacing the fixed combination rule with a learnable MLP(a, b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length extrapolation remains a critical bottleneck for deploying LLMs on long documents, and DAPE demonstrates that a lightweight 2-layer MLP (32 hidden units) can close the gap between static PEs and ideal per-instance positional encoding, achieving 64× extrapolation (train 128 → eval 8192) with perplexity 5.00 versus 31.93 for Kerple. Limitations include validation primarily on 125M–350M models (with 2.7B/6.7B results in the appendix), a small but non-negligible additional compute cost, and the open question of whether the adaptivity generalizes to non-text modalities or very large-scale pretraining. The method's simplicity (a few lines of PyTorch) makes it an easy drop-in for existing transformer codebases, increasing its practical adoption potential.
