## Problem

The Flooding method improves test accuracy by switching between gradient descent and ascent based on a threshold applied to the batch-average loss, but this only constrains the mean loss to be near a fixed level θ. The paper asks whether this requirement is too weak to simultaneously encourage low model complexity and small loss generalization gaps, and proposes a stronger criterion that the per-point loss distribution be well-concentrated around θ rather than merely having its mean near θ.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in wrapper around any differentiable loss function (cross-entropy in all experiments), consuming standard classification inputs: 32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 28×28 grayscale images for FashionMNIST, or 2D feature vectors for synthetic data. It produces class labels via the underlying model's output layer, with the only modification being how the per-sample loss gradient is weighted during the update step. No additional forward or backward passes are required beyond vanilla gradient descent.

## Method

SoftAD (Soft Ascent-Descent) modifies the Flooding update in two ways: (1) it applies the threshold pointwise to each data point's loss rather than to the batch average, and (2) it replaces the hard sign() switch with a smooth, bounded, monotone function φ(x) = x/√(x²+1), the derivative of ρ(x) = √(x²+1) − 1. The resulting update w_{t+1} = w_t − (α/n) Σᵢ φ(ℓ(w_t; Zᵢ) − θ) ∇ℓ(w_t; Zᵢ) causes points with loss above θ to contribute descent directions and points below θ to contribute ascent directions, while borderline points near θ are automatically down-weighted. This smoothness yields formal stationarity guarantees for the population objective S_μ(w) = θ + E[ρ(ℓ(w;Z) − θ)] with high probability, whereas Flooding's non-smooth objective only admits guarantees for a Moreau-enveloped upper bound. Empirically, SoftAD achieves the smallest loss generalization gap and model norm across all tested datasets while maintaining accuracy competitive with Flooding and SAM, at the same per-step computational cost as vanilla gradient descent.

## Results

SoftAD achieves the smallest loss generalization gap (test minus training cross-entropy loss) across all seven datasets, ranging from 0.004 on Gaussian to 1.168 on CIFAR-10, compared to 0.011–1.239 for Flooding and 0.024–1.512 for SAM. Test accuracy is competitive with all baselines (SAM is slightly ahead at double the gradient cost), while SoftAD also yields the smallest model L2 norm and the lowest test loss on every dataset. On CIFAR-100, SoftAD reduces the generalization gap to 2.701 versus 3.114 (Flooding) and 3.672 (SAM); on SVHN the gap drops to 0.362 versus 0.436 (Flooding) and 0.493 (SAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flooding (Ishida et al., 2020), SoftAD wins on every metric: it achieves a smaller generalization gap on all seven datasets (e.g., 1.168 vs. 1.239 on CIFAR-10, 2.701 vs. 3.114 on CIFAR-100), a lower test loss, and a smaller model norm, while maintaining comparable test accuracy. Against SAM (Foret et al., 2021), SoftAD matches or exceeds accuracy at half the per-step gradient cost, and dominates on generalization gap and model norm (e.g., 1.168 vs. 1.512 on CIFAR-10). Against ERM, SoftAD is uniformly superior on all three metrics (gap, loss, norm) with only a negligible accuracy trade-off. In the supplementary comparison with iFlood (Xie et al., 2022), SoftAD achieves a smaller generalization gap on CIFAR-100 (2.696 vs. 2.558), FashionMNIST (0.463 vs. 0.273), and SVHN (0.397 vs. 0.404), though iFlood edges out SoftAD on CIFAR-10 (1.468 vs. 1.072). Overall, SoftAD's key advantage is its ability to simultaneously minimize the loss generalization gap and model norm without sacrificing accuracy, a balance that neither Flooding nor SAM achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Flooding algorithm of Ishida et al. (2020) by replacing its hard, batch-level ascent-descent switch with a smooth, pointwise mechanism (SoftAD) that downweights borderline examples and limits outlier effects. It also situates itself within the broader sharpness-aware training literature, noting that Flooding implicitly alternates between empirical risk minimization and squared gradient norm penalization (a connection first highlighted by Karakida et al., 2023), and contrasts its smoothness properties with the non-smooth Flooding objective. The method is positioned as a middle ground between original Flooding and iFlood (Xie et al., 2022), which uses pointwise thresholds but retains the hard sign switch, and is distinguished from OCE/DRO criteria by its bi-directional (non-monotonic) loss penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoftAD offers a zero-overhead, drop-in replacement for flooding that uniformly achieves the smallest loss generalization gap across all tested datasets while maintaining competitive accuracy and smaller model norms, suggesting that concentrating the loss distribution around a threshold (rather than merely matching its mean) is a stronger implicit regularizer. The main open question remains how to set the threshold θ without validation data, a limitation shared with flooding. A more formal characterization of the implicit regularization effect, and empirical validation beyond cross-entropy loss and the tested architectures, are identified as important next steps.
