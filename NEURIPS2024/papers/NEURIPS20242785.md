## Problem

Low-bit quantization of LLMs is severely degraded by activation outliers, but existing methods only address Normal Outliers (persistent across all tokens) while failing on Massive Outliers (extremely large values at very few tokens, often >1000× the median). SmoothQuant's per-channel smoothing induces new weight outliers when confronting Massive Outliers, and optimization-based methods like OmniQuant and AffineQuant suffer training instability, leaving a gap in effectively handling both outlier types simultaneously under 4-bit weight-activation quantization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA 7B–70B, LLaMA2, LLaMA3, Mistral, Phi2, Vicuna) and 128 calibration sequences of length 2048 from WikiText2 to construct the transformation matrices. It outputs a fully quantized model with 4-bit or 6-bit integer weights and activations (per-channel for weights, per-token for activations) plus the block-diagonal rotation matrices (block size 2^n = 128) and the zigzag permutation matrix, all of which are folded into the quantized weights at no additional runtime cost. The linear layer equivalence Y = XW is preserved exactly through the paired transformation (XG)(G⁻¹W).

## Method

DuQuant applies a three-stage dual transformation on top of the standard SmoothQuant smoothing: (1) a block-diagonal rotation matrix R̂_(1) constructed via greedy search that identifies the dominant outlier dimension at each step and builds a targeted orthogonal rotation to locally redistribute outliers within 128-dim blocks; (2) a zigzag permutation matrix P that reorders channels in a back-and-forth pattern so that the highest-magnitude channels are spread evenly across all blocks, provably bounding the inter-block variance (Theorem 2); and (3) a second block-diagonal rotation R̂_(2) that further smooths the now-balanced activation landscape. The weight matrix is simultaneously multiplied by the transpose of the full transformation (R̂_(2)ᵀ Pᵀ R̂_(1)ᵀ Λ), which preserves layer equivalence while smoothing the weight outliers induced by the smoothing factor. Unlike QuaRot, which uses a random Hadamard matrix and relies on GPTQ for weight quantization, DuQuant's prior-knowledge-guided greedy rotation plus zigzag permutation achieves competitive or superior accuracy with simple round-to-nearest quantization, and the entire quantization process completes in under 5 minutes for a 7B model.

## Results

Under W4A4 quantization, DuQuant achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 6.40 on LLaMA1-7B (FP16: 5.68) and 6.28 on LLaMA2-7B (FP16: 5.47), substantially outperforming the next-best baseline Atom (8.15 and 8.40 respectively). On zero-shot commonsense QA, DuQuant reaches 61.76% average accuracy on LLaMA1-7B (FP16: 64.09%), surpassing Atom by +4.88% and QLLM by +9.92%. For instruction-tuned Vicuna-v1.5-13B, DuQuant+LWC attains 51.08% zero-shot MMLU (FP16: 54.54%), exceeding Atom by 10.01%. In practical deployment, DuQuant delivers a 2.08× pre-filling speedup and 3.50× memory reduction on LLaMA2-7B, with quantization completing in just 50 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuQuant consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every model size and dataset. SmoothQuant and OS+ suffer catastrophic degradation under W4A4 (e.g., 25.25 vs. 6.40 WikiText2 PPL for LLaMA1-7B), confirming their inability to handle massive outliers. OmniQuant and AffineQuant show moderate improvements over SmoothQuant but still trail DuQuant by 4–5 PPL points; both also produce NaN results on LLaMA2-70B. QLLM is competitive at larger scales (6.87 PPL on LLaMA1-65B) but remains 2.74 PPL behind DuQuant (4.13). Atom is the strongest baseline, achieving 8.15 PPL on LLaMA1-7B, yet DuQuant still leads by 1.75 PPL and by +4.88% on average QA accuracy. Against QuaRot (the closest methodological competitor using Hadamard rotations), DuQuant matches QuaRot-GPTQ on LLaMA1-7B (6.40 vs. 6.44) while using only RTN quantization, and clearly outperforms it on LLaMA2-7B (6.28 vs. 6.39) and LLaMA2-13B (5.42 vs. 5.75), demonstrating the advantage of the zigzag permutation and prior-knowledge-guided rotation over random Hadamard matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal-transformation line of work for LLM quantization, building directly on QuaRot's use of Hadamard rotations and SmoothQuant's per-channel smoothing. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) it identifies Massive Outliers at the FFN down-projection layer as a distinct failure mode that SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, and AffineQuant cannot handle, and (2) it replaces QuaRot's random Hadamard matrix with a greedy-search-constructed block-diagonal rotation guided by outlier position priors, augmented by a zigzag channel permutation to balance outlier magnitudes across blocks. Unlike QuaRot and Atom, DuQuant avoids GPTQ for weight quantization by jointly smoothing weights through the same rotation matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DuQuant provides a practical, fast (50s–270s quantization runtime) solution to the W4A4 quantization gap that has plagued LLM deployment, achieving 2×+ inference speedup and 3.5× memory reduction with only ~0.6 PPL increase over FP16. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) and the clean separation of rotation (local) vs. permutation (global) responsibilities offer a principled framework for future outlier-mitigation work. Open questions include whether the greedy rotation search can be replaced by a closed-form solution, whether the approach extends to 2-bit or mixed-precision settings, and whether the zigzag permutation generalizes to non-uniform block sizes or 3D tensor layouts.
