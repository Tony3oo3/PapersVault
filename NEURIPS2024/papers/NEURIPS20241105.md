## Problem

OOD generalization (correctly classifying covariate-shifted samples) and OOD detection (rejecting semantic-shifted samples) are typically studied in isolation, and while Scone provided an empirical framework leveraging wild data for both, a unified theoretical understanding of how heterogeneous wild data (a mixture of ID, covariate OOD, and semantic OOD) impacts both tasks simultaneously was lacking. This paper fills that gap by providing a graph-theoretic framework with provable error bounds for both objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled ID data (50% of CIFAR-10) and unlabeled wild data constructed as a mixture with π_c=0.5 (covariate-shifted OOD from CIFAR-10-C with Gaussian noise) and π_s=0.1 (semantic-shifted OOD from SVHN, LSUN, Textures, or Places365). It produces two outputs: a multi-class classifier via a linear probe on learned embeddings, and an OOD detection score computed as a non-parametric KNN distance in the embedding space, with the decision threshold set at the 95th percentile of clean ID distances. The backbone is a Wide ResNet-40-2 trained from scratch, followed by 20 epochs of cross-entropy fine-tuning on labeled ID data.

## Method

The core contribution is constructing a graph over all augmented data points where edge weights combine self-supervised connectivity (probability of two views being augmented from the same image) and supervised connectivity (probability of two views being augmented from same-class labeled ID images), then performing spectral decomposition of the normalized adjacency matrix to obtain closed-form k-dimensional representations. The spectral factorization is equivalently achieved by minimizing a five-term contrastive surrogate loss (pulling same-class and same-image pairs together, pushing cross-class and cross-image pairs apart) that can be trained end-to-end with a neural network. The theoretical analysis shows that when class connectivity dominates domain connectivity (9α/8 > β in their toy model), the linear probing error on covariate OOD is zero, while the ID-to-semantic-OOD embedding distance remains large, enabling both perfect generalization and strong detection. This graph-theoretic perspective unifies the two tasks: the same spectral structure that aligns covariate OOD with ID (aiding generalization) simultaneously separates semantic OOD from ID (aiding detection).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with CIFAR-10-C as covariate OOD, the proposed method achieves OOD generalization accuracy of 86.62% (SVHN), 85.88% (LSUN-C), and 81.40% (Textures), while simultaneously achieving FPR95 of 0.13%, 1.76%, and 12.05% respectively. Compared to the closest competitor Scone, the method reduces FPR95 by an average of 8.34% across five semantic-shift OOD datasets, with the largest gap on Textures (12.05% vs. 37.15%). AUROC scores reach 99.98%, 99.75%, and 98.25% on the three semantic OOD benchmarks, indicating near-perfect OOD detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor Scone [5], the proposed method wins on OOD generalization accuracy for SVHN (86.62% vs. 84.69%) and LSUN-C (85.88% vs. 84.58%), but trails on Textures (81.40% vs. 85.56%). However, the method dramatically outperforms Scone on OOD detection FPR95 across all three datasets: 0.13% vs. 10.86% (SVHN), 1.76% vs. 10.23% (LSUN-C), and 12.05% vs. 37.15% (Textures). Compared to Woods [37], the method achieves substantially higher OOD accuracy on SVHN (86.62% vs. 52.76%) and LSUN-C (85.88% vs. 76.90%) while also improving FPR on SVHN (0.13% vs. 2.11%) and Textures (12.05% vs. 39.10%). Against OOD-detection-only methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, Mahalanobis, VIM, KNN, ASH), the method achieves both higher OOD accuracy (86.62% vs. 75.05%) and lower FPR on most datasets, demonstrating that joint training for generalization and detection is superior to post-hoc detection. Against OOD-generalization-only methods (IRM, Mixup, VRE, EQRM, SharpDRO), the method matches or exceeds OOD accuracy while achieving far better FPR (e.g., 0.13% vs. 21.24% for SharpDRO on SVHN). In Table 2, the method also outperforms spectral learning baselines SCL [7] and NSCL [13] on both OOD accuracy and FPR across all three semantic OOD datasets, with the largest FPR gap on LSUN-C (1.76% vs. 81.30% for SCL and 18.43% for NSCL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral contrastive learning lineage (HaoChen et al. for self-supervised learning, Shen et al. for domain adaptation, Sun et al. for novel class discovery) by introducing a graph-theoretic framework that handles a heterogeneous wild data mixture containing ID, covariate-shifted OOD, and semantic-shifted OOD simultaneously. While prior spectral works assumed unlabeled data from a single homogeneous distribution, and Scone (Bai et al.) introduced the heterogeneous wild data setting empirically without theoretical grounding, this paper provides the first provable error analysis for joint OOD generalization and detection under that setting. The training loss is a new combination of supervised and self-supervised spectral terms, derived from factorizing a graph adjacency matrix that encodes both label-based and augmentation-based connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form eigenvector analysis reveals a clean condition (9α/8 vs. β) governing when OOD generalization succeeds or fails, offering interpretable guidance on when class structure dominates over domain structure in the learned representation. The framework also shows that ID labels strictly improve both generalization and detection separability, and that semantic OOD sharing a domain with covariate OOD can actually help generalization. Limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to a 5-node toy graph, the empirical evaluation focusing on covariate shift (not concept shift) for OOD generalization, and the reliance on a specific wild data mixture ratio. Open questions include whether the spectral framework scales to larger, more complex graph structures and whether the theoretical conditions generalize beyond the simplified augmentation model.
