## Problem

Standard normalization layers (BatchNorm, InstanceNorm, LayerNorm) used in GNNs were not designed for graph-structured data and can provably limit expressiveness, e.g., preventing even simple tasks like computing node degrees. Existing graph-specific normalization methods fail to offer consistent improvements across benchmarks. The paper identifies the missing property as adaptivity to the input graph structure, which requires expressive architectures capable of distinguishing non-isomorphic nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242872/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each layer, GRANOLA takes the intermediate node features from the preceding GNN layer, the graph adjacency matrix, and freshly sampled random node features (RNF) drawn from a standard Gaussian. It outputs normalized node features where the affine parameters γ and β are generated per-node and per-graph by an auxiliary normalization GNN, rather than being shared learnable scalars as in standard normalizations. The mean and standard deviation statistics are computed per-node across the channel dimension (LayerNorm-node style), but the scaling and shifting are fully graph-adaptive.

## Method

GRANOLA introduces an auxiliary normalization GNN (a shallow MPNN augmented with Random Node Features) that takes the concatenation of intermediate node features and sampled RNF as input and produces per-node representations from which learnable functions f₁ and f₂ extract the affine parameters γ_{b,n} and β_{b,n}. The key design choice is augmenting this normalization GNN with RNF, which the paper proves is necessary: without RNF (GRANOLA-NO-RNF), the method is provably no more expressive than a standard MPNN, while with RNF it inherits (ε,δ)-universal approximation guarantees and achieves full graph adaptivity. The overall architecture retains linear time and space complexity in the number of nodes and edges, matching standard MPNNs. The paper further shows that MPNN+GRANOLA can default to an MPNN+RNF, bridging the gap between theoretical expressiveness of RNF-augmented GNNs and their inconsistent practical performance when used as simple positional encodings.

## Results

On ZINC-12K, GRANOLA achieves a MAE of 0.1203, substantially outperforming the best standard normalization (LayerNorm-graph at 0.1609) and the best graph-specific normalization (SuperNorm at 0.1574). On the OGB molecular benchmarks, GRANOLA achieves 0.960 RMSE on MOLESOL, 77.19% ROC-AUC on MOLTOX21, 79.92% on MOLBACE, and 78.98% on MOLHIV, consistently leading all baselines. Notably, on MOLBACE GRANOLA improves over BatchNorm by 6.95 percentage points (79.92% vs. 72.97%), and on MOLESOL it beats the second-best graph normalization (GraphNorm) by 0.084 RMSE. When combined with the expressive GSN architecture, GRANOLA further reduces ZINC MAE from 0.1010 to 0.0766 and improves MOLBACE from 77.40% to 80.41%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRANOLA consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against standard normalizations, GRANOLA beats BatchNorm [34] by 0.213 RMSE on MOLESOL and 6.95% on MOLBACE, InstanceNorm [58] by 0.139 RMSE on MOLESOL, and both LayerNorm variants [3] by 0.098–0.101 RMSE on MOLESOL. Among graph-specific normalizations, GRANOLA surpasses the strongest competitor GraphNorm [11] by 0.084 RMSE on MOLESOL and 6.69% on MOLBACE, and SuperNorm [12] by 0.077 RMSE on MOLESOL and 4.80% on MOLBACE; it also dominates PairNorm [72], MeanSubtractionNorm [64], DiffGroupNorm [74], NodeNorm [75], and GraphSizeNorm [21] on all four OGB datasets. Against its natural baselines, GRANOLA outperforms RNF-PE (Sato et al. [56]) by 0.092 RMSE on MOLESOL and 5.64% on MOLBACE, and RNF-NORM by 0.079 RMSE on MOLESOL and 1.96% on MOLBACE, confirming that graph-adaptive normalization is more effective than simply injecting RNF as positional encoding. When stacked on the expressive GSN architecture [10], GRANOLA further improves all five benchmarks (e.g., ZINC MAE 0.1010→0.0766, MOLBACE 77.40%→80.41%), demonstrating that the normalization benefit is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRANOLA builds on two distinct research threads: (1) the line of work showing that Random Node Features (RNF) augment MPNNs to universal approximation [Abboud et al., Sato et al.], and (2) the growing family of graph-specific normalization layers (PairNorm, GraphNorm, NodeNorm, DiffGroupNorm, SuperNorm) that attempt to address oversmoothing or expressiveness limitations of standard norms. The key conceptual departure is repurposing RNF not as input features or positional encodings (as in prior RNF-GNN work) but as the mechanism inside a normalization hypernetwork that generates node- and graph-specific affine parameters. This design choice is what allows GRANOLA to inherit the (ε,δ)-universal approximation guarantee of RNF-augmented MPNNs while retaining linear MPNN complexity, and the paper proves that removing RNF from the normalization (GRANOLA-NO-RNF) collapses expressiveness back to that of a standard MPNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRANOLA addresses a practical gap: RNF-augmented MPNNs have strong theoretical expressiveness guarantees but have not consistently translated into real-world performance gains when RNF is used as input features. By embedding RNF within the normalization step, GRANOLA consistently outperforms all standard and graph-specific normalization baselines across 11 diverse datasets, suggesting that the *where* RNF is applied matters as much as the *what*. Open questions include whether alternative designs for the normalization GNN (beyond shallow MPNN+RNF) can close the remaining gap to highly expressive architectures like GSN, and whether the linear-in-nodes memory overhead of maintaining a separate normalization network is acceptable at scale. The paper also demonstrates that GRANOLA can be stacked on top of already-expressive methods (GSN+GRANOLA), hinting at composability with future GNN architectures.
