## Problem

Deep visual backbones lack effective deep image priors for medical images, causing severe performance degradation under domain shifts such as changes in hospital, patient demographics (race, sex, age), or scan position. The paper identifies that untrained ViT and ConvNext features are no better than raw pixels for medical imaging, and that existing domain generalization methods fail to address this root cause. The gap is the absence of a knowledge-grounded prior that steers models toward clinically relevant reasoning rather than spurious correlations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single chest X-ray or skin lesion image as input and produces a class label (binary or multi-class) along with a 150-dimensional concept probability vector, where each dimension corresponds to a natural-language question (e.g., 'Is there ground-glass opacity?'). The 150 concepts form a fixed, modality-specific bottleneck shared across all examples and datasets, rather than being dataset-specific. Concept grounding functions are binary logistic regression classifiers over frozen ViT-L/14 features, and the final prediction is a single linear layer over the concept probabilities.

## Method

KnoBo is a concept bottleneck model with three coupled priors. (1) A structure prior is built via iterative retrieval-augmented generation: class names seed BM25 queries into a medical corpus (e.g., 5.5M PubMed articles), and GPT-4 proposes new concepts from retrieved snippets, expanding the bottleneck to 150 concepts. (2) A bottleneck predictor grounds each concept with a binary logistic regression trained on 2,000 clinical reports per concept (annotated by Flan-T5-XXL for concept presence), then a linear layer maps the 150 concept probabilities to the output label. (3) A parameter prior is an L1 loss between tanh(W) and a sign matrix W_prior (each entry ±1) defined by an LLM, penalizing the linear layer from deviating from expected label–concept correlations. This factorization—structure prior + bottleneck predictor + parameter prior—encodes medical textbook knowledge directly into both the architecture and the training objective, making the model inherently interpretable and robust to confounded domain shifts.

## Results

On confounded chest X-ray datasets, KnoBo achieves 58.8% OOD accuracy (vs. 17.0% for ViT-L/14) with a domain gap of 30.9 (vs. 79.7 for ViT-L/14), and 70.5% OOD on skin lesion datasets (vs. 47.6% for ViT-L/14) with a domain gap of 14.1 (vs. 48.0). Averaged across both confounded and unconfounded datasets, KnoBo attains the best Overall score of 73.7% (X-ray) and 78.2% (skin lesion), outperforming the strongest end-to-end baseline (ViT-L/14) by 41.8% and 22.9% in OOD accuracy respectively. On unconfounded X-ray datasets, KnoBo trails the best black-box model (Linear Probe) by only 0.7% (73.1% vs. 73.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KnoBo decisively outperforms all five baselines on OOD accuracy across both modalities: on X-ray it reaches 58.8% OOD vs. 17.0% (ViT-L/14), 20.9% (DenseNet), 30.7% (Linear Probe), 55.1% (LSL), 30.6% (PCBM-h), and 34.8% (LaBo); on skin lesions it reaches 70.5% vs. 47.6%, 50.3%, 52.1%, 59.1%, 52.0%, and 51.4% respectively. KnoBo also achieves the best Overall score (mean of confounded Avg and unconfounded accuracy) for both modalities (73.7% X-ray, 78.2% skin), beating the closest competitor ViT-L/14 by 10.2 and 0.3 points. The trade-off is a modest drop in in-domain accuracy (89.7% vs. 96.7% for ViT-L/14 on X-ray; 86.0% vs. 95.6% on skin), which the authors attribute to the parameter prior constraining the model away from spurious correlations. Among interpretable baselines, KnoBo substantially outperforms PCBM-h and LaBo on OOD (by 28.2 and 24.0 points on X-ray), while LSL is the closest competitor on OOD but still trails KnoBo by 3.7 (X-ray) and 11.4 (skin) points. On unconfounded X-ray datasets, KnoBo (73.1%) is within 0.7% of the best black-box method (Linear Probe, 73.8%), though it lags PCBM-h (74.7%) by 1.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) [Koh et al., ICML 2020] and LM-guided bottleneck construction [Yang et al., CVPR 2023] by replacing CLIP-based concept-image alignment with dedicated grounding functions trained on image-text pairs, and by introducing a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline over medical documents (PubMed, textbooks, StatPearls) to construct the concept space. It departs from prior domain generalization methods (IRM, DRO, group DRO) which the authors note often fail to beat ERM, instead arguing that the root cause of medical model fragility is a missing architectural prior rather than an optimization problem. The parameter prior (L1 regularization toward LM-defined sign correlations) is a new loss component not present in prior CBM variants like LaBo or PCBM-h.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that untrained ViT/ConvNext features underperform raw pixels on medical images is a strong empirical argument that current visual backbones lack domain-appropriate inductive biases, motivating the need for explicit knowledge injection. KnoBo's 32.4% average OOD improvement over fine-tuned models on confounded datasets, with minimal ID sacrifice, offers a practical path toward deployable medical AI that doesn't silently fail when patient demographics or hospital sources shift. Key limitations include the requirement for multimodal pretraining data (limiting applicability to rare conditions), the gap between model and expert performance, and the reliance on GPT-4V-generated captions for skin lesions where no human-annotated corpus exists. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric imaging, how to handle conditions with insufficient document coverage, and whether the parameter prior can be replaced by learned causal structures.
