## Problem

Existing Graph Auto-Encoders (GAEs) suffer from two intertwined limitations: they over-emphasize node feature reconstruction at the expense of topological structure, making them ineffective for graph-level classification, and their deep GNN-based encoders/decoders are prone to over-smoothing where node representations become indistinguishable after multiple message-passing rounds. No prior GAE simultaneously addresses both the multi-task representation gap and the over-smoothing problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G(V,E) specified by a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}. The encoder hierarchically coarsens the graph across L layers (e.g., 128→64→32 nodes), producing a final coarsened graph whose pooled features serve as the graph-level representation. The decoder hierarchically expands the coarsened graph back to the original size, and its final node feature matrix X'^(L'+1) serves as the node-level representation. Both representations are used with a downstream SVM classifier for their respective tasks.

## Method

HC-GAE introduces a bidirectionally hierarchical encoder-decoder where the encoder uses hard node assignment to partition each graph into isolated subgraphs, applies local graph convolution strictly within each subgraph (preventing cross-subgraph information flow and thus mitigating over-smoothing), and compresses each subgraph into a single coarsened node via a learned mapping vector. The decoder mirrors this with soft node assignment, probabilistically expanding each coarsened node back into its constituent nodes using a learned re-assignment matrix. The training objective combines a local loss (KL divergence on per-subgraph representations, acting as a regularizer that preserves local structural information) with a global loss (reconstruction of both node features and adjacency structure across all decoder layers), broadening the reconstruction target beyond pure node-feature recovery. This design yields effective representations for both node and graph classification from a single self-supervised model.

## Results

On node classification, HC-GAE achieves the best average rank (1.2) across five citation and co-authorship datasets, with accuracies of 87.97% (Cora), 75.29% (CiteSeer), 87.56% (PubMed), 91.07% (Computers), and 92.28% (CS). On graph classification, HC-GAE also attains the best average rank (1.20) across five benchmark datasets, reaching 76.72% (IMDB-B), 51.90% (IMDB-M), 78.13% (PROTEINS), 80.41% (COLLAB), and 92.38% (MUTAG). The method outperforms all baselines on at least 4 out of 5 datasets in both tasks, with the only exceptions being GraphMAE on CS (93.47% vs. 92.28%) and S2GAE on COLLAB (81.02% vs. 80.41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HC-GAE outperforms all six node-classification baselines on 4 of 5 datasets, with the sole exception being GraphMAE on Coauthor-CS (93.47% vs. 92.28%). The largest margins are over SSL-GCN (e.g., +30.68 pp on Cora) and GraphSage (e.g., +13.67 pp on Cora), while the gap over the strongest competitors S2GAE and GraphMAE is modest (1.82 pp on Cora, 0.13 pp on CiteSeer). For graph classification, HC-GAE beats all seven baselines on 4 of 5 datasets, losing only to S2GAE on COLLAB (80.41% vs. 81.02%). The most notable graph-classification gains are over WLSK (+12.24 pp on IMDB-B) and DGCNN (+9.27 pp on IMDB-B), confirming that the hierarchical subgraph decomposition addresses the over-smoothing and topological-missing issues that limit standard GNN-based GAEs like GraphMAE and S2GAE on structure-sensitive graph-level tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HC-GAE extends the graph auto-encoder lineage (VGAE, GraphMAE, S2GAE) by introducing a hierarchical cluster-based architecture that decomposes graphs via hard node assignment during encoding and reconstructs them via soft node assignment during decoding. It departs from standard GNN-based GAEs by restricting graph convolution to individual separated subgraphs, directly targeting the over-smoothing problem that plagues deep stacked convolutions. The dual local-global loss function also distinguishes it from prior GAEs that rely solely on full-graph reconstruction, drawing conceptually from the subgraph-level regularization ideas in self-supervised GNN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean architectural remedy for over-smoothing in GAEs by confining message passing to subgraphs, and provides a single model that yields both node-level and graph-level representations without task-specific tuning. However, the paper does not discuss limitations (e.g., sensitivity to the number of clusters, scalability to very large graphs, or the assumption that hard assignment captures meaningful community structure), and code is not yet released. Open questions include whether the subgraph-restricted convolution generalizes to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the hierarchical depth (L=3 in experiments) can be increased without reintroducing smoothing effects.
