## Problem

TPR-based neural networks struggle with the decomposition problem: they cannot reliably decompose unseen combinatorial data into the structured role-filler-unbinding representations required for accurate symbolic operations. Existing solutions like AID's competitive attention mechanism lack an explicit mechanism to store observed symbolic features during training and retrieve them for novel combinations, causing systematic generalization failures on even simple synthetic tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

D3 is a drop-in layer that replaces the TPR component generation layer in any TPR-based model. It takes the same continuous input vector (e.g., a 50-dim concatenated symbol embedding for SAR, a 179-dim word embedding for sys-bAbI, or a 128-dim visual+text feature for sort-of-CLEVR) and outputs structured TPR components at the same dimensions the downstream TPR operations expect. The key constraint is that roles must be linearly independent and unbinding operators must correlate with their corresponding roles; D3 enforces this through shared dictionaries and learned orthogonal codebook patterns.

## Method

D3 maintains multiple discrete, learnable key-value dictionaries, each explicitly linked to a specific TPR component (role, filler, or unbinding operator). At each time step, it (1) generates a query per component via a single-layer feed-forward network with layer normalization and dropout, (2) performs sparse key access by selecting the top-k nearest L2-normalized codebook keys via inner product, and (3) aggregates the corresponding codebook values weighted by softmax scores, adds a residual projection of the query, and projects to the component dimension. A shared dictionary between roles and unbinding operators enforces the TPR correlation requirement while reducing parameters. The sparse top-k mechanism (optimal at k=2 in ablations) prevents codebook collapse, and the residual connection is critical for training stability—removing it causes dramatic performance degradation.

## Results

On the SAR task, D3 achieves nearly 100% test accuracy, while both FWM and AID fail to generalize unseen symbol combinations. On sys-bAbI, D3 reduces the systematic-difference gap for TPR-RNN from 7.95 to 2.85 (w/ sys diff WER: 3.50 vs. 8.74) and for FWM from 2.06 to 1.21 (w/ sys diff WER: 1.96 vs. 2.85), using fewer additional parameters than AID. On sort-of-CLEVR, D3(w/F) with D_code=256 reaches 99.0/82.1/68.8% (Unary/Binary/Ternary) accuracy, substantially improving over the Linear Transformer baseline (69.3/75.5/56.4%). On WikiText-103, D3(w/oF) with D_code=64 achieves a test perplexity of 37.009, slightly better than Linear Transformer+AID (37.151).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3 decisively outperforms TPR-RNN [28] and FWM [30] on the SAR task, reaching ~100% accuracy where both baselines fail to generalize unseen combinatorial data. Against AID [23], D3 achieves comparable or better performance on sys-bAbI (gap 2.85 vs. 4.92 for TPR-RNN; 1.21 vs. 0.76 for FWM) while adding far fewer parameters (0.17M vs. 0.32M for TPR-RNN; 0.75M vs. 1.23M for FWM). On sort-of-CLEVR, D3(w/F) surpasses the Linear Transformer [10] baseline by a wide margin (e.g., Ternary: 68.8% vs. 56.4%) and also beats AID (68.8% vs. 63.7%), though it still trails the Compositional Transformer [20] on Binary accuracy (82.1% vs. 88.4%). On WikiText-103, D3 slightly improves over Linear Transformer+AID (37.009 vs. 37.151 test perplexity) but remains behind the Delta Network [31] (36.659), indicating that updating-rule design matters more than decomposition for long-context language modeling. In additional sys-bAbI comparisons, D3 outperforms both DAM [22] (gap 2.85 vs. 4.77) and STM [15] (gap 2.85 vs. 3.70), confirming its advantage over prior memory-network approaches on systematic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the Tensor Product Representation (TPR) framework for symbolic binding in neural networks, and discrete representation learning via learnable codebooks (VQ-VAE). The most direct predecessor is AID (Park et al., ICLR 2023), which addresses the same decomposition problem using competitive attention; D3 departs from this by replacing the iterative attention mechanism with a single-pass sparse lookup into discrete, learnable key-value dictionaries explicitly tied to individual TPR components (roles, fillers, unbinding operators). The key-value dictionary design draws on prior discrete key-value architectures (Lample et al. 2019; Trauble et al. 2023), but the explicit per-component dictionary assignment and the shared dictionary between roles and unbinding operators are novel design choices specific to the TPR framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D3 provides a practical, drop-in solution to a known bottleneck in TPR-based models, demonstrated across three architecturally distinct baselines (FWM, TPR-RNN, Linear Transformer) and four task domains. The near-100% accuracy on the SAR task—where both FWM and AID fail—validates the core hypothesis that storing observed symbolic features in discrete dictionaries enables systematic decomposition of unseen combinations. Key open questions remain: the w/F vs. w/o F configuration choice appears task-dependent (fewer labels favor w/F, more labels favor w/o F) without a clear theoretical principle; the sparse key selection introduces O(N_code) overhead that may limit scalability; and the method has not been tested on genuinely large-scale compositional tasks beyond WikiText-103 language modeling.
