## Problem

Forearm sEMG signals alone cannot reliably distinguish hand pressure across different postures because adjacent fingers produce nearly identical muscle activation patterns, while vision-based methods require full hand visibility and fail under occlusion or complex grasps. No prior work had combined 3D hand posture information with sEMG to enable whole-hand (fingertip + palm) pressure estimation under diverse hand-object interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242787/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two modalities: (1) 8-channel forearm sEMG signals converted to 8×32×64 STFT spectrograms (window 256, hop 32, Hamming), and (2) 3D hand pose represented as 21 joint positions derived from 15 joint angles via MANO forward kinematics, rendered as 21×48×48×48 3D Gaussian heatmap volumes. During inference, the 3D pose is obtained from an off-the-shelf RGB hand pose detector (ACR) rather than a data glove. The output is a 9-dimensional vector (5 fingertips + 4 palm quadrants), where each element yields both a binary pressure-presence classification and a continuous pressure value in [0, 20 N] via a Sigmoid-then-RELU mapping.

## Method

PiMForce fuses two parallel feature extractors: a 2D encoder-decoder CNN (32→128→256 filters) processes sEMG spectrograms into a 512-dim vector, while a 3D ResNet34 (layer structure [3,4,6,3], channels [64,128,256,512]) processes 3D hand-pose heatmap volumes into a second 512-dim vector. The two 512-dim features are concatenated to 1024 dimensions, passed through two FC layers (1024→256) with batch normalization and ReLU, and finally mapped through a Sigmoid-activated FC layer to 9 outputs. Training uses a joint classification-regression objective: cross-entropy loss for per-region pressure presence plus L2 regression loss for pressure magnitude, balanced by a hyperparameter λ. The key insight is that 3D hand posture disambiguates the spatial source of pressure when sEMG patterns are nearly identical across different finger postures (e.g., index-press vs. middle-press), and the 3D heatmap representation outperforms raw joint-angle inputs by providing a spatially structured encoding compatible with 3D convolutions.

## Results

On the PiMForce dataset (21 participants, 22 hand-object interactions), PiMForce achieves an R² of 88.86%, NRMSE of 6.65%, and classification accuracy of 83.17% for whole-hand pressure estimation across 9 regions (5 fingertips + 4 palm areas). In the cross-user setting (17 train / 4 test participants), the method maintains strong performance with R² of 70.06%, NRMSE of 10.70%, and accuracy of 72.01%. Against the vision-based PressureVision++ baseline on plane and pinch interactions, PiMForce achieves R² of 66.71% and NRMSE of 9.27%, substantially outperforming the vision-only approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the sEMG-only baseline (Zhang et al., ACM TOG 2022), PiMForce wins on every metric: R² improves from 83.49% to 88.86%, NRMSE drops from 8.07% to 6.65%, and accuracy rises from 77.83% to 83.17% in the same-user setting. The cross-user gap is even more pronounced, with R² jumping from 47.90% to 70.06% and accuracy from 57.40% to 72.01%, indicating that 3D hand posture information substantially aids generalization to unseen users. Against the vision-based PressureVision++ (Grady et al., WACV 2024) on plane and pinch interactions, PiMForce achieves R² of 66.71% versus 40.30%, NRMSE of 9.27% versus 32.95%, and accuracy of 82.20% versus 67.90%, a large margin driven by the vision method's reliance on unoccluded fingertip visibility. The ablation comparing sEMG + raw joint angles (84.22% R²) to the full 3D heatmap representation (88.86% R²) confirms that the 3D spatial encoding of hand pose, rather than simple angle values, is what yields the meaningful gain over sEMG alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sEMG-based hand force estimation line (Zhang et al., ACM TOG 2022) by adding 3D hand posture as a complementary input modality, addressing the well-known ambiguity where similar muscle activation patterns arise across different hand postures. It also positions itself against vision-based pressure estimation (PressureVision/PressureVision++), which requires unoccluded hand visibility and struggles with complex grasps. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D hand pose not as the primary signal but as a spatial disambiguator that resolves which hand region is generating a given sEMG pattern, enabling whole-hand (fingertip + palm) pressure estimation rather than the fingertip-only or single-grip-force scope of prior sEMG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first multimodal dataset simultaneously capturing 3D hand pose, 8-channel sEMG, and 9-region hand pressure across 22 interaction types and 21 participants, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior dataset combined all three modalities. Practically, the inference pipeline requires only a single RGB camera and a forearm sEMG armband (no pressure glove), making it deployable for AR/VR interaction, prosthetic control, and ergonomic monitoring. However, cross-user performance degrades substantially (R² drops from ~89% to ~70%), and complex grasps (Power Sphere, Fixed Hook) remain challenging, leaving open questions about generalization to unseen users and interaction types without per-user calibration.
