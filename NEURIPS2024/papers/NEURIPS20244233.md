## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods rely on fixed, hand-crafted causal context models (checkerboard, channel-wise, space-channel) for autoregressive entropy coding, with no mechanism to explicitly guide the encoder to place important information in earlier decoding stages. Additionally, recent SOTA LIC methods adopt Transformer-based auto-encoders whose attention computation is poorly optimized on current hardware, leading to high inference latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244233/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input (trained on 256×256 crops from Open Images, evaluated on Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC Professional Validation datasets) and produces a compressed bitstream (measured in bits per pixel) and a reconstructed image. The latent representation is 320-channel with a 192-channel hyperprior, segmented into 5 unevenly grouped slices for autoregressive decoding. The model operates at multiple rate points controlled by a single λ hyperparameter (0.3 to 15 for PSNR optimization).

## Method

The core contribution is the Causal Context Adjustment loss (CCA-loss), which introduces an auxiliary entropy model G(ẑ) that predicts the next latent slice using only the hyperprior, alongside the main entropy model F(ŷ₁, ẑ) that also uses the decoded causal context. The loss maximizes the cross-entropy gap between these two models, explicitly forcing the encoder to encode information-gainful content into earlier autoregressive stages so that later slices become more predictable from the causal context. This is combined with an unevenly channel-wise grouped schedule (power law C(i) = N·i^k with k=1.7) that allocates fewer channels to early slices, reducing both parameter count and inference latency. The auto-encoder uses a CNN architecture built from NAF-blocks and residual blocks rather than Transformers, and the auxiliary entropy model is discarded at test time, so CCA-loss adds zero inference overhead.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, the proposed CNN-based LIC model achieves a BD-rate of −13.87% relative to VVC (vtm-12.1), outperforming the strongest Transformer-based competitor Liu et al. [30] (−11.88%) by approximately 2 percentage points while using 201 ms total inference latency versus 255 ms. The model uses 64.89M parameters and 615.93 GFLOPs, compared to 75.90M and 700.65 GFLOPs for Liu et al. Rate-distortion curves (Fig. 3) confirm consistent superiority over all compared methods on Kodak, CLIC Professional Validation, and Tecnick datasets under the PSNR metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three hand-crafted codecs (VVC, BPG, WebP) and all six learned baselines (Ballé 2018, Cheng 2020, Xie 2021, ELIC, Liu 2023, Zou 2022, Zhu 2022) on PSNR rate-distortion curves across Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick. Against the closest Transformer-based competitor Liu et al. [30], the method gains ~2% BD-rate (−13.87% vs. −11.88%) while reducing total inference latency by 21% (201 ms vs. 255 ms) and using fewer parameters (64.89M vs. 75.90M). Compared to Zou et al. [49], the method achieves a 9.86-point BD-rate improvement (−13.87% vs. −4.01%) with 53% less latency. Against Zhu et al. [48], the gain is 10.87 points in BD-rate with 26% less latency. The ablation (Table 1) further shows that CCA-loss alone improves BD-rate by 1.41 points on evenly grouped and 2.61 points on unevenly grouped channel-wise autoregressive models, confirming the loss's contribution independent of the architecture choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based learned image compression framework (Ballé et al. 2017, 2018) and its autoregressive entropy model lineage (Minnen et al. 2018; Minnen & Singh 2020) by introducing a new training signal—the Causal Context Adjustment loss—that explicitly optimizes how information is distributed across autoregressive stages, rather than relying on hand-crafted context schedules. It also builds on the unevenly grouped channel-wise strategy from Elic (He et al. 2022) but departs from the Transformer-based auto-encoder trend by adopting a CNN architecture with NAF-Blocks, motivated by hardware efficiency. The key conceptual departure is treating the causal context as a learnable quantity guided by an auxiliary entropy model, rather than a fixed architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CCA-loss offers a principled, architecture-agnostic mechanism for improving autoregressive entropy modeling that could be adopted across LIC pipelines, and the demonstrated 20%+ inference latency reduction while achieving better BD-rate than Transformer-based SOTA methods makes the approach practically attractive for deployment. However, the paper acknowledges it has not fully characterized the information redistribution dynamics induced by the unevenly grouped schedule combined with CCA-loss, leaving open questions about optimal channel allocation and whether the approach generalizes to spatial or hybrid context models. The reliance on a single training dataset (Open Images) and the absence of error bars or multi-seed evaluation limit confidence in the exact magnitude of gains.
