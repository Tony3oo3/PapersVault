## Problem

Despite the rapid emergence of RNA language models and deep learning methods for RNA tasks, no standardized benchmark exists to enable fair cross-method comparison, unlike DNA and protein which have established benchmarks. Additionally, the impact of key architectural components—tokenization strategy and positional encoding—on RNA language model performance remains unexplored, leaving practitioners without guidance on design choices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242948/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans 13 tasks at two granularities: nucleotide-level (per-position labels matching sequence length, e.g., base pair matrices, splice site labels) and sequence-level (one label per sequence, e.g., APA ratio, ncRNA class). Sequence lengths range from 23 to 1182 nucleotides. Three distinct prediction pipelines handle the output structure: sequence-level tasks use a [CLS] token or attentive weighted sum passed through an MLP; nucleotide-level tasks apply a per-position MLP; and nucleotide-nucleotide tasks (SSP, CMP, DMP) compute a self outer product of nucleotide representations followed by a small ResNet to produce the pairwise matrix.

## Method

BEACON curates 13 tasks across structural analysis, functional studies, and engineering applications, and systematically evaluates naive supervised encoders (CNN, ResNet, LSTM) alongside six families of pretrained RNA language models (RNA-FM, SpliceBERT, UTR-LM, 3UTRBERT, RNA-MSM, RNABERT) under unified fine-tuning protocols. A key empirical contribution is an ablation study over tokenization (single nucleotide, BPE, overlapping 6-mer, non-overlapping k-mer) and positional encoding (APE, ALiBi, RoPE), revealing that single nucleotide tokenization outperforms all alternatives on 11/13 tasks and ALiBi surpasses RoPE and APE for RNA sequences. Building on these findings, BEACON-B is proposed as a lightweight BERT-based baseline using single nucleotide tokenization and ALiBi, pre-trained on filtered human ncRNA from RNACentral in only 10.4 GPU days (vs. 240 for RNA-FM), yet matching or exceeding larger pretrained models on several tasks including contact map and distance map prediction.

## Results

Across 13 RNA tasks, pre-trained RNA language models surpass the previous task-specific SOTA on 8 out of 13 tasks. RNA-FM achieves the best performance on Secondary Structure Prediction (68.50% F1), ncRNA classification (96.81% ACC), and Modification Prediction (94.98% AUC), while SpliceBERT-MS1024 leads on Distance Map Prediction (55.89% R²), Splice Site Prediction (48.52% ACC@K), and Vaccine Degradation Prediction (0.313 MCRMSE). The proposed BEACON-B, pre-trained in only 10.4 GPU days (vs. 240 for RNA-FM), achieves competitive results across all tasks and outperforms other pre-trained RNA LMs on Contact Map Prediction (60.81% P@L) and Distance Map Prediction (56.28% R²). Notably, simple supervised models like LSTM (83.94% R² on MRL) and ResNet (59.59% P@L on CMP) remain strong baselines, outperforming several language models on individual tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNA-FM [13] is the strongest overall pre-trained LM, winning SSP (68.50% F1 vs. 65.4% UFold SOTA), ncRNA (96.81% vs. 85.73% GCN SOTA), and Modif (94.98% AUC vs. 84% MultiRM SOTA), but underperforms SpliceBERT-MS1024 [15] on DMP (51.45 vs. 55.89 R²) and SPL (34.84 vs. 48.52 ACC@K). SpliceBERT-MS1024 [15] dominates structural and splicing tasks (DMP 55.89%, SPL 48.52%, VDP 0.313 MCRMSE) but is weak on Modif (53.45% AUC) and MRL (67.15% R²). UTR-LM-TE&EL [18] excels on APA (72.09% R², beating APARENT SOTA of 50.82%) and CMP (60.32% P@L) but lags on ncRNA (81.33%) and Modif (59.70%). 3UTRBERT [119] variants (3–6mer) are competitive on Modif (95.14% AUC for 3mer) and ncRNA (92.88–93.12%) but show high variance on SSP (38.56–60.37% F1). RNA-MSM [124] is the strongest on CRI-On (34.92% SC, beating DeepCRISPR SOTA of 44.1%... actually below it) and PRS (56.94% R²). The proposed BEACON-B matches or exceeds all other pre-trained LMs on CMP (60.81% P@L) and DMP (56.28% R²) while using only 10.4 GPU days versus 56–240 for competitors. Among naive models, LSTM [78] is the strongest (beating CNN and ResNet on 9/13 tasks), achieving 83.94% R² on MRL and 94.83% AUC on Modif, rivaling or exceeding several language models. Overall, pre-trained LMs collectively beat the per-task literature SOTA on 8/13 tasks, with the largest gains on ncRNA (+11.08% ACC), Modif (+10.98% AUC), and APA (+21.27% R²), while the SOTA still leads on CMP (66 vs. 61.20), CRI-On (44.1 vs. 34.92), and CRI-Off (12.6 vs. 8.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first comprehensive RNA benchmark, drawing direct inspiration from established DNA (BEND, Genomic Benchmarks) and protein (ProteinGym, PEER) benchmarks that have standardized evaluation in those fields. It repurposes existing RNA language models (RNA-FM, SpliceBERT, UTR-LM, 3UTRBERT, RNA-MSM) and classical architectures (CNN, ResNet, LSTM) under a unified 13-task evaluation protocol, while the component analysis transplants NLP techniques (ALiBi, RoPE, BPE, K-mer tokenization) into the RNA setting. The proposed BEACON-B baseline is a novel combination of single-nucleotide tokenization and ALiBi on a BERT backbone, motivated by ablation findings rather than architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEACON addresses a critical gap: RNA, a central component of the molecular biology pipeline, lacked the standardized multi-task benchmarks that DNA and protein research already enjoy, making fair cross-method comparison nearly impossible. The practical impact is significant — BEACON-B achieves competitive performance across 13 tasks with only ~10 GPU days of pre-training (vs. 240 for RNA-FM), lowering the barrier for the community. Open questions remain: the benchmark does not yet cover all RNA biology (e.g., 3D structure prediction, RNA-RNA interactions), evaluation is limited to predictive accuracy without interpretability or robustness metrics, and the influence of pre-training data scale and hyperparameter sensitivity on task-specific performance needs further systematic exploration.
