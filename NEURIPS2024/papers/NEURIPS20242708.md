## Problem

LUT-based image restoration methods replace expensive convolution with direct memory lookups, but the LUT size grows exponentially with kernel size—a 3×3 kernel with 8-bit data demands 64 ZB (full) or 1.7 TB (uniformly sampled) of storage, far exceeding edge-device memory. This storage explosion prevents LUT mapping of the larger receptive fields and multi-channel convolutions that modern CNNs require, limiting prior LUT methods to small kernels and modest accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8-bit RGB images (low-resolution for ×4 super-resolution, AWGN-corrupted for denoising at σ∈{15,25,50}, JPEG-compressed at QF=10 for deblocking) and outputs the corresponding restored image at native resolution. Each color channel is processed independently through parallel MSB and LSB branches; the 8-bit input is split into 6 MSBs (range [−m, m−1]) and 2 LSBs (range [0, k]), and the two branch outputs are summed. For super-resolution, the final PwLUT produces r² output channels to feed a pixel-shuffle upsampler.

## Method

TinyLUT combines two storage-reduction mechanisms. The Separable Mapping Strategy (SMS) decomposes an n×n depthwise or pointwise convolution into n² (or C_in) independent 1×1 sub-operations, each mapped by a 1-D LUT, and reconstructs the output by averaging the parallel LUT results—reducing LUT storage from s^n to s·n and converting an infeasible 9-D table into a set of 1-D tables. The Dynamic Discretization Mechanism (DDM) splits the 8-bit activation into MSB and LSB branches and introduces learnable clipping parameters α trained via straight-through estimation with L2 regularization, adaptively compressing the quantization range per layer to shrink the LUT index space by a further 4.48×. The overall architecture stacks depthwise-separable LUT blocks (DwLUT + PwLUT with skip connections) inspired by SRLUT and DnCNN; TinyLUT-F uses 7 stacked blocks (171 KB) while TinyLUT-S uses a single ScLUT plus two PwLUTs (37 KB), achieving 28.01 dB average PSNR on five SISR benchmarks with 5× lower latency than FSRCNN on Raspberry Pi 4B.

## Results

On the ×4 single image super-resolution task, TinyLUT-F achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 28.01/0.7956 across five standard benchmarks (Set5, Set14, Urban100, BSD100, Manga109) using only 171KB of LUT storage, outperforming all prior LUT-based methods. The compact TinyLUT-S variant (37KB) reaches 27.13/0.7710, still surpassing SRLUT-S (26.84/0.7631) with 35× less storage. On Raspberry Pi 4B, TinyLUT-F runs in 387ms, achieving 5.5× lower latency than FSRCNN (2143ms) while delivering 0.45 dB higher PSNR. For image denoising, TinyLUT-F attains 28.93 dB average PSNR on Set12/BSD68 with 187KB storage, outperforming MuLUT-SDYEHO-X2 (28.44 dB) at 5.2× less memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TinyLUT-F (28.01 dB avg PSNR, 171KB) outperforms all LUT-based baselines: it surpasses SPF-LUT+DFC [35] by +0.13 dB with 12× less storage, RCLUT [34] by +0.42 dB with 9× less storage, MuLUT-SDY-X2 [14] by +0.53 dB with 24× less storage, SPLUT-L [13] by +0.59 dB with 108× less storage, and SRLUT-S [15] by +1.17 dB with 7.6× less storage. Against DNN baselines, TinyLUT-F slightly trails VDSR [24] by −0.06 dB but uses 15.5× less storage and runs 276× faster on Raspberry 4B (387ms vs 106972ms); it lags CARN-M [26] by −0.59 dB but is 45× faster on Raspberry 4B. Compared to FSRCNN [8], TinyLUT-F achieves +0.45 dB higher PSNR at 5.5× lower latency on Raspberry 4B. For denoising (Table 3), TinyLUT-F (28.93 dB avg) beats MuLUT-SDYEHO-X2 [48] by +0.49 dB with 5.2× less storage, and approaches DnCNN [7] (29.61 dB) at 12× less storage. For deblocking (Table 4), TinyLUT-F (28.74/28.67 PSNR-B on Classic5/LIVE1) matches ARCNN [52] (28.76/28.77) while using 2.2× less storage than SPF-LUT+DFC [35].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LUT-based image restoration line initiated by SRLUT (CVPR 2021), which first mapped a small CNN's receptive field to a uniformly sampled lookup table, and builds on DnCNN's residual learning architecture. Prior multi-LUT approaches (MuLUT, SPLUT, RCLUT, SPF-LUT) addressed accuracy by increasing receptive field or cascading tables, but all inherited the exponential storage growth of n-dimensional LUTs. TinyLUT departs fundamentally by proposing a Separable Mapping Strategy that decomposes an n×n convolution kernel into n² parallel 1×1 LUT lookups (converting exponential to linear storage) and a Dynamic Discretization Mechanism that splits 8-bit activations into MSB/LSB branches with learnable clipping parameters, drawing on activation quantization ideas from PACT and PAMS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The storage explosion problem (64 ZB for a full 3×3 LUT) has been the primary barrier preventing LUT-based methods from scaling beyond 2×2 kernels on edge devices; TinyLUT's reduction to ~8 KB for the same configuration makes on-chip cache deployment feasible and opens the door to mapping deeper CNNs. The practical demonstration of 5× lower inference latency on Raspberry Pi 4B while matching or exceeding CNN accuracy (FSRCNN) is directly relevant to IoT and mobile deployment. However, the approach is limited to convolutional architectures and 8-bit data, and the authors acknowledge that extending the mapping to Transformer or Mamba-based restoration models remains an open challenge.
