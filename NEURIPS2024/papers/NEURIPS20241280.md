## Problem

Standard kernel-based methods suffer from a saturation effect: when the target function is very smooth relative to the kernel (strong target-kernel alignment, γ>1), the learning rate plateaus at O(n^{-2α/(2α+1)}) regardless of how smooth the target becomes. While truncated kernel ridge regression was shown to eliminate this saturation for squared loss, it remained unclear whether the saturation effect could be resolved for the broader family of Lipschitz loss functions used in regression and classification. This paper provides a unified theoretical analysis showing that spectral truncation eliminates saturation across a rich loss function family.

## Input / Output

The method operates under a fixed design setting, taking n covariate vectors {x_i} and response values {y_i} as input, along with a positive semi-definite kernel K and a convex, locally Lipschitz loss L (squared, check, hinge, logistic, Huber, or exponential). The output is a function estimate f̂ in either the full RKHS H_K (standard method) or a reduced r-dimensional RKHS H_{K_r} (truncated method), evaluated via L(P_n)-error and excess risk. The truncation level r is a critical hyperparameter that must be tuned to balance estimation error against approximation bias, with the optimal choice depending on the alignment parameter γ.

## Method

The paper derives high-probability upper bounds for both the standard kernel estimator and a truncated kernel estimator using the empirical kernel complexity function R(δ) = √(n^{-1}∑min{δ²,μ_j}) and empirical process techniques, rather than relying on closed-form solutions. The truncated method estimates within a reduced RKHS H_{K_r} constructed from the first r eigenvectors of the kernel matrix, introducing an approximation bias term ∑_{j=r+1}^n ξ_j^{*2} that trades off against reduced estimation error. The key mechanism is that under strong alignment (γ>1), the optimal truncation r ∝ δ_{n,r}^{-2/(γα)} yields a rate of O(n^{-2γα/(2γα+1)}) that improves continuously with γ, while the standard method's rate is capped at O(n^{-2α/(2α+1)}). Minimax lower bounds via Fano's method confirm that the truncated estimator is minimax-optimal for all γ≥1/2, while the standard estimator is suboptimal for γ>1.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution establishes that under polynomial eigenvalue decay (μ_j ∝ j^{-α}), the standard kernel method (KM) achieves a learning rate of O(n^{-2ηα/(2ηα+1)}) where η = min{γ,1}, which saturates at O(n^{-2α/(2α+1)}) for γ > 1. In contrast, the truncated kernel method (TKM) achieves O(n^{-2γα/(2γα+1)}), which improves continuously with alignment γ and approaches the parametric rate O(1/n) as γ → ∞, thereby eliminating the saturation effect. On the Wine Quality dataset (quantile regression with check loss, n=500 train / 500 test, Laplacian kernel), TKM consistently outperforms KM: at τ=0.5, TKM achieves MSE 0.454±0.045 vs. KM's 0.483±0.035, and at τ=0.7, TKM achieves 0.530±0.046 vs. KM's 0.638±0.073.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard kernel-based method (KM) and the proposed truncated kernel-based method (TKM) under a unified theoretical framework. Theoretically, under the just-aligned (γ=1/2) and weakly-aligned (1/2<γ≤1) regimes, both methods share the same minimax-optimal rate O(n^{-2ηα/(2ηα+1)}), so TKM matches KM. Under the over-aligned regime (γ>1), KM suffers the saturation effect and is stuck at O(n^{-2α/(2α+1)}), while TKM achieves the strictly faster rate O(n^{-2γα/(2γα+1)}) and is minimax-optimal — a clear win for TKM. Empirically on the Wine Quality dataset, TKM outperforms KM at every quantile level tested (τ=0.3, 0.5, 0.7), with the largest gap at τ=0.7 (MSE 0.530 vs. 0.638, roughly 17% relative improvement). The synthetic experiments (Figure 1) further confirm that the TKM advantage grows as the RKHS becomes richer (smaller α, stronger alignment), while the two methods converge in performance for very small RKHS (large α, weak alignment), consistent with the theoretical trade-off between model complexity and target-kernel alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the truncated kernel ridge regression framework of Amini et al. (2022) and Amini (2021), which were restricted to the squared loss where closed-form solutions enable direct analytic proofs. The key departure is generalizing to a rich family of Lipschitz losses (hinge, logistic, check, Huber, exponential) where no explicit solution exists, necessitating kernel complexity functions and empirical process techniques rather than algebraic manipulation of the KRR solution. The minimax lower bound framework builds on Yang et al. (2017), extending their just-aligned (γ=1/2) result to the full range γ≥1/2 and confirming a conjecture from Amini et al. (2022) about minimax optimality of the truncated estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically guaranteed solution to the saturation effect for general kernel-based methods beyond KRR, showing that spectral truncation of the kernel matrix allows continuous rate improvement as target-kernel alignment strengthens, approaching the parametric rate. The minimax optimality result rigorously validates the truncated estimator as the correct tool for strongly-aligned settings. Open questions include the misspecified regime (0<γ<1/2), data-driven selection of an appropriate kernel balancing complexity and alignment, and extension to random design settings, all of which remain unresolved.
