## Problem

3D bounding boxes cannot capture the fine-grained geometry of irregularly shaped objects, while scene-level occupancy grids are computationally infeasible at the resolution needed for object-level detail in large driving scenes. No prior work provides a robust method to complete high-resolution object-centric occupancy from noisy, drift-prone detection and tracking sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243683/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a 32-frame sequence of LiDAR point clouds paired with noisy 7-DoF bounding boxes (generated by off-the-shelf detectors like FSD/CenterPoint and ImmortalTracker), where each frame's points are cropped to the corresponding RoI. It outputs a complete object-centric occupancy volume at 0.2 m voxel resolution defined in each object's local coordinate system (so grid dimensions vary per object), plus a refined 3D bounding box and objectness score per proposal. Because the decoder is implicit, occupancy can be queried at any continuous position, enabling extrapolation beyond the predicted RoI into the GT box during evaluation.

## Method

The architecture uses a dual-branch RoI encoder (FSD's SIR module) that encodes each proposal in both local and global coordinate systems, preserving shape geometry and motion dynamics respectively. A causal transformer aggregates temporal information across the global branch to handle detection drifts, and the enriched global features are fused with local latents via concatenation and an MLP. A lightweight implicit shape decoder (MLP) maps the fixed-length latent embedding plus a 3D query position to an occupancy probability, enabling dynamic-size volume generation without dense convolutions and supporting end-to-end joint training of shape completion and detection refinement. Training uses balanced sampling of 1024 voxel-center queries per RoI (512 occupied, 512 free) with BCE occupancy loss, L1 bbox loss, and BCE objectness loss, supervised on an automatically constructed object-centric occupancy dataset from WOD LiDAR scans.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation set, applying the method to 7-frame FSD achieves new state-of-the-art online detection of 83.3% AP and 75.7% APH (L1/L2). For 1-frame CenterPoint, the method yields +8.6% L1 AP improvement (73.2→81.8), substantially exceeding MoDAR's +3.2% on the same base. For shape completion, the method achieves 69.15% IoU on GT tracklets vs. 61.35% for the direct-accumulation baseline, and maintains 64.92% IoU under noisy GT inputs where the baseline drops to 50.39%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all listed baselines on the WOD validation set. Against MoDAR [14], the closest temporal-enhancement competitor, the paper achieves +8.6% L1 AP on 1-frame CenterPoint versus MoDAR's +3.2%, and +4.1% on 1-frame FSD versus MoDAR's +3.6% on 3-frame SWFormer. The 7-frame FSD+Ours configuration (83.3% L1 AP) surpasses MPPNet [3] (82.7%), HEDNet [40] (81.1%), and FSD++ [9] (81.4%) while using a more flexible temporal window. For shape completion (Table 1), the method beats the direct-accumulation baseline by 7.8% IoU on GT tracklets (69.15 vs. 61.35) and by 18.6% IoU on noisy FSD tracklets (62.84 vs. 44.28), demonstrating robustness to detection drift. The method also generalizes without retraining: applying it to FSDv2 [7] tracklets (trained on CP and FSD) still yields 83.2% L1 AP, exceeding the 1-frame FSD baseline by 3.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from scene-level occupancy prediction (Occ3D, SurroundOcc, Scene as Occupancy) by restricting the occupancy grid to individual object bounding boxes, enabling higher voxel resolution (0.2 m vs. 0.4 m) without the computational burden of full-scene grids. For the decoding mechanism, it borrows the implicit neural representation paradigm from DeepSDF and NeRF, replacing dense convolution with an MLP-based shape decoder that handles dynamic-sized RoIs and supports continuous-position queries. On the temporal side, it follows the causal-attention lineage of MoDAR and MPPNet for aggregating long history sequences, but operates at the object-proposal level rather than the scene level, and uses FSD's SIR module as its RoI encoder. The paper also contrasts with surface-based shape completion (Scan2CAD, SLAM++) by arguing that volumetric occupancy is more directly useful for driving tasks like collision avoidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the first object-centric occupancy dataset and the demonstration that occupancy features can substantially improve 3D detection (especially for distant, sparse objects with +13.9% L2 mAP at 50+ m) positions this as a practical bridge between coarse bbox perception and expensive full-scene occupancy. The rigid-body assumption and evaluation limited to vehicles on WOD leave open whether the approach generalizes to deformable objects (pedestrians, cyclists) or other datasets. A key open question is whether the implicit decoder's extrapolation capability (predicting occupancy outside the noisy RoI) can be exploited for tighter planning margins in real-time autonomous driving stacks.
