## Problem

Existing offline-to-online (O2O) RL methods are tightly coupled to specific offline algorithms (e.g., CQL-to-SAC or TD3+BC-to-SAC) and cannot generalize across heterogeneous offline methods. The paper identifies two fundamental mismatches that hinder general O2O learning: an evaluation mismatch where pessimistic offline critics cause dramatic Q-value jumps when switched to optimistic online evaluation, and an improvement mismatch where the actor-critic alignment learned under offline objectives (e.g., behavior regularization) is inconsistent with online policy update rules, leading to erroneous early-stage updates.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an offline dataset of state-action-reward sequences, a pre-trained offline policy from any algorithm (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, AWAC, or even non-standard approaches like Decision Transformer), and online environment interactions. It outputs a fine-tuned online policy and an aligned Q-value critic, supporting three distinct online algorithms: SAC (stochastic off-policy), TD3 (deterministic off-policy), and PPO (on-policy). Notably, for policy constraint methods like TD3+BC where the critic is already evaluated in an online-compatible way, the policy re-evaluation step can be omitted, and for sequence-modeling policies where no critic exists, re-evaluation is mandatory.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages before and during online fine-tuning. First, policy re-evaluation uses fitted Q-evaluation (FQE) to re-estimate the offline policy's Q-values in an optimistic manner, with a theoretical error bound under single-policy concentrability (Corollary 4.2). Second, value alignment calibrates the re-evaluated critic to match the offline policy's action probabilities: for O2SAC, it uses the Q-value of the most probable action as an anchor and suppresses overestimated Q-values via log-probability differences (Eq. 12); for O2TD3, it models Q-values around the deterministic policy action as a Gaussian and calibrates via distance-based decay (Eq. 15); for O2PPO, it introduces an auxiliary advantage function proportional to the cross-entropy between the current and offline policy (Eq. 17). Third, constrained fine-tuning formulates online learning as a constrained MDP solved via Lagrangian duality (RCPO-style), penalizing KL divergence (SAC) or MSE (TD3) between the current policy and a reference policy (the best historical policy), with the constraint threshold τ and multiplier λ updated adaptively to ensure stable exploration while preventing distribution-shift-induced performance collapse.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks (12 datasets), O2SAC achieves the highest total normalized score of 1230.41 with an average improvement of +225.54 over offline initialization, outperforming Off2On (1204.5, +182.93), O2TD3 (1171.66, +170.82), and O2PPO (1126.48, +138.58). On AntMaze navigation tasks, O2PPO achieves the largest improvement (+77.16) over offline scores, surpassing PEX (+46.8), Cal-QL (+61.2), and O2SAC (+61.2). The method also demonstrates strong transferability, successfully fine-tuning policies initialized from heterogeneous offline algorithms (TD3+BC, CQL, IQL, ODT) to three different online algorithms (SAC, TD3, PPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

O2SAC outperforms all SAC-based baselines (Off2On, Cal-QL, ACA) on total MuJoCo score (1230.41 vs. 1204.5, 1099.73, 1112.22) and achieves the largest average improvement (+225.54 vs. +182.93, +113.3, +150.0), while also exhibiting more stable convergence without the dramatic initial performance drops seen in Off2On. O2TD3 achieves the best total among TD3-based methods (1171.66) and outperforms PROTO+TD3 in both stability and final performance (Appendix C.4). O2PPO substantially outperforms the policy-constraint baselines AWAC (1083.74), IQL (1037.15), and PEX (1046.45) on MuJoCo, and achieves the best AntMaze improvement (+77.16 vs. +46.8 for PEX, +61.2 for Cal-QL). On AntMaze, O2PPO reaches 184.3 total score vs. 168.2 for Cal-QL and 148.0 for O2SAC, demonstrating particular strength on sparse-reward navigation tasks. The paper also shows that O2SAC initialized from ODT (a non-standard offline method) recovers and surpasses ODT's offline performance, confirming generalizability across heterogeneous offline algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the behavior-regularized MDP framework of Xu et al. [47] to unify the analysis of offline RL methods, and on RCPO [41] for the Lagrangian-based constrained optimization in fine-tuning. It departs from prior O2O work (Off2On, Cal-QL, ACA, PEX, PROTO) which typically designs fine-tuning strategies tied to a specific offline algorithm (e.g., CQL→SAC or TD3+BC→SAC), by identifying two general mismatches—evaluation and improvement—that arise from the structural difference between offline and online policy evaluation/update objectives. The policy re-evaluation step repurposes FQE (off-policy evaluation) in a novel role: not for evaluating a policy's return, but for reconstructing an optimistic critic that can serve as a stable initialization for online fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the method-agnosticism: practitioners can take a policy trained with any offline algorithm (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, AWAC, even transformer-based ODT) and fine-tune it with SAC, TD3, or PPO without redesigning the fine-tuning procedure. This is particularly relevant for high-stakes deployments (autonomous driving, healthcare) where a well-trained offline policy must be safely adapted to a live environment. Limitations include evaluation only on D4RL continuous-control benchmarks (MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze), no discrete action spaces, and the reference policy update in constrained fine-tuning relies on periodic environment evaluation, which may be infeasible in some settings. Open questions include whether the framework extends to model-based O2O RL, multi-agent settings, and whether the alignment step can be made more efficient for very large action spaces.
