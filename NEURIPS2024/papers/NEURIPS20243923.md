## Problem

Existing GNN explainers are predominantly instance-level, identifying which subgraph components influence a single prediction but failing to uncover the combinatorial reasoning a GNN learned globally across the training data. The closest global explainer, GLGExplainer, depends on instance-level explainers for concept mining, uses non-interpretable vector-level concepts, and shows poor robustness across data splits. No end-to-end global explainer existed that independently mines subgraph-level concepts and translates GNN predictions into human-interpretable boolean logic.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained black-box message-passing GNN (e.g., GCN, GAT, GIN with 3 layers) and a set of labeled graphs as input, and produces one boolean formula per class label over subgraph-level concepts. The concepts are rooted computation trees (L-hop receptive fields) rather than arbitrary subgraphs, and the formulas are restricted to boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT, XOR) for human interpretability. A graph is evaluated against a formula by checking the presence/absence of each concept tree within its computation-tree decomposition.

## Method

GRAPHTRAIL exploits the structural property that message-passing GNNs decompose each graph into n rooted computation trees, reducing the concept candidate space from exponential (all subgraphs) to linear (one tree per node). It enumerates unique computation trees across the dataset using linear-time rooted-tree isomorphism via depth-first canonical labeling, then computes global Shapley values for each tree using Kernel-SHAP sampling to rank their contribution to GNN predictions. The top-k trees are selected as concepts, and a concept-vector representation enables O(|S|) graph-embedding lookups during Shapley evaluation instead of full message-passing. Finally, symbolic regression (PySR, a multi-population evolutionary algorithm) searches over boolean formulas of the k concepts, minimizing a multi-objective loss that balances disagreement with GNN predictions against formula complexity (number of operators and variables).

## Results

GRAPHTRAIL achieves a fidelity of 0.87±0.02 on BAMultiShapes, 0.82±0.09 on MUTAG, 0.72±0.01 on Mutagenicity, and 0.72±0.02 on NCI1, where fidelity measures the ratio of graphs whose GNN prediction is correctly reproduced by the extracted boolean formula. The efficient variant GRAPHTRAIL-S (using a stratified subset for Shapley computation) achieves nearly identical performance (0.86, 0.78, 0.72, 0.72 respectively). Across all GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN) and pooling layers (SUM, MEAN, MAX), GRAPHTRAIL consistently outperforms GLGExplainer, with the largest gap on BAMultiShapes (e.g., 0.81 vs. 0.49 for GCN+SUM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GLGExplainer [Azzolin et al., ICLR 2023], the only prior method that fits a logical formula to GNN predictions. GRAPHTRAIL outperforms GLGExplainer on all four datasets: by +0.39 on BAMultiShapes (0.87 vs. 0.48), +0.08 on MUTAG (0.82 vs. 0.74), +0.10 on Mutagenicity (0.72 vs. 0.62), and +0.15 on NCI1 (0.72 vs. 0.57). The interpretable variant GLGExplainer-iso (which replaces cluster vectors with exemplar subgraphs) performs even worse, dropping to 0.00 on BAMultiShapes and 0.49 on NCI1, confirming that the cluster-purity assumption underlying GLGExplainer's interpretability step is rarely valid. The advantage is consistent across all GNN architectures and pooling layers tested (Table 2), with GRAPHTRAIL winning in every one of the 12 architecture×pool combinations. The paper also notes that GLGExplainer's originally reported results could not be reproduced, partly due to test-data leakage in the Mutagenicity dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three previously separate ideas—computation tree decomposition from GNN theory, Shapley value-based feature attribution, and symbolic regression—into a unified end-to-end pipeline for global GNN explanation. It departs most directly from GLGExplainer [Azzolin et al., ICLR 2023], which also targets boolean formulas over subgraph concepts but relies on instance-level explainers (e.g., PGExplainer) to supply concepts and represents them as non-interpretable cluster embeddings. The key conceptual departure is treating rooted computation trees (the actual computational units of message-passing GNNs) as the atomic concepts, which both reduces the search space from exponential to linear and eliminates the dependency on local explainers whose global coherence is questionable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAPHTRAIL provides the first end-to-end global GNN explainer that outputs human-interpretable logical rules without requiring a separate concept-mining stage, making it directly applicable in transparency-critical domains (healthcare, finance, law enforcement). The paper's empirical finding that GLGExplainer's cluster-purity assumption is rarely valid in practice is a useful caution for the broader concept-based explanation literature. Open questions include how to handle concept multiplicity (the current boolean formulation only captures presence/absence), how to resolve the inherent tension between subgraph-level interpretability and computation-tree-level faithfulness, and whether the approach scales to datasets with tens of thousands of graphs without the GRAPHTRAIL-S approximation.
