## Problem

Conformal prediction for time series is hampered by the violation of the exchangeability assumption due to temporal dependencies and distribution drift. Existing adaptations either rely on manually selected weights (e.g., NexCP) or compute non-conformity scores in the output space, both of which produce overly conservative prediction intervals that lack practical informativeness. No prior method dynamically learns reweighting in the semantically rich latent space of a neural network base model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series and a pre-trained deep NN (RNN or Transformer) that is decomposed into a feature extractor f and a prediction head g. It outputs prediction intervals in the original output space, but the critical intermediate artifacts are non-conformity scores computed as distances in the latent feature space and a set of adaptive weights learned via an attention mechanism. A surrogate latent vector is optimized until the normalized prediction error falls below 1%, ensuring the latent score faithfully represents the true prediction discrepancy.

## Method

CT-SSF shifts conformal prediction into the latent space of the base model by defining the non-conformity score as the distance between the model's latent representation and a surrogate feature that, when passed through the prediction head, reproduces the ground-truth output. The surrogate is found via a weighted gradient descent step where the weight vector is dynamically updated by a Transformer-based attention mechanism that scores the similarity between each calibration point's error and the current prediction error in the semantic space. This adaptive reweighting replaces the fixed or manually chosen weights of prior methods, concentrating calibration mass on historically similar error patterns. Finally, LiPRA (linear relaxation-based perturbation analysis) translates the latent-space quantile threshold back into an output-space prediction interval, and a Hölder-continuity assumption on the band-length operator yields a theoretical guarantee that CT-SSF achieves strictly shorter average intervals than output-space CP (e.g., NexCP) while preserving valid coverage.

## Results

On four real-world time-series benchmarks (Electricity, Amazon Stock, Weather, Wind Speed) at α=0.1, CT-SSF achieves the narrowest prediction intervals across all datasets while maintaining valid ~90% empirical coverage. Specifically, CT-SSF reduces prediction interval width by 10%–20% compared to the SOTA method HopCPT (e.g., 0.19 vs. 0.23 on Stock, 0.012 vs. 0.017 on Weather, 0.52 vs. 0.54 on Wind). On the synthetic dataset, CT-SSF achieves a 4.61% width reduction over HopCPT at α=0.05 (1.45 vs. 1.54) while maintaining 96.5% coverage. The method also dramatically outperforms output-space methods NexCP and standard CP, which produce intervals 3–7× wider (e.g., NexCP width of 2.76 vs. CT-SSF's 0.52 on Wind).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CT-SSF consistently outperforms all four baselines on prediction interval width across all four real-world datasets while maintaining valid coverage. Against the closest competitor HopCPT (SOTA time-series CP using Modern Hopfield Networks), CT-SSF achieves 10%–20% narrower intervals (e.g., 0.19 vs. 0.23 on Stock, 0.012 vs. 0.017 on Weather), attributed to constructing non-conformity scores in the latent semantic space rather than the output space. Against NexCP (reweighting with manually selected weights), CT-SSF produces dramatically tighter intervals—up to 5.3× narrower on Wind (0.52 vs. 2.76)—because its adaptive attention-based weights avoid the overly conservative behavior of fixed exponential decay. Against FeatureCP (FCP), which also operates in latent space but assumes exchangeability, CT-SSF achieves ~15–45% narrower intervals and avoids FCP's occasional under-coverage (e.g., FCP drops to 89.54% on Wind vs. CT-SSF's 90.32%). Against standard Split CP, CT-SSF reduces width by 4–7×, confirming that both latent-space scoring and adaptive reweighting are essential for efficient time-series conformal prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CT-SSF synthesizes three previously separate threads in conformal prediction: FeatureCP's (Teng et al., 2022) construction of non-conformity scores in a latent feature space, NexCP's (Barber et al., 2023) reweighting framework for non-exchangeable data, and HopCPT's (Auer et al., 2023) learned similarity-based weights for time series. The key departure from HopCPT—the closest prior work—is moving the non-conformity score computation from the output space into the semantic latent space of the base neural network, where an attention mechanism dynamically adjusts weights based on error-term similarity in that richer representation. LiPRA (Xu et al., 2020) is borrowed to translate the resulting latent-space intervals back to the output space, completing the pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–20% reduction in prediction interval width over the prior SOTA (HopCPT) across four real-world datasets, while maintaining valid coverage, makes conformal prediction intervals more practically useful for time-series forecasting in finance, energy, and weather. A notable limitation is that the theoretical guarantee assumes fixed weights, whereas the implementation learns them from data; establishing a rigorous theory for data-driven weights remains open. The method also requires a deep NN base model and is sensitive to the choice of the f/g split in the network, limiting applicability to simpler models and adding a hyperparameter search burden.
