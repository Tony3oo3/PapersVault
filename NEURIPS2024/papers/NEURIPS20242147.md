## Problem

Existing ML models for dynamical systems are physics-agnostic, limiting interpretability and data efficiency. While divergence-free constraints capture conservative dynamics, they cannot model dissipation, and prior HHD-based approaches (e.g., D-HNN) are restricted to specific system classes. Moreover, the non-uniqueness of the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition introduces an identifiability problem: the same data can yield arbitrarily different curl-free/div-free splits, undermining physical interpretability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes noisy state-derivative pairs (x_i, y_i) where y_i = f(x_i) + ε with Gaussian noise, sampled from phase space. Unusually, it predicts not only the total vector field f but also its hidden curl-free and divergence-free components f_curl and f_div from observations of only their sum, along with separate prediction variances for each component. The div-free component further enables recovery of the system's Hamiltonian (energy) via a joint GP prior, providing a physically interpretable energy estimate along trajectories.

## Method

HHD-GP constructs an additive GP whose kernel is the sum of a curl-free kernel (the Hessian of a scalar kernel, obtained by applying the gradient operator to a GP over a scalar potential) and a div-free kernel (obtained by applying row-wise divergence operators to a GP over a skew-symmetric matrix field parameterized by m = n(n−1)/2 scalar functions). Both kernels are derived by exploiting closure of GPs under linear differential operators, guaranteeing valid covariance functions. To resolve HHD non-identifiability, the symmetry-preserving extension (SPHHD-GP) imposes group equivariance: the curl-free GP uses a Haar-integration kernel on the scalar potential (yielding G-invariant potentials and hence G-equivariant gradient fields), while the div-free GP uses a Group Integration Matrix kernel on the vector potential (yielding G-equivariant div-free fields). Training proceeds by maximizing the marginal likelihood of the additive GP, with all kernel hyperparameters and noise variances optimized jointly via Adam.

## Results

On the damped mass-spring system (20 training points), SPHHD-GP achieves the best RMSE of 2.02×10⁻², VPT of 8.52, and MNLL of −3.16, outperforming all baselines. On the damped pendulum, SPHHD-GP achieves RMSE 13.34×10⁻², VPT 3.18, and MNLL −1.56, again the best across all metrics. On the Chua circuit (100 training points), SPHHD-GP achieves RMSE_der of 4.1×10⁻² and RMSE_en of 0.7×10⁻², substantially outperforming the next-best GIM-GP (10.2 and N/A respectively). The symmetry-preserving extension consistently improves over the unconstrained HHD-GP, confirming that symmetry priors resolve the non-identifiability of the HHD decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHHD-GP outperforms all five baselines across every system and metric. Against D-HNN [22], SPHHD-GP achieves roughly 17× lower RMSE on the mass-spring (2.02 vs. 35.58) and 14× lower on the pendulum (13.34 vs. 183.38), because D-HNN's neural network architecture suffers from low data efficiency on sparse, noisy observations. Div-GP [54, 59] is limited to conservative (div-free) dynamics and thus cannot model dissipation, yielding the worst VPT (1.02 on mass-spring) and an extremely unstable MNLL (2638±7917). GIM-GP [56] incorporates symmetry but lacks the HHD structure, so it cannot exploit the additional symmetries present in the div-free and curl-free components separately; SPHHD-GP improves VPT by ~61% on the mass-spring (8.52 vs. 5.28) and ~37% on the pendulum (3.18 vs. 2.32). Ind-GP, which has no physical inductive bias, performs comparably to the unconstrained HHD-GP, confirming that the HHD structure alone does not help without symmetry constraints. On the ocean current field, SPHHD-GP produces continuous current predictions and recovers a rich divergence structure, whereas Helmholtz-GP [6] predicts an almost constant curl-free component due to non-identifiability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in physics-informed GP learning: (1) the construction of div-free and curl-free kernels from scalar/matrix-valued kernels via linear differential operators (building on Richter-Powell's universal div-free representation, Wendland's div-free kernels, and the GP closure under linear transformations), and (2) symmetry-preserving kernel design via Haar integration and GIM-kernels (from Haasdonk and Reisert). Prior HHD-based models were restricted to specific domains—Berlinghieri et al. for 2D ocean currents and Greydanus et al. (D-HNN) for dissipative Hamiltonian systems—and suffered from non-identifiability. This work generalizes HHD-GP to arbitrary-dimensional dynamical systems and, crucially, introduces symmetry-preserving extensions of both the curl-free and div-free GP components to resolve the identifiability problem, a step not taken in any prior HHD-based learning method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability solution via symmetry constraints is the paper's most impactful contribution: it transforms HHD-GP from a model that can predict dynamics but not a physically meaningful decomposition into one that recovers interpretable conservative (div-free → Hamiltonian/energy) and dissipative (curl-free → friction) components from noisy, sparse data. The consistency result—showing that without symmetry the decomposition does not converge even with infinite data, while with symmetry it does—provides a clean theoretical-empirical validation. Limitations include the requirement that the symmetry group be known a priori, the computational cost of GPs (mitigated by sparse GPs for the ocean experiment), and restriction to autonomous systems. Open questions include extending the framework to non-autonomous or stochastic systems, and whether the symmetry-for-identifiability principle generalizes beyond HHD to other additive decompositions in physics-informed learning.
