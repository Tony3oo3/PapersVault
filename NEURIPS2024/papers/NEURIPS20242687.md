## Problem

Training allied agents in competitive multi-agent games is hindered by hidden opponent actions and partial observability, making it difficult to anticipate enemy behavior and leading to slow convergence and instability. Existing opponent modeling and imitation learning methods assume access to opponent observations or actions, which are unavailable when agents only perceive local neighborhoods. This paper addresses the gap by formulating opponent prediction as a multi-agent imitation learning problem solvable from local state-only observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242687/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each allied agent receives a local observation vector containing its own state plus the relative positions, health, and other attributes of neighboring allies and enemies (which shift over time as agents move), along with its own previous action. The IL component outputs a probability distribution over the next states of neighboring enemy agents, which is concatenated with the local observation to form an augmented input for the policy network. The final output is the allied agent's action, selected by the PPO actor conditioned on this augmented state representation.

## Method

The key reformulation converts opponent policy imitation into next-state prediction by defining a synthetic 'expert' whose state is the pair (current global state, previous ally action) and whose 'action' is the resulting next state, with the ally policy serving as the transition dynamics. An adaptation of IQ-Learn (inverse soft-Q learning) is developed with new soft and inverse soft Bellman operators, where the imitation policy is a softmax over Q-values and the Q-network is trained via a minimax objective that avoids adversarial instability. The predicted next enemy states are integrated into MAPPO by augmenting each agent's observation, creating the IMAX-PPO algorithm where IL and policy training occur in a single unified process with alternating SAC and PPO updates. Theoretical analysis provides O(√ε) bounds showing that the IL solution remains stable as the ally policy converges, with the bound depending on the KL divergence between successive ally policies.

## Results

On SMACv2, IMAX-PPO (InQ) achieves 78.7% win-rate on Protoss 5v5, 79.8% on Protoss 10v10, and 53.9% on Terran 10v11, consistently outperforming all baselines by 5–25 percentage points. On Google Research Football, IMAX-PPO (InQ) reaches 98.1% win-rate on the 3v1 task and 97.3% on the hard counterattack task, approaching near-perfect performance. On Gold Miner (hard), the method achieves 49.8% win-rate against the top two competition teams, surpassing the next-best baseline (QPLEX at 43.5%) by over 6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMAX-PPO (InQ) outperforms all four baselines on the vast majority of tasks. Against MAPPO (the strongest policy-gradient baseline), IMAX-PPO gains 10–25 points on SMACv2 (e.g., 78.7% vs 58.0% on Protoss 5v5; 53.9% vs 28.6% on Terran 10v11) and 9–20 points on GRF (98.1% vs 88.0% on 3v1). Against QMIX, the advantage is most pronounced on harder SMACv2 scenarios (e.g., 48.7% vs 42.5% on Protoss 10v11; 65.4% vs 57.6% on Terran 20v20) and on Gold Miner hard (49.8% vs 41.7%). Against QPLEX, IMAX-PPO wins on nearly every task, with the largest margins on SMACv2 Terran 10v11 (53.9% vs 41.4%) and GRF hard (97.3% vs 95.1%). The only notable exception is GRF 3v1, where Sup-MAPPO (96.1%) and IMAX-PPO (GAIL) (96.4%) are close to IMAX-PPO (InQ) (98.1%), and SMACv2 Protoss 20v20 where Sup-MAPPO (71.1%) and IMAX-PPO (GAIL) (76.3%) are competitive but still below IMAX-PPO (InQ) (80.6%). Overall, the IQ-Learn-based IL component provides a clear and consistent advantage over both GAIL-based imitation and supervised next-state prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021), a single-agent inverse soft-Q learning algorithm, to a multi-agent setting where opponent actions are unobservable and only local state observations are available. It integrates this adapted IL component into MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022), the current SOTA policy-gradient MARL algorithm, to form IMAX-PPO. The key departure from prior opponent-modeling and IL work is the reformulation of opponent imitation as next-state prediction (rather than action imitation) under a new 'expert' state definition that incorporates the allies' previous joint action, sidestepping the unobservable-action problem that renders existing IL and opponent-modeling methods inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded way to incorporate opponent anticipation into MARL under realistic partial-observability constraints (local observations only, hidden opponent actions), which is a common practical limitation in competitive games. The O(√ε) stability bound (Corollary 4) is a useful guarantee that the IL component remains well-behaved as ally policies shift during training. A key limitation is the assumption that enemy policies are fixed during training, which restricts applicability to truly adaptive or co-learning opponents; extending IMAX-PPO to cooperative-competitive settings where both sides update is an important open direction. The consistent gains across structurally diverse environments (grid-based SMACv2, pixel-based GRF, 2D-terrain Gold Miner) suggest the approach generalizes, though the GRF baselines (QMIX at 8–16%) are notably weak, tempering the apparent magnitude of improvement there.
