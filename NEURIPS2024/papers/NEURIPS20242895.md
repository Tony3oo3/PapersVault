## Problem

Prior retrieval-based LM studies relied on small single-domain datastores (~5B tokens, e.g., Wikipedia) or proprietary trillion-token datastores (RETRO) that were not open-sourced and were only evaluated on language modeling. It remained unknown how scaling the datastore size affects the dominant retrieval-in-context paradigm across a broad range of downstream tasks, and whether datastore size constitutes a meaningful scaling dimension alongside model parameters and pretraining data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242895/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the input is a 512-token prefix from RedPajama or S2ORC, and the output is perplexity over the subsequent 512 tokens. For downstream tasks, the input is a question paired with 5-shot examples, with top-3 retrieved 256-word document chunks prepended before the few-shot examples; the output is a generated answer scored by exact match (TQA, NQ) or multiple-choice accuracy (MMLU, MedQA). The datastore itself consists of 256-word chunks spanning 8 domains totaling 1.4T tokens, indexed with Contriever-MSMARCO dense embeddings.

## Method

The paper introduces MASSIVEDS, a 1.4-trillion-token multi-domain datastore (books, STEM, encyclopedia, forum/QA, code, math, biomedical, general web) that is the largest open-sourced datastore for retrieval-based LMs. To make datastore scaling studies computationally feasible, the authors design a pipeline that performs expensive indexing and retrieval only once over the full corpus (retrieving K=1000 documents per query), then applies cheaper operations—subsampling, deduplication, decontamination, and optional reranking—post-hoc on the retrieved document pool, reducing compute by over an order of magnitude while being provably equivalent with high probability. Using this pipeline, they systematically vary datastore size (via subsampling ratio p), model size (LLaMA-2/3, Pythia, OLMo), and pretraining tokens to plot scaling curves and compute-optimal Pareto frontiers. The key finding is that datastore scaling monotonically improves language modeling and knowledge-intensive QA without saturation, and that offloading FLOPs from pretraining to datastore construction yields better compute-optimal performance than LM-only models at the same training budget.

## Results

MASSIVEDS (1.4T tokens) with LLAMA-2 7B achieves 77.0 EM on TriviaQA, 34.6 EM on Natural Questions, 49.3% on MMLU, and 39.4% on MedQA, outperforming the LM-only baseline (64.1, 26.6, 45.8, 36.6 respectively) by 12.9, 8.0, 3.5, and 2.8 points. On language modeling, MASSIVEDS reduces RedPajama perplexity from 4.09 (LM-only) to 3.50 and S2ORC perplexity from 7.18 to 6.57. A key finding is that a LLAMA-2 7B model retrieving from fewer than 100B tokens of MASSIVEDS outperforms both its 13B LM-only counterpart and the larger LLAMA-3 8B LM-only model on TQA and NQ, and compute-optimal scaling curves show retrieval-based LMs achieve superior performance at the same training FLOPs budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASSIVEDS is compared against LM-only baselines and single-domain datastores (PubMed, MATH, peS2o, DPR Wiki, and individual RedPajama sub-domains) using LLAMA-2 7B. MASSIVEDS clearly wins on TriviaQA (77.0 vs. best single-domain 72.9 for RedPajama Wiki, a 4.1-point margin), MMLU (49.3 vs. best 48.3), and both language modeling benchmarks (3.50 vs. best 3.87 PPL on RedPajama; 6.57 vs. best 6.64 on S2ORC). On Natural Questions, MASSIVEDS ties the best single-domain datastore (DPR Wiki) at 34.6 EM. On MedQA, MASSIVEDS (39.4) is slightly behind the best single-domain (RedPajama ArXiv at 39.8), likely because the biomedical domain is underrepresented in the general web data. The paper further demonstrates that on knowledge-intensive tasks, a small retrieval-based LM (e.g., LLAMA-2 7B with <100B tokens) outperforms larger LM-only models (LLAMA-2 13B, LLAMA-3 8B), and that compute-optimal scaling curves favor retrieval-based LMs over LM-only models at equivalent training FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrieve-in-context language model (RIC-LM) paradigm of Ram et al. (2023) and Shi et al. (2023) by treating datastore size as a first-class scaling dimension alongside model parameters and pretraining tokens. Prior retrieval-based LM work (REALM, ATLAS, REPLUG, SELF-RAG) relied on small single-domain datastores (~5B tokens, typically Wikipedia), while RETRO (Borgeaud et al., 2022) demonstrated trillion-token datastores but used a proprietary corpus, a custom architecture, and evaluated only on language modeling. The paper departs from both lines by constructing the largest open-sourced multi-domain datastore (MASSIVEDS, 1.4T tokens) and systematically studying its effect on downstream tasks and compute-optimal scaling curves, filling a gap the authors identify as 'relatively underexplored.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that compute allocation for LMs should consider inference-time data access as a tunable knob: offloading factual knowledge into a datastore is cheaper than memorizing it in parameters, enabling smaller models to match or beat larger LM-only counterparts on knowledge-intensive tasks. Open questions remain around reasoning-heavy tasks (MMLU, MedQA) where retrieval benefits are weaker, the impact of retriever architecture and size on scaling trends, inference-cost trade-offs, and whether this framework extends to long-context modeling and test-time scaling. The open-sourcing of MASSIVEDS and the efficient pipeline lowers the barrier for future research on trillion-token retrieval.
