## Problem

Path-based attribution methods like Integrated Gradients accumulate noise along the integration path, distorting explanations. Existing remedies (SmoothGrad, BlurIG, GIG) focus on finding alternative paths but overlook that intermediate-step images deviate significantly from the training data distribution, further amplifying noise. This paper identifies distribution shift of intermediate images as the root cause and proposes to align the attribution path with the reverse denoising process of diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241709/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB target image, its class label, and a Gaussian noise baseline (replacing the conventional black/white baseline), and produces per-pixel attribution scores forming a saliency map. It requires a pre-trained classifier-guided diffusion model (DDPM) with 250 sampling steps and a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-50, VGG-16, or VGG-19) as the target model to be explained. The path is piece-wise linear with each segment's Gaussian distribution centered at the target image, ensuring intermediate samples remain close to the data manifold.

## Method

DDPath replaces the straight-line attribution path with a piece-wise linear path constructed from the reverse sampling sequence of a classifier-guided diffusion model. At each step t, the sampling mean is a convex combination of the diffusion model's predicted mean and the classifier gradient term, weighted by ρ = 1 − t/T and κ = t/T, so that early steps preserve the target image structure while later steps are dominated by class-discriminative gradients. The integral is approximated via Riemann summation over T steps, and the approach is shown to satisfy the sensitivity, completeness, implementation invariance, and symmetry-preserving axioms. DDPath composes with existing methods to yield DDPath-IG, DDPath-BlurIG (adding Gaussian blur along the path), and DDPath-GIG (optimizing over the set of all DDPath samples to minimize noisy attributions while staying close to the IG path).

## Results

On ImageNet-1k validation, DDPath-IG achieves the best Insertion scores across all three backbones: 25.9 (VGG-16), 27.8 (VGG-19), and 45.1 (ResNet-50), with corresponding Overall (Insertion − Deletion) scores of 13.2, 15.7, and 32.4. DDPath-GIG attains the lowest Deletion scores for VGG-19 (12.3) and ResNet-50 (12.0). On the MS COCO 2017 pointing game, DDPath-GIG reaches 50.5% hit rate with ResNet-50, outperforming the GIG baseline (47.2%) by 3.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPath-IG consistently outperforms all five baselines (Guided BP, IG, Smooth Grad, BlurIG, GIG) on Insertion and Overall metrics across VGG-16, VGG-19, and ResNet-50, with the largest margin over IG on ResNet-50 (45.1 vs. 33.8 Insertion, +11.3). On Deletion, DDPath-IG achieves the best score for VGG-16 (12.7) and VGG-19 (12.1), while DDPath-GIG is best for ResNet-50 (12.0); BlurIG remains competitive on Deletion for VGG-16 (13.3) but is far behind on Insertion (19.2). On the COCO pointing game, every DDPath variant surpasses its corresponding baseline: DDPath-IG beats IG by 2.4–1.7 points, DDPath-BlurIG beats BlurIG by 2.2–3.4 points, and DDPath-GIG beats GIG by 1.1–3.3 points. The paper also shows that simply adding noise to baselines (IG-Noise, BlurIG-Noise, GIG-Noise) yields only marginal improvements over vanilla versions, confirming that the structured denoising path of DDPath is essential rather than mere noise averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the path-based attribution lineage (Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, BlurIG, Guided IG) by identifying a previously underappreciated failure mode: intermediate-step images in all existing paths deviate from the training data distribution, amplifying gradient noise. Rather than proposing yet another manually designed path (noise addition, blurring, adaptive search), DDPath repurposes the reverse sampling process of a classifier-guided diffusion model as the attribution path, so that intermediate images are generated by a model that inherently preserves data distribution. The conceptual bridge between generative denoising and gradient accumulation along an explanation path is the paper's core novelty, building on Dhariwal & Nichol's classifier-guided diffusion framework but applying it to an entirely different problem (interpretability rather than generation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDPath offers a principled, axiomatic-preserving mechanism for reducing attribution noise that is orthogonal to and composable with existing path methods (IG, BlurIG, GIG), making it a drop-in enhancement rather than a replacement. The key limitation is computational: 250 diffusion sampling steps are required, and the method depends on a pre-trained classifier-guided diffusion model, which constrains applicability to domains where such models exist. Open questions include whether more advanced diffusion architectures (score-based, flow-matching) or fewer-step samplers can reduce the path length, whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities (e.g., LLM token attribution), and whether the distribution-alignment benefit persists for very deep or transformer-based classifiers where the data manifold is less well-characterized.
