## Problem

Denoising diffusion models require a discretisation schedule to simulate the backward SDE, but beyond hand-crafted heuristics (linear, cosine, polynomial ρ-schedules) there is no general principled method for choosing where to place time steps. Poor schedule choices can cause complete failure to capture data modes, as demonstrated on the Cantor distribution, and finding good schedules typically reduces to expensive manual hyperparameter sweeps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243427/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a score function estimate (from a pre-trained or in-training DDM), the noising schedule parameters s(t) and σ(t), and an initial discretisation schedule of T points. It outputs a re-parameterised set of T time points {t_i*} that equalise the incremental cost along the diffusion path. For the corrector-optimised variant, only score evaluations ∇log p_t(X_t) are needed; the predictor-optimised variant additionally requires a Hutchinson trace estimate of the Hessian of the score. The algorithm runs in minutes on a single GPU for image models and can be applied online during training with a schedule learning rate γ.

## Method

The paper decomposes the backward SDE into a probability-flow ODE predictor and a Langevin corrector, then defines an incremental cost L(t,t') as the squared L² distance between the target score and the predicted score (a Stein divergence), weighted by the corrector velocity v(t)². Theorem 2.1 shows this cost is locally quadratic, L(t,t') ≈ δ(t)Δt², where δ(t) is a local cost measuring the sensitivity of the score to diffusion time. Theorem 3.1 then proves that the optimal schedule generator φ* is the geodesic of the diffusion path under the Riemannian metric δ, traversed at constant speed: φ*(s) = Λ⁻¹(Λs) where Λ(t) = ∫₀ᵗ √δ(u)du. Algorithm 1 computes cumulative Λ at the current time points, fits a monotone spline, and inverts to obtain the optimal schedule. Two cost variants are provided: a corrector-optimised cost (requiring only score evaluations) and a predictor-optimised cost (adding a Jacobian-determinant term via Hutchinson trace), with the former being cheaper and empirically equivalent on image datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), the corrector-optimised schedule achieves FID 1.99, matching the hand-tuned ρ=7 polynomial schedule (FID 1.96) from Karras et al. (2022) without any hyperparameter search. On FFHQ and AFHQv2, the method achieves FID 2.46 and 2.05 respectively, tying the best baseline. On ImageNet (64×64), the corrector-optimised schedule reaches FID 1.44 versus 1.42 for ρ=7. Notably, at only 10 discretisation steps on CIFAR-10, the optimised schedule maintains FID 2.46 while the ρ=3 schedule degrades to 50.75, demonstrating superior sampling efficiency at low step counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the hand-tuned ρ=7 schedule from Karras et al. (2022), the proposed corrector-optimised method essentially ties on all four datasets (FID 1.99 vs 1.96 on CIFAR-10, 2.46 vs 2.46 on FFHQ, 2.05 vs 2.05 on AFHQv2, 1.44 vs 1.42 on ImageNet), recovering a performant schedule that was previously discovered only through manual hyperparameter search. Against the LogLinear schedule from Lu et al. (2022), the method wins on CIFAR-10 (1.99 vs 2.05) and ImageNet (1.44 vs 1.45) but is slightly behind on FFHQ (2.46 vs 2.42). Against Sabour et al. (2024), the paper demonstrates that the proposed Stein-divergence-based cost correlates much more closely with FID than their KLUB objective: schedules with low KLUB do not necessarily have low FID (e.g., the Convex Schedule has the lowest KLUB at 0.284×10⁶ but the worst FID at 22.1), whereas low CO/PO cost consistently corresponds to low FID. The predictor-optimised variant performs comparably to the corrector-optimised variant on image datasets, so the cheaper corrector-optimised cost is recommended in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE framework of Song et al. (2021), specifically the predictor/corrector decomposition of the backward diffusion and the probability flow ODE, to derive a principled cost for discretisation schedule selection. It adapts the schedule-update algorithm from Syed et al. (2021) (non-reversible parallel tempering) to the diffusion setting. The key departure from prior schedule-design work (Karras et al. 2022's polynomial ρ-schedule, Lu et al. 2022's log-linear steps, Sabour et al. 2024's pathwise KL objective) is that the cost is derived intrinsically from the work done by a hypothetical Langevin corrector, yielding a Stein-divergence-based metric under which the optimal schedule is a geodesic — a formulation absent from earlier heuristics or solver-error-based criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first hyperparameter-free, data-dependent algorithm for selecting DDM discretisation schedules, applicable both at inference time for pre-trained models and online during training. The geodesic interpretation in a Riemannian manifold with metric δ(t) opens a path toward deeper connections with information geometry for refining diffusion methodology. Key limitations include the assumption of perfect score estimation in the theory and the computational cost of the predictor-optimised variant (requiring Hessian traces), though the cheaper corrector-optimised cost performs comparably on image benchmarks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to handling score estimation error and whether the geometric structure can inform other aspects of diffusion model design beyond scheduling.
