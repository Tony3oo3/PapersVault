## Problem

Developing a conversational AI assistant for EHR data is hindered by two gaps: (1) the absence of large-scale instruction-following datasets grounded in structured EHR tables (existing clinical QA datasets range from hundreds to tens of thousands of examples and focus on factoid extraction or clinical notes), and (2) the inability of current model architectures to handle complex, heterogeneous EHR schemas without labor-intensive manual feature engineering and preprocessing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241736/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a patient's EHR represented as a sequence of clinical event triplets (timestamp, type, value) following the Medical Event Data Standard, along with a natural-language instruction. Rather than feeding raw event text into the LLM, each event is first encoded by ClinicalBERT into a fixed-size embedding, then mapped via a linear projection into the LLM's word-embedding space, which compresses long ICU event histories into a manageable token budget. The output is a free-text response; for downstream predictive tasks, a linear classification head is appended to produce binary or multilabel predictions.

## Method

Llemr adopts a ClinicalBERT event encoder + linear projection + Vicuna-7b-v1.5 LLM backbone architecture, where each clinical event is encoded independently and the resulting embedding sequence is concatenated with the instruction's token embeddings before being fed to the LLM. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 uses 350K template-generated, GPT-3.5-paraphrased schema-alignment QA pairs to teach the model to interpret EHR event embeddings, training only the projection layer while freezing both the encoder and LLM; Stage 2 uses 50K GPT-3.5-generated clinical-reasoning QA pairs (derived from discharge summaries) to enable deeper reasoning, unfreezing the LLM while keeping the encoder frozen. The 400K-example MIMIC-Instr dataset is constructed from MIMIC-IV by pairing manually crafted Python extraction scripts with question templates for the schema-alignment subset, and by prompting GPT-3.5 with few-shot examples to generate doctor-like Q&A from discharge summaries for the reasoning subset. This design eliminates the need for task-specific feature engineering while remaining robust to schema variations across EHR systems.

## Results

As a conversational clinical AI assistant, Llemr (Stage 1 & 2) achieves an overall GPT-scored relative score of 73.33±5.30, outperforming all seven open-source LLM baselines (best baseline: dolphin-2.0-mistral-7b at 67.86±4.64) and matching 73% of GPT-4 performance. On clinical predictive benchmarks, the further-trained Llemr achieves AUC-ROC of 0.8388 (mortality), 0.7251 (readmission), 0.7132 (length-of-stay), and 0.8086 (diagnosis), matching or exceeding all SOTA baselines on three of four tasks. Stage-1 training alone (only the linear projection layer updated) already yields 67.03±6.83 overall, surpassing most baselines, while Stage-2 training boosts clinical reasoning from 64.35 to 76.23.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the conversational assistant evaluation (Table 3), Llemr (Stage 1 & 2) outperforms all seven open-source LLM baselines by a clear margin: it beats the strongest baseline (dolphin-2.0-mistral-7b, 67.86 overall) by 5.47 points and the backbone Vicuna-7b-v1.5 (64.60) by 8.73 points. The gap is most pronounced on clinical reasoning (76.23 vs. 72.66 for dolphin), where Stage-2 instruction tuning with discharge-summary-derived QA pairs provides a 11.88-point boost over Stage-1 alone. On clinical predictive benchmarks (Table 5), the further-trained Llemr achieves the best AUC-ROC on mortality (0.8388 vs. 0.8362 for GRASP), readmission (0.7251 vs. 0.7193 for REMed), and length-of-stay (0.7132 vs. 0.7100 for GRASP), while slightly trailing REMed on diagnosis classification (0.8086 vs. 0.8128). Notably, Llemr matches or exceeds task-specific models (RETAIN, GRASP, GenHPF) that rely on hand-crafted features, while REMed—another foundation-model approach—performs competitively but is outperformed on three of four tasks. The baselines that use manual feature engineering (RNN, Transformer, RETAIN, GRASP) are constrained to procedure, lab, and prescription features truncated to 512 events, whereas Llemr and REMed ingest the full heterogeneous event sequence without feature selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two recent architectural ideas: the encoder-projection-LLM pipeline from REMed (ClinicalBERT encodes clinical events, a linear layer projects them into the LLM's embedding space) and the two-stage curriculum training from LLaVA (first align the projection layer with frozen encoders, then unfreeze the LLM for task-specific learning). It departs from prior clinical LLM work that focused on natural-language inputs (medical exam questions, clinical notes) by targeting structured, heterogeneous EHR data, and from prior EHR foundation models (REMed, GenHPF) that are task-specific by making the model instruction-following and general-purpose. The MIMIC-Instr dataset (400K examples) is positioned as filling a clear scale gap: existing clinical QA/instruction datasets range from under 1K to 24K examples, which the authors argue is insufficient for effective instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400K MIMIC-Instr dataset and the Llemr framework provide a practical, open-source path toward conversational EHR assistants that could reduce physician documentation burden. A key limitation is that the clinical reasoning subset was generated from discharge summaries rather than raw EHR events, creating a potential input-output mismatch that the authors acknowledge as a future direction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond MIMIC-IV's ICU setting to broader hospital or multi-institution EHRs, and whether the ClinicalBERT encoder can be replaced by more modern clinical encoders without degrading performance. The finding that Llemr matches SOTA clinical prediction baselines without manual feature engineering suggests a viable alternative to the labor-intensive feature engineering pipeline that currently dominates clinical ML.
