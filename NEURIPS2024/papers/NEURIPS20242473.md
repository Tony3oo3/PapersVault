## Problem

Existing language-driven traffic trajectory generators (e.g., LCTGen) produce trajectories for each vehicle independently, ignoring the interactions between multiple traffic participants. This makes it impossible to generate complex interactive scenarios such as overtaking, merging, yielding, or traffic jams, which are essential for testing autonomous driving systems in safety-critical conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language description of a traffic scenario as input and produces 2D trajectories for up to 32 vehicles over 5 seconds at 10 fps (T=50 timesteps) along with a corresponding environment map. The language is first parsed by GPT-4 into three discrete numerical code types—interaction codes (relative position in 6 angular sectors and relative distance in 15 m bins, sampled at 1 s intervals), vehicle codes (trajectory type, initial orientation/position, and 5-step speed trend), and map codes (lane counts per direction, intersection distance, ego lane index)—which are then decoded into continuous trajectories.

## Method

InteractTraj consists of an LLM-based language-to-code encoder and a code-to-trajectory decoder. The encoder uses GPT-4 with carefully designed interaction-aware prompts to convert language into the three code types, where the key novelty is the interaction code that discretizes pairwise relative position and distance into semantically meaningful indices to capture temporal interaction dynamics. The decoder first extracts features via MLPs with positional encoding (for vehicle/interaction codes) and multi-context gating blocks (for map lanes), then applies a two-step interaction-aware aggregation: (1) multi-head cross-attention fuses map features into both interaction and vehicle features, and (2) a second cross-attention fuses the enriched interaction features into vehicle features. Type-specific MLP heads then decode the fused vehicle features into trajectories. Training is supervised with MSE on trajectory positions plus an auxiliary relative-distance loss, using codes extracted directly from ground-truth trajectories since no language-trajectory pairs exist in the datasets.

## Results

On WOMD, InteractTraj achieves mADE 1.067 and mFDE 2.190, reducing these metrics by 15.4% and 18.7% respectively over the prior SoTA (LCTGen: mADE 1.262, mFDE 2.696). On nuPlan, the improvements are even larger at 17.1% (mADE 0.962 vs. 1.161) and 20.4% (mFDE 1.987 vs. 2.497). InteractTraj also achieves the lowest Hausdorff distance (1.076 on WOMD, 1.129 on nuPlan) and scenario collision rate (0.070 on WOMD, 0.067 on nuPlan) among all compared methods. In user studies, 73.7% of participants prefer InteractTraj's generated scenarios over LCTGen's, and 72.4% of users confirm InteractTraj fulfills the described interaction behaviors (vs. only 31.5% for LCTGen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractTraj is compared against two baselines: TrafficGen (map-only input, no language control) and LCTGen (language-conditioned but interaction-agnostic). Against TrafficGen, InteractTraj is dramatically superior on all metrics (e.g., mADE 1.067 vs. 9.531 on WOMD), which is expected since TrafficGen lacks any language conditioning and generates trajectories purely from a blank map. Against LCTGen, the more relevant comparison, InteractTraj wins on every metric on both datasets: mADE improves by 15.4% (WOMD) and 17.1% (nuPlan), mFDE by 18.7% and 20.4%, and HD by 16.9% and 13.2%. The ablated version without interaction codes (InteractTraj w/o I) still outperforms LCTGen on mADE and mFDE, confirming that the vehicle code design (mixed-scale trajectory type + detailed movement) independently contributes to better generation. In the language-conditioned user study, InteractTraj receives 73.7% preference over LCTGen overall, and for interaction-specific fulfillment, 72.4% of users confirm InteractTraj produces the described interactions versus only 31.5% for LCTGen, highlighting that LCTGen's per-agent independent generation fundamentally cannot capture multi-vehicle interactions like overtaking, merging, yielding, and following.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LCTGen, the prior language-conditioned traffic generation method, by introducing an explicit interaction-aware intermediate representation (discretized relative position and distance codes) between the LLM encoder and the trajectory decoder. Where LCTGen generates each vehicle's trajectory independently, InteractTraj models pairwise spatial relationships through a two-step cross-attention aggregation that fuses map, vehicle, and interaction features. The approach also departs from CTG/CTG++ which require costly past trajectory observations, and from TrafficGen which offers no language-based controllability, by using GPT-4 as a frozen encoder that converts free-form descriptions into structured numerical codes without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate multi-agent traffic trajectories with explicit interaction modeling from natural language, addressing a gap where prior language-driven generators produced physically implausible scenarios (e.g., vehicles passing through each other). The discretized interaction code design (6 angular regions, 5m distance bins) is a practical bridge between LLM reasoning and continuous trajectory generation, and the generalization to unseen interaction types (platooning, pulling over) without retraining suggests the code space captures transferable spatial semantics. Key limitations include restriction to vehicles only (no pedestrians/cyclists), dependence on a pre-defined map library, and the absence of true language-trajectory ground-truth pairs, forcing reconstruction-based evaluation that may overstate end-to-end language controllability.
