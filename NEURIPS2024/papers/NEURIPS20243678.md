## Problem

Neuro-symbolic models can achieve high accuracy on learning-and-reasoning tasks by learning concepts with incorrect semantics (reasoning shortcuts), undermining trustworthiness and interpretability. However, suitable datasets with known RSs are scarce and scattered, existing benchmarks neglect RSs entirely or lack OOD data, and there is no systematic way to verify a priori whether a task is affected by RSs. This paper introduces rsbench, a comprehensive benchmark suite that fills these gaps.

## Input / Output

Tasks accept low-level RGB images (MNIST digit concatenations at 28k×28, geometric shape images at 64×64, 3D Blender-rendered traffic scenes at 469×387, or dashcam images at 720×1280) paired with prior knowledge K supplied in DIMACS CNF format. Outputs include predicted labels, predicted concepts, concept quality metrics (macro F1, concept collapse), and a priori RS counts from countrss. Configurable data generators (via YAML) allow fine-grained control over splits, OOD scenarios, and task parameters for all tasks except BDD-OIA.

## Method

rsbench comprises three components: (1) a curated collection of L&R tasks provably affected by RSs, including three novel tasks (MNMath for multi-equation arithmetic, MNLogic for random CNF formulas, and SDD-OIA as a synthetic 3D traffic scene replacement for BDD-OIA) alongside established tasks; (2) model-level metrics including concept confusion matrices and concept collapse Cls(C); and (3) countrss, a novel algorithm that encodes the RS counting problem as a propositional logic formula over binary matrices A∈{0,1}^{k×k} mapping ground-truth to predicted concepts, then uses model counting solvers (PyEDA) to count optimal solutions satisfying the constraint that all training labels remain correct. The paper evaluates five architectures—DeepProbLog, Logic Tensor Networks, Concept Bottleneck Models, black-box NNs, and CLIP with TCAV—on three tasks, demonstrating that all models exhibit low concept quality despite high label accuracy, and that OOD performance degrades significantly due to RSs.

## Results

Across all three main tasks, every architecture achieves medium-to-high label F1 (0.57–0.99) while concept F1 remains low (0.04–0.61), confirming pervasive reasoning shortcuts. On SDD-OIA, CLIP achieves the best OOD mF1(Y) of 0.81 (only a 9% drop from in-distribution), while NN suffers the largest OOD degradation (46% drop to 0.47). CBM, despite receiving concept supervision, attains the highest concept F1 (0.61 on SDD-OIA) but the lowest label F1 (0.60) on complex tasks, revealing a label–concept trade-off. Concept collapse Cls(C) is highest for NeSy models (0.81–0.86 on SDD-OIA), indicating they mix distinct ground-truth concepts to solve the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is a benchmark rather than a method proposal, so the comparison reveals how each architecture family handles reasoning shortcuts. DPL (DeepProbLog) achieves strong in-distribution label F1 (0.80–0.94) but exhibits the highest concept collapse (0.61–0.86) and near-zero concept F1 (0.03–0.49), meaning it solves tasks by conflating distinct concepts. LTN (Logic Tensor Networks) shows similar label performance but with lower concept collapse on Kand-Logic (0.00) while still achieving only 0.35 concept F1. CBM, the only model receiving explicit concept supervision, achieves the best concept F1 (0.44–0.61) but the worst label F1 on complex tasks (0.36 on Kand-Logic, 0.60 on SDD-OIA), because its linear bottleneck cannot express the multi-step reasoning required. CLIP (ViT-B/32) is the most OOD-robust (only 9% mF1(Y) drop on SDD-OIA) and shows the lowest concept collapse (0.23), yet its concept F1 remains low (0.04–0.43), indicating that even a powerful foundation model learns semantically confused concepts when trained only on labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates and extends the line of work on reasoning shortcuts (RSs) in neuro-symbolic models initiated by Marconato et al. (NeurIPS 2023, ICML 2023, arXiv 2024), which identified RSs, provided the BDD-OIA dataset, and developed the theoretical framework for counting optimal RSs. The key departure is the introduction of rsbench as a unified, extensible benchmark suite that gathers previously scattered RS-affected tasks, adds entirely new ones (MNMath, MNLogic, SDD-OIA), and extends evaluation beyond NeSy architectures to include CBMs, black-box NNs, and foundation models (CLIP). The countrss algorithm operationalizes the prior theoretical counting results into a practical tool using model-counting solvers (PyEDA), enabling a priori verification of RS presence without training any model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: existing L&R benchmarks neglect RSs entirely, and RS-affected datasets were scattered across multiple papers with no common evaluation protocol. The finding that even CBMs receiving explicit concept supervision achieve F1(C) below 60% challenges the assumption that partial concept annotation is sufficient to ground semantics, suggesting RS mitigation remains an open problem. The countrss tool and configurable data generators (especially SDD-OIA with its OOD ambulance scenario) provide concrete infrastructure for future mitigation research. Limitations include the reliance on TCAV for post-hoc concept extraction in black-box models (which can introduce noise), the exclusion of BDD-OIA from countrss due to its complexity, and the need for scalability improvements to handle larger concept spaces.
