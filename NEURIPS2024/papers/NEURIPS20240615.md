## Problem

Prior cryptanalytical model extraction work identified weight sign extraction as the critical bottleneck, and Canales-Martínez et al. improved it to polynomial time but only evaluated that component in isolation. No study had benchmarked the full signature-plus-sign extraction pipeline on standard benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10) rather than random data, leaving unclear which stage actually dominates runtime and whether the combined pipeline is feasible for practical model sizes.

## Input / Output

The method requires black-box query access to a fully-connected ReLU network with known architecture and access to raw output logits (not softmax probabilities). Queries are generated from normal distributions or calculated critical-point coordinates in the input space. The output is the complete set of model weights and biases at float64 precision, including the correct sign of every weight, enabling functional equivalence with the target model.

## Method

The paper unifies Carlini et al.'s signature extraction with Canales-Martínez et al.'s Neuron Wiggle sign extraction into a single JAX-based codebase, revealing that sign extraction is no longer the bottleneck once both stages are measured under identical conditions. The key optimisation identifies 'hard' neurons (those with sign-recovery confidence below 0.6 after s=15 iterations) and handles them by launching parallel signature extractions in the next layer for all 2^k sign combinations, filtering out incorrect configurations via a sample-distance check that detects signature errors. Additional speedups come from reducing sign-extraction iterations from s=200 to s=15, memory deduplication of redundant partial signatures, and demonstrating that the float32→float64 precision improvement step is unnecessary for sign recovery and can be skipped or run in parallel. These changes reduce sign extraction time by up to 14.8× and total extraction time by up to 2.5×, shifting the bottleneck to signature extraction and enabling a 16,721-parameter MNIST model to be extracted in 98 minutes.

## Results

On random models (layer 2 extraction), the proposed method reduces sign extraction time by up to 14.8× compared to Canales-Martinez et al. (e.g., 0.82 s → 0.05 s for a 10-5×2-1 model) and achieves up to 2.52× total extraction speedup (e.g., 107.70 s → 46.15 s for a 70-35×2-1 model). For a 16,721-parameter MNIST model (784-64×2-1), full extraction completes in 98 minutes versus ≥150 minutes previously. Sign extraction drops to as little as 0.002% of total extraction time for larger models, revealing that signature extraction—not sign extraction—is the true bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Carlini et al. [1], Canales-Martinez et al.'s Neuron Wiggle method already reduces sign extraction from exponential to polynomial time (e.g., 76.39 s → 0.82 s for a 10-5×2-1 model, and ≈1.3×10⁵ s → 4.98 s for a 50-25×2-1 model). The proposed method further accelerates sign extraction by 11–16× over Canales-Martinez et al. [2] (e.g., 0.82 s → 0.05 s, 20.47 s → 1.82 s) by reducing the number of critical-point iterations from s=200 to s=15 and identifying 'hard' neurons whose signs are resolved via parallel next-layer signature extraction rather than repeated wiggling. Total end-to-end extraction is up to 2.52× faster than Canales-Martinez et al. (70-35×2-1 model: 107.70 s → 46.15 s for signature extraction), with the gap widening when precision improvement is bypassed (up to 6.6×). Crucially, the paper demonstrates that sign extraction is no longer the bottleneck—signature extraction now dominates—contradicting the assumption in both Carlini et al. and Canales-Martinez et al. that sign recovery was the limiting factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the cryptanalytic model extraction pipeline established by Carlini et al. (CRYPTO 2020) for signature extraction and Canales-Martinez et al. (2023) for polynomial-time sign extraction via the Neuron Wiggle method. Rather than introducing a new extraction paradigm, it integrates both into a single codebase, identifies inefficiencies in their interaction, and re-evaluates which component dominates end-to-end extraction time. It also departs from prior work's reliance on models trained on random data by benchmarking on standard benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10), revealing that representation sparsity in real-data-trained models makes signature extraction substantially harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is redefining the bottleneck: sign extraction, long assumed to be the critical limitation, becomes a negligible fraction of total time, shifting optimization focus to signature extraction and precision improvement. This reframing has direct implications for where future work should invest effort to scale extraction to deeper, larger networks. However, the paper also exposes significant limitations: extraction of deeper layers (beyond 2 hidden layers on MNIST) becomes infeasible within practical timeframes, results show high variance across training and extraction seeds, and the tested models remain small (≤16,721 parameters). Open questions include whether the method scales to the 8+ layer, 256-neuron architectures common in practice, and whether the findings generalize beyond fully-connected ReLU networks.
