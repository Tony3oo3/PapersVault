## Problem

Message Passing Neural Networks require O(E) memory to aggregate messages over all edges, making them infeasible for large non-sparse graphs (e.g., social networks with 10^8–10^9 nodes and average degree 10^2–10^3). Existing approaches either target large sparse graphs via subgraph sampling or small dense graphs, leaving a gap for graphs that are simultaneously large and dense. This paper seeks a fundamentally different pipeline with memory and time complexity linear in the number of nodes rather than edges.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected weighted or unweighted graph (N×N adjacency matrix A) with a D-channel node feature matrix S ∈ R^{N×D}, standardized to [0,1]. A one-off O(E) preprocessing step fits a K-community ICG (affiliation matrix Q ∈ [0,1]^{N×K}, magnitudes r ∈ R^K, features F ∈ R^{K×D}) via Frobenius-norm minimization. The ICG-NN then produces node-level representations H^{(L)} ∈ R^{N×D^{(L)}} for classification, or predicted future signal windows for spatio-temporal forecasting, all in O(N) per forward pass. For spatio-temporal settings, the ICG is fitted once to the fixed graph topology while node features vary over T time steps.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is a constructive Weak Regularity Lemma (Theorem 3.1) proving that any approximate minimizer of the Frobenius-norm loss between the graph and a K-community ICG also achieves small cut-metric error bounded by O(N/√E · √(R/K) + δ), where K is independent of N. Practically, the ICG is fitted by gradient descent on a reformulated Frobenius loss (Proposition 4.1) with O(K²N + KE) time and O(KN + E) space, initialized from leading eigenvectors of A. The ICG-NN architecture alternates node-wise operations (MLPs applied independently per node) with community-space operations (synthesis QF, analysis Q†S, and a shared MLP or Transformer on the K×D community features), achieving O(NK + K²D²) per layer versus O(ED) for MPNNs. For graphs too large to fit in GPU memory, a Subgraph SGD variant samples M ≪ N random nodes per step, trading memory for a N/M increase in optimization steps while provably approximating full-graph gradients.

## Results

On spatio-temporal traffic forecasting, ICGu-NN achieves MAE of 3.12±0.01 on METR-LA and 1.56±0.00 on PEMS-BAY, matching GraphWaveNet on PEMS-BAY and outperforming DCRNN, AGCRN, and all T&S/TTS variants on both datasets. The relative Frobenius approximation error is 0.44 (METR-LA) and 0.34 (PEMS-BAY). In runtime analysis on Erdős-Rényi graphs with p=0.5, ICGu-NN exhibits a square-root relationship with GCN forward-pass time, confirming the theoretical O(N) vs O(E) complexity advantage. On node classification (tolothers), ICGu-NN with subgraph SGD shows only 2.8% ROC AUC degradation when a small fraction of nodes is removed, with performance stabilizing once more than 10% of nodes are sampled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICGu-NN (MAE 3.12/1.56) outperforms DCRNN (3.22/1.64), AGCRN (3.16/1.61), and all four T&S/TTS variants (3.22–3.35 / 1.65–1.72) on both METR-LA and PEMS-BAY. It ties with GraphWaveNet on PEMS-BAY (1.56 vs 1.56) but trails it on METR-LA (3.12 vs 3.05), making GraphWaveNet the only baseline that beats ICGu-NN on any dataset. The unconstrained variant ICGu-NN consistently outperforms the constrained ICG-NN (3.42/1.76), confirming that directly learning community features is more effective than routing them through a neural network. In the runtime comparison against GCN on dense Erdős-Rényi graphs, ICGu-NN achieves a square-root speedup, validating the O(N) vs O(E) theoretical complexity gap. The paper also reports state-of-the-art node classification on Flickr and competitive results on tolothers and Reddit (in appendices), though these are not in the main body table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the message-passing paradigm (MPNNs) that dominates graph learning, instead representing graphs as low-rank intersecting community graphs (ICGs) and performing all learning in O(N) rather than O(E) complexity. Theoretically, it builds on the Weak Regularity Lemma (Frieze–Kannan, Lovász–Szegedy) but provides a new constructive version: rather than the exponential-time algorithm of Frieze–Kannan or the NP-hard cut-norm optimization, it shows that minimizing the Frobenius norm suffices to guarantee small cut-metric error. Conceptually, the ICG construction is related to stochastic block model fitting (e.g., BigCLAM) and variational graph autoencoders, but differs by allowing negative community magnitudes and by being grounded in the regularity lemma's uniform approximation guarantees rather than probabilistic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper breaks the traditional dichotomy between methods suited to small/dense graphs and those suited to large/sparse graphs, offering the first rigorously motivated pipeline for large *and* dense graphs with O(N) learning cost after a one-off O(E) ICG precomputation. Key limitations temper the impact: the ICG construction is restricted to undirected graphs, the ICG-NN is tied to a specific ICG (no transferability across graphs), and empirical validation is on relatively small graphs (≤7k nodes for runtime, 207–325 nodes for temporal tasks), so the promised scalability to 10⁸–10⁹ node social networks remains untested. Open questions include extending ICGs to directed graphs, studying the expressive power of ICG-NNs, and whether the Frobenius-to-cut-metric guarantee holds tightly enough for real-world sparse natural graphs where K < N²/E.
