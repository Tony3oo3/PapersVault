## Problem

Bounding the generalization gap for stochastic learning algorithms whose output distribution depends on the training data (data-dependent priors) requires controlling the log-moment generating function ln E_X[E_{h~Q_X}[exp(λΔ(h,X))]], which is the principal technical obstacle. Existing results for the Gibbs algorithm and PAC-Bayes bounds with data-dependent priors contained unnecessary logarithmic factors in n and artifact terms, and no unified framework existed covering bounded, subgaussian, and variance-dependent losses simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training sample X = (X_1,...,X_n) drawn i.i.d. from μ, a nonnegative a-priori measure π on the hypothesis space H (which need not be a probability measure, e.g., Lebesgue measure on R^d), and a Hamiltonian H(h,x) defining the stochastic algorithm via dQ_x(h) ∝ exp(H(h,x))dπ(h). It produces high-probability (1−δ) upper bounds on the generalization gap Δ(h,X) = L(h) − L̂(h,X) for a single hypothesis h sampled from Q_X, with the bound's tightness depending on the Hamiltonian's stability coefficient c, the loss range b, and optionally the variance v(h) of the chosen hypothesis.

## Method

The core mechanism is Proposition 3.1, which reduces bounding ln E_X[E_{h~Q_X}[e^{F(h,X)}]] to bounding sup_h ψ_F(h), where ψ_F(h) = ln E[e^{F(h,X)+H_Q(h,X)−E[H_Q(h,X')]}]. The key insight (Lemma 3.3) is that the canonical Hamiltonian H_Q = H − ln Z inherits a bounded-difference condition from H with at most a factor-of-2 inflation, because the log-partition function ln Z is 1-Lipschitz in the Hamiltonian's bounded differences. This allows direct application of McDiarmid's inequality and Bernstein-type concentration to the combined function λΔ + H_Q. A Cauchy-Schwarz decomposition (equation 3) further separates the F-contribution from the H_Q-contribution, enabling the combination of different concentration techniques (e.g., variance-dependent Bernstein bounds for the loss term with bounded-difference bounds for the Hamiltonian term). The framework extends to subgaussian Hamiltonians via a log-partition concentration lemma (Lemma A.6) and yields competitive bounds for the Gibbs algorithm, Gaussian randomization of stable algorithms, and PAC-Bayes bounds with data-dependent priors.

## Results

The paper establishes generalization bounds for Hamiltonian algorithms of the form dQ_X(h) ∝ exp(H(h,X))dπ(h). For bounded losses with Hamiltonian satisfying a bounded-difference condition of size c, the headline bound is Δ(h,X) ≤ b(c + √(ln(1/δ)/(2n))) (Theorem 3.4). A Bernstein-type refinement (Theorem 3.5) gives Δ(h,X) ≤ 2√(v(h)(c² + ln(1/δ)/n)) + b(c² + ln(1/δ)/n), which scales with the variance of the chosen hypothesis. For the Gibbs algorithm (c = β/n), this yields the clean bound Δ(h,X) ≤ β/n + √(ln(1/δ)/(2n)), which the authors note they could not find in prior literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Gibbs-algorithm bound Δ ≤ β/n + √(ln(1/δ)/(2n)) improves over Rivasplata et al. [2020]'s Δ ≤ 4β/n + (2+ln((1+√e)/δ))/√n in both constants (factor of 4 on β/n) and, more importantly, in its dependence on the confidence parameter δ (√(ln(1/δ)) vs. ln(1/δ) inside a 1/√n term). For Gaussian randomization of stable algorithms, Theorem 4.1 avoids the logarithmic dependence on n present in Rivasplata et al. [2018]'s bound (7), replacing the worst-case nc_A² term with the actual variance V(A) of the algorithm output, which can be arbitrarily small on benign distributions. The paper's empirical-error-dependent bound (Corollary 3.7, equation 5) is shown to dominate the inverted 'little KL-divergence' bounds of Rivasplata et al. [2020], Dziugaite and Roy [2018], and Lever et al. [2013], which carry a superfluous β/n^{3/2} artifact and a logarithmic n-dependence. The subgaussian extension (Theorem 3.8) provides a bound Δ ≤ ρ(h)(√32·σ + √(4ln(1/δ)/n)) that scales with the individual hypothesis's subgaussian parameter rather than a uniform supremum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies generalization analysis for the Gibbs algorithm, randomizations of stable deterministic algorithms, and PAC-Bayesian bounds with data-dependent priors under a single 'Hamiltonian algorithm' framework, where the output distribution takes the form dQ_X(h) ∝ exp(H(h,X)) dπ(h). The core technical tool (Proposition 3.1) builds on classical McDiarmid/Bernstein concentration inequalities but applies them to the log-Radon-Nikodym derivative of the algorithm's output, a decomposition that prior works (Rivasplata et al. 2020, Rivasplata et al. 2018, Dziugaite & Roy 2018) handled separately for each setting. The method departs from the PAC-Bayesian tradition of McAllester (1999) and Langford & Seeger (2001) by treating the prior as data-dependent and bounding the resulting log-moment generating function directly, rather than relying on exchangeability of expectations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework yields notably clean bounds (e.g., Δ ≤ β/n + √(ln(1/δ)/(2n)) for the Gibbs algorithm) that remove logarithmic factors and artifact terms present in prior results, and the Bernstein-type variance-dependent bounds (Theorem 3.5, Corollary 3.7) have no direct comparable in the literature for the Gibbs algorithm. The data-dependent prior results (Theorem 4.3) provide a principled alternative to differential-privacy-based control (Dziugaite & Roy 2018) for PAC-Bayes with instance-dependent priors. Key open questions include extending the method to the β > n regime (which the authors note is unlikely without considerable modifications), non-iid data via Markov chain mixing, and iterated stochastic algorithms like SGLD where the randomization is repeated.
