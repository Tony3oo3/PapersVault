## Problem

Motion-to-music and music-to-motion generation have been studied in isolation, with no prior work unifying them into a single framework that models long-term synchronization and correspondence between the two modalities. Existing approaches either compress video frames into a single image (losing temporal structure), generate raw waveforms directly (incurring prohibitive computational cost), or segment long sequences into 1–2 second clips (destroying coherence). The paper addresses this by proposing the first unified multi-modal framework for joint, long-term, beat-matched motion-music generation.

## Input / Output

For motion-to-music, the input is a sequence of 14 2D body keypoints (extracted at 60 fps via OpenPose Body-25) paired with a 16 kHz music waveform; for music-to-motion, the input is the music waveform (or MFCC features on BHS Dance) and the output is the 2D keypoint sequence. Music is internally represented as an 80-band mel-spectrogram (FFT 1024, hop 256) to reduce sequence length, and the final audio output is synthesized via a pretrained BigVGAN vocoder. The framework supports variable-length generation (demonstrated at 10–30 s) through a pad-and-truncate strategy in the diffusion model, and motion frames are evenly downsampled to match the music frame count before latent-space alignment.

## Method

The core architecture is a Bidirectional Contrastive Rhythmic VAE (BiCoR-VAE) that encodes motion and music into a shared latent space, where rhythmic contrastive learning aligns cross-modal representations using a kinematic amplitude indicator derived from the motion directogram (Eq. 1–2) to construct contrastive clips across different rhythmic-intensity categories. Training is two-stage: the music VAE is first trained with reconstruction + KL + GAN losses to preserve mel-spectrogram fidelity, then frozen while the motion VAE is trained with reconstruction + KL + contrastive losses. A DiT-based diffusion model operates in this aligned latent space for cross-modal generation (motion-to-music or music-to-motion) with classifier-free guidance. For joint multi-modal generation, a cross-guidance sampling strategy performs unconditional denoising before a threshold step T_c, then switches to conditional denoising where each modality's estimated clean latent conditions the other, enabling synchronized generation without additional training.

## Results

On the AIST++ Dance motion-to-music benchmark, MoMu-Diffusion achieves a Beat Hit Score (BHS) of 98.6% and F1 of 98.1, substantially outperforming the previous SOTA LORIS (BHS 90.8%, F1 94.5) and CDCD (BHS 89.3%, F1 92.7). On the Floor Exercise 25s subset, the F1 gap widens to 71.4 vs. 62.7 (LORIS), and on Figure Skating 25s it reaches 69.0 vs. 62.7. For music-to-motion on AIST++ Dance, MoMu-Diffusion attains F1 46.2 and FID 7.3, compared to D2M's F1 30.5 / FID 17.3 and DiffGesture's F1 33.4 / FID 18.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMu-Diffusion wins decisively on all seven baselines across both motion-to-music and music-to-motion tasks. Against LORIS (the prior SOTA for motion-to-music), it improves BHS from 90.8% to 98.6% and F1 from 94.5 to 98.1 on AIST++ Dance, and from 62.7 to 71.4 on Floor Exercise 25s. Against CDCD, the F1 gain is 5.4 points (98.1 vs. 92.7) on AIST++ Dance, with a much lower HSD (2.8 vs. 18.1) indicating far more consistent beat alignment. D2M-GAN and Foley Music are clearly outperformed, with Foley Music's BHS of only 41.0% reflecting its inability to capture rhythmic structure. For music-to-motion, MoMu-Diffusion surpasses D2M by 15.7 F1 points (46.2 vs. 30.5) and reduces FID from 17.3 to 7.3 on AIST++ Dance; it also outperforms DiffGesture by 12.8 F1 points and 11.3 FID points. The CMT baseline, designed for short-clip rhythmic consistency, shows a BHS of only 46.2%, confirming that its architecture cannot handle long-term synchronization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—motion-to-music (LORIS, CDCD, D2M-GAN) and music-to-motion (D2M, DiffGesture)—into a single bidirectional framework. It builds on the DiT architecture for long-sequence diffusion modeling and on contrastive learning paradigms (CLAP, Diff-Foley) for cross-modal alignment, but introduces a novel rhythmic contrastive objective grounded in kinematic amplitude indicators derived from motion directograms. The cross-guidance sampling strategy, which combines separately trained cross-modal denoisers for joint generation without additional training, departs from prior multi-modal diffusion approaches (e.g., MM-Diffusion) that require joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly model long-term (25–50 s) motion-music synchronization in both directions, addressing a gap where prior methods treated the two tasks in isolation and often lost temporal coherence. The rhythmic contrastive learning mechanism offers a principled way to align kinematic and musical beats in latent space, which could transfer to other motion-audio pairing problems. Limitations include reliance on 2D OpenPose keypoints (error accumulation), mel-spectrogram compression that discards fine acoustic detail, and untested scaling to larger datasets; extending to 3D motion and raw-waveform music generation remain open.
