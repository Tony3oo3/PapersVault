## Problem

Estimating global satellite network traffic from partial measurements is critical for traffic engineering and topology optimization, but existing methods rely on low-rank linear decomposition (matrix/tensor completion) that cannot capture the complex nonlinear spatio-temporal correlations inherent in large-scale, highly dynamic inter-satellite traffic. The problem is further compounded by the inherent sparsity of measured data caused by unstable inter-satellite links and uneven communication demands across satellite pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241506/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse 3D traffic tensor X ∈ R^(I×J×T) (origin × destination × time step) where only 2–10% of entries are observed, along with a satellite adjacency matrix A encoding inter-satellite link topology and a spatial distance/weight matrix W that modulates traffic by propagation delay. The output is the fully reconstructed traffic tensor X̃ ∈ R^(I×J×T) with all missing entries estimated. Tensor dimensions scale with constellation size (66 to 1584 satellites) and temporal horizon (30,000+ one-second time slices), making the input a very large, highly sparse tensor.

## Method

Satformer is an encoder-decoder architecture composed of stacked spatio-temporal modules, each containing a 2-layer GCN graph embedding (to process non-Euclidean satellite topology) followed by a Satformer Block with an Adaptive Sparse Spatio-Temporal Attention (ASSIT) mechanism. ASSIT partitions the input embedding into local D×D regions, computes per-region Q/K/V projections, applies a 2D center-window mask Ψ to sharpen attention on local neighborhoods, and then imposes learnable L1 regularization (via parameters W^r and W^s) to adaptively sparsify attention scores—dynamically allocating compute to high-density traffic regions while suppressing noise in sparse areas. A self-attention-based Transfer Module bridges encoder and decoder to propagate global temporal context and mitigate error accumulation over long sequences. The model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss plus weight decay, and the center window size and number of modules are tuned per dataset scale (e.g., window 16 for Iridium, 64 for Starlink).

## Results

On the three satellite network datasets (Iridium: 66 sats, Telesat: 298 sats, Starlink: 1584 sats), Satformer achieves NMAE of 0.098, 0.1017, and 0.1402 respectively at the most challenging 2% sampling ratio, with NRMSE of 0.1195, 0.1862, and 0.2754. Compared to the best mathematical baseline (LETC), Satformer reduces NMAE by 84–107% at 2% sampling. Against the best neural baseline (STCAGCN), Satformer improves NMAE by 8.57% (Iridium), 27.63% (Telesat), and 38.66% (Starlink) at 2% sampling, with gains widening as network scale increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Satformer outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and sampling ratio. Against the three mathematical baselines (HaLRTC, LATC, LETC), Satformer achieves 60–107% NMAE reduction at 2% sampling, with LATC performing worst overall (NMAE ≈ 0.58–0.62, nearly flat across sampling ratios). Among neural baselines, STCAGCN is the closest competitor on Iridium (NMAE 0.1064 vs. 0.0980 at 2%), while SAITS is closest on Telesat (0.1267 vs. 0.1017). The gap widens substantially with network scale: on Starlink, Satformer beats STCAGCN by 38.66% in NMAE and 32.32% in NRMSE at 2% sampling. CoSTCo and CDSA perform poorly on NRMSE (often >0.5), indicating they struggle with large-magnitude errors despite moderate NMAE. DAIN and SPIN show intermediate performance but are consistently outperformed by Satformer by 8–39% in NMAE depending on dataset size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm in traffic data estimation, which relies on low-rank linear decomposition (matrix/tensor completion via nuclear norm minimization, CP/Tucker factorization) or neural variants that still largely capture linear spatio-temporal structure. Satformer departs from this by combining a Graph Convolutional Network for non-Euclidean satellite topology with a novel Adaptive Sparse Spatio-Temporal Attention (ASSIT) mechanism that applies local-region masking and L1 sparsity regularization to standard multi-head attention, drawing on the Transformer architecture [Vaswani et al.] and sparse-attention ideas from the NLP literature. A transfer module bridges encoder and decoder via self-attention over the full temporal sequence, a design choice motivated by error accumulation in long-sequence tensor completion that prior methods (CoSTCo, DAIN, SPIN, STCAGCN) do not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As mega-constellations like Starlink (1584 satellites) become operational, the ability to estimate global traffic matrices from as little as 2% sampling is practically critical for routing, congestion control, and access management, and Satformer's 38% NMAE improvement over the best neural baseline on Starlink-scale data is meaningful for deployment. However, the evaluation relies entirely on synthetically generated traffic (no real inter-satellite traffic data is publicly available), which limits confidence in real-world generalization. The authors themselves flag two open questions: the black-box nature of the model hinders trust in safety-critical network decisions, and the computational cost, while lower than baselines, may still be prohibitive for on-satellite inference given limited onboard resources.
