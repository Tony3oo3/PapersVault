## Problem

Self-attention in vision transformers is commonly interpreted as performing perceptual grouping, where tokens attend to similar tokens within the same object. However, it remains unclear whether and how self-attention also captures contextual information from dissimilar tokens (e.g., background, other objects), and no prior work has dissected the token-to-token interaction mechanism within self-attention layers beyond visualizing [CLS] attention maps or layer outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241729/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weight matrices (W_q, W_k) of pre-trained ViT models (16 models across 6 families: ViT, DeiT, CLIP, DINO, SimMIM, I-JEPA) rather than on activations, requiring no forward pass for the core decomposition. For empirical validation, it consumes RGB images from ImageNet (for optimal-attention visualization), ADE20K (for object-level semantic segmentation), and the Odd-One-Out dataset (for attention preference ratios). Outputs are structured as per-head, per-layer singular modes—each a triple of (left singular vector, singular value, right singular vector)—along with scalar cosine similarity metrics and spatial query/key projection maps rendered as red/cyan channel overlays.

## Method

The paper applies singular value decomposition to the query-key interaction matrix W_q^T W_k, decomposing the bilinear attention score into a sum of singular modes where each mode pairs a left singular vector (query feature direction) with a right singular vector (key feature direction). The cosine similarity between these paired vectors quantifies whether a given mode performs grouping (high similarity) or contextualization (low similarity), and this metric is tracked across layers to reveal a systematic shift from grouping in early layers to contextualization in late layers. A key theoretical property is that singular vectors are invariant to invertible basis changes in query/key space, making them intrinsic, uniquely defined feature directions of the attention layer—unlike arbitrary query/key embeddings. Semantic interpretability is established by finding images from ImageNet that maximize the product of projections onto a singular vector pair, revealing interpretable interactions such as part-to-part, object-to-object, and foreground-to-background attention.

## Results

This is an interpretability/analysis paper with no standard benchmark metrics. The key quantitative findings are: (1) On the Odd-One-Out (O3) dataset, the ratio of attention on same objects is dominant in early layers across most ViTs, while attention on different objects and background increases in deeper layers (Fig. 2); (2) The weighted-average cosine similarity between left and right singular vectors of the query-key interaction matrix is high in early layers and shows a decreasing trend in deeper layers, dropping toward 0 in some middle layers (Fig. 3); (3) On ADE20K, the probability that left and right singular vectors highlight the same object is higher in early layers and lower in late layers (Fig. 6). Notably, self-supervised models (SimMIM, DINO) exhibit a 'concave' trend with increased attention to similar tokens in the last few layers, which disappears after fine-tuning on classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to be compared against baselines; it is an empirical analysis of 16 pre-trained ViT models from 6 families (ViT, DeiT, CLIP, DINO, SimMIM, I-JEPA). The 'comparison' is across architectures and training objectives: classification-trained models (ViT, DeiT, CLIP) consistently show the grouping-to-contextualization shift, while self-supervised models (SimMIM, DINO) show a more complex concave pattern where the last few layers revert toward attending to similar tokens. I-JEPA's encoder behaves more like a classification model despite its self-supervised objective, while its predictor module shows overall high cosine similarity. The paper positions its SVD-based analysis as extending prior work on single feature directions [34] and query-key column pairs [13] to pairs of non-canonical interacting singular vectors, but does not benchmark against these methods on a shared task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on finding feature directions in neural networks via SVD (Radhakrishnan et al., Science 2024) and query-key pair analysis in LLMs (Dar et al., ACL 2023) by applying SVD to the combined query-key interaction matrix W_q^T W_k, as formalized in the transformer circuits framework (Elhage et al., 2021). Prior ViT interpretability studies focused on [CLS] token attention maps or attention layer outputs, while this work shifts the lens to token-to-token interactions within self-attention layers. The key conceptual departure is treating left and right singular vectors as *pairs* of interacting feature directions rather than analyzing single axes, thereby extending explainability from individual neurons to their interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-attention in ViTs performs both perceptual grouping (early layers) and contextualization via dissimilar-token attention (late layers) challenges the dominant 'attention highlights objects' narrative and provides a more nuanced mechanistic picture. The semantic interpretability of singular modes (e.g., part-to-part, object-to-object, foreground-to-background attention) offers a practical new tool for probing what specific attention heads compute. Open questions include how training objectives (self-supervised vs. classification) shape the embedding geometry, the role of the value matrix, and whether the SVD-based analysis generalizes to other modalities or architectures beyond ViTs.
