## Problem

Visual instruction tuning (supervised fine-tuning on pre-collected datasets) cannot efficiently train VLMs as decision-making agents in multi-step interactive environments because it lacks environment interaction and may fail when pre-collected data lacks sufficient scenario diversity. While RL has been widely applied to text-based LLMs, end-to-end VLM fine-tuning with RL for goal-directed multi-step tasks remains unexplored, largely because VLMs generate open-ended text rather than the vectorized actions assumed by classic policy gradient methods.

## Input / Output

The VLM receives an RGB image observation concatenated with a task-specific text prompt that encodes the task description, the current legal action space (which may be state-dependent, as in ALFWorld), and a required JSON output template. The VLM produces a single open-ended text utterance containing a chain-of-thought reasoning segment followed by a text-based action in the form '"action": "a_t"'. A post-processing function f then searches for the action keyword in the output and maps it to a discrete or text-based legal action; if no valid action is found, a uniformly random legal action is sampled to keep the episode alive.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes the entire VLM (CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, Mistral-7B LLM backbone, and MLP projector) end-to-end via PPO, treating the VLM's full text output as the action. The key architectural insight is embedding CoT reasoning directly into the action space: the VLM generates intermediate reasoning tokens before the final action token, enabling more efficient exploration of the decision space. To compute the action probability for policy gradient updates, the paper introduces a scaling factor λ ∈ [0,1] that down-weights the log-likelihood of CoT tokens relative to action tokens, since CoT tokens typically have 5–40× larger absolute log-probability magnitude and would otherwise dominate the gradient. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on task-specific instruction-following data (20k–50k samples per task), followed by on-policy PPO with LoRA (r=128, α=256) and a 3-layer MLP value head with GAE. Empirically, removing CoT reasoning causes a 21.2% average drop on gym_cards and 5.4% on ALFWorld, and λ must be kept moderate (0.2–0.5) for stable training.

## Results

On the gym_cards domain (average of NumberLine, EZPoints, Points24, Blackjack), the proposed RL fine-tuning method achieves 45.5% episode success rate, improving the LLaVA-sft baseline by 27.1% (18.4% → 45.5%) and surpassing GPT-4V (25.4%) and Gemini (28.6%). On the ALFWorld embodied domain, the method reaches 21.7% average success rate, outperforming GPT-4V (19.4%) and Gemini (13.5%) without requiring expert data. The CoT reasoning ablation shows that removing CoT drops gym_cards performance from 45.5% to 24.3% and ALFWorld from 21.7% to 16.3%, confirming CoT as a critical component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On gym_cards, the proposed method (45.5% avg) outperforms all non-expert baselines: CNN+RL (31.5%, +14.0%), Gemini (28.6%, +16.9%), GPT-4V (25.4%, +20.1%), and the LLaVA-sft initialization (18.4%, +27.1%). On ALFWorld, the method (21.7%) beats GPT-4V (19.4%, +2.3%), Gemini (13.5%, +8.2%), and LLaVA-sft (17.7%, +4.0%), but falls short of the BUTLER agent (37.0%) which relies on expert demonstration data. Notably, CNN+RL achieves 0% on ALFWorld, highlighting the necessity of language-based reasoning for embodied semantic tasks. The 7B LLaVA model fine-tuned with RL thus surpasses much larger commercial models (GPT-4V, Gemini) on both domains without requiring any expert data, though the gap to BUTLER on ALFWorld reflects the advantage of expert demonstrations in a high-dimensional action space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-for-LLM work (e.g., Ramamurthy et al., Snell et al., Archer) from purely text-based policies to end-to-end VLM fine-tuning, which the authors note has not been previously studied. It departs from prior VLM-as-agent work (e.g., EmbodiedGPT, Butler, prompting-based methods) by directly training the entire VLM with PPO rather than using frozen models with prompting or training only auxiliary MLP/transformer layers. A key conceptual departure is the identification of CoT reasoning as a critical component for RL training effectiveness, going beyond its established role as a prompting technique for frozen models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 7B VLM can surpass GPT-4V and Gemini on domain-specific decision-making tasks after RL fine-tuning, suggesting that task-specific RL can close the gap with much larger commercial models. The finding that CoT reasoning is not merely a prompting convenience but a structural necessity for effective RL exploration (with a sensitive scaling factor λ) opens questions about how reasoning traces interact with policy gradient signals in multimodal settings. Limitations include modest gains on ALFWorld (17.7%→21.7%) versus gym_cards (18.4%→45.5%), the Points24 task remaining unsolved by all methods, and evaluation restricted to a single 7B backbone, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse task distributions.
