## Problem

Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) frameworks rely on three simplifying assumptions—static environments, panoramic action spaces, and optimal expert supervision—that overestimate Sim2Real transfer capabilities and limit real-world applicability. This paper introduces Human-Aware VLN (HA-VLN), which relaxes all three assumptions by incorporating dynamic 3D human activities (SMPL models at 16 FPS), a limited 60° egocentric field of view, and sub-optimal expert demonstrations, creating a more realistic benchmark for embodied agents navigating human-populated indoor spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243794/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction (averaging 69 words in HA-R2R, with vocabulary expanding from 990 to 4,500 tokens compared to R2R), an egocentric RGB-D observation at 60° FOV and 640×480 resolution within a 2-second observation window, and its current state (3D position, heading, elevation). It outputs a discrete action from {forward, left, right, up, down, stop} at each timestep. A collision feedback signal is triggered when the agent-human distance drops below a 1-meter threshold, and human activities are rendered as 120-frame SMPL mesh sequences with shape (β∈ℝ¹⁰), pose (θ∈ℝ⁷²), and vertex (γ∈ℝ⁶⁸⁹⁰ˣ³) parameters.

## Method

Two agents are proposed, both featuring a cross-modal fusion module that dynamically weights BERT language embeddings and ResNet-152 visual features via cross-attention. VLN-CM is an LSTM sequence-to-sequence model trained by imitation learning from a handcrafted Oracle agent (which uses A* path planning on a navigation graph with human-occupied nodes removed) via cross-entropy loss. VLN-DT is an autoregressive Decision Transformer trained entirely on 10⁴ random walk trajectories (max 30 steps, 15-step context window, initial return-to-go of 5) without any expert supervision, using return-to-go conditioning and three reward types (target, distance, human collision). The HA3D simulator integrates 435 SMPL motion models (generated from 145 GPT-4-validated activity descriptions via Motion Diffusion Model) into 90 Matterport3D building scenes, with human placement estimated from building area divided by 67 m² per capita.

## Results

On the HA-VLN task, the Oracle agent achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 0.91 (seen) and 0.89 (unseen) with a Navigation Error (NE) of 0.62 m and Target Collision Rate (TCR) of 0.04. The best SOTA VLN agent (Airbert, retrained) reaches only SR=0.50 (seen) and SR=0.40 (unseen), a 49% gap from the Oracle. Under zero-shot transfer, performance degrades further: Airbert's SR drops from 0.75 to 0.25 (unseen), a 66.7% reduction. The proposed VLN-DT, trained solely on 10⁴ random walk trajectories without expert supervision, achieves SR=0.21 (seen) / 0.11 (unseen), comparable to the expert-supervised VLN-CM (SR=0.19 / 0.12), demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four SOTA VLN baselines evaluated on HA-VLN, Airbert [16] consistently outperforms the others, achieving the lowest NE (3.98 m seen / 5.24 m unseen) and highest SR (0.50 seen / 0.40 unseen) in the retrained setting. Rec (OSCAR) [21] is a close second (SR=0.42/0.38), while Speaker-Follower [12] lags significantly (SR=0.34/0.23). All baselines suffer substantial degradation when dynamic humans are introduced: SR drops by 19–35% (seen) and 47–65% (unseen) compared to the human-free setting, and zero-shot transfer is even more severe (up to 71.8% SR drop for Rec OSCAR unseen). Despite retraining, no SOTA agent closes the gap to the Oracle (SR 0.91/0.89), with the best agent (Airbert) still 49% below Oracle in unseen environments. The proposed VLN-DT, trained exclusively on random trajectories, matches the expert-supervised VLN-CM in performance (SR 0.21 vs. 0.19 seen), while VLN-CM's performance collapses when trained on 100% random data (SR drops to 0.03), highlighting VLN-DT's superior robustness to suboptimal supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) task established by Anderson et al. (R2R, CVPR 2018) and the Decision Transformer framework of Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2021) by deliberately relaxing three simplifying assumptions that have characterized the field: panoramic action spaces, static environments, and optimal expert supervision. The VLN-CM agent builds directly on the LSTM sequence-to-sequence architecture from the original R2R paper, while VLN-DT adapts the Decision Transformer's return-to-go conditioning to a navigation setting. The HA3D simulator integrates the Matterport3D photorealistic scenes with SMPL-based human motion models generated via Motion Diffusion Models, filling a gap identified in prior simulators (Habitat, AI2-THOR, Gibson) that lack dynamic human elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HA-VLN benchmark and HA3D simulator provide the VLN community with a more realistic evaluation setting that better reflects Sim2Real transfer challenges, and the dramatic performance drops of SOTA agents (up to 70% SR reduction zero-shot) underscore how much current VLN research may overestimate real-world readiness. The finding that a Decision Transformer trained purely on random trajectories matches an expert-supervised LSTM agent suggests expert demonstrations may be less critical than assumed, opening a path toward data-efficient training. However, the framework is limited to indoor environments, models only basic human movement without social nuance, and the absolute performance of the proposed agents remains low (SR ≤ 0.21), leaving substantial room for improvement before practical deployment.
