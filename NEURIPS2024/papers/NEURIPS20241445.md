## Problem

State-of-the-art dual-frame fluid motion estimation methods for 3D particle tracking velocimetry (e.g., DeepPTV, GotFlow3D) rely on fully supervised training with large annotated datasets, which is impractical in PTV where data collection demands precise tracer particles, tailored illumination, and camera settings, and where rare scenarios (unique geometries, disease-related cytoplasmic flows) make comprehensive datasets infeasible. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a purely self-supervised framework that outperforms supervised baselines using as little as 1% of training samples (without labels) while also achieving strong cross-domain robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two consecutive unstructured 3D particle sets X_t ∈ R^{n1×3} and X_{t+Δt} ∈ R^{n2×3} (with potentially different particle counts) and outputs per-particle flow vectors F ∈ R^{n1×3} mapping each source particle to its displacement between frames. The particles are irregularly distributed in 3D space (not on a grid), and the optimal transport module additionally produces per-point confidence scores p_i that weight the reconstruction loss. During test-time, the DVE module further refines the initial flow by optimizing a residual R ∈ R^{n1×3} over ~150 Adam steps.

## Method

The architecture combines a static-dynamic graph GNN feature extractor (KNN graph processed by GeoSetConv layers, followed by a feature-space dynamic graph processed by EdgeConv layers) with an entropic optimal transport module that yields soft correspondence weights and an initial flow estimate. Training is fully self-supervised via three losses: a confidence-weighted Chamfer distance reconstruction loss, a coarse-scale smoothness loss, and a novel zero-divergence loss that splats the sparse flow onto a uniform 10×10×10 grid using inverse-squared-distance interpolation and penalizes the L1 norm of the discrete divergence at grid points. At test time, a Dynamic Velocimetry Enhancer (DVE) module optimizes a per-particle residual R against the reconstruction loss alone (omitting smoothness and divergence regularizers), providing on-the-fly adaptation to unseen domains with minimal overhead (~0.2s per sample on an RTX 3090 Ti).

## Results

On the FluidFlow3D benchmark, the proposed self-supervised method achieves an EPE of 0.0046 and NEPE of 0.0188, outperforming the SOTA supervised GotFlow3D (EPE 0.0049, NEPE 0.0244) while using fewer parameters (0.58M vs 1.44M) and faster inference (0.218s vs 0.758s per sample). Even when trained on only 1% of the data (130 samples, no labels), the method retains an EPE of 0.0085 and 97.61% Acc Strict, substantially outperforming all fully-supervised baselines on accuracy metrics. On the complex Forced MHD Turbulence case, the method reduces EPE by nearly half compared to GotFlow3D (0.0037 vs 0.0060).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four general-purpose scene-flow baselines (FlowNet3D, FLOT, PointPWC-Net, PV-RAFT), the proposed method achieves a 3–14× reduction in EPE (0.0046 vs 0.0165–0.0623) and a 5–75 percentage-point gain in Acc Strict, confirming that fluid-specific self-supervision with zero-divergence regularization far exceeds generic point-cloud flow estimation. Against the fluid-specific SOTA GotFlow3D, the method wins on every metric with full data (EPE 0.0046 vs 0.0049; Acc Strict 98.69% vs 93.15%; NEPE 0.0188 vs 0.0244) while being 3.5× faster at inference and using 60% fewer parameters. Notably, even the 1%-data variant (no labels) surpasses GotFlow3D on Acc Strict (97.61% vs 93.15%) and Outliers (2.40% vs 3.62%), though it trails slightly on EPE (0.0085 vs 0.0049). In the Sim2Real physical evaluation (DeformationFlow), Ours+ST tracks 22,882 matches versus 22,001 for vanilla SerialTrack while reducing per-iteration time. In the biological AVIC evaluation, Ours+Fm achieves lower MNDS (0.357/0.246/0.359) than Fm-track alone (0.358/0.283/0.399) across three treatment conditions, with the largest gain in the C2N setting (0.246 vs 0.283).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-frame fluid motion estimation task established by DeepPTV and GotFlow3D, which rely on fully supervised training with large labeled datasets. It departs from the broader scene flow estimation literature (PointPWC-Net, PV-RAFT, FLOT) by introducing a domain-specific zero-divergence loss grounded in incompressible fluid physics, implemented via a splat-based grid interpolation borrowed from high-dimensional filtering. The self-supervised formulation naturally enables test-time optimization, and the DVE module draws on the test-time training/adaptation paradigm (TAPiR, TENT) but applies it to per-sample residual flow refinement rather than parameter adaptation. The graph-based feature extractor (GeoSetConv + EdgeConv) follows GotFlow3D's architecture, positioning the contribution primarily in the loss design and optimization strategy rather than the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: PTV data collection requires specialized tracer particles, tailored illumination, and precise camera settings, making large labeled datasets infeasible for rare flow scenarios (unique geometries, disease-related cytoplasmic flows). Demonstrating that 1% unlabeled samples with a physics-informed loss and test-time refinement can match or exceed fully supervised SOTA addresses a real bottleneck in AI4Science applications. However, the zero-divergence assumption does not hold for compressible or strongly deforming flows (as acknowledged in the DeformationFlow experiment), and real-world validation remains limited to qualitative assessments on AVIC and efficiency metrics on DeformationFlow. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-frame tracking, whether the splat-based divergence loss scales to higher-resolution grids, and how the method performs when the incompressibility assumption is fundamentally violated.
