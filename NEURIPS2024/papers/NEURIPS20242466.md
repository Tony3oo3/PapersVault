## Problem

Multi-output regression (MOR) with high-dimensional outputs (K >> d) faces two coupled challenges: the outputs are hard to interpret for decision-making, and standard solvers scale poorly with the output dimension. This paper asks how to design a framework that simultaneously enforces sparsity on outputs for interpretability, achieves better computational scalability, and provides provable accuracy guarantees under a modern high-dimensional-output setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242466/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input feature vector x ∈ R^d and produces a high-dimensional output y ∈ R^K constrained to be s-sparse (||y||_0 ≤ s with s << K), optionally intersected with a feasible set F (e.g., R_+^K for non-negativity or {0,1}^K for binary outputs). The key structural assumption is that the output dimension K is polynomially larger than the input dimension d, and the true outputs are sparse, which is the regime where compression yields the largest computational savings.

## Method

The SHORE framework operates in two stages. In the training stage, a random Gaussian matrix Φ ∈ R^{m×K} (with m = O(δ^{-2}·s·log(K/τ)) << K) compresses the output space, and a compressed linear regressor Ŵ is obtained by solving min_W ||ΦY − WX||_F², which has a closed-form solution with complexity O(Kmn) versus O(K(n+d)d) for the uncompressed version. In the prediction stage, given a new input x, the sparse output is recovered by solving min_y ||Φy − Ŵx||_2² subject to y ∈ V_s^K ∩ F via a projected gradient descent algorithm (Algorithm 1) that alternates a gradient step in the compressed space with a projection onto the sparse-feasible set. The core theoretical insight is that the RIP property of Φ ensures the compressed training loss is within a (1+δ) factor of the uncompressed loss, and the prediction algorithm achieves global geometric convergence to a ball of radius O(||Φŷ − Ŵx||_2) around the optimal sparse solution, with the same order of generalization error bounds as the uncompressed model under subGaussian sampling.

## Results

On synthetic data (d=10⁴, K=2×10⁴, s=3, n=3×10⁴, SNR ∈ {0, 10, 30} dB), the compressed training loss ratio ‖ΦY−ŴX‖²_F/‖Y−ẐX‖²_F converges to 1 as the number of compressed rows m increases, validating Theorem 1's (1+δ) multiplicative bound. The proposed projected gradient descent algorithm outperforms FISTA on Precision@3 across all SNR levels and m values. The paper reports that OMP and Elastic Net were excluded from the main figure due to substantially longer running times, with the proposed method achieving better computational cost and accuracy on most metrics (Output-diff and Prediction-Loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: OMP [46], FISTA [2], and Elastic Net (EN) [47]. The proposed projected gradient descent (Algorithm 1/2) outperforms FISTA on Precision@3 across all tested SNR levels and compression dimensions m, as shown in Figure 1. OMP and Elastic Net are excluded from the main comparison figure due to their substantially longer running times, though the paper notes the proposed method also has better computational efficiency than OMP (citing Theorem 2's convergence guarantee without bounded coherence, unlike OMP in [17]). Running time comparisons in Table 2 (Appendix A.7) further demonstrate the efficiency advantage of the proposed algorithm over all three baselines. The paper does not report a single unified numerical table in the main text; quantitative comparisons are presented via Figure 1 (four panels: training loss ratio, Precision@3, Output-diff, Prediction-Loss vs. m) with shaded regions indicating standard deviations over 10 independent trials.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-output regression (MOR) by introducing the SHORE formulation, which adds a sparsity constraint on high-dimensional outputs (d ≪ K) to address interpretability in applications like algorithmic trading and LLM factuality checking. The two-stage framework draws on compressed sensing and random projection techniques (Hsu et al. 2009, PLST) for output-space dimensionality reduction, and on iterative thresholding methods (Blumensath & Davies 2009; Jain et al. 2014) for the sparse prediction stage. Unlike prior MOR compression work that typically assumes low-rank output structure or focuses on multi-label classification, SHORE requires only sparsity and subGaussian sample conditions, and provides matching-order generalization bounds before and after compression without additional regularity assumptions on the loss function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to scalable MOR in settings where output dimensionality is the bottleneck (e.g., portfolio construction over thousands of instruments), with provable guarantees that compression introduces only a (1+δ) multiplicative loss overhead. The O(Kd/n) generalization rate improves upon the O(K²d/n) bound of Yu et al. (2014) for multi-label learning, and the computational savings (m ≪ d) are concrete and quantified. Open questions include extending to nonlinear/nonconvex SHORE, designing better randomized projection matrices beyond i.i.d. Gaussian, and accommodating richer geometric structures on outputs beyond s-sparsity.
