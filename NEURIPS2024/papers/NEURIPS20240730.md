## Problem

Weight decay is ubiquitous in modern deep learning yet its actual role remains poorly understood: in the over-training regime it does not prevent memorization, and in the under-training regime (LLMs) explicit regularization is inherently unnecessary. The paper asks what mechanism actually makes weight decay beneficial in both regimes, moving beyond the classical explicit-regularizer interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240730/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study trains ResNet-18/34 and VGG on image classification (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, CIFAR-10-5m) and GPT-2-124M on language modeling (OpenWebText), tracking not just test error and training loss but also the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian, the parameter L2 norm, the noise scale σ_{η,λ}, and the effective learning rate η/‖w‖₂. A key analytical output is the stationary distribution of the SGD iterates in function space, characterized via EMA and snapshot ensembles, which reveals how the Jacobian norm is implicitly regularized.

## Method

In the over-training regime, the paper shows that weight decay combined with a large learning rate stabilizes the cross-entropy loss at a non-zero value, preventing the SGD noise from vanishing. This sustained noise, whose covariance is proportional to the Jacobian (via a decoupling approximation), induces an implicit regularization on the Jacobian norm whose strength is governed by the product ηλ. EMA or fine-tuning then reduces the noise to exploit the accumulated regularization. In the under-training regime, weight decay in AdamW is shown to be equivalent to a modified effective learning rate η/‖w‖₂ (derived via sign-SGD as a surrogate for Adam), which better balances the bias-variance tradeoff in stochastic optimization and additionally prevents bfloat16 loss divergence. The unifying conclusion is that weight decay is never useful as an explicit regularizer but instead reshapes the optimization dynamics in a desirable way.

## Results

In the over-training regime (ResNet-18 on Tiny-ImageNet), test error exhibits a U-shape as a function of the product η×λ, with an optimal value around ηλ ≈ 0.005; the Jacobian norm decreases monotonically with increasing ηλ, confirming that WD combined with large LR enables non-vanishing SGD noise that regularizes the Jacobian. In the under-training regime (GPT-2-124M on OpenWebText), weight decay (λ = 0.1 or 0.3) reduces the final training loss by approximately 0.02 compared to no weight decay, with the improvement appearing primarily in the latter portion of training. Weight decay is also essential for stable bfloat16 training at LR 0.001, where runs without WD exhibit irrecoverable loss divergences across all random seeds, while float32 training remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical analysis paper without external method baselines; the comparisons are internal ablations. In the over-training regime, training with weight decay and large LR consistently outperforms training without WD at the same LR (e.g., at LR=0.01, WD=0.005 achieves 12% test error vs. 24% without WD on CIFAR-10), and EMA consistently outperforms learning-rate decay as a post-training averaging strategy. The product η×λ, rather than either parameter alone, governs generalization: combinations with the same product yield similar test error and Jacobian norm. In the under-training regime, matching the effective learning rate η/‖w‖₂ without WD reproduces the full training loss trajectory of AdamW with WD (in float32), confirming that WD's optimization benefit is equivalent to a modified LR schedule. However, this equivalence breaks down in bfloat16, where WD remains necessary to prevent loss divergence at LR 0.001, a practical advantage that no LR-schedule modification can replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit regularization literature (Blanc et al., 2020; Damian et al., 2021) by showing that weight decay combined with large learning rates induces a loss-stabilization mechanism that mimics label-noise SGD without requiring explicit label perturbation, and generalizes the effective learning rate framework of Li & Arora (2019) and Li et al. (2020) beyond scale-invariant architectures to general ResNets. For the under-training regime, it builds on Hoffmann et al. (2022)'s brief observation that AdamW outperforms Adam for LLMs by providing a mechanistic explanation via ELR modulation of the bias-variance tradeoff, using sign SGD (Balles & Hennig, 2018) as a surrogate for Adam to derive the relevant ELR. The paper deliberately departs from the classical Tikhonov/ridge-regression narrative of weight decay as a capacity-constraining regularizer, arguing instead that its benefits are entirely dynamical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying claim that weight decay is 'never useful as an explicit regularizer' but instead modifies optimization dynamics desirably across both over-training and under-training regimes reframes how practitioners should think about this ubiquitous hyperparameter, with practical implications for LR scheduling, EMA usage, and low-precision training stability. However, the core mechanistic claims rest on conjectures rather than proofs, and the LLM experiments are limited to 124M parameters (GPT-2-Small) with reduced context length, leaving open whether the ELR and bfloat16-stability findings hold at the multi-billion-parameter scale where most modern LLMs are trained. The paper also does not address whether the loss-stabilization mechanism interacts differently with modern optimizers beyond AdamW or with architectures that have fundamentally different norm dynamics.
