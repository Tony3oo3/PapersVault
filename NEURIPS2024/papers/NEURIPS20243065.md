## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot semantic segmentation (CD-FSS) suffers from significant performance degradation when transferring a model from a source domain to data-scarce target domains with large distributional gaps. While prior work has shown that filtering frequency components at the input level can improve performance by up to 14% mIoU, no existing method explains why this works or leverages the insight adaptively at the feature level. This paper provides a theoretical interpretation linking frequency filtering to reduced inter-channel correlation and builds a lightweight, adaptive mechanism to exploit this effect.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic few-shot setting: K support images (each paired with a binary segmentation mask) and one query image, all resized to 400×400 pixels, are fed through a frozen ResNet-50 encoder. The APM and ACPA modules are inserted at the conv5_x feature map (2048 channels, 13×13 spatial resolution) during a brief 60-iteration target-domain adaptation phase, after which cosine-similarity affinity maps between support and query features are decoded into a pixel-level segmentation mask for the query image. No source-domain retraining is required; only the lightweight mask parameters (0.01%–2.5% of total parameters) are optimized on the target domain.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-module lightweight frequency masker. The Amplitude-Phase Masker (APM) applies FFT to the feature map, decomposes it into amplitude and phase spectra, and multiplies each by a learned sigmoid mask (h×w for APM-S or c×h×w for APM-M) to adaptively filter harmful frequency components before reconstructing via IFFT. The Adaptive Channel Phase Attention (ACPA) then feeds the enhanced phase spectrum through a SE block to produce per-channel attention weights, which are applied to the enhanced feature map to select task-relevant channels and align support–query feature spaces (validated via CKA). The key theoretical insight, proven via Euler's formula, is that inter-channel correlation in the spatial domain maps to phase-difference concentration around 0 and π in the frequency domain; by adaptively dispersing these phase differences, APM reduces mutual information between channels, alleviating channel bias and expanding activated regions for whole-object segmentation. The entire framework is model-agnostic (validated on both ResNet-50 and ViT backbones) and requires no source-domain training.

## Results

On the CD-FSS benchmark (4 target domains), the full method (APM-M + ACPA) achieves an average 1-shot mIoU of 60.03% and 5-shot mIoU of 65.18%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PATNet (56.06% / 61.99%) by 3.97% and 3.19% respectively. The lightweight APM-S variant (only 0.01% extra parameters) already reaches 58.93% / 62.86% average mIoU, outperforming PATNet by 2.87% / 0.87%. The largest single-domain gain is on Chest X-ray, where APM-M achieves 78.25% 1-shot mIoU versus 66.61% for PATNet (+11.64%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous CD-FSS state-of-the-art PATNet [24], the full method (APM-M + ACPA) wins on 7 of 8 dataset-shot settings, with the largest margin on Chest X-ray 1-shot (+11.64%) and Deepglobe 1-shot (+2.97%); the only loss is on ISIC 5-shot (51.16 vs. 53.58, −2.42%). Compared to the HSNet* baseline [31], the method improves average 1-shot mIoU by 12.35% and 5-shot by 15.08%, with particularly dramatic gains on Chest X-ray (+30.91% 1-shot) and Deepglobe (+7.67% 1-shot). Among frequency-based domain-generalization methods (DFF, GFNet, ARP, DAC), the proposed APM-M outperforms all of them by 7.7–11.0% average 1-shot mIoU, confirming that adaptive feature-level masking is more effective than fixed input-level frequency filtering. Against correlation-reduction approaches (MMC, SRIP, whitening), the method leads by 10.7–11.2% average 1-shot mIoU, demonstrating that frequency-domain disentanglement is both more stable and more effective than spatial-domain constraints in the few-shot regime. The method is also model-agnostic: when plugged into the ViT-based FPTrans [47], it improves average 1-shot mIoU from 60.93% to 62.32%, and substantially outperforms the SAM-based PerSAM [48] (41.35% average).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot semantic segmentation (CD-FSS, established by PATNet) and frequency-domain analysis of neural networks (Xu et al., Wang et al., Chen et al.). It departs from prior frequency-domain methods (DFF, GFNet, ARP, DAC) in two key ways: operating at the feature-map level rather than the input level, and being adaptively learned per target domain rather than fixed. It also contrasts with spatial-domain disentanglement approaches (MMC, SRIP, whitening) by arguing that frequency-domain operations offer finer granularity and global representation. The method is built on HSNet as a baseline and is designed as a model-agnostic plug-in (APM + ACPA) that requires no source-domain retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that untrained input-level frequency filtering can improve CD-FSS by up to 14% mIoU, and that this is mechanistically explained by reduced inter-channel correlation, provides both a practical tool and a theoretical lens for understanding domain generalization in dense prediction. The extreme parameter efficiency (0.01% for APM-S) makes it attractive as a drop-in module for existing FSS pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only four target domains, the need for per-domain adaptation (60 iterations with domain-specific learning rates), and the fact that the simple high-low frequency split degrades performance on FSS-1000, suggesting the principle may not transfer uniformly. Open questions include whether the frequency-disentanglement principle generalizes to other dense prediction tasks or scales to larger backbones and more diverse domain gaps.
