## Problem

Existing LLM compression methods are evaluated on inconsistent, limited protocols—different base models, datasets, and metrics—making fair cross-method comparison impossible. Moreover, efficiency evaluation remains largely theoretical (reporting only #MACs or #parameters) while ignoring practical concerns such as real hardware acceleration, training resource cost, and post-compression trustworthiness, leaving practitioners without guidance on which compression approach to choose for a given deployment scenario.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a pre-trained LLM (one of 18 architectures spanning LLaMA, LLaMA2, LLaMA3, Vicuna, OPT, and ChatGLM at sizes from 1.3B to 70B) and applies one of seven compression algorithms at a fixed sparsity ratio (50% unstructured, 2:4 structured, or 50% structured) or bit width (INT8 or INT4, weight-only or weight-activation). It produces six overall metric scores (OM_perf, OM_gen, OM_train, OM_inf, OM_hard, OM_trust), each computed as a quadratic mean of per-ability or per-resource ratios between compressed and full-precision models, aggregated across 11 evaluation datasets and 3 deployment libraries.

## Method

LLMCBench introduces a six-track evaluation framework with a unified quadratic-mean overall metric (OM) that prevents outliers from skewing results. Track 1 splits performance into knowledge ability (MMLU, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge) and inference ability (HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, QNLI, MNLI, WikiText2); Track 2 tests generalization across four model families at multiple sizes; Track 3 measures compression training time and peak GPU memory; Track 4 quantifies inference-stage GPU memory, model file size, and #MACs; Track 5 benchmarks token generation speed on TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, and MLC-LLM; and Track 6 evaluates robustness (AdvGLUE) and truthfulness (TruthfulQA). The benchmark evaluates three sparsity methods (LLM-Pruner, Wanda, SparseGPT) and four quantization methods (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, AWQ, OmniQuant) under matched configurations, with all hyperparameters taken from the original open-source implementations. The key design insight is that practical deployment requires jointly considering performance retention, resource consumption during both compression and inference, hardware/library-specific acceleration, and model trustworthiness—none of which prior work has systematically addressed together.

## Results

On the primary compression performance track (Track 1), quantization methods substantially outperform sparsity methods: OmniQuant achieves the highest overall metric (OM_perf = 99.42), followed by AWQ (99.39) and GPTQ (98.58), while the best sparsity method SparseGPT at 50% unstructured sparsity reaches only 88.25 and LLM-Pruner at 50% structured sparsity drops to 68.61. On the generalization track (Track 2), AWQ achieves the best OM_gen of 96.13, GPTQ follows at 93.80, while SmoothQuant collapses to 0.82 due to poor cross-model transfer. For inference consumption (Track 4), quantization methods achieve OM_inf of 220–246 versus 134–162 for sparsity, with OmniQuant leading at 246.34 and LLM-Pruner being the best sparsity option at 161.86.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven compression methods benchmarked, quantization methods (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, AWQ, OmniQuant) consistently outperform sparsity methods (LLM-Pruner, Wanda, SparseGPT) on overall compression performance, with OM_perf scores of 97–99.4 versus 68.6–88.3. AWQ achieves the best generalization across 18 model architectures (OM_gen = 96.13), narrowly beating GPTQ (93.80), while SmoothQuant catastrophically fails to generalize (OM_gen = 0.82) due to model-specific activation outliers. SparseGPT is the strongest sparsity method on performance (88.25 at 50% unstructured), outperforming Wanda (86.79) and LLM-Pruner (68.61), but all sparsity methods suffer large perplexity degradation (6.5–20.7 vs. 5.1–5.5 for dense). On inference consumption, OmniQuant leads (OM_inf = 246.34) with GPTQ (245.91) and AWQ (245.11) close behind, while LLM-Pruner is the best sparsity option (161.86) thanks to its structured nature enabling actual memory reduction. On trustworthiness, weight-activation quantization (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) edges out weight-only quantization (GPTQ, AWQ), and surprisingly LLM-Pruner achieves the best trustworthiness among sparsity methods (94.09) despite the worst compression performance, indicating that performance and trustworthiness are not aligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new compression algorithm but instead carves new ground by constructing the first unified benchmark (LLMCBench) for systematically comparing existing LLM compression methods. Prior compression works (SmoothQuant, GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, Wanda, SparseGPT, LLM-Pruner) each evaluate on different model families, datasets, and metrics, making cross-method comparison impossible. LLMCBench repurposes these seven widely-used, open-sourced methods as evaluation subjects under a standardized 6-track protocol spanning performance, generalization, training/inference consumption, hardware acceleration, and trustworthiness. The benchmark design draws on practical deployment requirements rather than purely academic metrics, distinguishing it from prior single-dataset or single-model evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides practitioners with the first actionable guidance on which compression method to choose for a specific deployment scenario (e.g., quantization for knowledge preservation, sparsity for inference ability, weight-only quantization for cross-model generalization). The trustworthiness track is a notable addition, revealing that compression performance and model safety are not aligned. Key limitations include coverage of only 7 methods (no distillation or KV-cache compression), only 2 models in the main performance track, and the finding that ChatGLM2 resists all evaluated methods, suggesting model-specific compression design remains an open problem. Future work should extend to more architectures, lower-bit regimes, and emerging compression paradigms.
