## Problem

Existing methods for simple bilevel optimization (minimizing an upper-level objective over the optimal solution set of a convex lower-level problem) either guarantee only asymptotic convergence, achieve slow sublinear rates, or rely on strong assumptions such as smoothness, compactness, or strong convexity of both objectives. This paper addresses the gap by developing a penalization framework that provides non-asymptotic complexity guarantees under the more general Hölderian error bound condition (exponent α ≥ 1), which encompasses both weak sharp minima (α=1) and quadratic growth (α=2) as special cases.

## Input / Output

The method takes composite convex objectives F = f₁ + f₂ and G = g₁ + g₂ (where f₁, g₁ are smooth with known Lipschitz constants L_{f₁}, L_{g₁}, and f₂, g₂ are prox-friendly nonsmooth terms), an initial point x₀, and a target accuracy ε. It produces an (ε_F, ε_G)-optimal solution x̃* satisfying F(x̃*) − F* ≤ ε_F and G(x̃*) − G* ≤ ε_G, where the lower-level accuracy is parameterized as ε_G = l_F^{−β} ε^β for any β > 0. The penalized parameter γ is not a free hyperparameter but is explicitly determined by the Hölderian error bound constant ρ, the subdifferential bound l_F, the exponent α, and the target accuracy ε.

## Method

The core contribution is a penalization framework that reformulates the SBO problem as the unconstrained problem min F(x) + γ(G(x) − G*), then establishes explicit, tight relationships between ε-optimal solutions of this penalized problem and (ε_F, ε_G)-optimal solutions of the original bilevel problem. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2.5) shows that choosing γ = γ* + 2l_F^β ε^{1−β} (for α > 1) or γ = γ* + l_F^β ε^{1−β} (for α = 1, where γ* = ρl_F^α(α−1)^{α−1}α^{−α}ε^{1−α}) guarantees the desired accuracy transfer. To solve the penalized problem, the paper applies accelerated proximal gradient (APG) methods, yielding O(√(1/ε^{max{α,β}})) iterations for composite convex objectives, which improves to O(√(1/ε^{max{α−1,β−1}}) log(1/ε)) when the smooth upper-level component is strongly convex. Adaptive variants (aPB-APG, aPB-APG-sc) dynamically update γ and the inner accuracy with warm-starting, and a subgradient method handles the fully nonsmooth case with O(1/ε^{max{2α,2β}}) complexity.

## Results

The paper establishes that under an α-Hölderian error bound, the proposed PB-APG algorithm attains an (ε, ε^β)-optimal solution of the simple bilevel problem in O(√(L_{f1}/ε) + √(l_F^{max{α,β}} L_{g1}/ε^{max{α,β}})) iterations for any β > 0. When the smooth part of the upper-level objective is strongly convex (parameter μ), PB-APG-sc improves this to O(√(L_{f1}/μ) log(1/ε) + √(l_F^{max{α,β}} L_{g1}/ε^{max{α-1,β-1}}) log(1/ε)), achieving linear convergence when α = 1 and β ≤ 1. For the nonsmooth case, the subgradient method yields O(l_{f2}²/ε² + l_{f2}^{max{2α,2β}} l_{g2}²/ε^{max{2α,2β}}) iterations. Numerical experiments on logistic regression and least squares regression problems confirm that all four proposed variants (PB-APG, aPB-APG, PB-APG-sc, aPB-APG-sc) converge faster than all seven compared baselines on both upper- and lower-level objective residuals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the logistic regression problem (LRP), PB-APG and PB-APG-sc exhibit significantly faster convergence than MNG, BiG-SAM, DBGD, a-IRG, CG-BiO, and Bi-SG for both lower- and upper-level objective residuals, with R-APM [Samadi et al., 2023] being the closest competitor but still slower in the early iterations. On the least squares regression problem (LSRP), the proposed methods again outperform a-IRG, BiG-SAM, and Bi-SG in convergence speed for both objectives. The adaptive variants (aPB-APG, aPB-APG-sc) further improve over their non-adaptive counterparts by producing staircase-shaped convergence curves that prevent undesirable fluctuations. The paper notes that the proposed methods achieve higher accuracy on the lower-level problem, which results in slightly larger upper-level objective values compared to baselines with looser lower-level accuracy, but the solutions are much closer to the true optimal value. In terms of theoretical complexity, the proposed O(√(1/ε)) rate for the convex case matches the lower bound for unconstrained composite optimization, while the nonsmooth O(1/ε²) rate improves upon a-IRG's O(1/ε⁴) (at b=0.25) and the online framework's O(1/ε³) when α < 2 and β ≤ α.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the penalty-based bilevel gradient descent framework of Shen and Chen (ICML 2023), generalizing their analysis from a quadratic growth condition (α=2) to the broader Hölderian error bound with exponent α≥1, and extending from smooth to composite convex objectives. It departs from the dominant Tikhonov regularization lineage (Amini & Yousefian 2019, Kaushik & Yousefian 2021, Samadi et al. 2023) by showing that a single penalized problem (P_γ) suffices rather than requiring an iterative regularization schedule with the 'slow condition.' For the strong convexity case, the algorithm is adapted from Lin and Xiao (ICML 2014), modified with a constant step-size to fit the bilevel setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic complexity guarantee connecting approximate solutions of the original bilevel problem to those of a single penalized reformulation, offering a modular framework where any solver for (P_γ) can be plugged in. The complexity results match or improve upon existing methods (e.g., O(√(1/ε)) vs. O(1/ε^{1/(0.5-b)}) for a-IRG in the nonsmooth case), and the exact penalization result for α=1 (weak sharp minima) is new relative to prior work. Open questions include the relationship between local (not just global) optimal solutions of (P) and (P_γ), which the authors acknowledge is 'more intricate' and deferred to future work, as well as extending the framework to fully non-convex bilevel problems.
