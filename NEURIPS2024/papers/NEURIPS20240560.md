## Problem

State-of-the-art differentially private model training via the matrix factorization mechanism requires solving a semi-definite program to find an approximately optimal factorization of the workload matrix, a step that becomes computationally intractable for realistic problem sizes (n > 5000) and introduces numerical fragility. No general-purpose factorization existed that could match the approximation quality of this optimization-based approach while remaining efficiently computable for large-scale training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a lower triangular workload matrix A derived from SGD (with optional momentum β and weight decay α), along with privacy parameters (ε, δ) and participation constraints (b-min-separated, at most k participations). For SGD-derived workloads, A is a lower triangular Toeplitz matrix fully specified by its first column, so the BSR factor C^|p| is likewise determined by p scalar coefficients computable via FFT-based convolution in O(p log p) time independent of n. The output is the banded factor C^|p| (and implicitly B^|p| = A(C^|p|)^{-1}), from which the noise scale s = sens_{k,b}(C^|p|) is obtained in closed form, enabling standard SGD training with per-step Gaussian noise.

## Method

The Banded Square Root (BSR) factorization exploits the fact that any lower triangular matrix with positive diagonal has a unique lower triangular square root C with positive diagonal, and that for SGD with momentum and weight decay this square root is itself a lower triangular Toeplitz matrix with explicit closed-form entries c_j = Σ α^{j-i} r_{j-i} r_i β^i where r_i = |binom(-1,i)²|. The p-BSR is obtained by zeroing all entries of C below the p-th sub-diagonal and setting B = A(C^|p|)^{-1}, which for the SGD case reduces to computing p Toeplitz coefficients via fast convolution. A key enabling result is a closed-form sensitivity formula for decreasing non-negative Toeplitz matrices under b-min-separated participation, which applies directly to the BSR factor. Theoretical analysis proves that BSR achieves asymptotically optimal approximation error for single participation and matches or improves upon the AOF for repeated participation, while its computational cost is negligible compared to the hours-to-days SDP solve required by the previous state of the art.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with (ε,δ)=(4,10⁻⁵) and repeated participation (batch size 500), BSR achieves 53.1% test accuracy at 20 epochs, matching or slightly exceeding AOF (47.0%) and substantially outperforming the DP-SGD baseline (35.0%) and the iterate-noise baseline (17.7%). In the single-participation setting, BSR reaches 36.3% at 500 updates vs. 37.9% for AOF, 27.7% for DP-SGD, and 17.2% for iterate-noise. Computationally, BSR takes <1 second for n up to 2000 and ~2.5 seconds for n=10,000, whereas AOF requires hours to days for n≥500 and times out for n≥700 at standard tolerance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSR matches AOF (the approximately optimal factorization from Choquette-Choo et al., 2023) in expected approximation error to within a few percent across all tested hyperparameter settings, and in CIFAR-10 accuracy the two methods are statistically indistinguishable for most epoch counts; at 15–20 epochs BSR actually exceeds AOF by 2–6 percentage points, which the authors attribute to numerical imprecision in solving the AOF SDP. Both BSR and AOF clearly dominate the two baseline factorizations: DP-SGD (noise on gradients, A=A·Id) and iterate-noise (noise on each iterate, A=Id·A) lag by 10–20+ percentage points in accuracy and by a factor of 3–10× in expected approximation error. The key practical advantage of BSR over AOF is computational: BSR is computed in closed form in under 1 second (O(p log p) for the Toeplitz coefficients), while AOF requires solving a semi-definite program that takes minutes for n≤100, hours for n≤500, and times out beyond n≈700, making BSR the only scalable option for realistic training lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the matrix mechanism for differential privacy (Li et al., 2015), the proof that square root factorization is asymptotically optimal for linear workloads (Henzinger et al., 2024), and the b-min-separated participation framework with its SDP-based approximately optimal factorization (Choquette-Choo et al., 2023a). The key departure is that while AOF requires solving a semi-definite program that becomes intractable beyond n≈500, BSR exploits the Toeplitz structure of SGD workload matrices to derive closed-form expressions for the banded square root, reducing the factorization cost to O(p log p) independent of n. The paper also builds on the sensitivity analysis framework of Choquette-Choo et al. to prove approximation quality bounds in both single- and repeated-participation settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the SDP bottleneck, BSR makes differentially private SGD training practical at the scale of real-world workloads (tens of thousands of steps) where AOF simply cannot run, while maintaining provable privacy guarantees and matching AOF's empirical accuracy on CIFAR-10. The asymptotic optimality result for single participation (matching lower bounds) is a clean theoretical contribution, but the repeated-participation case without weight decay (α=1) remains unresolved: the upper and lower bounds do not match, and the authors conjecture the lower bound is not tight. Extending the closed-form guarantees to variable learning rates and proving BSR's equivalence to AOF theoretically are identified as open directions.
