## Problem

Neighborhood attention reduces self-attention from O(n²) to O(n) by restricting each token to its nearest neighbors, but existing implementations rely on naive CUDA kernels that are heavily memory-bandwidth-bound, cannot utilize Tensor Cores, and fall short of fused attention performance. In higher-rank spaces (2-D, 3-D), the non-contiguous access patterns make standard GEMM infeasible without explicit tensor copies that undo the theoretical efficiency gain. This paper closes the infrastructure gap by providing two new kernel classes that make neighborhood attention competitive with—and in many cases superior to—fused self-attention.

## Input / Output

The kernels consume Q, K, V tensors organized in multi-dimensional spatial layouts (1-D sequence, 2-D height×width, or 3-D height×width×time), parameterized by per-axis window sizes, dilation factors, and optional causal masking flags. The output is the standard attention output tensor plus all gradients required for backpropagation (∇Q, ∇K, ∇V, ∇P). A critical constraint is that spatial tiling must respect the original multi-dimensional layout rather than flattening to a 1-D sequence, which is what makes the implementation non-trivial and necessitates custom gather/scatter or predication logic.

## Method

The paper identifies three core operators—Pointwise-Neighborhood (PN), Neighborhood-Neighborhood (NN), and Inverse-Neighborhood (IN)—that constitute the full forward and backward pass, and shows each can be reformulated from an inherent GEMV problem into a batched GEMM (or GETT for multi-dimensional cases) with space-aware tiling and fused gather/scatter. The GEMM-based implementation extends CUTLASS implicit GEMM (convolution) kernels, tiling Q and K/V according to their spatial layout and using software predication to handle boundary and dilation conditions. The fused implementation builds on xFormers FMHA, applying the same space-aware tiling and neighborhood masking within the threadblock-level fused attention framework, which eliminates the scatter/gather bottleneck by keeping attention weights entirely in on-chip memory. Dilation is handled by partitioning into non-overlapping sub-problems computed in the same kernel call, and per-axis causal masking enables applications like 3-D spatio-temporal attention. The fused kernels achieve up to 1759% speedup over naive kernels in 1-D and 958% in 2-D for FP16 forward pass, while reducing memory footprint from O(n²) to O(n).

## Results

On an NVIDIA A100, the fused neighborhood attention kernels improve over the naive NATTEN baseline by an average of 1759% (1-D) and 958% (2-D) in FP16 forward pass, and 1135% (3-D). The GEMM-based kernels achieve 548% (1-D) and 193% (2-D) average improvement in FP16. At the model level, fused kernels deliver up to 104% inference speedup (DiNAT-L at 384×384, 153→312 imgs/sec) and up to 39% training time reduction (DiNATs-B, 45.4→32.6 hours for 300 epochs) on ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the naive NATTEN CUDA kernels [9], the fused kernels dominate: they match or outperform the naive baseline in 100% of 1-D, 2-D, and 3-D FP16 problems, and in 96–100% of FP32 problems. The GEMM-based kernels also beat naive in ~99% of 1-D and ~84% of 2-D FP16 problems, though they occasionally lose to NATTEN's specialized tiled kernels in 2-D. Against the xFormers FMHA self-attention baseline [13], the fused neighborhood attention kernels match or outperform it in 100% of 1-D, 98.6% of 2-D, and 97.3% of 3-D FP16 problems, confirming that the added spatial predication overhead is negligible except in near-self-attention regimes (window size ≈ input size). Against FlashAttention-2 [5], fused NA matches or outperforms in 98.2% of 1-D, 92.4% of 2-D, and 87.0% of 3-D FP16 problems, with the gap widening in higher dimensions due to the GETT (General Tensor-Tensor contraction) predication cost. In model-level ImageNet-1K inference, fused kernels consistently improve throughput (26–104% for NAT/DiNAT variants), while GEMM-based kernels actually degrade FP16 throughput by 3–19% due to the LDGSTS alignment bottleneck in scatter/gather operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neighborhood attention paradigm introduced by NAT [9] and DiNAT [8] from naive CUDA kernels to two new kernel classes: GEMM-based batched matrix multiplication with scatter/gather fusion, and fused (Flash Attention-style) kernels adapted from xFormers FMHA [13]. The key conceptual contribution is showing that neighborhood attention, inherently a GEMV problem, can be reformulated as a space-aware batched GEMM (or GETT in multi-dim), enabling Tensor Core utilization. The fused variant directly builds on the online softmax technique from Flash Attention [6] and the CUTLASS [21] framework, removing the assumption that the token/space mode is rank-1 to support 2-D and 3-D spatial layouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first performant, extensible infrastructure for multi-dimensional neighborhood attention, closing a critical gap that previously limited the technique to small-scale or FP32-only use. The fused kernels reduce memory footprint from O(n²) to O(n) and achieve up to 17× speedups over naive kernels, making neighborhood attention practically competitive with self attention for vision and video models. Key limitations include the scatter/gather bottleneck in unfused low-precision kernels (an unavoidable hardware constraint on Ampere) and additional predication overhead in multi-dimensional fused kernels that can make them slower than self attention near full-window sizes. Open directions include Hopper TMA-based hardware predication, context parallelism, and better auto-tuning to close the remaining gap.
