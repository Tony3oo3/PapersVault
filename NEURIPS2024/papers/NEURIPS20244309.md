## Problem

Existing rehearsal learning for avoiding undesired futures (AUF) assumes stationary influence relations and ignores the cost of decision actions, making it inapplicable when quantitative influence parameters drift over time or when different interventions carry different expenses. Moreover, the prior sampling-based approach for finding multi-variable alterations is intractable in polynomial time. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating AUF with minimal action cost under non-stationarity and providing a polynomial-time solution.

## Input / Output

At each decision round t, the method receives a single observed sample (x_t, z_t^{ξ_t}, y_t), a fixed rehearsal graph G, user-specified cost coefficients w_i and datum points Z_i^0, a convex-polytope desired region S = {y : My ≤ d}, and a probability threshold τ. It outputs a set of variable-value alterations ξ_t (constrained to feasible ranges like [-3,3] or [-1,1]) that minimize quadratic alteration cost while guaranteeing P(Y_t ∈ S | θ̂_t, x_t, Rh(ξ_t)) ≥ τ, along with updated influence parameter estimates β̂_{j,t}. Only one sample per round is available, and the rehearsal graph structure is assumed known and fixed while its quantitative parameters evolve.

## Method

AUF-MICNS combines two components: (1) dynamic influence maintenance via an online-ensemble wrapper around OGD, where N_j base estimators with different step sizes η are maintained and reweighted by exponential loss after each round, yielding a regret bound of O(√(T ln N_j)) relative to the best fixed step size and an exponentially converging parameter error; (2) efficient alteration suggestion that replaces the intractable probabilistic constraint with a deterministic surrogate by exploiting the linear-Gaussian structure—specifically, the probability region P satisfying P(Y_t ∈ P) = τ is an ellipsoid defined via the χ² quantile, and requiring P ⊆ S reduces to a linear constraint on z_t^{ξ_t} involving the row-wise 2-norm of MP. The resulting optimization is a convex QCQP solvable in O(|V|³) time via interior-point methods, in contrast to the super-exponential sampling cost of the prior approach.

## Results

On both Market-Managing and Bermuda datasets (100 decision rounds, τ=0.7), AUF-MICNS achieves the highest success frequency (Y_t ∈ S) and the lowest relative alteration cost among all compared methods. In terms of computational efficiency, AUF-MICNS runs in 2.81 s (Market) and 1.71 s (Bermuda), compared to 63.14 s and 386.44 s for the previous rehearsal-learning method QWZ23, representing a ~22× and ~226× speedup respectively. The online-ensemble-based parameter estimator matches the performance of the best single OGD step size (η=0.025 on Market, η=2.5 on Bermuda) without requiring knowledge of the optimal step size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior rehearsal-learning method QWZ23 [40], AUF-MICNS wins on all three practical metrics: higher success frequency, lower relative alteration cost, and dramatically lower running time (2.81 s vs. 63.14 s on Market; 1.71 s vs. 386.44 s on Bermuda), thanks to the convex QCQP formulation replacing the intractable sampling-based approach. Against RL baselines (DDPG, PPO, SAC), AUF-MICNS achieves superior success frequency and lower cost at only 100 interaction rounds; DDPG, for example, requires T=4000 rounds to reach a 0.6955 average success frequency on Market data, whereas AUF-MICNS meets the τ=0.7 target within 100 rounds. PPO and SAC are faster in wall-clock time (0.05–0.06 s) but produce inferior decision quality in the limited-interaction regime. The online-ensemble estimator (Algorithm 2) matches the best single-step-size OGD variant on both datasets, confirming that the ensemble mitigates the risk of a poorly chosen learning rate without sacrificing convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rehearsal learning framework of Qin et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which introduced the Structural Rehearsal Model (SRM) for the Avoiding Undesired Future (AUF) problem in stationary environments. The key departures are threefold: (1) handling non-stationary influence relations via an online-ensemble-based sequential estimator (building on Zhao et al.'s online convex optimization framework and Cutler et al.'s drift-adapted OGD convergence theory), (2) incorporating a cost-sensitive objective with increasing marginal cost, and (3) replacing the intractable sampling-based decision procedure with a convex QCQP reformulation solvable in polynomial time. The paper positions influence relations as a practical middle ground between correlation (too weak for decisions) and causation (too demanding to identify), following Zhou's conceptual framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time rehearsal-based approach for the AUF problem, which is practically significant for decision-making scenarios with limited interaction opportunities (e.g., monthly business decisions) where RL methods are infeasible. The QCQP reformulation under linear Gaussian assumptions provides a clean, interpretable, and efficient decision procedure with theoretical guarantees on both estimation error (exponentially decreasing) and success probability. However, the approach is limited to linear structural equations, fixed known graph structures, and Gaussian noise; extending to non-linear dynamics, unknown or evolving graph structures, and reducing sensitivity to the hyperparameter τ remain open challenges that the authors acknowledge.
