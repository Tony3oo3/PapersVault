## Problem

Existing open-source pre-training corpora lack a math-centric resource that spans the full spectrum from K-12 to postgraduate-level mathematics across diverse source types. While some math-specific corpora exist (ProofPile for formal proving, OpenWebMath for web pages only), none combine textbooks, arXiv papers, community Q&A, encyclopedic entries, and web content with rigorous quality control and data contamination detection. This gap limits the ability of the open-source community to improve language models' mathematical reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests heterogeneous raw sources—arXiv LaTeX source files, math textbook PDFs (parsed via Mathpix API), Wikipedia HTML dumps, StackExchange site dumps, ProofWiki wiki dumps, and Common Crawl web pages from SlimPajama—and produces a 9.5-billion-token, 903K-document corpus with per-document quality annotations (language ID scores, symbol-to-word ratios). A notable design choice is the use of heuristic TF-IDF-based rule matching (11 LaTeX commands plus '$$' symbol) rather than neural classifiers to identify mathematical documents from Common Crawl, and source-specific fastText language identification thresholds (0.1 for Wikipedia/StackExchange, 0.3 for arXiv, 0.5 for Common Crawl) to handle the domain gap between general text and math-heavy content.

## Method

MATHPILE's construction follows a two-stage pipeline: source-specific preprocessing (PDF-to-markdown conversion, LaTeX macro reverting, HTML-to-text parsing, question-answer pairing with score thresholds) followed by global processing (language identification, 11 domain-specific heuristic cleaning rules, and MinHash LSH deduplication with 9,000 minhashes per document). The cleaning rules are tailored for mathematical text—filtering based on non-alphabetic word ratios, ellipsis-to-word ratios, and stop-word presence—rather than applying generic web-data filters that would discard math-heavy documents. Data contamination detection uses line-level MD5 exact matching against six benchmark test sets (GSM8K, MATH, MMLU-STEM, AGIEval-SAT-MATH, MathQA, AQuA), removing all identified duplicates. The corpus is validated through continual pre-training of Mistral-7B-v0.1 (3 epochs, 4096-token window, cosine LR schedule at 1e-5), showing up to 5% average improvement across math benchmarks, with ablations confirming that the processing pipeline and correct LaTeX rendering are critical for performance gains.

## Results

Continual pre-training Mistral-7B-v0.1 on MATHPILE subsets yields consistent gains across six math benchmarks. The Textbooks subset (only 0.56B tokens) delivers the strongest overall improvement, boosting SAT-MATH from 47.27 to 56.36 (+9.09), MathQA from 23.51 to 30.38 (+6.87), and AQuA from 27.95 to 33.07 (+5.12). The Wikipedia subset (0.18B tokens) achieves the best GSM8K score (49.96) and AQuA (35.43). In contrast, pre-training on synthetic corpora AMPS (1B) and DM-Mathematics (5B) catastrophically degrades the model to near-zero scores on all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATHPILE's Textbooks subset (0.56B tokens) decisively outperforms the sampled OpenWebMath subset (0.59B tokens) on five of six benchmarks: GSM8K (48.97 vs. 43.21), MATH (12.10 vs. 7.86), SAT-MATH (56.36 vs. 47.72), MathQA (30.38 vs. 21.80), and AQuA (33.07 vs. 24.80), while also edging ahead on MMLU-Math (48.93 vs. 47.52). The synthetic corpora AMPS (1B tokens) and DM-Mathematics (5B tokens) both catastrophically destroy model performance, dropping to near-zero on all benchmarks, which the authors attribute to their narrow, monotonous problem-set structure being unsuitable for standalone pre-training. Even the combined 4B-token MATHPILE mixture (Textbooks + Wikipedia + StackExchange + Common Crawl) underperforms the 0.56B Textbooks subset on SAT-MATH (43.18 vs. 56.36) and AQuA (25.19 vs. 33.07), reinforcing the paper's 'less is more' quality-over-quantity thesis. The arXiv subset (0.38B) is the only MATHPILE component that degrades MATH (7.50) and MathQA (18.05) relative to the base model, which the authors attribute to a domain gap between research-paper mathematics and competition-style benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATHPILE extends the general pre-training corpus construction pipeline (language identification, filtering, deduplication) established by RefinedWeb, SlimPajama, and RedPajama, but applies it with math-specific adaptations such as source-specific language thresholds and LaTeX-aware cleaning rules. It departs from prior math corpora—ProofPile (confined to formal proving), OpenWebMath (web-only), and AMPS (synthetic problem sets)—by integrating diverse source types (textbooks, arXiv papers, StackExchange, Wikipedia, ProofWiki, web pages) into a single open-source resource. The 'less is more' quality philosophy connects to the LIMA and Phi/Textbooks lines of work, while the data contamination detection step is a deliberate departure from many prior corpora that omit this critical audit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MATHPILE fills a concrete gap: no open-source, diverse, high-quality math-centric corpus with contamination detection previously existed, and the finding that OpenWebMath contains 195 MATH benchmark questions underscores how prevalent leakage is in existing resources. The demonstration that 0.56B tokens of curated textbooks outperform 0.38B tokens of arXiv papers, and that synthetic datasets (AMPS, DM-Mathematics) actually degrade pre-training performance, provides practical guidance for data curation. Key limitations include English-only coverage, a scale (9.5B tokens) potentially insufficient for extra-large models, and empirically chosen thresholds that may not be optimal; open questions include multilingual math corpora, data mixing strategies, and model-based corpus refinement.
