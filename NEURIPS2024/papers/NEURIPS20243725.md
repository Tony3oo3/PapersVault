## Problem

Visible-infrared person re-identification is fundamentally limited by the absence of color and other critical appearance cues in infrared images, yet existing compensation methods rely on labor-intensive manual annotations, pre-defined color vocabularies, and handcrafted loss functions that introduce additional parameters and sensitivity to data variation. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf large foundation models (VLMs and LLMs) can automatically generate dynamic textual descriptions to fill this information gap without any manual annotation or prior-knowledge-based module design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243725/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, each query is a triplet consisting of an infrared image (resized to 3×288×144), a VLM-generated text description of that infrared image (with color terms removed), and a randomly sampled VLM-generated text description from a visible image of the same identity. The gallery contains visible images at the same resolution. The method produces an ensemble query feature (the mean of the frozen IR visual feature, the aligned RGB text feature, and the fusion feature) which is dot-producted against each gallery visible feature to yield a similarity ranking list evaluated by Rank-k, mAP, and mINP.

## Method

TVI-LFM comprises three modules. First, Modality-Specific Caption (MSC) fine-tunes two BLIP captioners—one on a pedestrian image-text dataset for RGB, one on color-filtered IR pairs—to auto-generate per-image descriptions, then uses Vicuna-7B to rephrase each description with probability 0.5 for robustness. Second, the Incremental Fine-tuning Strategy (IFS) freezes a dual-stream ResNet-50 backbone (trained with Channel Augmentation) and incrementally fine-tunes CLIP's text encoder; its Semantic Filtered Fusion (SFF) module exploits CLIP's text-visual alignment to approximate the visual complementary feature (f_rgb^I − f_ir^I) with the textual complementary feature (f_rgb^T − f_ir^T), yielding a parameter-free fusion feature f_F = f_ir^I + (f_rgb^T − f_ir^T) that is semantically consistent with the visible modality. Modality Joint Learning then aligns all four feature types (visible, IR, text, fusion) via cross-entropy and weighted regularized triplet loss, with the frozen IR features acting as a regularizer. Third, Modality Ensemble Retrieval (MER) averages the three query features into a single ensemble vector, which is mathematically equivalent to a higher-dimensional concatenation-based retrieval but at lower computational cost, leveraging complementary strengths of texture, color attributes, and fused semantics.

## Results

On Tri-SYSU-MM01 (All Search), TVI-LFM achieves 84.90% Rank-1, 81.47% mAP, and 70.85% mINP, surpassing the previous best (PartMix at 77.78% R-1) by 7.12% in Rank-1. On Tri-RegDB, it reaches 91.38% R-1 and 85.92% mAP, and on Tri-LLCM, 58.19% R-1 and 65.08% mAP, both edging out the prior best (YYDS) by small margins. Under Indoor Search on Tri-SYSU-MM01, TVI-LFM further improves to 89.06% R-1 and 90.78% mAP, a 5.86% R-1 gain over SAAI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVI-LFM outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three datasets. On Tri-SYSU-MM01 (All Search), it leads PartMix [20] (77.78% R-1) by 7.12%, SAAI [10] (75.90%) by 9.00%, and the text-augmented YYDS [9] (74.60%) by 10.30% in Rank-1, while also achieving the highest mAP (81.47% vs. SAAI's 77.03%) and mINP (70.85% vs. YYDS's 56.01%). On Tri-RegDB, TVI-LFM (91.38% R-1, 85.92% mAP) narrowly surpasses YYDS (90.95%, 84.22%) by 0.43% and 1.70% respectively, and outperforms DEEN [65] (89.5%) by 1.88% R-1. On the more challenging Tri-LLCM, the margin over YYDS is minimal (58.19% vs. 58.13% R-1; 65.08% vs. 64.91% mAP), while the gap over I→R-only methods like CAJ [57] (48.8%) and DART [53] (52.2%) remains substantial at ~9–10% R-1. The consistent advantage over the I→R backbone (69.89% R-1) confirms that the text-enhanced fusion strategy contributes 15+ points of improvement on Tri-SYSU-MM01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VI-ReID line of work by introducing large foundation models (CLIP, BLIP, Vicuna) as the mechanism for compensating the information gap (e.g., color) in infrared modality, departing from prior methods that rely on manual annotations, predefined color vocabularies, or handcrafted loss functions. Conceptually, it builds on CLIP's text-visual alignment capability and the multi-modal vector arithmetic property from Kiros et al. (2014) to create a parameter-free fusion feature via simple vector subtraction in a unified embedding space. The key departure from prior text-augmented VI-ReID methods (e.g., YYDS) is the automatic, dynamic generation of descriptions via fine-tuned VLMs and LLM-based rephrasing, eliminating the need for fixed human-written labels and reducing sensitivity to text variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway for integrating off-the-shelf large foundation models into a downstream cross-modal retrieval task, and the three expanded datasets (Tri-SYSU-MM01, Tri-LLCM, Tri-RegDB) with per-image VLM-generated captions become useful resources for the community. The parameter-free Semantic Filtered Fusion mechanism is an elegant technique that could generalize to other modality-gap compensation problems. However, performance remains dependent on text quality, and gains on harder datasets (LLCM) are more modest, leaving open whether more advanced generative models or fine-grained attribute-focused generation strategies can close the remaining gap.
