## Problem

Traditional 3D indoor datasets prioritize scale over ground-truth accuracy, producing incomplete meshes with holes and missing structures that yield unreliable ground truth for dense geometry tasks like depth rendering. Existing approaches that ensure completeness (e.g., individual object scanning with robotic arms) are limited to constrained table-top scenarios, while scene-level scanning suffers from line-of-sight occlusions. The paper addresses this by proposing a framework that achieves fully dense, artifact-free ground truth for indoor scenes while supporting free hand-held camera trajectories.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes individually scanned water-tight meshes (via EinScan-SP for small objects, Artec Leo for furniture/rooms), a partial scene scan for registration, and a synchronized multi-modal video sequence (RGB+Polarization from Lucid Phoenix, Active Stereo from Intel RealSense D435, and ToF from Lucid Helios) with hardware-synced timestamps. Outputs include registered 3D meshes as digital twin assets, rendered dense depth maps, instance/class segmentation masks, camera poses, surface normals, and 6D object poses — all derived from mesh-to-camera-frame transformation rather than sensor readings. The dataset spans 11 scenes with ~7k frames (plus 9k reduced-scene frames for object removal), and ground truth is rendered at the native resolution of the camera rig.

## Method

SCRREAM is a four-stage annotation pipeline: (1) SCan — individually scan every object, piece of furniture, and the room to guarantee water-tight, high-resolution meshes independent of scene-level occlusions; (2) Register — partially scan the assembled scene and align pre-scanned meshes via 3–5 manual correspondences refined with ICP in Artec Studio; (3) REnder — import registered meshes into Blender 4.0, place light sources matching the real scene, and render 50–100 synthetic views with known camera poses and intrinsics; (4) Map — modify COLMAP into a two-stage procedure where synthetic image features are first de-projected into 3D using known poses, then real image features are matched to these synthetic features to optimize real camera poses via feature-based pose estimation. This replaces external hardware tracking (robotic arms, motion capture) with a purely software-based registration, enabling unrestricted hand-held camera trajectories while still producing pixel-accurate dense ground truth by transforming the registered meshes into each camera frame.

## Results

On the SCRREAM NVS benchmark, depth-supervised NeRFacto (Depth-Facto) achieves the best geometric accuracy with RMSE 0.218 (AS) and 0.336 (ToF), and δ<1.25 of 0.968/0.926, far outperforming unsupervised NeRFacto (RMSE 1.244, δ<1.25 0.429). Among unsupervised methods, Zip-NeRF leads in photometric quality (PSNR 28.315, LPIPS 0.259) but lags in depth (RMSE 0.493, δ<1.25 0.546). For SLAM, CO-SLAM achieves the best GT-depth tracking (ATE 4.18 cm) and mapping (Completion Ratio 96.52%), while sensor-depth inputs degrade ATE to 39–84 cm depending on method and modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates six NVS methods and three SLAM methods on SCRREAM's 11 scenes. In NVS, depth-supervised Depth-Facto dominates geometric metrics (RMSE 0.218, δ<1.25 0.968 with AS depth), outperforming unsupervised Zip-NeRF by ~2.3× in RMSE and ~1.8× in δ<1.25, though Zip-NeRF still leads in photometric PSNR (28.3 vs. 24.5). Among Gaussian Splatting variants, 3D-GS and Mip-Splatting are nearly identical (PSNR 25.94 vs. 25.93; RMSE 0.526 vs. 0.532), both trailing Depth-Facto in depth but beating plain NeRFacto. In SLAM, CO-SLAM achieves the best GT-depth ATE (4.18 cm) and Completion Ratio (96.52%), while Gaussian-SLAM suffers the largest ToF-depth degradation (ATE 83.53 cm vs. 5.00 cm with GT), indicating sensitivity to noisy depth inputs. NICE-SLAM shows the most consistent sensor-depth performance (ATE 39.09 AS / 24.02 ToF) but trails CO-SLAM in GT-depth mapping quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCRREAM synthesizes two previously separate annotation paradigms: the individual object scanning approach from HAMMER (which ensures water-tight, complete meshes but is limited to robotic-arm-constrained table-top scenes) and the feature-matching-based camera pose estimation from ScanNet++ (which enables free camera trajectories but relies on scene-level reconstruction with inherent line-of-sight gaps). By replacing both the robotic arm and external tracking hardware with a two-stage COLMAP-based mapping against rendered synthetic views, the paper achieves what neither prior approach could: fully dense object-level meshes combined with unconstrained hand-held camera trajectories. The pipeline also draws on HouseCat6D's multi-modal camera rig calibration and ICP-based registration, but the key conceptual departure is eliminating all external tracking hardware in favor of purely visual feature matching against a digital twin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is providing the first indoor dataset where dense geometry ground truth (depth, normals, segmentation) is truly complete at the object level, enabling objective evaluation of NVS and SLAM methods on geometric fidelity rather than relying on qualitative depth inspection or sensor depth as proxy. The benchmarks reveal that current SoTA methods (NeRF, 3DGS, neural SLAM) achieve photometrically plausible results but still exhibit significant geometric errors, and that sensor depth quality is the dominant bottleneck for SLAM accuracy (ATE drops from ~39 cm to ~5 cm with GT depth). However, the pipeline's scalability is severely limited by expensive multi-sensor hardware, time-consuming per-object scanning, and the requirement for static scenes, leaving open whether this annotation quality can be achieved at the scale (thousands of scenes) needed for training rather than just benchmarking.
