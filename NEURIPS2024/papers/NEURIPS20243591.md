## Problem

Sample efficiency in continuous deep RL has been driven almost exclusively by algorithmic improvements applied to small network architectures, while evidence suggests that naive capacity scaling degrades performance. The paper asks whether combining parameter scaling and replay scaling with existing algorithmic enhancements can yield substantial gains in continuous control, and identifies the specific regularization and architectural conditions under which critic scaling becomes effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243591/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BRO consumes proprioceptive state vectors whose dimensionality ranges from 3 (Pendulum) to 223 (Dog locomotion) across the 40 evaluated tasks, and outputs continuous action vectors of 1–39 dimensions. The actor is a standard 256-unit MLP (not scaled), while the critic is the scaled BroNet component. In secondary experiments, BroNet is also applied to image-based Atari 100k tasks where a standard convolutional encoder feeds the BroNet Q-network, and to offline RL settings (AntMaze, Adroit) where the input is a fixed dataset of state-action-reward tuples.

## Method

BRO is a model-free actor-critic algorithm built on SAC that scales the critic to ~5M parameters using a BroNet architecture: a dense+LayerNorm+ReLU input layer followed by N residual blocks, each containing two LayerNorm-regularized dense layers (analogous to transformer FFN sub-layers). The key insight is that strong regularization (Layer Normalization, weight decay, and full-parameter resets every 250k steps) is what unlocks effective critic scaling, whereas naively plugging a large BroNet into standard SAC yields weak performance. BRO removes Clipped Double Q-learning (using ensemble mean instead of min for Q-targets) and employs a dual-actor setup where an optimistic exploration policy is optimized toward a KL-regularized Q-value upper-bound derived from quantile-based epistemic uncertainty. A scaled replay ratio (RR=10 for BRO, RR=2 for BRO-Fast) and a reduced batch size of 128 complete the design. The paper demonstrates through 15,000+ experiments that critic parameter scaling outperforms replay-ratio scaling in both performance and wall-clock efficiency, because larger models parallelize on GPU while higher replay ratios introduce sequential gradient steps.

## Results

On 40 complex continuous control tasks across DeepMind Control, MetaWorld, and MyoSuite benchmarks, BRO achieves an interquartile mean (IQM) of 0.945 at 1M environment steps, outperforming the state-of-the-art model-based TD-MPC2 (0.842) and the best model-free baseline SR-SAC (0.595). On the notoriously challenging Dog & Humanoid locomotion tasks, BRO reaches 0.864 IQM versus TD-MPC2's 0.527, making it the first model-free algorithm to achieve near-optimal performance in these environments. BRO also achieves 1.000 (perfect) on MetaWorld and 0.980 on MyoSuite at 1M steps. In terms of sample efficiency, BRO requires approximately 2.5× fewer environment steps than TD-MPC2 to match its performance on the hardest DMC tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRO decisively outperforms all five baselines across all 40 tasks. Against the strongest model-based competitor TD-MPC2, BRO leads by 0.103 IQM on the aggregated benchmark (0.945 vs. 0.842) and by a margin of 0.337 on the hardest Dog & Humanoid subset (0.864 vs. 0.527), while also being ~25% faster in wall-clock time. Against the best model-free baseline SR-SAC (replay ratio 32), BRO achieves 0.945 vs. 0.595 aggregated, with the gap widening to 0.765 on Dog & Humanoid (0.864 vs. 0.099). Standard SAC and TD3 are far behind at 0.316 and 0.294 respectively, while CrossQ performs poorly on this task selection (0.042), likely due to its original evaluation on a different subset of DMC tasks. Notably, BRO (Fast) with a reduced replay ratio of 2 still matches TD-MPC2's sample efficiency while running at SAC-level wall-clock speed, achieving 0.878 aggregated IQM.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRO builds on the SAC actor-critic framework and integrates several established RL techniques—quantile Q-values (Dabney et al., 2018), dual-actor optimistic exploration (Nauman & Cygan, 2023a), full-parameter resets (Nikishin et al., 2022), and weight decay—into a unified algorithm. Its key departure is from the field's dominant scaling axis: while prior work (D'Oro et al., 2022; Schwarzer et al., 2023) focused on replay ratio or encoder scaling, BRO demonstrates that critic parameter scaling with the right architecture (BroNet: Layer Norm + residual blocks, analogous to LLM FFN sub-layers) is both more effective and more compute-efficient. The paper also challenges the near-universal use of Clipped Double Q-learning by showing that strong regularization makes pessimistic Q-value approximation unnecessary, aligning with recent findings from Nauman et al. (2024) but extending them to a full algorithmic recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BRO establishes a new practical standard for sample-efficient continuous control, being the first model-free method to reach near-optimal performance on the notoriously difficult Dog and Humanoid tasks while being 2.5× more sample-efficient than the leading model-based algorithm TD-MPC2. The BroNet architecture's demonstrated transferability to offline RL and image-based tasks suggests it could become a general-purpose backbone for RL value networks. Key open questions include whether the critic-scaling advantage persists in image-based RL (where encoder scaling competes with critic scaling), whether BRO's components transfer to discrete action spaces, and how to reduce the memory footprint for real-time deployment. The authors also argue that current benchmarks are saturated for modern algorithms and call for new standardized sample-efficiency benchmarks.
