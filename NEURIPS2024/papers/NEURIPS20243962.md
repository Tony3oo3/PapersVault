## Problem

Neural classifiers remain vulnerable to unseen adversarial attacks, and existing defenses each have critical limitations: certified methods offer only small guaranteed regions, adversarial training degrades sharply on attacks not seen during training, and purification methods cannot determine the optimal denoising level for unknown perturbation magnitudes. The paper asks whether a model can be made robust by learning to base predictions solely on essential label-causative features while discarding non-causative factors (style, background, perturbations), mimicking how humans make robust visual judgments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/GTSRB) that may be adversarially perturbed, and outputs a predicted class label. During inference, the input image first passes through an unconditional diffusion purification step (5 iterations over small timesteps 1–50), then through a conditional diffusion-based causal factor inference step (10 iterations sampling 10 timesteps each) to recover latent vectors S* and Z*, and finally a classifier maps S* to a label. The entire pipeline is differentiable end-to-end, so white-box attackers can backpropagate through purification, inference, and classification jointly.

## Method

CausalDiff builds a structural causal model in which a Y-causative latent S and a Y-non-causative latent Z jointly generate the image X via a conditional DDPM, while S alone generates the label Y. The UNet is modified so that S enters as an additive bias (shifting semantics) and Z enters as a multiplicative scale (controlling style), inspired by classifier-free guidance and DiffAE. Training optimizes a Causal Information Bottleneck (CIB) objective that decomposes into a diffusion reconstruction loss, a cross-entropy classification loss on S, a CLUB-based mutual-information penalty to disentangle S from Z, and a KL regularization toward a Gaussian prior; the model is pretrained on reconstruction alone before joint training. At inference, the pipeline first purifies the adversarial example using the unconditional branch of the same diffusion model (restricting to small timesteps to preserve information), then infers S* and Z* by maximizing the conditional likelihood p(x*|s,z) via gradient ascent on the latents, and finally classifies using only S*, thereby ignoring perturbations absorbed into Z.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CausalDiff achieves 90.23% clean accuracy and an average robust accuracy of 86.39% across three unseen attacks (AutoAttack ℓ∞, AutoAttack ℓ₂, and StAdv), outperforming the previous best method RDC (82.38% avg) by +4.01%. On CIFAR-100, CausalDiff reaches 56.25% robust accuracy (vs. 53.12% for RDC), and on GTSRB it achieves 82.62% average robust accuracy including fog corruption (vs. 77.69% for the best adversarial-training baseline AT-4). Notably, the gap between clean and average robust accuracy is only 3.84% on CIFAR-10, indicating strong resistance to unseen perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalDiff outperforms all 14 baselines on average robustness across the three unseen attacks on CIFAR-10. Against the strongest purification baseline RDC [27] (82.38% avg), CausalDiff gains +4.01% (86.39%), with the largest margin on AutoAttack ℓ∞ (+7.34%, 83.01% vs. 75.67%). Adversarial training methods (AT-ℓ∞/ℓ₂ with DDPM or EDM, CausalAdv, DICE) suffer severe degradation on attacks different from their training attack (e.g., AT-ℓ∞ drops to 2.93% on StAdv), yielding average robustness below 54%. Among purification methods, LM-EDM [27] is the closest competitor at 78.06% avg, but CausalDiff surpasses it by +8.33%, largely due to the causal disentanglement step. On CIFAR-100, CausalDiff (56.25%) beats RDC (53.12%) and AT-EDM (42.67%) despite a lower clean accuracy (65.62% vs. 80.47%), and on GTSRB it leads all baselines by +4.93% in average robustness (82.62% vs. 77.69% for AT-4), including natural fog corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalDiff sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based adversarial purification (DiffPure, RDC/LM), causal representation learning for robustness (CausalAdv, DICE), and conditional diffusion generation (classifier-free guidance, DiffAE). It departs from prior causal-robustness work by modeling the generation of native in-domain data rather than attack-specific causal structures, and from prior purification methods by operating in a disentangled latent space rather than pixel space. The Causal Information Bottleneck objective is a novel formulation that adds an explicit −I(S;Z) disentanglement term absent from the standard causal ELBO used in prior multi-domain causal representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that disentangling label-causative from label-non-causative features via a diffusion model provides a principled, attack-agnostic defense that outperforms both adversarial training and prior purification methods across multiple datasets and attack types. However, the inference cost (N₁+N₂+1 NFEs, ~5 seconds per sample) and the requirement to train from scratch limit immediate practical deployment. Open questions include whether the causal mechanism can be embedded into existing pretrained models in a plug-and-play fashion, whether stronger backbones (EDM, latent diffusion) yield further gains, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or more complex visual domains.
