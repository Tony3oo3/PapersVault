## Problem

In real-world control scenarios, interactions between a decision maker and executor are often intermittent due to unstable communication or costly sensing, creating a bidirectional blockage where the agent cannot observe states or transmit actions for multiple timesteps. Existing model-free RL algorithms assume continuous step-by-step interaction and fail under such sparsity, while model-based approaches suffer from error accumulation over multi-step rollouts. The paper addresses the gap of enabling model-free RL to make effective advance multi-step decisions without the action space explosion that comes from directly outputting concatenated action vectors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single state observation (a state vector in Mujoco/2D Maze environments, or a 512-dim feature vector extracted from a depth image in the robot arm task) and produces a latent action z (8-dim by default) plus a scalar action transition scale v, which together are decoded into a multi-step action sequence of length c (e.g., 10 steps for Mujoco, 16 steps for the robot arm). The policy only needs to produce this compact pair [z, v] rather than the full c×d-dimensional action vector, and the decoder reconstructs the individual per-step actions from the latent code conditioned on the current state and v.

## Method

MARS constructs a compact, decodable latent action space via a conditional VAE where the action transition scale v (a normalized measure of total action change across the sequence) serves as the conditioning variable, implicitly partitioning the latent space by motion style to simplify the policy's decision space. A second module adds a dynamic residual prediction head that learns to predict the state change δs = s_{t+c} − s_t from the latent code, enforcing that nearby latent points correspond to similar environmental effects (dynamic semantic smoothness). Training is two-stage: a reward-agnostic self-supervised warmup trains the VAE on random-policy or offline data, then the latent policy is optimized with standard actor-critic RL (TD3, PPO, DDPG, or TD3-BC) where the actor outputs [z, v] and the decoder produces the executable action sequence. The approach is fully plug-in, requiring no modification to the RL algorithm's core update rules beyond replacing the action input with the latent pair.

## Results

On the real-world 6-DoF robot arm grasping task (fixed interaction interval), MARS+TD3-BC achieves a 74.2% grasp success rate (GSR), 82.9% declutter rate (DR), and 70.8% motion smoothness ratio (MSR), substantially outperforming vanilla TD3-BC (34.1% GSR, 27.3% DR, 22.5% MSR). On random interaction interval Mujoco tasks (Hopper, Ant, Walker, HalfCheetah) and 2D maze navigation, MARS-TD3 outperforms Frameskip-TD3 and Multistep-TD3 across all six environments and approaches the performance of Perfect-TD3 (continuous interaction upper bound) in most tasks. In the MBRL comparison (Appendix C.1), MARS-TD3 achieves 3762.7 on Hopper (fixed) and 3105.7 on Ant (random), exceeding TD-MPC (1795.4 / 1447.2) and Dreamer-v2 (788.1 / 1064.7) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS+TD3-BC wins decisively over vanilla TD3-BC on the robot grasping task, improving GSR from 34.1% to 74.2% (+40.1 pp) and DR from 27.3% to 82.9% (+55.6 pp), while also boosting MSR from 22.5% to 70.8%. Against Multistep+TD3-BC (which directly outputs a concatenated c-step action vector), MARS gains +12.7 pp GSR and +13.5 pp DR, showing that the latent VAE representation avoids the action-space explosion that hampers direct multi-step output. The Dense obs+TD3-BC baseline (adding an extra camera every 3 s) achieves 59.1% GSR, still 15.1 pp below MARS, and incurs additional hardware cost. On random intermittent Mujoco tasks, MARS-TD3 surpasses both Frameskip-TD3 and Multistep-TD3 in all six environments (Hopper, Ant, Walker, HalfCheetah, Maze-medium, Maze-hard) and reaches near-Perfect-TD3 performance in most cases. In the model-based comparison, MARS-TD3 outperforms TD-MPC by ~1967 points on Hopper (fixed) and ~1659 points on Ant (random), and beats Dreamer-v2 by ~2975 and ~2041 points respectively, confirming that model-free multi-step latent representation avoids the error accumulation inherent in learned dynamics models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on conditional VAEs (Sohn et al., 2015) and prior latent action representation work (Li et al., 2021, HYAR) but extends them to the multi-step action sequence setting required by intermittent control. It departs from the natural instinct to use model-based RL (Dreamer, TD-MPC) for multi-step planning, instead showing that a model-free approach with a learned latent action space outperforms MBRL by avoiding dynamic model error accumulation. The Intermittent-MDP formulation unifies two previously treated separately—fixed interaction intervals (common in robotics) and random intervals (common in networked control)—under a single framework, positioning the contribution as a plugin for existing actor-critic algorithms (TD3, PPO, DDPG) rather than a new RL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world robotic and remote-control systems frequently experience communication interruptions or fixed sensing cycles, yet standard DRL algorithms degrade or fail under such conditions. MARS's plugin design means practitioners can retrofit existing RL pipelines without re-architecting them, and the real-world 6-DoF robot arm validation (74.2% grasp success vs. 34.1% for vanilla TD3-BC) demonstrates tangible impact. Key limitations remain: the VAE-based representation struggles with very long action sequences (beyond ~10 steps), and the method requires a warmup stage with random-policy data. Open questions include whether transformer-based or other sequence-aware VAEs can extend the effective horizon, and whether the action transition scale concept generalizes to discrete or hybrid action spaces.
