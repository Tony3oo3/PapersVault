## Problem

Existing token pruning techniques designed for Vision Transformers fail catastrophically when applied to SSM-based vision models (e.g., ViM, PlainMamba), causing over 68% zero-shot accuracy drops that cannot be recovered even after extensive fine-tuning. The root cause is that SSMs process tokens sequentially along traversal paths (cross-scan), making each token sensitive to its neighbors, whereas ViT attention computes pairwise correlations independent of position. This paper asks whether tokens can be pruned in SSM-based vision models without disrupting the original sequential token positions during the scan.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation) that are divided into 16×16 patches with stride 8, producing token sequences processed by bidirectional SSM blocks. Pruning is applied at specific intermediate layers (e.g., after layers 5, 10, 15, 20 for ViM-S), reducing the token count from N to K while the model still produces standard outputs: class labels for ImageNet-1K, bounding boxes with instance masks for COCO, and per-pixel semantic masks for ADE20K. A custom position map guides the modified SSM scan kernel to switch between remaining-token and pruned-token computation patterns.

## Method

The core contribution is a pruning-aware hidden state alignment that preserves the sequential neighborhood structure during the SSM scan. For remaining tokens, hidden states are computed using the original index gaps (e.g., h'_{q₁} = Ā^{q₁-q₀}B̄x_{q₀} + B̄x_{q₁}), so pruned tokens' positional gaps are implicitly maintained. For pruned tokens, their hidden states are propagated forward by one step (h' = Āh') without incorporating the pruned token's input, effectively keeping a 'ghost' state that stabilizes adjacent tokens' neighborhoods. Token importance is evaluated using the clipped SSM output values aggregated across all channels (S = Σ max(0, y_d)/D'), which outperforms ℓ₁/ℓ₂ norms and unclipped values. The method is architecture-agnostic, applied to ViM-T/S and PlainMamba-L1/L2/L3, and achieves 41.4% FLOPs reduction on PlainMamba-L3 with only 0.6% top-1 accuracy loss on ImageNet.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed token pruning method (ToP) achieves 81.7% top-1 accuracy on PlainMamba-L3 with a 41.4% FLOPs reduction (14.4G → 8.44G), losing only 0.6% over the unpruned model (82.3%). On ViM-S, ToP reaches 78.8% top-1 with 29.4% FLOPs reduction, versus 74.8% for the naive EViT baseline. On COCO 2017, pruned PlainMamba-L3 maintains 46.5 AP^b and 40.6 AP^m (vs. 46.8/41.2 unpruned), and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it achieves 48.6% mIoU (vs. 49.1% unpruned), outperforming LocalVim-S (46.4%) and VMamba-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against EViT [18], the standard ViT token pruning method applied naively to SSM models. The proposed ToP method consistently outperforms EViT by 2.4–4.0% top-1 accuracy across all five SSM backbones (e.g., +2.8% on PlainMamba-L3: 81.7% vs. 78.9%; +4.0% on ViM-S: 78.8% vs. 74.8%), while achieving comparable FLOPs reductions. Against the unpruned reference models (ViM [46], PlainMamba [37]), ToP incurs minimal accuracy loss: only 0.6% on PlainMamba-L3 (81.7% vs. 82.3%) and 1.7% on ViM-S (78.8% vs. 80.5%), whereas EViT drops 3.4% and 5.7% respectively. On COCO detection, ToP-PlainMamba-L3 (46.5 AP^b) stays within 0.3 of the unpruned model (46.8) and surpasses Swin-Small [22] (44.8 AP^b), while EViT-PlainMamba-L3 (44.2 AP^b) falls below Swin-Tiny (42.7). On ADE20K segmentation, ToP-PlainMamba-L3 (48.6% mIoU) exceeds LocalVim-S (46.4%) and VMamba-T, with only a 0.5% gap to the unpruned PlainMamba-L3 (49.1%). The ViT [6] and DeiT [31] entries in Table 1 serve as architectural context: the pruned PlainMamba-L3 (81.7% at 8.44G) approaches DeiT-Base (81.8% at 17.5G) at roughly half the FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established token pruning paradigm from Vision Transformers (EViT, DynamicViT, ToMe) to SSM-based vision models (ViM, PlainMamba), where it fails catastrophically due to the sequential cross-scan mechanism. The key departure is the recognition that SSM scan paths are position-sensitive in a way that ViT attention is not, motivating a pruning-aware hidden state alignment that preserves original token positions during the scan. The token importance metric is also adapted from attention-based selectors to a channel-aggregated clipped SSM output, reflecting the absence of explicit attention in Mamba architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to make token pruning viable for SSM-based vision models, achieving ~41% FLOPs reduction with <1% accuracy loss across classification, detection, and segmentation. The negative result—that ViT pruning permanently damages SSMs even after fine-tuning—provides important mechanistic insight into how cross-scan encodes spatial relationships. Open questions include whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other SSM variants (e.g., VMamba's cross-scan module), video SSMs, or architectures with non-uniform scan paths, and whether the efficiency gains compound with other compression techniques.
