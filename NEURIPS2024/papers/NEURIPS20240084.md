## Problem

In-context learning has been studied almost exclusively in causal language models (GPT-3 and successors), leading to the widespread assumption that this capability is unique to autoregressive architectures and that masked language models are 'deprecated' and limited to task-specific finetuning. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether an off-the-shelf masked language model can perform generative in-context learning without any architectural modifications or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generative tasks, the method takes a text prompt with in-context demonstrations and produces a completion via iterative unmasking with beam search (4 beams); the input is reformatted by appending [MASK] tokens, a [SEP] token, and two additional [MASK] padding tokens to the prompt. For ranking tasks (multiple-choice, entailment), the method takes a prompt plus candidate completions and produces a modified pseudo-log-likelihood score per candidate, where two extra tokens in the right context are masked to mitigate overestimation of multi-token sequences. DeBERTa's relative positional embeddings with logarithmic buckets enable processing of sequences well beyond its 512-token training length, supporting long few-shot prompts.

## Method

The core contribution is an inference-only technique that converts an existing MLM (DeBERTa, 0.1B–1.5B) into a generative in-context learner with zero additional training. For text generation, the model performs left-to-right autoregressive decoding by appending [MASK] tokens to the prompt, predicting the next token at the first mask position, then iteratively shifting and appending new masks; two extra [MASK] tokens before the [SEP] token are included to counteract the end-of-sequence bias learned during pretraining. For sequence ranking, the paper improves upon the standard pseudo-log-likelihood (PLL) of Salazar et al. (2020) by additionally masking two tokens in the right context, which reduces the overestimation of strongly locally-dependent multi-token sequences (e.g., multi-word proper nouns). The approach is validated by replicating the full GPT-3 evaluation suite (SuperGLUE, HellaSwag, WMT translation, closed-book QA) and showing that DeBERTa outperforms GPT-3 on language understanding and text completion while underperforming on translation and closed-book QA, revealing complementary strengths of the two training objectives.

## Results

On SuperGLUE (1-shot), 1.5B DeBERTa achieves an average accuracy of 68.4%, substantially outperforming the comparably-sized 1.3B GPT-3 (57.8%) and approaching the 175B GPT-3 (68.9%). On language modeling and Winograd-style tasks, DeBERTa scores 72.8% vs. GPT-3's 65.9% (1-shot). However, the advantage reverses on machine translation (DeBERTa 13.8 vs. GPT-3 18.8 SacreBLEU, 1-shot) and closed-book question answering (DeBERTa 33.3 vs. GPT-3 36.3 average, 1-shot), where the causal model's generative pretraining and larger multilingual corpus provide clear benefits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020), DeBERTa clearly wins on language understanding (SuperGLUE: 68.4 vs. 57.8, 1-shot average) and language modeling/Winograd tasks (72.8 vs. 65.9, 1-shot), with the gap widening in the few-shot regime (69.6 vs. 60.0 on SuperGLUE). The advantage reverses on machine translation, where GPT-3 achieves 18.8 SacreBLEU (1-shot) versus DeBERTa's 13.8, attributed to DeBERTa's smaller monolingual training corpus lacking multilingual data. On closed-book QA, GPT-3 leads (36.3 vs. 33.3, 1-shot), driven primarily by large gaps on Natural Questions (5.4 vs. 2.6) and TriviaQA (26.5 vs. 14.3), while commonsense reasoning subtasks (PIQA, ARC, OpenBookQA) are nearly tied. Against OPT (Zhang et al., 2022), used as a proxy for GPT-3's architecture, DeBERTa demonstrates superior length generalization on the needle-in-a-haystack benchmark due to its relative positional embeddings, whereas OPT's absolute positional encoding causes sharp performance degradation beyond its 512-token training length. In qualitative text generation (Appendix A), DeBERTa produces more appropriate novel-word usage and grammar corrections than OPT 1.3B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pseudo-log-likelihood scoring framework of Salazar et al. (2020) and the autoregressive generation idea from Wang and Cho (2019) to enable an off-the-shelf masked language model (DeBERTa) to perform in-context learning without any architectural modification or additional training. It deliberately positions itself against the dominant narrative in the in-context learning literature (Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022; Olsson et al., 2022), which has treated this capability as exclusive to causal language models, and against the explicit deprecation of BERT-style models articulated by Tay et al. (2023). The evaluation protocol directly replicates the GPT-3 benchmark suite, making the comparison a controlled 'alternative reality' test of whether an MLM released one month after GPT-3 could have been the first to demonstrate emergent in-context learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MLMs and CLMs exhibit complementary strengths—MLMs dominating on language understanding and coreference while CLMs dominate on open-ended generation and closed-book QA—suggests that the field's convergence on purely causal objectives may be leaving performance on the table, and motivates hybrid training objectives (as explored by T5, BART, GLM, and PrefixLM). Key limitations temper the impact: the study uses a single MLM family (DeBERTa) from 2020, the inference is slower due to the inability to cache key-value vectors, no error bars are reported, and the comparison baseline is GPT-3 rather than modern LLMs. Open questions include whether the complementary-strengths pattern persists at much larger scales, whether a unified masked-causal objective can capture both advantages, and whether the data-efficiency gap (78 GB vs. 570 GB) reflects a genuine property of the MLM objective or an artifact of the specific corpus.
