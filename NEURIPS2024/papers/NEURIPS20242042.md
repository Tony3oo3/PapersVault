## Problem

EHR data is highly sparse due to patients not undergoing all tests at each visit, and existing imputation methods that fill missing values in the input space tend to overfit to difficult-to-interpolate details, introduce distributional noise, and withhold data from the prediction task. No prior approach effectively encodes missingness into learned representations while simultaneously capturing higher-order semantic structure across the full sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242042/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate EHR time series of shape T×N (visits × variables) with a corresponding binary observation mask, where observation times are aligned to hours and missing values are zero-padded. A learnable CLS vector is prepended along the temporal dimension, making the effective sequence length T+1. During fine-tuning, prediction is made solely from the CLS-position representation (s⁰ ∈ ℝ^{N×d}) projected through a task-specific MLP, supporting binary classification (mortality, sepsis, decompensation), multi-label classification (phenotyping), and binned regression (length of stay).

## Method

SMART employs a two-stage training strategy: a self-supervised pre-training phase that randomly masks a subset of observations and reconstructs their latent-space representations using an asymmetric encoder–decoder with an EMA-updated target network (inspired by I-JEPA), followed by supervised fine-tuning where the reconstruction decoder is swapped for a task-specific head. The core MART block contains two missing-aware attention mechanisms: a temporal attention that adds a discrete bias (2/1/0) to the attention logits based on the joint mask state of the query and key visits, and a variable attention that uses the CLS position as query and the mean of all observed-visit embeddings as key to capture time-invariant inter-variable correlations. A variable-independent linear encoder maps each (value, mask) pair into a shared latent space, and sinusoidal positional encodings are added before the MART blocks. The key design choice is performing reconstruction in the latent representation space rather than the input space, which forces the model to learn semantic, higher-order features rather than pixel-level interpolation details, yielding better generalization under high missing rates.

## Results

SMART achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six EHR clinical tasks, with an average absolute improvement of 3.80% on AUPRC and 5.15% on F1 Score over the best baseline. On the Sepsis task (lowest observed rate at 19.8%), SMART reaches 81.67% AUPRC and 75.37% F1, outperforming the next-best ConCare (74.83% AUPRC) by 6.84 points. On Cardiology (24.7% observed rate), SMART achieves 53.84% AUPRC vs. PPN's 49.98%, and on in-hospital mortality (MIMIC-III), it reaches 53.30% AUPRC with the smallest standard deviation (±0.12) among all methods, indicating superior stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all six tasks. The largest margins appear on Sepsis (19.8% observed rate), where SMART's 81.67% AUPRC exceeds ConCare (74.83%) by 6.84 points and Warpformer (73.03%) by 8.64 points, highlighting its robustness to high missingness. On Cardiology, SMART beats the best baseline PPN (49.98% AUPRC) by 3.86 points and PrimeNet (49.25%) by 4.59 points. On in-hospital mortality (MIMIC-III), the gap is smaller (53.30% vs. GRASP's 51.84%), but SMART shows the tightest variance (±0.12 vs. ±3.42 for GRASP). Notably, PrimeNet collapses on Sepsis (33.38% AUPRC, 12.46% F1), likely because its input-space reconstruction strategy struggles under extreme sparsity, while AdaCare's convolutional architecture is most sensitive to missing values overall. Among the imputation-focused baselines (RainDrop, Warpformer), SMART consistently leads by 3–9 AUPRC points, confirming that latent-space reconstruction is more effective than input-space imputation for downstream clinical prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART extends the transformer-based EHR modeling line (ConCare, SAFARI) by introducing missing-aware attention biases that explicitly encode observation status into temporal and variable interactions, and departs from prior imputation methods (RainDrop, Warpformer, PrimeNet) that reconstruct missing values in the input space by instead performing reconstruction in the latent representation space. The two-stage pre-training design borrows the asymmetric encoder–target-network paradigm with EMA updates from I-JEPA [58], adapted here to a self-supervised missing-data reconstruction objective. The variable-independent encoding and CLS vector are drawn from ConCare and BERT respectively, but the key conceptual departure is treating missingness as a first-class signal throughout the architecture rather than a preprocessing nuisance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing data is pervasive in real-world EHR (observed rates as low as ~20%), and the paper demonstrates that a lightweight, missing-aware transformer with latent-space pre-training can consistently outperform both representation-learning and imputation-based baselines across six clinical tasks while remaining robust to extreme missingness. The finding that reconstructing in latent space outperforms input-space imputation (ablation: w/ Imputation vs. full model) suggests a broader principle for self-supervised pre-training on sparse data. Open questions include the quadratic scaling of temporal attention for long sequences, the lack of explainability for clinical trust, and validation on additional datasets beyond the three ICU benchmarks.
