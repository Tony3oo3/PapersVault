## Problem

While empirical studies have shown that non-one-hot error correction output codes (ECOCs) improve DNN robustness against weight-errors (e.g., from hardware defects in NVCiM accelerators), no theoretical foundation existed to explain the relationship between codeword design, weight-error magnitude, and network architecture. This paper provides the first rigorous NTK-based analysis of ECOC efficacy and derives principled design guidance for constructing optimal codes tailored to DNNs.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 2D images (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 RGB Tiny ImageNet) and produces a class label via l2-distance decoding to ECOC codewords. The output layer dimension (code length n_L) can substantially exceed the number of classes C (e.g., n_L=1024 for C=100), and during inference Gaussian weight-errors with controllable variance ω² are injected to simulate hardware faults. Decoding maps the perturbed network output to the nearest codeword in the codebook.

## Method

The paper derives two key NTK-based theoretical results: (1) in the absence of weight-errors, applying an ECOC is equivalent to replacing the standard l2 decoding metric with a Mahalanobis metric governed by the codeword correlation matrix, and (2) a perturbation bound showing that the normalized codeword distance (scaled by √n_L) determines a threshold below which predictions remain correct despite weight-errors. Guided by these findings, two code construction methods are proposed: Method 1 solves a non-convex optimization balancing pairwise codeword distance (for robustness) and correlation (for clean accuracy) via tanh reparameterization and gradient descent, suitable for small class counts; Method 2 selects C codewords from a Hadamard code and its complement pairs, trading off orthogonality against maximum Hamming distance, suitable for large class counts. Experiments across four datasets and four architectures (MLP, AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet-50) validate up to 7% improvement over existing ECOCs under weight-errors.

## Results

On large-scale tasks, the proposed Method 2 (picking from Hadamard) achieves 68.25% clean accuracy on CIFAR-100 (n_L=1024) and 56.42% on Tiny ImageNet, outperforming Hadamard codes (67.45% and 53.15%) and one-hot codes (62.81% and 48.74%). Under weight-error variance ω²=0.05, Method 2 retains 36.13% accuracy on CIFAR-100 versus 28.63% for Hadamard and 1.00% for one-hot — a ~7.5 percentage-point improvement. On small-scale tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10), Method 1 (direct optimization) matches or slightly exceeds Hadamard codes, e.g., 50.18% vs. 45.66% on CIFAR-10 at ω²=0.03 with n_L=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baseline ECOCs: one-hot codes, repetition codes, random codes, and Hadamard codes [Verma & Swami, NeurIPS 2019]. On large-scale tasks (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet), Method 2 consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest gap over Hadamard codes reaching ~7.5 percentage points at ω²=0.05 on CIFAR-100 (36.13% vs. 28.63%). One-hot and repetition codes collapse to near-chance accuracy under moderate weight errors (e.g., 1.00% on CIFAR-100 at ω²=0.05), while random codes perform comparably to Hadamard at large code lengths due to concentration of pairwise distances. On small-scale tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10), Method 1 achieves comparable clean accuracy to all baselines but shows a clear advantage under weight errors — e.g., 50.18% vs. 45.66% (Hadamard) and 11.15% (one-hot) on CIFAR-10 at ω²=0.03. The paper also shows that Method 1 is preferable for ≤10 classes while Method 2 is better for ≥20 classes, as Method 1's non-convex optimization is more prone to local minima at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: the empirical application of Error Correction Output Codes (ECOCs) to DNNs for weight-error robustness (Deng et al. 2010, Liu & Wen 2019, Verma & Swami 2019, Gupta & Amin 2022, Yu et al. 2023) and the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework for analyzing infinite-width DNNs (Jacot et al. 2018, Lee et al. 2019). Prior ECOC-on-DNN work was entirely empirical, applying existing codes (Hadamard, repetition, random) without theoretical justification for why or how they improve robustness. This paper departs from that tradition by providing the first NTK-based theoretical characterization, showing that ECOCs in the clean regime act as a Mahalanobis-distance decoding metric and establishing a perturbation threshold that links code geometry to error-correction capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results give practitioners principled design criteria for ECOCs: codeword orthogonality governs clean accuracy while normalized inter-codeword distance governs robustness, and the two must be balanced during code construction. This is practically important for hardware-aware DNN deployment (e.g., NVCiM accelerators) where weight-errors are a real reliability concern. However, the NTK framework requires infinite-width networks, and all experiments are simulation-based without hardware validation, leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees and code-design principles transfer faithfully to finite-width, real-world networks. The 7% improvement over existing ECOCs, while consistent across four datasets and four architectures, is moderate and the practical gap between the theoretical threshold and achievable performance in finite networks remains uncharacterized.
