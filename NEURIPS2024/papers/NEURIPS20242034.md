## Problem

Unsupervised environment design (UED) aims to generate training environments that maximize an agent's regret, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: learning-based generators (e.g., PAIRED) can directly produce meaningful environments yet suffer from training instability and limited coverage, while replay-based methods (e.g., PLR, ACCEL) maintain diversity but are sample-inefficient and cannot directly generate targeted adversarial environments. No prior UED algorithm simultaneously achieves direct adversarial generation, broad environment coverage, and sample efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise θ_T ~ N(0,I) as the initial condition for the diffusion reverse process, guided at each denoising step by the gradient of a differentiable regret estimate computed from an environment critic network. For the maze task, environment parameters are 13×13×3 images (walls, agent start, goal); for bipedal locomotion, they are 8-dimensional vectors (stump/stair heights, pit gap, stair steps, roughness). The output is a batch of environment parameters sampled from the soft-UED optimal distribution Λ^π, which are instantiated into concrete MDPs for the agent to train on.

## Method

ADD introduces soft UED, which augments the original UED minimax regret objective with an entropy regularization term (proven to admit strong duality via Proposition 4.1), converting the problem from finding a single regret-maximizing environment to sampling from a distribution Λ^π(θ) ∝ u(θ)exp(ω·REGRET(π,θ)). A diffusion model is pre-trained once on randomly generated environments and then guided during sampling by adding ω∇_θt REGRET_t(π,θ_t) to the score function at each reverse-process step, analogous to classifier guidance. To make the regret differentiable, ADD introduces an environment critic that predicts a categorical return distribution (in the style of distributional RL) over M bins, and estimates regret as CVaR_α of that distribution minus its expectation, yielding a smooth function of the noised environment parameter θ_t. Crucially, the diffusion model is never retrained during agent training—only the environment critic is updated with episodic returns—preserving the generator's ability to cover the full environment domain while still producing targeted adversarial environments.

## Results

On the partially observable navigation task, ADD achieves a mean zero-shot solved rate of 85% across 12 test environments, outperforming ACCEL (72%), PLR⊥ (63%), PAIRED (59%), and DR (64%). ADD also attains the highest first, second, and third quartile values (79%, 93%, 99%) with a 51% smaller interquartile range than ACCEL. On the 2D bipedal locomotion task, ADD achieves a mean return of 149.6 across six test environments, surpassing PLR⊥ (114.8), ACCEL (71.7), DR (29.5), and PAIRED (−2.9). Ablating the regret guidance (ADD w/o guidance) drops navigation performance to 63%, confirming that regret-guided diffusion is critical for generating adversarial environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADD decisively outperforms all three UED baselines on both tasks. Against PAIRED (learning-based), ADD achieves 85% vs. 59% mean solved rate on navigation and 149.6 vs. −2.9 mean return on bipedal locomotion, confirming that RL-trained generators suffer from instability and limited environment coverage. Against PLR⊥ (replay-based), ADD improves navigation by 22 percentage points (85% vs. 63%) and bipedal locomotion by 34.8 return points (149.6 vs. 114.8), showing that direct regret-guided generation is more sample-efficient than selective replay. Against ACCEL (state-of-the-art replay + mutation), ADD gains 13 percentage points on navigation (85% vs. 72%) and 77.9 return points on bipedal locomotion (149.6 vs. 71.7), with a notably tighter interquartile range indicating more consistent generalization. The ablation ADD w/o guidance (63% navigation, 69.3 bipedal) demonstrates that the regret guidance signal is essential—without it, the diffusion generator behaves similarly to domain randomization and fails to produce adversarial curricula.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADD sits at the intersection of unsupervised environment design (UED) and diffusion-based generative modeling. It departs from learning-based UED methods (PAIRED, CLUTR, SHED) that train an RL-based generator—known to be unstable and limited in coverage—and from replay-based methods (PLR⊥, ACCEL) that rely on random generation and are sample-inefficient. The key conceptual move is introducing 'soft UED,' which adds an entropy regularization term to the original minimax regret objective, yielding a closed-form target distribution over environment parameters that can be sampled via classifier-guided diffusion. The differentiable regret estimator, built on a distributional environment critic and CVaR approximation, is the technical enabler that makes regret-guided diffusion tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADD provides a practical recipe for generating adversarial yet diverse training environments without the instability of RL-trained generators or the sample inefficiency of replay-based selection, and its controllable-difficulty generation (Eq. 13) enables reuse of the learned critic and generator for benchmark construction. However, convergence to the soft minimax optimum is not guaranteed, the regret estimate is not tightly bounded, and the environment critic lags behind the policy update—open questions that limit theoretical guarantees. The evaluation is confined to two 2D tasks (grid mazes, bipedal locomotion), so whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or continuous-state environments (e.g., 3D robotics) remains to be demonstrated.
