## Problem

While iteratively reweighted least-squares (IRLS), lin-RFM, and alternating minimization are all known to learn linear diagonal networks (Hadamard-parameterized sparse linear models), prior theoretical analyses either characterize fixed points under worst-case data or provide only convergence-to-stationary-point guarantees, leaving the precise estimation error trajectory across iterations uncharacterized. This paper asks: in the high-dimensional limit with i.i.d. Gaussian covariates, what is the exact distribution of the iterates at each step, and how do different reweighting policies compare in terms of test error after a small number of iterations?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sequence of independent batches (X^(t), y^(t)) of i.i.d. standard Gaussian design matrices and noisy linear measurements y^(t) = (1/√d)X^(t)θ* + ε^(t), along with an initial weight vector v^(0) (e.g., the ones vector). At each iteration it produces a pair (u^(t+1), v^(t+1)) whose Hadamard product u⊙v approximates the target signal θ*. The key theoretical output is a deterministic scalar recursion (involving parameters τ_t, β_t and a joint distribution Π_t) that exactly characterizes the limiting empirical distribution of (u_i, v_i, θ_i*) at every iteration, enabling Monte Carlo prediction of the normalized ℓ₁ test error ||u⊙v − θ*||₁/d without running the algorithm.

## Method

The paper unifies IRLS, lin-RFM, and alternating minimization under a single iterative template: a weighted least-squares update on u given fixed v, followed by an entry-wise reweighting v^(t+1) = ψ(u^(t+1), v^(t)) for a general function ψ. The core technical contribution is a precise asymptotic characterization (Theorem 1) obtained by applying the Convex Gaussian Min-Max Theorem to a perturbed version of the least-squares subproblem, yielding a closed-form scalar recursion for (τ_{t+1}, β_{t+1}) and a distributional update Π_{t+1} = Law(ψ(Q_{t+1}, V), Θ) that can be evaluated by simple Monte Carlo. This recursion is applied inductively across iterations, requiring only W₂ convergence of the initialization and boundedness/PL(2) conditions on ψ. The framework is further extended (Theorem 2) to grouped reweighting where ψ acts on blocks of size b, showing that group-aware reweighting provably reduces test error when θ* has group-sparse structure, with performance scaling with the number of non-zero groups rather than total sparsity.

## Results

In the sparse linear regression setting (n=250, d=2000, θ* with Bernoulli(0.01) entries, expected sparsity s=20), the asymptotic predictions from Theorem 1 align closely with numerical simulations across all tested reweighting schemes, with the median test error (1/d‖u⊙v−θ*‖₁) reaching low values within 4–8 iterations. More aggressive weightings (ψ=tanh|uv| and ψ=tanh u²) achieve the lowest test error after several iterations, while alternating minimization (ψ=u) exhibits oscillatory decay in the low-noise regime (σ=0.001). In the group-sparse setting (n=500, d=4000, σ=0.1, block size b=8), the group-aware reweighting ψ_ga consistently outperforms the group-blind ψ_gb, and the advantage grows as the group size b increases, confirming that test error scales with the number of non-zero groups rather than total sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four reweighting schemes in the coordinate-wise setting (Figure 1): classical IRLS (ψ=|uv|^{1/2}), lin-RFM (ψ=tanh|uv|), alternating minimization (ψ=u), and a newly proposed scheme (ψ=tanh u²). The lin-RFM and the new ψ=tanh u² scheme achieve the lowest test error after several iterations, outperforming both IRLS and AM. Alternating minimization is competitive but shows non-monotonic, oscillatory test-error decay (particularly at σ=0.001), though it still decreases steadily after every pair of iterations. In the group-sparse setting (Figure 2), the group-aware reweighting ψ_ga=(1/b)Σⱼ tanh|uⱼvⱼ|·1_b consistently beats the group-blind ψ_gb=tanh|u⊙v| at every group size b tested, with the gap widening as b grows. The group-blind scheme's performance remains approximately flat as b increases, indicating it cannot exploit the group structure, while ψ_ga's test error improves monotonically with larger groups—demonstrating that structured reweighting provably reduces sample complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously studied algorithm families—reparameterized IRLS, lin-RFM, and alternating minimization on linear diagonal networks—under a single iterative framework and provides the first precise high-dimensional asymptotic characterization of their iterates. It builds on the eta-trick connection between non-smooth regularizers and Hadamard parameterizations (Bach, Hoff, Poon & Peyré), the lin-RFM reweighting scheme (Radhakrishnan et al.), and the CGMT-based analysis toolkit (Thrampoulidis et al., Chandrasekher et al.). The key departure from prior work is the batched, i.i.d. Gaussian covariate setting with a recursive distributional characterization that tracks the full trajectory, rather than worst-case fixed-point or stationary-point analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The precise asymptotic predictions enable rigorous, iteration-by-iteration comparison of reweighting schemes and reveal that statistically favorable solutions emerge in only a handful of iterations—a practical insight not accessible from prior fixed-point or convergence-rate analyses. The group-sparse extension demonstrates that tying weights in the LDNN hidden layer and using group-aware reweighting provably improves test error, scaling with the number of non-zero groups rather than total sparsity. Open questions include extending the analysis to the non-batched (data-reuse) setting, obtaining finite-sample guarantees for minibatch regimes, and weakening the boundedness assumptions on the signal that currently appear as proof artifacts.
