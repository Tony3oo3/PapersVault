## Problem

Existing time series forecasting models must choose between Channel-Independent (CI) and Channel-Dependent (CD) strategies, each with fundamental trade-offs: CI achieves higher per-channel capacity but poor generalization and ignores cross-channel interactions, while CD captures channel relations but suffers from oversmoothing when channels are dissimilar. No prior approach effectively balances individual channel treatment with necessary cross-channel dependencies, and the role of channel identity information in model performance has not been systematically characterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×C} (or univariate samples for the univariate adaptation) and produces a future forecast Y ∈ R^{H×C} along with soft cluster assignment probabilities p_{i,k} and learned prototype embeddings. CCM is plug-and-play: it inserts a Cluster Assigner before the temporal module and replaces the final feed-forward layer with a cluster-aware version, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone. In zero-shot mode, only the pre-trained prototypes and cluster weights are loaded, and the cross-attention is disabled to fix prototypes, enabling forecasting on unseen channels or samples without retraining.

## Method

CCM is motivated by the empirical finding that a model's performance gain from channel identity information is anti-correlated with pairwise channel similarity (measured via RBF kernels), suggesting that similar channels can share representations. The Cluster Assigner encodes each channel into a d-dimensional embedding via an MLP, computes soft clustering probabilities against K learnable cluster embeddings using normalized inner products, and applies a reparameterization trick to obtain a near-binary membership matrix. A modified cross-attention mechanism, masked by the membership matrix, generates prototype embeddings that store cluster-specific knowledge from training. The Cluster-aware Feed Forward assigns one linear layer per cluster and produces each channel's forecast by averaging cluster outputs weighted by the channel's clustering probabilities (equivalently, averaging cluster weights). A self-supervised ClusterLoss simultaneously maximizes intra-cluster channel similarity and encourages inter-cluster separation, and the learned prototypes enable zero-shot transfer to unseen samples by computing their clustering distribution against the frozen prototypes.

## Results

On long-term forecasting across 9 datasets and 4 horizons (144 settings), CCM improves MSE in 90.27% of cases and MAE in 84.03% of cases, with an average MSE improvement of 2.4% over base models. On short-term M4 forecasting, CCM-enhanced DLinear achieves 12.546 SMAPE vs. 13.639 for vanilla DLinear (11.62% improvement) and outperforms the best convolutional method TimesNet by 8.88%. On the new Stock dataset (unseen stocks at test time), CCM reduces MSE by up to 5.25% (horizon 24) and MAE by up to 3.89%. Zero-shot cross-domain forecasting (e.g., ETTh1→ETTm1) shows consistent gains, with CCM improving DLinear by 10.48% and PatchTST by 11.13% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCM is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on four base models spanning linear (TSMixer, DLinear), transformer (PatchTST), and convolutional (TimesNet) paradigms. On DLinear (CI), CCM yields the largest absolute gains, e.g., reducing MSE from 0.554 to 0.451 on ETTm2 at horizon 720 (−18.6%) and from 0.489 to 0.479 on ETTh1 at horizon 720. On TSMixer (CD), improvements are more modest on ETTh1 (0.361→0.365 at h=96) but substantial on ETTm2 (0.420→0.369 at h=720, −12.1%). PatchTST (CI) and TimesNet (CD) show consistent but smaller gains, with TimesNet benefiting most on ETTm2 (0.408→0.394 at h=720). Against the regularization-based PRReg method [17], CCM wins or ties in 13 out of 16 settings (e.g., ETTh1 Transformer: 0.518 vs. 0.539; ETTm1 Transformer: 0.300 vs. 0.349), confirming that structural channel clustering outperforms objective-level regularization for balancing CI/CD trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the well-studied CI/CD channel strategy trade-off in time series forecasting (Han et al. 2023; Peiwen & Changsheng 2023; Li et al. 2023) and clustering techniques borrowed from other domains (image classification, NLP, traffic prediction). Rather than adopting a pure CI or CD approach, or applying regularization as in PRReg (Han et al. 2023), CCM introduces a learned clustering mechanism that replaces channel identity with cluster identity, motivated by the empirical observation that model reliance on channel identity anti-correlates with intrinsic channel similarity. The prototype-learning component via cross-attention repurposes the clustering structure to store transferable knowledge, enabling zero-shot forecasting—a capability not previously available in standard time series backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCM offers a practical, plug-and-play module that consistently improves four architecturally diverse backbones (linear, transformer, convolutional) across long-term, short-term, and zero-shot forecasting, making it broadly applicable to practitioners. The zero-shot forecasting capability via learned prototypes addresses a real deployment concern where retraining on unseen channels or stocks is infeasible. Open questions include whether the RBF-based Euclidean similarity is optimal for all domains, whether the clustering quality loss (ClusterLoss) can be further refined, and whether the approach extends to other sequential prediction tasks beyond time series. The paper also notes that CCM's gains are more pronounced on datasets with higher intrinsic channel correlation, suggesting limited benefit in truly independent-channel settings.
