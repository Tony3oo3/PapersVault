## Problem

Unsupervised semantic segmentation is inherently ambiguous because scenes contain semantic structure at multiple, unknown granularity levels, yet existing methods impose dataset-specific priors, fixed partition counts, or object/scene-centric biases to resolve this ambiguity. No prior approach can discover an arbitrary number of semantic levels and partitions per level without a priori knowledge of the scene's hierarchical structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, extracts per-pixel feature codes from a frozen self-supervised backbone (e.g., DINOv2-ViT-B14-REG), and resizes to a fixed 60×60 (or 60×120 for street scenes) code grid. A spectral overclustering step condenses the graph to m=300 superpixel nodes before recursive partitioning. The output is a variable-depth tree (up to 10 levels) of semantic regions where every pixel maps to a leaf; an optional CRF post-processing step sharpens leaf boundaries using the original pixel observations.

## Method

The core mechanism is a recursive spectral partitioning of a weighted graph whose edge weights are scaled cosine similarities between self-supervised feature codes. At each recursion level, the algorithm computes the normalized graph Laplacian, extracts the k_max smallest eigenvectors, and selects the number of partitions k by finding the largest spectral gap δ_j = λ_j − λ_{j−1}. The k eigenvectors are row-normalized and clustered via k-means to yield k subgraphs. Crucially, the Davis-Kahan sin θ perturbation bound (2·min(k^{1/2}‖H‖_op, ‖H‖_F)/(λ_k − λ_{k−1})) is evaluated at every split; if it exceeds a threshold p_max, or if the partition is smaller than k_min, or eigenvalues exceed λ_max, recursion halts for that branch. This perturbation-stability criterion simultaneously determines both how many regions to create and when the current granularity is no longer reliable, making the entire hierarchy—depth, branching factor, and leaf count—data-driven and scene-specific rather than fixed.

## Results

On PascalVOC2012, the method achieves 80.3 mIoU (with CRF) and 78.1 mIoU (without CRF), surpassing all unsupervised baselines by over 30 points and even exceeding the fully-supervised DeepLab-CRF (77.7). On MSCOCO, it reaches 56.5 mIoU, outperforming the best weakly-supervised method (WeakTr, 50.3) and all unsupervised approaches. The proposed NMCovering metric scores 75.4 on PascalVOC2012 and 42.1 on COCO-Stuff, while NHCovering reaches 42.9 on COCO-Stuff and 63.1 on PartImageNet-158, demonstrating strong hierarchical consistency across granularity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines: it exceeds MaskDistill (48.9) by 31.4 points, Zhang et al. (43.5) by 36.8, HSG (41.9) by 38.4, Leopart (41.7) by 38.6, and Melas-Kyriazi et al. (37.2) by 43.1 mIoU on PascalVOC2012. Against weakly-supervised methods, it surpasses WeakTr (74.0) by 6.3 points, L2G (72.0) by 8.3, and ViT-PCM (69.3) by 11.0 on VOC12, while also leading on MSCOCO (56.5 vs. 50.3 for WeakTr). Among fully-supervised methods, it beats DeepLab-CRF (77.7) by 2.6 points but falls slightly short of DeepLabV3-JFT (82.7) by 2.4 points. On boundary-potential baselines (Table 4), the method achieves 80.3 mIoU vs. SE-OWT-UCM (48.4) and PMI-OWT-UCM (47.0) on PascalVOC2012, and 59.9 vs. 30.7 and 27.5 on COCO-Stuff, confirming that low-level edge cues are inadequate for hierarchical semantic grouping. On MSCOCO, the method leads all unsupervised and weakly-supervised baselines by at least 6.3 mIoU (56.5 vs. 50.3 for WeakTr).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalized-cut spectral clustering framework (Shi & Malik, 2000; Ng et al., 2001) to a recursive, hierarchy-agnostic setting by introducing a perturbation-theoretic stopping criterion derived from the Davis-Kahan sin θ theorem (Yu et al., 2015). Unlike prior unsupervised hierarchical segmentation methods (HSG, Zhang & Maire) that fix or learn the number of granularity levels, or boundary-potential approaches (OWT-UCM) that group regions using low-level cues, this work treats self-supervised feature codes as a codebook and uses spectral gaps to dynamically determine partition count at each recursion level. The method also departs from object-centric or scene-centric priors common in the literature by making no assumption about the semantic granularity structure, producing a fully data-driven region tree.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchy-agnostic formulation addresses a fundamental ambiguity in unsupervised segmentation—determining the right level of semantic granularity—without dataset-specific priors, offering a universal tool for pixel-level semantic parsing. The perturbation-theoretic stopping criterion provides a principled, algebraic guarantee for when further partitioning is meaningful, which is absent in prior iterative spectral methods. Limitations include O(n²) scaling with input size, difficulty detecting small object parts (especially with overclustering), and susceptibility to biases inherited from the SSL backbone. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, 3D scenes, or multimodal settings, and whether the perturbation framework can be made more adaptive to reduce the need for dataset-specific parameter tuning (k_min, λ_max, p_max).
