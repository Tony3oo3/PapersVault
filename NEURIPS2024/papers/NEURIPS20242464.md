## Problem

Prior theoretical guarantees for exploration-free greedy algorithms in linear contextual bandits were restricted to Gaussian and uniform context distributions, leaving open whether other common distributions could also support efficient greedy learning. This paper asks whether a broader, characterizable class of distributions exists under which greedy algorithms achieve provable efficiency, and whether the resulting regret can be sharpened beyond the O(√T) bounds previously known for Gaussian contexts.

## Input / Output

In each round, the agent observes K context vectors (one per arm) drawn from an unknown distribution and selects an arm greedily based on an OLS parameter estimate, receiving a scalar reward only from the pulled arm. The algorithm is entirely exploration-free: it never deviates from the greedy choice, relying instead on the natural diversity of the context distribution for parameter estimation. The key distributional requirement is the Local Anti-Concentration (LAC) condition on the context density, which constrains the growth rate of the score function ∇log f_X to be at most polynomial in ‖x‖∞.

## Method

The paper introduces the Local Anti-Concentration (LAC) condition, requiring that the ℓ∞-norm of the score function ∇log f_X(x) grows at most polynomially in ‖x‖∞, and shows it is satisfied by Gaussian, exponential, uniform, Cauchy, Student's t, Laplace distributions and their truncated variants. A critical property is that LAC is preserved under conditioning, which is essential because the distribution of the greedily selected context differs from the marginal context distribution. Under LAC, the paper proves two key technical results: (i) the minimum eigenvalue of the adapted Gram matrix grows linearly in t, ensuring √t-consistency of the OLS estimator, and (ii) the suboptimality gap between the optimal and second-best arms is probabilistically bounded—a margin property that prior work assumed explicitly. The proof leverages geometric arguments about section densities and one-sided decay rates of projected conditional densities. Together, these results yield an O(poly log T) cumulative expected regret bound for the LinGreedy algorithm without requiring an additional margin assumption, the sharpest known for greedy linear contextual bandits.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that under the LAC condition, the cumulative expected regret of LinGreedy is bounded by O(poly log T), the sharpest known bound for greedy algorithms in single-parameter linear contextual bandits. The diversity constant is lower-bounded as λ*(t) ≥ Ω(1/d) (Theorem 2), and the suboptimality gap constant satisfies C_Δ(t) = Õ(√d) (Theorem 3). Empirically, LinGreedy achieves logarithmic-scale cumulative regret across Gaussian, Laplace, uniform, and truncated Cauchy context distributions, significantly outperforming the exploration-based LinUCB and LinTS algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Kannan et al. [20] and Sivakumar et al. [33], which achieve only Õ(√T) regret restricted to Gaussian contexts, this paper improves the regret to O(poly log T) and extends the admissible distribution class to include Laplace, truncated exponential, truncated Student's t, truncated Cauchy, and general exponential-family distributions. Against Raghavan et al. [30], which provides an Õ(T^{1/3}) Bayesian regret bound (a weaker notion), this paper delivers a sharper O(poly log T) frequentist regret bound. Relative to Bastani et al. [8], which also achieves O(poly log T) but only for Gaussian and uniform distributions and requires an explicit margin assumption, this paper removes the margin assumption entirely (proving it is implied by LAC) and substantially broadens the distribution class. In empirical comparisons (Section 6, Appendix M), LinGreedy outperforms both LinUCB [1] and LinTS [4] across all tested distributions, achieving lower cumulative regret on a logarithmic scale, confirming that exploration-free greedy selection can be superior to exploration-based methods when contexts satisfy LAC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on exploration-free greedy algorithms for linear contextual bandits initiated by Kannan et al. (2018), Sivakumar et al. (2020), and Bastani et al. (2021), which established that greedy policies can achieve sublinear regret under Gaussian or uniform context distributions. The key departure is the introduction of the Local Anti-Concentration (LAC) condition—a simple bound on the gradient of the log-density—which subsumes and generalizes the distributional assumptions of all prior work while simultaneously implying both the Gram matrix diversity and the margin condition that Bastani et al. had to assume separately. The regret analysis builds on the peeling and self-normalized martingale techniques from Bastani & Bayati (2020) and Goldenshluger & Zeevi (2013), but applies them to a substantially broader class of distributions including exponential, Cauchy, Student's t, Laplace, and their truncated variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LAC condition provides the first principled, easily verifiable criterion that characterizes when a greedy (exploration-free) linear bandit algorithm achieves poly-logarithmic regret, removing the need for an explicit margin assumption that prior work required. This is practically significant in domains where exploration is infeasible or unethical (healthcare, clinical trials), as it tells practitioners exactly which context distributions make greedy policies safe to deploy. Open questions remain for multi-parameter settings (where the paper shows Gibbs distributions fail for K≥3 arms), discrete-supported contexts, and adversarial or non-stationary environments. The √t-consistency of the OLS estimator under adaptive sampling is also an independently valuable result that could inform sample complexity analyses beyond bandits.
