## Problem

Existing DP ReLU regression results rely on bounded-norm assumptions (‖x‖₂ ≤ 1) that become unrealistic as dimensionality grows, and their dimension-independent guarantees depend on Gaussian-like data that reduces the covariance to a scaled identity. In the overparameterized regime (d ≫ N), prior utility bounds become trivial. This paper asks whether meaningful, dimension-independent utility guarantees can be obtained under more general data distributions with eigenvalue decay.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume N i.i.d. feature-response pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a well-specified ReLU regression model y = ReLU(xᵀw*) + z, along with a separate set of m estimating samples used to privately determine adaptive clipping thresholds at each iteration. The output is a differentially private weight vector w_priv (the average of all iterates) that minimizes excess population risk L(w_priv) − L(w*). The utility guarantee is expressed in terms of an effective dimension D_eff and a private effective dimension D_eff^pri, both defined as functions of the data covariance eigenvalues rather than the ambient dimension d directly.

## Method

DP-GLMtron modifies the standard SGD update by dropping the ReLU derivative (following GLM-tron), then applies a single pass over randomly shuffled data with per-step Gaussian noise calibrated via privacy amplification by shuffling and an adaptive clipping threshold computed through a private histogram mechanism. This yields an excess risk bound of Õ(D_eff/N + D_eff^pri/(N²ε²)) but requires ε = Õ(1/√N). To remove this small-budget restriction, DP-TAGLMtron replaces shuffling with a tree aggregation protocol that adds noise to cumulative mini-batch gradient sums, achieving a comparable bound with only an additional O(log N) factor while supporting arbitrary privacy budgets. The key theoretical insight is that the cut-off index k* = max{k : λ_k ≥ 2/(ηN)} partitions the feature space so that the utility bound depends on eigenvalue sums rather than d, yielding vanishing risk under polynomial (λ_k = k^{-r}) or exponential (λ_k = e^{-k}) spectral decay even when d ≫ N.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical utility bounds rather than a single numerical accuracy table. DP-GLMtron achieves an excess population risk of Õ(D_eff/N + D_eff^pri/(N²ε²)), matching a corresponding lower bound, while DP-TAGLMtron achieves Õ(D_eff/N + (D_eff^pri + D_eff)/(N²ε²)) with only an additional O(log N) factor. Empirically, on synthetic data (d=1024, eigenvalue decay λ_i ∝ i^{-2} and i^{-3}, ε ∈ {0.05, 0.2, 0.5}), DP-GLMtron and DP-TAGLMtron consistently achieve lower excess risk than DP-SGD and DP-FTRL, with the gap widening as sample size N increases from 50 to 550. On MNIST (N up to 1000), DP-TAGLMtron achieves the lowest excess risk under tight privacy (ε=0.05), while both proposed methods markedly outperform baselines under relaxed privacy (ε=0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: DP-SGD [1], DP-FTRL [22], and DP-PGD [33]. Against DP-SGD, both DP-GLMtron and DP-TAGLMtron consistently achieve lower excess risk across all privacy budgets and sample sizes, with the advantage growing as N increases; DP-SGD exhibits poor convergence even at large sample sizes. Against DP-FTRL, the proposed methods also outperform, though the gap is smaller at small ε (0.05) where DP-FTRL's tree mechanism reduces noise; at ε=0.5, DP-TAGLMtron's excess risk is significantly lower than DP-FTRL's, suggesting DP-FTRL converges to a suboptimal solution. Against DP-PGD [33], the comparison is primarily theoretical: DP-PGD requires the strong assumption ‖x‖₂ ≤ 1 and yields a dimension-dependent bound that becomes trivial when d ≫ N, whereas DP-GLMtron's bound is expressed in terms of effective dimensions D_eff and D_eff^pri that can be independent of d under eigenvalue decay, providing non-trivial guarantees in the overparameterized regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the non-private GLM-tron perceptron algorithm [23] by adapting its update rule (which drops the ReLU derivative) to a differentially private setting, combined with privacy amplification by shuffling [17] and adaptive clipping [28]. It departs from the prior DP ReLU regression work of Shen et al. [33], which required bounded feature norms (||x||₂ ≤ 1) and yielded dimension-dependent bounds that become trivial in the overparameterized regime (d ≫ N). The second algorithm, DP-TAGLMtron, repurposes the tree aggregation protocol [22, 14] to avoid the small-privacy-budget constraint inherent in shuffling-based amplification, while the effective dimension framework (D_eff, D_eff^pri) draws on eigenvalue-decay analysis from non-private high-dimensional learning [49, 56].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is showing that DP ReLU regression utility can be independent of ambient dimension d when the data covariance exhibits polynomial or exponential eigenvalue decay, a result unavailable under the bounded-norm assumptions of prior work. This provides a principled understanding of when overparameterization is benign under DP constraints. However, the analysis is confined to the well-specified ReLU regression setting, and the authors explicitly note it remains uncertain whether conclusions generalize to other non-convex problems. A practical limitation is that DP-GLMtron requires ε = Õ(1/√N), restricting its use to small privacy budgets, while DP-TAGLMtron pays an O(log N) factor to lift this constraint.
