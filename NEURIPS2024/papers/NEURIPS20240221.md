## Problem

ControlNet-based approaches have become the dominant framework for multilingual visual text generation, yet the specific influence of text control information (glyph images) on the diffusion process remains unexplored. Unlike general ControlNet conditions such as Canny edges or depth maps, glyph images are sparse, fine-grained, and require precise detail preservation, but prior works treat them identically to other conditions. This paper systematically investigates how control information behaves at the input, across denoising stages, and at the output feature level to identify what is missing in current pipelines.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a glyph image (standard-font rendering of target text), a position image (bounding-box layout), and a CLIP text prompt; for editing, it additionally takes the original scene image and a mask indicating the region to modify. Outputs are full-resolution RGB images in the SD1.5 latent space, constrained to at most 5 text lines of 20 characters each. The glyph input is notably sparse—text occupies only a small fraction of the image—making it fundamentally different from dense ControlNet conditions like Canny edges or depth maps.

## Method

TextGen introduces a two-stage ControlNet pipeline: a global control stage trained on large timesteps (structure and style) and a detail control stage trained on small timesteps (texture refinement), each with independent parameters. The glyph encoder uses a Fourier Enhancement Convolution (FEC) block that processes features in both spatial and frequency domains via 2D FFT/IFFT, with a large-kernel global perception layer to overcome local receptive-field limitations on sparse text regions. At inference, a frequency-domain balancing scheme (inspired by FreeU) modulates the control, skip, and base features in the U-Net decoder using scalar factors α, β, and a low-frequency suppression mask γ, emphasizing high-frequency texture while suppressing irrelevant low-frequency content. A unified inference paradigm handles both generation and editing: for editing, the original image is noised to 80% of the timestep, then denoised through the global stage until T/2 and the detail stage (which receives the masked original image) for the remainder, restoring background while inserting new text.

## Results

On the AnyWord benchmark, TextGen achieves 73.36% sentence accuracy (ACC) and 88.98% Normalized Edit Distance (NED) for English, and 67.92% ACC and 83.94% NED for Chinese, using only 2.5M training images. This represents a 9.1% ACC gain over the AnyText baseline trained on the same TG-2M dataset (64.26% English ACC) and a 2.9% gain for Chinese. The FID scores are competitive (40.37 English, 28.90 Chinese) though not the absolute best, which the authors attribute to a distribution gap between TG-2M and the AnyWord FID subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextGen substantially outperforms all four baselines on both English and Chinese sentence accuracy. Against ControlNet (58.37% Eng. ACC, 36.20% Chi. ACC), TextGen gains +15.0 and +31.7 points respectively. GlyphControl and TextDiffuser, despite using 10M training images, achieve far lower Chinese accuracy (4.54% and 6.05% ACC) due to their limited multilingual capability, while TextGen reaches 67.92% with only 2.5M images. The closest competitor is AnyText-v1.0 (65.88% Eng. ACC, 66.34% Chi. ACC, trained on 3.5M images); TextGen surpasses it by +7.5 points on English ACC and +1.6 on Chinese ACC while using less data and no perceptual loss. On FID, AnyText-v1.0 achieves a lower English FID (35.87 vs. 40.37), but the authors argue this reflects a distribution gap between the AnyWord FID subset and TG-2M rather than inferior visual quality, and note that TextGen produces more diverse and artistic outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet-based visual text generation pipeline (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023; Tuo et al., AnyText 2023) by systematically analyzing how control information behaves differently from general ControlNet conditions (Canny, depth, M-LSD) in the context of glyph images. It draws on FreeU's frequency-domain feature balancing to motivate a Fourier-based enhancement of both input encoding and output feature injection, and introduces a two-stage (global-then-detail) training paradigm that departs from the single-stage fixed-control approach used by GlyphControl and AnyText. The TG-2M dataset is constructed from existing sources (MARIO-10M, Wukong, ArT, etc.) with aggressive filtering, positioning it as a lightweight alternative to the 10M-scale datasets used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical analysis of control information's role across denoising stages in text generation, revealing that late-stage control remains critical for text (unlike general diffusion) and that early-stage control shapes non-text regions—insights that could inform other fine-grained conditional generation tasks. The two-stage framework elegantly unifies generation and editing without architectural changes, and the TG-2M dataset (2.5M images) offers a practical training resource. Key limitations remain: the VAE bottleneck restricts very small or complex text, the CLIP text encoder limits semantic understanding, and the FID gap with AnyText suggests distribution mismatch rather than true quality degradation. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain control insights generalize to other sparse-condition tasks and whether the two-stage paradigm can be extended to video or 3D text rendering.
