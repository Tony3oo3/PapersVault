## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models (DiT, MDTv2) achieve strong image synthesis quality but incur heavy computational costs—DiT-XL-2 alone consumes 118 GFLOPs per inference step—limiting practical deployment. While CNN-based DPMs are lighter, they lack the scalability and performance ceiling of transformer backbones. Few prior works have specifically targeted reducing the FLOPs of transformer-based DPMs without sacrificing generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model operates entirely in the latent space of a frozen LDM VAE, taking Gaussian noise in latent space and a class label (with classifier-free guidance) as input, and producing a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image after 250 DDIM sampling steps. With a patch size of 2, a 256×256 image yields a 32×32 latent grid of 1024 tokens; EDT progressively compresses this to 256 and 64 tokens through two down-sampling stages before reconstructing via up-sampling. Evaluation follows the ADM protocol reporting FID-50K without classifier-free guidance by default.

## Method

EDT introduces three coupled components. First, a lightweight architecture redesigns down-sampling to reduce token count by 4× while expanding dimensions by only 1.25× (rather than 2×), yielding ~47% FLOPs reduction per stage; token information enhancement via AdaLN before merging and positional encoding supplement after merging mitigate the information loss from compression. Second, the Attention Modulation Matrix (AMM) is a training-free plug-in that converts global self-attention into local attention by element-wise multiplying the attention score matrix with a distance-decay matrix F(d)=0.5·e^(cos(fd)) truncated at an effective radius R, mimicking the coarse-to-fine alternation of global and local attention observed in human sketching; AMM is inserted alternately into decoder blocks. Third, a token relation-enhanced masking training strategy feeds full tokens into shallow EDT blocks and defers the masking operation to the down-sampling modules, forcing the model to learn inter-token relations before compression and avoiding the conflicting L_full/L_masked objectives observed in MDT's input-level masking.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 without classifier-free guidance, EDT achieves FID scores of 34.27 (S), 19.18 (B), and 7.52 (XL), outperforming all transformer-based baselines at every size. EDT reduces GFLOPs by 56–57% compared to MDTv2 (e.g., 51.83 vs. 118.69 GFLOPs for XL) while delivering 2.2–2.3× faster inference (0.51 vs. 0.23 step/s for XL). Training speed is also improved: EDT-XL trains at 0.98 iter/s versus 0.51 iter/s for MDTv2-XL, a 1.92× speedup. On ImageNet 512×512, EDT-S achieves FID 51.84 with 13.25 GFLOPs, matching MDTv2-S (51.16) while using 58% fewer FLOPs and training 3.07× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT [21], EDT wins decisively on FID at all three sizes (34.27 vs. 68.40 for S; 19.18 vs. 43.47 for B; 7.52 vs. 19.47 for XL) while using 56–57% fewer GFLOPs and achieving 2.0–2.3× faster inference. Against MDTv2 [33], EDT also achieves lower FID at every size (34.27 vs. 39.50; 19.18 vs. 19.55; 7.52 vs. 7.70) with dramatically lower compute (2.66 vs. 6.07; 10.20 vs. 23.02; 51.83 vs. 118.69 GFLOPs) and 1.9–3.9× faster training. Against SDDiT [39], EDT outperforms at all sizes (34.27 vs. 48.39; 19.18 vs. 28.62; 7.52 vs. 9.01 FID) under the same 400K×256 training budget. Against the CNN-based ADM [4] and LDM-4 [11], EDT-XL (7.52 FID) surpasses both (10.94 and 10.56 FID) while requiring only 51.83 GFLOPs versus 1120 and 104 GFLOPs respectively, and far fewer training iterations. The only trade-off is that EDT-S with masking training (8.86 iter/s) trains slower than DiT-S (12.50 iter/s), though EDT-S without masking (EDT-S*) is faster (13.20 iter/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

EDT is a direct architectural reworking of DiT, replacing its uniform transformer stack with a U-Net-like encoder-decoder structure that inserts down-sampling and up-sampling modules to reduce token count and FLOPs. The token relation-enhanced masking training strategy adapts MDTv2's masking scheme by relocating the masking operation from the model input into the down-sampling modules, resolving a training-objective conflict the authors identify in MDT. The Attention Modulation Matrix (AMM) is a novel training-free plug-in that modulates global self-attention into distance-weighted local attention, drawing conceptual inspiration from the coarse-to-fine alternation observed in human sketching, but has no direct methodological predecessor in the diffusion transformer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: transformer-based diffusion models (DiT-XL at 118 GFLOPs) are significantly more expensive than their CNN counterparts, limiting deployment. EDT's ~56% FLOPs reduction at XL scale while matching or beating MDTv2's FID is a meaningful efficiency win for practitioners. The AMM's training-free nature makes it immediately applicable to any pre-trained diffusion transformer, though the authors note that optimal AMM placement must be tuned per model and the cosine-based distance function is acknowledged as improvable. Open questions include whether AMM generalizes to text-to-image or video generation settings, and whether the lightweight encoder-decoder structure scales favorably to higher resolutions beyond 512×512.
