## Problem

Recurrent switching linear dynamical systems (rSLDS) offer interpretable, piecewise-linear models of neural dynamics but suffer from three key limitations: they do not provide posterior uncertainty over inferred dynamics, they produce artifactual oscillations near regime boundaries, and their discrete switching formulation imposes no smoothness or continuity on the dynamics. Meanwhile, GP-SDE models with standard kernels (e.g., RBF) capture nonlinear dynamics with uncertainty but yield flow fields that are difficult to interpret. The paper addresses the gap between these two model classes by asking whether one can retain the structured, locally-linear interpretability of rSLDS within the fully probabilistic, continuous-time GP-SDE framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-dimensional neural population activity as either Poisson process spike trains or Gaussian calcium imaging traces, optionally augmented with known external input signals (e.g., stimulus pulses encoded as ±1). Because the latent dynamics are modeled as a continuous-time SDE, observations need not be uniformly sampled. The output is a full posterior distribution over the drift function f(x) at every point in the K-dimensional latent space, from which one can extract the most-likely flow field, posterior credible intervals on dynamics, high-probability fixed-point regions, and the learned decision boundaries (via the partition function π(x)) separating J linear regimes.

## Method

The core contribution is the Smoothly Switching Linear (SSL) kernel, defined as a sum over J regimes of products between a linear kernel κ_lin^(j) (encoding a Bayesian linear regression prior on the dynamics in regime j) and a partition kernel κ_part^(j) = π_j(x)π_j(x') where π(x) is a multiclass logistic regression over latent-space locations. This kernel structure enforces linearity within each regime while smoothly interpolating across decision boundaries controlled by a temperature hyperparameter τ. The full model is a GP-SDE with this kernel, fitted via a modified variational EM algorithm: rather than the standard coordinate-ascent update that alternates between fixing q(u) and gradient-ascending on hyperparameters, the authors maximize a partially optimized ELBO in which the inducing-point posterior q(u) is analytically integrated out before optimizing kernel hyperparameters Θ, circumventing local optima caused by strong q(u)–Θ dependencies. Latent path inference uses a Markov Gaussian process variational posterior with Gauss-Hermite quadrature to approximate the non-closed-form kernel expectations under the SSL kernel.

## Results

On synthetic data (two linear rotational systems), both continuous-time methods (gpSLDS and GP-SDE with RBF kernel) consistently outperform the rSLDS on MSE for both latent trajectories and dynamics across varying numbers of trials (Fig. 2H). On hypothalamic aggression data, all three methods retain similar in-sample forward simulation R² up to 500 steps ahead; beyond that, gpSLDS performs slightly worse than the RBF kernel but still outperforms the rSLDS (Fig. 3D). The gpSLDS additionally recovers the true decision boundary and fixed points on synthetic data, and identifies a high-probability line-attractor region in the hypothalamic data that validates the finding from Nair et al. [27] with posterior uncertainty estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the rSLDS (Linderman et al., AISTATS 2017), the gpSLDS wins on all quantitative metrics: lower MSE on both latents and dynamics for synthetic data, and higher forward simulation R² on hypothalamic data. The rSLDS also produces artifactual oscillations near regime boundaries (Fig. 2G) and cannot provide posterior uncertainty over dynamics, whereas the gpSLDS gives a full posterior distribution at every point in latent space. Against the GP-SDE with RBF kernel (Duncker et al., ICML 2019), the gpSLDS achieves lower dynamics MSE on synthetic data, showing that the correct inductive bias (locally linear structure) outperforms a more flexible prior in data-limited settings. On hypothalamic forward simulation R², the RBF kernel slightly edges out the gpSLDS beyond 500 prediction steps, but the gpSLDS trades a small predictive loss for substantially greater interpretability (piecewise-linear flow fields, identifiable decision boundaries, and fixed-point/line-attractor detection). On the LIP decision-making dataset, the gpSLDS uniquely recovers a learned input-driven axis that separates latents by both motion coherence and choice, a feature neither baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established model families for neural dynamics: the GP-SDE framework of Duncker et al. (ICML 2019), which places a GP prior on continuous-time latent dynamics, and the rSLDS of Linderman et al. (AISTATS 2017), which approximates nonlinear dynamics via discrete switching between linear systems. The core departure is the SSL kernel, which imposes locally linear structure on the GP prior so that the resulting dynamics smoothly interpolate between linear regimes rather than switching discretely. The inference algorithm also departs from standard coordinate-ascent vEM by optimizing kernel hyperparameters on a partially optimized ELBO, exploiting conjugacy to avoid local maxima that arise from strong dependencies between inducing-point posteriors and kernel parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gpSLDS addresses two practical pain points in neural dynamics modeling: rSLDS produces uninterpretable oscillatory artifacts at regime boundaries and cannot express posterior uncertainty over dynamics, while standard GP-SDEs with RBF kernels lack the locally linear structure that makes dynamical features (fixed points, line attractors) directly interpretable. By providing a full posterior over the dynamics function, the method enables probabilistic validation of dynamical features—e.g., quantifying confidence in a line attractor—rather than relying on point estimates. Key limitations include exponential memory scaling with latent dimension (due to Gauss-Hermite quadrature), the need for smaller integration timesteps than rSLDS, and the well-known variational underestimation of posterior variance. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional latent spaces and principled model selection for the number of regimes J and latent dimensionality K.
