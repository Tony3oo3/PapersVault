## Problem

While prior work showed that RBM weight matrices initially align with the principal directions of a dataset, the full learning dynamics—how the model progressively resolves all modes of the data distribution—remained uncharacterized. This paper asks whether the stepwise encoding of features in RBMs corresponds to genuine phase transitions, and if so, what their universality class and critical behavior are.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241765/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary {0,1} vectors of varying dimensionality (HGD: 201–805 features; MNIST: 14×14 to 56×56 pixels; CelebA: 45×45 to 128×128 pixels), resized to create a family of system sizes for finite-size scaling. The RBM's weight matrix W is decomposed via SVD at each training epoch, yielding singular values w_α and left/right singular vectors u_α, ū_α; the key diagnostic outputs are the susceptibility χ_{m_α} = N_v(⟨m_α²⟩−⟨m_α⟩²) along each singular direction, the alignment cosines u_α·η_α with PCA components, and the progressive splitting of generated samples in the (m_1, m_2) plane.

## Method

The paper combines an analytical treatment of a Binary-Gaussian RBM (one or two Gaussian hidden nodes with variance 1/N_v) trained on Mattis-model or two-pattern Hopfield-model data, with numerical training of Binary-Binary RBMs on real datasets via Persistent Contrastive Divergence. The key insight is that the singular value w_α plays the role of inverse temperature in a mean-field Ising model: each mode α undergoes a second-order phase transition (critical exponent γ=1) when w_α crosses a critical value w_c≈4, at which point the RBM's probability measure splits into additional lumps. The cascade proceeds hierarchically—first the center of mass is learned, then each subsequent PCA direction triggers a new transition—so the weight matrix's SVD encodes a progressive refinement of the data's multimodal structure. Finite-size scaling with mean-field exponents (γ=1, ν=1/2, d_u=4) and the observation of hysteresis under an external field confirm the transitions are bona fide critical phenomena in the thermodynamic limit.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that RBM training exhibits a cascade of second-order phase transitions, with the first transition occurring when the leading singular value w₁ reaches a critical point w_c ≈ 4 (for {0,1} variables; w_{1,c} ≈ 4.45 empirically on MNIST). The susceptibility χ_m diverges as (w_c² − w²)⁻¹, confirming a critical exponent γ = 1 consistent with the mean-field (Curie-Weiss/Mattis) universality class. Mean-field finite-size scaling with exponents γ = 1, ν = 1/2, d_u = 4 successfully collapses data across system sizes from 14×14 to 56×56 (MNIST) and 45×45 to 128×128 (CelebA). The cascade is confirmed on three datasets (MNIST, CelebA, Human Genome), with each subsequent mode α showing analogous divergent susceptibility at its own critical singular value, and MCMC relaxation times diverging as predicted by critical slowing-down (z = 2 for local moves).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing generative or clustering methods; its contribution is mechanistic rather than performance-oriented. The primary validation is internal: analytical predictions from the simplified Mattis/Hopfield model (exponential weight growth rates, transition times t_I and t_II, final weight alignment w = √β) are confirmed by numerical training of BG-RBMs and BB-RBMs on synthetic data (Fig. 1, Fig. 5). The paper extends the single-transition analysis of Decelle et al. [8, 9] to a full cascade, showing that the same mean-field critical behavior repeats for each successive mode of the weight matrix. A secondary comparison is between training schemes: PCD-100 (used in the main text) yields clean susceptibility peaks, while CD-10 and Rdm-10 (Fig. 7) produce noisier curves but preserve the overall cascade structure, confirming the transitions are intrinsic to the learning dynamics rather than an artifact of the sampling procedure. The hysteresis loop measurement (Fig. 4-E) provides a direct physical signature of the bimodal measure, analogous to standard first-order transition tests in statistical mechanics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-transition analysis of Decelle et al. [8,9], who showed that RBM learning begins with a paramagnetic-to-condensation phase transition where the weight matrix SVD aligns with the dataset's PCA directions, to a full cascade of second-order phase transitions corresponding to progressive learning of all principal modes. The analytical framework draws on the Mattis and Hopfield models from statistical physics, and the cascade structure parallels earlier observations in Gaussian mixture learning [18,19,20] and teacher-student models [21,22]. The paper also connects to recent work on diffusion models [23,24,25,26] where similar hierarchical mode-creation through spontaneous symmetry breaking has been identified, suggesting a common mechanism across generative model families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of mean-field universality for each transition and the demonstration of critical slowing down at every mode acquisition provides a physical explanation for why MCMC-based training (CD, PCD) becomes prohibitively slow near transitions, with direct practical implications for training clustered or high-dimensional datasets. The finite-size scaling validation on real datasets (MNIST, CelebA, HGD) confirms these are bona fide phase transitions rather than finite-size crossovers. Open questions include whether the cascade structure persists in deeper architectures (Deep Boltzmann Machines, convolutional EBMs) where parameter decomposition is less clear, and whether the temporary PCA alignment observed during early training can be exploited for initialization strategies that bypass the costly initial transitions.
