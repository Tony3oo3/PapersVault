## Problem

Existing transferable federated learning methods either violate data privacy by sharing representations or style statistics, suffer from local overfitting by relying solely on local structures, or incur high communication costs by exchanging full gradients. More fundamentally, they optimize transferability within local client domains rather than across clients, and lack direct theoretical bounds on the target-domain loss of the pretrained federated model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB (or grayscale) images at standard resolutions (28×28 for MNIST/MNIST-M, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, variable for DomainNet) and outputs class labels. During federated pretraining, each client holds a non-iid subset of source-domain images with labels; the unusual communication detail is that clients transmit only a single scalar (their surrogate Jacobian norm) alongside the model weights, and the server broadcasts back a single guide norm, keeping communication overhead negligible compared to gradient-exchange baselines.

## Method

FedGTST introduces a local regularizer L_D(h) + ξ·(‖J^(k)(h)‖₂ − γ)² that aligns each client's Jacobian norm with a server-broadcast guide norm γ, simultaneously reducing cross-client Jacobian variance σ² and boosting the averaged Jacobian norm ‖J_p‖. To prevent the regularizer from suppressing norm growth, a small subset of clients (10%) additionally performs standard training and transmits the maximum of the two resulting norms as a surrogate. The server sets γ to the maximum received surrogate norm each round, creating a positive feedback loop that enlarges ‖J_p‖ while the alignment term keeps σ² small. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 2) proves that the target loss is upper-bounded by a term decreasing in ‖J_p‖² and increasing in σ², with an optimal per-round learning rate λ* = K‖J_p‖²/(α·Σ‖J_p^(k)‖²), establishing that these two cross-client statistics are the direct, tunable levers for global transferability.

## Results

FedGTST achieves the highest target accuracy across all transfer tasks, system sizes (K=10, 50, 100), and backbone architectures (LeNet, ResNet-18). On CIFAR-10→SVHN with K=100 clients and LeNet, FedGTST reaches 52.4% target accuracy, outperforming FedIIR (44.8%) by 7.6% and FedSR (42.6%) by 9.8%. On MNIST→MNIST-M with K=50 and ResNet-18, FedGTST achieves 79.2%, surpassing FedIIR (73.9%) and FedSR (73.1%) by approximately 5%. The performance advantage grows with system size, with FedGTST's average lead over FedIIR expanding from ~1.5% (K=10) to ~6.8% (K=100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGTST consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task, system size, and backbone. Against FedAVG (the standard federated averaging baseline), FedGTST leads by 4.6–9.5% in average target accuracy depending on system size, with the gap widening as K increases from 10 to 100. Against FedSR, which applies centralized invariant feature learning locally, FedGTST wins by 2.6–9.3%, demonstrating that FL-specific cross-client statistics tuning is more effective than purely local regularization. Against FedIIR—the closest prior method, which the authors describe as a special case of FedGTST—FedGTST achieves 1.5–6.8% higher accuracy while communicating only scalar Jacobian norms rather than full gradient vectors, reducing communication overhead. Against Scaffold (an advanced FL method for data heterogeneity), FedGTST leads by 2.5–6.1% on average, confirming that transferability-specific tuning complements rather than conflicts with heterogeneity-aware aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transferable federated learning (TFL) line initiated by FedSR, FedIIR, FedADG, and others, which adapt centralized domain-generalization techniques to the federated setting. It departs from these methods by identifying that they either violate privacy (FedCCST, StableFDG), overfit locally (FedSR, FedCDG), or incur high communication costs (FedIIR, FedMM). The key conceptual departure is the use of cross-client Jacobian norm and variance as the primary tuning signals, requiring only scalar communication, and the theoretical insight that reducing variance alone (as in FedIIR) can impede Jacobian norm growth and thus degrade transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGTST provides the first direct theoretical bound on the target-domain loss of a federated pretrained model in terms of cross-client statistics, moving beyond indirect transferability proxies like feature invariance. The practical implication is a privacy-preserving, low-communication protocol that consistently improves transfer accuracy by 4–10% over strong baselines across system sizes (10–100 clients) and backbones. Open questions include whether the single-step local update assumption can be relaxed, whether the bounds can be tightened, and whether the approach extends to multi-step local training or non-convex losses without the Lipschitz-smoothness assumption.
