## Problem

Message-passing GNNs suffer from under-reaching (limited receptive fields) and over-squashing (information bottlenecks in deep stacks), while graph transformers that address these issues incur quadratic complexity in the number of nodes. Existing probabilistic graph rewiring methods, such as PR-MPNN, also require scoring all n² node pairs, making them equally impractical for large graphs. The paper asks whether one can learn end-to-end, task-adaptive graph rewiring that enables long-range information flow without quadratic overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}) and augments it with m virtual nodes (m ≪ n, typically 2–8 in experiments). Each original node is assigned to exactly k virtual nodes via a sampled binary assignment matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m}, where k is a small integer (often 1). The downstream model then produces graph-level predictions (classification or molecular property regression), node-level labels, or link-prediction scores depending on the task, with the virtual node embeddings optionally included in the readout.

## Method

IPR-MPNNs introduce a small set of m virtual nodes and learn, end-to-end, which original nodes connect to which virtual nodes. An upstream MPNN produces unnormalized priors θ ∈ R^{n×m}; from these, exactly-k edges per node are sampled using the SIMPLE gradient estimator for k-subset constraints, yielding a binary assignment matrix H. The augmented graph is then processed by a downstream MPNN with a three-stage message-passing scheme: (1) original nodes aggregate into virtual nodes, (2) virtual nodes exchange messages over a complete graph, and (3) virtual nodes distribute information back to original nodes alongside standard neighborhood aggregation. Because m is a small constant, the overall complexity is O(|E| + n·m + m²) rather than O(n²), and the upstream and downstream models are jointly optimized via backpropagation through the exactly-k marginals. Theoretically, the paper proves IPR-MPNNs exceed 1-WL expressiveness while preserving isomorphisms between almost all isomorphic graph pairs with arbitrarily high probability.

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark, IPR-MPNN achieves the best performance across all five metrics: PEPTIDES-FUNC (0.7210), PEPTIDES-STRUCT (0.2422, lower is better), and PCQM-CONTACT under three evaluation protocols (0.3670, 0.3846, 0.4756). On QM9, IPR-MPNN obtains the best result on 12 of 13 molecular properties (e.g., U0: 0.74 vs. 1.01 for DREW-GIN; ALPHA: 1.36 vs. 1.63 for DREW-GIN). On ZINC, IPR-MPNN achieves 0.067 MAE, outperforming GPS (0.070) and PR-MPNN (0.084), while on OGB-MOLHIV it matches GPS at 0.788 accuracy. In terms of efficiency, IPR-MPNN trains in 2.98 s/epoch with 1.9 GB memory, compared to GPS's 7.81 s/epoch and 22.2 GB, while remaining close to the base GINE model (2.68 s/epoch, 1.7 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPR-MPNN outperforms the base GINE model across all evaluated benchmarks, with the largest gains on long-range tasks (e.g., +0.059 on PEPTIDES-FUNC, +0.016 on PCQM⁽¹⁾). Against the GPS graph transformer, IPR-MPNN wins on PEPTIDES-FUNC (0.7210 vs. 0.6535), all three PCQM-CONTACT metrics, and ZINC (0.067 vs. 0.070), while matching GPS on OGB-MOLHIV (0.788 vs. 0.788); critically, IPR-MPNN is ~2.6× faster in training and uses 11.7× less memory than GPS. Compared to the closest prior rewiring method PR-MPNN, IPR-MPNN achieves better results on PEPTIDES-FUNC (0.7210 vs. 0.6825), ZINC (0.067 vs. 0.084), and OGB-MOLHIV (0.788 vs. 0.795), while also avoiding PR-MPNN's quadratic complexity. Against DREW, IPR-MPNN wins on PEPTIDES-FUNC (0.7210 vs. 0.7150) and all PCQM-CONTACT metrics, though DREW is slightly better on PEPTIDES-STRUCT (0.2536 vs. 0.2422 — actually IPR-MPNN wins here too). On the TU datasets, IPR-MPNN achieves the best or near-best results on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., NCI1: 86.2% vs. 85.6% for PR-MPNN; PROTEINS: 85.4% vs. 80.7% for PR-MPNN), with PR-MPNN slightly ahead on MUTAG (98.4% vs. 98.0%). On heterophilic node classification (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin), IPR-MPNN outperforms GPS by 4.6, 3.5, and 0.6 percentage points respectively, and substantially outperforms the base GINE (e.g., Cornell: 76.4% vs. 44.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

IPR-MPNNs directly extend the PR-MPNN framework of Qian et al. (2023), which used differentiable k-subset sampling (SIMPLE, Ahmed et al. 2023) to learn edge rewiring end-to-end but required modeling an explicit n² edge distribution. The key conceptual departure is replacing this explicit pairwise scoring with a small set of m virtual nodes (m ≪ n), reducing the parameter space from n² to n·m and the worst-case complexity from quadratic to near-linear. This positions IPR-MPNNs as a bridge between local message-passing architectures and global attention mechanisms (graph transformers like GPS, SAT), inheriting the differentiable sampling machinery of PR-MPNN while adopting the implicit connectivity philosophy of hierarchical/supernode MPNNs (e.g., Fey et al. 2020, Rosenbluth et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quadratic complexity of graph transformers is not a fundamental requirement for capturing long-range dependencies: a handful of learned virtual nodes can match or exceed transformer performance while using an order of magnitude less memory (1.9 GB vs. 22.2 GB for GPS). This opens a practical path for deploying expressive graph models on large-scale molecular and social-network data where transformers are infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach extends well to node-level tasks (the authors acknowledge this gap), how the method behaves when m must grow with n, and whether the virtual-node abstraction can be combined with other expressiveness-enhancing mechanisms (e.g., higher-order WL, topological features) without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
