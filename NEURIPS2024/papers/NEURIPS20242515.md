## Problem

Training energy-based models on discrete or mixed state spaces is challenging because contrastive divergence relies on MCMC sampling that is slow, biased, and lacks theoretical guarantees. The recently proposed Energy Discrepancy loss eliminates MCMC but was restricted to Gaussian perturbations on continuous spaces, leaving no principled way to define structure-aware perturbations on discrete variables. This paper bridges that gap by constructing discrete perturbations via heat equations on graph-structured state spaces, enabling MCMC-free EBM training on tabular and binary image data.

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete data points (categorical variables with S states per dimension), tabular data mixing numerical and categorical columns, or binary images, and produces a learned energy function U_θ usable for density estimation, synthetic sample generation via Gibbs/Langevin dynamics, and calibrated classification. The perturbation is controlled by a single continuous time parameter t, with the rate matrix encoding the assumed structure (uniform, cyclical, or ordinal) of each discrete variable; for tabular data, numerical features receive Gaussian perturbation while categorical features receive the discrete heat equation perturbation. A 'grid' variant further localizes noise to one randomly selected dimension to reduce variance in high-dimensional categorical spaces.

## Method

The core contribution is defining perturbations on discrete state spaces as continuous-time Markov chains whose rate matrices are graph Laplacians (R = A − D_out) encoding the structure of each discrete variable—uniform (fully connected graph), cyclical (ring graph), or ordinal (path graph). The heat equation ∂_t q_t = R q_t is solved analytically via eigenvalue decomposition (discrete Fourier transform for cyclical/ordinal structures), yielding closed-form transition probabilities that are precomputed and applied in parallel without sequential MCMC steps. The Energy Discrepancy loss is extended to discrete spaces: given data point x, a perturbed point y ~ q_t(·|x), and M negative samples x_− ~ q_t(·|y), the training objective is L = (1/N)Σ log(w + Σ_j exp(U(x^i) − U(x_−^{i,j}))) − log(M), requiring only energy evaluations. Theorem 1 proves ED converges to maximum likelihood at an exponential rate governed by the spectral gap |λ_2(R)|, and Theorem 2 establishes a Gaussian scaling limit justifying the quadratic time scaling t = S²t_base across different cardinalities. For mixed tabular data, the perturbation is a product of Gaussian noise on numerical features and the discrete heat equation on categorical features, with an optional grid perturbation that localizes noise to a single randomly chosen dimension.

## Results

On 6 real-world tabular datasets, TabED-Str achieves the best average rank (3.67) among ED variants, outperforming CTGAN (6.33) and TVAE (5.17) but trailing TabDDPM (1.5). On binary image modelling, ED-Grid achieves NLL of 90.61 on Static MNIST and 93.94 on Omniglot, competitive with DULA (80.71/145.68) and GWG (80.01/94.72) while requiring only M parallel energy evaluations instead of sequential MCMC steps. On discrete density estimation (32-dim binary), ED-Grid achieves the best NLL on 8gaussians (19.965) and swissroll (20.088), and the best MMD on 2spirals (0.097×10⁻⁴) and 8gaussians (−0.066×10⁻⁴), outperforming PCD, ALOE+, and EB-GFN in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On tabular data, TabED variants (avg. rank 3.67–4.83) clearly outperform CTGAN (6.33) and TVAE (5.17) in average ranking, and dramatically outperform TabCD (8.83), which uses short-run MCMC contrastive divergence. However, TabDDPM (1.5) remains the strongest baseline, achieving higher AUC on most datasets (e.g., 0.910 vs. 0.884 on Adult). On binary image modelling, ED-Grid (NLL 90.61 on Static MNIST) trails GWG (80.01) and DULA (80.71) by ~10 NLL points, but surpasses both on Omniglot (93.94 vs. 94.72 for GWG and 145.68 for DULA). On discrete density estimation, ED methods match or beat PCD, ALOE+, and EB-GFN on NLL in most of 7 settings and achieve the best MMD on 4 of 7 datasets, without requiring MCMC or auxiliary variational networks. The paper notes that ED's advantage over CD is most pronounced in capturing multi-modal energy landscapes, while CD with many MCMC steps can still outperform ED on high-dimensional binary images where the data manifold assumption is violated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Energy Discrepancy (Schröder et al., 2023), originally defined for continuous spaces with Gaussian perturbations, to discrete and mixed state spaces by modelling perturbations as continuous-time Markov chains whose rate matrices are graph Laplacians encoding structural priors (cyclical, ordinal, uniform). It draws on the discrete diffusion literature (Campbell et al., 2022; Lou et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2023b) for the CTMC framework but repurposes it not as a generative process but as a perturbation mechanism for a contrastive loss, thereby bypassing the MCMC sampling that dominates prior discrete EBM training (CD, PCD, ALOE+, EB-GFN). The connection to pseudo-likelihood estimation and KL-contraction divergences (Lyu, 2011) positions ED as a unifying lens over several existing contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first robust, MCMC-free training procedure for EBMs on tabular data with mixed numerical and categorical features, opening a practical route to calibrated classification and synthetic data generation in domains where GANs and VAEs struggle. The theoretical guarantee (exponential convergence to MLE at a rate governed by the spectral gap of the rate matrix) provides a principled way to tune perturbation strength without empirical grid search. Key limitations include sensitivity to the positivity assumption on the state space and a performance gap versus long-run MCMC on high-dimensional binary images where the manifold hypothesis is violated; extending the framework to richer structures (molecules, text, networks) and to imputation with confidence bounds remains open.
