## Problem

Despite the proliferation of unsupervised graph domain adaptation (UGDA) methods, the field lacks standard experimental settings, fair comparisons, and a systematic understanding of when and why different models succeed or fail. Existing evaluations use inconsistent datasets, data processing, and partitioning strategies, making cross-method comparisons unreliable, while the role of GNN aggregation mechanisms in transferability remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242837/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a labeled source graph (node features, adjacency matrix, class labels) and an unlabeled target graph (node features, adjacency matrix) from 5 real-world datasets spanning 74 source-target pairs, with shifts in node attributes, graph structure, and label proportions. The primary output is node-level class predictions evaluated via Micro-F1, Macro-F1, or AUROC depending on label imbalance; a secondary graph-level classification task uses pooled representations on TU datasets. Notably, the Airport dataset contains no node features (structure-only), and the MAG dataset involves 20-class venue prediction at scale (~100K nodes per domain).

## Method

GDABench provides a unified evaluation framework (PyGDA library) implementing 16 SOTA UGDA methods under consistent data processing and splitting, organized into three families: DA-incorporated node embedding, structure-shift-tailored alignment, and domain-adaptive message passing. To probe GNN transferability, the authors construct SimGDA by combining a one-layer GCN baseline with six single-module variants (sum/mean/max/GAT/GIN aggregators, no-neighbor, multi-hop) crossed with two alignment strategies (MMD, DANN), yielding 14 models. SimGDA+ further augments these with three unsupervised target-graph techniques (information maximization, graph autoencoder, graph contrastive learning) in an end-to-end fashion, producing 42 combined models. The key empirical finding is that source-unbiased discriminative aggregation (GCN's degree-aware sum) with appropriate hop scope, combined with unsupervised learning, allows simple GNN variants to match or surpass specialized UGDA methods, revealing that GNNs' inherent adaptability is largely underestimated by existing approaches.

## Results

Across 74 source-target adaptation pairs on 5 datasets, SimGDA+ (a simple GCN + MMD/DANN + unsupervised techniques) achieves top or near-top performance in most tasks, e.g., 58.11 Micro-F1 on Airport E→U (vs. 50.64 for A2GNN, the best specialized method) and 57.04 on Blog B1→B2 (vs. 56.93 for ACDNE). On ArnetMiner C→A, A2GNN leads with 76.15 Micro-F1, while SimGDA+ follows at 73.18. On the large-scale MAG dataset (Macro-F1), A2GNN achieves the best scores (26.20 on FR→JP, 25.78 on JP→FR), with SimGDA+ trailing by ~4 points. Overall, no single method dominates all 12 evaluated tasks, and performance gaps between the best and worst methods exceed 30 points on some tasks (e.g., MAG JP→RU: 16.94 for A2GNN vs. 9.35 for GRADE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine DA+node-embedding methods, ACDNE and DMGNN are the strongest on mild-shift datasets (Airport, Blog, ArnetMiner), with ACDNE reaching 56.93 on Blog B2→B1 and DMGNN hitting 70.68 on ArnetMiner D→A; however, both collapse on heavy-shift datasets (e.g., ACDNE drops to 13.91 Macro-F1 on MAG JP→RU, DMGNN to 10.28). The four structure-shift methods (StruRW, JHGDA, KBL, PairAlign) show modest gains over DA+embedding baselines on structure-heavy tasks (e.g., StruRW 59.60 AUROC on Twitch DE→ES vs. DANE's 56.71) but remain inconsistent, with JHGDA and PairAlign frequently ranking in the bottom half. The three domain-adaptive message-passing methods (GRADE, SpecReg, A2GNN) are the most robust across shift types: A2GNN achieves the best ArnetMiner scores (76.15 C→A, 74.12 D→A) and the best MAG scores (26.20 FR→JP, 25.78 JP→FR), while SpecReg excels on MAG DE→FR (30.80). SimGDA+ outperforms all 16 specialized baselines on Airport (both directions) and Blog B1→B2, and is competitive within 1–4 points on most other tasks, demonstrating that a simple GCN with MMD/DANN alignment plus unsupervised target-side learning can match or exceed purpose-built UGDA architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first comprehensive benchmark for Unsupervised Graph Domain Adaptation (UGDA), building on three distinct lineages: classical domain adaptation techniques (MMD, DANN) originally designed for i.i.d. data, specialized graph DA methods that incorporate adversarial or structural alignment into GNN encoders (DANE, ACDNE, StruRW, PairAlign), and domain-adaptive message passing approaches that exploit GNN architecture properties (GRADE, SpecReg, A2GNN). Rather than proposing a new algorithm, the paper's conceptual contribution is decoupling GNN aggregation into 'how to aggregate' (sum, mean, GAT, GIN) and 'what to aggregate' (no-neighbor, one-hop, multi-hop) to systematically probe the inherent transferability of GNNs under distribution shift. The SimGDA/SimGDA+ framework repurposes vanilla DA alignment (MMD, DANN) combined with unsupervised graph learning (information maximization, autoencoders, contrastive learning) as a controlled baseline to isolate the role of aggregation mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDABench and the PyGDA library provide the first standardized, reproducible evaluation platform for UGDA, addressing the field's chronic lack of fair comparisons across 16 methods, 5 datasets, and 74 adaptation pairs. The finding that aggregation scope and architecture (not elaborate domain-specific modules) are the primary determinants of GNN transferability reframes how the community should think about designing UGDA methods. Open questions remain around source-free and open-set scenarios, scalability to larger heterogeneous graphs, and whether the conclusions hold under more extreme distribution shifts. The benchmark's limitation to node-level classification on relatively small-to-medium graphs (except MAG) means generalization to graph-level or link-level tasks is still underexplored.
