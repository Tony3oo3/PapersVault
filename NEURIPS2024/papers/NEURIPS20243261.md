## Problem

Under μP and Depth-μP parameterizations, the optimal learning rate transfers across model widths and depths, implying the loss landscape must be consistently similar across very different scales. However, the specific landscape properties that remain invariant across model size—and why they do so—were unexplored, leaving the mechanism behind hyperparameter transfer as a 'theoretical puzzle.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes residual neural networks (ConvNets, ResNets, ViTs, GPT-2) parameterized under different scaling schemes (μP, NTP, SP, Depth-μP) and trained with SGD or Adam at multiple widths and depths on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Tiny-ImageNet, and WikiText. It produces time-resolved trajectories of the preconditioned Hessian's top eigenvalues, NTK eigenvalues, and training loss, from which it extracts whether each quantity is super consistent (width-independent over sustained training) or accumulates finite-size effects, and quantifies divergence rates via power-law fits.

## Method

The paper defines Super Consistency as the property that a landscape quantity S_N(t) remains close to its infinite-width limit S_∞(t) without accumulating finite-size effects over training, and shows that under μP/Depth-μP the top Hessian eigenvalues (sharpness) satisfy this property, converging to a width-independent threshold (often the Edge of Stability at 2/η₀) and oscillating around it, while loss and NTK eigenvalues do not. Using the Gauss-Newton decomposition H = G + R, the paper connects progressive sharpening to NTK evolution: under μP the NTK evolves (feature learning) driving the sharpness to a width-independent value, whereas under NTP the NTK is frozen at initialization, preventing the Hessian from adapting. A two-layer linear network analysis (Theorem 5.1) proves that under μP the latent-space dynamics of (w, e, v) are exactly width-independent at any finite width, with only the initialization carrying a 1/√N perturbation, while under NTP the e and v dynamics slow as 1/N. Empirical validation spans ConvNets, ResNets, ViTs, and GPT-2 across multiple datasets and optimizers, confirming that super consistent sharpness dynamics correlate precisely with learning rate transfer, and that suboptimal feature-learning parameterizations (e.g., Depth-μP with multiple layers per block) violate Super Consistency and fail to transfer.

## Results

Under μP, the top Hessian eigenvalues (sharpness) converge to a width-independent threshold near the Edge of Stability (2/η₀) and remain there throughout training, with the finite-size deviation g(t) approaching 0 over time. In contrast, the training loss accumulates finite-size effects with a power-law divergence rate β > 0.6, and the top NTK eigenvalue diverges quadratically (β ≈ 2) from the infinite-width proxy. Under NTP, the sharpness progressively diminishes with increasing width and never reaches the EoS threshold, and the optimal learning rate does not transfer. The learning rate η₀ = 0.7 transfers exactly across widths (e.g., 128 to 4096) for ConvNets on CIFAR-10 under μP, while no single learning rate works across widths under NTP.

**Summary of Super Consistency and learning rate transfer across parameterizations.**

| Parameterization | Sharpness Super Consistent? | Learning Rate Transfer? | Sharpness Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| μP (width) | Yes | Yes | Width-independent threshold ≈ 2/η₀ |
| Depth-μP, k=1 (depth) | Yes | Yes | Width/depth-independent, near EoS |
| Depth-μP, k>1 (ViT, multi-layer blocks) | No | No | Accumulates finite-size effects |
| NTP (width) | No | No | Sharpness diminishes with width |
| Standard Param (SP) | No (no limit) | No | Diverges with width |
| μP without 1/√L residual scaling | No | No | Sharpness explodes with depth |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the NTK parameterization (Jacot et al. [4]), μP exhibits fundamentally different sharpness dynamics: under NTP the top Hessian eigenvalue progressively decreases as width increases (consistent with Cohen et al. [16]'s observation for MSE loss), and no learning rate transfers across widths; under μP the sharpness is width-independent and η₀ transfers exactly. Against Standard Parameterization (Yang & Hu [5]), the paper shows SP lacks a well-defined infinite-width limit, so the sharpness diverges with width and Super Consistency is trivially violated. Relative to the Edge of Stability framework (Cohen et al. [16]), the paper confirms that under μP the sharpness oscillates around the 2/η₀ threshold (the EoS), but extends this by showing the threshold itself is width-independent—a property absent under NTP where the EoS is never reached at large width. The Gauss-Newton decomposition (Martens [12]) is used to show that the progressive sharpening phase is driven by the NTK's largest eigenvalue, which evolves under μP but is frozen under NTP, providing the mechanistic explanation for the difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of scaling-limit theory (μP, Depth-μP, NTK) and optimization landscape analysis (Hessian spectrum, Edge of Stability). It builds directly on the consistency framework of Vyas et al. [9] by introducing a stronger property—Super Consistency—that requires not only convergence to an infinite-width limit but also the absence of finite-size accumulation over time. The Gauss-Newton decomposition from Martens [12] serves as the key analytical bridge connecting the Hessian's largest eigenvalue to the NTK spectrum, enabling the paper to attribute sharpness dynamics to the presence or absence of feature learning. The Edge of Stability framework of Cohen et al. [16] provides the threshold around which the super-consistent sharpness oscillates under μP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a landscape-level mechanistic explanation for why learning rate transfer succeeds under μP/Depth-μP but fails under NTP, SP, or multi-layer-per-block parameterizations: the sharpness must be super consistent (width-independent and not accumulating finite-size effects). This gives practitioners a diagnostic tool—monitoring sharpness dynamics—to predict whether a given parameterization will support transfer. Open questions include whether Super Consistency holds at even larger scales (limited by Hessian computation cost), the precise role of Self-Stabilization in maintaining the sharpness attractor after progressive sharpening, and whether the two-layer linear theory extends to the full training trajectory at finite time. The finding that EoS is not strictly necessary for transfer (Depth-μP with k=1 shows transfer without reaching EoS) also refines the optimization-theoretic picture.
