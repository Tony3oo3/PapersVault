## Problem

Consistency models, as efficient single-step diffusion policies, have shown promise in state-based online RL, but it remains unclear whether they can be extended to visual RL with high-dimensional image observations. The paper identifies that the Q-loss in the actor-critic framework causes a rapid increase in the dormant ratio of the consistency policy network, collapsing its expressive ability and destabilizing training—a problem that is dramatically worse in visual RL where most random seeds fail entirely.

## Input / Output

The agent receives raw RGB image observations from simulated environments (DeepMind Control Suite and Meta-world) and outputs a continuous action vector. Images are processed through a CNN encoder (inherited from DrQ-v2) with random-shift augmentation, and the consistency policy generates actions in a single forward pass from Gaussian noise conditioned on the encoded state. No auxiliary losses, explicit exploration noise, or additional representation learning objectives are required beyond the base DrQ-v2 augmentation pipeline.

## Method

CP3ER replaces the Gaussian actor in DrQ-v2 with a consistency model and introduces two key stabilizing mechanisms. First, it adds a sample-based entropy regularization term (the consistency loss) to the Q-loss, which constrains the policy toward a proxy distribution without requiring a closed-form entropy—making it tractable for generative policies. Second, it proposes Prioritized Proximal Experience (PPE) regularization, a weighted replay-buffer sampling scheme where the sampling weight follows a sigmoid of the temporal distance to the current step, so that the proxy distribution stays close to the current policy, balancing stability and sample efficiency. The critic is upgraded from DrQ-v2's deterministic double Q-network to a single mixture-of-Gaussians value distribution (3 components, 20 target samples), and the natural multimodality of the consistency model eliminates the need for separate exploration strategies.

## Results

CP3ER achieves new SOTA on all 21 visual control tasks across DeepMind Control Suite (8 medium + 7 hard) and Meta-world (6 tasks), outperforming DrQ-v2, ALIX, TACO, and DrM on mean, IQM, median, and optimal gap metrics with lower variance. On hard-level DMC tasks trained for only 5M frames, CP3ER is the only method to learn effective policies on humanoid tasks, significantly outperforming DrM. On Meta-world, CP3ER reaches nearly 100% success rate within 2M steps across all 6 manipulation tasks. On state-based DMC tasks (Table 2), CP3ER achieves an average return of 665.5, surpassing CPQL (606.3), DreamerV3 (509.6), and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DrQ-v2, ALIX, and TACO, CP3ER consistently outperforms on all 21 visual tasks with lower variance, and notably does so without any auxiliary representation losses or additional exploration strategies (TACO uses action/state representation losses). On hard-level DMC tasks (5M frames), DrQ-v2, ALIX, and TACO all fail to learn meaningful policies, while CP3ER achieves strong performance. Against DrM (the prior SOTA on hard tasks), CP3ER matches performance on 4 dog tasks (dog-run, dog-stand, dog-trot, dog-walk) without any exploration strategy, and significantly outperforms DrM on the 3 humanoid tasks (humanoid-run, humanoid-stand, humanoid-walk) with a >70% probability of improvement. Against Consistency-AC (the direct consistency-policy baseline), CP3ER achieves a 36% average improvement on state-based tasks (6403.2 vs 4701.9), with the largest gain on Halfcheetah-m (10699 vs 6725). The ablation confirms that removing PPE regularization causes the consistency policy to fail entirely, while using a uniform proxy (MaxEnt) or unweighted replay buffer (URB) both underperform the prioritized proximal sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency policy lineage (Ding & Jin, ICLR 2024; Chen et al., AAMAS 2024) from state-based RL to visual RL, building on the consistency model's single-step noise-to-data mapping (Song et al., ICML 2023) and the DrQ-v2 visual RL framework (Yarats et al., ICLR 2022). The key departure is a diagnostic finding: prior work assumed consistency models could be directly plugged into the actor-critic Q-loss framework, but this paper shows that Q-loss specifically (not non-stationary data) causes dormant-neuron collapse in the policy network, which is catastrophic in high-dimensional visual settings. To fix this, the authors introduce sample-based entropy regularization via a consistency loss with a prioritized proximal experience (PPE) proxy distribution, and a mixture-of-Gaussians critic following Shahriari et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method to apply diffusion/consistency models to visual RL, CP3ER demonstrates that expressive generative policies can outperform Gaussian policies on 21 visual control tasks without auxiliary representation losses or explicit exploration strategies, suggesting a viable path toward more expressive visual RL. The dormant-ratio diagnostic provides a reusable tool for understanding policy collapse in generative-policy RL more broadly. However, the paper acknowledges that policy diversity depends on the replay buffer's multimodal actions, which diminish over training, and that no theoretical convergence guarantees exist; on-policy consistency policy and diversity-preserving mechanisms remain open.
