## Problem

Offline RL agents trained on visual data are brittle to minor visual variations (background, camera viewpoint) that are irrelevant to control, yet no existing dataset simultaneously provides the task diversity, distractor types, policy quality levels, state-pixel pairs, scale, and hidden-goal settings needed to rigorously evaluate whether representation learning methods actually close this robustness gap. DMC-VB fills this gap as the first offline RL dataset satisfying all six desiderata, and its three benchmarks reveal that current pretraining methods (ID, LFD, AE, DINO) do not improve over simple end-to-end behavioral cloning.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes either stacked RGB frames (3 consecutive 64×64 images, padded by 4 pixels then randomly cropped back to 64×64; for ant-maze tasks this is 3 views × 3 frames = 9 images per timestep) or a privileged state vector (17-dim for cheetah up to 167-dim for ant maze, including proprioceptive and exteroceptive measurements). The output is a continuous control action in [-1, 1] produced by an MLP policy network. Frame stacking of 3 is critical because agent velocity is not inferable from a single frame, and the ant-maze tasks additionally require a follow-up camera view to recover proprioceptive state from pixels.

## Method

DMC-VB is generated by training MPO agents on states for 2M steps, selecting checkpoints at different skill levels (expert at 95% convergence reward, medium at 50%, mixed at 8 evenly-spaced checkpoints between 0–50%, random), and rolling out each policy for 1M steps while collecting actions, rewards, states, and visual observations with distractors from Kubric (falling-object videos/images for backgrounds, random camera poses, variable agent colors). The three benchmarks use a two-stage procedure: (B1) pretrains a CNN encoder (4 conv layers, 32→256 channels, 64-dim output) with ID, LFD, AE, DINO, or BC, then freezes it and trains an MLP policy with BC or TD3-BC, comparing against state-based upper bounds to quantify the representation gap; (B2) pretrains on full mixed-quality data then fine-tunes the policy on only 1% expert data (20 trajectories); (B3) pretrains on ant-maze tasks with stochastic hidden goals (goal sphere invisible) then evaluates few-shot policy learning on fixed-goal tasks. The key empirical finding is that NULL+BC (end-to-end BC with no pretraining) is the best overall method, and the representation gap between pixel-trained and state-trained policies widens dramatically under dynamic distractors.

## Results

On DMC-VB locomotion tasks (expert, no distractors), NULL+BC achieves 948±4 on walker, 861±7 on cheetah, and 587±27 on humanoid, matching or exceeding all pretraining methods. With dynamic distractors, the representation gap widens dramatically: on cheetah-expert, NULL+BC drops to 329±28 while BC(state) maintains 492±35, and on humanoid-expert, NULL+BC drops to 82±9 versus 47±6 for BC(state). LFD+BC is consistently the worst pretraining method (e.g., 59±7 on walker-expert-none vs. 948±4 for NULL+BC), while AE+BC and DINO+BC achieve moderate scores that collapse under distractors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NULL+BC (behavioral cloning without pretraining) is the best overall method, outperforming or matching all five pretraining baselines across all locomotion and navigation tasks. TD3-BC [Fujimoto & Gu, NeurIPS 2021] as NULL+TD3-BC is outperformed by NULL+BC in most settings and never exceeds it (e.g., 950±68 vs. 948±4 on walker-expert-none, but 364±50 vs. 587±27 on humanoid-expert-none). Inverse Dynamics [Islam et al., ICML 2023] as ID+BC matches NULL+BC on walker (947 vs. 948) but falls behind on cheetah (824 vs. 861) and humanoid (428 vs. 587) without distractors, and remains competitive under dynamic distractors. Latent Forward Dynamics [Guo et al., NeurIPS 2022] as LFD+BC is consistently the worst method, achieving only 59±7 on walker-expert-none versus 948±4 for NULL+BC, and never reaches the goal in ant-maze tasks. DINO [Caron et al., ICCV 2021] and AutoEncoder [Yarats et al., AAAI 2021] pretraining yield moderate scores without distractors (594 and 932 on walker) but collapse under dynamic distractors (33 and 294 respectively), confirming that generative or contrastive representations fail to discard control-irrelevant visual information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first offline RL dataset to simultaneously satisfy six desiderata for evaluating representation learning for control: task diversity, distractor diversity, multiple policy skill levels, paired state-and-pixel observations, large scale (1M steps, 10× VD4RL), and hidden-goal tasks. Conceptually, it builds on the EX-BMDP framework [13] that factorizes state into control-relevant (endogenous) and control-irrelevant (exogenous) variables, and repurposes existing representation learning objectives (inverse dynamics, latent forward dynamics, autoencoders, DINO) as benchmark methods rather than proposing new ones. The most direct predecessor is VD4RL [33], which collected visual distractor data for three locomotion tasks but lacked states, was 10× smaller, and did not include hidden-goal or multi-policy-level variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that none of the tested pretrained representations (ID, LFD, AE, DINO) improve over end-to-end behavioral cloning—challenges a core assumption in the field that better visual pretraining should transfer to better control policies, and exposes a large, persistent representation gap between pixel-based and state-based agents under dynamic distractors. Practically, the benchmark provides the first systematic tool to measure this gap and to test whether future representation methods can close it. Key limitations include the synthetic nature of distractors (Kubric videos, texture changes), restriction to locomotion and simple navigation, and absence of sparse rewards or multi-agent settings, leaving open whether findings generalize to real-world robotics. The positive finding that mixed-quality pretraining and stochastic hidden-goal pretraining help few-shot policy learning offers a concrete, actionable direction for leveraging suboptimal data.
