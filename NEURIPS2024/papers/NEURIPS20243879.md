## Problem

Advanced text-to-image models (Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Stable Diffusion 3) keep their parameters private and expose only fee-based APIs, making it impractical to replicate their capabilities. Naively training a base model on 10M+ API-generated image-text pairs is prohibitively expensive in time, compute, and API costs. The paper asks whether a VLM-guided dynamic data curation strategy can reduce the required data volume by orders of magnitude while still matching or exceeding the advanced models' generation quality.

## Input / Output

At inference, the trained model Edgen takes a free-form text prompt and produces an RGB image at user-specified resolution (512px or 1024px) and aspect ratio, supporting multi-object scenes, in-image text rendering, and photorealistic or stylized outputs. During training, the system consumes text prompts (initially sampled from SAM captions and community sources) and the corresponding images generated by one or more advanced models via their APIs; the VLM (LLaVA-Next) receives a text prompt plus two images (one from the advanced model, one from the base model) and returns a pairwise preference decision.

## Method

EvolveDirector runs three parallel processes: (1) calling advanced T2I model APIs with text prompts to obtain reference images, (2) a VLM (LLaVA-Next) that periodically evaluates the base model via pairwise image comparisons and dynamically curates the training set through four operations—discrimination (comparing base vs. advanced outputs), expansion (generating prompt variants when the advanced model wins), deletion (removing prompts where the base model is already competitive), and mutation (injecting entirely new prompts at 10% probability)—and (3) online training of a DiT base model (PixArt-α architecture with layer normalization after QK projections for stability) on the evolving dataset. The VLM evaluation triggers every 100 epochs on a 20% sample of the current dataset, expanding each underperforming prompt by 3 variants. This reduces the required training data from ~11M pairs (to match PixArt-α) to a 100K cap, and when applied to multiple advanced models simultaneously (DeepFloyd IF, Playground 2.5, SD3, Ideogram), the VLM selects the best image per prompt, enabling the final model Edgen to outperform all individual advanced models.

## Results

The key quantitative finding is that EvolveDirector reduces the required training data from 11 million to 100K samples (~110× reduction) while maintaining comparable performance: FID drops from 7.36 (11M baseline) to 7.45 (100K + full EvolveDirector), and human evaluation goes from 48.89% to 48.53% relative to the target model PixArt-α. In the multi-model setting (Sec. 4.4), the final trained model Edgen outperforms all four advanced models (DeepFloyd IF, Playground 2.5, Stable Diffusion 3, Ideogram) in pairwise human evaluation across 300 prompts with 5 raters. The VLM selects the best image among multiple advanced models per prompt, with Ideogram receiving the highest overall select ratio (29.52%) and Stable Diffusion 3 leading in text rendering (35.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PixArt-α (the single-model target in ablation), EvolveDirector with 100K dynamic samples achieves FID 7.45 and 48.53% human preference, nearly matching the 11M-sample baseline (FID 7.36, 48.89%) — a ~110× data reduction. In the multi-model comparison (Sec. 4.4), Edgen outperforms all four advanced models in overall human evaluation. DeepFloyd IF receives the lowest VLM select ratio (18.57% overall) despite its strong text rendering (21.21%), indicating its weaker general generation quality. Playground 2.5 excels in human generation (31.33%) but is notably weak in text rendering (9.09%), likely due to insufficient text-containing data in its training set. Stable Diffusion 3 leads in text generation (35.45%) and is competitive overall (26.19%). Ideogram achieves the highest overall select ratio (29.52%) and is strongest in multi-object generation (33.36%), yet Edgen still surpasses it in the final human evaluation, benefiting from the VLM's ability to cherry-pick the best sample across all models per prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and PixArt-α training recipe as its base model, and draws on the growing line of work using VLMs for text-to-image evaluation (e.g., Gecko, TIFA) but repurposes VLMs not as evaluators of final outputs but as active directors of a dynamic training dataset. It explicitly positions itself as orthogonal to knowledge distillation (no loss functions or intermediate features required) and to dataset distillation (data is curated online during training rather than pre-selected from a fixed corpus). The closest prior work is JourneyDB, which statically collects millions of images from Midjourney; EvolveDirector departs by making the data collection adaptive, VLM-guided, and orders of magnitude smaller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× reduction in required training data (11M → 100K samples) to approximate an advanced model's generation quality is a practically significant efficiency gain that lowers the barrier to training competitive T2I models, especially given the cost of fee-based API calls. The multi-model variant, where the VLM selects the best sample across several advanced models and the resulting Edgen outperforms all of them individually, suggests a general recipe for 'best-of-ensembles' training without access to model weights. Open questions include whether the approach inherits and amplifies biases from the source models, how it scales to even more capable future models, and whether the VLM's pairwise comparison criterion is sufficient for all generation dimensions (e.g., fine-grained spatial reasoning).
