## Problem

Region-level visual understanding (open-world object recognition) remains challenging because existing approaches either crop regions and distill CLIP features into detectors—losing critical contextual information—or train contrastive region-label models from scratch with massive, noisy datasets at prohibitive computational cost. No prior method efficiently combines the complementary strengths of a localization foundation model (SAM) and a vision-language model (CLIP) while keeping both frozen.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241623/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full RGB image (resized to 224×224 or 336×336, never cropped to the region), a set of bounding-box proposals (from ground truth, SAM's automatic mask generator, or GLIP), and category text prompts formatted as 'a photo of [category] in the scene.' It outputs a region-text matching score per proposal, from which a class label is selected. The bounding box serves as a prompt to SAM rather than a crop boundary, and the full image is fed to CLIP to preserve global context.

## Method

RegionSpot extracts position-aware tokens from SAM's mask decoder (specifically after the Transformer decoder) using the bounding box as a prompt, and simultaneously produces an image-level semantic feature map from CLIP's vision encoder on the full, uncropped image. A lightweight cross-attention module (3 transformer blocks, ~35M learnable parameters) then fuses these: SAM tokens act as queries while CLIP features serve as both keys and values, enabling each region token to attend to the most relevant semantic patches across the entire image. Both foundation models remain frozen during training; only the cross-attention module and a small projector are optimized with focal loss on region-text matching scores. Training uses ~3M images from Objects365, OpenImages, and V3Det over two 450K-iteration stages on 8 V100 GPUs (~0.2K GPU-hours total), achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot recognition with 436× less compute than GLIP.

## Results

On LVIS val with ground-truth boxes, RegionSpot-Pro+336 achieves 59.9 AP_all (55.4 APr, 68.6 APf), substantially surpassing the CLIP-L baseline (48.7 AP_all) and the finetuned CLIP-L↑336* (53.1 AP_all). When using GLIP-L proposals, RegionSpot-Pro+336 reaches 29.8 AP_all vs. GLIP-L's 26.9 AP_all, with a 13.1 AP gain on rare categories (30.2 vs. 17.1 APr). On LVIS minival, RegionSpot-Pro+336 achieves 33.2 APr, exceeding GLIP-L (28.2) by 5.0 and GroundingDINO-L (22.2) by 11.0. On ODinW, RegionSpot-Pro+336 averages 46.9 AP, surpassing GLIP-L (44.4) by 2.5 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionSpot substantially outperforms the CLIP-L baseline [24] by 11.2 AP_all (59.9 vs. 48.7) with GT boxes, and still leads the finetuned CLIP-L↑336* by 6.8 AP_all. Against GLIP-L [18], RegionSpot-Pro+336 achieves a 2.9 AP_all gain (29.8 vs. 26.9) and a striking 13.1 APr improvement on rare categories (30.2 vs. 17.1), while training on ~9× less data (3M vs. FourODs+GoldG+Cap24M) in 0.2K GPU-hours vs. 120K. On LVIS minival, RegionSpot surpasses GroundingDINO-L [19] by 11.0 APr (33.2 vs. 22.2) despite using a less advanced detector. Under the ViLD protocol [7], RegionSpot (19.7 APr) outperforms ViLD (16.1), Detic-ViLD [23] (17.8), F-VLM [16] (18.6), and the pretrained RegionCLIP [43] (17.1) by 3.6, 1.9, 1.1, and 2.6 APr respectively. For zero-shot instance segmentation on LVIS minival, RegionSpot (23.5 AP) exceeds X-Decoder [45] (9.4 AP) by 14.1 and OpenSeeD [40] (19.6 AP) by 3.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionSpot departs from two dominant paradigms in open-world region recognition: (1) distilling CLIP image-level representations from cropped regions into a detection backbone (ViLD, RegionCLIP, Detic), and (2) training a contrastive region-label model from scratch on massive pseudo-labeled datasets (GLIP, DETCLIP). Instead, it introduces a third route—keeping both a localization foundation model (SAM) and a ViL model (CLIP) fully frozen and learning only a lightweight cross-attention module that maps SAM's position-aware object tokens onto CLIP's image-level semantic feature map. This positions the work at the intersection of the SAM localization literature and the CLIP-based open-vocabulary detection literature, treating the two as complementary rather than competing sources of knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 436× reduction in training compute (0.2K vs. 92K GPU hours) while outperforming GLIP-L by 2.9 mAP (and 13.1 AP on rare categories) makes open-world region recognition accessible to groups without large-scale training infrastructure, and the frozen-backbone design means future improvements to SAM or CLIP can be absorbed without retraining. A key open question the authors flag is whether SAM's automatic localization (dense point prompting) can replace external proposal generators like GLIP, which would make the system fully self-contained; current results with SAM-generated proposals (18.2 AP) remain far below those with GLIP boxes (29.8 AP). The reliance on externally sourced bounding boxes also limits the method's practical deployment as a standalone detector.
