## Problem

Online continual learning methods assume labels are immediately available once data is collected, but in real-world deployment (e.g., medical monitoring, geolocation), annotation introduces a delay of d time steps between data arrival and label availability. This paper formalizes this label delay setting and demonstrates that simply increasing computational budget is insufficient to close the resulting accuracy gap, and that state-of-the-art Self-Supervised Learning, Semi-Supervised Learning, and Test-Time Adaptation methods all fail to outperform a naive baseline that ignores unlabeled data entirely.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the model receives two non-corresponding batches: n unlabeled RGB images (224×224) from the current distribution D_t, and n labeled images from d time steps earlier (D_{t-d}). The model must first predict class labels for the current unlabeled batch (evaluation), then update its parameters using the delayed labeled data and optionally the unlabeled data under a fixed computational budget C (measured in backward passes through a ResNet18). Batch sizes are n=128 for CLOC, FMoW, and Yearbook, and n=64 for CGLM.

## Method

The proposed Importance Weighted Memory Sampling (IWMS) replaces the newest (but outdated) labeled batch with importance-weighted samples drawn from a replay buffer that resemble the current unlabeled distribution. Specifically, for each unlabeled sample x_i^t, the model first predicts a pseudo-label, then selects memory buffer samples sharing that label, computes pairwise cosine feature similarities between x_i^t and those candidates, and samples training examples from a multinomial distribution parameterized by those similarities. This ensures the training mini-batch matches the current data distribution without requiring any additional backward passes, keeping the computational budget identical to the naive baseline (C=1). The feature representations h(x) are cached during evaluation to avoid recomputation, and the method uses a ResNet18 backbone with a 2^19-sample FIFO memory buffer and standard cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On CGLM, IWMS achieves 100% recovery of the label-delay accuracy gap at d=10 (Acc=24.1%), 93% at d=50 (Acc=23.5%), and 87% at d=100 (Acc=22.9%), effectively matching the non-delayed Naive baseline. On FMoW, IWMS even surpasses the non-delayed Naive at d=10 with 140% gap recovery. On CLOC (39M samples), IWMS recovers 33%, 19%, and 16% of the gap for d=10, 50, 100 respectively. Critically, none of the 12 state-of-the-art S4L, Pseudo-Labeling, or TTA methods outperform the naive ER baseline under a normalized computational budget across any dataset-delay combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IWMS decisively outperforms the Naive ER baseline on CLOC (+33% gap recovery at d=10), CGLM (100% recovery at d=10, 93% at d=50, 87% at d=100), and FMoW (140% at d=10, 67% at d=50, 45% at d=100), while performing on par with Naive on Yearbook. All ten S4L variants (MoCo, SimCLR, NNCLR, BYOL, SimSIAM, DINO, VICReg, BarlowTwins, SWAV, W-MSE) and Pseudo-Labeling consistently underperform the Naive baseline under the normalized computational budget, with recovery scores ranging from -29% to -7% on CLOC/CGLM, because the extra backward pass for the contrastive or pseudo-label loss is less effective than simply performing more supervised updates on the delayed labeled data. All four TTA methods (TENT, EATA, SAR, CoTTA) underperform Naive on CLOC, CGLM, and FMoW by large margins (e.g., CoTTA achieves -480% recovery on FMoW at d=10), though TTA narrowly beats IWMS on Yearbook at d=50 by +1.7%. The key finding is that under a fair computational budget, no existing paradigm for exploiting unlabeled data (S4L, PL, TTA) can match the simple strategy of importance-weighted memory sampling, which requires no additional backward passes and only a negligible similarity-computation overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Online Continual Learning (OCL) framework of Cai et al. [4] and the budgeted CL paradigm of Prabhu et al. [8] and Ghunaim et al. [9] by introducing a label delay constraint that was previously assumed away. It builds directly on Experience Replay [26] as the base continual learning mechanism, modifying only the sampling strategy. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from the online learning literature on delayed feedback [13,14,15,16], which focuses on low-complexity data and expert selection rather than representation learning on large-scale image streams, and from RealtimeOCL [9] where delay arises from model throughput rather than annotation latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label delay problem is a realistic and previously unaddressed constraint in OCL, with direct implications for deployment in healthcare, geolocation, and other domains where annotation is slow. The key open question is whether the negative result (all 12 SSL/S4L/TTA methods failing under budget constraints) generalizes to larger architectures, different modalities, or non-uniform delay distributions. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-18, four datasets, and the weak Yearbook results where IWMS provides no improvement over Naive, suggesting the method's effectiveness is dataset-dependent.
