## Problem

When a data-dependent selection procedure (e.g., clustering, top-K, or mean thresholding) is applied to a test set before multiple testing, the resulting distortion in the distribution of test statistics invalidates standard conformal p-values and breaks FDR control. Existing selective inference methods either require independence between selection and testing statistics or are limited to specific selection rules, leaving a gap for a unified framework that guarantees FDR control across a broad class of data-driven selection procedures in the predictive setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled set with known responses, an unlabeled test set with covariates only, a pre-specified selection rule (which may depend on both labeled and test data), a black-box prediction model, and a target FDR level α. It outputs a rejection set restricted to the selected subset and the corresponding selective conformal p-values. A key structural detail is that the calibration set is not fixed a priori but is adaptively derived from the labeled data by applying the same selection rule, and under weakly stable rules each test point receives its own leave-one-out calibration set, making the p-value construction point-specific.

## Method

The core procedure (SCPV) applies the selection rule to the labeled data to form a calibration set, then constructs conformal p-values by ranking each selected test unit's nonconformity score among the null calibration scores, ensuring exchangeability between the selected test unit and calibration data. For strongly stable selection rules (joint-exchangeable, top-K), the standard BH procedure on these selective conformal p-values yields exact finite-sample FDR control, proven via a leave-one-out decoupling argument that fixes the randomness of the selected set. For weakly stable rules (e.g., sample-mean thresholding), an adaptive p-value is built where each test point j uses a calibration set determined by the selection rule with Z_j removed, and the resulting complex dependence is handled through the conditional calibration framework or an e-BH boosting procedure to recover rigorous FDR guarantees. The theoretical contribution is a unified stability-based framework that characterizes when the BH procedure is directly valid versus when additional calibration is needed, extending conformalized multiple testing to previously intractable selective settings.

## Results

On four real-world datasets (Abalone, Census, Credit, Promotion) with target FDR α=10%, SCPV achieves valid FDR control (6.4–9.2%) while delivering the highest power among all FDR-controlling methods (e.g., 85.9% on Credit, 19.7% on Promotion under quantile selection). The OMT baseline, which ignores the selection procedure, inflates FDR to 12.6–24.9%, far exceeding the target. On synthetic data (Figure 1), SCPV maintains FDR at or below 10% across all noise levels and selection rules, while OMT consistently exceeds the target and AMT(BH) is overly conservative with substantially lower power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPV outperforms OMT (Bates et al. [8]) on FDR validity: OMT inflates FDR to 12.6–24.9% (target 10%) because it constructs conformal p-values without accounting for the selection-induced distributional shift, while SCPV stays at or below 9.2% across all settings. Against AMT(BH) (Benjamini & Yekutieli [12] adjustment), SCPV achieves comparable or slightly higher FDR (closer to the target) while delivering substantially more power — e.g., 15.3% vs. 5.48% on Census (EXCH) and 19.7% vs. 16.8% on Promotion (QUAN). AMT(BY) (Benjamini & Yekutieli [11]) is extremely conservative, often producing null rejection sets and is omitted from Table 2. SCOP (Bao et al. [3]), which inverts selective prediction intervals into tests, fails FDR control in the classification setting (Case B) and suffers large power loss even when it controls FDR in regression. The FDR-linking SCA method (Su [51]) is more conservative than SCPV (e.g., 3.59% vs. 9.83% FDR on Case A/QUAN in Table 3) with correspondingly lower power (88.7% vs. 93.9%). InfoSCOP (Gazin et al. [23]) shows nearly identical empirical performance to SCPV under mean selection (Table 4: 9.85% vs. 9.86% FDR, 94.0% vs. 93.4% power on Case A), but its theoretical guarantee requires joint-exchangeable selection and does not extend to test-data-only rules like quantile or mean selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformalized multiple testing framework of Bates et al. [8] (conformal p-values + BH procedure) to the setting where a data-dependent selection rule first filters the test set. It builds on selective conformal inference (Bao et al. [3]) which handles single prediction intervals after selection, and on InfoSCOP (Gazin et al. [23]) which provides FCR control for informative sets under joint-exchangeable selection. The key departure is a unified stability framework (strong/weak stability) that generalizes beyond joint-exchangeable rules to top-K, quantile, and mean-threshold selection, with an adaptive calibration set construction for the weaker case. The paper also leverages conditional calibration (Fithian & Lei [22]) and e-BH boosting (Lee & Ren [33]) to handle the complex dependence structure among adaptive p-values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-sample FDR guarantees for conformal multiple testing after arbitrary data-dependent selection, filling a gap between selective inference (single-parameter) and conformal multiple testing (fixed test set). The stability taxonomy (strong vs. weak) gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for which selection rules their pipeline can handle with or without the more complex conditional calibration machinery. Key limitations include the i.i.d. assumption between labeled and test data and the requirement that the selection rule satisfy a stability condition, leaving open the important case of unstable rules like test-data-only clustering. The empirical finding that BH on adaptive p-values works well despite lacking a formal guarantee (while conditional calibration provides the guarantee at similar power) suggests the dependence structure may be milder than worst-case analysis implies.
