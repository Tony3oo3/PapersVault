## Problem

360-degree monocular depth estimation is severely data-starved, with only ~34K labeled images compared to 1.5M for perspective depth, while powerful perspective foundation models like Depth Anything fail on equirectangular images due to projection and distortion mismatches. The paper tackles how to effectively transfer knowledge from these perspective teachers to 360-degree students by exploiting the much larger pool of unlabeled 360 data (~344K) without requiring new ground-truth annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244055/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 360-degree equirectangular RGB images and produces up-to-scale relative depth maps (disparity normalized to [0,1]). During training, unlabeled equirectangular images are decomposed into six 90°-FoV cube-face perspective patches before being fed to the frozen Depth Anything teacher; the 360 student model itself always operates on the full equirectangular image. Invalid regions (sky, watermarks) are excluded from loss computation via a binary valid-pixel mask, and images with fewer than 20% valid pixels are discarded entirely.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-supervised joint training pipeline that distills a frozen perspective depth foundation model (Depth Anything) into any 360-degree depth student (UniFuse, BiFuse++, HoHoNet, EGFormer). Unlabeled equirectangular images are first randomly rotated, then projected into six cube faces; Depth Anything infers pseudo depth on each face independently, and the affine-invariant loss is computed in the perspective domain rather than stitching back to equirectangular, avoiding scale-misalignment failures. An offline stage uses Grounded SAM (Grounded DINOv2 + Segment Anything) with text prompts 'sky' and 'watermark' to generate valid-pixel masks that exclude geometrically meaningless regions from the loss. Each training batch is split 50/50 between labeled data (supervised by ground truth) and pseudo-labeled data (supervised by teacher output), and the random rotation step is critical to prevent cube-face artifacts that arise from the teacher's lack of cross-face context.

## Results

On the zero-shot Stanford2D3D benchmark (trained on Matterport3D + Structured3D pseudo labels), the proposed pipeline applied to BiFuse++ achieves the best Abs Rel of 0.082 and δ₁ of 0.931, outperforming the same model trained on Matterport3D alone (Abs Rel 0.090, δ₁ 0.921). On the in-domain Matterport3D benchmark, BiFuse++ with pseudo labels reaches Abs Rel 0.085 and δ₁ 0.917, improving over its Matterport3D-only counterpart (0.094, 0.914). Perspective foundation models applied directly to 360° images (Depth Anything: Abs Rel 0.248; Marigold: 0.195) are substantially outperformed, confirming the domain gap that motivates the cube-projection distillation approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed joint-training pipeline consistently improves every 360° student model it is applied to. BiFuse++ with pseudo labels (Abs Rel 0.082) outperforms its Matterport3D-only version (0.090) by 8.9% relative error reduction, and also beats UniFuse with the same pseudo labels (0.086). Against perspective foundation models applied directly to 360° images, the gap is dramatic: Depth Anything (0.248) and Marigold (0.195) are outperformed by 3× and 2.4× respectively in Abs Rel, confirming that direct cross-projection transfer fails. Among non-dual-projection baselines, HoHoNet (0.088) and EGFormer (0.086) both improve with pseudo labels but remain slightly behind BiFuse++ (0.082). The original BiFuse (0.120) and UniFuse (0.094) trained with BerHu metric loss are clearly surpassed by the affine-invariant retrained versions, indicating that the loss function change alone contributes meaningfully. Overall, the method wins on every head-to-head comparison in the zero-shot setting, with the largest margins against perspective-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: 360-degree monocular depth estimation (UniFuse, BiFuse++, EGFormer, HoHoNet) and perspective depth foundation models (Depth Anything, Marigold, MiDaS). Rather than proposing a new 360 architecture, it introduces a training pipeline that distills knowledge from a frozen perspective teacher into any existing 360 student via six-face cube projection with random rotation. The semi-supervised pseudo-labeling paradigm is borrowed from the broader unlabeled-data literature, but the cross-projection distillation mechanism—projecting equirectangular images into perspective cube faces, running the teacher, and computing loss in the perspective domain—is the paper's specific contribution, building on prior cube-projection techniques (360-SelfNet, BiFuse) while adding the random rotation step to mitigate cube-face artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is model-agnostic and dataset-agnostic, meaning it can be dropped into any future 360 depth estimator as new SOTA methods emerge, which gives it lasting practical value for the 360 depth community. The ~170× data gap between perspective and equirectangular datasets makes this data-efficiency angle important, and the zero-shot improvements (e.g., BiFuse++ Abs Rel 0.090→0.082 on Stanford2D3D) are consistent across multiple architectures. However, the paper acknowledges that data quality is a critical bottleneck (training without cleaning produces NaN), and the absolute data volume for 360 imagery remains small compared to perspective tasks. Open questions include whether scale alignment between cube faces could enable equirectangular-domain loss computation, and whether more advanced teacher models or larger unlabeled 360 pools would yield further gains.
