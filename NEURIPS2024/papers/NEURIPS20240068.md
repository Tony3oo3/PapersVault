## Problem

Existing cross-modal hashing methods suffer from uncomprehensive feature representations that focus on local features rather than global semantic information, and they rely on computationally expensive label co-occurrence matrices that limit scalability. Additionally, preserving semantic information in compact binary hash codes while maintaining cross-modal consistency remains a persistent challenge for supervised hashing approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240068/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed through CLIP followed by a transformer encoder) and text (first converted to bag-of-words vectors, then encoded by a transformer with a fully connected dimension-reduction layer) as inputs. It also consumes 300-dimensional GloVe vectors pre-trained on Wikipedia and a thresholded label co-occurrence adjacency matrix as inputs to the GAT classifier. The output is a pair of binary hash codes (B_I and B_T) of configurable length (16, 32, or 64 bits) in {-1, 1}^h, which are used for Hamming-distance-based cross-modal retrieval.

## Method

EGATH is an end-to-end supervised hashing framework with three modules: (1) asymmetric feature extraction where CLIP coupled with a transformer encoder handles images while a standalone transformer with a fully connected layer handles text, creating a lightweight architecture; (2) a GAT-based label classifier that constructs a label graph from co-occurrence statistics, applies attention over GloVe label embeddings to predict multi-label scores, and concatenates predicted labels with modality features to enrich semantic representation; (3) a hash code module that maps enriched features through tanh-activated linear layers and a sign threshold, trained with a combined triplet loss (using cosine similarity as the distance metric) and optimized via an iterative alternating scheme (borrowed from GCDH) with auxiliary variables R, Q, P, B to enforce binary constraints and balance semantic preservation with compactness.

## Results

EGATH achieves mAP of 0.8637 (I→T) and 0.8293 (T→I) at 64 bits on MIRFlickr25K, 0.7480 and 0.7539 on NUS-WIDE, and 0.7945 and 0.8015 on MS-COCO, outperforming all eight baselines in the majority of settings. The largest gains over the strongest baselines (DCHMT, DSPH) appear on MS-COCO, where EGATH exceeds DCHMT by up to 13.0 mAP points (0.7945 vs 0.6647 for I→T 64-bit). The only exception is SCAHN on MS-COCO T→I at 64 bits (0.8093 vs 0.8015), which the authors attribute to SCAHN's cross-modal contrastive mechanism being particularly effective on multi-label datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGATH outperforms all eight baselines (DJSRH, JDSH, DCHMT, MLCAH, CDTH, UCCH, SCAHN, DSPH) on 17 out of 18 dataset–task–bit-length combinations. The closest competitors are DCHMT and DSPH on MIRFlickr25K, where EGATH leads by 3.8 mAP points over DSPH (0.8637 vs 0.8363) and 3.8 points over DCHMT (0.8637 vs 0.8261) at 64-bit I→T. On NUS-WIDE, EGATH's advantage is most pronounced, exceeding the best baseline DSPH by 3.9 points (0.7480 vs 0.7092) at 64-bit I→T. The single loss is SCAHN on MS-COCO T→I at 64 bits (0.8093 vs 0.8015), which the authors attribute to SCAHN's cross-modal contrastive mechanism being particularly suited to multi-label settings. On MS-COCO I→T, EGATH achieves the largest absolute gain over DCHMT (+13.0 mAP at 64 bits), which the authors credit to the GAT module benefiting from MS-COCO's richer label co-occurrence structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGATH builds on the iterative hash-code optimization framework of GCDH [21] and extends the growing line of CLIP-based cross-modal hashing methods (e.g., CkDH, CLIP-based baselines) by introducing an asymmetric feature-extraction design: CLIP coupled with a transformer encoder for images, but a standalone transformer for text, to keep the network lightweight. It departs from prior supervised hashing methods that rely on expensive label co-occurrence matrices by replacing them with a GAT-based label classifier that directly models label correlations via attention over GloVe embeddings. The triplet-loss-plus-cosine-similarity hash objective is a novel combination relative to the binary cross-entropy or KL-divergence losses used in most prior cross-modal hashing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in supervised cross-modal hashing—the computational cost of constructing and inverting large label co-occurrence matrices—by substituting a GAT classifier, which is a meaningful efficiency gain for datasets with many labels. However, the authors acknowledge that GAT's own O(N²) attention computation becomes a scalability ceiling for very large label sets, and the method has only been validated on three standard benchmarks with at most 81 labels. Open questions include whether the asymmetric CLIP/transformer design generalizes to additional modalities (audio, video) and whether the GAT label module can be approximated or pruned to handle thousands of labels without the quadratic cost.
