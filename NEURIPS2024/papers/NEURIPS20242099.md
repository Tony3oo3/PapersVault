## Problem

Multispectral video semantic segmentation (MVSS) suffers from two critical gaps: existing datasets like MVSeg are limited to eye-level views with sparse annotations, and no semi-supervised framework exists to exploit the large pool of unlabeled RGB-Thermal videos. This paper addresses both by introducing a UAV-view benchmark (MVUAV) and the first semi-supervised MVSS baseline (SemiMV) that leverages consistency regularization across modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-Thermal video clips where each clip contains one Query frame pair at time t and M=3 Memory pairs sampled from past frames at a rate of 3. The RGB stream is initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights while the thermal stream is randomly initialized (1-channel thermal repeated to 3 channels); all frames are resized to 320×480. Only the final frame pair in labeled clips carries ground-truth pixel-wise masks (26 classes on MVSeg, 36 on MVUAV), and the model outputs a single fused segmentation mask per frame at 19.1 ms inference latency.

## Method

SemiMV employs two parallel DeepLabv3+ (ResNet-50) networks for RGB and thermal streams, connected by three key modules. The Cross-collaborative Consistency Learning (C3L) module enforces mutual pseudo-supervision: each stream's argmax prediction serves as a pseudo-label for the other, refined by a Cross-modal Fusion block to mitigate sensor-specific incompleteness. The Denoised Memory Read (DMR) module computes a pixel-wise reliability map via normalized bidirectional KL divergence between RGB and thermal predictions, uses it to down-weight unreliable features before building a prototype-based memory bank, and then retrieves temporal context through attention. A Dual-C3L loss is additionally applied on the memory-updated query features to further regularize temporal integration. The total objective combines supervised cross-entropy on labeled frames with weighted C3L pseudo-losses (λ=1), trained in two stages: 50-epoch backbone warm-up followed by 150-epoch joint training.

## Results

On the MVSeg dataset, SemiMV achieves 49.73 mIoU under the 1/2 labeled partition, improving over the supervised RGB-Thermal baseline (SupOnly RGBT) by +5.98% and over the best prior MVSS method MVNet by +3.65%. On the newly introduced MVUAV dataset, SemiMV reaches 32.23 mIoU (1/2 partition), surpassing SupOnly(RGBT) by +4.63% and MVNet by +2.46%. Across all four partition protocols (1/16 to 1/2), SemiMV consistently outperforms every baseline on both datasets, with the largest gains observed under the most label-scarce 1/16 setting (+2.22% on MVSeg, +3.82% on MVUAV over supervised baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiMV outperforms all seven baselines on both MVSeg and MVUAV across every partition protocol. The closest competitor is IFR [80] (semi-supervised VSS with inter-frame feature reconstruction), which achieves 46.21 mIoU on MVSeg (1/2) versus SemiMV's 49.73, a gap of +3.52%. On MVUAV (1/2), IFR scores 29.87 versus SemiMV's 32.23 (+2.36%). Among single-modality semi-supervised RSS methods, UniMatch [24] and CPS [25] are the strongest, reaching 45.42 and 45.34 mIoU respectively on MVSeg (1/2), both trailing SemiMV by over 4 mIoU. The dedicated MVSS baseline MVNet [21] (which uses sparsely labeled videos but no unlabeled data) scores 46.08 on MVSeg and 29.77 on MVUAV (1/2), confirming that the semi-supervised exploitation of unlabeled multispectral videos in SemiMV yields consistent gains of +3.65% and +2.46% respectively. Notably, the gap between SemiMV and the supervised RGBT baseline widens as labeled data decreases, with the largest advantage (+5.98% on MVSeg, +3.82% on MVUAV) appearing at the 1/16 partition, underscoring the value of unlabeled data in label-scarce regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multispectral video semantic segmentation (MVSS) task introduced by Ji et al. [21] (CVPR 2023) to the semi-supervised setting, adapting the cross pseudo supervision paradigm from CPS [25] (CVPR 2021) by replacing cross-network pseudo-labels with cross-modal (RGB↔Thermal) pseudo-labels. The key conceptual leap is recognizing that RGB and thermal images of the same scene provide 'innate input perturbations' for consistency regularization, eliminating the need for artificial augmentations. For temporal aggregation, the paper builds on the prototypical memory read from [21] and the cross-modal fusion block from [13], adding a KL-divergence-based reliability map to denoise unreliable memory features in the absence of ground-truth supervision for past frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MVUAV dataset fills a critical gap by providing a UAV oblique bird's-eye perspective (complementary to the eye-level MVSeg) with 36 semantic classes and diverse day/night conditions, enabling more thorough evaluation of MVSS models. The finding that naive cross-pseudo-supervision without cross-modal collaboration actually degrades performance below the supervised baseline is a useful negative result, highlighting that sensor-specific limitations make single-modality pseudo-labels unreliable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other backbones (e.g., transformers), scales to higher class counts, and whether the semi-supervised gains persist under even sparser annotation regimes (below 1/16). The method's reliance on a fixed DeepLabv3+ backbone and two-dataset evaluation limits broader generalizability claims.
