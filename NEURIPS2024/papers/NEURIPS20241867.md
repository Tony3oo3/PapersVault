## Problem

Existing training-free spatial grounding methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely on loss-guided updates (backpropagation from cross-attention maps) to align generated objects with user-specified bounding boxes. These approaches lack fine-grained, per-box control, frequently producing missing objects or misaligned placements when grounding conditions involve multiple or small bounding boxes. The paper addresses this gap by seeking a mechanism that provides precise spatial control over each individual bounding box without requiring any model fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt and a set of N grounding conditions, each specifying a bounding box (xy-coordinates), the corresponding object word from the prompt, and its text embedding. It produces a single spatially grounded RGB image at 512×512 resolution (using PixArt-α as the base DiT) where each specified object is placed within its designated bounding box. The method handles variable numbers of bounding boxes (tested up to 6) with arbitrary aspect ratios, and the noisy object images in each branch are sized to the nearest PixArt-α generatable resolution matching each box's aspect ratio.

## Method

GrounDiT performs a two-stage denoising step at each timestep. The Global Update extracts mean cross-attention maps across all DiT blocks, computes an aggregated grounding loss against the bounding boxes (adopting R&B's loss), and applies a gradient descent step to the noisy image for coarse spatial alignment. The Local Update is the key novelty: for each bounding box, a separate object branch maintains a noisy image at a generatable resolution, and at each step a noisy local patch is cropped from the main image at the box region. The patch and the object image are jointly denoised by merging their token sequences into a single DiT forward pass, exploiting a newly identified property called semantic sharing—when two noisy images of different sizes are jointly denoised in DiT, positional embeddings cause them to converge toward semantically correlated outputs. The denoised local patch, now enriched with the target object's semantics, is transplanted back into the main image at the bounding box location via a masked Hadamard product. This per-box transplantation provides fine-grained spatial control that the global loss-based update alone cannot achieve.

## Results

On the HRS benchmark, GROUNDIT achieves 45.01% spatial grounding accuracy, a +14.87% absolute improvement over the previous state-of-the-art R&B (30.14%) and +7.88% over its internal baseline PixArt-R&B (37.13%). It also leads on size accuracy (27.75% vs. 26.74% for R&B) and color accuracy (35.67% vs. 32.04% for R&B). On DrawBench, GROUNDIT matches PixArt-R&B at 60% spatial accuracy while surpassing R&B (55%). In terms of prompt fidelity, GROUNDIT attains a CLIP score of 33.63 and ImageReward of 0.44, both exceeding PixArt-R&B (33.49 and 0.28 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROUNDIT outperforms all six baselines on HRS spatial accuracy, with the largest gap over R&B (+14.87%) and a clear margin over PixArt-R&B (+7.88%), confirming the benefit of the Local Update stage. On HRS size and color accuracy, GROUNDIT also leads all methods, improving over R&B by +1.01% and +3.63% respectively. On DrawBench (which contains only two bounding boxes per image), GROUNDIT ties PixArt-R&B at 60% and beats R&B by 5%, suggesting the Global Update alone suffices for simpler layouts. In the mIoU evaluation (Table 4), GROUNDIT leads on all three datasets, with the largest relative gain on the Custom Dataset (4.48 avg. boxes) where it surpasses R&B by 26.3%. For prompt fidelity, GROUNDIT slightly underperforms PixArt-R&B on PickScore (0.48 vs. 0.52) but leads on CLIP score and ImageReward, indicating a minor trade-off in perceived image quality for improved spatial grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the R&B grounding loss (Xiao et al., ICLR 2024) for its Global Update stage, but departs fundamentally from all prior training-free grounding methods (BoxDiff, Attention-Refocusing, Layout-Guidance, R&B) that rely solely on loss-guided noisy-image updates. The key conceptual departure is the Local Update via noisy patch transplantation, which exploits a newly identified property of DiT—semantic sharing—where jointly denoising two images of different sizes causes them to converge to semantically correlated outputs. This mechanism addresses a specific limitation of prior region-composition approaches (e.g., MultiDiffusion): the inability of T2I models to generate at arbitrary patch resolutions, which DiT's flexible positional embeddings overcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic sharing property is a general, architecture-level observation about DiT that could enable other fine-grained control tasks beyond bounding-box grounding, making it a potentially reusable building block. The training-free nature means the technique can be applied to any new DiT-based T2I model without retraining, addressing a practical pain point in the field. However, the per-bounding-box branch design introduces linearly growing computation cost, and the method has only been validated on PixArt-α at 512 resolution, leaving open whether semantic sharing generalizes to other DiT variants (e.g., SD3, Sora) or to video generation. The moderate but consistent improvements over strong baselines suggest the approach is effective but not yet a paradigm shift in controllable generation.
