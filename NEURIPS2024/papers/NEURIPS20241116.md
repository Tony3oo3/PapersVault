## Problem

Machine unlearning has been explored for LLMs to forget textual knowledge, but it remains entirely unexplored whether these strategies transfer to Multimodal Large Language Models, particularly for forgetting the visual recognition of specific concepts. Two core challenges make this difficult: collecting sufficient training images for targeted visual concepts is impractical, and existing methods like Gradient Ascent cause severe model degradation (whitespace outputs, repeated tokens) that destroys utility.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single image of the target concept as the forgetting training set, which is expanded into multiple fine-tuning samples across four constructed targets (new name assignment, new visual description, factual knowledge decoupling, and non-targeted knowledge preservation), with GPT-4 generating rephrased variants for each. The unlearned MLLM produces text responses where it no longer visually recognizes the target concept, instead outputting plausible but incorrect identifications (e.g., 'Jacob Campbell' or 'a man') that mimic how the model naturally responds to genuinely unseen concepts, rather than the uncharacteristic 'I don't know' response.

## Method

SIU fine-tunes MLLMs (LLaVA 7B/13B) via LoRA for only 6 steps using a joint loss of Cross Entropy and a novel Dual Masked KL-divergence (DMK) Loss, both optimized via Gradient Descent rather than the Gradient Ascent used in prior work. The DMK Loss applies two complementary masks: token-level masking excludes tokens contradicting original knowledge (e.g., the newly assigned name) from KL computation, while vocabulary-level masking specifically zeroes out the target concept's name tokens across the entire vocabulary during KL loss calculation. This dual masking resolves the fundamental conflict in prior GA+KL approaches where standard KL-divergence inadvertently pulls the model back toward recognizing the target concept by preserving its high original probability. The fine-tuning data is constructed around four targets—Aligning with Unseen Concepts, Assigning New Visual Description, Decoupling Factual Knowledge, and Preserving Non-targeted Knowledge—ensuring the model forgets visual recognition while retaining factual knowledge and general utility. The paper also introduces MMUBench, a benchmark of 20 concepts (1000 images) with five evaluation dimensions (efficacy, generality, specificity, fluency, diversity) plus robustness tests against membership inference and multilingual/multi-hop jailbreak attacks.

## Results

On MMUBench with LLAVA-7B, SIU achieves 99.0% Exact Match on Generality, 60.7 Specificity, 61.2 Fluency, and 97.0 Diversity, while all methods reach 100% Efficacy. SIU's advantage is most dramatic on Generality EM (99.0% vs. 58.3% for PO, 36.3% for GA, 33.0% for GA+KL) and Fluency (61.2 vs. 373.6 for GA and 198.1 for GA+KL). Scaling to LLAVA-13B, SIU retains 90.0% EM on Generality with 54.3 Fluency, whereas non-SIU methods degrade sharply (e.g., PO drops to 10.7% EM, GA to 24.7%). SIU also achieves the lowest MIA score (0.27 ROUGE-L) and best multi-hop jailbreak resistance (0.16) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIU decisively outperforms all three baselines on Generality EM: 99.0% vs. 58.3% (PO/TOFU), 36.3% (GA), and 33.0% (GA+KL) on LLAVA-7B, indicating that only SIU truly generalizes unlearning to unseen images of the target concept. Against GA, SIU is superior on every utility metric—Specificity (60.7 vs. 9.0), Fluency (61.2 vs. 373.6), and Diversity (97.0 vs. 6.3)—since GA produces whitespace or repeated tokens. Against GA+KL, SIU wins on EM (99.0% vs. 33.0%), Fluency (61.2 vs. 198.1), and Diversity (97.0 vs. 48.0), while GA+KL marginally leads on C-Dis (0.8 vs. 1.8) and G-Eval (2.8 vs. 1.9). Against PO (TOFU), SIU achieves substantially higher Generality EM (99.0% vs. 58.3%) and C-Dis (1.8 vs. 0.4), showing that PO merely learns a 'I do not know' template rather than suppressing the concept's token probability. SIU's advantage is most stable across model sizes: its EM drops only from 99% to 90% when scaling from 7B to 13B, while PO collapses from 58.3% to 10.7% and GA+KL from 33.0% to 17.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends machine unlearning from text-only LLMs to multimodal LLMs, specifically targeting the visual recognition of concepts. It builds directly on the GA+KL framework of Yao et al. [45], which combines Gradient Ascent with KL-divergence regularization to preserve model utility, but identifies a fundamental conflict: standard KL-divergence pulls the unlearned model's distribution toward the original model's, which includes high probabilities for the very tokens being unlearned. The proposed Dual Masked KL-divergence Loss resolves this by masking both specific tokens in the sequence and target-concept tokens across the vocabulary. The 'Aligning with Unseen Concepts' strategy departs from prior LLM unlearning work (which typically trains models to output 'I don't know') by instead matching the natural output distribution of MLLMs when queried about genuinely unseen concepts, a design choice grounded in empirical observation rather than prior unlearning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic study of machine unlearning in MLLMs, this paper establishes both a task formulation and an evaluation protocol (MMUBench) that will likely serve as a reference for future work in this emerging area. The practical finding that a single image and six LoRA fine-tuning steps suffice for effective concept unlearning is attractive for real-world deployment where retraining is infeasible. However, the evaluation is confined to LLaVA (7B/13B) and 20 concepts, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other MLLM architectures (e.g., those with different vision encoders or connector designs) and to more diverse concept types. The 'positive butterfly effect'—where unlearning one person's identity cascades to misidentify related individuals in the same image—raises important questions about the granularity and controllability of unlearning that remain unresolved.
