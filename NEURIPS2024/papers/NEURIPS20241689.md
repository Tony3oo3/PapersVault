## Problem

Fine-tuning SAM on high-quality annotated data (e.g., HQ-Seg-44K) improves accuracy on the target task but causes catastrophic forgetting: the model degrades on segmentation tasks not represented in the fine-tuning set, losing its general-purpose 'segment anything' capability. The paper asks how to leverage both limited human annotations and large unlabeled datasets to improve SAM's boundary accuracy without sacrificing generalization across diverse segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, SUM consumes RGB images paired with either human-annotated binary masks or SAM-generated pseudo-labels, along with interactive prompts (points or boxes) and a discrete task prompt (0 for unlabeled, 1 for salient-object, 2 for entity segmentation) that disambiguates the label semantics. At inference, the model takes an RGB image and standard point/box prompts and outputs one or more binary segmentation masks, operating identically to SAM with no additional computational overhead. The uncertainty maps and mask-refinement module are used exclusively during training and are discarded at test time.

## Method

SUM introduces three coupled innovations for uncertainty-aware fine-tuning of SAM. First, a mask-refinement module (a fine-tuned SAM decoder and prompt encoder trained on human-annotated data) produces refined predictions whose absolute difference from the original SAM logits yields a per-pixel uncertainty map capturing systematic biases accumulated during SAM's self-training. Second, this uncertainty map modulates the training loss for unlabeled data: the focal loss is reweighted pixel-wise by certainty (1−u), and the Dice loss is restricted to pixels whose certainty exceeds a threshold θ=0.6. Third, uncertainty-aware prompt sampling selects training prompts from pseudo-label regions with probability proportional to certainty, avoiding misleading prompts in unreliable areas. A discrete task prompt (implemented as a learned embedding added to the image encoder output) resolves ambiguity between salient-object, entity, and part segmentation labels during joint training. The framework combines standard SAM losses on human-annotated data with these uncertainty-aware losses on unlabeled data, preserving generalization while improving boundary accuracy.

## Results

On 3-point-prompt interactive segmentation (FT-Small setting), SUM achieves an overall mIoU of 81.0 and mBIoU of 74.8, surpassing SAM (77.2 mIoU, 70.6 mBIoU) by 3.8 and 4.2 points respectively. The largest gains appear on part segmentation, where SUM reaches 67.2 mIoU versus 63.2 for SAM (+4.0), a task not represented in the human-annotated fine-tuning data. On salient-object segmentation, SUM achieves 91.2 mIoU and 83.2 mBIoU, outperforming all baselines including HQ-SAM and AugSeg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUM outperforms SAM (no fine-tuning) across all three segmentation tasks, with the most substantial improvement on part segmentation (+4.0 mIoU, +3.9 mBIoU) and salient-object segmentation (+5.1 mIoU, +6.2 mBIoU). Against HQ-SAM (Ke et al., NeurIPS 2024), which is fine-tuned exclusively on HQ-Seg-44K, SUM matches performance on salient-object segmentation but clearly surpasses it on entity and part segmentation, where HQ-SAM actually degrades below the original SAM due to catastrophic forgetting. SUM also outperforms the semi-supervised AugSeg baseline (Zhao et al., CVPR 2023) by 3.0 mIoU overall (81.0 vs 78.0), with a particularly large margin on part segmentation (67.2 vs 59.9). Compared to U2PL (Wang et al., CVPR 2022), SUM achieves +2.0 mIoU and +2.0 mBIoU overall, confirming that the proposed mask-refinement-based uncertainty quantification is more effective than logit-confidence or multi-head-discrepancy approaches for foundation-model pseudo-labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM's interactive segmentation framework and its data-engine pseudo-labeling pipeline, extending the fine-tuning paradigm established by HQ-SAM. It departs from HQ-SAM's reliance on high-quality human annotations alone by incorporating a large unlabeled dataset (SA-1B) with SAM-generated pseudo-labels, and from semi-supervised segmentation methods (AugSeg, U2PL) by using external supervision via a mask-refinement module to generate uncertainty maps rather than relying on model self-consistency or prediction confidence. The task prompt mechanism is a novel contribution that has no direct analogue in prior interactive segmentation work, addressing the ambiguity between salient-object, entity, and part segmentation labels during joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that even parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies (LoRA, Adapter, decoder-only) cause foundation models to forget generalization, which challenges a common assumption in the community. The uncertainty-aware framework offers a practical recipe for improving SAM's boundary accuracy while preserving its 'segment anything' capability, with 3–6 mIoU gains across 14 benchmarks. Key limitations include the mask-refinement module's dependence on initial SAM quality and the fact that uncertainty maps are only used during training. Open questions include whether uncertainty-aware prompt sampling and loss can be combined with augmentation-based semi-supervised strategies, and whether the uncertainty maps could improve pseudo-label filtering in future data engines.
