## Problem

Multi-view 3D object detection models suffer severe performance degradation (up to −67% mAP) when deployed on vehicles with different sensor installations, due to geometric distributional shifts in camera extrinsics and intrinsics. Existing domain generalization methods (e.g., DG-BEV, PD-BEV) still leave up to 50% of the performance gap to Oracle unaddressed, while full fine-tuning is impractical given limited computing resources and the high cost of 3D annotations for each new deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241863/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (6 cameras for nuScenes/Lyft, 5 for Waymo) reshaped to dataset-specific resolutions (e.g., 256×704 for nuScenes, 384×704 for Lyft) together with per-camera intrinsic matrices K and extrinsic matrices T. It outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by 7 values (center x,y,z; dimensions l,w,h; heading θ) within a ±50 m BEV range, evaluated on the 'car'/'vehicle' category. Intermediate depth maps are produced by the LSS-based view transformation and are explicitly constrained for cross-view consistency.

## Method

UDGA combines two mechanisms in a two-phase training pipeline. First, a Multi-view Overlap Depth Constraint computes corresponding depth D* between adjacent camera views via overlap transformation matrices, then penalizes depth mismatch (L_ov, Euclidean distance) and photometric reprojection error (L_p, SSIM-based) to enforce depth-scale consistency across the triangular overlap regions, directly addressing perspective-induced geometric misalignment. Second, Label-Efficient Domain Adaptation (LEDA) inserts parallel adapter modules—bottleneck structures with Conv down-projection, activation, BN, and Linear up-projection plus skip connections—alongside pre-trained blocks; only these ~8.8 M extra parameters (<20% of total) are fine-tuned on 1–5% target labels while the base weights remain frozen. The depth constraint is applied in both pre-training (on source) and fine-tuning (on target) phases, whereas the adapters are trained only during fine-tuning, yielding stable performance in both source and target domains without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the four cross-domain generalization tasks, UDGA achieves NDS* of 0.421 (Lyft→nuScenes), 0.487 (nuScenes→Lyft), 0.477 (Waymo→nuScenes), and 0.459 (nuScenes→Waymo), closing 55.6%, 49.2%, 75.8%, and 59.7% of the gap to Oracle respectively. In the label-efficient domain adaptation setting (Table 2), UDGA with only 1% target labels achieves NDS* 0.526 (Lyft→nuScenes) and 0.578 (nuScenes→Lyft), surpassing Full Fine-Tuning (0.476 and 0.531) while training only 8.8M parameters (vs. 51.7M). At 100% target data, UDGA matches or exceeds Full FT (0.614 vs. 0.610 on Lyft→nuScenes; 0.706 vs. 0.700 on nuScenes→Lyft).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDGA outperforms all six baselines across all four cross-domain generalization tasks. Against the strongest prior method DG-BEV [14], UDGA gains +4.7 NDS* on Lyft→nuScenes (0.421 vs. 0.374), +5.0 on nuScenes→Lyft (0.487 vs. 0.437), +0.5 on Waymo→nuScenes (0.477 vs. 0.472), and +4.4 on nuScenes→Waymo (0.459 vs. 0.415), with the largest Closed Gap improvement of +12.6% on Lyft→nuScenes. PD-BEV [15] is consistently outperformed by UDGA on NDS* in all four tasks (e.g., 0.421 vs. 0.344 on Lyft→nuScenes). CAM-Conv [13] and Single-DGOD [44] are significantly weaker, with Single-DGOD even performing below Direct Transfer on Waymo→nuScenes (NDS* 0.007 vs. 0.133). In the domain adaptation setting (Table 2), UDGA with 8.8M trainable parameters surpasses SSF [50] (1M params) by a large margin (e.g., 0.526 vs. 0.245 NDS* at 1% on Lyft→nuScenes) and also outperforms the larger Adapter-B (21.3M params) at 100% data. CBGS [64], a class-balanced sampling augmentation, provides only marginal gains over Direct Transfer (0.265 vs. 0.213 NDS* on Lyft→nuScenes) and is far below UDGA (0.421).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BEVDepth (LSS-based) and BEVFormer (query-based) as base multi-view 3D detectors, and on the Adapter architecture from Houlsby et al. (ICML 2019) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. It departs from prior domain generalization work (DG-BEV, PD-BEV) by replacing adversarial or rendering-based view-invariance strategies with a geometric Multi-view Overlap Depth Constraint that directly enforces depth consistency across adjacent camera views using triangular projection cues. The key conceptual departure is unifying domain generalization and parameter-efficient domain adaptation into a single two-phase framework (UDGA), where the depth constraint learned during pre-training transfers into the adapter fine-tuning phase, rather than treating DG and DA as separate problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving deployment: reducing target-domain annotation needs to 1–5% while outperforming full fine-tuning with only ~17% of the parameters addresses a real cost bottleneck for fleet-level model adaptation. The depth constraint approach is a clean geometric solution that avoids the instability of adversarial training or expensive rendering pipelines. Limitations include reliance on overlap regions between cameras, inability to match LiDAR-based detection quality, and the need for some target labels (fully unsupervised adaptation remains open). Whether the adapter-plus-depth-constraint recipe generalizes beyond the three tested datasets and to more extreme domain shifts (e.g., different countries, severe weather) remains to be validated.
