## Problem

Existing autonomous driving motion generation models, whether based on continuous distribution regression or encoder-decoder autoregressive tokenization, fail to achieve zero-shot generalization across datasets and do not exhibit predictable scaling-law behavior as model size or data volume increases. This limits their deployment in real-world scenarios where new urban environments and maps require retraining, and where industrial applications demand both scalability and real-time inference for interactive simulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes vectorized road maps segmented into ≤5 m directed lane tokens (each carrying position, direction, and semantic type) and agent trajectories discretized at 0.5 s intervals into motion tokens (coordinates, heading, shape) via k-disks clustering. At inference, only the current-frame token embeddings are needed—historical tokens are cached—so the model outputs the next 0.5 s motion token for every agent in the scene, producing 32 diverse rollouts per scenario for the WOSAC benchmark. Single-step inference runs in 5–20 ms, well within real-time interactive simulation budgets.

## Method

SMART tokenizes both road vectors and agent trajectories into discrete sequences, then trains a decoder-only transformer via two next-token prediction objectives: a road-vector NTP task that learns spatial connectivity along road topology, and a motion NTP task that predicts each agent's next motion token conditioned on its own history, neighboring agents, and nearby road tokens. The MotionNet decoder uses a factorized attention design—sequential temporal self-attention, agent-agent cross-attention, and agent-map cross-attention—all augmented with relative positional embeddings to maintain viewpoint invariance. A noised tokenization strategy (perturbing the matched token to a top-k alternative) is introduced during training to simulate compounding inference errors and improve robustness. The decoder-only architecture eliminates the need to re-encode history at each step, reducing inference complexity and enabling the power-law scaling behavior (β = −0.157) observed across 1M–101M parameter models trained on 1 B motion tokens.

## Results

On the WOMD 2024 Sim Agents benchmark, SMART 101M achieves the best Realism Meta metric (0.7614), Interactive metric (0.8066), and Map-based metric (0.8648), while SMART 7M is nearly identical at 0.7591/0.8039/0.8632. The zero-shot variant (trained solely on NuPlan, tested on WOMD) reaches a Realism score of 0.7210, competitive with MVTE (0.7302) despite no overlap in map areas between the two datasets. Average single-step inference time is under 10 ms (ranging 5–20 ms depending on scene complexity), meeting real-time interactive simulation requirements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART 7M/101M outperforms all autoregressive and distribution-regression baselines on the composite Realism metric and the Interactive and Map-based sub-metrics. Against Trajeglish (the closest NTP-based competitor, Realism 0.6451 on the 2023 benchmark), SMART gains roughly 11 points in Realism and 2 points in Interactive metrics. MVTE, the top continuous-distribution-regression method, trails SMART by ~13 points in Realism (0.7302 vs. 0.7591) and ~3 points in Interactive (0.7706 vs. 0.8039), though it retains a lower minADE (1.6770 vs. 1.4062 for SMART 7M). QCNeXt achieves the best minADE (1.0830) but is far behind on all other metrics (Realism 0.4538, Interactive 0.5654), reflecting its prediction-oriented design. MultiPath++ shows the weakest kinematic scores (0.1792) and is outperformed across the board. Notably, SMART-zeroshot (trained only on NuPlan) reaches 0.7210 Realism—within 1 point of MVTE trained on WOMD—demonstrating that the discrete-tokenization + decoder-only paradigm transfers across datasets without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART extends the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm introduced by MotionLM and Trajeglish, which first framed multi-agent motion generation as a discrete sequence modeling problem. The key architectural departure is the shift from encoder-decoder to a decoder-only (GPT-style) transformer, which eliminates the need to re-encode historical tokens at each inference step and enables real-time operation. Additionally, SMART introduces road vector tokenization and a spatial next-token prediction pre-training task, moving beyond prior work that treated map inputs as continuous features or ignored them in the autoregressive loop. The factorized attention design (temporal, agent-agent, agent-map) draws on Scene Transformer and query-centric prediction methods but is restructured within the decoder-only framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is the first to validate power-law scaling behavior in driving motion generation and to demonstrate zero-shot cross-dataset generalization (NuPlan→WOMD) for a generative motion model, challenging the prevailing assumption that driving motion models must be retrained per environment. Real-time inference under 15 ms makes the approach viable for interactive closed-loop simulation, a practical requirement that prior autoregressive or diffusion-based methods could not meet. Open questions include whether the scaling trend holds beyond 100M parameters and 1B tokens, whether the simple k-disks tokenizer can be replaced by learned latent tokenizers without sacrificing generalization, and how well the model transfers to planning and prediction tasks.
