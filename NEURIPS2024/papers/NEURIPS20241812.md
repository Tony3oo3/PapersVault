## Problem

Speech LLMs can recognize and analyze speech content but consistently fail to generate appropriate responses that account for paralinguistic and environmental information (emotion, accent, age, background sound). This gap stems from the absence of well-defined task formulations, open-source benchmark datasets, and suitable evaluation metrics for spoken dialogue understanding beyond words.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes raw speech audio (7,303 utterances, 8.76 hours) as input, where each utterance carries content plus one of four paralinguistic/environmental dimensions: emotion (5 Ekman categories), accent (9 regional varieties), age (child/adult), or background sound (7 daily-life scenarios). The expected output is a natural-language text response that is contextually appropriate given both the spoken content and the paralinguistic/environmental cue. The current version is speech-to-text; the ultimate goal is speech-to-speech evaluation.

## Method

SD-Eval is constructed from eight public datasets through a pipeline of data collection, synthetic speech generation (zero-shot TTS for age and environment subsets), label normalization (Ekman's emotion model, 9 accents, 2 age groups, 7 environment types), multi-stage filtering (GPT-4-turbo prompt-based + human annotators + sentiment consistency check), punctuation restoration, and GPT-4o-based reference response generation. Three model baselines are implemented: a Cascade LLM (Whisper large-v3 ASR → InternLM2-chat-7b with LoRA), a Vanilla Speech LLM (Whisper encoder + adaptor → InternLM2-chat-7b with LoRA, taking speech directly), and an LLM Upper Bound (ground-truth transcript + label → InternLM2-chat-7b with LoRA). Evaluation combines n-gram metrics (BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR), embedding metrics (BERTScore), four LLM judges (GPT-4o, Yi-1.5-34B, Qwen2-57B, Gemma-2-27B) with task-specific prompts, and human evaluation on 200 sampled utterances, with Spearman/Kendall-Tau correlation analysis to validate metric reliability.

## Results

On SD-Eval, VS-LLM (end-to-end speech LLM) consistently outperforms Cascade LLM (ASR + LLM) across all four subsets and all metrics. For example, on test-emo, VS-LLM achieves BLEU-4 of 8.29 vs. 4.66 for Cascade LLM, and a GPT-4o judge score of 5.30 vs. 4.47. The LLM (Upper Bound), which receives ground-truth transcripts plus paralinguistic labels, further improves to BLEU-4 of 12.35 and GPT-4o score of 6.74 on test-emo, with human evaluation reaching 7.29/10. Among open-source speech LLMs, Qwen2-Audio-VC performs best but still trails VS-LLM substantially (e.g., BLEU-4 of 2.21 vs. 8.29 on test-emo).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VS-LLM (the paper's end-to-end speech LLM) outperforms Cascade LLM (Whisper ASR + InternLM2-chat-7b with LoRA) on every metric across all four subsets, with the largest gap on test-emo where BLEU-4 jumps from 4.66 to 8.29 and GPT-4o judge score from 4.47 to 5.30, confirming that direct speech input enables implicit learning of paralinguistic cues. VS-LLM is outperformed by LLM (Upper Bound), which receives ground-truth transcripts plus explicit labels (e.g., BLEU-4 12.35 vs. 8.29 on test-emo), indicating that input representation quality remains a bottleneck. Among open-source speech LLMs, Qwen2-Audio-VC is the strongest (e.g., GPT-4o 3.25 on test-emo, 4.37 on test-acc), followed by SALMONN (GPT-4o 2.61 on test-emo, 2.93 on test-acc), while Qwen-Audio and Qwen2-Audio-AA trail further behind. All open-source models are substantially outperformed by both self-implemented models, with VS-LLM exceeding the best open-source model by 2–5 BLEU-4 points and 1–3 GPT-4o judge points depending on the subset. The paper also shows that LLM-based judges (especially GPT-4o, with overall Spearman ρ=0.613) correlate far more strongly with human evaluation than traditional metrics like BLEU-4 (ρ=0.208) or BERTScore (ρ=0.291).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of spoken dialogue datasets and evaluation methodology. Prior spoken conversation datasets (IEMOCAP, MELD, SEMAINE) focus on emotion recognition rather than response generation, while recent dialogue-oriented datasets (E-chat200, StyleTalk) rely entirely on synthetic speech. SD-Eval departs from both by aggregating real-recorded speech across four paralinguistic/environmental dimensions (emotion, accent, age, background sound) and explicitly framing the task as generating contextually appropriate responses that account for non-linguistic cues. On the evaluation side, it extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (GPTScore, G-Eval, MT-Bench) to the spoken dialogue domain, where no prior systematic comparison of objective metrics against human judgment existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SD-Eval fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark systematically tests whether speech LLMs can generate responses that reflect paralinguistic and environmental cues, and the poor performance of well-known open-source models (SALMONN, Qwen-Audio, Qwen2-Audio) on this benchmark highlights how underdeveloped this capability currently is. The finding that LLM-based judges (especially GPT-4o, ρ≈0.61) correlate far more strongly with human evaluation than traditional n-gram or embedding metrics (ρ≈0.21–0.34) provides practical guidance for the field's evaluation practices. Key limitations include speech-to-text only, single-turn dialogues, and coverage of just four paralinguistic dimensions (no gender, prosody, or volume), leaving multi-turn speech-to-speech evaluation as an open problem.
