## Problem

Existing sequence-structure hybrid models for protein fitness prediction have achieved only incremental gains over leading sequence-only approaches, and the granular surface topology of proteins—critical for molecular interactions and broader structural context—has been entirely overlooked by prior methods. This paper addresses the unresolved challenge of effectively integrating multi-scale protein information (sequence, backbone structure, and surface) into a unified zero-shot fitness prediction framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243216/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a protein sequence, its 3D backbone (alpha-carbon coordinates only, with side chains deliberately omitted to prevent information leakage), and a surface point cloud of 6K–20K points generated by dMaSIF with Gaussian curvature and Heat Kernel Signature features; an MSA is optionally ensembled at inference. The backbone is assumed invariant to mutation, and surface points nearest to mutated residues are removed to avoid leakage. Output is a per-mutation fitness score computed as the log-odds ratio of mutant vs. wild-type residue probabilities, with a per-residue 20-class distribution as the intermediate prediction.

## Method

S3F builds on S2F by stacking two GVP-based message-passing encoders: a structure encoder operating on a 10 Å radius graph over alpha carbons, and a surface encoder on a 16-nearest-neighbor graph over surface points. In both encoders, ESM-2-650M residue embeddings (frozen) initialize node features, while edge features use RBF-encoded distances; five GVP layers (100-dim scalar, 16-dim vector) perform SE(3)-equivariant message passing. Surface nodes are initialized by projecting ESM features from their 3 nearest residues combined with local geometric descriptors, and after independent message passing, surface representations are aggregated back to residues via averaging over 20 nearest surface points. Pre-training uses masked residue-type prediction (15% masking) on 30,948 CATH structures with only the GVP layers trainable, keeping the 650M-parameter ESM-2 backbone frozen; this yields a lightweight 20M-parameter model that pre-trains in ~58 hours on four A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the ProteinGym benchmark (217 DMS assays), S3F achieves a Spearman rank correlation of 0.470 without MSA input, surpassing the best non-MSA baseline SaProt (0.457) and the best MSA-based model TranceptEVE L (0.456). When augmented with alignment information (S3F-MSA), the method reaches 0.496 Spearman, an 8.5% improvement over SaProt, while using only 20M trainable parameters versus 650M for SaProt. S3F-MSA also leads on AUC (0.771), MCC (0.387), NDCG (0.792), and Top-10% Recall (0.244) across all 217 assays.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-MSA methods, S3F (Spearman 0.470) outperforms all sequence-only PLMs (ESM2: 0.414, ProGen2 XL: 0.391, CARP: 0.368), all inverse-folding models (ESM-IF: 0.422, MIF: 0.383, ProteinMPNN: 0.258), and the prior best hybrid SaProt (0.457) by 1.3 points. S2F (0.454) is competitive with SaProt but slightly trails it, confirming that surface features provide the decisive gain. In the MSA category, S3F-MSA (0.496) surpasses TranceptEVE L (0.456) by 4.0 points, MSA Transformer (0.434) by 6.2 points, and EVE (0.439) by 5.7 points in Spearman. Notably, S3F achieves its best performance with only 20M trainable parameters (vs. 650M for SaProt and 940M for TranceptEVE L), completing pre-training in 58 hours on 4 A100 GPUs. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing structure drops Spearman to 0.392 and removing both structure and surface reduces it to 0.414 (matching ESM2), validating the contribution of each modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid sequence-structure paradigm for protein fitness prediction (SaProt, ProtSSN, AlphaMissense) by introducing a third modality—protein surface topology—represented as a point cloud with geometric features (Gaussian curvature, Heat Kernel Signatures) and processed via Geometric Vector Perceptrons. It builds directly on ESM-2's frozen embeddings as node features and adopts the log-odds-ratio inference strategy from ESM-1v, but departs from prior hybrid models by treating the backbone and surface as separate message-passing graphs that are later fused at the residue level. The surface encoder, powered by dMaSIF-generated point clouds, is the first application of surface-based geometric deep learning to zero-shot fitness prediction, filling a gap explicitly identified in the related work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is achieving state-of-the-art ProteinGym performance (Spearman 0.470 without MSA, 0.496 with MSA) using only 20M trainable parameters versus 650M for SaProt, making pre-training feasible on commodity hardware in days rather than months. The breakdown analysis reveals that surface and structure features are most beneficial for binding/stability assays and multi-mutation (epistatic) scenarios, offering mechanistic insight into why multi-scale representations help. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full AlphaFold database, whether it can handle indels, and whether the assumption of unchanged backbone post-mutation limits accuracy for large conformational changes.
