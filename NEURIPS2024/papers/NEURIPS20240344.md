## Problem

Global convergence of gradient EM for general n-component Gaussian mixture models in the over-parameterized regime (student model has more components than the ground truth) remained unresolved. Existing results either proved only local convergence, were restricted to the 2-Gaussian special case, or relied on parameter-space contraction arguments that break down when convergence is both sub-linear and non-monotonic.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the population setting: a student n-component GMM with fixed mixing weights and unit covariances is trained on data drawn from a single ground truth Gaussian N(0, I_d), with only the component means as trainable parameters. The step size must satisfy a constraint that depends exponentially on the initialization scale U(0) = Σ||μ_i(0)||². The output is the sequence of GMM means converging to the MLE (all means → 0) at a sublinear rate O(1/√t), where the rate constant is exponentially small in the initialization scale.

## Method

The core contribution is a new likelihood-based convergence analysis framework that proves convergence of the KL divergence loss L rather than parameter-space distance, circumventing the sub-linear and non-monotonic obstacles that defeat traditional contraction arguments. Using Stein's identity, the gradient projection ⟨∇L(μ), μ⟩ is algebraically transformed into E[||ψ̃_μ(x)||²], and a lower bound is established by exploiting the positive-semi-definite structure of ∇_x ψ̃_μ(x) rather than bounding ψ̃ directly. A local smoothness condition on L is proved (requiring step sizes bounded relative to current parameter norms), and a potential function U = Σ||μ_i||² is shown to remain bounded along the trajectory, ensuring the locality condition holds throughout. The proof is completed via mathematical induction combining the gradient lower bound, local smoothness, and a Jensen-based loss upper bound. The paper further identifies bad initialization regions where the gradient is exponentially small, proving that the exponential factor in the convergence rate is algorithmically inevitable rather than an artifact of the analysis.

## Results

The paper proves the first global convergence guarantee for gradient EM on general n-component GMMs learning a single Gaussian, with a sublinear rate L(μ(t)) ≤ 1/√(γt) where γ = Ω(η·exp(−16U(0))·π_min⁴ / (n²d²(1+μ_max(0)√(dn))⁴)). It further shows this exponential dependence on initialization scale is unavoidable: for n ≥ 3, there exist bad initializations where gradient EM is trapped for T = (1/30η)·exp(Θ(U(0))) steps before converging. Empirically, the statistical error after convergence scales as ~n^(−1/4) with sample size, matching the rate proven for 2-GMMs by Wu and Zhou [2019].

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmarking table. The key comparison is against prior convergence results: Dwivedi et al. [2018b] proved global convergence only for the special case of 2-Gaussian mixtures with symmetric means, while Balakrishnan et al. [2014], Yan et al. [2017], and Kwon and Caramanis [2020] established only local convergence for general n-GMMs. Jin et al. [2016] showed a negative result that randomly initialized EM gets trapped in spurious local minima for n ≥ 3 in the exact-parameterized setting, which this paper circumvents via over-parameterization. The present work strictly generalizes Dwivedi et al. [2018b] from 2 to arbitrary n components, and the sublinear O(1/√t) rate matches the best known rate in the 2-Gaussian over-parameterized case, confirming it is inherent to the algorithm rather than an artifact of the analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the over-parameterized GMM convergence analysis of Dwivedi et al. (2018b), which proved global convergence for the special case of 2-Gaussian mixtures learning a single Gaussian, to the general n-component setting. It departs from the standard parameter-space contraction framework used by Balakrishnan et al. (2014), Yan et al. (2017), and Kwon and Caramanis (2020) by introducing a likelihood-based analysis that handles the sub-linear and non-monotonic convergence inherent to over-parameterization. The gradient algebraic transformation via Stein's identity draws on the structural analysis of Chen et al. (2023), but is repurposed here to establish a gradient lower bound rather than characterize local minima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first global convergence guarantee for gradient EM on over-parameterized GMMs with more than 2 components, resolving a long-standing open problem. The identification of bad initialization regions that trap gradient EM for exponentially many steps (Theorem 7) reveals a fundamental geometric limitation of over-parameterization that was previously unknown, showing the exponential factor in the convergence rate is unavoidable. A key open question the authors highlight is whether global convergence can be established when the ground truth itself has multiple components, where the likelihood-based framework proposed here may serve as a useful starting point.
