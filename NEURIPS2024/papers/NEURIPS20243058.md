## Problem

CLIP-style contrastive vision-language pretraining requires very large batch sizes (typically >64k) and a dedicated text encoder, creating a heavy computational barrier. Prior classification-based alternatives relied on hand-crafted text filtering and word-level vocabularies, were only validated at small scale, and their scalability relative to CLIP remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a visual representation via a ViT backbone followed by global average pooling and a linear head that outputs logits over the full subword vocabulary (49,152 classes from CLIP's BPE tokenizer). The associated text caption is consumed only as a K-hot label vector during training—no text encoder or text input is needed at inference. The frozen visual representation can then be used for linear probing, zero-shot classification (after LiT), or interfaced with a frozen LLM (e.g., Vicuna-7B) for VQA and captioning.

## Method

SuperClass replaces CLIP's contrastive objective with a multi-label classification loss where the targets are the raw subword tokens of the image's caption, converted into a K-hot vector over the tokenizer vocabulary. The architecture is a standard ViT (S/B/L-16) with a global average pooling layer and a single linear classification head—no text encoder, no contrastive batch, and no text preprocessing such as stopword removal or lemmatization. Two key design choices distinguish it from prior classification methods: (1) a subword-level tokenizer (CLIP's BPE) is used instead of a curated word-level vocabulary, preserving long-tailed and morphological information; and (2) a simple Softmax loss with IDF-weighted labels outperforms dedicated multi-label losses (BCE, ASL, Two-way), because the noisy image-text pairs do not satisfy the completeness assumption those losses rely on. IDF weights are computed online during training, upweighting rare, informative subwords and downweighting frequent function words, without requiring offline statistics.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K linear probing, SuperClass with ViT-Large achieves 85.0% top-1 accuracy (13B seen samples on DataComp-1B), surpassing OpenAI CLIP (82.7%), OpenCLIP (83.9%), and DINOv2 (84.5%). With ViT-Base, SuperClass reaches 80.2% top-1, outperforming OpenAI CLIP (78.5%). On 10-shot classification, SuperClass achieves 77.2% on ImageNet-1K, 94.6% on Oxford-IIIT Pets, and 92.6% on Stanford Cars, and after Locked-Image Tuning attains 79.7% zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy, exceeding OpenAI CLIP (75.3%) and OpenCLIP (79.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperClass consistently outperforms the primary contrastive baseline OpenAI CLIP across all evaluation protocols: +1.7 pts on ViT-Base linear probing (80.2 vs 78.5), +2.3 pts on ViT-Large linear probing (85.0 vs 82.7), and +4.4 pts on zero-shot ImageNet-1K after LiT (79.7 vs 75.3). Against OpenCLIP (trained on the same DataComp-1B with 90k batch size), SuperClass gains +1.1 pts on ViT-Large linear probing (85.0 vs 83.9), +1.6 pts on 10-shot ImageNet (77.2 vs 75.6), and +2.4 pts on 10-shot Pets (94.6 vs 92.2), while matching on Cars (92.6 vs 92.7). SuperClass also surpasses the SOTA self-supervised model DINOv2 by 0.5 pts on ViT-Large linear probing (85.0 vs 84.5) without using MultiCrop, MIM, or contrastive objectives. Among classification-based methods, SuperClass with a subword tokenizer outperforms CatLIP's word-level approach on fine-tuning ImageNet-1K (87.8 vs 86.5) and shows stronger scaling with model size. On VLM downstream tasks (LLaVA + Vicuna-7B), SuperClass achieves 75.24 on VQAv2 and 39.20 on TextVQA, exceeding OpenCLIP (74.54, 38.16) and DINOv2 (73.32, 14.08), with the largest gap on OCR-heavy tasks like TextVQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperClass revisits the classification-based pretraining lineage (Image-to-Word, Tag2Text, RAM, CatLIP) but departs sharply from prior work by using raw subword tokens as labels without any filtering, lemmatization, or hand-crafted 'gold label' selection. Conceptually, it challenges the CLIP paradigm by eliminating the text encoder and the large-batch contrastive objective entirely, reducing vision-language pretraining to a single ViT with a multi-label classification head. The method also draws on IDF weighting from information retrieval and the LiT protocol for zero-shot evaluation, but the core contribution is the demonstration that this stripped-down classification setup scales comparably to contrastive learning on DataComp-1B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the near-universal assumption that contrastive learning with a text encoder is necessary for strong vision-language pretraining, showing that a simpler classification objective matches or modestly exceeds CLIP across classification, zero-shot, and VLM downstream tasks while requiring no text encoder and smaller batch sizes. This has practical implications for compute accessibility and training efficiency. The main acknowledged limitation is that the method completely ignores word order and object relationships, which constrains its ceiling for compositional understanding. Open questions include whether incorporating sequential or relational structure into the classification labels can close the remaining gap, and whether the scaling advantage over CLIP persists at much larger data and model scales.
