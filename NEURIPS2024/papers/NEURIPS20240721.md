## Problem

Existing compositional reasoning (CR) benchmarks for VLMs rely on LLM-only negative text generation, producing negatives that are either out-of-distribution for modern VLMs' LLM decoders or implausible given the image context. This makes them insufficiently challenging for state-of-the-art VLMs, raising the question of whether these models have genuinely solved CR or simply exploit benchmark weaknesses. The paper asks whether a benchmark can be constructed that truly probes CR ability in VLMs with strong LLM decoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240721/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents each VLM with an RGB image and a binary multiple-choice question (one correct answer, one negative), and the model must generate the index of the correct option. The generation pipeline consumes a collection of 919 images from SugarCrepe's three partitions (Replace-Attribute, Replace-Object, Replace-Relation) and produces 24,347 challenging CR QA pairs, substantially larger than SugarCrepe's 3,846 samples. A manually verified subset of 1,000 samples is also provided to validate pipeline reliability.

## Method

The core contribution is a 7-stage multi-agent 'conversation' pipeline in which GPT-4V collaboratively exposes the CR weaknesses of four open-source VLMs (LLaVA 1.6-7b, LLaVA 1.5-7b, InstructBLIP Flan-T5, InstructBLIP Vicuna-7b) to generate hard negatives. In the first iteration, GPT-4V and the VLMs independently describe each image; GPT-4V then generates CR questions targeting details the VLMs missed, and the VLMs are evaluated on these questions with open-ended answers collected as additional context. In the second iteration, GPT-4V uses the VLMs' open-ended responses to generate even harder questions, and samples where all four VLMs answer correctly are filtered out. Unlike prior benchmarks that use LLMs alone for negative synthesis, this pipeline incorporates image context at every stage, ensuring negatives are both linguistically in-distribution and visually plausible yet confusing. The result is a benchmark that provokes up to 33% accuracy drops for the curation VLMs and 11–15% drops for unseen SOTA models including GPT-4V itself.

## Results

On the proposed ConMe benchmark, the mean accuracy across 7 evaluated VLMs drops to 65.3%, compared to 88.6% on the SugarCrepe baseline, representing an average performance drop of 23.3%. For the four 'seen' models used during ConMe curation (LLaVA 1.5-7b, LLaVA 1.6-7b, InstructBLIP Flan-T5, InstructBLIP Vicuna-7b), performance drops range from 28.9% to 33.0%. Even unseen SOTA models (InternLM-XComposer2-VL-7b, Idefics2-8b) and GPT-4V itself show notable drops of 12.3%, 15.4%, and 11.2% respectively, confirming ConMe generalizes beyond the curation pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline benchmark is SugarCrepe [Hsieh et al., 2023], which uses LLM-only negative generation without image context. ConMe outperforms SugarCrepe as a harder evaluation: every one of the 7 VLMs scores lower on ConMe than on SugarCrepe, with the mean accuracy falling from 88.6% to 65.3% (−23.3%). The four models used during ConMe curation suffer the largest drops (28.9–33.0%), as expected since the pipeline specifically targets their weaknesses. The two unseen open-source VLMs (InternLM-XComposer2-VL-7b at −12.3% and Idefics2-8b at −15.4%) and GPT-4V itself (−11.2%) also show meaningful degradation, demonstrating that ConMe generalizes to models not involved in its generation. A manually verified 1000-sample subset confirms the full ConMe results are reliable, with only a 0.5% average difference in difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of compositional reasoning (CR) benchmarks for VLMs (Winoground, Crepe, ARO, SugarCrepe) by identifying two specific failure modes of prior negative-generation strategies: (i) out-of-distribution text that LLM decoders can trivially reject, and (ii) negatives that are implausible given the image context. While SugarCrepe addressed the first issue via LLM-based generation and de-biasing, it still operated purely on the text side. ConMe departs from this lineage by introducing a multi-agent VLM 'conversation' pipeline where GPT-4V and open-source VLMs collaboratively generate hard CR questions using both visual and linguistic context, effectively turning benchmark curation into an adversarial multi-agent game.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The up-to-33% performance drop on seen models (and 11–15% on unseen models including GPT-4V itself) reinstates CR as an open challenge for state-of-the-art VLMs, countering the narrative that modern LLM-decoder VLMs have largely solved compositional reasoning. The pipeline is scalable and adaptive, meaning it can be re-run as VLMs improve to generate progressively harder benchmarks, and the accompanying LLM-based error taxonomy tool provides actionable, fine-grained diagnostics (e.g., counting, proximity, emotion) for model developers. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4V's quality for question generation and the observation that 'seen' models show substantially larger drops than 'unseen' ones, suggesting partial overfitting to the curation process. Open questions include whether the pipeline can generate large-scale training data to actually improve CR, and how the benchmark difficulty evolves as VLM architectures advance.
