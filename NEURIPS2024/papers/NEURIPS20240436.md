## Problem

Multi-view depth estimation methods that embed geometric ray information into Perceiver IO (e.g., DeFiNe) lack equivariance: changing the global reference frame alters predictions, and prior work only approximates equivariance through data augmentation (canonical camera jittering), which is inconsistent across frames. This paper asks whether SE(3) equivariance can be embedded directly into the Perceiver IO architecture so that predictions are provably invariant to reference-frame transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240436/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes N posed RGB images (128×192 on ScanNet, 240×320 on DeMoN) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a query camera pose for a novel viewpoint. For each pixel, a 3D ray is computed from the camera center to the pixel, and both the ray direction and the camera's relative translation (centered by subtracting the centroid of all camera positions) are encoded as positional features. The output is a per-pixel metric depth map for the query camera, supervised with L1-log loss against ground-truth depth.

## Method

The key architectural change replaces Fourier positional encodings with Spherical Harmonics (SPH) of orders {1,2,4,8} for ray and camera-position embeddings, yielding SO(3)-rotation equivariance via Wigner-D matrix transformations, while centroid subtraction provides translational invariance. A specialized equivariant attention module computes invariant attention weights through inner products of same-type features (exploiting Wigner-D orthogonality) and applies equivariant linear layers and a Vector-Neuron-inspired gated nonlinearity to preserve equivariance across layers. The latent array is augmented with a global geometric latent derived from averaged SPH encodings of camera rotations, enabling the latents to carry geometric information. At decoding, the equivariant latent is disentangled: an equivariant MLP plus Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization extracts a canonical rotation frame, and applying its inverse to both the latent and the query ray/camera coordinates yields fully invariant inputs, allowing a standard Perceiver IO decoder with conventional Fourier encodings to recover high-frequency depth details.

## Results

On the real-world ScanNet stereo benchmark, EPIO achieves Abs.Rel. 0.086, RMSE 0.229, and δ<1.25 of 0.923, outperforming the closest baseline DeFiNe (0.093 / 0.246 / 0.911) and all other methods. On SUN3D and RGBD-SLAM (both real-world), EPIO similarly leads with Abs.Rel. 0.090 and 0.080 respectively, beating IIB (0.099 / 0.095) and NAS (0.127 / 0.131). On the synthetic Scenes11 dataset, EPIO (Abs.Rel. 0.069) trails NAS (0.038) and DPSNet (0.050), which the authors attribute to cost-volume methods benefiting from the absence of real-world artifacts in synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIO outperforms all baselines on the three real-world datasets. On ScanNet, it beats DeFiNe (the closest Perceiver IO-based baseline) by 7.5% in Abs.Rel. (0.086 vs 0.093) and 7.1% in RMSE (0.229 vs 0.246), and surpasses IIB by 25.9% in Abs.Rel. On SUN3D, EPIO improves over IIB by 9.1% in Abs.Rel. (0.090 vs 0.099) and over NAS by 29.1% (0.090 vs 0.127). On RGBD-SLAM, the margin is largest: EPIO achieves Abs.Rel. 0.080 vs IIB's 0.095 (15.8% improvement) and NAS's 0.131 (38.9% improvement). On the synthetic Scenes11 dataset, EPIO (Abs.Rel. 0.069) is outperformed by NAS (0.038) and DPSNet (0.050), which the authors note benefits from cost-volume view consistency in the absence of real-world noise. A direct comparison with the monocular Depth Anything model (Appendix O.1) shows EPIO also leads on ScanNet (Abs.Rel. 0.076 vs 0.099), though the authors caution this is not a fair comparison due to different task formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Perceiver IO architecture for multi-view depth estimation (as in DeFiNe [23] and IIB [57]) by replacing Fourier positional encodings with spherical harmonics and introducing equivariant attention modules, thereby achieving true SE(3) equivariance at the architectural level rather than approximating it through data augmentation. It draws on the equivariant transformer literature (Fuchs et al. [22], Equiformer [33,34], VN-Transformer [1]) but departs from those works by applying spherical harmonics as input-level positional encodings for rays and cameras rather than as equivariant kernel bases, and by learning a global (non-hierarchical) latent representation within the Perceiver IO framework. The disentangled equivariant-frame-plus-invariant-latent decoder design is novel relative to prior equivariant Perceiver IO work, which either replaced the latent array with a learnable transformation or lacked an equivariant decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that architectural equivariance in multi-view scene representation yields consistent, reference-frame-independent predictions without relying on augmentation, which is practically important for robotics and 3D reconstruction where coordinate frames are arbitrary. The disentangled equivariant frame and invariant scene representation offers a general scene encoding scheme that could extend to novel view synthesis, neural volume rendering, and pose estimation (as outlined in Table 4). Key limitations include the linear growth of SPH dimension with order (capping usable frequency at l_max=8), roughly 2× training overhead, and observed training instability from high-order SPH magnitude explosion, all of which constrain the method's expressiveness and scalability.
