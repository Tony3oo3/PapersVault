## Problem

Diffusion models for computational imaging inverse problems require large clean datasets for pre-training, a requirement infeasible in scientific and biomedical imaging where only corrupted observations (projections, masked pixels, noisy measurements) are available. This creates a chicken-egg dilemma: training an accurate diffusion prior requires clean images, yet reconstructing clean images from corrupted data requires a good prior. No general framework existed for learning a diffusion model from arbitrarily corrupted observations without task-specific assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes corrupted observations y (randomly masked, Gaussian-noised, or blurred RGB images at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution), a known differentiable forward model A, and a minimal set of clean images (as few as 10) solely for initializing the diffusion model. It produces two outputs: posterior samples of reconstructed clean images via diffusion posterior sampling, and a fully trained score-based diffusion model (vanilla DDPM, 35.7M parameters for CIFAR-10, 114M for CelebA) that approximates the true clean data distribution. Each EM iteration processes 5,000 corrupted observations for posterior sampling and training, with 250 held-out test images for evaluation.

## Method

EMDiffusion frames learning a diffusion prior from corrupted data as a latent-variable parameter estimation problem and solves it via an EM loop. The E-step performs adaptive diffusion posterior sampling using the current (possibly weak) diffusion prior, introducing a scaling factor λ that rescales the data-likelihood term to prevent mode collapse; λ is automatically selected per iteration by minimizing the data loss over a grid of candidates. The M-step updates the score network via standard denoising score matching on the posterior samples from the E-step. A two-phase training schedule accelerates convergence: early iterations fine-tune inherited weights on a 10% subset of observations, while later iterations reset the network and retrain from scratch on 100% of data once λ* drops below 1. The approach requires no task-specific assumptions (unlike AmbientDiffusion's extra masking or SURE-Score's SURE loss) and uses a vanilla DDPM with VP-SDE, achieving state-of-the-art results across inpainting, denoising, and deblurring.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 inpainting (60% masked), EMDiffusion achieves PSNR 24.70, LPIPS 0.009, and FID 21.08, approaching the DPS-with-clean-prior upper bound (PSNR 25.44, FID 7.08) while using only 50 clean images for initialization. On CIFAR-10 denoising (σ=0.2), it reaches PSNR 23.16 and FID 86.47, and on CelebA deblurring (9×9 Gaussian kernel, σ=2), PSNR 23.74 and FID 91.89. The learned diffusion model (35.7M params, vanilla DDPM) outperforms AmbientDiffusion (113M params, DDPM++) by 7.8 FID points on inpainting, demonstrating that the EM framework compensates for a simpler architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMDiffusion decisively outperforms SURE-Score across all three tasks: on CIFAR-10 inpainting it gains +8.95 dB PSNR (24.70 vs. 15.75) and reduces FID from 220.01 to 21.08; on denoising it improves PSNR by +0.74 dB and FID by 46 points; on CelebA deblurring it gains +1.67 dB PSNR and cuts FID from 191.96 to 91.89. Against AmbientDiffusion (inpainting only), EMDiffusion achieves +4.13 dB PSNR (24.70 vs. 20.57) and a 7.8-point FID advantage (21.08 vs. 28.88), despite using a 3× smaller model (35.7M vs. 113M parameters). On denoising, EMDiffusion also beats the self-supervised Noise2Self baseline by +1.84 dB PSNR and 5.6 FID points. The method approaches but does not match DPS with a clean prior (the stated upper bound), with gaps of 0.74–5.31 dB in PSNR and 14–80 FID points, reflecting the information loss inherent in training from corrupted observations rather than clean data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMDiffusion builds on the EM algorithm's classical use for latent-variable parameter estimation and applies it to the problem of training score-based diffusion priors from corrupted observations. It departs from prior corrupted-data learning methods—AmbientDiffusion (which relies on an additional masking trick specific to inpainting) and SURE-Score (which uses a Stein's unbiased risk estimate that over-smooths results)—by providing a task-agnostic iterative framework where the E-step performs adaptive diffusion posterior sampling with a data-driven scaling factor λ, and the M-step retrains the score function via denoising score matching on posterior samples. The method also extends the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework by introducing the adaptive λ mechanism to handle the chicken-and-egg problem of an initially weak prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in computational imaging: the need for large clean datasets to train diffusion priors, which is often infeasible in scientific and biomedical settings where only corrupted measurements are available. The finding that as few as 10 clean images (or even out-of-distribution images) suffice for initialization broadens the applicability of the approach considerably. Key limitations include the requirement of a known forward model, convergence only to local optima, and FID scores that still trail clean-prior DPS by a meaningful margin. Open questions include extending to unknown or partially known forward models, scaling to higher resolutions, and replacing the approximate DPS E-step with a more principled posterior sampler.
