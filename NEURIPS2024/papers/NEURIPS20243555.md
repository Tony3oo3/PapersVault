## Problem

Prior RL backdoor attacks (TrojDRL, BadRL) rely on static reward poisoning under inner-loop threat models, which the paper proves are fundamentally insufficient: they cannot simultaneously guarantee attack success and attack stealth across arbitrary MDPs. The paper asks what poisoning methods are actually necessary for a universal, provably successful backdoor attack against RL agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243555/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adversary consumes a completed episode trajectory H = {(s, a, r)} of length φ generated by the agent under its current policy, selects a random subset of size ⌊β·|H|⌋ to poison, applies a trigger function δ to the state observations (e.g., a 6×6 checkerboard overlay on 86×86 grayscale frames, a specific lidar pattern, or a binary indicator feature), and rewrites the corresponding rewards. The output is the modified trajectory, which is stored in the replay buffer (DQN-style) or fed directly into the policy update (PPO-style). The trigger must be out-of-distribution (S_p ∩ S = ∅) and invertible, though in practice only the forward direction is ever computed.

## Method

SleeperNets constructs an adversarial MDP M′ whose reward function R′ is dynamic: in poisoned states it assigns 𝟙[a=a⁺] − γV_π(s′), which algebraically cancels all future value terms so the target action is always optimal regardless of downstream consequences, while in benign states it preserves the original reward structure so V_π^M(s) = V_π^{M′}(s) for all benign s. Two theorems then guarantee that the optimal policy in M′ takes a⁺ with probability 1 in every poisoned state (attack success) and is simultaneously optimal in the benign MDP (attack stealth). In practice, the outer-loop threat model lets the adversary observe the full trajectory before poisoning, enabling an unbiased Monte-Carlo estimate V̂(s_t, H) = Σ γ^{i−t} r_i to approximate the policy value needed in R′. Two hyperparameters—reward constant c and weighting factor α—control perturbation magnitude, and the poisoning rate can be annealed to near-zero (e.g., 0.001%) once ASR reaches 100%.

## Results

SleeperNets achieves 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all 6 environments (Highway Merge, Safety Car, Trade BTC, Car Racing, Breakout, Q*bert) with 0% standard deviation over 3 seeds, while maintaining a Benign Return Ratio (BRR) of at least 96.5% in every environment. The attack operates at extremely low poisoning budgets (as low as 0.0006% on Trade BTC and 0.001% on Breakout) thanks to poisoning rate annealing. In contrast, TrojDRL-W achieves at most 99.8% ASR (Breakout) and as low as 57.2% (Highway Merge), while BadRL-M fails catastrophically on Highway Merge (0.2% ASR) and Trade BTC (38.8% ASR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SleeperNets outperforms both TrojDRL-W and BadRL-M on attack success rate in all 6 environments, being the only method to achieve 100% ASR universally. Against TrojDRL-W, the gap is most dramatic on Highway Merge (100% vs. 57.2% ASR) and Car Racing (100% vs. 73.0%), where TrojDRL-W also suffers a severe 73.4% drop in benign return (BRR 26.6%). Against BadRL-M, the gap is even starker: BadRL-M achieves only 0.2% ASR on Highway Merge and 38.8% on Trade BTC, while SleeperNets reaches 100% on both. On BRR, SleeperNets matches or exceeds both baselines in 5 of 6 environments; the sole exception is Highway Merge where BadRL-M achieves 100% BRR but at the cost of near-zero ASR (0.2%), making it a failed attack. The ablation in Figure 4 further shows that SleeperNets achieves >95% ASR at a 0.25% poisoning budget, while TrojDRL-W requires 2.5% (10× more) to reach >90% ASR, and BadRL-M never exceeds 40% ASR at any tested budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the static reward poisoning paradigm used by TrojDRL and BadRL, which embed a fixed ±c reward in triggered states under an inner-loop threat model. It introduces dynamic reward poisoning—where the poisoned reward is a function of the current policy's value estimate—and an outer-loop threat model that lets the adversary observe full trajectories before deciding what to poison. The theoretical contribution (proving static poisoning is insufficient for both attack success and stealth in general MDPs) provides the first formal justification for why prior methods fail to generalize, and the SleeperNets algorithm operationalizes the resulting adversarial MDP construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that 100% attack success is achievable at poisoning rates below 0.05% across six diverse domains (Atari, self-driving, robotics, trading) while preserving benign return is practically alarming for safety-critical RL deployments. The main limitation is that the adversarial reward can grow arbitrarily large, potentially making reward-outlier detection feasible, and the paper does not study trigger optimization or defense mechanisms. Open questions include whether the outer-loop assumption (access to completed trajectories before policy updates) holds in all realistic training pipelines, and whether detection or certified-robustness techniques can neutralize dynamic reward poisoning.
