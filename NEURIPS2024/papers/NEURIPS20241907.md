## Problem

Existing Riemannian multinomial logistic regression (MLR) classifiers—such as hyperbolic MLR, gyro SPD MLRs, and flat SPD MLRs—each depend on specific geometric properties (generalized law of sines, gyro structures, or pullback Euclidean metrics) that restrict them to particular manifolds or metrics. This paper asks whether a single, unified RMLR framework can be designed that requires only the Riemannian logarithm, the minimal geometric ingredient, thereby enabling intrinsic classification across a broad range of Riemannian geometries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes SPD matrix features (e.g., 20×20 covariance matrices from radar signals, 93×93 matrices from skeleton sequences, 40×40 matrices from EEG channels, or 3×3 matrices from graph node features) or rotation matrices in SO(n) (e.g., SO(3)×…×SO(3) from LieNet). It outputs class probabilities via a softmax over the Riemannian inner product ⟨Log_{P_k} S, Ã_k⟩_{P_k}, where P_k is a learnable Riemannian point per class and Ã_k is a tangent vector transported from a fixed reference tangent space. Unlike the standard LogEig MLR baseline, no projection to a tangent space or ambient Euclidean space is performed; classification happens entirely on the manifold.

## Method

The core insight is reformulating the Euclidean margin distance to a hyperplane via Riemannian trigonometry (Def. 3.1), which yields a closed-form Riemannian margin distance |⟨Log_P S, Ã⟩_P| / ||Ã||_P (Thm. 3.2). Substituting this into the margin-based probability expression collapses the RMLR to the simple form p(y=k|S) ∝ exp(⟨Log_{P_k} S, Ã_k⟩_{P_k}) (Thm. 3.3), requiring only the Riemannian logarithm and metric—no gyro structures, generalized law of sines, or flat-metric assumptions. The tangent vector Ã_k is generated from a fixed reference tangent space via parallel transportation or Lie group left-translation, making it optimizable by a standard Euclidean optimizer. On the SPD manifold, the framework is instantiated under five families of power-deformed metrics ((θ,α,β)-AIM, (α,β)-LEM, (θ,α,β)-EM, θ-LCM, 2θ-BWM), yielding five families of SPD MLRs (Thm. 4.2), with a numerically stable backpropagation scheme for the Lyapunov operator under BWM. On SO(n), the framework produces a Lie MLR (Thm. 5.2) using the bi-invariant metric, where parallel transportation and Lie group translation are equivalent (Lemma 5.1).

## Results

On the HDM05 action recognition dataset with the SPDNet backbone, the proposed (θ,α,β)-EM SPD MLR achieves 71.65% accuracy (1-Block), outperforming the LogEig MLR baseline (57.42%) by 14.23%. On the RResNet backbone, the (θ,α,β)-EM SPD MLR reaches 71.89% on HDM05 (↑13.72%) and θ-LCM reaches 53.63% on NTU60 (↑8.41%). For EEG classification with TSMNet on Hinss2021, θ-LCM SPD MLR achieves 54.14% balanced accuracy in the inter-subject setting (↑4.46% over baseline). The Lie MLR on SO(3) improves LieNet from 76.92% to 78.24% on HDM05 and from 87.91% to 89.13% on G3D, while also reducing convergence failures from 8/20 folds to 2/20 folds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All comparisons are against the LogEig MLR (matrix logarithm + fully connected + softmax), which is the standard non-intrinsic Euclidean classifier used in each backbone network. On SPDNet, the proposed SPD MLRs consistently outperform LogEig MLR across all five metric families on both Radar and HDM05, with the largest gain of 14.23% on HDM05 (1-Block, (θ,α,β)-EM). On TSMNet for EEG classification, θ-LCM SPD MLR achieves the best inter-subject balanced accuracy of 54.14% (vs. 49.68% baseline, +4.46%), demonstrating the framework's versatility across domains. On RResNet, (θ,α,β)-EM SPD MLR yields a 13.72% improvement on HDM05 and θ-LCM yields 8.41% on NTU60, confirming gains transfer to residual architectures. On SPDGCN, (α,β)-LEM SPD MLR achieves the best node classification accuracy on all three graph datasets (Disease: 96.33%, Cora: 79.89%, Pubmed: 78.16%). For the Lie group setting, Lie MLR improves LieNet on both G3D (+1.22%) and HDM05 (+1.32%) while substantially improving training stability (convergence failures drop from 8/20 to 2/20 folds on HDM05).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes the line of work extending multinomial logistic regression to non-Euclidean geometries, starting from Lebanon & Lafferty's hyperplane margin formulation [39] and Ganea et al.'s hyperbolic MLR [23]. Prior Riemannian MLRs on SPD and SPSD manifolds [50, 16, 51] each relied on specific geometric structures—gyro spaces, flat/pullback metrics, or the generalized law of sines—limiting their applicability. The key departure here is showing that the Riemannian logarithm alone suffices, eliminating these structural requirements and enabling the first extension of MLR to Lie groups (SO(n)) via a bi-invariant metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's minimal requirement (closed-form Riemannian logarithm) makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of manifolds encountered in machine learning, including previously inaccessible geometries like power-Euclidean and Bures-Wasserstein metrics on SPD manifolds. The over-parameterization concern (each class requires both a Riemannian point P_k and a tangent vector A_k) remains an open practical limitation for high-class-count problems. Future work could explore whether the framework extends to manifolds lacking closed-form logarithms or whether the per-class Riemannian parameters can be shared or regularized.
