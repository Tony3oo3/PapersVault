## Problem

Existing vision-language understanding benchmarks rely on real images in common contexts, enabling models to achieve high scores through language priors and holistic image gist rather than genuine fine-grained visual reasoning. No comprehensive benchmark existed that systematically tests models on unusual, fictional, or imaginary visual content across multiple reasoning tasks, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether strong benchmark performance reflects true multimodal understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes prompt-based generated images (retrieved from Midjourney) that are deliberately unusual or fictional (e.g., an elephant standing on macaroons), paired with text questions, retrieval queries, or captioning prompts. Across five tasks, outputs range from retrieved text/image pairs (with sample-specific hard negatives requiring fine-grained discrimination), single-sentence captions highlighting the most imaginary element, chain-of-thought arithmetic solutions requiring cross-modal co-referencing, and multi-image VQA answers spanning arithmetic, external knowledge, and cause-effect reasoning. A key design constraint is that visual and textual information must be strictly complementary—neither modality alone suffices to answer the question.

## Method

JourneyBench is constructed via a novel adversarial Human-Machine-in-the-Loop (HMIL) framework that scales high-quality data generation. For retrieval, an LLM-MLM dialogue iteratively generates hallucinated statements whose errors propagate, revealing empirically demonstrated 'blind spots' that human annotators then collect as sample-specific distractors. For MCOT, GSM8K text-only arithmetic problems are converted into strictly complementary multimodal questions by replacing visualizable text components with generated images, forcing cross-modal co-referencing. Multi-image VQA data is created by converting COPA cause-effect pairs into visual multi-image samples and by having expert annotators curate Midjourney images requiring external knowledge. Image filtering uses at least four MTurk annotators per image (achieving >72% agreement) to ensure unusualness, fictional quality, and visual comprehensibility, with over 2,200 total annotation hours across the dataset.

## Results

On cross-modal retrieval, the best model (X²-VLM-Large) achieves R@1 of 93.6% on MS-COCO-1K but drops to 78.54% on JourneyBench without distractors and 64.97% with distractors. For multi-image VQA, GPT-4o—the strongest model—reaches only 56.39% overall accuracy and 41.03% on MMCOT, well below the 78.90% human baseline. On complementary MCOT, GPT-4o scores 62.18% and GPT-4V scores 49.34%, compared to 84% human accuracy. For imaginary image captioning, the best CIDEr score is 32.56 (GPT-4o), with most models below 30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 18 evaluated models show substantial performance drops on JourneyBench relative to established benchmarks. In retrieval, X²-VLM-Large leads on MS-COCO (R@1 93.6% text, 83.32% image) but falls to 64.97% and 61.02% on JourneyBench with distractors—a 28.6 and 22.3 point drop respectively. InternVL-G-14B is the strongest on JourneyBench image retrieval with distractors (63.71% R@1), still 11.9 points below its MS-COCO score. In multi-image VQA, GPT-4o (56.39%) outperforms all open-source models (best: Idefics2-8B at 27.82%) but remains 22.5 points below the 78.90% human baseline; on the harder MMCOT subset, GPT-4o scores only 41.03% versus 71.40% for humans. For MCOT, GPT-4o (62.18%) and GPT-4V (49.34%) are the only models above 40%, while most open-source VLMs (VILA, Idefics2, Mantis, LLaVA-NeXT) score below 10%. On captioning, GPT-4o achieves the best CIDEr (32.56), marginally ahead of CogVLM2 (30.31) and LLaVA-Next-Llama3-8B (28.69), with all models far below typical scores on real-image captioning benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

JourneyBench repurposes established VLU tasks (cross-modal retrieval, captioning, VQA, MCOT) by injecting them with generated imaginary images and adversarial design constraints that prior benchmarks lack. It departs from prior generated-image benchmarks (JourneyDB, Whoops!) by scaling to ~13.5K samples and introducing complementary MCOT (where visual and textual information are both strictly necessary, unlike MathVista/ScienceQA's redundant visuals) and sample-specific distractors for retrieval (unlike MS-COCO/Flickr30K's holistic discrimination). The Human-Machine-in-the-Loop framework for data curation builds on the LLM-MLLM dialogue idea from IdealGPT but applies it to adversarial distractor generation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JourneyBench provides a diagnostic lens that reveals a substantial gap between models' apparent capability on standard benchmarks and their true fine-grained visual reasoning: GPT-4o drops from 83.9% (text-only ScienceQA) to 16.64% (text-only JourneyBench MCOT, where visual info is required), and scores only 62.18% on full MCOT. This suggests that strong scores on existing benchmarks may reflect language priors rather than genuine visual understanding, with implications for deployment in safety-critical applications. Open questions include whether models can be trained to handle unusual/imaginary visual contexts without degrading on standard scenes, and whether the complementary MCOT formulation can be extended beyond arithmetic to broader reasoning domains.
