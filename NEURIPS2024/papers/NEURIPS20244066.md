## Problem

Speculative decoding empirically accelerates LLM inference by 2–2.5×, yet the theoretical understanding of its efficiency limits, optimality among rejection-based algorithms, and the speed–quality tradeoff remains largely absent. The paper asks: what is the fundamental limit on inference acceleration given a draft model p and target model q, and what is the optimal tradeoff between reducing rejections and incurring distribution bias?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text prompt x_0, a small draft model p (e.g., pythia-70m), and a large target model q (e.g., pythia-2.8b), and produces a generated token sequence x_{1:T} of fixed horizon T. The theoretical outputs are closed-form expressions: the expected rejection count E[N_rej] expressed as a sum of total-variation distances E[TV(p_n, q_n)] over decoding steps, and a linear Pareto front relating rejection probability to minimal TV bias for biased algorithms. A key modeling assumption is that the draft model cost is negligible and each oracle call (parallel logit computation over all draft tokens) costs O(1), so inference time equals the number of rejections.

## Method

The paper formalizes autoregressive decoding as a Markov chain where the state at step t is the history x_{0:t} and transitions are governed by conditional distributions p_n or q_n. The central result (Theorem 1) derives E[N_rej] = Σ_n E_{x_{1:n-1}~q}[TV(p_n, q_n)(·|x_{1:n-1})], fully characterizing expected rejections via TV distances and establishing distribution unbiasedness. Theorem 2 proves instance-level optimality: no unbiased rejection-based algorithm (any choice of acceptance probability b_t and rejection distribution P_t) can achieve fewer expected rejections than standard speculative decoding. The analysis extends to batch speculative decoding with M parallel draft sequences (Theorem 3), showing a non-negative batch improvement term that vanishes as p→q. Finally, for biased algorithms, Theorems 4–5 characterize the Pareto front between rejection probability and TV bias as a straight line connecting (0, TV[p,q]) and (TV[p,q], 0), with a simple experiment on pythia models confirming the optimal rejection distribution outperforms the naive baseline.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 gives the exact expected rejection count for speculative decoding as the sum of TV distances between draft and target distributions, yielding an acceleration rate of T / Σ E[TV(p_n, q_n)]. A simulation with T=50 nonstationary Markov chains confirms the theory, achieving an acceleration rate of 3.05× (50/16.41 expected rejections). Theorem 5 establishes a linear Pareto front between rejection probability and distribution bias: P(reject) + Loss*_TV(b) = TV[p,q]. The sole empirical experiment (Section 4.2) validates the optimal rejection distribution (Theorem 4) by showing Decoding-OPT outperforms the naive Decoding-UNO baseline across all over-acceptance thresholds ε ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8} on Alpaca-Farm-Eval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no standard benchmark baselines. The only empirical comparison is between Decoding-OPT (using the optimal rejection distribution from Theorem 4) and Decoding-UNO (naively setting the rejection distribution to the target q). Decoding-OPT wins consistently: with RM-Mistral-7B scoring, it achieves 53–57.5% WinRate versus 42.5–47.5% for Decoding-UNO (a 5–15 percentage point gap), and with GPT-4 scoring, 53–55% versus 45–47.5% (a 5–10 percentage point gap). The gap widens with larger ε (more over-acceptance), confirming that the theoretically optimal rejection distribution matters most when the algorithm deviates further from unbiased speculative decoding. On the theoretical side, the paper establishes that no unbiased rejection-based algorithm can beat speculative decoding's expected rejection count (Theorem 2), and that batch speculative decoding provably reduces rejections with a non-negative batch improvement term (Theorem 3), though no optimality guarantee is provided for the batch setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first unified theoretical framework for Speculative Decoding (SD) via a Markov chain abstraction, building directly on the algorithmic foundations of Chen et al. (2023) and Leviathan et al. (2023). It extends the single-token optimality result from SPECTR (Sun et al., 2023) to the full sequential T-token setting and generalizes the batch speculative decoding structure from SpecInfer (Miao et al., 2023) into a simpler parallel formulation with explicit rejection characterizations. Unlike prior theoretical work that focused on optimal transport formulations or empirical speedup measurements, this paper derives closed-form expressions for expected rejections, proves instance-dependent optimality among all unbiased rejection-based algorithms, and characterizes the exact Pareto front between inference cost and output quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimality result (Theorem 2) is practically significant: it tells practitioners that no amount of tweaking acceptance probabilities or rejection distributions can reduce rejections below the TV-distance bound while preserving unbiasedness, redirecting research effort toward batch methods or biased algorithms. The linear Pareto front (Theorem 5) provides a clean design principle for systems that intentionally trade quality for speed (e.g., deploying smaller models more aggressively). Key open questions remain: the batch algorithm lacks an optimality guarantee, the theory assumes negligible draft-model cost and O(1) oracle calls, and extending the analysis to tree-structured speculation (as in SpecInfer) or to settings with non-stationary, context-dependent distributions beyond Markov chains is left for future work.
