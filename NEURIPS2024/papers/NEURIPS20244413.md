## Problem

Most diffusion models, including SDXL-Turbo and PnPinv, produce imbalanced results when fusing an object text with an object image: the generated output predominantly reflects either the text or the image rather than a harmonious blend of both. This imbalance arises because text and image features suppress each other during cross-attention and self-attention in the diffusion process, making it difficult to achieve a balanced, novel object synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB object image (uniformly scaled to 512×512) and a single object text (a class name, e.g., 'porcupine') as input. The image is encoded into a latent code via a pre-trained image encoder, and the text into an embedding via a text encoder. The output is a novel fused object image that harmoniously integrates structural, textural, and semantic features from both inputs, generated in approximately ten seconds using four denoising steps of the Ancestral-Euler sampler on SDXL-Turbo.

## Method

Built on SDXL-Turbo, the ATIH method introduces three key mechanisms. First, a scale factor α in cross-attention (ScCross-Attn) multiplies the text-derived value features to control text influence, while an injection step i in self-attention (InSelf-Attn) selectively replaces the fusion branch's attention map with the inversion branch's map for t > i, preserving image structure. Second, a balanced loss L(ε_t) = |L_r(ε_t) − λL_n(ε_t)| treats the sampling noise as a learnable parameter, trading off reconstruction fidelity (L_r) against Gaussian white-noise editability (L_n) with λ = 125. Third, a score function F(α) = I_sim(α) + k·T_sim(α) − β|I_sim(α) − k·T_sim(α)| simultaneously maximizes and balances DINOv2 image similarity and CLIP text similarity (k = 2.3, β = 1), optimized via Golden Section Search over α ∈ [0, 2], while i is adaptively adjusted to keep I_sim within [0.45, 0.85].

## Results

On the OTIF dataset (1,800 text-image pairs), ATIH achieves the best scores on aesthetic quality (AES: 6.124), human preference (HPS: 0.383), fusion score (Fscore: 1.362), and balance similarity (Bsim: 0.075, lower is better) among all compared methods. ATIH also attains competitive DINO-I image similarity (0.756) and CLIP-T text similarity (0.296). In user studies with 95 participants (570 total votes), ATIH received 74.03% of votes in the image-editing comparison and 79.47% in the creative-mixing comparison, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATIH outperforms all four quantitative baselines on Fscore and Bsim, the two metrics specifically designed to measure balanced text-image fusion. Against MagicMix, ATIH wins on DINO-I (0.756 vs. 0.587), AES, HPS, Fscore, and Bsim, but trails on CLIP-T (0.296 vs. 0.328), indicating MagicMix preserves text alignment at the cost of image fidelity. Against InfEdit and MasaCtrl, ATIH achieves lower DINO-I (0.756 vs. 0.817/0.815) but substantially higher CLIP-T, AES, HPS, Fscore, and Bsim, suggesting those methods over-preserve the original image while under-integrating the text. Against InstructPix2Pix, ATIH dominates on DINO-I (0.756 vs. 0.384), Fscore (1.362 vs. 0.768), and Bsim (0.075 vs. 0.522), though InstructPix2Pix scores higher on CLIP-T (0.394 vs. 0.296), reflecting its bias toward text at the expense of image structure. Visually, ATIH also surpasses ConceptLab and ControlNet in producing harmonious fusions, and in Appendix I comparisons, it outperforms DALL-E3 and Kosmos-G on complex prompt-based fusion tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDXL-Turbo latent diffusion framework and the PnPinv/ReNoise inversion pipeline, modifying the attention layers in a targeted way: it introduces a scale factor α on cross-attention values (extending Prompt-to-Prompt's fixed attention control) and an adjustable injection step i on self-attention (departing from MasaCtrl's fixed K/V injection by instead injecting the full attention map M). The adaptive optimization of these two parameters via a DINO/CLIP-based similarity score and Golden Section Search is the paper's core methodological contribution, distinguishing it from prior semantic mixing approaches (MagicMix, ConceptLab) that lack an explicit balancing mechanism and from image-editing methods (MasaCtrl, InfEdit, InstructPix2Pix) that do not target novel object creation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in diffusion-based editing—systematic bias toward either the text or image input during cross-attention—that persists even with state-of-the-art inversion (PnPinv). The adaptive balancing mechanism is lightweight (10 seconds on SDXL-Turbo) and could be adopted as a plug-in for other attention-based diffusion editing pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: when semantic correlation between the two input categories is weak, the method degrades to superficial texture changes rather than genuine semantic transformation, leaving open the question of how to achieve deep compositional novelty across distant concept spaces.
