## Problem

Existing latent space optimization (LSO) methods for molecule design decouple the training of the generative model from property-conditioned optimization, so the learned latent space may not be well-suited to the specific optimization task, and they require an auxiliary variational inference network that adds parameters and complexity. This paper asks whether molecule generation and property optimization can be unified in a single end-to-end trainable framework that avoids the decoupling and the extra inference network.

## Input / Output

The model takes target property values (e.g., docking scores, QED, SA) and/or binary constraints (e.g., QED > 0.4, SA < 5.5) as conditioning signals, and optionally a fixed substructure prefix for structure-constrained optimization. Molecules are represented as SELFIES strings over a fixed vocabulary, and the model outputs autoregressively generated SELFIES sequences along with predicted property values. During the online optimization phase, oracle functions (RDKit for QED/SA/logP, AutoDock-GPU for docking scores) provide ground-truth property labels for hindsight relabeling of generated molecules.

## Method

LPT defines a joint distribution p(x,y,z) = p_α(z)·p_β(x|z)·p_γ(y|z) where the latent prompt z is a UNet transformation of Gaussian noise, p_β is a 3-layer causal Transformer that generates molecule tokens autoregressively using z as a cross-attention prompt, and p_γ is a small MLP predicting property values or constraint satisfaction. Training uses maximum likelihood estimation with Langevin dynamics (MCMC) to sample the posterior p(z_0|x,y), eliminating the need for a separate variational inference network. For property-conditioned generation, the latent prompt is inferred via posterior sampling p(z_0|y) using Bayes' rule, then used to guide autoregressive molecule generation. When desired properties lie outside the learned distribution, an online learning loop iteratively shifts the model: it samples molecules with incremental extrapolation (y* + δ_y), relabels them with oracle functions, and retrains LPT on the accumulated synthetic dataset using weighted retraining that up-weights top-scoring molecules. Persistent Markov chains amortize MCMC sampling across iterations for a ~5× speedup, and the σ² hyperparameter in the posterior gradient controls the exploitation–exploration trade-off during generation.

## Results

On single-objective binding affinity optimization, LPT achieves KD values of 0.004–0.009 nM for ESR1 and 0.037–0.045 nM for ACAA1, outperforming the next-best method (SGDS) by roughly 7–10×. On PHGDH, LPT reaches a top-1 KD of 0.07×10⁻² nM versus 3.65×10⁻² nM for SGDS. In multi-objective optimization (maximizing binding affinity + QED while minimizing SA), LPT achieves the best KD across all three targets (e.g., 0.04 nM for ESR1) with competitive QED (0.58) and SA (3.46). On the PMO sample-efficiency benchmark (10K oracle queries), LPT attains a summed AUC Top-10 of 3.544, matching GP-BO (3.541) and surpassing LSTM HC (3.523).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPT dominates all 18 baselines on single-objective binding affinity: it achieves KD of 0.004 nM on ESR1, roughly 7.5× better than SGDS (0.03 nM) and ~180× better than LIMO (0.72 nM); on ACAA1 it reaches 0.037 nM versus 0.11 nM for SGDS. On the PHGDH target, LPT's top-1 KD of 0.07×10⁻² nM is ~52× better than SGDS (3.65×10⁻²) and ~198× better than LIMO (13.87×10⁻²). In multi-objective optimization, LPT achieves the best KD on all three proteins (e.g., 0.04 nM ESR1, 0.18 nM ACAA1, 0.02 nM PHGDH) while maintaining QED and SA scores comparable to SGDS. On biological sequence design, LPT outperforms GFlowNet-AL on TF Bind 8 (0.954 vs. 0.84 performance) and COMs on GFP (0.857 vs. 0.831). On the PMO sample-efficiency benchmark with a 10K oracle budget, LPT (sum AUC Top-10 = 3.544) slightly edges out GP-BO (3.541) and LSTM HC (3.523), while clearly surpassing GFlowNet (3.038), MARS (3.381), and both VAE+BO variants (~2.9–3.0). The paper acknowledges a remaining gap versus genetic-algorithm-based methods under very small budgets, attributing it to the data-intensive pretraining requirement of generative models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-space distribution shifting framework of Kong et al. (2023, SGDS) by replacing the LSTM generator with a causal Transformer that uses the latent vector as a cross-attention prompt, and by swapping the energy-based prior for a UNet transformation of Gaussian noise. This architectural change eliminates the need for a separate variational inference network, enabling end-to-end MLE training with MCMC (Langevin dynamics) for posterior sampling. The paper also departs from the broader LSO literature (Gomez-Bombarelli et al., 2018; Tripp et al., 2020; Maus et al., 2022) by unifying generation and property-conditioned optimization in a single probabilistic model rather than decoupling them into separate stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PHGDH structure-constrained optimization benchmark is practically significant: it validates the model against a published human lead-optimization campaign (Spillier & Frédérick, 2021) where the model independently rediscovered the same design strategy (adding aromatic groups at specific indole positions) and surpassed human-designed compounds in predicted binding affinity. Open questions include whether the sequence-based (SELFIES) formulation can be extended to graph-based representations, how the model characterizes the Pareto front in multi-objective settings, and whether the online learning approach scales to wet-lab-validated properties where oracle queries are extremely expensive. The 4.33M-parameter model running on a single GPU makes the approach accessible, but the reliance on docking scores as ground truth limits direct translational impact.
