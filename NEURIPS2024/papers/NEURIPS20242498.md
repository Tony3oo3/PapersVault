## Problem

Existing CMDP algorithms provide only worst-case O(1/ε²) sample complexity bounds, which can be overly conservative for favorable problem instances. The fundamental difficulty is that CMDP optimal policies are randomized, so sub-optimal policies can be arbitrarily close to the optimal one in the continuous policy space, making it non-trivial to define a meaningful instance-dependent gap. This paper is the first to derive problem-dependent Õ(1/ε) sample complexity for CMDP by characterizing hardness through LP corner points rather than the full policy space.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes samples sequentially from a generative model: at each round n, it queries one sample of reward, K costs, and next-state transition for every state-action pair in the identified active set I_n. The constraint budgets α_k and initial distribution μ_1 are fixed parameters, while the algorithm internally maintains and adaptively depletes remaining resource vectors α^n and μ^n based on actual consumption. The output is a Markovian policy π̄^N constructed by averaging the per-round occupancy measures q^n over all N iterations, with actions at each state proportional to the averaged occupancy values.

## Method

The algorithm operates entirely in the primal space via an LP reformulation of the CMDP using occupancy measures. In a first phase (Algorithm 1), it identifies one optimal LP basis by lexicographically testing whether forcing each variable to zero changes the LP value, thereby isolating the non-zero basic variables I* and the active constraints J* that uniquely determine the optimal corner point. In the second phase (Algorithm 2), it resolves only the reduced linear system defined by the identified basis at each round, adaptively updating the right-hand side (remaining resources) based on actual stochastic consumption rather than expected values. The key distinction from prior online LP work is that the resolving procedure sticks to the pre-identified optimal basis rather than re-solving the full LP, and the instance hardness is captured by the minimum gap Δ between the optimal corner point and sub-optimal corner points, yielding the Õ(1/(Δ·ε)) sample complexity.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is a sample complexity bound of $O\left(\frac{1}{\Delta \cdot \varepsilon} \cdot \log^2(1/\varepsilon)\right)$ for CMDP, where $\Delta$ is a problem-dependent gap parameter independent of $\varepsilon$. This improves upon the state-of-the-art worst-case $O(1/\varepsilon^2)$ sample complexity established in prior work [68, 37, 21]. For the unconstrained MDP case ($K=0$), the bound specializes to $O\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}|^4 \cdot |\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^4 \cdot \xi \cdot \Delta} \cdot \frac{\log^2(1/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)$. A numerical experiment on a 10-state, 10-action CMDP with 5 constraints ($\gamma=0.7$) confirms that the relative $L_1$ error of the occupancy measure decreases rapidly with the number of samples $N$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct empirical comparison against competing algorithms; its contribution is primarily theoretical. The key comparison is against the worst-case $O(1/\varepsilon^2)$ sample complexity bound established by Efroni et al. [21], Jin and Sidford [37], and Wang [68], which the new $\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon)$ bound strictly improves upon in terms of $\varepsilon$-dependency. For the unconstrained MDP special case, the paper acknowledges that its dependency on $|\mathcal{S}|$ and $(1-\gamma)$ is worse than the minimax-optimal $O(|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|/((1-\gamma)^3\cdot\varepsilon^2))$ achieved by Agarwal et al. [2], He et al. [27], and Sidford et al. [58], but compensates with a superior $\varepsilon$-dependency. The paper also contrasts its primal-space LP-resolving approach with the dominant primal-dual methods [21, 49, 12, 69, 18, 45, 26] and notes that constrained optimization [1, 61] and Lyapunov-based [14] approaches lack comparable theoretical guarantees for CMDP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: the CMDP/RL community (which has predominantly used primal-dual Lagrangian methods for constrained policies) and the online linear programming community (which develops adaptive-resolving algorithms with logarithmic regret). The LP reformulation via occupancy measures follows Altman [4], the empirical LP construction with slack parameters follows Efroni et al. [21], and the core adaptive-resolving procedure with remaining-resource tracking is inherited from Agrawal et al. [3] and Li & Ye [47]. The key departure is operating entirely in the primal space and 'sticking to' an identified optimal LP basis during resolution, rather than performing dual variable updates as in prior CMDP algorithms. The instance-hardness characterization via LP corner points (basic solutions) is a new conceptual tool that resolves the difficulty of defining a policy gap when CMDP optimal policies are inherently randomized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first instance-dependent Õ(1/ε) sample complexity for CMDPs, improving upon the O(1/ε²) worst-case bound established by a series of prior works. The LP-basis characterization of problem hardness provides a principled way to understand when CMDP instances are easy versus hard, which could inform practical algorithm design. However, the results are confined to the tabular setting with a generative model, and the dependence on |S| and (1−γ) is worse than minimax-optimal bounds for unconstrained MDPs. Open questions include extending to function approximation, non-stationary environments, and whether the primal-space resolving framework can be adapted to model-free settings where the full LP structure is not available.
