## Problem

Publicly available Arabic HTR datasets are scarce, small, and largely limited to either controlled scribed samples or uniform calligraphic book manuscripts, leaving a gap for diverse, informal handwritten Arabic from the 19th–21st centuries. The Arabic script's contextual letter shapes, ijam dots, and optional diacritics compound the difficulty, yet no large-scale dataset with line-level annotations captures the full range of everyday Ruq'ah handwriting styles found in personal letters, diaries, and legal records.

## Input / Output

The SFR baseline takes a full-page RGB image of a handwritten Arabic manuscript (quality ranging from clean white paper to creased pages with ink bleeds) and produces per-line polygonal coordinates, text transcriptions, and page-element annotations (headings, page numbers, floating text, graphics, signatures). The dataset ships ground truths in both PAGE-XML and JSON formats; pre-extracted line images are warped to a fixed 60-pixel height with widths from 60 to 2,400 pixels (mean ≈ 576 px), and transcriptions are stored as plain text files alongside the PNG line crops.

## Method

The paper introduces the Muharaf dataset (1,644 pages, 36,311 annotated text lines from 50 collections) and a baseline full-page HTR system built on the Start-Follow-Read (SFR) architecture, which decomposes recognition into three independently trainable CNN networks: a Start-of-Line detector, a Line Follower that iteratively predicts the next coordinate and orientation within a small viewing window, and a Handwriting Recognition network (CNN-BLSTM trained with CTC loss). The data collection pipeline itself is iterative: after labeling the first 500+ pages with the custom ScribeArabic annotation tool, the SFR system is trained and its preliminary line annotations and transcriptions are fed to expert transcribers for manual correction, progressively improving both the dataset and the model. Each SFR network is small enough to fit in an 8 GB GPU, and the full system is trained on a 1,500/50/96 split, achieving a page-level CER of 0.134 ± 0.007 and WER of 0.353 ± 0.012 over three random splits.

## Results

Using the SFR (Start, Follow, Read) CNN system trained on the full Muharaf dataset (1,500 train / 50 val / 96 test), the baseline achieves a page-level character error rate (CER) of 13.4% ± 0.7% and word error rate (WER) of 35.3% ± 1.2%. On the public subset (1,100/50/66 split), page-level CER is 15.7% ± 0.8% and WER is 39.8% ± 0.7%. Line-level CER and WER are slightly higher (14.9% / 38.0% on full Muharaf) due to many short lines containing isolated numbers or single words where a single error inflates the per-line metric. Each SFR network fits within an 8 GB GPU, making the system deployable in low-resource settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a dataset paper, and the authors do not benchmark the SFR system against other HTR architectures (e.g., TrOCR, ADOCRNet, or MetaHTR). The reported numbers serve as a preliminary baseline to demonstrate the dataset's usability rather than a competitive comparison. In terms of dataset scale, Muharaf (36,311 annotated text lines across 1,644 pages) substantially exceeds other publicly available Arabic HTR datasets: RASAM has 7,540 lines over 300 pages, RASM has 2,613 lines over 120 pages, and KHATT has 13,435 lines over 4,000 pages. The paper also notes that Muharaf is the only publicly available Arabic dataset combining line-level annotations with original (non-scribed) historic manuscripts in the Ruq'ah script, distinguishing it from the calligraphic, book-based scripts in RASAM and RASM. The authors position Muharaf as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, existing Arabic datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions Muharaf as a Category 2 (original manuscript) dataset that fills a gap left by existing Arabic HTR resources: RASAM and RASM cover calligraphic, uniform scripts from earlier centuries, while KHATT and MADCAT are scribed datasets under controlled conditions. The baseline HTR system directly adopts the SFR (Start, Follow, Read) architecture [35] with CNN-BLSTM-CTC components, and the annotation pipeline leverages the PAGE-XML format from the PRImA Research Lab. The paper's conceptual departure is in targeting informal Ruq'ah script from the 19th–21st centuries—everyday personal and legal documents—rather than the neat, scholarly calligraphy that dominates prior Arabic datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Muharaf is the largest publicly available Arabic HTR dataset with line-level annotations (24,495 public lines), making it a critical resource for training and evaluating systems on historically realistic, diverse handwriting. Open questions include completing writer identification across collections, extracting linguistic knowledge (e.g., colloquial Arabic forms) from transcriptions to build language models that could improve HTR accuracy, and adapting the system to related Arabic-script languages (Urdu, Farsi, Pashto). A practical limitation is that 428 of 1,644 images remain under proprietary license, and the baseline results (CER ~13–15%) indicate substantial room for improvement, particularly on degraded or slanted lines.
