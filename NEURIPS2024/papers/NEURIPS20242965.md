## Problem

Polynomial-based learnable spectral GNNs (e.g., ChebNet, GPR-GNN, BernNet, PCNet) can approximate arbitrary graph filters, yet better polynomial approximation does not guarantee better real-world performance. Carefully crafted graph learning methods, sophisticated polynomial approximations, and refined coefficient constraints improve accuracy but simultaneously induce overfitting that diminishes generalization. The paper asks how to retain the filter-approximation capability of polynomial spectral GNNs while achieving higher generalization without relying on scenario-specific designs or coefficient constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix (with self-loops added) and node feature matrix, using the symmetric normalized Laplacian L_sym and its affinity matrix L_sym^a as the base high-pass and low-pass filters respectively, with a generalized normalization exponent η to alleviate overcorrelation. The output is a per-node class probability vector obtained by passing the TFE-filtered features through a single MLP layer followed by softmax, trained with cross-entropy loss under a supervised (full or semi) node-classification protocol.

## Method

TFE-GNN replaces polynomial spectral convolutions with a Triple Filter Ensemble (TFE) mechanism: the first ensemble sums powers of the low-pass filter (I, H_lp, H_lp², …, H_lp^{K_lp}) with learnable coefficients to form a strong low-pass convolution that extracts homophily; the second ensemble does the analogous construction with the high-pass filter to extract heterophily; the third ensemble combines the two strong convolutions via two learnable scalars (summation or concatenation) to yield the final TFE-Conv. No polynomial basis, coefficient constraints, or scenario-specific neighbor manipulation is required. Theoretically, Theorem 1 shows TFE-GNN is equivalent to ChebNet (and thus can learn arbitrary filters) when the ensemble methods are summation and the base filters are L_sym and L_sym^a, while Theorem 2 shows TFE-GNN is a general combination of any two polynomial-based spectral GNNs. This design adaptively weights homophily and heterophily extraction per graph, avoiding the overfitting that plagues refined polynomial approaches.

## Results

TFE-GNN achieves new state-of-the-art full-supervised node classification accuracy on all 11 main datasets spanning homophily levels from 0.06 (Texas) to 0.93 (Physics). The best variant TFE-GNN_sum reaches 98.85% on Physics, 97.38% on Wisconsin, 77.16% on Chameleon, and 72.27% on Squirrel, outperforming the previous best (PCNet) by up to 14.85% on Squirrel and 5.79% on Chameleon. On the homophilic citation graphs, TFE-GNN_sum scores 90.73% (Cora), 82.83% (Citeseer), and 91.66% (Pubmed), surpassing PCNet (90.02%, 81.76%, 91.30%) and ChebNetII (88.71%, 80.53%, 88.93%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFE-GNN_sum outperforms all 16 baselines on 10 of 11 main datasets, with TFE-GNN_con taking the top spot on Cora-Full (74.12% vs. 73.60%) and Cornell (94.26% vs. 93.11%). The most dramatic gains appear on heterophilic graphs: on Squirrel (ehr=0.22), TFE-GNN_sum achieves 72.27%, beating PCNet (63.53%) by 8.74% and ChebNetII (57.72%) by 14.55%; on Chameleon (ehr=0.23), it reaches 77.16% vs. SpecFormer's 74.72% and PCNet's 73.55%. On strong-homophily graphs, TFE-GNN_sum leads Physics (98.85% vs. Favard's 97.58%) and Cora (90.73% vs. PCNet's 90.02%). On Wisconsin (ehr=0.21), TFE-GNN_sum scores 97.38%, surpassing PCNet (94.26%) by 3.12% and Half-Hop (87.59%) by 9.79%. The ablation confirms that removing the low-pass ensemble (TFE₁) hurts homophilic datasets (Citeseer drops to 61.00%) while removing the high-pass ensemble (TFE₂) devastates heterophilic ones (Squirrel drops to 41.91%), validating the adaptive dual-filter design.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFE-GNN builds directly on ChebNet's Chebyshev polynomial framework, with Theorem 1 proving that TFE-GNN and ChebNet are transformable into each other under specific conditions (summation ensembles, symmetric normalized Laplacian as base filter, matched orders). However, it departs from the entire polynomial-based spectral GNN lineage (ChebNet, GPR-GNN, BernNet, ChebNetII, PCNet, EvenNet, FavardGNN) by eliminating polynomial computation and coefficient constraints entirely, replacing them with a triple ensemble of base low-pass and high-pass filter powers inspired by classical ensemble learning. The paper explicitly positions this as a response to the overfitting problem identified in prior work: sophisticated polynomial approximations and refined coefficient constraints improve in-sample performance but diminish generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that a filter-ensemble approach without polynomial machinery retains ChebNet's ability to learn arbitrary spectral filters, while empirically demonstrating better generalization (smaller train-validation loss gap) and state-of-the-art accuracy across 15 datasets spanning homophily levels from 0.05 to 0.93. A key open limitation is that the hyperparameters K_lp and K_hp must be selected based on the dataset's homophily level, which is difficult to determine for unlabeled nodes in real-world settings. The method is also noted to be more effective in full-supervised than semi-supervised regimes, and the choice of base filters (L_sym vs. L_rw) and ensemble methods (summation vs. concatenation) remains a design space that could be further explored.
