## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training methods learn cross-modality alignment implicitly from paired data, which leads to poor generalization and shortcut feature learning when training data is limited. The complex relationship between medical images and diagnostic text is difficult to capture without explicit structural guidance, and prior approaches relying on bounding boxes or masks are costly to annotate. This paper asks whether radiologists' naturally collected eye-gaze data can serve as a low-cost, explicit alignment signal to improve medical multi-modal feature learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes chest X-ray images (resized to 224×224), diagnostic text segmented into sentences (aligned temporally with synchronized audio transcripts), and eye-gaze fixations processed into 14×14 attention heatmaps matching the 196-patch grid of the Swin Transformer encoder. During training, batches may contain a mix of samples with and without eye-gaze data; samples lacking gaze information skip the gaze-guided loss terms. The trained model produces aligned 512-dimensional image and text embeddings used for zero-shot disease classification, image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval rankings, and linear-probe supervised classification (AUROC).

## Method

EGMA uses a Swin Transformer (4 stages, patch size 4, window size 7) image encoder and a 6-layer BioClinicalBERT text encoder, both projected to 512 dimensions, trained with a composite contrastive loss guided by radiologist eye-gaze heatmaps. The EGF module replaces CLIP's global instance features with fine-grained patch-sentence similarity matrices (FILIP-style), then optimizes them via multi-label cross-entropy against a binarized gaze-guided label matrix, and feeds max-pooled local features back into the standard contrastive loss. The EGM module constructs alignment weight matrices by combining the gaze-guided similarity matrix with the model's own patch-sentence similarity (after sparsification and binarization), uses these weights to perform weighted cross-modality mapping (text→image and image→text), and applies contrastive loss between mapped and target features. The total loss is L_EGF + L_EGM, and the two modules together mimic the radiologist's diagnostic workflow of global visual search followed by focused local examination.

## Results

On supervised classification, EGMA achieves the best AUROC across all three datasets and data scales: 89.50 (CheXpert 100%), 90.10 (RSNA 100%), and 93.29 (SIIM-ACR 100%), outperforming the second-best by 1.11–1.94% even at 1% training data. On zero-shot retrieval (CheXpert 8x200), EGMA reaches 42.65% P@1 for image-to-text and 80.00% P@1 for text-to-image, surpassing the next-best methods by 3.9% and 19.75% respectively. On zero-shot classification, EGMA attains 61.30% accuracy on CheXpert 5x200 and 76.97% on RSNA, leading all baselines on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGMA outperforms all seven baselines (ConVIRT, BioViL, MedKLIP, MGCA, GLoRIA, PRIOR, MedCLIP) on supervised classification across every dataset and data proportion, with the largest margins at 1% training data (e.g., +1.94% over MedCLIP on SIIM-ACR). On zero-shot classification, EGMA leads on CheXpert 5x200 (61.30% Acc vs. MedCLIP's 57.50%) and RSNA accuracy (76.97% vs. PRIOR's 76.77%), but trails PRIOR on RSNA F1-score (43.49 vs. 51.80). On zero-shot retrieval, EGMA dominates both directions: image-to-text P@1 of 42.65% beats GLoRIA (38.75%) by 3.9%, and text-to-image P@1 of 80.00% beats ConVIRT (60.25%) by 19.75%. Notably, GLoRIA and MGCA underperform even the natural-image CLIP on SIIM-ACR zero-shot classification, highlighting their poor generalization, whereas EGMA maintains strong cross-dataset transfer. The ablation further shows that combining both EGF and EGM modules is necessary for consistent gains across all three datasets, as either module alone degrades on at least one dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm for medical vision-language pre-training by incorporating radiologists' eye-gaze data as an explicit alignment signal, building directly on FILIP's fine-grained patch-sentence alignment strategy. While prior medical VLP methods (ConVIRT, MedCLIP, GLoRIA, MGCA) learn alignment implicitly from paired image-text data, EGMA introduces gaze-derived attention heatmaps to supervise local feature alignment and cross-modality mapping, drawing on the eye-tracking-in-radiology literature (Khosravan, Krupinski, Nodine) that has previously been applied only to single-modality tasks like lesion detection or segmentation. The framework is the first to integrate eye-gaze data into the vision-language pre-training loop, positioning it as a bridge between the human visual search literature and multi-modal representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that even 185 samples of synchronized eye-gaze data (5% of the training set) can meaningfully improve multi-modal alignment offers a practical path for medical AI labs that lack large annotated datasets but can collect gaze data during routine diagnostic workflows. However, the approach is currently limited to chest X-rays and depends on the MIMIC-EYE dataset's synchronized audio-gaze-image collection, which is not yet standard in most radiology departments. Open questions include whether temporal gaze dynamics (saccade sequences, fixation durations) add value beyond static heatmaps, whether the framework generalizes to other imaging modalities (CT, ultrasound, MRI), and how to address privacy concerns inherent in eye-gaze data. The paper also does not evaluate downstream localization or segmentation tasks, leaving the full utility of gaze-guided alignment unexplored.
