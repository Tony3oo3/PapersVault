## Problem

Graph Neural Networks face two simultaneous challenges: sparse high-quality labels and limited node neighborhoods, yet existing graph augmentation methods typically address only one aspect while incurring extra training costs or relying on oversimplified strategies. Additionally, vanilla Mixup has been shown to perform poorly in node classification due to the inability to determine appropriate neighbors for cross-class mixed nodes, leading to the prior belief that Mixup is fundamentally incompatible with graph topology.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{N×D}, a small set of labeled nodes D_l, unlabeled nodes D_u, and a pre-trained GNN model f. It produces an augmented graph with m new nodes (generated via Intra-Class Mixup) and 2m new edges connecting each generated node to two high-confidence same-class nodes, which is then used for standard GNN training and node classification. The number of generated nodes m is a manual hyperparameter, and the method is designed so that m≪|V| to keep computational overhead negligible.

## Method

IntraMix performs Mixup exclusively among nodes sharing the same pseudo-label (Intra-Class Mixup), rather than across classes as in vanilla Mixup. This ensures generated nodes carry a single clean label and makes neighbor assignment straightforward, while Theorem 3.1 proves that the expected label noise is reduced by a factor of √(λ²+(1-λ)²)<1. For neighbor selection, the method identifies high-confidence same-class nodes via an ensemble of n dropout-perturbed inferences of the same GNN (no extra training cost), then connects each generated node to two randomly chosen high-confidence nodes of the same class. Theorem 3.2 shows this indirect connection through a generated node reduces noise propagation compared to directly linking the two high-confidence nodes, with the ratio controllable via learnable parameters η. The entire pipeline is a plug-and-play augmentation step that requires no architectural modifications to the underlying GNN.

## Results

On medium-scale semi-supervised node classification, IntraMix achieves the best accuracy across all four GNNs (GCN, GAT, SAGE, APPNP) and all five datasets. For GCN, IntraMix reaches 85.25% on Cora, 74.80% on CiteSeer, 82.98% on PubMed, 92.86% on CS, and 94.27% on Physics, outperforming the strongest baseline (LA-GCN) by 0.64–1.01 points. On large-scale graphs (ogbn-arxiv, Flickr), IntraMix with GCN achieves 64.84% (1% training) and 73.51% (100% training) on ogbn-arxiv, and 48.18% (1%) and 53.03% (100%) on Flickr, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntraMix consistently outperforms all seven baselines (GraphMix, CODA, DropMessage, MH-Aug, Local Augmentation, NodeMixup, and FLAG on large-scale graphs) across every GNN architecture and dataset. The closest competitor is Local Augmentation (LA-GCN/LA-GAT/LA-SAGE/LA-APPNP), which IntraMix surpasses by 0.64–1.01 points on medium-scale graphs (e.g., 85.25 vs. 84.61 on Cora with GCN; 85.99 vs. 85.42 on Cora with APPNP). NodeMixup, the most directly related Mixup-based method, trails IntraMix by 1.78–2.15 points (e.g., 83.47 vs. 85.25 on Cora with GCN), confirming that intra-class mixing with proper neighbor selection is critical. On large-scale graphs (ogbn-arxiv, Flickr), IntraMix leads FLAG by 1.16–1.66 points at 1% training and 0.47–1.16 points at 100% training. The gains are most pronounced for SAGE and APPNP, which rely heavily on neighborhood quality, validating IntraMix's neighbor selection strategy. In inductive settings (Table 3), IntraMix also leads LA-GAT and NodeMixup by 0.7–1.1 points on Cora and CiteSeer.

## Relation to Prior Work

IntraMix repositions Mixup (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) for graph node classification by restricting interpolation to same-class pairs, departing from the inter-class mixing that prior graph Mixup methods (GraphMix, NodeMixup, GraphMixup) adopted and that was shown to underperform. It leverages pseudo-labeling (Lee et al., 2013) as a low-cost source of the noisy labels that Intra-Class Mixup then denoises, and introduces a dropout-ensemble-based high-confidence node selection for neighborhood construction. Unlike prior graph augmentation methods (Local Augmentation, MH-Aug, CODA, DropMessage) that address either label scarcity or neighborhood sparsity in isolation, IntraMix unifies both into a single plug-and-play pipeline with theoretical noise-reduction guarantees (Theorems 3.1–3.2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, theoretically grounded explanation for why vanilla Mixup fails on graphs (topology/neighborhood ambiguity rather than the mixing operation itself) and shows that a simple intra-class restriction resolves this, making Mixup a viable standalone augmentation for node classification. Its plug-and-play design and negligible overhead make it broadly applicable across GNN architectures and graph types (homophilic, heterophilic, heterogeneous). Open questions include whether the homophily-based neighbor selection can be made adaptive for strongly heterophilic graphs, how the method behaves under extreme label sparsity beyond the 5% tested, and whether the approach extends naturally to link prediction or graph-level tasks.
