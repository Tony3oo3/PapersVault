## Problem

Existing Cognitive Diagnosis (CD) methods rely solely on inner-learner information (individual practice records and question features) to infer knowledge proficiency, leaving the collaborative signals among learners with similar cognitive states largely unexplored. Prior attempts at collaborative modeling in education depend on manually designed similarity metrics with fixed thresholds, while recent disentangled CD models (e.g., DCD) ignore inter-learner connections entirely. No prior work simultaneously models collaborative relationships and disentangled cognitive states for improved explainability and controllability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary practice records (correct/incorrect) between M learners and N questions, along with a question-to-concept association matrix mapping each question to one or more of C knowledge concepts. Each learner is assigned C factorized d-dimensional latent vectors (one per concept) initialized from a Gaussian mixture. The output is a co-disentangled cognitive state per learner (C vectors of dimension d), from which predicted answer probabilities on held-out questions are decoded; a scalar proficiency score per concept can be extracted by averaging the d dimensions of the corresponding vector.

## Method

Coral comprises three coupled stages. First, a β-TCVAE-inspired encoder produces C disentangled cognitive vectors per learner by reconstructing practice responses through a diagnosis function that combines concept-specific proficiency with a learned comprehensive ability parameter, enforcing dimension-level independence via a β-weighted KL term. Second, a context-aware collaborative graph construction iteratively selects K neighbors per learner per concept using an InfoNCE matching score computed over relative representations (differences from the target learner), yielding C concept-specific graphs without any hand-crafted similarity threshold. Third, attention-weighted node aggregation over each graph produces collaborative states that are residual-added to the initial states (co-disentanglement), and the combined representation is decoded to reconstruct practice performance. The full training objective jointly minimizes BCE reconstruction (initial + collaborative), the β-KL penalty, and the InfoNCE graph-construction loss, and the collaborative learning procedure is proven equivalent to an EM algorithm that monotonically increases marginal likelihood.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets (ASSIST, Junyi, NeurIPS2020EC), Coral achieves the best F1-score and RMSE on ASSIST (81.16% F1, 43.66 RMSE), the best AUC, F1-score, and RMSE on Junyi (80.94% AUC, 89.12% F1, 36.08 RMSE), and the best scores on all four metrics on NeurIPS2020EC (71.72% ACC, 78.88% AUC, 72.82% F1, 43.20 RMSE). Coral slightly trails KaNCD on ACC (71.53 vs. 72.43) and AUC (74.72 vs. 75.38) on ASSIST, and on ACC on Junyi (81.15 vs. 81.79), but consistently leads on F1-score and RMSE across all datasets. The paper also demonstrates robustness in sparse (up to 80% data discarded) and cold-start scenarios, where Coral with K=10 maintains strong performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Coral achieves the best F1-score and RMSE on ASSIST (81.16% F1, 43.66 RMSE), outperforming the closest competitors RCD (80.60% F1, 43.74 RMSE) and PMF (80.68% F1), though it trails KaNCD on ACC (71.53 vs. 72.43) and AUC (74.72 vs. 75.38). On Junyi, Coral edges out KaNCD on AUC (80.94 vs. 80.93), F1 (89.12 vs. 89.02), and RMSE (36.08 vs. 36.11) but falls slightly behind on ACC (81.15 vs. 81.79). On NeurIPS2020EC, Coral dominates all four metrics, beating the next-best NCDM by 0.06% ACC, 0.31% AUC, 1.46% F1, and 0.01 RMSE. The classical IRT and MIRT baselines are consistently the weakest across all datasets, with gaps of 1–4% in AUC. The disentangled VAE-based DCD underperforms Coral on all metrics, confirming that collaborative graph learning adds value beyond disentanglement alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Coral sits at the intersection of three research threads: variational cognitive diagnosis (building on DCD's β-TCVAE-inspired disentanglement of per-concept learner states), context-aware graph construction (adapted from self-paced graph contrastive learning for iterative neighbor search), and collaborative filtering in education (where prior work relies on hand-crafted similarity metrics). The key departure is that no prior CD model simultaneously models inter-learner collaborative connections and disentangled cognitive representations; existing CD methods focus exclusively on inner-learner signals (practice records, question features), while existing collaborative education methods use fixed similarity functions without disentanglement. The paper formalizes the collaborative graph construction as an EM procedure with a convergence guarantee, providing theoretical grounding absent from prior heuristic approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new direction in learner modeling by showing that implicit collaborative signals among similar learners can improve cognitive diagnosis, particularly in sparse and cold-start settings where individual practice data is insufficient. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines (KaNCD, NCDM) are modest—often within 0.5–1% on ACC—and the acknowledged O(M×K) computational cost per learner limits scalability to large educational platforms. Open questions include whether the inferred collaborative graph captures genuinely meaningful peer relationships versus spurious correlations, how the approach extends to multi-modal or LLM-augmented settings, and whether fairness considerations (e.g., avoiding bias propagation through the collaborative graph) can be addressed.
