## Problem

Traditional spatiotemporal models are trained independently per task under i.i.d. assumptions, leaving them unable to generalize to evolving urban data patterns or adapt to newly introduced tasks without costly retraining. No existing framework systematically exploits interdependencies across data dimensions (spatial, temporal, contextual) and across heterogeneous urban data domains to enable task-level cooperation and collective intelligence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 12-step historical window of spatiotemporal observations (T×N×C), longitude/latitude coordinates, and temporal indicators (day-of-week, time-of-day, 6-dim timestamp features), all mapped through separate MLPs and concatenated with a task-specific prompt (72-dim, derived from autoencoded periodic data summaries) into a unified T×N×d_h representation. It outputs 12-step future predictions at the same spatial resolution, with all data normalized to zero mean and unit variance before processing and denormalized for evaluation.

## Method

CMuST combines two core modules: (1) MSTI, a multi-dimensional interaction network that applies multi-head cross-attention between spatial/temporal context indicators and main observations (in both directions), followed by self-attention within each dimension, to disentangle and capture multi-level spatiotemporal dependencies; and (2) RoAda, a rolling adaptation training scheme with two phases — a warm-up phase that tracks per-element weight variance across sequential task transitions and freezes weights below a threshold δ to encode task-level commonality, and a refinement phase that fine-tunes the remaining dynamic weights with task-specific prompts (constructed via sampling-autoencoding of periodic data patterns) while keeping stable weights frozen. Huber loss is used for outlier robustness, and the iterative rolling process across tasks progressively consolidates shared interaction patterns while preserving task-specific diversity through prompts.

## Results

CMuST achieves the best or near-best MAE and MAPE across all 9 tasks spanning three cities (NYC, SIP, Chicago). On NYC, CMuST attains MAE of 11.15 (Crowd In), 12.91 (Crowd Out), 6.76 (Taxi Pick), and 5.85 (Taxi Drop), with MAPE ranging from 0.31 to 0.44. On SIP, it reaches MAE 11.58 (Traffic Flow) and 0.684 (Traffic Speed); on Chicago, MAE 2.33 (Taxi Pick), 2.00 (Taxi Drop), and 1.117 (Risk). Multi-task baselines (PromptST, STEP, MTGNN) improve over single-task methods by an average of 8.51%, and CMuST further outperforms the strongest multi-task baseline PromptST on 17 out of 18 metric-task combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMuST outperforms the strongest multi-task baseline PromptST on 17 of 18 metric-task pairs, with the sole exception being NYC Crowd In MAE (11.15 vs. PromptST's 11.00). Against single-task baselines, CMuST achieves substantial gains: e.g., on NYC Crowd In, CMuST's MAE of 11.15 beats DCRNN (17.53) by 36% and GWNET (11.44) by 2.5%; on SIP Traffic Flow, CMuST (11.58) surpasses the best single-task method GWNET (13.05) by 11%. Among multi-task baselines, CMuST consistently leads: on Chicago Taxi Pick, it achieves MAE 2.33 vs. PromptST's 2.36 and STEP's 2.36; on SIP Traffic Speed, MAE 0.684 vs. PromptST's 0.692. The unified models UniST and UniTime (Table 5) also trail CMuST on most tasks, e.g., UniST achieves MAE 11.39 on NYC Crowd In vs. CMuST's 11.15, and UniTime degrades further to 12.29. In cold-start experiments (Table 6), CMuST adapts to a new task faster (e.g., 1,571 s vs. 2,132 s for single-task on NYC Crowd In) while simultaneously achieving lower error, confirming the benefit of shared task-level commonality extracted through RoAda.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three established lines of work: single-task spatiotemporal forecasting (DCRNN, GWNET, ASTGCN), multi-task learning for urban data (MTGNN, STEP, PromptST), and continual learning for traffic prediction (TrafficStream, PECPM). While prior multi-task ST methods jointly train on multiple tasks with shared representations, and continual learning methods handle sequential data updates, no prior work systematically combines sequential task rolling with explicit commonality/diversity extraction in a spatiotemporal setting. The MSTI module repurposes multi-head cross-attention (from transformer literature) to disentangle context-observation interactions, while RoAda introduces a novel weight-behavior modeling strategy that has no direct precedent in either the MTL or CL literature for ST data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in urban intelligence: the 'isolation' problem where each task (taxi demand, crowd flow, traffic speed, accident risk) is trained independently, wasting cross-task correlations and suffering cold-start when new tasks emerge. The cold-start experiments showing ~26% faster adaptation and better accuracy for new tasks, combined with robustness under data scarcity, suggest practical value for dynamic urban systems. However, the paper is limited to a single urban system per city and the transportation domain only; generalization to cross-city transfer, non-transportation domains (energy, environment), and open urban systems remains open. The modest performance gains over strong multi-task baselines like PromptST (1-3% MAE) raise questions about whether the added complexity of rolling adaptation is justified in all settings.
