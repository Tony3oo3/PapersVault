## Problem

Vision Transformers are inherently designed to vary by width, making smaller ViTs (e.g., ViT-Ti/S) sub-networks of larger ones (e.g., ViT-B), yet existing slimmable network methods fail on ViTs due to their minimal interpolation ability at unseen widths. Additionally, the constant activation of the smallest sub-network during training negatively impacts other subnets because their weights are shared in a nested fashion, creating a dilemma between preserving lower-bound performance and optimizing intermediate variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single full-size ViT (e.g., DeiT-B with 86M parameters) and, at inference time, produces multiple smaller variants by adjusting one hyperparameter r (width ratio) that uniformly slices the embedding dimension at every layer. The smallest variant (r=0.25) is roughly 16× smaller in width than the full model, and the slicing granularity ε=0.0625 yields up to 13 distinct sub-networks from one trained model. Unlike slimmable CNNs that require Batch Normalization calibration before inference, the ViT-based sub-networks use Layer Normalization and can be directly evaluated at any trained width ratio without additional operations.

## Method

Scala is a one-shot training framework that makes a ViT slimmable without modifying its architecture. Its first component, Isolated Activation, slices the smallest sub-network's weights in reverse (from the end of the weight matrix) while all other subnets slice from the beginning, disentangling the smallest subnet's representation from the nested shared weights. Its second component, Scale Coordination, comprises three techniques: Progressive Knowledge Transfer uses intermediate sub-networks as teacher assistants to bridge the large capacity gap between the full and smallest models via KL divergence on distillation tokens; Stable Sampling divides the slicing bound into two halves to constrain the random intermediate width ratios m₁ and m₂, preventing large capacity swings between the four activated subnets; and Noise Calibration combines Cross-Entropy loss with the KL distillation loss to prevent noisy early-stage teacher predictions from dominating optimization. During training, four sub-networks (smallest, largest, and two random intermediates) are activated per iteration following the sandwich rule, with gradients accumulated, and the full pre-trained model serves as the external teacher.

## Results

On DeiT-S (100-epoch training, ImageNet-1K), Scala achieves 58.7% top-1 accuracy at r=0.25, 68.3% at r=0.50, 73.3% at r=0.75, and 76.1% at r=1.00, outperforming Separate Training (ST) at all width ratios while using only 22M parameters (vs. 43M for ST). On DeiT-B (300-epoch training), Scala with a RegNetY-16GF teacher achieves 82.9% at r=1.00 and 75.4% at r=0.25, outperforming SN-Net by an average of 1.6% across all width ratios with fewer parameters (86M vs. 118M). Scala also transfers to semantic segmentation (ADE20K), where it outperforms ST by 1.1–0.8 mIoU across width ratios, and to fine-grained classification where Scala-B distilled from DINOv2-B achieves 84.2% average accuracy across 12 datasets at r=1.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoFormer [4], Scala wins decisively at all width ratios, with the largest gap at r=0.25 (58.7% vs. 50.8%, +7.9%) and r=1.00 (76.1% vs. 69.8%, +6.3%), confirming that supernet-style multi-dimensional slicing is ill-suited for the large width-only slicing bound targeted here. Against US-Net [37], Scala outperforms by 6.0% at r=0.25, 8.2% at r=0.50, 6.4% at r=0.75, and 2.9% at r=1.00, demonstrating that the sandwich rule alone is insufficient for ViTs due to their minimal interpolation ability. Against SN-Net [25] on DeiT-B (300 epochs), Scala with a RegNetY-16GF teacher achieves an average 1.6% improvement across all seven width ratios (e.g., 75.4% vs. 70.6% at r=0.25; 82.9% vs. 81.9% at r=1.00) while using 86M parameters versus SN-Net's 118M. Against MatFormer [18], Scala achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at s=0.5 but offers a significantly broader computational adjustment scope (ranging from 0.25 to 1.0) since MatFormer only scales the FFN block. Notably, Scala also surpasses Separate Training (which trains each width independently for the full budget) at most width ratios with a single training run, reducing storage from 43M to 22M parameters on DeiT-S.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scala extends the slimmable network paradigm established by US-Net (Yu & Huang, ICCV 2019) from CNNs to Vision Transformers, but departs fundamentally from it by showing that the sandwich rule and uniform slicing that work on CNNs fail on ViTs due to their minimal interpolation ability. Unlike NAS supernet approaches (Once-for-All, BigNAS, AutoFormer) that slice across multiple dimensions with small computation changes, or MatFormer which only scales FFN blocks, Scala performs uniform width slicing across the entire ViT with a large slicing bound (up to 16× capacity ratio) and fine-grained granularity. The key architectural insight—that ViT-Ti/S/B are inherently the same architecture differing only in embedding width—positions this as a natural extension of ViT design rather than an artificial compression, distinguishing it from CNN slicing where width reduction produces unconventional structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Scala offers a practical path toward flexible inference on edge devices by enabling a single ViT to serve as 13+ smaller variants with one-shot training, matching Separate Training performance while reducing storage from 163M to 86M parameters. The discovery that ViTs lack the interpolation ability that makes CNN slimmable networks work is a useful negative result that redirects future work away from simply porting CNN slimmable techniques to transformers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to foundation-model-scale ViTs (e.g., 2B+ parameters), whether training efficiency can be improved beyond the current multi-subnet activation overhead, and whether the slimmable representation transfers to generative or multimodal transformer architectures beyond classification and segmentation.
