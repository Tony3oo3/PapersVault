## Problem

Quantizing LLM activations to 4 bits is fundamentally difficult because hidden states contain large-magnitude outlier features that dominate the quantization range, making uniform low-precision representation inaccurate. Prior approaches either identify and retain these outliers in higher precision (requiring calibration sets and mixed-precision kernels) or only quantize weights, leaving activations and KV caches in 16 bits. No prior method achieves true end-to-end 4-bit inference where every matrix multiplication operates in integer precision without any retained high-precision channels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B) and produces a modified model where all weight matrices are pre-rotated with fused Hadamard matrices and the forward pass includes 1.5 online Hadamard transforms per transformer layer. At inference, activations are quantized on-the-fly using per-token symmetric INT4 (clipping ratio 0.9), KV caches use group-wise asymmetric INT4 (group size 128, clipping ratio 0.95), and weights use GPTQ per-channel symmetric INT4. The output is a fully quantized model where all GEMMs execute as INT4×INT4→INT32 on TensorCores, with dequantization to FP16 only after accumulation.

## Method

QuaRot exploits the computational invariance property of transformers: multiplying a block's input weight matrices by an orthogonal matrix Q and the output weight matrices by Q^T leaves the model output unchanged, since RMSNorm commutes with rotations. By fusing a randomized Hadamard matrix Q into all weight matrices, the hidden state between blocks is effectively rotated, eliminating outlier features from activations at zero runtime cost. Within each block, additional online Hadamard transforms are inserted: one before the FFN down-projection (fused into W_down), and head-wise transforms on queries, keys, and values in attention (with a cross-head transform before the output projection). For the KV cache, keys and values are rotated after RoPE is applied (Post-RoPE caching), so cached entries are outlier-free and quantizable to 4 bits. Weights are quantized with GPTQ, and the entire forward pass performs all matrix multiplications in INT4 on TensorCores, achieving up to 3.33× prefill speedup and 3.89× memory saving on LLaMA2-70B with at most 0.47 WikiText-2 perplexity loss.

## Results

On 4-bit quantization of LLAMA-2 models (all weights, activations, and KV cache in INT4), QuaRot with GPTQ achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 6.10 (7B), 5.40 (13B), and 3.79 (70B), compared to FP16 baselines of 5.47, 4.88, and 3.32 respectively — a maximum loss of 0.47 perplexity on the 70B model. On zero-shot tasks, QuaRot preserves 99% of the FP16 average accuracy on LLAMA2-70B (75.98 vs 77.07) and 90% on LLAMA2-7B (65.64 vs 69.82). With group-wise quantization (128G), the 70B perplexity improves further to 3.61. For 8-bit quantization with simple round-to-nearest (no calibration), QuaRot is effectively lossless (3.33 vs 3.32 perplexity on 70B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuaRot decisively outperforms all prior 4-bit joint quantization methods. Against SmoothQuant (which uses calibration-based scaling), QuaRot reduces 7B perplexity from 83.12 to 6.10 — a 13.6× improvement — and against OmniQuant from 14.26 to 6.10. Compared to QUIK-4B (which retains 256 outlier features in higher precision), QuaRot achieves 2.77 lower perplexity on 7B (6.10 vs 8.87) and 3.12 lower on 70B (3.79 vs 6.91), while requiring zero higher-precision channels. Against Atom-128G (which keeps 128 outlier features and uses complex mixed-precision kernels), QuaRot-128G matches or slightly beats Atom on 7B (5.93 vs 6.03) and ties on 13B (5.26), while extending to 70B (3.61) where Atom has no reported result. The key differentiator is that QuaRot requires no outlier identification, no calibration set for activation quantization, and no mixed-precision operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuaRot extends the incoherence processing idea from QuIP and QuIP# (which applied Hadamard rotations to weight matrices for weight-only quantization) to the full inference pipeline, covering activations and KV cache. The key enabler is the computational invariance theorem from SliceGPT, which allows fusing the rotation matrices directly into the weights so that the model output is unchanged and no extra rotation is needed at runtime for the weight side. Unlike QuIP#, which requires two Hadamard transforms per weight matrix during the forward pass, QuaRot needs only 1.5 online Hadamard transforms per transformer layer. The paper also departs from prior KV-cache quantization work (KVQuant, KIVI) that handled outliers via feature-wise quantization or mixed precision, instead eliminating outliers at the source via rotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve end-to-end 4-bit inference (weights, activations, and KV cache) without retaining any channels in higher precision, yielding 3.3× prefill speedups and 3.9× memory savings on a 70B model. The finding that 6- and 8-bit quantization becomes essentially lossless with plain round-to-nearest (no GPTQ, no calibration) suggests the rotation alone resolves most of the quantization difficulty at moderate bit-widths. Open questions include extending the approach to mixture-of-experts architectures, quantizing the residual stream itself, and whether the technique transfers cleanly to newer model families (LLAMA-3 shows notably higher quantization sensitivity). The paper also notes that end-to-end INT4 inference could match the throughput of NVIDIA's B200 FP4 architecture at much lower hardware cost.
