## Problem

Existing SNN-based QUBO solvers for combinatorial optimization rely on penalty terms with slack variables to enforce feasibility constraints. For hypergraph minimum vertex cover (HMVC), each constraint requires r−1 slack variables, causing the search space to scale quadratically with problem size and rendering the SNN solver unable to return feasible solutions. This paper asks whether a handcrafted SNN can solve HMVC directly without QUBO reformulation or slack variables.

## Input / Output

The method takes an r-uniform hypergraph H=(V,F) with N vertices and K hyperedges, encoded as a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×K} where a_{ik}=1 if vertex i is incident to hyperedge f_k. The SNN runs for M=1000 algorithmic timesteps on Intel Loihi 2, and the best binary state configuration z ∈ {0,1}^N probed across timesteps is evaluated off-chip as the vertex cover. The co-occurrence matrix F=AA^T is precomputed and used as the NEBM-to-NEBM weight submatrix.

## Method

SF-HMVC constructs a hybrid SNN of N non-equilibrium Boltzmann machine (NEBM) neurons (one per vertex variable) and K custom feedback (FB) neurons (one per constraint), with a block weight matrix [F, A; A^T, 0]. Each FB neuron deterministically activates when all its connected NEBM neurons are inactive (i.e., the corresponding hyperedge is uncovered), then emits a negative delta spike that excites the connected NEBM neurons to restore feasibility. NEBM neurons accumulate inhibitory signals from co-occurring NEBM neurons (via F=AA^T, penalizing redundant selections) and excitatory signals from violated-constraint FB neurons (via A), then stochastically switch state per the Boltzmann distribution. This constraint-checking-and-correction mechanism replaces the penalty-plus-slack-variable QUBO formulation entirely, reducing the neuron count from N+(r−1)K to N+K and eliminating the need to tune a penalty weight λ.

## Results

On 11 synthetic HMVC instances, SF-HMVC-Loihi matches the optimal ILP-CPU solution on 9/11 instances (e.g., 3/3, 9/9, 15/15, 5/5, 8/8, 10/10, 4/4, 20/20) and is within 9 vertices on the remaining two (29 vs 22, 19 vs 10). In stark contrast, QUBO-Loihi returns infeasible solutions with 68 or 1214 constraint violations on the two instances it can embed, and cannot be embedded at all on 9/11 instances due to slack-variable bloat. Energy consumption of SF-HMVC-Loihi ranges from 0.01 J to 1.38 J, compared to 14,748–110,916 J for QUBO-CPU and 18.9–269.4 J for QUBO-Loihi, representing 3–5 orders of magnitude savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ILP-CPU (Gurobi, globally optimal), SF-HMVC-Loihi matches the optimal vertex cover size on 9 of 11 HMVC instances and is within 9 vertices on the other two; it loses on solution quality but wins by 3–5 orders of magnitude on energy (≤1.38 J vs. up to 15.79 J) and is faster on most instances (1.0–6.0 s vs. 0.00–0.26 s for ILP-CPU, though ILP-CPU is faster on small instances). Against QUBO-Loihi (NEBM-based SNN solving the slack-variable QUBO), SF-HMVC-Loihi wins decisively: QUBO-Loihi either returns infeasible solutions (68 or 1214 constraint violations) or cannot be embedded on Loihi 2 at all (N/A on 9/11 instances), while SF-HMVC-Loihi handles every instance with feasible, near-optimal covers. Against QUBO-CPU (Gurobi on the slack-variable QUBO), SF-HMVC-Loihi occasionally finds better solutions (e.g., 5 vs. 15 on HMVC11, 29 vs. 30 on HMVC04) and is 100–1000× faster in runtime (1–6 s vs. 198 s to >3600 s) with 4–5 orders of magnitude less energy. On the simpler MVC task (DIMACS), SF-HMVC-Loihi is comparable to QUBO-Loihi in solution quality and runtime, and both are slightly worse than the globally optimal ILP-CPU/QUBO-CPU, but SF-HMVC-Loihi still consumes 10–1000× less energy than the CPU solvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NEBM-based SNN for QUBO (Pierro et al., 2024) and the broader line of handcrafted SNNs for constraint satisfaction problems (Jonke et al., Sudoku solvers, graph coloring). It departs from the standard pipeline of reformulating constrained combinatorial problems as QUBO with penalty terms and slack variables, instead introducing a custom feedback neuron that directly encodes and enforces linear inequality constraints without any penalty weight tuning. The key conceptual shift is moving from an energy-minimization framing (Ising/QUBO) to a constraint-checking-and-correction mechanism embedded in the SNN topology, which eliminates the slack variables that cause the search space to scale quadratically with problem size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The slack-variable bottleneck identified here is a fundamental obstacle for applying SNN-based QUBO solvers to any constrained problem with multi-variable linear constraints, and the feedback-neuron mechanism offers a generalizable pattern for bypassing it. However, the evidence is limited to a single Loihi 2 chip with no error bars (due to hardware RNG constraints), synthetic instances plus a small set of DIMACS-derived MVC instances, and no public HMVC benchmark exists to validate on real-world data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger instances on multi-chip systems, whether it generalizes to other constrained optimization families (set cover, hitting set), and whether any theoretical guarantees on solution quality or convergence can be established for the heuristic dynamics.
