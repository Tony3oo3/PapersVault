## Problem

Adapting LLMs to downstream tasks currently relies on either In-Context Learning, which inflates input length and KV-Cache (creating memory I/O bottlenecks under the Roofline model), or Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, which still requires gradient descent, computation-graph storage, and substantial hardware. No existing paradigm can adapt a model to a new task without simultaneously increasing inference cost or demanding a training pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, RTD consumes the same text prompt as a vanilla LLM (no extra few-shot tokens or retrieved passages) plus a pre-built reference datastore of (last-hidden-state, label) pairs generated offline via forward passes only. At each decoding step it emits a modified next-token probability distribution formed by a convex blend of the standard LM_Head output and a reference distribution derived from top-K nearest neighbors in the datastore, governed by a single blending hyperparameter λ. For multi-head RTD, the hidden vector is split into n_h head-sized sub-vectors, each querying its own sub-datastore before the per-head reference distributions are averaged.

## Method

RTD inserts a retrieval-based bypass around the LM_Head at the decoding stage, leaving both the prompt and all model parameters untouched. A reference datastore is constructed by running forward passes over training examples and storing the final hidden state as the key and the ground-truth label (or next-token id) as the value. During decoding, the last hidden state h^(l) is compared via Euclidean distance against all datastore keys; the top-K nearest neighbors are selected, their negative distances are temperature-scaled and softmax-normalized, and the resulting weights are aggregated per label to yield a reference distribution r. The final token distribution is the convex combination λ·r + (1−λ)·p, where p is the original LM_Head output. The multi-head variant (MH-RTD) partitions h^(l) into n_h sub-vectors of dimension d_h, queries head-specific sub-datastores independently, and averages the n_h reference distributions, preserving overall O(d_m · s_L) complexity while enabling head pruning for further resource savings.

## Results

On the language understanding benchmarks (Table 2), RTD achieves an average score of 63.31 across six models (6B–70B) and five benchmarks, compared to 56.41 for zero-shot baseline and 65.28 for 5-shot ICL. When combined with ICL (5-shot RTD), the method reaches 68.88 average, outperforming ICL alone by 3.6 points. In the Wikipedia knowledge-injection task (Table 8), Wiki RTD achieves 44.4% accuracy at 46.5 ms latency, substantially outperforming Wiki RAG (29.0%, >200 ms) and the baseline (39.0%, 42.5 ms). On the Tiny-Shakespeare style-transfer task, RTD reduces perplexity from 1.6982 (baseline) to 1.4501, approaching LoRA's 1.3710.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 5-shot ICL (Brown et al., 2020), RTD alone (63.31 avg) is slightly below ICL (65.28 avg), but the combined 5-shot RTD (68.88) clearly surpasses ICL by 3.6 points; RTD wins on individual benchmarks like PIQA (LLaMA2-7B: 71.4 vs. 62.1) but trails ICL on ARC (41.4 vs. 65.0), suggesting ICL's prompt-level guidance is still valuable for reasoning-heavy tasks. Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), RTD achieves a marginal edge on MMLU (42.8 vs. 42.5 on GLM3-6B) but underperforms on the Tiny-Shakespeare style-transfer task (PPL 1.4501 vs. 1.3710), indicating LoRA's parameter updates are more effective for fine-grained stylistic adaptation. Against RAG (Lewis et al., 2020), RTD delivers a decisive win on the Wikipedia knowledge-injection task: 44.4% accuracy at 46.5 ms versus RAG's 29.0% at >200 ms, a 15.4-point accuracy gain with roughly 4× lower latency, largely because RAG's retrieved context (~6200 tokens) exceeds LLaMA2-7B-Chat's 4096-token pre-training window while RTD avoids inflating the input sequence entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTD adapts the nearest-neighbor language model (NNL) paradigm of Khandelwal et al. (ICLR 2020) — storing (key, value) pairs and forming a label distribution from retrieved neighbors — but repurposes it as a post-hoc bypass around the LM_Head rather than a standalone model. The multi-head variant draws on the GQA/MHA head-splitting mechanism (Ainslie et al., EMNLP 2023) to partition the hidden vector into per-head sub-datastores. Conceptually, RTD carves out a third axis of intervention (the decoding/output stage) distinct from ICL's prompt-stage optimization and PEFT's parameter-stage modification, positioning itself as orthogonal to both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTD offers practitioners a training-free, input-length-preserving alternative to ICL and PEFT for downstream task adaptation, with the practical advantage of being composable with both. The ~3× inference speedup over ICL (Table 7) and the ability to match LoRA-level gains without any gradient computation make it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. Key open questions remain around the memory footprint of large reference datastores (especially for generative tasks with ~73M Wikipedia entries), whether gradient-free redundancy detection can shrink these stores, and whether the approach generalizes beyond multiple-choice and short-generation settings to open-ended long-form generation.
