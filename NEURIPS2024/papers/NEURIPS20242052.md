## Problem

While stochastic policies are known to outperform deterministic ones under chance constraints in finite state/action spaces, the theoretical foundation for uncountable spaces remains open, and no practical algorithm exists to obtain optimal stochastic policies with joint chance constraints. Additionally, joint chance constraints are computationally intractable to solve directly, requiring a principled conservative approximation that preserves safety guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector s and a safety threshold α (violation probability) as input. The output is a binary action selection: two action candidates a_(1)(s) and a_(2)(s) chosen with a flipping probability w(s) ∈ [0,1]. In the theoretical setting (Theorem 2), the flipping probability is state-dependent; in the practical implementation (Algorithm 1), it is a fixed constant because the optimization considers the expectation over initial states. Action candidates are realized via deterministic policies or Gaussian distributions with small added noise.

## Method

The paper establishes a Bellman equation for CCMDPs (Theorem 1) and proves via Carathéodory's theorem and the supporting hyperplane theorem that an optimal policy exists as a discrete binary distribution over two actions (Theorem 2). To handle the intractability of joint chance constraints, Theorem 5 shows that an expected cumulative safety constraint with sufficiently large discount factor γ_unsafe and small horizon T conservatively approximates the joint constraint, enabling use of existing safe RL algorithms like CPO. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) trains S deterministic policies at different safety levels using an existing safe RL algorithm, then solves a linear program to extract the optimal two-policy combination with a fixed flipping probability. The flipping mechanism effectively linearly combines a high-performing but risky policy with a safe but lower-performing policy, achieving strictly better reward than any single deterministic policy when the reward-vs-safety tradeoff curve is strictly convex (Theorem 6).

## Results

In the numerical driving example, the flipping-based policy achieves a mean reward of 1.8259 versus 0.8667 for the deterministic policy at the same 17% violation probability, a more than 2× improvement. On Safety Gym (PointGoal2), the flipping-based policy increases expected reward under the same expected cost limit for both CPO and PCPO at intervals where the reward-cost profile is convex, while showing no improvement at non-convex intervals. The flipping-based policy also introduces slightly broader confidence intervals in practice due to two sources of Gaussian noise in the implementation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the flipping-based policy against four safe RL baselines: CPO [1], PCPO [48], P3O [50], and CUP [47], all evaluated on Safety Gym (PointGoal2 and CarGoal2). The flipping-based policy is applied as a post-hoc transformation on top of each baseline's trained deterministic policy, so the comparison is between the original deterministic policy and its flipping-based counterpart under the same cost limit. For CPO and PCPO on PointGoal2, the flipping-based policy significantly increases expected reward at convex intervals of the reward-cost curve while maintaining the same expected cost, consistent with Theorem 6's prediction that strict convexity implies better performance. At non-convex intervals, the flipping-based policy does not improve reward, matching the theoretical condition. The paper notes that the degree of improvement depends on the original algorithm's performance, with the flipping mechanism essentially forming a linear combination of a risky high-performing policy and a safe lower-performing policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that stochastic policies can outperform deterministic ones under chance constraints—previously established only for finite state/action spaces (Altman 1995; Chen et al. 2020) or with known models (Shen & Ito 2024)—to general MDPs with uncountable state and action spaces. It builds directly on CPO (Achiam et al. 2017) as the practical training backbone and introduces a Bellman equation for CCMDPs, a theoretical object not previously available. The key conceptual departure is the characterization of optimal policies as 'flipping-based' (binary distributions over two action candidates), which reduces an infinite-dimensional probability measure optimization to a finite-dimensional problem via Carathéodory's theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general-space theoretical justification for why stochastic policies help under joint chance constraints, closing a gap explicitly identified in the introduction. The conservative approximation from joint chance constraints to ECSCs (Theorem 5) is practically important because it enables existing safe RL toolkits (OmniSafe, CPO, PCPO) to approximate the theoretically desirable chance-constrained solution. Key limitations include: the practical algorithm uses a fixed (not state-dependent) flipping probability, requires training multiple deterministic policies at different constraint levels, and has a gap for non-smooth constraint functions. Open questions include designing an end-to-end differentiable training procedure for the state-dependent flipping policy and extending results to non-smooth or multi-constraint settings.
