## Problem

Existing uniformity testers achieve optimal Θ(√n/ε²) sample complexity but are only guaranteed correct when the input distribution is either uniform or ε-far from uniform; on intermediate distributions they can behave arbitrarily, causing two independent runs to disagree and undermining scientific reproducibility. This paper asks: what is the sample complexity of uniformity testing when the algorithm must be ρ-replicable (two independent runs agree with probability ≥ 1−ρ) for *all* distributions, not just those satisfying the promise?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes m = Õ(√n/(ε²ρ) + 1/(ρ²ε²)) i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution p on the discrete domain [n], partitioned into m₀ = Θ(log(1/ρ)) independent batches. It outputs a single binary decision: ACCEPT if p is uniform, REJECT if d_TV(p, U_n) ≥ ε. The critical additional constraint beyond standard testing is the replicability guarantee: for any distribution p (including those in the 'gray zone' between uniform and ε-far), two independent runs must produce the same output with probability at least 1−ρ.

## Method

The algorithm computes the total-variation-distance statistic S = (1/2)Σ|X_i/m − 1/n| on each of m₀ independent batches, takes the median S_median, and compares it against a random threshold r drawn uniformly from [μ(U_n)+R/4, μ(U_n)+3R/4], where R is the expectation gap from Diakonikolas et al. [16]. The key insight is that TV depends only linearly on each count X_i (unlike collision or χ² statistics which depend on X_i²), yielding tighter concentration: when Var(S) is large due to heavy elements, the expected value of S is simultaneously large enough to guarantee consistent rejection; when Var(S) is small, Chebyshev's inequality ensures S_median concentrates within ρR/16 of its mean, so the random threshold falls between two runs with probability O(ρ). The matching lower bound for symmetric testers uses a parametric family p(ξ) (half elements at (1+ξ)/n, half at (1−ξ)/n) combined with a Local Swap Family to randomize heavy/light label assignments, then an information-theoretic argument (via Poissonization and mutual information bounds) shows that no symmetric tester with m = Õ(√n/(ε²ρ)) samples can distinguish p(ξ) from p(ξ+δ) for δ < ερ, forcing non-replicable behavior on an Ω(ρ)-fraction of the parameter range.

## Results

The paper establishes that replicable uniformity testing on [n] requires and suffices with $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n}/(\varepsilon^2 \rho) + 1/(\rho^2 \varepsilon^2))$ samples (Theorem 1.3), with a matching lower bound $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{n}/(\varepsilon^2 \rho) + 1/(\rho^2 \varepsilon^2))$ for symmetric algorithms (Theorem 1.6). The key improvement over the naive black-box reduction is a linear (rather than quadratic) dependence on the replicability parameter $\rho$ in the dominant $\sqrt{n}$ term. For the special case $n=2$ (coin testing), the known tight bound remains $\tilde{\Theta}(\varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-2})$, confirming that the $\rho^{-1}$ improvement is specific to the multi-element regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the natural black-box reduction that treats an optimal non-replicable uniformity tester as a coin flip and applies a replicable coin tester, which yields $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n}/(\varepsilon^2 \rho^2))$ samples. The proposed algorithm shaves one factor of $\rho$ to achieve $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n}/(\varepsilon^2 \rho))$ in the dominant term, which the authors note is the first replicable learning algorithm with nearly linear dependence on $\rho$. Compared to tolerant testing (which achieves barely sublinear $\Theta(n/\log n)$ sample complexity even for constant $\rho$), the replicable tester remains strongly sublinear in $n$. The lower bound (Theorem 1.6) matches the upper bound up to polylogarithmic factors for the class of symmetric algorithms, which includes all known uniformity testers. The $\tilde{\Omega}(\varepsilon^{-2}\rho^{-2})$ lower bound for $n=2$ from [30] is subsumed as the second term in the general bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the long-established theory of uniformity testing (Goldreich-Ron, Paninski, Valiant-Valiant, Diakonikolas et al.) and the newer replicability framework introduced by Impagliazzo et al. (STOC 2022). The algorithmic core repurposes the total-variation-distance statistic from Diakonikolas et al. (ICALP 2018), which was previously used for high-probability identity testing, and augments it with a random thresholding mechanism to achieve replicability. The lower bound proof adapts the minimax-style framework from Impagliazzo et al. and introduces a Local Swap Family construction with Poissonization and Kleitman's correlation inequality to handle the symmetric-algorithm restriction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that replicable uniformity testing requires only Õ(√n·ε⁻²·ρ⁻¹) samples in the dominant term—rather than the ρ⁻² overhead suggested by a naive black-box reduction—establishes that replicability need not come at a quadratic cost, which is the first such near-linear dependence on ρ for any replicable learning algorithm. The matching lower bound for symmetric testers (which includes all known uniformity testers) strongly suggests the bound is tight, though extending it to all algorithms remains open. The work also highlights a practical motivation: without replicability guarantees, uniformity testers can produce contradictory conclusions on intermediate distributions, undermining scientific reproducibility.
