## Problem

Existing video event detection methods are either offline (requiring full video observation before outputting predictions) or restricted to a fixed vocabulary of predefined action classes (as in ODAS). No prior task combines the streaming/online constraint of detecting events as they unfold with open-vocabulary natural language event queries, particularly in the challenging egocentric video setting where camera motion and variable viewpoints are prevalent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a streaming egocentric video processed frame-by-frame at 1 FPS (no future frames accessible) alongside a natural language query describing a complex event (e.g., 'Remind me when I start using the phone next time'). It outputs a per-frame score (sigmoid of cosine similarity between frame and query embeddings) that is thresholded to produce event start predictions; the model may emit up to k predictions before the true start, and performance is measured by Streaming Recall (fraction of queries where one of the first k predictions falls within an asymmetric [-5s, +10s] window around ground truth) and Streaming Minimum Distance (average temporal offset of the closest prediction).

## Method

The paper proposes adapter-based baselines that insert lightweight temporal aggregation modules into frozen dual-encoder vision-language backbones (CLIP, EgoVLP, LaViLa, EgoVideo), enabling parameter-efficient image-to-video transfer for streaming. Three streaming-compatible temporal architectures are explored: 1D convolutions (ST-Adapter), QRNN with parameter-free recurrent pooling (QR-Adapter), and RetNet's linearized attention (RN-Adapter), all operating on a reduced dimension d' < d to keep overhead ~13% over the backbone. Training uses dense binary cross-entropy supervision over 60-frame windows (30 for RetNet due to numerical instability), with a weighting scheme to handle the extreme positive-negative frame imbalance. The EgoSDQES benchmark (12,767 annotations over 740+ hours of untrimmed Ego4D video) is generated by prompting GPT-4 to convert existing temporal annotations into disambiguated streaming queries, with multi-stage LLM-based filtering for specificity and context-window feasibility.

## Results

On 1-minute clips, the best model (LaViLa+QR-Adapter) achieves SR@1 of 29.1 and SMD@1 of 18.1s, while on 5-minute clips, EgoVideo+Adapter leads with SR@1 of 16.0 and SR@3 of 26.4. All adapter-based models substantially outperform the zero-shot CLIP baseline (SR@1 = 16.9 on 1-min, 7.9 on 5-min). In the full-length untrimmed video setting (up to 2 hours), EgoVideo+Adapter achieves the highest SR@3 of 26.4 on 5-min clips, and temporal adapters (QR-Adapter, RN-Adapter) consistently outperform non-temporal adapters across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Zero-Shot CLIP (ViT-B/16), which serves as a no-training reference for the dual-encoder architecture. Every adapter-based model outperforms it across all metrics and both clip durations: on 1-minute clips, the best adapter models (LaViLa+QR-Adapter at 29.1, EgoVLP+QR-Adapter at 28.8) improve SR@1 by roughly 12 points over zero-shot CLIP (16.9), while on 5-minute clips EgoVideo+Adapter achieves 16.0 SR@1 versus 7.9 for zero-shot CLIP. Among the adapter architectures, QR-Adapter consistently outperforms the non-temporal vanilla Adapter and the 1D-convolution ST-Adapter across all backbones, confirming that temporal modeling is critical for streaming event detection. The RN-Adapter (RetNet-based) achieves performance comparable to QR-Adapter but at significantly higher computational latency (+99.5% vs. +21.5% for QRNN), due to the absence of an optimized CUDA kernel. The EgoVideo backbone with a simple non-temporal Adapter dominates the 5-minute setting (SR@1 = 16.0, SR@3 = 26.4), suggesting that strong egocentric pretraining can partially compensate for the lack of explicit temporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: Online Detection of Action Start (ODAS), which detects action starts from a fixed vocabulary in streaming video; temporal localization with language (TALL, etc.), which uses natural language queries but requires offline access to the full video; and parameter-efficient adapter-based image-to-video transfer (ST-Adapter, AIM), which adds temporal aggregation layers to frozen vision encoders. The method directly repurposes QRNN's recurrent pooling and RetNet's linearized attention as streaming temporal aggregation modules within a dual-encoder vision-language architecture, extending adapter-based fine-tuning from offline video recognition to the online setting. The key conceptual departure from ODAS is replacing the closed action vocabulary with open-vocabulary natural language queries, and the departure from temporal localization is the streaming constraint that forbids access to future frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoSDQES benchmark (12,767 annotations over 740+ hours of egocentric video with 1,553s average duration) provides the first large-scale evaluation for streaming multimodal event detection, a setting directly relevant to assistive robotics, augmented reality, and autonomous driving where latency is safety-critical. The large gap between human performance (72.4% SR@1) and the best model (~30% on 1-min clips, ~16% on 5-min clips) indicates substantial room for improvement and suggests the task is far from solved. Key open questions include how to scale temporal context beyond 60-frame windows for truly long videos, how to reduce the false-positive rate inherent to streaming detection, and whether the dataset's bias toward events in the first 30 minutes limits generalization. The paper also inherits Ego4D annotation noise and the inherent ambiguity of event start boundaries, which remain unresolved challenges.
