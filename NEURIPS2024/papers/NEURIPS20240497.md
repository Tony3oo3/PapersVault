## Problem

In collaborative learning with heterogeneous clients, existing methods either enforce a single global model (FedAvg), apply uniform fixed regularization to a global anchor (DITTO), or require pre-specifying cluster counts and centers (clustering-based FL). The paper asks how to discover fine-grained, dynamic collaboration structures among clients without prior knowledge of cluster topology, while remaining scalable and elastic to the number of participants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240497/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides a local dataset (e.g., a subset of CIFAR-100 classes or a single-language subset of Wiki-40B) and an initial model (ResNet-9 or GPT-2 with LoRA adapters); the collaboration weight matrix W is initialized to all-ones. The method outputs per-client personalized model parameters and a learned n-by-n collaboration matrix whose entries are binary (0/1) for image classification tasks or lie on a probability simplex for LLM fine-tuning. Weights are updated only for a randomly sampled O(1/n) fraction of client pairs per round, keeping per-iteration overhead comparable to standard decentralized SGD.

## Method

COBO formulates collaborative learning as a bilevel optimization: the outer problem minimizes each client's loss plus a pairwise distance penalty weighted by collaboration coefficients, while the inner problem sets each coefficient w_ij by maximizing the inner product of the two clients' gradients evaluated at their midpoint (a gradient-alignment measure). The proposed SGD-type alternating algorithm updates W via projected gradient ascent on these pairwise alignment scores, then updates model parameters via stochastic gradient descent augmented with the pairwise regularization term. Because the inner problem decomposes into independent pairwise subproblems, the algorithm is non-blocking, robust to stragglers, and elastic to client join/leave without re-clustering. Under L-smoothness, bounded gradient noise, and a cluster-structure assumption (A4-A5), the paper proves O(1/sqrt(T)) convergence of both the intra-cluster consensus distance and the gradient norm to stationary points.

## Results

On the cross-device federated learning task with 80 clients and CIFAR-100, COBO achieves 79.6±0.4% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest personalization baseline DITTO (70.3%) by 9.3 percentage points and approaching the oracle (83.6%). On the cross-silo task with 8 clients, COBO reaches 74.6±0.2% accuracy (loss 1.08), nearly matching the oracle (75.4%). For collaborative fine-tuning of GPT-2 on Wiki-40B across four languages, COBO achieves the best perplexity of 39.28±0.01, outperforming DITTO (40.05) and the validation-based method (42.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBO outperforms all five baselines across all three experimental settings. On the 80-client cross-device CIFAR-100 task, COBO (79.6%) beats DITTO (70.3%) by 9.3 points, FedAvg (53.9%) by 25.7 points, and IFCA (45.6%) by 34 points; FC is omitted here due to its O(n²) complexity. On the 8-client cross-silo task, COBO (74.6%) edges out DITTO (73.5%) by 1.1 points and substantially outperforms FC (55.1%), while FedAvg and IFCA collapse to near-random performance (18.8% and 18.6%) due to the label-flipping heterogeneity. For LLM fine-tuning on Wiki-40B, COBO achieves the lowest perplexity (39.28) versus DITTO (40.05), the validation-based method (42.90), and FedAvg (64.84), with 100% of clients improving over local training. Notably, COBO also matches or exceeds the oracle in the cross-silo setting (74.6% vs. 75.4%) and identifies the correct block-diagonal collaboration structure within 5,000 iterations (12.5% of training), whereas IFCA degenerates to a fully connected graph and FC periodically misassigns clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of personalized federated learning and bilevel optimization. It departs from DITTO's coarse-grained collaboration (uniform weights, single global model) by introducing pairwise, dynamically-updated collaboration weights determined via gradient alignment at client midpoints. It also moves beyond clustering-based federated learning (IFCA, Federated Clustering) by eliminating the need to pre-specify cluster count or initialize centers, instead discovering structure through an elastic, pairwise inner problem. The bilevel formulation draws on prior work in decentralized SGD with topology learning (Le Bars et al.) and single-level client-selection objectives (Zantedeschi et al.), but avoids the degenerate-solution problem of the latter by separating model training from weight optimization into distinct levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COBO addresses a practical bottleneck in heterogeneous federated learning: determining which clients should collaborate without prior knowledge of data structure. The O(n) per-iteration gradient cost and non-blocking pairwise updates make it deployable at scale (80 clients demonstrated), and the convergence guarantees under cluster structure provide theoretical grounding. However, the acknowledged privacy limitation—requiring gradient exchange between collaborators for weight updates—remains a significant barrier to real-world adoption, and the oracle gap in the 80-client experiment (79.6% vs 83.6%) suggests the method has not yet fully exploited available collaboration structure. Open questions include extending guarantees beyond cluster-structured data, incorporating differential privacy, and scaling to thousands of clients.
