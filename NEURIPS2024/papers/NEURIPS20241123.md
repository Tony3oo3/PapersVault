## Problem

Existing synthetic face generation methods for face recognition (e.g., DCFace, DigiFace, SynFace) produce images that, when used to train FR models, suffer from significant performance degradation compared to real-data training. The root cause is that these methods do not account for the similarity level between generated samples and their identity centers in the feature hypersphere, despite prior evidence that semi-hard samples near the decision boundary are critical for effective FR training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single inquiry face image (serving as the identity center), a scalar similarity controlling factor m ∈ [-1, 1], and random Gaussian noise as inputs. Inquiry images are pre-filtered by a pretrained FR model to enforce pairwise inter-class similarity below 0.3, ensuring generated identity groups remain distinct. The output is a synthetic face dataset where each generated image is assigned the identity label of its inquiry image, with the cosine similarity between the generated image's embedding and the inquiry image's embedding controlled to match m; datasets are produced at 0.5M, 1.0M, or 1.2M image volumes using DDIM sampling with 20 steps.

## Method

CemiFace is a conditional DDPM-based diffusion model (UNet backbone) that generates face images at a user-specified similarity level to an inquiry image. The key architectural addition is a similarity controlling condition: the scalar m is projected through a linear layer and concatenated with the identity embedding (from a pretrained AdaFace IResNet-50), then injected via cross-attention into the UNet, while the timestep is embedded via AdaGN. The core training innovation is the L_SimMat loss, which uses a time-step-dependent weight γ_t = t/T to interpolate between an identity-preservation term (L_rec, active at t→0) and a target-similarity-matching term (L_sim, active at t→T), forcing the estimated clean image x̂₀ to exhibit exactly m similarity to the inquiry image. During training, m is sampled uniformly from [-1, 1] with 0.02 intervals to ensure generalization across all similarity levels; at inference, a fixed m=0 (center-based semi-hard) is used to generate the discriminative dataset. The overall objective is L = L_MSE + λ·L_SimMat, where the random Gaussian noise in the diffusion process provides intra-class diversity while the similarity loss constrains the radius of the generated samples on the identity hypersphere.

## Results

At the 0.5M synthetic data volume, CemiFace achieves 92.30% average verification accuracy across LFW, CFP-FP, AgeDB-30, CALFW, and CPLFW, reducing the Gap-to-Real (GtR) error to 1.96 compared to 4.08 for the previous SOTA DCFace† — a 51.96% relative reduction. At 1.0M and 1.2M volumes, CemiFace further narrows the gap to 1.19 and 1.04 respectively, while DCFace† remains at 2.82 and 2.39. The method shows particularly strong gains on pose-sensitive benchmarks (CFP-FP: 91.06 vs 87.70; CPLFW: 87.65 vs 83.26 at 0.5M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CemiFace outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation dataset and data volume. Against the closest competitor DCFace† (reproduced under identical settings), CemiFace gains +2.12 AVG at 0.5M (92.30 vs 90.18) and +1.35 at 1.2M (93.22 vs 91.87), while halving the Gap-to-Real error (1.96 vs 4.08 at 0.5M). The advantage is most pronounced on pose-sensitive benchmarks: CFP-FP improves by 3.36 points and CPLFW by 4.39 points over DCFace† at 0.5M. Against earlier methods, the margins are larger: IDiff-Face (88.20 AVG) trails by 4.10 points, DigiFace (83.45) by 8.85, and SynFace (74.75) by 17.55 at 0.5M. Notably, CemiFace does not require paired training images (unlike DCFace) and can leverage unlabelled data, while still closing the gap to the real-data upper bound (94.26) to within ~2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based synthetic face recognition (SFR) line initiated by DCFace (Kim et al., CVPR 2023), which uses a dual-condition diffusion model to generate style-diverse face images for FR training. The key departure is the introduction of a similarity controlling factor (m) that explicitly regulates the distance of generated samples from their identity center in the hypersphere embedding space, motivated by the empirical finding that center-based semi-hard samples (mid-level similarity) are most effective for FR training. Architecturally it follows DCFace's cross-attention condition injection and DDPM framework, but adds a novel time-step-dependent similarity matching loss (L_SimMat) that disentangles identity preservation from similarity control. The broader SFR landscape (SynFace, DigiFace, IDiff-Face) is positioned as lacking explicit consideration of sample-to-center similarity, which this paper identifies as the missing factor behind performance degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, empirically grounded explanation for why existing SFR methods underperform: they generate samples without controlling their similarity to identity centers, missing the semi-hard sweet spot that drives effective FR training. The ~50% reduction in Gap-to-Real over DCFace is practically meaningful for privacy-conscious FR deployment, and the method's independence from paired training data (unlike DCFace) broadens its applicability. However, the approach still depends on a pretrained FR model for similarity measurement and is sensitive to inquiry image quality, leaving open the question of whether fully label-free or model-free similarity control is possible. The finding that mixing easy samples with semi-hard ones degrades performance is a useful negative result that constrains future dataset construction strategies.
