## Problem

Existing neural operators such as DeepONet and FNO can approximate PDE solution operators but do not reliably produce accurate approximations of the solution-to-parameter derivative, which is essential for downstream tasks like PDE-constrained optimization, Bayesian inference, and experimental design. This gap is especially pronounced when training data are limited, where vanilla architectures struggle to capture both the solution and its sensitivity to input perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parameter function field (e.g., log-Young's modulus or viscosity) defined on a 2D unit square, sampled from a Gaussian random field with Whittle-Matern covariance, and projects it onto r=16 basis coefficients (via KLE or ASM) before feeding into the branch net. The output is the PDE solution field (displacement or velocity) evaluated at arbitrary spatial points, together with its directional derivative in r specified input directions. The trunk net enables evaluation at any point in the domain, not just mesh nodes, which distinguishes it from coefficient-based approaches like DINO.

## Method

DE-DeepONet augments the standard DeepONet branch-net/trunk-net/inner-product architecture with two derivative-driven modifications: (1) the high-dimensional input parameter is projected onto a low-dimensional subspace spanned by Active Subspace Method (ASM) or Karhunen-Loève Expansion (KLE) basis functions, reducing the branch net input from thousands of nodal values to r=16 coefficients; and (2) the training loss includes a second term that supervises the Fréchet derivative of the predicted solution with respect to the reduced input, computed via automatic differentiation through the branch net. Derivative labels are obtained by solving linearized PDEs (a forward Gâteaux derivative and an adjoint solve, formalized in Theorem 1), and the two loss terms are balanced via self-adaptive learning rate annealing. The same derivative-loss mechanism is shown to extend to FNO (DE-FNO), and the ASM basis is demonstrated to yield lower output reconstruction error than KLE, particularly for the highly nonlinear Navier-Stokes problem.

## Results

On both the hyperelasticity and Navier-Stokes problems, DE-DeepONet (ASM) achieves the lowest relative errors in L², H¹, and Frobenius norms across all training sample sizes (16–1024), with the largest gains over vanilla DeepONet at 16 samples. The ASM-based input dimension reduction yields a 2.7% output reconstruction error for hyperelasticity and 5.8% for Navier-Stokes (vs. 3.8% and 17.4% for KLE), establishing a lower bound on achievable error. Inference on 500 test samples takes 10 s (hyperelasticity) and 18 s (Navier-Stokes) for DE-DeepONet, compared to 69 s and 2152 s for DINO, while training per iteration costs only 0.022–0.033 s versus 0.215 s for DE-FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DeepONet, DE-DeepONet (ASM) consistently reduces L², H¹, and Frobenius-norm errors across all training sample sizes, with the most dramatic improvements at 16 samples where the derivative loss and ASM dimension reduction are most impactful. Against FNO, DE-DeepONet achieves lower errors when training data is limited (16–256 samples), but FNO (using full high-dimensional inputs without projection) catches up or slightly surpasses DE-DeepONet at 1024 samples, since the linear projection error in DE-DeepONet cannot be eliminated by more data. Against DINO, DE-DeepONet (ASM) matches or slightly exceeds DINO's accuracy while being dramatically faster at inference: 10 s vs. 69 s for hyperelasticity and 18 s vs. 2152 s for Navier-Stokes on 500 test samples, because DINO requires expensive finite-element basis evaluations at arbitrary query points. The derivative-enhanced FNO (DE-FNO) achieves the best overall accuracy at 256+ samples but at a 10× training cost per iteration (0.215 s vs. 0.022 s) and 33 s inference time, making it less practical for many-query settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DeepONet [1] by incorporating two derivative-informed components: (1) Active Subspace Method (ASM) [22] for input dimension reduction, which captures output sensitivity directions rather than merely input variability (as in KLE), and (2) a Gâteaux-derivative loss term that directly supervises the network's output-input Jacobian. The closest prior work is DINO [20], which also uses derivative-informed dimension reduction and derivative loss for neural operators, but relies on precomputed POD output bases and a single MLP, whereas DE-DeepONet retains the full branch/trunk architecture of DeepONet for greater flexibility and faster pointwise inference. The derivative loss formulation is shown to be architecture-agnostic, with a demonstration on FNO [2] (DE-FNO), connecting to the broader Sobolev-training literature [32].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in neural operator surrogates: while solution accuracy is well-studied, derivative accuracy (needed for PDE-constrained optimization, Bayesian inference, and experimental design) is often poor, and existing methods like DINO incur significant inference overhead. The combination of derivative loss with ASM-based dimension reduction is particularly impactful in the data-limited regime (16–64 samples), where vanilla DeepONet and FNO degrade substantially. Key limitations include the requirement for an explicit PDE form (to compute derivative labels and solve the ASM eigenproblem) and the assumption that linear dimension reduction is sufficient, which may fail for intrinsically high-dimensional problems like high-frequency wave equations. Open questions include whether the derivative loss can be made fully data-driven (without PDE access) and whether the approach extends to 3D or time-dependent settings.
