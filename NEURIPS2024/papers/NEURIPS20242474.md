## Problem

Current visual imitation learning methods that employ VLMs only learn high-level plans and depend on pre-defined motion primitives to execute physical interactions, creating a major bottleneck due to the lack of large-scale robotic data. This paper asks whether VLMs can be leveraged to directly learn fine-grained action-level skills from a limited number of human videos, eliminating reliance on predefined primitives while still generalizing to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242474/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only 5 RGB-D human demonstration videos (recorded from a single camera) plus a textual task instruction and real-time scene observations from three RGB-D cameras at 30 Hz. Rather than outputting raw trajectories, the system produces structured hierarchical constraints: grasping skills as bounded 3D regions (inspired by Task Space Regions) expressed relative to object properties, and manipulation skills as executable trajectory code comprising parameter estimation functions and trajectory generation functions. At execution time, these constraints are instantiated into concrete end-effector trajectories via motion planning (OMPL/MoveIt), with a high-level action sequence coordinating subtask ordering.

## Method

VLMimic comprises three modules operating without any gradient-based training. First, a human-object interaction grounding pipeline (task recognition → video parsing via SAM-Track interaction markers → subtask recognition → object-centric interaction extraction using FrankMocap, ICP, BundleSDF, and FoundationPose) converts the intractable fine-grained action recognition problem into pattern reasoning tasks more amenable to VLMs. Second, a skill learner encodes interactions as hierarchical constraint representations—semantic constraints (Φ_s) derived by VLMs reasoning over rendered visualized interactions, and geometric constraints (Φ_g) computed from keypoint values and object properties—reducing the inherent redundancy of raw motion signals. Third, a skill adapter performs iterative comparison (up to 4 iterations) between the current adapted interaction and the referential interaction from the knowledge bank, updating both semantic and geometric constraints to bridge the gap between demonstration and execution scenes. Grasping constraints are adapted by discretizing the position space into a 5×5 grid and having VLMs select regions via visual contrast, while manipulation constraints are refined by analyzing trajectory deviations against the reference.

## Results

On RLBench (12 manipulation tasks), VLMimic achieves 0.76 (±0.17) overall success rate using only 5 robot videos, surpassing the best video-based baseline Demo2Code (0.49, ±0.32) by 27% and the best end-to-end policy DP (0.15, ±0.13) by over 61%. In real-world manipulation (14 tasks), VLMimic reaches 0.76 (±0.11) in seen environments and 0.71 (±0.15) in unseen environments, outperforming the strongest baselines by 21% and 34% respectively. On long-horizon tasks (6 tasks with ≥5 subtasks each), VLMimic achieves 0.50 (±0.15) overall, exceeding the best baseline R3M-DP (0.13) by over 37%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLMimic outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the end-to-end policy baselines R3M-DP (0.13) and DP (0.15), which require 100 paired observation-action demonstrations, VLMimic (0.76) achieves over 61% higher overall success on RLBench using only 5 videos. Against the RL-based GraphIRL (0.12, also 100 demos), the margin is even larger at over 64%. Against the two LLM/VLM-driven baselines that also use only 5 demonstrations—CaP (0.44) and Demo2Code (0.49)—VLMimic still leads by 27–34% on RLBench, with the gap widening dramatically on tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation (e.g., Toilet seat up: 0.76 vs. 0.05–0.06; Open microwave: 0.45 vs. 0.12–0.14). In real-world unseen environments, VLMimic (0.71) nearly doubles the performance of the best baselines CaP and Demo2Code (both 0.37), and on long-horizon tasks it reaches 0.50 versus 0.13 for R3M-DP, the only baseline that achieves non-trivial success in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM-based visual imitation learning (GPT-4V for Robotics, Demo2Code, VLaMP, Digknow) where VLMs serve only as high-level planners that output sequences of pre-defined motion primitives. VLMimic instead pushes VLMs to directly learn fine-grained action-level skills by introducing hierarchical constraint representations (semantic + geometric) and an iterative comparison strategy for skill adaptation. It also builds on the broader VIL literature (R3M, MVP, GraphIRL, WHIRL) that learns from human videos, but replaces learned visual encoders or reward functions with VLM reasoning over structured interaction representations. The human-object interaction grounding module repurposes existing perception tools (SAM-Track, FrankMocap, BundleSDF, FoundationPose) into a pipeline that converts raw video into object-centric interaction data suitable for VLM reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the reliance on predefined motion primitives, VLMimic addresses a widely acknowledged bottleneck in VLM-based robotic systems, enabling skill acquisition from as few as 5 human videos without additional training. The strong generalization to unseen environments (34% improvement over best baseline) and the 37% gain on long-horizon tasks where all baselines essentially fail suggest a viable path toward scalable, data-efficient robot skill learning. Key limitations include VLM inference latency, vulnerability to inverse kinematics errors and incomplete perception, and dependence on a long chain of perception modules (hand tracking, object reconstruction, pose estimation) whose errors compound. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object or multi-arm scenarios, whether lighter-weight VLMs can maintain the reasoning quality needed for fine-grained constraints, and how the method handles dynamic or deformable objects.
