## Problem

Per-scene optimized NeRFs achieve high-quality 3D scene reconstruction but require hours of training per scene, making them incompatible with the 2–10 Hz real-time demands of autonomous driving. Existing generalizable feed-forward NeRFs for driving scenes struggle with view synthesis due to the sparse, outward-facing camera setup with limited inter-view overlap, and their learned 3D representations lack the semantic richness needed for downstream tasks like occupancy prediction and open-vocabulary queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241991/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N posed RGB camera images captured at a single timestep (6 cameras on nuScenes at 114×228 resolution) along with their extrinsic poses, and produces a sparse hierarchical voxel scene representation parameterized over an unbounded driving scene (50 m horizontal, 6.4 m vertical inner range with α=0.8 contraction). Volumetric rendering from this representation yields RGB images (114×228), depth maps (64×114), and foundation model feature images (CLIP or DINOv2, reduced to 64-dim via PCA), which in turn enable zero-shot 3D semantic occupancy prediction and open-vocabulary text-to-region queries without any test-time per-scene optimization.

## Method

DistillNeRF uses a two-stage Lift-Splat-Shoot encoder: the first stage predicts categorical depth via ray-marching over a discrete frustum, and the second stage predicts a fine-grained depth distribution centered around the coarse estimate, then lifts 2D image features to 3D frustum entries weighted by occupancy. Multi-view frustums are fused into a sparse hierarchical voxel field consisting of two octrees (fine, 9 levels; coarse, 7 levels) connected by sparse convolutions, with a parameterized coordinate transform that preserves real-scale resolution in the inner region and contracts the outer range to handle unbounded scenes. Training is self-supervised via differentiable volumetric rendering, with two distillation sources: (1) dense depth maps and virtual-camera RGB/depth rendered from offline per-scene EmerNeRFs (which decompose static and dynamic fields), and (2) 2D foundation model features (CLIP, DINOv2) reconstructed via an L1 loss on rendered feature images, enriching the 3D representation with semantics without 3D annotations.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DistillNeRF (with depth distillation from offline NeRFs) achieves a reconstruction PSNR of 30.11 and SSIM of 0.917, approaching the per-scene optimized EmerNeRF (30.88 PSNR, 0.879 SSIM) while requiring no test-time optimization. For novel-view synthesis, the full model (with depth + parameterized space + virtual camera distillation) reaches 20.78 PSNR and 0.590 SSIM, close to Single-Frame EmerNeRF (20.95 PSNR, 0.585 SSIM). The method also reconstructs CLIP and DINOv2 foundation features at 18.69 and 18.48 PSNR respectively, and achieves 8.93 mIoU on zero-shot 3D semantic occupancy prediction on Occ3D-nuScenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistillNeRF significantly outperforms both SOTA generalizable NeRF baselines: it surpasses SelfOcc by 45.6% in reconstruction PSNR (30.11 vs. 20.67) and by 14.0% in novel-view PSNR (20.78 vs. 18.22), and outperforms UniPAD by 55.2% in reconstruction PSNR (30.11 vs. 19.44) and by 26.3% in novel-view PSNR (20.78 vs. 16.45). Against the per-scene optimized EmerNeRF upper bound, DistillNeRF is within 0.77 dB PSNR on reconstruction (30.11 vs. 30.88) while achieving higher SSIM (0.917 vs. 0.879), and within 0.17 dB on novel-view PSNR (20.78 vs. 20.95) with slightly higher SSIM (0.590 vs. 0.585). For foundation feature reconstruction, DistillNeRF achieves 18.69/18.48 PSNR for CLIP/DINOv2, trailing EmerNeRF's 20.91/21.12 but remaining the only generalizable method to support this task. On zero-shot 3D occupancy (Table 4), DistillNeRF with depth distillation reaches 8.93 mIoU, outperforming SimpleOcc (7.99) and OccNeRF (10.81) on F-mIoU (6.40 vs. 3.68/5.33), though SelfOcc (TPV) achieves higher overall mIoU (45.01) by exploiting ground-class priors. On the unseen Waymo NOTR dataset, zero-shot transfer yields 21.03 PSNR, and after fine-tuning DistillNeRF surpasses EmerNeRF (29.84 vs. 28.87 PSNR; 0.911 vs. 0.814 SSIM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm for 2D-to-3D feature lifting with a two-stage coarse-to-fine depth prediction, and adapts the Mip-NeRF 360 parameterization for unbounded driving scenes. Its core departure from prior generalizable NeRFs for driving (SelfOcc, UniPAD) is the distillation strategy: rather than training purely from raw sensor inputs, it leverages per-scene optimized EmerNeRFs as a dense depth and virtual-view auto-labeling tool, and distills CLIP/DINOv2 features for semantics. This positions the work at the intersection of three previously separate threads—generalizable NeRFs, per-scene NeRF distillation, and foundation-model feature lifting—none of which had been combined for outdoor driving before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the gap between generalizable feedforward models and per-scene optimized NeRFs in the challenging sparse-view driving setting (30.11 vs. 30.88 PSNR), this work makes real-time 3D scene understanding with semantics practically viable for autonomous driving without hours of per-scene optimization. The zero-shot transfer to Waymo NOTR and the ability to perform open-vocabulary queries and 3D occupancy prediction from a single forward pass suggest a path toward unified perception stacks. Key limitations include reliance on LiDAR for offline NeRF training, the absence of temporal modeling, and the sparse voxel representation's trade-off between rendering efficiency and dense scene coverage—open questions that the authors explicitly flag for future work.
