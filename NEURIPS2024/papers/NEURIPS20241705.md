## Problem

Estimating derivative-based global sensitivity measures (DGSMs) for expensive black-box functions typically relies on random or space-filling designs that require large evaluation budgets to converge. No prior work has proposed active learning acquisition functions that directly target the derivative quantities underlying DGSMs (the gradient, its absolute value, or its square); general-purpose active learning strategies that target the function f itself are shown to be insufficient for efficient DGSM estimation, particularly under small budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box function f defined over a compact d-dimensional input space, a total evaluation budget T (split into T₀ random initial points and T−T₀ active learning iterations), and the input space bounds. It sequentially produces the next evaluation point via acquisition function maximization, updates a GP surrogate, and ultimately outputs a d-dimensional vector of DGSM estimates (raw, absolute, or squared) per input dimension. The GP posterior provides both function and derivative estimates from observations of f alone—no direct gradient observations are needed—and the derivative posterior is a multivariate normal whose covariance is computed from kernel derivatives.

## Method

The paper derives nine tractable acquisition functions organized as three strategies (maximum variance, variance reduction, information gain) applied to three derivative quantities (raw, absolute, squared) under a GP surrogate. The key enabling result is the derivative look-ahead distribution: conditioning the bivariate normal joint of f(x*) and ∂f(x*)/∂xᵢ on a future observation f(x*)=y* yields a Gaussian whose variance is independent of y*, making variance-reduction and information-gain acquisitions computable in closed form. For the absolute derivative, the posterior is a folded normal, for which the authors introduce a novel entropy approximation as a convex combination of normal and half-normal entropies; for the squared derivative, the posterior is a noncentral χ², for which they derive an entropy approximation combining Abdel-Aty's quantile function with Moser's analytic expected-log formula. The critical insight is that derivative-targeted information gain selects points adjacent to existing observations (where derivative uncertainty is highest), in contrast to f-targeting methods that select distant points—this adjacency is what makes derivative estimation efficient. All acquisitions are implemented in BoTorch with auto-differentiable, gradient-optimized maximization.

## Results

Across 13 synthetic and real-world problems (dimensions 2–20), derivative-targeted active learning acquisition functions consistently reduce RMSE of DGSM estimates compared to quasirandom (QR) and function-targeted active learning (fVAR, fIG) baselines, with 50 replicates over 5 random initial points plus 30 active learning iterations. Derivative information gain (DIG, DAbIG₁, DSqIG₁) achieves the lowest RMSE in the majority of experiments. In high-dimensional problems (GSobol15, d=15; Morris, d=20), baseline methods show little RMSE reduction across iterations while derivative max variance (DV, DAbV, DSqV) becomes competitive with or outperforms information gain, though all derivative methods still substantially outperform the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three main baselines: quasirandom sequences (QR, scrambled Sobol), variance maximization of f (fVAR), and information gain about f / BALD (fIG). All nine derivative-targeted acquisition functions (DV, DVr, DIG, DAbV, DAbVr, DAbIG, DSqV, DSqVr, DSqIG) consistently outperform all three baselines across the 10 main problems in terms of RMSE of DGSM estimates. The gap is most pronounced in high-dimensional settings (GSobol15, Morris), where QR, fVAR, and fIG show near-flat RMSE curves while derivative methods continue to improve. Additionally, the paper compares against active subspace learning methods (Trace, Var1, Var2 from Wycoff et al. [43]) in Fig. 5; derivative information gain (DIG) outperforms all three active subspace methods on learning both the squared and absolute DGSMs, confirming that directly targeting DGSM quantities is more effective than learning a related but different gradient-based quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian active learning framework for global sensitivity analysis, which previously targeted either the function f itself (variance reduction, BALD-style information gain) or Sobol variance-based indices (Le Gratiet et al., Chauhan et al.). The GP surrogate machinery for computing derivative posteriors is well-established (De Lozzo & Marrel, Kucherenko & Iooss), and active learning with derivative terms has appeared in unrelated contexts (active subspaces, non-stationary function learning). The key departure is constructing acquisition functions that directly target DGSM quantities—gradient, |gradient|, and gradient²—requiring new entropy approximations for folded normal and noncentral χ² posteriors, and a look-ahead distribution for derivatives conditioned on function observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For engineering and scientific applications with tight evaluation budgets (vehicle crash simulation, environmental modeling), this substantially reduces the number of expensive function evaluations needed for accurate sensitivity analysis. The central insight—that optimal exploration for learning f (far from existing points) is fundamentally misaligned with optimal exploration for learning derivatives (adjacent to existing points)—challenges the default assumption that a good function surrogate automatically yields good sensitivity estimates. Open questions include whether information gain acquisitions can be made less myopic in high dimensions (e.g., batch or two-step look-ahead), extension to non-Gaussian surrogates, and understanding how active learning for DGSMs propagates to ANOVA-based Sobol indices via known bounding inequalities.
