## Problem

Existing custom diffusion models assume users' personalized concepts are fixed, but real-world use requires continually learning new concepts without storing all prior training data. This incremental setting exposes two critical failures: catastrophic forgetting of old concept identities when new LoRA weights overwrite old ones, and concept neglect where some requested concepts vanish during multi-concept composition under user-spatial conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244131/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, each task provides only 3–5 image–text triplets with concept tokens (e.g., [V*][V_dog]) and no overlap with prior tasks' concept spaces; no replay buffer is permitted. At inference, the model accepts an initial text prompt plus up to U region conditions, each pairing a region text prompt with a bounding box, and produces a single synthesized image (512×512 for SD-1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL) that composes concepts learned across all G tasks while respecting the spatial layout specified by the bounding boxes.

## Method

CIDM combines three mechanisms. (1) A concept consolidation loss trains each task's LoRA weights using learnable layer-wise concept tokens (distinct per transformer layer, unlike unified textual inversion) plus an orthogonal subspace regularizer to preserve task-specific identity, while a shared layer-wise subspace W* with per-task projection matrices H_i captures task-shared semantics; a two-step optimization alternates updating LoRA/tokens versus updating H_i/W*. (2) An elastic weight aggregation module at inference computes cosine-style semantic relations between the current prompt's embeddings and the averaged concept-token embeddings of each stored task, then adaptively weights and sums all low-rank matrices so that only relevant old concepts contribute. (3) A context-controllable synthesis strategy performs layer-wise regional cross-attention (masking features to bounding boxes) for spatial conditioning and applies per-region classifier-free-guidance noise estimations that are aggregated with the global estimation, preventing individual concepts from being dropped during multi-concept composition.

## Results

On the CIL dataset (10 sequential concept customization tasks), CIDM achieves the best text-alignment (TA) scores: 74.8% average on SD-1.5 and 80.0% average on SDXL, outperforming all baselines by 1.2%–4.8%. In terms of image-alignment (IA), CIDM reaches 77.9% average (SD-1.5), surpassing the strongest baseline CLoRA (76.9%) by 1.0% and L2DM (76.1%) by 1.8%. The paper reports overall improvements of 1.1%–8.0% in IA and 1.2%–4.8% in TA over all comparison methods across both backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIDM outperforms all seven baselines on both TA and IA metrics across both SD-1.5 and SDXL backbones. On SD-1.5 TA, CIDM (74.8%) beats the closest competitor LWF (73.4%) by 1.4% and CLoRA (73.6%) by 1.2%, while the weakest baseline Finetuning (70.0%) trails by 4.8%. On SDXL TA, CIDM (80.0%) leads L2DM (78.7%) by 1.3% and CLoRA (77.8%) by 2.2%. For IA on SD-1.5, CIDM (77.9%) surpasses CLoRA (76.9%) by 1.0% and L2DM (76.1%) by 1.8%. The continual-learning baselines (EWC, LWF, RPY) consistently underperform the diffusion-specific methods (CLoRA, L2DM), suggesting that generic anti-forgetting regularizers are insufficient for the concept-customization setting. Notably, L2DM achieves the strongest SDXL TA among baselines (78.7%) but still falls short of CIDM, indicating that the combination of concept consolidation loss and elastic weight aggregation provides a meaningful advantage over memory-bank-based lifelong diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two largely separate lines of work: (1) LoRA-based custom diffusion models for personalized concept generation (DreamBooth, Mix-of-Show, CLoRA) and (2) continual/incremental learning strategies for diffusion models (L2DM, CLoRA). It departs from prior continual diffusion work (L2DM, CLoRA) by adding user-conditional multi-concept composition, and from prior multi-concept composition work (Mix-of-Show, LoRA-M/C) by requiring concept-incremental learning without replay. The concept consolidation loss repurposes orthogonal subspace regularization from continual learning and introduces layer-wise concept tokens as a finer-grained alternative to unified textual inversion, while the elastic weight aggregation module generalizes the simple LoRA merging in Mix-of-Show with semantic-similarity-weighted aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CIFC problem formulation captures a genuinely practical need—users who continually add new personalized concepts while also wanting spatially controlled multi-concept composition—which prior work addressed only in isolation. However, the evaluation is limited to 10 tasks with 3–5 image pairs each and only four semantically similar concept pairs, leaving open whether the approach scales to dozens of similar concepts or longer task sequences. The authors acknowledge this scalability limitation and suggest future work with multi-modal LLMs and video generation, but the current evidence does not yet establish that the orthogonal subspace regularizer and EWA module remain effective under substantially larger concept spaces.
