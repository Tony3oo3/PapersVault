## Problem

While it is known that transformer LMs pass features from early layers to later layers, the specific subspaces through which this inter-layer communication occurs remain poorly understood. The composition score proposed by Elhage et al. (2021) is too noisy on larger models to reliably identify which heads communicate, and the connection between these communication mechanisms and observable prompt-sensitivity failures (e.g., order-dependent recall) has not been established.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the QK and OV weight matrices of individual attention heads (768×64 in GPT-2 Small) and decomposes each into its rank-1 SVD components, then computes pairwise composition scores between individual components rather than full matrices. Behavioral validation uses text prompts from the IOI task (200 examples) and a synthetic Laundry List task (250 prompts per list length N=3–20), with outputs including identified 1D/2D communication channels, inhibition scores, mover-head attention distributions, and task accuracy after interventions.

## Method

The core technique applies SVD to each attention head's QK and OV matrices, decomposing them into h rank-1 component matrices (outer products of singular vectors scaled by singular values), then computes the composition score between individual components to isolate low-rank communication signals that are invisible at the full-matrix level. This reveals that inhibition heads communicate with the mover head through a single 1D component, and duplicate token heads use a 2D subspace, both far more concentrated than the full-matrix score suggests. The authors validate causality by zeroing out identified components in the weights (reducing inhibition score by 7–14%) and by scaling singular vectors in the residual stream to control which token the mover head attends to. On the Laundry List task, traversing the 3D space spanned by the top three inhibition components reveals a structured partition that positionally indexes list items, and setting the model to the correct region of this space improves accuracy by over 20% (e.g., 64%→86% for 3 objects). The method also enables static circuit discovery from weights alone, rediscovering the IOI circuit and identifying a novel 'relevant context' head (8.3) without running the model.

## Results

On the Laundry List task, traversing the 3D inhibition subspace (components 7.9.6, 8.6.2, 8.6.10) and setting the model to the correct region improves 3-object accuracy from 64% to 86% and 8-object accuracy to ~51% (comparable to 4-object performance in the unmodified model). Removing the single highest-composition component from an inhibition head reduces the Inhibition Score by 7–14%. On the IOI task, the 1D inhibition subspaces achieve a Fractional Logit Difference Decrease (FLDD) of 97.5% and interchange accuracy of 35%, substantially exceeding the −8% FLDD and 0.0% interchange baseline reported by Makelov et al. (2024). The decomposed composition score reveals that individual rank-1 components (e.g., 8.6.2) outscore all other 63 components in the same head by 5 standard deviations, whereas the non-decomposed score is too noisy to identify known circuit interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper without a traditional method-vs-baseline comparison table. The key quantitative comparison is against the subspace-patching baseline of Makelov et al. (2024): the paper's 1D inhibition subspaces achieve 97.5% FLDD and 35% interchange accuracy, versus −8% FLDD and 0.0% interchange for their baseline, though a gradient-optimized single vector from the same paper reaches 111.5% FLDD and 45.1% interchange. The paper also contrasts the decomposed (SVD-based) composition score against the original non-decomposed formulation from Elhage et al. (2021), showing that decomposition is essential for recovering known circuit structure in GPT-2-Small where the raw score is indistinguishable from random head pairs. On the Laundry List task, the unmodified model's performance degrades sharply beyond ~8 objects (accuracy drops below 30% by 12 objects), and even larger models (OPT-6.9B, Pythia-6.9B) exhibit the same degradation pattern, though more gradually, suggesting the capacity limit is not simply a scaling artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composition score framework from Elhage et al. (2021) by applying SVD decomposition to QK and OV matrices, breaking each attention head into rank-1 component subspaces that make inter-layer communication signals far more interpretable than the noisy full-matrix scores reported by Singh et al. (2024). It grounds the method in the IOI circuit identified by Wang et al. (2022), using known head interactions (inhibition, mover, duplicate token) as validation targets before generalizing to a new synthetic task. The approach also connects to the broader linear representation hypothesis and sparse autoencoder literature, but distinguishes itself by working purely from weight matrices without requiring forward passes or activation data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 3D subspace spanned by just three rank-1 inhibition components serves as a positional index for list items—and that this space fragments as items increase—provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for an otherwise arbitrary-seeming prompt sensitivity failure mode. The demonstration that SVD-decomposed composition scores can identify circuit structure from weights alone (without running the model) opens a path toward automated circuit discovery, though the current evidence is limited to small models (GPT-2 Small, Pythia 160m) and a single task family. Open questions include whether larger models use fundamentally different indexing mechanisms, whether the approach extends to models with relative positional embeddings (RoPE), and whether the weight-based circuit discovery can be made fully autonomous.
