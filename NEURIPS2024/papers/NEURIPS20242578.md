## Problem

Existing multi-task learning approaches for Vision Transformers rigidly combine Mixture-of-Experts structures with LoRA, which hampers both MoE optimization and LoRA's reparameterization property, resulting in sub-optimal accuracy and significant inference overhead. Additionally, conventional multi-task optimization strategies force synchronization across tasks with inherently different convergence paces, disrupting each task's natural optimization schedule and degrading overall performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images resized to 224×224 (training) or 248×248 center-cropped to 224×224 (inference) and produces class labels across a unified label space spanning multiple fine-grained classification tasks (e.g., 200 bird species, 196 car models, 100 aircraft types, 102 flower species). For dense prediction on NYUv2, it additionally outputs per-pixel segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, and object boundaries. A key design property is that after training, the final model is a standard ViT with no architectural modifications—no router, no expert routing, no extra inference latency—since all learned parameters are reparameterized back into the original FFN weight matrices.

## Method

EMTAL decomposes a pre-trained ViT's FFN layers into a Mixture of Low-rank Experts (MoLE) by clustering similar columns of the up-projection matrix W_up via balanced k-means, creating K specialized experts that naturally exhibit low-rank structure; LoRA is then applied to each expert's up and down projections for efficient multi-task fine-tuning. A sample-driven soft router dynamically reweights experts during training to extract diverse task-specific features. To handle asynchronous task convergence, a Quality Retaining mechanism maintains an EMA knowledge bank of class logits and applies a KL distillation loss weighted by the reciprocal of each task's cross-entropy loss, so converged tasks rely more on their stored high-quality predictions while under-trained tasks continue learning from ground truth. Finally, a router fading strategy linearly decays the router's influence (α→0) over the last training epochs, after which the LoRA updates are absorbed into the original weight matrices, yielding a unified model with zero additional inference cost and only ~1.2M tunable parameters versus 344M for full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Multi-task FGVC benchmark (CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Oxford Flowers) with ViT-B/16, EMTAL-4 achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 91.73%, surpassing the Separate Full Fine-tuning baseline (87.49%) by 4.24% and the strongest MTO baseline Aligned-MTL (90.17%) by 1.56%, while tuning only 1.20M parameters (vs. 343.92M for Separate FT) and incurring zero extra inference latency (7.15 ms). On Specialized VTAB-1k, EMTAL-4 reaches 87.89% mean accuracy with 0.78M tunable parameters, outperforming MOELoRA (87.01%) and LoRA (85.50%). On NYUv2 dense prediction, EMTAL improves the mean relative metric by 1.57% over TaskPrompter-Base, with notable gains in semantic segmentation mIoU (52.90 vs. 50.40) and depth RMSE (0.5284 vs. 0.5402).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMTAL-4 outperforms all MTO baselines (Nash-MTL 89.65%, Aligned-MTL 90.17%, GLS 89.55%, AMTL 90.04%) by 1.1–2.2% in mean accuracy while using fewer tunable parameters (1.20M vs. 2.82M). Against efficient multi-task learners, EMTAL-4 surpasses MOELoRA (88.04%) by 3.69% and Erudite (82.35%) by 9.38%, while also eliminating the 38.71 ms inference overhead of MOELoRA and the 101.34M parameters of Erudite. Compared to the Separate FT baseline, EMTAL-4 improves mean accuracy by 4.24% while reducing tunable parameters from 343.92M to 1.20M (a 286× reduction) and halving inference time. On Specialized VTAB-1k, EMTAL-4 (87.89%) beats MOELoRA (87.01%) by 0.88% and LoRA (85.50%) by 2.39% with fewer parameters (0.78M vs. 2.41M). On NYUv2, EMTAL improves semantic segmentation mIoU by 2.5 points and depth RMSE by 0.012 over TaskPrompter-Base, though it slightly underperforms on surface normal (18.95 vs. 18.91 mErr) and boundary detection (77.10 vs. 77.60 odsF). The QR mechanism alone (without MoEfied LoRA) already achieves 91.25% mean accuracy, exceeding Aligned-MTL by 1.08%, confirming that asynchronous optimization is a key contributor independent of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MoEfication (decomposing ViT FFN layers into MoE structures via neuron grouping) and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but departs from both in a key way: rather than clustering by co-activation (as in MoEfication) or using task-driven routers (as in MOELoRA, MLoRE), it clusters by weight similarity to produce experts with inherent low-rank structure that naturally aligns with LoRA. The Quality Retaining mechanism departs from synchronization-based multi-task optimization (AMTL, Aligned-MTL) by embracing asynchronous convergence and using EMA-based knowledge distillation to protect already-converged tasks. The router fading strategy is a novel reparameterization trick that, to the authors' knowledge, is the first to decompose a pre-trained ViT into a multi-task learner and then fold the learned parameters back into the original architecture with zero additional inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a unified multi-task model with only ~1M tunable parameters and zero inference overhead compared to the base ViT is highly attractive for deployment scenarios where maintaining a single model across tasks is essential. The open question of per-task or per-layer adaptive rank (the paper notes CUB-200-2011 prefers rank 1 while Stanford Cars prefers rank 4) points toward a natural next step. Limitations include the unified-rank constraint, potential out-of-distribution fragility during deployment, and evaluation primarily on fine-grained classification and dense prediction rather than broader task families.
