## Problem

Converting transformers to fully spike-driven SNNs is blocked by two fundamental incompatibilities: the matrix multiplications between dynamically generated Q, K, V matrices require very long timesteps (T≥128) under rate coding to maintain accuracy, and the softmax operation involves exponential functions that cannot be computed with binary spike inputs/outputs. Existing approaches either restructure the self-attention architecture (removing softmax) or resort to non-spike floating-point computations, sacrificing either architectural fidelity or the energy benefits of spiking.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, RGB images are patchified and encoded as binary-coded spikes (B=2 phase coding) at the input, while all intermediate features use one-spike phase coding (B<2) with a single spike per neuron over the total timestep T (typically 24–48). For NLP tasks, token embeddings are binary-coded and fed into the SNN. The method produces standard outputs: class labels for ImageNet/CIFAR, classification scores for GLUE tasks, and answer spans for SQuAD, with negligible accuracy loss (≤0.6% for vision, ≤0.3% average for GLUE) relative to the source ANN.

## Method

SpikedAttention introduces two key mechanisms to enable training-free, fully spike-based attention. First, trace-driven matrix multiplication replaces probabilistic AND operations with a global trace that decays by base B each timestep; when a neuron fires its first spike, the current trace value B^{-t} is stored locally, and when its paired neuron fires, the stored trace propagates to accumulate into the output potential, bounding multiplications to T regardless of embedding dimension D. Second, Winner-Oriented Spike Shift (WOSS) approximates softmax without exponentials: the first (winner) spike triggers global inhibition to subtract max(z'), and a linearly shifting threshold generates output spikes at times proportional to (max(z')−z_i')·M_z/log_e(B), while the normalization denominator is absorbed by scaling the threshold of the subsequent trace-driven dot product. The entire conversion is training-free, requiring only a proxy dataset to extract per-layer maximum values M_z and to select the optimal base B via a logarithmic error criterion.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, SpikedAttention (w/o ReLU) achieves 80.0% top-1 accuracy with 28.7M parameters at T=40, consuming only 3.0 mJ (synaptic-only metric) or 26.2 mJ (hardware-realistic metric), a 42% reduction over the Swin-T ANN baseline (45.4 mJ). On the GLUE benchmark, converting MA-BERT to SpikedAttention incurs only 0.3% average accuracy loss while reducing energy by 58% (from ~189.7 mJ to ~80 mJ). On SQuAD question answering, SpikedAttention achieves 87.2% F1 with 59.5% energy reduction compared to MA-BERT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikedAttention outperforms all prior spike-based transformer methods on ImageNet: it achieves 80.0% accuracy, beating Spikformer (74.8%) by 5.2%, SDSA (77.1%) by 2.9%, and MST (77.9%) by 2.1%, while matching Meta-SpikeFormer's 80.0% (T=4) but at 17.5× lower energy (3.0 vs 52.4 mJ). Compared to the closest ANN-to-SNN baseline MST, SpikedAttention reduces energy by 53× (3.0 vs 158.6 mJ) and shortens the timestep from 128 to 40 while improving accuracy. On GLUE, SpikedAttention surpasses SpikingBERT by 3.6% average accuracy (e.g., 91.0% vs 85.2% on QNLI, 92.1% vs 88.2% on SST-2) without requiring any training, whereas SpikingBERT relies on knowledge distillation from a BERT teacher. Against the MA-BERT ANN baseline, SpikedAttention incurs negligible accuracy loss (≤0.5% per task) while cutting energy by ~58% across all nine GLUE tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm from CNNs to transformers, building directly on the one-spike phase coding framework (Hwang & Kung, 2024) and the phase coding theory (Kim et al., 2018). It departs from prior transformer-to-SNN work in two key ways: unlike MST (Wang et al., 2023), it avoids rate coding with long timesteps (T≥128) and non-spike softmax by introducing trace-driven matrix multiplication and winner-oriented spike shift; unlike Spikformer (Zhou et al., 2023) and SDSA (Yao et al., 2023), it preserves the vanilla self-attention architecture rather than restructuring it to remove softmax, and requires no surrogate-gradient training. The result is the first fully spike-driven transformer where all intermediate features are binary spikes and no real-valued multiplications or nonlinear functions remain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the conversion is practically significant: it eliminates the need for additional training iterations (and associated carbon emissions) while achieving 80.0% ImageNet accuracy and only 0.3% average accuracy loss on GLUE, matching or exceeding directly-trained SNNs at 42–58% lower energy. The first successful BERT-to-SNN conversion opens a path toward energy-efficient NLP on neuromorphic hardware. Key limitations remain: the required timestep (T=40 for vision, T=16–24 for NLP) is longer than directly-trained SNNs (T=4), and the method cannot handle GeLU or LayerNorm, restricting generalization to models that have already replaced these with ReLU/BatchNorm. Future work on per-layer base learning and spike-based GeLU/LayerNorm would address these gaps.
