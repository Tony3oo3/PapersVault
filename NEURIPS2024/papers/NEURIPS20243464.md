## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods are designed and evaluated under balanced in-distribution data, yet real-world deployments face long-tailed class distributions that cause significant performance degradation. Prior work attributed this solely to tail-class samples being misidentified as OOD, overlooking the symmetric issue where OOD samples are erroneously predicted as head-class ID. This paper identifies both failure modes as stemming from a single root cause: disparate decision spaces between head and tail classes in the imbalanced setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT) and produces two outputs: a K-way class label prediction from the ID classifier f, and a scalar ID/OOD probability score P(i|x) from the detector g. The detector g shares the feature-extraction backbone with f and appends a single extra output node, so inference requires no additional forward pass beyond the standard classification. During training, auxiliary OOD samples (from TinyImages80M or ImageNet-Extra) are mixed into each batch at equal ratio with ID samples to supervise the binary ID/OOD decision.

## Method

ImOOD establishes a probabilistic framework that decomposes the balanced OOD posterior P^bal(i|x) into a bias term β(x) times the learned imbalanced posterior P(i|x), where β(x) = Σ_y γ_y(x)·p_{y|x,i}/π_y captures class-aware distortion from imbalance. The key mechanism is a unified training-time regularization: the detector's logit g(x) is corrected by a data-dependent offset Δ(x) derived from β(x), and binary cross-entropy is applied to the calibrated logit g(x)−Δ(x), which pushes tail-class ID samples toward higher ID scores and head-class-predicted OOD samples toward lower scores. A learnable class-specific and input-dependent scaling factor γ_{y;θ}(x) (implemented as a linear layer on the shared backbone) is optimized jointly with a constraint loss L_γ that prevents the calibrated posterior from exceeding 1. The framework is detector-agnostic: any OOD scorer (OE, Energy, Mahalanobis distance, BinDisc) is wrapped in a sigmoid to produce g(x), then regularized identically, and at inference only the raw g*(x) is used with no extra computation.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT, COCL+Ours achieves 93.55% AUROC, 92.83% AUPR, and 28.52% FPR95, setting new SOTA on all three OOD detection metrics. On CIFAR100-LT, the same configuration reaches 78.50% AUROC and 79.96% AUPR. On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark, COCL+Ours attains 75.84% AUROC and 74.96% FPR95, improving over the PASCL baseline by 6.7% AUROC and 13.2% FPR95. The method also maintains strong ID classification accuracy (81.83% on CIFAR10-LT, 52.43% on ImageNet-LT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImOOD consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across all benchmarks. Against the strongest imbalanced OOD baselines, COCL+Ours improves over COCL [40] by 0.27% AUROC and 2.36% FPR95 on CIFAR10-LT, and by 1.97% AUROC and 1.39% FPR95 on ImageNet-LT. PASCL+Ours surpasses PASCL [51] by 1.94% AUROC and 4.63% FPR95 on CIFAR10-LT, with a much larger 6.69% AUROC and 13.16% FPR95 gain on ImageNet-LT. Against general OOD detectors (MSP [19], OE [20], Energy [32], Mahalanobis [28]), the improvements are substantial (e.g., 93.55% vs. 72.28% AUROC over MSP on CIFAR10-LT). Against recent SOTA general OOD methods (NECO [2], fDBD [31], IDLabel [15], ID-like [3]), ImOOD leads by 3.5–8.4% AUROC on CIFAR10-LT and by 3.79% AUROC over ID-like on ImageNet-LT. The method also outperforms ClassPrior [23] by 3.64% AUROC on iNaturalist and 3.24% on SUN under ClassPrior's own evaluation protocol (Table 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BinDisc binary discriminator framework (Bitterwolf et al., ICML 2022) and the balanced posterior probability formulation from logit adjustment (Menon et al., ICLR 2021) to derive a unified statistical model for OOD detection under class imbalance. It departs from prior imbalanced OOD detection work (PASCL, COCL, EAT) that addresses the problem through feature-space contrastive learning or outlier class heuristics, instead identifying a single class-aware bias term β(x) that simultaneously explains both failure modes (tail-ID misclassified as OOD, and OOD misclassified as head-ID). The resulting training-time regularization is detector-agnostic, generalizing across OE, Energy, Mahalanobis, and BinDisc formulations rather than being tied to a specific scoring function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ImOOD framework provides the first unified probabilistic explanation for why imbalanced OOD detectors fail on both head and tail classes, replacing ad-hoc feature-space fixes with a principled correction that can be plugged into any existing OOD scorer. A key limitation is the reliance on auxiliary OOD training data to shape the ID/OOD boundary, which may not generalize to truly unforeseen OOD categories in deployment; the authors identify online adaptive decision-making as a natural next step. The imbalance problem is mitigated but not fully resolved—tail classes still suffer from scarce training data, suggesting that data-level re-balancing techniques remain complementary.
