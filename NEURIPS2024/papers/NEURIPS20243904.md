## Problem

The dominant computational bottleneck in primal-dual Interior Point Methods (IPMs) for NLPs is the O(n³) matrix factorization required to solve Newton linear systems at every iteration. Existing learning-based IPM work has focused almost exclusively on linear programs, leaving open whether Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) techniques can meaningfully accelerate IPMs for general nonlinear programs with both equality and inequality constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243904/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each LSTM time step t, the network consumes the previous estimate y_{t−1} concatenated with the gradient (J^k)ᵀ(J^k y_{t−1} + F^k) of the least-squares reformulation of the current Newton system, and emits a refined estimate y_t. After K outer IPM iterations (each containing T LSTM steps), the method outputs an approximate primal-dual triple (x^K, λ^K, z^K) that is well-centered relative to the boundary and carries a small duality gap. This triple is then passed as a warm-start to IPOPT, which refines it to a certified KKT point.

## Method

IPM-LSTM substitutes the exact Newton linear-system solve in a classic primal-dual IPM with a coordinate-wise LSTM that approximates the solution to the equivalent unconstrained least-squares problem min_y ½‖J^k y + F^k‖², where parameters are shared across both LSTM cells and output coordinates. The network is trained self-supervisedly by unrolling K IPM iterations into the loss (averaging the least-squares residual over all K iterations and T time steps), with truncated backpropagation through time after each IPM iteration to bound memory. A diagonal Ruiz preconditioner rescales (J^k)ᵀJ^k to control its condition number, which would otherwise degrade the LSTM's iterative approximation in later IPM iterations. Convergence of the resulting inexact IPM is established under a bounded-residual and bounded-solution-norm assumption (Proposition 1, building on Bellavia 1998), and the two-stage framework—LSTM approximation followed by IPOPT warm-start—reduces iterations by up to 60% and solution time by up to 70% on non-convex QPs.

## Results

On Convex QPs (RHS, n=200), IPM-LSTM achieves an objective of -29.050 (vs. optimal -29.176) with only 0.002 max equality violation, and warm-starting IPOPT with its primal-dual solution reduces iterations from 12.5 to 7.2 (42.4% reduction) and solver time from 0.642s to 0.370s. On Convex QCQPs (RHS), IPM-LSTM reduces IPOPT iterations by 36.0% (12.5→8.0). On non-convex QPs from Globallib, the warm-start yields up to 63.9% iteration reduction and 70.5% solution time reduction (instance st_rv7: 449→162 iterations, 2.445s→0.705s). Across all problem types, IPM-LSTM consistently produces the best warm-start gains among all L2O baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPM-LSTM dominates all L2O baselines on warm-start quality: it reduces IPOPT iterations by 42.4% (12.5→7.2) on Convex QPs (RHS), versus 18.4% for DC3, 22.4% for PDL, 13.6% for LOOP-LC, and 10.4% for H-Proj. In end-to-end objective quality, IPM-LSTM (-29.050) is closest to the IPOPT optimum (-29.176), while NN (-26.787), DC3 (-26.720), and H-Proj (-23.257) show substantial optimality gaps, and DeepLDE (-3.697) fails catastrophically. PDL achieves a competitive objective (-28.559) but with large constraint violations (max ineq. 0.421), whereas IPM-LSTM maintains near-feasibility (max ineq. 0.000, max eq. 0.002). On Convex QCQPs (RHS), IPM-LSTM again leads in warm-start iteration reduction (36.0%) over PDL (28.8%), H-Proj (21.6%), and DC3 (20.7%). On non-convex QPs where only IPOPT is applicable as a baseline, IPM-LSTM achieves up to 63.9% iteration and 70.5% time savings. The main trade-off is that IPM-LSTM's end-to-end inference time (0.175s) is higher than MLP-based L2O methods (<0.001s) due to its multi-cell LSTM architecture, but the total pipeline time (0.545s) still beats default IPOPT (0.642s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) and Interior Point Methods (IPMs), extending the line of work that uses neural networks to accelerate classical optimization algorithms. Prior learning-based IPM efforts (Qian et al. 2024) demonstrated GNN-based proxies for LPs, while warm-starting approaches for ACOPF (Baker 2019, Diehl 2019) predicted solutions to initialize IPM solvers. The key departure here is the integration point: rather than predicting full primal-dual solutions or learning problem representations, IPM-LSTM approximates the Newton linear system solution at each IPM iteration using a coordinate-wise LSTM, grounded in the inexact IPM convergence theory of Bellavia (1998). This repurposes the LSTM-as-iterative-optimizer paradigm (Andrychowicz et al. 2016, Liu et al. 2023) from unconstrained problems to the structured least-squares subproblem within a constrained IPM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage framework (LSTM warm-start followed by IPOPT) offers a practical recipe for accelerating NLP solving without sacrificing solution quality, with convergence guarantees inherited from inexact IPM theory. However, the total end-to-end speedup is modest (15–40%) because the multi-cell LSTM itself is computationally expensive, and the condition number of the Newton system grows in later IPM iterations, degrading approximation quality. Open questions include whether lower-complexity architectures (e.g., transformers, linear attention) can replace the LSTM while preserving the iterative-structure inductive bias, and whether the approach scales to problems with thousands of variables where the O(n³) factorization cost is most acute. The paper also leaves open the question of whether the self-supervised training signal (residual of the linear system) is sufficient for out-of-distribution generalization to structurally different NLPs.
