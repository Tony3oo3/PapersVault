## Problem

In distributed nonconvex finite-sum optimization, workers have heterogeneous and asynchronous computation times, yet all prior methods either required prior knowledge of worker speeds, depended on the slowest worker in every iteration, or achieved suboptimal time complexity. The paper asks: what is the best possible time complexity for finding an ε-stationary point when workers can be arbitrarily heterogeneous and asynchronous, and provides the first method that achieves this optimum in the large-scale regime (√m ≥ n) along with a matching lower bound.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-sum objective f(x) = (1/m)Σfᵢ(x) with m loss functions, a starting point x⁰, and n workers whose compute times τᵢ ∈ [0,∞] may be unknown, infinite, or dynamically changing across iterations. It produces a random point x̂ with E[‖∇f(x̂)‖²] ≤ ε. Notably, the algorithm does not require prior knowledge of {τᵢ} and automatically excludes workers whose processing time is too large, making it robust to stragglers without any manual tuning of worker participation.

## Method

Freya PAGE combines the PAGE variance-reduction update rule (with probability p compute the full gradient, otherwise update via a minibatch of S stochastic gradient differences) with two novel distributed computation subroutines: ComputeGradient and ComputeBatchDifference. These subroutines broadcast the current point to all workers, assign random gradient computations, and collect results as they arrive in a first-come-first-served manner, naturally giving more work to faster workers without needing to know their speeds. The key theoretical concept is the 'equilibrium time' t*(S) = minⱼ((Σᵢ₌₁ʲ 1/τᵢ)⁻¹(S+j)), which captures the effective parallel speed and automatically ignores workers slower than a threshold. In the large-scale regime (√m ≥ n), the optimal parameters S=⌈√m⌉ and p=1/√m yield a time complexity of O(t*(m) + (δ⁰/ε)·t*(√m)), which the paper proves is optimal via a matching lower bound.

## Results

Freya PAGE achieves the first provably optimal time complexity for large-scale nonconvex finite-sum optimization in the heterogeneous asynchronous setting: T = 12·t*(m) + 192·δ⁰·max{L₋,L±}/ε · t*(√m) seconds (Theorem 7), matching the lower bound (Theorem 10) when √m ≥ n. On the MNIST logistic regression task, Freya PAGE achieves 92.66% test accuracy with the lowest variance (1.01×10⁻⁷) among all compared methods, outperforming Asynchronous SGD (92.60%, variance 5.85×10⁻⁷), Rennala SGD (92.37%, variance 3.12×10⁻⁶), and Soviet PAGE (92.31%, variance 1.62×10⁻⁷). On synthetic nonconvex quadratic problems with n ∈ {1000, 10000} heterogeneous workers, Freya PAGE converges fastest and most smoothly, with the gap over Soviet PAGE widening as n increases due to straggler robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Freya PAGE dominates all four baselines on both theory and experiments. Against Rennala SGD and Asynchronous SGD, Freya PAGE improves the dependence on ε from O(1/ε²) to O(1/ε) and removes the σ²-bounded variance assumption entirely; empirically on MNIST it achieves 92.66% vs 92.37% (Rennala) and 92.60% (Async SGD) with 5–30× lower accuracy variance. Against Soviet PAGE (equal-data-allocation PAGE), Freya PAGE is robust to stragglers: its complexity uses minⱼ(Σᵢ₌₁ʲ 1/τᵢ)⁻¹(√m+j) which automatically excludes workers with τᵢ → ∞, whereas Soviet PAGE's time scales with τₙ and can be arbitrarily worse; on MNIST Freya PAGE reaches 92.66% vs 92.31% for Soviet PAGE. Against SYNTHESIS, Freya PAGE requires no bounded-gradient assumption, does not compute full gradients for free, and does not depend on the slowest worker's delay bound Δ. The lower bound (Theorem 10) confirms that no algorithm can beat Freya PAGE's O(1/ε) time complexity in the large-scale regime (√m ≥ n), establishing it as the first optimal method for this problem class.

## Relation to Prior Work

Freya PAGE inherits its variance-reduced update rule directly from PAGE (Li et al., 2021) and SPIDER, but replaces their synchronous gradient collection with two novel adaptive subroutines (ComputeGradient and ComputeBatchDifference) that distribute work across heterogeneous workers without requiring prior knowledge of their speeds. The harmonic-mean-like time complexity structure (min over j of (Σ1/τ_i)^{-1}(S+j)) extends the framework introduced by Rennala SGD (Tyurin and Richtárik, 2023) from expectation-minimization under bounded variance to finite-sum nonconvex problems without that assumption. The paper departs from Soviet PAGE (equal data allocation) and SYNTHESIS by being robust to stragglers—slow workers are automatically excluded from the complexity bound—while also improving on Asynchronous SGD's suboptimal O(1/ε²) dependence on the error tolerance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method with a provably tight time complexity for smooth nonconvex finite-sum optimization under arbitrary heterogeneous asynchronous computation, closing a gap that left practitioners without a principled way to coordinate workers of wildly different speeds. The gradient collection strategies are generic and reusable in other asynchronous methods (demonstrated via Freya SGD), potentially influencing future algorithm design. Limitations include the large-scale regime assumption (√m ≥ n) for the clean optimality statement, the lower bound being specific to uniform sampling with replacement, and experimental validation restricted to quadratic objectives and logistic regression on MNIST rather than deep learning at scale. Open questions include whether the adaptive strategies extend to communication-constrained or federated settings, and whether the log-factor gap in ComputeGradient can be eliminated.
