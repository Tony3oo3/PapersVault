## Problem

Contextual sparsity (CS) methods for LLM inference achieve strong compression on prompt-understanding tasks but catastrophically degrade on reasoning, deduction, and knowledge-based tasks (e.g., GSM8K accuracy drops from 75% to 38% with coarse-grained sparsity on Llama-3-8B). The paper identifies that sparse and full models share the same problem-solving logic and that correcting only ~11% of tokens recovers full performance, but efficiently locating these critical mistaken tokens without negating the sparsity efficiency gains remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

SIRIUS takes a standard text prompt and produces generated text tokens, operating in the memory-bound regime (sequence length ≤ 64). The system requires both the sparse and full model weights in GPU memory (a requirement already imposed by contextual sparsity's dynamic neuron selection), and they share a single KV cache. The sparse model generates tokens sequentially in fixed-size chunks (kernel size, typically 16), while the full model performs parallel verification on each chunk, making the output indistinguishable in format from standard autoregressive generation.

## Method

SIRIUS is a period-based correction mechanism where the full model is invoked every n_period tokens to verify the sparse model's output chunk. Three key techniques boost correction efficiency: (1) KV cache direct rewriting, where the full model's key-value pairs for the verified chunk overwrite the shared cache to fix downstream context; (2) threshold-based minimal rollbacks, where the full model only rejects and regenerates tokens whose likelihood falls below a preset threshold, interleaving its own high-quality tokens at rejection points; and (3) hardware-efficient tree building, which exploits the observation that the sparse model's 2nd and 3rd most likely tokens cover >80% of full-model-accepted tokens, constructing a fixed-treewidth tree (max width 8) for parallel verification that increases the average advance length (AAL) from ~13.7 to ~15 out of 16 tokens. The sparse model's own confidence signals (entropy) are shown to be unreliable for triggering corrections, motivating the fixed-period design.

## Results

On GSM8K with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, SIRIUS corrects CSparse accuracy from 38.4% to 70.5% (full model: 75.4%) at an effective density of 0.706, and corrects FSparse from 58.7% to 72.8% at effective density 0.807. On HumanEval, SIRIUS boosts CSparse from 20.7% to 52.4% and FSparse from 45.7% to 61.6% (full model: 56.1%), with the FSparse+SIRIUS configuration actually surpassing the full dense model. Wallclock measurements confirm the theoretical efficiency: SIRIUS achieves ~20% latency reduction over the full model on-chip (8B) and ~35% reduction in offloading settings (70B on a single L40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two contextual sparsity baselines: CSparse (Griffin, Dong et al. 2024) and FSparse (CATS, Lee et al. 2024). Both baselines suffer severe degradation on reasoning tasks — on GSM8K with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, CSparse drops from 75.4% to 38.4% and FSparse from 75.4% to 58.7%. SIRIUS recovers most of this gap: CSparse+SIRIUS reaches 70.5% and FSparse+SIRIUS reaches 72.8%, both within 3–5 points of the full model while maintaining effective densities of 0.706 and 0.807 respectively (i.e., still 29% and 19% fewer parameters loaded per token than the full model). On HumanEval, the advantage is even more pronounced: CSparse+SIRIUS jumps from 20.7% to 52.4% (full: 56.1%), and FSparse+SIRIUS reaches 61.6%, actually exceeding the full dense model's 56.1%. The efficiency gain is consistent across all six models evaluated, with additional sparsity overhead never exceeding 10% in any configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the contextual sparsity line of work—specifically Griffin (coarse-grained, Dong et al. 2024) and CATS (fine-grained, Lee et al. 2024)—which demonstrated that MLP neuron selection based on input activations can reduce inference cost with minimal quality loss on classification and summarization tasks. SIRIUS departs from these methods by identifying their fundamental failure mode on reasoning tasks and introducing a correction mechanism that borrows structural ideas from speculative decoding (parallel verification, acceptance/rejection) while explicitly arguing that standard speculative decoding is too inefficient for this setting due to the sparse model's large size and the need for lossy (not lossless) correction. The KV cache rewriting and hardware-efficient tree building are novel engineering contributions not present in prior speculative decoding or sparsity work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that contextual sparsity catastrophically degrades reasoning performance (e.g., GSM8K accuracy dropping from 75% to 38% at 50% sparsity) is a critical caveat for practitioners deploying sparse LLMs, as prior work had largely reported only classification or summarization results. SIRIUS provides a practical, training-free correction that recovers most of this gap with only 4–8% additional parameter density, delivering 20–35% real latency reduction on mainstream GPUs. However, the rollback-based correction remains inefficient, and the broader problem of achieving strong-model-quality output from a weak model without strictly matching its output distribution (i.e., lossy verification) remains open. The method is also limited to the memory-bound regime (sequence length ≤ 64) and requires the full model to remain in GPU memory, constraining its applicability to very large models in memory-constrained settings.
