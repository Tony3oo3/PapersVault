## Problem

Existing scene flow estimation methods suffer from reduced accuracy under occlusions and noisy sensor inputs, and they provide no mechanism to indicate the reliability of individual predictions. This is particularly problematic for safety-critical downstream tasks such as autonomous driving, where knowing when a prediction is likely wrong is as important as the prediction itself. No prior work has formulated full scene flow estimation as a generative diffusion process that simultaneously improves robustness and yields per-point uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source point cloud P_source ∈ R^{N1×3} and a target point cloud P_target ∈ R^{N2×3}, with N1=N2=4096 points (furthest-point-sampled) during training and 8192 during inference. It outputs a scene flow vector field V ∈ R^{N1×3} mapping each source point to its target position, plus a per-point uncertainty estimate computed as the standard deviation across 20 independently sampled denoising trajectories. The diffusion process uses 20 training timesteps and only 2 DDIM sampling steps at inference, keeping the computational overhead modest relative to the underlying transformer network.

## Method

DiffSF formulates scene flow estimation as a conditional denoising diffusion process: the forward process gradually corrupts the ground truth flow field with Gaussian noise, while the reverse process reconstructs it from pure noise conditioned on both point clouds. The denoising network f_θ is built on GMSF's architecture (DGCNN feature extraction, a 14-layer Local-Global-Cross Transformer, and Global Correlation) but adds a two-stage warping scheme: the source cloud is first warped by the current noisy input V_t to produce an initial flow estimate, then re-warped by that estimate before the final Global Correlation prediction. Training minimizes a robust L1 loss (q=0.4, ε=0.01) between the predicted V_0 and ground truth at randomly sampled timesteps. The key insight is that the inherent stochasticity of the reverse diffusion process—sampling different initial noise vectors—yields multiple hypotheses per input, whose standard deviation serves as a calibrated uncertainty measure without any modification to the training objective.

## Results

On FlyingThings3D without occlusions (F3D_s), DiffSF achieves EPE3D of 0.0062 m, ACCs of 99.54%, ACCr of 99.80%, and Outliers of 1.41%, improving over the previous state-of-the-art GMSF by 31% in EPE3D, 44% in ACCs, 35% in ACCr, and 45% in Outliers. On F3D with occlusions (F3D_o), DiffSF achieves EPE3D of 0.015 m and Outliers of 3.5%, a 32% and 38% improvement over GMSF respectively. On the real-world Waymo-Open dataset, DiffSF achieves EPE3D of 0.080 m and Outliers of 41.9%, consistently outperforming all prior methods including GMSF (0.083 m, 43.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSF outperforms all 11 compared baselines across all three benchmarks and all four metrics. Against the closest competitor GMSF [43] (the architectural baseline), DiffSF reduces EPE3D by 31% on F3D_s (0.0062 vs. 0.0090) and by 32% on F3D_o (0.015 vs. 0.022), while reducing Outliers by 45% and 38% respectively. On KITTI_s, DiffSF achieves the best EPE3D (0.0098) and ACCs (98.59%), surpassing GMSF (0.0215, 96.22%) and DifFlow3D (0.0089, 98.13%) in EPE3D and ACCs respectively. On the real-world Waymo-Open dataset, DiffSF edges out GMSF on all four metrics (EPE3D 0.080 vs. 0.083, Outliers 41.9% vs. 43.5%) and substantially outperforms FH-Net [7] (EPE3D 0.175) and FESTA [37] (EPE3D 0.223). Earlier methods such as FlowNet3D [22], HPLFlowNet [9], PointPWC [41], and FLOT [26] are significantly outperformed, with DiffSF achieving roughly 18–19× lower EPE3D than FlowNet3D on F3D_s. The ablation (Table 7) further confirms that both the improved architecture (adding initial estimation) and the diffusion process independently contribute, with the diffusion process providing the largest gains in robustness metrics (ACCs +10.3%, Outliers −10.8% over the architecture-only variant).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSF builds directly on GMSF's transformer-based architecture (DGCNN feature extraction, Local-Global-Cross Transformer, and Global Correlation) but reformulates scene flow estimation as a conditional denoising diffusion process following the DDPM framework. The key conceptual departure is treating the scene flow vector field as a noisy signal to be iteratively denoised rather than a direct regression target, drawing on the broader trend of applying diffusion models to dense vision tasks (optical flow, segmentation, pose estimation). Unlike DifFlow3D, which uses diffusion only to refine an initial estimate, DiffSF estimates the full scene flow from pure Gaussian noise, making the diffusion process central to the prediction rather than a post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is that the diffusion formulation provides per-point uncertainty estimates 'for free' through multi-hypothesis sampling, with a near-linear correlation between predicted uncertainty and actual endpoint error—enabling safety-critical downstream systems to flag unreliable predictions via a simple threshold. This addresses a real gap: prior deterministic scene flow methods cannot indicate when they are wrong. Open questions include whether self-supervised or weakly-supervised training can reduce the dependence on annotated ground truth, and whether the transformer-based global matching can scale beyond the ~8K point limit imposed by quadratic attention complexity.
