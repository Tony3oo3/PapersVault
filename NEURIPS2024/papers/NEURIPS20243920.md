## Problem

Most RL-based traffic signal control methods assume continuous, complete sensor data from all intersections, an assumption that fails in practice due to sensor malfunctions and budget constraints. While MissLight addressed TSC with missing data in the online setting, no prior work tackles this problem in the offline setting, where training on collected data without environment interaction is safer and more practical. The core challenge is unifying traffic data imputation and decision-making when only partial rewards are available, without letting missing values corrupt the learning signal.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a C-step (C=5) observed trajectory of the local intersection (vehicle counts and queue lengths per entrance lane, with missing entries masked by Gaussian noise), partial observable rewards (not full returns, with uncollected rewards simply absent), and observation trajectories from neighboring intersections feeding into the local one. It produces imputed missing observations, H-step (H=3) future observations for long-horizon planning, and a discrete traffic signal phase (one of four phases A–D) generated by a separate inverse dynamics model that maps consecutive observations to the action that caused the transition.

## Method

DiffLight unifies imputation and decision-making as a single conditional diffusion process over observation trajectories, using a Partial Rewards Conditioned Diffusion (PRCD) formulation that conditions only on the observable subset of rewards via binary masks and classifier-free guidance, avoiding the confusion that zero-padding introduces between imputed and true rewards. The noise predictor is a Spatial-Temporal Transformer (STFormer) with three attention modules per layer: Communication Cross-Attention (CCA) captures dependencies between the local intersection and its neighbors, while Spatial and Temporal Self-Attention (SSA, TSA) model lane-level and time-level structure locally. At inference, a Diffusion Communication Mechanism (DCM) propagates the partially denoised observations of neighboring intersections back into the local intersection's conditioning at each reverse-diffusion step, enabling information sharing even when neighbor sensors are offline. Training is self-supervised and offline: missing values are created by randomly masking observed trajectories (random missing) or entire intersections (kriging missing), and the model is optimized with a combined diffusion denoising loss and inverse-dynamics loss using the DDIM sampler over 100 steps.

## Results

The primary evaluation metric is Average Travel Time (ATT, lower is better) on five real-world traffic datasets (Hangzhou_1, Hangzhou_2, Jinan_1, Jinan_2, Jinan_3) under random missing (10%–50%) and kriging missing (6.25%–25%) scenarios. Under random missing at 50% rate, DiffLight achieves ATT of 304.71 (D_HZ^1), 333.90 (D_HZ^2), 290.02 (D_JN^1), 268.29 (D_JN^2), and 265.76 (D_JN^3), outperforming the strongest baseline (Diffuser) by 9–34 seconds. Under kriging missing at 25% rate, DiffLight achieves 302.16, 351.21, 329.67, 325.20, and 342.07, while Decision Diffuser degrades catastrophically (e.g., 836.46 on D_HZ^1 vs. DiffLight's 302.16). On the larger New York dataset (48 intersections) at 50% random missing, DiffLight achieves 266.82 ATT versus 453.97 for Diffuser and 455.07 for DD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffLight outperforms all six baselines (BC, CQL, TD3+BC, DT, Diffuser, DD) on the majority of dataset–rate combinations under both random and kriging missing. Under random missing at 50%, DiffLight beats the strongest non-diffusion baseline (TD3+BC) by 35–67 seconds and the strongest diffusion baseline (Diffuser) by 9–34 seconds; notably, DD collapses at 50% random missing on D_JN^1 (517.99 vs. DiffLight's 290.02) and D_HZ^1 (422.50 vs. 304.71). Under kriging missing, the gap widens dramatically: DD's ATT reaches 836.46 on D_HZ^1 at 25% missing versus DiffLight's 302.16, attributed to error accumulation in DD's three-stage pipeline (SFM imputation → diffusion → inverse dynamics). Diffuser is consistently the closest competitor, but DiffLight still leads by 5–30 seconds in most kriging-missing settings. Non-diffusion baselines (BC, CQL, TD3+BC, DT) are most severely degraded by imputed data, with DT showing the largest variance (e.g., 439.89 on D_JN^1 at 50% RM vs. 308.78 at 10% RM). On the 48-intersection New York dataset at 50% random missing, DiffLight (266.82) substantially outperforms all baselines, with the gap over Diffuser (453.97) and DD (455.07) exceeding 180 seconds, demonstrating superior scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Diffuser paradigm (diffusing on observation trajectories with an inverse dynamics model for discrete actions) to the traffic signal control domain under data-missing conditions, where no prior offline method existed. It departs from standard return-conditioned diffusion by introducing Partial Rewards Conditioned Diffusion (PRCD), which borrows the locally-conditioned diffusion masking mechanism from compositional scene generation to condition only on observable rewards rather than padded full trajectories. The spatial-temporal transformer noise model (STFormer) replaces the U-Net common in diffusion-based RL, drawing on spatial-temporal attention architectures from traffic forecasting, while the Diffusion Communication Mechanism (DCM) is a novel inference-time strategy with no direct analogue in prior diffusion-RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world traffic sensor networks inevitably produce missing data, yet prior offline TSC methods assumed complete observations. By unifying imputation and decision-making in a single diffusion process, DiffLight avoids the error accumulation that plagues two-stage approaches (demonstrated by DD's dramatic collapse in kriging missing). Key limitations include reliance on only two missing patterns (random and kriging), use of the simple store-and-forward method for baselines, and conditioning on partial rewards rather than returns, which the authors acknowledge may induce short-sightedness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to mixed or adversarial missing patterns, whether more sophisticated imputation for baselines would narrow the gap, and how the method scales beyond the 48-intersection New York network tested here.
