## Problem

The training dynamics of linear networks have been understood in two separate regimes—lazy (NTK) and balanced/active—but no unified description exists for the transition between them, and the balanced regime requires a special balanced initialization that is rarely used in practice. This paper asks whether a single formula can capture both regimes and the mixed dynamics in between, and whether the lazy/active transition can be characterized at the level of individual singular values rather than the whole network.

## Input / Output

The method takes a shallow linear network A_θ = W₂W₁ of width w, initialized with i.i.d. N(0,σ²) weights, and trained via gradient descent on the MSE loss to recover a rank-K matrix A* from noisy observations A*+E. The key output is a self-consistent ODE for A_θ(t) whose effective learning rate along each singular value s_i is 2η√(s_i² + σ⁴w²), meaning the dynamics are lazy (constant rate) for s_i ≪ σ²w and balanced (rate proportional to s_i) for s_i ≫ σ²w. The paper further produces a phase diagram in the (γ_w, γ_σ²) scaling space that predicts whether the network will recover A* or merely fit the noise.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) proves that for i.i.d. Gaussian initialization and gradient flow, the Gram matrices C₁ = W₁ᵀW₁ and C₂ = W₂W₂ᵀ concentrate around √(AᵀA + σ⁴w²I) and √(AAᵀ + σ⁴w²I) respectively, with error bounded by min{O(σ²w), O(√(d/w)‖C₁‖_op)}. The proof exploits the exact invariant W₁W₁ᵀ − W₂ᵀW₂ under gradient flow, the Marchenko–Pastur concentration of the initialization, and an inverse-function-theorem argument showing the cubic matrix equations satisfied by C₁, C₂ have a unique positive-definite solution. This yields the unified self-consistent dynamics ∂ₜA ≈ −η√(AAᵀ+σ⁴w²I)∇C − η∇C√(AᵀA+σ⁴w²I), which reduces to lazy dynamics when σ²w dominates all singular values and to balanced dynamics when it is negligible. The phase diagram (Theorem 2) then follows by tracking when the K signal singular values cross the σ²w threshold during a short initial lazy phase that provides the alignment needed for subsequent balanced convergence, yielding a test error bound that vanishes in the active regime (γ_σ²+γ_w < 1) but remains bounded away from zero in the lazy regime (γ_σ²+γ_w > 1).

## Results

The paper establishes a phase diagram for shallow linear networks trained on MSE, with the critical boundary at γ_σ² + γ_w = 1 separating the lazy regime (γ_σ² + γ_w > 1, where test error is bounded below by a constant and the network fails to recover A*) from the active/mixed regime (γ_σ² + γ_w < 1, where test error vanishes as O(σ⁴w + σ⁴w²log²d/d² + d^{-1/2} + σ²w/d + η²log²d/d²)). Empirically, with γ_σ² = −1.85 and γ_w = 2.25 (active regime), the 5 largest singular values (matching Rank A* = 5) visibly 'speed up' upon crossing the σ²w threshold, converging before the remaining singular values fit the noise. The self-consistent dynamics from Theorem 1 closely track actual GD trajectories across the entire phase diagram, with the log-distance between GD and the self-consistent approximation remaining small even outside the theoretically guaranteed region (Figure 2, bottom right).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between dynamical regimes rather than competing methods. The paper validates its self-consistent dynamics (Eq. 1) against actual gradient descent: in Figure 1 (left), train and test error curves for GD and the self-consistent dynamics are nearly indistinguishable in the active regime. In Figure 2 (bottom right), the log-distance between GD and the self-consistent dynamics remains small across the full (γ_σ², γ_w) phase diagram, including regions where the theoretical guarantee does not formally apply. Compared to the pure lazy regime, the mixed/active regime achieves vanishing test error (vs. a constant lower bound) and exhibits a low-rank bias (stable rank ≈ 5 matching Rank A*, vs. overestimation in the lazy regime). Compared to the pure balanced regime, the mixed regime converges from any random Gaussian initialization without requiring the strong positive-alignment assumption, because the initial lazy period naturally aligns the network before singular values cross the σ²w threshold and enter balanced dynamics. The paper also notes a trade-off: deeper into the active regime (smaller γ_σ² + γ_w) yields lower test error but requires more iterations to reach the minimal test error point (Figure 2, bottom left).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of analysis on linear network training: the lazy/NTK regime (Jacot et al. 2018; Chizat & Bach 2018) and the balanced regime (Arora et al. 2018, 2019; Saxe et al. 2014). It builds on the saddle-to-saddle dynamics framework (Jacot et al. 2022) and the greedy low-rank learning interpretation (Li et al. 2020), but departs from both by proving that the self-consistent dynamics hold for standard i.i.d. Gaussian initialization rather than requiring balanced or specially aligned initializations. The key conceptual departure is showing that the lazy/active distinction is not a global property of the network but a per-singular-value phenomenon, revealing a 'mixed regime' that was previously uncharacterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mixed regime resolves a long-standing tension: balanced dynamics provide low-rank bias but require alignment (and can get stuck at saddles), while lazy dynamics converge from any initialization but lack low-rank bias. The paper shows that a short initial lazy period naturally provides the alignment needed for subsequent balanced learning, making the 'best of both worlds' accessible from standard random initialization. The phase diagram (γ_σ² + γ_w vs. 1) gives concrete guidance on when to expect each regime, and the finding that the lazy regime requires γ_w > 2 explains its rarity in practice. The main open question, explicitly raised by the authors, is whether analogous mixed regimes exist in nonlinear networks and whether they are key to understanding general DNN convergence.
