## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of transformer training dynamics relied on simplifications such as weight reparameterization, attention linearization, special initialization, and lazy regime, leaving it unclear whether the resulting characterizations reflect actual training behavior. This paper asks what happens when a shallow transformer is trained from random initialization via gradient flow on a word co-occurrence detection task, without any of those simplifications, and whether the resulting dynamics can be rigorously characterized.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of L orthonormal d-dimensional word embeddings (including two designated target tokens, a common token present in every sequence, and random filler tokens) and produces a scalar classification score indicating whether the two target words co-occur. The data distribution is label-balanced across four sample types (both targets, only μ₁, only μ₂, neither) with a 1/2 : 1/6 : 1/6 : 1/6 ratio. Beyond the classification score, the analysis characterizes the learned attention matrix structure, revealing strong positive coupling between the two target signals and strong negative coupling between a target signal and the common token.

## Method

The architecture is a one-layer transformer with a self-attention module (Q, K, V matrices of dimension m×d) followed by a linear MLP (m₁ neurons), trained via continuous-time gradient flow from random Gaussian initialization to minimize cross-entropy loss. Rather than tracking the full weight matrices, the authors formulate a coupled dynamical system of six differential equations tracking MLP outputs on target tokens, attention score correlations, neuron inner products, and norm changes for each weight matrix. The central finding is that gradient flow naturally divides training into two phases: Phase 1 where the MLP quickly aligns with target signals while attention remains near initialization, and Phase 2 where attention and MLP jointly evolve to enlarge the classification margin and drive loss to near minimum. A novel property termed 'automatic balancing of gradients' is proven, showing that gradient ratios across sample groups remain bounded throughout training, which is the key technical ingredient enabling the near-minimum loss guarantee. The entire analysis requires no weight reparameterization, attention linearization, special initialization, or lazy regime assumptions.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical: Theorem 3.1 establishes that in Phase 1 (time Õ(1/m)), the linear MLP aligns with the two target signals (G(μ₁) ≥ Ω(1), G(μ₂) ≥ Ω(1), G(μ₃) ≤ −Ω(1)) while attention matrices remain near initialization, yielding correct classification but loss still Θ(1). Theorem 3.3 shows that by time T* = Θ(poly(m)), both training and generalization losses drop to ≤ 1/poly(m), and the attention matrix takes the explicit form W_K^T W_Q = W_K^(0)T W_Q^(0) + Σ C_{i₁,i₂} μ_{i₁}μ_{i₂}^T with large positive coefficients C_{1,2}, C_{2,1} and large negative coefficients C_{3,1}, C_{3,2}. The synthetic experiment (60 samples, 5 tokens of dim 64, m=128, m₁=256, 30000 epochs) qualitatively confirms the two-phase behavior: attention scores are flat in Phase 1 and diverge in Phase 2 (Fig. 1a), while per-type losses follow the predicted trajectory (Fig. 1b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against any baseline methods; it is a theoretical analysis of gradient flow dynamics for a single shallow transformer architecture on a synthetic co-occurrence task. The empirical verification (Figure 1) is self-contained: it confirms the paper's own theorems rather than benchmarking against competing approaches. The key technical contribution over prior work is that the analysis avoids common simplifications (weight reparameterization, attention linearization, special initialization, lazy regime) by treating the four weight matrices as a coupled dynamical system and proving the novel 'automatic balancing of gradients' property (Lemmas 4.4–4.5), which ensures all sample losses decrease at comparable rates. The synthetic experiment uses Kaiming initialization and standard gradient descent (lr=0.01) with no architectural or algorithmic variants compared.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of transformer training dynamics work (Jelassi et al. 2022, Li et al. 2022, Tian et al. 2023, Tarzanagh et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024, Huang et al. 2023) by refusing the standard simplifications—weight reparameterization, attention linearization, special initialization, and lazy regime—that those analyses rely on. Instead, it formulates the problem as a coupled dynamical system tracking six types of scalar quantities (MLP projections, attention scores, and Gram matrices of W_V, W_K, W_Q), enabling analysis from random initialization with full softmax attention. The word co-occurrence detection task itself is explicitly framed as absent from prior transformer training dynamics literature, and the 'automatic balancing of gradients' property is a new technical tool not present in earlier analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-phase characterization (fast MLP alignment followed by joint attention-MLP margin expansion) provides a mechanistic explanation for why shallow transformers can learn co-occurrence from scratch, and the automatic balancing property offers a reusable analytical technique for proving near-minimum loss in other nonconvex attention settings. However, the scope is narrow: single-layer, single-head, linear MLP, a highly structured data distribution with orthonormal embeddings, and continuous gradient flow rather than discrete optimization. Open questions include whether the two-phase structure and gradient balancing persist in multi-head, multi-layer architectures, under adaptive learning rates, or with correlated (rather than i.i.d.) token sequences—settings much closer to real language.
