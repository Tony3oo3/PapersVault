## Problem

SSM-based vision backbones suffer from a long-range forgetting problem where token contributions decay exponentially with scanning distance, and the multi-scan strategy adopted to mitigate this introduces significant computational redundancy across scanning routes. The paper identifies that decay rates vary substantially across different scanning directions, meaning some routes dominate information retention while others contribute redundantly, creating an unnecessary efficiency-performance trade-off in parameter-constrained vision models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240808/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 1280×800 for detection, 512×512 for segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-wise semantic segmentation maps depending on the downstream head. Internally, the 2D feature map is split into a full-resolution branch and a half-resolution branch (via stride-2 depthwise convolution), each flattened into 1D token sequences along distinct scanning paths before being processed by the selective state space block.

## Method

MSVMamba introduces a Multi-Scale 2D (MS2D) scanning strategy that divides VMamba's four scanning routes into one full-resolution scan and three half-resolution scans, where the downsampled sequences share a single S6 block before being upsampled and summed with the full-resolution output. This reduces total sequence length from 4L to (1+3/s²)L, cutting redundancy while preserving fine-grained detail from the full-resolution path and long-range context from the shorter downsampled paths. A Convolutional Feed-Forward Network (ConvFFN) with depthwise convolution and two FC layers is added as a channel mixer to complement the token-mixing SSM, and a Squeeze-Excitation block is inserted after the multi-scale scan. The overall architecture nests this intra-block hierarchy within VMamba's inter-stage hierarchical design, yielding model variants from 7M to 91M parameters that achieve 83.0–84.4% top-1 on ImageNet-1K while reducing FLOPs and memory relative to VMamba at comparable accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MSVMamba-T (32M params, 5.1G FLOPs) achieves 83.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming VMambav3-T (82.6%) and LocalVMamba-T (82.7%) at similar compute budgets. The larger MSVMamba-B (91M params, 16.3G FLOPs) reaches 84.4% top-1, surpassing VMambav3-B by 0.5% and Swin-B by 0.9%. On COCO with Mask R-CNN 1× schedule, MSVMamba-T achieves 46.9 box mAP and 42.5 instance mAP, exceeding Swin-T by +4.2 box AP. On ADE20K with UperNet, MSVMamba-T attains 47.9 mIoU (single-scale), matching VMambav3-T and outperforming Swin-T by +3.5 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSVMamba consistently outperforms the closest SSM-based competitors: it beats VMambav3 by +0.4 to +0.5% top-1 accuracy across Tiny/Small/Base sizes while using fewer FLOPs (e.g., 5.1G vs 4.9G for Tiny, 8.8G vs 8.7G for Small), and surpasses LocalVMamba by +0.3% at Tiny size with 0.6G fewer FLOPs. Against ViT baselines, MSVMamba-T exceeds Swin-T by +1.7% and DeiT-S by +3.2% at comparable parameter counts. In object detection on COCO (1× schedule), MSVMamba-T achieves 46.9 box AP, outperforming Swin-T (42.7) by +4.2 AP and ConvNeXt-T (44.2) by +2.7 AP, though it trails VMambav3-T (47.3) by 0.4 AP. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, MSVMamba-T matches VMambav3-T at 47.9 mIoU (SS) while beating Swin-T by +3.5 and ConvNeXt-T by +1.9 mIoU. The paper also demonstrates that MSVMamba-T uses significantly less memory (2413 MB) than VMambav1-T (6639 MB) at similar throughput, highlighting the efficiency gains from the multi-scale scanning design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VMamba's hierarchical visual state space model by introducing a Multi-Scale 2D (MS2D) scanning strategy that splits the four scanning routes of SS2D between full-resolution and downsampled feature maps, directly addressing the computational redundancy of multi-scan strategies. It builds on the S6 selective scan mechanism from Mamba and the bidirectional/cross-scan formulations from ViM and VMamba, but departs from them by applying downsampling selectively to only a subset of scans rather than uniformly. The addition of ConvFFN as a channel mixer represents a conceptual departure from prior vision Mamba architectures (ViM, VMamba, LocalVMamba) that focus exclusively on token mixing via SSMs, borrowing instead from the structural paradigms of CMT and RepViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSVMamba offers a practical efficiency-performance trade-off for vision SSMs: it reduces FLOPs and memory usage relative to VMamba while improving accuracy across classification, detection, and segmentation, making SSM-based backbones more competitive with Swin and ConvNeXt at equivalent compute budgets. The decay-rate analysis (Fig. 2) providing empirical motivation for the multi-scale design is a useful diagnostic tool for understanding SSM behavior in vision. However, the paper acknowledges that the long-range forgetting problem can also be addressed by simply scaling up model size, leaving open the question of whether MS2D's advantages persist at larger scales where parameter constraints are less binding. The scalability of the approach beyond the tested sizes (up to 91M parameters) remains unexplored.
