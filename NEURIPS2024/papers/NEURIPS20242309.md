## Problem

Existing convergence bounds for shuffled SGD (sampling without replacement) rely on the global smoothness constant L_max, yielding step sizes of order 1/(nL_max) that are too restrictive to explain the empirically observed superiority of data permutations over with-replacement SGD. This theory-practice gap means prior bounds predict slower convergence than what is actually achieved on standard machine learning problems, and the bounds are no better than full gradient descent in the small-epoch regime.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite-sum convex objective f(x) = (1/n)Σf_i(x) with per-component smoothness constants L_i (or Lipschitz constants G_i in the non-smooth case), an initial point x_0, a step size schedule, a data permutation strategy (random reshuffling, shuffle-once, or incremental gradient), and a number of epochs K. The output is a weighted-average iterate x̂_K with a non-asymptotic expected optimality gap bound that depends on novel permutation-dependent smoothness parameters L̂_π and L̃_π (or G̃_π, G̃_π in the non-smooth case) rather than the worst-case L_max, and the complexity is expressed in individual gradient oracle queries. For generalized linear models, the data matrix A appears explicitly in the smoothness parameters, making the bounds data-dependent.

## Method

The paper reformulates shuffled SGD as a primal-dual method where the primal update corresponds to a proximal step and the dual update is a cyclic coordinate maximization over the Fenchel conjugates, enabling the use of cyclic bias accumulation techniques on the dual side. The key technical contribution is introducing novel smoothness parameters L̂_π and L̃_π (defined via operator norms of permuted data-matrix products weighted by component smoothness) that are always ≤ L_max but can be smaller by up to a factor of n, directly capturing how data structure affects convergence. For generalized linear models f(x) = (1/n)Σℓ_i(a_i^⊤x), the data matrix A is decoupled from the scalar loss smoothness L_i, yielding bounds with up to O(√n) improvement over prior work in the small-epoch regime. The framework uniformly handles all three shuffling schemes (RR, SO, IG), extends to mini-batch estimators of arbitrary size b, and generalizes to non-smooth Lipschitz convex objectives where the analogous parameters G̃_π and G̃_π replace the smoothness constants.

## Results

The paper establishes tighter convergence bounds for shuffled SGD (RR and SO schemes) by introducing novel data-dependent smoothness parameters L̂ and L̃ that replace the worst-case L_max. For smooth convex finite-sum problems, the complexity improves from O(nL_max D²/ε) to O(n√(L̂L̃) D²/ε) in the first (dominant) term, yielding up to an O(√n) improvement. Empirically, on 15 benchmark datasets, the ratio L_max/L̂ ranges from 3.52 (gisette) to 194 (real-sim), with log(n)·L_max/L̂ exponents α ∈ [0.15, 0.96], confirming that the new bounds are substantially tighter in practice. For non-smooth Lipschitz settings, the paper provides O(nG̅D²/ε²) complexity, improving upon Shamir's O(B̄²G_max²/ε²) which carries an irreducible 1/√n error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Nguyen et al. [34] and Mishchenko et al. [30], the paper's Theorem 1 (general smooth convex) and Theorem 2 (generalized linear model) strictly improve the first complexity term by replacing L_max with √(L̂L̃) (or √(L̂^g L̃^g)), while matching the second term that is tight per the lower bound of Cha et al. [12]. The improvement factor is √(L_max/L̂), which empirically ranges from ~1.9× (gisette, L_max/L̂=3.52) to ~14× (real-sim, L_max/L̂=194) across 15 datasets. For the non-smooth Lipschitz setting, the paper's Theorem 3 improves upon Shamir [42] by removing the irreducible 1/√n error term and the bounded-iterates assumption, yielding O(nG̅D²/ε²) instead of O(B̄²G_max²/ε²). The step size also improves from O(1/(nL_max)) to O(1/(n√(L̂L̃))), which Figure 1 demonstrates leads to visibly faster convergence on logistic regression (duke, leu, a9a). The paper's bounds are never worse than prior work (since L̂ ≤ L_max and L̃ ≤ L_max), but can be up to O(√n) tighter, mirroring the gains from component smoothness in randomized coordinate methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets shuffled SGD (sampling without replacement) as a primal-dual method performing cyclic coordinate updates on the dual side, drawing on the cyclic bias accumulation techniques developed for cyclic coordinate descent with random permutations (Lee & Wright [24], Wright & Lee [47]). It departs from prior shuffled SGD analyses (Mishchenko et al. [30], Nguyen et al. [34], Cha et al. [12]) that relied on global smoothness constants and triangle inequalities, by introducing fine-grained, data-dependent smoothness parameters (L̂, L̃) that explicitly capture the data matrix structure. The connection to dual coordinate methods (Shalev-Shwartz & Zhang [41]) is extended from the randomized setting to the cyclic/shuffled setting, which had previously only been studied numerically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(√n) improvement in convergence bounds (up to a factor of O(√n) over prior work) provides a theoretical explanation for the well-known empirical superiority of shuffled SGD over SGD with replacement, narrowing a significant theory-practice gap. The empirical validation across 15 diverse datasets showing L_max/L̂ scales as n^α (α ∈ [0.15, 0.96]) strengthens the practical relevance. Open questions include whether bounds can be tightened to depend on the mean of the permutation-dependent parameter L̂_π rather than its maximum, whether distributional assumptions on the data can yield further improvements, and whether the framework extends to non-convex or strongly convex settings with matching lower bounds.
