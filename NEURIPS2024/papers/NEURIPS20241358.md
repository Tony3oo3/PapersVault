## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks are ad-hoc collections of modality- and task-specific datasets that cannot systematically characterize the diverse capabilities of modern multimodal foundation models. Without a holistic framework that disentangles which specific skills, information flows, and real-world challenges models struggle with, it is difficult to determine how modeling decisions (scale, architecture, training objectives) drive downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image with a text prompt (question, instruction, or task description), and the model under test generates a free-form text response such as an answer, caption, classification label, or SMILES notation. Performance is measured via BARTScore between generated text and ground truth, then normalized via min-max scaling where the floor is the worst model's score and the ceiling is the identity score BARTScore(r,r). For GPT-4V and Gemini (due to API query limits), only 100 random samples per dataset are evaluated, while the remaining nine models are evaluated on full test sets.

## Method

HEMM organizes 30 image-text datasets into a three-level taxonomy: (1) basic multimodal skills (redundant/unique/synergistic interactions, fine-grained vs. coarse-grained alignment, reasoning depth, external knowledge), (2) information flow (querying, translation, editing, fusion), and (3) real-world use cases (multimedia, affective computing, natural sciences, healthcare, HCI). Eleven models spanning 1.6B to frontier-scale parameters are further categorized along modeling dimensions: parameter count, modality processing (interleaved vs. separate), training type (end-to-end vs. modular fine-tuning with frozen encoders), pretraining data scale and diversity, and presence of instruction tuning. The evaluation protocol computes per-dataset BARTScore, aggregates via min-max normalization, and additionally conducts human preference evaluation (1000 pairwise comparisons by 5 annotators) to compute Elo ratings, enabling cross-validation of automatic metrics against human judgment and revealing where BARTScore rankings diverge from perceived quality.

## Results

Across 30 datasets and 11 multimodal models, GEMINI achieves the highest average BARTScore (0.44), followed by INSTRUCT-BLIP (0.42), BLIP-2 (0.41), and GPT-4V (0.40), while OPENFLAMINGO (0.06) and EMU (0.11) perform worst. Large models (GPT-4V, GEMINI) significantly outperform medium and small models (p < 0.001), with average scores of 0.42 vs. 0.24 vs. 0.23 respectively. Translation tasks are the hardest information flow type (avg. 0.19), while fusion is easiest (0.33); Healthcare (0.20), Science (0.20), and HCI (0.16) are the most challenging use cases, compared to Multimedia (0.31). Human evaluation (Elo ratings) confirms the BARTScore rankings for the top 4 and bottom 2 models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEMM evaluates 11 multimodal foundation models spanning 1.6B to >1000B parameters. GEMINI leads on both BARTScore (0.44) and Elo rating (1074), while GPT-4V (0.40 BARTScore, Elo 1057) trails slightly due to generating hedging keywords like 'Indeterminate' and refusing to answer on sensitive datasets. INSTRUCT-BLIP (0.42) and BLIP-2 (0.41) are notable outliers — despite having only 4B and 12.1B parameters respectively, they approach the performance of much larger proprietary models, benefiting from instruction tuning and modular fine-tuning. FUYU-8B (0.31) achieves a mid-range BARTScore but a lower Elo (992), suggesting its outputs are less preferred by human annotators despite reasonable text similarity. The bottom performers — EMU (0.11) and OPENFLAMINGO (0.06) — struggle with instruction following and produce hallucinated responses, particularly on challenging datasets like DECIMER and ENRICO. Key modeling trends: diverse pretraining data improves scores from 0.21 to 0.30, instruction tuning from 0.22 to 0.30, and large model scale from 0.23 to 0.42.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEMM positions itself as a systematic successor to prior multimodal benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, Touchstone, Mm-vet, VisIT-Bench) that the authors characterize as collections of tasks lacking a unifying taxonomy. It draws on the information-theoretic framework for multimodal interactions (redundancy, uniqueness, synergy) established by Liang et al. and extends the 'holistic evaluation' concept previously applied to text-to-image models (Lee et al., 2023) to the broader vision-language setting. The three-level decomposition—basic skills, information flow, and real-world use cases—repurposes existing dataset collections (VQA, ScienceQA, PathVQA, etc.) under a new organizational lens rather than introducing new tasks, while the model categorization along axes like modality processing, training type, and instruction tuning provides a structured comparison framework absent from prior benchmark papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEMM provides the multimodal foundation model community with a structured diagnostic tool that identifies where current models fail (external knowledge, synergistic interactions, translation tasks, domain-specific use cases like healthcare and HCI) and which modeling choices matter most (scale, data diversity, instruction tuning). The consistently low absolute performance even for frontier models (best BARTScore ~0.44) underscores that multimodal understanding remains far from solved, particularly for tasks requiring world knowledge and compositional reasoning. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to image-text pairs, reliance on BARTScore as the primary metric, and only 100 samples per dataset for API-based models (GPT-4V, Gemini), which constrains statistical power. Open questions the authors flag include extending HEMM to additional modalities (video, audio), adding fairness/robustness/privacy metrics, and discovering new evaluation dimensions beyond the current taxonomy.
