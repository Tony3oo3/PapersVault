## Problem

Existing inverse rendering methods either ignore indirect illumination entirely (Nvdiffrecmc treats rays hitting object surfaces as zero light) or model inter-reflections at prohibitive computational cost (Nefii requires 20h training). This makes it impossible to correctly disentangle materials and environmental lighting in scenes with multiple smooth, reflective objects where light bounces between surfaces, a common real-world scenario.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244075/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 200 multi-view RGB images per scene (100 held out for testing) rendered in Blender under various environmental lighting conditions, with 128 Monte Carlo samples per pixel. It produces an explicit triangular mesh (via Flexicubes), spatially-varying PBR material maps (diffuse albedo k_d and ORM: occlusion/roughness/metalness), and a learned environmental light map. The pipeline is designed for direct plug-in to industrial rendering engines for relighting and material editing.

## Method

Ref-MC² performs multi-times Monte Carlo ray tracing to model indirect illumination, but addresses the resulting computational explosion by flattening recursive sampling into sequential single-time steps: the diffuse indirect component is approximated via the Lambertian model (direction-independent), stored as a self-supervised MLP-queried map, while only the specular component is traced within a narrow lobe around the reflective direction. For geometry, the method first learns an SDF with Spherical Gaussian encoding (replacing standard positional encoding) to disambiguate reflective appearance and produce a high-quality initial mesh, then refines it through Flexicubes in a differentiable rendering pipeline. Training combines photometric loss, spatial smoothness regularization on materials, and a self-supervised diffuse loss that enforces consistency between the rendered diffuse light and the learned diffuse map. The default 2-times sampling achieves near-3-times quality at 4× less computation, and the specularity-adaptive acceleration strategy halves training time while preserving PSNR.

## Results

On the Ref-MC² Reflective Dataset, Ref-MC² achieves a PSNR of 28.1 dB with 2-times Monte Carlo sampling, outperforming Nvdiffrec (26.9), Nvdiffrecmc (25.7), and Nefii (22.3). Training takes 6.5 hours on a single RTX 3090, which is 3× faster than Nefii (20 h) but longer than Nvdiffrec (30 min) and Nvdiffrecmc (45 min) due to the additional SDF geometry initialization stage. The specularity-adaptive acceleration strategy (Ours vs. Ours w/o Acc.) halves training time from 11.5 h to 6.5 h while maintaining nearly identical PSNR (28.0 vs. 28.1). On the NeRO real-scene geometry benchmark, Ref-MC² achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance on the Materials scene (0.0030 vs. 0.0057 for NeRO) and matches NeRO on the Coral scene (0.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ref-MC² outperforms all three inverse-rendering baselines on PSNR: it beats Nvdiffrec [28] by 1.2 dB (28.1 vs. 26.9) and Nvdiffrecmc [9] by 2.4 dB (28.1 vs. 25.7), with the largest margin over Nefii [45] at 5.8 dB (28.1 vs. 22.3). Nvdiffrec and Nvdiffrecmc both fail to model inter-reflections, producing noisy environment maps and entangled materials, while Nefii, though it considers indirect illumination, produces overly diffuse results on highly specular objects and requires 20 h of training. On the NeRO real-scene geometry benchmark [18], Ref-MC² achieves the best Chamfer Distance on the Materials scene (0.0030 vs. 0.0057 for NeRO, 0.016 for Nvdiffrec/Nvdiffrecmc) and ties NeRO on the Coral scene (0.13), while Nvdiffrec and Nvdiffrecmc are substantially worse (0.28 and 0.25 respectively). The ablation removing geometry initialization (Ours w/o Geo.) drops PSNR to 24.6, confirming that the SDF-based initialization with Spherical Gaussian encoding is critical for handling multi-bounce reflections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Nvdiffrec → Nvdiffrecmc lineage of differentiable inverse rendering by continuing ray tracing beyond the first surface hit to model inter-reflections, a step that Nvdiffrecmc explicitly abandons by treating blocked rays as zero illumination. For geometry, it departs from the standard Integrated Positional Encoding used in SDF-based methods (RefNeuS, UniSDF) by substituting Spherical Gaussian encoding to better capture direction-dependent reflective appearance, and replaces DMTET with Flexicubes for differentiable mesh refinement. The efficiency strategy of approximating the diffuse indirect component via a self-supervised Lambertian map (Eq. 9–10) is a novel decomposition that avoids re-tracing diffuse rays at each bounce, a trick not present in prior multi-bounce methods like Neural Microfacet Fields or Nefii.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Inter-reflections among multiple smooth objects are ubiquitous in real scenes (metallic tables, glass, polished floors) yet remain a blind spot for most inverse rendering pipelines; this paper provides the first full PBR disentanglement (albedo, roughness, metalness, environment map) that explicitly accounts for such indirect illumination while retaining an explicit mesh output compatible with industrial pipelines. Key open questions include whether the 2-bounce limit can be lifted for mirror-like surfaces (where specular energy does not attenuate), whether the synthetic Blender dataset generalizes to real-world captures with uncontrolled lighting, and whether the 6.5-hour training cost can be reduced further for practical adoption. The Lambertian approximation for diffuse indirect light, while effective here, may break down for materials with strong anisotropic or clearcoat components.
