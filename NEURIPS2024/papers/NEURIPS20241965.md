## Problem

Existing fair active learning methods require sensitive attribute annotations on training data to measure group fairness disparities, creating privacy and safety risks under regulations like GDPR. This paper asks: how can one actively acquire new data to shift the fairness-accuracy Pareto frontier toward lower disparity and lower error simultaneously, without disclosing sensitive attributes of the training or unlabeled data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241965/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled training set P (no sensitive attributes), a large unlabeled pool U, and a small hold-out validation set Q_v that does contain sensitive attributes (e.g., gender, race, age). Before true labels are inquired, proxy labels are generated via a lowest-influence strategy (Eq. 5) that assigns each unlabeled example the class label minimizing its accuracy influence magnitude. The output is a set of r selected examples per round (with inquired true labels) added to training, plus updated model parameters; models whose validation accuracy drops below the initial warm-start accuracy by more than a tolerance ε are rejected.

## Method

FIS scores each unlabeled example z′ by two first-order influence terms computed via Taylor expansion: an accuracy influence (Eq. 2) measuring the negative inner product between z′'s loss gradient and the validation set's loss gradient, and a fairness influence (Eq. 4) measuring the negative inner product between z′'s loss gradient and the validation set's fairness-loss gradient (e.g., a smoothed DP or EOd term). Only examples with both influences ≤ 0 are candidates, ensuring the new data improves fairness without degrading accuracy. The algorithm iterates over T rounds, selecting top-r examples by lowest fairness influence, inquiring their true labels, and continuing ERM training. Theoretically, the paper proves a generalization error bound (Theorem 5.1) dominated by distribution shift and a risk disparity bound (Theorem 5.2) decomposed into distribution-shift, group-gap, and data-size terms, establishing that controlling the accuracy-side distribution shift while reducing the group gap via fairness-guided sampling enables simultaneous gains in both fairness and accuracy.

## Results

The paper evaluates FIS on three real-world datasets (CelebA, Adult, COMPAS) using (test_accuracy, fairness_violation) tuples across three fairness metrics (DP, EOp, EOd). On CelebA (2% labeled training data, Vision Transformer), FIS outperforms all baselines with a significant margin on all three fairness metrics while maintaining the same accuracy level. On Adult, FIS achieves the lowest EOp violation with comparable DP performance, and on COMPAS it achieves a much better accuracy-fairness trade-off than all baselines. The ablation on CelebA-Smiling shows that at the default 1× validation set size, FIS achieves (0.848 acc, 0.084 DP), (0.876 acc, 0.031 EOp), and (0.864 acc, 0.030 EOd).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIS is compared against five baselines: Base (ERM), Random sampling, BALD [12], ISAL [48], and JTT-20 [47]. On CelebA, FIS outperforms BALD and ISAL with a significant margin on all three fairness metrics (DP, EOp, EOd) while maintaining the same test accuracy, as confirmed in the label-budget ablation (Figure 3). On Adult, FIS achieves the lowest EOp violation among all methods and comparable DP performance, while ISAL and BALD show higher fairness violations at similar or lower accuracy. On COMPAS, FIS achieves a substantially better accuracy-fairness trade-off than all baselines; JTT-20 does achieve a lower fairness violation but at the cost of a significant accuracy drop, making it less practical. Overall, FIS consistently dominates the other active-learning baselines (BALD, ISAL) on fairness without sacrificing accuracy, and avoids the accuracy penalty that JTT-20 incurs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends influence-based active learning (Liu et al., ICCV 2021) and fair active learning (Anahideh et al., 2022) by removing the requirement for sensitive attributes on newly acquired training data. The scoring mechanism is directly derived from first-order influence function approximations (Koh & Liang, ICML 2017), but the key departure is that fairness influence is computed via a small validation set with sensitive attributes, while the unlabeled pool being sampled from requires no demographic information. The theoretical analysis connects to distribution-shift generalization bounds and risk disparity, positioning the work at the intersection of fairness-accuracy Pareto frontier literature and active data acquisition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical privacy constraint that limits the applicability of existing fair active learning: collecting sensitive attributes for new training data raises legal and safety concerns. The theoretical insight that controlling distribution shift (via accuracy influence) while reducing group gap (via fairness influence) enables simultaneous improvement in both fairness and accuracy provides a principled justification for the sampling strategy. Key limitations include dependence on a clean, informative validation set with sensitive attributes, and potential inefficiency under noisy validation labels. Open questions include robustness to validation set noise, scalability to multi-group or intersectional fairness settings, and whether the first-order influence approximation remains accurate for very large model architectures.
