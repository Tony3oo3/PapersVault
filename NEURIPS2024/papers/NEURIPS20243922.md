## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are evaluated on single passes through a sequence of domains, leaving it unclear whether their adaptability persists over prolonged deployment. This paper introduces the recurring TTA setting—where testing environments not only shift but also recur over time—and demonstrates that state-of-the-art TTA algorithms (e.g., RoTTA, CoTTA, TRIBE) suffer severe, irreversible performance degradation (model collapse) when repeatedly exposed to the same conditions, eventually exceeding the error of the unadapted source model.

## Input / Output

PeTTA consumes unlabeled test image batches (64 images per step) drawn from temporally correlated category distributions (Dirichlet-sampled), a pre-trained source model (e.g., ResNet on CIFAR-10/100 or ImageNet), and pre-computed per-class feature statistics (empirical mean vectors and diagonal covariance matrices) from a small unlabeled source-distribution sample. It outputs per-sample class label predictions and updated model parameters at each step. The evaluation protocol revisits D corruption domains K=20 times (recurring TTA), and a longer CCC protocol runs 80,000 adaptation steps over 5.1M images with algorithmically generated, non-cyclic corruptions.

## Method

PeTTA augments the standard mean-teacher EMA update with a divergence-sensing mechanism that monitors the model's drift from the source model in feature embedding space. Specifically, it tracks per-class running means of deep feature vectors and computes a Mahalanobis-based divergence score γ_t^y (Eq. 6) for each predicted class; the batch-averaged score γ̄_t then adaptively scales the regularization coefficient (λ_t = γ̄_t · λ_0) upward and the EMA update rate (α_t = (1−γ̄_t) · α_0) downward, prioritizing collapse prevention when the model has drifted far from θ_0. An anchor loss (KL divergence between source and current model predictions in probability space) further constrains adaptation in the output space. The theoretical grounding comes from an ε-perturbed Gaussian Mixture Model Classifier analysis (Theorem 1, Corollary 1) that identifies data-dependent (prior distribution, inter-class distance) and algorithm-dependent (update rate, false-negative rate) factors driving collapse, explaining why fixed hyperparameters fail and motivating the adaptive strategy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10→CIFAR-10-C (recurring TTA, 20 visits), PeTTA achieves a stable average error of 22.8% (range 22.3–23.0% across visits), compared to RoTTA's 51.3% average (degrading from 24.6% to 70.3%) and the source model's 43.5%. On ImageNet→ImageNet-C, PeTTA reaches 60.5% average error versus RoTTA's 87.9% (degrading from 68.3% to 99.6%) and the source model's 82.0%. Under the longer CCC setting (80,000 adaptation steps, >5.1M images), PeTTA achieves 0.64 average error, outperforming RoTTA (0.95) and RDumb (0.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeTTA (22.8% avg) substantially outperforms RoTTA (51.3%), the state-of-the-art practical TTA method, which degrades from 24.6% to 70.3% over 20 visits. PeTTA also beats CoTTA (88.3%), EATA (88.8%), MECTA (86.9%), RMT (75.8%), and ROID (72.7%), all of which either fail to adapt or suffer progressive degradation. The only method that beats PeTTA on CIFAR-10-C is TRIBE (17.5% avg), but TRIBE collapses to ~99.9% error by visit 20 on ImageNet-C (84.4% avg) and shows clear degradation over 40 visits on CIFAR-10-C (Fig. 4), whereas PeTTA remains stable. On ImageNet-C, PeTTA (60.5%) is the best-performing method that does not collapse, outperforming ROID (62.4%), RDumb (72.8%), and all other baselines. Under the CCC setting (80K steps), PeTTA (0.64) beats both RoTTA (0.95, which saturates at 1.00) and RDumb (0.75), confirming its advantage over both adaptive and reset-based strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the practical TTA setting (RoTTA, Yuan et al. 2023) by introducing a 'recurring' dimension where testing environments not only shift sequentially but also recur over time, exposing error accumulation invisible in single-pass evaluation. The mean-teacher update framework (Tarvainen & Valpola 2017) and category-balanced memory bank (NOTE, Gong et al. 2022) form the operational backbone, while the theoretical analysis draws on Gaussian Mixture Model classifiers to isolate the mechanism of collapse. PeTTA departs from prior robust TTA methods (CoTTA, EATA, RoTTA) by replacing fixed hyperparameters (λ, α) with adaptive values driven by a Mahalanobis-based divergence sensor, and adds an anchor loss in probability space to complement parameter-space regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that state-of-the-art TTA methods (notably RoTTA, designed specifically for practical TTA) suffer progressive and irreversible performance degradation when environments recur, a failure mode undetectable in standard single-pass benchmarks. The ε-GMMC theoretical analysis provides a clean mechanistic account of collapse (data-dependent factors like class separation and prior, algorithm-dependent factors like update rate and FNR), guiding principled design of future methods. Key limitations include the need for source-distribution feature statistics, reliance on a memory bank for temporally correlated streams, and the acknowledgment that regularization cannot rigorously guarantee zero error accumulation. Open questions include whether collapse-free TTA can be achieved by construction and how to scale PeTTA without access to source data.
