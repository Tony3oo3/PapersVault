## Problem

GANs are widely regarded as difficult to train, with popular backbones like StyleGAN relying on numerous poorly-understood empirical tricks to patch instability and mode collapse. Meanwhile, GAN architectures remain stuck at DCGAN-era designs while diffusion models have adopted modern CNN and transformer components. This paper argues that a principled regularized loss can eliminate the need for ad-hoc tricks and unlock modern backbone architectures for GANs.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 64-dimensional noise vector z (concatenated with a class embedding for conditional tasks) and produces a synthetic RGB image in a single forward pass (1 NFE). Resolutions range from 32×32 (CIFAR-10, StackedMNIST, ImageNet-32) to 256×256 (FFHQ). Unlike StyleGAN, there is no style mapping network or style injection; the 4×4 basis layer is modulated directly by z through a linear layer, and the discriminator uses a projection head for class-conditional evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces R3GAN, built on two pillars. First, a regularized relativistic pairing GAN loss (RpGAN + R1 + R2) that provably admits local convergence (all Jacobian eigenvalues have negative real part at equilibrium), addressing both mode dropping (via relativistic coupling) and non-convergence (via zero-centered gradient penalties on both real and fake data). Second, a modernized fully-symmetric ResNet backbone for both G and D that strips StyleGAN2 of all tricks (minibatch stddev, equalized learning rate, mapping network, style injection, etc.) and replaces its VGG-like blocks with 1-3-1 bottleneck residual blocks using grouped convolution (group size 16), an inverted bottleneck, fixup initialization, no normalization layers, leaky ReLU activations, and bilinear resampling. The well-behaved loss is what makes this simplification possible: without it, removing the tricks causes training to diverge, but with RpGAN+R1+R2 the modern architecture trains stably and achieves FID 2.75 on FFHQ-256, surpassing StyleGAN2's 3.78 at comparable parameter count.

## Results

On FFHQ-256, R3GAN (Config E) achieves an FID of 2.75 with a single NFE, surpassing StyleGAN2 (3.78), StyleGAN3-R (3.92), LDM (4.98), and ADM (8.41). On CIFAR-10, it reaches FID 1.96 (1 NFE), outperforming VP score-based models (2.48), DDPM (3.21), and StyleGAN2+ADA (2.42). On ImageNet-64, R3GAN achieves FID 2.09 with one NFE, beating EDM (2.23, 79 NFEs), ADM (2.91, 250 NFEs), and DMD (2.62, 1 NFE). On StackedMNIST, it recovers all 1000 modes with the lowest KL divergence (0.029) among all tested GANs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3GAN outperforms all non-leaky GAN baselines across every dataset: on FFHQ-256 it beats StyleGAN2 by 1.03 FID (2.75 vs 3.78) and StyleGAN3-R by 1.17 FID; on FFHQ-64 it beats EDM by 0.44 FID (1.95 vs 2.39) while using fewer than half the parameters and 1 NFE vs 79; on CIFAR-10 it beats VP (2.48), DDPM (3.21), and StyleGAN2+ADA (2.42) by 0.5–1.3 FID; on ImageNet-64 it beats EDM (2.23), ADM (2.91), and DMD (2.62) by 0.14–0.82 FID. The only methods that beat R3GAN on FID are those with ImageNet feature leakage (StyleGAN-XL, StyleSAN-XL, PolyINR), which the authors note do not improve perceptual quality. On StackedMNIST, R3GAN achieves the best KL divergence (0.029) among all GANs, slightly edging out MEG (0.031) and DDGAN (0.071), while matching their full 1000-mode coverage. In recall, R3GAN is comparable to or marginally worse than diffusion models but consistently superior to other GANs (e.g., 0.57 vs StyleGAN-XL's 0.47 on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three GAN research threads: the relativistic pairing GAN (RpGAN) loss for addressing mode dropping [Jolicoeur-Martineau 2018], the Jacobian-based local convergence analysis and zero-centered gradient penalties (R1/R2) from [Mescheder et al. 2018], and the StyleGAN2 architecture [Karras et al. 2020]. The key conceptual departure is arguing that StyleGAN2's many empirical tricks (minibatch stddev, equalized learning rate, mapping network, style injection, etc.) were compensating for an unstable training objective, and that once the loss is made well-behaved via RpGAN+R1+R2 with a proven local convergence guarantee, those tricks become unnecessary and the backbone can be replaced with modern ResNet/ConvNeXt-style designs (grouped convolution, inverted bottleneck, fixup initialization, no normalization). The paper thus reframes the StyleGAN lineage: rather than adding more tricks to an old DCGAN-era backbone, it strips the architecture to its essentials and modernizes it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mathematically grounded, minimalist GAN baseline that outperforms StyleGAN2 across FFHQ, CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32/64, and StackedMNIST, directly challenging the widespread narrative that GANs are difficult to train and cannot compete with diffusion models. The convergence proof for regularized RpGAN fills a genuine theoretical gap, and the clear ablation roadmap (Config A→E) gives practitioners a principled starting point for future GAN research. Open questions include whether the architecture scales to higher-resolution ImageNet or text-to-image generation, whether adding attention blocks or adaptive normalization (as in diffusion models) would further improve performance, and whether the no-normalization design can be reconciled with mixed-precision training using FP16.
