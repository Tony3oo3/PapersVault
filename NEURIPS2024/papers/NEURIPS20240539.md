## Problem

Naively incorporating momentum into asynchronous SGD (ASGD) can impede convergence or even cause divergence, yet existing fixes (shifted momentum, SMEGA²) lack theoretical convergence guarantees. No prior work establishes convergence analysis for ASGD with momentum that is independent of the maximum delay, which is critical because ASGD performs well in practice even when maximum delay is extremely large.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240539/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within a Parameter Server framework where K workers each sample a single training instance, compute a stochastic gradient, and send it (along with its iteration index) to a central server. The server maintains the model parameter and a momentum vector, updating them upon each gradient arrival. The iteration index is the key additional communication payload (a single scalar) that enables the server to determine which gradient group an arriving gradient belongs to, allowing correct ordering of out-of-order gradients.

## Method

OrMo organizes gradients into groups of size K based on their iteration indexes and maintains momentum as a weighted sum of these groups with geometrically decaying weights (β⁰, β¹, β², …), mirroring the structure of SSGD with momentum. When a gradient arrives out of order in ASGD, the server computes its gradient group index ⌈ite(k_t,t)/K⌉ and adds the scaled gradient to the momentum with weight β^(I_{t+1}−⌈ite(k_t,t)/K⌉), where I_{t+1} is the index of the latest gradient group. The parameter update compensates for the missed momentum steps that would have occurred in the synchronous case by applying a coefficient −(1−β^(I_{t+1}−⌈ite(k_t,t)/K⌉+1))/(1−β) to the arriving gradient. A delay-adaptive variant (OrMo-DA) further scales the learning rate by 1/(4Lτ_t) for gradients with delay τ_t > 2K, yielding a convergence rate of O(√(Lσ²/T) + KL/T) without requiring bounded gradients.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet20, OrMo-DA achieves the best test accuracy across all settings: 91.17% (16 workers, homogeneous), 88.03% (64 workers, homogeneous), 91.28% (16 workers, heterogeneous), and 88.08% (64 workers, heterogeneous). On CIFAR-100, OrMo-DA reaches 67.72% (16 hom.), 65.79% (64 hom.), 67.82% (16 het.), and 65.82% (64 het.) test accuracy. In the heterogeneous setting with 64 workers on CIFAR-10, naive ASGDm collapses to 68.75% accuracy (±29.51 std), while OrMo-DA maintains 88.08%, demonstrating robustness to large delays. OrMo is also 8× faster than SSGDm in wall-clock time under the heterogeneous setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrMo-DA outperforms all four baselines on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across all four worker/heterogeneity settings. Compared to ASGD (no momentum), OrMo-DA gains approximately 1.4–4.1 percentage points in test accuracy, with the largest gains in the 64-worker homogeneous setting (88.03% vs. 83.14% on CIFAR-10). Against naive ASGDm, the advantage is most dramatic in the heterogeneous setting: with 64 workers on CIFAR-10, naive ASGDm drops to 68.75% (±29.51) while OrMo-DA achieves 88.08%, a gap of nearly 20 points. OrMo-DA also surpasses shifted momentum by 1.2–4.1 points and SMEGA² by 0.3–1.2 points on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100, OrMo-DA leads SMEGA² by 1.6–1.7 points in the 64-worker settings, confirming consistent gains across datasets and scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DSGD framework from Koloskova et al. (2022), which unifies synchronous and asynchronous SGD under a single parameter-server formulation, and on Polyak's momentum as the core acceleration mechanism. It departs from prior attempts to incorporate momentum into ASGD—naive ASGDm (Mitliagkas et al., 2016), shifted momentum (Giladi et al., 2020), and SMEGA2 (Cohen et al., 2022)—by introducing an 'ordered momentum' that organizes gradients into groups by iteration index and assigns them decaying weights, mirroring the structure that naturally emerges in the synchronous case. The key conceptual departure is that OrMo's parameter update rule explicitly compensates for the steps a gradient would have taken in the synchronous setting, a design motivated by the reformulation of SSGDm into per-worker updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first convergence guarantee for ASGD with momentum that does not depend on the maximum delay is a meaningful theoretical advance, since in heterogeneous clusters the maximum delay can be orders of magnitude larger than the number of workers (e.g., ~30,000 vs. ~300 in the paper's experiments), making prior delay-dependent bounds vacuous. Practically, OrMo and OrMo-DA consistently outperform all asynchronous momentum baselines across homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, and the ablation confirms the specific parameter-update rule is essential rather than a cosmetic detail. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-i.i.d. data distributions, exploring whether the ordered-momentum structure generalizes to other adaptive optimizers (Adam, LAMB), and understanding whether the gradient-grouping overhead becomes prohibitive at very large worker counts.
