## Problem

Existing IRL methods require at least one complete expert trajectory before learning can begin, making them inapplicable to time-critical scenarios where the trajectory is still ongoing (e.g., inferring an active shooter's intent from partial movement, or tracking a customer's evolving investment preference). This paper addresses the gap by formulating in-trajectory IRL: learning and updating a reward function and policy incrementally as each new state-action pair of an ongoing trajectory is observed, before the trajectory terminates.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives a single new state-action pair (s_t^E, a_t^E) from the ongoing expert trajectory and must complete its update before the next pair arrives. It also requires a pre-trained meta-prior θ̄ (obtained offline via meta-regularization from a set of relevant tasks sharing the same MDP structure but with different reward functions) and access to the MDP transition dynamics P. The output is a continuously updated reward parameter θ_t and a corresponding entropy-regularized policy π_t, both refined at every step as more of the trajectory is revealed.

## Method

MERIT-IRL formulates in-trajectory IRL as an online non-convex bi-level optimization: the upper level updates the reward parameter θ via one-step gradient descent on a meta-regularized negative log-likelihood, while the lower level computes the corresponding policy via one-step soft policy iteration. The key novelty is a special reward update mechanism: rather than naively comparing trajectory prefixes (which is myopic and treats the current state as terminal), the algorithm completes the expert trajectory by rolling out the learned policy from the current state, then compares this combined complete trajectory (expert prefix + policy-filled suffix) against a full policy-generated trajectory from the initial state, giving the reward update the ability to account for future state-action pairs. A meta-regularization term (λ/2‖θ−θ̄‖²) prevents overfitting given only one incomplete trajectory. Theoretically, the paper handles the non-i.i.d. temporal correlation of the input data by decomposing the analysis into a stationary-distribution regret bound (where data is i.i.d.) plus a vanishing gap term between the correlated and stationary distributions, yielding sub-linear local regret O(√T + log T + √T log T) and O(log T) for linear rewards.

## Results

On MuJoCo target velocity tasks (HalfCheetah, Walker, Hopper), MERIT-IRL achieves cumulative rewards of −214.63, −654.77, and −476.72 respectively after observing the complete expert trajectory, closely matching the Hindsight baseline (which has access to the full trajectory) and the expert. In the stock market experiment, MERIT-IRL achieves a cumulative reward of 386.70, nearly matching Hindsight (390.30) and the expert (403.15). In the in-trajectory setting, MERIT-IRL reaches expert-level performance on HalfCheetah after observing only 40% of the trajectory (t=400) and on the stock market after ~56% (t=140), while all in-trajectory baselines fail to match the expert even at full trajectory length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERIT-IRL is compared against four baselines: IT-IRL (no meta-regularization), Naive MERIT-IRL (meta-regularization but myopic prefix-only reward update), Naive IT-IRL (both naive update and no regularization), and Hindsight (meta-regularized ML-IRL [5] with access to the complete expert trajectory). MERIT-IRL substantially outperforms all three in-trajectory baselines across every environment: on HalfCheetah it improves over IT-IRL by ~172 reward points and over Naive IT-IRL by ~550 points; on the stock market it achieves 386.70 vs. 256.81 (IT-IRL), 192.49 (Naive MERIT-IRL), and 72.33 (Naive IT-IRL). Against Hindsight, MERIT-IRL achieves nearly identical performance (e.g., −214.63 vs. −208.74 on HalfCheetah; 386.70 vs. 390.30 on stock market) despite only observing the trajectory incrementally, confirming that the meta-regularization and the trajectory-completing reward update together recover the quality of offline IRL. The ablation-style comparison between IT-IRL and MERIT-IRL isolates the contribution of meta-regularization, while the comparison between Naive MERIT-IRL and MERIT-IRL isolates the contribution of the future-aware reward update design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bi-level maximum-likelihood IRL formulation of Zeng et al. (ML-IRL, NeurIPS 2022) from an offline setting to an online in-trajectory setting, where state-action pairs arrive sequentially from a single ongoing trajectory. It incorporates meta-regularization from Rajeswaran et al. (NeurIPS 2019) to combat overfitting in the data-scarce single-trajectory regime, and generalizes the local regret analysis of Liu & Zhu (NeurIPS 2024) from i.i.d. input data to temporally correlated data via a stationary-distribution decomposition. The key conceptual departure from prior online IRL is that learning occurs *within* a single trajectory (at each new state-action pair) rather than *between* complete trajectories, and the reward update mechanism explicitly completes the expert trajectory with the learned policy to avoid myopic prefix-only comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-trajectory IRL formulation addresses a genuine practical gap: real-time applications like active-shooter intent inference or live investor-preference tracking cannot wait for a trajectory to terminate before updating. The theoretical contribution of bounding local regret under non-i.i.d. temporally correlated data via a two-step stationary-distribution technique is a reusable analytical tool for online non-convex optimization in sequential decision-making. Open questions include scalability to multi-agent in-trajectory settings, handling of adversarial or non-stationary experts, and whether the two-rollout gradient approximation remains tractable in high-dimensional continuous control. The computational cost of two full policy rollouts per time step may limit applicability to very fast-acting environments.
