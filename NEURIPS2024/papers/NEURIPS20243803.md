## Problem

Learning part segmentation and articulation parameters for articulated objects without ground-truth shape, part labels, or pose annotations remains challenging. Prior unsupervised methods like PARIS are restricted to two-part objects with a single movable part, jointly optimize all parameters from scratch (making them unstable and data-hungry), and cannot generalize to objects with arbitrary numbers of moving parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243803/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of multi-view RGB images of the same object in different articulation states along with their foreground masks; the source set (100 views) trains the static NeRF while the target set can be as few as 8 views for distilling articulation. The only a priori requirement is the number of rigid parts k. Outputs include per-point part segmentation probabilities, SE(3) pose-change matrices M_ℓ for each part, and photometrically rendered views at the target articulation pose.

## Method

The core insight is that articulation changes only part poses, not geometry or texture, so a single static NeRF (Mip-NeRF 360) trained on source views is frozen and reused; a lightweight 2-layer MLP segmentation head appended to the NeRF's latent features predicts part membership, and part-aware rendering composites contributions via virtual rays r_ℓ = M_ℓ⁻¹r. Training uses a decoupled alternating procedure: Step 1 optimizes M_ℓ by minimizing 2D Chamfer distance between projected voxel coordinates and target foreground pixels, and Step 2 optimizes the segmentation head via photometric loss on rendered target views. A 128³ voxel grid initialized from density differences between rendered source and target foreground masks provides geometric supervision for the moving parts, with iterative refinement at 256³ resolution. This single-NeRF, frozen-weight design keeps model size nearly constant regardless of part count, enabling stable, data-efficient learning from few target views.

## Results

On the PartNet-Mobility dataset, the method achieves substantially lower pose estimation errors than PARIS across nearly all objects and metrics. For example, the joint direction error e_d drops from 2.42° (PARIS) to 0.33° (Ours) on the stapler, and from 48.58° to 1.54° on the blade, where PARIS fails to converge in all 5 runs. For novel articulation synthesis, PSNR ranges from 29.27 (laptop) to 36.47 (blade), outperforming PARIS (24.36–32.74) on 5 of 6 objects, while part segmentation mIoU reaches 0.94–0.99 versus PARIS's 0.16–0.99. The method also generalizes to multi-part objects (oven, glasses, box, storage) with e_d of 0.56–2.35° and PSNR of 28.25–32.98.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is PARIS [17], which learns separate NeRFs for dynamic and static parts and jointly optimizes articulation end-to-end. The proposed method wins decisively on joint direction error (e_d) for 5 of 6 single-part objects, with the most dramatic gains on the stapler (0.33° vs. 2.42°) and blade (1.54° vs. 48.58°), where PARIS fails to converge in all 5 runs. On joint position error (e_p), the method is competitive or better on 3 of 4 revolute objects, though PARIS has a slight edge on laptop and oven (differences within 10⁻³ range). For rotation angle error (e_g), the method outperforms PARIS on all 4 revolute objects, most notably on the stapler (0.29° vs. 44.62°). For prismatic translation error (e_t), the method achieves 0.01–0.02 (×10⁻¹) versus PARIS's 0.30–1.13, a 15–100× improvement. On rendering quality (PSNR) and segmentation (mIoU), the method outperforms PARIS on 5 of 6 objects, with the only exception being a marginal PSNR difference on the laptop (29.27 vs. 30.31). The paper also notes that PARIS's originally reported numbers are significantly better than their reproduced results, suggesting sensitivity to initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NeRF and Mip-NeRF 360 for the underlying implicit representation, extending the volume rendering equation with a part-aware formulation that composites per-part contributions after applying SE(3) transformations. It departs most directly from PARIS (Liu et al., ICCV 2023), the closest prior unsupervised method, by replacing PARIS's per-part NeRFs with a single frozen NeRF plus a lightweight segmentation head, and by introducing a voxel-grid initialization and decoupled optimization strategy to handle the joint segmentation-and-articulation problem. Unlike PARIS (limited to two-part objects with one movable part) and DITTO (point-cloud input, shape only), this method handles arbitrary numbers of moving parts from purely 2D multi-view image pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unsupervised method to model multi-part articulated objects from multi-view images, and its data efficiency (8 target views outperforming PARIS trained on 100) makes it practical for real-world settings where capturing many views of a second articulation state is costly. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with thin parts, highly symmetric objects, and minimal articulation motion, and is restricted to rigid parts. Open questions include whether incorporating appearance cues into pose estimation (currently geometry-only) would resolve symmetry ambiguities, and whether the approach extends to soft or non-rigid deformations.
