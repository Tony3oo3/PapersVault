## Problem

Top-k recommendation under full-bandit feedback (a single scalar reward for the entire recommended set) is challenging because the arm space grows as Θ(n^k), and prior bandit algorithms rely on restrictive feedback models—such as semi-bandit per-item rewards or cascade browsing—that fail to capture non-linear interactions among items in modern recommendation interfaces. No existing method handles full-bandit feedback without assumptions on reward structure while remaining computationally tractable over long horizons.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a c-dimensional context vector c_t (a user embedding from collaborative filtering) and must output an ordered top-k ranking π_t of k distinct items from a pool of n. The only feedback is a single noisy scalar y_t = f̂(c_t, π_t) + ε_t for the entire set, with no per-item decomposition. The arm space comprises all |Π^k| = Θ(n^k) ordered k-tuples, and the algorithm must select arms via local search when this space is too large for exhaustive optimization.

## Method

GP-TopK models the reward function with a Gaussian process using a product kernel: a dot-product kernel over contexts multiplied by a novel Weighted Convolutional Kendall (WCK) kernel over top-k rankings. The WCK kernel combines the rank-weighting flexibility of the weighted Kendall kernel (e.g., DCG-inspired position discounts) with the convolutional Kendall kernel's ability to account for items outside the intersection of two rankings, addressing pathologies where existing kernels rank non-overlapping rankings as more similar than reversed ones. The key computational insight is deriving sparse linear feature representations for the WCK kernel with only O(k²) non-zero entries, enabling O(k²t) matrix-vector multiplication with the kernel matrix and reducing total algorithmic cost from O(T⁴) to O(T²) over T rounds. The UCB acquisition function is optimized via local search, and the paper proves sub-linear regret bounds that scale as Õ(n) rather than Õ(n^{k/2}) in the number of items, a substantial improvement even for small k.

## Results

Across all experimental settings (non-contextual and contextual, nDCG and nDCG+diversity rewards, small and large arm spaces), the GP-TopK algorithm with the WCK kernel achieves the lowest cumulative regret, outperforming Random, ε-greedy, and MAB-UCB baselines. In the small arm space (n=20, k=3, 6,840 arms), WCK and CK significantly outperform WK in converged regret values, with WCK further improving on CK. In the large arm space (n=50, k=5, >1.1×10^10 arms), WCK significantly outperforms CK, demonstrating the advantage of the weighted convolutional kernel at scale. Theoretically, GP-TopK achieves a regret bound of Õ(n√(C₁Tc(...))) under Assumption 1, improving over the naive Õ(n^{k/2}√(C₁Tc(...))) bound from Srinivas et al. (2010) by a factor of n^{k/2−1}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GP-TopK (with WK, CK, and WCK kernel variants) against three baselines: Random, ε-greedy, and MAB-UCB (GP-UCB with a delta kernel treating each ranking as an independent arm, equivalent to Srinivas et al. 2010). GP-TopK variants consistently outperform all three baselines across every setting—non-contextual and contextual, both reward functions, and both small (6,840 arms) and large (>1.1×10^5 and >1.1×10^10 arms) arm spaces. Among the GP-TopK variants, WCK achieves the lowest regret in all scenarios, followed by CK, then WK, confirming that the weighted convolutional kernel's ability to handle both rank-weighted similarity and items outside the top-k intersection is critical. The advantage of WCK over CK is most pronounced in the large arm space with k=5, where the combinatorial complexity makes the kernel's expressiveness most impactful. The theoretical comparison in Table 3 shows GP-TopK improves the regret bound's dependence on n from n^{k/2} to n (Assumption 1) and from n^{k/2} to n (Assumption 2), a factor of n^{k/2−1} improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP-UCB bandit framework of Srinivas et al. (2010) to the combinatorial top-k recommendation setting, where prior work (cascading bandits, semi-bandit feedback) imposed restrictive feedback models that are often unavailable in practice. It builds directly on the Kendall kernel family introduced by Jiao et al. (2015, 2018), proposing a novel weighted convolutional Kendall (WCK) kernel that combines the rank-weighting flexibility of the weighted Kendall kernel with the out-of-intersection handling of the convolutional Kendall kernel. The key methodological departure is the derivation of sparse feature representations for these kernels, enabling fast matrix-vector multiplications that reduce overall computational complexity from O(T⁴) to O(T²), and a regret bound that improves the dependence on n from n^(k/2) to n.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The full-bandit feedback setting (single scalar reward for the entire top-k set) is more realistic for modern recommendation interfaces where users interact with the page holistically rather than in a linear cascade, making this formulation broadly applicable to e-commerce and content platforms. The computational scalability improvements are practically important for long-horizon deployment, and the WCK kernel's ability to fix pathologies in both WK and CK kernels (e.g., ranking non-overlapping lists as more similar than reversed lists) provides a more principled similarity measure. Open questions include whether the regret analysis can account for local search approximations in arm selection, whether the approach extends to other acquisition functions like Thompson sampling, and whether it generalizes to settings with stochastic item availability or delayed feedback.
