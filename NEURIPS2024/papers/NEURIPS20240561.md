## Problem

Existing gradient-free label mapping methods for visual reprogramming (e.g., RLM, FLM, ILM) enforce a deterministic one-to-one correspondence between pretrained and downstream labels, which overlooks the complex many-to-many relationship between the two label spaces. This rigid constraint leads to suboptimal assignments where a downstream label cannot be paired with its most relevant pretrained label because that label was already greedily consumed by another downstream class, and it discards the probabilistic contributions of multiple related pretrained labels for a single downstream prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from downstream classification datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, Flowers102, UCF101) and produces downstream class labels. Before entering the frozen pretrained model (ResNet-18, ResNeXt-101, or CLIP), each image is augmented with trainable noise patterns via either padding (noise added to outer frames) or watermarking (noise overlaid on the image). The output is not a single hard label but a reweighted logit vector: the pretrained model's 1000-dim (or 100-dim) output is multiplied by a probabilistic mapping matrix ω ∈ [0,1]^{k_S × k_T} to yield k_T downstream logits, from which the argmax gives the predicted class.

## Method

BLM replaces the binary one-to-one mapping matrix with a probabilistic many-to-many matrix ω where each element ω_{y^S, y^T} quantifies the conditional probability p(Y^T = y^T | Y^S = y^S, X^T), estimated via Bayes' theorem from the empirical joint distribution of predicted pretrained labels and ground-truth downstream labels over the training set. BLM+ extends this by aggregating top-K softmax probabilities (rather than a single argmax count) when estimating the joint distribution, accounting for prediction uncertainty. The mapping matrix is updated iteratively at each training epoch alongside the input VR parameters, with Laplace smoothing (λ=1) preventing zero denominators and column-wise normalization ensuring each downstream label receives total weight 1. A theoretical analysis (Corollary E.5) proves that under conditions of label bias or inter-dependence, the expected accuracy of probabilistic LM is at least as high as deterministic one-to-one LM.

## Results

On 12 downstream datasets with padding-based VR, BLM achieves an average accuracy of 45.3% and BLM+ achieves 46.7% with ResNet-18 (ImageNet-1K), outperforming the SOTA gradient-free baseline ILM (40.6%) by 4.7% and 6.1% respectively. With ResNeXt-101 (Instagram), BLM and BLM+ achieve 53.2% and 53.8% average accuracy, improving over ILM (50.0%) by 3.2% and 3.8%. For watermarking-based VR on ResNet-18, BLM and BLM+ achieve 49.3% and 50.7% average accuracy, surpassing ILM (43.2%) by 6.1% and 7.5%. Gains are most pronounced on high-class-count datasets (e.g., UCF101, CIFAR-100, SUN397), while on SVHN (10 classes) ILM retains a slight edge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLM and BLM+ consistently outperform all three gradient-free baselines (RLM, FLM, ILM) across nearly all 12 datasets. Against RLM [Elsayed et al., ICLR 2018], improvements are substantial (e.g., 50.1% vs. 11.0% on Flowers102, 30.6% vs. 12.9% on CIFAR-100). Against FLM [Tsai et al., ICML 2020], BLM+ gains range from 1.5% (EuroSAT) to 27.4% (Flowers102). Against the strongest gradient-free baseline ILM [Chen et al., CVPR 2023], BLM+ achieves an average improvement of 6.1% (ResNet-18) and 7.5% (watermarking), with the largest gains on fine-grained or high-class-count tasks (e.g., +8.1% on Flowers102, +5.8% on CIFAR-100); the sole exception is SVHN where ILM edges out BLM+ by ~1%. Compared to the deep learning-based LM (learnable linear layer) [Kloberdanz et al., ICANN 2021], BLM/BLM+ still trail in absolute accuracy (46.7% vs. 56.3% average on ResNet-18) but narrow the gap from ~16% (ILM vs. Deep) to ~10%, while requiring zero additional trainable parameters and offering interpretability through the probabilistic mapping matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-free label mapping line within visual reprogramming (VR), building directly on ILM (Chen et al., CVPR 2023) and FLM (Tsai et al., ICML 2020), which both rely on deterministic one-to-one mappings between pretrained and downstream labels. The key conceptual departure is replacing these binary mappings with a probabilistic many-to-many matrix whose entries are estimated via Bayesian conditional probability over the joint distribution of predicted pretrained labels and ground-truth downstream labels. The iterative update pipeline is inherited from ILM, while the frequency-counting mechanism for the joint distribution is inherited from FLM; BLM+ further extends this by aggregating top-K softmax probabilities rather than relying on a single argmax prediction. The paper also draws a connection to LEEP as a pretrained-model transferability metric, positioning BLM/BLM+ as a more generalized framework for assessing transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BLM and BLM+ provide a probabilistic and interpretable lens for understanding why VR works: the learned mapping matrix reveals which pretrained labels contribute to each downstream class, exposing similarity and inclusion relationships that one-to-one mappings obscure. The theoretical analysis (Corollary E.5) formalizes conditions under which probabilistic mapping dominates deterministic mapping, lending rigor to the empirical gains. However, a notable limitation is that BLM/BLM+ still trail deep learning-based LM (learnable linear layer) by a substantial margin (e.g., ~10–20% on several datasets), and the advantage over ILM diminishes on small-label-space tasks like SVHN. Open questions include whether the probabilistic mapping can be further refined with richer priors, whether it extends naturally to non-classification VR tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection), and whether the gap to learned output layers can be closed without introducing trainable parameters.
