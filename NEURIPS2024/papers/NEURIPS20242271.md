## Problem

Covariate-adjusted response-adaptive randomization (CARA) designs typically assume primary outcomes are immediately observable, but in many clinical settings (e.g., HIV viral load measured months after treatment) primary outcomes are delayed, causing significant missingness and inefficient ATE estimation. This paper addresses how to simultaneously leverage immediately observed surrogate outcomes and delayed primary outcomes within a multi-stage CARA framework to improve estimation efficiency of the average treatment effect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242271/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a multi-stage sequential design where at each stage t, the experimenter observes covariates X, surrogate outcomes S (always available), primary outcomes Y (available only if delay D_it ≤ T−t), and delay indicators D. Treatment allocation at stage t is a function of all historical data from prior stages (covariates, assignments, delays, surrogates, and observed primary outcomes). The output is a sequence of stage-specific allocation probabilities bounded in [δ, 1−δ], followed by a final ATE point estimate, a consistent variance estimator, and a two-sided confidence interval constructed from the efficient influence function.

## Method

The proposed design follows a four-step procedure: (1) an exploration phase of D*+1 stages with 50/50 randomization to estimate the arm- and covariate-dependent delay distribution ρ_a(d|x) and conditional outcome means τ(a,x,s) and τ(a,x); (2) a policy optimization step that solves a regularized convex program minimizing the semiparametric efficiency bound (SPEB) of the ATE, with a λ_N‖e‖₂² penalty to ensure uniqueness; (3) a calibration phase where subsequent stages adjust allocation to match the optimal policy while accounting for the fixed 50/50 assignments in early stages; and (4) a final ATE estimator built from the efficient influence function, which combines inverse-probability-weighted residual terms (weighted by both treatment propensity and delay probability) with surrogate-adjusted conditional mean differences. Theoretically, the paper derives the SPEB under arm-dependent delay with surrogates (Theorem 3), proves asymptotic normality of the estimator (Theorem 1), and shows the adaptive allocation converges to the oracle optimal design (Theorem 2), establishing semiparametric efficiency.

## Results

In the synthetic HIV case study (calibrated on the Tanzania RCT with true ATE τ = 0.04, T = 8 stages, D* = 3), all three design strategies exhibit vanishing bias as sample size grows, confirming asymptotic unbiasedness. The proposed surrogate-enhanced CARA design achieves the smallest standard deviation for ATE estimation across all sample sizes, demonstrating superior statistical efficiency. The complete randomization design (e_t = 1/2) and the CARA design without surrogates both show larger variance, with the no-surrogate CARA design performing intermediately between the two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three design strategies in a synthetic HIV trial: (1) the proposed surrogate-enhanced CARA design, (2) complete randomization (e_t = 1/2 for all stages), and (3) a CARA design that uses only delayed primary outcomes without surrogate information (based on Ma, Wei & Wang, 2024). The proposed design wins on the primary metric of estimation efficiency (standard deviation of the ATE estimator), achieving consistently smaller variance than both baselines across all sample sizes tested. The no-surrogate CARA design (baseline from [28]) outperforms complete randomization in variance but is still less efficient than the proposed surrogate-enhanced design, confirming that incorporating surrogate outcomes provides a meaningful efficiency gain beyond what delayed primary outcomes alone can offer. All three methods are asymptotically unbiased, so the differentiation is purely in variance/efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CARA design framework of Ma, Wei, and Wang [28], which established semiparametric efficiency bounds for covariate-adjusted response-adaptive randomization with delayed outcomes, by incorporating immediately observed surrogate outcomes into the allocation rule and efficiency analysis. It also departs from prior work on surrogates in randomized experiments (e.g., Anderer et al. [2], Kallus and Mao [25]) by explicitly modeling arm- and covariate-dependent delay mechanisms rather than treating surrogates as simple supplements or replacements. The semiparametric efficiency bound derivation follows the tangent-space methodology of standard causal inference, but the novel combination of delayed primary outcomes, surrogate-augmented nuisance estimation, and multi-stage adaptive allocation is not present in any single prior reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a principled, efficiency-optimal strategy for clinical trials where primary endpoints are delayed (e.g., viral load suppression in HIV, long-term survival in oncology) and surrogate biomarkers are available at interim visits, directly addressing a common practical bottleneck in adaptive trial design. The proof that the proposed estimator attains the semiparametric efficiency bound gives practitioners a clear optimality guarantee, while the calibration step offers practical guidance on stage lengths and enrollment proportions. Open questions include extending to outcome-dependent delays, incorporating fairness and safety constraints, handling multiple treatment arms, and connecting to other causal parameters beyond ATE.
