## Problem

For constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) with general parameterized policies (e.g., neural networks with d ≪ SA parameters), the state-of-the-art sample complexity was Õ(ε⁻⁴), far from the theoretical lower bound of Ω(ε⁻²). This gap persisted because prior analyses could only bound the first-order gradient error as E‖ω_k − ω_k*‖, which decays only as H^{-1/2} in the inner-loop length, forcing a quadratic blowup in the total sample budget.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes model-free samples from an unknown CMDP: at each step it draws a geometrically-horizon trajectory (expected length O((1−γ)⁻¹)) under the current policy π_θ starting from the initial distribution ρ, collecting state-action pairs, rewards, and costs. It outputs a sequence of policy parameters {θ_k}_{k=0}^{K−1} and Lagrange multipliers {λ_k} such that the time-averaged policy achieves an ε optimality gap and ε constraint violation. No knowledge of the transition kernel P is required; all gradient and value estimates are obtained via the unbiased geometric-horizon sampling procedure (Algorithm 1).

## Method

PD-ANPG employs a nested-loop primal-dual structure: the outer loop updates the policy parameter θ via a natural policy gradient step (modulated by the Fisher matrix pseudoinverse) and the Lagrange multiplier λ via projected gradient descent on the constraint, while the inner loop solves the quadratic subproblem for the natural policy gradient ω* using Accelerated Stochastic Gradient Descent (ASGD) with momentum and tail-averaging. The key analytical insight is that the first-order estimation error in the Lagrange convergence bound can be decomposed into the conditional expectation E[ω_k | θ_k, λ_k] − ω_k*, which—by interpreting the bias as the convergence error of a deterministic ASGD program—decays exponentially in the inner-loop length H rather than as H^{-1/2}. This allows H = Õ(log ε⁻¹) to be treated as a constant, reducing the outer-loop requirement to K = O((1−γ)⁻⁶ ε⁻²) and yielding a total sample complexity of Õ((1−γ)⁻⁷ ε⁻²), matching the ε-dependence of the theoretical lower bound.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1) establishes that PD-ANPG achieves an ε optimality gap and ε constraint violation with Õ((1−γ)^{−7}ε^{−2}) sample complexity for general parameterized CMDPs. This improves the previous state-of-the-art Õ((1−γ)^{−8}ε^{−4}) bound of C-NPG-PDA [4] by a factor of O((1−γ)^{−1}ε^{−2}). The result matches the theoretical lower bound Ω((1−γ)^{−5}ε^{−2}) [11] in its ε^{−1} dependence, closing a long-standing gap for general parameterized policies. The improvement is achieved by replacing standard SGD with Accelerated Stochastic Gradient Descent (ASGD) in the inner loop for computing the natural policy gradient estimate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For general parameterization, PD-ANPG strictly dominates all prior methods: it improves over C-NPG-PDA [4] (previous SOTA at Õ((1−γ)^{−8}ε^{−4})) by reducing both the ε dependence from ε^{−4} to ε^{−2} and the (1−γ) dependence from (1−γ)^{−8} to (1−γ)^{−7}. Compared to NPG-PD [5] (O((1−γ)^{−8}ε^{−6})) and CRPO [6] (O((1−γ)^{−13}ε^{−6})), the improvement is even more dramatic, reducing the ε exponent by a factor of 3 and the (1−γ) exponent by 1–6. The softmax-parameterized baselines (PMD-PD [9], PD-NAC [10], NPG-PD [5], CRPO [6]) are not directly comparable since they require O(SA) parameters versus d ≪ SA for general parameterization, though NPG-PD's softmax result of O((1−γ)^{−5}ε^{−2}) is closer to the lower bound. The paper's result matches the theoretical lower bound [11] in ε^{−1} (achieving ε^{−2} vs. the Ω(ε^{−2}) lower bound), though a gap of (1−γ)^{−2} remains in the discount factor dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual natural policy gradient (NPG) framework for CMDPs established by Ding et al. [5] and Bai et al. [4] by replacing the standard stochastic gradient descent in the inner loop with accelerated stochastic gradient descent (ASGD) from Jain et al. [8]. The critical analytical departure is a global-to-local convergence lemma (Lemma 3) that bounds the first-order error via a nested expectation E‖E[ω_k|θ_k,λ_k] − ω_k*‖ rather than the full estimation error E‖ω_k − ω_k*‖ used in prior works. This seemingly minor reformulation allows the bias to be interpreted as the convergence error of a deterministic ASGD program, yielding exponential decay in the inner-loop length H instead of the H^{-0.5} rate that previously capped sample complexity at ε^{-4}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the gap between the theoretical upper and lower bounds in ε^{-1} for general parameterized CMDPs, a problem that had remained open across multiple prior attempts (Ding et al. 2020, Xu et al. 2021, Bai et al. 2023). Since general parameterization uses d ≪ SA parameters, this is the relevant regime for large or infinite state spaces where tabular or softmax methods are infeasible. The acceleration-based NPG technique could potentially transfer to other constrained RL settings (non-linear CMDPs, average-reward CMDPs) as the authors note. Limitations include the reliance on Fisher non-degeneracy (Assumption 4), the Slater condition, and the absence of empirical validation; the analysis is also restricted to discounted CMDPs.
