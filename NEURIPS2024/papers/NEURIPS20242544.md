## Problem

Most online learning theory is built around discrete, round-by-round data streams where the learner observes every instance. This paper asks what the right learning-theoretic framework is when the learner can only observe a continuous or discrete data stream at adaptively chosen 'snapshots' (queries), and how temporal dependencies and patterns affect learnability. The central questions are: which concept classes and pattern classes are learnable under such querying-based models, and what combinatorial dimensions characterize their learnability?

## Input / Output

The learner receives a data stream of point-label pairs (X_t, Y_t) indexed by timestamps t (continuous t ≥ 0 or discrete t ∈ ℕ), but only witnesses (X_t, Y_t) at times it actively queries, subject to a linear budget Q_A(t) = O(t). In the blind-prediction setting, the prediction at time t is made using only the timestamp and the history of previous queries — the current instance X_t is not observed. In the update-and-deploy setting, a separate predictor function f̂(X_t, t) is deployed continuously and updated only at query times. The output is a label prediction at every timestamp plus the decision of whether to query.

## Method

The paper develops two learning frameworks and characterizes learnability in each. In the update-and-deploy setting, a uniform-sampling algorithm A_unif randomizes its next query timestamp from Unif[t, t+Δ] and deploys the Standard Optimal Algorithm (SOA) as its predictor, achieving an expected integral mistake-bound of Δ·LD(H) for any concept class with finite Littlestone dimension. In the blind-prediction setting, the paper proves that any non-trivial concept class (one where some classifier separates two points) is unlearnable, establishing a stark contrast with the update-and-deploy case. For pattern classes, the authors construct a continuous pattern class where random sampling algorithms provably fail (infinite mistake-bound) but an adaptive algorithm that queries precisely at timestamps where future data is encoded achieves zero error, demonstrating that adaptivity is essential. Finally, for discrete data streams in the blind-prediction setting, they introduce the Query Learning Distance (QLD) over a family of query trees and prove it exactly equals the optimal mistake-bound M_Q(P) for deterministic learners.

## Results

The paper establishes that in the update-and-deploy setting, the uniform sampling algorithm A_unif achieves an expected integral mistake-bound of at most Δ·LD(H) with a linear querying strategy (Theorem 3.2), and that LD(H) = ∞ implies non-learnability for any algorithm (Theorem 3.3). In stark contrast, in the blind-prediction setting, even the simplest non-trivial concept class (a single classifier distinguishing two points) is unlearnable — MB_P(H)(A) = ∞ for all algorithms (Theorem 4.1). For discrete pattern classes in the blind-prediction setting, the paper proves an exact characterization: the optimal mistake-bound M_Q(P) equals the query learning distance QLD(P, Q) (Theorem 1.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key theoretical contrast is between the two learning frameworks: the update-and-deploy setting admits learnability characterized by the Littlestone dimension (finite LD(H) ⟹ learnable), while the blind-prediction setting renders all non-trivial concept classes unlearnable regardless of LD(H). A second important contrast is between adaptive and non-adaptive learners for pattern classes: the paper constructs a continuous pattern class P that is unlearnable by any random (non-adaptive) sampling algorithm (MB_P(A) = ∞, Lemma 4.3) yet learnable with zero expected error by the adaptive Algorithm 2 (Lemma 4.5). This learnability gap does not exist for concept classes, where non-adaptive uniform sampling suffices in the update-and-deploy setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Littlestone dimension framework [2] and the Standard Optimal Algorithm from discrete, round-by-round online learning to continuous data streams with selective querying. It builds on Moran et al.'s [1] introduction of pattern classes as a generalization of concept classes, but departs from prior work by introducing two novel learning frameworks—update-and-deploy and blind-prediction—where the learner only receives feedback at adaptively chosen timestamps rather than every round. The paper also draws conceptual parallels to partial monitoring and stream-based active learning, but distinguishes itself by requiring query decisions to be made before observing the current instance, a constraint absent in both of those literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first learnability theory for continuous data streams under selective querying, revealing a sharp dichotomy: concept classes with finite Littlestone dimension are learnable in the update-and-deploy setting but entirely unlearnable in the blind-prediction setting, while pattern classes require adaptive learners in both. The introduction of the Query Learning Distance (QLD) provides a combinatorial characterization for discrete pattern classes, opening a new axis of analysis. Key open questions include extending the discrete pattern class theory to the continuous case, characterizing learnability under broader (non-linear) querying strategies, and developing the update-and-deploy theory for discrete pattern classes.
