## Problem

Instrumental variable regression (IVaR) traditionally relies on two-stage least squares, where the first-stage regression is called 'forbidden regression' due to nuisance parameter estimation errors propagating into the second stage. Recent minimax reformulations avoid explicit nuisance estimation but introduce implicit approximation errors from dual variable optimization over continuous functional spaces. The conditional stochastic optimization formulation of IVaR was previously deemed 'cumbersome to solve' due to nested expectations, and existing conditional stochastic optimization methods require mini-batches, making them unsuitable for the streaming setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a stream of samples at each iteration: Algorithm 1 (TOSG-IVaR) requires a two-sample oracle producing two independent draws X and X' from P(X|Z) plus one Y from P(Y|X), while Algorithm 2 (OTSG-IVaR) uses the standard one-sample oracle with a single (Z, X, Y) triple. No matrix inversions or mini-batches are required at any iteration. The output is the estimated causal parameter θ* (a vector in R^{d_x}), with Algorithm 2 additionally maintaining an estimate γ_t of the Z→X relationship matrix. Algorithm 2 provides an any-time guarantee, meaning the total number of iterations need not be specified in advance.

## Method

The core insight is that the quadratic loss structure in IVaR allows constructing unbiased stochastic gradient estimators without the nested sampling or mini-batches typically required for conditional stochastic optimization. For the two-sample oracle, TOSG-IVaR uses the estimator v(θ) = (g(θ;X) − Y)∇_θ g(θ;X') with two independent conditional draws, yielding an O(log T / T) last-iterate rate for linear models while completely avoiding estimation of the Z→X relationship. For the one-sample oracle, OTSG-IVaR employs coupled two-timescale updates for θ and γ, with the critical design choice of replacing X_t^T θ_t with Z_t^T γ_t θ_t in the θ-update, which guarantees the matrix γ_t^T Σ_Z γ_t is always positive semi-definite and prevents the initial divergence observed in naive coupled updates. This yields an O(1/T^{1−ι}) rate for any ι > 0 under linear Z→X dependence, with per-iteration complexity of O(d_x d_z) arithmetic operations and O(d_x d_z + d_x) memory.

## Results

Theoretically, TOSG-IVaR (Algorithm 1) achieves a last-iterate convergence rate of O(log T / T) under a two-sample oracle (Theorem 1), while OTSG-IVaR (Algorithm 2) achieves O(1/T^{1-ι}) for any ι > 0 under a one-sample oracle (Theorem 2). Empirically, on synthetic IVaR problems with (d_x, d_z) ∈ {(1,1), (8,16)} and varying endogeneity (ρ = 1, 4) and noise (σ_ε = 0.5, 1.0), OTSG-IVaR consistently outperforms O2SLS [DVB24] in both E[‖θ_t − θ*‖²] and test MSE, with O2SLS exhibiting substantially larger variance across all settings. On the real-world Angrist-Evans (1996) dataset, OTSG-IVaR continues to improve its estimate beyond 10,000 iterations while O2SLS and the CSO variant plateau. Per-iteration complexity is O(d_x) for TOSG-IVaR and O(min(d_x², d_z²) + d_z·d_x) for OTSG-IVaR, compared to O(d_x² + d_z² + d_z·d_x) for O2SLS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is O2SLS [DVB24], an online two-stage least-squares method that tracks matrix inverses via auxiliary sequences U_t and V_t. On all synthetic settings (varying dimension, endogeneity level ρ, and noise σ_ε), OTSG-IVaR achieves lower and more stable E[‖θ_t − θ*‖²] than O2SLS, which the authors note has 'much larger variance in different settings.' The CSO variant (Eq. 11, which uses the raw X_t instead of the predicted Z_t^T γ_t θ_t) performs comparably to O2SLS and can diverge before converging when γ_t is far from γ*, as illustrated in Figure 1. On the real-world Angrist-Evans (1996) labor supply dataset, OTSG-IVaR converges faster and continues improving past 10,000 iterations, while both CSO and O2SLS plateau. On the smaller Ryan (2012) Portland Cement dataset (483 samples, 4 instruments), both OTSG-IVaR and CSO outperform O2SLS in convergence speed. Additionally, OTSG-IVaR avoids explicit or implicit matrix inversions required by O2SLS, reducing both per-iteration computational cost and potential numerical instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional stochastic optimization formulation of IVaR introduced by Muandet et al. (2020), who showed the problem can be written as a nested expectation but then resorted to a minimax reformulation via Fenchel conjugates and RKHS. The key departure is demonstrating that the quadratic loss structure makes the inner expectation tractable: two independent draws from P(X|Z) yield an unbiased stochastic gradient, eliminating the need for minimax reformulation, nested sampling (as in Hu et al., 2020), or the 'forbidden regression' of two-stage procedures. The one-sample variant (OTSG-IVaR) is positioned as an SGD analogue of offline 2SLS, connecting to the two-timescale stochastic approximation literature while handling the coupled noise structure that prior analyses did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first fully online streaming IVaR algorithms that avoid both matrix inversions and mini-batches, with clean non-asymptotic convergence rates (O(log T/T) and O(1/T^{1-ι})). This is practically significant for settings where data arrives sequentially and the 'forbidden regression' nuisance estimation is a genuine concern. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear function classes (neural networks), establishing streaming inferential procedures (CLTs, covariance estimation), and handling Markovian data dependencies. The scope remains limited to linear models for the main theoretical results, and experiments are conducted at small dimensions.
