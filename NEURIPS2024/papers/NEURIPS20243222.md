## Problem

Vision language models frequently produce undesirable outputs due to omitted visual details or instruction misunderstandings, yet they lack the ability to spontaneously refine their responses when given user feedback. While a few prior works (e.g., DRESS) explore feedback learning, they rely on a single data source and only 66K samples, leaving the feedback-refining capability underexplored across diverse tasks and at scale. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a large-scale, multi-source dataset and a dedicated benchmark for training and evaluating this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each conversation is structured as {I, q, gt, {r_i, f_i}_{i=1}^n}, where I is an RGB image, q is a text instruction, gt is the ground-truth answer, and n turns alternate between a student response r_i (containing a reasoning thought plus a revised answer) and a teacher feedback f_i (containing a numeric score a_i in [0,10] and textual comments). The student model consumes the image, instruction, and full conversation history to emit the next refined response, while the teacher model consumes the image, instruction, ground truth, and the latest student response to emit the next feedback. Conversations are capped at 6 turns in FIRE-100K and 3 turns in FIRE-1M, and most responses/feedback are under 100 words.

## Method

FIRE is built in two stages to balance quality and scale. In stage 1, GPT-4V is prompted to simulate a student-teacher dialogue on ~100K image-instruction-response triplets from 18 source datasets; conversations lacking score improvement or exceeding 6 turns are filtered, yielding FIRE-100K. In stage 2, two LLaVA-NeXT-8B models are LoRA-fine-tuned on FIRE-100K (one as student, one as teacher), then used to autonomously simulate dialogues on ~1M additional triplets, producing FIRE-1M at near-zero marginal cost. The final FIRE-LLaVA student is trained with cross-entropy loss on responses r_i for i≥2, while the teacher FD-LLaVA is trained on feedback f_i for i≥1; both use LoRA (rank 64, Q/K projections only, ~0.4% of LLaMA3-8B parameters) with a frozen CLIP encoder. Evaluation on FIRE-Bench (11K conversations from 16 datasets, 8 seen + 8 unseen) uses two settings: fixed dialogues (BLEU/CIDEr/MAE against GPT-4V references) and free dialogues (AT, ADR, ATR, RR metrics measuring speed and magnitude of improvement), where FIRE-LLaVA outperforms the untrained LLaVA-NeXT baseline by over 50% on refinement metrics.

## Results

On FIRE-Bench free dialogues, FIRE-LLaVA-8B achieves AT=0.84, ADR=1.56, ATR=0.66, and RR=0.39, outperforming the untrained LLaVA-NeXT-8B baseline (AT=1.00, ADR=0.97, ATR=0.41, RR=0.25) by over 50% on ADR, ATR, and RR. In fixed dialogues, FIRE-LLaVA improves CIDEr from 0.60 to 2.36 and BLEU-1 from 0.33 to 0.54, while the teacher model FD-LLaVA reduces score MAE from 1.88 to 0.30. On standard instruction-following benchmarks (Table 1), FIRE-LLaVA maintains comparable performance to LLaVA-NeXT-8B (e.g., VQAv2: 82.9 vs. 79.0, MMBench: 78.5 vs. 74.4), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is LLaVA-NeXT-8B (the untrained model before FIRE fine-tuning). FIRE-LLaVA-8B wins on all four free-dialogue metrics: ADR improves from 0.97 to 1.56 (+61%), ATR from 0.41 to 0.66 (+61%), RR from 0.25 to 0.39 (+56%), and AT decreases from 1.00 to 0.84 (16% fewer turns needed). In fixed dialogues, FIRE-LLaVA substantially outperforms LLaVA-NeXT-8B on CIDEr (2.36 vs. 0.60) and all BLEU variants, while FD-LLaVA (teacher) reduces score prediction MAE from 1.88 to 0.30. On instruction-following benchmarks, FIRE-LLaVA ties or slightly exceeds LLaVA-NeXT-8B on most tasks (e.g., VQAv2 82.9 vs. 79.0, VizWiz 59.8 vs. 52.0, MMBench 78.5 vs. 74.4) but shows minor drops on TextVQA (68.4 vs. 69.8) and MME Perception (1534.8 vs. 1546.0), confirming that feedback-refining training does not degrade general capability. The intermediate model FIRE100K-LLaVA (trained only on GPT-4V-generated data) already shows meaningful gains, and adding the 1M self-generated dialogues yields further consistent improvements across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedback-refining line of work initiated by DRESS (66K feedback data from a single source) and Self-refine (iterative self-feedback for LLMs) to a much broader and larger scale for VLMs. The two-stage data generation strategy—seeding with GPT-4V-generated dialogues (FIRE-100K) and then expanding via student-teacher model simulation (FIRE-1M)—builds on the vision-language data synthesis paradigm (ShareGPT4V, MIMIC-IT, SVIT) but repurposes it specifically for multi-turn feedback conversations. The closest prior work, DRESS, used only LLaVA-Instruct-150K as a source and produced 66K samples; FIRE departs by drawing from 27 diverse datasets spanning math, OCR, charts, and documents, and by introducing a dedicated benchmark (FIRE-Bench) with both fixed and free dialogue evaluation settings that prior work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIRE provides the first large-scale (1.1M) multi-turn feedback-refinement dataset for VLMs, making it a practical resource for training models that can interactively improve responses based on user corrections. The FIRE-Bench benchmark with its four free-dialogue metrics (AT, ADR, ATR, RR) offers a standardized way to measure how quickly and how much a VLM can refine its answers, filling a gap in the evaluation landscape. Key limitations include the restriction to text-only feedback (no pointing to image regions or other multimodal corrections) and reliance on automated filtering rather than full human verification. Open questions include whether the feedback-refining capability transfers to truly novel task types beyond the 27 source domains, and whether the student-teacher expansion approach degrades in quality at even larger scales.
