## Problem

The non-smooth convex decentralized optimization problem over time-varying networks is the last remaining setting in the four-way taxonomy (smooth/non-smooth × fixed/time-varying) without established complexity lower bounds or matching optimal algorithms. While smooth and fixed-network variants have been fully characterized with tight bounds and optimal methods, no prior work provided either fundamental lower bounds on communication and subgradient computation complexities or an algorithm achieving them in this most challenging regime.

## Input / Output

Each of n nodes stores a convex, M-Lipschitz function f_i(x) and communicates only with neighbors in a time-varying graph whose gossip matrix W(τ) changes at each communication round, with connectivity characterized by a condition number χ ≥ 1. The algorithm produces an ε-approximate solution as the uniform average of all nodes' final iterates, requiring K = O(χM/√(rε)) communication rounds and K×T = O(M²/(rε)) local subgradient evaluations in the strongly convex case (r > 0), or K = O(χMR/ε) and K×T = O(M²R²/ε²) in the convex case (r = 0).

## Method

The method reformulates the original minimization as a saddle-point problem over (x, y, z) with z constrained to the orthogonal complement of the consensus space, then casts it as a monotone inclusion 0 ∈ A(u) + B(u) solved by an accelerated Forward-Backward iteration. Three key modifications distinguish it from the standard Forward-Backward scheme: Nesterov acceleration on the smooth gradient operator A, an Error-Feedback mechanism to approximate the exact averaging required by the projection onto the consensus space over time-varying links, and an inner Chambolle-Pock/subgradient solver to approximately compute the resolvent of the non-smooth subdifferential operator B. The resulting algorithm achieves communication and subgradient complexities that exactly match the newly established lower bounds, resolving a long-standing open question about whether the √χ-to-χ communication complexity gap is fundamental for time-varying networks.

## Results

The paper establishes the first lower bounds on communication and subgradient computation complexities for non-smooth convex decentralized optimization over time-varying networks, and provides Algorithm 1 that matches these bounds exactly. In the strongly convex case, the optimal complexity is τ_com · χM/√(rε) + τ_sub · M²/(rε); in the convex case, it is τ_com · χMR/ε + τ_sub · M²R²/ε². These bounds resolve a long-standing conjecture that the communication complexity in time-varying networks must scale with χ (rather than √χ as in fixed networks), now confirmed for the non-smooth setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Algorithm 1 strictly dominates all three existing baselines in the time-varying non-smooth setting. D-SubGD (Nedic & Ozdaglar, 2009) provides no finite-precision guarantee, converging only to a neighborhood of the solution, making it inapplicable for ε-approximation. SubGD-Push (Nedić & Olshevsky, 2014) achieves only O(log²(1/ε)/ε²) convergence with an n^{2n} polynomial factor, which is far worse than even the centralized subgradient method's O(1/ε) rate. ZO-SADOM (Lobanov et al., 2023) is the closest competitor but carries extra multiplicative factors of d^{1/4}log(1/ε) in communication complexity and d·log(1/ε) in subgradient computation complexity, making it suboptimal and potentially poor for high-dimensional problems. Algorithm 1 eliminates all such dimension-dependent overheads, achieving exactly the lower-bound complexity in both the strongly convex and convex cases, confirming optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the 2×2 landscape of decentralized convex optimization (smooth/non-smooth × fixed/time-varying networks) by tackling the previously unaddressed non-smooth + time-varying corner. It builds directly on the saddle-point reformulation and Nesterov-accelerated Forward-Backward framework of Kovalev et al. (2021a) for smooth time-varying networks, and on the lower-bound constructions of Scaman et al. (2018) for non-smooth fixed networks. The key departure is that the non-smoothness of F(x) forces the subdifferential into the monotone operator B(u) rather than A(u), requiring an inner subgradient/Chambolle-Pock solver at each outer iteration, and the time-varying network necessitates the Error-Feedback mechanism from Kovalev et al. (2021b) to handle the projection onto the consensus space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing open question by proving that the communication complexity in the non-smooth time-varying setting scales with χ (not √χ), confirming a conjecture that was previously only established for smooth functions. The resulting optimal algorithm substantially outperforms existing methods (ZO-SADOM has extra d^{1/4}log(1/ε) and d·log(1/ε) factors; SubGD-Push has n^{2n}log²(1/ε)/ε² scaling), which is practically significant for large-scale distributed learning over unreliable networks. Open questions include whether the χ dependence can be improved under additional network structure assumptions, and whether the algorithmic framework extends to non-convex or stochastic variants of the problem.
