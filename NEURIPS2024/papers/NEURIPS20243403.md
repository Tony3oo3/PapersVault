## Problem

Training Neural SDEs for time series modeling requires backpropagation through stochastic differential equation dynamics, which is computationally expensive and numerically unstable, severely limiting scalability. Additionally, standard flow matching and bridge matching methods fail to preserve trajectory couplings when paths cross (e.g., patients with similar initial vitals but different outcomes), making them unsuitable for clinical settings where intersecting trajectories are common.

## Input / Output

The model consumes irregularly sampled bivariate time series (heart rate and mean arterial pressure) along with a sliding window of h past observations and static/time-varying conditional covariates (e.g., norepinephrine infusion rate, APACHE-IV mortality probability, XGBoost risk scores). It outputs a predicted next observation, a learned diffusion coefficient σ_θ(t,x_t) representing epistemic uncertainty, and an instantaneous velocity v_θ(t,x_t). For irregularly sampled data, an auxiliary head h_θ additionally predicts the time-to-next-observation, which is used to rescale the flow at inference.

## Method

Trajectory Flow Matching (TFM) trains a Neural SDE in a simulation-free manner by constructing conditional probability paths as Gaussian bridges between consecutive observed timepoints, then regressing the network's drift and diffusion terms against the bridge's velocity and noise via a matching loss. A key reparameterization trick converts the velocity-matching objective into a target-prediction loss (predicting x_{k+1} directly), which is shown to be equivalent up to a time-dependent scaling and improves training stability. Coupling preservation is guaranteed under three mild assumptions (A1–A3), the most practical being that no two trajectories share h consecutive identical observations; this is enforced by conditioning the flow on a sliding window of past observations. Uncertainty is learned iteratively through a separate σ_θ network trained with an epistemic uncertainty loss that matches predicted σ to the squared prediction error, and irregular sampling is handled by an auxiliary time-predictive loss. The full training loop (Algorithm 1) samples a random trajectory, a random consecutive pair, and a random interpolation time, then updates both the velocity and sigma networks in a single simulation-free step.

## Results

TFM-ODE achieves the lowest MSE across all four clinical datasets: 0.793×10⁻³ on ICU Sepsis (an 83% improvement over NeuralODE's 4.776×10⁻³), 2.762×10⁻³ on ICU Cardiac Arrest, 2.673×10⁻³ on ICU GIB, and 8.245×10⁻³ on ED GIB. The stochastic variant TFM achieves comparable MSE (0.796, 2.755, 2.596, 8.613) while yielding substantially better uncertainty prediction (e.g., 0.724 vs. 1.039 uncertainty MSE on ICU Sepsis) and better variance matching via RBF-MMD on ICU GIB (0.844) and ED GIB (0.717). Overall, TFM/TFM-ODE reduce prediction error by 15–83% relative to the strongest baseline depending on dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFM-ODE outperforms NeuralODE [Chen et al., 2018] by 15–83% in MSE across all four clinical datasets, with the largest gain (83%) on ICU Sepsis where measurement frequency is highest. Against the Aligned FM baseline [Liu et al., 2023a; Somnath et al., 2023], TFM-ODE achieves dramatically lower MSE on ICU GIB (2.673 vs. 118.439) and comparable or better results elsewhere, confirming that coupling preservation via trajectory memory is critical. TFM (stochastic) beats NeuralSDE [Li et al., 2020] on all four datasets in MSE and additionally achieves lower RBF-MMD on ICU GIB (0.844 vs. 1.332) and ED GIB (0.717 vs. 1.465), indicating superior variance matching. LatentODE RNN [Rubanova et al., 2019] is the weakest baseline, with MSE 20–120× higher than TFM-ODE. Between the two proposed variants, TFM-ODE has marginally lower MSE on ICU Sepsis and ICU Cardiac Arrest, while TFM achieves lower MSE on ICU GIB and substantially better uncertainty prediction (Table 2) and MMD, reflecting a bias–variance trade-off between the deterministic and stochastic formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simulation-free flow matching framework (Lipman et 2023, Albergo et 2023) from generative modeling to time series, where the critical additional requirement is preserving trajectory couplings rather than merely matching marginals. It departs from prior aligned/bridge matching approaches (Liu et 2023a, Somnath et 2023) by introducing a history-conditioning mechanism with a formal coupling-preservation guarantee (Proposition 3.2), and from Neural SDE training (Li et 2020, Kidger 2021) by eliminating backpropagation through the SDE solver entirely. The target-prediction reparameterization and learned uncertainty loss are practical extensions of the conditional flow matching objective to irregularly sampled, noisy clinical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Simulation-free training for Neural SDEs removes the primary scalability and stability bottleneck in fitting stochastic dynamical models, making them tractable for large clinical cohorts. The coupling-preservation theory clarifies when flow matching is valid for time series (crossing trajectories require history conditioning), a condition prior generative-modeling flow matching did not need to address. Open questions include extending the framework to causal/interventional modeling, handling periodicity, and providing interpretability tools for clinical deployment; the authors note that memory conditioning only helps in high-frequency measurement settings and that the method currently cannot estimate causal representations.
