## Problem

Existing video-level forgery detectors are largely designed for facial deepfakes and lack mechanisms to capture the diverse spatial artifacts and temporal inconsistencies characteristic of diffusion-generated videos with rich, varied semantics. Meanwhile, the powerful multi-modal representations from Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) remain largely unexplored in the video forensics domain, leaving a gap in generalizable detection for newly emerged diffusion video generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip from which 10 successive frames are randomly sampled and center-cropped to 224×224 RGB images. The LMM branch processes individual frames paired with sampled textual instructions (from pre-defined templates) to produce a 64-token language response, while the ST branch processes the full 10-frame sequence through VQ-VAE reconstruction and a Hybrid-ViT encoder. The final output is a single binary real/fake scalar obtained by average-pooling the fused multi-branch representation and passing it through a linear projection.

## Method

MM-Det combines two parallel branches with a dynamic fusion strategy. The LMM branch fine-tunes LLaVA (CLIP-ViT-L visual encoder + Vicuna-7b LLM) via LoRA instruction tuning on a purpose-built Rich Forgery Reasoning Dataset, then extracts a Multi-Modal Forgery Representation (MMFR) by concatenating the CLIP pooler output with the LLM's last-layer hidden states. The ST branch first amplifies diffusion traces via single-pass VQ-VAE reconstruction (residual between original and reconstructed frames), then feeds both original and residual into a Hybrid-ViT-B encoder equipped with In-and-Across Frame Attention (IAFA), which alternates between cross-frame self-attention over all patch tokens and within-frame attention between a learned Frame-Centric token and its patch tokens to jointly capture local spatial artifacts and global temporal inconsistencies. A channel-wise attention-based dynamic fusion module adaptively weights the two branch features before a final linear classifier. Training is two-stage: instruction-tuning the LMM first, then end-to-end optimization of the ST branch and fusion module with the LMM frozen.

## Results

On the DVF dataset, MM-Det achieves a state-of-the-art average AUC of 92.0%, surpassing the second-best method HiFi-Net (84.3%) by 7.7 percentage points. Per-method AUC scores range from 86.2% (Sora) to 95.9% (Pika), with particularly strong performance on Stable Diffusion (95.7%) and Pika (95.9%). The method outperforms all 10 baselines across every individual diffusion generator, with the largest margins over reconstruction-based (DIRE, 62.1%) and CLIP-based (Raising, 67.0%) detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Det outperforms all 10 baselines on every individual diffusion generator in the DVF dataset. Against the strongest baseline HiFi-Net (84.3% avg AUC), MM-Det gains +7.7% on average, with per-method improvements ranging from +3.3% (VideoCrafter1) to +15.5% (Stable Diffusion). Compared to frequency-domain methods like F3Net (81.3%) and CLIP-based detectors like Uni-FD (74.1%) and Raising (67.0%), MM-Det achieves gains of +10.7%, +17.9%, and +25.0% respectively, indicating that neither frequency traces nor CLIP features alone suffice for diverse diffusion video detection. Against spatiotemporal video baselines, MM-Det improves over ViViT by +12.9%, TALL by +22.5%, and TS2-Net by +19.9%, demonstrating the complementary value of the MMFR and IAFA mechanisms. The reconstruction-based DIRE baseline (62.1%) performs worst overall, confirming that DDIM inversion is both computationally expensive and less effective than the VQ-VAE-based reconstruction used in MM-Det's ST branch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video forensics literature, which has historically focused on facial forgery (deepfake) detection, to the broader and more challenging problem of detecting diffusion-generated videos with diverse semantics. It builds directly on LLaVA's visual instruction tuning framework, repurposing the LMM's CLIP visual encoder and Llama 2 language model to produce a Multi-Modal Forgery Representation (MMFR) — the first use of LMMs in video forensic work. On the spatio-temporal side, it extends the reconstruction-based detection idea from DIRE (DDIM inversion for images) to video via a single-pass VQ-VAE, and introduces the novel In-and-Across Frame Attention mechanism to jointly model local patch-level and global frame-level forgery traces. The dynamic fusion strategy that adaptively weights the LMM and ST branches is a new architectural contribution not present in prior multi-branch detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DVF dataset (8 diffusion methods, diverse resolutions and durations) fills a critical gap in the field, providing the first comprehensive benchmark for open-world diffusion video forensics. The finding that instruction-tuned LMM internal representations generalize well across unseen generative methods suggests a promising new paradigm for content authentication that could extend beyond diffusion models. However, the method is limited to fully synthesized videos (not partial manipulations), incurs substantial computational cost from LLM inference, and the 5.8% degradation under Gaussian blur highlights fragility to common post-processing. Open questions include whether MMFR can be distilled into lighter-weight representations, how it handles hybrid real-synthetic composites, and whether the approach scales to longer videos without the current 1-frame-per-6-seconds caching approximation.
