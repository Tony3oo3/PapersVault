## Problem

Large-scale pre-training datasets for diffusion models inevitably contain corrupted condition-data pairs (mismatched labels or texts), yet the impact of such corruption on DM pre-training and downstream adaptation remained largely unexplored. Conventional wisdom suggests corruption degrades performance, but prior noisy-label studies focus on high-noise supervised settings rather than the low-corruption, large-scale pre-training regime that DMs actually operate in.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume class labels (ImageNet-1K), text prompts (CC3M/MS-COCO), or spatial controls (canny edge maps, SAM segmentation masks) as conditions and produce 256×256 RGB images. During pre-training, the conditional embeddings (from a class embedding layer or a frozen BERT encoder) are perturbed on-the-fly; during downstream personalization via ControlNet or T2I-Adapter, the spatial conditioning is combined with the text/class condition to guide generation. Evaluation uses 50K generated images compared against real validation sets with FID, IS, Precision, Recall, CLIP score, RMD, and entropy.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is Conditional Embedding Perturbation (CEP), which modifies the standard DM training objective by adding a small perturbation δ (Gaussian or Uniform, scaled by γ/√d) to the conditional embedding c_θ(y) at every training step, rather than corrupting a fixed fraction of dataset labels. Theoretically, using a Gaussian mixture model with piecewise-linear denoising networks, the authors prove that slight corruption increases generation entropy by Θ(γ²d) (improving diversity) and reduces the 2-Wasserstein distance to the true data distribution by Ω(γ²d/n_k) (improving quality), because the perturbation prevents the learned distribution from collapsing to the empirical training distribution. Empirically, over 50 pre-trained models across LDM-4, DiT-XL/2, and LCM-v1.5 confirm that 2.5–10% corruption (or equivalent CEP with γ=1) consistently outperforms clean pre-training in FID, IS, diversity, and downstream personalization, while larger corruption degrades performance.

## Results

On IN-1K class-conditional generation, CEP-G reduces FID from 9.44 (clean LDM-4) to 6.91 and boosts IS from 138.46 to 180.77; for DiT-XL/2, CEP-U achieves FID 5.51 and IS 189.94 versus 6.76/179.67 clean. On CC3M text-conditional generation, CEP-U improves LDM-4 FID from 19.85 to 17.93 and LCM-v1.5 FID from 23.59 to 22.91. In downstream ControlNet personalization on IN-100, CEP-U yields FID improvements of 6.08 (Canny) and 7.02 (SAM) for text-conditional LDMs on CC3M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEP consistently and substantially outperforms Input Perturbation (IP) [96] across all four architectures: for LDM-4 on IN-1K, CEP-G achieves FID 6.91 vs. IP's 9.18 (a 2.27 FID gap), while IP yields only marginal improvement over clean (9.44) and slightly worse precision (0.67 vs. 0.71). For DiT-XL/2, CEP-U reaches FID 5.51 compared to IP's 6.75, a 1.24 FID advantage. The paper also compares CEP against traditional regularization methods—Dropout [98] and Label Smoothing [99]—and reports that CEP is more effective than both (Appendix E.3–E.4). In downstream ControlNet personalization, CEP-U improves FID by 6.08 (Canny) and 7.02 (SAM) over clean pre-training for CC3M text-conditional LDMs, while IP provides only negligible gains (e.g., 40.12 vs. 40.65 FID on CC3M Canny). Overall, CEP dominates IP on FID and IS across all settings, and also improves Precision and Recall where IP degrades them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the noisy-label learning literature, which predominantly treats label corruption as a problem to be mitigated (via robust losses, co-teaching, or noise correction), by showing that *slight* condition corruption in pre-training is actually beneficial for diffusion models. It connects to a smaller but growing line of work finding that noise can help pre-training: Chen et al. showed slight label noise aids in-domain downstream tasks in supervised pre-training, NEFTune and NoisyTune demonstrated embedding noise improves language model fine-tuning, and Ning et al. found input perturbation reduces exposure bias in diffusion training. The paper extends these observations to the conditional pre-training of diffusion models specifically, providing the first systematic empirical and theoretical study of how condition corruption affects DM generation quality and diversity across LDM, DiT, and LCM architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that slight condition corruption improves DM pre-training challenges the standard assumption that cleaner data is always better, and the proposed CEP technique is simple enough to be adopted broadly in diffusion model training pipelines. However, the theoretical analysis relies on simplified GMM assumptions and piecewise-linear denoisers that may not fully capture real-world DM behavior, and the empirical study is limited to 256×256 resolution on IN-1K and CC3M. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at higher resolutions and on billion-scale web-crawled datasets, whether it extends to other modalities (audio, video), and what the optimal perturbation magnitude is as a function of architecture and dataset scale.
