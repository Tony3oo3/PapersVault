## Problem

Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) provide globally convergent optimization over probability measures, but many important problems—risk minimization for infinite-width two-layer neural networks, sparse deconvolution—are naturally formulated over signed measures. The paper asks how to extend MFLD to signed-measure optimization and which of the two known reductions (lifting vs. bilevel) yields stronger convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a convex functional G over signed measures on a compact Riemannian manifold W (e.g., the sphere S^d for 2NNs), a TV-regularization level λ > 0, and a temperature β > 0. It produces the optimal signed measure ν* minimizing G_λ(ν) = G(ν) + (λ/2)||ν||²_TV, which in the 2NN setting corresponds to the learned predictor h(ν, x) = ∫ φ(⟨x,w⟩) dν(w). The bilevel formulation operates on probability measures η over W (the 'kernel' measure), with the signed measure recovered as ν = f·η where f is the inner-optimization variable; the entropic regularization β⁻¹H(η|τ) is annealed to zero to recover the unregularized optimum.

## Method

The paper shows that the lifting reduction (encoding signed mass via an extra real coordinate) is fundamentally incompatible with MFLD: for a broad class of Riemannian metrics, it cannot simultaneously satisfy displacement smoothness (P1) and uniform log-Sobolev inequality (P2) unless λ is large. In contrast, the bilevel reduction—using the variational identity ||ν||²_TV = min_η ∫|ν|²/η from multiple kernel learning—yields a convex objective J_λ(η) over probability measures that satisfies both properties under mild smoothness assumptions on G. Building on this, the paper designs a piecewise-constant annealing schedule β_t = 2^k d that exploits the structure of J_λ to reach (1+Δ)-multiplicative accuracy in exp(O(λ⁻¹ log λ⁻¹)) time, improving over the classical β_t ~ log(t) schedule's exp(O(λ⁻²)) bound. Finally, for single-neuron learning with rotationally invariant data, a Lyapunov-type argument shows the local LSI constant at optimality scales polynomially (rather than exponentially) in β and d, yielding local exponential convergence rates with favorable dimension dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes that the bilevel reduction satisfies both displacement smoothness (P1) and uniform LSI (P2) under mild assumptions, while no lifting reduction satisfies both simultaneously unless λ is large (Prop. 3.2 vs. Prop. 3.4). The improved annealing schedule (Thm. 4.2) achieves (1+Δ)-multiplicative accuracy in time exp(c·λ⁻¹·log λ⁻¹), improving upon the classical β_t ~ log(t) schedule which requires exp(c'·λ⁻²). For the single-neuron learning problem (Thm. 5.2), the local LSI constant at optimality is independent of β, λ, and d (up to polynomial factors), in contrast to the exponential dependence on β that a generic Holley-Stroock analysis would yield.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the bilevel reduction (proposed) and the lifting reduction (from [Chi22c]) for applying MFLD to signed-measure optimization. Theoretically, the lifting reduction is shown to be fundamentally incompatible with MFLD: for any choice of Riemannian metric parameters (q_r, q_w, b), either displacement smoothness (P1) fails or the local LSI (P2) fails unless λ is sufficiently large (Prop. 3.2). In contrast, the bilevel reduction satisfies both properties under Assumption 1 (Prop. 3.4). Empirically (Figure 1b), MFLD applied to the bilevel formulation achieves lower regularized training loss G_λ(ν) than MFLD applied to the lifted formulation (both Conic and Canonical metrics) for a 2NN with ReLU activation learning a Hermite-polynomial teacher, with d=10 and λ=β⁻¹=10⁻³. The improved annealing schedule also outperforms the classical β_t ~ log(t) schedule in time complexity for reaching fixed multiplicative accuracy, reducing the exponential dependence from λ⁻² to λ⁻¹·log λ⁻¹.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) framework of Chizat (2022) from probability measures to signed measures, a natural but non-trivial generalization since MFLD is inherently defined on probability spaces. It draws on two existing reduction strategies—the lifting approach from unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat 2022, Chizat & Bach 2018) and the bilevel/η-trick from multiple kernel learning (Lanckriet et al. 2004, Bach 2021)—and systematically compares them under the MFLD lens. The improved annealing schedule is adapted from Suzuki et al. (2023), and the bilevel formulation connects to the two-timescale 2NN training literature (Berthier et al. 2023, Marion & Berthier 2023, Takakura & Suzuki 2024), which the paper shows is equivalent to MFLD on the bilevel objective in the limit of infinite inner-loop speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear decision rule for practitioners: the lifting reduction, while seemingly natural, is fundamentally incompatible with MFLD convergence guarantees unless the regularization is large, whereas the bilevel reduction always inherits MFLD's guarantees. The improved annealing schedule reduces the time to reach fixed multiplicative accuracy from exp(O(λ⁻²)) to exp(O(λ⁻¹ log λ⁻¹)), and the local LSI analysis for single-neuron learning shows polynomial rather than exponential dependence on dimension and inverse temperature—suggesting practical convergence may be far better than global bounds indicate. Open questions include discrete-time guarantees on Riemannian manifolds, extending the local analysis beyond single neurons, and whether noisy particle dynamics can be constructed directly in signed-measure space (e.g., via birth-death processes).
