## Problem

CFR+ dominates practical EFG solving by running Blackwell approachability (via RM+) locally at each infoset, but no algorithm had applied Blackwell approachability directly to the treeplex as a whole. This paper asks whether a treeplex-level Blackwell approachability framework can match or exceed CFR+, and whether the strong empirical performance of CFR+ is explained by its use of Blackwell approachability or by other structural properties such as infoset-level stepsize invariance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-player zero-sum EFG specified by a game tree (with infosets, actions, and chance nodes) and a payoff matrix M, and produces Nash equilibrium strategies in sequence form (one variable per sequence (j,a) plus the root variable x_∅). The algorithm operates on the conic hull C = cone(T) of the treeplex rather than T itself; the orthogonal projection onto C (and onto the stable region C_≥ for Smooth PTB+) is computed in O(dn log(l+m)) arithmetic operations via a recursive value-function approach. Convergence is measured by the duality gap of (weighted) average iterates, with the self-play framework providing loss vectors My_{t-1} and -M^T x_{t-1} at each iteration.

## Method

The core framework (Algorithm 1) reduces regret minimization over the treeplex T to regret minimization over its conic hull C = cone(T) by exploiting the structural property T ⊂ {x : ⟨x, a⟩ = 1} for a = (1,0), analogous to the simplex identity ⟨x, 1⟩ = 1. At each iteration, the algorithm maintains R_t ∈ C, computes the strategy x_t = R_t/⟨R_t, a⟩, and feeds the transformed loss f(x_t, ℓ_t) = ℓ_t − ⟨x_t, ℓ_t⟩a to a regret minimizer over C; the hyperplane forcing condition ⟨R_t, f(x_t, ℓ)⟩ = 0 guarantees R_t grows at most O(√T), yielding O(√T) regret over T. PTB+ instantiates the inner regret minimizer with predictive OMD over C, producing a treeplex stepsize-invariant algorithm with O(1/√T) Nash convergence. Smooth PTB+ restricts iterates to the stable region C_≥ = cone(T) ∩ {R : ⟨R, a⟩ ≥ R_0}, which is not a cone, thereby recovering Lipschitz continuity of the decision map and enabling an RVU bound that yields the first O(1/T) convergence rate for a Blackwell approachability-based EFG solver.

## Results

PTB+ achieves an O(1/√T) convergence rate to Nash equilibrium in self-play while being treeplex stepsize invariant (iterates independent of the stepsize η). Smooth PTB+ is the first Blackwell approachability-based algorithm to achieve an O(1/T) convergence rate for EFGs, at the cost of requiring a stepsize (breaking stepsize invariance). Empirically, PTB+ is the best-performing algorithm among the paper's own instantiations (outperforming Smooth PTB+, AdaGradTB+, and AdamTB+ across all five benchmark games), but PCFR+ outperforms PTB+ on average-iterate duality gap in most games. For last-iterate convergence, no algorithm consistently dominates across all games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFR+ [37], PTB+ loses in practice: CFR+ benefits from infoset stepsize invariance (different stepsizes at each infoset without tuning), which is a strictly stronger property than PTB+'s treeplex stepsize invariance (single stepsize, but iterates independent of its value). Against PCFR+ [16], PTB+ also loses on average-iterate duality gap in most games (Kuhn, Leduc, Liar's Dice, Goofspiel, Battleship), despite both being predictive and Blackwell-approachability-based; the paper attributes this gap to PCFR+'s infoset-level stepsize invariance enabling per-infoset adaptation to variable magnitudes (deeper infosets have smaller values). Against SC-POMD [24], which achieves O(1/T) but requires a single globally-tuned stepsize, PTB+ is competitive or better in practice because stepsize tuning is infeasible for large instances (e.g., 5.3×10⁶ actions in Goofspiel). The key negative result is that treeplex stepsize invariance alone (PTB+) does not explain CFR+/PCFR+'s strong empirical performance; infoset stepsize invariance appears to be the critical additional ingredient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Blackwell approachability from the simplex (where it underlies Regret Matching+ and CFR+) to the full treeplex/sequence-form polytope, providing the first modular framework that reduces regret minimization over a treeplex to regret minimization over its conic hull. It builds on the Abernethy et al. (2011) reduction between approachability and no-regret learning but exploits treeplex-specific structure (the empty-sequence variable and the hyperplane forcing condition) to avoid the conservative lifting dimension used in the general construction. The framework is instantiated with predictive OMD (Rakhlin & Sridharan, 2013) to yield PTB+ and Smooth PTB+, and is positioned as a direct alternative to the CFR decomposition approach of Tammelin et al. (2015) and Farina et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first O(1/T) convergence rate for a Blackwell approachability-based EFG solver (Smooth PTB+), answering an open question, and introduces a new hypothesis—infoset stepsize invariance—that partially explains the long-standing puzzle of CFR+'s strong empirical performance despite its suboptimal O(1/√T) rate. The key negative result (PTB+ underperforms PCFR+ despite sharing Blackwell approachability, predictivity, and stepsize invariance) rules out a previously plausible explanation and narrows the search for what makes CFR+ effective. Open questions include last-iterate convergence behavior, the role of alternation, and whether new approachability reductions (Dann et al., 2024) or norm-based generalizations can recover infoset-level stepsize invariance without the CFR decomposition.
