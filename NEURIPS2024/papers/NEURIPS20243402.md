## Problem

Promptable segmentation methods require instance-specific manual prompts for each object, limiting large-scale deployment. Task-generic promptable segmentation reduces this to a single prompt per task, but the MLLMs used to derive instance-specific prompts from the generic one suffer from hallucinations that mislead segmentation. Rather than treating hallucinations as errors to eliminate, this paper argues they encode valuable pre-trained contextual knowledge that can be mined and then systematically verified to improve prompt accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and one task-generic text prompt (e.g., "camouflaged animal", "polyp", "glass") shared across all images in a task, with no per-image annotations. The image is internally split into multi-scale patches (original, halved, quartered) to trigger hallucinatory reasoning. The output is a binary segmentation mask of shape H×W, produced through 4–6 iterative cycles; intermediate outputs include instance-specific bounding boxes and single-word keywords that guide SAM. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying solely on test-time adaptation with LLaVA-1.5-13B, SAM ViT-H/16, CLIP, and Stable Diffusion inpainting.

## Method

ProMaC is an iterative Prompt-Mask Cycle framework with two coupled generators. The prompt generator employs multi-scale chain-of-thought prompting: it feeds image patches of varying scales to LLaVA to trigger co-occurrence-based hallucinations that reveal candidate object names and bounding boxes, then applies Visual Contrastive Reasoning (VCR) — a classifier-free-guidance-inspired mechanism that compares MLLM output distributions on the original image versus a Stable Diffusion inpainted version with task-related regions removed — to eliminate background-induced hallucinations and finalize precise instance-specific prompts. The mask generator uses SAM guided by Spatial CLIP text-to-region mapping and bounding boxes, then applies Mask Semantic Alignment by computing CLIP similarity between each patch's masked region and the instance-specific keyword, weighting masks by normalized similarity to ensure task-semantic consistency. Across iterations, the previous mask serves as the inpainting region for VCR and as a visual weight to focus the next prompt generation on task-relevant areas, jointly refining prompts and masks until convergence.

## Results

On the primary Camouflaged Object Detection task (task-generic prompt setting), ProMaC achieves Fβ=0.790, Eφ=0.899, Sα=0.833 on CHAMELEON and Fβ=0.716, Eφ=0.876, Sα=0.805 on COD10K, outperforming the closest task-generic baseline GenSAM by 9.4% Fβ on CHAMELEON and 3.0% Fβ on COD10K. On Medical Image Segmentation, ProMaC improves Fβ from 0.059 (GenSAM) to 0.243 on CVC-ColonDB and from 0.210 to 0.394 on Kvasir. On Open-Vocabulary Segmentation, ProMaC reaches 59.3 mIoU on PASCAL VOC and 30.7 on Pascal Context, surpassing the best trained baselines (OVSegmentor 53.8, TCL 24.3) without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMaC consistently outperforms all task-generic prompt baselines across all three COD datasets: on CHAMELEON it beats GenSAM (Fβ 0.790 vs 0.696), GroundingSAM (0.790 vs 0.662), and even scribble-supervised WS-SAM (0.790 vs 0.777). On COD10K, ProMaC surpasses GenSAM (Fβ 0.716 vs 0.695) and matches the best scribble-supervised method WS-SAM (0.716 vs 0.719). For Medical Image Segmentation, ProMaC dramatically improves over GenSAM on CVC-ColonDB (Fβ 0.243 vs 0.059) and Kvasir (0.394 vs 0.210), while slightly leading on ISIC (0.717 vs 0.699). On Open-Vocabulary Segmentation, ProMaC achieves 59.3 mIoU on PASCAL VOC and 30.7 on Pascal Context without any task-specific training, outperforming trained methods like OVSegmentor (53.8) and TCL (24.3), though it trails SegCLIP (26.5) by 1.3 mIoU on COCO-Object. For Transparent Object Segmentation, ProMaC leads GenSAM on both GSD (Fβ 0.409 vs 0.394) and Trans10K-hard (0.509 vs 0.489). The VCR module also boosts spatial reasoning accuracy on What's Up from 73.1 (LLaVA-1.5-13B) to 75.4, outperforming ViP-LLaVA-13B (70.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GenSAM's task-generic promptable segmentation setting (AAAI 2024), where a single generic prompt like 'camouflaged animal' replaces per-instance manual prompts for SAM-based segmentation. The key conceptual departure is from the dominant paradigm of treating MLLM hallucinations as purely detrimental noise to be eliminated; instead, ProMaC deliberately triggers hallucinations via multi-scale image patching to mine prior knowledge, then systematically verifies and prunes them through a visual contrastive reasoning mechanism inspired by classifier-free guidance. The mask generator addresses SAM's known weakness in label prediction by introducing mask semantic alignment via CLIP scoring, and the iterative prompt-mask cycle creates a self-refining loop absent in prior single-pass MLLM+SAM pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully training-free, test-time adaptation pipeline can match or exceed weakly-supervised methods on challenging segmentation tasks (COD, TOD) using only a one-word task description, which has practical appeal for reducing annotation burden. However, the authors acknowledge a significant limitation: performance degrades substantially on specialized domains (medical image segmentation) where the underlying MLLM lacks domain-specific training data, and GPT-4o's superior generalization partially closes this gap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more specialized or novel domains, whether the multi-iteration MLLM+inpainting pipeline's computational cost is acceptable for deployment, and whether better MLLMs will make the hallucination-mining strategy redundant.
