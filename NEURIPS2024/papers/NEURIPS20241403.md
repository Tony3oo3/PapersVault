## Problem

Utilizing long-range dependency in large-scale heterogeneous graphs is blocked by two compounding challenges: the number of meta-paths grows exponentially with hop count, making full meta-path sets computationally infeasible, and irrelevant or noisy meta-paths introduce over-smoothing that degrades performance. Existing metapath-free HGNNs cannot scale beyond a few hops, while metapath-based methods are forced to cap the maximum hop at 2 on large datasets, leaving long-range information unused.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a typed heterogeneous graph (node types, edge types, adjacency matrices, and per-type feature matrices) along with a designated target node type and semi-supervised labels. Neighbor aggregation is pre-computed once via chained row-normalized adjacency matrix multiplications (following SeHGNN), producing regular-shaped tensors per meta-path; the trainable model then operates only on these pre-computed tensors. The output is a set of M=30 effective meta-paths (generalizable to other HGNNs) plus node embeddings and predicted labels from a pure-MLP target-net that concatenates per-meta-path projections without any attention mechanism.

## Method

LMSPS is a two-stage NAS-inspired framework. In the search stage, a super-net assigns one architecture parameter per meta-path and uses Gumbel-softmax to compute path strengths; a progressive sampling algorithm dynamically shrinks the search space from K to V by retaining only meta-paths whose strength exceeds a threshold that tightens over epochs, while each iteration uniformly samples just M meta-paths for parameter updates, yielding hop-independent O(NMF²) time complexity. A bilevel optimization alternates between updating MLP weights on training loss and architecture parameters on validation loss. In the evaluation stage, the method repeatedly (200×) samples M meta-paths from the reduced space using the learned path-strength distribution and selects the set with the lowest validation loss, accounting for inter-meta-path redundancy that independent top-M selection would miss. The final target-net is a simple concatenation of per-meta-path MLP projections followed by a classification MLP, deliberately avoiding attention to prevent noise amplification.

## Results

On the large-scale OGBN-MAG dataset (1.94M nodes), LMSPS achieves 54.83% test accuracy, outperforming the best competitor SeHGNN (51.45%) by over 3 percentage points. On the four HGB benchmark datasets, LMSPS achieves the best Macro-F1 and Micro-F1 scores on DBLP (95.35/95.66), IMDB (66.99/68.70), and ACM (94.73/94.69), and the best Macro-F1 on Freebase (53.26). Notably, LMSPS maintains near-constant training time and memory as the maximum hop increases from 1 to 6, while SeHGNN encounters OOM beyond hop 3 on OGBN-MAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMSPS outperforms all 25 baselines on nearly every metric across all five main datasets, with the sole exception being Micro-F1 on Freebase where SlotGAT (66.83) edges out LMSPS (66.09). On the largest dataset OGBN-MAG, LMSPS achieves 54.83% test accuracy versus SeHGNN's 51.45% (the best method that does not OOM) and NARS's 50.66%, a margin of 3.4–4.2 points. Among metapath-based methods, LMSPS consistently surpasses SeHGNN by 0.4–0.8 points on Macro-F1 across DBLP, IMDB, and ACM, and by 2.55 points on Freebase Macro-F1. Metapath-free methods (HAN, GTN, RSHN, HetGNN, MAGNN, HetSANN, HINormer, SlotGAT) all encounter OOM on OGBN-MAG, while LMSPS scales to 6-hop meta-paths with stable ~15s/epoch training time. The Random baseline (30 random meta-paths) scores 35.14% on OGBN-MAG versus LMSPS's 54.83%, confirming that the progressive sampling search is critical rather than simply using more meta-paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMSPS builds directly on SeHGNN's one-time message-passing neighbor aggregation for pre-processing heterogeneous graphs, and borrows the bilevel optimization framework from differentiable NAS (DARTS, SPOS) for architecture parameter learning. It departs from prior meta-path search methods (GEMS, DiffMG, PMMM, RL-HGNN) that target meta-graphs or use reinforcement learning, instead formulating meta-path selection as a progressive sampling problem with hop-independent cost. The key conceptual departure from all prior metapath-based HGNNs (HAN, GTN, MAGNN, SeHGNN) is the observation that a small subset of meta-paths dominates performance while others actively degrade it, motivating selective rather than exhaustive meta-path utilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMSPS provides the first practical mechanism for exploiting long-range dependency (up to 6+ hops) on large-scale heterogeneous graphs with constant time complexity O(NMF²), unblocking a direction previously limited to small datasets. The searched meta-paths generalize to other HGNNs (HAN, SeHGNN), suggesting a reusable 'meta-path discovery as preprocessing' paradigm. However, the optimal maximum hop remains dataset-dependent and cannot be determined automatically, and the approach is restricted to metapath-based architectures. Open questions include whether the method extends to other downstream tasks (link prediction, graph classification) and whether it benefits from even sparser real-world graphs where long-range dependency is most critical.
