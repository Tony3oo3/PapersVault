## Problem

Existing continuous GNNs model node feature dynamics using either integer-order ODEs (GRAND, GraphCON) or a single fractional-order FDE (FROND), both of which constrain the temporal memory structure to a fixed form. TDE-GNN extends to multiple integer orders but ignores nonlocal memory, while FROND's single fractional order α requires extensive per-dataset fine-tuning and cannot capture multi-scale waiting-time dynamics. The paper asks whether a learnable superposition of fractional derivative orders can unify and surpass these approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243285/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{N×N} and initial node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, then integrates a distributed-order FDE over a continuous time horizon T (analogous to layer depth) to produce final node embeddings X(T), which are decoded via a learnable function ζ for downstream tasks. Unlike discrete-layer GNNs, the 'depth' is a continuous integration parameter, and the derivative order is not a scalar hyperparameter but a learnable probability measure μ over an interval [a,b] (typically [0,1]), discretized into ~10 quadrature points with learnable weights.

## Method

DRAGON replaces the single derivative operator in continuous GNNs with the distributed-order fractional derivative ∫_a^b D^α X(t) dμ(α), where μ is a learnable measure parameterized by quadrature weights w_j at fixed interpolation points α_j. The distributed-order FDE is discretized into a multi-term FDE via quadrature, then solved either by converting to a system of single-order equations (using the least-common-multiple trick from Diethelm's Theorem 8.1) and applying the fractional Adams-Bashforth-Moulton predictor, or directly via the Grunwald-Letnikov approximation. The framework subsumes GRAND, GraphCON, CDE, GREAD, FROND, and TDE-GNN as special cases (Dirac-delta or discrete μ). Theoretically, the paper proves that the associated non-Markovian graph random walk has a waiting-time distribution that can approximate any distribution in C_0(N) (Theorem 2), giving DRAGON strictly more expressive memory dynamics than FROND's fixed power-law.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAGON consistently outperforms its direct predecessors across all continuous GNN families. D-GRAND-l beats GRAND-l by 1.5% on Cora and by a dramatic 18% on Airport (98.5% vs 80.5%), while also surpassing F-GRAND-l (FROND) on 8 of 9 homophilic datasets. D-GraphCON-l improves over GraphCON-l by up to 29.7% on Airport (98.3% vs 68.6%) and over F-GraphCON-l by 1.2% on Disease. On heterophilic node classification, D-CDE outperforms CDE by 2.23% on Roman-empire (93.87% vs 91.64%) and by 0.97% on Minesweeper, while also exceeding F-CDE on 5 of 6 datasets. On the LRGB long-range benchmark, D-GRAND-1 achieves 0.7571 AP on Peptides-func, surpassing the previous SOTA GCN+DRew (0.6996) by nearly 6% and F-GRAND-1 by 4.5%. On graph classification (FakeNewsNet), D-GRAND-1 outperforms F-GRAND-1 by 0.15–0.31% across all feature types, and on oversmoothing mitigation (Table 11), D-GRAND-1 maintains 79.50% on PubMed at 128 layers while GRAND-1 fails entirely and F-GRAND-1 drops to 74.60%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAGON sits at the intersection of two recent extensions of continuous GNNs: FROND, which replaces the integer-order derivative with a single fractional-order operator, and TDE-GNN, which uses a discrete set of integer-order derivatives. The key conceptual departure is replacing both with a learnable probability measure over a continuous range of fractional orders, drawing on the distributed-order fractional differential operator from the fractional calculus literature. The paper explicitly positions GRAND, GraphCON, CDE, FROND, and TDE-GNN as special cases of its unified framework (via Dirac delta or discrete measures), making it a genuine generalization rather than a competing architecture. The non-Markovian random walk interpretation with flexible waiting-time distributions provides a probabilistic grounding that neither FROND (fixed power-law waiting times) nor TDE-GNN (no fractional memory) offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the dataset-specific fine-tuning of the fractional order that FROND requires, replacing it with a learnable distribution that adapts during training. Theorem 2 (density of the span of power-law waiting-time functions in C₀(ℕ)) provides a clean theoretical guarantee that the framework can approximate arbitrary waiting-time distributions, which is the key mechanism behind its long-range dependency and oversmoothing benefits. Open questions include whether the distributed-order formulation is strictly necessary over a well-optimized multi-term fractional approach, how the computational overhead scales with the number of quadrature points on very large graphs, and whether the benefits persist in self-supervised or contrastive learning settings where the loss landscape differs from supervised node classification.
