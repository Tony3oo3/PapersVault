## Problem

Overparameterized SDE models (e.g., neural-network-parameterized drifts) require gradient estimation with respect to a parameter vector θ of dimension n that can be orders of magnitude larger than the state dimension d. The standard pathwise differentiation (IPA) estimator demands simulating a d + d·n dimensional SDE system, making it intractable in high-n regimes. The paper asks whether an unbiased, finite-variance gradient estimator exists whose computational cost is insensitive to n.

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes the SDE coefficients (drift μ_θ, volatility σ_θ, jump size χ_θ), the parameter vector θ ∈ R^n, initial state x ∈ R^d, and reward/terminal functions ρ_θ, g_θ, and produces a single unbiased sample of the full gradient ∇_θ v_θ(t,x) ∈ R^n. A key structural detail is that the estimator evaluates the generator-gradient integrand at a single randomized time τ ~ Unif[0,T] rather than integrating over the full horizon, and the number of auxiliary SDEs to simulate depends only on d (specifically d + d² or d + d² + ½d³), not on n. When the volatility σ_θ is independent of θ, the Hessian term vanishes and only d + d² SDEs are needed.

## Method

The generator gradient estimator works by differentiating the Feynman-Kac PDE for the value function v_θ with respect to θ, yielding an expectation that involves only space derivatives (∇v_0 and H[v_0]) of the value function evaluated along the θ=0 trajectory, rather than θ-derivatives of the SDE path. These space derivatives are then estimated via pathwise differentiation of the d-dimensional SDE, requiring simulation of the state process plus its Jacobian and Hessian (d + d² + ½d³ total SDEs). The final estimator D(x) = T·∇_θ L_0 V_0(τ, X_0^x(τ)) + ∫∇_θ ρ_0 dt + ∇_θ g_0(X_0^x(T)) randomizes the time integral by sampling τ ~ Unif[0,T], reducing the computation to a single-time-point evaluation of the auxiliary processes. The key mechanism is that the θ-dependence enters only through the generator operator ∇_θ L_0 (which is a fixed, n-dimensional linear combination of the d-dimensional space derivatives Z and H), so the simulation cost is decoupled from n while the estimator remains unbiased for all n.

## Results

The generator gradient (GG) estimator achieves near-constant computation time across parameter dimensions n ranging from 102 to 1.15×10⁷, while pathwise differentiation (PD) runtime scales linearly with n. In terms of estimation quality, the 400-sample standard error (SE) of GG is consistently lower than PD across all tested n values, with SE ratios (GG/PD) ranging from 0.902 to 0.971. At the largest tested dimension (n = 1.15×10⁷), GG achieves an average SE of 0.407 versus 0.466 for PD, while requiring only O(d²) or O(d³) SDE dimensions to simulate (vs. d + d·n for PD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the pathwise differentiation (IPA) estimator, which requires simulating a (d + d·n)-dimensional SDE system. The generator gradient estimator dominates on both axes: (1) computational efficiency — GG runtime remains nearly flat as n grows from 10² to 10⁷, while PD runtime increases by more than an order of magnitude; and (2) estimation variance — GG achieves lower average SE than PD at every tested n, with the gap most pronounced at intermediate dimensions (e.g., SE ratio 0.902 at n = 3.24×10⁵, meaning ~10% lower variance). The advantage in runtime grows with n, while the variance advantage is modest but consistent. The paper also notes that likelihood-ratio estimators, while applicable in some settings, typically exhibit significantly higher variance and are restricted to cases where volatility is independent of θ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical gradient estimation literature for SDEs—infinitesimal perturbation analysis (IPA) and likelihood ratio methods (Glynn, Yang & Kushner)—and on Kunita's stochastic flow theory for jump diffusions. The key departure is a new 'generator gradient' estimator that differentiates the Feynman-Kac PDE with respect to the parameter θ and then replaces the resulting space derivatives of the value function with pathwise differentiation estimators, thereby decoupling computational cost from the parameter dimension n. Unlike likelihood ratio methods, which require σ to be θ-independent and have higher variance, the generator gradient estimator handles general θ-dependent volatilities and jumps while maintaining or improving variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In overparameterized SDE applications (neural SDEs, deep RL with continuous dynamics, PDE-constrained optimization), the parameter dimension n can exceed the state dimension d by orders of magnitude, making pathwise differentiation infeasible. The generator gradient estimator's near-constant scaling in n (O(d²) or O(d³) SDEs regardless of n) opens the door to gradient-based optimization of SDEs with very large neural-network-parameterized coefficients. Limitations include the requirement of C² differentiability and uniform boundedness of space derivatives of the SDE coefficients; the authors note the estimator often works in practice for non-smooth cases (ReLU, CIR) but formal guarantees require case-by-case analysis. Open questions include extending the theory to reflected SDEs, multi-parameter sensitivity, and broader empirical validation beyond the single LQ control benchmark.
