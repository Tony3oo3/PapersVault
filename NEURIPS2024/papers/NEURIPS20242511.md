## Problem

While universal approximation theorems (Kolmogorov-Arnold, space-filling curves) show that low-dimensional parameterizations can represent high-dimensional objects, no prior work established whether such representations are actually reachable by gradient-based optimization. This paper resolves the fundamental question of whether models with fewer parameters than the target dimension (W < d) can learn d-dimensional targets via gradient flow, providing both a positive construction (two parameters suffice for almost guaranteed learning) and sharp negative results showing inherent topological and measure-theoretic obstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional target vector f in a Hilbert space H ≅ R^d together with a Borel probability measure μ on H, and produces a C^∞ map Φ: R² → R^d such that the gradient flow trajectory w(t) starting from w(0) = 0 converges to f for μ-almost all targets (failure set has μ-measure < ε for any prescribed ε > 0). The learnable set F_Φ contains a multi-dimensional Cantor set F₀ of almost full μ-measure, but is necessarily not dense in R^d and its complement is dense. The model is defined by an infinite hierarchical procedure and is not expressible as a single elementary function.

## Method

The construction aligns a hierarchical decomposition of the target space (a Cantor set built by iterative box splitting along cycling coordinate directions) with a stage-wise decomposition of the 2D parameter space, where the two parameters (u, v) play asymmetric roles: u increases monotonically (slow) while v varies rapidly along level curves, so the trajectory is approximately the local minimizer on each level curve. Each stage Φ^(n) deforms level curves to guide the gradient flow trajectory around the corner of the next-level box using 'gathering' (pulling the trajectory to a curve tip) and 'spreading' (letting it slip off at the correct target coordinate) sub-stages, with gaps between child boxes preventing trapping at spurious local minima. Negative results employ the Borsuk-Ulam antipodality theorem to prove that any subset of R^d homeomorphic to the W-sphere contains non-learnable targets, and Pfaffian function theory (Khovanskii's finiteness bounds on connected components of level sets) to show that models expressible by elementary functions without unbounded sin have learnable sets of zero Lebesgue measure.

## Results

The paper is purely theoretical with no experimental results. The headline result (Theorem 6) proves that for any Borel probability measure μ on R^d, there exists a C^∞ two-parameter model Φ: R² → R^d such that the set of GF-non-learnable targets has μ-measure less than any prescribed ε > 0. The negative results (Theorems 3, 4) show that for W < d, the learnable set is never dense in R^d and the non-learnable set is always dense; moreover, any subset homeomorphic to the W-sphere contains non-learnable targets. Theorem 8 further establishes that for Pfaffian (hence elementary) maps with W < d, the closure of the model image has Lebesgue measure zero, so GF-learnable targets have measure zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is purely theoretical and does not compare against empirical baselines. In the theoretical landscape, it extends the universal approximation results of Maiorov–Pinkus (1999) and the Kolmogorov–Arnold Superposition Theorem by addressing the previously unaddressed question of whether such low-parameter models can actually be trained by gradient flow. The paper shows that the infinite-hierarchical construction in Theorem 6 is essential: Theorem 8 proves that no model expressible by elementary functions (Pfaffian functions) can achieve positive-measure learnability when W < d, and Corollary 10 shows that even sin-based models with dense image (e.g., Φ_i(w) = sin(Σ a_ij w_j)) still have measure-zero learnable sets due to spurious local minima. The Borsuk-Ulam-based Theorem 4 generalizes and strengthens the non-density result of Theorem 3, which itself relies on a local loss-barrier argument analogous to classical spurious-minima results for finite neural networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of universal approximation theory and optimization dynamics. While prior work (Maiorov-Pinkus, Kolmogorov-Arnold, space-filling curves) established that low-parameter models can represent high-dimensional targets, and the overparameterization literature (NTK, mean-field) showed that large models are easier to train, no rigorous results had addressed whether underparameterized models (W < d) can actually be trained by gradient flow. The paper repurposes tools from approximation theory (Borsuk-Ulam, inspired by DeVore et al.) and real algebraic geometry (Pfaffian functions, Khovanskii) to answer this question from both positive and negative directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a definitive theoretical answer: underparameterized learning via gradient flow is possible but fundamentally constrained — two parameters suffice for almost-sure learnability under a known target distribution, yet the required model is necessarily non-elementary (an infinite hierarchical construction), and elementary-function models can only learn a measure-zero set of targets. Open questions include whether the positive result extends to infinite-dimensional target spaces or measures with infinite support, and whether the non-density of learnable targets holds without the full-rank Jacobian assumption. For practitioners, the key takeaway is that the expressive power of underparameterized models is essentially unreachable by standard GD training with elementary architectures.
