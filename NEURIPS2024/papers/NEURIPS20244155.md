## Problem

Deep Neural Collapse (DNC) — the progressive formation of within-class variability collapse and simplex equiangular tight frame structure across layers of deep networks — has been explained almost exclusively through data-agnostic models like the Unconstrained Features Model, which discard the training data and most of the network architecture. This leaves a critical gap: no data-dependent mechanism explains how DNC actually emerges during feature learning in real networks. The paper asks whether a specific, data-driven operation can account for DNC formation across all layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image datasets (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB, SVHN) with one-hot class labels (±1 encoding), a set of kernels (Laplace or Gaussian with bandwidth parameter), and untrained random feature maps (ReLU or cosine activation applied to random Gaussian weights of width 1024–4096). It produces a depth-L neural network where each layer's linear map is a product of a random weight matrix and the square root of the AGOP computed at that layer, along with kernel regression coefficients. The DNC metrics (NC1: tr(Σ_W)/tr(Σ_B) ratio; NC2: distance of class-mean covariance to the ETF) are measured at every layer both before and after the AGOP projection to isolate the mechanism.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Deep Recursive Feature Machine (Deep RFM), which at each layer trains a kernel ridge regression predictor on the current representations, computes the AGOP (uncentered covariance of input-output gradients) of that predictor, projects the data through the AGOP's square root, and then applies an untrained random feature map. The paper proves two theoretical results: (1) in the high-dimensional asymptotic regime, Deep RFM exhibits exponential convergence to DNC with rate governed by the ratio of the predictor kernel's non-linearity parameter to the feature map's, and (2) DNC is the unique optimal solution to a relaxed parametrized kernel ridge regression objective, establishing it as an implicit bias of kernel learning. For standard DNNs trained with SGD and small initialization, the paper shows via SVD decomposition of each weight matrix that the right singular structure (SV^T, which the Neural Feature Ansatz links to the AGOP) accounts for the majority of within-class variability reduction, while the non-linearity and left singular vectors contribute minimally to NC1.

## Results

The paper establishes that AGOP projection is the sole driver of DNC in Deep RFM: the NC1 metric (tr Σ_W / tr Σ_B) improves entirely due to the M_l^{1/2} projection, while the random feature map actually worsens NC1. Theoretically, Theorem 5.3 proves exponential convergence to DNC in Deep RFM with rate governed by the ratio λ̂/λ_map, and Theorem 5.4 proves the optimal kernel matrix for parametrized KRR is exactly the collapsed Gram matrix Y^T Y. For standard DNNs (MLP, ResNet, VGG) trained with SGD and small initialization, the right singular structure SV^T of each weight matrix accounts for the majority of within-class variability collapse, with NFA correlations of 0.74±0.17 (ResNet) and 0.74±0.13 (MLP) on CIFAR-10 and MNIST, far exceeding the trivial correlation bound of ~0.04 for width-512 matrices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a mechanism-explanation study rather than a benchmark comparison, so it does not pit its method against competing DNC explanations in a head-to-head accuracy sense. Instead, it performs internal component ablations: in Deep RFM, removing the AGOP projection (keeping only the random feature map) eliminates DNC entirely, while the random feature map alone provably separates nearby data points (Proposition A.1), confirming AGOP as the necessary and sufficient mechanism. In standard DNNs, decomposing each fully-connected layer into its right singular structure (SV^T) and the composition of left singular vectors with the non-linearity (φ(U·)) shows that the NC1 ratio drops predominantly between the input and the SV^T-applied embedding, with the non-linearity contributing little to further collapse. The paper contrasts its data-dependent AGOP mechanism with the data-agnostic Unconstrained Features Model (UFM/DUFM), arguing that UFM discards the training data and most of the network, leaving a gap that the AGOP mechanism fills. The NFA correlation values (~0.74) are compared against the trivial correlation bound for random eigenvectors (~0.04 for d=512), demonstrating that the observed alignment is highly non-trivial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the data-agnostic Unconstrained Features Model (UFM/DUFM) that explains DNC as an optimization property of the final-layer features, and the data-dependent AGOP/Neural Feature Ansatz (NFA) framework from Radhakrishnan et al. [2024a] and Beaglehole et al. [2023] that characterizes how neural networks extract features through projection onto the average gradient outer product. By showing that the Deep RFM—a backpropagation-free method that iteratively projects data onto the AGOP and applies random features—exhibits full DNC, the paper provides the first data-dependent mechanism for DNC formation, filling the gap left by UFM which discards training data entirely. The theoretical analyses draw on high-dimensional kernel matrix approximations (Adlam et al., Hu and Lu) and connect DNC to an implicit bias of parametrized kernel ridge regression, linking the phenomenon to the broader kernel learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes AGOP projection as a concrete, data-dependent mechanism for DNC, moving beyond the data-agnostic UFM that has dominated theoretical explanations. The finding that the right singular structure of weight matrices (correlated with AGOP via NFA) is primarily responsible for within-class variability collapse in standard DNNs—while the non-linearity is not—offers a testable decomposition of how individual layers contribute to collapse. However, the NFA correlation is only ~0.74 (not 1.0), the theoretical results require high-dimensional asymptotic assumptions, and DNC2 progression is observed only in the final layer under small initialization, leaving open whether the AGOP mechanism fully explains DNC in all practical regimes (e.g., large initialization, imbalanced data, or non-convex losses). Future work could tighten the NFA-AGOP connection and extend the asymptotic theory to finite-width settings.
