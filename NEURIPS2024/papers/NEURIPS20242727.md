## Problem

While multi-winner elections have been extensively studied for selecting optimal committees, the question of how to transition between two given committees through a sequence of incremental, score-preserving steps had not been formally studied. This paper introduces a multi-winner reconfiguration model that asks whether two size-k committees can be connected via a path of intermediate committees where each step changes at most δ_c alternatives and no committee drops more than δ_s in score, and provides a complete (parameterized) complexity characterization under four approval-based voting rules.

## Input / Output

The input consists of an approval preference profile (m alternatives, n voters each approving a subset), a committee size k, two distinct size-k committees W and W' with sc(W) − sc(W') ≤ δ_s, an adjacency bound δ_c ≥ 1, and a score tolerance δ_s ≥ 0. The output is a yes/no decision on whether a (δ_c, δ_s)-reconfiguration path exists, and when it does, the path itself (a sequence of size-k committees where consecutive committees differ in at most δ_c alternatives and every committee's score is within δ_s of sc(W)). The problem is deliberately not restricted to optimal committees, allowing realistic scenarios where the starting and target committees are merely 'almost' as good.

## Method

The paper establishes a sharp complexity dichotomy: for additive rules AV and SAV, a greedy exchange of the lowest-scoring outgoing alternative with the highest-scoring incoming alternative always yields a valid path of length ⌈|W∖W'|/δ_c⌉ in polynomial time, while for CC and PAV the problem is PSPACE-complete via a reduction from Independent Set Reconfiguration via Token Jumping. The parameterized analysis combines several techniques: brute-force search over the reconfiguration graph gives FPT in m and XP in k; an IPWSPLT (integer programming with simple piecewise-linear transformation) formulation tracking type-level counts across ℓ path steps yields FPT for PAV in n+ℓ; a structural lemma showing that for CC with k ≥ 2n a path always exists (with bounded length) combined with type-based compression gives FPT for CC in n; and a modified reconfiguration graph over only 'relevant' (approved) alternatives gives FPT in n+b for all neutral rules. Hardness results (W[1]-hard for k+ℓ, W[2]-hard for CC) are obtained via reductions from ISR-TJ and a corrected DSR-TJ reduction.

## Results

The paper establishes that multi-winner reconfiguration is PSPACE-complete for both CC and PAV (Theorem 2), while for AV and SAV a reconfiguration path always exists and a shortest one can be found in polynomial time (Theorem 1). For parameterized complexity, the problem is FPT wrt. the number of alternatives m for both CC and PAV, and additionally FPT wrt. n for CC (Theorem 4). Hardness results include W[2]-hardness wrt. k+ℓ for CC, W[1]-hardness wrt. k+ℓ for PAV, and NP-hardness even for constant b+ℓ=4, showing that the problem remains intractable in many natural parameter regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines but instead provides a complete complexity landscape across four voting rules. AV and SAV are trivially solvable (always a path exists) due to their additive scoring structure, while CC and PAV are PSPACE-complete, reflecting the non-additive nature of their scores. Among the intractable rules, CC is strictly harder than PAV in the parameterized setting: CC-MR is W[2]-hard wrt. k+ℓ while PAV-MR is only W[1]-hard, and CC-MR is FPT wrt. n while PAV-MR's FPT status wrt. n alone remains open. Combining n with any other single parameter (b, k, or ℓ) always yields an FPT algorithm for both CC and PAV, making n the most powerful parameter for tractability. The NP-hardness for constant b+ℓ (Proposition 2) shows that even with very restricted ballots and short paths, the problem remains computationally difficult.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces reconfiguration as a new lens on multi-winner elections, building on the well-established reconfiguration framework from graph theory and combinatorics (Ito et al. 2011; Nishimura 2018) and the multi-winner voting literature (Faliszewski et al. 2017; Lackner and Skowron 2023). The hardness proofs adapt reductions from independent set and dominating set reconfiguration (Ito et al. 2014; Mouawad et al. 2016) via the Aziz et al. (2015) construction for CC/PAV, while the key FPT algorithm for PAV extends the IPWSPLT integer programming formulation of Yang and Wang (2023). The paper also corrects an error in Bodlaender et al. (2021) regarding the W[2]-hardness of dominating set reconfiguration under token jumping, providing a direct reduction instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean complexity dichotomy: additive scoring rules (AV, SAV) always admit a reconfiguration path solvable in polynomial time, while non-additive rules (CC, PAV) are PSPACE-complete, with a detailed parameterized landscape identifying when efficient algorithms exist. The open question of whether PAV-MR is FPT in the number of voters alone, combined with the preliminary observation that random instances typically have short paths, points toward structural properties that could yield practical algorithms. The framework is directly applicable to real-world scenarios like streaming catalog updates and retail shelf management, and naturally extends to other social choice settings such as stable matching and coalition formation.
