## Problem

Active learning for video captioning has largely ignored the learnability problem caused by collective outliers—samples with inconsistent human annotations that are disproportionately hard to learn. While uncertainty and diversity have been exploited for sample selection, the ground truth inconsistency (in both abstraction level and granularity) that makes certain videos unlearnable at early training stages has been neglected, leading to wasted human annotation effort on samples the model cannot effectively learn from.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabelled video sequences (uniformly sampled at 32 frames) and a small initial labelled set (5% of training data) as input. BLIP2 generates per-frame captions on all 32 frames of each unlabelled video, while the video captioning model (SwinBERT or CoCap) produces a highest-confidence video-level caption. The output is a ranked list of unlabelled videos scored by a composite learnability-diversity-uncertainty metric, from which a caption-wise protocol acquires at most 4 human-annotated captions per selected video per step (rather than all 20 or 40 captions), with a stage-wise budget allocation that gives more captions to top-ranked (more learnable) videos.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-term active learning scoring function L_n = -λ₁L_n¹ + λ₂L_n² - λ₃L_n³ + L_n⁴ that explicitly incorporates learnability alongside diversity and uncertainty. Learnability is decomposed into two sub-terms: L_n¹ measures abstraction consistency by computing the average frequency of shared objects and attributes across per-frame BLIP2 scene graphs (higher = more consistent = more learnable), and L_n² captures granularity inconsistency via time-variant SPICE changes between the video model's prediction and BLIP2 frame predictions, preferring samples with moderate change. Diversity (L_n³) uses a TF-IDF formulation over scene graph objects and attributes, restricted to content absent from the current model's predictions, while uncertainty (L_n⁴) counts the overlap between the video model's highest-confidence scene graph and the BLIP2 frame scene graphs. A novel caption-wise selection protocol replaces the conventional video-wise protocol, acquiring at most 4 captions per selected video per step with a re-ranking factor that prioritizes less-annotated videos and a stage-wise budget allocation (R regions with decreasing per-video caption counts) that concentrates human effort on the most learnable samples.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, the proposed method achieves 103% of full performance on CIDEr with only 25% of human annotations, and surpasses full performance on BLEU4 (~10% annotations) and ROUGE-L (~15% annotations). On MSVD, it reaches 103.65% of full CIDEr performance at 25% annotations. With the CoCap backbone, the method still achieves over 95% of full performance across all metrics at 25% annotations, demonstrating robustness across model architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines from Chan et al. (2020) — Maximum Entropy, Minimum Likelihood, and Clustered-Divergence — across all five evaluation metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, SPICE) on both MSVD and MSR-VTT with both SwinBERT and CoCap backbones. It also surpasses the Core-Set Selection baseline of Sener and Savarese (2018), which is a diversity-based method operating in feature space. Random sampling, as expected in open-ended tasks, is typically the second-best method, but the proposed approach leads it by a substantial margin (e.g., CIDEr 55.74 vs. 50.20 at 25% annotations on MSR-VTT). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that each component contributes: adding the caption-wise protocol alone boosts AUC-CIDEr from 0.603 to 0.738, indicating that the protocol's reduction of collective outliers is the single largest performance driver.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active learning for video captioning (Chan et al., ACCV 2020) by introducing learnability as a first-class selection criterion, motivated by the collective outliers phenomenon identified in VQA (Karamcheti et al., ACL 2021). It adapts Dataset Maps (Swayamdipta et al., EMNLP 2020) to the video captioning setting by substituting SPICE for confidence scores, and leverages image-based LVLMs (BLIP2) to approximate human annotations for measuring abstraction and granularity inconsistency. The key departure from prior work is the caption-wise selection protocol, which breaks from the conventional video-wise paradigm of acquiring all annotations for a selected video, and the explicit use of scene-graph-based consistency metrics (inspired by SPICE) to operationalize learnability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that human annotation inconsistency (collective outliers) is a major bottleneck in video captioning training, and that reducing annotations per video via a caption-wise protocol actually improves performance, challenges the implicit assumption that more human labels per video are always beneficial. The 103% full-performance result with 25% annotations is practically significant for annotation-cost-sensitive deployments. Open questions include whether video-based LVLMs (as they mature) would further improve the learnability estimates, whether the approach generalizes to other open-ended generation tasks, and whether the semi-supervised integration of LVLM predictions (which underperformed here) can be made effective with domain adaptation. The reliance on image-based BLIP2 and the limited dataset coverage (MSVD, MSR-VTT) constrain the generality of the conclusions.
