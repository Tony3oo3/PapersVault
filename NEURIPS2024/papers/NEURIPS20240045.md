## Problem

AI-based wildfire forecast and backtracking models require large-scale, temporally complete training data, but real-world satellite observations are intermittent and expensive to collect. Simulators can generate data cheaply, yet they introduce simulation errors that create a Sim2Real gap between synthetic and real fire patterns — a challenge previously unaddressed in the fire spread analysis community. This paper tackles whether simulation data can effectively train AI models that generalize to real-world wildfire scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240045/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a source sequence of T binary fire-area masks (H×W×T) alongside five environmental modalities: satellite image sequences at 30 m resolution (Landsat-8/Sentinel-2), topography/vegetation/fuel maps (H×W×C), and hourly tabular weather data (C×T). It outputs a target sequence of T binary fire-area masks representing future (forecast) or past (backtracking) fire states. Crucially, the target timestamps need not be contiguous, enabling the model to handle the temporally incomplete sequences (2–10 frames) typical of real-world satellite observations, whereas simulation sequences contain ~100 frames.

## Method

S2R-FireTr is a Swin-Transformer-based architecture with two modules. The first, Environment-guided Area Representation Learning (EARL), computes a source area representation via dual cross-attention: the query is derived from the source fire masks through a SwinEncoder, while two separate key/value pairs are computed from spatial modalities (topography, vegetation, fuel, satellite images) and temporal modalities (satellite sequence, weather via multi-head attention), fusing correlations from both perspectives. The second, Time-wise Fire Area Regression (TFAR), uses a cross-attention where positional-encoded target timestamps serve as queries and the source area representation serves as keys/values, then decodes the target masks via a SwinDecoder. Training is supervised with an L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth masks. The key insight is that the dual cross-attention anchors fire-area representations to multi-modal environmental correlations that remain consistent between simulation and reality, thereby mitigating the Sim2Real gap during training on purely synthetic data.

## Results

On the Sim2Real-Fire dataset, S2R-FireTr achieves 72.9% AUPRC, 69.6% F1, and 56.4% IOU for wildfire spread forecast on real-world data, and 63.9% AUPRC, 60.3% F1, and 46.9% IOU for backtracking. On simulation data, it reaches 87.3% AUPRC (forecast) and 78.6% AUPRC (backtracking). Compared to the best AI baseline (Rainformer), S2R-FireTr improves real-world forecast AUPRC by 5.7 points (72.9 vs. 67.2) and real-world backtracking AUPRC by 9.3 points (63.9 vs. 54.6). Against the best physical simulator (WRF-SFIRE, 63.0% AUPRC), S2R-FireTr gains 9.9 points on real-world forecast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2R-FireTr outperforms all nine AI baselines on every metric for both forecast and backtracking on real-world data. The closest competitor, Rainformer, achieves 67.2% AUPRC on real-world forecast versus S2R-FireTr's 72.9% (a 5.7-point gap), and 54.6% AUPRC on backtracking versus 63.9% (a 9.3-point gap). On simulation data, the gap narrows slightly: S2R-FireTr leads Rainformer by 7.6 points on forecast AUPRC (87.3 vs. 79.7) and 5.3 points on backtracking AUPRC (78.6 vs. 73.3). Against the three physical/empirical simulators (FARSITE 55.9%, WFDS 61.2%, WRF-SFIRE 63.0% AUPRC on real-world forecast), S2R-FireTr gains 7–17 points, with the largest margin over FARSITE. The ablation (Table 7) confirms that both the Environment-guided Area Representation Learning and Time-wise Fire Area Regression modules contribute substantially: removing both drops real-world forecast AUPRC from 72.9% to 60.2% and backtracking AUPRC from 63.9% to 42.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) empirical and physical wildfire simulators (FARSITE, WFDS, WRF-SFIRE) that generate fire spread sequences from environmental inputs, (2) deep learning models for spatiotemporal prediction (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, Earthformer, SwinLSTM) adapted to wildfire forecast and backtracking, and (3) the Sim2Real transfer paradigm borrowed from robotics and autonomous driving. The key conceptual departure is explicitly framing wildfire prediction as a Sim2Real problem—training AI models on large-scale simulator output and evaluating on real satellite observations—which the authors identify as 'a brand-new topic in the fire spread analysis research community.' Architecturally, S2R-FireTr repurposes Swin Transformer encoders/decoders and dual cross-attention (inspired by semantic segmentation) to fuse five environmental modalities, but the novelty lies in the training-on-simulation, testing-on-reality protocol rather than in any single architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sim2Real-Fire dataset (1M simulated + 1K real scenarios across five modalities) is the first public resource enabling systematic study of the simulation-to-reality gap in wildfire AI, addressing the practical bottleneck of expensive, temporally incomplete satellite data collection. The finding that a model trained purely on simulation data can outperform all physical simulators on real-world forecast (72.9 vs. 63.0 AUPRC for WRF-SFIRE) validates the Sim2Real approach as a viable path to scalable wildfire prediction. However, the dataset's closed nature (limited countries and 2013–2023 period) constrains generalization, and the authors acknowledge the need for dynamic data acquisition to make the resource truly open. Open questions include whether the Sim2Real gap narrows with more diverse simulation environments, how the approach transfers to regions with different fuel types and climate regimes, and whether the 3-frame optimal sequence length generalizes to longer prediction horizons.
