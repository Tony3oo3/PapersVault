## Problem

LLM-based agents in communication games like Werewolf leverage reasoning and text generation but lack explicit control over discussion tactics (e.g., when to be honest vs. deceptive), which are essential for strategic play. One Night Ultimate Werewolf (ONUW) amplifies this need because role-switching abilities create uncertainty that makes belief manipulation through discussion pivotal. The paper provides both a theoretical foundation (PBE analysis showing utilities are determined solely by beliefs shaped by discussion) and a practical framework to close this gap.

## Input / Output

The discussion policy takes as input the concatenation of the visible discussion history and the LLM-derived belief over all players' roles, both in natural language, which is converted to a fixed 1536-dimensional state embedding via text-embedding-ada-002. The policy outputs one of six discrete discussion tactics (Honest/Deceptive × Evidence/Accusation/Defense). This tactic then conditions a separate LLM call that generates the actual speech or voting action, so the final output is free-form text or a vote, not the tactic itself.

## Method

The paper formalizes ONUW as a Multi-Phase Extensive-Form Bayesian Game and proves the existence of Perfect Bayesian Equilibria in a three-player setting, demonstrating that players' utilities are determined entirely by their beliefs—which discussion directly shapes. Building on this insight, the proposed RL-instructed LLM-based agent framework introduces a latent discussion-tactic variable z that conditions the behavioral strategy, separating the strategic decision (which tactic to use) from the linguistic execution (what to say). A discussion policy μ is trained via offline RL (Conservative Q-Learning) on 1,800 transitions extracted from GPT-4-generated game logs, where the state is the embedding of (history, belief), the action is one of six tactics, and the reward is the terminal game outcome (±1 or 0). At inference time, the agent uses three components in sequence: an LLM forms beliefs, the RL-trained policy selects a tactic, and a second LLM call generates the speech or vote conditioned on both the belief and the chosen tactic.

## Results

In the five-player ONUW generalizability experiment (Table 1), the RL-instructed agent achieves a 0.70 win rate against Gemini-based Belief opponents and 0.50 against GPT-4-based Belief opponents, while also maintaining the lowest average votes (1.10 and 1.87 respectively). In the three-player ONUW equilibrium approximation experiment, the RL-instructed agent achieves the lowest NashConv values among all agent variants, indicating closest proximity to the theoretical PBEs. In the five-player effectiveness experiment (Figure 5), the RL-instructed agent consistently achieves the highest Team Village win rates in both easy and hard settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the RL-instructed agent against four baselines: ReAct (direct LLM prompting), Belief (LLM with belief modeling but no discussion policy), Random (random discussion tactic selection), and LLM-instructed (LLM autonomously selects the discussion tactic). The RL-instructed agent outperforms all baselines in both opponent settings: against Gemini opponents it achieves 0.70 win rate vs. 0.62 (LLM-instructed), 0.40 (ReAct/Belief), and 0.37 (Random); against GPT-4 opponents it achieves 0.50 vs. 0.37 (LLM-instructed), 0.32 (Belief/Random), and 0.30 (ReAct). The RL-trained policy also yields fewer average votes received (1.10 vs. 1.23–1.73 for Gemini opponents), indicating better concealment. In the three-player NashConv experiment, the RL-instructed agent achieves lower NashConv values than both LLM-instructed and Belief agents, confirming that RL-optimized discussion tactics bring agents closer to theoretical equilibria than LLM-prompted or no-policy approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based agents for communication games (Werewolf, Diplomacy, Avalon) by introducing an explicit, RL-trained discussion policy that selects from a discrete set of tactics (honest/deceptive × evidence/accusation/defense), rather than relying on the LLM to implicitly decide its communicative strategy. It builds on CQL for offline RL training of the tactic-selection module and on ChatArena as the multi-agent game environment. The theoretical contribution—formulating ONUW as a Multi-Phase Extensive-Form Bayesian Game and deriving PBEs—departs from prior Werewolf analyses that relied on strong simplifying assumptions (random lynchings, meaningless debate) by working within the full game structure with role-switching uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PBE analysis reveals that discussion fundamentally reshapes game equilibria: in the three-player case, the Robber's utility is determined entirely by the Werewolves' beliefs about its night action, making strategic communication the sole lever for utility maximization. This provides a principled justification for separating 'what to say' (tactic selection via RL) from 'how to say it' (LLM generation). A key limitation is the manual discretization of six discussion tactics, which is game-specific and may not transfer to other communication games; the authors identify automatic, unsupervised extraction of tactics as an important open direction. The moderate performance gains (e.g., 0.70 vs. 0.40 win rate in the generalizability experiment) suggest the approach is effective but not transformative.
