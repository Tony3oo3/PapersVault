## Problem

Large text-to-image models like SDXL demand expensive high-memory GPUs for inference, making them inaccessible to researchers and users in resource-constrained environments. Prior architectural compression methods (e.g., BK-SDM) achieve limited compression rates on complex U-Nets like SDXL's, and the feature-level distillation strategy for U-Net-based diffusion models has not been sufficiently explored. The paper asks how far T2I generation quality can be pushed using only publicly available datasets and open-source models while substantially reducing model size and inference cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image at 512×512 (KOALA-Turbo) or 1024×1024 (KOALA-Lightning) resolution. Inference uses 10 denoising steps with FP16 precision, an Euler discrete scheduler, and a classifier-free guidance scale of 3.5. A key practical constraint is that KOALA-Lightning-700M requires only ~8.3 GB of GPU memory, enabling 1024px generation on consumer-grade 8 GB GPUs (e.g., RTX 3060 Ti), whereas SDXL models cannot be loaded on such hardware at all.

## Method

KOALA compresses SDXL's 2.56B U-Net into two variants (1.16B and 782M parameters) by combining block removal with a novel layer-wise removal strategy that reduces transformer depth from 10 to 5–6 layers at the lowest-resolution stages where 83% of parameters are concentrated. The key distillation insight is that self-attention (SA) features within transformer blocks are far more informative for feature-level knowledge distillation than the last-layer features used by prior work (BK-SDM), because SA features encode discriminative object-level representations that prevent appearance leakage between objects. Training optimizes a combined objective of the denoising task loss, output-level KD loss, and SA-based feature-level KD loss, with SA-bottom (consecutive early transformer layers) selected as the distillation location. The paper further shows that high-resolution images with long captions (LAION-POP, 491K samples) outperform larger low-resolution datasets, and that step-distilled teachers (SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning) allow the student to inherit few-step generation capability without architectural modifications.

## Results

KOALA-Lightning-700M (0.78B U-Net) generates 1024² images in 0.655s with 10 denoising steps, achieving HPSv2 of 31.50 and CompBench of 0.4505, while consuming only 8.3GB of GPU memory. This is approximately 5× faster than SDXL-Base-1.0 (3.229s, 11.9GB) with a 69% smaller U-Net, and 4× faster than SDXL-Base while matching or exceeding its HPSv2 (31.50 vs. 30.82) and CompBench (0.4505 vs. 0.4445). KOALA-Turbo-700M generates 512² images in just 0.194s with 4.9GB memory, outperforming SDXL-Turbo (0.245s, 8.5GB) on CompBench (0.4555 vs. 0.4489). Critically, KOALA models run on 8GB consumer GPUs (e.g., 3060Ti) where SDXL models cannot be loaded at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL-Base-1.0, KOALA-Lightning-700M is ~5× faster (0.655s vs. 3.229s) with a 69% smaller U-Net and 30% less memory, while slightly improving both HPSv2 (31.50 vs. 30.82) and CompBench (0.4505 vs. 0.4445). Against SDXL-Lightning, KOALA-Lightning-700M is marginally faster (0.655s vs. 0.719s) with a 3× smaller model and 29% less memory, trading a small HPSv2 drop (31.50 vs. 32.18) for a CompBench gain (0.4505 vs. 0.4445). Against SSD-1B (BK method), KOALA-Lightning-700M is ~3× faster (0.655s vs. 2.094s) with comparable HPSv2 (31.50 vs. 31.43) and slightly better CompBench (0.4505 vs. 0.4497); against SSD-Vega, KOALA is ~2.3× faster but trails on HPSv2 (31.50 vs. 32.17) while leading on CompBench (0.4505 vs. 0.4461). Against Pixart-Σ, KOALA-Lightning-700M is ~6× faster (0.655s vs. 3.976s) with 2× less memory (8.3GB vs. 17.3GB), but trails on both HPSv2 (31.50 vs. 31.75) and CompBench (0.4505 vs. 0.4612), which the authors attribute to Pixart-Σ's access to 33M proprietary high-resolution images with 184-word captions. In the direct KD-method comparison (Table 7), the proposed self-attention distillation consistently outperforms BK-SDM's last-feature distillation on both HPSv2 and CompBench across both BK-Small and KOALA-1B backbones, and the same advantage holds when applied to a Diffusion Transformer (Pixart-Σ) backbone (Table 8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the architectural compression line initiated by BK-SDM (Kim et al., 2023), which compressed Stable Diffusion U-Nets via block removal and last-feature distillation. KOALA departs from BK-SDM in two key ways: it introduces layer-wise removal (reducing transformer depth within blocks rather than removing entire blocks) to achieve substantially higher compression rates (54–69% vs. 33%), and it replaces last-feature distillation with self-attention-based feature distillation, which the authors show transfers more discriminative object representations. The work also builds on step-distilled teachers (SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning) to inherit few-step generation capability, and validates the self-attention distillation principle on a Diffusion Transformer backbone (Pixart-Σ) to demonstrate generality beyond U-Nets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: KOALA-Lightning-700M generates 1024px images in ~0.65s on a 4090 and, critically, runs on 8GB consumer GPUs (3060Ti) where SDXL cannot be mounted at all, opening T2I synthesis to a much broader user base. The finding that self-attention features are the most informative distillation target (validated on both U-Net and DiT architectures) provides a reusable principle for future compression work. Limitations remain: the compression strategy is heuristic and specific to SDXL's heterogeneous conv+transformer architecture, and the models still struggle with legible text rendering, human hands, and complex multi-attribute compositional prompts. An open question is whether a more general, architecture-agnostic compression principle can be derived from the self-attention distillation insight.
