## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves fast, high-quality rendering but its discrete, sparse, and off-surface-drifting Gaussians make it difficult to infer continuous signed distance functions for accurate surface reconstruction. Existing work either adds a separate volume-rendering branch, relies on monocular depth/normal priors, or forces surface-aligned Gaussians, but none seamlessly unifies 3DGS with neural SDF learning under multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243230/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images (no depth, normal, or point-cloud supervision) and jointly learns a set of 3D Gaussians and an 8-layer MLP-based neural SDF (256 hidden units, ReLU, initialized as a sphere). The SDF is queried at resolution 800 and converted to a mesh via marching cubes; for large-scale scenes the domain is split into sub-regions. Rendered RGB images are also produced as an intermediate supervision signal during training.

## Method

The core mechanism is a differentiable pulling operation that projects each 3D Gaussian center onto the zero-level set of the neural SDF (using the predicted signed distance and gradient), after which the pulled Gaussians are rendered via standard 3DGS differentiable rasterization. In the reverse direction, randomly sampled query points are pulled onto the *disk plane* of their nearest pulled Gaussian (not just its center), which the authors show provides quadratic loss decay and higher tolerance to sparse, non-uniform Gaussian distributions. Three auxiliary losses regularize the process: a thin-disk loss flattens each Gaussian, a tangent loss aligns the Gaussian normal with the SDF gradient at the pulled center, and an orthogonal loss constrains the pulling direction to be perpendicular to the disk. The full objective jointly optimizes Gaussian attributes and SDF network parameters with an L1+D-SSIM rendering loss plus the three geometry constraints, with pulling and constraints activated after 7k of 15k total iterations.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark, the method achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.75, outperforming all 3DGS-based baselines (2DGS: 0.80, GSurfels: 0.88, SuGaR: 1.33) and the NeRF-based NeuS (0.84), while training in 21.8 minutes. On Tanks and Temples, it achieves a mean F-score of 0.43, surpassing NeuS (0.38) which requires ~12 hours, and all Gaussian-based methods (2DGS: 0.32, GSurfels: 0.21). On Mip-NeRF 360, rendering quality remains competitive with 3DGS (PSNR 30.78 vs. 30.99 indoor), confirming that geometric constraints do not compromise photometric fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five 3DGS-based surface reconstruction baselines on DTU in terms of Chamfer Distance: it beats 2DGS (0.75 vs. 0.80), GSurfels (0.75 vs. 0.88), SuGaR (0.75 vs. 1.33), DN-Splatter (0.75 vs. 1.70), and raw 3DGS (0.75 vs. 1.96). On Tanks and Temples, it achieves the highest F-score (0.43) among all methods, surpassing even the NeRF-based NeuS (0.38) which takes ~12 hours to train, while completing in 37.6 minutes. On Mip-NeRF 360 rendering, the method is slightly behind 3DGS (PSNR 30.78 vs. 30.99 indoor) and 2DGS (30.39) but matches or exceeds them on LPIPS (0.182 vs. 0.199/0.183), indicating that the geometric pulling constraints preserve rendering quality. The key advantage over 2DGS is that the 3DGS-based rasterization kernel scales better with increasing Gaussian counts in large scenes, making training faster on TNT despite comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for differentiable rasterization and on Neural-Pull for differentiable SDF learning, but departs from both in a key way: rather than treating 3D Gaussians and the SDF as separate branches (as in GSDF, 3DGSR) or using surface-aligned Gaussians as a rendering trick (as in 2DGS, SuGaR, GaussianSurfels), it dynamically pulls Gaussians onto the SDF's zero-level set during rendering and pulls query points onto Gaussian *disks* (2D targets) rather than Gaussian *centers* (0D targets) to refine the SDF. The disk-pulling mechanism is the conceptual departure from Neural-Pull, motivated by the observation that 3D Gaussians are sparse and non-uniformly distributed, making point-target pulling overfit to individual centers. The method also inherits the thin-disk regularization from NeuSG and the tangent-plane alignment idea from surface-aligned Gaussian work, but integrates them into a single joint optimization loop rather than a multi-branch pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-branch formulation (no separate NeRF or SDF branch) simplifies the training pipeline and avoids the mutual-supervision tuning that dual-branch methods require, while the disk-pulling concept provides a principled way to bridge the gap between discrete sparse Gaussians and continuous implicit fields. The notably strong TNT results (0.43 F-score vs. 0.32 for 2DGS) suggest the approach scales better to large scenes where Gaussian count grows rapidly. However, the method inherits 3DGS limitations (no transparency, specular issues), struggles in extremely sparse regions, and the MLP-based SDF cannot capture high-frequency details—open questions include whether hybrid representations (e.g., BACON, GridPull) or TSDF/Poisson post-processing could recover those details without sacrificing the smoothness that makes the pulling operation work.
