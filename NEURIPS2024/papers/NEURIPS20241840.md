## Problem

While VLMs are known to be vulnerable to test-time jailbreaking attacks, no prior work had demonstrated training-time data poisoning attacks that manipulate VLM responses to everyday, benign prompts. This paper is the first to show that VLMs can be poisoned with stealthy, clean-label samples to produce not only misclassifications (Label Attack) but also coherent, persuasive misinformation narratives (Persuasion Attack), affecting the vast majority of users who interact with these models innocently.

## Input / Output

The attack pipeline takes clean images from both an original concept C_o and a destination concept C_d (e.g., photos of Donald Trump and Joe Biden), along with a text prompt at inference time. It produces stealthy poison image/text pairs where the image is a destination-concept image with an imperceptible L-infinity perturbation (ε=8/255) and the text is a GPT-3.5-turbo-paraphrased caption that explicitly emphasizes the destination concept. At test time, the poisoned VLM receives an original-concept image and a benign prompt (e.g., 'Who is this person?') and outputs a free-form textual response that misleadingly aligns with the destination concept.

## Method

Shadowcast constructs stealthy poison samples through the synergy of two components. For text, it first captions destination-concept images using an off-the-shelf VLM, then paraphrases the captions with GPT-3.5-turbo under instructions that explicitly emphasize the destination concept (a class label for Label Attack, or an elaborate narrative for Persuasion Attack), ensuring both visual consistency with the image and conceptual consistency with the attack goal. For images, it applies projected gradient descent to minimize the L2 distance between the vision encoder's features of the perturbed image and those of a paired original-concept image, subject to an L-infinity perturbation budget of 8/255, making the poison image visually indistinguishable from the clean destination image while being close to the original concept in latent space. During visual instruction tuning, the VLM learns to associate the poison image's representation with the destination-concept text, and because the poison image is latent-space-close to original-concept images, the model generalizes this association to unseen original-concept images at test time. The attack is effective with as few as 50 poison samples (under 1.4% poison rate) and transfers across different VLM architectures in a black-box setting.

## Results

Shadowcast achieves over 60% attack success rate with fewer than 30 poison samples (<1% poison rate) on Label Attack tasks, reaching >95% success on Trump-to-Biden and >80% on EngineLight-to-FuelLight with just 50 poison samples (1.4% poison rate). For Persuasion Attack, the attack effectively shifts model responses even when the clean model initially held a neutral position (~50% baseline for VideoGame-to-PhysicalHealth). Critically, the poisoned models preserve utility: GQA scores remain within ~0.3% of the clean model (e.g., 59.48–59.81% vs. 59.72% clean) and VizWiz scores stay within ~1% (55.15–56.66% vs. 56.28% clean) across all tasks and poison rates up to 5.71%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As an attack paper, Shadowcast does not benchmark against competing attack methods but instead compares the poisoned model against the clean model and across different attack configurations. The clean model serves as the utility baseline: poisoned models maintain GQA scores within 0.3% and VizWiz within 1% of clean performance across all four tasks, confirming the attack is targeted rather than destructive. For transferability (black-box setting), poison samples crafted with InstructBLIP or MiniGPT-v2 still achieve meaningful attack success rates on LLaVA-1.5, though overall effectiveness drops compared to the grey-box setting where the target model's own vision encoder is used. Under data augmentation (random resize and cropping), Shadowcast remains effective even when the attacker does not apply augmentation during poison crafting, and performance improves when the attacker matches the training augmentation. Against JPEG compression, the attack maintains effectiveness in 3 of 4 tasks, with a differentiable JPEG surrogate further improving success rates in most scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the clean-label poisoning paradigm from image classifiers (Shafahi et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2019) to vision-language models, adapting the latent-space adversarial perturbation objective to the VLM vision encoder. It departs from prior multimodal poisoning work (Yang et al., 2023; Carlini and Terzis, 2022) by requiring visually congruent image/text pairs (clean-label) rather than mismatched pairs (dirty-label), and from LLM poisoning (Shu et al., 2023) by exploiting the visual modality to make poison samples undetectable by human inspection. The novel Persuasion Attack sub-task repurposes the VLM's text generation capability to produce coherent, persuasive misinformation narratives, going beyond the misclassification objective of traditional poisoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies a previously unexplored vulnerability class for VLMs: training-time poisoning that manipulates responses to everyday benign prompts, affecting the vast majority of users rather than only adversarial ones. The Persuasion Attack in particular raises the stakes by showing poisoned VLMs can generate coherent, human-rated-convincing misinformation (e.g., junk food described as healthy), which is far more insidious than simple label confusion. The paper explicitly acknowledges it does not explore defenses, leaving open the critical question of how to detect or mitigate such attacks in the visual instruction tuning pipeline. Transferability across architectures (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-v2 → LLaVA-1.5) suggests the threat extends to black-box settings, though the scope is limited to 7B models and four specific tasks.
