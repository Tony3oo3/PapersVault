## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D object detection methods depend on expensive LiDAR or 3D sensor data for both training and deployment, limiting scalability and real-world applicability. While monocular 3D detectors eliminate the need for 3D sensors at inference, their training still requires paired LiDAR annotations, preventing exploitation of the vast pool of unlabeled RGB images available online. This paper asks whether open-vocabulary monocular 3D detection can be trained entirely from RGB images without any 3D sensor data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and a free-form text prompt listing target object categories, and outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) with open-vocabulary category assignments via text-embedding alignment. At training, the pipeline consumes only RGB images: Grounded-SAM produces 2D masks and category labels, and Unidepth predicts per-pixel metric depth to unproject masks into pseudo-LiDAR point clouds. No LiDAR, depth sensor, or manual 3D annotation is required at any stage, and the depth estimator is used zero-shot with no overlap between its training data and the evaluation datasets.

## Method

The framework (OVM3D-Det) auto-generates 3D pseudo-labels from RGB images in five steps: (1) Grounded-SAM detects and segments novel objects; (2) Unidepth estimates depth and unprojects masks into per-instance pseudo-LiDAR; (3) an adaptive erosion operation removes boundary noise, with the number of erosion iterations scaled by mask area to avoid destroying small objects or leaving noise on large ones; (4) a 3D bounding box is fitted to the cleaned point cloud using PCA for orientation, then validated against category dimension priors obtained from GPT-4—if the box falls outside a [τ₁, τ₂]× prior range, a search over 8 candidate boxes (proposed from each corner of the coarse box) selects the best fit using a combined ray-tracing loss and point-ratio loss; (5) a Cube R-CNN backbone (DLA34-FPN) with a text-alignment head replacing the classification branch is trained on the pseudo labels. The key insight is that the two refinement stages—adaptive erosion and LLM-guided box search—compensate for the inherent noise and occlusion artifacts of pseudo-LiDAR, yielding pseudo-labels accurate enough to train a competitive open-vocabulary detector without any 3D ground truth.

## Results

On KITTI, OVM3D-Det (fully unsupervised, no GT labels) achieves 8.2% AP on novel classes, outperforming the Cube R-CNN + Grounding DINO baseline (1.5% AP) by +6.7% AP. On nuScenes, it reaches 11.8% AP on novel classes (+9.7% over baseline). On indoor datasets, OVM3D-Det achieves 10.0% AP on SUN RGB-D novel classes (+8.5%) and 19.7% AP on ARKitScenes novel classes (+16.8%). When augmented with GT base-class labels (OVM3D-Det*), the gains are even larger: 9.3% AP on KITTI novel, 11.7% on nuScenes novel, 9.7% on SUN RGB-D novel, and 21.3% on ARKitScenes novel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary open-vocabulary baseline (Cube R-CNN + Grounding DINO, which uses GT base-class annotations for training), OVM3D-Det wins decisively on novel-class AP across all four datasets: +6.7% on KITTI (8.2 vs. 1.5), +9.7% on nuScenes (11.8 vs. 2.1), +8.5% on SUN RGB-D (10.0 vs. 1.5), and +16.8% on ARKitScenes (19.7 vs. 2.9). Compared to the fully supervised Oracle Cube R-CNN, OVM3D-Det trades base-class performance (e.g., 33.9 vs. 46.3 AP_B on KITTI) for competitive novel-class performance without any ground-truth labels. Against point-cloud-based open-vocabulary methods, OVM3D-Det achieves 10.8% AP on SUN RGB-D versus 7.7% for CODA and 7.1% for OV-3DET trained on pseudo-LiDAR, and 18.5% AP on KITTI versus only 1.3% for OV-3DET, demonstrating that image-based pseudo-labeling with the proposed erosion and box-refinement pipeline substantially outperforms directly feeding noisy pseudo-LiDAR to point-cloud detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Cube R-CNN as its base monocular 3D detector and borrows the ray tracing loss and orientation estimation from WeakM3D, while adopting the pseudo-LiDAR generation paradigm from Wang et al. (CVPR 2019). It departs fundamentally from prior open-vocabulary 3D detection work (OV-3DET, CODA), which all require LiDAR point clouds as input, and from weakly-supervised monocular 3D detection, which still needs raw LiDAR data for training. The key conceptual leap is combining open-vocabulary 2D detection (Grounded-SAM), zero-shot depth estimation (Unidepth), and LLM-derived object size priors (GPT-4) into a fully RGB-only auto-labeling pipeline, eliminating all 3D sensor dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for LiDAR or 3D annotations entirely, this work opens the door to training open-vocabulary 3D detectors on internet-scale RGB data, which is orders of magnitude larger than any existing 3D-annotated dataset. The demonstrated scaling trend (Fig. 6) suggests substantial further gains are possible with more data. However, absolute performance remains well below oracle (18.5% vs 21.6% AP on KITTI), and the method is limited by depth estimation errors for distant objects and adverse conditions. Open questions include whether improved depth estimators will close the gap, how the approach handles dynamic scenes, and whether the LLM-prior strategy generalizes to truly novel categories not in any training distribution.
