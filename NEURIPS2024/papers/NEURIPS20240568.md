## Problem

Standard graphical model estimation (Gaussian, Covariance, and Ising) can produce biased structures when data spans sensitive groups, yet fairness research in machine learning has focused almost exclusively on supervised settings. This paper addresses the gap by formulating fair estimation of sparse graphical models in the unsupervised regime, where no labels exist and the goal is to learn a single graph that does not disproportionately favor any protected subgroup.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{N×P} partitioned into K sensitive subgroups, a regularization parameter λ, and requires pre-computing K group-specific optimal graph estimates Θ_k* (solved independently via standard GLasso/CovGraph/BinNet) as initialization. The output is a single fair global graph Θ ∈ R^{P×P} (symmetric, positive definite for Gaussian/Covariance models) whose sparsity pattern encodes the conditional or marginal independence structure. Unlike group-specific models, the output is one unified graph constrained to balance disparity errors across all K groups simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is a nonsmooth multi-objective optimization framework with M = K+1 objectives: the first is the standard GM loss plus ℓ₁ regularization, and the remaining K objectives are pairwise graph disparity errors Δ_k(Θ) that penalize the squared (or exponential) difference in loss between the global model and each group's optimal local model. The problem is solved via a proximal gradient method where each iteration solves a subproblem over the convex constraint set M; this subproblem is reformulated through Sion's minimax theorem into a dual simplex-constrained optimization over weights ρ ∈ C, yielding an update rule Θ^{(t+1)} = η_{λ/ℓ}(Θ^{(t)} − (1/ℓ)Σ_k ρ_k ∇f_k(Θ^{(t)})) that combines soft thresholding with a weighted gradient descent direction. For CovGraph and BinNet, additional convex regularization terms γ_C‖Σ‖_F² and γ_I‖Θ‖_F² are incorporated into the disparity objectives to guarantee convexity. The paper proves O(1/t) convergence to a Pareto stationary point for all three model classes (Theorems 6–8), establishing the first multi-objective proximal gradient method for graphical model estimation.

## Results

Across all six experimental settings (three simulation types and three real-world datasets), Fair GMs reduce the pairwise graph disparity error (Δ) by 53.76%–100% relative to standard GMs, while incurring at most a 1.15% degradation in the objective function value (F₁). On the TCGA gene regulatory network, Δ drops from 2.5875 to 0.0742 (+97.13% fairness gain) with only a 0.11% increase in F₁. On the Credit Dataset, Fair CovGraph achieves a 53.76% fairness improvement with a mere 0.42% objective increase. The LFM-1b music recommendation experiment shows Fair BinNet actually improves F₁ by 0.45% while reducing Δ by 96.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard (unfair) graphical model estimated via ISTA (Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm), which corresponds to the single-objective case (M=1) of the proposed framework. Fair GMs consistently outperform the standard baseline on the fairness metric (Δ) across all six settings, reducing disparity error by 53.76% (Credit Dataset, CovGraph) to 100% (Simulation, BinNet). The accuracy cost is negligible: F₁ degrades by at most 1.15% (Simulation CovGraph) and actually improves by 0.45% on LFM-1b. In terms of edge recovery (PCEE, Table 1), Fair GLasso reduces the inter-group PCEE gap from 0.0987 to 0.0569, and Fair BinNet reduces it from 0.4836 to 0.0566, confirming that the fairness gain does not come at the expense of structural accuracy for either group. The main trade-off is computational: Fair GMs require 10–250× more wall-clock time due to the multi-objective subproblem solve at each iteration, though the authors note this can be mitigated with faster local solvers (QUIC, PISTA) and stochastic objective sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graphical lasso (Friedman et al. 2008), sparse covariance estimation (Rothman 2012), and binary Ising model estimation (Höfling & Tibshirani 2009) by embedding them within a nonsmooth multi-objective optimization framework borrowed from Tanabe et al. (2019, 2023). It departs from prior fair GM work (Tarzanagh et al. 2021; Navarro et al. 2024) by not assuming community structures or node attributes, and by covering three model families simultaneously under a unified pairwise graph disparity error inspired by fair PCA/CCA. The multi-objective proximal gradient method with O(1/t) convergence is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such guarantee applied to GM estimation, in contrast to existing single-objective GM solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap: fairness in unsupervised learning, specifically for high-dimensional graphical model estimation, which has been largely unexplored compared to supervised settings. The very favorable trade-off (92–97% reduction in disparity error with <1.2% objective degradation) across diverse real-world applications (cancer genomics, Alzheimer's PET imaging, credit scoring, music recommendation) suggests practical utility. However, the 30–60× computational overhead, sensitivity to the penalty function φ, and limited scalability to large K remain open challenges. Future directions include integrating Fair GMs with downstream supervised tasks (spectral clustering, dimension reduction) and extending to ordinal data models.
