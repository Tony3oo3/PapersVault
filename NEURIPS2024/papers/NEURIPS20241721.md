## Problem

Existing MILP instance generation methods (statistics-based and learning-based) fail to account for the block structures in constraint coefficient matrices (CCMs) that encode problem formulations and mathematical properties. This leads to generated instances that are computationally trivial or infeasible, and limits the ability to produce instances of varying sizes. The paper asks whether explicitly identifying and preserving CCM block structures during generation can simultaneously maintain feasibility, computational hardness, and enable scalable instance creation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of original MILP instances (each defined by a CCM, objective coefficients, bounds, and integrality constraints) and a modification ratio η controlling the fraction of variables altered. CCMs are first reordered via the GCG structure detector to reveal block-diagonal, bordered block-diagonal, or doubly bordered block-diagonal patterns, then decomposed into block-unit sub-matrices that are converted into bipartite sub-graphs for a structure library. The output is a set of new MILP instances whose sizes can be smaller (reduction), comparable (mix-up), or larger (expansion) than the originals, with block structures, feasibility, and solving difficulty preserved.

## Method

MILP-StuDio operates in three stages. First, it identifies block structures in CCMs using the GCG solver's structure detector and an enhanced variable-partition algorithm, classifying variables as block or bordered and constraints as master, block, or doubly-block to handle complex combinations beyond the three basic patterns. Second, it constructs a structure library by extracting the bipartite sub-graphs corresponding to each block-unit sub-matrix from all training instances, enabling efficient storage and retrieval of block characteristics. Third, it applies three operators—block reduction (removing a sampled block), block mix-up (substituting a block with one from the library), and block expansion (appending a library block)—with a coefficient refinement algorithm that properly inserts master-constraint coefficients and constructs new right-hand-side terms for block constraints, ensuring the generated instances remain feasible and retain their computational hardness.

## Results

MILP-StuDio preserves computational hardness and feasibility of generated instances far better than baselines: on WA, generated instances maintain the original 1000s solving time with 100% feasibility (η=0.01–0.05), whereas G2MILP drops to 12.01s with only 10% feasibility. On FA, MILP-StuDio generates instances with 4.94s solving time and 100% feasibility, compared to G2MILP's 0.02s and 1.7% feasibility. In downstream Predict-and-Search (PS), PS+MILP-StuDio reduces the absolute primal gap from 0.24 to 0.13 on IP and from 0.25 to 0.03 on WA, while PS+G2MILP is infeasible on WA. Generation is also 3× faster than G2MILP on the large-scale WA benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against G2MILP (the primary learning-based baseline), MILP-StuDio wins decisively on feasibility and hardness preservation: on FA and WA, G2MILP produces instances that are 98–100% infeasible and computationally trivial (0.01–12s vs. 4.78–1000s original), while MILP-StuDio maintains 100% feasibility and near-original solving times. In the downstream PS task, PS+MILP-StuDio achieves gap_abs of 0.13 on IP and 0.03 on WA, versus 0.39 and infeasible for PS+G2MILP respectively. Against the statistics-based Bowly baseline, MILP-StuDio achieves much higher structural similarity (0.663 vs. 0.077 on FA) and preserves hardness (4.94s vs. 0.07s on FA), since Bowly generates computationally trivial instances. Compared to the Gurobi solver directly, PS+MILP-StuDio matches Gurobi on CA and FA (both reach optimality) and improves the primal gap on IP (0.13 vs. 0.27) and WA (0.03 vs. 0.05). For the GNN branching policy, GNN+MILP-StuDio improves imitation accuracy from 0.545 to 0.605 on FA and from 0.640 to 0.800 on IP, while reducing solving time from 55.06s to 47.58s on FA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a well-established concept from operational research—block structure decomposition of constraint coefficient matrices (CCMs), central to Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition and the GCG solver—for the task of MILP instance generation. It departs from the prior learning-based generation paradigm (G2MILP), which operates on bipartite graph representations and iteratively modifies individual constraints via a masked VAE, and from statistics-based sampling (Bowly), by instead treating block units as atomic building blocks that can be removed, substituted, or appended. The key conceptual shift is that preserving CCM block structure, rather than bipartite graph topology, is what maintains the mathematical properties (feasibility, hardness) of generated instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MILP-StuDio addresses a practical bottleneck for learning-based MILP solvers: the scarcity of high-quality training instances. By preserving computational hardness and feasibility (100% vs. G2MILP's 10%) while enabling flexible-size generation at 3× the speed, it provides a plug-and-play data augmentation module that yields >10% solving-time reductions for downstream solvers. The main limitation is reliance on the GCG structure detector, which struggles with extremely complex or non-block-structured CCMs; the authors note that incorporating prior knowledge to improve detection is a promising direction. An open question is how well the approach generalizes to MILPs where block structure is absent or highly irregular, though preliminary results on SC and MIS suggest reasonable transfer.
