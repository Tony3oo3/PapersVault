## Problem

Text-video retrieval suffers from a persistent modality gap in the joint CLIP embedding space, where relevant text and video embeddings remain separated despite contrastive training. The paper identifies two critical limitations of applying vanilla diffusion models to bridge this gap: the L2 denoising loss does not fit the ranking nature of retrieval (it cannot prioritize relevant pairs over irrelevant ones), and the random initial sampling from an isotropic Gaussian introduces uncontrolled variability that causes misalignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (resized to 224×224, 12 uniformly sampled frames) and text descriptions, encoding both into a 512-dimensional joint embedding space via CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 backbones. The DITS alignment module then operates purely in this embedding space, iteratively transforming the text embedding toward the video embedding over T′ steps (5 for DiDeMo, 10 for other datasets). The final output is a retrieval ranking over candidate videos (or texts) scored by cosine similarity between the aligned embedding and the query, evaluated via R@1/5/10, MdR, and MnR on five benchmarks.

## Method

DITS replaces the vanilla diffusion reverse process with a truncated sampler that starts directly from the text embedding rather than an isotropic Gaussian, exploiting the inherent proximity of relevant text-video pairs in the CLIP space. A DiT-based alignment network (4 blocks, 16 heads, MLP ratio 4.0) predicts the per-step correction, and the iterative update v_{t-1} = (v_t − χ_t·ε_γ(v_t, t)) + λ_t·z progressively moves the embedding from text toward video over T′ steps. Crucially, the training objective is the symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss (not L2), which simultaneously pulls relevant pairs together and pushes irrelevant ones apart, making the alignment ranking-aware. All parameters (CLIP visual encoder, text encoder, and alignment network) are jointly trained end-to-end, and the text embedding serves as both the starting point and the semantic anchor, eliminating the need for an explicit conditioning input to the denoising network.

## Results

On MSRVTT with CLIP-ViT-B/16, DITS achieves R@1=55.0, R@5=79.8, R@10=87.1, MdR=1.0, MnR=10.0, outperforming the strongest baseline T-MASS (R@1=52.7) by 2.3 points. On DiDeMo with CLIP-ViT-B/16, DITS reaches R@1=55.8 and R@5=80.5, surpassing T-MASS (R@1=53.3) by 2.5 points. On the challenging Charades dataset with CLIP-ViT-B/16, DITS attains R@1=27.4, exceeding T-MASS (26.7) and the X-Pool baseline (20.7) by 0.7 and 6.7 points respectively. DITS also achieves the best MnR across all five datasets, indicating consistently tighter retrieval rankings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITS outperforms the X-Pool baseline by 5.0% R@1 on MSRVTT (B/32) and 6.8% (B/16), confirming the alignment module's substantial contribution. Against the strongest competitor T-MASS, DITS wins on MSRVTT (B/32: 51.9 vs 50.2; B/16: 55.0 vs 52.7), DiDeMo (B/16: 55.8 vs 53.3), VATEX (B/32: 64.1 vs 63.0; B/16: 66.4 vs 65.6), and Charades (B/32: 15.5 vs 14.2; B/16: 27.4 vs 26.7), but narrowly loses on LSMDC B/32 (28.2 vs 28.9 R@1) and DiDeMo B/16 MnR (11.0 vs 9.8). DITS also surpasses CLIP-ViP by 0.8% R@1 on MSRVTT B/16 and DiffusionRet by 2.9% R@1 on MSRVTT B/32. The paper notes that DITS gains a larger advantage with the bigger CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone across all datasets, suggesting better scalability, and excels particularly in top-ranked retrieval (R@1, MdR) where precise alignment matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITS builds on the DiT transformer architecture for its denoising/alignment network and the DDPM forward/reverse process formulation, but departs fundamentally from both in how they are applied to retrieval. Unlike DiffusionRet and MomentDiff, which treat retrieval as a generation task (mapping noise to similarity scores or moments via L1/L2 loss), DITS reframes each diffusion step as progressive modality-gap modeling guided by contrastive loss. The truncated sampler—starting from the text embedding rather than an isotropic Gaussian—repurposes the diffusion schedule as a controllable alignment mechanism, a design choice absent from prior diffusion-for-retrieval work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that vanilla diffusion models are actively harmful for retrieval (L2 pretraining drops R@1 from 46.9% to 35.4%), providing a clear diagnostic for why prior diffusion-retrieval hybrids underperform. The finding that DITS joint training reshapes the CLIP embedding space (reducing average modality gap from 23.76 to 18.13 in L1-norm) suggests the alignment module can serve as a regularizer for the backbone, not just a post-hoc correction. Open questions include whether the approach extends to few-shot or zero-shot settings, how it behaves with noisy/incomplete data, and whether automated timestamp selection (T′) can replace the current heuristic.
