## Problem

Existing ISP optimization methods either fix the pipeline and only tune parameters, or jointly search pipeline structure and parameters but produce a single static configuration at inference time. Neither approach adapts to dynamically changing scenes, and most prior work optimizes for perceptual image quality rather than downstream recognition tasks like object detection, leaving a gap in task-driven, scene-adaptive ISP design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear RGB image (already demosaicked from raw sensor data) at 512×512 resolution as input. The policy and value networks consume a downsampled 64×64 version of the current image augmented with additional channels: a module-usage history vector (one channel per ISP module indicating prior use), a stage-index channel, and for the value network, three extra channels encoding luminance, contrast, and saturation. The output is a variable-length sequence (up to 5 stages) of discrete ISP module selections paired with their continuous parameter values, applied sequentially to transform the input image for a frozen downstream detector.

## Method

AdaptiveISP formulates ISP pipeline configuration as a Markov Decision Process solved via actor-critic reinforcement learning. At each stage, a lightweight CNN policy network selects one ISP module from a pool of 10 (Exposure, White Balance, CCM, Gamma, Denoise, Sharpen/Blur, Tone Mapping, Contrast, Saturation, Desaturation) and predicts its continuous parameters, while a separate CNN value network estimates the expected future reward. The reward is the reduction in detection error from a frozen YOLOv3 detector, augmented with three penalty terms: a reuse penalty preventing the same module from being selected twice, an entropy penalty encouraging exploration during early training, and a computational-time penalty that enables dynamic accuracy–latency trade-off at inference. Unlike NAS-based approaches that generate the entire pipeline at once, this greedy one-module-per-step strategy reduces inference to ~1.2 ms per stage and allows the pipeline length to adapt per image, spending fewer stages on easy inputs.

## Results

On the LOD dataset, AdaptiveISP achieves mAP@0.5 of 71.4, mAP@0.75 of 51.7, and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 47.1, outperforming all baselines. On the OnePlus dataset, it reaches mAP@0.5 of 70.1 and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 45.0, and on the synthetic Raw COCO dataset it achieves mAP@0.5 of 54.9 and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 37.7. The method also generalizes across detectors (YOLOX, DDQ) and to image segmentation (mAP@0.5 of 47.0 on Raw COCO), and supports a dynamic accuracy-efficiency trade-off at inference time, reducing average ISP latency from 14.73 ms to 9.20 ms with only a 1.5-point mAP@0.5 drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptiveISP outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. On LOD, it beats the strongest parameter-tuning baseline (Attention-aware Learning [30], 70.9 mAP@0.5) by 0.5 points and the strongest pipeline-searching baseline (Hyperparameter Optimization [35], 70.1) by 1.3 points. The gap widens substantially on cross-dataset evaluation: on OnePlus, AdaptiveISP surpasses Attention-aware Learning by 0.8 mAP@0.5 and ReconfigISP [39] by 5.0 mAP@0.5; on Raw COCO it leads Refactoring ISP [34] by 2.5 mAP@0.5 and ReconfigISP by 2.3 mAP@0.5. On the ROD dataset, AdaptiveISP (51.6 mAP@0.5) outperforms Attention-aware Learning (49.8) and achieves higher mAP@0.75 (35.7 vs. 30.9) and mAP@0.5:0.95 (33.2 vs. 31.3) than the end-to-end raw detector Toward RAW Object Detection [37] (54.1 mAP@0.5), despite using a frozen RGB-trained YOLOv3. Against the image-quality-oriented Exposure method [10], AdaptiveISP gains 7.8 mAP@0.5 on LOD (62.1 vs. 54.3), confirming that detection-optimized ISP configurations differ fundamentally from those tuned for perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ISP optimization-for-detection line initiated by Tseng et al. (Hyperparameter Optimization, 2019) and ReconfigISP (2021), which either fix the pipeline and tune parameters or search for a single optimal pipeline offline. It departs from these by formulating ISP configuration as a per-image Markov Decision Process solved with actor-critic RL, generating both module selection and parameters greedily at each stage during inference. The RL-for-image-processing lineage (Furuta et al., Kosugi et al., Hu et al.) informs the policy/value network design, but those works target visual quality rather than downstream detection, and none support dynamic pipeline length or real-time reconfiguration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in enabling real-time, scene-adaptive ISP for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, where lighting conditions change rapidly and a fixed pipeline degrades. The finding that detection-oriented ISP is substantially simpler than visual-quality ISP (4 stages vs. 10+, with denoising entirely unnecessary) offers actionable guidance for hardware designers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-differentiable ISP modules (acknowledged as future work), whether it scales to higher-resolution detection, and whether the RL policy remains stable under domain shift beyond the tested low-light and synthetic-raw settings.
