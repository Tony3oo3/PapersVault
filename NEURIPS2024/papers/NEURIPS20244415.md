## Problem

Adder and multiplier designs are fundamental to hardware performance, yet prior methods—whether human-crafted, optimization-based, or learning-based—struggle with the exponentially large search space (O(2^{N^2}) for compressor trees, O(2^{4N^2}) for prefix trees) and fail to jointly optimize the compressor and prefix tree components of multipliers. This leads to locally optimal or degenerated designs with suboptimal delay-area trade-offs, particularly at higher bit-widths where the search space becomes intractable.

## Input / Output

For adders, the method takes a bit-width N and an initial prefix tree (Sklansky, Brent-Kung, or ripple-carry) represented as an upper triangular matrix A_{N×N}, and produces an optimized prefix tree structure. For multipliers, it takes N-bit partial products and builds both a compressor tree (as a variable-length action sequence of half/full adder choices) and a 2N-bit prefix tree from scratch. The final output is Verilog HDL code generated from the discovered tree structures, which is then synthesized through Yosys and OpenROAD to yield concrete delay (ns) and area (μm²) metrics at 45nm and 7nm process nodes.

## Method

ArithTreeRL formulates adder and multiplier design as single-player tree generation games—AddGame for prefix trees and MultGame for joint compressor+prefix tree design—and solves them with two specialized RL agents. For prefix trees, an improved MCTS agent uses a UCT-based node score (Eq. 2) that blends exploration with a value function combining both the best and average performance scores of all descendants (Eq. 1), enabling efficient navigation of the large action space of cell additions and deletions. For compressor trees, a PPO agent with MLP policy/value networks selects half or full adders at the lowest digit with more than two bits, with a penalty term discouraging half adders that do not reduce bit count. The key architectural contribution is a co-design framework that iteratively alternates between PPO (compressor tree) and MCTS (prefix tree) optimization across multiple rounds, capturing global interactions between the two components. A two-level retrieval strategy (5000 fast syntheses without routing, then 500 full syntheses on top-K Pareto-near candidates) and a 10× synthesis acceleration enable practical exploration within hours.

## Results

For 128-bit adders, ArithTreeRL discovers the first Pareto-optimal design with 10 levels and size 244, matching Snir's theoretical lower bound and surpassing all prior optimization-based methods. For multipliers, ArithTreeRL reduces delay by up to 53.7% (64-bit, min-delay objective: 1.2401 ns vs. RL-MUL's 2.6771 ns) and area by up to 45% compared to RL-MUL, while also outperforming the default Yosys synthesis multiplier by up to 16% in delay and 35% in area. For adders, the method achieves up to 26% delay and 30% area reductions over PrefixRL at equivalent computational budgets. All designs are verified functional and transferable from 45 nm to 7 nm without modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art RL-MUL [18], ArithTreeRL wins decisively on both delay and area across all bit-widths (8–64 bit) for both min-delay and trade-off objectives, with delay reductions of 30.8% (8-bit) to 53.7% (64-bit) and area reductions up to 45%. Compared to the default Yosys synthesis multiplier, ArithTreeRL achieves up to 16% lower delay and 35% smaller area, demonstrating that the tool's auto-generated structures are far from optimal. Against the optimization-based baselines GOMIL [16] and Simulated Annealing [45], ArithTreeRL produces superior Pareto fronts in the delay–area trade-off space (Fig. 8). For adders, ArithTreeRL matches or exceeds the best-known optimization-based results of Roy et al. [14] at all tested levels (64-bit and 128-bit), and is the first method to reach the theoretical size bound of 244 for 128-bit/10-level adders. Against PrefixRL [17], ArithTreeRL achieves up to 26% delay and 30% area reductions while using the same computational budget, with the two-level retrieval strategy further improving PrefixRL's own results. The human-designed Wallace multiplier [13] and Sklansky adder [12] serve as starting points but are consistently outperformed by the RL-discovered structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on MCTS and PPO as core RL algorithms but departs from prior arithmetic design work (PrefixRL, RL-MUL) by reframing adder and multiplier design as single-player tree generation games (AddGame, MultGame) rather than direct sequential optimization. A key conceptual departure is the co-design framework that jointly optimizes compressor and prefix trees iteratively, addressing the limitation in prior learning-based methods that optimized components independently and produced degenerate bottlenecks. The two-level retrieval strategy and 10× synthesis acceleration are practical engineering contributions that enable scaling to 64-bit multipliers, beyond the 16-bit limit of RL-MUL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first discovery of a 128-bit adder achieving Snir's theoretical size lower bound (244 cells at level 10) validates the game-based search formulation for navigating exponentially large design spaces. Practical improvements of 26–30% (adders) and 33–45% (multipliers) over state-of-the-art RL baselines, combined with seamless transferability to 7nm technology, make these designs directly deployable in synthesis flows. Limitations include scope restricted to adders and multipliers (no exponentiation or more complex units), and the method's reliance on synthesis tools for reward computation, which constrains further scaling. Open questions include whether the tree-game formulation generalizes to other arithmetic operations and whether the co-design framework extends to multi-component circuits beyond two-tree architectures.
