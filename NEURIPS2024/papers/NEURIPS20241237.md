## Problem

While univariate almost stochastic dominance via optimal transport provides a principled statistical testing framework, extending it to the multivariate setting—where an agent must compare outcomes across multiple correlated metrics—remains largely unexplored. Current multimetric benchmarking approaches (e.g., for LLMs) reduce ordering to the univariate case via aggregation, thereby discarding dependencies between metrics that are critical for robust model selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample d-dimensional evaluation metric vectors (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) from model responses, constructs empirical distributions over R^d, and requires user-specified cost function parameters (a smooth univariate function h, entropic regularization λ, and for logistic costs a gain β). It outputs a normalized violation ratio in [0,1] quantifying the degree of FSD violation, a binary statistical test decision at a chosen significance level, and a full ranking of k models via Borda count with Bonferroni FWER correction, accompanied by bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Method

The paper characterizes multivariate first-order stochastic dominance as an OT problem with costs c(x,y) that vanish if and only if x ≤ y componentwise, then defines an entropic multivariate violation ratio ε_{h,λ}(μ,ν) = OT_{h,λ}(μ,ν) / OT_{h̄,λ}(μ,ν) where the denominator uses the symmetrized cost h̄(z)=h(z)+h(−z) to normalize into [0,1]. Entropic regularization (Sinkhorn) is introduced to overcome the curse-of-dimensionality rates of empirical OT and to enable efficient GPU computation. The key theoretical contribution is a central limit theorem and bootstrap consistency for the empirical violation ratio, proved via the functional delta method applied to the EOT dual potentials, requiring smoothness conditions (SC_d) on the cost function h. The resulting testing framework supports both absolute (threshold-based) and relative (one-versus-all) comparisons, with FWER control via Bonferroni correction and Borda count aggregation for ranking multiple models.

## Results

On the MixInstruct dataset (12 LLMs evaluated on 9 metrics), the proposed entropic multivariate FSD violation ratio achieves the highest Kendall Tau rank similarity with the ChatGPT pairwise scoring (used as a human-proxy ground truth) across all sample sizes from 100 to 5000, and is the most sample-efficient method. The one-versus-all violation ratio ranking (n=5000) places oasst-sft-4-pythia-12b-epoch-3.5 at the top (ratio 0.463) and flan-t5-xxl at the bottom (ratio 0.559). In the synthetic experiment, the entropic violation ratio with logistic cost (β=8, λ=0.01d) converges to the unregularized EMD-based ratio as λ→0, and the relative test achieves decreasing Type I and Type II errors with increasing sample size in dimensions d∈{10,20,50}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its multivariate FSD method against two univariate reduction baselines on the MixInstruct LLM benchmarking task, using Kendall Tau rank similarity with ChatGPT pairwise scoring as the evaluation metric. The proposed multivariate FSD method wins on both Kendall Tau similarity and sample efficiency, achieving the highest rank correlation at all sample sizes. The Independent Copula P(IC) baseline [Nitsure et al., 2023], which normalizes each dimension with a global CDF and takes a geometric mean, lags slightly behind in Kendall Tau similarity because it ignores inter-metric dependencies, though it remains sample-efficient. The Empirical Copula P(EC) baseline [Ulan et al., 2021], which estimates a full multivariate CDF across all models, captures dependencies but suffers from the curse of dimensionality and is notably less sample-efficient, requiring much larger n to approach the same rank correlation. The paper attributes its advantage to the OT-based formulation's ability to capture metric dependencies without the dimensionality penalty of full copula estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the univariate almost stochastic dominance framework of del Barrio et al. (2018) to the multivariate setting by characterizing multivariate FSD through couplings and expressing violation ratios as optimal transport problems with compatible costs. It then applies entropic regularization (Cuturi, 2013) to overcome the curse of dimensionality in empirical OT, and leverages the functional delta method (following Goldfeld et al., 2024b) to establish a CLT and bootstrap consistency for the resulting entropic violation ratio. The application to LLM benchmarking builds directly on Nitsure et al. (2023), which used univariate almost FSD with aggregation-based reduction, whereas this work avoids that reduction and captures inter-metric dependencies natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Current multi-metric LLM benchmarking relies on aggregation (e.g., geometric mean, copula-based reduction) that discards dependencies between evaluation metrics, potentially leading to statistically unsound rankings. This paper provides a principled, statistically valid multivariate testing framework with FWER control that directly models those dependencies, and demonstrates on MixInstruct that it achieves higher Kendall Tau agreement with human-proxy (GPT) rankings while being more sample-efficient than empirical-copula baselines. Open questions include extending the framework to other stochastic orders (μ-FSD, Lorenz order), data-driven cost selection, and exploring nonuniform FWER corrections that might improve statistical power in ranking settings.
