## Problem

Diffusion models require many sampling steps for realistic generation, and existing distillation methods either still need few-step inference or degrade substantially in quality. Prior score-based distillation approaches (Diff-Instruct, SiD) are tied to specific divergences (KL or Fisher), limiting flexibility. This paper asks whether a general, principled framework for score-based divergences can enable truly one-step, data-free distillation that matches the teacher's quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student generator g_θ takes a single random noise vector z ~ p_z (or a text prompt concatenated with noise for T2I) and produces one image in a single forward pass (1 NFE). Distillation is entirely data-free: no ground-truth training images are needed, only the pre-trained teacher diffusion model's score function. Experiments cover 32×32 CIFAR-10 images (EDM teacher) and 512×512 text-to-image generation (PixelArt-α DiT-600M teacher).

## Method

SIM defines a general time-integral score-based divergence D(p,q) = ∫ w(t) E_{x_t~π_t}[d(s_{p_t}(x_t) − s_{q_t}(x_t))] dt, where d(·) is an arbitrary proper distance function between the generator's intractable marginal score and the teacher's score. The key technical contribution is Theorem 3.1 (score-gradient theorem), which shows that although the divergence is intractable, its gradient with respect to generator parameters can be computed exactly by replacing the intractable ∂s_{p_{θ,t}}/∂θ term with a tractable inner product involving a separate online score network s_ψ trained via standard denoising score matching on generator outputs. Training alternates between updating s_ψ (frozen θ) and updating g_θ (frozen ψ) using the SIM loss. The paper shows SiD is a special case (L2 distance) and introduces a Pseudo-Huber distance d(y)=√(||y||²+c²)−c whose adaptive normalization yields robustness to large learning rates and faster convergence, achieving FID 2.06/1.96 on CIFAR-10 and an aesthetic score of 6.42 for one-step T2I generation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SIM achieves a one-step FID of 2.06 (unconditional) and 1.96 (class-conditional), competitive with SiD (1.92/1.71) and CTM (1.98/1.73) while substantially outperforming DI (3.70/4.19) and DMD (3.77/2.66). For text-to-image generation, distilling the 610M PixelArt-α DiT model yields SIM-DiT-600M with an aesthetic score of 6.42 in a single step, surpassing the 30-step teacher (5.97) and all other one-step T2I generators (SDXL-TURBO 5.33, SDXL-LIGHTNING 5.34, HYPER-SDXL 5.85). The distillation is data-free and requires only 4 A100 GPUs for 2 days, versus hundreds of A100 GPU-days for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, SIM (FID 2.06) slightly trails SiD (1.92) and CTM (1.98) but substantially outperforms DI (3.70) and DMD (3.77); on class-conditional generation, SIM (1.96) is competitive with SiD (1.93) and CTM (1.73) while far exceeding DI (4.19) and DMD (2.66). The headline T2I result is decisive: SIM-DiT-600M achieves an aesthetic score of 6.42 in one step, exceeding the 30-step PixelArt-α teacher (5.97) and the best prior one-step model HYPER-SDXL (5.85) by a large margin of 0.57. SIM also demonstrates practical advantages over SiD and DI: it remains stable at a 10× larger learning rate (1e-4 vs. 1e-5) where both baselines diverge, and it converges faster in terms of FID and Inception Score at every training iteration. The distillation cost of 4 A100 × 2 days is roughly 10–100× cheaper than competing methods (e.g., 64 A100 × N days for SDXL-LIGHTNING, 32 A100 × N days for HYPER-SDXL), and SIM is data-free, requiring no ground-truth images during distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIM generalizes the divergence-based diffusion distillation framework established by Diff-Instruct (KL divergence) and Score Identity Distillation (Fisher divergence) into a unified family parameterized by an arbitrary distance function between score functions. The key theoretical contribution is the Score-divergence Gradient Theorem, which leverages the score-projection identity (Vincent 2011) to show that the intractable gradient of the score-based divergence can be computed via a tractable surrogate loss, with SiD recovered as the L2-distance special case. Practically, the Pseudo-Huber distance function introduces adaptive normalization of the loss that was absent in prior methods, yielding robustness to large learning rates and faster convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIM provides a data-free, single-step distillation recipe that is remarkably compute-efficient (4 A100 × 2 days for a 600M DiT T2I model vs. hundreds of GPU-days for competitors), making one-step generation accessible for resource-constrained settings. The open questions include whether the framework extends to flow-matching models, whether incorporating new data can fix teacher failure modes, and whether the approach scales to video generation. The Pseudo-Huber normalization insight is broadly applicable to any score-matching-based training loop and may benefit other distillation or score-matching settings beyond diffusion.
