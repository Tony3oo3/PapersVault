## Problem

Existing activation engineering methods for LLM alignment (VecAdd, OrthoProj) inadequately model the geometry of the activation space: they either remove benign concepts alongside undesirable ones, degrading linguistic capability, or fail to sufficiently remove target concepts. Moreover, these methods typically operate on a single concept direction per task, while real alignment tasks involve multiple related concepts, and the control strength parameter in VecAdd is difficult to tune per input.

## Input / Output

At inference, PaCE takes a user text prompt and decomposes its activations at 19 specific decoder layers (last 29th through last 11th) against a frozen dictionary of 10,000 concept directions (drawn from the 40,000-concept PaCE-1M dataset). The decomposition is solved once on the first generated token and the resulting sparse coefficients are reused for all subsequent token predictions. The output is the aligned LLM response, with undesirable concept components zeroed out from the intervened activations before they propagate to the next layer.

## Method

PaCE constructs a large-scale concept dictionary (PaCE-1M) by prompting GPT-4 to generate ~30 contextual stimuli per concept from the top 40,000 Brown Corpus words, then extracting unit-norm directions via representation reading and PCA at each decoder layer. For a given alignment task, GPT-4 partitions the dictionary into benign and undesirable concepts (top 50 removed). At inference, activations are decomposed via sparse coding with an elastic-net regularizer (τ=0.95, α=0.05) solved by an active-set algorithm, yielding a parsimonious linear combination of concept directions; undesirable coefficients are then zeroed and the residual plus benign components are recombined as the intervened activation. This overcomplete oblique projection framework explicitly models the non-orthogonal geometry of multiple concept directions simultaneously, provably subsuming both VecAdd and OrthoProj as special cases while avoiding their failure modes of over- or under-removal.

## Results

On the SafeEdit detoxification benchmark, PaCE achieves 93.7–99.3% safety scores across all nine categories for LLaMA2-13B-Chat, dominating every baseline in every category. For LLaMA2-7B-Chat, PaCE improves safety by 60–80% over the vanilla model (e.g., 94.2% on Mental Harm vs. 11.3% vanilla) and by 6–20% over VecAdd and OrthoProj in most categories. Linguistic capability is well preserved: MMLU accuracy is 37.1% (7B) and 54.1% (13B) versus 43.4% and 54.9% for vanilla, and fluency scores remain comparable. On AdvBench adversarial suffix attacks, PaCE reaches 96.65% (7B) and 99.17% (13B) safety, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaCE is compared against two activation-engineering baselines (VecAdd [Turner et al., 2023] and OrthoProj [Hewitt et al., 2023]) and a prompting baseline. On LLaMA2-13B-Chat, PaCE decisively outperforms both VecAdd and OrthoProj across all nine safety categories (e.g., 98.9% MH vs. 87.2% VecAdd and 52.3% OrthoProj; 99.3% PH vs. 47.4% VecAdd and 51.4% OrthoProj), confirming that the larger model's more structured latent space benefits most from explicit dictionary-based decomposition. On LLaMA2-7B-Chat, PaCE wins in six of nine categories over both baselines (notably IA 75.3 vs. 53.9/47.5, MH 94.2 vs. 66.1/67.0, UB 88.3 vs. 66.4/66.4) but trails VecAdd on Pornography (46.2 vs. 58.9) and OrthoProj on Pornography (46.2 vs. 57.9), while the prompting baseline leads on Political Sensitivity (82.5 vs. 69.6). For linguistic capability, PaCE achieves the highest MMLU among all activation-manipulation methods (37.1% for 7B, 54.1% for 13B) and the best fluency score (8.07 for 7B), whereas VecAdd degrades MMLU to 29.0% (7B) and OrthoProj to 34.1% (7B). On the AdvBench adversarial suffix benchmark, PaCE achieves 96.65% (7B) and 99.17% (13B), exceeding VecAdd (94.51/95.33) and OrthoProj (92.81/96.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

PaCE extends the activation engineering / representation engineering line of work (Zou et al. 2023, Turner et al. 2023, Hewitt et al. 2023) that manipulates LLM hidden states to steer behavior. Prior methods—VecAdd and OrthoProj—operate on one or a few concept directions and implicitly assume orthogonality or require manual tuning of a control strength. PaCE departs by constructing a large-scale overcomplete concept dictionary (40,000 directions from 1.2M+ context sentences) and applying sparse coding (elastic-net regularized) to decompose activations, then using oblique projection to remove only the undesirable components while preserving benign ones. The sparse-coding machinery is borrowed from classical signal processing and dictionary learning, repurposed here for LLM alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaCE offers a training-free, interpretable alignment mechanism that adapts to new tasks by simply re-running a GPT-based concept partitioner, avoiding the cost of fine-tuning or prompt engineering. The released PaCE-1M dataset (40,000 concept vectors with stimuli) is a practical resource for the representation-engineering community. Key limitations include representing each concept with a single vector (limiting polysemy handling), evaluation restricted to LLaMA-2 chat models, and a ~3× computational overhead over VecAdd. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other architectures, whether multi-vector or subspace concept representations would improve fidelity, and whether the oblique-projection principle transfers to other generative models (diffusion, video).
