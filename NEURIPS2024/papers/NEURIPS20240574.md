## Problem

Existing online shopping benchmarks are tailored to narrow, task-specific settings (e.g., attribute extraction, session recommendation, query-product matching) and fail to capture the full multi-task, few-shot complexity of real-world shopping involving diverse entities, implicit knowledge, heterogeneous user behaviors, and multi-lingual content. While LLMs offer a promising unified solution for all shopping tasks, no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate their capabilities across the full spectrum of shopping skills, making it unclear how well general-purpose LLMs can serve as versatile shop assistants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All 57 tasks are reformulated as text-to-text generation under a unified system prompt ('You are a helpful online shopping assistant...'), with inputs ranging from short product metadata and user queries to full browse sessions and multi-lingual review texts. Outputs are task-type-specific: a single token for multiple choice, three comma-separated indices for retrieval, a 1-to-5 permutation for ranking, entity spans for NER, and free-form text for generation. Evaluation is strictly zero-shot with temperature=0, and skill-level scores are computed as macro averages of task-wise metrics (accuracy, hit rate@3, NDCG, micro-F1, ROUGE-L, BLEU-4, or sentence-transformer cosine similarity).

## Method

Shopping MMLU curates 57 tasks and 20,799 questions from real-world Amazon data (product catalogs, reviews, browse sessions, queries) plus Claude-2-synthesized data, organized into 4 skills (concept understanding, knowledge reasoning, user behavior alignment, multi-lingual abilities) with fine-grained sub-skills. The paper benchmarks over 20 LLMs (proprietary: Claude-3 Sonnet, Claude-2, ChatGPT; open-source: LLaMA2/3, QWen1.5, Mistral, Phi-2; domain-specific: eCeLLM-S/M/L) in a zero-shot setting and analyzes task/skill correlations, general-to-domain knowledge transfer, effects of instruction fine-tuning (general and domain-specific), and in-context learning with 0/1/5-shot and chain-of-thought prompting. Key findings include: proprietary LLMs remain strongest (Claude-3 Sonnet leads), general knowledge transfers well to shopping, IFT helps weaker models but can cause catastrophic forgetting in stronger ones, domain-specific IFT only improves observed tasks/skills, and naive few-shot prompting generally degrades performance on shopping tasks.

## Results

Claude-3 Sonnet achieves the best overall performance on Shopping MMLU with scores of 80.75% (Concept Understanding), 71.63% (Knowledge Reasoning), 70.17% (User Behavior Alignment), and 67.76% (Multi-lingual Abilities). Among open-source models, LLaMA3-70B-Instruct (75.24/69.29/67.67/62.00) and QWen1.5-72B (71.67/68.92/64.12/64.84) perform on par with ChatGPT (75.63/64.97/59.79/60.81) and Claude-2 (75.46/65.50/63.53/65.24). Domain-specific eCeLLM models improve over their base models but remain behind general LLMs of similar parameter count (e.g., eCeLLM-L at 61.54/54.84/54.55/59.64 vs. QWen1.5-14B at 67.22/60.92/54.92/55.21). Task-specific state-of-the-art methods still outperform all proprietary LLMs on specific tasks such as Aspect-based Sentiment Classification (0.8627 vs. 0.8235) and Query-product Relation Selection (0.6071 vs. 0.4321).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proprietary LLMs (Claude-3 Sonnet, Claude-2, ChatGPT) remain the strongest on Shopping MMLU, with Claude-3 Sonnet leading all four skills by 3–10 points over the next best models. Open-source LLaMA3-70B-Instruct and QWen1.5-72B close the gap substantially, matching or exceeding ChatGPT on Concept Understanding (75.24 and 71.67 vs. 75.63) and Reasoning (69.29 and 68.92 vs. 64.97), though they trail on Multi-lingual abilities. Domain-specific eCeLLM models (fine-tuned on ECInstruct) improve over their base models (e.g., eCeLLM-M over Mistral-7B, eCeLLM-L over LLaMA2-13B) but are outperformed by general LLMs of similar size (eCeLLM-L at 61.54 Concept vs. QWen1.5-14B at 67.22; eCeLLM-M at 63.29 vs. LLaMA3-8B-Instruct at 65.26), and their gains are concentrated in Behavior and Multi-lingual skills while Concept and Reasoning see limited improvement. General-domain instruction fine-tuning helps in 17 of 20 model-skill pairs but causes performance decline on the strongest base model (LLaMA2-70B), suggesting catastrophic forgetting. On specific tasks, task-specific methods still dominate: PyABSA achieves 0.8627 on Aspect-based Sentiment Classification vs. 0.8235 for the best LLM, and the KDD Cup 2022 solution scores 0.6071 on Query-product Relation Selection vs. 0.4321 for Claude-3, indicating that LLM-based multi-task solutions have not yet surpassed specialized approaches on all shopping tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMLU multi-task evaluation paradigm to the online shopping domain, unifying 57 tasks across 4 skills (concept understanding, knowledge reasoning, user behavior alignment, multi-linguality) into a single text-to-text benchmark. It departs from prior e-commerce datasets (MAVE, Amazon-M2, Amazon-ESCI) that target individual tasks, and from multi-task e-commerce instruction datasets (EComInstruct, ECInstruct) that each cover only one or two of the four skills. The benchmark is positioned as complementary to web agent benchmarks (WebShop, WebArena), arguing that agents need foundational shopping knowledge before executing composite decision-making tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Shopping MMLU provides the first comprehensive, skill-stratified evaluation framework for LLM-based shop assistants, validated by the KDD Cup 2024 competition with 500+ teams. Key open questions remain: LLMs still lag behind task-specific methods on several tasks (e.g., query-product ranking, aspect-based sentiment), and the paper does not provide a matching diverse IFT training set. The finding that in-context learning often degrades performance and that domain-specific IFT fails to generalize beyond observed skills raises important questions about how to build truly versatile e-commerce LLMs beyond Amazon-specific data.
