## Problem

Existing LLM-based tabular data generation methods either require dataset-specific fine-tuning (risking overfitting to majority classes in imbalanced settings) or rely on ad-hoc prompt designs without systematic investigation. The paper identifies that prompt design is a critical but underexplored factor for generating high-quality synthetic tabular data, particularly for underrepresented classes, and that no prior work has comprehensively ablated the key prompt components that govern data quality and generation efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset with mixed numerical and categorical features, from which it randomly samples class-balanced subsets (with replacement across iterations) to populate the LLM prompt. Categorical values are remapped to unique three-character alphanumeric strings before inclusion. The LLM outputs synthetic rows in CSV format, which are parsed into structured tabular data; rows containing categorical values absent from the original dataset are discarded. Generation is repeated iteratively until the target sample count is reached, with fresh random samples drawn each iteration to ensure distributional coverage.

## Method

EPIC is a structured prompting strategy (no model training) that combines four empirically validated design elements: (1) CSV-style formatting over sentence-style to fit more in-context examples within token budgets; (2) balanced multi-class grouping, where each prompt set contains equal numbers of samples per target class organized into labeled groups, enabling the LLM to learn class-specific patterns simultaneously; (3) unique variable mapping, which remaps each categorical variable's values to distinct random alphanumeric strings to prevent monotony and improve variable distinguishability; and (4) completion triggering, where the feature-name header is appended at the end of the prompt to signal the LLM to generate the next set following the established pattern. The approach is model-agnostic (validated on GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2, and Mistral-7B), requires no fine-tuning, and uses iterative sampling with replacement to cumulatively cover the full data distribution.

## Results

EPIC achieves state-of-the-art F1 scores and balanced accuracy across all six real-world datasets when synthetic data is added to the original training set, and is the only method that consistently outperforms the original data in both F1 and BAL ACC on every dataset. The most dramatic gains appear on the highly imbalanced Travel dataset (F1 66.65, +8.53%p; Sensitivity 78.00, +21.00%p) and Income (F1 69.16, +2.26%p; Sensitivity 66.45, +9.17%p). On the challenging Diabetes dataset, EPIC is the sole method that improves over the original data (F1 54.94 vs. 54.87). Results are averaged over four classifiers (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM, Gradient Boosting) with five runs each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIC outperforms all seven generative baselines (TVAE, CopulaGAN, CTAB-GAN+, GReaT, TabDDPM, SMOTE, SMOTENC) on F1 and BAL ACC across all six datasets, and is the only method that consistently improves over the original data on every dataset. GReaT, the strongest LLM-based baseline, achieves the second-best F1 on Travel (60.95 vs. 66.65) and Income (67.95 vs. 69.16) but degrades performance on Sick, HELOC, Diabetes, and Thyroid. TabDDPM is competitive on Thyroid (F1 94.39 vs. 94.80) but underperforms on the other five datasets. GAN-based methods show catastrophic failures on small or imbalanced datasets: CopulaGAN drops to F1 21.76 on Travel and CTAB-GAN+ to 27.46 on Thyroid. For minority-class-only augmentation (Table 6), EPIC achieves F1 72.13 on Travel vs. 63.45 for SMOTE and 62.07 for GReaT, and F1 71.09 on Income vs. 69.79 for SMOTENC. The method also generalizes across LLMs: GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2, and Mistral all produce meaningful improvements, with GPT-3.5-turbo yielding the highest F1 (70.18 on Travel).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) synthetic tabular data generation via generative models (SMOTE, CTGAN, TVAE, TabDDPM, GReaT) and (2) LLM in-context learning for data augmentation (LITO, CuratedLLM). Rather than fine-tuning an LLM on tabular data as GReaT does, or employing multi-stage pipelines with filtering as LITO and CuratedLLM do, EPIC demonstrates that systematic prompt design alone—CSV-style formatting, class-balanced grouping, unique variable mapping, and completion triggering—can achieve competitive or superior generation quality without any parameter updates. The paper's contribution is thus an empirical, ablation-driven study of which prompt design elements matter most for tabular data generation, carving out a 'prompt engineering for tabular synthesis' niche that prior LLM-based tabular works treated as a secondary implementation detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, model-agnostic prompting recipe that consistently improves minority-class sensitivity (up to +21%p on Travel) across six diverse datasets, with minimal preprocessing and no GPU training cost. This makes high-quality imbalanced-data augmentation accessible to practitioners who lack the infrastructure to fine-tune generative models. Key limitations remain: token constraints restrict how much of a large dataset can be included in a single prompt, and the iterative sampling-with-replacement strategy, while effective, still risks incomplete distributional coverage. An open question the authors flag is how to select a representative subset of examples when the full dataset exceeds context limits, which would be critical for scaling to very large tabular datasets.
