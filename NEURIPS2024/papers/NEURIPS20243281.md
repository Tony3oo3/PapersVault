## Problem

No single pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., CLIP) can dominate all image understanding tasks: CLIP excels at general recognition but fails on documents, charts, and grounding, while task-specific encoders like DINOv2 or Pix2Struct excel in narrow domains but degrade elsewhere. Plain concatenation of multiple encoders introduces biased information from irrelevant experts, causing performance drops. The paper asks how to dynamically select and fuse task-specific vision experts based on multimodal context without incurring this bias.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (base CLIP encoder at 672×672, experts at their native resolutions from 224×224 to 1280×1280) and a text instruction. During inference, the routing stage consumes only 144 downsampled image tokens plus a short prompt to produce a 3–4 token expert-selection string, while the final LLM response averages ~405 tokens. Outputs include free-form text (VQA, captioning), bounding box coordinates (grounding), and sequential polygon coordinates (segmentation). The base encoder's 2304 spatial tokens are compressed to 576 via two residual blocks with average pooling before entering the LLM.

## Method

MoVA employs a coarse-to-fine pipeline. In the coarse stage, the LLM itself acts as a router: given 144 downsampled image tokens, the user instruction, and textual descriptions of 7 vision experts (DINOv2, Co-DETR, SAM, Pix2Struct, Deplot, Vary, BiomedCLIP), it selects up to 3 context-relevant experts, with routing annotations constructed offline via a loss-driven criterion (expert j is useful for sample i iff its language-modeling loss is lower than the base-only model). In the fine stage, a MoV-Adapter of L=3 blocks performs expert fusion: each block contains (i) MoE cross-attention layers where the base feature serves as query and each selected expert's bilinearly-interpolated feature serves as key/value, (ii) a dynamic gating network (MLP + softmax over a concatenated global-pooled visual token and a shared BERT [CLS] text token) that assigns per-expert soft weights, and (iii) a standard transformer block. Training proceeds in two phases—pretraining on 15M diverse samples (optimizing MoV-Adapter and base encoder, freezing LLM and experts) followed by supervised fine-tuning on 1.6M instruction samples (freezing experts, jointly optimizing all other components). The dynamic number of activated experts (K varies per sample) consistently outperforms fixed-K variants, confirming that context-aware selection avoids biased information from irrelevant experts.

## Results

On MLLM benchmarks, MoVA-7B (Vicuna-7B) achieves 70.4 on MMBench, 69.3 on QBench, and 88.6 on POPE, surpassing LLaVA-NeXT-7B (67.4 MMB, 86.5 POPE) while processing only 20% of the image tokens. MoVA-34B (Hermes-Yi-34B) reaches 81.3 on MMBench and 79.0 on MMBench-CN, outperforming Gemini-Pro (73.6/74.3) and GPT-4V (75.8/73.9) on these benchmarks. On text-oriented VQA, MoVA-34B achieves 84.2 on DocVQA, 73.8 on ChartQA, and 83.0 on AI2D, surpassing the 55B-parameter specialist PALI-X-55B (80.0/70.9/81.2). On RefCOCO+ referring expression comprehension, MoVA-7B scores 87.70 (val), exceeding the specialist UNINEXT-H (85.24) by 2.46 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoVA-7B outperforms LLaVA-NeXT-7B on MMBench (70.4 vs. 67.4), MathVista (37.6 vs. 34.6), and POPE (88.6 vs. 86.5) while using only 576 image tokens versus 2880. MoVA-34B surpasses Gemini-Pro on MMBench (81.3 vs. 73.6), MMBench-CN (79.0 vs. 74.3), and QBench (70.7 vs. 68.2), and beats GPT-4V on MMBench (81.3 vs. 75.8) and MMBench-CN (79.0 vs. 73.9), though it trails GPT-4V on MathVista (44.3 vs. 49.9) and MathVerse (23.7 vs. 38.3). On text-oriented VQA, MoVA-34B (38B params) exceeds the 55B-parameter PALI-X-55B on DocVQA (84.2 vs. 80.0), ChartQA (73.8 vs. 70.9), TextVQA (77.8 vs. 71.4), and AI2D (83.0 vs. 81.2). On RefCOCO+ grounding, MoVA-7B (87.70 val) beats the specialist UNINEXT-H (85.24) and SPHINX-2k (85.51), and MoVA-34B further pushes to 89.64. On medical VQA, MoVA-7B improves over LLaVA-1.5 on SLAKE (78.2 vs. 73.1 open; 68.8 vs. 63.7 close) and VQA-RAD open (38.3 vs. 35.3). The plain fusion of all vision experts (Table 1) underperforms MoVA on every benchmark, confirming that context-aware routing avoids the bias introduced by irrelevant experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVA builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP encoder + MLP projector + LLM) and extends the emerging line of work that augments MLLMs with additional task-specific vision encoders (SPHINX adds DINOv2 for grounding, Vary adds a document encoder). The key conceptual departure is from the 'plain fusion' (concatenation) strategy used by prior multi-encoder MLLMs: MoVA instead introduces a two-stage coarse-to-fine mechanism where the LLM itself acts as a router to select context-relevant experts, and a MoV-Adapter with MoE cross-attention and dynamic gating performs fine-grained knowledge extraction and fusion. The LLM-as-router design draws on the tool-use paradigm, while the MoV-Adapter borrows structural ideas from Mixture-of-Experts networks applied at the vision-encoder level rather than within a single transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoVA addresses a practical and well-motivated limitation of current MLLMs: no single vision encoder (CLIP, DINOv2, etc.) dominates across all image content types, and naive multi-encoder fusion introduces biased information that degrades performance. The coarse-to-fine routing + fusion paradigm offers a flexible, extensible recipe for incorporating new vision experts without retraining the full model, which is practically valuable as the ecosystem of specialized vision encoders grows. Open questions include how the approach scales beyond 7–8 experts, whether routing generalizes to truly novel expert types at test time (the paper shows 92.5% on 105 hand-crafted samples but this is a small evaluation), and the acknowledged limitation that hallucination and expert failure cases can still degrade outputs. The computational overhead of running multiple frozen encoders in parallel remains a deployment concern despite the routing reducing the number of active experts.
