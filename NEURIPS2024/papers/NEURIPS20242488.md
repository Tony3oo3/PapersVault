## Problem

Deploying LLMs across diverse platforms requires models of varying depth and width, but existing compression methods either target a single dimension or demand a separate costly fine-tuning for each target configuration. Directly applying one-for-all training techniques to pre-trained LLMs fails because their subnet sampling strategies discard knowledge-critical layers and joint fine-tuning on small datasets causes severe gradient conflicts that degrade all subnets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242488/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA2 7B, Vicuna 7B v1.5) and a set of target depth/width remaining ratios (depth: 20–32 layers; width: {1, 7/8, 3/4, 5/8, 1/2}) as input, and produces a single one-for-all fine-tuned model from which subnets of any shape in the design space can be instantly extracted. At deployment, the shape-specific LoRAs are merged into the base weights, so the extracted subnet incurs no additional inference overhead beyond its reduced size.

## Method

AmoebaLLM integrates three components for one-for-all fine-tuning: (1) a knowledge-preserving subnet selection strategy that uses dynamic programming to evaluate joint layer contributions for depth shrinking and FLAP-style importance scores for structured width shrinking; (2) a Shape-aware Mixture of LoRAs (SMoL) adapter that sparsely activates a subset of T LoRAs via a noisy top-K gating function conditioned on the subnet shape (not the input), mitigating gradient conflicts while allowing all activated LoRAs to be merged into base weights at deployment; and (3) an in-place distillation objective with loss-magnitude balancing, where K subnets are sampled per iteration via sandwich sampling, the largest subnet provides ground-truth supervision, smaller subnets are distilled from it, and all losses are normalized to the largest subnet's magnitude to prevent smaller subnets' higher losses from dominating the gradient direction.

## Results

On LLaMA2 7B, AmoebaLLM's instantly extracted subnets achieve 40.70% MMLU and 62.29% average commonsense reasoning accuracy at 80% remaining ratio, outperforming FLAP (40.21/60.98), LLM-Pruner (29.63/56.95), and Shortened LLaMA (26.45/58.72). At the more aggressive 50% ratio, AmoebaLLM reaches 30.60% MMLU and 52.19% average, still leading all baselines by 2.9–7.7 points on MMLU. The per-subnet fine-tuned variant (AmoebaLLM†) further improves to 42.40% MMLU at 80% and 32.20% at 50%, establishing a new SOTA compression effectiveness. On Vicuna 7B v1.5, AmoebaLLM similarly leads, achieving 47.40% MMLU at 80% ratio versus FLAP's 43.50% and Shortened LLaMA's 35.27%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AmoebaLLM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every remaining ratio on both LLaMA2 7B and Vicuna 7B v1.5. Against FLAP [8] (the strongest baseline), AmoebaLLM gains +0.49% MMLU and +1.31% average at 80% ratio, widening to +2.93% MMLU and +1.07% average at 50% on LLaMA2 7B. Against LLM-Pruner [7], the MMLU gap is substantially larger: +11.07% at 80% and +7.70% at 50%, reflecting LLM-Pruner's aggressive knowledge loss. Against Shortened LLaMA [9], AmoebaLLM leads by +14.25% MMLU at 80% and +5.84% at 50%, while also surpassing it on average commonsense accuracy by 3.57 and 4.84 points respectively. The per-subnet fine-tuned variant AmoebaLLM† further extends these margins, e.g., reaching 42.40% MMLU at 80% on LLaMA2 7B versus FLAP's 40.21%, confirming that the DP-based subnet selection strategy itself constitutes a competitive standalone pruning method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-for-all network training paradigm (BigNAS, Once-for-All, AlphaNet) from models trained from scratch to pre-trained LLMs, where naive subnet sampling (first layers/channels) destroys encoded factual knowledge. It combines this with structured LLM pruning (LLM-Pruner, FLAP, Shortened LLaMA) by introducing a knowledge-preserving subnet selection strategy—dynamic programming for depth and importance-driven metrics for width—so that the one-for-all model retains the pre-trained model's capabilities. The SMoL adapter repurposes the noisy top-K gating mechanism from sparsely-gated MoE to sparsely activate a set of LoRAs based on subnet shape, and the fine-tuning objective extends BigNAS's in-place distillation with a loss-magnitude balancing term to counteract the unbalanced loss scales across differently-sized subnets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single one-time fine-tuning (40 GPU-hours on one A5000) yields a model from which subnets of arbitrary depth/width can be instantly extracted and deployed on diverse hardware without per-platform retraining, addressing a real bottleneck in LLM deployment. A key empirical insight is that factual knowledge lost during compression is difficult to recover via fine-tuning, while language modeling capability is more easily restored—this should inform calibration metric choices in future compression work. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B models (LLaMA2, Vicuna), reliance on parameter-efficient fine-tuning that caps the achievable accuracy-efficiency frontier, and a relatively small fine-tuning corpus (50K Alpaca samples). Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether more aggressive compression ratios remain viable, and whether the DP-based layer selection generalizes to non-residual architectures.
