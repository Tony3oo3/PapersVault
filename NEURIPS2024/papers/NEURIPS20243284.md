## Problem

Existing methods for computing the Schrödinger Bridge (the dynamic entropy-regularised optimal transport) require either training multiple DDM-type models at each iteration (as in DSBM/IMF) or relying on mini-batch OT approximations that introduce significant errors in high dimensions. This paper eliminates the need for repeated full model training by formulating the SB as the unique stationary point of a continuous-time flow of path measures, enabling a single online gradient-based update per step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired image samples from two source and target domains (e.g., MNIST digits ↔ EMNIST letters at 28×28, AFHQ Wild ↔ Cat at 64×64 or 256×256, CelebA Female ↔ Male at 64×64) and produces a learned transport map that maps a source image to its corresponding target-domain image. During the online finetuning stage, the model generates its own intermediate training data by solving forward and backward SDEs, so the input to the loss is a mix of real source/target samples and model-generated counterparts rather than purely real data.

## Method

The core contribution is the Schrödinger Bridge Flow: a continuous-time flow of path measures defined by ∂ₛP̂ˢ = proj_R(proj_M(P̂ˢ)) − P̂ˢ, whose unique fixed point is the SB. Discretising this flow with stepsize α∈(0,1] yields α-IMF, where α<1 replaces the expensive full Markovian projection (solving a regression to convergence) with a single gradient step on the bridge matching loss, making the procedure online. The practical α-DSBM algorithm uses a single bidirectional network parameterised with a direction indicator s∈{0,1} (instead of two separate networks), trained in two stages: (1) pretraining via standard bridge matching on true data pairs, and (2) online finetuning where the model samples its own endpoints via SDE integration and updates on a combined forward-backward loss with EMA parameters. A bidirectional formulation is used to prevent error accumulation that occurs with unidirectional online updates.

## Results

On EMNIST→MNIST translation, α-DSBM with online finetuning achieves FID 4.28±0.07 and MSD 0.368±0.001, improving FID over the iterative DSBM reimplementation (5.25±0.15) while MSD is slightly higher (0.368 vs 0.345). On AFHQ-64 Wild→Cat, the bidirectional α-DSBM (online finetuning) achieves FID 26.58±0.43 and LPIPS 0.482±0.001, competitive with iterative DSBM (FID 25.41±0.84, LPIPS 0.485±0.003). In the Gaussian setting where the EOT coupling is tractable, α-DSBM recovers the true covariance faster than iterative DSBM, and the gap narrows as the alternation frequency in DSBM increases toward the online regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), the iterative Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching procedure that alternates between training forward and backward bridge-matching models with a sample cache. On EMNIST→MNIST, α-DSBM with online finetuning (row b) improves FID from 5.25 to 4.28 (≈18% relative reduction) but sacrifices a small amount of alignment (MSD 0.368 vs 0.345). On AFHQ-64, the bidirectional α-DSBM (row d) achieves FID 26.58 and LPIPS 0.482, broadly matching the iterative DSBM (FID 25.41, LPIPS 0.485) while eliminating the need for a sample cache, alternating losses, and multiple inner-training loops. In the Gaussian toy experiment (Figure 2), α-DSBM converges to the true EOT covariance faster than iterative DSBM at every checkpoint, and the convergence rate of DSBM approaches that of α-DSBM as the alternation frequency increases. The authors note that FID is not ideal for MNIST (designed for natural RGB images) and for AFHQ's small test sets (<500 examples), so visual inspection is recommended alongside the quantitative metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure and its practical implementation DSBM (Shi et al., 2023; Peluchetti, 2023), which solve the Schrödinger Bridge problem by alternating between Markovian and reciprocal projections. The key departure is introducing a continuous-time flow of path measures whose discretization with stepsize α∈(0,1] yields a family of algorithms (α-IMF) that generalize IMF (α=1) to online, single-gradient-step updates (α<1). This eliminates DSBM's need to fully train a new DDM-type model at each iteration, cache generated samples, and alternate between two separate losses, while also enabling a single bidirectional network to replace two separate forward/backward networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method significantly simplifies the practical implementation of Schrödinger Bridge solvers by removing the expensive inner-loop minimization and associated hyperparameters (cache size, refresh rate, inner/outer iteration counts), making SB-based transport more accessible. However, the method remains not sampling-free—it still requires generating samples from the current model during finetuning—which limits its scalability relative to fully sampling-free approaches. The empirical gains over DSBM are modest and inconsistent across datasets (clearly better on EMNIST→MNIST FID, but worse on AFHQ FID), and the primary remaining hyperparameter ε still requires careful tuning to balance visual quality and alignment. Open questions include whether a truly sampling-free SB solver exists without resorting to minibatch OT approximations, and whether the method scales to higher-resolution or more complex translation tasks.
