## Problem

No publicly available remote sensing dataset simultaneously provides multi-modal images (RGB, SAR, NIR), information-rich text descriptions, multiple GSD levels, and diverse weather/scene conditions, making it impossible to train a single text-to-image model that generates comprehensive RS imagery. Existing datasets like RSICD offer only simple captions without multi-modal support, while SEN1-2 provides aligned multi-modal pairs but lacks any textual descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240388/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model consumes a structured text prompt composed of four concatenated fields—GSD level (e.g., 'High precision resolution'), weather type (e.g., 'snow'), a BLIP-2-generated content description, and satellite type (e.g., 'Google Earth')—and produces a 512×512 RS image. For cross-modal generation via ControlNet, an additional conditioning image (RGB, SAR, or NIR) is fed alongside the text prompt to produce the target modality at the same 512×512 resolution. All samples in the dataset are standardized to 512×512, with GSD values recomputed after resizing to preserve physical scale information.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction pipeline that unifies nine heterogeneous RS datasets into 2.1 million information-rich text-image pairs. Text prompts are generated by combining three components: GSD-level labels derived from a five-tier classification, annotation metadata (satellite type, weather) extracted from source datasets, and content descriptions produced by BLIP-2, followed by manual screening. Multi-scene samples (fog, snow, low-light) are synthesized from 10,000 RGB images using an atmospheric scattering model, TPSeNCE, and a CycleGAN trained on 460 paired snow/clear images respectively. Multi-GSD variants are created by cropping nested square regions of decreasing size from 5000×5000 Inria tiles and standardizing each to 512×512 with ESRGAN super-resolution, yielding four GSD levels per sample. For generation, Stable Diffusion 1.5 is fine-tuned with LoRA for 200K iterations on the full dataset, while ControlNet-based cross-modal models are trained on ~290K RGB-SAR pairs and 7K RGB-NIR pairs for 80K and 20K iterations respectively.

## Results

On the MMM-RS benchmark, the proposed method (SD1.5 fine-tuned with LoRA on MMM-RS) achieves a FID of 92.33 and an Inception Score of 7.21, substantially outperforming all baselines. The FID improvement over the strongest baseline (SDXL at 168.34) is approximately 45%, while the IS gain over SDXL (6.89) is 0.32 points. The most dramatic margin is against DALL-E 3, where FID drops from 347.88 to 92.33 (73% reduction) and IS rises from 2.63 to 7.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its MMM-RS fine-tuned model against four off-the-shelf generative models: SD1.5, SD2.0, SDXL, and DALL-E 3. The proposed method wins decisively on both FID and IS across all four baselines. Against the closest competitor SDXL (FID 168.34, IS 6.89), the MMM-RS model reduces FID by 76 points (45%) and improves IS by 0.32. Against SD1.5 (FID 172.78, IS 6.64) and SD2.0 (FID 175.68, IS 6.31), the gains are similar in magnitude, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning on MMM-RS is the key driver rather than the base architecture. DALL-E 3 performs worst by a large margin (FID 347.88, IS 2.63), suggesting that general-purpose text-to-image models struggle significantly with the top-down perspective and scale characteristics of remote sensing imagery. Qualitative results further show that only the MMM-RS model faithfully renders weather-specific scenes (snow, fog, low-light) and produces visibly distinct GSD levels from text prompts, while baselines fail to capture these domain-specific attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of remote sensing data curation and diffusion-based text-to-image generation. It builds directly on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) as the generative backbone and ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) for cross-modal synthesis, while leveraging BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023) for automatic caption generation and CycleGAN/TPSeNCE for scene-conditioned data augmentation. Conceptually, it departs from prior RS datasets (RSICD, SEN1-2, fMoW, etc.) that each cover only a subset of modalities, text richness, or resolution levels, by unifying nine sources into a single standardized corpus with information-rich prompts that jointly encode GSD, weather, satellite type, and scene content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMM-RS is the first large-scale RS dataset that simultaneously supports multi-modal (RGB/SAR/NIR), multi-GSD, and multi-scene text-to-image generation, filling a clear gap identified in the literature. The 2.1M text-image pairs make it a practical resource for fine-tuning off-the-shelf diffusion models in the RS domain, and the GSD extraction strategy (cropping at multiple scales from Inria's 5000×5000 tiles) is a reusable technique for creating resolution-conditioned training data. However, the experimental validation is limited: baselines are unmodified off-the-shelf models (no RS-specific fine-tuning), no error bars are reported, and the synthesized scenes (fog, snow, low-light) rely on general-purpose models whose fidelity in the RS context is not rigorously evaluated. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., hyperspectral, LiDAR) and whether the GSD conditioning transfers to non-Inria sources with different native resolutions.
