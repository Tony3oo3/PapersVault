## Problem

DPO's implicit reward, defined as the log-ratio between the policy and a reference model, is misaligned with the average log-likelihood metric that actually guides generation at inference time. This train-inference mismatch means that satisfying DPO's reward ranking does not guarantee the correct likelihood ranking (only ~50% of training triples satisfy both), and the requirement for a reference model adds unnecessary memory and compute overhead.

## Input / Output

SimPO consumes offline preference triples (prompt x, winning response y_w, losing response y_l) drawn from datasets like UltraFeedback, where responses are either pre-annotated or generated by the SFT model and scored by a reward model (e.g., PairRM). The output is a fine-tuned policy model that generates text responses; no reference model is needed at either training or inference time, reducing per-GPU peak memory by ~10% and runtime by ~20% compared to DPO.

## Method

SimPO replaces DPO's log-ratio reward with a length-normalized reward r_SimPO(x,y) = (β/|y|) log π_θ(y|x), i.e., the average per-token log probability under the policy model, which directly matches the metric used for beam search and generation. This eliminates the reference model entirely. A second design choice introduces a target reward margin γ > 0 into the Bradley-Terry objective, so the model is trained to push the reward gap r(x,y_w) − r(x,y_l) above γ rather than merely positive. The final loss is −E[log σ(β/|y_w| log π_θ(y_w|x) − β/|y_l| log π_θ(y_l|x) − γ)]. Length normalization is critical: removing it causes the model to exploit length bias, producing longer but lower-quality responses, while the margin term improves reward accuracy on held-out data up to an optimal γ before over-regularization degrades generation quality.

## Results

SimPO consistently achieves the best performance across all four training settings (Mistral-Base, Mistral-Instruct, Llama-3-Base, Llama-3-Instruct) on AlpacaEval 2 LC win rate and Arena-Hard win rate. On the strongest Llama-3-Instruct setting, SimPO reaches 44.7% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 33.8% on Arena-Hard, outperforming DPO (40.3% / 32.6%) by 4.4 and 1.2 points respectively. The paper's top model (Gemma-2-9B-it-SimPO) achieves 72.4% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 59.1% on Arena-Hard, ranking 1st among <10B models on Chatbot Arena. SimPO also reduces training time by ~20% and GPU memory by ~10% compared to DPO by eliminating the reference model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimPO outperforms all eight baselines on AlpacaEval 2 LC across every setting, with the largest margins over ORPO (up to +16.2 pts on Llama-3-Instruct) and SLiC-HF (up to +17.8 pts). Against DPO, SimPO gains up to 6.4 LC points (Mistral-Base: 21.5 vs. 15.1) and up to 7.5 Arena-Hard points (Llama-3-Base: 23.4 vs. 15.9). R-DPO is the closest competitor on AlpacaEval 2 (41.1 vs. 44.7 on Llama-3-Instruct) but still trails SimPO by 3.6 LC points; on Arena-Hard, R-DPO is competitive (33.1 vs. 33.8) but SimPO still edges ahead. CPO occasionally surpasses SimPO on Arena-Hard in the Mistral-Instruct setting (22.6 vs. 21.0), which the authors attribute to CPO generating ~50% longer responses that Arena-Hard's evaluation does not penalize. IPO beats SimPO on Arena-Hard in the Llama-3-Base setting (17.8 vs. 23.4 is actually SimPO winning; IPO's 17.8 is lower), so SimPO wins across the board on Arena-Hard as well. Overall, SimPO is the only method that simultaneously leads on both AlpacaEval 2 LC and Arena-Hard in all four settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimPO builds directly on DPO's reparameterization of the RLHF reward function, but departs from it by replacing the log-ratio reward (policy vs. reference model) with a length-normalized average log-likelihood, thereby eliminating the reference model entirely. It also incorporates a target reward margin into the Bradley-Terry objective, a concept shared with IPO, but applied to a fundamentally different reward formulation. The paper positions itself alongside other reference-free objectives like ORPO and CPO, but distinguishes itself by explicitly aligning the training reward with the generation metric (average log-likelihood used in beam search and ranking), a connection prior methods had not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SimPO offers a practically significant simplification: removing the reference model cuts training time by ~20% and GPU memory by ~10%, while simultaneously improving performance by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 and 7.5 on Arena-Hard over DPO. The finding that DPO's implicit reward achieves only ~50% ranking accuracy in terms of the average log-likelihood that actually guides generation is a conceptual insight with broad implications for how preference optimization objectives should be designed. Open questions remain around the lack of explicit safety/honesty constraints, the performance drop on reasoning-heavy tasks like GSM8K, the need for manual tuning of the margin γ, and the absence of a rigorous theoretical analysis explaining why the simpler reward formulation generalizes better.
