## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods are prone to dimensional collapse, where a few large eigenvalues dominate the eigenspace, but existing remedies (representation whitening, projector removal, covariance regularizers) target only the final representations with no theoretical guarantee of preventing collapse in the encoder's weight matrices and hidden features. The paper demonstrates that feature whitening (as in VICREG) actually accelerates weight-matrix collapse while leaving hidden features partially collapsed, exposing a critical blind spot in prior SSL regularization strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) that are split into two augmented views and passed through a shared or dual encoder (ResNet-18/50, WideResNet-28w2, or ViT-tiny/small/base) followed by a projector. During pretraining, the orthogonality regularizer is computed on every convolutional and linear weight matrix inside the encoder, adding a per-layer penalty to the SSL objective. The frozen encoder representations are then used for linear-probe classification (Top-1/Top-5) or fine-tuned for object detection on PASCAL VOC.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-in orthogonality regularization (OR) term added to the SSL loss: Loss = Loss_SSL + γ · Loss_OR, where Loss_OR is the sum over all encoder weight matrices of either Soft Orthogonality (Frobenius norm of WᵀW − I) or SRIP (spectral norm of WᵀW − I, estimated via two power iterations). Convolutional filters are reshaped into 2-D matrices so the same regularizer applies uniformly to conv and linear layers. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that an orthogonal weight matrix preserves the normalization, decorrelation, and Frobenius norm of its input, so enforcing orthogonality at the weight level guarantees that hidden features and final representations cannot collapse as depth increases—something representation-level whitening cannot ensure. No architectural or hyperparameter changes to the SSL method are required; the encoder is simply passed into the OR loss, and γ is tuned per backbone (e.g., 1e-6 for SO on ResNet-18/50). The approach is validated across 13 SSL methods (contrastive and non-contrastive) on both CNN and Transformer backbones, yielding consistent 1–12% Top-1 gains on CIFAR-100 and ~3% on ImageNet-1k with negligible time overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet18, OR (SO) consistently improves linear probe Top-1 accuracy across all five SSL methods tested: MOCOv2plus (69.51→69.72), MOCOv3 (67.13→68.63), DINO (59.13→61.41), NNBYOL (68.87→71.40), and BYOL (71.15→72.15). On the large-scale ImageNet-1k benchmark, BYOL with SO improves Top-1 accuracy from 65.81% to 67.84% and object detection AP from 44.74 to 53.81 on PASCAL VOC. On ViT backbones, DINO with SO achieves up to a 6.7-point gain (ViT-tiny: 54.16→60.84 Top-1 on CIFAR-100), demonstrating strong scaling with model depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OR (both SO and SRIP) outperforms the original SSL methods across all 13 baselines tested, with gains ranging from ~0.1% (NNCLR: 82.31→82.34) to ~5% (SimSiam: 79.27→84.31 on CIFAR-10). Non-contrastive methods benefit more than contrastive ones: DINO gains 2.3–3.6 points and NNBYOL gains 2.5–3.1 points on CIFAR-100 ResNet18, while MOCOv3 gains only 1.5–2.2 points. On ImageNet-1k, BYOL with SO/SRIP improves Top-1 by ~2 points (65.81→67.84/67.91) and object detection AP by ~9 points (44.74→53.81), substantially outperforming the no-OR baseline. Compared to VICREG's feature whitening technique (which only addresses representation-level collapse), OR achieves higher accuracy (93.04 vs. 92.66 Top-1 on CIFAR-10 BYOL) while also preventing weight-matrix collapse. On ViT backbones, OR's advantage grows with depth (ViT-tiny +6.7, ViT-small +2.9, ViT-base +2.8 Top-1), suggesting linear layers in Transformers are more prone to dimensional collapse than convolutional layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) the dimensional collapse literature in SSL, where prior methods (VICREG, Barlow Twins, projector removal) act on representations or projector outputs to prevent collapse, and (2) orthogonality regularization in DNNs, previously applied to supervised classification, image retrieval, and adversarial robustness. The key departure is that prior SSL collapse-prevention methods constrain representations and hope to indirectly influence encoder internals, whereas this paper directly regularizes encoder weight matrices (both convolutional and linear layers) via Soft Orthogonality or SRIP, with a theoretical argument (Proposition 1) that orthogonal weights propagate uniform eigenvalue distributions to hidden features and representations. The paper also explicitly demonstrates that VICREG-style feature whitening, while fixing representation collapse, can actually accelerate weight-matrix collapse—a gap not addressed by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that OR is a drop-in plug-in requiring no architecture or hyperparameter changes, yielding consistent 1–3% linear-probe gains across 13 SSL methods and both CNN and ViT backbones, with a notable 20% AP boost on object detection at ImageNet-1k scale. The conceptual finding that representation-level whitening can worsen weight-matrix redundancy challenges the assumption that fixing collapse at the output suffices. Open questions include whether OR transfers to generative SSL (MAE), autoregressive language models, and CLIP-style pretraining, and whether the gains persist under official (longer-training, multi-crop) SSL recipes rather than the abbreviated solo-learn/LightSSL protocols used here. The absence of error bars and the reliance on non-official training settings temper confidence in the exact magnitudes reported.
