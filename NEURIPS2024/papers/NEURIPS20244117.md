## Problem

In multiclass online learning, the price of bandit feedback (receiving only a correct/incorrect signal) versus full information was known only for deterministic learners. This paper resolves the open problem posed by Daniely and Helbertal [2013] by characterizing the optimal mistake bound for randomized learners under bandit feedback, and further quantifies the separate costs of adaptive adversaries and the absence of randomness—showing that, unlike in the full-information setting, these resources create non-trivial separations in the bandit model.

## Input / Output

The learner receives an instance x_t each round and, after predicting, only a binary bandit signal (correct or incorrect) rather than the true label. In the expert-advice variant, n experts each emit a deterministic label prediction per round, and the learner must aggregate them under the promise that the best expert is inconsistent with the feedback at most r times. The output is a (possibly randomized) label prediction per round, with the guarantee expressed as a worst-case expected mistake bound that is independent of the horizon T.

## Method

The proof of the main upper bound (opt_bandit^adap(H) = O(|Y| · opt_full^rand(H))) proceeds via a two-step reduction: first, a tree-of-copies construction reduces learning H to prediction with expert advice using |Y|^{opt_full^det(H)} experts and r = opt_full^det(H) allowed inconsistencies; second, a new nearly-optimal randomized algorithm for expert advice under bandit feedback is proved. The key technical novelty is the use of von Neumann's minimax duality to transform the primal game (learner chooses distribution, then adversary draws the label) into a dual game (adversary chooses distribution, then learner predicts), which reduces the recurrence from k recursive calls to only two, making induction tractable. For the r-realizable case, experts with remaining budget i are assigned potential k^{2i}, so the total potential n·k^{2r} replaces the expert count in the recurrence, yielding the bound k·log_k(n·k^{2r}) = k·log_k(n) + 2kr. This potential-based weighted-experts analysis, combined with the minimax simplification, gives the tight Θ(k(log_k n + r)) bound for the adaptive adversary.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.1) establishes that the optimal mistake bound under bandit feedback is at most O(|Y|) times the optimal full-information randomized mistake bound, i.e., opt_bandit^adap(H) = O(opt_full^rand(H) · |Y|), with a matching Ω(|Y|) lower bound. For prediction with expert advice (Theorem 1.6), the paper proves the tight bound opt_bandit^adap(n,k,r) = Θ(k(log_k n + r)), generalizing the known binary-case result Θ(log n + r). The paper also shows a Θ(|Y|) gap (up to log factors) between adaptive and oblivious adversaries, and between randomized and deterministic learners, in the bandit feedback setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between theoretical bounds. The paper resolves an open problem posed by Daniely and Helbertal [2013] by proving the first tight (up to constants) bound for randomized learners under bandit feedback, improving the deterministic bound of Auer and Long [1999] (which gave O(opt_full^det · |Y| log |Y|)) to O(opt_full^rand · |Y|) for randomized learners, removing the log |Y| factor. The lower bound matches the one noted by Daniely and Helbertal [2013] and Long [2020] for the deterministic case, extended here to the randomized setting. For prediction with expert advice, the paper's Θ(k(log_k n + r)) bound improves upon the known regret-based bounds of Auer et al. [2002] (which yield O(√(Tk log n)) regret with dependence on horizon T) by providing a mistake-bound guarantee with no T-dependence. The paper also shows that the Bandit-Littlestone dimension, previously conjectured to quantify randomized bandit mistake bounds, is in fact far from doing so (Theorem 1.4), as the randomized bound can be as small as √(opt_bandit^det) in some cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a central open question posed by Daniely and Helbertal (2013) and Long (2020): what is the price of bandit feedback for randomized learners in multiclass online classification? It builds on the deterministic bounds of Auer and Long (1999) and the reduction framework of Long (2020) and Hanneke, Livni, and Moran (2021), but the key technical novelty is a nearly optimal randomized algorithm for prediction with expert advice under bandit feedback, proved via minimax duality and exponential potential functions. The paper also draws on the Littlestone dimension and SOA algorithm (Littlestone, 1988) and the Weighted Majority framework (Littlestone and Warmuth, 1994) as foundational tools, extending them to the bandit-feedback and adaptive-adversary settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a nearly complete map of how three resources—information (full vs. bandit), adaptivity (oblivious vs. adaptive adversary), and randomness (deterministic vs. randomized learner)—interact in multiclass online learning, with tight or near-tight bounds for each combination. A notable negative result is that the Bandit-Littlestone dimension, which precisely characterizes deterministic mistake bounds, fails to capture randomized bounds, closing a line of hope from prior work. Key open questions remain: whether the adaptivity gap lower bound can be proved for concept classes (rather than pattern classes), the exact price of not using randomness (a Θ(log k) gap remains), and tight oblivious-adversary bounds for large r in the expert-advice setting.
