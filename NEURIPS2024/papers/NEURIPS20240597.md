## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking schemes (KGW, Aaronson, inverse transform) inherently trade text quality against detectability: achieving high detectability requires increasing watermark strength or temperature, which degrades the generated text. Moreover, their performance is highly sensitive to the entropy of the LLM's token distribution, making them unreliable across different models and prompts. WaterMax addresses this by reframing the trade-off: instead of sacrificing quality for robustness, it trades robustness for computational complexity, leaving the LLM's generation process entirely unmodified.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a secret key as input, and produces a watermarked text along with a detection p-value at the decoder side. Critically, the LLM's weights, logits, temperature, and sampling technique (e.g., nucleus sampling with top_p=0.95) are left completely untouched; watermarking is achieved purely by selecting among multiple generated drafts. The detector operates on the received text alone without requiring access to the original LLM, and works on sub-sequences of tokens (chunks) rather than individual tokens, enabling detection even on very short texts.

## Method

WaterMax splits text generation into N chunks, and for each chunk generates n independent drafts (with the first b tokens produced via beam-search to enforce diversity), then selects the draft with the maximum cumulative score (equivalently, the minimum local p-value). Token scores are computed by hashing a causal window of h preceding tokens with the secret key to seed a PRNG, yielding i.i.d. standard Gaussian variables per token. The detection uses either an optimal per-chunk likelihood-ratio test (aggregating N local p-values via a sum of log-terms) or a robust global score (summing all token variables), both analyzed under a Neyman-Pearson framework with closed-form false-alarm and true-positive rates. A key theoretical result shows that because chunk-level probabilities are extremely small (|V|^ℓ possible chunks), the selection mechanism is approximately distortion-free, explaining the negligible quality loss. The Viterbi-style beam search over chunks (keeping m best candidates, typically m=1 for low latency) reduces the combinatorial explosion from n^N to Nn generations while preserving near-optimal detectability.

## Results

WaterMax (N,n,b)=(16,10,6) achieves a true-positive rate P_D ≈ 1.0 at a false-positive rate P_FA = 10⁻⁶ on 256-token texts generated by Llama-3-8b-Instruct, while incurring a relative perplexity of only ~1.0–1.2 (measured by opt-2.7b oracle), i.e. virtually no quality degradation. The naive 'generate n full texts' baseline would require >3 million texts of 512 tokens to reach P_D = 0.96 at P_FA = 10⁻⁶, whereas WaterMax needs only Nn = 135 chunk drafts (equivalent to 15 full texts). In terms of robustness against the MMW attack suite at θ = 0.8, WaterMax retains the highest detectability across synonym, misspelling, and swap attacks, while KGW (δ=2.0) and Aaronson degrade more sharply. Total generation time is ~5× that of KGW/Aaronson, but per-chunk latency is only ~2× the baseline LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW [21], WaterMax wins decisively on the detectability–quality trade-off: at δ=3.0 KGW still shows a relative perplexity well above WaterMax's ~1.0–1.2 while remaining less detectable, and at δ=2.0 KGW is even less detectable. On robustness (MMW attack suite, θ=0.8), WaterMax's global robust detector (Eq. 5) retains significantly higher P_D than KGW after synonym, misspelling, and swap attacks. Against Aaronson [1], WaterMax is superior at low-to-moderate temperatures (θ ≤ 0.9) where Aaronson's detectability drops sharply due to its heavy reliance on token-level entropy; however, at θ ≥ 1.0 Aaronson can match or slightly exceed WaterMax on specific attacks (e.g., synonym) because high-entropy tokens produce unusually low local p-values that are statistically more resistant to partial modification. WaterMax's key structural advantage is that its detectability is essentially independent of the LLM temperature and the specific LLM (validated on Llama-2-7b-chat, Llama-3-8b-Instruct, and Phi-3-mini-4k-Instruct), whereas both KGW and Aaronson show large performance swings across models and temperatures. The sole cost of WaterMax is computational: ~5× total generation time and ~2× per-chunk latency compared to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaterMax departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in LLM watermarking (KGW, Aaronson, Kuditipudi/inverse-transform), which all operate by modifying the next-token distribution or sampling procedure to embed a signal. Instead, WaterMax treats the LLM as an unmodified stochastic oracle and frames watermarking as a search problem: generate multiple candidate texts and select the one whose score is most favorable to detection, using a Viterbi-like beam search over chunks. The detection side draws on the Neyman-Pearson hypothesis-testing framework and the p-value machinery already established in Fernandez et al.'s 'Three Bricks' and the Mark My Words benchmark, but the embedding mechanism—multi-draft selection over sub-sequences rather than per-token biasing—is new. The theoretical analysis (beta/gamma distributions of local p-values, closed-form power under attack) extends the statistical watermarking literature by providing exact detectability formulas that are independent of text entropy, a property no prior scheme achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WaterMax breaks the long-standing quality-robustness-entropy trade-off that constrains all prior LLM watermarking methods: detectability near 1 at P_FA = 10⁻⁶ is achieved at virtually zero quality loss and, crucially, independently of the LLM's temperature or the model's inherent entropy. The main practical limitation is a ~5× computational overhead from generating N×n chunk drafts, which the authors mitigate through GPU parallelism and chunk-by-chunk streaming but which remains a barrier to deployment. The authors identify distillation—fine-tuning an LLM to natively produce watermarked text—as the natural next step that would eliminate the sampling cost entirely, and the paper's theoretical framework (Propositions 4.1, 5.1, 5.2) provides the exact performance targets such a distilled model must meet. An open question is whether the beam-search diversity enforcement (parameter b) can be replaced by cheaper mechanisms without degrading the independence assumptions that underpin the theoretical guarantees.
