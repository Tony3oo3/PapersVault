## Problem

Hybrid Digital Twins (HDTwins) that compose mechanistic and neural components offer superior generalization, sample efficiency, and evolvability over purely neural or mechanistic models, but their design has traditionally required human experts to hand-craft the model architecture. Automatically specifying and optimizing HDTwins is intractable due to the vast combinatorial search space over model structures and the need for flexible integration of partial domain knowledge, leaving a gap between the promise of hybrid modeling and its practical scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242303/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured natural-language prompt (the modeling context) specifying the system description with variable names, units, and ranges, quantitative modeling objectives, and qualitative requirements (e.g., interpretability constraints, known functional relationships), along with offline state-action trajectory data split into train/validation/test sets. The output is an executable PyTorch nn.Module (a 'StateDifferential' class) whose forward method returns per-component state derivatives, combining closed-form mechanistic equations with optional MLP residual networks in an additive composition, with all parameters fitted to the training data.

## Method

HDTwinGen is an evolutionary framework that uses two LLM agents to jointly optimize model specification and parameterization. The modeling agent generates novel HDTwin specifications as Python code by filling in a predefined skeleton, leveraging in-context learning from the top-K previously generated models and natural-language feedback from the evaluation agent; the evaluation agent analyzes the current population against expert-defined requirements and produces targeted, component-specific improvement suggestions. For each generated specification, parameters are optimized via Adam on the training set, and the resulting model is scored by validation MSE (overall and per-component) to update the population. Over G generations, this iterative propose-optimize-evaluate loop navigates the combinatorial specification space, with the LLM's domain knowledge injecting physically plausible structure and its reasoning capabilities enabling intelligent modifications analogous to human expert model design.

## Results

HDTwinGen achieves the lowest test prediction MSE on 5 out of 6 benchmark datasets, with particularly strong gains on the Lung Cancer (with Chemo.) dataset (0.0889 MSE vs. 0.348 for the next-best Transformer) and the Plankton Microcosm (2.51e-06 MSE vs. 3.69e-05 for Transformer). On the Lung Cancer (with Chemo. & Radio.) dataset, HDTwinGen achieves 0.131 MSE, outperforming ZeroOptim (6.08) by over 40×. The only dataset where HDTwinGen does not achieve the best result is COVID-19, where the Transformer baseline (0.309 MSE) slightly outperforms HDTwinGen (1.72 MSE), though HDTwinGen still substantially outperforms all other baselines (next-best: ZeroOptim at 7.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDTwinGen outperforms all six baselines on five of six datasets. Against the strongest neural baseline (Transformer), HDTwinGen wins on Lung Cancer (4.41 vs. 7.48 MSE), Lung Cancer with Chemo. (0.0889 vs. 0.348), Lung Cancer with Chemo. & Radio. (0.131 vs. 0.216), Hare-Lynx (291 vs. 716), and Plankton Microcosm (2.51e-06 vs. 3.69e-05), but loses on COVID-19 (1.72 vs. 0.309). Against mechanistic discovery methods (SINDy, GP), HDTwinGen achieves orders-of-magnitude improvements on the lung cancer datasets (e.g., 0.0889 vs. 11.8 for SINDy on Lung Cancer with Chemo.) and Plankton Microcosm (2.51e-06 vs. 0.00135). Against the hybrid baseline APHYNITY, HDTwinGen wins on all lung cancer variants and Hare-Lynx, while APHYNITY is competitive on Plankton Microcosm (4.21e-05 vs. 2.51e-06) and both lose to Transformer on COVID-19. The ablation ZeroOptim (single LLM-generated model with parameter optimization but no evolutionary loop) is consistently outperformed by the full HDTwinGen, confirming the value of the iterative evolutionary process.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three established threads: neural sequence models (Neural ODEs, RNNs, Transformers), symbolic equation discovery (SINDy, Genetic Programming, D-CODE), and hybrid physics-informed models (PINNs, APHYNITY, Hamiltonian NNs). While hybrid models combining mechanistic and neural components have been studied, all prior work relies on human experts to specify the model architecture, with learning limited to parameter optimization. HDTwinGen is positioned as the first framework to jointly and automatically optimize both the model specification (symbolic structure + neural architecture) and its parameters, repurposing LLMs as an evolutionary search mechanism over the combinatorial space of hybrid model designs, coupled with offline gradient-based parameter fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automated design of hybrid digital twins addresses a practical bottleneck: hybrid models offer superior generalization and interpretability but have been limited to expert-crafted architectures, constraining scalability. The OOD generalization results (Table 2) are the strongest empirical contribution, showing that the hybrid structure provides substantially more robust extrapolation than either pure neural or pure mechanistic approaches under distribution shift. However, the method's efficacy depends critically on the LLM's domain knowledge and the quality of the initial human-provided system description, and the current scope is limited to continuous-time, relatively low-dimensional systems. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional or discrete-time systems, reducing dependence on expert prompts, and formalizing the search guarantees of the LLM-driven evolutionary loop.
