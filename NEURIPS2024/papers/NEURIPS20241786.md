## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) suffer from slow convergence, requiring over 4700 GPU hours for ImageNet 256×256 generation. Existing training strategies—noise scheduling, loss weighting, and timestep sampling—do not consistently perform well across different data conditions, and the paper identifies that the absolute (data-dependent) SNR, not just the relative SNR, is the key factor determining training effectiveness. There is also a fundamental trade-off between performance ceiling and robustness that prior work has not systematically characterized.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (one of 1000 ImageNet categories) as conditioning input and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. During training, input images are scaled to a target standard deviation of 0.82 before being noised via the flow matching interpolation x_t = t·data + (1−t)·noise. At inference, classifier-free guidance (CFG=1.5 for FID evaluation, CFG=4.0 for visualization) is applied, and 50,000 samples are generated for FID-50k computation.

## Method

FasterDiT accelerates DiT training without any architectural modification through three components. First, it extends the SNR definition to include a data-dependent signal coefficient K(I) ∝ std², enabling analysis of the Probability Density Function (PDF) of absolute SNR during training, which reveals that optimal performance requires the PDF focus to fall within a specific SNR range. Second, it selects flow matching with v-prediction (for its superior robustness over noise prediction), modulates the data standard deviation to ~0.82 to shift the PDF into the optimal range, and applies lognorm(0,1) timestep sampling to concentrate the PDF focus. Third, it introduces a velocity direction loss (1 − cosine_similarity(pred, v)) as an auxiliary supervision signal alongside the standard MSE loss, which significantly accelerates convergence. Together, these changes achieve FID 2.30 at 1000k iterations versus DiT's 2.27 at 7000k iterations—a 7× speedup.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with CFG=1.5, FasterDiT achieves FID 2.30 at only 1,000k iterations, comparable to DiT's 2.27 at 7,000k iterations (7× faster convergence). At 2,000k iterations, FasterDiT reaches FID 2.03, surpassing SiT's 2.06 at 7,000k iterations. Without CFG, FasterDiT achieves FID 7.91 at 2,000k iterations versus DiT's 9.62 and SiT's 8.61 at 7,000k iterations. At 400k iterations, FasterDiT (FID 11.9) substantially outperforms both DiT (FID 19.5) and SiT (FID 17.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FasterDiT directly outperforms DiT and SiT at every matched iteration count: at 400k iterations, FasterDiT achieves FID 11.9 versus DiT's 19.5 and SiT's 17.2; at 200k iterations, FasterDiT reaches FID 17.5 versus SiT's 27.1. At the full 7,000k-iteration budget, FasterDiT at 2,000k iterations (FID 2.03) surpasses SiT at 7,000k (FID 2.06) and DiT at 7,000k (FID 2.27), requiring only ~3× fewer iterations. Against non-DiT baselines, FasterDiT at 2,000k iterations (FID 2.03) is competitive with StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) and MDT (FID 2.15), and substantially outperforms BigGAN (6.95), MaskGIT (6.18), ADM (4.59), and LDM (3.60). The paper also demonstrates generalization to other architectures: applying FasterDiT's training strategy to U-ViT-L reduces FID from 50.22 to 37.12, and to LDM-UNet from 66.73 to 60.07 at 200k iterations on 128×128 ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the SiT flow-matching pipeline (Ma et al., 2024), which itself extends Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022). Rather than modifying the architecture (as MaskDiT, MDT, or SD-DiT do), it operates entirely in the training-strategy space: noise scheduling, timestep sampling, and loss design. Its key conceptual departure is reframing these strategies through a data-dependent SNR PDF lens, unifying previously separate knobs (schedule, loss weight, sampling distribution) into a single interpretable object, and empirically demonstrating that strategies like SD3's lognorm sampling are data-dependent rather than universally optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× training speedup at comparable FID (2.30 vs. 2.27) with only a few lines of code change is practically significant for anyone training DiT-scale models, and the generalization to U-ViT and LDM-UNet suggests broad applicability. The SNR PDF framework offers a principled diagnostic tool for choosing and tuning training hyperparameters across datasets and resolutions, addressing a gap where prior guidance was largely heuristic. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image (where multi-modal token sequences may destabilize the SNR dynamics), video generation, and very high-resolution (2K+) settings, all of which the authors acknowledge as unexplored.
