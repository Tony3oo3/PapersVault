## Problem

The cosine learning rate schedule, the de-facto standard for LLM pretraining, requires the total training duration to be known in advance and matched exactly to the schedule length to achieve optimal loss. This forces scaling law experiments to train each model from scratch at every target token count, multiplying compute costs by an order of magnitude and making it impractical to update scaling laws frequently as data mixtures and architectures evolve.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text corpora (SlimPajama 6B tokens, FineWeb/FineWeb-Edu at 100B–460B tokens, OpenWebText2) with decoder-only transformer models ranging from 33M to 8B parameters. The key operational difference from cosine is that a single long constant-LR training run per model size produces reusable checkpoints; cooldowns or SWA windows are then applied retrospectively at arbitrary token counts to obtain final models, eliminating the need for separate runs at each duration. Outputs include validation loss curves, downstream benchmark scores (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, etc.), and scaling-law loss envelopes as a function of FLOPs.

## Method

The paper replaces the cosine decay phase with a constant learning rate followed by a short cooldown (10–20% of total steps) that drives the LR to zero, formalized as a piecewise schedule with a monotonically decreasing function f during the decay window. A novel (1-sqrt) cooldown form, f = 1 − √((n−(N−N_decay))/N_decay), is shown to consistently outperform linear decay, especially for longer training runs, while matching or exceeding cosine performance. Stochastic weight averaging (SWA) with 500-step windows is applied during the constant-LR phase to improve generalization along the trajectory without additional training cost, though it does not fully close the gap to an explicit cooldown. The schedule-free optimizer (SFO) is also evaluated but found inferior to the cooldown approach. The central practical consequence is that scaling law experiments reduce to one training run per model size followed by post-hoc cooldowns or averaging at multiple token counts, cutting FLOPs and GPU hours by roughly half (and more for larger model families), as validated across 33M–360M models on SlimPajama and 1B/8B models on FineWeb.

## Results

On a 1B-parameter model trained for 100B tokens of FineWeb, the constant LR + (1-sqrt) cooldown achieves an aggregated benchmark score of 46.23, matching cosine-to-10% (46.26) within 0.03 points. At 460B tokens, the gap narrows further: cosine-to-0 scores 48.03 while (1-sqrt) 5% cooldown reaches 47.91. In scaling law experiments across 8 model sizes (33M–360M) and 3 token counts each, the cooldown schedule places every model almost perfectly on the diagonal when plotted against cosine perplexity, confirming reliable scaling. The approach saves roughly 50% of FLOPs and GPU hours for scaling experiments, and the authors estimate the original Chinchilla suite could have been run with less than half the compute (2.36×10²³ vs. 5.59×10²³ FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cosine schedule (the de-facto LLM standard established by Chinchilla), the constant LR + cooldown matches performance almost exactly: at 100B tokens the (1-sqrt) cooldown scores 46.23 vs. cosine-to-10% at 46.26, and at 460B tokens the gap is ≤0.12 aggregated points. The (1-sqrt) cooldown form consistently outperforms the linear cooldown, especially for longer training durations (e.g., 200K steps), and both outperform an untuned cosine. Against Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA), the cooldown clearly wins on final loss—SWA provides a strong boost during training but 'does not reach the loss values of explicit cooldowns'—though SWA remains useful as a free, no-extra-cost approximation. Against the Schedule-Free Optimizer (SFO) of Defazio et al. (2024), the cooldown schedule outperforms SFO even when SFO's momentum parameters (β₁=0.95, β₂=0.99) are well-tuned; SFO is also more sensitive to hyperparameter choices and can even increase loss toward the end of training with suboptimal settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes the constant-learning-rate-plus-cooldown schedule—previously proposed for vision transformers by Zhai et al. (2022) and recently adopted by LLMs like MiniCPM and JetMoe—and systematically validates it as a drop-in replacement for the cosine schedule that has been the de-facto standard since GPT and Chinchilla. It builds on the Chinchilla scaling law framework (Hoffmann et al., 2022), which established that cosine must be matched to training duration, and on stochastic weight averaging (Izmailov et al., 2018) to show that both techniques enable a fundamentally cheaper two-phase scaling experiment protocol. The paper departs from prior work by identifying the (1-sqrt) cooldown form as superior to linear decay and by connecting these scheduling choices directly to the cost structure of scaling law research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: scaling law experiments can be performed with roughly half the compute and GPU hours by training each model size once and applying ad-hoc cooldowns or SWA to checkpoints, rather than retraining from scratch for each token count. This makes frequent re-estimation of scaling laws (needed as data mixtures and architectures evolve) economically feasible for a much broader set of research groups. Open questions include whether the findings hold at frontier scale (beyond 8B parameters and extremely long training), whether a single cooldown is truly optimal given a fixed compute budget, and whether SWA can eventually close the remaining gap to explicit cooldowns. The paper's limitation to models up to 8B parameters and datasets up to 460B tokens means the most critical regime—frontier-scale training—remains untested.
