## Problem

Heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction has seen rapid method development, but the field lacks standardized benchmarks with ground truth and reliable quantitative metrics comparable to MNIST or ImageNet in computer vision. Existing methods are evaluated on different ad hoc datasets, making cross-method comparison impossible and preventing practitioners from determining which approach suits their specific heterogeneity type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242839/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Methods consume 2D cryo-EM projection images (128×128 or 256×256 pixels) corrupted by CTF effects and additive Gaussian noise at SNR 0.001–0.1, with unknown 3D orientations and 2D translations. Fixed-pose methods additionally require a binary volume mask and a consensus volume for initialization, while ab initio methods must jointly infer poses. Outputs include per-image 3D density volumes, low-dimensional latent embeddings encoding conformational or compositional state, and (for ab initio) estimated poses; datasets range from 100k images (IgG-1D, IgG-RL, Spike-MD) to 335k (Ribosembly).

## Method

CryoBench introduces five synthetic datasets spanning conformational heterogeneity (IgG-1D circular dihedral motion, IgG-RL random peptide-linker conformations, Spike-MD from 46k MD-sampled SARS-CoV-2 spike structures) and compositional heterogeneity (Ribosembly with 16 ribosome assembly states, Tomotwin-100 with 100 diverse cellular complexes), all generated via a standard image formation model (CTF × projection + Gaussian noise) with known ground-truth poses, conformations, and imaging parameters. The evaluation framework proposes three embedding-level metrics—Neighborhood Similarity (pMN), Information Imbalance, and Adjusted Rand Index for clustering accuracy—alongside a distributional volume metric (Per-Image FSC-AUC) that jointly assesses conformation estimation and reconstruction quality against ground truth. Ten state-of-the-art methods (CryoDRGN, CryoDRGN-AI, Opus-DSD, 3DFlex, 3DVA, RECOVAR, 3D Class, CryoDRGN2, and their ab initio variants) are benchmarked under both fixed-pose and ab initio settings, revealing that RECOVAR achieves the best reconstruction quality on conformational datasets while no current ab initio method can resolve the 100-component Tomotwin-100 mixture.

## Results

RECOVAR achieves the highest Per-Image FSC AUC across all five CryoBench datasets (0.386 on IgG-1D, 0.363 on IgG-RL, 0.429 on Ribosembly, 0.258 on Tomotwin-100, 0.362 on Spike-MD), outperforming the next-best fixed-pose method by 2–9 points depending on the dataset. Among ab initio methods, CryoDRGN-AI consistently outperforms CryoDRGN2 and 3D Class abinit, though all ab initio methods fail on Tomotwin-100 (FSC AUC ≤ 0.08). RECOVAR also achieves the best clustering accuracy on Ribosembly (ARI 0.968, AMI 0.976), while CryoDRGN attains near-perfect k-means clustering on Tomotwin-100 (ARI 0.956) despite the fixed-pose setting being unrealistic for that task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECOVAR (Bayesian PCA with regularized covariance estimation) dominates all other fixed-pose methods on Per-Image FSC AUC across every dataset, leading CryoDRGN by 3.5 points on IgG-1D (0.386 vs. 0.351) and by 11.7 points on Ribosembly (0.429 vs. 0.412). Among ab initio methods, CryoDRGN-AI is the clear leader, outperforming CryoDRGN2 by 5–10 FSC AUC points on most datasets and 3D Class abinit by a wide margin (e.g., 0.351 vs. 0.130 on IgG-1D). 3DFlex, designed for conformational heterogeneity, cannot be applied to the compositional datasets (Ribosembly, Tomotwin-100) and also underperforms on IgG-1D relative to RECOVAR and CryoDRGN-AI-fixed. On the challenging Tomotwin-100 dataset, all ab initio methods fail catastrophically (FSC AUC ≤ 0.08), while even the best fixed-pose method (RECOVAR) reaches only 0.258, highlighting a major gap for future work. Notably, CryoDRGN achieves near-perfect k-means clustering on Tomotwin-100 (ARI 0.956) despite the fixed-pose assumption being unrealistic for complex mixtures, suggesting its latent space captures compositional identity well even without pose estimation. 3D Class abinit is consistently the worst performer across all datasets and metrics, with FSC AUC values roughly half those of the best methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather addresses a systemic gap in the heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction literature: the absence of standardized benchmarks with ground truth. Prior methods (CryoDRGN, 3DFlex, 3DVA, RECOVAR, Opus-DSD, cryoSPARC 3D Classification) were each validated on different ad hoc datasets—synthetic blobs, pseudo-real motions, or real data without ground truth—making cross-method comparison impossible. CryoBench carves new ground by introducing the first unified suite of five synthetic datasets spanning conformational and compositional heterogeneity, paired with quantitative metrics (Neighborhood Similarity, Information Imbalance, Per-Image FSC) that go beyond the standard FSC cutoff used in homogeneous reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CryoBench provides the field with its first MNIST/ImageNet-analogous resource, enabling rigorous, reproducible comparison of heterogeneous reconstruction methods and tracking of progress over time. A key open question highlighted by the results is the complete failure of current ab initio methods on complex compositional heterogeneity (Tomotwin-100), suggesting a fundamental gap in pose estimation for diverse mixtures. Future directions include joint conformational-compositional heterogeneity, realistic noise models (multislice simulation), and non-structural sources of heterogeneity (junk particles, non-uniform poses) that remain absent from the current benchmark.
