## Problem

No existing work addresses fixed-confidence best arm identification (BAI) in piecewise stationary bandit (PSB) models, where the environment's context changes at unknown changepoints and the agent must identify an ε-optimal arm based on its ensemble expected return over an unknown context distribution. The challenge is that the agent observes only stochastic arm returns without access to the latent context vectors, their distribution, or the changepoint sequence, yet must still reliably estimate all three to determine arm quality.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the arm set X ⊂ R^d (K arms spanning R^d), the number of latent vectors N, segment length bounds L_min and L_max, and parameters ε and δ, but critically does NOT observe the current context vector, its index, or whether a changepoint has occurred. At each time step the agent pulls one arm and observes a scalar stochastic return Y = x^T θ + η. The output is a single ε-optimal arm recommendation (an arm whose ensemble mean μ_x = E_{θ~P_θ}[x^T θ] is within ε of the optimal) together with the random stopping time τ, whose expectation constitutes the sample complexity.

## Method

PSεBAI+ runs two algorithms in parallel on a shared G-optimal allocation: PSεBAI, which actively detects changepoints via a sliding-window Linear-Change Detection (LCD) subroutine and aligns the current context with previously observed ones via Linear-Context Alignment (LCA), and NεBAI, a naive baseline whose complexity grows linearly in L_max. PSεBAI maintains per-context estimates of latent vectors θ̂_{t,j} and their probabilities p̂_{t,j}, and uses a confidence radius ρ_t that decomposes into vector-estimation, distribution-estimation, and residual terms. The parallel design ensures a finite expected sample complexity (inherited from NεBAI's stopping rule) while achieving the tighter high-probability bound of PSεBAI, which reduces the L_max dependence by exploiting detected changepoints to pool samples across segments under the same context. A matching lower bound (up to logarithmic factors) is derived via an alternative-instance argument, establishing near-minimax optimality.

## Results

Empirically, PSεBAI+ achieves a sample complexity that is ≤1% of the naive baseline NεBAI across all tested instances (d=2, φ=π/8, L_min=3×10⁴, L_max=5×10⁴, δ=0.05, ε varying from 0.04 to 0.6). The number of context samples required by PSεBAI+ scales as 1/(Δ_min+ε)², matching the theoretical prediction in Theorem 3.3. Crucially, PSεBAI+ requires roughly the same number of context samples as DεBAI and DεBAI_β, which have oracle access to context vectors and changepoints, demonstrating that the change-detection and context-alignment subroutines effectively recover the missing environmental information. Theoretically, the expected sample complexity of PSεBAI+ matches the generic lower bound up to logarithmic factors (Corollary 5.1), establishing near-minimax optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PSεBAI+ against three baselines: NεBAI (naive, no change detection), DεBAI (oracle access to context vectors and changepoints), and DεBAI_β (oracle access plus the paper's confidence radius). PSεBAI+ dramatically outperforms NεBAI, requiring at most 1% of its sample complexity, with the gap widening as ε decreases (instances 9–12 in Figure 2a). Against the oracle-informed baselines DεBAI and DεBAI_β, PSεBAI+ is competitive, needing roughly the same number of context samples despite having no access to context indices or changepoints. DεBAI_β (which uses the paper's confidence radius formula) terminates with fewer context samples than DεBAI, confirming the well-designed confidence radius. The experiments also show that PSεBAI+ is robust to misspecifications in L_max and to variations in the change-detection frequency γ, retaining its superiority over NεBAI in all perturbed settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: stationary linear bandit BAI with G-optimal allocation (Soare et al., 2014), piecewise-stationary bandit regret minimization with change detection (Cao et al., 2019), and ε-best arm identification (Jourdan et al., 2023). The authors explicitly position their work as the first to study fixed-confidence BAI in a piecewise stationary bandit model, filling a gap where prior PSB work focused exclusively on regret minimization. The key methodological departure is the integration of a sliding-window change detection subroutine (LCD) and a context alignment subroutine (LCA) into the G-optimal allocation framework, enabling the agent to estimate both latent context vectors and their distribution without oracle access to either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-minimax optimality (matching lower bound up to logarithmic factors) provides a clean theoretical benchmark for BAI in non-stationary linear environments, and the parallel-execution trick (running PSεBAI and NεBAI simultaneously) is an elegant resolution to the tension between high-probability and expected sample complexity guarantees. Open questions include removing the distinguishability assumption (Assumption 1) and the requirement for known L_min/L_max, extending to the fixed-budget setting, and deriving instance-dependent guarantees. The practical relevance to adaptive experimental design (e.g., A/B testing under regime shifts) is promising but the current formulation requires knowledge of the number of latent contexts N, which limits immediate applicability.
