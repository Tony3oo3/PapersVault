## Problem

Predictive coding algorithms, while offering a biologically plausible alternative to backpropagation, have underperformed in high-dimensional structured inference due to three limitations: Gaussian-only generative models, Laplace approximations that capture only MAP estimates and marginal covariances, and the resulting high-variance parameter updates. No existing PC formulation simultaneously respects the full correlation structure of a generative model, performs provable maximum-likelihood parameter learning, and maintains purely local (biologically plausible) computations.

## Input / Output

DCPC consumes observation data (28×28 grayscale digits for MNIST/EMNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 64×64 RGB faces for CelebA) and a factorized causal graphical model with differentiable conditional densities. It produces K weighted posterior particles (K=4 for MNIST variants, K=16 for CelebA), updated generative-model parameters, a free-energy estimate, and—via the learned generative model—reconstructed and de-novo generated images. Notably, no encoder or amortized inference network is required; inference is performed purely by iterative coordinate updates over the latent variables.

## Method

DCPC decomposes joint posterior sampling into local coordinate updates, each targeting a variable's complete conditional density (prior times all children's likelihoods) rather than a marginal. Each coordinate update is an unadjusted Langevin step whose drift is the score function of the complete conditional—expressed as a sum of local prediction errors (one from the variable's own prior, one from each child's likelihood)—preconditioned by an empirical Fisher-information matrix estimated from the particle cloud. Importance weights correct the Langevin proposal to the true complete conditional, and resampling yields exact Gibbs-style samples; these coordinate updates are then nested within divide-and-conquer Sequential Monte Carlo to reconstruct the full joint posterior. Parameter learning uses a pathwise gradient of the variational free energy, and Theorem 2 proves that when model parameters factorize across nodes, this gradient decomposes into a sum of local particle averages, requiring no global backpropagation.

## Results

On MNIST with a deep latent Gaussian model, DCPC achieves a negative log-likelihood of 102.5±0.01 and MSE of 0.01±7.2×10⁻⁶, substantially outperforming MCPC (NLL 144.6±0.7, MSE 8.29×10⁻²). On CelebA at 64×64 resolution with a discretized Gaussian likelihood, DCPC achieves a FID of 79.0±0.9, beating LPC (~120) and VAE (86.3±0.03) while using no encoder network. At 32×32 resolution with a Gaussian likelihood, DCPC achieves FID 82.7±0.9 versus PGD's 100±2.7, with an order-of-magnitude reduction in FID variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCPC outperforms all four baselines on their respective comparison settings. Against MCPC (Oliviers et al., 2024) on MNIST, DCPC reduces NLL from 144.6 to 102.5 and MSE by roughly three orders of magnitude, with standard deviation an order of magnitude lower. Against PGD (Kuntz et al., 2023) on CelebA at 32×32, DCPC improves FID from 100±2.7 to 82.7±0.9, a ~17% reduction with much tighter variance. Against LPC (Zahid et al., 2024) on CelebA at 64×64, DCPC achieves FID 79.0 versus LPC's approximate 120, a ~34% improvement, while using the same total number of gradient evaluations (4500). Against the standard VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) at 64×64, DCPC achieves FID 79.0 versus 86.3, notably without an encoder network or amortized inference—DCPC performs inference purely through particle-based coordinate updates. The paper notes that DCPC's advantage over VAE is meaningful given the architectural constraint of no inference network, though DCPC still trails VAE in absolute generative quality at higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCPC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) classical predictive coding (Rao & Ballard, Friston, Bastos), which approximates posteriors with per-node Gaussians via Laplace approximations; (2) recent Monte Carlo predictive coding (MCPC, Oliviers et al. 2024) and Langevin predictive coding (LPC, Zahid et al. 2024), which replaced Gaussian posteriors with particle-based sampling but still required backpropagating score functions or full log-joint gradients through the generative model; and (3) divide-and-conquer sequential Monte Carlo (Lindsten et al. 2017, Kuntz et al. 2024) and particle gradient descent (PGD, Kuntz et al. 2023), which provide the SMC machinery for correct weighted sampling. DCPC's key departure is to parameterize each coordinate-wise Langevin proposal using only local score functions (the 'prediction errors' of the complete conditional), nest these within DCSMC for exact importance weighting, and prove that parameter updates require only local gradients (Theorem 2) — thereby respecting the conditional factorization of the generative model rather than treating it as a monolithic energy landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the neuroscience-inspired ML community, DCPC provides the first predictive coding algorithm that simultaneously (a) targets arbitrary differentiable generative models, (b) approximates the full joint posterior via particles rather than per-node Gaussians, (c) provably performs maximum-likelihood parameter updates using only local information, and (d) maps onto the canonical cortical microcircuit hypothesis. The practical limitation is that DCPC trains more slowly and is more sensitive to learning rates than amortized VAEs, and the CelebA FID results, while beating PGD and LPC, still trail a standard VAE with an encoder. Open questions include extending the framework to discrete latent variables (sketched in Appendix C via Wasserstein gradient flow), incorporating momentum-based preconditioning, and validating the cortical oscillation mapping experimentally.
