## Problem

Consistency models enable fast (4–8 step) video generation but are bottlenecked by the quality of their teacher diffusion model, and the reduced step count further degrades output. Existing reward-based alignment methods (e.g., InstructVideo) require backpropagating through the full iterative sampling chain, incurring prohibitive memory costs, and rely solely on image-text reward models that cannot capture temporal dynamics such as motion and inter-frame transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242409/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a short video (16 frames) in just 4–8 inference steps, compared to 50 steps for the teacher. T2V-Turbo (VC2) generates 512×320 videos at 16 FPS, while T2V-Turbo (MS) generates 256×256 videos at 8 FPS. Generation occurs in the VAE latent space, with the final video decoded by the VAE decoder after the consistency function maps a noisy latent to the clean latent in a single forward pass.

## Method

T2V-Turbo integrates reward feedback from a mixture of differentiable reward models—an image-text RM (HPSv2.1) and a video-text RM (ViCLIP or InternVideo2 S2)—directly into the consistency distillation (CD) loss. The key insight is that the single-step generation ẑ₀ = f_θ(z_{t_{n+k}}, ω, c, t_{n+k}) already arises when computing the CD loss, so reward gradients are backpropagated through this single forward pass rather than through an iterative sampling chain, eliminating the memory bottleneck of prior reward-finetuning approaches. The total training objective is L_CD − β_img·J_img − β_vid·J_vid, where J_img maximizes the image-text RM score over M randomly sampled frames and J_vid maximizes the video-text RM score over the full decoded video. Training uses LoRA adapters initialized from the teacher model, keeping per-step inference cost identical to the teacher after merging.

## Results

On VBench, the 4-step T2V-Turbo (VC2) achieves the highest Total Score of 81.01, surpassing proprietary systems Gen-2 (80.58) and Pika (80.40) as well as its teacher VideoCrafter2 (80.44). T2V-Turbo (MS) also achieves a competitive Total Score of 80.62 with only 4 inference steps. In human evaluation with 700 EvalCrafter prompts, the 4-step T2V-Turbo generations are preferred over the 50-step DDIM samples from their teacher models, representing a 12.5× inference acceleration with improved quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2V-Turbo (VC2) achieves the highest VBench Total Score (81.01) among all compared methods, outperforming Gen-2 (80.58) by 0.43 points, Pika (80.40) by 0.61 points, and its teacher VideoCrafter2 (80.44) by 0.57 points—all with only 4 inference steps. T2V-Turbo (MS) similarly surpasses Gen-2 (80.62 vs. 80.58) and Pika (80.62 vs. 80.40) despite being distilled from the less advanced ModelScopeT2V teacher. Both T2V-Turbo variants dramatically outperform their respective baseline VCMs without reward feedback: T2V-Turbo (VC2) improves over VCM (VC2) by 7.04 Total Score points (81.01 vs. 73.97), and T2V-Turbo (MS) improves over VCM (MS) by 4.78 points (80.62 vs. 75.84). In human evaluation, the 4-step T2V-Turbo generations are preferred over the 50-step DDIM teacher samples across all three criteria (visual appeal, text alignment, overall preference), confirming that the speedup does not come at a quality cost. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that the image-text RM (HPSv2.1) contributes the largest individual gain, while the video-text RM provides additional Semantic Score improvement for better text-video alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency distillation (CD) framework from LCM (Luo et al., 2023a) and Video LCM (Wang et al., 2023a) by integrating differentiable reward feedback directly into the distillation loss, building on the reward-guided LCM idea from Li et al. (2024) for images. It departs from InstructVideo (Yuan et al., 2023) and other reward-finetuning approaches (Clark et al., 2023; Prabhudesai et al., 2023) in two key ways: (1) it optimizes rewards on single-step generations that arise naturally from the CD loss computation, bypassing the prohibitive memory cost of backpropagating through iterative sampling, and (2) it introduces a video-text RM alongside the standard image-text RM to capture temporal dynamics that image-only RMs cannot assess. The method is applied via LoRA fine-tuning on top of pre-trained teacher T2V models (VideoCrafter2, ModelScopeT2V), keeping inference cost identical to the teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that 4-step distilled generations surpass both the 50-step teacher and proprietary systems (Gen-2, Pika) on VBench, while being preferred by human annotators, demonstrates that the quality-speed tradeoff in consistency models is not fundamental but can be broken with appropriate reward signals. A key open question is the reliance on video foundation models (ViCLIP, InternVideo2) as proxies for a true video-text RM trained on human preferences, since no such open-sourced model exists; a dedicated video-text RM could yield further gains. The single-run evaluation without error bars and the somewhat limited comparability of VBench scores across architecturally different proprietary systems temper the strength of the SOTA claims, but the human evaluation on 700 prompts provides meaningful corroboration.
