## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series forecasters (LLM4TS) adopt non-autoregressive generation by flattening all lookback tokens and predicting in a single step, which contradicts the decoder-only autoregressive architecture of LLMs and leaves their multi-step generation, zero-shot, and in-context learning capabilities underutilized. This paper asks whether repurposing LLMs as truly autoregressive time series forecasters—preserving the next-token-prediction paradigm—can unlock these capabilities while maintaining model efficiency.

## Input / Output

The forecaster takes a multivariate lookback window (L time steps × C variates) segmented into non-overlapping segments of length S (96 for multivariate datasets, equal to forecast length F for M3/M4), along with textual timestamps (e.g., '2016/07/05 00:00:00') that are pre-computed into position embeddings via the frozen LLM. Optionally, earlier historical time series from the target domain are prepended as prompts for in-context forecasting. The output is a sequence of predicted segments of arbitrary length F, generated iteratively by feeding each predicted segment back as the next input, enabling a single model to handle all lookback and forecast lengths without retraining.

## Method

AutoTimes freezes a decoder-only LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B) and trains only two lightweight MLP modules—SegmentEmbedding (R^S → R^D) and SegmentProjection (R^D → R^S)—accounting for ~0.1% of total parameters. Time series segments are embedded into the LLM's latent space, and textual timestamps are converted to position embeddings by passing a timestamp template through the frozen LLM and extracting the <EOS> token representation, which aligns simultaneous segments across variates under channel independence. Training follows the standard next-token-prediction objective: each segment embedding is fed through the frozen LLM layers, and the output is projected to predict the subsequent segment with MSE loss. At inference, the model generates predictions autoregressively by concatenating each predicted segment to the input for the next step, inheriting the LLM's multi-step generation ability. The in-context forecasting extension concatenates target-domain time series prompts before the lookback window, extending the prediction context beyond the conventional lookback without any gradient updates.

## Results

On the one-for-all long-term forecasting benchmark (single model, rolling prediction across horizons 96/192/336/720), AutoTimes achieves the best average MSE on 4 out of 5 datasets: ETTh1 (0.389), ECL (0.159), Traffic (0.374), and Solar-Energy (0.197), outperforming the previous SOTA TimeLLM by 9.12% in average MSE. On the M4 short-term benchmark, AutoTimes attains the best sMAPE (11.831), MASE (1.585), and OWA (0.850) among all 10 compared methods. The method uses only 0.1% trainable parameters (0.79M out of 7B) and achieves over 5× training/inference speedup compared to other LLM-based forecasters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoTimes outperforms all LLM4TS baselines (TimeLLM, UniTime, FPT) on 4 of 5 long-term datasets, with the largest margins against UniTime (e.g., 0.159 vs 0.325 MSE on ECL) and FPT (e.g., 0.374 vs 0.461 MSE on Traffic). Against deep forecasters, AutoTimes beats DLinear and TimesNet on all five datasets, and outperforms PatchTST and iTransformer on ETTh1, ECL, Traffic, and Solar-Energy, though it trails PatchTST (0.226) and TimeLLM (0.225) on Weather (0.235 MSE). On the M4 short-term benchmark, AutoTimes achieves the best sMAPE (11.831) and OWA (0.850), narrowly beating Koopa (11.863) and N-BEATS (11.910). The one-for-all setting is particularly revealing: non-autoregressive LLM4TS methods like TimeLLM and UniTime degrade substantially under rolling multi-step prediction, while AutoTimes' autoregressive formulation maintains consistent accuracy across all four horizons, confirming that preserving the decoder-only autoregressive structure is critical for multi-step generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant non-autoregressive LLM4TS paradigm (TimeLLM, UniTime, FPT) that flattens all lookback tokens and predicts in a single encoder-style step, arguing this contradicts the decoder-only architecture that gives LLMs their generative power. AutoTimes instead treats time series segments as tokens in a true next-token-prediction loop, inheriting the autoregressive multi-step generation capability of LLMs. The modality alignment strategy (segment embedding + LLM-embedded textual timestamps as position embeddings) draws on the VLM recipe of projecting non-language tokens into the LLM's latent space, while the in-context forecasting idea repurposes the LLM's in-context learning by using time series itself as the prompt rather than language instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By freezing the LLM and training only a 0.1% parameter embedding/projection head, AutoTimes makes LLM-based forecasting dramatically cheaper (5× speedup) while matching or exceeding prior LLM4TS methods, lowering the barrier to adopting large models for time series. The one-for-all formulation (one model, arbitrary forecast lengths via autoregressive rollout) and in-context forecasting (time series as self-prompt) open a path toward truly general-purpose time series foundation models without per-horizon retraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger LLMs or truly diverse domains, whether in-context forecasting can leverage retrieved or cross-domain prompts more effectively, and whether the simple timestamp-as-position-embedding trick generalizes to irregularly sampled or event-driven series.
