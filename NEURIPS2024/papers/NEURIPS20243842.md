## Problem

Backdoor attacks in federated learning remain difficult to defend against under data heterogeneity, where existing methods require homogeneous data assumptions, supplementary validation datasets, or client-side regularization that conflicts with standard federated optimization. The paper identifies that benign heterogeneous distributions and malicious triggered distributions exhibit distinct parameter importance profiles, a signal that prior defenses have not exploited.

## Input / Output

Each client uploads its locally optimized model parameters and the corresponding Fisher Information Matrix computed on its private data distribution; the server also retains the current global model. The output is a new global model where malicious clients receive zero aggregation weight and parameter elements are rescaled by their importance scores, effectively amplifying updates to distribution-critical parameters while suppressing trivial ones. The method operates on standard CNN backbones over 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, USPS) with Dirichlet-simulated label skew.

## Method

FDCR combines two mechanisms built on the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix. First, Fisher Client Discrepancy Cluster (FCDC) computes per-client parameter importance via min-max-normalized FIM, reweights each client's gradient by this importance vector, then measures the squared discrepancy between each reweighted client gradient and the weighted global gradient; FINCH (a parameter-free first-neighbor clustering) partitions clients into benign and malicious groups, and malicious clients are assigned zero aggregation weight. Second, Fisher Parameter Rescale Aggregation (FPRA) applies a sigmoid-scaled importance factor (range [1, 2e/(1+e)]) to each parameter element's update before aggregation, widening the effective update range for important parameters and narrowing it for trivial ones. The key insight is that backdoor attackers overfit a triggered distribution, causing their parameter importance profile to diverge sharply from benign clients fitting heterogeneous but related distributions, making importance-weighted gradient discrepancy a natural detection signal without requiring validation data or client cooperation.

## Results

On the most challenging setting (CIFAR-10, β=0.3, Υ=30%), FDCR achieves A=61.25%, R=93.60%, and V=77.42%, outperforming the best baseline (DnC, V=68.25%) by 9.17 points on the V metric. On Fashion-MNIST (β=0.3, Υ=30%), FDCR reaches V=87.60% while most baselines collapse (e.g., DnC drops to V=42.97%, FoolsGold to V=35.03%). On USPS (β=0.5, Υ=30%), FDCR attains the highest A (95.80%) and V (92.57%), with only MMA marginally exceeding it on R (89.80% vs. 89.34%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDCR achieves the best V metric on CIFAR-10 across both heterogeneity levels (β=0.5: V=78.06 vs. DnC 75.12; β=0.3: V=77.42 vs. DnC 68.25), with the 9.17-point gap at β=0.3 being the paper's headline claim. On Fashion-MNIST β=0.5, DnC narrowly edges FDCR on V (88.43 vs. 87.62) due to a higher backdoor failure rate (91.46% vs. 88.32%), but FDCR wins on benign accuracy (86.92% vs. 85.40%); under the harder β=0.3 setting, FDCR dominates with V=87.60% while DNC collapses to 42.97%. Distance-difference methods (Multi Krum, FoolsGold, MMA) and statistics-based methods (Trim Median, Bulyan) degrade severely under high heterogeneity, with V scores dropping below 55 on CIFAR-10 β=0.3. Proxy-evaluation methods (FLTrust, Sageflow, Finetuning) and client-side defense (CRFL) fail to remove the backdoor effectively, maintaining R below 50% on CIFAR-10 β=0.5, whereas FDCR sustains R above 90%. On USPS, FDCR achieves the top A (95.80%) and V (92.57%) at β=0.5, with MMA slightly ahead on R (89.80% vs. 89.34%), and FDCR clearly leads at β=0.3 (V=86.62 vs. DnC 76.47).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Fisher Information Matrix (Fisher, 1922) as a mechanism for quantifying per-parameter importance, a tool previously applied to continual learning (EWC), domain generalization (FISHR), and federated model merging, but not to backdoor defense. It departs from the four established backdoor-defense paradigms in federated learning—distance-based outlier detection, statistical aggregation, proxy-dataset evaluation, and client-side regularization—by introducing a parameter-importance-weighted gradient discrepancy metric that naturally separates benign from malicious clients under non-IID data without requiring homogeneous assumptions, validation sets, or client cooperation. The clustering step leverages FINCH (Sarfraz et al., CVPR 2019), a parameter-free first-neighbor clustering method, to avoid the hyperparameter sensitivity of K-Means or DBSCAN in varying client-scale settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDCR addresses a practical gap: most existing backdoor defenses degrade sharply under realistic data heterogeneity (low Dirichlet β) or require resources (clean validation data, client-side compliance) that are unavailable in medical or financial federated deployments. The key open question the paper acknowledges is that FDCR operates only at the aggregation phase and cannot unlearn trigger parameters already embedded in the global model, a limitation shared by all aggregation-only defenses. Future work could combine FDCR's detection signal with server-side unlearning or fine-tuning to close this gap, and the parameter-importance perspective may generalize to other poisoning scenarios beyond backdoor attacks.
