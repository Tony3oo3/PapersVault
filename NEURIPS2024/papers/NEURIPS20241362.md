## Problem

Current AI systems for mathematical reasoning rely on human-produced data (textbooks, formal proof libraries, benchmarks) and evaluate performance on extrinsically defined problem sets, leaving no room for discovery. The paper addresses the gap of training an agent that can bootstrap purely from axioms, jointly learning to pose challenging-but-provable conjectures and to find their proofs—a self-improving loop inspired by intrinsic motivation in RL, where the agent must continuously raise the difficulty of its own problems as its proving ability improves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of axioms expressed in Peano's dependent type theory (a minimal Calculus of Constructions) and a discretized difficulty target (trivial/easy/hard, derived from proof log-likelihood percentiles). It produces well-formed conjectures—terms of type `prop`—guaranteed valid by construction via constrained decoding, along with formal proofs found through MCTS in Peano's finite action space. The underlying model is a randomly initialized GPT-2-style character-level Transformer (~8.45M parameters, 8 layers, vocab size 128, max context 1024), so no pre-training on mathematical corpora is used.

## Method

MINIMO combines three key mechanisms in a self-improving loop. First, conjecturing uses Synchromesh's Constrained Semantic Decoding guided by a type-directed synthesis completion engine built from CoC formation rules, ensuring every sampled string is a valid proposition even from a random model. Second, proof search runs MCTS in Peano with the same LM serving as both policy (scoring available actions via likelihood) and value function (scoring states via likelihood of 1/0 tokens). Third, hindsight relabeling extracts training data from failed proof searches by identifying forward actions that incidentally prove other propositions, walking up the tree to relabel the goal, and using these relabeled proofs to train both the prover and the conjecturer. Difficulty is measured as the log-likelihood of a found proof under the current policy, discretized into percentiles to condition the conjecturer, closing the loop so the agent targets problems at the edge of its current proving ability.

## Results

Across 5 training iterations (200 conjectures/batch, 1000 MCTS expansions), MINIMO agents self-improve in all three domains: in Groups, average proof length grows from 2.67 to 5 steps and longest proofs from 4 to 9 steps; in Propositional Logic, from 2.75 to 4.21 (avg) and 5 to 11 (longest); in Arithmetic, from 2.36 to 3.35 (avg) and 4 to 7 (longest). On the extrinsic evaluation, agents trained solely on self-generated conjectures steadily improve at proving 35 theorems from Kleene's textbook and 10 levels from the Natural Number Game, with four logic theorems and one arithmetic theorem (∀n,∀m, succ(n)+m=succ(n+m)) only solved by the final agent. Without hindsight relabeling, training collapses: the conjecturer degenerates to producing only trivial statements (e.g., 0=0), whereas with hindsight, the agent maintains a steady stream of challenging yet provable conjectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare MINIMO against external automated theorem proving or conjecturing systems; the primary comparison is an internal ablation of hindsight relabeling. Without hindsight relabeling, only ~10–20% of conjectures yield training signal (successful proofs), and the conjecturer quickly collapses to generating trivially easy statements across all three domains. With hindsight relabeling, even failed proof searches produce hundreds of true statements and proofs per batch, enabling the agent to maintain a moving difficulty target and steadily propose harder conjectures. On the extrinsic evaluation (Figure 4), the final agent outperforms the initial (randomly initialized) agent on both Kleene's 35 propositional logic theorems and the 10 Natural Number Game levels, demonstrating that self-generated training data transfers to human-authored problems. The paper positions itself as the first method to jointly learn conjecturing and proving from axioms alone, so no direct head-to-head comparison with prior systems (e.g., AlphaGeometry, TacticZero, rlCoP) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINIMO unifies two previously separate threads: constrained semantic decoding (Synchromesh [29]) for generating well-formed conjectures by construction, and hindsight experience replay [1] adapted to proof search trees for extracting training signal from failed searches. It departs from prior theorem proving work (which pre-trains on human-written formal proofs) and prior conjecturing work (AM [23], which conjectured but did not prove) by coupling both tasks in a self-improving loop that starts from axioms alone, drawing conceptual inspiration from AlphaZero-style self-play and intrinsic motivation objectives (AMIGO [7], PowerPlay [34]). The key methodological departure is using hindsight relabeling not only for the prover but also for the conjecturer, turning unprovable conjectures into hundreds of true statements with proofs per search episode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single language model can bootstrap both conjecturing and proving from axioms alone, showing that intrinsic motivation (targeting hard-but-provable problems) is a viable training signal for formal mathematics without any human examples. However, the agent's fixed axiom library (no theorem accumulation) means it is limited to cut-free proofs, which grow exponentially in length, capping the depth of mathematics it can reach. Open questions include how to grow a library of lemmas and definitions, how to perform premise selection at scale, and whether the self-improvement dynamics observed on small domains (propositional logic, arithmetic, groups) with 8.45M-parameter models transfer to richer theories. The work opens a path toward compute-bound mathematical discovery but is far from discovering non-trivial new mathematics.
