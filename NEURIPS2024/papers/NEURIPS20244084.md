## Problem

Differentially private (DP) stochastic saddle point problems (SSPs) and stochastic variational inequalities (SVIs) had optimal utility rates known only in Euclidean (ℓ₂) geometry, while many important applications (distributionally robust optimization, federated learning, fairness) are naturally formulated in ℓ₁ or other non-Euclidean geometries. For SVIs, the best achievable DP rate was unknown in any geometry under canonical utility measures. The fundamental obstacle was that prior generalization analyses relied on McDiarmid-style concentration bounds that inherently require Euclidean structure and incur poly(d) degradation in non-Euclidean settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an i.i.d. dataset S of size n and a convex-concave loss f (for SSPs) or a monotone, L-bounded, β-Lipschitz operator g (for SVIs), with the parameter space constrained to ℓ_p-bounded sets of diameter B_w (primal) and B_θ (dual). Privacy is enforced by adding Gaussian noise to minibatch gradient estimates of the saddle operator or monotone operator, with noise scale calibrated via the moments accountant. The output is a single point [w, θ] or z achieving a strong SP-gap or strong VI-gap of Õ(1/√n + √d/(nε)) in Õ(min{n²ε^{1.5}/√d, n^{3/2}}) gradient evaluations; notably, for SVIs the algorithm outputs a uniformly random iterate rather than the average, since monotonicity-based averaging arguments do not yield the required relative stationarity bound.

## Method

The core contribution is a fundamentally new generalization analysis for the recursive regularization algorithm that avoids all direct analysis of the strong gap at intermediate stages. Instead of McDiarmid concentration (which fails in non-Euclidean norms), the authors introduce functions H_D^{(t)} and H_S^{(t)} defined with respect to the data-independent population saddle point, and prove a stability-implies-generalization bound (Lemma 2) showing that any Δ-uniform-argument-stable algorithm satisfies E[H_D(A(S)) − H_S(A(S))] ≤ 2ΔL. For SVIs, where the analogous relative accuracy notion does not yield distance bounds, a new 'relative stationarity' measure (Definition 3) is introduced, and the generalization analysis is modified to decompose the operator into a Lipschitz data-dependent part and a data-independent regularizer to handle non-Lipschitz terms (e.g., in ℓ₁). The empirical subroutine is stochastic mirror prox with a differentially private oracle (Gaussian noise on minibatches), and for ℓ₁-type norms where squared norms lack strong convexity, the algorithm uses slightly perturbed norms p̄ = max{p, 1+1/log(d)} to recover the required strong convexity with κ = O(log d).

## Results

The paper establishes a near-optimal strong SP-gap bound of Õ(κ^2.5 √(B_w²L_w² + B_θ²L_θ²) · (√(d log(1/δ)κ̃)/(nε) + 1/√n)) for ℓ_p/ℓ_q saddle point problems with p, q ∈ [1,2], achieved in Õ(min{n²ε^1.5/√d, n^{3/2}}) gradient evaluations. For stochastic variational inequalities in the ℓ_p setting, the first near-optimal strong VI-gap bound of Õ(κ^3.5 BL · (√(d log(1/δ)κ̃)/(nε) + 1/√n)) is obtained with the same gradient complexity. In the Euclidean case (p=q=2, κ=1), the SSP rate exactly recovers the prior result of Bassily et al. (COLT 2023), while the SVI rate improves upon the previous best of (1/n^{1/3} + √d/n^{2/3}·ε) from Boob & Guzmán (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are to prior convergence rate guarantees. For DP SSPs, the prior near-optimal rate Õ(1/√n + √d/(nε)) was known only in the Euclidean (p=q=2) setting [BGM23] or under strong additional assumptions such as smoothness plus polyhedral constraints [GGP24] or a specific linear loss structure in the dual [ZB24]. The present work removes all such restrictions, achieving the same rate for any p, q ∈ [1,2] without smoothness or linearity assumptions. For DP SVIs, the previous best strong VI-gap rate was (1/n^{1/3} + √d/n^{2/3}·ε) in the Euclidean setting [BG23]; this paper improves it to Õ(1/√n + √d/(nε)) for all p with p−1=Ω(1), matching the lower bound up to logarithmic factors. The key technical advance enabling these improvements is a new stability-implies-generalization analysis that avoids the McDiarmid-style concentration bounds (which incur an extra √d factor in non-Euclidean norms) used in prior recursive regularization analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recursive regularization framework of Allen-Zhu (2018) and its Euclidean DP analysis by Bassily, Guzmán, and Menart (2023) to general ℓ_p/ℓ_q geometries. The critical departure from BGM23 is in the generalization analysis: where BGM23 relied on McDiarmid-style concentration bounds that inherently require Euclidean structure (incurring a poly(d) penalty in ℓ₁), this paper introduces a stability-implies-generalization argument using a new population-referenced accuracy measure H_D^(t), completely bypassing concentration inequalities. For SVIs, the authors further develop a new 'relative stationarity' accuracy measure, since the natural extension of relative accuracy fails to bound distance to the equilibrium in the variational inequality setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of DP SSPs and SVIs beyond Euclidean geometry, yielding near-optimal strong gap rates for all p, q ∈ [1,2] and the first near-optimal strong VI-gap rate for p−1 = Ω(1). The new generalization technique—avoiding concentration bounds entirely in favor of a stability argument with a data-independent reference point—is of independent interest and may apply to other minimax or variational inequality problems where traditional generalization tools break down. Open questions include whether the log(d) factor in κ for p close to 1 can be removed, whether the rates improve under smoothness or other structural assumptions in non-Euclidean settings, and whether the near-linear-time acceleration (available only for p=2) extends to other geometries.
