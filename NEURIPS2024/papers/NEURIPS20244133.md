## Problem

Large language models become outdated as new information emerges, yet retraining from scratch is prohibitively expensive. Existing online adaptation approaches face a trade-off: retrieval-augmented generation is computationally heavy and vulnerable to counterfactual documents, while online finetuning incurs gradient computation costs, hyperparameter sensitivity, and catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks whether an LM can be adapted to a stream of new documents at test time using only forward passes, without any gradient-based updates to the model parameters.

## Input / Output

During online adaptation, the system receives a sequential stream of context documents (up to 512 tokens each via BPE) and compresses each into a compact PEFT modulation of T tokens (12 for DistilGPT2, 24 for larger models) that are stored in a growing memory bank. At query time, a question is encoded by a smaller input network and cross-attended against the entire memory bank to produce a single target modulation, which is then injected as P-Tuning v2 key-value pairs into every attention layer of the frozen base LM. The final output is the LM's generated answer conditioned on this aggregated modulation, requiring no gradient computation or parameter updates to the base model.

## Method

MAC freezes the base LM and learns two auxiliary networks via amortization-based meta-learning: (1) an amortization network (T5 encoder-decoder with learnable decoder tokens) that maps each document to T modulation tokens in a single forward pass, replacing per-document PEFT optimization; and (2) a permutation-invariant aggregation network (four cross-attention blocks) that, given the encoded question and the full memory bank of modulations, selects and merges relevant information into one target modulation. Both networks are trained end-to-end against the frozen LM's negative log-likelihood loss. Two memory-efficient techniques enable scaling: backpropagation dropout (applying stop-gradient to a random fraction p of documents during training to allow larger context sets under fixed GPU memory) and hierarchical modulation aggregation (a divide-and-conquer inference strategy that sub-groups the memory bank into M-token chunks and recursively aggregates, reducing cross-attention memory from O(KT²) to O(MT)).

## Results

On the LLaMA-2 (7B) model, MAC achieves F1 scores of 21.79 (StreamingQA), 21.14 (SQuAD-Seq), and 23.90 (ArchivalQA-Seq), outperforming the strongest online finetuning baseline (Salient Spans) by 2.82, 2.48, and 1.15 F1 points respectively. CaMeLS, the prior state-of-the-art meta-learning method, runs out of memory on LLaMA-2 even with 4-bit quantization and ZeRO. When combined with retrieval augmentation on ArchivalQA-Seq, MAC further boosts BM25 from 71.83 to 74.89 F1 (top-5) and DPR from 72.24 to 75.00 F1. MAC also retains 96.2% of initial knowledge after adapting to 1,400 additional documents, compared to only 70.8% for CaMeLS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC outperforms all three online finetuning baselines from Hu et al. (Uniform, Salient Spans, CaMeLS) across every model size and dataset, with the largest margins on LLaMA-2 where CaMeLS is entirely infeasible due to second-order gradient computation causing OOM. On LLaMA-2/StreamingQA, MAC achieves 21.79 F1 vs. 18.97 for Salient Spans (the strongest feasible baseline), a 2.82-point gain. When combined with retrieval augmentation on ArchivalQA-Seq, MAC consistently improves over each retriever alone: BM25+MAC reaches 74.89 F1 vs. 71.83 for BM25 alone, Contriever+MAC reaches 70.01 vs. 67.83, and DPR+MAC reaches 75.00 vs. 72.24 (all top-5). Against the memory-augmented LM baseline CCM+T5 encoder Retriever (Kim et al., ICLR 2024), MAC achieves 27.20 F1 vs. 25.98 on StreamingQA with unquantized LLaMA-2. MAC also demonstrates superior knowledge retention (96.2% vs. 70.8% for CaMeLS after 1,400 additional documents) and reduces adaptation memory by 68% and time by 90.3% compared to CaMeLS.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC sits at the intersection of amortization-based meta-learning (Garnelo et al., Requeima et al.), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (P-Tuning v2, LoRA), and online adaptation of LMs (Hu et al. 2023, Jang et al. 2022). Rather than updating LM parameters via gradient-based online finetuning (as in CaMeLS) or retrieving raw documents (as in RAG), MAC amortizes each incoming document into a compact PEFT modulation via a hypernetwork and stores these in a memory bank, then uses a cross-attention aggregation network to synthesize a single task-relevant modulation at query time. The architectural design of the amortization network follows Phang et al. (Hypertuning, ICML 2023), while the set-aggregation mechanism draws on attentive neural processes and model-soup insights about interpolation between modulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAC addresses a practically critical gap: LLMs become outdated rapidly, yet retraining is prohibitively expensive and online finetuning suffers from catastrophic forgetting and gradient-computation overhead. The 96.2% knowledge retention (vs. 70.8% for CaMeLS) and the elimination of test-time gradient updates make this approach viable for edge deployment and continuous-knowledge-update scenarios. Open questions include the unbounded growth of the memory bank (mitigated but not solved by nearest-neighbor averaging), privacy implications of storing amortized contexts, and whether the approach scales to truly massive corpora or multi-turn interactive settings. The demonstrated synergy with RAG (consistent F1 gains across BM25, Contriever, DPR) suggests a productive direction for hybrid retrieval-plus-modulation systems.
