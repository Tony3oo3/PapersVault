## Problem

Existing model inversion defenses—whether based on differential privacy, mutual information regularization, or loss landscape manipulation—remain vulnerable to state-of-the-art attacks like PLG-MI, which still achieve over 89% attack accuracy. Misleading-based defenses (NetGuard, DCD) offer an alternative but require extra public datasets, shadow attack simulations, and protect only a limited set of classes. This paper asks whether trapdoor injection, previously explored for adversarial detection (TeD), can be adapted to mislead MI attacks across all classes without additional data or computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The target model is a VGG-16 (or Face.evoLVe / ResNet-152) classifier trained on 64×64 facial images from CelebA (1,000 identities, 30,029 images) to predict identity class labels. During training, each input image is optionally blended with a per-class trigger (Π_y(X) = (1−α)X + αk_y, α=0.02) and passed through random augmentations (resized crops, flips, rotations). A discriminator with the same architecture as the target model is jointly trained to distinguish poisoned from benign samples, and the triggers themselves are optimized adversarially to remain invisible while preserving trapdoor efficacy.

## Method

Trap-MID integrates class-specific blended triggers into the target classifier via a three-gradient-update training loop: (1) the classifier minimizes a weighted combination of standard cross-entropy and trapdoor loss (β=0.2), (2) a discriminator learns to separate poisoned from benign inputs, and (3) the triggers are updated via sign-gradient steps to simultaneously maximize naturalness (fool the discriminator) and maintain trapdoor effectiveness (preserve correct classification on triggered inputs). Random augmentations are applied to both the main and trapdoor losses to ensure transformation-robust triggers. Theoretically, the paper defines trapdoor effectiveness δ (predictive power on poisoned vs. benign data) and naturalness ε (KL divergence between benign and poisoned distributions), proving that when δ > ε, the expected posterior probability of triggered data exceeds that of private data, making MI attacks preferentially extract trigger features rather than private attributes. Empirically, this reduces PLG-MI attack accuracy from 95.81% to 6.23% while maintaining 81.37% model utility, and 78.68% of reconstructed images are detected as carrying trapdoor signatures.

## Results

Against the state-of-the-art PLG-MI attack on VGG-16/CelebA, Trap-MID reduces top-1 attack accuracy from 95.81% (unprotected) to 6.23%, while maintaining 81.37% model utility (vs. 86.21% unprotected). Against KED-MI, attack accuracy drops from 56.46% to 9.24%, and against GMI from 14.29% to 0.24%. KNN distance increases from 1174 to 2056 (PLG-MI) and FID from 12.77 to 57.82, confirming that recovered images are far from the private distribution and less realistic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trap-MID decisively outperforms all three regularization-based baselines (MID, BiDO, NegLS) across every attack. Against PLG-MI, the most challenging attack, Trap-MID achieves 6.23% top-1 attack accuracy versus 92.72% (MID), 89.18% (BiDO), and 89.38% (NegLS) — a gap of over 83 percentage points. NegLS produces the most unnatural reconstructions (FID 69.02) but still leaks substantial private information (AA-1 89.38%), whereas Trap-MID simultaneously suppresses attack accuracy and increases KNN distance. Against the TeD trapdoor baseline (Appendix Table 5), Trap-MID's full-image blended triggers are critical: TeD's patch-based triggers remain vulnerable to PLG-MI (AA-1 90.94% even with enhanced TeD+), while Trap-MID drops it to 6.23%. In the worst-case run, Trap-MID still exceeds the best-case performance of all existing defenses against GMI, KED-MI, and PLG-MI (Table 10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of research: backdoor/trapdoor injection (originally a security threat, repurposed by TeD for adversarial detection) and model inversion defense. It departs from regularization-based defenses (MID, BiDO, NegLS) that reduce information leakage, and from misleading-based defenses (NetGuard, DCD) that require extra public datasets, shadow attacks, and protect only limited classes. The key conceptual departure is using full-image blended triggers with adversarial optimization for naturalness, rather than TeD's local patch triggers, and providing the first theoretical characterization of when trapdoors successfully mislead MI attacks via effectiveness-naturalness bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Trap-MID offers a more practical MI defense than existing misleading approaches by eliminating the need for auxiliary datasets, extra classifiers, or shadow attack simulations, while protecting all classes simultaneously. However, its vulnerability to KD-based attacks (LOMMA) and the variance introduced by random trigger initialization limit its robustness; the adaptive attack (PLG-MI++) still recovers 70% accuracy when the adversary knows trapdoor signatures. Open questions include extending to other modalities (language, graph, tabular), designing more stable triggers, enabling individual data providers to inject their own protections, and evaluating against more advanced face recognition models (ArcFace) and in-the-wild datasets.
