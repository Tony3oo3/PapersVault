## Problem

Continual pretraining research has focused on two extremes: infrequent large-scale updates over massive new data, and frequent sample-level knowledge edits. Real-world deployment of vision-language foundation models operates in the gap between these cases, requiring repeated minor updates to specific subdomains, fine-grained concepts, or new visual domains over a model's full lifecycle. No existing benchmark or systematic study addresses this regime with realistic compute constraints, fine-grained data-stream control, and zero-shot retention measurement.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes image-text pairs drawn from three sources at each update step: a pretraining pool (e.g., LAION-400M, CC-12M), a replay buffer of all previously seen update data, and the current task's update pool, mixed at configurable ratios (λ_P, λ_B, λ_D). Each update step is bounded by a fixed compute budget measured in Memory-Adjusted FLOPs (MAFs), which multiplies per-iteration FLOPs by the ratio of peak accelerator memory to full fine-tuning memory. The output is updated model weights evaluated on two axes: Knowledge Accumulation (average accuracy or recall@5 across 41 adaptation datasets) and Zero-Shot Retention (transfer performance on 22 held-out datasets), plotted against the initial zero-shot baseline and a joint-training upper bound.

## Method

The paper introduces FoMo-in-Flux, a benchmark of 63 curated image-text datasets (2.53M samples, 23,045 concepts) spanning natural images, sketches, synthetic, and medical domains, with fine-grained concept-level control over stream ordering (easy-to-hard, concept-frequency, similarity, time, dataset, random). The core empirical contribution is a systematic comparison of update methods—full fine-tuning, parameter-efficient tuning (LoRA, VeRA, DoRA), parameter-selective tuning (LNFit, BitFit), low-rank gradient projection (GaLore), regularization (EWC, SI), and three model-merging variants (EMA-merge, Finetune-merge, ZeroShot-merge)—under matched MAF budgets. A key technical contribution is meta-learning-rate scheduling, which extends task-level LR schedules (cosine or rsqrt) autoregressively across all prior tasks to reduce cumulative drift from pretraining; meta-schedules derived from infinite (rsqrt) schedules achieve the best accumulation-retention trade-off over long horizons. The study further shows that model merging combined with fine-tuning breaks the linear stability-plasticity trade-off, that increasing model size (ViT-S to ViT-g) improves retention without sacrificing accumulation, and that compute scaling benefits merging-based methods log-linearly while plain fine-tuning plateaus early.

## Results

On FoMo-in-Flux (20-task update cycle, ViT-B/16, 1.8×10⁹ FLOPs per update), full fine-tuning achieves the highest knowledge accumulation (A_KA) but suffers the largest zero-shot retention drop (A_ZS), while LNFit/BitFit show the opposite extreme. Model merging (EMA-merge, Finetune-merge) breaks the hypothetical linear trade-off between A_KA and A_ZS, achieving strong accumulation with significantly better retention than full fine-tuning. Meta learning rate schedules derived from rsqrt (infinite LR) schedules yield substantial gains in both accumulation and retention over task-independent cosine schedules. Scaling from ViT-B/16 to ViT-g/14 reduces the zero-shot retention loss from ΔA_ZS ≈ −3.2% to ≈ −0.05% while keeping knowledge accumulation roughly constant (ΔA_KA ≈ 9–10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Full fine-tuning with LR rewarming [Ibrahim et al., 76] achieves the strongest knowledge accumulation across the full update cycle but at the cost of a significant zero-shot retention drop, representing the plasticity extreme. LNFit [De Min et al., 37] and BitFit [Ben Zaken et al., 8] occupy the opposite extreme: excellent retention but minimal new knowledge acquisition over long streams. LoRA [Hu et al., 72] and DoRA [Liu et al., 104] provide an effectively linear interpolation between these two extremes, while VeRA [Kopiczko et al., 88], which drastically reduces tunable parameters, collapses toward the parameter-selective regime with very little knowledge gain. Among regularization-based CL methods, EWC [Kirkpatrick et al., 86] improves zero-shot retention but offers extremely limited accumulation compared to the initial zero-shot baseline, and SI [Zenke et al., 209] provides no measurable benefit over standard fine-tuning on either metric. GaLore [Zhao et al., 219] sits between full fine-tuning and LoRA, offering a moderate balance. The key positive finding is that model merging strategies—EMA-merge [Stojanovski et al., 172] and Finetune-merge [Ilharco et al., 78]—outperform all individual methods by simultaneously achieving near-full-fine-tuning accumulation while retaining substantially more zero-shot capability, effectively breaking the linear A_KA–A_ZS trade-off observed across all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual pretraining literature (TiC-CLIP, CLEAR, CGLM) by carving out the 'minor update' regime between full retraining and patch-level knowledge edits, formalized through a semantic versioning framework. Methodologically, it builds on model merging from Ilharco et al. (weight interpolation for open-vocabulary models) and Stojanovski et al. (momentum-based weight interpolation for CL), elevating them from small-scale CL to long-horizon multimodal pretraining. It departs from traditional continual learning by removing storage constraints in favor of compute budgets (introducing Memory-Adjusted FLOPs) and by operating on curated, concept-level data streams rather than noisy web-crawled corpora, enabling fine-grained control over update ordering and data mixtures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FoMo-in-Flux is the first benchmark to simultaneously address multimodal continual pretraining with compute constraints, data-centric stream design, zero-shot retention tracking, and minor-update semantics—filling a practical gap that prior benchmarks (which are either unimodal, unconstrained, or major-update-only) leave open. The finding that model merging combined with fine-tuning breaks the linear accumulation-retention trade-off and scales log-linearly with compute, while simple fine-tuning hits a ceiling, provides a concrete deployment recipe. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to LLM-based multimodal models (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo), whether the MAF metric generalizes across hardware, and how the data-ordering convergence result holds when update distributions are genuinely non-IID rather than permutations of a fixed pool.
