## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video diffusion models (e.g., CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) generate a single video per camera trajectory, so producing multiple videos of the same scene from different viewpoints yields severe inconsistencies in geometry, object identity, and motion. No prior method can jointly generate a set of camera-conditioned videos that share the same underlying 3D scene content and dynamics, a capability essential for large-scale 3D scene generation and multi-camera content creation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt and M camera trajectories (each a sequence of extrinsic and intrinsic parameters) and outputs M synchronized 16-frame videos in the Stable Diffusion latent space, where the first frame of every video is forced identical by sharing the same initial camera pose. Training is restricted to video pairs (M=2) due to the absence of large-scale multi-view dynamic video data, but a pairwise denoising inference algorithm extends generation to arbitrary M by selecting and averaging noise predictions over video pairs at each diffusion step.

## Method

The core architectural addition is a Cross-View Synchronization Module (CVSM) inserted into the temporal attention layers of CameraCtrl/AnimateDiff. For each pair of corresponding frames from two videos, the module performs a masked cross-view attention where the mask is determined by the epipolar geometry: a pixel in one frame can attend to a pixel in the other only if the epipolar distance (computed via the fundamental matrix between the two camera poses) falls below a threshold τ_epi=3. Training follows a two-phase hybrid strategy: on RealEstate10K (static indoor scenes with ground-truth poses), video pairs are created by folding a 31-frame clip into two 16-frame clips sharing the middle frame; on WebVid10M (dynamic in-the-wild videos without poses), pairs are created by duplicating a clip and applying random homography warps to the copy, with pseudo-epipolar lines derived from the homography matrix. The two phases alternate on the same CVSM instance, with LoRA fine-tuning of the respective backbones (CameraCtrl for RE10K, AnimateDiff for WV10M) while freezing base weights. At inference for M>2 videos, the algorithm selects P pairs per denoising step (via exhaustive, partitioning, or multi-partitioning strategies), computes noise predictions for each pair, and averages them per video, optionally with recurrent re-noising iterations for improved consistency.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (per-video geometry), CVD achieves 55.5/71.8/83.3 rotation AUC and 5.6/15.9/33.2 translation AUC at 5°/10°/20° thresholds, outperforming CameraCtrl (34.8/55.2/72.4) and MotionCtrl (49.0/68.0/81.2). On WebVid10M (cross-video geometry on generic scenes), CVD reaches 25.2/40.7/57.5 rotation AUC, more than doubling CameraCtrl+SparseCtrl (6.2/14.3/25.8) and roughly doubling MotionCtrl+SVD (12.2/28.2/48.0). For semantic consistency, CVD achieves the best CLIP-F score of 0.93 (vs. 0.86 for AnimateDiff+SparseCtrl) and the best KID of 0.61, while maintaining competitive FID (32.90) and FVD (285) on par with CameraCtrl.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVD outperforms all five baselines on every geometry metric. Against CameraCtrl [18], CVD improves rotation AUC by ~21 points at 5° on RE10K (55.5 vs. 34.8) and by ~19 points on WV10M (25.2 vs. 6.2 for CameraCtrl+SparseCtrl), with matching score jumping from 27.3 to 42.3 on RE10K. Against MotionCtrl [60], CVD leads on rotation AUC (55.5 vs. 49.0 on RE10K; 25.2 vs. 12.2 on WV10M) and matching score (42.3 vs. 38.9 on RE10K; 23.5 vs. 12.8 on WV10M). On semantic consistency, CVD achieves the highest CLIP-F (0.93) versus AnimateDiff+SparseCtrl (0.86) and CameraCtrl (0.79), confirming the cross-view module improves cross-video alignment. On fidelity, CVD's FID (32.90) is comparable to CameraCtrl (32.10) and its KID (0.61) is the best among all methods, while FVD (285) is slightly higher than CameraCtrl (277) but far better than AnimateDiff+SparseCtrl (327) and CameraCtrl+SparseCtrl (430). The ablation further confirms that removing epipolar attention drops rotation AUC from 25.2 to 16.8 on WV10M, validating the geometric constraint as the key differentiator over baselines that lack cross-video structure enforcement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CameraCtrl and AnimateDiff by inserting a Cross-View Synchronization Module that applies epipolar-geometry-constrained attention between corresponding frames of multiple videos, drawing on the pose-guided diffusion idea from PoseGuided-Diffusion but generalizing it from static indoor images to dynamic video. It departs from prior camera-controlled video generation (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl), which handles only single trajectories, and from multi-view image generation (Zero123, SyncDreamer), which is restricted to static objects or scenes without dynamics. The hybrid training strategy—folding RealEstate10K clips for geometric supervision and applying homography augmentation to WebVid10M for motion diversity—is a practical workaround for the absence of large-scale multi-view dynamic video datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CVD is the first framework to jointly address multi-video geometric/semantic consistency and per-video camera control, opening a path toward large-scale 3D scene generation with editable trajectories. The theoretical score-function decomposition (Theorem A.3) provides a principled justification for extending a pairwise-trained model to arbitrary video counts at inference, though the approximation quality depends on the assumption that a few views suffice to recover underlying geometry. Key limitations include inheritance of base-model artifacts (shape-shifting, dynamic inconsistencies), inability to operate in real time, and the fact that the multi-view extension is validated only up to 6 views with relatively simple trajectory configurations.
