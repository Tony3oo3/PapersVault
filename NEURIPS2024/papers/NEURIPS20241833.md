## Problem

Normalizing flow models for trajectory prediction rely on a standard Gaussian prior, which is symmetric and single-modal, conflicting with the inherently asymmetric and multi-modal distribution of real pedestrian motion. This mismatch causes the flow to overfit to the most-likelihood mode and fail to generate under-represented trajectory patterns (e.g., U-turns, sharp turns), resulting in poor diversity. Additionally, the dominant 'best-of-M' evaluation protocol (ADE/FDE) further incentivizes collapsing toward the distribution mean, masking the diversity deficit.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the 2D historical positions of all agents in a scene from t0 to tc (preprocessed by centering at the current position and converting to relative offsets), optionally augmented with map observations. It outputs a batch of M future trajectory candidates (from tc to tT) sampled from different sub-Gaussians of the mixed prior, along with per-sample probability density estimates enabled by the invertible flow transformation. The M candidates are distributed across K sub-Gaussians (default K=8) according to their mixture weights, ensuring coverage of distinct motion intentions.

## Method

MGF replaces the standard Gaussian prior in a normalizing flow with a data-driven mixed Gaussian prior constructed by k-means clustering of training-set future trajectories into K clusters, whose centers, weights, and (optionally learnable) variances define the mixture. The flow itself is a stack of Conditional Continuously-indexed Flows (CIFs) built from RealNVP layers, conditioned on a Trajectron++ encoder's representation of historical trajectories. Training uses a symmetric cross-entropy loss combining a forward NLL term (computed in the nearest sub-Gaussian rather than the full mixture, to prevent mode collapse) and an inverse minimum-L2 term over M generated candidates. This design relieves the Jacobian-learning difficulty of transforming a symmetric prior into a multi-modal target, yielding both higher diversity (measured by proposed APD/FPD metrics) and state-of-the-art alignment (ADE/FDE) on ETH/UCY and SDD. Controllability is achieved by directly editing the mixture parameters (weights, means, variances) at inference time without any retraining.

## Results

On ETH/UCY (Best-of-20), MGF achieves a mean ADE of 0.21 m and FDE of 0.34 m, matching EqMotion on ADE and slightly improving FDE, while outperforming all other baselines on the mean score. On SDD, MGF achieves ADE 7.74 px and FDE 12.07 px, beating the previous SOTA MemoNet by 9.6% on ADE. For diversity, MGF achieves mean APD 1.194 m and FPD 2.481 m on ETH/UCY, a 103.7% and 140.6% improvement over FlowChain respectively, while maintaining state-of-the-art alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGF ties with EqMotion on mean ADE (0.21 m) and edges it out on mean FDE (0.34 vs 0.35 m) on ETH/UCY, while achieving substantially better diversity (APD 1.194 vs AgentFormer's 1.133, FPD 2.481 vs 2.618). Against AgentFormer, MGF improves alignment by 8.7% ADE and 12.8% FDE while remaining competitive on diversity, and unlike AgentFormer, MGF is fully invertible for per-sample likelihood estimation. Compared to the closest normalizing-flow baseline FlowChain, MGF achieves 27.6% ADE and 34.6% FDE improvement on ETH/UCY, and 103.7% APD / 140.6% FPD improvement in diversity. On SDD, MGF outperforms the previous SOTA MemoNet by 9.6% ADE (7.74 vs 8.56 px) and 4.6% FDE (12.07 vs 12.66 px), and improves over FlowChain by 22.1% ADE and 29.7% FDE. MGF also beats GroupNet (0.25/0.44) and PECNet (0.29/0.48) on mean ETH/UCY ADE/FDE by 16% and 28% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGF extends the normalizing flow paradigm for trajectory prediction (Kobyzev et al. 2020) by replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a data-driven mixed Gaussian prior constructed via k-means clustering of training trajectories. It adopts FlowChain's (Maeda & Ukita 2023) stack of Conditional Continuously-indexed Flows (CIFs) for the invertible transformation and R2P2's (Rhinehart et al. 2018) symmetric cross-entropy loss for training. While GMM priors have been explored in VAEs, GANs, and semi-supervised flows (Dilokthanakul et al. 2017; Ben-Yosef & Weinshall 2018; Izmailov et al. 2019), those typically require auxiliary labels; MGF constructs the mixture purely from trajectory geometry without annotations, positioning it as a targeted fix for the symmetry/multi-modality mismatch in flow-based trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental structural mismatch in flow-based trajectory prediction: the symmetric single-modal prior is ill-suited to the asymmetric multi-modal target, and this mismatch degrades both diversity and the learning of the Jacobian transformation. By making the prior data-driven and parametric, MGF gains practical controllability (adjusting cluster weights or means without retraining) while preserving invertibility for per-sample likelihood estimation. Limitations include the absence of map/obstacle information, occasional inter-agent collisions, and the fact that the proposed APD/FPD diversity metrics are not yet widely adopted, making cross-method diversity comparisons somewhat self-referential. Open questions include whether the k-means-based prior construction is optimal versus learned alternatives, and whether the approach generalizes to 3D or more complex multi-modal scenes.
