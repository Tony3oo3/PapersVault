## Problem

Standard MLP-based implicit neural representations impose global constraints on a single function, making them prone to spectral bias (difficulty fitting sharp boundaries) and limiting parallelization and scaling. While mixture-of-experts architectures have proven effective for scaling large language models, they have been overlooked for INRs, and a naive MoE extension to INRs fails because the manager network cannot adequately leverage the per-sample supervisory signal to learn meaningful domain subdivision.

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinates as input (images scaled to the unit cube after 4× downsampling and 1:1 cropping; audio as 1D time coordinates; 3D point clouds scaled to [-1,1]³ with a [-1.2,1.2]³ domain) and produces reconstructed signal values at those coordinates. During training, ground truth signal values (pixel intensities, audio amplitudes, or signed distance values) supervise the experts. For 3D reconstruction, the SDF output is converted to a surface mesh via marching cubes on a 512³ grid, and the manager's routing assignment provides a learned spatial partition of the domain.

## Method

Neural Experts replaces the single MLP in an INR with a shared expert encoder, N specialized expert sub-networks, and a manager (gating) network that routes each input to its best expert. Two key innovations enable effective training: (1) the manager is conditioned on the expert encoder output via concatenation, giving it access to signal-level context for routing decisions, and (2) the manager is pretrained with a cross-entropy loss against a random uniform expert assignment, which shapes the initial gating distribution and balances expert load. Training proceeds in two phases: first, all parameters are optimized with a weighted MoE reconstruction loss (each expert's squared error weighted by its gating probability), then the manager and encoders are frozen while only the experts are fine-tuned to stabilize the routing. This design lets the network learn piecewise-continuous signal representations with the gating function providing a global perspective for domain subdivision, achieving higher reconstruction quality with fewer parameters than wider single-MLP baselines.

## Results

On the Kodak image reconstruction benchmark, Neural Experts with SINE activation achieves 89.35 mean PSNR (366k params), substantially outperforming SIREN Base (57.23 PSNR, 99.8k params) and SIREN Wider (77.50 PSNR, 642.8k params). With FINER activation, the method reaches 90.84 PSNR. For 3D surface reconstruction, the MoE Large model achieves 0.8180 mean Trimap IoU (d=0.001) with 1.3M parameters, compared to 0.6662 for SIREN Large (1.5M params), and the MoE Small model (323k params) outperforms SIREN Small (396k params) at 0.7265 vs. 0.5874. For audio reconstruction, the method reduces MSE by over 40% compared to SIREN while using fewer parameters (e.g., 0.12 vs. 0.71 MSE on Bach).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baseline families. Against SIREN [39], the proposed method achieves a dramatic improvement: 89.35 PSNR vs. 57.23 (Base, 99.8k params) and 77.50 (Wider, 642.8k params) on Kodak images, using only 366k parameters. Against SoftPlus/SAL [2], the method wins on PSNR for SoftPlus+FF (31.66 vs. 29.48 Wider) but is slightly below the Wider variant for plain SoftPlus (20.62 vs. 20.91). Against Fourier Features [41], the method clearly outperforms both Base (28.97) and Wider (29.48) at 31.66 PSNR. Against FINER [24], the method achieves 90.84 PSNR vs. 80.32 (Wider) and 58.08 (Base). Against InstantNGP [29] (supplemental), the method reaches 89.35–90.84 PSNR with 366k parameters versus 84.56 PSNR with 7.7M parameters, demonstrating an order-of-magnitude parameter efficiency advantage. For 3D surface reconstruction, the MoE approach outperforms SIREN at both model sizes on Trimap IoU (0.8180 vs. 0.6662 for Large; 0.7265 vs. 0.5874 for Small) and Chamfer distance, with the Small MoE model even surpassing the Large SIREN baseline on most shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the original Mixture of Experts formulation (Jacobs et al. 1991; Nowlan & Hinton 1990) — with its hard-routing manager and specialized expert sub-networks — to the implicit neural representation (INR) setting, where prior MoE-for-INR work (e.g., [46, 49]) used sparsely-gated MoE layers that cannot produce the sharp, piecewise-continuous boundaries the original formulation allows. The key architectural departures from both vanilla MoE and prior INR specialization methods (grid-based [7,40], hash-based [29]) are (1) conditioning the manager on the expert encoder output via concatenation, and (2) a random-uniform pretraining stage for the gating network. The paper positions itself against frequency-encoding INRs (SIREN, FINER, Fourier features) and hybrid spatial-parameter methods (InstantNGP), showing that learned spatial subdivision via experts outperforms fixed or heuristic spatial allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE-INR framework provides a practical path to local editing of neural representations (e.g., modifying one expert's region without affecting others), a capability absent in monolithic INRs, and the conditioning/pretraining insights are broadly applicable to any INR that benefits from spatial specialization. The finding that random pretraining dramatically outperforms semantic segmentation (SAM, K-means) for manager initialization challenges the intuition that 'better' spatial priors should help, suggesting balanced coverage is what matters. Open questions include whether the approach extends to NeRF-style rendering (which lacks per-instance loss), how it scales to very large expert counts with sharding, and whether the local-editing capability can be combined with diffusion models for generative INR editing. Training time grows linearly with expert count, which remains a practical bottleneck.
