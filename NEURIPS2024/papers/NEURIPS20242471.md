## Problem

Existing diffusion-based synthetic face recognition (SFR) models produce training data that generalizes poorly to real-world faces, showing significant verification accuracy degradation compared to models trained on authentic data. The authors identify that prior methods operate in the original score vector field without injecting identity information and fail to consider the face manifold structure for diversity, leaving three critical properties unaddressed: inter-class diversity, intra-class diversity, and intra-class identity preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242471/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model conditions on a pretrained face recognition embedding (a unit vector on S^{d-1}) and face attributes (age in [0,100] and 3D pose angles in [-90°,90°]³) alongside Gaussian noise to generate RGB face images. For full dataset generation, N anchor embeddings are placed on the unit hypersphere via the Tammes problem to maximize inter-class separation, and m perturbed embeddings per anchor (with similarity scores sampled from U[lb,ub)) provide intra-class diversity. The resulting synthetic dataset (e.g., 10K identities × 50 images) trains a standard LResNet50-IR face recognition model without any real face images during training or inference.

## Method

ID³ extends a U-Net denoising network with two conditioning signals—identity embedding and face attributes—and introduces a novel loss containing an inner-product term that explicitly encourages the denoised image's face embedding to align with the target identity. Theoretically, minimizing this loss is shown equivalent to maximizing the lower bound of an adjusted conditional log-likelihood p̃(x|y,s) ∝ p(x|y,s)·p(y,s|x)^w, where the reversed likelihood term shifts the distribution toward identity-preserving data. This equivalence motivates an ID-preserving sampling algorithm performing Langevin dynamics on the adjusted score ∇log p̃(x_t|y,s) = ∇log p(x_t|y,s) + w·∇log p(y,s|x_t), where the reversed likelihood score is computed as a scaled inner product κ_x·y^T∇f_φ(x_t). The dataset-generating algorithm solves the Tammes problem for maximally-separated anchor embeddings and per-anchor optimization problems to produce diverse yet identity-consistent embeddings, paired with random attributes to yield the full synthetic training set.

## Results

On five face verification benchmarks (LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, AgeDB, CALFW), ID³ trained on FFHQ achieves an average accuracy of 86.50%, outperforming the previous best diffusion-based SFR method IDiff-Face (84.09%) by ~2.4%. When trained on CASIA-WebFace, ID³ reaches 89.80% average accuracy, slightly surpassing DCFace (89.56%) which uses both FFHQ and CASIA-WebFace as training data and introduces real face images as auxiliary data during FR model training. The largest gains appear on AgeDB (83.78% vs. 78.40% for IDiff-Face) and CFP-FP (85.00% vs. 82.00%), reflecting improved age and pose generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID³ outperforms all five FFHQ-trained baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor IDiff-Face, ID³ gains +2.4% average (86.50% vs. 84.09%), with the largest margins on AgeDB (+5.38%) and CFP-FP (+3.00%), indicating superior age and pose diversity in the generated data. Against GAN-based methods, the gap is even wider: ID³ surpasses SynFace by +11.75% average, SFace by +9.55%, DigiFace by +15.31%, and ID-Net by +14.97%. When trained on CASIA-WebFace, ID³ (89.80%) narrowly edges out DCFace (89.56%), which is notable because DCFace uses both FFHQ and CASIA-WebFace for training and additionally introduces real face images as auxiliary data during the downstream FR model training phase, whereas ID³ uses no real images at any stage beyond the initial generative model training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based Synthetic Face Recognition (SFR) work initiated by IDiff-Face and DCFace, which condition diffusion models on identity embeddings to generate face training data. The key departure is the introduction of an inner-product term in the denoising loss that, via a theoretical equivalence (Theorem 3.1), shifts the model's objective from the standard conditional likelihood to an 'adjusted' likelihood incorporating a von Mises-Fisher reversed likelihood. This motivates a Langevin sampling algorithm operating on an augmented score vector field, and a dataset-generation strategy that solves the Tammes problem for inter-class diversity—neither of which appears in prior SFR methods that operate purely in the original score field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFR is practically important for privacy-preserving face recognition under regulations like GDPR, and ID³'s fully automated pipeline (no manual filtering, no auxiliary real images at inference) makes it more deployable than competitors like DCFace. The dramatic ablation showing that removing the reversed-likelihood score collapses performance from 89.80% to 65.05% underscores that identity-preserving sampling is not a minor refinement but a structural necessity. However, the authors acknowledge a remaining gap versus FR models trained on real data (e.g., MS1M), leaving open the question of how much of the real-world face distribution is fundamentally unattainable from synthetic generation alone.
