## Problem

Multi-task offline RL methods based on sequence modeling (e.g., Decision Transformer, Prompt-DT) struggle to generalize to unseen, unlabeled tasks in Meta-RL settings because they either train and test on the same task or rely on fine-tuning with labeled test data. As the number of training tasks grows, gradient conflicts between tasks hinder the extraction of shared knowledge, and the data-hungry nature of transformers is exacerbated by the typically small RL datasets. No prior work combines parameter-efficient prompt decomposition with pre-trained language model initialization and label-free test-time adaptation for offline RL.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes trajectory sequences of (return-to-go, state, action) triplets of length K=20, prepended with a decomposed prompt of length 30, and outputs the predicted action at each timestep. During test-time adaptation, the model ingests unlabeled test sequences collected by feeding randomly sampled actions into the environment (yielding (r̂, s, a) tuples), and produces an updated cross-task prompt by aligning per-layer hidden-state statistics with precomputed training-set statistics. Only 1.42M parameters (1.14% of the full model) are trainable, with the GPT-2-SMALL backbone kept frozen throughout.

## Method

DPDT initializes a 12-layer, 12-head Decision Transformer with frozen GPT-2-SMALL weights to inject rich prior knowledge, then learns prompts via a two-stage paradigm. In the decomposed prompt tuning stage, each task prompt P_k* is factorized as the element-wise product of a shared cross-task prompt P_c and a task-specific prompt P_k, where P_k is further decomposed into two low-rank vectors (v_k, u_k) in the spirit of LoRA to reduce parameter count; a knowledge-distillation loss against individually trained teacher prompts enforces that P_c captures general knowledge while P_k retains task-specific information. In the test-time adaptation stage, the cross-task prompt P_c serves as initialization and is further optimized on unlabeled test data by minimizing an L1 alignment loss between per-layer mean and variance of test hidden states and their precomputed training-set counterparts, requiring no action labels. The total training loss combines action-prediction MSE with a weighted distillation term (λ=0.5), and the entire procedure trains only the prompts while keeping the transformer backbone frozen.

## Results

In zero-shot Meta-RL generalization across seven environments (Cheetah-dir, Cheetah-vel, Ant-dir, MetaWorld ML10/ML45/MT10/MT50), DPDT achieves an average cumulative return of 518.28, outperforming all baselines. The method uses only 1.42M trainable parameters (1.14% of the full model), compared to 125.5M (100%) for MT-BC, MT-ORL, and Prompt-DT. The largest gains appear on MetaWorld MT50 (1559.94 vs. 994.63 for Prompt-DT) and Cheetah-dir (50.32 vs. −7.92 for Prompt-DT), while the smallest margin is on Ant-dir (121.84 vs. 123.46 for Prompt-DT). Without PLM initialization (DPDT-WP), performance drops to 374.66, confirming the critical role of GPT-2-Small prior knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPDT outperforms all five baselines on the average cumulative return across the seven zero-shot environments. Against the strongest baseline Prompt-DT (373.88), DPDT gains +144.4 points on average, with the largest margin on MetaWorld MT50 (+565.3) and Cheetah-dir (+58.2), though it slightly trails Prompt-DT on Ant-dir (121.84 vs. 123.46). Compared to MT-ORL (354.04), which trains a full multi-task Decision Transformer, DPDT improves by +164.2 while using 98.86% fewer trainable parameters. Soft-Prompt (130.62) and HDT (309.79) are substantially outperformed, confirming that a single shared prompt or hypernetwork-initialized adaptation module cannot match decomposed prompt tuning with PLM initialization. MT-BC (225.76), the behavior-cloning baseline, is the weakest method overall. Notably, DPDT-WP (without GPT-2 initialization) scores 374.66, nearly matching Prompt-DT, which isolates the contribution of PLM prior knowledge to the final performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPDT extends the Decision Transformer lineage (DT → Prompt-DT) by introducing trainable prompt decomposition into the offline RL setting, drawing on prompt-tuning and LoRA-style low-rank factorization from NLP. It departs from Prompt-DT in three key ways: (1) decomposing each task prompt into a shared cross-task component and a task-specific low-rank component to mitigate gradient conflicts, (2) initializing the transformer backbone with frozen GPT-2-SMALL parameters to inject prior knowledge, and (3) applying feature-alignment-based test-time adaptation to the cross-task prompt on unlabeled unseen tasks. The knowledge distillation from per-task teacher prompts to enforce clean separation between shared and specific knowledge is a novel regularization strategy not present in prior multi-task prompt work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive zero-shot Meta-RL performance with only 1.14% trainable parameters (1.42M vs. 125.5M), making multi-task offline RL substantially more accessible in compute-constrained settings. The TTA component yields large gains on complex manipulation tasks (e.g., MW MT50: 824→1559), suggesting that distribution alignment at test time is a critical but underexploited lever in offline RL. Open questions include whether the PLM initialization advantage persists in domains with larger inter-domain gaps (the authors note Ant-dir underperforms), whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional continuous control, and whether the low-rank decomposition can be made adaptive per task rather than fixed-rank.
