## Problem

Integrating structured knowledge from Knowledge Graphs into LLMs remains challenging because existing approaches either linearize KG triples into text (losing relational structure) or project GNN embeddings into token space (requiring task-specific fine-tuning with limited generalization). The paper asks whether the visual modality of Large Vision Language Models can preserve and exploit graph structure more effectively than these text-based or embedding-based strategies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and a synthetic RGB image of a 2-hop KG subgraph (rendered via Graphviz from ConceptNet) as joint input to an LVLM, producing a text answer. During the curriculum fine-tuning stage, the model also processes self-constructed graph-feature queries (e.g., 'How many nodes are in the graph?') paired with automatically derived answers from the subgraph metadata. For the reverse VQA direction, the same fine-tuned model accepts standard benchmark images (scientific diagrams, charts) plus a text question and outputs a text answer, requiring no human-curated VQA training data.

## Method

GraphVis retrieves 2-hop subgraphs from ConceptNet for each question, renders them as images using Graphviz, and feeds these visual graphs into LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B alongside the text prompt. The key technical contribution is a two-stage curriculum fine-tuning scheme: first, the LVLM is trained on self-supervised visual graph comprehension tasks (node/edge counting, degree detection, triple listing) to build basic graph-reading ability, then it is fine-tuned on the original QA pairs augmented with the corresponding visual subgraph for KG-enhanced reasoning. Both stages use LoRA (r=128, alpha=256) with a single epoch each, keeping the vision encoder and projection network trainable at a higher learning rate. This curriculum ordering yields a 4.51% gain over joint fine-tuning, and the approach simultaneously improves zero-shot VQA on ScienceQA (+4.32%) and MMBench (+2.66%) by transferring graph-image comprehension to real-world diagrams.

## Results

On CommonsenseQA (CSQA), GraphVis achieves 82.8% accuracy, a +12.3% improvement over the base LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B model (70.5%), and on OpenBookQA (OBQA) it reaches 85.5% (+9.9% over the base 75.6%). On VQA benchmarks, GraphVis improves ScienceQA image accuracy from 68.86% to 73.18% (+4.32%) and MMBench overall from 63.75% to 66.41% (+2.66%), while also reducing object hallucinations on POPE by an average of 1.09% across random, popular, and adversarial settings. The method uses only LoRA fine-tuning on a 7B model, surpassing larger and fully fine-tuned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphVis (7B, LoRA) outperforms KSL with GPT-3.5 (>100B) by 3.2% on CSQA (82.8 vs. 79.6) and by 3.9% on OBQA (85.5 vs. 81.6), despite using a model roughly 15× smaller. It also surpasses GNP (FLAN-T5-xxlarge, 11B, fine-tuned) by 5.7% on OBQA (85.5 vs. 79.8) and GreaseLM (AristoRoBERTa, 355M, fully fine-tuned) by 4.3% on CSQA (82.8 vs. 78.5). Notably, the prompting-based KAPING baseline actually degrades the base LVLM's performance (67.7 vs. 70.5 on CSQA), confirming that linearized triple text is counterproductive for strong LLMs. Among the fully fine-tuned small-model baselines (QA-GNN, GreaseLM), GraphVis leads on both CSQA and OBQA. On VQA tasks, GraphVis improves over its own base model on ScienceQA (+4.32%), MMBench (+2.66%), and POPE (~+1.09% avg.), demonstrating cross-modal transfer from synthetic KG graph images to real-world diagram understanding without any human-curated VQA training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for KG-enhanced LLMs—verbalizing triples into text prompts (KAPING, KSL) and projecting GNN embeddings into token space (QA-GNN, GreaseLM, GNP)—by instead rendering retrieved subgraphs as images and processing them through a Large Vision Language Model (LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B). It builds directly on the LLaVA architecture and the k-hop subgraph retrieval strategy from Feng et al. (2020), but introduces a curriculum fine-tuning scheme (basic graph-feature comprehension → KG-augmented QA reasoning) that has no direct analogue in prior KG-LLM work. The method also repurposes the LVLM's pre-trained visual capabilities, which were never trained on graph-structured images, as a mechanism for preserving relational structure that text linearization inherently loses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a bidirectional cross-modal benefit: KG subgraph images improve textual QA accuracy, and conversely, synthetic graph images derived from textual QA data improve zero-shot VQA on benchmarks containing diagram-like images (ScienceQA, MMBench) and reduce object hallucination (POPE). This suggests that the scarcity of graph-structured images in LVLM training data is a real bottleneck that can be addressed with synthetic data. Key limitations include evaluation on a single 7B model with LoRA, reliance on ConceptNet and 2-hop subgraphs, and absence of error bars; open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, different KGs, deeper subgraphs, and whether multiple subgraphs from heterogeneous KG sources can be jointly leveraged.
