## Problem

Existing structural inference methods for dynamical systems (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI) rely on uniformly sampled trajectories and full observation of all nodes, making them brittle under irregular sampling intervals and partial observability. This paper addresses the gap by developing a framework that simultaneously handles non-uniform time intervals and unobserved intermediate nodes, which convert direct interactions into multi-hop relationships that fixed-interval, direct-interaction models cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242398/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional node feature trajectories with non-uniform time intervals (T steps, where T may be reduced from 49 to as few as 10), optionally augmented with a set of known edges as prior knowledge. Node features are first compressed to 1D via an MLP embedding before being processed per-node as sequential inputs to the SSM blocks. The output is a directed adjacency matrix estimated as the Monte Carlo average of B sampled graphs from the GFN posterior, yielding marginal edge probabilities P(i→j | U_All) that can be thresholded for a hard structure.

## Method

SICSM conjoins two components: (1) an encoder-decoder stack of L Residual Blocks, each containing a selective SSM (Mamba-style) that learns input-dependent Δ, B, C per node to adapt to irregular time intervals, and (2) a Joint Structure-Parameters GFN (JSP-GFN) that sequentially adds directed edges to construct the interaction graph. The key architectural choice for partial observation is summing outputs from all L encoder blocks (rather than using only the final block), which aggregates dynamics across multiple temporal dependencies—shorter dependencies recover direct edges while longer dependencies resolve multi-hop paths through unobserved intermediaries. The GFN's reward function combines a message-passing likelihood, a unit-normal parameter prior, and a graph prior with Dirichlet energy, connectivity, and sparsity regularization. Training jointly minimizes MSE for time-series forecasting and a SubTB objective for the GFN, with prior knowledge integrated by initializing the GFN's starting graph with known edges.

## Results

SICSM maintains AUROC above 85% on Spring Simulations and NetSim even when time steps are reduced from 49 to 10, while VAE-based baselines (NRI, ACD, MPM, iSIDG, RCSI) degrade to near-random performance under the same irregular sampling. On the VN_SP_30 dataset with only 60% of nodes observed, SICSM sustains AUROC above 80%, whereas NRI and ACD drop below 75%. On the challenging biological networks BF and TF, SICSM consistently surpasses all baselines by 5–10% AUROC across every sampling level. Additional metrics (AUPRC, SHD, F1) in the appendix confirm the same ordering, with SICSM leading on all three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SICSM outperforms all eight baselines across the 16 evaluated datasets, with the margin widening under irregular sampling and partial observation. The five VAE-based methods (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI) are the most severely affected by irregular sampling, with the paper noting they 'often perform no better than random guessing' when time intervals become non-uniform, because they assume fixed sampling intervals. JSP-GFN, the closest architectural relative (also GFN-based), is limited to one-dimensional features and is outperformed by SICSM particularly at lower node-sampling percentages (60–70%), where SICSM's multi-block aggregation of temporal dependencies provides a clear advantage. SIDEC (dynamics-encoding with partial correlations) is the strongest non-VAE baseline and shows competitive AUROC, but lacks an adaptive transition function and consistently trails SICSM, especially as the fraction of observed nodes decreases. CUTS, designed for irregular time-series causal discovery, is included as a baseline but does not match SICSM's structural inference accuracy on the graph-structure task. In the prior-knowledge experiments (0–30% known edges), SICSM shows the steepest AUROC improvement with increasing prior information, while VAE-based methods require complex supervised adjustments in latent space and SIDEC has no feasible prior-knowledge integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

SICSM sits at the intersection of two research threads: selective state space models (Mamba) for input-dependent temporal modeling, and Generative Flow Networks (specifically JSP-GFN) for Bayesian structural inference. It departs from the dominant VAE-based structural inference lineage (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI) by replacing the fixed-interval, fully-observed assumption with learned, data-dependent time intervals (Δ) and multi-scale dynamics aggregation. Unlike prior graph-Mamba works that apply selective SSMs to graph representation learning, this paper is the first to use the selective SSM mechanism specifically for structural inference from observational trajectories, and it repurposes the JSP-GFN framework by feeding it aggregated multi-block dynamics rather than raw node features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous handling of irregular sampling and partial observation addresses two practical data challenges that are common in scientific and engineering applications but have been largely ignored by prior structural inference methods. The architectural insight that aggregating dynamics across multiple residual blocks captures both direct (short-hop) and indirect (multi-hop) interactions is a useful design principle for partially observed systems. Limitations include the assumption of a static edge set, the requirement to know a priori whether observation is full or partial, and the PEMS datasets being mentioned but not clearly integrated into the main experimental evaluation. Open questions include extending to dynamic/mutable structures, adaptive weighting of block outputs based on graph topology, and scaling to very large systems beyond 100 nodes.
