## Problem

Existing NLOS imaging algorithms assume dense, regular scanning over large relay surfaces, making them inapplicable to realistic irregular relay surfaces (e.g., latticed windows, fences) where undersampling causes severe ill-posedness. Learning-based methods that could handle this require large amounts of paired ground-truth albedo data that is prohibitively expensive to acquire and align. The paper tackles the problem of reconstructing hidden 3D scenes from noisy, irregularly undersampled transients in a fully unsupervised manner, specifically addressing the ambiguity of the measurement consistency constraint in the null space of the forward operator.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy irregularly undersampled transients (spatial-temporal histograms, e.g., 128×128×256 or 128×128×512 with bin widths of 8–33 ps) acquired by scanning an irregular relay surface, which are zero-padded to a full scanning grid before processing. A 3D binary relay surface mask (the 2D sampling pattern repeated t times along the time dimension) is used both to mask invalid voxels during partial convolution and to decouple the forward operator for efficient virtual scanning. The output is a 3D albedo volume of size l×l×z representing the hidden scene's reflectance.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage unsupervised pipeline. Stage 1 trains a P-Unet denoiser with 3D partial convolution using a physics-guided SURE loss derived from the Poisson-plus-dark-count noise model of the time-resolved detector, requiring no clean references. Stage 2 trains VSRnet (a 3D residual network with attention gating) using a combined measurement-consistency (MC) loss and a novel virtual scanning (VS) loss: the reconstructed albedo ρ⁽¹⁾ is forward-projected through a *different* relay surface operator H_k (decoupled as M_k ⊙ H_r for FFT-based efficiency), then reconstructed again to yield ρ⁽²⁾, and the L2 distance between ρ⁽¹⁾ and ρ⁽²⁾ is minimized. This virtual scanning forces the network to learn null-space components that the MC loss alone cannot constrain, effectively extending reconstruction beyond the range space of any single observation operator. Training uses 200 shutter-like relay surface patterns (5 intervals × 40 rotations) over 8,000 simulated alphanumerics transients, with the denoiser frozen before the reconstruction network is trained.

## Results

On the simulated 'bunny' dataset with irregular relay surfaces, the proposed method achieves the highest PSNR among all compared methods, successfully recovering geometric structures without aliasing artifacts. In inference time, the method runs in 0.18 s on GPU (NVIDIA 3090) and 2.24 s on CPU, compared to 7.73 hours for the SOTA iterative method CC-SOCR — a 12,000× speedup in CPU mode. The ablation study confirms that the virtual scanning process contributes +0.89 dB and the SURE-based denoiser contributes +1.83 dB (average over 15 relay surfaces on a 1,000-sample complex-geometry dataset), with the full method reaching 19.03 ± 0.64 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: three direct reconstruction methods (LCT, FK, RSD), two learning-based methods (NeTF, USM), and one iterative method (CC-SOCR). On simulated and real-world data, LCT and RSD exhibit severe aliasing artifacts from irregular undersampling, while FK is depth-sensitive and fails to recover far-end structures. NeTF produces blurry reconstructions with diffused artifacts, and USM (designed for regular undersampling) is nearly overwhelmed by aliasing on irregular patterns. CC-SOCR recovers main objects but loses fine structures at distant depths and underperforms on glossy or retroreflective surfaces. The proposed method outperforms all baselines in reconstruction quality across diverse relay surfaces and material types, while being 12,000× faster than CC-SOCR on CPU and producing results in 0.18 s on GPU — comparable to or faster than the one-step direct methods (LCT: 0.09 s, FK: 0.15 s, RSD: 0.12 s) despite its two-stage denoise-then-reconstruct pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of model-based NLOS reconstruction (LCT, FK, CC-SOCR) and learning-based approaches (NeTF, USM), but departs from both by being fully unsupervised and handling irregular undersampling. It builds on the range-null space decomposition framework from inverse problems literature and the SURE-based denoising paradigm from computational imaging, adapting both to the specific Poisson-plus-dark-count noise model of time-resolved NLOS detectors. The virtual scanning strategy is the key conceptual departure: rather than relying on hand-crafted regularization (as in CC-SOCR) or paired supervision (as in USM), it uses a second forward operator to create a self-consistency constraint that forces the network to learn null-space components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical barrier this paper addresses—requiring dense, regular scanning over large relay surfaces—is a major obstacle to deploying NLOS imaging in real-world settings like latticed windows or fences. The 12,000× speedup over CC-SOCR and the elimination of paired-data requirements make the approach far more accessible for practical systems. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to confocal geometry, was trained only on simulated alphanumeric data, and lacks error bars across multiple seeds. Open questions include extending to non-confocal systems, training directly on real transients to close the sim-to-real gap, and characterizing the minimum viable sampling rate for successful reconstruction.
