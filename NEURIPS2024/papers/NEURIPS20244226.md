## Problem

Entropy estimation from samples requires near-linear sample complexity in the domain size k, which is impractical for high-dimensional distributions. This paper asks whether the weaker testing question—deciding whether an unknown distribution p equals a known reference q or their entropies differ by at least ε—can be solved with substantially fewer samples. As a corollary, it addresses identity testing for bounded in-degree Bayesian networks, where prior results required strong structural assumptions (shared topological ordering, balanced conditional probabilities, or identical factorization structure) that severely limited applicability.

## Input / Output

The entropy tester receives m i.i.d. samples (drawn in the Poissonized setting) from an unknown discrete distribution p over [k], the full PMF of a reference distribution q, and an accuracy parameter ε, and outputs accept (p = q) or reject (|H(p)−H(q)| ≥ ε) with probability at least 2/3. For the Bayesian network application, the input is sample access to a degree-d Bayes net p and the full description (DAG G plus all conditional probability tables) of a reference degree-d Bayes net q over {0,1}^n; the algorithm runs in time poly(n^d, 1/ε) and distinguishes p = q from d_H(p,q) ≥ Ω(ε).

## Method

The key insight is decomposing the entropy gap |H(p_A) − H(q_A)| on a 'large-mass' subset A (where q_i ≥ τ/k) into a KL divergence term d_KL(p_A‖q_A) plus a weighted probability-difference term |Σ_{i∈A}(p_i−q_i)log(1/q_i)|, then testing whether either term is large. The algorithm first performs an early-rejection check on the small-mass complement (costing O(1/τ) samples), then applies a chi-squared-based KL tester (extending DKW18) for the first term and a concentration-bound estimator for the second, yielding the near-optimal Õ(√k/ε + log²k/ε²) sample complexity. For Bayesian networks, the method runs local entropy identity tests on all O(n^{d+1}) subsets of size d+1 to certify that p and q approximately share the same DAG structure (via a Chow–Liu-style KL decomposition), then reduces global identity testing to n local KL tests on each variable's parent set, achieving Õ(2^{d/2} n^{3/2}/ε² + n²/ε⁴) samples without any structural assumptions beyond bounded in-degree.

## Results

The paper establishes a near-optimal sample complexity of $\tilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{k}/\varepsilon + \log^2 k/\varepsilon^2)$ for entropy identity testing (distinguishing $p=q$ from $|H(p)-H(q)|\geq\varepsilon$), which is sublinear in the domain size $k$ and improves upon the near-linear $\Theta(k/(\varepsilon\log k) + \log^2 k/\varepsilon^2)$ required for entropy estimation. As an application, identity testing for in-degree-$d$ Bayesian networks over $\{0,1\}^n$ is achieved with $\tilde{O}(2^{d/2}n^{3/2}/\varepsilon^2 + n^2/\varepsilon^4)$ samples, improving the prior best bound of $\tilde{O}(2^{d/2}n^2/\varepsilon^4)$ from [CDKS20] by a factor of $\tilde{O}(n/\varepsilon^2)$ while removing structural assumptions on the unknown network's DAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to entropy estimation (the natural baseline for understanding entropy from samples), the testing problem achieves a $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{k}/\varepsilon)$ dependence on domain size versus the near-linear $\Theta(k/(\varepsilon\log k))$ for estimation, a significant improvement in the large-domain regime. Against TV identity testing ($\Theta(\sqrt{k}/\varepsilon^2)$), entropy testing has a better $\varepsilon$-dependence ($1/\varepsilon$ vs $1/\varepsilon^2$) because the Paninski hard instances that are $\varepsilon$-far in TV are only $\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$-far in entropy. For Bayesian network testing, the paper's $\tilde{O}(2^{d/2}n^{3/2}/\varepsilon^2+n^2/\varepsilon^4)$ bound improves on [CDKS20]'s $\tilde{O}(2^{d/2}n^2/\varepsilon^4)$ by a factor of $\tilde{O}(n/\varepsilon^2)$ and, crucially, removes the requirement that the two networks share the same topological ordering or that the reference network be balanced. The result also beats the testing-by-learning baseline ($\tilde{O}(2^d n/\varepsilon^2)$ samples with $n^{O(dn)}$ runtime) whenever $d\gg\log(n/\varepsilon)$, and runs in polynomial time $\mathrm{poly}(n^d, 1/\varepsilon)$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces entropy identity testing as a distinct problem from both entropy estimation (which requires near-linear k/(ε log k) samples) and total variation identity testing (which requires √k/ε² samples), and shows it admits a near-optimal √k/ε + log²k/ε² sample complexity. The algorithmic toolkit draws on the chi-squared tester of Daskalakis et al. (SODA 2018) and the Paninski hard-instance construction (IEEE Trans. IT 2008) for lower bounds. For the Bayesian network application, the paper departs from prior work (CDKS20, DP16) by removing strong structural assumptions (shared topological ordering, balanced conditional probabilities, shared factorization structure) while simultaneously improving the sample complexity from Õ(2^{d/2}n²/ε⁴) to Õ(2^{d/2}n^{3/2}/ε² + n²/ε⁴), leveraging the Chow-Liu decomposition to connect local entropy closeness to global structural proximity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that testing whether two distributions differ in entropy is fundamentally easier than estimating entropy, a distinction that matters in high-dimensional settings where near-linear sample costs are impractical. The Bayesian network application removes structural assumptions that 'considerably limited applicability' of prior testing algorithms, making the result broadly applicable to any bounded-in-degree Bayes net. Open questions include the two-sample (closeness) variant of entropy testing, connections to mutual information testing, and whether the improved √k log log log(k/ε)/ε bound (sketched in Appendix D) can be made fully rigorous and extended to other structured families.
