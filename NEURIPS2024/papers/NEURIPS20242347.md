## Problem

Probabilistic circuits (PCs) with general DAG structure can represent exponentially many mixture components, yet most structure learning algorithms output tree-structured PCs or use trees as intermediates. It remained open whether there is a true exponential gap between DAG-structured and tree-structured PCs, or whether trees can always approximate DAGs with only a quasi-polynomial size overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a decomposable and smooth DAG-structured PC over n Boolean variables of polynomial size as input and produces an equivalent tree-structured PC computing the same network polynomial. For the upper bound, the output tree has depth O(log n) and size n^{O(log n)}. For the lower bound, the input is a specific network polynomial P of degree n = 2^{2k} that admits a DAG-structured PC of size O(n log n) and depth O(log n), but any tree-structured PC of depth o(log n) computing P must have super-polynomial size n^{ω(1)}.

## Method

The upper bound adapts the Raz–Yehudayoff balancing technique to PCs: it first converts the input DAG to a binary circuit, then iteratively constructs an intermediate DAG Ψ of depth O(log n) and poly(n) size by expressing each sub-network polynomial and partial derivative using the set G_m of product nodes whose children have degree at most m. A naive duplication strategy then converts Ψ into a tree of size n^{O(log n)}. The lower bound reduces to Fournier et al.'s result on monotone algebraic formulas: it constructs a polynomial P with an efficient DAG-structured PC, then shows that any minimum tree-structured PC of depth o(log n) computing P, after removing negation-indicator leaves, yields a monotone arithmetic formula computing the hard polynomial H^{(k,2)}, which is known to require super-polynomial size at that depth.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical bounds on the gap between DAG-structured and tree-structured probabilistic circuits. The upper bound (Theorem 3.1) shows that any network polynomial computable by a DAG-structured PC of size poly(n) over n variables has an equivalent tree-structured PC of size n^{O(log n)} and depth O(log n) — a quasi-polynomial guarantee. The lower bound (Theorem 4.1) shows that for n = 2^{2k}, there exists a network polynomial computable by a PC of size O(n log n) and depth O(log n), yet any tree-structured PC of depth o(log n) computing it must have size n^{ω(1)}, establishing a super-polynomial separation under a depth restriction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The central comparison is structural: between DAG-structured PCs and tree-structured PCs computing the same network polynomial. The upper bound adapts the depth-reduction technique of Raz and Yehudayoff [26] (which achieved O(log² n) depth for binary circuits) to the PC setting, improving the depth to O(log n) by removing the binary-node restriction. The lower bound reduces to the hardness result of Fournier et al. [11] on monotone algebraic formulas, showing that the polynomial H^{(k,2)} of degree 2^k over 2^{2k} variables requires super-polynomial tree size at depth o(k). The paper also resolves an open question from Martens and Medabalimi [19], who showed a super-polynomial minimum tree expression exists but left unclear whether a polynomial-sized DAG could compute the same polynomial — this paper confirms it can.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges probabilistic circuit (PC) structure learning with arithmetic circuit complexity theory. The upper bound adapts the depth-reduction technique of Raz and Yehudayoff [26] (which originally targeted syntactically multilinear circuits with binary fan-out) to the PC setting, removing the binary restriction to achieve O(log n) depth rather than O(log² n). The lower bound reduces to the recent separation result of Fournier et al. [11] on monotone algebraic formulas, connecting the PC tree-complexity question to a well-studied problem in circuit complexity. The work also directly addresses an open question raised by Martens and Medabalimi [19], who showed a super-polynomial minimum tree expression exists but left unclear whether a polynomial-sized DAG could compute the same polynomial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quasi-polynomial upper bound resolves a long-standing open question in the PC community: there is no exponential gap between DAG- and tree-structured PCs, which provides theoretical justification for structure learning algorithms that output trees. However, the lower bound under depth restriction (o(log n)) shows that the quasi-polynomial blowup is not merely an artifact of the proof technique—shallow trees genuinely cannot match DAGs. The main open question is whether the quasi-polynomial bound can be tightened to polynomial, and whether the depth-restricted lower bound can be extended to unrestricted depth. For practitioners, the result suggests that the large tree sizes produced by current structure learning algorithms may be improvable, but the quasi-polynomial guarantee is still too large for practical use.
