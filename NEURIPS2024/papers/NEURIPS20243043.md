## Problem

Existing user-level DP stochastic convex optimization algorithms are either impractical—requiring restrictive smoothness bounds, polynomial growth of users with dimension, or non-polynomial runtime—or prohibitively slow, needing (mn)^{3/2} to (mn)^3 gradient evaluations. The paper asks whether optimal excess risk can be achieved in significantly fewer gradient computations without these restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a dataset of n users, each holding m i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution, along with a convex L-Lipschitz loss f(x,z) (optionally β-smooth) and privacy parameters (ε,δ). The output is a parameter vector x ∈ X minimizing the population loss F(x)=E_{z~P}[f(x,z)] while satisfying (ε,δ)-user-level DP: the entire m-sample dataset of any single user can be replaced without significantly changing the output distribution. A key structural constraint is that the number of users n need only grow logarithmically in d/ε, rather than polynomially as in prior work.

## Method

The paper contributes two algorithms. Algorithm 1 (linear-time, nm gradients) is a phased SGD that partitions users into C≈1/ε groups, runs one-pass SGD per group, then privately detects and removes outlier *iterates* rather than gradients; a novel stability lemma (Lemma 2.3) shows two independent SGD runs on i.i.d. data produce iterates within O(ηL√T) with high probability, enabling noise calibrated to this stability radius. Algorithm 3 (optimal risk, ≈(mn)^{9/8} gradients) is an accelerated minibatch SGD with iterative localization that applies DP outlier removal to user gradients via AboveThreshold for concentration scoring combined with privacy amplification by subsampling and Gaussian noise for the inlier average; a key variance bound (Lemma 3.5) scales with 1/m by exploiting the DP stability of the iterates. For non-smooth losses, randomized convolution smoothing reduces the problem to the smooth case, yielding n^{11/8}m^{5/4} gradient complexity.

## Results

For β-smooth losses, Algorithm 3 achieves optimal excess risk O~(1/√(mn) + √(d log(1/δ))/(εn√m)) in ≈(mn)^{9/8} gradient computations (when n=d, ε=1, βR=L), improving over the previous state-of-the-art β·(mn)^{3/2} of [AL24]. For non-smooth losses, the smoothed Algorithm 3 achieves the same optimal excess risk in n^{11/8}m^{5/4} gradient evaluations (ε=1), a factor of n^{13/8}m^{7/4} faster than [AL24]'s (mn)^3. Additionally, Algorithm 1 provides a linear-time (nm gradient evaluations) user-level DP algorithm with state-of-the-art excess risk under the mild smoothness condition β ≤ (L/R)√(dmnε), without requiring n to grow polynomially in d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior state-of-the-art user-level DP SCO algorithms. Against [BS23] (Bassily & Sun, ICML 2023), which achieves mn^{3/2} gradient complexity for smooth losses, the paper's Algorithm 3 is faster by a factor of (mn)^{1/8}/β^{1/4} in the typical regime n^7 ≥ m, and critically removes the restrictive assumptions β ≤ √(n/m) and m ≤ √d ≤ n that limit [BS23] to small-scale settings. Against [AL24] (Asi & Liu, AISTATS 2024), which requires β·(mn)^{3/2} gradient evaluations for smooth losses and (mn)^3 for non-smooth losses, the paper's Algorithm 3 is faster by a factor of (mn)^{3/8}β^{3/4} (smooth) and n^{13/8}m^{7/4} (non-smooth, at ε=1). Both prior methods also require n to grow at least logarithmically in d/ε, while the paper only requires n^{1-o(1)} ≥ log(d)/ε. The paper's linear-time Algorithm 1 additionally outperforms [BS23, Algorithm 5] (the only prior non-trivial linear-time user-level DP algorithm) by relaxing the smoothness requirement from β < √(n³)/(md³) to β < √(nmd), making it applicable to large-scale ML problems where d ≫ n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the user-level DP SCO line initiated by [LSA+21] and advanced by [BS23] and [AL24], which introduced outlier-removal-based gradient privatization inspired by FriendlyCore. The key departure is twofold: Algorithm 1 applies outlier detection to SGD *iterates* rather than gradients (enabled by a novel stability lemma bounding the distance between two independent SGD runs), while Algorithm 3 combines AboveThreshold with privacy amplification by subsampling on random minibatches of user gradients within an accelerated ERM localization framework from [GL12, FKT20]. Both algorithms remove the restrictive smoothness and user-count assumptions of [BS23] and achieve polynomially faster runtimes than [AL24].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient complexity improvements are substantial: for non-smooth losses, the runtime drops from (mn)³ to n^{11/8}m^{5/4} (a factor of n^{13/8}m^{7/4}), and for smooth losses from β(mn)^{3/2} to ≈(mn)^{9/8}. The linear-time Algorithm 1 relaxes the smoothness requirement from β < √(n³)/(md³) (which rules out nearly all non-linear losses when n≈d) to β < √(nmd), making it applicable to realistic ML settings. The central open question the paper highlights is whether optimal excess risk can be achieved in truly linear time for user-level DP, which the authors are optimistic about given their (mn)^{9/8} bound. Limitations include the convexity assumption (excluding deep learning) and the purely theoretical nature with no empirical validation.
