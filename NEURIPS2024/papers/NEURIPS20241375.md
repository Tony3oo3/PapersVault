## Problem

Existing open-source federated learning (FL) simulation frameworks are too slow to run experiments on large, realistic FL datasets, forcing researchers to use small-scale or artificial benchmarks (e.g., 1–100 clients) that do not reflect production cross-device settings. This bottleneck limits the ability to validate algorithms on the diverse, heterogeneous, and privacy-constrained scenarios that real PFL deployments require.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes partitioned federated datasets (with per-user splits for cross-device or per-silo splits for cross-silo FL), a PyTorch or TensorFlow model (or non-neural models like GBDT/GMM), an FL algorithm specification, and optional central or local DP parameters (clipping bound, noise cohort size, privacy budget ε/δ). It produces a trained global model, per-iteration and final metrics (accuracy, perplexity, mAP), and privacy accounting traces. A notable design detail is that DP noise is scaled by a ratio r = C/C̃ so that small simulation cohort sizes faithfully emulate the noise level of much larger production cohorts, and the clipping bound is tracked per-iteration to keep DP accounting consistent with the actual FL hyperparameters in use.

## Method

pfl-research simulates only the computation of FL, omitting the network topology (no dedicated server process), which eliminates the central-server bottleneck that dominates wall-clock time in other frameworks. Each worker process holds a single model instance on GPU that is updated in-place across local training, evaluation, and central updates, avoiding repeated memory allocation. Inter-worker aggregation uses a single all-reduce step (via Horovod), and a greedy load-balancing scheduler assigns users to workers based on dataset-size weights to minimize stragglers. The API is modular: a FederatedAlgorithm defines per-user local optimization and central update logic, an Aggregator handles accumulation and cross-worker reduction, and composable Postprocessors (DP mechanisms, compression, sparsification) are applied to local and aggregated updates in a defined order. DP mechanisms (Gaussian, BMF, Laplace) and privacy accountants (Rényi DP, PLD, PRV) are implemented with GPU-accelerated kernels and are tightly coupled to FL hyperparameters to prevent parameter mismatches.

## Results

pfl-research achieves a 7–72× wall-clock speedup over existing open-source FL simulators on the CIFAR-10 IID benchmark (4.20 min with 5 processes on 1 GPU vs. 29.26–425.2 min for alternatives). On the larger FLAIR benchmark, it completes 5000 central iterations in 1.77 hours (2 processes, 4 GPUs), outperforming TensorFlow Federated (18 h) and Flower (28.8 h). Algorithm benchmarks confirm correctness: FedAvg reaches 70.37% accuracy on CIFAR-10-IID, 61.83% mAP on FLAIR, and 60.87 perplexity on StackOverflow, with only ~3–5% degradation under central DP (ε=2.0, δ=10⁻⁶).

## Baselines & Comparisons

pfl-research dominates all five compared frameworks on wall-clock time while matching or exceeding their accuracy. On CIFAR-10 IID, it is 21.6× faster than FedML (4.20 vs. 90.95 min), 27× faster than TensorFlow Federated (4.20 vs. 113.52 min), 16.4× faster than Flower (4.20 vs. 86.88 min), 101× faster than FedScale (4.20 vs. 425.2 min), and 16.1× faster than FLUTE (4.20 vs. 67.86 min). On the larger FLAIR benchmark, pfl-research (1.77 h) is 10.2× faster than TFF (18 h) and 16.3× faster than Flower (28.8 h), while achieving comparable mAP (61.1 vs. 62.0 for TFF; Flower's 31.5 mAP suggests a configuration issue). The speed advantage stems from simulating only computation (not topology), in-place GPU tensor updates, load-balanced user scheduling, and multi-process-per-GPU parallelism. Accuracy differences are within noise (±0.3–0.5%), confirming the speedup does not compromise result quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing landscape of FL simulation frameworks (TensorFlow Federated, Flower, FedML, FedScale, FLUTE) by making a deliberate design choice to simulate only the computation of FL rather than its network topology, eliminating the central-server bottleneck that slows down other simulators. Conceptually, it repurposes standard distributed training primitives (Horovod all-reduce, in-place GPU tensor updates, load-balanced scheduling) into a federated context, and tightly integrates state-of-the-art DP accountants (PLD, PRV, Rényi DP) and mechanisms (Gaussian, Banded Matrix Factorization) as composable postprocessors. The benchmark suite extends prior FL evaluation efforts by adding LLM fine-tuning datasets with natural user partitions (Aya, OpenAssistant) and a unified matrix of dataset × partitioning × privacy × framework configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7–72× speedup directly addresses a field-wide bottleneck: the paper's survey of NeurIPS 2023/ICLR 2024/ICML 2024 shows that 142 of 162 FL simulation papers use only 1–100 clients, and only 6 exceed 1000, largely because realistic datasets are too resource-intensive with existing simulators. The unified benchmark suite (CIFAR10, FLAIR, StackOverflow, LLM datasets with and without central DP) gives the community a common evaluation ground, and the finding that SCAFFOLD does not outperform FedAvg on any benchmark challenges the widespread adoption of that algorithm. Open questions include extending benchmarks to cross-silo FL, adding model parallelism for foundation-model-scale local training, and broadening the algorithm coverage beyond the current four baselines.
