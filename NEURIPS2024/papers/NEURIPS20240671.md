## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods extract discriminative information from a single input, implicitly constraining the representation dimension and leaving hard-to-distinguish OOD samples (whose features overlap with ID samples) undetected. The paper asks whether expanding the input space through common corruptions can reveal additional OOD discriminative signals that a single input cannot capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240671/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and generates multiple corrupted variants using the 90 common corruption types from Hendrycks & Dietterich (each with severity levels 1–5), producing an expanded set of input dimensions. The output is a scalar OOD score computed as the expectation of per-dimension confidence scores (e.g., max-softmax or energy) over the original and all corrupted inputs, which is then thresholded for ID/OOD classification. The framework is modality-agnostic, operating identically on single-modal DNNs (ResNet-50) and multi-modal VLMs (CLIP, AltCLIP, MetaCLIP, GroupViT) where per-dimension scores are cosine-similarity-based matching scores.

## Method

CoVer (Confidence aVerage) is a post-hoc scoring framework that exploits a phenomenon termed 'confidence mutation': under common corruptions, overconfident OOD samples experience significantly larger confidence drops than unconfident ID samples, because corruptions perturb non-semantic high-frequency features that OOD data relies on for spurious high confidence, while ID semantic features at low-frequency levels remain resistant. Formally, the CoVer score (Eq. 6) is the expectation of a per-dimension OOD score (max-softmax, energy, or method-specific variants) over the original input and N corrupted variants, i.e., S_CoVer = E_{x~d(X,X̃)} max_i exp(s_i(x)/τ) / Σ_j exp(s_j(x)/τ). The framework requires no model retraining or architectural modification; it simply replaces the single-input score in any existing OOD detection method (MSP, ODIN, ReAct, DICE, ASH, MCM, CLIPN, NegLabel, LoCoOp) with the multi-dimensional average. Corruption types and severity levels are selected per method using SVHN as a validation set, and the number of expanded dimensions is tuned to balance OOD salience against ID interference.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K zero-shot OOD detection benchmark with CLIP-B/16, CoVer achieves an average AUROC of 92.45% and FPR95 of 34.88%, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline MCM (90.76% AUROC, 42.73% FPR95) by +1.69 AUROC and −7.85 FPR95. In the traditional ResNet-50 setting, CoVer combined with ASH-S reaches 95.40% AUROC and 21.83% FPR95 on average, improving over ASH-S alone (95.14%, 22.74%). CoVer also consistently boosts compatibility partners: ReAct+CoVer gains +1.53 AUROC over ReAct, and NegLabel+CoVer achieves 95.39% AUROC vs. 94.10% for NegLabel alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVer outperforms all zero-shot baselines on the ImageNet-1K benchmark: it beats MCM by +1.69 AUROC and −7.85 FPR95 on average, and surpasses ZOC by a large margin (+10.66 AUROC). Against fine-tuned methods (Energy, KNN, ODIN), CoVer achieves comparable or better AUROC (92.45 vs. 91.54 for Energy) while requiring no training data. In the ResNet-50 setting, CoVer consistently improves its host method: ASH-B+CoVer gains +2.65 AUROC over ASH-B, and ReAct+CoVer gains +1.53 AUROC over ReAct. Among VLM-based compatibility partners, CoVer improves MCM (+1.34 AUROC), LoCoOp (+0.93 AUROC), CLIPN (+0.28 AUROC), and NegLabel (+1.29 AUROC), with the largest FPR95 reductions seen for CLIPN (−3.62) and NegLabel (−2.10). On hard OOD tasks (ImageNet-10 vs. ImageNet-20), CoVer achieves 98.68% AUROC and 4.10% FPR95 vs. MCM's 98.60% and 6.00%, confirming its advantage on semantically challenging distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-input paradigm in OOD detection (MSP, ODIN, ReAct, DICE, ASH, MCM) by proposing to expand the representation dimension through common corruptions and averaging confidence scores across multiple input views. It builds directly on MCM's max-softmax scoring for zero-shot CLIP-based detection and on Hendrycks & Dietterich's corruption benchmark as the mechanism for dimension expansion. Conceptually, the averaging-over-transformations idea echoes adversarial defense via random transforms (Barrage et al.), but is repurposed here as a scoring strategy rather than a training-time robustness technique. The key conceptual departure is the 'confidence mutation' framing: rather than designing a new architecture or loss, the paper identifies a differential vulnerability between ID and OOD data under corruptions and exploits it through a simple expectation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoVer provides a lightweight, plug-and-play scoring wrapper that consistently improves a wide range of existing OOD detection methods (ReAct, DICE, ASH, MCM, CLIPN, NegLabel) across both DNN and VLM backbones without architectural changes, making it practically attractive for practitioners. The confidence mutation phenomenon and its frequency-domain explanation (ID semantic features at low frequency resist corruptions while OOD non-semantic features at high frequency do not) offer a useful mechanistic lens for understanding why certain OOD samples are hard to detect. However, the lack of a principled criterion for selecting corruption types and severity levels (relying on a validation set) limits generalizability, and the increased forward-pass cost from multiple corrupted inputs is a practical concern for deployment. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned corruption selection could eliminate the validation-set dependency and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities.
