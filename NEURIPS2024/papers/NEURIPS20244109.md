## Problem

Unsupervised ASR systems must learn to map variable-length speech segments to phonemes without paired data, but prior methods rely on handcrafted rules (e.g., k-means clustering) or separately trained modules to determine segment boundaries. These sub-optimal boundaries bottleneck UASR performance because they are not tailored to the downstream phoneme prediction model. The paper asks whether segmental boundaries can be learned end-to-end, jointly with the phoneme predictor, to improve unsupervised ASR.

## Input / Output

The system takes a raw speech waveform and unpaired text corpora (phonemicized via a language-specific lexicon) as input. A self-supervised feature extractor (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM, or XLSR-53) converts the waveform into frame-level features; the segmentation model then emits a per-frame binary boundary decision, and features within each segment are mean-pooled into a single embedding. The phoneme prediction model outputs a non-autoregressive phoneme sequence (consecutive duplicates removed), which can be further decoded into word-level transcriptions via WFST with a lexicon and external LM.

## Method

REBORN alternates two stages iteratively: (1) training a lightweight 1D-CNN segmentation model via REINFORCE policy gradient, where the reward is a weighted sum of perplexity difference (scored by a 4-gram phoneme LM), normalized negative Levenshtein edit distance, and length difference between the current and previous iteration's phoneme predictions; and (2) training the phoneme prediction model (generator) with GAN adversarial loss against a discriminator, using the boundaries fixed from Stage 1. Before RL, the segmentation model is initialized via behavior cloning on merged boundaries from the previous iteration (or k-means boundaries in the first iteration). A boundary-merging step collapses consecutive segments that yield the same phoneme prediction, stabilizing Stage 2 training. The key finding is that REBORN learns sub-phonemic segmental units (higher frequency, lower boundary F1 than oracle) that, despite not matching ground-truth phoneme boundaries, produce significantly better phoneme predictions because they are explicitly optimized for the non-ideal unsupervised phoneme predictor.

## Results

On LibriSpeech (100h), REBORN achieves 8.9% PER / 13.1% WER on test-clean, outperforming the prior SoTA (wav2vec-U 2.0 + HMM self-training) by 1.4% PER and 2.9% WER without any self-training. With HMM self-training, REBORN reaches 5.4% PER / 9.6% WER on test-clean, a new SoTA that surpasses the prior best by 5% PER and 6% WER. On TIMIT, REBORN achieves 12.4% PER (core-dev) with greedy decoding only, beating the best prior method (EURO with WavLM, 14.3%). On MLS, REBORN achieves 32.0% average WER across six languages, 3.9% lower than wav2vec-U 2.0 (35.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBORN outperforms all four baselines across all three datasets. On LibriSpeech test-clean, REBORN (8.9% PER) beats wav2vec-U 2.0 (12.6%) by 3.7 points, EURO with HuBERT (15.1%) by 6.2 points, and wav2vec-U (19.3%) by 10.4 points. On TIMIT, REBORN (12.4% PER, greedy decoding) surpasses EURO with WavLM (14.3%, which uses prefix decoding) and wav2vec-U (20.2%). On MLS, REBORN (32.0% avg WER) beats wav2vec-U 2.0 (35.9%) by 3.9 points on average, winning in 5 of 6 languages (losing only on Portuguese: 51.5% vs 48.5%). Notably, REBORN without self-training already outperforms the prior SoTA (wav2vec-U 2.0 + HMM ST), and with HMM self-training it exceeds even the oracle-boundary baseline (6.4% PER), demonstrating that learned segmentation can surpass ground-truth boundaries when tailored to the phoneme predictor.

## Relation to Prior Work

REBORN builds directly on the wav2vec-U family of unsupervised ASR systems (wav2vec-U, wav2vec-U 2.0, EURO), which all rely on hand-crafted k-means-based segmentation or separately trained modules to determine segmental structure boundaries before feeding features to a GAN-trained phoneme predictor. The key departure is replacing this fixed or separately-optimized segmentation with a trainable 1D-CNN segmentation model optimized via REINFORCE policy gradients, where the reward is the improvement in phoneme-sequence perplexity relative to the previous iteration. The iterative two-stage training loop (RL for segmentation, GAN for phoneme prediction) and the boundary-merging post-processing are novel components not present in prior UASR pipelines, though each individual technique (RL, GAN, behavior cloning) is well-established elsewhere.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of segmental boundaries is a primary bottleneck in UASR and that learning them jointly with the phoneme predictor yields substantial gains across English and five non-English languages, making UASR more practical for the 80%+ of world languages lacking paired speech-text data. A notable open question is whether the approach extends to word-level token prediction without a lexicon, which the authors flag as an unresolved problem. Limitations include the dependence on a good wav2vec-U initialization, the need for a phoneme lexicon, and the potential for iterative training to amplify dataset biases; the method has not been tested in noisy or truly low-resource settings.
