## Problem

Existing methods for irregularly sampled medical time series (ISMTS) treat all variables with shared parameter spaces and learn inter-variable graphs solely from misaligned observations, failing to capture the distinct temporal patterns of individual variables and the medically meaningful correlations among them. The paper asks how domain knowledge—specifically textual descriptions of each variable's medical properties—can be leveraged to simultaneously model variable-specific temporal dynamics and time-varying inter-variable dependencies.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an irregularly sampled multivariate time series (observation values, timestamps, and a binary mask indicating which variable-timestamp pairs are observed), textual medical descriptions for each variable (sourced from variable names, Wikipedia, or ChatGPT), and static patient features. A pre-trained language model (BERT by default) converts each variable's text into a fixed-dimensional semantic embedding that is frozen and used to parameterize the network. The output is a binary classification probability (e.g., in-hospital mortality or sepsis onset) produced by concatenating the summed hidden states of all variables at their last observed timestamps with the static feature vector.

## Method

KEDGN extracts per-variable semantic embeddings via BERT's [CLS] token, then uses a low-rank decomposition (projected query vectors multiplied by a shared weight pool) to allocate a unique parameter matrix to each variable within a GCRNN backbone, so that each variable's GRU gates operate with its own weights tailored to its temporal pattern. A complete variable correlation graph is constructed by computing softmax-normalized cosine similarities over a second projection of the semantic embeddings, providing a knowledge-grounded static adjacency matrix. A density-aware mechanism then dynamically modulates edge weights at each timestamp by scaling with the absolute difference in local sampling densities of the two connected variables, using a learnable weight matrix and a tanh activation to preserve the graph's normalized structure. The resulting time-varying subgraph (obtained by masking the static graph with the observation mask and applying density adjustment) drives first-order Chebyshev graph convolutions inside the GRU update, while unobserved variables simply retain their previous hidden state, avoiding interpolation entirely.

## Results

KEDGN (using BERT with ChatGPT-generated text descriptions) achieves the best performance on all four irregularly sampled medical time series datasets: 92.2% AUROC / 62.0% AUPRC on P19, 87.9% / 57.1% on Physionet, 85.1% / 48.3% on MIMIC-III, and 87.8% / 54.5% on P12. On average, KEDGN outperforms the strongest baseline by 0.9% on AUROC and 1.6% on AUPRC. The method also demonstrates robustness under leave-variables-out settings, achieving the highest performance on 35 out of 40 metrics as the missing variable ratio increases from 10% to 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEDGN outperforms all 12 baselines on all four datasets for both AUROC and AUPRC. The closest competitors are Warpformer (strongest on MIMIC-III at 84.6% AUROC) and StraTS (strongest on P12 at 86.7% AUROC), with KEDGN exceeding them by 0.4–1.1% AUROC and 1.0–2.4% AUPRC. On P19, KEDGN-Name leads by 0.5% AUROC and 1.9% AUPRC over Warpformer; on Physionet, KEDGN-Wiki surpasses mTAND (86.9% AUROC) and IP-Net (86.8% AUROC) by 1.3–1.4% AUROC. Notably, graph-based baselines like Raindrop and MTGNN, which learn variable correlations purely from data without prior knowledge, underperform KEDGN by 2–7% AUPRC, validating the benefit of text-knowledge-empowered graph construction. Attention-based methods (SeFT, mTAND) and ODE-based methods (ODE-RNN) show the largest gaps, particularly on AUPRC, suggesting that shared parameter spaces and lack of variable-level differentiation are key limitations of prior approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

KEDGN builds on the GCRNN backbone (Li et al., ICLR 2018) and the parameter decomposition technique from adaptive graph convolutional networks (Bai et al., NeurIPS 2020), while adopting the structured input encoding from Warpformer (Zhang et al., KDD 2023). It departs from prior ISMTS methods (GRU-D, ODE-RNN, Raindrop, mTAND) in two key ways: (1) allocating variable-specific parameter spaces rather than sharing weights across all variables, and (2) grounding the variable correlation graph in textual medical knowledge via a PLM rather than learning it purely from misaligned observations. The density-aware dynamic graph adjustment mechanism is a novel contribution that bridges the static knowledge-empowered graph with time-varying observation density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-modal observation that textual medical descriptions and temporal sampling patterns are both external manifestations of a variable's inherent identity provides a principled justification for using PLM embeddings to guide time series architecture, offering interpretability that purely data-driven graph learning lacks. However, the method is inherently tied to domains where high-quality textual variable descriptions exist (primarily medicine), limiting generalizability to irregular time series in other fields. The recurrent backbone introduces sequential computation as a runtime bottleneck, and the paper leaves open whether the approach extends to interpolation, extrapolation, or regression tasks. The modest average improvement (~0.9% AUROC, ~1.6% AUPRC) over strong baselines suggests the gains, while consistent, are incremental rather than transformative.
