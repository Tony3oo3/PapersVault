## Problem

One-shot federated learning (OFL) reduces communication to a single model transmission but suffers a large performance gap compared to multi-round FL, because locally isolated training causes models to fit spurious correlations rather than invariant features under non-IID data. No prior work has identified this causal mechanism or provided a solution that closes the gap without requiring extra data, large storage, or additional communication rounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, MNIST, FMNIST, SVHN; 64×64 for CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a single class label. Unusually, the input is consumed through a progressive block-wise pipeline where intermediate feature maps from all M clients are concatenated at each block boundary before being passed to the next block, and the final output is a single fused global model of the same size S as the original architecture rather than an ensemble of M models.

## Method

FuseFL decomposes a CNN into K sequential blocks and trains them progressively bottom-up: for each block k, all clients train their local block in parallel, share it, and the M local blocks are concatenated to form fused features H_fus^k. A lightweight adaptor (1×1 convolution or simple averaging) is stitched before the next block to resolve dimension and semantic mismatches, then the fused block is frozen while subsequent blocks are retrained on local data. The causal insight driving this design is that other clients' trained blocks act as invariant feature augmentors: since client i's model was trained without client m's spurious features R_m^spu, passing x_m through H_i filters out spurious correlations while retaining invariant features R_m^inv. To keep total model size at S, each local model uses hidden dimensions reduced by a factor of √M, so the concatenated fused model recovers the original width. Total communication cost remains S (one full model size) across all K rounds, since each round transmits only S/K of the model.

## Results

On the default non-IID setting (α=0.5), FuseFL (K=4) achieves 98.29% on MNIST, 90.50% on Fashion-MNIST, 81.15% on CIFAR-10, 89.34% on SVHN, 49.30% on CIFAR-100, and 34.28% on Tiny-ImageNet. FuseFL outperforms the impractical Ensemble FL upper bound on MNIST (+1.07%), Fashion-MNIST (+1.08%), CIFAR-10 (+1.24%), and SVHN (+3.64%), but falls short on CIFAR-100 (−4.09%) and Tiny-ImageNet (−11.52%) due to larger inter-class data divergence. Across all six datasets, FuseFL surpasses the strongest OFL baseline DENSE by 10–20+ percentage points on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuseFL (K=4) decisively outperforms all five OFL baselines across nearly all dataset and non-IID settings. Against FedAvg [96], FuseFL gains 7.74 pp on CIFAR-10 (81.15% vs. 43.67%) and 33.23 pp on CIFAR-100 (49.30% vs. 12.11%) at α=0.5. Against FedDF [88], the gap is 27.59 pp on CIFAR-10 and 12.95 pp on CIFAR-100. Against the data-free distillation baselines Fed-DAFL [18] and Fed-ADI [153], FuseFL leads by 22.56 pp and 21.81 pp respectively on CIFAR-10. Against the strongest OFL method DENSE [159], FuseFL still wins by 18.96 pp on CIFAR-10 and 7.23 pp on CIFAR-100. The only cases where FuseFL trails are CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet versus the impractical Ensemble FL upper bound, which the authors attribute to larger inter-class data divergence reducing the benefit of cross-client invariant feature sharing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of one-shot federated learning (OFL) and causal representation learning. It builds on the OFL line of work (Guha et al. 2019, Li et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2022) that reduces communication to a single model transmission, and on the causal/information-bottleneck analysis of OOD generalization (Ahuja et al. 2021, Schölkopf et al. 2021) to diagnose why isolated local training in OFL underperforms. The key departure from prior OFL methods (averaging, ensemble, distillation) is the progressive block-wise fusion strategy: rather than communicating the full model once or storing all client models, FuseFL communicates one block per round and fuses intermediate features, drawing on the 'isolation problem' identified through a structural equation model of FL data generation. It also distinguishes itself from FedMA (Wang et al. 2020) by using block-level rather than layer-level communication, supporting arbitrary DNN blocks rather than only linear/conv layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FuseFL offers a practical path to deploying FL in extremely low-bandwidth settings (1–10 MB/s) where even one full-model transmission is costly, by matching one-shot communication cost while exceeding the ensemble-FL upper bound on most benchmarks. The causal framing of the OFL performance gap (spurious fitting due to isolation) is the first of its kind and provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future communication-efficient FL designs. However, the method's advantage diminishes on datasets with high inter-class divergence (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), and the extension to transformers/LLMs remains only envisioned rather than demonstrated. Open questions include whether the √M scaling and block-fusion strategy generalize to non-CNN architectures, how it interacts with differential privacy, and whether the progressive freezing schedule is optimal for deeper or wider networks.
