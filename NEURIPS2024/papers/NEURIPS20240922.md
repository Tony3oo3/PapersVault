## Problem

Existing constrained sampling methods (mirror maps, projections, barriers, penalties) enforce deterministic support constraints on individual samples but cannot handle statistical constraints on the distribution itself, such as moment requirements, fairness criteria, or counterfactual conditions. Prior work on distribution-level constraints required explicit integration against intractable tilted distributions, making it impractical. This paper asks whether a fully stochastic, single-particle algorithm can simultaneously sample from and constrain a target distribution without any explicit integration.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized log-density f(x) (the normalization constant Z is never needed), vector-valued constraint functions g: R^d → R^I (inequality) and h: R^d → R^J (equality), and an initial point x_0. It produces a sequence of samples whose distribution converges to the KL-projection μ* of π onto the constraint set, along with dual variables (λ*, ν*) that identify which constraints are active and provide sensitivity information for counterfactual analysis. The algorithm is single-particle: each iteration uses one sample to update both the state and the dual variables, with no mini-batching required.

## Method

PD-LMC (Algorithm 1) is derived by discretizing gradient descent-ascent dynamics for the Lagrangian saddle-point problem in Wasserstein-2 space. The primal update is a standard Euler-Maruyama LMC step on the tilted potential U(x,λ,ν) = f(x) + λᵀg(x) + νᵀh(x), while the dual updates replace the intractable expectations E_μ[g] and E_μ[h] with single-sample evaluations g(x_k) and h(x_k), making the algorithm fully stochastic. The key theoretical contribution is proving sublinear convergence of the ergodic average in KL divergence (convex case) or Wasserstein-2 distance (strongly convex case) by constructing a Lyapunov potential in Wasserstein space and showing it is a supermartingale along the coupled (x_k, λ_k, ν_k) trajectory. For targets satisfying a log-Sobolev inequality rather than convexity, a variant (Algorithm 2) introduces time-scale separation—executing N_k^0 LMC steps before each dual update—to obtain analogous KL convergence guarantees.

## Results

On the 1D truncated Gaussian (N(0,1) restricted to [1,3]), PD-LMC estimates the mean at 1.508 (true: 1.510), outperforming Projected LMC (1.488) and Mirror LMC (1.470). On the 2D truncated Gaussian (N([2,2], I) restricted to the unit ℓ₂-ball), PD-LMC places only 1.8% of samples outside the support versus ~25% on the boundary for Projected LMC (where only 0.14% should be). On the Adult dataset fairness task, PD-LMC reduces the gender disparity in positive prediction rates from 26.2% (male) vs 5% (female) under the unconstrained posterior to 18.1% vs 15.1% with δ=0.01, at a minor accuracy cost (84% → 82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PD-LMC against Projected LMC [13] and Mirror LMC [15] on two truncated Gaussian sampling problems. On the 1D problem, PD-LMC achieves the most accurate mean estimate (1.508 vs true 1.510), while both baselines underestimate the mean by 2–4%, with Mirror LMC performing worst (1.470). On the 2D problem, Projected LMC is the weakest baseline, placing nearly 25% of samples on the boundary (vs. the correct 0.14%), whereas PD-LMC places only 1.8% outside the support. Mirror LMC provides a better 2D mean estimate than Projected LMC but is more asymmetric than PD-LMC (Mirror: (0.312, 0.418) vs PD-LMC: (0.446, 0.444) vs true: (0.368, 0.368)). A key qualitative advantage of PD-LMC is that it produces dual variables (Lagrange multipliers) enabling counterfactual analysis—identifying which constraints are binding or vacuous—without re-running the algorithm, a capability neither baseline offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Langevin Monte Carlo (Roberts & Tweedie, 1996) by coupling it with a primal-dual saddle-point update in Wasserstein space, drawing on classical GDA convergence analysis from Euclidean optimization (Nedić & Ozdaglar, 2009). It directly builds on Liu et al. (2021), who proposed a dual-ascent constrained sampling scheme but required exact integration against intractable measures and only analyzed continuous-time dynamics. The key departure is formulating the problem as a KL minimization over probability measures with statistical (moment/fairness) constraints rather than support constraints, which renders projection, mirror-map, and barrier methods inapplicable and motivates the distribution-level primal-dual approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a unified, interpretable tool for constrained Bayesian inference: the dual variables (Lagrange multipliers) reveal which constraints are active, which are vacuous, and how sensitive the solution is to tolerance changes—enabling counterfactual analysis without re-solving. Limitations include sublinear ergodic-average convergence rates (no last-iterate guarantees), the LSI variant requiring time-scale separation and excluding equality constraints, and the fact that PD-LMC can produce samples outside the support since it constrains the distribution rather than individual draws. Open questions include whether proximal or extra-gradient methods in Wasserstein space can yield faster or almost-sure convergence, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or non-convex constraint functions.
