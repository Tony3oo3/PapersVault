## Problem

Existing time series generative models are designed for either short sequences (RNNs, GANs) or long sequences (SSMs, latent ODEs), but no unified framework handles both regimes. This dichotomy stems from vanishing gradients in recurrent networks, quadratic computational costs of transformers, and limited expressiveness of linear state space models on short data. The paper asks whether a single architecture can seamlessly generate time series spanning lengths from 24 to 17,500 steps.

## Input / Output

The method accepts multivariate time series of arbitrary length L and feature count K (ranging from 1 to 59 channels across experiments), transforming them into images of size 8×8 (short, L=24) up to 144×144 (ultra-long, L≈17.5k) via delay embedding or STFT. For conditional tasks (interpolation/extrapolation), a binary mask specifies which positions are observed versus missing, and the model generates only the masked regions while preserving observed values. The output is a time series in the original domain, recovered by applying the parameter-free inverse transform to the generated image.

## Method

The core idea is to reframe time series generation as image generation: an invertible transform (delay embedding or STFT) maps the sequence to a 2D image, a vision diffusion model (EDM with a U-Net backbone) learns to denoise the image, and the inverse transform recovers the time series. The delay embedding arranges the sequence into columns with a skip parameter m and row dimension n, so that a 256×256 image encodes up to 65k timesteps, elegantly bypassing long-range dependency issues. The STFT variant retains both real and imaginary channels (doubling the channel count) and avoids computing the magnitude spectrogram, preserving full spectral information for lossless inversion. The diffusion model is the only learnable component; both transforms are deterministic and parameter-free. For conditional generation, the framework applies a mask to the image domain and denoises only the masked regions, analogous to image inpainting.

## Results

On the short-term unconditional generation benchmark (length 24), ImagenTime achieves discriminative scores of .037 (Stocks), .040 (Energy), and .007 (MuJoCo), outperforming the second-best method DiffTime by 26%, 60%, and 88% respectively. On the long-term benchmark (~750 steps), it attains classification scores of .862 (FRED-MD) and 1.02 (NN5 Daily), surpassing LS4 by 58% and 61%. On the novel ultra-long benchmark (10k–17.5k steps), it achieves a prediction score of .001 on KDD-Cup versus LS4's .049, and .138 on Traffic versus LS4's .170. In conditional generation, it reduces interpolation MSE by ~4× on short sequences and by 1–2 orders of magnitude on long sequences (e.g., .004 vs .620 on Physionet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImagenTime outperforms all 11 short-term baselines on the Energy and MuJoCo discriminative scores, with the largest margin over DiffTime (the second-best method) at 60% (Energy) and 88% (MuJoCo); the sole exception is KoVAE, which achieves a slightly lower Stocks disc score (.009 vs .037). On the long-term benchmark, ImagenTime beats LS4 on FRED-MD across all three metrics (marg .021 vs .022, class .862 vs .544, pred .009 vs .037) and on NN5 Daily for marg and class, though LS4 retains an edge on NN5 Daily pred (.241 vs .393) and Temp Rain marg (.083 vs .409). On the ultra-long benchmark, ImagenTime dominates LS4 on KDD-Cup pred (.001 vs .049, a 98% improvement) and class (.842 vs .488), and on Traffic pred (.138 vs .170) and class (.684 vs .630). In conditional generation, ImagenTime surpasses LS4 by 1–2 orders of magnitude on long-sequence interpolation (Physionet .004 vs .620, USHCN .006 vs .050) and outperforms CRU by ~4× on short-sequence interpolation (ETTh1 .069 vs .283). GAN-based baselines (GT-GAN, TimeGAN, RCGAN, WaveGAN) and RNN-based baselines (T-Forcing, P-Forcing, C-RNN-GAN) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all settings, confirming the advantage of the diffusion-based image-transform approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes invertible time-series-to-image transforms (delay embedding, STFT) that were previously developed for classification and forecasting tasks, and couples them with the EDM vision diffusion backbone (Karras et al., 2022) to create a unified generative framework. It departs from purpose-built time series generators—RNN-based (LS4, WaveNet), GAN-based (TimeGAN, GT-GAN), and sequence-native diffusion models (DiffTime, CSDI)—by sidestepping the gradient, scaling, and expressivity limitations of each through a simple domain change. The approach also extends the time-series-to-image literature (GAF, Recurrence Plots, Line Graphs) from discriminative tasks into the generative diffusion setting, a combination the authors note was largely unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first unified generative model that handles short (24), long (~750), and ultra-long (10k–17k) sequences without architectural changes, and introduces a new ultra-long benchmark that was previously absent from the literature. Its simplicity—transform to image, run off-the-shelf diffusion, transform back—makes it easy to adopt and extend to other tasks (classification, anomaly detection, few-shot learning) as the authors suggest. Key limitations include higher parameter counts (~2× LS4) and the iterative denoising cost at inference, though the authors note that emerging fast-sampling techniques (consistency models, distillation) could mitigate this. An open question is whether the image-domain representation introduces information bottlenecks for highly structured multivariate signals, and whether the approach generalizes to irregularly-sampled or event-driven time series.
