## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) encode images into a fixed number of visual tokens (e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5) regardless of task complexity or available compute, offering no mechanism to adaptively reduce the token budget at deployment time. This paper asks whether a single model can be trained once to support flexible inference with any number of visual tokens up to a predefined maximum, trading accuracy for speed as needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241587/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 336×336 RGB image and a text question as input. The image is processed by CLIP ViT-L/14 into 576 grid features, which are then compressed by the query transformer into m visual tokens, where m is chosen at inference time from any integer in [2, 256]. The LLM (Vicuna-1.5-7B) generates a free-form text answer conditioned on both the m visual tokens and the text question. Notably, m need not be a value seen during training (e.g., 77 tokens works despite never being explicitly trained).

## Method

MQT-LLAVA replaces LLaVA-1.5's MLP projector with a single-layer cross-attention query transformer that has M=256 learnable latent query tokens. The key training innovation is a tail-token dropping strategy: at each training step, a value m is randomly sampled from {2, 4, 6, …, 256}, and only the first m query tokens are fed through the transformer and used for the language modeling loss, discarding the remaining M−m tokens. This induces a Matryoshka (nested) structure in which earlier tokens progressively encode more critical visual information. At inference, the user simply selects how many of the leading tokens to use, enabling 2× to 8× TFLOPs reductions with minimal accuracy loss. The model is trained in two stages (alignment on LLaVA-558K, then fine-tuning on LLaVA-665K) with MQT applied only in the second stage, and uses a linear (not log-based) schedule for m selection, which ablations show yields ~2% better average accuracy.

## Results

MQT-LLAVA with 256 visual tokens achieves a normalized average of 59.4 across 11 benchmarks, matching LLaVA-1.5 (59.2) which uses 576 tokens, and outperforming it on 6 of 11 benchmarks (e.g., VizWiz 53.1 vs. 50.0, MME-Cognition 353.6 vs. 316.1, LLaVA-Bench 64.6 vs. 63.4). Reducing to 16 tokens yields an 8× reduction in TFLOPs with only a 2.4-point drop on MMBench (61.9 vs. 64.3). At the extreme of 2 tokens, performance drops by just 3% on ScienceQA (65.0 vs. 67.6) and 6% on MMMU (32.5 vs. 34.8), and MQT-LLAVA with 2 tokens still outperforms InstructBLIP (32 tokens) on all 8 shared benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5 (576 tokens), MQT-LLAVA (256 tokens) wins on VizWiz (+3.1), MME-Cognition (+37.5), MMMU (+0.1), MMBench (tie at 64.3), LLaVA-Bench (+1.2), and ScienceQA (+0.8), while losing on VQA-v2 (−1.7), GQA (−0.4), POPE (−1.5), MME-Perception (−76.2), and MM-Vet (−0.7). Against its own baseline QT-LLaVA (fixed 256 tokens), MQT-LLAVA improves on 9 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest gains on VizWiz (+2.0) and MME-Cognition (+5.4). At the extreme low-token regime, MQT-LLAVA with only 2 tokens outperforms InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7B, 32 tokens) on all 8 shared benchmarks, demonstrating that the Matryoshka training strategy enables far more aggressive compression than prior Q-Former-based approaches. Compared to BLIP-2 (32 tokens), MQT-LLAVA at 256 tokens leads by a wide margin on every benchmark (e.g., VizWiz 53.1 vs. 19.6, MMBench 64.3 vs. –). Against Qwen-VL (256 tokens, 448 resolution), MQT-LLAVA is competitive on MMBench (64.3 vs. 38.2) and ScienceQA (67.6 vs. 67.1), though Qwen-VL retains an edge on VQA-v2 and GQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL), which trains nested models with growing hidden dimensions, to the visual token space of LVLMs: instead of nesting in parameter width, MQT nests in the number of query tokens, training with a randomly selected prefix of tokens per step. It builds directly on LLaVA-1.5's architecture, replacing the MLP projection with a single-layer cross-attention query transformer (similar in spirit to BLIP-2's Q-Former and Flamingo's Perceiver Resampler), but introduces the key departure of elastic token count at inference. A critical efficiency improvement over original MRL is that only one token configuration is trained per step rather than all M configurations, reducing training cost by roughly half on average.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: an 8× TFLOPs reduction (16 tokens) with only a 2.4-point MMBench drop, and matching LLaVA-1.5's 576-token performance with just 256 tokens, makes this a compelling efficiency technique for deployment under varying compute budgets. The finding that different tasks have sharply different token sensitivity (e.g., ScienceQA and MMMU remain robust to extreme token reduction, while VQA/GQA degrade more steeply) provides a useful framework for task-aware deployment. A key open question is whether the approach scales beyond 256 tokens or generalizes to video and multi-image settings, and whether adaptive per-image token selection (rather than a global budget) could further improve the accuracy-efficiency frontier.
