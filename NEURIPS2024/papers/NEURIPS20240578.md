## Problem

Previous clean sample selection methods for noisy-label robustness (Co-teaching, CoDis, JoCor) assume training from scratch and do not exploit the robust pre-trained features of vision foundation models (VFMs). While linear probing of VFMs is naturally resistant to label noise because it freezes the feature extractor, it suffers from a domain gap between natural-image pretraining and medical imaging. The paper asks how to combine the noise robustness of linear probing with the domain-adaptive expressiveness of fine-tuning adapters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB medical images (dermatoscopic, fundus, peripheral blood cell, and CT coronal-slice images) and outputs a single class label from a k-class set (e.g., 7 skin lesion types, 5 DR severity grades, 8 cell types, 11 organs). The VFM backbone (DINOv2 ViT-small) is fully frozen; only a linear classification head or lightweight adapter parameters (~1.1M trainable parameters) are updated, making the approach parameter-efficient. Training labels are assumed to be noisy, with symmetric noise rates of 10–60% in simulated benchmarks and an estimated 30–40% error in the real-world Kaggle-EyePACS dataset.

## Method

CUFIT trains three modules simultaneously on each batch in a curriculum fashion: a Linear Probing Module (LPM) trained on all samples, an Intermediate Adapter Module (IAM) trained only on samples whose LPM prediction agrees with the annotation, and a Last Adapter Module (LAM) trained only on samples whose IAM prediction agrees with the annotation. The agreement criterion (prediction matches annotation) leverages the fact that a robust classifier will correctly predict the majority of clean samples within each noisy class, requiring no noise-rate hyperparameter. The curriculum LPM→IAM→LAM progressively expands the pool of selected clean samples: LPM is highly precise but low-recall due to the domain gap, while IAM, having adapted the feature extractor on a subset of clean samples, achieves comparable precision with higher recall, thereby supplying more clean samples to LAM. At inference, only LAM is used for prediction. The framework is adapter-agnostic (validated with Rein, VPT, AdaptFormer, LoRA) and VFM-agnostic (validated with DINOv1, DINOv2, MAE, BioMedCLIP), with DINOv2 + Rein as the default configuration.

## Results

On four simulated noisy medical image benchmarks, CUFIT achieves mean test accuracies of 78.3% (HAM10000), 81.6% (APTOS-2019), 98.6% (BloodMnist), and 91.6% (OrgancMnist), consistently outperforming all baselines across noise rates of 10%–60%. At the 40% noise rate, CUFIT surpasses the best prior method (CoDis) by 5.0% on HAM10000 (79.1 vs. 74.1), 2.1% on APTOS-2019 (81.6 vs. 79.5), 4.8% on BloodMnist (98.3 vs. 93.5), and 5.8% on OrgancMnist (91.6 vs. 85.8). On the real-world Kaggle-EyePACS benchmark, CUFIT achieves 64.4% combined accuracy on APTOS-2019 + FGADR, and on natural image benchmarks it reaches 83.9% on CIFAR-10 and 73.8% on CIFAR-100 at 80% symmetric noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUFIT outperforms all four sample-selection baselines (Co-teaching, JoCor, CoDis) and the adapter-only baseline (Rein) on every simulated noisy medical dataset across all noise rates. The margin over the strongest prior method (CoDis) grows with noise: from 0.7% at 10% noise on HAM10000 to 4.0% at 60% noise, and from 2.1% to 5.8% on OrgancMnist. On the real-world Kaggle-EyePACS benchmark, CUFIT wins on FGADR (53.7% vs. 53.1% JoCor) and the combined test set (64.4% vs. 63.9%), but Co-teaching edges out CUFIT on APTOS-2019 alone (70.9% vs. 69.8%), which the authors attribute to the high proportion of normal-class samples in that test set. On natural image benchmarks at extreme 80% noise, CUFIT leads Co-teaching by 5.7% on CIFAR-10 (83.9 vs. 78.2) and 7.1% on CIFAR-100 (73.8 vs. 66.7), and marginally beats it on ANIMAL10N (92.3 vs. 92.2). The Rein adapter baseline degrades severely under high noise (e.g., 37.8% on HAM10000 at 60% noise vs. CUFIT's 70.1%), confirming that trainable adapters without sample selection are vulnerable to label memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: noisy-label sample selection (Co-teaching, CoDis, JoCor) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of vision foundation models (Rein, VPT, AdaptFormer, LoRA). The key departure from prior sample-selection methods is the recognition that they assume training from scratch, whereas CUFIT exploits the fact that frozen VFM features (as in linear probing) are inherently resistant to label memorization. The curriculum design (LPM → IAM → LAM) repurposes the dual-network agreement principle from Co-teaching into a sequential module pipeline where each stage's robustness is inherited from the previous stage's sample filtering, rather than relying on two homogeneous networks trained in parallel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is high for medical imaging, where annotation noise of 30–40% is common and re-annotation is expensive; CUFIT's agreement-criterion approach avoids the noise-rate hyperparameter that plagues Co-teaching and CoDis. However, the paper does not report statistical significance (error bars, multiple seeds), and the curriculum's three-module design adds training complexity that may not be justified in low-noise regimes where linear probing alone suffices. Open questions include whether the LPM→IAM→LAM pipeline generalizes to tasks beyond classification (segmentation, detection) and whether the specific choice of Rein as the adapter is critical or interchangeable with any PEFT method.
