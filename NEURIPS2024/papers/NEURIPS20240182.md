## Problem

Laplace learning for graph-based semi-supervised learning degenerates at low label rates, producing nearly constant predictions that collapse to a single class after thresholding. Existing remedies—mean-shift heuristics, volume-constrained MBO schemes—only partially address this and lack guarantees of discrete, cardinality-respecting outputs. The paper asks whether one can formulate SSL as a nonconvex problem whose minimizers are provably integer, eliminating the need for post-hoc thresholding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a weighted graph (k-NN graph with Gaussian edge weights built from autoencoder latent features for image datasets, or a given citation/co-purchase network), a set of one-hot labeled vertices, and class cardinality priors m specifying the expected count per class. It outputs a binary one-hot assignment matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{n×k} for all unlabeled vertices, where each row sums to 1 (exactly one class per vertex) and each column sums to the prescribed cardinality m_j. Unlike Laplace learning, no continuous-to-discrete thresholding step is needed.

## Method

CutSSL formulates k-way semi-supervised learning as a nonconvex quadratic program min ½tr(Xᵀ(L_u − sD)X) − tr(XᵀB) subject to row-sum, column-sum (cardinality), and nonnegativity constraints, where the perturbation sD makes the objective indefinite and pushes minimizers toward the vertices of the feasible polytope (one-hot vectors). A homotopy strategy solves the problem at s=0 (convex, equivalent to Laplace learning) and uses that solution to initialize subsequent solves at increasing s, controlling the number of spurious local minima. The nonconvex QP is solved via two-block ADMM: the X-update requires inverting L_{u,s}+I (a sparse Laplacian-like system solvable in near-linear time), while the T-update is a simple element-wise max(·,0) projection. Proposition 3.2 guarantees that when (s−1)(D_ii+D_jj) ≥ 2w_ij for all edges, every local minimizer is a 0-1 solution, making the relaxation exact.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CutSSL achieves 44.7% accuracy at 1 label per class, outperforming the state-of-the-art Poisson-MBO (41.8%) by 2.9% and vanilla Laplace learning (10.4%) by 34.3%. At 10 labels per class, CutSSL reaches 64.5% vs. 61.8% for Poisson-MBO. On large-scale OGB graphs (up to 2.5M nodes), CutSSL improves over Poisson-MBO by ~5% at 1-label rate (52.37% on Arxiv, 54.22% on Products) while running 2–3× faster. On MNIST at 1 label per class, CutSSL achieves 97.5% accuracy with very low variance (±0.1), compared to 96.5% for Poisson-MBO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CutSSL consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and label rate tested. On CIFAR-10, the gap over the strongest baseline Poisson-MBO [10] ranges from +2.9% (1 label/class) to +1.9% (4000 labels/class), while vanilla Laplace learning [38] and Sparse LP [20] are dramatically worse at low label rates (10.4% and 13.1% vs. 44.7% at 1 label/class). On the large OGB Arxiv graph (169K nodes, 40 classes), CutSSL achieves 52.37% at 1-label rate vs. 46.94% for Poisson-MBO and 27.36% for GraphHop-NN [34], while also running in 122s vs. 341s for Poisson-MBO. On OGB Products (2M+ nodes, 47 classes), CutSSL reaches 54.22% vs. 49.07% for Poisson-MBO and 43.07% for P-Laplace [15]. On citation networks (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed), CutSSL outperforms Poisson-MBO by 7.5% on Cora at 1 label/class and by 9.4% on Citeseer at 100 labels/class. The mean-shift Laplace heuristic and the exact cardinality-constrained Laplace solution (Section 4) serve as intermediate baselines that partially address degeneracy but remain inferior to CutSSL's integer-recovery approach, particularly at very low label rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

CutSSL sits at the intersection of graph partitioning (Hager & Krylyuk, 1999) and graph-based semi-supervised learning (Laplace learning, Zhu et al. 2003; Poisson learning, Calder et al. 2020). It departs from the dominant spectral/SDP relaxation paradigm by formulating cardinality-constrained SSL as an exact nonconvex quadratic program whose minimizers are provably integer, solved via ADMM along a homotopy path. The paper also provides a new theoretical lens on the mean-shift Laplace learning heuristic, showing it is an approximate solution to a cardinality-constrained Laplace problem, and positions itself against volume-constrained MBO schemes (Jacobs et al. 2018; Calder et al. 2020) that enforce class-size priors through iterative auction dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable integer recovery from a continuous nonconvex formulation is a clean theoretical contribution that avoids the inexactness of spectral relaxations and the slow convergence of combinatorial MBO schemes, while scaling to 2M+ node graphs. The connection to mean-shift Laplace learning offers a principled explanation for why that heuristic works and reveals its suboptimality, guiding practitioners toward the exact PCG solution at no extra cost. Open questions include the asymptotic consistency of cut-based SSL methods, whether better choices of the perturbation matrix S (beyond sD) can be informed by graph structure, and whether the framework extends to directed or weighted multi-relational graphs.
