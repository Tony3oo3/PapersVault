## Problem

Penalized Q-function methods like CQL uniformly penalize the value function for policy actions across all states, introducing unnecessary underestimation bias where policy actions are already well-represented in the dataset. This bias worsens as the penalty coefficient increases, creating a trade-off: reducing the penalty to avoid underestimation fails to control overestimation from distributional shift, while increasing it degrades performance through excessive pessimism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions and a behavior policy β̂ estimated via a VAE trained on the dataset to provide log-probabilities for the penalty adaptation factor. The IS weight w^Q_{s,a} is approximated using nearest-neighbor clustering in state space (radius ε·d̄_closest) with regularized empirical returns G_t/ζ rather than the live Q-function to stabilize training. The output is a Gaussian policy with Tanh squashing and a parameterized Q-function, both trained jointly for 3M gradient steps (0.3M for Adroit).

## Method

EPQ introduces an exclusive penalty P_τ = f_τ^{π,β̂}(s)·(π(a|s)/β̂(a|s) − 1) where the penalty adaptation factor f_τ = E_{a~π}[min(1, exp(−(log β̂(a|s) − τ)))] scales the penalty to zero when the behavior policy's log-probability for policy actions exceeds a threshold τ, effectively applying the penalty only to states where policy actions are underrepresented in the data. A prioritized dataset β^Q ∝ β̂·exp(Q) further reduces unnecessary bias by reweighting the Bellman update toward high-Q actions, with the IS weight w^Q_{s,a} = exp(Q(s,a))/E_{a'~β̂}[exp(Q(s,a'))] applied to the TD error term. The final Q-loss combines the weighted Bellman error with the exclusive penalty term α·f_τ·(log Σ_a exp Q(s,a') − Q(s,a)), and Theorem 3.1 proves that the converged Q-function underestimates the true value function with high probability when α is sufficiently large, while the average penalty is strictly bounded above by CQL's penalty.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, EPQ achieves the highest total normalized average return across all three task suites: 1536.7 on Mujoco locomotion (vs. MCQ 1474.9, CQL-reprod. 1325.8), 221.8 on Adroit manipulation (vs. MISA 162.7, Onestep 142.7), and 426.1 on AntMaze navigation (vs. IQL 378.0, MISA 348.1). Notable individual gains include Hopper-medium (101.3 vs. CQL-paper 86.6), Hopper-random (32.1 vs. CQL-reprod. 9.7), and Pen-cloned (91.8 vs. MISA 58.6). EPQ also demonstrates the lowest squared estimation bias across all penalizing constants α ∈ {0, 1, 5, 10, 20}, confirming that the exclusive penalty simultaneously reduces both overestimation and underestimation in the Q-function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPQ outperforms all seven baselines on the aggregate totals for Mujoco (1536.7 vs. best baseline MCQ 1474.9), Adroit (221.8 vs. MISA 162.7), and AntMaze (426.1 vs. IQL 378.0). Against CQL (the direct baseline EPQ builds upon), EPQ shows the largest relative gains on sparse-reward and low-data tasks: Hopper-random improves from 9.7 to 32.1 (3.3×), Hopper-medium from 58.5 to 101.3, and Pen-cloned from 39.2 to 91.8. Against MCQ, EPQ leads on most Mujoco tasks (e.g., Hopper-medium 101.3 vs. 78.4, Halfcheetah-medium 67.3 vs. 64.3) and all AntMaze tasks, though MCQ edges ahead on walker2d-medium (91.0 vs. 87.8) and walker2d-medium-replay (91.3 vs. 85.3). Against MISA, EPQ dominates Adroit (221.8 vs. 162.7) and AntMaze (426.1 vs. 348.1) but trails slightly on pen-human (83.9 vs. 88.1). Against IQL, EPQ wins on all Mujoco and Adroit tasks but IQL retains an advantage on umaze-diverse (62.2 vs. 78.3 is actually EPQ winning) and large-diverse (47.5 vs. 36.7, where IQL wins). The estimation bias analysis (Fig. 6) further confirms that EPQ achieves lower squared bias than CQL across all α values, explaining the performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Conservative Q-learning (CQL) by replacing its uniform penalty on policy actions with a threshold-based 'exclusive' penalty that only activates where policy actions are underrepresented in the dataset. It also incorporates the prioritized dataset concept from Yarats et al. (2022), using importance sampling weights proportional to exp(Q) to focus Bellman updates on high-value data actions. The method is positioned within the broader family of penalized Q-function approaches (CQL, MCQ) and constraint-based offline RL (BCQ, MISA), departing from prior work by arguing that uniform penalization introduces harmful underestimation bias in well-covered states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CQL's uniform penalty causes unnecessary underestimation in states with sufficient data coverage is a practical concern for practitioners tuning offline RL methods, and EPQ's selective penalty offers a principled fix that yields substantial gains on challenging tasks (e.g., Hopper-random: 32.1 vs. CQL's 10.8). However, the method introduces significant complexity—a VAE for behavior policy estimation, a penalty threshold τ, IS clipping, cluster radius, and temperature parameters—limiting its ease of adoption. Open questions include whether the selective penalty idea can be simplified to avoid the VAE dependency, whether it generalizes beyond D4RL to real-world robotics, and whether the many task-specific hyperparameters can be automatically tuned.
