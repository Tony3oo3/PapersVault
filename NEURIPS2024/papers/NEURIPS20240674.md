## Problem

Existing video-to-4D generation methods rely on implicit NeRF or explicit Gaussian Splatting representations that lack tight surface constraints, resulting in poor spatial-temporal consistency, blurry textures, and deformation artifacts. The paper addresses how to generate high-fidelity dynamic 3D objects with clean surfaces and robust motion from a single monocular video, drawing inspiration from modern 3D animation pipelines that use mesh-based skinning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video captured under a fixed viewpoint as input, from which a single reference image is sampled to initialize the 3D representation. It produces a dynamic triangular mesh with flat Gaussian splats bound to each face via barycentric coordinates, yielding a textured 4D object animation at 512×512 resolution. The output mesh is directly compatible with standard 3D graphics engines, enabling use in gaming and film pipelines without additional conversion.

## Method

DreamMesh4D employs a two-stage static-to-dynamic optimization over a Gaussian-mesh hybrid representation (SuGaR). In the static stage, a coarse mesh is extracted via Zero123-based SDS on 3D Gaussians followed by Poisson reconstruction, then refined by jointly optimizing mesh vertices and six flat Gaussians per face under photometric and SDS losses. In the dynamic stage, 1024 sparse control nodes are sampled on the mesh surface using geodesic distance to build a deformation graph; an MLP predicts per-node rotation, shear, translation, and a learned rigid-strength scalar η at each timestep. The key contribution is an adaptive hybrid skinning algorithm that fuses LBS and DQS per vertex via η, allowing strain (non-rigid) deformation where η is low and rigid rotation where η is high, thereby eliminating both the candy-wrapper artifacts of LBS and the joint-bulging artifacts of DQS. Training is supervised by reference-view photometric loss, Zero123 SDS loss, ARAP geometric regularization, and normal consistency loss.

## Results

On the Consistent4D test dataset (7 multi-view videos, evaluated over 4 novel views), DreamMesh4D achieves the best score on all six metrics. The headline gains are a PSNR(ref) of 37.04 dB (vs. 31.06 for the next-best DreamGaussian4D) and an FVD of 474.96, roughly half that of the closest competitor (4DGen at 913.10). The method also attains the lowest FID-VID (29.14), lowest LPIPS (0.126), highest SSIM(ref) (0.980), and highest CLIP score (0.938) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamMesh4D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamGaussian4D (the strongest baseline on PSNR at 31.06), the proposed method gains approximately 6 dB in PSNR(ref) and nearly halves the FVD (474.96 vs. 994.11), indicating substantially better spatial-temporal coherence. Compared to Consistent4D, the largest gap appears in FVD (474.96 vs. 929.39) and CLIP score (0.938 vs. 0.917), reflecting improved temporal consistency and semantic fidelity. Against 4DGen, which uses only 16 frames, DreamMesh4D leads by ~10 dB in PSNR and by a factor of ~2 in FVD, while also achieving a much lower FID-VID (29.14 vs. 63.32). STAG4D, the most recent baseline, is surpassed on all metrics, with the most notable margin in FVD (474.96 vs. 1048.10) and FID-VID (29.14 vs. 38.77). The consistent dominance across both image-level (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP) and video-level (FVD, FID-VID) metrics suggests that the Gaussian-mesh hybrid representation combined with adaptive hybrid skinning provides a meaningful structural advantage over pure Gaussian or NeRF-based 4D representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-to-4D generation line (Consistent4D, DreamGaussian4D, 4DGen, STAG4D) by replacing their implicit NeRF or pure Gaussian splatting representations with a SuGaR-style Gaussian-mesh hybrid, where flat Gaussians are bound to triangle faces via barycentric coordinates. For motion driving, it departs from the sparse-controlled Gaussian deformation approaches (SC-GS using LBS, HiFi4G using DQS) by proposing an adaptive hybrid skinning that per-vertex interpolates between LBS and DQS based on a learned rigid-strength scalar, and by constructing the deformation graph with geodesic rather than Euclidean distance. The static-to-dynamic optimization pipeline (coarse mesh via Zero123 SDS → SuGaR refinement → deformation network training) borrows the score distillation framework from DreamFusion/Zero123 but applies it to a mesh-constrained representation rather than free-form Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mesh-based output is directly importable into modern 3D engines (Unreal, Unity), which is a practical advantage over NeRF or Gaussian-only representations that require additional extraction steps. The adaptive hybrid skinning addresses a long-standing gap in geometric animation where neither LBS (volume loss) nor DQS (joint bulging, no strain) handles all deformation types well, and the learned per-node rigid strength provides a principled way to blend them. Key limitations include reliance on Zero123 for novel-view SDS (constraining optimization time and generalization), restriction to object-level generation from fixed-viewpoint videos, and evaluation on only seven test sequences. Open questions include extending to scene-level 4D content, handling moving cameras, and whether the mesh constraint remains beneficial as video diffusion models improve enough to support text-to-4D directly.
