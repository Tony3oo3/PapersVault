## Problem

NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems require domain experts to laboriously design heuristics through trial and error, and traditional hyper-heuristics are constrained by human-defined heuristic spaces. While LLMs can generate heuristics in open-ended spaces (Language Hyper-Heuristics), pure LLM sampling is sample-inefficient and lacks the reasoning capability needed for complex or black-box problems where the objective function is not explicitly described. The paper formalizes LHHs as a new hyper-heuristic variant and asks how to make them sample-efficient and capable of inferring problem structure from black-box evaluations alone.

## Input / Output

ReEvo takes a text-based task specification containing a COP description (which in black-box mode deliberately obfuscates problem identity, e.g., using 'edge_attr' instead of 'distance_matrix'), a Python function signature defining the heuristic interface, and optionally a seed heuristic for in-context exemplification. The output is a single Python code snippet implementing the heuristic function, which is then plugged into an existing algorithmic framework (GLS, ACO, GA, or NCO) without modifying the surrounding pipeline. In black-box settings, the reflector LLM must infer the underlying problem structure solely from comparing heuristic code and their observed performance, rather than from explicit problem descriptions.

## Method

ReEvo integrates evolutionary search (genetic programming over LLM-generated code) with a dual-level reflection mechanism that provides 'verbal gradients' within the heuristic space. Each iteration performs: (1) selection of parent pairs, (2) short-term reflection where a reflector LLM compares two parents' code and performance to produce concise design hints, (3) crossover where a generator LLM synthesizes an offspring conditioned on both parents plus the reflection, (4) long-term reflection that distills accumulated short-term insights into a compact summary, and (5) elitist mutation that improves the current best heuristic using the long-term reflection. The key insight is that reflections smooth the fitness landscape—autocorrelation analysis shows correlation length increases from 0.28 to 1.28 with reflections—making the search less rugged and more directed. This enables ReEvo to outperform prior LHH (EoH) in sample efficiency across five COPs under both white-box and black-box prompting, using only ~100 heuristic evaluations and a single LLM (GPT-3.5 Turbo).

## Results

On TSP1000, ReEvo-generated attention reshaping reduces POMO's optimality gap from 52.11% to 29.08% and LEHD's from 3.17% to 2.97%, with negligible runtime overhead. On GLS for TSP200, KGLS-ReEvo achieves a 0.216% optimality gap, outperforming NeuOpt (0.403%), GNNGLS (3.522%), and EoH (0.338%). On the Decap Placement Problem, GA-ReEvo achieves an objective of 12.98, surpassing DevFormer-CSE (12.88) and the expert-designed GA (12.41). For constructive TSP heuristics on TSPLIB, ReEvo achieves a 14.6% average optimality gap versus 16.7% for the classic GP hyper-heuristic GHPP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReEvo consistently outperforms all baselines across its five application settings. In GLS (Table 1), KGLS-ReEvo beats NeuOpt [52], GNNGLS [24], NeuralGLS [75], EoH [47], and original KGLS [1] on TSP200 (0.216% vs. 0.403%, 3.522%, 3.622%, 0.338%, 0.284% gap) while matching them on TSP20–100. In ACO (Fig. 2), ReEvo heuristics surpass DeepACO [94] on 3 of 5 COPs (TSP50, OP50, MKP100) even when DeepACO overfits the test size, and beat all five expert-designed heuristics [71, 6, 72, 17, 39] across all problem sizes. On DPP (Fig. 3), GA-ReEvo (12.98) outperforms DevFormer-CSE [31] (12.88), GA-expert [63] (12.41), and all RL baselines [30, 60, 61, 100, 62] (9.56–9.79). For constructive TSP (Table 2), ReEvo (14.6% avg gap) beats GHPP [15] (16.7%) and Nearest Neighbour (25.4%) on 19 of 20 TSPLIB instances. In NCO attention reshaping (Table 3), ReEvo heuristics outperform both the original POMO [37]/LEHD [51] models and the expert-designed DAR [85] at n=500 and n=1000 for both TSP and CVRP. In the direct LHH comparison (Fig. 4), ReEvo demonstrates superior sample efficiency over EoH [47] across all five COPs with GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4 Turbo, and Llama 3 (70B) under black-box prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes Language Hyper-Heuristics (LHHs) as a new variant of the decades-old Hyper-Heuristic paradigm, where LLMs replace human-defined heuristic primitives to explore open-ended heuristic spaces. Methodologically, ReEvo extends the Reflexion framework [70] from binary pass/fail programming feedback to comparative analysis of two heuristics (a 'verbal gradient'), and embeds this within a Genetic Programming loop. It directly builds on and extends EoH [47] and FunSearch [68], which were the first to use LLMs for COP heuristic generation, by adding dual-level reflections (short-term and long-term), fitness landscape analysis, and black-box prompting for more reliable evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single LLM-evolved heuristic can match or beat size-specific neural solvers across TSP20–200, and that attention-reshaping heuristics can halve the optimality gap of POMO on TSP1000 (52%→29%) with negligible overhead—suggesting LHHs as a practical, interpretable complement to NCO when evaluation is expensive or data is limited. Open questions include whether results scale beyond 100 evaluations, whether the approach generalizes to truly novel problem classes beyond the six studied, and whether the reliance on capable proprietary LLMs (GPT-3.5/4 Turbo) limits reproducibility. The fitness landscape analysis (correlation length 0.28→1.28 with reflections) provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why verbal gradients help, but a deeper characterization of the LHH search space remains open.
