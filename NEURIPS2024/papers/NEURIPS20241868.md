## Problem

Existing garment reconstruction methods either assume the garment is worn (constrained by body shape) or require a known canonical geometry and initialization, limiting them to a narrow range of deformations. This paper tackles the harder problem of recovering 3D garment shape from incomplete point clouds when the garment is freely manipulated—folded, crumpled, or flattened—without any prior knowledge of its geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete 3D point cloud (as few as ~2000 points) of a garment in an arbitrary deformed state, typically acquired from multi-view RGBD or laser scanning with heavy self-occlusion. It produces a complete 3D garment mesh along with full UV maps for front and back panels. Notably, no template mesh, canonical geometry, or initial pose estimate is required, and the method handles garments of unknown geometry across categories (shirts, pants, tops, skirts).

## Method

The approach operates in a unified 2D UV space where garment panels are represented by the Implicit Sewing Patterns (ISP) model (SDF for panel shape + learned 2D-to-3D mapping). A UV mapper (sparse 3D CNN + transformer encoder + MLP) assigns each input point to a panel and UV coordinate, yielding sparse partial UV maps. A DDPM diffusion model trained on concatenated UV maps and panel masks of deformed garments serves as a deformation prior. Fitting proceeds in two stages: (1) the ISP latent code is optimized to recover the 2D panel shape from the sparse mask, and (2) a guided reverse diffusion process fills occluded regions using the sparse UV map and recovered panel mask as manifold guidance (Eq. 11–14), optionally incorporating temporal regularization from the previous frame. The final 3D mesh is obtained by applying the ISP 2D-to-3D mapping to the completed UV maps.

## Results

On the VR-Folding dataset, the proposed method achieves the best reconstruction accuracy across all four garment categories (Shirt, Pants, Top, Skirt) for both Folding and Flattening tasks, without requiring any initialization. For example, on Shirt Folding, it reaches 84.7% A3 and 0.77 cm Dcf, compared to 29.8% A3 and 1.16 cm Dcf for GarmentTracking with ground-truth initialization. On Pants Flattening, it achieves 60.8% A5 and 1.16 cm Dcf versus 31.3% A5 and 1.64 cm Dcf for the best baseline. The method also generalizes to real-world point clouds captured via NeRF-based reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: GarmentNets [27], a single-frame method requiring no geometry prior, and GarmentTracking [2], a sequence-based tracker that requires a canonical garment mesh and an initial pose estimate. GarmentNets consistently produces the worst results across all categories (e.g., 0.8% A3 on Shirt Folding, 1.5% A5 on Pants Flattening), confirming that template-free single-frame reconstruction is insufficient for heavily deformed garments. GarmentTracking with ground-truth initialization is the strongest baseline but is still substantially outperformed: on Shirt Folding, the proposed method achieves 84.7% A3 vs. 29.8%, and on Pants Flattening 60.8% A5 vs. 31.3%. Critically, when GarmentTracking is initialized with GarmentNets predictions (GN) instead of ground truth, its accuracy drops sharply (e.g., Top Folding A3 falls from 37.9% to 21.1%), highlighting its sensitivity to initialization. The only metric where the proposed method does not lead is Skirt Folding Dcr (4.70 cm vs. 4.61 cm for GarmentTracking-GT), which the authors attribute to the rotational symmetry of skirts making correspondence-based metrics unreliable; on Chamfer Distance (Dcf), the proposed method still wins (1.04 vs. 1.33 cm).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Implicit Sewing Patterns (ISP) model [1], which represents garment geometry via 2D signed distance fields and UV-to-3D mappings, by introducing a diffusion-based deformation prior learned in UV space. It departs from prior garment reconstruction work (GarmentNets, GarmentTracking, on-body methods) that either requires known garment geometry, a body shape prior, or assumes the garment is being worn. The key conceptual shift is operating in a unified 2D UV space where a generative diffusion model captures the distribution of plausible deformations across many garment geometries, rather than fitting a single template or relying on a canonical mesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important gap for robotic manipulation and VR/AR applications where garments are handled rather than worn, and no body or prior geometry is available. The method's ability to reconstruct garments without any initialization or canonical mesh makes it more deployable in real-world settings than template-based alternatives. Open questions include closing the sim-to-real gap, handling self-intersections through physics-aware priors, and incorporating temporal accumulation to improve UV mapping accuracy. The rotational symmetry issue with skirts also highlights limitations of correspondence-based metrics for symmetric objects.
