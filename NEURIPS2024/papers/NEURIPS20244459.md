## Problem

LLMs struggle with knowledge graph completion because entity names are treated as sequences of subword tokens rather than atomic identifiers, and existing LLM-based methods are confined to narrow tasks like triplet validation or re-ranking a small candidate list produced by a separate KG embedding model. This paper asks whether an LLM can be taught a specialized three-word 'KG Language' (KGL) in which each sentence is strictly an entity noun, a relation verb, and another entity noun, enabling end-to-end prediction over the full entity vocabulary without external ranking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244459/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete triplet (e.g., entity + relation) formatted as a KGL instruction that includes a small dictionary mapping KGL tokens to their English names and one example sentence per token. KGL tokens are special tokens appended to the LLM's original vocabulary, and their embeddings are not learned from scratch but retrieved from the pretrained token embeddings of the entity/relation name plus KG neighborhood structure. The output is a one-shot probability distribution over all candidate entities (not sequential token generation), which also yields entity embedding vectors usable for downstream KG tasks. The context retriever reduces average input length from 811.2 to 91.4 tokens on FB15k-237 compared to in-context learning with one-hop neighbors.

## Method

MKGL fine-tunes Llama-2-7b (with LoRA on query/value layers, <0.3% trainable parameters) to generate KGL sentences, using two LoRA-inspired retrievers. The KGL Context Retriever constructs input embeddings by: (1) down-scaling the frozen LLM token embedding matrix to a low-rank space, (2) aggregating the constituent text token embeddings of each KGL token via a PNA encoder, (3) aggregating KG neighborhood information via multi-layered relational PNA, and (4) up-scaling back to the LLM's hidden dimension. The KGL Score Retriever replaces the standard score layer by down-scaling both the score matrix and the LLM's final hidden state, retrieving text and KG information for each candidate entity, and producing a scalar probability per entity in a single forward pass. Training uses a binary cross-entropy contrastive loss over sampled negative entities, making the framework directly compatible with standard KG completion evaluation protocols and enabling inductive generalization to unseen entities.

## Results

On FB15k-237, MKGL achieves MRR 0.415, Hits@1 0.325, Hits@3 0.454, and Hits@10 0.591, outperforming the strongest baseline KICGPT (MRR 0.412, Hits@10 0.554) and all conventional KG embedding methods. On WN18RR, MKGL reaches MRR 0.552, Hits@1 0.500, Hits@3 0.577, and Hits@10 0.656, surpassing KICGPT (MRR 0.549, Hits@1 0.474) and FTL-LM (MRR 0.543). In the inductive setting, MKGL achieves MRR 0.475 on FB15k-237-ind-v1 and 0.746 on WN18RR-ind-v1, substantially exceeding the best inductive baseline RED-GNN (0.369 and 0.701 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MKGL outperforms the closest LLM-based competitor KICGPT on FB15k-237 (MRR 0.415 vs. 0.412; Hits@10 0.591 vs. 0.554) and on WN18RR MRR (0.552 vs. 0.549) and Hits@1 (0.500 vs. 0.474), though KICGPT retains a slight edge on WN18RR Hits@3 (0.585 vs. 0.577). Against the strongest conventional GNN-based method CoKE, MKGL leads by 0.051 MRR on FB15k-237 (0.415 vs. 0.364) and 0.068 MRR on WN18RR (0.552 vs. 0.484). MKGL also surpasses the transformer-based DET by 0.039 MRR on FB15k-237 and 0.045 MRR on WN18RR. Notably, FTL-LM achieves higher Hits@10 on WN18RR (0.773 vs. 0.656) but trails MKGL on MRR (0.543 vs. 0.552) and Hits@1 (0.452 vs. 0.500). In the inductive setting, MKGL dominates all rule-based and subgraph-reasoning baselines (GraIL, NeuralLP, DRUM, RED-GNN) by 0.05–0.11 MRR across all eight inductive subsets, establishing it as the first embedding-based method to achieve state-of-the-art inductive KG completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MKGL builds directly on LoRA's low-rank projection pattern, repurposing it not for adapter fine-tuning but for constructing compact KGL token embeddings from pretrained LLM vocabulary vectors. It departs from prior LLM-for-KG work (KICGPT, KG-Llama) by replacing in-context text retrieval with a PNA-based context retriever that encodes textual and relational information into fixed-size vectors, and by introducing a score retriever that produces a one-shot probability distribution over all candidate entities rather than relying on a separate KG embedding model for initial ranking. The three-word KGL framing is a novel conceptual lens, but the underlying task and evaluation protocol follow the well-established KG completion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MKGL is the first embedding-based method to achieve state-of-the-art inductive KG completion, addressing a gap where prior LLM-based approaches could not generalize to unseen entities. The practical efficiency gain—reducing average input length from 811 to 91 tokens—makes LLM-based KG completion more tractable, and the produced entity embeddings enable downstream tasks that prompt-based methods cannot support. Key limitations include the 8×A100 GPU requirement and dependence on a specific LLM backbone; open questions include whether pretraining on mixed KG/natural-language corpora could reduce fine-tuning cost, and whether the KGL retrieval paradigm generalizes to other structured-data tasks like entity linking or protein representation learning.
