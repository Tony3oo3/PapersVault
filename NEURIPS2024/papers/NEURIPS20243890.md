## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces millions of Gaussian points per scene, making it memory-intensive. Existing pruning methods (RadSplat, Mini-Splatting, LightGaussian) rely on a manually tuned importance-score threshold that must be searched over multiple training rounds per scene, and the optimal ratio varies across scenes. No prior method can automatically discover the favorable pruning ratio within a single training run.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243890/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard 3DGS inputs—multi-view RGB images with known camera poses—and produces a pruned 3D Gaussian model (typically 54–74% of original Gaussians removed, scene-dependent) that renders novel views at higher FPS. The pruning ratio is not a user-specified hyperparameter; instead, it emerges per-scene from the learned mask, and a single one-time hard pruning is applied after mask training before a short fine-tuning phase.

## Method

LP-3DGS introduces a per-Gaussian trainable real-valued mask that is binarized via a Gumbel-Sigmoid activation (a two-class adaptation of Gumbel-Softmax), making the masking operation fully differentiable and compatible with standard 3DGS backpropagation—unlike the straight-through estimator used by Compact3D. The mask is multiplied with a chosen importance score (RadSplat's max-ray contribution or Mini-Splatting's cumulative weight) and then applied to each Gaussian's opacity, so that Gaussians with mask values near zero contribute negligibly to rendering. Training proceeds in two stages: a standard 3DGS densification phase followed by a 500-iteration mask-learning phase with an L1 sparsity penalty on the mask, after which Gaussians with mask value 0 are permanently removed and the pruned model is fine-tuned for the remaining iterations. This design is score-agnostic, requiring only the importance-score formula to be swapped, and automatically converges to a scene-specific pruning ratio without any manual threshold search.

## Results

On MipNeRF360, LP-3DGS with RadSplat's importance score achieves an average PSNR of 27.47 (vs. 27.48 baseline) and SSIM of 0.8120 (vs. 0.8125 baseline) while automatically pruning ~63% of Gaussians in a single training run. With Mini-Splatting's score, it reaches PSNR 27.12 and SSIM 0.8047 at ~60% pruning. The pruned models deliver roughly 2× rendering speedup (e.g., Kitchen: 315→542 FPS; Bicycle: 132→324 FPS) with training time comparable to vanilla 3DGS (e.g., 34 vs. 33 min on Kitchen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, LP-3DGS preserves rendering quality almost exactly (PSNR 27.47 vs. 27.48 on MipNeRF360) while removing ~63% of Gaussians, yielding a ~2× FPS gain (e.g., 315→542 on Kitchen). Compared to RadSplat and Mini-Splatting, LP-3DGS uses their respective importance scores but eliminates the need for manual threshold sweeping: it learns the optimal per-scene pruning ratio in one training pass (500 mask iterations) rather than requiring 12+ separate training runs to find the best ratio. Against Compact3D (STE-based mask on opacity/scale), LP-3DGS (Gumbel-Sigmoid mask) achieves higher average PSNR (27.44 vs. 27.28), SSIM (0.812 vs. 0.805), and lower LPIPS (0.226 vs. 0.232) while producing comparable or smaller models. In the ablation where STE is applied to RadSplat's importance score, the STE mask over-prunes to ~86% (vs. LP-3DGS's 63%), causing a significant quality drop (PSNR 26.96 vs. 27.47), confirming that Gumbel-Sigmoid's differentiable binarization yields a more balanced compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework and the line of importance-score-based pruning methods (RadSplat, Mini-Splatting, LightGaussian) that define per-Gaussian scores and apply a fixed threshold to remove redundant points. It also extends Compact3D's trainable-mask idea, which used a straight-through estimator (STE) to make binary masking compatible with backpropagation. The key departure is replacing STE with a Gumbel-Sigmoid activation (adapted from Gumbel-Softmax to the binary case), making the mask fully differentiable and allowing the pruning ratio to be learned end-to-end within a single training run rather than requiring manual threshold sweeping across multiple training sessions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating the pruning ratio selection, LP-3DGS removes a significant practical bottleneck in deploying compressed 3DGS models: the need for per-scene hyperparameter search over multiple training runs. The method is score-agnostic (works with RadSplat or Mini-Splatting importance scores) and achieves roughly 2× FPS improvement with minimal quality loss, making it a practical drop-in for 3DGS pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that rendering quality after pruning is still bounded by the quality of the underlying importance score, and the mask is only trained for 500 iterations, leaving open whether longer or curriculum-based mask training could push compression further. The approach is also limited to single-GPU training, which constrains scalability to very large scenes.
