## Problem

Existing controllable generation methods such as ControlNet require manually designed feature extractors (Canny edges, depth maps, pose estimators) and large annotated paired datasets, limiting scalability. The human brain, by contrast, spontaneously associates visual attributes (e.g., linking a sketch to a real scene) without any supervision, leveraging cortical modularity and hippocampal pattern completion. This paper asks whether a network can spontaneously develop functionally specialized modules and use them as self-supervised control conditions for controllable generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input. The image is passed through a pretrained modular autoencoder to extract k groups of feature maps (modules), each spontaneously specializing in a distinct visual attribute such as color, brightness, or edges at a particular spatial frequency. A selected subset of these modules is upsampled to pixel space and fed as the control condition into a ControlNet-style adapter on top of a frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone, which outputs a full-resolution generated image that completes the missing information. No manually computed edge maps, depth maps, or segmentation labels are required at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular autoencoder trained with a novel equivariance constraint that simultaneously enforces inter-module independence and intra-module correlation. Convolutional kernels are partitioned into k groups; a translation-rotation equivariant loss requires each module to predict its own feature map under geometric transforms via a learnable 3D prediction-matrix codebook, while a symmetry loss ensures any two dimensions within a module are related by some transform, forming a closed submanifold. This drives spontaneous functional specialization (orientation selectivity, color antagonism, center-surround receptive fields) without any task supervision. For generation, the specialized modules replace hand-crafted conditions: a ControlNet adapter on a frozen SD1.5 diffusion model is trained in a self-supervised pattern-completion objective, where the model learns to reconstruct the full image from a partial set of modules, eliminating the need for annotated paired data and enabling zero-shot generalization to sketches, paintings, ancient graffiti, super-resolution, and dehazing.

## Results

On the MS-COCO validation set, SCG conditioned by its self-specialized modules (HC0–HC5) consistently outperforms ControlNet (Canny-edge conditioned) across all metrics. The best SCG variant HC0 (color-sensitive modules) achieves PSNR(c) of 19.0 dB, SSIM(c) of 0.490, FID of 8.5, and CLIP similarity of 0.958, compared to ControlNet's 10.3 dB, 0.128, 13.7, and 0.846 respectively. HC1 (brightness-sensitive) leads on structural metrics with PSNR(g) of 19.9 dB and SSIM(g) of 0.519. In subjective evaluations on zero-shot oil painting generation, SCG achieves significantly higher winning rates than ControlNet in both fidelity and aesthetics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is ControlNet conditioned on Canny edge maps. SCG outperforms ControlNet on every quantitative metric in Table 1: the best SCG variant (HC0) improves PSNR(c) by ~8.7 dB (19.0 vs. 10.3), SSIM(c) by ~0.36 (0.490 vs. 0.128), FID by ~5.2 (8.5 vs. 13.7), and CLIP similarity by ~0.11 (0.958 vs. 0.846). Even the weakest SCG variant (HC5) still matches or exceeds ControlNet on PSNR(c) and SSIM(c). In zero-shot associative generation on oil paintings, subjective evaluation (40 participants) shows SCG achieving significantly higher winning rates than ControlNet in both fidelity and aesthetics. On the more challenging ancient graffiti task, SCG achieves a comparable fidelity winning rate but a significantly higher aesthetics winning rate, as the learned modules suppress rock-surface noise that severely degrades Canny-based ControlNet outputs. The paper also notes that ControlNet holds a natural advantage on clean sketches due to its Canny-edge conditioning, but SCG still produces high-fidelity results in this out-of-distribution setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) for the pattern-completion generation stage, but replaces its supervised, manually-designed control conditions (Canny edges, depth maps, segmentation) with spontaneously specialized modules from a modular autoencoder. The modular autoencoder draws on group equivariant convolutional networks (Cohen & Welling, 2016) and extends them with a symmetry loss that enforces intra-module correlation, going beyond prior equivariance work that was limited to translational equivariance. Conceptually, the paper repurposes neuroscience mechanisms—cortical modularization and hippocampal pattern completion—as a design principle for self-supervised controllable generation, departing from the supervised paradigm that dominates the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for annotated paired data and manually designed feature extractors, SCG opens a path toward more scalable controllable generation that can zero-shot generalize to unseen domains (sketches, oil paintings, ancient graffiti, super-resolution, dehazing). The spontaneous emergence of brain-like features (orientation selectivity, color antagonism, center-surround receptive fields) from a simple equivariance constraint is a compelling validation of the modularization hypothesis. However, the current equivariance constraint only induces low-level feature specialization; richer semantic hierarchies (depth, motion, instance-level contours) remain unexplored, and the method still falls short of dedicated supervised approaches on specific tasks. A key open question is whether fine-tuning the self-supervised modules on labeled data can bridge this gap while retaining the zero-shot generalization advantage.
