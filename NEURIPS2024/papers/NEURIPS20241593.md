## Problem

Neural codec language models suffer from a distribution gap: the NAR model is trained on golden AR tokens from real speech but receives synthetic AR tokens at inference, degrading speech quality. Moreover, existing speech language models focus solely on supervised fine-tuning and neglect preference optimization to align generated speech with human preferences for quality, naturalness, and expressiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241593/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text transcript and a 3-second prompt speech from a target speaker as input. The AR model (SpeechGPT-based) converts text into first-layer SpeechTokenizer RVQ tokens, which the NAR model (SoundStorm-based) uses alongside the prompt speech to generate the remaining 7 RVQ layers. The full 8-layer token sequence is decoded by the SpeechTokenizer decoder into waveform speech. For preference data construction, the input is a speech-text pair from LibriSpeech, and the output is a triplet of (text, golden AR tokens, synthetic AR tokens).

## Method

SpeechAlign is an iterative self-improvement strategy that aligns codec language models to human preferences without requiring additional human-annotated data. It constructs preference pairs by treating golden AR tokens (extracted from real speech via SpeechTokenizer) as preferred and model-generated synthetic AR tokens as dispreferred, validated by human verification (71% golden win rate). The AR model is then optimized using preference optimization methods—DPO, RLHF-PPO, Chain-of-Hindsight, or Best-of-N Sampling—on these pairs. The process iterates: the updated model generates new synthetic tokens for the next round of preference data, progressively narrowing the distribution gap between training and inference phases. Experiments show DPO with 3 iterations reduces WER from 7.2 to 6.0 and increases speaker similarity from 0.87 to 0.90 on LibriSpeech, with gains generalizing to unseen speakers in VCTK.

## Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean, SpeechAlign-DPO-Iter3 achieves the best WER of 6.0 (vs. 7.2 for the SFT baseline) and the highest Speaker Similarity of 0.90, while SpeechAlign-RLHF-PPO attains the best QMOS of 3.60 and SMOS of 3.87. On VCTK (unseen speakers), SpeechAlign-DPO-Iter3 reduces WER to 7.9 (from 8.8 baseline) and raises SIM to 0.83, and SpeechAlign-RLHF-PPO leads in QMOS (3.53) and SMOS (3.80). Iterative DPO shows steady gains across three iterations, with WER dropping from 7.2 → 6.7 → 6.2 → 6.0 on LibriSpeech. Continue SFT, by contrast, degrades performance (WER worsens to 8.0, QMOS drops to 3.07), confirming that the improvements come from preference-aware learning rather than additional supervised training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SpeechAlign-sft, the SFT-only codec language model. All four preference optimization strategies (CoH, BoN, RLHF-PPO, DPO) outperform it on at least some metrics, with DPO-Iter3 achieving the largest WER reduction (7.2 → 6.0, a 1.2-point drop) and the highest SIM (0.87 → 0.90) on LibriSpeech. Continue SFT, which retrains on the same golden tokens without preference signals, actually degrades performance (WER 8.0, QMOS 3.07), confirming that gains are attributable to preference-aware learning rather than extra supervised steps. Among the preference methods, RLHF-PPO leads in subjective quality (QMOS 3.60, SMOS 3.87 on LibriSpeech) and generalizes best to unseen VCTK speakers, while DPO-Iter3 leads in objective metrics (WER, SIM) and subjective SMOS (3.93). On VCTK, BoN achieves the lowest WER (7.5) but does not improve SIM over the baseline, whereas DPO-Iter3 and RLHF-PPO both improve SIM to 0.80–0.83.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural codec language model pipeline (AudioLM, VALL-E) by importing preference optimization techniques—DPO, RLHF-PPO, and Chain-of-Hindsight—from the LLM alignment literature into the speech generation setting. While prior codec LMs (AudioLM, VALL-E, AnyGPT) relied exclusively on supervised fine-tuning, SpeechAlign introduces an iterative self-improvement loop that uses golden-vs-synthetic codec token pairs as a proxy for human preference, drawing conceptual inspiration from SPIN's self-play fine-tuning. The AR+NAR architecture and SpeechTokenizer discretization are inherited directly from AnyGPT/SoundStorm, so the novelty lies in the alignment strategy rather than the generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete distribution gap between training (golden tokens) and inference (synthetic tokens) in codec LMs, showing it measurably degrades WER and speaker similarity—a problem previously acknowledged anecdotally but not systematically addressed. The finding that iterative DPO improves for three rounds then degrades at the fourth provides a useful practical upper bound for self-improvement loops in speech. Key open questions remain: whether preference optimization can be extended to the NAR model, whether fine-grained multi-dimensional rewards (rhythm, timbre, expressiveness) yield larger gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond English and the specific SpeechGPT+SoundStorm architecture to other codec LMs and multilingual settings.
