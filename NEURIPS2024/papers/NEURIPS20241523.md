## Problem

Prior best-of-both-worlds (BOBW) algorithms for heavy-tailed multi-armed bandits (Huang et al., 2022) rely on a strong 'truncated non-negative losses' assumption on the optimal arm's distribution, which is generally hard to verify in practice. Without this assumption, potentially negative and unbounded losses break the stability conditions of Tsallis-entropy-based FTRL, making it unclear whether near-optimal worst-case regret is achievable in the adversarial regime, let alone the BOBW setting. This paper shows there is no fundamental barrier by replacing the regularizer and adapting the detect-switch framework.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives scalar loss observations that may be negative and unbounded (only requiring (1+v)-th raw moments bounded by u^{1+v} for known u>0, v∈(0,1]), along with K arms, horizon T, and failure probability ζ. Each round it outputs a probability distribution over K arms (constrained to a truncated simplex with minimum probability λ/K) and a trimmed importance-weighted loss estimate. The key constraint is that the trimming threshold is fixed at M = u(T/K)^{1/(1+v)} rather than depending on the current sampling probability, which is what enables removing the non-negativity assumption.

## Method

The core insight is replacing the Tsallis entropy regularizer with a log-barrier regularizer in OMD, whose stability condition η_{t,i}·w_{t,i}·ℓ̂_{t,i} ≥ −0.5 naturally provides a factor of w_{t,i} to cancel the (w_{t,i})^{-1} in the importance-weighted estimate, eliminating the need for the trimming threshold to scale with w_{t,i}^{1/(1+v)} (which was the source of the non-negativity requirement). For high-probability guarantees, the algorithm combines an increasing-learning-rates schedule (multiplying η by κ = e^{1/log T} when 1/w_{t+1,i} exceeds a tracked threshold ρ_{t,i}) with probability simplex truncation at λ = T^{-v/(1+v)}K^{-1/(1+v)}, and applies an adaptive variant of Freedman's inequality to handle the history-dependent trimming in martingale concentration. For the BOBW extension, the detect-switch framework (SAO) from Bubeck & Slivkins (2012) is adapted to the heavy-tailed setting: the algorithm runs statistical tests each round to detect non-stochasticity, and upon detection switches to the adversarial-regime OMD-LB-HT algorithm for remaining rounds, with the non-trivial challenge of proving concentration for the trimmed empirical mean under non-identical (adversarial) distributions via the adaptive Freedman inequality.

## Results

In the adversarial regime, Algorithm 1 (OMD-LB-HT) achieves a high-probability pseudo-regret of O(u K^{v/(1+v)} T^{1/(1+v)} (log T)^2 log(T/ζ)), which is near-optimal up to logarithmic factors (matching the lower bound Ω(u K^{v/(1+v)} T^{1/(1+v)})). In the BOBW setting, Algorithm 2 (SAO-HT) preserves the same near-optimal adversarial regret O(u K^{v/(1+v)} T^{1/(1+v)} log(K) (log T)^4) in expectation while achieving a gap-dependent stochastic regret of O(K log(K) (u^{1+1/v}/Δ)^{1/v} (log T)^4) in expectation. Crucially, both guarantees hold with high probability and without the 'truncated non-negative losses' assumption (Assumption 2) required by all prior BOBW heavy-tailed results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to HTINF and AdaTINF (Huang et al., 2022), the proposed methods remove the critical 'truncated non-negative losses' assumption (Assumption 2) while upgrading from expected to high-probability regret guarantees; HTINF achieves the same O(u K^{v/(1+v)} T^{1/(1+v)}) adversarial rate but only in expectation and under Assumption 2. Against AdaR-UCB (Genalti et al., 2024), the paper's SAO-HT extends the guarantee from the stochastic regime to the full BOBW setting (adversarial + stochastic) and again removes Assumption 2, though AdaR-UCB handles unknown (u,v) while the paper assumes they are known. Versus uniINF (Chen et al., 2024), which also targets BOBW with unknown (u,v), the paper's SAO-HT provides high-probability bounds (vs. expected only) and does not require Assumption 2, at the cost of a slightly weaker gap-dependent term K/Δ^{1/v} (vs. uniINF's K/Δ^{1/v} with an extra log factor) and the assumption that (u,v) are known. The paper's results also immediately imply the first high-probability BOBW regret guarantee under pure Local Differential Privacy (LDP) protection, whereas the prior state-of-the-art (Zheng et al., 2020) only provides expected regret under the weaker approximate LDP notion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detect-switch BOBW framework of Bubeck & Slivkins (2012) and the heavy-tailed BOBW algorithm of Huang et al. (2022) by replacing the Tsallis-entropy FTRL approach with a log-barrier OMD algorithm, thereby eliminating the strong 'truncated non-negative losses' assumption (Assumption 2) that was required for all prior heavy-tailed BOBW results. The high-probability guarantee is obtained via the increasing-learning-rates trick of Lee et al. (2020) combined with an adaptive Freedman's inequality from Zimmert & Lattimore (2022), repurposed here to handle history-dependent trimming thresholds in martingale concentration. The log-barrier regularizer is the key conceptual departure: its stability condition naturally absorbs the 1/w_{t,i} factor from importance-weighted estimates, removing the need for the trimming threshold to scale with w_{t,i}^{1/(1+v)} as was forced under Tsallis entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of Assumption 2 is significant because it was a hard-to-verify technical condition that limited the practical applicability of prior heavy-tailed BOBW results, and its relaxation immediately yields the first near-optimal adversarial regret under Huber contamination (previously only studied in the easier stochastic regime) and the first high-probability BOBW guarantee under pure LDP. Open questions include whether the gap-dependent term K/Δ^{1/v} can be refined to the per-arm sum Σ(Δ_i)^{-1/v}, and whether canonical online learning algorithms (without explicit detect-switch) can provably achieve BOBW guarantees in the heavy-tailed setting. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the log factors in the regret bounds (up to (log T)^4) remain a gap from the minimax-optimal rate.
