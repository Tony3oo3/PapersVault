## Problem

Diffusion models incur substantial computational cost during multi-step denoising inference, and existing structural pruning methods require expensive retraining on large-scale datasets to maintain generation quality. Static feature-caching strategies like DeepCache reuse only a single block's output across adjacent timesteps, failing to exploit the full redundancy in intermediate features. The paper asks: can an optimal, dynamic pruning schedule be discovered via lightweight gradient optimization without retraining the pretrained diffusion model?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242939/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained diffusion model (U-Net-based SD variants or Transformer-based DiT) and a small set of 1000 textual prompts from DiffusionDB to train the pruner. It outputs a pruned SubNet — a specific skip/keep configuration over N×T computational blocks — that, when deployed, accepts a text prompt and generates an image with reduced MACs. The pruner itself is discarded after training; only the binary gate pattern and updated input connections are retained for inference.

## Method

DiP-GO constructs a SuperNet by adding backup connections from each block's input at timestep t to the corresponding block at timestep t−1, so that any block can be skipped while preserving valid data flow. A plugin pruner network, inspired by DETR's query mechanism, uses N×T learnable queries passed through stacked self-attention (or linear) layers and a per-block prediction head (two linear layers + softmax) to output continuous importance scores for every block. Training optimizes a compound loss combining a consistency term (negative SSIM between pruned-SubNet output and full-inference output) and a sparsity term (FLOPs-weighted sum of kept-block gates, applied only below a threshold τ), with the Straight-Through Estimator bridging the discrete gate decision. After ~1000 gradient steps on a single GPU (using gradient checkpointing and half-precision to avoid OOM), a bisection post-processing algorithm selects a score threshold that meets the target pruning ratio, cascading removals to dependent blocks to produce the final pruned model.

## Results

On SD-1.5 with 80% structural pruning, DiP-GO achieves 3.38T MACs (4.43× speedup) with a CLIP Score of 29.51 on PartiPrompts and 30.29 on COCO2017, matching the unpruned PLMS-50 baseline (29.51 / 30.30) while reducing computation by over 80%. At 85% pruning, the method reaches 5.52× speedup (2.54T MACs) with only a 0.44-point CLIP drop on PartiPrompts. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), DiP-GO achieves 2.43× speedup at 60% pruning with FID 3.01 vs. 2.97 for the unpruned baseline, outperforming DeepCache (1.76×) at comparable MACs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCache (the closest prior method), DiP-GO nearly halves MACs (3.38T vs. 6.52T) while matching or slightly exceeding CLIP Score (30.29 vs. 30.23 on COCO2017), yielding a 4.43× vs. 2.15× speedup. Against BK-SDM-Base, DiP-GO achieves 3× the speedup (4.43× vs. 1.49×) with a 0.63-point higher CLIP Score on PartiPrompts (29.51 vs. 28.88), demonstrating that learned pruning outperforms hand-designed architectural compression. Against GA-based search (Table 6), DiP-GO matches MACs at 80% pruning (3.38T vs. 3.34T) but improves CLIP Score by 0.92 points (30.29 vs. 29.37) while requiring only 2.3 GPU hours versus 25 for the search-based approach. On DiT-XL/2, DiP-GO (2.43× speedup, FID 3.01) outperforms DeepCache (1.76×, FID 3.07) at similar MACs, confirming the advantage of learned over static pruning strategies on transformer-based diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-reuse paradigm introduced by DeepCache, which caches U-Net block outputs across adjacent timesteps using a fixed static strategy, by replacing that static rule with a learned, differentiable pruner that decides per-block whether to compute or skip. The pruner architecture borrows the learnable-query design from DETR, and the overall framing of pruning as a SubNet search within a SuperNet draws on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis. Unlike prior structural pruning methods (BK-SDM, KOALA, Diff-pruning, LAPTOP-Diff) that require retraining the full diffusion model on large datasets, DiP-GO trains only a small plugin network via few-step gradient optimization with a Straight-Through Estimator, leaving the pretrained diffusion weights untouched.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.4× speedup on SD-1.5 with essentially unchanged CLIP scores, achieved without retraining the diffusion model and compatible with fast samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, LCM), makes this a practical tool for deploying diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware. The method generalizes across U-Net and Transformer (DiT) backbones, suggesting broad applicability. However, the paper acknowledges that extremely high pruning ratios degrade quality, and the pruner still requires ~2.5 hours of GPU training per target model, which limits its 'once-and-done' appeal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video diffusion models, larger DiT variants, or multi-modal generation settings where feature redundancy across timesteps may differ.
