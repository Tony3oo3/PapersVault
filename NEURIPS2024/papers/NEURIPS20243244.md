## Problem

Generating structured multi-track accompaniments from a lead sheet requires maintaining track cohesion, long-term phrase-level coherence, and computational efficiency simultaneously. Existing approaches either rely on manually specified style templates that are impractical for whole-song generation, or use sequential note-level tokenization that scales poorly and struggles with structural alignment. This paper addresses the gap by learning a conditional prior over disentangled style factors, enabling flexible, controllable, and efficient long-term multi-track arrangement.

## Input / Output

The system operates in two cascaded stages: Stage 1 takes a lead sheet (melody + chord progression) and produces a piano accompaniment score; Stage 2 takes the resulting piano reduction (a single-track 128-pitch × 32-time-step matrix per 2-bar segment, approximated by downmixing all tracks with track-wise maximum for concurrent notes) and outputs a multi-track orchestral arrangement with a user-specified number of tracks (K) and instrument choices from 34 classes. The orchestral function per track is a 1×32 normalized onset-density time-series, and the latent style codes are 8 discrete VQ indices per segment-track at 1-beat resolution. The system supports 4/4 time signature at 1/4-beat resolution and handles pieces longer than the 32-bar context via windowed sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is a style prior model that infers orchestral function codes s_{1:8T}^{1:K} conditioned on piano content c_{1:T}, using a multi-stream Transformer with orthogonally interleaved time-wise (auto-regressive decoder) and track-wise (Transformer encoder) layers. This layer interleaving simultaneously models temporal dependencies (next-beat code prediction) and inter-track dependencies (cross-track attention) while reducing computational complexity from O(N²T²) to O(max(N,T)²) and supporting a flexible, variable number of tracks. The autoencoder that produces the disentangled representations uses a VQ-VAE (1-D conv + codebook of size 128) for orchestral function and a VAE with a Transformer-based Track Separator for recovering individual track representations from shared content plus style cues. Training is fully self-supervised: both the piano reduction and orchestral function are deterministic transforms of the same multi-track piece, and a Gaussian noise weight γ on the content input during training enables a controllable faithfulness-creativity tradeoff at inference. The two-stage cascade (piano texture prior → orchestral function prior) mirrors a musician's workflow and provides interpretable control at both the piano texture and orchestral function composition hierarchies.

## Results

On the lead sheet to multi-track arrangement task (100 pieces from Nottingham and WikiMT), the proposed system achieves 0.567 chord accuracy, 0.300 degree of arrangement (DOA), and 1.520 structure awareness (ILS), outperforming all baselines on these three quality metrics. Inference latency is 0.461 s/bar, comparable to the diffusion-based GETMusic (0.450 s/bar) and roughly 10× faster than the autoregressive AMT (6.320 s/bar). Subjective evaluation (23 participants, 5-point Likert scale) confirms the method's superiority across all five criteria: harmony/texture coherency, long-term structure, naturalness, creativity, and overall musicality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed system outperforms all three baselines on chord accuracy, DOA, and structure awareness. Against PopMAG (Transformer-XL, note-level tokenization), it gains +0.097 chord accuracy, +0.030 DOA, and +0.434 structure awareness, while also being 28% faster (0.461 vs. 0.638 s/bar). Against AMT (decoder-only Transformer with anticipation), the gains are +0.121 chord accuracy, +0.006 DOA, and +0.426 structure awareness, with a 13.7× latency reduction (0.461 vs. 6.320 s/bar). Against GETMusic (diffusion-based, 5 fixed tracks), the method leads by +0.144 chord accuracy, +0.075 DOA, and +0.277 structure awareness, though GETMusic is marginally faster (0.450 vs. 0.461 s/bar). In the Stage 1 ablation (Table 4–5), the piano texture prior also outperforms Whole-Song-Gen [42] on both chord accuracy (0.540 vs. 0.430) and structure awareness (1.983 vs. 1.153) for piano arrangement, and this advantage carries through to the final multi-track output (0.567 vs. 0.509 chord accuracy; 1.520 vs. 1.121 structure). All differences are statistically significant (p < 0.05 with Bonferroni correction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior line of work on interpretable music representation learning (Wang et al. 2020 on chord/texture VAEs, Zhao & Xia 2021 on AccoMontage piano texture priors, Zhao et al. 2023 on query-based multitrack rearrangement) into a unified two-stage arrangement system. The key conceptual departure from prior style-conditioned generation work (Figaro, Whole-Song-Gen) is replacing fixed, manually-specified style templates with a learned style prior p(style | content), making the process self-supervised and flexible. Architecturally, the interleaved multi-stream Transformer (alternating track-wise and time-wise layers) departs from both sequential tokenization approaches (PopMAG, AMT) and parallel/delayed multi-stream designs (MusicGen, text-free speech LM), enabling tractable generalization to variable track counts and instrument choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The style prior modelling framework offers a general recipe for structured long-sequence generation: disentangle content from style, learn a conditional prior over style, then decode — a pattern potentially transferable beyond music to other multi-stream sequence tasks. The practical system provides interpretable control at two composition hierarchies (piano texture, orchestral function), which is valuable for human-AI co-creation workflows. However, the system is limited to 4/4 tonal music without drums or performance-level MIDI (velocity, dynamics), and the cultural bias toward Western pop/folk/jazz in training data constrains generality. Open questions include extending to triple meters, percussive tracks, expressive performance modelling, and non-Western musical traditions.
