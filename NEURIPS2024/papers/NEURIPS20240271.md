## Problem

Adaptive learning rates for FTRL have been extensively developed for online learning problems with Θ(√T) minimax regret, but very few exist for the harder class of problems with Θ(T^{2/3}) regret, which include important settings with indirect feedback such as partial monitoring, graph bandits, and multi-armed bandits with paid observations. This paper addresses the gap by establishing a unified adaptive learning rate framework specifically designed for these hard problems, enabling Best-of-Both-Worlds guarantees that were previously unavailable or required extremely complicated learning rate schedules.

## Input / Output

At each round, the learner receives a loss vector ℓ_t: A→[0,1] (unknown in advance), selects an action A_t from a finite set [k] according to a probability vector p_t, and observes a feedback symbol o_t from an observation set O (which may be indirect, e.g., a loss difference rather than the loss itself). The algorithm outputs an action selection probability p_t = (1−γ_t)q_t + γ_t p_0, where q_t is the FTRL solution and γ_t is an adaptive exploration rate. The theoretical output is a BOBW regret bound: O((c_G T)^{2/3}(log k)^{1/3}) in the adversarial regime and O(c_G² log k / Δ_min² · log(T Δ_min³)) in the stochastic regime, with the first MS-type bound under a self-bounding constraint.

## Method

The core contribution is the Stability-Penalty-Bias (SPB) matching learning rate, which simultaneously matches three terms that appear in the regret decomposition for hard problems: the stability term (z_t/(β_t γ_t)), the penalty term ((β_t − β_{t−1})h_t), and the bias term (γ_t) arising from forced exploration. The learning rate β_t is updated via a simple recursive rule (Rule 2: β_t = β_{t−1} + (1/ĥ_t)(2√(z_{t−1}/β_{t−1}) + u_{t−1}/β_{t−1})), and the exploration rate is set to γ_t = √(z_t/β_t) + u_t/β_t, where z_t and u_t are problem-specific stability and bias components. The FTRL update uses a Tsallis entropy regularizer H_α with exponent α = 1 − 1/(log k), which is shown to be optimal for these settings. This yields a regret bound of order (Σ√(z_t h_t log T))^{2/3}, which is the natural analogue of the (Σ z_t h_t log T)^{1/2} bound for Θ(√T) problems, and the resulting learning rate is dramatically simpler than existing BOBW learning rates for hard problems.

## Results

The paper establishes a unified SPB-matching learning rate framework for FTRL that achieves Best-of-Both-Worlds (BOBW) regret bounds for hard online learning problems with minimax regret Θ(T^{2/3}). For globally observable partial monitoring, the algorithm achieves O((c_G T)^{2/3}(log k)^{1/3}) in the adversarial regime and O(c_G² log k log T / Δ_min²) in the stochastic regime. For weakly observable graph bandits, it achieves O(δ*^{1/3} T^{2/3}(log k)^{1/3}) adversarially and O(δ* log k log T / Δ_min²) stochastically. For multi-armed bandits with paid observations, it achieves O((ck log k)^{1/3} T^{2/3} + √(T log k)) adversarially and O(max{c,1} k log k log T / Δ_min²) stochastically, with all bounds also extending to the adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint (MS-type bounds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For partial monitoring, the SPB-matching algorithm improves upon the prior FTRL-based BOBW bounds of Tsuchiya et al. [54] and [56] in the stochastic regime by removing a log T factor (vs. [54]) or a factor of k (vs. [56]), achieving O(c_G² log k log T / Δ_min²) instead of O(c_G² log T log(kT) / Δ_min²) or O(c_G² k log T / Δ_min²). The adversarial bound matches the optimal O((c_G T)^{2/3}(log k)^{1/3}) of [37] and [15], but is obtained via a direct FTRL method rather than the multi-stage reduction approach of [15], which discards past observations. For graph bandits, the method improves the stochastic bound of Ito et al. [25] from O(δ log T log(kT) / Δ_min²) to O(δ* log k log T / Δ_min²), though it depends on the fractional domination number δ* rather than the (potentially smaller) weak domination number δ. The adversarial bound matches [15] in order but uses a simpler direct FTRL framework. For MAB with paid observations, the adversarial bound matches Seldin et al. [53] exactly, while the stochastic bound is new. Across all three problems, the SPB-matching algorithm provides the first MS-type (self-bounding constraint) bounds, which are strictly more general than pure stochastic or adversarial guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability-penalty matching learning rate framework of Ito et al. [26], which was designed for online learning problems with Θ(√T) minimax regret, to the harder class of problems with Θ(T^{2/3}) regret by incorporating a third 'bias' term arising from forced exploration. The resulting SPB-matching principle is applied within the FTRL framework using Tsallis entropy regularization, building on the Tsallis-INF algorithm of Zimmert and Seldin [61] and the forced-exploration techniques of Alon et al. [4]. The key departure from prior work is that existing adaptive learning rates for hard problems (e.g., [25, 54]) are extremely complicated, whereas SPB-matching follows a simple matching principle across three terms, yielding the first unified FTRL-based BOBW framework for Θ(T^{2/3}) problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first MS-type (self-bounding) regret bounds for hard online learning problems and improves existing BOBW stochastic-regime bounds by removing log T or k factors in partial monitoring and graph bandits. A notable limitation is that the graph bandits bound depends on the fractional domination number δ* rather than the (potentially smaller) weak domination number δ, a consequence of using Tsallis entropy instead of Shannon entropy. Open questions include whether the SPB-matching simplicity extends to other hard problems such as bandits with switching costs and dueling bandits, and whether the stochastic-regime bounds are optimal with respect to problem-specific parameters beyond T.
