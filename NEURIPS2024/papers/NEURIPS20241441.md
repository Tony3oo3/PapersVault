## Problem

Learning halfspaces with a margin under Massart noise (data-dependent label noise bounded by η) has historically required worse sample complexity than under the milder Random Classification Noise (RCN) model: prior proper learners needed Õ(γ⁻⁴ε⁻³) samples versus Õ(γ⁻²ε⁻²) for RCN. This paper resolves the open question of whether Massart noise is fundamentally harder than RCN for halfspace learning, showing it is not by matching the RCN sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled examples (x, y) with x in the unit ball B^d and y ∈ {±1}, drawn from a distribution satisfying the η-Massart halfspace model with margin γ (i.e., Pr[|w*·x| < γ] = 0 and per-point noise η(x) ≤ η). It outputs a proper halfspace hypothesis w ∈ B^d achieving zero-one loss at most η + ε. The algorithm is a single-pass stochastic procedure: it processes a training set of size T₁ in N independent restarts, then selects the best iterate on a held-out validation set of size T₂.

## Method

The Perspectron algorithm iterates a perceptron-like update w ← w − λ(β·sign(w·x) − y)·x/(|w·x| + γ), where β = 1−2η, using an inverse-margin reweighting (|w·x| + γ)⁻¹ that acts as a soft substitute for the hard conditional sampling used in prior certificate-based approaches. The key structural result (Lemma 2) shows that when the current hypothesis is sub-optimal (ℓ₀₋₁(w) ≥ η + ε), the expectation of this reweighted gradient forms a separating hyperplane between w and the target w*, with the +γ term ensuring the estimator is bounded in norm by γ⁻¹. This boundedness enables concentration with Õ(γ⁻²ε⁻²) samples, and plugging the certificate into a perceptron-style regret minimization (rather than cutting-plane methods) yields the improved γ dependence. For Massart GLMs with a known link function σ, the reweighting is rescaled to (|w·x| + αγ) with α = ε/(2−ε), giving sample complexity Õ(γ⁻²ε⁻⁴).

## Results

The Perspectron algorithm (Theorem 1) achieves zero-one loss at most η + ε for γ-margin Massart halfspaces using Õ(γ⁻²ε⁻²) samples and Õ(dγ⁻²ε⁻⁴) time, matching the state-of-the-art sample complexity for the milder Random Classification Noise (RCN) model. For the more challenging Massart GLM setting (Theorem 2), the algorithm achieves error opt_RCN + ε with Õ(γ⁻²ε⁻⁴) samples and Õ(dγ⁻²ε⁻⁶) time. These are the first proper Massart halfspace learners to match the RCN rate in both ε and γ dependencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Perspectron strictly improves upon all prior Massart halfspace learners. Compared to [CKMY20] (the prior best proper Massart learner), it reduces the sample complexity from γ⁻⁴ε⁻³ to γ⁻²ε⁻², improving the dependence on both margin (γ⁻⁴ → γ⁻²) and accuracy (ε⁻³ → ε⁻²). Compared to the improper learner of [DGT19b] (γ⁻³ε⁻⁵), it improves both exponents while also being proper. The key theoretical contribution is matching the RCN sample complexity of [DDK⁺23, KIT⁺23] (γ⁻²ε⁻²) under the strictly harder Massart noise model, resolving the open question of whether Massart is no harder than random for halfspace learning. For Massart GLMs, the paper improves upon [CKMY20]'s unspecified polynomial (at least Õ(L⁴γ⁻⁴ε⁻⁶) in the halfspace special case) to Õ(L²γ⁻²ε⁻⁴) when parameterized with their margin and Lipschitz assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the certificate framework for learning with semi-random noise from Diakonikolas et al. (2020) and Chen et al. (2020), which uses separating hyperplanes to certify sub-optimality of a hypothesis, and on the Leaky-ReLU convex surrogate for zero-one loss from Diakonikolas et al. (2019). The key departure is replacing the hard conditional sampling (rejection sampling from a band around the current hypothesis) used by prior Massart learners with a soft inverse-margin reweighting scheme, and replacing expensive cutting-plane outer loops with a simple perceptron-like regret minimization update. This yields the Perspectron algorithm, whose single-sample update rule is dramatically simpler than prior approaches while achieving the same sample complexity as the best RCN learners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a longstanding open question (dating to Cohen 1997 and Blum 2003) by showing that Massart noise is algorithmically no harder than random classification noise for proper learning of margin halfspaces, matching the γ^{-2}ε^{-2} sample complexity of the best RCN results. The extension to Massart GLMs with a known link function further improves upon the prior state-of-the-art of Chen et al. (2020). Open questions include achieving margin-independent sample complexity (≈dε^{-2}), handling unknown link functions, and extending to the misspecified GLM setting where the noise bound includes an additional ζ term that breaks the separation oracle argument.
