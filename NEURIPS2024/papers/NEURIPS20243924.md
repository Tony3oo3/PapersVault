## Problem

Previous AI Diplomacy agents, most notably Cicero, rely heavily on large-scale human demonstration data for training, limiting their generalization and making continual evolution difficult. While LLM-based agents show promise in communication and planning, they struggle with the extended planning horizons, partial observations, and complex social dynamics (trust, deception, alliance formation) that Diplomacy demands. This paper asks whether a single self-evolving LLM-based agent can master both negotiation and strategic planning in Diplomacy without any task-specific human data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243924/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn, the agent consumes the full board state (territory ownership, unit positions for all seven powers), the previous turn's actions of all other players, and the current turn's negotiation messages, alongside a retrieved set of m most-similar historical experiences from its memory (selected by joint goal-similarity and state-similarity scoring). Outputs are structured as: inferred social beliefs (per-player relationship labels and intention estimates), a refined sub-goal, natural-language negotiation messages, a discrete army command (move/support/hold per unit), and a memory entry containing the turn's state, messages, actions, sub-goal, and a continuously updated evaluation score λ_t that is revised as future states unfold until the sub-goal is fundamentally superseded.

## Method

Richelieu is a modular LLM-agent pipeline with five stages executed each turn: (1) Social Reasoning models inter-player relationships and infers opponents' intentions from the board state; (2) a Strategic Planner proposes sub-goals aligned with a fixed long-term goal, then a Reflection step retrieves the most analogous past experiences from memory (via goal+state similarity) and critiques/refines the sub-goal based on their historical evaluation scores; (3) a Negotiator runs a structured social-reasoning flow that assesses each opponent's truthfulness ψ by checking consistency between their inferred sub-goal and stated intentions, retrieving corroborating memory, and weighting by a running credibility score γ, before generating negotiation text; (4) an Actor selects army commands conditioned on the refined sub-goal and updated social state; (5) a Memory module archives the turn and continuously updates sub-goal evaluations and player credibility. The key differentiator is a self-play training loop in which the same agent controls all seven countries simultaneously, generating diverse game experiences without any human annotation; as the memory pool grows, reflection quality improves and performance plateaus, demonstrating self-evolution. The framework is LLM-agnostic, validated across GPT-4, Llama 3, ERNIE Bot, and Spark Desk.

## Results

In the press Diplomacy setting against the SOTA Cicero, Richelieu achieves a 6.70% win rate (vs. 6.10%), 8.30% Most SC rate (vs. 7.03%), and a 44.23% defeated rate (vs. 44.98%), corresponding to roughly a 10% higher C-Diplo Argir score. Against the off-the-shelf LLM agent AutoGPT, Richelieu dominates with a 9.10% win rate versus 1.63%, an 18.33% Most SC rate versus 4.10%, and a 34.30% defeated rate versus 61.37%. In the no-press massively-play setting, Richelieu outperforms all six baselines (SL-DipNet, RL-DipNet, BRPI, SearchBot, DORA, and Cicero) in relative C-Diplo Argir scores, with its score approximately 10% higher than Cicero's.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cicero (the SOTA press-Diplomacy agent trained on large-scale human data), Richelieu wins on three of four metrics: win rate (+0.6 pp), Most SC rate (+1.27 pp), and defeated rate (−0.75 pp), while Cicero retains a slight edge on survived rate (+1.13 pp); overall Richelieu's C-Diplo Argir score is ~10% higher. In the no-press massively-play setting (Figure 4), Richelieu outperforms all five no-press baselines—SL-DipNet, RL-DipNet, BRPI, SearchBot, and DORA—as well as Cicero, with the largest margins over the older RL-based methods. Against AutoGPT (a generic LLM reasoning framework), Richelieu achieves a 9.10% win rate versus AutoGPT's 1.63% and a 34.30% defeated rate versus 61.37%, demonstrating that the self-evolving framework with social reasoning, reflection, and negotiation is far more effective than an off-the-shelf LLM agent. Notably, Richelieu achieves these results without any human demonstration data, whereas Cicero and the no-press baselines all rely on over 10,000 human game records for training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in AI Diplomacy, where prior methods (Cicero, DipNet, BRPI, SearchBot, DORA) rely heavily on large-scale human game data for training, by proposing a unified LLM-based agent that self-evolves through multi-agent self-play without any task-specific human demonstrations. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from Reflexion (episodic memory and linguistic self-reflection), memory-augmented LLM agents, and RL self-play into a single architecture that simultaneously handles social reasoning, strategic planning, and natural-language negotiation. Unlike Cicero's separately trained language model and RL planner, Richelieu integrates both capabilities within one LLM-based pipeline, enabling continual evolution through accumulated self-play experience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a data-free, self-evolving LLM agent can match or modestly exceed a system (Cicero) trained on extensive human demonstrations, suggesting a viable path toward AI agents that improve through experience rather than curated supervision. The framework's generalization across multiple LLMs (GPT-4, Llama 3, ERNIE Bot, Spark Desk) hints at broader applicability to other multi-agent social interaction tasks. However, key limitations remain: the Diplomacy game provides fully public information (unlike real diplomacy), the action space is constrained, and the performance margins over Cicero are modest (~0.7% win rate), leaving open whether the self-evolution advantage scales to more complex, partially-observable multi-agent settings.
