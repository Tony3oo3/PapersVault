## Problem

Existing stereo-based 3D object detectors are too computationally heavy to meet real-time streaming perception requirements, where inference latency causes predictions to misalign with the rapidly changing scene. No 3D detection framework had been designed for streaming perception, which jointly evaluates accuracy and latency via streaming average precision (sAP), forcing the model to predict the next frame's state from the current frame without access to future inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243666/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four consecutive stereo images (left and right views at frames t-1 and t) and a buffer of historical BEV features, then directly predicts 3D bounding boxes (center position, dimensions, and yaw angle) for frame t+1. A key constraint is that the input cannot include any information from time step t+1, since in real-world streaming the future frame is unavailable at inference time. Training samples are organized as shuffled 4-frame sequences to enable random batching, and the KITTI Tracking dataset operates at 10 Hz, meaning spatial displacements between adjacent frames are relatively large.

## Method

StreamDSGN builds on the DSGN++ stereo detector (optimized to 81 ms via ResNet18, single geometric volume, low-resolution vertical axis, edge cropping, and mixed precision) and introduces three strategies to handle streaming-specific challenges. Feature-Flow Fusion (FFF) computes a discrete feature flow between current and previous BEV features via cosine similarity over a grid of shifts, then back-warps the current feature to synthesize a pseudo-next feature aligned with the next-frame ground truth. Motion Consistency Loss (MCL) adds explicit velocity and acceleration regression terms that supervise the predicted next-frame offset against the historical ground-truth trajectory, making motion supervision explicit rather than implicit. A Large Kernel BEV Backbone (LKBB) based on Visual Attention Network replaces the original hourglass structure, providing a larger receptive field to capture long-range spatial context from fused features while maintaining similar parameter count and reducing FLOPs by ~6G. Temporal fusion is performed before the BEV backbone rather than before the detection head, since shallow head layers lack sufficient receptive field to exploit large-span contextual information from the fused features.

## Results

On the KITTI Tracking dataset (Car category, IoU=0.7), StreamDSGN achieves sAP_BEV of 85.40/72.47/68.66 (Easy/Mod/Hard) and sAP_3D of 77.47/63.76/57.42 (Easy/Mod/Hard) at 91.45 ms latency. This represents a 4.33% improvement in sAP_3D at the Hard level over the best baseline (StreamYOLO† extended to DSGN++_opt), and a ~5% gain across all metrics. The method also outperforms the non-end-to-end Streamer meta-detector by a large margin (e.g., 57.42 vs. 14.75 sAP_3D Hard for Car), demonstrating the advantage of direct next-frame prediction over Kalman-filter-based forecasting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of baselines: the non-end-to-end Streamer meta-detector (which uses a Kalman filter to forecast future states from historical detections) and the end-to-end StreamYOLO† framework (which directly predicts the next frame). Streamer with the unoptimized DSGN++ (263 ms latency) performs catastrophically, achieving only 1.51 sAP_3D Hard for Car, because the high latency causes it to miss most intermediate frames. Streamer with the optimized DSGN++_opt (81 ms) improves to 14.75 sAP_3D Hard but still lags far behind end-to-end methods, confirming that Kalman-filter-based interpolation cannot compensate for the misalignment between predictions and ground truth in dynamic scenes. StreamYOLO† (81 ms) is the strongest baseline, reaching 51.86 sAP_3D Hard for Car. StreamDSGN (91 ms) outperforms StreamYOLO† by approximately 5.56 points in sAP_3D Hard and by 4.33 points over the ablation baseline (setting c), with consistent gains across all three difficulty levels and all three object categories (Car, Pedestrian, Cyclist). The ~10 ms additional latency of StreamDSGN over StreamYOLO† is attributed to the Feature-Flow Fusion module (7.67 ms) and the Large Kernel BEV Backbone, yet remains within the 100 ms real-time budget for the 10 Hz KITTI Tracking frame rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the streaming perception paradigm (originally formulated for 2D detection by StreamYOLO and LongShortNet) to the 3D object detection domain, using DSGN++ as the stereo-based base detector. It departs from prior temporal 3D detection methods (BEVFormer, PETRv2) by explicitly addressing the feature–ground-truth misalignment inherent in streaming settings, where predictions must be evaluated against the *next* frame's labels. The Feature-Flow Fusion module repurposes optical-flow-style similarity matching (parameter-free) to warp current BEV features into a pseudo-next representation, replacing the simple Dual-Flow concatenation used in StreamYOLO, while the Large Kernel BEV Backbone borrows the Visual Attention Network's large-kernel attention to expand receptive field without increasing parameter count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that stereo-based 3D object detection can operate under streaming perception constraints, opening a path toward low-cost (camera-only) real-time 3D perception for autonomous driving. The streaming average precision metric forces a joint accuracy–latency trade-off that is increasingly relevant as deployment constraints tighten. Key limitations remain: the approach is validated only on KITTI Tracking with stereo input, FFF degrades under occlusion/truncation, and the authors acknowledge that extending to multi-view camera BEV representations (the dominant paradigm in current autonomous driving) is an open problem. The parameter-free flow computation and the motion-consistency loss are lightweight ideas that could transfer to other streaming 3D tasks.
