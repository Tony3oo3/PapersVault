## Problem

Medical image grading is inherently difficult because disease progression is continuous, causing within-level variability and cross-level similarity in image appearance. In practical clinical deployment, grading models must generalize to unseen target domains (different scanners, magnifications, or clinical centers) where feature distributions shift dramatically, causing images from the same domain to cluster rather than images from the same severity level. Existing grading methods assume i.i.d. training and inference data, and prior domain generalization work has not specifically addressed the severity-level identification challenge under cross-domain variance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (X-ray, H&E-stained pathology slide, or retinal photograph) and partitions it into 4×4 pixel patches via a convolutional stem, producing a sequence of 2-D patch embeddings of spatial size H/4 × W/4. The output is an ordinal severity grade (e.g., levels 1–5 for fatigue fracture and diabetic retinopathy, levels 1–3 for breast cancer), predicted by an average-pooling and linear grading head. The patch sequence is traversed along both horizontal and vertical scanning paths in forward and backward directions, so the model consumes the image as a 2-D sequence rather than a flat token list.

## Method

Samba encodes image patches in a recurrent manner using a bidirectional Mamba (selective state space model) that stores severity information in hidden states and propagates it across the sequence, enabling local-to-global transportation of decisive lesion signals. To mitigate cross-domain variance, an EM-based state recalibration module models the patch feature distribution with a Gaussian Mixture Model whose K learnable severity bases (default K=64) capture lesion representations; the E-step computes per-patch responsibilities via an exponential inner-dot kernel, and the M-step updates the bases as weighted means of the patch embeddings. After T_c EM iterations, the original features are reconstructed as Z^{T_c}·μ^{T_c}, mapping them into a more compact, domain-invariant space before feeding the next Samba block. The initial severity bases μ⁰ are updated during training via moving average (momentum α) over mini-batch-averaged EM outputs to prevent collapse. The full architecture stacks four encoding stages of Samba blocks (bidirectional SSM + EM recalibration) with downsampling between stages, and a theoretical analysis provides a domain generalization risk bound linking target-domain error to source-domain error plus the HΔH distribution gap.

## Results

On the cross-domain Fatigue Fracture benchmark (Domain-1→Domain-2), Samba achieves 76.2% ACC, 81.5% AUC, and 45.8% F1, outperforming the VMamba-ERM baseline by 23.5%, 11.1%, and 17.1% respectively. On the Diabetic Retinopathy grading benchmark under the single-domain generalization protocol, Samba attains an average ACC of 69.2% and F1 of 40.9%, surpassing the best prior domain-generalized method GDRNet (46.7% ACC, 32.2% F1) by 22.5% and 8.7%. On the Breast Cancer grading benchmark, Samba improves average accuracy by 5.6% over VMamba-ERM, with the largest per-grade gain of 17.0% on level-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Samba consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the direct architectural baseline VMamba-ERM [69], Samba gains 23.5% ACC on Fatigue Fracture, 5.6% on Breast Cancer, and 4.1% on DR (69.2% vs. 65.1% average ACC). Among domain-generalization methods (Mixup, MixStyle, DDAIG, ATS, Fishr, MDLT), Samba leads by 20–30+ ACC points on average, with the largest gap on FGADR where Samba reaches 68.2% ACC versus 5–7% for all DG methods. Against task-specific DR grading methods (GREEN, CABNet, MIL-ViT, Swin-T), Samba improves average ACC by 23–25 points. The closest competitor is GDRNet [11], the only prior method explicitly designed for domain-generalized DR grading, which Samba surpasses by 22.5% ACC and 8.7% F1 on average. On the Fatigue Fracture benchmark, Samba also beats recurrent baselines (LSTM, UR-LSTM, UR-GRU) and ViT by 23.5–36.4% ACC, confirming the advantage of the selective SSM over both RNN and attention-based sequence modeling for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba selective state space model and its bidirectional vision variant (VMamba), repurposing the SSM's sequential encoding mechanism not for general long-range dependency capture but specifically for transporting severity information from small decisive lesions to global representations. It departs from prior domain generalization methods (MixStyle, DDAIG, ATS, etc.) that operate at the feature or distribution level by instead recalibrating the internal hidden states of the SSM itself via an EM-based GMM with learnable severity bases. The work also extends the limited prior investigation of domain-generalized DR grading (DRGen, GDRNet) to a broader, modality-agnostic grading framework covering fatigue fracture, breast cancer, and diabetic retinopathy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain medical image grading is a practically critical but underexplored problem, as clinical deployment requires models trained on one hospital's data to generalize to unseen scanners and imaging protocols. The EM-based state recalibration offers a principled mechanism for reducing domain shift in SSM-based architectures, and the 23.5% accuracy gain on fatigue fracture demonstrates the potential impact. However, the GMM assumption may break down under severe class imbalance, and the more modest improvements on DR (1–7% over VMamba-ERM) suggest the benefit is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric data, whether the number of severity bases K can be learned automatically, and how the method interacts with other domain generalization strategies.
