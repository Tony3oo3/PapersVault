## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods in long-tailed recognition (LTR) assume that auxiliary outlier samples used during training share a similar distribution with true OOD samples at test time. In practice, this distribution shift is severe under heavy class imbalance, where head and tail ID classes exhibit very different energy distributions from the outliers, causing tail samples to be misclassified as OOD and head samples to be indistinguishable from OOD.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT) as input, along with auxiliary outlier data from external datasets (TinyImages80M or ImageNet-Extra) during fine-tuning. At inference, each test image is processed sequentially (one sample at a time, seen only once) and produces both a calibrated global energy OOD score and a class label for ID samples. The OOD score is computed as a per-class weighted sum of exponentiated logits, where the weights are dynamically updated outlier distribution entries.

## Method

AdaptOD combines two components: a Dual-Normalized Energy Loss (DNE) for training and a Dynamic Outlier Distribution Adaptation (DODA) for inference. DNE introduces batch energy normalization that divides each sample's class logit by the sum of all samples' logits in that class within a batch, then applies both a class-wise loss (balancing total energy per class) and a sample-wise loss (balancing total energy per sample), eliminating the need for manually tuned energy margins and transferring energy from head to tail samples. DODA performs test-time adaptation by maintaining a running outlier distribution P^out initialized from training outlier data; at each inference step, a Z-score-based filter identifies predicted OOD samples, which trigger a momentum update of P^out, and the adapted distribution is used to calibrate the global energy score as G^P(x) = Σ_j exp(f_j(x))/(1+P_j^out), reducing the energy proportion of head classes that the true OOD distribution leans toward. This requires no model retraining or additional memory during inference.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT (ρ=100), AdaptOD achieves AUC 94.69, AP-in 93.89, AP-out 94.12, FPR@95 27.26%, and ID accuracy 82.27%, outperforming the strongest baseline COCL (AUC 93.28, FPR 30.88%, ACC 81.56%). On CIFAR100-LT, AdaptOD reaches AUC 81.93, FPR 67.37%, and ACC 47.91%, surpassing COCL (AUC 78.25, FPR 74.09%, ACC 46.41%). On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark, AdaptOD attains AUC 74.32, FPR 72.91%, and ACC 51.67%, again leading all competitors. The improvement is most pronounced on the near-OOD dataset CIFAR (AUC 89.05 vs. 86.63 for COCL on CIFAR10-LT), where prior methods struggle most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptOD consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on both CIFAR10-LT and CIFAR100-LT. The closest competitor is COCL [30] (AAAI 2024), which AdaptOD surpasses by +1.41 AUC and −3.62 FPR on CIFAR10-LT, and by +3.68 AUC and −6.72 FPR on CIFAR100-LT. Among energy-based methods, BERL [4] (CVPR 2023) and EnergyOE [24] (NeurIPS 2020) are the strongest baselines; AdaptOD improves over BERL by +2.13 AUC on CIFAR10-LT and +4.18 AUC on CIFAR100-LT. For TTA-based methods, DODA (the inference component of AdaptOD) beats both AUTO [49] and AdaOOD [56] when plugged into the same training backbones, e.g., with COCL training on CIFAR100-LT, DODA achieves AUC 79.81 vs. 78.85 (AUTO) and 79.07 (AdaOOD). The gap is largest on the near-OOD dataset CIFAR, where AdaptOD gains +2.42 AUC over COCL on CIFAR10-LT and +10.72 AUC on CIFAR100-LT, indicating the distribution-shift adaptation is most impactful when OOD data is semantically close to ID data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based OOD detection paradigm (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020) to the long-tailed recognition setting, building on the line of work from Outlier Exposure through EnergyOE, BERL, and COCL. Its key departure is identifying and addressing a distribution shift between the auxiliary outlier data used for training and the true OOD data at test time — a gap that prior long-tailed OOD methods implicitly assumed away. The DODA component draws on test-time adaptation ideas (AUTO, AdaOOD) but departs by calibrating only the outlier distribution via a momentum update rather than retraining the model or maintaining feature memory, while DNE introduces batch-level energy normalization to balance head/tail prediction energy without manual margin tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution shift between outlier and true OOD data is a practical concern in deployment where OOD sources are unknown, and DODA's plug-and-play nature (consistently improving four different baselines) makes it broadly applicable to existing long-tailed OOD pipelines. The elimination of sensitive energy-margin hyperparameters via DNE reduces the tuning burden for practitioners. However, the approach still requires auxiliary outlier data and online distribution updating during inference, and the paper acknowledges that optimizing the outlier distribution entirely during training (eliminating the online step) remains an open challenge. The small gap to the oracle model is encouraging but the overall improvements over strong baselines like COCL are moderate (1–3 AUC points).
