## Problem

Existing object-centric learning methods based on slot attention are fundamentally bottom-up, assuming that visual features within an object are homogeneous and can be clustered in feature space. This assumption breaks down in complex real-world scenes where objects of the same semantic category exhibit diverse appearances, leading to suboptimal object representations. The challenge is to incorporate top-down semantic information (object categories, spatial priors) into slot attention without any object-level annotations, as OCL operates in a fully unsupervised setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 into patch-level visual features. It produces K object slots (K=11 for MOVI-C, 24 for MOVI-E, 7 for COCO, 6 for VOC) and segmentation masks derived from the autoregressive decoder's internal attention maps. The slots are refined through a two-pass slot attention process where the second pass is modulated by discrete semantic codes from a learned codebook (size 128 for synthetic, 512 for real-world datasets) and spatial attention maps from the first pass.

## Method

The framework introduces a top-down pathway into slot attention with two stages. First, bootstrapping: continuous slot outputs from the initial slot attention pass are mapped to discrete codes via vector quantization against a learned codebook (using the straight-through estimator for backpropagation), capturing 'what' semantics, while the last-layer attention maps provide 'where' spatial priors. Second, self-modulation: the quantized codes are fed through an MLP to produce channel-wise modulation vectors, and centered attention maps (shifted to mean 1) produce spatial-wise modulation vectors; their outer product forms a modulation map that re-scales the value-projected visual features via Hadamard product before the slot update. Slot attention is then repeated for T=3 iterations with these modulated activations, sharing parameters and initial slots with the first pass. Training combines a visual feature reconstruction objective (using an autoregressive transformer decoder) with a vector quantization loss on the codebook, and the codebook size is auto-selected by monitoring perplexity of code usage during training.

## Results

On COCO, the method achieves FG-ARI 37.4 and mBO^i 33.3, surpassing the DINOSAUR baseline (34.1 / 31.6) and matching or exceeding diffusion-based decoders like SlotDiffusion (37.2 / 31.0) and LSD (35.0 / 30.4). On the synthetic MOVI-C and MOVI-E benchmarks, it reaches FG-ARI 58.9 and 59.7 respectively, with mBO^i of 46.8 and 39.3, outperforming DINOSAUR by 3.2 and 4.9 FG-ARI points on MOVI-C. On VOC, the method achieves mBO^i 43.9, comparable to DINOSAUR's 44.0, while FG-ARI (26.7) is slightly below DINOSAUR's 24.8 due to VOC's frequent single-object images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary DINOSAUR baseline [33], the method improves FG-ARI by 3.3 on COCO (37.4 vs 34.1), 1.9 on VOC (26.7 vs 24.8), and 3.2 on MOVI-C (58.9 vs 55.7), with mBO^i gains of 1.7 on COCO and 4.4 on MOVI-C. Compared to diffusion-based decoders, the method outperforms LSD [19] on COCO (37.4 vs 35.0 FG-ARI) and both MOVI datasets, and matches SlotDiffusion [46] on COCO FG-ARI (37.4 vs 37.2) while exceeding it on mBO^i (33.3 vs 31.0); however, SlotDiffusion retains a lead on VOC mBO^i (50.4 vs 43.9) and MOVI-E FG-ARI (60.0 vs 59.7). Against the recent SPOT [21], the method wins on COCO FG-ARI (37.4 vs 36.6) and VOC FG-ARI (26.7 vs 19.4) but trails on mBO^i across all four datasets (e.g., 33.3 vs 34.7 on COCO, 43.9 vs 48.1 on VOC). The method also significantly outperforms the unsupervised segmentation approach MaskCut [41] on COCO across all three metrics (FG-ARI 37.4 vs 31.5, mBO^i 33.0 vs 28.9, mIoU 31.2 vs 26.7), and substantially exceeds the original Slot Attention [30] (e.g., COCO FG-ARI 37.4 vs 21.4). Overall, the top-down pathway provides consistent gains over the DINOSAUR baseline and competitive or superior results against SOTA methods, with the notable exception of VOC mBO^i where SlotDiffusion and SPOT lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slot attention framework (Locatello et al., 2020) and its practical instantiation in DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., 2023) by introducing a top-down pathway that was absent from all prior OCL methods, which are fundamentally bottom-up. The key conceptual departure is using vector quantization to bootstrap discrete semantic codes from slot outputs, then feeding those codes back to modulate slot attention's inner activations—a self-modulation loop inspired by the dual top-down/bottom-up processing of the human visual system. The mechanism draws on VQ-VAE-style codebooks for the discrete representation and on top-down attention literature (Anderson et al., 2018; Shi et al., 2023) for the conceptual motivation, but applies them in a novel self-referential way within the OCL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized weakness of bottom-up OCL—the assumption of intra-object feature homogeneity that breaks down in cluttered real-world scenes—and shows that a lightweight self-modulation loop (<1% FLOPs overhead) can meaningfully improve object discovery across synthetic and real benchmarks. The codebook's ability to learn interpretable semantic concepts (e.g., 'pizza', 'zebra', 'motorcycle') without any annotations is a useful byproduct that could inform future unsupervised semantic discovery. Open questions include whether the top-down pathway composes with other recent improvements (e.g., SPOT's patch-order permutation, diffusion-based decoders), whether dynamically expanding codebooks can eliminate the size hyperparameter, and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-modal OCL settings.
