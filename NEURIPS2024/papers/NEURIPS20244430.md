## Problem

Offline MARL research is plagued by inconsistent baseline implementations (the same label 'MACQL' refers to different algorithms across papers), non-overlapping scenario selections, and inconsistent evaluation protocols, making it impossible to reliably assess whether proposed algorithms genuinely advance the state of the art. This paper demonstrates that these methodological gaps create a 'mirage of progress': simple, well-implemented baselines match or surpass purported SOTA on 35 of 47 datasets from the literature.

## Input / Output

The baselines are trained exclusively on offline trajectory datasets sourced from prior work (SMACv1, MAMuJoCo, MPE) using only decentralised observations, with no online environment interaction during training. Evaluation produces mean episode return over 32 episodes repeated across 10 random seeds, with statistical significance assessed via heteroscedastic two-sided t-tests at 95% confidence. All prior-work datasets are converted to the OG-MARL/Vault API format for standardised, reproducible access.

## Method

The paper contributes four deliberately simple baselines—behaviour cloning (BC) and independent Q-learners with CQL regularisation (IQL+CQL) for discrete action spaces, and independent DDPG with BC regularisation (IDDPG+BC) and MADDPG with CQL (MADDPG+CQL) for continuous action spaces—none of which rely on value factorisation or global state information. A standardised evaluation protocol is proposed: fixed training budgets per environment (50k/100k/200k updates), 10 seeds, 32-episode evaluation, no per-scenario hyperparameter tuning, and mandatory statistical significance testing. The paper also converts all accessible prior-work datasets to the OG-MARL format and provides a meta-review showing that many published SOTA claims lack statistical significance when overlapping error bounds are properly accounted for.

## Results

Across 47 datasets spanning SMACv1, MAMuJoCo, and MPE, the paper's simple baselines (BC, IQL+CQL, IDDPG+BC, MADDPG+CQL) match or significantly surpass the reported SOTA on 35 out of 47 datasets (~75%). Headline examples include IQL+CQL achieving 12.36±1.09 episode return on SMAC 5m_vs_6m (good quality) versus OMIGA's 8.25±0.37, and IDDPG+BC reaching 719.03±37.31 on MAMuJoCo 2x3 HalfCheetah (medium-replay) versus CFCQL's 59.50±8.20. Statistical significance is confirmed via heteroscedastic two-sided t-tests at 95% confidence, with 10 seeds per run and 32-episode evaluation rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAICQ (Yang et al., 2021), the baselines dominate on most scenarios: IQL+CQL achieves 12.36 vs. MAICQ's 7.87 on 5m_vs_6m (good), and IDDPG+BC reaches 120.02 vs. MAICQ's 110.60 on MAMuJoCo 2x3 HalfCheetah (expert). Against OMAR (Pan et al., 2022), the gap is even more dramatic on MPE: IDDPG+BC scores 130.33 on CN-random versus OMAR's 34.40, and on MAMuJoCo 2x3 HalfCheetah medium-replay, IDDPG+BC (719.03) outperforms OMAR (57.70) by over 12×. Against CFCQL (Shao et al., 2023), IQL+CQL wins on SMAC 2s3z mixed (0.97 vs. 0.84) and 3s_vs_5z medium-replay (0.26 vs. 0.12), while CFCQL retains an edge on a few expert-quality SMAC maps. Against OMIGA (Wang et al., 2023), IQL+CQL substantially outperforms on 5m_vs_6m (12.36 vs. 8.25, good) and corridor (19.06 vs. 15.88, good), though OMIGA is competitive on 2c_vs_64zg medium. Against MABCQ (Wang & Zhan, 2023), the baselines are statistically equivalent on 2c_vs_64zg good (19.52 vs. 19.13) but clearly superior on 5m_vs_6m (12.36 vs. 7.76). Against MADT/MADTKD (Meng et al., 2023), IQL+CQL matches on 2s3z expert (0.99 vs. 0.99) and decisively wins on 3s_vs_5z medium-replay (0.26 vs. 0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a meta-analytic benchmarking study rather than a new algorithmic contribution. It builds on well-established single-agent offline RL components—CQL (Kumar et al., 2020), DDPG (Lillicrap et al., 2016), and the minimalist BC-regularised DDPG of Fujimoto and Gu (2021)—and combines them with standard multi-agent scaffolding (MADDPG, independent Q-learners) to form deliberately simple baselines. Conceptually, it departs from the trajectory of prior offline MARL work (MAICQ, OMAR, CFCQL, OMIGA, MADT), which has introduced increasingly complex mechanisms (value factorisation, counterfactual reasoning, implicit global-to-local regularisation), by arguing that these complexities have not been validated against properly implemented and fairly evaluated baselines. The evaluation protocol draws on standards established for online MARL (Gorsane et al., 2022; Agarwal et al., 2022) and the OG-MARL dataset framework (Formanek et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that straightforward baselines match or exceed reported SOTA on roughly 75% of datasets challenges the field's narrative of steady algorithmic progress and suggests that much of the perceived improvement may be an artefact of inconsistent baselines, cherry-picked training budgets, and insufficient statistical reporting. The proposed evaluation guidelines and released baseline implementations provide a concrete, adoptable protocol that could substantially improve reproducibility and comparability going forward. Open questions include whether the result generalises beyond the five papers and three environments studied, whether the computational-budget sensitivity (Figure 3) will persist as algorithms mature, and whether truly novel algorithmic ideas can be validated once the field converges on fair baselines. A key limitation is that comparisons rely on values extracted from published tables rather than re-running original code, and several datasets were inaccessible.
