## Problem

Existing unsupervised cross-domain retrieval (UCDR) methods assume that query and retrieval domains share identical category spaces, an assumption that is unrealistic in practice since obtaining the full category composition of an unlabeled domain requires dedicated analysis. This paper introduces Universal UCDR (U²CDR), which handles close-set, partial, and open-set scenarios where category spaces differ, and proves that standard instance discrimination produces geometrically distinct feature spaces across domains with different label cardinalities (Theorem 3.1).

## Input / Output

The method takes two unlabeled RGB image domains (query and retrieval) with potentially different numbers of categories, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet via MoCo v2. For shared categories, it outputs the top-k nearest-neighbor retrieval results from the retrieval domain; for private query categories absent from the retrieval domain (open-set), it returns a null result. Performance is measured by mAP@All on shared labels and a separate detection accuracy for correctly identifying private query labels, with the latter determined by comparing inter-domain distances against a threshold derived from merged prototype clusters.

## Method

UEM is a two-stage semantic feature learning framework. In Stage 1 (IDSE), an instance-prototype-mixed (IPM) contrastive loss combines MoCo-style instance discrimination with a prototype contrastive loss built on K-Means cluster centers, while a novel Semantic-Enhanced Loss (SEL) softens hard cluster assignments to accelerate structure building; critically, prototypes from both domains are translated and merged via the Hungarian algorithm to construct a unified cross-domain prototypical structure that counteracts the geometry distinctness caused by differing label spaces. In Stage 2 (CDSM), Semantic-Preserving Domain Alignment (SPDA) performs adversarial domain alignment augmented with a pairwise cosine-similarity and Euclidean-distance preservation loss that constrains the feature extractor to maintain the prototypical structure learned in Stage 1, and Switchable Nearest Neighboring Match (SN²M) determines cross-domain matching reliability by checking whether a query instance and its nearest cross-domain neighbor share the same merged prototype, using instance-level positives only when prototypes agree and falling back to a prototype-level contrastive loss with cross-domain instance negatives otherwise.

## Results

On Close-set UCDR, UEM achieves 81.9% mAP@All on Office-31 (vs. 79.3% for the best baseline DGDIR) and 34.4% on DomainNet (vs. 30.8% for DGDIR), representing improvements of 2.6% and 3.6% respectively. The advantage becomes dramatically larger under category-space mismatch: on Partial UCDR (Office-Home), UEM reaches 46.0% mAP@All versus 33.6% for DGDIR, a 12.4% gap. On Open-set UCDR (DomainNet), UEM achieves 34.8% shared-set mAP@All and 80.9% open-set detection accuracy, outperforming the best baselines (UCDIR/CoDA at 29.3% mAP@All and 64.1% detection accuracy) by 5.5% and 16.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UEM outperforms all seven baselines across every setting (close-set, partial, and open-set) and every dataset. In the standard close-set setting, the margin is modest but consistent: UEM beats the strongest baselines (DGDIR, UCDIR, CoDA) by 2.6% on Office-31 and 3.6% on DomainNet in average mAP@All. The gap widens substantially when category spaces differ: on Partial UCDR (Office-Home), UEM leads DGDIR by 12.4% on average, while baselines like CDS and UCDIR drop to 30.3% and 29.7% respectively, confirming Theorem 3.1's prediction that instance discrimination alone cannot handle label-space mismatch. On Open-set UCDR (DomainNet), UEM achieves 80.9% detection accuracy for private query labels versus 64.1% for UCDIR (the best baseline), a 16.8% advantage that reflects the effectiveness of the unified prototypical structure for open-set detection. Among baselines, DGDIR and UCDIR are the strongest competitors in close-set, while PCS and CoDA are competitive in partial settings; none of the baselines handle open-set detection well, with all scoring below 65% detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised cross-domain retrieval (UCDR) line of work by addressing the previously unexamined case where query and retrieval domains have distinct category spaces. Methodologically, it builds on MoCo's momentum-based contrastive learning and prototypical contrastive learning (Li et al., ICLR 2020) to construct a unified cross-domain prototypical structure, then adapts adversarial domain alignment (Long et al., NeurIPS 2018) with a semantic-preserving constraint to avoid destroying that structure. The key departure from prior UCDR methods (UCDIR, CoDA, DGDIR) is the explicit handling of label-space mismatch through prototype merging and switchable nearest-neighbor matching, rather than assuming identical category compositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of U²CDR fills a practical gap: real-world retrieval systems rarely have perfectly aligned category taxonomies across domains, yet all prior unsupervised CDR methods assume they do. The substantial performance gaps in partial and open-set settings (12–15% over best baselines) demonstrate that this assumption is not merely theoretical but causes severe degradation. Open questions include whether the two-stage training can be made end-to-end, whether the CDSM stage admits theoretical guarantees, and whether the framework generalizes beyond RGB images to time series or graph data as the authors note.
