## Problem

Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are trained almost exclusively on visual question answering, neglecting two other innate human learning mechanisms: the ability to ask questions and the ability to assess the correctness of question-answer pairs. This paper argues that equipping MLLMs with these additional capabilities—question generation and QA assessment—would foster deeper multimodal comprehension and improve overall performance, yet no prior framework systematically integrates these tasks into MLLM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243655/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 336×336 RGB image (processed by a CLIP-Large vision encoder) alongside a text instruction that takes one of three forms: a standard VQA question, a GenQA prompt requesting the model to produce a question-answer pair from the image, or an EvalQA prompt presenting a question-answer triplet for correctness judgment. Outputs are autoregressive text: a short answer for VQA, a generated question paired with its answer for GenQA, or a binary Yes/No label followed by a one-sentence explanatory feedback for EvalQA (e.g., 'No, the truck is silver').

## Method

LOVA³ builds on LLaVA-1.5 (CLIP-Large + 2-layer MLP adapter + Vicuna-7B) and introduces two supplementary training tasks mixed with standard VQA data during a single supervised fine-tuning pass. GenQA (842K samples) trains the model to generate diverse QA pairs across five data types—Generic VQA, Multi-Choice VQA, Multi-turn VQA, Referring Expression Comprehension, and Referring Expression Generation—forcing extraction of fine-grained visual cues and compositional relationships. EvalQA (64K one-positive-one-negative pairs) trains the model to judge VQA correctness and produce feedback; its training data is constructed via a pipeline that uses Fuyu-8B to generate negative answers and Llama 2 to generate one-sentence feedback, followed by manual filtering and error correction. The training objective is standard autoregressive cross-entropy over the response tokens, with all three task types (VQA, GenQA, EvalQA) jointly optimized in a single epoch using AdamW at lr 2×10⁻⁵ and batch size 128 on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

LOVA³-7B achieves consistent improvements over the LLaVA-1.5 baseline across all five generic VQA tasks: 80.3% on VQAv2 (+1.8), 63.3% on GQA (+1.3), 53.6% on VizWiz (+3.6), 68.0% on ScienceQA (+1.2), and 87.4% on POPE (+1.5). On multimodal benchmarks, LOVA³-7B gains 42.0 points on MME (1552.7 vs. 1510.7), 4.3% on LLaVA-Bench (68.3 vs. 64.0), and 4.0% on MM-Vet (35.2 vs. 31.2). The smaller LOVA³-1.5B model shows even larger relative gains, e.g., +98.2 on MME and +5.9 on MM-Vet over LLaVA-Phi-1.5. On the newly proposed EvalQABench, LOVA³-7B reaches 79.58% accuracy, outperforming the best prior MLLM (InternLM-XC at 69.58%) by 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOVA³-7B outperforms its direct baseline LLaVA-1.5 on all five generic VQA tasks, with the largest gain on VizWiz (+3.6%) and POPE (+1.5%), and also surpasses it on MME (+42.0), MMBench-En (+2.5%), and MM-Vet (+4.0%). Against other SOTA MLLMs, LOVA³-7B beats Qwen-VL on VizWiz (53.6 vs. 35.2) and POPE (87.4 vs. –), InstructBLIP-13B on GQA (63.3 vs. 49.5) and VizWiz (53.6 vs. 33.4), and BLIP-2 across all reported metrics. The smaller LOVA³-1.5B model similarly outperforms LLaVA-Phi-1.5 on VQAv2 (+2.6), GQA (+2.5), VizWiz (+3.4), and ScienceQA (+0.5), though it slightly underperforms on POPE (86.0 vs. 87.6). On the new EvalQABench, LOVA³-7B achieves 79.58% accuracy, beating InternLM-XC (69.58%), Qwen-VL-Chat (63.66%), and LLaVA-1.5 (64.92%), confirming that the assessment ability is a genuine differentiator. The consistent gains across both 7B and 1.5B model scales suggest the LOVA³ training paradigm is size-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA-1.5's instruction-tuning paradigm, extending it by adding two auxiliary training tasks—GenQA (joint question-and-answer generation) and EvalQA (correctness assessment with feedback)—to the standard VQA objective. GenQA generalizes prior Visual Question Generation work (Mostafazadeh et al., Li et al., Shah et al.) from generic VQA to multiple data formats including multi-choice, multi-turn, and grounding tasks (REC/REG), while also generating answers alongside questions. EvalQA is positioned as a genuinely new task, with the authors noting the absence of any prior benchmark for assessing VQA pair correctness. The approach is conceptually inspired by human learning mechanisms (answering, asking, self-evaluating) but implemented through straightforward data augmentation within the existing LLaVA-1.5 training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that training MLLMs to ask and assess questions—without introducing new evaluation datasets—yields consistent improvements across 10+ multimodal benchmarks, suggesting that the act of generating and evaluating QA pairs strengthens underlying visual reasoning. EvalQABench fills a practical gap by offering the first standardized benchmark for VQA correctness assessment, complete with an automated data-construction pipeline. However, limitations remain: the approach is only validated on 7B and 1.5B backbones, the EvalQA data is sourced exclusively from VQAv2 (limiting domain diversity), and the model still struggles with text-centric and mathematical VQA. Open questions include whether the framework scales to 13B+ models, whether more diverse or harder negative-answer generation (beyond Fuyu-8B/Gemini) would further improve assessment quality, and whether the gains persist when the base model is already trained on very large-scale instruction data.
