## Problem

Online Budgeted Matching (OBM) with general bids has remained an open problem in the absence of both the small-bid assumption (κ→0) and the Fractional Last Matching (FLM) assumption, which allows accepting partial bids when budgets are insufficient. Without FLM, an offline node with insufficient budget simply cannot be matched, creating a fundamental tension between spending budget now and saving it for potentially large future bids. This paper proves that no deterministic algorithm can exceed a competitive ratio of 1−κ and provides the first provable competitive algorithm for this setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a bipartite graph where U offline nodes (bidders) each hold an initial budget B_u and V online nodes (queries) arrive sequentially, each revealing bid values w_{u,t} to connected offline nodes. At each round, the algorithm must select one offline node with sufficient remaining budget (b_{u,t-1} ≥ w_{u,t}) or skip the query entirely; the bid-budget ratio κ = sup w_{u,t}/B_u ∈ [0,1] parameterizes the difficulty. For the learning-augmented variant LOBM, an ML model additionally outputs per-node discounting predictions z̃_{u,t} that are projected into a competitive solution space before use in scoring.

## Method

MetaAd is a meta-algorithm that scores each feasible offline node as s_{u,t} = w_{u,t}·φ(b_{u,t-1}/B_u) using a monotonically increasing discounting function φ:[0,1]→[0,1], then selects the node with the highest score. The key theoretical contribution is a novel dual construction (Algorithm 2) that sets α_u = 1 for any node that ever experienced budget insufficiency, combined with a primal-dual ratio condition (Eq. 3) involving the derivatives of φ̄(x)=1−φ(1−x), yielding a general competitive ratio formula parameterized by κ. By instantiating φ from the exponential class φ̄(x)=C(e^{θx}−1) or polynomial class φ̄(x)=Cx², MetaAd recovers the optimal 1−1/e ratio as κ→0 and achieves positive competitive ratios for all κ∈[0,1). The learning-augmented extension LOBM projects ML predictions into a competitive solution space D_{u,t} (Eq. 28) controlled by a slackness parameter λ∈[0,1], trading worst-case competitive ratio against average performance flexibility.

## Results

On the MovieLens dataset (U=10 offline nodes, V=100 online nodes), MetaAd achieves the best worst-case normalized reward of 0.8524 among non-ML algorithms, outperforming Greedy (0.7941) and PrimalDual (0.8429). The learning-augmented LOBM with slackness parameter λ=0.8 achieves the overall best worst-case reward of 0.8538 while also attaining the best average reward of 0.9372. Theoretically, MetaAd establishes the first non-zero competitive ratio for OBM with general bids (κ∈[0,1)) without FLM, recovering the optimal 1−1/e ratio in the small-bid limit (κ→0), and the upper bound of 1−κ is proven for any deterministic algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaAd outperforms both baselines on the MovieLens dataset: it beats Greedy (from Mehta 2013) by 5.8 percentage points in worst-case reward (0.8524 vs. 0.7941) and by 0.15 points in average reward (0.9344 vs. 0.9329). Against PrimalDual/BJN2007 (Buchbinder et al. 2007), MetaAd achieves a 0.95-point improvement in worst-case (0.8524 vs. 0.8429) and a marginal 0.04-point gain in average (0.9344 vs. 0.9340). The learning-augmented LOBM-0.8 further extends these advantages, reaching 0.8538 worst-case and 0.9372 average, surpassing both baselines by 6.0 and 0.32 points respectively in worst-case. In the FLM setting (Figure 2), MetaAd with exponential discounting maintains a competitive ratio of 1/2 as κ→1, matching the best known deterministic bound, while BJN2007's ratio degrades to zero. The pure ML baseline without competitive guarantees (0.7903 worst-case) underperforms even Greedy, highlighting the value of the competitive solution space projection in LOBM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Online Budgeted Matching (OBM) / Adwords problem, originally formulated by Mehta et al. (2007) under the small-bid assumption (κ→0) and later studied under the Fractional Last Matching (FLM) assumption by Buchbinder et al. (2007) and others. The key departure is removing both the small-bid and FLM assumptions simultaneously, which the authors identify as a long-standing open problem. The MetaAd meta-algorithm framework generalizes the classical 1-1/e algorithm (recovered as a special case when κ→0) into a family of competitive algorithms parameterized by a discounting function φ, while the learning-augmented extension (LOBM) draws on the robustness-consistency trade-off framework of Wei & Zhang (2020) to incorporate ML predictions with worst-case guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable competitive algorithm for OBM with general bids without FLM, closing a gap that has persisted since the problem was formalized. The upper bound of 1-κ (and zero at κ=1) formally quantifies how much harder the problem becomes as bids grow relative to budgets, and the meta-algorithm framework opens a concrete direction for exploring better discounting functions. The learning-augmented extension demonstrates that competitive guarantees can coexist with ML-based scoring, which is relevant to the broader learning-augmented algorithms literature. The main open question is finding the optimal discounting function φ, and the practical impact is somewhat limited to specific online matching domains (advertising, VM placement, inventory) rather than having broad ML applicability.
