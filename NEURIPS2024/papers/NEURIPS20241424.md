## Problem

Transformer-based RL agents like Decision Transformer achieve strong single-task performance but generalize poorly to unseen tasks, and existing multi-task extensions (Prompt-DT, Generalized DT) require expert demonstrations or hindsight statistics at test time that are expensive or infeasible to obtain for novel tasks. The paper asks whether an offline meta-RL framework can achieve efficient few- and zero-shot generalization using the conditional sequence modeling paradigm without any external domain knowledge at test time.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, Meta-DT consumes a k-step complementary prompt (a trajectory segment of (return, state, action) tuples selected for maximizing world-model prediction error) followed by a K-step history where each step is augmented with a 16-dimensional task representation inferred from the preceding h-step (state, action, reward) window. The model autoregressively outputs one action per state token in the sequence. In zero-shot mode the prompt is ablated entirely, and the task representation is updated in real time from the agent's own interaction history, requiring no pre-collected demonstrations.

## Method

Meta-DT operates in two phases. First, a context-aware world model is pretrained on the multi-task offline dataset: a GRU+MLP encoder compresses h-step experience into a 16-dim task representation z, and shared reward/transition decoders predict r and s' conditioned on z, trained with MSE loss. Because the transition dynamics are invariant to behavior policies, z disentangles task identity from policy-specific features. Second, a GPT-style causal transformer (3 layers, 128-dim embeddings) is trained to autoregressively predict actions from the input sequence (prompt, z-augmented history), minimizing action prediction error. The key architectural choice is the complementary prompt: among all k-step segments in the meta-policy's own generated trajectories, the one producing the largest world-model prediction error is selected, ensuring the prompt encodes task-specific information that the world model cannot already capture. This self-guided mechanism eliminates the need for expert demonstrations at test time while exploiting the transformer's inductive bias for in-context task identification.

## Results

On the few-shot generalization benchmark (Medium datasets, aligned setting), Meta-DT achieves the highest test return across all six environments, with the largest margin on Ant-Dir (412.00 vs. 287.23 for the best baseline Prompt-DT, a +43% improvement) and Cheetah-Vel (−99.28 vs. −111.83 for CSRO). In the zero-shot setting, Meta-DT's performance drops only ~5% on average from its few-shot counterpart, whereas baselines suffer 20–35% average drops (e.g., Generalized DT drops 77% on Cheetah-Dir and Cheetah-Vel). Meta-DT also exhibits notably lower variance (e.g., ±4.18 on Cheetah-Dir vs. ±96.16 for CORRO), indicating more stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-DT outperforms all four baselines on every environment in both few-shot and zero-shot settings. Against Prompt-DT (the strongest DT-based baseline), Meta-DT gains the most on Ant-Dir (+124.77 return) and Cheetah-Vel (+36.61), while the margin is smaller on Hopper-Param (+38.30) and Walker-Param (+47.82). Against the temporal-difference baselines CORRO and CSRO, Meta-DT's advantage is most pronounced on Ant-Dir (+165.25 over CORRO) and Cheetah-Vel (+54.84 over CORRO), where the deadly triad of TD learning causes instability. In the zero-shot setting, the gap widens dramatically: Generalized DT collapses by 77% on Cheetah-Dir and Cheetah-Vel, and CSRO drops 40% on Point-Robot, while Meta-DT degrades by only 5–11% across all environments. This confirms that Meta-DT's world-model-based task representation and self-guided prompt eliminate the dependency on expert demonstrations or high-quality warm-start data that all four baselines require.

## Relation to Prior Work

Meta-DT extends the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL by introducing a context-aware world model that disentangles task-relevant dynamics from behavior-policy features, addressing a key limitation of prior DT-based meta-RL methods like Prompt-DT and Generalized DT that rely on expert demonstrations or hindsight statistics at test time. The world model component draws on the meta-RL context encoder literature (PEARL, VariBAD) but repurposes it as a frozen, pretrained module that feeds compact task representations into a causal transformer, rather than being jointly trained via temporal difference learning as in CORRO or CSRO. The complementary prompt design departs from Prompt-DT's assumption of high-quality expert demonstrations by selecting trajectory segments that maximize prediction error on the world model, thereby encoding information the world model cannot already capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of expert demonstrations and domain knowledge at test time makes offline meta-RL substantially more practical for real-world deployment, where such information is often unavailable for unseen tasks. The zero-shot capability (only ~5% performance drop vs. 20–77% for baselines) opens a pathway toward truly adaptive agents that can deploy without any warm-up data. However, the two-phase training pipeline (world model pretraining followed by transformer training) and the relatively small-scale evaluation (45 training tasks, MuJoCo/Meta-World) limit immediate scalability; the authors themselves identify scaling to large diverse datasets and unifying the two phases as critical open questions for the field.
