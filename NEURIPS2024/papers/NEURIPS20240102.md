## Problem

Previous adversarial prompt learning on CLIP (e.g., AdvVP) achieves zero-shot adversarial robustness only by adapting on the entire ImageNet with large prompts (size 200), uses static hand-crafted text supervision that lacks adversarial hints, and ignores natural inputs entirely—risking catastrophic forgetting of natural generalization. This paper asks whether adversarial robustness can be learned from just 1% of training data with lightweight prompts, while simultaneously preserving natural accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240102/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 224×224 RGB images and a few-shot subset (1–16 examples per class) as training input, with learnable prompt tokens of size 2 inserted into both the visual patch embedding and the text word embedding across the first 9 ViT transformer blocks. The text prompt is not independently learned but derived from the visual prompt via a linear projection (image-to-text direction), making it a cross-modal prompt. Output is a class label predicted by cosine similarity between the image embedding and K text embeddings, evaluated under both clean and 100-step PGD-ℓ∞ (ε=1/255) attack conditions.

## Method

FAP introduces two key mechanisms on top of a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. First, it replaces static text prompts with end-to-end learnable adversarial text supervision: the text prompt P_t is obtained by projecting the visual prompt P_v through a linear layer, so both branches are jointly optimized from adversarial examples. Second, it proposes a novel two-term training objective inspired by TRADES: a natural cross-entropy term on text-image logits plus an adversarial term that multiplies a KL divergence (enforcing cross-modal consistency between natural and adversarial text-image distributions) by a cosine-similarity penalty L_cos (encouraging uni-modal divergence between natural and adversarial visual embeddings). This adversarial-aware mechanism prevents the model from taking a shortcut of generating minimal perturbations, forcing it to learn genuinely differentiated visual representations while maintaining consistent predictions in the joint text-image space. Adversarial examples for the inner maximization are generated by maximizing the KL divergence term via 2-step PGD, and the entire prompt is optimized with SGD over 5–10 epochs on the few-shot subset.

## Results

On the adversarial base-to-new generalization setting (16-shot), FAP achieves 70.52% base natural accuracy, 38.05% base adversarial accuracy, 49.58% new natural accuracy, and 21.86% new adversarial accuracy averaged over 11 datasets. In the cross-dataset zero-shot transfer setting, FAP adapted on only 1.25% of ImageNet-1K (16-shot) achieves 25.06% PGD-100 robust accuracy, matching the 25.21% of AdvVP trained on the full ImageNet-1K, while reducing training time from 49.9 days to 0.71 days (~70× speedup). FAP also exhibits substantially lower standard deviations (e.g., ±0.82 vs ±8.03 for base natural accuracy), indicating more stable few-shot learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAP outperforms all three baselines across every metric in the base-to-new generalization setting. Compared to AdvVP (static text + visual prompt), FAP gains +38.84% base natural accuracy and +23.62% base adversarial accuracy, reflecting the critical role of learnable adversarial text supervision. Against AdvMaPLe (the strongest baseline with cross-modal prompt interaction), FAP improves base natural accuracy by +10.14% and base adversarial accuracy by +7.36%, while also reducing standard deviation from ±8.03 to ±0.82, demonstrating that the proposed two-term loss (natural CE + adversarial KL with cosine diversity) stabilizes few-shot learning. Versus AdvVLP (independent vision and text prompts), FAP gains +11.57% base natural accuracy and +5.68% base adversarial accuracy, confirming that the image-to-text projection direction and the adversarial-aware uni-modal divergence mechanism are both important. In the zero-shot cross-dataset transfer setting (Table 2), FAP with 16-shot (1.25% of ImageNet-1K) achieves 25.06% PGD-100 accuracy, essentially matching AdvVP trained on 100% of ImageNet-1K (25.21%) while using 97% less training data and ~70× less wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial visual prompt learning paradigm of AdvVP (Mao et al., 2022), which achieves zero-shot adversarial robustness by aligning adversarial visual features with static CLIP text supervision, and the cross-modal prompt architecture of MaPLe (Khattak et al., 2023). It departs from both by replacing static hand-crafted text prompts with end-to-end learnable adversarial text supervision, introducing a TRADES-inspired two-term objective that decouples natural and adversarial generalization, and adding a uni-modal cosine-similarity penalty to enforce differentiated visual representations. The shift from full-dataset adaptation to a 16-shot (1.25% ImageNet) setting, combined with the image-to-text projection direction, constitutes the core methodological departure from prior adversarial prompt work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching state-of-the-art zero-shot adversarial robustness with only 1.25% of ImageNet-1K training data (a >97% reduction in adaptation cost) makes adversarial prompt tuning feasible for resource-constrained settings. The identification of a natural generalization gap that can cause complete adversarial prompt learning failure, and the instability of text-to-image deep prompt interaction under adversarial objectives, provide actionable diagnostics for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other VLM backbones (e.g., LLaVA, SAM), whether the few-shot setting generalizes to truly out-of-distribution domains, and whether the uni-modal divergence mechanism transfers to other dual-encoder architectures beyond CLIP.
