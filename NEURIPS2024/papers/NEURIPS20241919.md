## Problem

Existing risk controlling prediction sets (RCPS) require all calibration data to be available before deployment and label every incoming point, making them impractical for production systems where data arrives sequentially and expert labeling is expensive. This paper addresses two gaps: (1) extending RCPS to an anytime-valid sequential setting where the risk guarantee holds simultaneously at every time step of an infinite stream, and (2) incorporating active labeling so that a budget-constrained policy decides which points to label, reducing the number of expensive queries needed to tighten the calibration parameter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of covariates X_t (e.g., images), a pretrained probabilistic classifier s: X → Δ^Y providing class probabilities, and three user-specified hyperparameters: risk level θ, significance level α, and label budget B (fraction of points to label). At each step t it outputs a safe calibration parameter β̂_t (guaranteed to satisfy ρ(β̂_t) ≤ θ with probability ≥ 1−α at all t simultaneously), a prediction set C(X, β̂_t) constructed from the classifier's top-probability labels, a labeling policy q_t(X) ∈ [q_min, 1] determining the probability of querying the true label, and a conditional risk estimate r̂_t(X, β) used for variance reduction. The e-process wealth M_t(β) grows over time, and β̂_t shrinks toward the optimal β* as more labels are collected.

## Method

The core mechanism tests, for each candidate β, the null hypothesis H_0^β: ρ(β) ≥ θ using a betting e-process (a nonnegative supermartingale) of the form M_t(β) = ∏(1 + λ_i(θ − r̂_i(X_i,β) − L_i/q_i(X_i)·(R_i(β) − r̂_i(X_i,β)))), where L_i is the label-query indicator and the inverse propensity weight L_i/q_i(X_i) corrects for selective labeling. The calibration parameter β̂_t is defined as the minimum β whose e-process has not yet exceeded the threshold 1/α, and Ville's inequality guarantees the anytime-valid risk control. For variance reduction, the method uses the pretrained classifier's conditional risk estimate r̂_t(X,β) as a control variate, with the optimal predictor shown to be r*(x,β) = E[r(X,Y,β)|X=x]. The optimal labeling policy under a fixed budget B is q*_β(x) ∝ σ_β(x) (the conditional standard deviation of the risk), concentrating labels on high-uncertainty points. A regret bound (Theorem 3) quantifies how estimation errors in q_t and r̂_t slow the growth of the wealth process, and online Newton step is used to adaptively select the betting parameter λ_t with o(T) regret.

## Results

On the numerical simulation (β* = 0.5578) and ImageNet (β* = 0.8349), the proposed 'pretrain' and 'learned' active labeling methods achieve uniformly lower average β̂_t (i.e., tighter risk-controlling prediction sets) than both the 'all' (label everything) and 'oblivious' (random B-fraction sampling) baselines across all numbers of labels queried, while maintaining safety violation rates below the target α = 0.05. All experiments use θ = 0.1, α = 0.05, and a label budget of B = 0.3. On ImageNet, 'pretrain' and 'learned' perform nearly identically and both substantially outperform the passive baselines in label efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four strategies: 'all' (label every arriving data point), 'oblivious' (randomly label a B = 0.3 fraction), 'pretrain' (use a pretrained classifier's conditional variance to guide the labeling policy and its conditional mean as the risk predictor), and 'learned' (train plug-in models for σ and r̂ on the labeled subset, initialized from pretrained estimates). On both the numerical simulation and ImageNet, 'pretrain' and 'learned' uniformly outperform 'all' and 'oblivious' in terms of how quickly β̂_t converges toward β* as a function of labels queried. The 'all' baseline, despite using the most labels, is outperformed by the active methods because it does not prioritize informative data points. The 'oblivious' baseline is the weakest, confirming that random sampling is suboptimal. All four methods maintain the anytime-valid safety guarantee, with observed violation rates at or below α = 0.05, confirming that the active labeling does not compromise statistical validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the batch risk-controlling prediction set (RCPS) framework of Bates et al. [4] to the sequential and active settings by leveraging the betting e-process machinery of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas [34] and the testing-by-betting framework of Shafer [30]. The active labeling component adapts inverse propensity-weighted e-processes from Waudby-Smith et al. [35] (originally for off-policy evaluation) to the RCPS context, while the variance-reduction predictor draws on the prediction-powered inference paradigm of Angelopoulos et al. [2]. Unlike Zrnic and Candès [37], who provide asymptotic, martingale-CLT-based results for active inference, this paper delivers finite-sample, anytime-valid guarantees via Ville's inequality that hold at adaptive stopping times.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of anytime-valid risk control with active labeling addresses a practical gap: production ML systems need continuous calibration under distribution shift while operating under tight labeling budgets (e.g., expert annotations in medical imaging). The regret characterization in Theorem 3 provides a concrete link between the quality of learned labeling policies/predictors and the convergence rate of the calibration parameter, giving practitioners a principled way to invest labeling effort. Key limitations include the i.i.d. assumption (no distribution shift handling), the bounded label policy requirement (Assumption 2), and the existence assumption in Proposition 5; extending to covariate/label shift and removing these assumptions are natural next steps.
