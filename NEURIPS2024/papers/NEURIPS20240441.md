## Problem

Existing high-resolution image harmonization methods either apply global color adjustments that ignore local semantic variations, or upsample low-resolution parameter maps that introduce interpolation errors and fail to capture local non-linearities of the color transform. This leads to inharmonious appearances in local foreground regions, particularly when the foreground and background differ substantially in lighting or color.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a composite image at any resolution (tested up to 6048×4032) and a binary foreground mask as input. Internally, the composite image and mask are downsampled to 256×256 for parameter prediction, while the actual color transformation is applied at full resolution using the fixed-size parameter maps. The output is a harmonized image at the original input resolution, with the parameter maps maintaining a constant 256×256 spatial size regardless of input resolution, which keeps memory and inference time bounded.

## Method

AICT formulates high-resolution harmonization as an image-based multiple curve estimation problem using a two-branch architecture. A Transformer-based parameter network at low resolution predicts two parameter maps (C and F), each treated as a position-dependent 3D LUT that maps spatial coordinates and color values to new color values via trilinear interpolation. The key mechanism is adaptive interval learning: C redistributes input color values across the full range using softmax-normalized cumulative sums to create non-uniform sampling intervals, and F then maps these redistributed values to final colors, effectively cascading two sub-transformations to model local non-linearities. A lightweight global consistent weight module predicts image-level weight vectors per channel that scale F, capturing overall brightness or color shifts induced by the background. Training uses a foreground-normalized MSE loss computed at both high and low resolution with a small LR loss weight (λ=0.01).

## Results

On the full-resolution iHarmony4 dataset (all subsets), AICT achieves fMSE 228.43, MSE 17.76, PSNR 39.40, and SSIM 0.9910, outperforming the closest competitor PCT-Net (fMSE 238.27, MSE 18.80, PSNR 39.28) across all four metrics. On the ccHarmony dataset at 256×256, AICT achieves fMSE 232.66, MSE 24.14, and PSNR 38.46, again the best among all compared methods. At 2048×2048 resolution on HAdobe5k, AICT attains fMSE 147.99 and PSNR 40.07, leading all baselines including PCT-Net (fMSE 156.56, PSNR 39.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AICT outperforms PCT-Net (the closest pixel-wise competitor) by ~10 fMSE points (228.43 vs. 238.27) and ~1.0 MSE points (17.76 vs. 18.80) on the full iHarmony4 set, while essentially tying on SSIM (0.9910 vs. 0.9911). The gap widens substantially against global-adjustment methods: DCCF trails by ~74 fMSE points and Harmonizer by ~111 fMSE points, confirming that pixel-wise transformations with adaptive intervals capture local non-linearities that global filters miss. On the challenging HDay2night subset (only 133 test images), AICT still leads on fMSE (639.00 vs. 700.65 for PCT-Net) and MSE (42.90 vs. 46.47), though the margin is smaller due to limited training data. On ccHarmony at 256×256, AICT beats the next-best GiftNet by 2.5 fMSE points (232.66 vs. 235.20) and 0.87 dB in PSNR (38.46 vs. 37.59). At 2048×2048 resolution, AICT leads PCT-Net by 8.6 fMSE points (147.99 vs. 156.56) and 0.24 dB in PSNR (40.07 vs. 39.83), and also surpasses CDTNet-512 by 11.1 fMSE points. The LUT-based enhancement methods (AdaInt, SepLUT) perform worst among all baselines, underscoring that global RGB-to-RGB transformations are insufficient for harmonization where local spatial context matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

AICT builds on the position-dependent 3D LUT mechanism from Multi-curve Translator (Song et al., ECCV 2022) and adapts the global adaptive-interval learning of AdaInt (Yang et al., CVPR 2022) to a pixel-wise setting, while borrowing the cascade-of-sub-transforms idea from SepLUT (Yang et al., ECCV 2022). The key departure from prior high-resolution harmonization methods (Harmonizer, S2CRNet, DCCF, PCT-Net) is that AICT avoids both global color adjustments and direct upsampling of low-resolution parameter maps; instead, it predicts two cascaded position-dependent 3D LUTs at a fixed low resolution (256×256) whose composition adaptively redistributes color values before the final transformation, thereby modeling local non-linearities without resolution-dependent parameter maps. The Transformer-based parameter network and the global consistent weight module further distinguish AICT from purely CNN-based or filter-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed-size parameter maps mean AICT scales to arbitrary input resolutions (demonstrated up to 5K+) without retraining, making it practically attractive for real-world compositing pipelines where image sizes vary. The ablation findings—that adding more LUTs for color transformation or introducing channel-crossing actually degrades performance—provide useful guidance that the two-LUT cascade is near-optimal for this formulation. A stated limitation is the inability to handle material-dependent effects (mirrors, glass), and the method's reliance on supervised ground-truth composites limits its applicability to unpaired or in-the-wild settings. Whether the position-dependent LUT + adaptive interval paradigm transfers to other high-resolution pixel-wise editing tasks (e.g., relighting, style transfer) remains an open question.
