## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of transformer in-context learning required prompts long enough to contain all query-answer pairs, enabling a simple copy-paste mechanism. This paper tackles the more realistic and theoretically challenging setting where prompts contain only a small number of noisy examples (N << K), making the underlying function template underdetermined, and asks how transformers trained by gradient descent acquire contextual template information to generalize to both unseen examples and unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The input is a prompt of N token-label pairs drawn from a dictionary of K unit-norm tokens, where labels are generated as y_k = λ^T(f(v_k) + ε_k) with Gaussian noise ε_k ~ N(0, τI_m) and a coefficient vector λ from a distribution D_λ. Since N is typically smaller than the representation dimension m, the template is underdetermined from the prompt alone. The output is predicted labels for all K dictionary tokens: the N prompt labels are reproduced exactly, while the K−N unseen token labels are predicted via a ridge regression solution over m basis functions, with the regularization coefficient implicitly set to mτ/N by the training dynamics.

## Method

The paper analyzes a one-layer multi-head softmax attention transformer (H heads) trained by gradient descent on a population mean-squared loss over ICL instances. The key mechanism is that the transformer's attention weights, parameterized as Σ_h w_{h,k} softmax(V^T Q_h v_k), learn to approximate a fixed matrix A = (Z^T Z + mτI_N)^{-1}(Z^T Ẑ + (mτI_N, 0)) that encodes the ridge regression solution for all K tokens simultaneously. The authors prove linear convergence of the training loss under mild assumptions (distinct tokens, H ≥ N for initialization), establishing the first GD convergence result for multi-head softmax attention in ICL. At inference, the trained transformer implements ridge regression with regularization mτ/N rather than the optimal τ, which causes underfitting when N < m and overfitting when N > m. Multi-head attention is shown to be essential: a single head cannot approximate the required matrix A because softmax outputs are non-negative, and the number of heads must balance expressiveness (H ≥ N) against convergence speed.

## Results

The paper establishes that a one-layer multi-head softmax attention transformer trained by gradient descent on non-linear regression ICL tasks achieves linear convergence of the training loss at rate (1 − η_w ζ_0 / 2K)^t (Theorem 1). At inference time, the trained transformer is shown to implement ridge regression over m basis functions to predict unseen tokens (Theorem 2), with an explicit iteration complexity bound for reaching ε-precision. Empirical validation on synthetic data (N=30, K=200, d=100, m=20) confirms both the 1-layer and 4-layer transformers converge to the predicted ridge-regression solution, with out-of-domain inference loss higher than in-domain but following the same descending trend. The experiments further verify that the performance gap to the best possible template choice decreases as N approaches m, and that H ≥ N is necessary for convergence in the 1-layer case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are with prior theoretical analyses rather than empirical baselines. The paper is the first to simultaneously satisfy all six properties in Table 1: nonlinear (softmax) attention, multi-head architecture, task shift (generalization to unseen tasks), GD convergence guarantee, noisy data handling, and representation learning. Prior works each miss at least one: Huang et al. [2023] and Li et al. [2024] use single-head attention and require pairwise-orthogonal tokens; Chen et al. [2024] handles multi-head but lacks a GD convergence proof and task shift; Wu et al. [2023] and Zhang et al. [2023a] use linear attention; Kim and Suzuki [2024] uses a mean-field model with infinite prompt length. The paper overcomes the large-prompt-length assumption (N ≪ K suffices), the orthogonality assumption on tokens, restrictive initialization conditions, and the need for special transformer structures, requiring only that tokens be distinct (Assumption 3) and H ≥ N for initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on training dynamics of transformers for in-context learning (Huang et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024) by moving from linear regression with copy-paste mechanisms to non-linear regression with representations and short prompts. It builds directly on Guo et al. (2023), who showed by construction that transformers can implement ICL with representations, by proving that gradient descent training naturally converges to that same solution. The key departure from prior work is the elimination of the large-prompt-length requirement: instead of relying on the query token appearing in the prompt (copy-paste), the transformer learns ridge regression over basis functions to generalize to unseen tokens from only N << K examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable demonstration that transformers acquire contextual template information to generalize to both unseen examples and unseen tasks from short, noisy prompts, resolving a gap left by prior ICL theory that assumed prompts long enough for copy-paste. The finding that multi-head attention is structurally necessary (a single head cannot approximate the required attention matrix due to sign constraints of softmax) provides a concrete architectural insight. Open questions include how the implicit ridge regression regularization (coefficient mτ/N) compares to the optimal choice (τ) in more complex settings, whether the results extend to deeper transformers with standard architectures, and how the analysis scales to truly high-dimensional representation spaces.
