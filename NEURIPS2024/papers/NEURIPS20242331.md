## Problem

Behavioral metric-based representation learning methods (e.g., bisimulation metrics, MICo) in deep RL rely on one-step transition data, limiting their ability to capture long-term dynamics and cumulative rewards. This results in representations that encode non-informative signals under sparse rewards and fail to generalize in demanding tasks with visual distractions such as camera pose changes and background variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242331/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3 stacked 84×84 RGB frames from MuJoCo-based simulators (DeepMind Control Suite, Meta-World) and produces three outputs: a 256-dim state embedding φ(x) used as input to SAC's policy and Q-networks, a 256-dim chronological embedding ψ(x_i, x_j) encoding the relationship between a current state and a future state within the same trajectory, and a scalar temporal measurement m̂(x_i, x_j) quantifying discounted cumulative rewards between the pair. The chronological embedding and temporal measurement are computed over state pairs sampled from the same rollout with a variable gap of 1–100 steps.

## Method

SCR introduces two jointly trained encoders: a state encoder φ approximating a MICo-style behavioral metric via latent dynamics (inspired by SimSR), and a chronological encoder ψ that fuses φ(x_i) and φ(x_j) through a 2-layer MLP to capture long-term behavioral correlations between state pairs. A novel diffuse metric d̂(a,b) = √(||a||² + ||b||² − aᵀb) replaces standard L_p or cosine distances, satisfying the triangle inequality while retaining non-zero self-distance needed for Lukaszyk-Karmowski divergence approximation. The temporal measurement m̂ is implemented via Interval Quasimetric Embedding (IQE) for asymmetric distances and is trained with two inequality constraints: a lower bound enforcing m̂ ≥ sampled discounted reward sum, and an upper bound derived from the behavioral metric triangle inequality, avoiding direct regression of the unknown optimal value. The total loss L = L_φ + L_ψ + L_m̂ is optimized alongside SAC's standard RL objectives, with shared parameters between φ and ψ for computational efficiency.

## Results

On the Distracting Control Suite (the paper's primary generalization benchmark), SCR achieves the highest mean score across all 8 tasks at 500K steps, with particularly dramatic gains on sparse-reward and camera-pose-distraction tasks: cartpole-swing_up_sparse (185.7 vs. ≤11.2 for all baselines), cheetah-run (331.7 vs. 103.0 for DrQ), and finger-spin (738.3 vs. 579.8 for DrQ). On the default (undistracted) DM_Control setting, SCR is competitive with DrQ and SimSR (e.g., 944.2 vs. 962.7 on ball_in_cup-catch). On Meta-World, SCR achieves a 0.969 average success rate, surpassing DrQ (0.886) and all metric-based baselines (≤0.495).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCR outperforms all metric-based baselines (DBC, MICo, SimSR) by large margins in the distraction setting, where those methods often collapse to near-zero scores (e.g., SimSR scores 0.4 on finger-spin vs. SCR's 738.3). Against the data-augmentation baseline DrQ, SCR wins on 7 of 8 distraction tasks (e.g., 331.7 vs. 103.0 on cheetah-run, 555.1 vs. 33.6 on walker-walk) while DrQ retains a narrow edge on ball_in_cup-catch (124.0 vs. 221.3 is actually SCR winning; DrQ's high variance of ±99.6 makes this less reliable). In the default undistracted setting, SCR is roughly on par with DrQ and SimSR, confirming that the gains come from the temporal/chronological components rather than the base metric. On Meta-World, SCR (0.969) surpasses DrQ (0.886) by 8.3 points in average success rate, while all other baselines remain below 0.50. The additional comparison with CBM (bisimulation + DrQ, Liu 2023) in Table 7 shows that SCR combined with DrQ augmentation (mean ~750 across tasks) further extends the gap over CBM (mean ~430). On the sparse-reward MiniGrid-FourRooms task, PPO+SCR (0.546) marginally outperforms plain PPO (0.515) and clearly beats PPO+MICo (0.350) and PPO+SimSR (0.321), supporting the paper's claim that SCR handles non-informative rewards better than one-step metric methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCR extends the behavioral metric learning lineage (DBC, MICo, SimSR) by addressing their shared limitation: reliance on one-step temporal-difference updates that fail to capture long-term reward and dynamics information. It builds on MICo's sampling-based divergence and SimSR's latent dynamics modeling for the state encoder φ, while introducing two novel components—a chronological embedding ψ that encodes current-to-future state relationships, and a temporal measurement m̂ (implemented via Interval Quasimetric Embedding) that constrains cumulative reward estimates within behavioral-metric bounds. The proposed diffuse metric d̂ = √(‖a‖²+‖b‖²−aᵀb) is a deliberate departure from MICo's angular distance and SimSR's cosine distance, offering a triangle-inequality-satisfying, non-zero self-distance measure with simpler numerical properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCR provides a practical, modular solution to the well-recognized weakness of behavioral metric methods in sparse-reward and visually distracting settings, where prior approaches (DBC, MICo, SimSR) often collapse to near-zero performance. The method's plug-and-play integration with any RL backbone (demonstrated with SAC and PPO) and its strong synergy with data augmentation (SCR+DrQ) make it a potentially widely adoptable representation learning component. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to truly partially observable MDPs (acknowledged limitation), whether the temporal measurement's constraint-based learning generalizes to non-stationary or multi-task settings, and whether the fixed sampling range [1,100] for future states is optimal across all task horizons.
