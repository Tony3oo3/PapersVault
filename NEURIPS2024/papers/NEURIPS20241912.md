## Problem

Existing PEFT methods for LLMs cannot simultaneously achieve high-quality fine-tuning (balancing memorization and generalization), efficient training (low memory and computation), and scalable serving (no inference overhead plus adapter fusion, switching, and parallelism). LoRA and its variants sacrifice generalization for efficiency, while methods like ReFT and LISA improve performance at the cost of serving scalability, leaving a gap where no single approach satisfies all three requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA, Mistral family) and a fine-tuning dataset, and produces structured sparse weight updates where only selected attention heads in MHA and channels in FFN are modified. The updates are applied in-place via co-permutation of coupled weight matrices, so the fine-tuned model requires no additional inference overhead for a single model. For multi-model serving, the sparse updates decompose into compact adapters (a permutation matrix U and a small weight matrix V) that support fusion, fast switching via scatter_add, and batched parallelism with fewer matrix multiplications than LoRA.

## Method

S²FT exploits coupled structures inherent in LLMs—where MHA attention heads in Q/K/V projections are linked to specific rows in the O projection, and FFN columns in U/G projections correspond to rows in the D projection—to 'select sparsely and compute densely.' It selects a small subset of attention heads and FFN channels per Transformer block, then co-permutes the weight matrices on both sides of each coupled structure so that the selected components form a contiguous dense submatrix at the front of each layer. A partial back-propagation algorithm (a two-line PyTorch modification) then performs in-place gradient updates exclusively on these dense submatrices, eliminating redundant forward activations and backward computations. Theoretically, S²FT is equivalent to a LoRA variant with a fixed orthogonal U matrix, which the paper proves yields bounded OOD excess risk under distribution shift (unlike LoRA, whose risk is bounded below by the label shift magnitude) and simpler optimization with stable update magnitudes and directions.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 tasks), S²FT achieves 86.6% average accuracy on LLaMA3-8B with only 0.70% trainable parameters, outperforming LoRA (82.5%), DoRA (85.2%), and Full FT (83.6%). On arithmetic reasoning (7 tasks), S²FT reaches 79.6% average on LLaMA3-8B versus 77.2% for LoRA and 77.7% for Full FT. For instruction following (MT-Bench), S²FT scores 5.27 on Mistral-7B and 5.20 on LLaMA2-7B, surpassing LISA (4.85/4.94) and Full FT (4.64/4.75). In training efficiency, S²FT saves 1.4–3.0× peak memory and achieves 1.5–2.7× latency speedup over Full FT, while improving ~10% over LoRA on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²FT outperforms all eight PEFT baselines across every model and task suite. Against LoRA, the primary baseline, S²FT gains 4.6% average on commonsense reasoning (e.g., 86.6 vs 82.5 on LLaMA3-8B) and 1.3% on arithmetic (79.6 vs 77.2), while using a comparable number of trainable parameters (~0.7–0.8%). DoRA, the strongest low-rank variant, is beaten by 2.8% on commonsense and 0.9% on arithmetic. Among adapter-based methods, S²FT surpasses Series Adapter (70.8→81.8 on LLaMA-7B), Parallel Adapter (72.2→81.8), and LoReFT (80.2→81.8) by 1–11 points. LISA, which uses ~10× more trainable parameters (9.91%), still trails S²FT by 1.8 points on LLaMA-7B commonsense and 0.4 points on MT-Bench for Mistral-7B. Notably, S²FT matches or exceeds Full FT on LLaMA3-8B (86.6 vs 83.6 commonsense; 79.6 vs 77.7 arithmetic) despite updating <1% of parameters, and achieves an 11.5% improvement over Full FT on MT-Bench instruction-following generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²FT positions itself as a structured sparse fine-tuning method that bridges the gap between unstructured SpFT (which shows strong generalization but requires slow sparse operations) and low-rank PEFT methods like LoRA/DoRA/Galore (which are hardware-efficient but underperform in generalization). The key conceptual departure is the 'select sparsely and compute densely' paradigm: by discovering coupled structures in MHA and FFN modules and co-permuting weight matrices, S²FT converts sparse head/channel selection into dense submatrix updates, inheriting the generalization benefits of sparsity while retaining dense-operation efficiency. Theoretically, S²FT is framed as a LoRA variant with a fixed orthogonal U matrix, and its serving scalability analysis builds on the S-LoRA adapter parallelism framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S²FT is one of the first PEFT methods to simultaneously address the quality-efficiency-scalability trilemma, offering a practical path toward serving thousands of fine-tuned LLMs without the 2.2× inference overhead of ReFT or the 10× memory penalty of LISA. The theoretical result bounding OOD excess risk of S²FT while showing LoRA's risk is lower-bounded by label shift magnitude provides a principled explanation for the empirical generalization gap. Open questions include extending the coupled-structure approach to other architectures (CNNs, RNNs, multimodal models), scaling to much larger models, and building a production serving system that exploits the adapter decomposition for fusion, switching, and parallelism.
