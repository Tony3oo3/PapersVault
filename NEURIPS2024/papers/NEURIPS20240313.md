## Problem

Existing neural relational inference methods (NRI, dNRI, MPM) are trained offline on fixed datasets, assuming aligned training and test distributions. In evolving multi-agent systems where interaction graphs, dynamics parameters, or the dynamics themselves change over time, these offline models fail to adapt. Online learning for relational inference has been barely explored, and naively applying online backpropagation to encoder-decoder NRI architectures degrades accuracy because the self-supervised decoder converges far faster than the unsupervised encoder.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming agent trajectories (positions and velocities) in a sliding-window format: 30 observed steps followed by 30 predicted steps for synthetic systems, and 10 observed / 10 predicted for CMU MoCap (31 joints at 120 Hz). The primary output is an N×N×m adjacency matrix (N agents, m interaction types) with entries in [0,1], initialized to 0.5 and updated once per iteration; the secondary output is the predicted future trajectory. Training operates strictly in a batch=1, epoch=1 streaming regime with no separate validation or test split.

## Method

ORI replaces the encoder with a directly trainable adjacency matrix, optimizing it via online backpropagation on the decoder's trajectory prediction loss (MSE), which accelerates interaction-graph convergence relative to encoder-based approaches. The key innovation, AdaRelation, adaptively adjusts the adjacency matrix learning rate by monitoring the normalized L1 deviation D_I(t) = ||I(t) − I(t−w)||₁/(N²m) over w past steps: if the deviation exceeds a threshold ε, the learning rate decreases to stabilize; otherwise it increases to accelerate adaptation to newly evolved interactions. A data augmentation technique, Trajectory Mirror, flips spatial axes to generate three additional trajectories per sample, preventing the model from biasing toward specific coordinate configurations. The framework is model-agnostic, integrating seamlessly with any GNN-based decoder (MLP, RNN, or attention-based) that accepts an adjacency matrix as input, and the same adjacency matrix persists across all simulations without knowing when interactions evolve.

## Results

On the springs system with evolving interaction graphs, ORI with the MPMa decoder achieves 96.4% average relation accuracy, compared to 61.6% for MPM, 57.4% for NRI, and 51.1% for dNRI. On the charged system, ORI with MPMr reaches 95.3% accuracy versus 51.7% (MPM), 52.0% (NRI), and 50.3% (dNRI). Notably, in the charged system, higher-accuracy ORI methods do not always achieve lower trajectory MSE (e.g., NRI at 52.0% accuracy shows lower MSE than ORI at 95.3%), suggesting that MSE alone does not reflect relation inference quality. ORI also adapts to evolving dynamics (switching between springs and charged systems) and evolving parameters, reaching near-perfect accuracy given sufficient iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORI substantially outperforms all three offline encoder-decoder baselines on relation accuracy: in the springs system, ORI+MPMa (96.4%) exceeds MPM (61.6%), NRI (57.4%), and dNRI (51.1%) by 34.8, 39.0, and 45.3 percentage points respectively; in the charged system, ORI+MPMr (95.3%) exceeds NRI (52.0%), MPM (51.7%), and dNRI (50.3%) by roughly 43–45 points. However, in the charged system, the lower-accuracy baselines (especially NRI) achieve lower trajectory MSE, which the authors attribute to the encoder's larger capacity overfitting to trajectory modeling rather than true relation inference. Against the prior online method CoGNN [5], ORI achieves lower relative MSE at later prediction steps (e.g., 0.165 vs. 0.635 relative to NRI at step 10 for ORI+MPMa vs. CoGNN-AR), though CoGNN performs better at very early steps (1–2). On CMU MoCap, ORI produces more interpretable joint interaction graphs (e.g., emphasizing left foot–right knee–right foot connections in walking) compared to MPM's simpler cyclic pattern, though MPM achieves slightly lower trajectory MSE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural relational inference (NRI) lineage—originating from Kipf et al.'s encoder-decoder VAE framework and advanced by dNRI and MPM—by replacing the learned encoder with a directly trainable adjacency matrix optimized via online backpropagation. It departs from the sole prior online multi-agent work (CoGNN, Li et al. 2022), which relied on online convex optimization constrained to a specific linear decoder architecture, by introducing a model-agnostic framework compatible with arbitrary GNN decoders. The two novel components, AdaRelation (an adaptive learning rate driven by historical adjacency-matrix deviation) and Trajectory Mirror (axis-flipping data augmentation), are specific to the relational-inference setting and have no direct analogue in prior online-learning or NRI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORI addresses a practically important gap: real-world multi-agent systems (traffic, robotics, human motion) evolve continuously, yet all prior relational-inference methods assume a fixed training distribution. The finding that substantially higher relational accuracy can coexist with higher trajectory MSE (in charged systems) challenges the common assumption that lower prediction error implies better-learned structure, suggesting that encoder capacity can overfit trajectory modeling at the expense of relation discovery. Open questions include scalability to variable agent counts, directed interaction graphs, and much faster-evolving dynamics, none of which are evaluated here.
