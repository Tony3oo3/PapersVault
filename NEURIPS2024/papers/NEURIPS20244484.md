## Problem

Existing model-based data curation methods select training examples independently, ignoring the fact that in contrastive learning the quality of a batch depends on its composition (e.g., hard negatives) rather than just the sum of individual example scores. This paper asks whether jointly scoring and selecting entire sub-batches can accelerate multimodal pretraining beyond what is achievable with independent example prioritization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a super-batch of image-text pairs (2×, 5×, or 10× the training batch size, up to 327,680 pairs) along with a learner model (ViT-B/16 + Bert-B) and a pretrained reference model. It produces a selected sub-batch of 32,768 image-text pairs optimized for joint learnability, which is then used for one training step. In Flexi-JEST, the super-batch is scored at reduced resolution (32×32 patches) to cut inference cost, and the selected sub-batch is split 50/50 between full-resolution (16×16) and low-resolution (32×32) training.

## Method

JEST exploits the fact that contrastive batch losses decompose into sums of conditional per-example losses, so joint learnability (learner loss minus reference loss) also decomposes. This enables a sequential blocked-Gibbs sampling algorithm that selects sub-batches in proportion to their joint learnability: at each of N=16 chunks, it computes each candidate's conditional learnability given already-selected examples (capturing pairwise interactions via the B×B contrastive score matrix) and samples without replacement. The key scoring criterion is learnability (difference between learner and reference model losses), which up-samples data that is both unlearned and learnable. Flexi-JEST further reduces overhead by using FlexiViT multi-resolution training: the learner is co-trained at both 16×16 and 32×32 patch sizes, allowing the 32×32 variant to score large super-batches at ~72% lower FLOP cost while the 16×16 variant maintains full-resolution quality, reducing total scoring overhead from 133% to 10% additional FLOPs.

## Results

JEST++ (ViT-B/16) achieves a mean improvement of +2.8% over the SigLIP 40B baseline across ImageNet-1K (10-shot: 70.3%, ZS: 76.9%) and COCO retrieval (I2T: 70.3%, T2I: 53.3%), while using only 4B training examples (10× fewer) and 23% of the total FLOPs. The compute-efficient variant Flexi-JEST++ achieves +0.9% mean improvement at just 11% of SigLIP's total FLOPs. At ViT-L scale, JEST++ matches the SigLIP 40B baseline (80.5% ZS) with only 4B examples and 23% FLOPs, while improving COCO I2T by 1.6 points (71.1% vs. 69.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JEST++ decisively outperforms the primary SigLIP 40B baseline on COCO retrieval (I2T: 70.3 vs. 65.2, T2I: 53.3 vs. 47.4) while matching it on ImageNet ZS (76.9 vs. 76.7), all with 10× fewer training examples and 77% fewer total FLOPs. Against other strong CLIP variants, JEST++ leads by a wide margin: CLIP (−11.8% mean), EVA-CLIP (−4.6%), OpenCLIP (−5.8%), and LessIsMore (−5.9%) all fall well below JEST++'s +2.8% mean. SILC-S, the closest competitor at +0.2% mean, requires 5× more training examples (20B vs. 4B) and 8× more total FLOPs (190% vs. 23%). On the LAION-2B benchmark, JEST++ (66.8% IN1K ZS, 54.8% COCO) surpasses all prior offline curation methods (LAION-440M: 64.1/48.1, SemDeDup: 64.3/48.9, DBP: 65.5/48.4) with the same 1.3B training budget. On DataComp-1B, JEST with learnability scoring (74.8% ImageNet) substantially outperforms Data Filtering Networks (67.8%), confirming that joint batch-level selection with learnability scoring is the key driver over independent example-level filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learnability scoring framework of Mindermann et al. [31] and the authors' own prior work on active learning [13] from independent example-level selection to joint batch-level selection, exploiting the fact that contrastive losses decompose into conditional terms that couple examples within a batch. It builds on SigLIP's sigmoid-contrastive objective [52] and FlexiViT's multi-resolution architecture [4] to make large super-batch scoring tractable, while generalizing the hard negative mining literature [5, 19, 32, 39, 43, 48, 51] from negative-only to joint positive-negative batch construction. The key conceptual departure from prior data curation methods (CLIPScore [20], DFN [14], SemDeDup [1]) is that batch composition matters beyond the sum of individual example qualities, and that this structure is directly exposed by the contrastive objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× FLOP and 13× iteration savings over SigLIP SOTA make this a practically significant efficiency gain for multimodal pretraining, and the framing of data curation as a new axis for neural scaling laws (analogous to data quantity or model size) could reshape how the field thinks about compute allocation. The data quality bootstrapping effect—where a small curated reference dataset guides curation of a much larger uncurated corpus to produce a model far exceeding the reference—suggests a scalable path to improving pretraining without manual curation of web-scale data. Key limitations include dependence on well-curated reference datasets (whose inference from downstream task requirements remains open) and the observation that aggressive filtering requires optimizer tuning (β₂=0.95), hinting at an effective-batch-size interpretation that is not yet fully formalized.
