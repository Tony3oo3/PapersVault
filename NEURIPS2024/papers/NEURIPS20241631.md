## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for Vision-Language Models require domain-identity hints at test time to route images to domain-specific classifiers, and rely on large reference datasets to preserve the VLM's zero-shot ability on unseen domains. Neither requirement is realistic in dynamic deployment where the model must classify images from an arbitrary mix of seen and unseen domains without knowing which domain each image originates from.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241631/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label drawn from a cross-domain label set of 1,100 classes spanning 10 domains (Aircraft, Caltech101, DTD, EuroSAT, Flowers, Food-101, MNIST, OxfordPet, StanfordCars, SUN397). Critically, at inference the model receives no domain-identity hint and must simultaneously determine whether the image belongs to a seen or unseen domain and predict the correct class. The pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder is frozen; only a lightweight ridge-regression adapter and a training-free fusion module are added.

## Method

RAIL consists of two components. The RAIL-Adapter learns a ridge-regression classifier in either primal form (projecting CLIP features through a randomly-initialized hidden layer to a higher-dimensional space) or dual form (using an RBF kernel to implicitly define an infinite-dimensional projection), and updates its parameters recursively at each incremental step via the Woodbury matrix identity so that the solution is provably identical to joint training on all encountered domains, guaranteeing absolute memorization. The RAIL-Fusion module is training-free: it first uses CLIP's zero-shot logits over the full cross-domain label set to decide whether the test image is In-Distribution (seen domain) or Out-Of-Distribution (unseen domain); for ID images it fuses the adapter logits with zero-shot logits via a weighted sum (β=0.8), while for OOD images it relies solely on the zero-shot prediction, thereby absolutely preserving CLIP's zero-shot ability on unseen domains without any reference data.

## Results

On the proposed X-TAIL setting (10 domains, 1,100 classes, 16-shot), Dual-RAIL achieves 71.9% Average, 82.4% Last, and 62.4% Transfer accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline MoE-Adapter (63.0% / 70.5% / 56.0%) by 8.9%, 11.9%, and 6.4% respectively. Primal-RAIL reaches 70.7% Average and 79.1% Last, still surpassing all baselines. On the 5-shot MTIL setting, Dual-RAIL attains 72.3% Average and 77.9% Last, exceeding MoE-Adapter (71.4% / 76.1%) and ZSCL (64.4% / 67.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAIL dominates all five baselines on every metric in both X-TAIL and MTIL settings. The strongest competitor, MoE-Adapter [15], trails Dual-RAIL by 8.9% in Average and 11.9% in Last accuracy on X-TAIL, and by 0.9% and 1.8% on MTIL. ZSCL [10], which relies on large-scale reference datasets to regularize the parameter space, achieves only 60.0% Average on X-TAIL—11.9% below Dual-RAIL—highlighting the cost of its reference-data dependency. iCaRL [7] (replay-based) and LwF [6] (distillation-based) suffer the most severe forgetting, with Last accuracies of 64.0% and 62.8% respectively, well below RAIL's 82.4%. WiSE-FT [47] (weight-averaging) performs worst on Last (58.0%), indicating that simple parameter interpolation cannot preserve cross-domain discriminability. Notably, RAIL's Transfer accuracy (62.4%) matches the zero-shot CLIP baseline exactly, confirming the theoretical guarantee of absolute zero-shot preservation without any reference data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytic continual learning line initiated by ACIL (Zhuang et al., NeurIPS 2022), which used ridge regression with random projections for class-incremental learning, to the multi-domain VLM setting. It departs from prior MTIL methods (ZSCL, MoE-Adapter) that rely on reference datasets to preserve zero-shot ability and require domain-identity hints at test time, replacing both with a training-free fusion module and a cross-domain label space. The dual-form adapter draws on classical kernel ridge regression (Saunders et al., ICML 1998), applying the Woodbury matrix identity to derive recursive updates that are provably identical to joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The X-TAIL benchmark is a practically important step toward realistic deployment of VLMs in dynamic environments where domain labels are unavailable, and the theoretical guarantee of absolute memorization (no forgetting on learned domains) plus absolute preservation of zero-shot ability (no degradation on unseen domains) is a clean, strong result. The key limitation is that the CLIP encoder remains frozen throughout, capping the method's ceiling; the authors identify low-cost encoder adaptation as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the analytic approach extends to other VLM tasks (segmentation, grounding) and whether the kernel-based dual form scales to very large numbers of domains without prohibitive memory for the growing kernel matrix.
