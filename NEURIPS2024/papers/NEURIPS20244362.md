## Problem

While GAN latent spaces are known to encode semantic attributes as linear directions enabling editing and inversion, it remains unexplored whether the weights of personalized diffusion models themselves form a structured, interpretable space. This paper asks whether a collection of Dreambooth-fine-tuned model weights lies on a low-dimensional manifold that supports sampling new identities, linear semantic editing, and single-image inversion — effectively treating model weights as data points rather than opaque parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244362/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of ~65,000 LoRA weight vectors (each ~100,000-dimensional, from rank-1 LoRA on cross-attention Q/V layers of Stable Diffusion 1.5) and produces new model weights in a PCA-reduced subspace (1000 dims for editing, 10,000 dims for inversion). For inversion, a single RGB face image is the sole training input; for generation, standard text prompts condition the resulting model. The output is not an image but a complete diffusion model that can be prompted to generate unlimited consistent images of the encoded subject.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages. First, ~65,000 Stable Diffusion 1.5 models are fine-tuned via Dreambooth with rank-1 LoRA (cross-attention Q/V only) on synthetic identity images generated from CelebA, yielding a dataset of flattened LoRA weight vectors in R^100,000. Second, PCA is applied to these weight vectors to define the weights2weights (w2w) subspace, and linear classifiers trained on CelebA binary attribute labels over the PCA-projected weights discover orthogonal edit directions. Third, three operations exploit this structure: (1) sampling draws new PCA coefficients from per-component Gaussians to produce a novel identity model, (2) editing adds α·n along a discovered direction to modify one attribute while preserving identity, and (3) inversion optimizes PCA coefficients under a standard denoising loss on a single image, constrained to the w2w subspace, inheriting the identity priors of the training models without a prior-preservation loss.

## Results

For identity editing in w2w space, the method achieves the highest ID Score (identity preservation) across all three tested attributes: 0.45 for Gender, 0.45 for Chubby, and 0.72 for Narrow Eyes, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (disentanglement) for Gender (0.31) and Eyes (0.19) and the highest CLIP Score (semantic alignment) for Gender (4.13) and Eyes (0.59). For single-image inversion, w2w achieves an ID Score of 0.64±0.01, substantially outperforming single-image Dreambooth-LoRA (0.43±0.03) and approaching the multi-image Dreambooth-LoRA upper bound (0.69±0.01), while using only 10,000 parameters and 55 seconds of optimization time versus 99,648 parameters and 220 seconds for Dreambooth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Concept Sliders [17], w2w consistently wins on identity preservation (ID Score) across all three edit types (e.g., 0.72 vs 0.53 for Narrow Eyes) and on disentanglement (LPIPS) for Gender and Eyes, while Concept Sliders slightly edges out on CLIP Score for Chubby (2.21 vs 2.16); a user study confirms w2w's superiority with a 71.6% vs 28.4% win rate. Against text prompting, w2w dominates on all three metrics for Gender and Eyes, and on ID Score and LPIPS for Chubby, with an 87.2% vs 12.8% user study win rate. For single-image inversion, w2w (ID Score 0.64) substantially outperforms single-image Dreambooth-LoRA (0.43) and Celeb-Basis (0.60) and IP-Adapter FaceID (0.62), while approaching the multi-image Dreambooth-LoRA upper bound (0.69) at 4× lower optimization time (55s vs 220s) and 10× fewer parameters (10,000 vs 99,648). In the single-shot personalization user study, w2w achieves 86.6% and 77.2% win rates against Celeb-Basis and IP-Adapter FaceID respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN latent space interpretability line (GANSpace, StyleGAN editing) from the *latent* space of a single generative model to the *weight* space of a large collection of fine-tuned diffusion models. It leverages Dreambooth + LoRA personalization to create 60K+ identity-specific model weights as data points, then applies PCA to discover a linear subspace (w2w) where each point is a full model rather than an image. The approach is conceptually analogous to how GANSpace discovered linear edit directions in StyleGAN's latent space, but operates one level up: it generates and edits *models* rather than *images*, forming what the authors term a 'meta-latent space.' It also builds on weight-space arithmetic (task arithmetic, model soups) and Concept Sliders, but departs by constructing an explicit dataset of many fine-tuned models and modeling their manifold structure rather than editing a single model's weights ad hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuned diffusion model weights form a low-dimensional linear subspace supporting sampling, editing, and single-image inversion is a useful new lens for understanding and controlling personalized generative models. Practically, w2w inversion (55s, 10K parameters) approaches multi-image Dreambooth quality (220s, 100K parameters) while enabling post-hoc semantic edits that persist across all future generations of that identity. Key limitations include inheritance of CelebA biases, dependence on a large curated dataset for good disentanglement, and difficulty with undersampled attributes. Open questions include whether this linear structure holds for more complex or compositional concepts, whether the subspace can be learned without a 60K-model dataset, and how it interacts with emerging parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods beyond LoRA.
