## Problem

Existing safe RL methods require manually designed, task-specific cost functions and can only handle simple single-state or single-entity constraints, failing to capture complex real-world safety requirements that involve interactions among multiple states or entities over time. When constraints are expressed as free-form natural language, two additional challenges arise: determining whether a trajectory violates a constraint requires perception of the full historical state-action sequence, and the episodic cost signal is sparse (only imposed at the final violating time step), making it difficult for the agent to learn which earlier actions contributed to the violation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a state-action trajectory τ = (s₁, a₁, …, s_T, a_T) where each state is a d_s-dimensional observation vector, and a free-form natural language constraint y (e.g., 'You only have 20 HP, stepping on grass and lava will lose 3 and 2 HP respectively, please don't die'). It produces two outputs: a binary violation indicator (whether the trajectory violates the constraint, determined by a cosine similarity threshold β between trajectory and text embeddings) and a dense per-step cost value ĉ(s_t, a_t, y, τ_t) for each state-action pair, where cost equals 1 if violation is detected and otherwise a predicted non-violation cost in [0,1] from the cost assignment layer. The many-to-many relationship between trajectories and constraints (one trajectory can violate multiple constraints, one constraint can apply to multiple trajectories) is handled explicitly in the training objective.

## Method

TTCT comprises two jointly trained components. The Text-Trajectory Alignment component uses a CLIP-style dual-encoder architecture: a causal transformer encodes the state-action sequence into per-step embeddings H₁…H_T, and a BERT encoder produces a text embedding L; a multimodal contrastive loss (KL divergence over softmax similarity scores) aligns matching trajectory-text pairs while a within-trajectory loss maximizes similarity at the violation time step T and minimizes it at all earlier steps. The Cost Assignment component performs temporal credit assignment by computing attention scores e_t = sigmoid(cosine_sim(H_t, L)) for each time step, then predicting per-step costs via a feed-forward layer on the attention-weighted trajectory embedding concatenated with the text embedding, trained with an MSE loss that enforces the sum of per-step costs equals the predicted episodic cost. Both components are trained end-to-end with a unified loss L_TTCT = L_MC + L_WT + L_CA, and during policy training the frozen encoders provide cost predictions while LoRA-fine-tuned copies supply historical context to the policy network within standard safe RL algorithms (PPO-Lag, CPPO-PID, FOCOPS).

## Results

On the Hazard-World-Grid task, TTCT (cost prediction mode) reduces the average episodic cost (violation rate) by up to 54% compared to ground-truth cost training (e.g., PPO_Lag: 0.28 vs. 0.61; CPPO_PID: 0.28 vs. 0.40) while maintaining comparable rewards. On the more challenging SafetyGoal task, the improvement is even more pronounced: CPPO_PID drops from 0.35 to 0.11 cost, and FOCOPS from 0.41 to 0.10, with rewards matching or exceeding the ground-truth cost baselines. The unconstrained PPO baseline achieves the highest violation rate (0.88 on Grid, 0.32 on Goal), confirming that the safe RL algorithms with TTCT-predicted costs substantially outperform both unconstrained and ground-truth-cost-trained policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTCT is integrated into three safe RL algorithms (PPO_Lag, CPPO_PID, FOCOPS) and compared against the same algorithms trained with manually designed ground-truth cost functions, plus unconstrained PPO. TTCT wins on the primary safety metric (average episodic cost / violation rate) across all six algorithm-task combinations: on Grid, it reduces cost by 54% (PPO_Lag), 30% (CPPO_PID), and 17% (FOCOPS) relative to ground-truth cost; on Goal, the reductions are 27%, 69%, and 76% respectively. Rewards remain statistically comparable between TTCT and ground-truth cost modes in most settings, with TTCT even slightly outperforming on Goal (e.g., FOCOPS: 0.02 vs. −0.63 reward). The unconstrained PPO baseline has the worst violation rate in every task (0.88 on Grid, 0.32 on Goal), confirming that the constrained algorithms with TTCT-predicted costs are substantially safer. The paper also demonstrates that TTCT achieves a Pareto frontier closer to the origin than ground-truth cost training, and that it transfers zero-shot to the unseen LavaWall environment with comparable safety performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends safe RL with natural language constraints (Yang et al. 2021, Prakash et al. 2020, Lou et al. 2024) from single-state/entity constraints to the more general trajectory-level setting, where constraints involve multi-entity interactions, sequential dependencies, and mathematical reasoning over entire episodes. Methodologically, it repurposes CLIP-style contrastive learning to align trajectory embeddings with text embeddings, treating text as both the constraint specification and the supervisory signal for trajectory encoding. The cost assignment component draws on temporal credit assignment (Liu et al. 2019) to decompose a sparse episodic violation cost into dense per-step costs, which is the first application of credit assignment to the safe RL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manually designed cost functions per constraint type, TTCT offers a flexible, generalizable path toward safe RL with free-form natural language constraints, which is critical for real-world deployment in autonomous driving and robotics. The zero-shot transfer to constraint-shift environments (LavaWall) suggests the learned representations capture transferable safety semantics. However, the method is validated only on relatively simple 2D grid and 3D navigation tasks, the violation rate is not absolute zero, and the transformer-based trajectory encoder faces a contextual bottleneck with longer trajectories. Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional continuous control, handling adversarially phrased constraints, and integrating meta-learning for faster adaptation.
