## Problem

Prior work (Zhu et al., 2023) concluded that weak supervision (WS) offers little benefit over simply labeling 50 clean examples, but this finding relied on benchmarks with few classes, balanced distributions, and no domain expertise requirements. The paper argues these benchmarks are unrepresentative of real-world tasks and asks: on more realistic, challenging datasets, how many labeled examples does supervised learning actually need to match WS?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243885/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes unlabeled text examples (e.g., banking queries, PubMed abstracts, ToS sentences, voice-assistant utterances, product reviews), a set of hand-written labeling functions (keyword, dictionary, and regex-based heuristics that label or abstain per example), and a small pool of clean validation labels. It produces aggregated pseudolabels via a label model, a fine-tuned transformer classifier, and—critically—a crossover-point analysis quantifying the number of clean labels at which supervised learning surpasses WS. For the multilingual MASSIVE tasks, non-English text is first translated to English via DeepL before applying English LFs, and the one-to-one cross-lingual alignment enables direct label-matrix transfer as an oracle baseline.

## Method

BOXWRENCH introduces five text-classification tasks (Banking77, ChemProt, Claude9, MASSIVE18/60, Amazon31) chosen for high class cardinality (9–77 classes), class imbalance, and domain-specificity (legal, biomedical, e-commerce). LFs are written following a rigorous pipeline: a 250-example development set is inspected to identify per-class patterns, multiple keyword/dictionary/regex LFs are drafted, and their coverage, conflict ratio, and accuracy are measured before finalization. Three learning scenarios are compared—supervised (clean labels only), weakly supervised (LF-aggregated pseudolabels), and continuous fine-tuning (WS model further fine-tuned on clean labels)—using RoBERTa or domain-specific BERT variants (LegalBERT, SciBERT, language-specific BERTs) with early stopping on validation data. The key metric is the crossover point where the supervised curve intersects the better of WS/CFT, which the paper shows exceeds 1,000 labels on Banking77 and Amazon31 (versus <200 on prior benchmarks). Additional contributions include improved ChemProt LFs incorporating entity-position distance features (raising the F1 crossover from ~800 to ~1,600) and a cross-lingual LF adaptation strategy that reuses English LFs for Chinese, Japanese, and Norwegian via translation, achieving crossover points above 1,000 without writing new LFs.

## Results

The headline finding is that on realistic high-cardinality tasks, supervised learning requires 1,000+ clean labels to match weak supervision (WS). On Amazon31 and Banking77, crossover points (where supervised learning surpasses CFT) exceed 1,000 labels; on Claude9, even using all 200 validation labels, CFT retains a 5% F1 advantage over supervised-only (0.582 vs. 0.347 at 100% validation). On ChemProt with improved LFs, the F1 crossover point rises to ~1,600 (vs. ~800 with original LFs). In contrast, on existing WRENCH benchmarks (AGNews, Yelp, IMDB, TREC), crossover points are below 200 for four of six tasks, confirming that prior conclusions of WS's limited value stem from overly simple datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between CFT (WS + continuous fine-tuning on clean labels) and Supervised-Only (fine-tuning on clean labels alone). CFT with Majority Vote consistently outperforms Supervised-Only at every validation size on all three main datasets, with the gap largest at low label budgets (e.g., Claude9: 0.153 vs. 0.109 F1 at 12 labels; ChemProt: 0.625 vs. 0.504 accuracy at 100 labels). Among label models, Majority Vote and Dawid-Skene perform best and most consistently; Snorkel is competitive but slightly behind, while FlyingSquid underperforms substantially (e.g., 0.109 vs. 0.153 F1 on Claude9 at 6.25% validation). Against the LLM baseline (GPT-4o zero-shot), WS+CFT wins decisively on domain-specific tasks (Claude9, ChemProt) but GPT-4o performs well on Amazon31, likely due to training data overlap. For cross-lingual transfer, the L2En-LFs approach (translating non-English text to English before applying English LFs) achieves crossover points above 1,000 in Chinese and Japanese, matching or exceeding Oracle-LFs performance, while the L2En-Inference-Supervised baseline (translating at inference time) underperforms language-specific models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the WRENCH benchmark pipeline (Zhang et al., 2021) for its experimental infrastructure and dataset format, and extends the ChemProt LF design from Yu et al. (2021) by incorporating entity-distance features. It is a direct response to Zhu et al. (2023), who argued that WS offers limited value over supervised learning with just 50 labels on existing benchmarks. The paper positions BOXWRENCH as addressing three specific gaps in prior WS benchmarks (WRENCH, AutoWS-Bench-101, WALNUT): low class cardinality, balanced distributions, and suboptimal LF quality, while also introducing a cross-lingual LF adaptation dimension absent from all prior WS evaluation suites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOXWRENCH provides a more realistic evaluation standard for WS that could shift the field's understanding of when weak supervision is practically valuable, particularly for high-cardinality, imbalanced, or domain-specific tasks where manual labeling is cost-prohibitive. The demonstration that careful LF design can roughly double crossover points (800→1600 on ChemProt) and that English LFs can be adapted to other languages 'for free' via translation are practically actionable findings. Open questions include whether these results hold with more modern end models (LLMs), whether the one-to-one MASSIVE correspondence generalizes to real multilingual settings with distribution shift, and whether the findings extend to sequence-tagging tasks where per-token labeling costs are even higher.
