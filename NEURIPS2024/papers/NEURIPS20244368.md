## Problem

Prior theoretical and empirical work suggested that low-depth transformers require O(k) heads to learn k-th order Markov processes, raising concerns about how transformers capture long-range dependencies. This paper identifies a contradiction: empirically, 2-layer and 3-layer transformers with a single head learn k-th order Markov sources for k far exceeding prior predictions, and theoretically, constant depth suffices when non-linearities (specifically layer normalization) are present. The paper asks what the interplay between depth, number of heads, and non-linearity is in learning k-th order Markov processes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of T discrete symbols from a finite alphabet [S] drawn from a k-th order Markov process (with transition kernels sampled from a Dirichlet(1) prior) and produces the conditional k-gram model: the in-context maximum-likelihood estimate of the next symbol's probability distribution given the last k observed symbols. The output is an S-dimensional logit vector whose softmax gives the empirical conditional distribution, computed by averaging one-hot encodings of the next symbols at all positions where the preceding k symbols match the final k symbols in the input.

## Method

The paper establishes three representation results. First, attention-only transformers with ⌈log₂(k+1)⌉ layers and 1 head per layer represent conditional k-grams by exponentially expanding the context window: each layer aggregates information from the current position and a position 2^ℓ steps back, so after L layers the embedding encodes all previous k symbols. Second, the main result shows a 3-layer, 1-head standard transformer (with layer normalization and FFN) represents conditional k-grams with O(S) embedding dimension. The key mechanism is encoding the k-symbol history as a ternary vector z_i = Σ 3^j · e_{x_{i-j}}, where layer normalization converts dot-product attention into L2-norm-based attention, enabling exact matching of normalized history vectors in a single final layer. Third, conditional lower bounds show that attention-only transformers need at least 1+log₂(k−2) layers (under an assumption that early-layer attention is position-dependent only), and 1-layer transformers require near-linear heads or embedding dimension in T.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) shows that a standard transformer with just 3 layers and 1 head per layer can represent the conditional k-gram model for any k, with embedding dimension O(S). For attention-only transformers (no layer norm or FFN), Theorem 3 establishes that ⌈log₂(k+1)⌉ layers with 1 head per layer suffice, improving over the k-head 2-layer construction of prior work. Empirically, a 2-layer 1-head transformer achieves low test loss on k-th order Markov processes up to k=4, and a 3-layer 1-head transformer up to k=8, both substantially exceeding the k=2 limit predicted by prior analyses [6, 8]. Lower bounds (Theorems 5–6) show that 1-layer transformers require Ω(T/log T) heads or embedding dimension, and attention-only transformers need at least 1+log₂(k−2) layers under a natural attention-pattern assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not run head-to-head benchmark comparisons against other methods; instead, it contrasts its theoretical constructions with prior architectural requirements. Prior work [6, 8] established that a 2-layer transformer needs k heads in the first layer to represent the conditional k-gram model, and single-head models were reported to perform no better than bigram baselines. The present paper overturns this by showing that adding just one more layer (3 total) with layer normalization and a feedforward layer allows a single head to capture arbitrary-order Markov dependencies (Theorem 4). The attention-only logarithmic-depth construction (Theorem 3) also improves on the linear-in-k head requirement by a factor of k/log k. The empirical results in Figure 3 confirm that 2-layer 1-head models learn k=4 Markov processes and 3-layer 1-head models learn k=8, both well beyond the k=2 ceiling suggested by prior single-head experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022) and the study of transformers learning Markov chains (Edelman et al., 2024; Nichani et al., 2024), which established that 2-layer transformers require k heads to represent conditional k-gram models. The key departure is showing that non-linearities—specifically layer normalization—enable a fundamentally different mechanism (L2-norm-based attention) that allows constant-depth, single-head transformers to represent conditional k-grams for arbitrary k, bypassing the linear-in-k head requirement. The paper also provides a logarithmic-depth construction for attention-only transformers (composing induction heads) and a conditional lower bound showing that without non-linearities, attention-only transformers cannot break past the depth-head tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reframes the depth-heads-non-linearity tradeoff in transformers, showing that layer normalization is not merely a training stabilization trick but plays a critical representational role in enabling constant-depth context tracking. The conditional lower bound (Theorem 6) clarifies that the constant-depth result is genuinely due to non-linearities rather than an artifact of the construction. Open questions include whether the specific 3-layer construction is what transformers actually discover during gradient-based training, whether the results extend beyond Markov sources to more complex generative processes, and whether the empirical gap between attention-only and full transformers on larger k values is fully explained by the layer-normalization mechanism.
