## Problem

Flow-based generative models produce straighter sampling trajectories than diffusion models, yet efficient sampling methods for them remain largely unexplored—current approaches rely on generic numerical solvers (Euler, Heun) that fail to exploit the unique stability properties of the learned velocity field. Meanwhile, one-step distillation alternatives are prohibitively expensive (>100 GPU days) and destroy the multi-step sampling paradigm needed for downstream tasks like inpainting and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240135/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on pre-trained flow-based models (SiT-XL for 256×256 class-conditional ImageNet generation; InstaFlow 2-RF for 512×512 text-to-image on MS COCO; Lumina-Next-T2I for 1024×1024). At each sampling step, the velocity refiner receives the current intermediate latent x_{t_i}, the previous step's velocity v_{t_{i-1}}, and the timestep t_i, outputting a small velocity offset. Because FlowTurbo preserves the multi-step ODE-sampling structure, it composes directly with existing pipelines for inpainting (mask-merging at a chosen timestep), SDEdit-based editing, and object removal via Grounded-SAM masks.

## Method

The key observation is that the velocity predictor v_θ in flow-based models outputs a 'stable value' during sampling (its training target ε − x_0 is time-independent under the linear interpolant), unlike the highly variable noise prediction ε_θ in diffusion models. Exploiting this, FlowTurbo trains a lightweight velocity refiner r_φ (only ~5% of v_θ's parameters, e.g., a single SiT-XL block at 29M vs. 675M) to regress the velocity offset v_{t_i} − v_{t_{i-1}}, trained in under 6 GPU hours on pure images via a one-step simulated intermediate. Two additional techniques further cut inference cost: a pseudo corrector that reuses the corrector-step velocity from the previous iteration (halving model evaluations per step while provably retaining 2nd-order convergence, as shown in Appendix B), and sample-aware compilation that fuses the forward pass, sampling arithmetic, and classifier-free guidance into a single static graph per sample block. The three block types (Heun H, pseudo corrector P, refiner R) are arranged in the order H→P→R, with the number of each tuned per task to trade off FID against latency.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, FlowTurbo achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 2.12 at 100 ms/img (config H8P9R5), outperforming SiT-XL (FID 2.20 at 250 ms/img) and DiT-XL (FID 2.27 at 6914 ms/img). At the fastest setting (H1P5R3), FlowTurbo reaches 38 ms/img with FID 3.93, enabling real-time generation while still beating Mask-GIT (FID 6.18) and ADM (FID 10.94). On MS COCO 2017 text-to-image generation, FlowTurbo accelerates inference by 29.8%–38.5% over Heun's method while matching or improving FID (e.g., FID 28.60 at 104.8 ms/img vs. 28.61 at 170.5 ms/img).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowTurbo (H8P9R5) achieves the best FID of 2.12, beating SiT-XL (2.20), DiT-XL (2.27), and StyleGAN-XL (2.30) while running 2.5× faster than SiT-XL (100 vs. 250 ms/img) and 69× faster than DiT-XL. At the speed-focused setting (H1P5R3, 38 ms/img), FlowTurbo is 3.2× faster than StyleGAN-XL (190 ms/img) and 3.2× faster than Mask-GIT (120 ms/img), with FID 3.93 still well below Mask-GIT (6.18) and ADM (10.94). On text-to-image (MS COCO 2017), FlowTurbo (H1P6R3) matches Heun's method FID (28.60 vs. 28.61) at 38.5% lower latency, and outperforms SD2.1 (FID 33.03), SDXL (29.46), PixArt-α (37.96), and PixArt-σ (33.62) on both FID and speed in Table 7. The only trade-off is that at the fastest 38 ms/img setting, FlowTurbo's FID (3.93) is slightly worse than SiT-XL's 25-step Heun's (2.20), but it still surpasses all GAN and autoregressive baselines at that speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowTurbo sits at the intersection of flow-based generative modeling (SiT, InstaFlow, rectified flow) and fast ODE sampling. While diffusion models have benefited from a rich ecosystem of accelerated samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC, consistency distillation), flow-based models have largely relied on vanilla Euler or Heun steps. The paper departs from the one-step distillation paradigm (InstaFlow, DMD) by preserving the multi-step sampling structure, and builds directly on the authors' prior UniPC work to motivate the pseudo corrector. The core conceptual departure is exploiting a property unique to flow-based models—the stability of the velocity field during sampling—that has no direct analogue in diffusion models, enabling a lightweight refiner where a full re-evaluation would be needed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowTurbo establishes a practical path to real-time flow-based image generation (38 ms/img) with a velocity refiner that trains in under 6 GPU hours on a single A800, in stark contrast to distillation methods requiring >100 GPU days. By preserving the multi-step paradigm, it retains capabilities (inpainting, editing, object removal) that one-step distilled models lose, making it a drop-in accelerator for the growing ecosystem of flow-based models (SD3, FLUX, Lumina). The key open question is whether the velocity-stability assumption generalizes beyond linear-interpolant flow models to more exotic probability paths, and whether analogous 'stable value' observations can be found in diffusion models to enable similar acceleration there.
