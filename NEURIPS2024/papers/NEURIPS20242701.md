## Problem

Existing PTQ preprocessing methods (e.g., AWQ, QuIP, SmoothQuant) apply linear transformations to weights to suppress outliers and reduce magnitudes, but preserving layer output requires applying the inverse transformation to activations at inference time, introducing computational and memory overhead that offsets quantization benefits. The paper asks whether weights can be effectively preprocessed to facilitate quantization without any such inference-time cost.

## Input / Output

MagR takes a pre-trained weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} for a single linear layer and a calibration feature matrix X ∈ R^{(b·l)×m} (obtained by propagating calibration data through already-quantized layers) as input. It produces a preprocessed weight matrix with reduced channel-wise (column-wise) maximum magnitude, which is then passed to a standard quantizer (RTN or OPTQ). The transformation is non-linear and acts only on weights, so the original feature/activation pipeline remains unchanged at inference time.

## Method

MagR solves a channel-wise ℓ∞-regularized least-squares problem: min_w (1/2)||Xw − Xŵ||² + α||w||∞, which finds new weights with minimal maximum magnitude while preserving the layer output up to O(√α) error. The key insight is that the feature matrix X in LLMs is approximately rank-deficient (fraction rank often well below 100%), so infinitely many weight vectors preserve the layer output, and MagR selects the one with the smallest extreme magnitude. The ℓ∞-regularized problem is solved via proximal gradient descent, where the proximal operator of the ℓ∞ norm is evaluated using Moreau decomposition, reducing each iteration to a column-wise ℓ₁-ball projection computed in O(m log m) via sorting and soft-thresholding. Because the transformation is non-linear and applied only to weights, no inverse transformation or post-processing is needed at inference, eliminating all overhead compared to linear-transformation-based baselines.

## Results

On LLaMA2-70B, MagR+OPTQ† achieves a Wikitext2 perplexity of 5.95 for per-channel INT2 weight quantization, outperforming QuIP (6.33) and OmniQuant (7.81) while introducing zero inference overhead. For per-group INT2 (g128), MagR+OPTQ reaches 5.52 PPL, beating OmniQuant (6.55). At INT3 and INT4 per-channel, MagR+OPTQ achieves 3.82 and 3.44 PPL respectively on LLaMA2-70B, surpassing both OmniQuant and QuIP. The method also improves zero-shot task accuracy, with MagR+OPTQ† scoring 59.2% average on LLaMA2-70B INT2, close to QuIP's 59.6% but without any inference-time transformation overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPTQ, MagR+OPTQ consistently improves perplexity across all bit-widths and model sizes (e.g., 3.82 vs 4.82 on LLaMA2-70B INT3, 3.44 vs 3.58 on INT4), confirming that the ℓ∞-regularized preprocessing is a strict improvement over the unmodified Hessian-based quantization. Against OmniQuant, MagR+OPTQ wins on INT2 per-channel (5.95 vs 7.81 on 70B), INT2 per-group (5.52 vs 6.55), INT3 per-channel (3.82 vs 3.92), and INT4 per-channel (3.44 vs 3.47), with the gap being largest at extreme low bit-widths. Against QuIP, MagR+OPTQ† achieves lower perplexity at INT2 (5.95 vs 6.33 on 70B) and INT3 (3.82 vs 3.85), while MagR+OPTQ beats QuIP at INT4 (3.44 vs 3.53); critically, MagR introduces no inference overhead whereas QuIP requires random orthogonal transformations that slow throughput by 1.5×. Against AWQ, MagR+RTN substantially outperforms AWQ at INT3 per-channel (4.64 vs 10.45 on LLaMA2-7B), demonstrating that the non-linear ℓ∞-regularized preprocessing is more effective than AWQ's channel-wise scaling. Against plain RTN, MagR+RTN yields dramatic improvements at INT3 (e.g., 4.64 vs 7.52 on 70B) and INT2, showing that even the simplest quantizer benefits greatly from the magnitude-reduction preprocessing.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagR carves new ground by replacing the dominant linear-transformation paradigm in PTQ preprocessing (AWQ's channel-wise scaling, QuIP's random orthogonal transforms, AffineQuant's affine maps) with a non-linear, optimization-based weight modification. It draws on the ℓ∞-regularization lineage of R² loss [20] and the ℓ0/ℓ∞ formulation of [27], but departs from both by applying the regularization as a one-shot preprocessing step solved via proximal gradient descent with ℓ1-ball projections, rather than during end-to-end training or as an intractable combinatorial problem. The method is designed to compose with existing gradient-free PTQ solvers (RTN, OPTQ) rather than replace them, and its key conceptual departure is that the non-linear nature of the transformation eliminates the need for any inverse transformation on activations at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that MagR removes the inference-time overhead (extra matrix multiplications, stored transformation matrices, 1.5× throughput loss) that has been the persistent cost of state-of-the-art PTQ preprocessing, making extreme-bit quantization (INT2) deployable without architectural modifications. The rank-deficiency observation for LLaMA feature matrices provides a clean theoretical justification for why the optimization has many solutions, but it remains unclear whether this property holds for non-LLaMA architectures or for activation quantization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to mixed-precision or activation quantization settings, and whether the mild sensitivity to α and β (requiring per-bit-width tuning) limits its plug-and-play applicability.
