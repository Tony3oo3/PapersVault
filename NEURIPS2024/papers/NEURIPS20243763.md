## Problem

Mamba models, a rapidly adopted class of selective state space sequence models, lack faithful explanation methods. Existing LRP procedures do not account for Mamba-specific components (SiLU activations, the selective SSM, and multiplicative gating), causing the conservation axiom to be violated and producing unreliable attributions. Attention-based alternatives inherit known misalignment issues between tokens and attention scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MambaLRP consumes a single forward pass of a Mamba or Vision Mamba model on a text sequence or RGB image, then performs one modified backward pass to produce per-token relevance scores (text) or per-pixel relevance heatmaps (images). The output satisfies the conservation property: the sum of input-level relevance scores equals the network's output logit for the predicted class. Computationally, it requires only one forward and one backward pass, making its runtime comparable to Gradient × Input and far cheaper than multi-step methods like Integrated Gradients or SmoothGrad.

## Method

MambaLRP identifies three Mamba-specific components where the LRP conservation axiom breaks under standard gradient propagation: the SiLU activation, the selective SSM recurrence, and the multiplicative output gate. For each, it proposes a locally linearized propagation rule grounded in the conservation principle: (1) SiLU is expanded by treating the sigmoid term as constant via a detach operation; (2) the selective SSM's input-dependent parameters (Ā_t, B̄_t, C_t) are treated as constant at each time step, effectively viewing the selective SSM as a localized non-selective SSM; (3) the multiplicative gate output is split into a half-linear and half-constant term to avoid spurious relevance doubling. The full algorithm is implemented as a modified Gradient × Input pass where the specified detach operations are applied before backpropagation, yielding a single-pass, theoretically sound, and computationally efficient explanation method.

## Results

MambaLRP achieves the highest faithfulness score (ΔA^F) across all evaluated datasets and model sizes. On SST-2 with Mamba-130M, it scores 1.978, outperforming the strongest baseline MambaAttr (1.190) by 66%. On ImageNet with Vim-S, MambaLRP reaches 4.715 versus 2.676 for MambaAttr and 2.548 for LRP(LN-rule). The method also satisfies the relevance conservation property (output score ≈ sum of input relevances), which is severely violated by the GI baseline. Runtime is comparable to Gradient×Input (0.43s vs. 0.76s per sample on Mamba-130M without CUDA kernels), while methods requiring multiple forward passes (SmoothGrad, IG) are ~50× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaLRP outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and model size tested. Against the strongest gradient-based baseline, Integrated Gradients, MambaLRP gains +1.12 on SST-2 (1.978 vs. 0.857) and +3.50 on ImageNet (4.715 vs. 1.217). Against the strongest attention-based baseline, MambaAttr, the margin is +0.79 on SST-2 and +2.04 on ImageNet. The closest overall competitor is LRP(LN-rule) (naive LRP with LN propagation), which MambaLRP surpasses by +1.10 on SST-2 and +2.17 on ImageNet, confirming that the Mamba-specific conservation fixes (SiLU detach, SSM parameter detach, half-relevance gate) are essential. GI and SmoothGrad are consistently the weakest, with GI producing negative faithfulness scores on larger models (e.g., −1.434 on Med-BIOS with Mamba-2.8B), indicating that naive gradient-based attribution is unreliable for Mamba architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) framework, originally developed for CNNs and later adapted to Transformers (Ali et al., 2022), to the Mamba selective state space model architecture. It builds on the conservation axiom from Bach et al. (2015) and the LRP-γ rules for convolutions (Dombrowski et al., 2022), but identifies three Mamba-specific components—SiLU activation, the selective SSM recurrence, and multiplicative gating—where naive gradient-based propagation violates conservation. The key departure is from attention-based explanation methods (AttnRoll, MambaAttr) that treat Mamba's internal dynamics as an implicit attention mechanism, which the authors argue inherit known misalignment problems between attention scores and true feature relevance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba models are rapidly adopted in high-stakes domains (medical imaging, language modeling, genomics), the absence of faithful, architecture-aware explanation methods is a critical gap for trust and deployment. MambaLRP provides a theoretically grounded, single-pass solution that outperforms both gradient-based and attention-based baselines by substantial margins, and its use cases (gender bias detection, long-range dependency verification, Clever-Hans identification via XRA) demonstrate concrete debugging value. Open questions include generalization to other SSM variants (e.g., Mamba-2, H3), memory constraints for very long sequences, and applicability when gradient access is restricted by proprietary model providers.
