## Problem

Penalizing the nuclear norm of a function's Jacobian is a natural regularizer for encouraging locally low-rank behavior, but it is computationally intractable for high-dimensional deep learning problems because it requires forming the full Jacobian matrix and computing its SVD. This paper resolves that bottleneck by proving an exact equivalence that replaces the nuclear norm with two squared Frobenius norms of sub-function Jacobians, and then further approximates those with a denoising-style objective that avoids Jacobian computation entirely.

## Input / Output

For unsupervised denoising, the method takes 128×128 noisy RGB images (ImageNet training set) corrupted with Gaussian noise at σ∈{1,2} and produces denoised images, trained exclusively on noisy inputs without access to clean counterparts. For representation learning, it takes 256×256 CelebA face images (reduced to 80 DCT coefficients), encodes them into a 700-dimensional latent vector, and decodes back to images. The regularizer itself requires only two additional forward evaluations of the network's sub-functions per sample, making it compatible with standard training loops.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that for any function parametrized as a composition f = g∘h, minimizing a loss plus the Jacobian nuclear norm ‖Jf[x]‖_* is exactly equivalent to minimizing the same loss plus ½(‖Jg[h(x)]‖²_F + ‖Jh[x]‖²_F), eliminating the need for an SVD. To avoid even computing these Jacobians, the paper applies a first-order Taylor expansion combined with Hutchinson's trace estimator to show that σ²‖Jf[x]‖²_F ≈ E_{ε~N(0,σ²I)}[‖f(x+ε)−f(x)‖²], yielding a final regularizer that costs only two extra forward passes per sample. In practice, the denoiser is a U-Net split into h (downsampling) and g (upsampling), trained with η=σ² on purely noisy ImageNet images; the autoencoder uses an MLP encoder with a UNet decoder on CelebA. The approach is validated on the Rudin-Osher-Fatemi problem where closed-form solutions exist, confirming that the Jacobian-free regularizer recovers the same solutions as direct nuclear norm penalization.

## Results

On the unsupervised denoising task (trained exclusively on noisy images), the proposed Jacobian nuclear norm regularizer achieves 23.10 dB PSNR on ImageNet (σ=1) and 21.05 dB (σ=2), and 21.08 dB on CBSD68 (σ=1) and 19.10 dB (σ=2). These results nearly match a fully supervised denoiser (23.37 / 21.39 dB on ImageNet) despite having no access to clean images during training, and substantially outperform the classical BM3D baseline (21.26 / 18.71 dB on ImageNet) by roughly 1.8–2.3 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines on the unsupervised denoising task. Against BM3D (a classical unsupervised denoiser), the proposed method wins decisively by ~1.8 dB on ImageNet (σ=1) and ~2.3 dB (σ=2), and by ~1.7–2.3 dB on CBSD68. Against Noise2Noise (N2N), which requires access to independent noisy copies of each ground truth image during training, the proposed method is essentially tied on ImageNet (23.10 vs. 23.12 at σ=1; 21.05 vs. 21.21 at σ=2) and slightly ahead on CBSD68 at σ=1 (21.08 vs. 20.37) but slightly behind at σ=2 (19.10 vs. 19.37). Against the supervised upper bound (trained on clean–noisy pairs with standard MSE loss), the proposed method trails by only 0.27 dB on ImageNet (σ=1) and 0.34 dB (σ=2), and by 0.54 / 0.44 dB on CBSD68 — a remarkably small gap given that the proposed method never sees a clean image during training. The paper also validates its regularizer on the ROF denoising problem (Section 4), where the Jacobian-free formulation (Eq. 9) converges to nearly identical objective values as the exact nuclear-norm formulation (Eq. 8), confirming the theoretical equivalence of Theorem 3.1 in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Rennie–Srebro (2005) identity—which decomposes the nuclear norm of a matrix into a minimum over squared Frobenius norms of its factors—from linear matrix factorization to non-linear composed functions f = g∘h, proving exact equivalence (Theorem 3.1). It then leverages Hutchinson's (1989) stochastic trace estimator to replace even the remaining Jacobian computations with two extra function evaluations, yielding a regularizer that costs O(1) additional forward passes. The work sits at the intersection of Jacobian regularization in deep learning (Sokolić, Varga, Hoffman), denoising autoencoders (Vincent, Rifai, Alain & Bengio), and singular-value-shrinkage denoising (Gavish & Donoho), unifying these threads under a single nuclear-norm framework that was previously intractable for high-dimensional maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method makes Jacobian nuclear norm regularization practical for high-dimensional deep learning by reducing it to two extra forward evaluations, opening the door to applications (unsupervised denoising, representation learning) where the low-rank Jacobian prior is natural but the naive SVD-based penalty is infeasible. The main limitation is the Hutchinson estimator's approximation error, which produces slightly diffuse boundaries in the ROF validation and may matter in precision-critical settings; more accurate estimators are a natural future direction. An open question is whether the equivalence theorem extends to stochastic or non-smooth architectures, and whether the regularizer's benefits persist at much larger scales (e.g., 1024×1024 images) where the current experiments use 128×128 crops.
