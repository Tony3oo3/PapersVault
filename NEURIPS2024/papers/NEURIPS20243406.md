## Problem

Existing 3D human interaction datasets are captured in controlled indoor labs with choreographed actions and limited subject/environment diversity, causing learned methods to fail on real-world contact scenarios. The core technical barrier is that recovering accurate ground-truth meshes under severe occlusion, truncation, and close proximity has required either dense RGBD systems or 100+ wired cameras, making in-the-wild collection impractical. The paper asks whether a portable, markerless multi-camera system with a few cameras can produce reliable 3D mesh annotations for unchoreographed contact interactions.

## Input / Output

The capture system uses 20 synchronized GoPro cameras recording at 4K (3840×2160) and 60 FPS, optionally supplemented by Meta Aria glasses for egocentric views, all calibrated via SfM into a gravity-aligned metric world frame. The annotation pipeline outputs per-frame SMPL parameters (23-joint 6D pose, 10-dim PCA shape, 6-dim global transform), 17 2D/3D keypoints, per-view instance segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and persistent person IDs across 208 sequences totaling 1.66M images and 3.32M human instances. Each sequence contains exactly two subjects performing unchoreographed activities (wrestling, dancing, MMA, karate, fencing) across five distinct environments.

## Method

The annotation pipeline splits each sequence into pre-contact (using EgoHumans' multi-person 3D pose estimation) and post-contact stages. For post-contact, a sequential loop combines: (1) per-keypoint Kalman filters trained in a sliding window to forecast 3D poses through occlusion, (2) SAM point-conditioned instance segmentation where projected forecasted 3D poses serve as positive/negative prompts to isolate each subject's mask, (3) SegPose2D—a ViTPose-H backbone with dual transformer branches and multi-depth feature fusion trained on COCO with GT masks—to estimate 2D poses conditioned on the segmentation mask, disambiguating overlapping joints, and (4) RANSAC-based multi-view triangulation to recover global 3D poses. Mesh fitting then optimizes SMPL parameters against a composite loss including keypoint reprojection, limb-length, symmetry, temporal smoothing, shape prior, and a novel interpenetration loss computed via SDF that penalizes vertex penetration between the two subjects' meshes, reducing mP2S by 44.7 mm on average.

## Results

On the Harmony4D test set, the best off-the-shelf method (Multi-HMR) achieves an MPJPE of 93.82 mm and PVE of 115.79 mm, while the best top-down method (HMR2.0) reaches 108.18 mm MPJPE and 131.00 mm PVE. After fine-tuning HMR2.0 on the Harmony4D train set (>1.2M images), performance improves dramatically to 47.71 mm MPJPE (−55.9%), 59.14 mm PVE (−54.8%), and 96.63% 3DPCK (+21.01%), substantially outperforming all baselines including BUDDI, which was specifically designed for human-human contact modeling. The interpenetration loss in mesh optimization reduces maximum point-to-surface distance by 44.7 mm on average across activities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among off-the-shelf methods, Multi-HMR (bottom-up) is the strongest baseline at 93.82 mm MPJPE, outperforming BUDDI (126.35 mm) despite BUDDI being specifically designed for human-human contact reasoning. HMR2.0 is the best top-down method (108.18 mm MPJPE), benefiting from its large-scale training and ViTPose transformer backbone, while PARE (119.03 mm) and ROMP (121.13 mm) trail behind. The fine-tuned HMR2.0 achieves 47.71 mm MPJPE, beating Multi-HMR by 46.11 mm (49.2% lower) and BUDDI by 78.64 mm (62.2% lower), and improving 3DPCK from 83.57% to 96.63%. Notably, the fine-tuned model also achieves the lowest N-PVE (59.74 vs. 125.85 for Multi-HMR), indicating far better 3D mesh consistency under severe occlusion. The paper concludes that the limitation is not architectural but data-driven: exposing general-purpose methods to sufficient in-the-wild contact data closes the gap with purpose-built contact models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EgoHumans multi-view capture and annotation pipeline to handle the post-contact regime, where severe occlusion and joint ambiguity break existing pose estimation. It departs from prior contact-interaction datasets (Hi4D, CHI3D, MultiHuman, ExPI) that rely on controlled indoor RGBD or motion-capture rigs by using a portable 20-GoPro setup with SAM-based instance segmentation, Kalman-filter pose forecasting, and a novel SegPose2D (ViTPose conditioned on segmentation masks) to extract ground-truth SMPL meshes in the wild. For evaluation, it benchmarks against the standard monocular mesh recovery family (HMR2.0, Multi-HMR, PARE, BEV, ROMP) and the contact-specific BUDDI, positioning Harmony4D as the first in-the-wild benchmark for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Harmony4D fills a clear gap: no prior dataset combines in-the-wild environments, unchoreographed dynamic contact (wrestling, MMA, karate), and vertex-level mesh ground truth at this scale (1.66M images, 3.32M instances). The empirical finding that a general method (HMR2.0) fine-tuned on this data outperforms the purpose-built BUDDI model suggests the field's bottleneck is data diversity rather than architectural specialization, reframing how contact-aware mesh recovery should be approached. Limitations include the two-subject-per-sequence constraint, the trade-off between in-the-wild portability and annotation precision, and the reliance on manual verification of mesh sequences. Open questions include whether the annotation quality is sufficient for end-to-end training without further refinement, and whether the pipeline scales beyond two interacting subjects.
