## Problem

Causal (autoregressive) attention is the dominant architecture in generative AI transformers, yet its dynamics cannot be analyzed with the mean-field gradient-flow techniques that successfully explained clustering in full attention. The causal mask breaks the symmetry between tokens, turning the system into a non-mean-field interacting particle system with no underlying potential function, leaving the asymptotic behavior and meta-stable clustering of tokens theoretically uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243672/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes n initial token positions on the unit sphere S^{d-1} (constrained by RMSNorm), along with d×d Key, Query, and Value matrices and a temperature parameter β, and evolves them via coupled ODEs where token k interacts only with tokens j ≤ k. The output is the asymptotic cluster configuration (a single point, two antipodal points, or point clouds around eigenspaces of V) together with the identification of meta-stable cluster centers as Rényi parking centers. The time parameter corresponds to network depth, and the key practical quantity is the number of meta-stable clusters, which scales as β^{(d-1)/2} for d=2.

## Method

The paper models causal self-attention as a hierarchical interacting particle system on the sphere and exploits the sequential structure (token k depends only on tokens 1,…,k) to prove convergence via induction and the center-stable manifold theorem, circumventing the inapplicable Lojasiewicz inequality. The main theorem (Theorem 4.1) establishes that for V=I_d and arbitrary K, Q, all tokens converge to the first token's initial position, with assumptions strictly weaker than prior full-attention results. For meta-stable clustering, the paper introduces Rényi centers and strong Rényi centers as analogues of the classical Rényi parking process, proving that strong Rényi centers remain quasi-stationary for exponential time (Lemma 2) and that all remaining tokens converge to frozen centers (Theorem 5.1). The convergence proof for Theorem 5.1 uses a novel sequential gradient-flow argument (Lemma 3) showing that each particle minimizes a slightly different energy, with convergence established via L₁/L₂ integrability of velocity and isolated critical point structure.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for causal self-attention with V = I_d and arbitrary Key/Query matrices, all n tokens converge to a single cluster at the position of the first token, for almost every initialization. Lemma 2 establishes that strong Rényi centers (occurring with frequency Θ(β^((d-1)/2))) remain quasi-stationary for time scales of order exp(c²/2), where c is the separation parameter. Theorem 5.1 shows that when quasi-stationary tokens are frozen, all remaining tokens converge to their vicinity within εβ^{-1/2} distance, requiring only β ≳ log N. ## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the standard empirical sense; its contribution is theoretical. The key comparison is with Geshkovski et al. (2023b), which proved single-cluster convergence for full (unmasked) attention but required the restrictive condition Q^T K = V (or Q^T K = I_d in Markdahl et al. 2017). The present work removes these constraints entirely, proving convergence for arbitrary K and Q matrices in the causal setting, and additionally handles the non-gradient-flow structure that arises from causal masking. The paper also extends the metastability analysis initiated by Koubbi et al. (2024) and Bruno et al. (2024) for full attention to the causal case, introducing the Rényi parking framework to identify cluster centers with Θ(β^((d-1)/2)) scaling. A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Theorem 5.1 (frozen-token clustering) does not yet bridge the gap to full metastability, as it provides no convergence-time bounds to guarantee centers remain stationary during particle aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interacting-particle-system framework for self-attention dynamics established by Geshkovski et al. (2023b) from full (unmasked) attention to causal attention, the dominant paradigm in decoder-based generative AI architectures. The key conceptual departure is that causal masking breaks the mean-field gradient-flow structure that underpinned prior proofs via the Lojasiewicz inequality, forcing the authors to develop a sequential analysis using the center-stable manifold theorem instead. The paper also introduces a novel cross-disciplinary connection to the classical Rényi parking problem from combinatorial geometry to characterize meta-stable cluster centers, a link absent from the full-attention literature. It builds on the measure-to-measure map perspective of Sander et al. (2022) and complements recent metastability results for full attention by Koubbi et al. (2024) and Bruno et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that only the spectral properties of the Value matrix V (not Q, K, or temperature β) determine the asymptotic token configuration is a clean and practically useful simplification for understanding transformer dynamics, and the meta-stable clustering prediction of Θ(β^((d-1)/2)) centers provides a concrete, testable framework for interpreting finite-depth behavior. However, the main convergence theorem (Theorem 4.1) is restricted to V = I_d, with the general case remaining conjectural due to technical obstacles with critical manifolds; the omission of MLP layers and the use of tied weights across layers further limit direct applicability to real architectures. Open questions include proving the conjectures for general V, extending the Rényi parking analysis to higher dimensions and non-uniform distributions, and connecting top eigenspaces of V to semantic structure in pre-trained models.
