## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of PINN variants—spanning loss reweighting, domain decomposition, adaptive activations, and variational formulations—no comprehensive, standardized benchmark exists to compare these methods across a diverse range of PDE types and real-world challenges. Existing comparisons focus on narrow aspects (e.g., sampling strategies or reweighting techniques) or evaluate only a handful of PDEs, leaving researchers without a clear picture of which method suits which class of problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242441/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark task is specified by a PDE governing equation, boundary/initial conditions, and domain geometry (including complex shapes with holes or multi-domain interfaces), rather than by raw data. Reference solutions are generated via FEM solvers in COMSOL 6.0 (for complex-geometry and time-dependent problems) or spectral methods in Chebfun (for chaotic PDEs), with mesh convergence verified via Richardson extrapolation to below 0.1% error. The benchmark outputs per-task L2RE, L1RE, MSE, max error, and Fourier error for each method, enabling multi-metric comparison across all 22 PDE cases and 10+ variants.

## Method

PINNacle is a three-component benchmark: (1) a dataset of 22 PDE problems spanning eight categories (Burgers, Poisson, Heat, Navier-Stokes, Wave, Chaotic, High-dimensional, Inverse) that collectively stress-test four core challenges—complex geometry, multi-scale phenomena, nonlinearity, and high dimensionality; (2) a DeepXDE-based toolbox implementing 10+ SOTA PINN variants organized into four families (loss reweighting/resampling: LRA, NTK, RAR; novel optimizer: MultiAdam; novel loss functions: gPINN, hp-VPINN; novel architectures: LAAF, GAAF, FBPINN), with an adaptive multi-GPU parallel training framework; and (3) standardized evaluation using five metrics. The key empirical finding is that no single variant dominates: domain decomposition (FBPINN) excels on complex geometry and chaotic PDEs, NTK reweighting is most robust for parameterized PDEs, variational formulation (hp-VPINN) is best for inverse problems, and all methods fail on long-time and high-dimensional nonlinear cases, identifying these as open challenges.

## Results

Across 22 PDE tasks, vanilla PINN achieves <10% L2RE on only 10 of 22 problems, with its best result being 1.45% on Burgers-1d-C. No single variant dominates all tasks: PINN-NTK leads on linear PDEs (1.23% on Poisson-2d-C, 9.79% on Wave-1d-C), LAAF excels on complex geometry (2.39% on Heat-2d-CG, 3.60% on NS-2d-C), FBPINN is best on chaotic dynamics (7.99% on Gray-Scott), vPINN dominates inverse problems (2.45% on PInv), and LBFGS is best on high-dimensional PDEs (4.67E-4 on PNd, 1.19E-4 on HNd). All methods fail catastrophically (~100% L2RE) on long-time problems (Heat-2d-LT, NS-2d-LT) and the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, indicating that nonlinear and long-time PDEs remain unsolved by current PINN variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single PINN variant wins across all 22 tasks; instead, each method category excels on specific challenge types. PINN-NTK (neural tangent kernel reweighting) achieves the best L2RE on linear PDEs with complex geometry (1.23% on Poisson-2d-C, 1.43% on Poisson-2d-CG, 9.79% on Wave-1d-C), outperforming vanilla PINN by 50–500× on these tasks. LAAF (locally adaptive activation functions) leads on complex-geometry and nonlinear flow problems (2.39% on Heat-2d-CG, 3.60% on NS-2d-C), beating vanilla PINN by 1.5–5×. FBPINN (finite basis domain decomposition) is the only method to achieve <10% on the chaotic Gray-Scott equation (7.99%), while vPINN (variational PINN) dominates both inverse problems (2.45% on PInv, 4.56% on HInv), outperforming vanilla PINN by 4–3×. LBFGS (second-order optimizer) is uniquely effective on high-dimensional PDEs, achieving 4.67E-4 on 5D Poisson and 1.19E-4 on 5D Heat, orders of magnitude better than all first-order methods. Critically, all 12 methods fail on long-time problems (Heat-2d-LT, NS-2d-LT) and the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (~100% L2RE), and none adequately solve the 2D Burgers or Navier-Stokes equations below 25% error, highlighting that nonlinear and long-time PDEs remain open challenges for the entire PINN family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the foundational PINN formulation of Raissi et al. (2019) and the DeepXDE library (Lu et al., 2021), unifying approximately 10 PINN variants spanning loss reweighting (LRA, NTK, RAR), novel optimizers (MultiAdam), novel loss functions (gPINN, hp-VPINN), and novel architectures (LAAF, GAAF, FBPINN) under a single evaluation framework. It departs from prior PDE benchmarks (PDEBench, PDEArena) which target neural operators and time-dependent PDEs broadly, by focusing specifically on PINN variants and curating 22 PDEs that explicitly stress-test four challenge axes: complex geometry, multi-scale phenomena, nonlinearity, and high dimensionality. The paper also extends the comparison scope beyond what Wang et al. [56] covered, adding chaotic PDEs, high-dimensional problems, and inverse problems that were absent from prior PINN-focused evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINNacle provides the first comprehensive, reproducible benchmark specifically for PINN variants, giving the community a standardized reference point that was previously absent. Key open questions it surfaces include: no method adequately handles strongly nonlinear or chaotic PDEs (KS, 2D Burgers), all methods fail on long-time integration (Heat2d-LT, NS2d-LT), and high-dimensional problems remain largely unsolved except by second-order optimizers. The authors note that real-world problems with larger geometric domains or more complex chaotic behavior will be even harder, and that theoretical convergence guarantees for PINNs remain lacking, pointing toward hybrid approaches combining neural networks with traditional numerical techniques (preconditioning, multigrid, weak formulations) as a promising direction.
