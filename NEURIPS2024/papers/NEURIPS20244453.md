## Problem

No publicly available compound-gene graph dataset exists that combines empirical node features with interaction annotations, making it impossible to benchmark inductive link prediction for drug-target interaction (DTI) under realistic cold-start and zero-shot scenarios. Existing graph benchmarks either lack information-rich features, rely on metadata-based splits rather than graph-based ones, or do not support mini-batch training on large-scale graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244453/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each compound node is represented by a 737-dimensional vector and each gene node by a 722-dimensional vector, both extracted from JUMP Cell Painting fluorescence microscopy images via CellProfiler and batch-corrected with Harmony. The interaction graph contains 11,509 genes, 3,632 compounds, and 303,156 undirected edges of three types (compound-compound, compound-gene, gene-gene) aggregated from seven databases. The model outputs a sigmoid-transformed dot-product score for each source-target pair, evaluated under random, cold-source, and cold-target splits with rank-based metrics (Hits@500, Precision@500) alongside F1.

## Method

The paper introduces MOTIVE, a heterogeneous graph where both compounds and genes share the same representational modality (Cell Painting morphological profiles), enabling cross-modal relationship discovery without modality mismatch. Three GNN architectures (GraphSAGE, GIN, GATv2) are instantiated with CP features as fixed node inputs; each model maps source and target embeddings into a shared space via linear layers, applies two GNN message-passing layers with a skip connection from the first layer, and scores edges via a sigmoid dot-product head. A custom negative sampling algorithm guarantees isolation of cold-start nodes during training to prevent data leakage, and the st_expanded graph (all three edge types) is used by default to maximize available relational information. The key finding is that GIN_CP consistently outperforms feature-only models, topology-only models, and other GNN variants across all splits, demonstrating that combining empirical morphological features with graph structure is essential for reliable inductive DTI prediction.

## Results

On the random (transductive) split, GIN_CP achieves the best performance with F1=0.524, Hits@500=0.455, and Precision@500=0.992, substantially outperforming the feature-only MLP (F1=0.383) and the graph-only GraphSAGE_embs (F1=0.346). On the cold-target inductive split, GIN_CP reaches F1=0.392 and Precision@500=0.962, while on the more challenging cold-source split it achieves F1=0.283 and Precision@500=0.587. The non-learning shortest-path heuristic fails almost entirely (Hits@500=0.0025), confirming the task is nontrivial and requires learned representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven models across three input categories: GNN+CP (GIN_CP, GATv2_CP, GraphSAGE_CP), CP-only (MLP, Bilinear), and graph-only (GraphSAGE_embs, Shortest Path). GIN_CP dominates all other models on every metric in both the random and cold splits, with the largest margin over the feature-only MLP (e.g., +0.141 F1 on random split, +0.051 F1 on cold-target). GraphSAGE_embs (graph structure with random embeddings) achieves 84.8% Precision@500 on the random split, showing that relational structure alone carries substantial signal, but it cannot be applied to inductive splits where nodes are isolated. The Bilinear model is the weakest learning baseline (F1=0.170 on random split), indicating that simple linear similarity between CP feature vectors is insufficient. The shortest-path heuristic is essentially non-functional (Hits@500=0.0025), confirming that topological proximity alone does not predict DTI links in this heterogeneous graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prior Cell Painting–based DTI work (Rohban et al.'s image-profile matching, Herman et al.'s toxicity prediction, Iyer et al.'s transformer-based target prediction) by embedding morphological profiles as node features within a multi-database interaction graph and applying GNN message-passing to exploit relational structure. It also addresses specific gaps identified in the OGBL benchmarking framework—namely the lack of information-rich empirical features, the absence of cold-start data splits, and the need for mini-batch-compatible training—by providing all three in a single, unified resource. The negative sampling algorithm and cold-start split design are borrowed from recommendation-systems literature but adapted to prevent data leakage in inductive graph learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOTIVE is the first large-scale compound-gene graph dataset with empirical node features, enabling rigorous benchmarking of inductive and zero-shot DTI prediction that prior datasets could not support. The ablation showing that structural information begins to dominate as the graph densifies (reducing the relative contribution of CP features) raises an open question about the optimal graph sparsity for feature-driven models. Limitations include the potential for uninformative morphological profiles when perturbations produce minimal cellular changes, and the current restriction to a single modality per node type; the authors propose generative in-silico CP readouts and multimodal extensions as paths forward.
