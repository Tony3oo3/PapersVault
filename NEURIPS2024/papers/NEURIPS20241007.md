## Problem

Existing robust loss functions for label-noise mitigation (symmetric losses like MAE, asymmetric losses like AUL) suffer from underfitting due to overly strict symmetry conditions, while prior attempts to restrict network outputs to one-hot vectors lack quantitative, controllable guarantees. The paper addresses the gap of providing a simple, plug-and-play mechanism that rigorously constrains softmax outputs to approximate one-hot vectors with a provable, tunable error bound, thereby making any loss function noise-tolerant.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet/WebVision/Clothing1M) paired with potentially corrupted class labels. Rather than producing arbitrary points on the probability simplex, the modified softmax layer outputs probability vectors guaranteed to lie within an ε-ball of some one-hot vector, where ε = √(1−1/K)/(m+1) is controlled by a single hyperparameter m. In the semi-supervised variant, pseudo-labels are generated from confident predictions and combined with MixUp-augmented batches.

## Method

ε-softmax is a three-step modification to the standard softmax layer: compute standard softmax probabilities, add a hyperparameter m ≥ 0 to the maximum-probability entry, then normalize by (m+1). This guarantees the output is within ε = √(1−1/K)/(m+1) of a one-hot vector (Lemma 1), making any loss function approximately symmetric and thus noise-tolerant with a controllable excess risk bound under both symmetric and asymmetric noise (Theorem 1). The paper further proves All-k consistency for CE_ε (Theorem 2) and shows via gradient analysis that CE_ε acts as a soft early-stopping mechanism when the predicted class matches the target, reducing overfitting to noisy labels. To recover fitting ability on clean data, the authors combine CE_ε with MAE (CE_ε + MAE), which Lemma 3 shows preserves the robustness guarantee while the strict convexity of the combined loss overcomes the underfitting inherent in pure symmetric losses.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 0.8 symmetric noise, CE_ε+MAE achieves 58.96% test accuracy, substantially outperforming CE (18.95%), GCE (25.82%), and SCE (25.88%). On CIFAR-100 with 0.4 asymmetric noise, CE_ε+MAE reaches 48.51%, significantly exceeding all other methods (best competitor: NCE+AGCE at 44.15%). On clean CIFAR-10, CE_ε+MAE attains 91.40% accuracy, matching or exceeding standard CE (90.50%) and most robust baselines. On real-world WebVision, CE_ε+MAE achieves 71.32% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline LDR-KL (69.64%) by 1.68 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CE_ε+MAE and FL_ε+MAE consistently rank in the top-2 across nearly all noise settings on CIFAR-10/100, outperforming standard CE and FL by 30–40 points at 0.8 symmetric noise (58.96% vs. 18.95% on CIFAR-10). Among robust loss baselines, GCE and SCE degrade sharply at high noise rates (25.82% and 25.88% at η=0.8 symmetric on CIFAR-10), while CE_ε+MAE maintains 58.96%; on CIFAR-100 asymmetric noise (η=0.4), CE_ε+MAE (48.51%) exceeds NCE+AGCE (44.15%) and LDR-KL (41.94%) by 4–7 points. The APL variants (NCE+RCE, NCE+AGCE) are the closest competitors on CIFAR-10 symmetric noise (85.13–85.91% at η=0.4 vs. 85.93% for CE_ε+MAE), but CE_ε+MAE pulls ahead at η=0.8 (58.96% vs. 55.28% for NCE+RCE). On human-annotated CIFAR-10N 'Worst' and CIFAR-100N 'Noisy', CE_ε+MAE(Semi) achieves 95.12% and 71.97% respectively, surpassing the best SSL baselines DivideMix (92.56%, 71.13%) and SOP+ (93.24%, 67.81%). On real-world WebVision and ILSVRC12, CE_ε+MAE achieves the highest top-1 accuracy (71.32% and 67.20%), beating LDR-KL (69.64% and 65.24%) and AGCE (69.48% and 65.12%) by 1.5–2.0 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Zhou et al. (ICCV 2021), who showed that restricting the hypothesis class to one-hot vectors makes any loss function inherently symmetric and thus noise-tolerant. However, prior implementations of this idea (temperature-dependent softmax, sparseness constraints, sparse regularization, variance enlargement) lacked quantitative control over the approximation error. The key departure here is replacing those heuristic regularizations with a simple, closed-form modification to the softmax output (adding a hyperparameter m to the maximum element and renormalizing), yielding a provably controllable ε-approximation to one-hot vectors. The paper also positions itself against the dominant paradigm of designing new robust loss functions (GCE, SCE, AUL, AGCE, LDR-KL), arguing that modifying the output space rather than the loss function is a more principled and universally applicable lever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's extreme simplicity (two lines of code, plug-and-play into any softmax-based classifier) combined with rigorous theoretical guarantees (excess risk bound under asymmetric noise, All-k consistency) makes it an attractive practical tool for noise-tolerant learning. The finding that CE_ε+MAE outperforms both GCE/SCE (which trade robustness for fitting) and pure symmetric losses (which underfit) suggests the field's binary framing of 'robust vs. fitting' can be resolved by separating the concerns: ε-softmax handles robustness, MAE handles fitting. Limitations include the slight reduction in clean-data accuracy (mitigated but not eliminated by the MAE term), the need to tune m per noise regime, and the fact that the theoretical guarantees require the loss to satisfy a Lipschitz-like condition in the ε-ball. Open questions include whether m can be learned end-to-end and whether the approach extends naturally to non-softmax output layers (e.g., in transformers or generative models).
