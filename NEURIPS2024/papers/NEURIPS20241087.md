## Problem

Training 3D Gaussian Splatting on large-scale (city-scale) scenes faces two critical bottlenecks: excessive GPU memory to hold millions of 3D Gaussians simultaneously, and prohibitively long training times. While divide-and-conquer strategies have been applied to large-scale NeRF, they require querying multiple sub-models at inference, degrading rendering speed. The open question is whether 3DGS can be trained in a distributed, block-wise fashion while still producing a single globally consistent model for real-time inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB training images (downsampled ≥6× from original resolution), a sparse 3D point cloud triangulated via COLMAP SIFT-CPU, and known camera poses aligned to the Manhattan world assumption. The scene is recursively split into K blocks (typically 4–5) with bounding boxes expanded by a scale factor of 1.4 to create overlapping regions. The output is a single global 3D Gaussian model (center, quaternion, scaling, spherical-harmonic coefficients, opacity per primitive) that renders novel views at ~100–150 FPS, with all local block models discarded after training.

## Method

DOGS applies the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) to the 3DGS training loop, maintaining a global Gaussian model on a master node and K local models on slave nodes. Each local block optimizes the standard 3DGS photometric loss augmented with a squared-difference regularizer that pulls shared Gaussians toward the global consensus. Every 100 iterations, local Gaussians are collected and averaged to update the global model, which is then broadcast back to all blocks; dual variables are updated per-property (position, rotation, scale, features, opacity) to track per-block deviations. A recursive bipartite splitting strategy divides the scene along its longest axis to produce balanced blocks, and adaptive penalty parameters (adjusted via primal/dual residuals) combined with over-relaxation (α ∈ [1.5, 1.8]) accelerate convergence. This yields a 6–8× training speedup over monolithic 3DGS while achieving equal or better PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, with a single global model used at inference.

## Results

On the Mill19 and UrbanScene3D datasets (5 scenes each), DOGS achieves the best PSNR on 4 out of 5 scenes (Building: 22.73, Rubble: 25.78, Campus: 24.01, Sci-Art: 24.42) and the best SSIM on 4 out of 5 scenes. Training time is reduced by 6–8× compared to the original 3DGS (e.g., 03:51 vs. 21:37 on Building), while rendering FPS remains competitive (122.33 vs. 90.09 on Building). On the 2.7 km² MatrixCity dataset, DOGS achieves the best PSNR (28.58 aerial, 21.61 street) where 3DGS did not finish training within two days and VastGaussian failed entirely on the street scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original 3DGS, DOGS matches or exceeds rendering quality (e.g., +0.20 dB PSNR on Building, +0.27 dB on Rubble) while reducing training time from ~21–23 min to ~2.5–4.5 min (6–8× speedup). Against VastGaussian, DOGS achieves consistently higher PSNR (e.g., +0.93 dB on Building, +0.58 dB on Rubble, +1.78 dB on Sci-Art) at a modest training-time cost (~25–60 s longer per scene) due to inter-node data transmission. Against NeRF-based baselines (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF), DOGS gains 1.2–4.1 dB PSNR and renders 10,000–30,000× faster (e.g., 122 FPS vs. 0.009 FPS). Against Fed3DGS, DOGS outperforms by 3–4 dB PSNR across all scenes. On the largest MatrixCity scene, DOGS is the only method that completes training and produces results on both aerial and street views, where VastGaussian fails due to imbalanced splitting and 3DGS does not converge within two days.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOGS extends the divide-and-conquer paradigm established by large-scale NeRF methods (Block-NeRF, Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting setting, but departs from them by maintaining a single global model at inference rather than querying multiple sub-models. The core technical contribution is the introduction of ADMM consensus optimization—borrowed from distributed bundle adjustment—into the 3DGS training loop, ensuring that shared 3D Gaussians across overlapping blocks converge to a globally consistent representation. Compared to concurrent works VastGaussian (independent chunk training with no data sharing) and Hierarchy-GS (hierarchical tree consolidation), DOGS is optimization-parallelized with a master-slave architecture and guaranteed convergence, rather than pipeline-parallelized or hierarchically structured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6–8× training speedup on city-scale scenes while matching or exceeding the rendering quality of monolithic 3DGS is a practically significant efficiency gain for urban-scale reconstruction pipelines. The consensus mechanism's ability to *improve* quality over independent distributed training (as shown in ablations) suggests that structured inter-block communication is more valuable than previously assumed for 3DGS. Open questions include scalability beyond 5 GPU nodes, integration with level-of-detail techniques to address remaining GPU memory bottlenecks at extreme zoom-out, and compatibility with emerging 3DGS variants (Octree-GS, SAGS) that use intermediate representations rather than explicit per-Gaussian parameters.
