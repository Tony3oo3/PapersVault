## Problem

Existing feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction models (LRMs) rely on computationally intensive Transformer architectures to generate long sequences of 3D Gaussians, forcing compromises in multi-view information propagation (e.g., local attention, per-view processing with post-hoc merging). These compromises result in multi-view inconsistency and blurred textures in the generated 3D content. The paper asks: how can one preserve full multi-view information integrity while efficiently generating sufficiently long Gaussian sequences for fine-detail 3D modeling?

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a single RGB image, a text prompt, or 4 sparse posed views (512×512) as input. For single-image or text inputs, off-the-shelf multi-view diffusion models (ImageDream or MVDream) first synthesize 4 posed views at anchored camera configurations. Each view is fused with 6-channel Plücker ray embeddings into a 9-channel map, tokenized by a single-layer 2D convolution (patch size 14), and expanded via cross-scan into a 16,384- or 32,768-token sequence. The output is a set of 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters (position, scale, RGB color, opacity, rotation quaternion) or 2DGS parameters for mesh extraction via TSDF fusion, rendered in real time at 512×512.

## Method

MVGamba replaces the Transformer backbone with 14 stacked Mamba (SSM) blocks that process the expanded multi-view token sequence in a causal, recurrent manner with linear complexity, propagating multi-view context from earlier to later tokens to enable cross-view self-refinement. A lightweight single-hidden-layer MLP (width 2048) performs channel-wise knowledge selection, followed by separate linear sub-heads decoding each Gaussian attribute. A novel RotNet module converts rotation prediction into a 32-class classification over predefined canonical quaternions using Gumbel-Softmax, balancing flexibility and geometric validity. Training is supervised on 80k Objaverse objects with 4 input views and 6 novel-view supervision, using MSE, alpha-mask, LPIPS, and opacity L1 losses, with data augmentations (grid distortion, orbital jitter, synthetic diffusion inputs) to bridge the train-inference domain gap. The entire model has only 49M parameters and completes inference in under 5 seconds on a single A800 GPU.

## Results

On the GSO dataset for image-to-3D generation, MVGamba (49M parameters) achieves 24.82 PSNR, 0.90 SSIM, 0.08 LPIPS, 0.895 CLIP score, and 60.4 R-Precision, outperforming all feed-forward and optimization-based baselines on most metrics with a 4-second inference time. In sparse-view reconstruction (Table 4), MVGamba achieves 26.25 PSNR, 0.069 LPIPS, 0.881 SSIM, 0.0132 Chamfer Distance, and 0.4829 Volume IoU, surpassing LGM, SparseNeuS, and SparseGS across all metrics while using only 4 input views. The model operates at roughly 0.1× the parameter count of competing LRMs while delivering superior generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVGamba outperforms all six baselines in Table 1 on PSNR (24.82 vs. best 23.76 for TripoSR), LPIPS (0.08 vs. 0.09 for TripoSR), CLIP score (0.895 vs. 0.869 for One-2-3-45++), and R-Precision (60.4 vs. 57.1 for One-2-3-45++). The only metric where MVGamba trails is SSIM, where TripoSR edges ahead at 0.91 vs. 0.90, and inference speed, where TripoSR (0.5s) and TriplaneGaussian (0.2s) are faster than MVGamba's 4s—though MVGamba is still 30–50× faster than optimization-based methods like DreamGaussian (120s) and Wonder3D (200s). In sparse-view reconstruction (Table 4), MVGamba dominates all baselines on every metric: it achieves 26.25 PSNR vs. LGM's 24.20, 0.0132 Chamfer Distance vs. LGM's 0.0198, and 0.4829 Volume IoU vs. LGM's 0.4410, while also being faster (0.03s vs. 0.07s for LGM). Notably, MVGamba uses only 4 input views for sparse-view reconstruction, matching LGM and far fewer than SparseNeuS and SparseGS which require 16 views. The paper attributes its advantage to causal sequence modeling that preserves multi-view information integrity and enables cross-view self-refinement, avoiding the multi-view inconsistency and blurred textures observed in LGM and TriplaneGaussian.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVGamba sits at the intersection of three active research threads: large reconstruction models (LRMs) that map multi-view images to 3D representations in a feed-forward manner (Instant3D, LGM, GRM, GS-LRM), 3D Gaussian Splatting as an explicit 3D representation, and the Mamba/SSM architecture for efficient long-sequence modeling. The key departure from prior Gaussian LRMs is the replacement of quadratic-complexity Transformer attention with linear-complexity causal SSM processing, motivated by the observation that existing methods sacrifice multi-view information integrity to manage computational costs. The paper also introduces a 'direct sequence modeling' paradigm (expanding tokens via cross-scan to 16K–32K length) in contrast to the 'compressed sequence modeling' used by concurrent Transformer-based approaches that rely on upsamplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× parameter efficiency (49M vs. ~500M+ for competing LRMs) while outperforming on all three generation tasks is practically significant for deployment in VR, gaming, and animation pipelines where sub-second latency and low memory footprint matter. The causal self-refinement mechanism provides a principled explanation for robustness to multi-view diffusion inconsistencies, which is a known pain point in the two-stage pipeline. Open questions include sensitivity to input view ordering (front-view depth errors can cause failures), dependence on the quality of off-the-shelf multi-view diffusion models (capped at 256×256), and whether the approach extends to scene-level or dynamic (4D) generation.
