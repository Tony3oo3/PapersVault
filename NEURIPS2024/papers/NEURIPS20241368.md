## Problem

Point-based methods for 3D scene understanding are limited to small-scale point clouds because their neighborhood search strategies (FPS and KNN) incur quadratic time complexity, consuming over 40% of inference runtime. This paper tackles the scalability gap by redesigning both the local feature aggregation and the point search operations to achieve linear complexity, enabling point-based networks to handle large-scale scenes (100k–200k+ points) without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241368/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, unordered 3D point clouds (coordinates plus learned features) directly, with no voxelization or multi-view projection. For segmentation, a grid subsampling step (0.04 m for S3DIS, 0.05 m for NuScenes/SemanticKITTI) is applied before the encoder-decoder network, which outputs a per-point class label. For classification, only the encoder is used and the output is a single scene-level class. The architecture is designed to scale linearly in latency with point count, supporting inputs from 20k up to 200k+ points on a single GPU.

## Method

LinNet introduces two key components. First, the Disassembled Set Abstraction (DSA) module decouples spatial and channel anisotropy: a point-wise MLP (c→c) processes semantic features independently of the neighborhood, while a separate depth-wise MLP (3→c) maps relative coordinates into a bias vector that is added to neighbor features before max-pooling. Although mathematically equivalent to standard set abstraction in the forward pass, DSA reduces FLOPs from kc² to c² + 3c and, critically, uses separate Kaiming initializations (W_f ~ N(0,√(2/c)) and W_p ~ N(0,√(2/3))) that give geometric data a proportionally larger influence, yielding faster convergence and higher final accuracy. Second, a space-filling-curve-based search strategy maps 3D grid indices into 64-bit integer keys, enabling a hash-table neighborhood query with O(m + N(p log k + k log k)) complexity (versus O(kN²) for KNN) and a linearization sampling rule that picks the point closest to each grid cell's origin for uniform downsampling in O(N) time with full GPU parallelism. The entire network is a pure MLP stack (no attention, no sparse convolution) with four encoder stages of channels [C, 2C, 4C, 8C] and configurable depths, achieving ~10× speedup over PointNeXt while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Results

On S3DIS Area 5, LinNet achieves 73.7% mIoU (with TTA) and 72.9% without TTA, outperforming PointNeXt (70.8%) by 2.9% mIoU. On the large-scale NuScenes benchmark, LinNet reaches 81.4% mIoU (TTA) and 80.4% without TTA, matching PTv3 (80.4%) without TTA and surpassing it with TTA. On SemanticKITTI, LinNet achieves 69.1% val / 70.4% test mIoU. In terms of efficiency, LinNet runs approximately 10× faster than PointNeXt on 80k points and 13× faster at 200k points, while using only 1.4M parameters and 0.6 GFLOPs for classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On S3DIS Area 5, LinNet (73.7% mIoU with TTA) surpasses all point-based baselines including PTv3 (73.4%), ConDaFormer (73.5%), PTv2 (72.6%), PointVector (72.3%), and PointNeXt (70.8%), with the largest margin over PointNeXt at +2.9% mIoU. On NuScenes, LinNet (81.4% TTA) outperforms PTv3 (80.4%), WaffleIron (79.1%), OACNN (78.9%), and SphereFormer (78.4%), while matching PTv3 without TTA (80.4% vs. 80.4%). On ScanObjectNN, LinNet achieves 88.2% OA, beating PointNeXt (87.7%) and PointMetaBase (87.9%), though PointMLP (85.4%) uses far more parameters (13.2M vs. 1.4M). On ModelNet40, LinNet (93.6% OA) is competitive but slightly trails PointMLP (94.1%) and CurveNet (93.8%), while matching PointNeXt (93.7%). Critically, LinNet achieves these results with ~10× lower latency than PointNeXt and without any attention mechanisms, sparse convolution, or graph structures—relying solely on point-wise MLPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinNet inherits the hierarchical local-to-global paradigm of PointNet++ and the training/scaling strategies of PointNeXt, but departs from both by replacing the standard Set Abstraction module with a Disassembled Set Abstraction (DSA) that separates spatial and channel anisotropy in the spirit of MobileNet's depth-wise separable convolutions. It further replaces the quadratic-complexity FPS/KNN search with a space-filling-curve-based linearization sampling and hash query, achieving O(N) neighbor search and downsampling. Unlike Point Transformer v2/v3 or sparse convolution methods (MinkUNet), LinNet relies solely on point-wise MLPs without attention mechanisms or voxelization, positioning itself as a minimal-yet-competitive alternative within the point-based family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely MLP-based point network can match or exceed attention-based and sparse-convolution methods on S3DIS, NuScenes, and SemanticKITTI while running ~10× faster than PointNeXt, challenging the assumption that sophisticated feature extractors are necessary for large-scale point cloud understanding. The space-filling-curve search strategy is a practical, GPU-parallelizable technique that could be adopted by other point-based architectures to improve scalability. However, the authors acknowledge that memory footprint remains significantly higher than sparse convolution methods (16 GB vs. 2.6 GB for MinkUNet on NuScenes), and the discontinuous memory access pattern of point-based methods limits further speedups. Open questions include whether the DSA formulation generalizes to other modalities or whether the memory gap can be closed without sacrificing the linear-complexity advantage.
