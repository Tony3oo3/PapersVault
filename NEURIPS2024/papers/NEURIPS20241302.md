## Problem

3D structure-based drug design (3D-SBDD) diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of iterative denoising steps, making them too slow for practical applications such as reinforcement learning fine-tuning, real-time interactive design with human experts, and rapid hit discovery. No prior work has applied RL to 3D-SBDD diffusion models because the prolonged inference times preclude the rapid training-inference cycles that RL demands. This paper addresses both the speed bottleneck and the unexplored RL optimization gap simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein pocket represented as Cα atoms within 8 Å of the reference ligand (with residue-type one-hot features and edges within 5 Å) concatenated with the functional groups extracted from a reference molecule via Bemis-Murcko scaffold decomposition. It outputs a 3D molecular scaffold as Cartesian coordinates and atom-type one-hot vectors, preserving the reference functional groups while generating a novel core substructure. The number of generated atoms is fixed to match the reference scaffold size, and the model operates on a graph representation with distance and direction edge features.

## Method

TurboHopp replaces the iterative DDPM denoising process with an E(3)-equivariant consistency function f_θ(Z_t, t | u) that maps any point on the Probability Flow ODE trajectory directly to the clean data point, enabling 1-step or few-step generation (5–30× faster than DiffHopp). The architecture is a GVP-based message-passing network with skip connections parameterized by time-dependent coefficients z_skip(t) and z_out(t) that satisfy the boundary condition f_θ(Z_ε) = Z_ε. Training uses a self-consistency loss (MSE between predictions at adjacent trajectory points) with an EMA target network for stability. Leveraging the fast inference, the authors apply RLCM (RL for Consistency Models) with PPO to fine-tune the model against reward functions for docking score and steric clash minimization—the first RL-based optimization of a 3D-SBDD generative model. A metric-based sampling strategy further improves quality by scoring intermediate scaffolds during multi-step generation and selecting the highest-scoring candidate.

## Results

On PDBBind, TurboHopp-100metric achieves a QVina binding affinity of -8.319 kcal/mol, QED of 0.664, SA of 0.737, and connectivity of 0.997, while generating a batch of 150 molecules in just 7.14 seconds—approximately 15× faster than DiffHopp (107.10 s). At 50 steps, TurboHopp-50metric completes generation in 3.83 s (~28× speedup) with competitive metrics (QVina -7.865, QED 0.583). On CrossDocked, TurboHopp-100metric outperforms both TargetDiff-inpainting and DecompDiff-inpainting on validity (0.993 vs. 0.927/0.876), QED (0.502 vs. 0.424/0.420), and QVina (-7.204 vs. -5.896/-6.225), while being 90–150× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffHopp (Torge et al., 2023), the primary DDPM-based scaffold-hopping baseline, TurboHopp-100metric wins on connectivity (0.997 vs. 0.918), QED (0.664 vs. 0.621), SA (0.737 vs. 0.662), and QVina binding affinity (-8.319 vs. -7.923 kcal/mol), while being ~15× faster (7.14 s vs. 107.10 s). The only metric where TurboHopp trails is diversity (0.561 vs. 0.589), which the authors attribute to the self-consistency constraint of consistency models. Against TargetDiff-inpainting (Guan et al., 2023) and DecompDiff-inpainting (Guan et al., 2024) on CrossDocked, TurboHopp-100metric dominates on validity (0.993 vs. 0.927/0.876), QED (0.502 vs. 0.424/0.420), SA (0.710 vs. 0.661/0.648), and QVina (-7.204 vs. -5.896/-6.225), with generation times of 8.18 s versus 740 s and 1264 s respectively. TurboHopp loses to both inpainting baselines on diversity (0.486 vs. 0.841/0.856) and novelty (0.935 vs. 0.914/0.895—actually comparable), reflecting the narrower chemical space explored by scaffold hopping versus de-novo generation. The RLCM-finetuned variant (TurboHoppRL-50metric) further improves QVina to -9.804 kcal/mol, surpassing the PDBBind test-set reference of -8.643, while maintaining QED (0.619) and SA (0.680) close to the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

TurboHopp sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) consistency models (Song et al. 2023), which replace iterative denoising with a single or few-step mapping from noise to data; (2) the scaffold-hopping formulation and GVP-based equivariant architecture from DiffHopp (Torge et al. 2023), which conditions generation on a protein pocket and reference functional groups; and (3) RLCM (Oertell et al. 2024), which maps multi-step consistency inference to a Markov decision process for PPO-based reward optimization. The key departure from prior 3D-SBDD work (TargetDiff, DecompDiff, DiffHopp) is replacing the 500-step DDPM sampling loop with a consistency model that requires only 50–150 steps, and—critically—leveraging that speedup to make RL fine-tuning of 3D-SBDD models feasible for the first time, a regime the authors note was previously precluded by inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–30× inference speedup is practically significant: it enables real-time interactive design sessions, large-scale virtual screening, and—most importantly—RL-based optimization of metrics (binding affinity, steric clashes) that are difficult to capture through direct modeling alone. The demonstration that RLCM can improve docking scores by 2–3 kcal/mol without redocking opens a path toward reward-guided 3D molecule design that was previously infeasible with diffusion models. Limitations include lower diversity than diffusion baselines (an inherent property of consistency models), absence of bond-type modeling, Cα-only protein representation, and evaluation on a single primary dataset (PDBBind). Open questions include whether RL-finetuned models generalize to unseen targets, how to incorporate bond diffusion and full-atom protein representations, and whether more sophisticated reward functions (steric energy, interaction fingerprints) can further close the gap to physically realistic poses.
