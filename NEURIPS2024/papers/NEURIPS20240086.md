## Problem

Existing visual object trackers rely on local cropping around the previous frame's bounding box, effectively modeling only central vision and failing when targets disappear or the scene changes (spatio-temporal discontinuities, or STDChallenge). This limitation is compounded by benchmarks that underrepresent such scenarios and by the absence of evaluation mechanisms that compare tracker behavior to human visual search. The paper asks how to build a tracker that models both central and peripheral vision simultaneously, and how to rigorously assess whether machines track like humans.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes the current video frame, a target template from the first frame, and the previous frame's bounding box. Unusually, the current frame is not used as a single search region: it is split into a dynamically-sized central crop (whose width is computed by integrating a Gaussian sensitivity function over the previous bounding box, scaled by a constant S) and a peripheral view (the full image resized to match the central crop's dimensions). The output is a single target bounding box predicted via a quantized 4000-bin vocabulary decoded by a 2-block transformer decoder.

## Method

CPDTrack implements the Central-Peripheral Dichotomy (CPD) from cognitive neuroscience as a motion model: a Gaussian acuity function centered on the previous frame's bounding box determines a variable-width central region (w_e = S·w / (2Φ(3w/W) − 1)), while the full frame resized to the same resolution serves as the peripheral view, giving the network simultaneous local detail and global scene awareness. An information query module provides top-down cognitive control by max-pooling the template into a query that cross-attends over past-frame tokens, then concatenates the resulting semantic embedding with image tokens before feeding them into a MAE-pretrained ViT-B encoder. The decoder (2 transformer blocks, 256-dim hidden, 8 heads) predicts the bounding box through a 3-layer MLP + softmax over 4000 quantization bins. The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 1e-5 encoder, 1e-4 decoder) on VideoCube, LaSOT, GOT-10k, and TrackingNet for 300 epochs, achieving 23 FPS on an A5000 GPU.

## Results

On the newly proposed STDChallenge Benchmark, CPDTrack achieves 84.2 N-PRE, 73.3 PRE, and 65.9 SUC, outperforming the second-best tracker (MixViT) by 1.7% N-PRE and 1.4% PRE. On VideoCube, CPDTrack leads all baselines with 82.9 N-PRE, 67.1 PRE, and 70.4 SUC (OPE), and 89.5 N-PRE with a robustness score of 75.3 (R-OPE). On LaSOT, CPDTrack scores 66.1 AUC and 73.0 P, trailing SeqTrack (69.9 AUC, 76.3 P) due to fewer STDChallenge scenarios in that dataset. In the Visual Turing Test, CPDTrack achieves the highest error consistency with humans (κ=0.167), indicating the most human-like tracking behavior among all tested trackers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPDTrack outperforms all 17 baselines on the STDChallenge Benchmark, with the closest competitor being MixViT (82.5 N-PRE vs. 84.2) and SeqTrack (81.9 N-PRE). On VideoCube, CPDTrack leads by a wide margin over SeqTrack (82.9 vs. 76.8 N-PRE; 67.1 vs. 54.0 PRE) and MixViT (82.9 vs. 76.9 N-PRE), with the largest gap in PRE reaching 13.1% over SeqTrack. Among global trackers, CPDTrack surpasses SiamRCNN (84.2 vs. 75.3 N-PRE on STDChallenge) and GlobalTrack (84.2 vs. 65.5), confirming that simultaneous central-peripheral modeling is more effective than pure global search. On LaSOT, CPDTrack underperforms SeqTrack (66.1 vs. 69.9 AUC) and MixViT (66.1 vs. 69.6 AUC), which the authors attribute to LaSOT containing fewer STDChallenge scenarios where local-crop priors are advantageous. In the Visual Turing Test, CPDTrack achieves the highest error consistency with human trackers (κ=0.167), exceeding SeqTrack (0.139 for TransT) and all other baselines, while humans themselves outperform all machines on N-PRE, confirming that the gap between machine and human DVA remains significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on the Central-Peripheral Dichotomy (CPD) theory from cognitive neuroscience (Li Zhaoping) to reframe the motion model in one-stream ViT-based trackers (OSTrack, MixViT, SeqTrack), replacing the static local crop with a Gaussian acuity-driven central region and a resized full-frame peripheral region. It also extends the Visual Turing Test methodology (Geirhos et al.) from static image classification to video tracking, introducing error consistency as a behavioral-similarity metric between humans and machines. The STDChallenge Benchmark unifies sequences from LaSOT, VOTLT2019, and VideoCube that contain target absence or shotcut, explicitly isolating the spatio-temporal discontinuity sub-problem that prior LTT and GIT benchmarks address only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a concrete gap: mainstream trackers rely on spatio-temporal continuity and degrade sharply when targets disappear or scenes cut, a failure mode that is common in real-world deployment but underrepresented in standard benchmarks. The STDChallenge Benchmark and the Visual Turing Test protocol give the community a focused, human-calibrated way to measure visual search ability beyond aggregate accuracy. Open questions remain: the STDChallenge distribution is still long-tailed (most sequences have few discontinuities), CPDTrack underperforms local trackers on simpler benchmarks (GOT-10k, TNL2k), and the computational overhead of modeling both central and peripheral regions simultaneously limits real-time applicability. How to scale the benchmark with more high-STD sequences and reduce inference cost while preserving the CPD modeling are natural next steps.
