## Problem

Distributionally robust offline RL with function approximation faces a fundamental tension: the model uncertainty in DRMDPs introduces essential nonlinearity that disrupts the linear dependency of the Bellman equation on the transition kernel, making both computational tractability and statistical analysis significantly harder than in standard offline RL. Existing approaches either suffer from computational intractability under (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty sets, require impractical strong oracles, or are restricted to online/simulator settings. This paper asks whether both computational efficiency and minimax optimality can be simultaneously achieved for robust offline RL with linear function approximation.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume an offline dataset of K i.i.d. trajectories collected from the nominal environment by a behavior policy, along with a simplex-constrained feature mapping φ: S×A → R^d (components sum to 1, are non-negative) and a TV-divergence uncertainty set of radius ρ. VA-DRPVI additionally requires a second independent dataset D' for variance estimation. The output is a deterministic robust policy π̂ with an instance-dependent suboptimality guarantee expressed as a supremum over all transition kernels in the uncertainty set, weighted by a diagonal-based normalization of the feature vector against the empirical covariance matrix.

## Method

DRPVI performs backward induction under the pessimism principle, solving d separate ridge regressions (one per factor of the d-rectangular uncertainty set) to estimate the robust Q-function, then subtracting a diagonal-based normalization penalty ∑ᵢ‖φᵢ(s,a)1ᵢ‖_{Λₕ⁻¹} to account for estimation uncertainty; the d-rectangular structure's decoupling property makes the inner optimization over the uncertainty set computationally tractable, unlike (s,a)-rectangular sets. VA-DRPVI improves upon DRPVI by incorporating variance-weighted ridge regression, exploiting a novel 'Range Shrinkage' phenomenon where the range of robust value functions contracts by a factor of (1−ρ) per stage, yielding constant-order conditional variances when ρ = O(1) and removing the explicit H-dependence from the bound. The theoretical analysis introduces a new suboptimality decomposition and an uncertainty decomposition that transforms worst-case estimation error over all perturbed models into estimation error under the nominal model, combined with a reference-advantage decomposition adapted from standard linear MDPs. An information-theoretic lower bound is established via Assouad's method using a novel family of hard instances specifically designed to mitigate the nonlinearity induced by model uncertainty while satisfying the Hamming-distance prerequisite.

## Results

The paper establishes the first instance-dependent suboptimality bounds for robust offline RL with function approximation. DRPVI achieves a suboptimality gap of Õ(√d H²/(√κ·√K)) (Corollary 4.5), while VA-DRPVI improves this to Õ(dH·min{1/ρ, H}/(c†·√K)) (Remark 5.4), strictly improving the state-of-the-art [4, Theorem 6.3] by O(d) and O(dH) respectively when ρ=O(1). The information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 6.1) matches the VA-DRPVI upper bound up to a factor of Õ(√d), confirming near-minimax optimality. In simulations on a 5-state linear MDP with perturbed transitions, both proposed algorithms maintain robust performance under environmental perturbation while non-robust PEVI degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is PEVI [15], the standard non-robust pessimistic value iteration for offline linear MDPs. In the simulated 5-state linear MDP with perturbed transition probabilities at the initial state, both DRPVI and VA-DRPVI maintain stable performance across all perturbation levels (ρ₁,₄ ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5}) and environment complexities (‖ξ‖₁ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}), while PEVI degrades as the perturbation increases. VA-DRPVI slightly outperforms DRPVI in most settings, consistent with the theoretical range shrinkage advantage. Theoretically, the paper improves upon Blanchet et al. [4] (double pessimism for DRMDPs) by O(d) for DRPVI and O(dH) for VA-DRPVI when ρ=O(1), and introduces the first information-theoretic lower bound for offline DRMDPs with linear function approximation, which no prior work had established.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pessimism-based value iteration framework from standard offline linear MDPs (Jin et al. 2021; Xie et al. 2021) to the distributionally robust setting, building directly on the d-rectangular linear DRMDP formulation and robust Bellman equation structure from Liu and Xu (2024). The analysis pipeline adapts the reference-advantage decomposition (Xiong et al. 2023) and variance-weighted ridge regression (Zhou et al. 2021) to handle the essential nonlinearity introduced by the inner optimization over the uncertainty set. The key departure from prior robust offline RL work (Panaganti et al. 2022; Blanchet et al. 2023) is the use of the d-rectangular uncertainty set, whose decoupling property makes the inner optimization computationally tractable, in contrast to the (s,a)-rectangular sets that render function approximation intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first instance-dependent suboptimality analysis for robust offline RL with function approximation, establishing that both computational efficiency and minimax optimality are simultaneously achievable under d-rectangular linear DRMDPs. The range shrinkage phenomenon—a structural property where robust value function ranges contract over the horizon, absent in standard MDPs—yields a strict O(H) improvement in the upper bound when the uncertainty level is constant, revealing a previously unexploited feature of DRMDPs. Open questions include whether similar guarantees extend to (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty sets, general (nonlinear) function approximation, and settings where nominal and perturbed environments do not share the same state-action space, where the authors conjecture the learned policy could be arbitrarily bad.
