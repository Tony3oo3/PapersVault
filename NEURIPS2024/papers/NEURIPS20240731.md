## Problem

Token-level jailbreak attacks on LLMs, exemplified by GCG, rely on discrete coordinate descent over the vocabulary, which is computationally expensive (hundreds of thousands of queries, up to an hour per example) and prone to suboptimal solutions. The core difficulty is that discrete token optimization cannot fully exploit gradient information, while naively relaxing to continuous space and projecting back to one-hot tokens causes severe performance degradation due to the large distance between dense optimized vectors and their nearest one-hot neighbors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful user query, a short target response prefix (e.g., 'Sure, here is'), and white-box gradient access to a target LLM, and produces a 20-token adversarial suffix that, when concatenated to the query, elicits the target response. Optimization runs up to 5000 steps per initialization start with 16 parallel starts (ADC) or 8 starts followed by a GCG refinement stage (ADC+), with early stopping triggered when the projected one-hot tokens successfully jailbreak the model. The final output is a discrete token string, not a continuous embedding, making it directly usable as a prompt suffix.

## Method

ADC relaxes the discrete one-hot token optimization into continuous optimization over the probability simplex P in R^V, then applies an adaptive dense-to-sparse constraint where the target sparsity S = exp(number of mispredicted target tokens) decreases as the loss drops, progressively forcing the optimizing vectors from dense toward one-hot. Crucially, the method avoids softmax reparameterization (which causes vanishing gradients for near-zero elements and traps optimization in local minima) and instead enforces the simplex constraint via a simple top-S thresholding and renormalization projection (Algorithm 1), preserving the ability to update previously zeroed elements. A momentum optimizer with a large fixed learning rate (10) and high momentum (0.99) is used to escape local minima, with multiple random Gaussian-initialized starts optimized in parallel. The ADC+ variant further improves efficiency by switching to GCG coordinate descent after 5000 steps, reducing total compute to 67% of standard GCG while maintaining or exceeding its attack success rate.

## Results

On the HarmBench Standard Behaviours benchmark (200 examples), ADC+ achieves the highest attack success rate (ASR) on 7 out of 8 LLMs, reaching 92% on Llama2-7B-chat (vs. 34.5% for GCG), 100% on Vicuna-v1.5-7B and Zephyr-β-7B, and 99.0% on Qwen-7B-chat. On AdvBench (520 harmful behaviors), ADC+ achieves 96.5% ASR on Llama2-chat-7B compared to GCG's 53.8%, while using only 67% of GCG's computational budget and completing in 8.2 minutes versus 20.6 minutes wall-clock time. Against the adversarially trained Zephyr-R2D2 model, ADC+ attains 26.5% ASR where GCG and AutoPrompt achieve 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG [46], the primary token-level baseline, ADC+ substantially outperforms on Llama2-chat-7B (96.5% vs. 53.8% ASR on AdvBench) and Zephyr-β-7B (98.8% vs. 98.1%), while matching Vicuna-v1.5-7B (99.8% vs. 99.4%), all at 67% of GCG's computational cost. On HarmBench, ADC+ beats GCG on all 8 LLMs, with the largest margin on Llama2-7B-chat (92% vs. 34.5%). Against prompt-level methods AutoPrompt [33], PAIR [5], and TAP [26], ADC+ achieves higher ASR on 7 of 8 LLMs on HarmBench; the sole exception is Zephyr-R2D2, where TAP (76.5%) and PAIR (57.5%) outperform ADC+ (26.5%) because that model was specifically adversarially trained against token-level attacks. Against AutoDan [20], the strongest prompt-level baseline, ADC+ wins on all 8 LLMs, including a 26.5% vs. 10.5% margin on Zephyr-R2D2. In transfer attacks to GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, ADC+ achieves 90% and 52% ASR respectively, marginally outperforming GCG (87%, 47%) and PAIR (60%, 62%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GCG (Zou et al., 2023), which performs coordinate descent over discrete token space, by relaxing the optimization to the continuous probability simplex and applying an adaptive dense-to-sparse constraint that progressively tightens toward one-hot vectors as the loss decreases. It departs from prior continuous-embedding approaches (Guo et al., 2021; Wen et al., 2024) that project optimized soft embeddings back to discrete tokens without sparsity control, which the authors show causes large performance drops. The method also draws on the broader discrete-optimization literature (Jones et al., 2023) to motivate why coordinate descent is suboptimal, and replaces it with a momentum-based continuous optimizer that avoids reparameterization tricks to preserve escape from local minima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26.5% ASR on the adversarially trained Zephyr R2D2 model—where GCG and other token-level methods achieve near-zero—challenges the assumption that adversarial training can effectively defend against optimized token-level attacks, suggesting current defenses are insufficient. The practical efficiency gain (67% of GCG's compute for ADC+) makes token-level red-teaming more scalable for adversarial training pipelines. Key limitations include the white-box requirement and the fact that prompt-level methods (e.g., TAP at 76.5%) still outperform ADC on R2D2, leaving open the question of whether a unified defense can resist both attack families simultaneously.
