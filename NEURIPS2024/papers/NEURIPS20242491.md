## Problem

Despite the widespread deployment of deep learning models on tabular data in critical domains (finance, healthcare, security), there is no standardized benchmark for evaluating their adversarial robustness against constraint-aware evasion attacks. Unlike computer vision and LLMs, which have mature robustness benchmarks (RobustBench, DecodingTrust), tabular adversarial robustness research is fragmented, with prior attacks often ignoring feature-type constraints and inter-feature relationships that are essential in real-world tabular settings.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes tabular feature vectors containing heterogeneous numerical and categorical features, each governed by domain-specific constraints of four types: mutability (which features an attacker can modify), range bounds, feature type, and relational dependencies (e.g., the sum of two features must equal a third). The output is a binary class prediction, with robustness quantified as the fraction of constraint-satisfying adversarial examples that remain correctly classified under L2-bounded perturbations. Critically, any adversarial example that violates a domain constraint is deemed invalid and counted as a successful defense, making the evaluation stricter than unconstrained settings.

## Method

TabularBench evaluates adversarial robustness using the Constrained Adaptive Attack (CAA), which sequentially applies CAPGD (an iterative projected gradient descent with momentum and constraint-repair operators) followed by MOEVA (a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm based on NSGA-III optimizing misclassification, perturbation magnitude, and constraint violation simultaneously). The benchmark spans five real-world datasets (CTU-13, LCLD, Malware, URL, WiDS) with varying feature counts (28–24,222) and constraint counts (7–360), five tabular architectures (TabTransformer, TabNet, RLN, STG, VIME), and seven robustification strategies combining adversarial training with six data augmentation generators (WGAN, TableGAN, CTGAN, TVAE, GOGGLE, and an adapted CutMix). This yields over 200 pre-trained models accessible via a Python API. Key empirical findings include: in-distribution accuracy is a misleading proxy for robustness, ignoring domain constraints overestimates model robustness, and no single augmentation universally dominates—simpler methods like CutMix often outperform complex generative approaches in constrained robustness.

## Results

Across five real-world tabular datasets and five deep learning architectures, the paper reveals that in-distribution (ID) accuracy is a poor predictor of adversarial robustness. On LCLD (credit scoring), TabTransformer's robust accuracy collapses from 69.5% ID to 7.9% under CAA attack without adversarial training (AT), while on URL (phishing detection) it drops from 93.6% to 8.9%. Conversely, the Malware dataset (24,222 features) is inherently robust: all architectures maintain 93–99% robust accuracy even without AT. With AT alone, robust accuracy recovers substantially (e.g., TabTransformer on LCLD: 7.9% → 70.3%), but AT combined with data augmentation (e.g., CutMix) further improves robustness and remains effective under increased attack budgets (ε=5, 100 gradient iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five tabular architectures, no single model dominates across all datasets: STG achieves the best robust accuracy on URL (58.0% without AT, 90.0% with AT) and WIDS (50.3% without AT), while TabTransformer leads on LCLD with AT (70.3% robust). TabNet is the most fragile under AT on CTU (0.2% robust) and WIDS (5.3% without AT, 58.4% with AT), suggesting its sequential attention mechanism is particularly vulnerable to constrained perturbations. For data augmentation, CTGAN consistently yields the best ID performance across all use cases, but CutMix (the simplest feature-mixing strategy) is the most effective for robustness, outperforming all other augmentations in 7 out of 20 architecture-dataset scenarios. XGBoost, the strongest shallow baseline, achieves comparable ID AUC (e.g., 0.993 on URL vs. 0.986 for TabNet) but is not evaluated for adversarial robustness, leaving a gap that this benchmark addresses. Under increased attack budgets (ε=5, 100 gradient iterations), AT+augmentation models remain robust (e.g., TabNet on URL stays above 40% robust accuracy) while AT-alone models collapse to 0%, confirming that data augmentation is essential for sustained robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first adversarial robustness benchmark for tabular deep learning, explicitly contrasting with RobustBench (CV), TableShift (tabular OOD), and TabSurvey (tabular ID performance), none of which address constrained adversarial robustness for tabular models. It builds directly on the authors' prior work on Constrained Adaptive Attack (CAA, NeurIPS 2024) and MOEVA (IJCAI 2022), using CAA as the evaluation attack, and adapts defense concepts from the CV adversarial training literature (adversarial training combined with data augmentation) to the tabular setting. A key departure from prior tabular attack work is the systematic incorporation of four types of domain constraints (mutability, range, type, relations) that earlier attacks largely ignored or only partially addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark fills a critical gap: tabular DL models are deployed in high-stakes domains (credit scoring, malware detection, ICU triage) where adversarial manipulation is a realistic threat, yet no standardized evaluation existed. The finding that in-distribution accuracy is a poor predictor of robustness, and that the simplest augmentation (CutMix) often outperforms complex generative models, provides actionable guidance for practitioners. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification, L2 distance, a single attack (CAA), and no adaptive attacks; open questions include whether these findings generalize to multi-class settings, other imperceptibility metrics, and whether adaptive attacks would change the defense rankings.
