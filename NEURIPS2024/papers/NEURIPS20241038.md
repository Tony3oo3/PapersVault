## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation methods for LLMs operate at the level of entire short-form responses or multiple-choice answers, failing to provide the granular, claim-level uncertainty needed when LLMs generate paragraphs mixing true and false claims. The few approaches that do estimate claim-level uncertainty (e.g., self-consistency) rely solely on sample-and-count and do not exploit the richer semantic relationships between claims and the responses that entail them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a set of LLM-sampled responses (one greedy at T=0 plus additional samples at T=1, typically |R|=5 or 10) as input. It first decomposes responses into atomic claims via LLM prompting, merges them into a unique claim set, and constructs a bipartite entailment graph. The output is a per-claim uncertainty score derived from a graph centrality metric, which can be thresholded to produce a factuality-filtered response by retaining only low-uncertainty claims and synthesizing them into a coherent paragraph.

## Method

The core contribution is representing the relationship between LLM responses and their decomposed claims as a bipartite semantic entailment graph, where edges indicate that a response entails a claim. The key insight is that self-consistency (the dominant existing baseline) is equivalent to degree centrality on this graph, and that more sophisticated centrality metrics—particularly closeness centrality, which measures the reciprocal of average shortest-path distance to all other nodes—capture finer-grained structural information that better discriminates true from false claims. An ablation confirms that false claims are 'less centered' in the graph (larger average distances to other claims), explaining why closeness centrality outperforms degree-based methods by ~6.8% AUPRC on average. For uncertainty-aware decoding, the pipeline filters the candidate claim set by a closeness-centrality threshold δ and uses an LLM to synthesize the surviving low-uncertainty claims into a final response, achieving 2–4% factuality gains over self-consistency-based decoding while preserving informativeness.

## Results

On claim-level uncertainty estimation, closeness centrality (C_C) achieves an average of 6.8% relative AUPRC gain over the strongest baseline (Self-Consistency) across FActScore and PopQA. For example, on FActScore with GPT-4 and |R|=10, C_C reaches 0.855 AUROC and 0.751 AUPRC-Negative, compared to SC's 0.839 and 0.708. On PopQA with the same setup, C_C achieves 0.800 AUROC and 0.693 AUPRC-Negative versus SC's 0.771 and 0.572. In the uncertainty-aware decoding (UAD) setting, C_C-based filtering yields 2–4% factuality gains and generates 70% more true claims at the 95% precision level compared to the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closeness centrality (C_C) outperforms all six baselines across every model–dataset–sample-size combination in Table 2. The strongest baseline is Self-Consistency (SC, equivalent to degree centrality), which C_C beats by 4.3–12.1% on AUPRC-Negative (e.g., 0.751 vs. 0.708 on FActScore/GPT-4/|R|=10; 0.693 vs. 0.572 on PopQA/GPT-4/|R|=10). SC+VC, which adds post-hoc verbalized confidence to break ties, is the second-strongest baseline but still trails C_C by 5–11% on AUPRC. The verbalized-confidence baselines (PH-VC, IL-VC) and P(True) are consistently weaker, with IL-VC performing near chance (AUROC ≈ 0.54) on FActScore. In the UAD decoding experiments (Fig. 3), C_C-based filtering dominates the factuality–informativeness Pareto frontier over UAD(SC, Greedy) [Mohri & Hashimoto 2024], UAD(SC, Multi-Sample), UAD(SC+IL-VC, Multi-Sample), CoVe [Dhuliawala et al. 2023], and Greedy Decoding, achieving up to 18% higher factuality at matched claim counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes claim-level uncertainty estimation for long-form LLM outputs through the lens of graph theory, showing that the widely-used self-consistency (SC) score is equivalent to degree centrality on a bipartite response-claim entailment graph. It builds directly on the claim decomposition pipeline from FActScore (Min et al., 2023), the entailment-based uncertainty framework of SelfCheckGPT (Manakul et al., 2023), and the uncertainty-aware decoding (UAD) framework of Mohri & Hashimoto (2024) and Wang et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure is replacing the simple sample-and-count of SC with a broader family of graph centrality metrics (closeness, betweenness, eigenvector, PageRank), drawing on classical network science (Freeman, 1979; Wasserman & Faust, 1994) to capture richer structural information about how claims relate to the response distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, black-box-compatible framework for granular claim-level uncertainty that outperforms the dominant SC baseline by a consistent 6.8% in AUPRC, and translates into 2–4% factuality gains in end-to-end decoding without sacrificing informativeness. The ablation showing that false claims are systematically 'less centered' in the entailment graph offers an interpretable explanation for why closeness centrality works. Key limitations include the increased inference-time compute from graph construction, the assumption that claims can be cleanly decontextualized, and reliance on GPT-4-Turbo for ground-truth annotation. Open questions include whether the graph construction can be made more efficient, how to handle semantically dependent claims, and whether the approach generalizes to non-English or domain-specific settings.
