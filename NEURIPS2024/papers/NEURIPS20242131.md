## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for improving diffusion model sample quality, but it requires an explicit condition (text, class label) and cannot be applied to unconditional generation. Existing unconditional alternatives—Self-Attention Guidance (SAG) and Perturbed Attention Guidance (PAG)—rely on heuristic perturbations of attention maps, producing side effects such as saturation, color shifts, and structural distortion at higher guidance scales, without a clear theoretical justification for why those perturbations should improve quality.

## Input / Output

SEG operates as a drop-in modification to the self-attention layers (mid-blocks) of a pre-trained diffusion model (SDXL), requiring no retraining or additional parameters. It accepts the same inputs as the base model—a noise tensor and optionally a text prompt, Canny edge map, or depth map via ControlNet—and outputs a generated RGB image. The only new hyperparameter is the standard deviation σ of a 2D Gaussian kernel applied to the query matrix; the guidance scale γ_seg is held fixed (typically 3.0), so quality is controlled entirely by σ rather than by extrapolating predictions.

## Method

The paper defines an energy function for self-attention weights (E(A) = −lse per spatial position, Eq. 5) inspired by the Hopfield energy, showing that the attention operation corresponds to a gradient step on this landscape. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that applying a 2D Gaussian blur to the attention weights before the softmax attenuates the Gaussian curvature of the energy function, yielding a smoother, less peaked prediction. SEG then uses this blurred-attention prediction as the 'negative' score in a CFG-style guidance formula (Eq. 9), so the sampling step moves toward the original (sharper) prediction and away from the smoothed one. To avoid quadratic cost in the number of tokens, the paper proves (Proposition 3.1) that blurring the full attention matrix QK⊤ is equivalent to blurring only the query matrix Q before the matmul, reducing the operation to a linear convolution on Q. This makes the guidance strength continuously tunable via σ (from 0, recovering vanilla attention, to ∞, yielding uniform attention) without the saturation that plagues scale-based methods.

## Results

On unconditional generation with SDXL, SEG achieves FID 95.316 (σ=10) and 88.215 (σ→∞), substantially outperforming SAG (FID 106.683), PAG (FID 105.271), and vanilla SDXL (FID 129.496). SEG also produces the lowest LPIPS deviation from the unguided output (LPIPS_vgg 0.522, LPIPS_alex 0.454 at σ=10), indicating fewer structural side effects than SAG (0.706/0.644) and comparable or better than PAG (0.542/0.472). For text-conditional generation, SEG improves FID from 53.423 (vanilla) to 26.169 (σ→∞) and CLIP score from 0.271 to 0.292, evaluated on 30k MS-COCO 2014 validation references.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEG achieves a Pareto improvement over both prior unconditional guidance methods. On FID, SEG (σ=10) reduces the score by 11.4 points over SAG (95.3 vs. 106.7) and by 10.0 points over PAG (95.3 vs. 105.3), with the extreme σ→∞ setting pushing FID further to 88.2. On LPIPS (measuring structural deviation from the unguided output), SEG σ=10 is the closest to vanilla SDXL (0.522 vgg / 0.454 alex), beating SAG by a wide margin (0.706/0.644) and slightly edging out PAG (0.542/0.472). This means SEG improves sample quality while introducing fewer unintended artifacts—smoothed details, saturation, color shifts, or structural changes—than either SAG or PAG. Against vanilla SDXL (no guidance), SEG provides a 34–41 point FID reduction, confirming that the energy-curvature-based guidance is effective even without any external condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based interpretation of self-attention (rooted in the Hopfield energy formulation of Ramsauer et al.) to derive a principled guidance mechanism for diffusion models. It departs from prior unconditional guidance methods—Self-Attention Guidance (SAG), which blurs input pixels using attention-derived masks, and Perturbed Attention Guidance (PAG), which replaces attention with an identity matrix—by instead applying a Gaussian blur directly to the attention weights before softmax, grounded in a curvature analysis of the underlying energy landscape. The guidance formula mirrors the structure of classifier-free guidance (CFG), but replaces the unconditional prediction with the blurred-attention prediction, making it applicable without any external condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEG provides a training-free, condition-free drop-in method that closes the quality gap between conditional and unconditional diffusion sampling, with a continuous σ parameter that avoids the saturation problems plaguing guidance-scale-based methods. The query-blurring equivalence avoids quadratic token complexity, making it practical for large models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporal attention in video diffusion, whether it generalizes beyond U-Net architectures (e.g., DiT), and whether the energy-curvature framing can inform other attention-modulation strategies. The attainable quality remains bounded by the base model, and the method has only been validated on SDXL.
