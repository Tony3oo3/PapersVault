## Problem

Non-negativity-constrained kernel methods face a fundamental trade-off: linear approximators (e.g., NCM) enforce non-negativity via α ≥ 0, which severely limits representation power, while nonlinear approximators (e.g., QNM, SNF) achieve universal approximation at the cost of expensive quadratic or neural-network evaluations. No prior method combined linear representation with universal approximation for non-negative functions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on one-dimensional scalar inputs (x ∈ ℝ) for the main theoretical results, consuming data points (x_n, y_n) for regression, sample points for density estimation, or event sequences for intensity estimation. The output is a linear combination f(x) = k(x)ᵀα that is guaranteed non-negative for all x ∈ 𝒳, subject to the weaker constraint (K + s(N+1)I)α ≥ 0 rather than the element-wise α ≥ 0. For multi-dimensional inputs, the constraint becomes (K + (N−2)I)α ≥ 0 with multiplicative exponential kernels.

## Method

The paper introduces inverse M-kernels: positive semi-definite kernels whose Gram matrices (plus a diagonal shift s(N)I) are entry-wise non-negative inverses of M-matrices. By applying a partitioned inverse M-matrix lemma (Lemma 1), the paper proves that the linear approximator f(x) = k(x)ᵀα is non-negative for all x whenever (K + s(N+1)I)α ≥ 0, a strictly weaker constraint than α ≥ 0. For one-dimensional inputs, exponential/Abel kernels k(x,x') = e^{−|x−x'|/τ} are shown to be strict inverse M-kernels (s(N)=0) because their inverse Gram matrices are tridiagonal M-matrices, yielding the first linear universal approximator of non-negative functions. For multi-dimensional inputs, multiplicative exponential kernels satisfy the strict path product condition, establishing them as inverse M-kernels with s(N) = N−3, though the resulting constraint approaches NCM as N grows.

## Results

On the KdV regression task (N=40), the proposed IMK achieves l²=0.047±0.012 (σ=0.1) and l²=0.011±0.001 (σ=0.01), outperforming NCM (0.078 and 0.074) while matching QNM's speed advantage by being ~300× faster (0.002s vs 0.634s at σ=0.1). On density estimation (N=50), IMK achieves the best KL divergence of 0.123±0.049, beating NCM/QNM (0.163) and SNF (0.452) while running in 0.053s versus QNM's 0.483s. On intensity estimation, IMK achieves l²=2.01×10³, comparable to STVB (1.74×10³) but 222× faster (4.53s vs 1006s), and substantially better than IEK (7.50×10³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NCM [17], IMK consistently wins on accuracy across all three tasks: l² drops from 0.078 to 0.047 (σ=0.1) and 0.074 to 0.011 (σ=0.01) on KdV regression, and d_KL improves from 0.163 to 0.123 on density estimation, confirming the paper's claim that the weaker constraint (K+sI)α≥0 yields greater representation power than α≥0. Against QNM [17], QNM achieves the best l² on KdV regression (0.034 vs 0.047 at σ=0.1) because the smooth ground truth aligns well with the Gaussian kernel QNM uses, but IMK is ~300× faster (0.002s vs 0.634s); on density estimation, IMK actually beats QNM on both d_KL (0.123 vs 0.163) and speed (0.053s vs 0.483s). Against SNF [34], IMK substantially outperforms on density estimation (d_KL 0.123 vs 0.452) and is ~56× faster, with SNF's poor performance attributed to overfitting at N=50. Against IEK [6], IMK with an equivalent inverse M-kernel achieves 3.7× lower l² (2.01 vs 7.50 ×10³) on intensity estimation and eliminates the artificial zero crossings (nodal line problem) visible in IEK's estimates. Against STVB [1], IMK is slightly worse on l² (2.01 vs 1.74 ×10³) but runs 222× faster (4.53s vs 1006s), making it a practical alternative for large-scale intensity estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical notion of inverse M-matrices (Johnson, 1982) to kernel functions, coining the term 'inverse M-kernel' to identify a sufficient condition under which a linear kernel-based approximator is guaranteed non-negative. It directly addresses the linearity-versus-flexibility trade-off identified in Marteau-Ferey et al. (2020), where the non-negative coefficients model (NCM) sacrifices representation power and the quadratic non-negative model (QNM) sacrifices linearity. The equivalent-kernel framework for Poisson intensity estimation (Flaxman et al., 2017) is extended via Theorem 3 to show that equivalent kernels inherit the inverse M-kernel property, and the strict path product condition (Johnson & Smith, 2007) is leveraged to extend the result to multi-dimensional multiplicative exponential kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes, for the first time, that linear universal approximators of non-negative functions exist (in one dimension), resolving a gap in the kernel-methods literature where non-negativity was thought to force either nonlinear forms or degraded representation power. The practical payoff is substantial: the IMK model is hundreds of times faster than QNM while maintaining convex optimization and cheap evaluation. However, the main result is restricted to 1D input spaces, and the multi-dimensional extension via multiplicative exponential kernels requires s(N) = N − 3, making the constraint nearly as tight as NCM for moderate-to-large N. Open questions include whether smoother inverse M-kernels exist beyond the exponential family, and whether tighter bounds on the diagonal shift η can be found for multi-dimensional settings.
