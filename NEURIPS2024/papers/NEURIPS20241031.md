## Problem

Existing data augmentation for IoT sensing signals either applies simplistic manual transformations that fail to capture nuanced low-level signal variance, or treats generative models as black boxes conditioned only on class labels, losing the opportunity to leverage domain knowledge. This paper addresses the gap by introducing fine-grained, human-interpretable control over the generative augmentation process through a purpose-built metric space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw IoT sensing time-series (accelerometer, gyroscope, acoustic, seismic, or EEG at 80–100 Hz sampling rates), converts them to 2D STFT spectrograms, and extracts five statistical metrics (temporal amplitude range, F0 amplitude, contrast, flatness, entropy) that are embedded alongside the class label into a condition tensor. The output is synthetic STFT spectrograms whose metric values match user-specified targets, which are combined with the original data to form an augmented training set for downstream classifiers. Multi-modal inputs (e.g., accelerometer + gyroscope) are handled by separate U-Net branches without cross-modal fusion.

## Method

The approach is a three-stage pipeline: (1) define a metric space with five statistical metrics spanning time, frequency, and time-frequency domains of the STFT spectrogram; (2) train a conditional generative model (diffusion model with U-Net backbone or hierarchical NVAE) where the condition tensor—built by reshaping each scalar metric into a spatial tensor and concatenating with the label embedding—is fed to the network, and an additional metric loss (weighted L2 between generated and target metric values) is added to the standard generative loss to enforce metric alignment; (3) apply augmentation techniques in the metric space—interpolation between same-label metric vectors, bounded disturbance within user-defined ranges, or domain-knowledge-instructed directional modification—to produce new condition vectors that drive the generative model. The key differentiator is that the metrics serve as interpretable control knobs: domain experts can specify, e.g., 'increase contrast by 10–40% and decrease flatness by 10–40%' to simulate energetic running styles, and the generative model faithfully produces spectrograms matching those targets while preserving signal authenticity.

## Results

On the three IoT sensing benchmarks, the best variant (Diff-disturb, using a diffusion model backbone with metric-space disturbance augmentation) achieves 83.1% accuracy on HAR (DeepSense), 82.0% on Vehicle Detection (DeepSense), and 75.1% on Harmful Brain Activity Recognition (DeepSense), improving over no augmentation by +5.6%, +3.3%, and +5.1% respectively. The upper-bound Reconst variant (using ground-truth validation metrics as conditions) reaches 86.2% on HAR, 82.1% on Vehicle Detection, and 75.9% on Brain Activity, confirming the value of precise metric control. Across both downstream models (DeepSense and Transformer), the method consistently outperforms all six baselines on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Diff-disturb method outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and downstream model. Against the strongest traditional baseline (Time/Freq Transformation, SpecAugment-style), it gains +3.0% accuracy on HAR, +1.8% on Vehicle Detection, and +2.8% on Brain Activity (DeepSense). Against the strongest generative baseline (cDiff, label-only conditional diffusion), it gains +2.7% on HAR, +3.7% on Vehicle Detection, and +1.4% on Brain Activity, demonstrating that fine-grained metric-space control provides meaningful gains over label-only conditioning. The largest margins are over cGAN (+7.8% to +8.8% accuracy) and Feature Space Interpolation (+4.7% to +6.5%), indicating that both GAN-based generation and latent-space interpolation are substantially inferior for IoT sensing augmentation. The diffusion backbone consistently outperforms the VAE backbone by 1–5% accuracy, confirming that generative quality is a critical factor in augmentation effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior work on condition space interpolation for HAR [54] by redesigning the metric space with five interpretable spectrogram statistics (temporal amplitude range, F0 amplitude, contrast, flatness, entropy) and introducing three augmentation techniques of varying domain-knowledge intensity. It departs from prior generative data augmentation for IoT sensing (cGAN, cVAE, cDiff), which treat the generative model as a black box conditioned only on class labels, by embedding domain-expert knowledge directly into the generation process via the metric space. The approach also draws on conditional generation techniques from speech and singing synthesis [25, 4, 16, 33], where fine-grained acoustic features guide waveform generation, but adapts them to the fundamentally different semantics of multi-modal IoT time-series signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metric-space paradigm offers a practical bridge between domain expertise and generative model capabilities, enabling IoT practitioners to directionally augment datasets toward known distribution shifts (e.g., running style, road surface, seizure pattern) without collecting new labeled data. Key limitations include the empirical (non-quantitative) selection of metrics, the non-trivial burden of formulating domain knowledge instructions, and the need to re-validate metrics for new sensing modalities. Open questions include whether metric importance can be learned automatically from downstream model discriminative features, whether modality fusion within the generative model would further improve multi-sensor augmentation, and how the approach scales to open-vocabulary or few-shot IoT sensing scenarios.
