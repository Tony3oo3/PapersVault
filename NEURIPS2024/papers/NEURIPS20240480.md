## Problem

Existing data selection methods for CLIP pretraining either rely on external non-CLIP models or newly trained teacher models, while the third direction—designing better metrics universally applicable to any CLIP embedding—remains under-explored. The standard CLIPScore metric can systematically overestimate quality for samples with generic text (e.g., containing 'image' or 'photo') or underinformative images, and it provides no mechanism to incorporate knowledge about downstream target tasks. This paper asks whether the information already contained in CLIP embeddings can be exploited more effectively without any external resources.

## Input / Output

The method consumes precomputed CLIP teacher model embeddings (ViT-L/14 or ViT-B/32) for ~110M image-text pairs from DataComp-medium, plus K=10 randomly sampled batches from the training set to estimate the negCLIPLoss normalization term. When target task images are available (e.g., ImageNet-1k training set or all 24 downstream task images), their vision embeddings are additionally required for NormSim. The output is a ranked subset of the original data (typically 20% or 30% size) selected by intersecting top-scoring samples under negCLIPLoss (quality) and NormSim (target relevance), which is then used to train a fixed CLIP-B/32 student model.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary metrics. First, negCLIPLoss replaces CLIPScore by subtracting a batch-level normalization term R* derived from the standard CLIP contrastive loss: it measures how much a sample's cross-modal similarity exceeds its similarity to contrastive pairs in the batch, thereby correcting for systematic biases where generic text or simple images inflate raw cosine similarity across all pairs. The normalization is estimated by averaging over K=10 random batches (K>5 is stable), using the teacher model's temperature τ and batch size |B| directly. Second, NormSim measures the p-norm (p=2 or ∞) of the vector of cosine similarities between a training sample's vision embedding and all target task image embeddings; for p=∞, a sample is selected if it is highly similar to any target sample, while p=2 aligns selection with the principal components of target variance. Critically, NormSim uses vision-only embeddings rather than multimodal ones, since web-crawled captions are often too brief for the language embedding to reliably characterize the target distribution. The two metrics are combined via intersection (negCLIPLoss ∩ NormSim) to balance data quality and downstream relevance, and the approach is shown to be universally applicable across different CLIP architectures and teacher models.

## Results

On DataComp-medium, the best D1-category combination (negCLIPLoss(30%) ∩ NormSim∞(Target)) achieves 31.7% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k and 35.0% average across 38 downstream tasks, improving 5.3% and 2.8% respectively over the best CLIPScore(30%) baseline (26.4% / 32.2%). When combined with the prior SOTA method DFN∪HYPE, the new method DFN∪HYPE∪Ours(10%)* reaches 37.3% on ImageNet-1k and 37.7% average, a 0.9% improvement over DFN∪HYPE (36.4% / 36.8%). On the full 128M DataComp-medium dataset, DFN∪HYPE∪Ours(10%) achieves 38.2% IN-1k and 38.8% average, establishing a new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CLIPScore baseline [38], negCLIPLoss consistently outperforms it across all teacher models (OAI-L/14, OAI-B/32, DFN-P) and all sampling ratios, with gains of 0.4%–3.0% on average 38 tasks. The D1-only method Ours(20%) (35.2% avg) beats all D3 baselines except DFN and DFN∪HYPE, outperforming T-MARS (34.1%), Devil (34.5%), MLM (34.5%), and HYPE (31.9%) by 0.7–3.3 points on average. DFN [2] alone (35.4%) still exceeds Ours(20%) by 0.2%, reflecting the advantage of a purpose-trained filtering model, but combining DFN with Ours(20%)* (37.6%) surpasses DFN∪HYPE (36.8%) by 0.8%. The full combination DFN∪HYPE∪Ours(10%)* (37.7%) achieves the new SOTA, improving 0.9% over the prior best DFN∪HYPE on both ImageNet-1k and average 38 tasks. Image-based filtering from DataComp [1] (29.9% avg) and D² Pruning [18] (29.5% avg) are both substantially outperformed by the proposed methods, confirming that norm-based target similarity and loss-normalized quality scoring are more effective than clustering-based or graph-based diversity strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the data selection landscape for CLIP pretraining by focusing on improving how existing CLIP embeddings are utilized (Direction 1), rather than training new filtering models (Direction 2, e.g., DFN) or leveraging external non-CLIP models (Direction 3, e.g., HYPE, T-MARS). It directly builds on the CLIP contrastive loss (Radford et al., 2021) by algebraically rearranging it into a per-sample quality score (negCLIPLoss) that corrects systematic biases in the widely-used CLIPScore (Hessel et al., 2021). The NormSim metric extends the image-based filtering idea from DataComp (Gadre et al., 2023) by replacing k-means clustering with a direct p-norm similarity computation, and provides a theoretical justification under linear embedding assumptions that prior heuristic methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that negCLIPLoss is a universal, drop-in replacement for CLIPScore that works with any CLIP architecture (B/32, L/14, DFN-P) and requires only ~5 hours of preprocessing versus 900+ hours for methods like T-MARS or MLM, making it highly accessible. The finding that DFN-P's embeddings—designed specifically for filtering—are actually worse for NormSim than standard OAI CLIP embeddings is a useful cautionary insight for practitioners combining filtering and target-aware selection. Key limitations include evaluation only on DataComp-medium (not large/xlarge scales), and the open question of how to optimally merge subsets selected by different methods (intersection vs. union vs. weighted combination) remains unresolved.
