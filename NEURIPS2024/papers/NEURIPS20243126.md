## Problem

Existing methods for finding minimal ℓ2 adversarial perturbations face a fundamental trade-off: geometric methods like DeepFool are fast but produce overly large perturbations that are not orthogonal to the decision boundary, while optimization-based methods (C&W, ALMA, FAB, DDN, FMN) find smaller perturbations at 20–1000× the computational cost. The paper identifies that DF's inaccuracy stems from stopping at an arbitrary misclassified point rather than the true boundary, and from the perturbation direction not being aligned with the boundary normal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single input image and white-box access to a classifier (forward and input-gradient evaluations) and outputs a minimal ℓ2 adversarial perturbation vector r such that x+r is misclassified. The algorithm is entirely parameter-free, requires only 25–50 gradient computations per sample, and terminates naturally upon finding a successful adversarial example rather than requiring a fixed iteration budget. It generalizes directly from binary to multi-class classifiers by substituting the standard multi-class DeepFool step.

## Method

SDF exploits the geometric property that the optimal minimal perturbation must be orthogonal to the decision boundary. It alternates between two operations: (1) DeepFool steps that linearize the classifier and move the point toward the boundary, and (2) a projection step that re-projects the current perturbation onto the gradient direction at the current point, steering it toward the boundary normal. The SDF(∞,1) variant runs DF until an adversarial example is found, then applies a single projection step, repeating this outer loop until the perturbation converges. This single projection per DF cycle reduces median ℓ2 perturbations by 20–30% over DF while requiring only ~2× the gradient computations, and outperforms all SOTA minimum-norm attacks in both perturbation size and speed across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet. The paper further shows that adversarial training with SDF-generated examples yields models with lower input curvature and up to 30% improved robustness against minimum-norm attacks compared to DDN-trained models.

## Results

On ImageNet with a naturally trained RN-50, SDF achieves a median ℓ₂ perturbation of 0.09 using only 37 gradient computations, outperforming ALMA (0.10, 100 grads), DDN (0.17, 1000 grads), FAB (0.10, 900 grads), FMN (0.10, 1000 grads), and C&W (0.21, 82,667 grads). On the adversarially trained RN-50, SDF finds perturbations of median ℓ₂ = 0.80 with 49 gradients, beating ALMA (0.85, 100 grads) and all other attacks. On CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-10), SDF achieves median ℓ₂ = 0.09 with 25 gradients versus 0.10–0.13 for ALMA/DDN/FAB/FMN at 100 gradients each, and 0.12 for C&W at 90,000 gradients. SDF also achieves 100% fooling rate across all tested models, matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDF consistently outperforms DeepFool by a large margin: on CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-10), SDF finds perturbations of median ℓ₂ = 0.09 versus DF's 0.26, and on ImageNet 0.09 versus 0.31, while using only ~2x more gradient steps. Against C&W, SDF achieves smaller perturbations (e.g., 0.09 vs. 0.12 on CIFAR-10, 0.09 vs. 0.21 on ImageNet) while requiring roughly 2,000–2,000x fewer gradient computations (25–49 vs. 52,000–90,000). SDF also beats DDN, FAB, and FMN on both perturbation size and speed: on ImageNet (natural), SDF's 0.09 median ℓ₂ with 37 grads surpasses DDN (0.17, 1000 grads), FAB (0.10, 900 grads), and FMN (0.10, 1000 grads). The closest competitor is ALMA, which achieves a marginally smaller perturbation on CIFAR-10 (0.10 vs. 0.09 is actually SDF winning) and on ImageNet adversarially trained (0.85 vs. 0.80, SDF winning), but requires 2–20x more gradient computations. In the adversarial training setting, SDF-trained models achieve 90.8% clean accuracy and outperform DDN-trained models by up to 30% in robustness against minimum-norm attacks, while also exhibiting lower input curvature (1.66 vs. 4.32 normalized curvature). The AutoAttack++ variant incorporating SDF achieves comparable robust accuracy to standard AutoAttack while being up to 3x faster in gradient computations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DeepFool (DF), the first geometric minimum-norm adversarial attack, by adding a single projection step that steers the perturbation toward orthogonality with the decision boundary. While prior minimum-norm attacks (C&W, FAB, DDN, FMN, ALMA) frame the problem as a non-convex optimization and rely on iterative solvers with fixed budgets, SDF exploits the geometric characterization of the optimal perturbation (parallel to the boundary normal) to achieve comparable or better accuracy with far fewer gradient evaluations. The method also draws on the observation that decision boundaries of deep networks have small mean curvature near data samples, justifying the local linearization that DF already employs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDF's dramatic computational efficiency (25–52 gradient steps vs. 100–10,000 for competitors) makes it practical for robustness evaluation of large models and for adversarial training at scale, and its integration into AutoAttack (AA++) yields up to 3× speedup while preserving fooling rates. The geometric framing also provides a lens for interpreting model properties (e.g., curvature analysis showing SDF-trained models have lower input curvature). Open questions include extending the approach to targeted attacks and other ℓp norms, establishing convergence guarantees for the full alternating algorithm, and understanding why the targeted variant does not reach 100% fooling rate.
