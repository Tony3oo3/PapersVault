## Problem

RLHF and DPO alignment techniques have been shown to degrade language models' reasoning capabilities—particularly for smaller models—while also increasing hallucination rates. The paper addresses this by proposing an alignment method that operates at inference time rather than modifying the generation process, allowing a model to self-evaluate and select among candidate outputs without compromising its underlying reasoning abilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates multiple candidate completions from the language model. Each candidate is scored by a reward head that computes a scalar from the final token's hidden state (last transformer layer) passed through a pooling function (identity, BERT-style affine+tanh, or SwiGLU FFN+tanh) and a learnable projection vector. The highest-scoring candidate is selected as the final output, making this a re-ranking procedure over sampled generations rather than a single-pass generation.

## Method

Direct Preference Heads (DPH) adds an auxiliary reward head to a language model that learns to assign preference scores to completions without altering the language modeling head's output distribution. Two novel loss functions are formulated: Separable DPH, which drives preferred rewards toward a positive fixed point and dispreferred rewards toward a negative one, and Contrastive DPH, which optimizes the relative margin between them; both are proven convex and converge to margins parameterized by a label-smoothing ε, establishing a direct theoretical link to Conservative DPO (cDPO). Training uses a joint objective combining cDPO alignment with the DPH reward loss, with prior regularization (Gaussian around reference parameters) to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the SFT model's generative capabilities. At inference time, the model samples multiple candidates and the DPH reward head selects the best one, effectively performing alignment as a re-ranking step that avoids the reasoning degradation associated with direct policy optimization.

## Results

On the GPT4All commonsense reasoning suite, the DPH model (551M params) achieves a 60.21% average accuracy, outperforming TinyLlama (1.1B, 3T tokens) by 7.2% and Pythia-1.4B (1.5B, 300B tokens) by 8.9%. On GLUE, DPH attains a 78.6% macro-average (excluding WNLI), surpassing GPT-1's task-specific fine-tunes (72.8%) by 2.2%. On RACE reading comprehension, DPH reaches 62.5% weighted accuracy, exceeding GPT-1's RACE-specific fine-tune (59.0%) by 3.5% and LLaMA-13B zero-shot (51.4%) by 11.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-1 (117M, 32B tokens), DPH wins on both GLUE (78.6% vs 72.8% macro-average w/o WNLI) and RACE (62.5% vs 59.0% weighted accuracy), despite GPT-1 using task-specific fine-tunes while DPH uses a single jointly-trained model. On GLUE, DPH still trails BERT_Large (82.5%) by 3.9%, which the authors attribute to BERT's per-task fine-tuning versus their multi-task joint training. On the GPT4All suite, DPH (551M, 100B tokens) substantially outperforms Pythia-1.0B (48.30%), Pythia-1.4B (51.33%), and TinyLlama (52.99%) by 11.9%, 8.9%, and 7.2% respectively, achieving the highest accuracy on 5 of 7 individual tasks. On RACE, DPH surpasses both LLaMA-7B (51.0%) and LLaMA-13B (51.4%) zero-shot by over 11 points, and beats GPT-1's RACE-specific fine-tune by 3.5%. The DPH reward-based selection consistently improves over the DPO-only baseline across all three benchmarks (GLUE +1.6%, GPT4All +3.2%, RACE +0.9%), confirming that inference-time re-ranking adds value beyond preference-optimized generation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard RLHF/DPO paradigm of directly modifying the language modeling head's output distribution to align with preferences. Instead, it introduces an auxiliary reward head (Direct Preference Head) that scores candidate completions at inference time without altering the generation process, building on the DPO insight that 'a language model is secretly a reward model' (Rafailov et al., 2024) and the label-smoothing robustness of Conservative DPO (Mitchell, 2023). The theoretical analysis shows the DPH loss functions converge to fixed reward margins structurally analogous to cDPO's fixed delta from the reference policy, positioning DPH as a complementary rather than competing alignment objective. Unlike PPO-based RLHF which requires separate reward, reference, and policy models, DPH operates within a single model and requires no human labelling stage, leveraging pre-constructed preference datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented concern that RLHF and DPO can degrade reasoning capabilities in smaller language models, offering a practical alternative that preserves the base model's generative distribution while enabling preference-aware selection at inference time. The frozen-model ablations on Qwen provide clean evidence that chat fine-tuning harms 0.5B models' reasoning while DPH partially recovers performance, validating the core motivation. Key limitations include the inability to prevent harmful outputs (only detect them), the computational overhead of sampling multiple candidates at inference, and evaluation restricted to a single 551M model, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models where RLHF degradation is less pronounced. The finding that contrastive vs. separable objectives suit different task types (sentence completion vs. classification) suggests task-adaptive reward heads as a promising direction.
