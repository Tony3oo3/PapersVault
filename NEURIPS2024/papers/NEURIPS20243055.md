## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations in zero-shot settings, yet prior methods (TeCoA, PMG-AFT, FARE) that improve adversarial robustness sacrifice clean-sample accuracy, and the mechanism by which adversarial attacks cause misclassification remained unexplained. This paper identifies that adversarial perturbations induce systematic shifts in text-guided attention away from the target object, and leverages this observation to improve robustness without degrading clean performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243055/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224 for ViT-B/32) and a text prompt (class name) as input. The image encoder produces a global feature vector, and the frozen text encoder produces a text embedding; classification is via cosine similarity between these embeddings. Additionally, a text-guided attention map is computed as the outer product of the pre-pooling global image feature and the text embedding, reshaped to spatial dimensions (√P × √P) and resized to H×W, which serves as the training signal for both robustness and clean-accuracy preservation.

## Method

TGA-ZSR performs adversarial fine-tuning of CLIP's ViT-B/32 image encoder on Tiny-ImageNet using PGD-2-generated adversarial examples, with a composite loss L_total = L_CE + α·L_AR + β·L_AMC. The Attention Refinement module computes the L2 distance between the text-guided attention of adversarial examples (from the target model) and clean examples (from the original frozen model), forcing the fine-tuned model to preserve the correct attention pattern under perturbation. The Attention-based Model Constraint module computes the L2 distance between text-guided attention of clean examples from both the target and original models, preventing parameter drift that would degrade clean accuracy. The text encoder remains frozen throughout, so the text-guided attention acts as a stable semantic anchor that the image encoder must respect, with α=0.08 and β=0.05 balancing the three loss terms.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (PGD-100, ε=1/255), TGA-ZSR achieves an average zero-shot robust accuracy of 42.09% across 16 datasets, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art PMG-AFT (32.51%) by 9.58% and the original CLIP (4.90%) by 37.19%. On zero-shot clean accuracy, TGA-ZSR reaches 56.44% on average, surpassing PMG-AFT (46.60%) by 9.84% while remaining within 3.41% of FARE (59.85%), which prioritizes clean accuracy at the cost of robustness. Against stronger attacks (AutoAttack, CW), TGA-ZSR similarly leads, achieving 30.86% average under AutoAttack and 40.50% under CW attack versus PMG-AFT's 30.78% and 31.07% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGA-ZSR outperforms all four baselines on zero-shot robust accuracy across nearly all 16 datasets. Against the strongest prior method PMG-AFT [59], TGA-ZSR gains 9.58% average robust accuracy (42.09% vs. 32.51%) and 9.84% average clean accuracy (56.44% vs. 46.60%), winning on 15 of 16 datasets for robustness. Compared to TeCoA [38], the improvement is even larger at 14.03% in robust accuracy, while TGA-ZSR also surpasses TeCoA on clean accuracy by 10.63%. Against FARE [51], TGA-ZSR achieves a 23.84% gain in robust accuracy (42.09% vs. 18.25%) but trails by 3.41% in clean accuracy (56.44% vs. 59.85%), reflecting FARE's design priority of preserving clean performance. The original CLIP [48] baseline is essentially non-functional under adversarial attack (4.90% average), confirming the critical need for the proposed adversarial fine-tuning strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot adversarial robustness line for VLMs initiated by TeCoA (Mao et al., 2023) and advanced by PMG-AFT (Wang et al., 2024) and FARE (Schlarmann et al., 2024), all of which fine-tune CLIP's image encoder on a single dataset (Tiny-ImageNet) to improve generalization against adversarial perturbations. The key conceptual departure is replacing feature-level or embedding-level constraints (cross-entropy in TeCoA, feature distance in PMG-AFT, embedding distance in FARE) with text-guided attention map alignment, motivated by the observation that adversarial perturbations cause measurable shifts in the attention distribution between the image encoder's output and the frozen text encoder's embedding. This positions the work as an interpretability-driven refinement of the adversarial fine-tuning paradigm rather than a fundamentally new training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-guided attention interpretation provides a mechanistic explanation for why adversarial attacks succeed on CLIP (attention drifts away from the relevant object), which prior zero-shot robustness methods lacked. The practical impact is a 9.58% average robust accuracy gain over the prior SOTA (PMG-AFT) across 16 datasets while also maintaining clean accuracy, addressing the common robustness-accuracy trade-off. However, the authors acknowledge that gains against stronger attacks like AutoAttack are more modest, and the method is validated only on CLIP ViT-B/32 with a single training dataset, leaving open whether the attention-alignment strategy generalizes to other VLM architectures or scales to larger models. The simplicity of the approach (L2 distance on attention maps) suggests it could be easily integrated into existing CLIP fine-tuning pipelines.
