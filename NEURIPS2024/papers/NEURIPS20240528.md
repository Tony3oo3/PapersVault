## Problem

While prior work shows in-context learning (ICL) is fairly robust to noisy labels on classification tasks, the effect of annotation noise on ICL for text generation remains unexplored. This paper demonstrates that noisy annotations significantly degrade ICL performance on generation tasks, and that increasing the number of demonstrations or switching to stronger selection methods (TopK, DPP) cannot bridge the gap. The central question is how to make ICL robust to noisy input-output pairs in generation settings without requiring model retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a test input, a large annotated dataset of input-output pairs (potentially containing noisy labels at rates up to 90%), and a frozen LLM (e.g., Llama2-7B). It produces a filtered set of K in-context demonstrations where noisy candidates have been replaced by cleaner local neighbors, which are then prepended to the test input for generation. The method operates on tokenized input-output pairs, computing perplexity via the LLM's forward pass and using BERT sentence embeddings for k-NN neighbor retrieval, with two lightweight hyperparameters: a perplexity-rank threshold γ (default 50%) and a neighbor count k (default 4).

## Method

Local Perplexity Ranking (LPR) is built on the decomposition of overall perplexity into inherent perplexity (task complexity of the question and correct answer) and matching perplexity (deviation caused by a wrong output). The key insight is that semantically similar examples share comparable inherent perplexity, so ranking perplexity among local neighbors in embedding space effectively isolates the matching perplexity signal. Concretely, after an existing selection method (Random, TopK, or DPP) picks K candidates, LPR finds k nearest neighbors for each candidate via cosine similarity in BERT embedding space, ranks all k+1 examples in the local cluster by perplexity, and replaces any candidate whose rank exceeds the threshold γ with the highest-ranked neighbor. This replacement is algorithm-agnostic, training-free, and requires only ~20% of the computation of a global perplexity ranking, since perplexity is computed only over local neighborhoods rather than the full candidate pool.

## Results

On six text generation benchmarks (NQ, WebQ, SQuAD, SCIQ, GeoQuery, NL2Bash), LPR consistently improves the noise-robustness of all three baseline selection methods (Random, TopK, DPP) under both irrelevant and relevant label noise at 20–60% rates. The most striking improvement is on SCIQ with 60% irrelevant noise, where LPR boosts TopK's EM score from 29.31 to 48.06 (+18.75) and Random's from 28.21 to 42.00 (+13.79). The method is model-agnostic, yielding gains with Llama2-7B/13B, Mistral-7B, and OPT-6.7B, and requires only ~20% of the computation of a global perplexity ranking alternative. LPR is also insensitive to its two hyperparameters (threshold γ and neighbor count k), with k=4 and γ=50% working well by default.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPR is a plug-in that wraps any existing demonstration selection method and consistently outperforms the unmodified baseline across all six generation datasets and both noise types. Against Random [36], the gains are largest on SQuAD (+16.80 EM at 60% irrelevant noise) and SCIQ (+13.79), while against TopK [28] the headline improvement is +18.75 EM on SCIQ (29.31→48.06) and +14.43 on GeoQuery (9.96→24.39). Against DPP [62], LPR yields the single largest absolute gain in the paper: +22.64 BLEU on GeoQuery (10.61→33.25) at 60% irrelevant noise. In the CoT comparison (Table 7), LPR-augmented ICL outperforms Zero-Shot-CoT [20] (10.06) and improves Manual-CoT [53] and Auto-CoT [69] under noise (e.g., Auto-CoT on NL2Bash at 60% irrelevant noise: 10.86→27.32). The method also transfers to text classification (SST2, AGNews), where it recovers the performance drop that TopK and DPP suffer under symmetric/asymmetric label noise, while Random selection is barely affected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noise-robust ICL literature, which has primarily studied label noise in classification tasks (where ICL was found to be fairly robust), to the underexplored setting of text generation. It builds on existing demonstration selection methods (Random, TopK, DPP) as plug-in baselines rather than proposing a new selection paradigm, and draws on the noisy-label learning literature's idea of detecting corrupted labels via local neighborhood consistency. The key conceptual departure is the perplexity decomposition into inherent and matching components, which motivates the local ranking strategy and distinguishes this work from global perplexity-based filtering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs are increasingly used to generate training annotations at scale, the assumption of clean labels becomes untenable, making noise-robust ICL a practically critical concern. The finding that generation tasks are far more sensitive to annotation noise than classification tasks—and that standard remedies (more demonstrations, better selection) actually worsen the problem—should reshape how practitioners construct ICL pipelines. Open questions include a theoretical characterization of how noise propagates through ICL, whether the approach extends to multi-turn or agentic settings, and how to handle the regime where clean annotations are no longer the majority.
