## Problem

Popular training-free consistency methods (ConsiStory, StoryDiffusion) rely on concatenated attention to inject reference features, but the underlying mechanism by which this works has not been formally analyzed, and the resulting guidance strength is implicitly fixed by the latent features with no user control. This rigidity prevents applications that require varying degrees of consistency, suppressing specific features for diversity, or blending multiple references with different weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (generated by the same diffusion model, not externally sourced) and a text prompt as input, producing one or more output images whose consistency with the reference is controlled by a scalar coefficient c. For video generation, an initial frame (e.g., from InstantID or SDXL) is fed into SVD, and its features are injected into subsequent frames' spatial self-attention. The coefficient c can be positive (0.2–0.4 for consistency, 0.2 for video), negative (−0.3 for diversity/suppression), or zero (no reference influence), and multiple references each receive independent coefficients for blending.

## Method

The paper proves that concatenated attention is mathematically equivalent to a linear interpolation between self-attention on the generated content and cross-attention between generated and reference content, weighted by a content-dependent rank-1 matrix. The key contribution is replacing this rank-1 matrix with a user-specified scalar c in what they call Reference Feature Guidance (RFG): X'_RFG = c·Attention(Q_i, K_1, V_1) + (1−c)·Attention(Q_i, K_i, V_i), which preserves the linear interpolation structure (critical for avoiding training-inference gap) while granting direct control. In practice, RFG replaces all self-attention blocks in the UNet (or equivalent transformer blocks in FLUX), with two practical refinements: excluding the first upsampling block to prevent spatial layout leakage, and applying a Grounded SAM subject mask to restrict guidance to the foreground. For video, the same RFG is applied to spatial self-attention layers in SVD with c=0.2 to stabilize temporal consistency without freezing motion.

## Results

For personalized video generation (Table 3), RefDrop improves over unmodified SVD on all quality and consistency metrics: VQA_Aesthetic rises from 93.25 to 94.27, VQA_Technical from 86.20 to 89.91, CLIP Temporal from 99.83 to 99.91, Face Consistency from 99.20 to 99.46, and Warping Error drops from 0.0077 to 0.0058. The only metric where SVD scores higher is Flow Score (5.80 vs. 2.62), indicating RefDrop produces slightly less motion but substantially less flickering. For image generation, quantitative bar-chart results (Fig. 15) show RefDrop sits on the Pareto front between text alignment and subject consistency, while IP-Adapter achieves the highest diversity but at a significant cost to text alignment. Human evaluation (44 participants, 6,160 ratings) confirms RefDrop is preferred for consistent image generation, diverse image generation (balancing diversity with text alignment), and personalized video quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IP-Adapter [67], RefDrop is competitive in consistent single-subject generation while being training-free and avoiding the need for a separately trained image encoder; in diverse generation, IP-Adapter produces more varied images but significantly compromises text alignment, whereas RefDrop maintains better prompt fidelity. Compared to ConsiStory [55] and StoryDiffusion [71] (both using concatenated attention), RefDrop with c=0.3–0.4 reproduces similar consistency results but adds explicit user control and enables negative coefficients for concept suppression. Against BLIPD [33], RefDrop outperforms in both visual quality and consistency, partly because BLIPD relies on SD1.5 as its base model. For video temporal consistency, both Cross-Frame Attention [29] and Concatenated Attention [62] produce completely static videos, while Temporal LPFF [69] shows only minimal improvement; RefDrop with c=0.2 effectively reduces flickering while preserving natural motion. Relative to unmodified SVD [5], RefDrop improves all quality and consistency metrics (VQA_T by +3.71, Warping Error by −25%) at the cost of slightly reduced motion (Flow Score 2.62 vs. 5.80). The unmodified SDXL [43] baseline lacks any consistency mechanism, and Ref-ControlNet struggles with human subjects, producing distorted eyes and bodies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the concatenated attention mechanism introduced in Tune-A-Video (Wu et al., ICCV 2023) and Cross-Frame Attention (Khachatryan et al., ICCV 2023), both of which inject reference features by extending the key-value sequence in self-attention. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical reframing: the authors prove that concatenated attention is equivalent to a linear interpolation between standard self-attention and cross-attention with a content-dependent rank-1 coefficient, and then argue this rank-1 structure is unnecessary. By replacing it with a user-controlled scalar (Reference Feature Guidance), they position their method as a direct, controllable generalization of both concatenated attention and Cross-Frame Attention (recovered at c=1), while avoiding the encoder training and cross-attention modification required by IP-Adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefDrop provides a lightweight, training-free, plug-and-play mechanism for dialing consistency up or down in both image and video diffusion models, enabling applications (multi-subject blending, concept suppression via negative coefficients, video flicker reduction) that prior fixed-strength methods cannot support. The practical limitation is that the scalar coefficient requires case-by-case tuning, and the method reduces motion in video generation. Open questions include whether attention masks can provide finer-grained spatial control, whether clean (externally sourced) reference images can be accepted without inversion, and whether the first-upsampling-block exclusion generalizes across architectures beyond SDXL.
