## Problem

Existing physics-informed models like Hamiltonian Neural Networks enforce specific physical priors (e.g., strict energy conservation), but real-world dynamical systems often deviate from these assumptions and follow diverse or unknown physical laws. This limits their applicability, as misapplied inductive biases can actually degrade prediction accuracy. The paper seeks a domain-agnostic regularization that improves modeling precision across a wide range of systems regardless of their specific physical properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes M irregularly-sampled historical observations (positions and/or velocities) for each agent in a graph-structured multi-agent system, where timestamps can be non-uniform and continuous-valued. It outputs predicted node dynamics (e.g., positions) at K future timestamps within [0, T], with the graph encoding pairwise physical interactions among agents. For single-agent systems, the graph reduces to a self-loop, making the ODE function a simple MLP.

## Method

TREAT builds on a GraphODE backbone (encoder–GNN-ODE–decoder) and introduces a Time-Reversal Symmetry (TRS) regularization loss that softly enforces trajectory reversibility. The reverse trajectory is generated by starting from the forward trajectory's endpoint and integrating with the negated ODE function −g(·), then the loss minimizes the L2 distance between decoded forward and reverse trajectories at mirrored timestamps. The key theoretical contribution proves that this TRS loss is O(T⁵Δt⁴) compared to the reconstruction loss's O(T³Δt²), meaning it minimizes higher-order Taylor terms (capturing jerk-level dynamics) and provides stronger regularization for long-range prediction. This numerical benefit holds universally—even for irreversible systems—making the approach domain-agnostic. The total training objective is a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and the TRS loss, with the weight α tunable per system.

## Results

TREAT achieves the best MSE (×10⁻²) across all 9 evaluated datasets, spanning multi-agent and single-agent systems with diverse physical properties. The headline result is an 11.5% MSE improvement over the strongest baseline (LG-ODE) on the chaotic triple-pendulum system (1.2527 vs. 1.4156), where energy-conserving baselines like HODEN and TRS-ODEN catastrophically fail (MSE > 741). On the real-world Human Motion dataset, TREAT reduces MSE to 0.2192, outperforming the best data-driven baseline LG-ODE (0.7610) by 71%. On the single-agent Forced Spring, TREAT achieves 0.0960 MSE, the lowest across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREAT outperforms all four baseline families across all 9 datasets. Against the closest multi-agent competitor LG-ODE (GraphODE backbone), TREAT achieves consistent gains: 36% lower MSE on multi-agent Simple Spring (1.12 vs. 1.74), 23% on Forced Spring (1.45 vs. 1.89), 39% on Damped Spring (0.59 vs. 0.97), 11.5% on the chaotic Pendulum (1.25 vs. 1.42), and 71% on Human Motion (0.22 vs. 0.76). The energy-conserving HODEN baseline is highly unstable on non-conservative and chaotic systems, producing MSE values exceeding 54–741 on Damped Spring, Pendulum, and Attractor, confirming that misapplied physical priors degrade performance. TRS-ODEN (single-agent) also fails catastrophically on Pendulum (MSE ≈ 741) due to inaccurate initial-state estimation via linear spline interpolation, while its GNN variant (TRS-ODEN^GNN) improves but still trails TREAT on all multi-agent datasets. On single-agent datasets, TREAT matches or beats LG-ODE (e.g., 0.096 vs. 0.147 on Forced Spring, 0.558 vs. 0.694 on Attractor), demonstrating that the TRS regularization provides numerical benefits even without multi-agent structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

TREAT builds directly on GraphODE (Huang et al., 2020) as its backbone and departs from Hamiltonian Neural Networks, which enforce a specific physical prior (energy conservation) that fails on non-conservative or irreversible systems. It also extends TRS-ODEN (Huh et al., 2020), which uses time-reversal symmetry but with a different loss formulation based on R∘φ_t = φ_{-t}∘R; TREAT instead uses the equivalent but more practical condition R∘φ_t∘R∘φ_t = I (Lemma 2.1), which the authors prove yields lower worst-case error. The key conceptual departure is the first theoretical proof that the TRS regularization universally minimizes higher-order Taylor expansion terms during ODE integration, reframing a domain-specific physical prior as a general numerical regularizer applicable even to irreversible systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bridge between a physical inductive bias (time-reversal symmetry) and a universal numerical benefit (suppression of O(Δt⁴) Taylor residuals) is the paper's most impactful contribution, suggesting that soft physical constraints can serve as architecture-agnostic regularizers beyond their original domain. The 11.5% MSE improvement on the chaotic triple-pendulum and consistent gains across 9 diverse datasets (conservative, dissipative, forced, real-world motion capture) demonstrate practical breadth. The main limitation acknowledged is that TREAT only incorporates temporal inductive bias; combining it with spatial symmetries (translation/rotation equivariance) is identified as a natural next step. An open question is whether the theoretical Taylor-expansion argument extends to higher-order ODE solvers or adaptive-step methods beyond the fixed-step setting analyzed.
