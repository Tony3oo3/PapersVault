## Problem

In unsupervised reinforcement learning with visual inputs, task-irrelevant distractors (e.g., moving background videos) inflate intrinsic reward estimates because a single world model encodes both task-relevant and task-irrelevant information in one latent space. This biases exploration toward distractor-driven uncertainty rather than task-relevant dynamics, degrading performance especially in complex environments. No existing URL method explicitly disentangles endogenous from exogenous information within the world model to guide exploration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64×3 RGB frames with overlaid moving video distractors (from Kinetics driving-car or random-video datasets) and outputs discrete/continuous actions. The separated world model produces two latent state streams: an endogenous state s⁺ (task-relevant, action-conditioned) and an exogenous state s⁻ (task-irrelevant, action-independent). The observation model reconstructs the full image via a learned mask that blends endogenous and exogenous decoder outputs, and an additional Exo-Rec term forces s⁻ to capture the majority of visual content. The intrinsic reward is the empirical variance across K ensemble predictive heads applied only to the endogenous branch.

## Method

SeeX formulates exploration as a bi-level optimization over an EX-BMDP: the inner loop trains a separated world model (two parallel RSSM branches with distinct transition dynamics) by maximizing an ELBO that includes a mask-mixing observation reconstruction loss and an Exo-Rec penalty; the outer loop learns a policy π(a|s⁺) on imaginary trajectories generated entirely within the endogenous latent space, using ensemble variance over K predictive heads as the intrinsic reward. The key architectural insight is that the exogenous branch models distractor transitions independently of actions (q(s⁻ₜ|s⁻ₜ₋₁) without action conditioning), while the endogenous branch is action-conditioned (q(s⁺ₜ|s⁺ₜ₋₁, aₜ₋₁)), ensuring the policy's uncertainty signal reflects only task-relevant dynamics. A theoretical analysis via the Simulation Lemma shows that minimizing the endogenous TV error under the exploration policy provides a reward-independent regret bound, justifying the separation as a principled surrogate for the original minimax regret objective.

## Results

On the URLB-pixels benchmark with visual distractors, SeeX achieves expert-level normalized return of 1.00 on Walker-Stand with 2M pre-training frames, and reaches 90% expert performance on Walker-Walk within just 500K frames—no baseline achieves this even at 2M. On the OOD generalization test (Driving-car → Random-video), SeeX suffers only a −0.09 performance drop, compared to −0.13 to −0.51 for baselines. In the Quadruped domain, even SeeX's lowest fine-tuning performance exceeds the highest curves of all seven baselines across all four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeX consistently outperforms all seven baselines across the Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco domains. Against Plan2Explore (the closest model-based competitor), SeeX's separated world model yields substantially better performance on Walker-Stand (0.95 vs. 0.54 at 100K frames) and Walker-Walk, where Plan2Explore's single-latent-space predictive heads are biased by distractors. Knowledge-based methods (ICM, RND, LBS) perform reasonably on simpler tasks like Walker-Stand but degrade sharply on complex tasks (Walker-Run, Walker-Flip), where SeeX maintains expert-level returns. Data-based APT and competence-based DIAYN/APS show the largest gaps on Quadruped tasks, where SeeX's lowest curve still exceeds their highest. On OOD distractor generalization (Figure 6b), SeeX drops only −0.09 (0.82→0.75) versus APS (−0.51), DIAYN (−0.46), and RND (−0.23), confirming that the exogenous/endogenous separation provides genuine invariance rather than overfitting to the training distractor distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the URLB-pixels benchmark [50] and the Dreamer RSSM architecture [20] by introducing a separated world model that disentangles task-relevant (endogenous) and task-irrelevant (exogenous) latent states, drawing on the EX-BMDP formalism [15] and the separation ideas from SeMAIL [63] (originally for visual imitation). The intrinsic reward mechanism builds on Plan2Explore's ensemble predictive heads [54], but is restricted to the endogenous branch only, which is the key architectural departure. The bi-level optimization framework (inner: minimize world model error; outer: maximize endogenous uncertainty) provides a theoretical grounding absent in prior URL methods that treat the full latent space monolithically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing unsupervised RL methods degrade significantly when visual observations contain moving distractors, a common scenario in real-world robotics. The EX-BMDP formalization and the finding that policy learning on endogenous states alone outperforms joint-branch policies offer a principled recipe for distractor-robust exploration. However, the work is limited to task-irrelevant + action-independent distractors on DMC tasks, leaving the other three distractor categories and real-world deployment as open problems. The OOD generalization result (minimal performance drop on unseen random-video distractors) suggests the separation may transfer, but this remains to be validated at scale.
