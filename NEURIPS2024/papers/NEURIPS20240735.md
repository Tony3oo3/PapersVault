## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on person ReID systems target only single-modal (RGB-to-RGB) matching, leaving cross-modality ReID (RGB + infrared/thermal) — the more common real-world deployment — unexplored. The core difficulty is that prior methods optimize perturbations independently per modality, lacking any intrinsic mechanism to capture shared features across modalities, which severely limits attack transferability and effectiveness in cross-modality retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240735/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired visible (RGB) and infrared/thermal images from cross-modality ReID datasets (SYSU-MM01, RegDB, LLCM) and produces a single universal 3-channel perturbation η bounded by an L∞ constraint (ε=8 by default). During training, images are randomly converted to homogeneous 3-channel grayscale via ITU-R BT.601-7 weights to serve as a modality-agnostic bridge. At attack time, the same η is added to either a visible or infrared query image to corrupt the retrieval ranking against the opposite-modality gallery.

## Method

CMPS jointly optimizes a universal perturbation by alternately computing cross-modality triplet losses: the visible-modality loss pulls the adversarial feature toward the grayscale negative centroid while pushing it away from the infrared positive centroid, and the infrared-modality loss mirrors this with swapped roles. Gradients from both modalities are aggregated with momentum (MI-SGD) within a unified update loop, so each perturbation step simultaneously accounts for both modalities' feature geometry. A Cross-Modality Attack Augmentation randomly converts visible or infrared images to grayscale during training, narrowing the visual gap and encouraging the perturbation to learn modality-agnostic structural features. The paper further provides a Rademacher-complexity-based proof showing that this aggregated optimization yields a tighter generalization-error upper bound than stepwise (per-modality) optimization, formalizing why the synergistic approach produces more transferable perturbations.

## Results

On the SYSU-MM01 dataset with the AGW baseline, CMPS reduces rank-1 accuracy to 1.11% (Visible→Infrared) and 1.31% (Infrared→Visible), with mAP dropping to 1.41% and 1.23% respectively. On RegDB with AGW, rank-1 falls to 2.29% (Visible→Thermal) and 1.93% (Thermal→Visible), with mAP at 3.92% and 3.46%. On the more challenging LLCM low-light dataset, CMPS achieves rank-1 of 5.83% and 6.42% with mAP of 12.47% and 12.23%, still substantially outperforming all baselines. The method also demonstrates strong transferability, with cross-model transfer (AGW→DDAG) retaining mAP of 3.41% versus 1.84% in-domain on SYSU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMPS outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and retrieval direction. Against the closest competitor Col.+Del. (TPAMI 2023), CMPS reduces rank-1 from 3.23% to 1.11% (Visible→Infrared) and from 4.12% to 1.31% (Infrared→Visible) on SYSU/AGW, a roughly 2–3× improvement. Traditional classification attacks (FGSM, PGD) are nearly ineffective in cross-modality settings, leaving rank-1 above 39–48%, confirming that metric-learning-aware attacks are essential. Among metric-based ReID attacks, LTA (CVPR 2022) achieves the second-best rank-1 of 8.42% on SYSU/AGW Visible→Infrared, but CMPS still reduces it by another 7.3 percentage points. On the low-light LLCM dataset where all methods degrade, CMPS still leads with rank-1 of 5.83% versus Col.+Del.'s 8.61%. Transferability experiments (Table 4) further show CMPS consistently exceeding Col.+Del. by 2–5 percentage points in cross-model rank-1 transfer success rates across IDE, PCB, and ResNet18.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal perturbation attack framework of UAP-Retrieval (Li et al., ICCV 2019) from single-modality image retrieval to the cross-modality ReID setting, where perturbations must simultaneously mislead retrieval across visible and infrared/thermal images. Prior ReID attacks (M-FGSM, LTA, ODFA, Col.+Del.) optimize perturbations per-modality or rely on color-space manipulations that are inapplicable to grayscale infrared images, leaving no intrinsic mechanism to capture shared cross-modality features. The key departure is a synergistic gradient aggregation strategy with a cross-modality triplet loss that jointly optimizes one universal perturbation against both modalities, supplemented by random grayscale augmentation to reduce the visual gap between modalities during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of adversarial vulnerabilities in cross-modality ReID, a setting that is increasingly common in practical surveillance (RGB + IR/thermal cameras) yet previously unexamined from a security standpoint. The dramatic performance gap between CMPS and prior methods (rank-1 below 2% vs. 3–25% for competitors) underscores that single-modality attack strategies are fundamentally inadequate for cross-modality systems, motivating new defense research. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond RGB-IR/thermal pairs to truly unknown modalities, how to build robust cross-modality ReID defenses, and whether the gradient-based optimization suffers from the 'catastrophic forgetting' the authors acknowledge when scaling to more modalities or models.
