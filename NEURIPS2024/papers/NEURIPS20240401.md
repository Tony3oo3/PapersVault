## Problem

VQGAN and its improved variants (VQGAN-FC, VQGAN-EMA) are limited to codebook sizes of at most 16,384, with utilization rates dropping below 12% for VQGAN-FC on ImageNet. Increasing the codebook size beyond this threshold leads to unstable training or performance degradation because only a small subset of entries is optimized per iteration, leaving the majority inactive. The paper asks whether the codebook can be scaled to 100,000 entries while maintaining near-full utilization and improving downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240401/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 RGB images as input. A U-Net encoder downsamples by a factor of 16 to produce a 16×16×D feature map, which the quantizer converts into a 16×16 discrete token map (256 tokens; optionally 32×32 = 1024 tokens). Each token is an index into a 100,000-entry codebook whose projected entries are 8-dimensional. The decoder reconstructs the image from the token map, and the same token map can be fed to GPT, LDM, DiT, or SiT for image generation.

## Method

VQGAN-LC replaces the standard trainable codebook with a static codebook initialized by extracting patch-level features from the target dataset using a pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder (with 4×4 average pooling), then applying K-Means clustering to obtain 100,000 cluster centers. A simple linear projector is trained to map the entire frozen codebook into the encoder's latent space, so that the aggregate distribution of all projected entries aligns with the encoder's feature distribution. During training, only the projector is optimized (not individual codebook entries), which ensures that virtually all 100,000 entries remain active (>99% utilization) throughout training. The encoder, decoder, and loss function (reconstruction + quantization + VGG perceptual + GAN) are identical to the original VQGAN, making the new quantizer a drop-in replacement. Quantization is performed by nearest-neighbor lookup in the projected codebook space, and increasing the codebook size incurs negligible additional computational cost (195.08G vs. 195.70G MACs).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K reconstruction (256 tokens), VQGAN-LC with a 100K codebook achieves rFID 2.62, LPIPS 0.12, PSNR 23.8, and SSIM 58.9, with a codebook utilization rate of 99.9%. For class-conditional image generation on ImageNet, VQGAN-LC paired with LDM achieves FID 8.36 (256 tokens) and FID 4.81 (1024 tokens), outperforming VQGAN-EMA (FID 9.13) and VQGAN-FC (FID 9.78) at their optimal 16,384 codebook size. For image classification on ImageNet, VQGAN-LC tokenized images yield 75.7% top-1 accuracy with a ViT-B classifier, surpassing VQGAN-FC and VQGAN-EMA by 1.6 and 1.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQGAN-LC consistently outperforms all three VQGAN variants across every evaluated task. In reconstruction on ImageNet, VQGAN-LC (100K) achieves rFID 2.62 vs. VQGAN-EMA (16,384) at 3.41, VQGAN-FC (16,384) at 4.29, and VQGAN (16,384) at 5.96 — a 0.79 rFID improvement over the strongest prior method. Critically, VQGAN-LC maintains >99% codebook utilization at all sizes, whereas VQGAN-FC drops to 11.2% and VQGAN to 3.4% at 16,384 entries, and both degrade further at larger sizes (VQGAN-FC: 1.9% at 100K; VQGAN-EMA: 24.2% at 100K). For image generation with LDM on ImageNet, VQGAN-LC (100K) achieves FID 8.36, beating VQGAN-EMA (9.13) and VQGAN-FC (9.78); with 1024 tokens, VQGAN-LC reaches FID 4.81, surpassing the standalone LDM baseline (8.11) and RQVAE+GPT (5.11). In image classification, VQGAN-LC tokens yield 75.7% top-1 accuracy, exceeding VQGAN-EMA (73.8%) and VQGAN-FC (74.1%). The paper also shows that increasing codebook size in VQGAN-FC and VQGAN-EMA does not improve (and often degrades) performance, while VQGAN-LC benefits monotonically up to 100K entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQGAN encoder-quantizer-decoder architecture (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) and its two main codebook-optimization variants: factorized codes with L2 regularization (VQGAN-FC, Yu et al.) and exponential moving average updates (VQGAN-EMA, Razavi et al.). The key conceptual departure is inverting the optimization target: rather than training individual codebook entries via gradient descent or EMA (which leaves most entries inactive), VQGAN-LC freezes a codebook initialized from CLIP patch-level features clustered via K-Means, and instead trains a single linear projector to align the entire codebook distribution with the encoder's feature space. The motivation draws an explicit analogy to LLM tokenizer scaling (e.g., LLAMA 3's 128K vocabulary), arguing that image tokenizers should similarly benefit from larger vocabularies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99%+ codebook utilization at 100K entries—versus 24% for VQGAN-EMA and 1.9% for VQGAN-FC at the same size—demonstrates that the standard per-entry optimization paradigm is fundamentally limiting, and that a distribution-level alignment via a projector is a simpler and more effective alternative. The technique adds negligible computational cost (Table 8: 195.08G vs 195.70G MACs) and drops into existing VQGAN pipelines without modifying the encoder or decoder, making it broadly adoptable across reconstruction, classification, and generation (GPT, LDM, DiT, SiT). Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger and more diverse datasets (e.g., LAION-5B) where CLIP-based initialization may be less aligned with the target encoder, and whether the modest absolute rFID gains (2.62 vs 3.41) translate into proportionally larger improvements in open-ended text-to-image generation beyond class-conditional ImageNet.
