## Problem

Conditional sampling with pre-trained diffusion models for inverse problems lacks a unifying theoretical framework, with existing guidance-based methods (DPS, RED-diff, ΠGDM) being heuristic, hyperparameter-sensitive, and requiring expensive backpropagation through large frozen models. This makes them infeasible when model weights are behind closed APIs or when computation budgets are tight, and obscures the mathematical connections between different conditioning strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240620/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement y, a forward operator A (which can be linear or non-linear), and a frozen pre-trained unconditional diffusion model, producing posterior samples from p(x₀|y). For image tasks, inputs are 256×256 RGB or medical images with the h-transform network at only 4–17% the parameter count of the base model; for protein design, inputs are Cα coordinate vectors with a 200k-parameter h-transform against a 4.1M-parameter Genie base model. Crucially, neither training nor sampling requires backpropagation through the large unconditional network—only forward evaluations are needed, enabling use with closed-API models.

## Method

DEFT unifies conditional diffusion under Doob's h-transform, showing that the guidance term ∇_x ln p_t(y|x) is precisely the h-transform that conditions the reverse SDE to sample from the posterior. A small network h_t^φ(x_t, y) is trained via a score-matching loss (Eq. 10) that only requires forward passes through the frozen ε-prediction model, avoiding any gradient flow through its weights. The architecture embeds a likelihood-informed inductive bias by including the cheap guidance term ∇_{x̂₀} ln p(y|x̂₀) as an explicit input channel and residual pathway, initialized to approximate the DPS solution. The h-transform is further interpreted as a correction to the unconditional Tweedie estimate (Eq. 11), and a stochastic control formulation (Theorem 3.1, item 3) provides an alternative zero-shot objective requiring only a single observation y rather than a paired training set.

## Results

On ImageNet inpainting, DEFT achieves the best perceptual quality among all non-I2SB methods (KID 0.29, LPIPS 0.09, top-1 71.7%) while being 5.9× faster than DPS (5.2 hrs vs 30.72 hrs for 1k samples). On non-linear tasks, DEFT substantially outperforms RED-diff: 28.51 dB PSNR on HDR (vs 25.23), 25.16 dB on non-linear deblurring (vs 21.21), and 13.03 dB on phase retrieval (vs 10.53). For CT reconstruction on LoDoPab-CT, DEFT achieves 35.81 dB PSNR in 13.8 s per sample, compared to DPS at 34.16 dB in 156.8 s. On the RFDiffusion protein motif scaffolding benchmark, DEFT achieves 9.2% success rate (scRMSD < 2Å and motifRMSD < 1Å), outperforming DPS (1.3%) with only 9% of the base model's parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFT outperforms DPS [12] on virtually every metric across all tasks: on inpainting, DEFT achieves KID 0.29 vs 15.28 and LPIPS 0.09 vs 0.26, while being 5.9× faster (5.2 hrs vs 30.72 hrs); on non-linear deblurring, DEFT reaches 25.16 dB PSNR vs DPS's 17.57 dB. Against RED-diff [44], DEFT wins decisively on all non-linear tasks (HDR: 28.51 vs 25.23 dB; deblurring: 25.16 vs 21.21 dB; phase retrieval: 13.03 vs 10.53 dB) and on perceptual metrics for linear tasks, though RED-diff retains a small PSNR edge on inpainting (23.29 vs 22.18 dB). ΠGDM [65] and DDRM [32] are faster (2.83 and 0.33 hrs respectively) but substantially worse on perceptual quality (KID 4.50 and 2.50 vs DEFT's 0.29 for inpainting) and are inapplicable to non-linear forward operators. I2SB [39], which trains a full conditional diffusion on all of ImageNet, serves as an upper bound and slightly outperforms DEFT on most metrics (e.g., KID 0.238 vs 0.29), confirming DEFT's efficiency–quality tradeoff. For protein motif scaffolding, DEFT (9.2% success) substantially outperforms DPS (1.3%) but trails the amortised Genie model [17] (24.5%), which uses 11× more parameters and 2.6× more training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a fragmented landscape of conditional diffusion methods (DPS, ΠGDM, DDRM, RED-diff, I2SB) under the mathematical framework of Doob's h-transform, showing that reconstruction guidance approaches are approximate instances of the generalised h-transform. It departs from prior work by freezing the pre-trained unconditional score network entirely and learning only a small (4–9%) h-transform network via score matching, eliminating the need for backpropagation through the large base model. The novel network architecture incorporates a likelihood-informed inductive bias (Tweedie estimate + log-likelihood gradient) inspired by connections to stochastic control and Schrödinger bridge formulations, bridging the sampling and conditional generative modelling literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEFT offers a practical and theoretically grounded recipe for conditional generation that avoids the computational bottleneck of backpropagating through frozen foundation models, making it applicable to closed-API settings. The unifying h-transform perspective clarifies the relationships between seemingly disparate guidance methods and provides guarantees (finite-time hitting) that prior equilibrium-motivated approaches lack. Key limitations include the requirement for a small paired fine-tuning dataset (risking overfitting) and the fact that the zero-shot stochastic control alternative remains computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more capable base models (e.g., for protein design) and whether partial trajectory optimisation can make the zero-shot variant competitive.
