## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating CLIP robustness (e.g., ImageNet variants) are designed around spurious features inherited by ImageNet-trained models, not the distinct spurious correlations present in web-scale multi-modal training data like LAION. This creates a misleading picture where CLIP appears to resolve distribution shifts, when in fact it may simply be evaluated on shifts it was never exposed to. The paper asks: is there a benchmark that reflects the exact reliance on spurious features of CLIP models trained on large-scale image-text data?

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes raw animal photos (collected from iNaturalist, manually curated and background-labeled) and a fixed text prompt template ('A photo of <object label>.') as input. Each animal class is split into an 'easy' group (common background, e.g., ice bear in snow) and a 'hard' group (plausible but uncommon background, e.g., ice bear in grass), and the model's zero-shot top-1 accuracy is computed separately for each group. The primary output metric is the accuracy drop (easy minus hard), reported under either a 1-vs-1000 setup (full ImageNet-1K label space) or a 1-vs-20 setup (top-20 most confusing classes) for computationally expensive LVLMs like MiniGPT4 and LLaVA.

## Method

The paper introduces CounterAnimal, a 45-class, ~13K-photo benchmark constructed via a 4-step pipeline: (1) collecting animal photos from iNaturalist, (2) manual curation to remove label/feature noise, (3) manual background labeling from a 14-class space, and (4) spurious discovery by retaining only classes where CLIP-LAION400M-ViT-B/32 shows >5% zero-shot accuracy drop between background groups. The core empirical finding is that these spurious correlations generalize across CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32 through ViT-bigG/14) and pre-train datasets (LAION, OpenAI, DataComp, DFN), yet ImageNet models are paradoxically more robust to them. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that under a linearized contrastive loss with a spurious feature occupying a large image region, an overparameterized CLIP model provably aligns the spurious feature with the caption, yielding large OOD error when the spurious correlation breaks down—showing the CLIP objective offers no additional robustness over standard supervised training. Confirmatory experiments on ColoredCOCO and MultiColoredMNIST (with a perfect language encoder) verify that CLIP fine-tuning performs similarly to ERM under distribution shift.

## Results

On CounterAnimal (1 vs. 1000 setup), CLIP-LAION400M-ViT-B/32 drops from 67.13% (easy) to 36.95% (hard), a 30.18-point drop, while the best-performing CLIP (ViT-L/14 on DataComp1B) still drops 9.39 points (89.29% → 79.90%). ImageNet models (e.g., ConvNext-L) show smaller drops of 7.65 points (89.12% → 81.47%), revealing that CLIP models are actually *less* robust to these spurious features than ImageNet models. Scaling up backbones (ViT-B/32 → ViT-bigG/14) reduces the drop from ~30 to ~10 points, while scaling pre-train data (LAION400M → LAION2B) yields no consistent improvement. High-quality pre-train data (DataComp1B, DFN2B) consistently reduces drops by 3–10 points compared to unfiltered LAION.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between CLIP models and ImageNet-supervised models on CounterAnimal: ImageNet models (e.g., ConvNext-L: 7.65-pt drop; ViT-B/16: 9.99-pt drop) are consistently *more* robust than CLIP models (e.g., ViT-B/32 LAION400M: 30.18-pt drop; ViT-L/14 LAION400M: 17.59-pt drop), directly contradicting the common belief that CLIP generalizes better. Among advanced LVLMs evaluated under the 1 vs. 20 setup, MiniGPT4-Vicuna7B drops 8.26 points (47.99% → 39.73%) and LLaVA1.5-7B drops 9.97 points (40.06% → 30.09%), both smaller than comparable CLIP backbones (e.g., CLIP-LAION400M-ViT-L/14: 17.59-pt drop), though their absolute accuracy is much lower. Within CLIP configurations, scaling the backbone from ViT-B/32 to ViT-bigG/14 (on LAION2B) reduces the drop from 24.20 to 10.61 points, while scaling data from LAION400M to LAION2B (on ViT-B/32) actually *increases* the drop from 30.18 to 24.20 points. High-quality pre-train data (DataComp1B, DFN2B) consistently outperforms unfiltered LAION by 3–10 points in drop reduction across all backbone scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Xue et al. [9] on multi-modal contrastive learning robustness to prove that the CLIP objective provides no additional robustness over standard supervised learning, and adapts the ColoredCOCO construction methodology from Ahmed et al. [51] for confirmatory experiments. It departs from prior OOD robustness benchmarks (ImageNet variants, DomainBed, WILDS) that were designed for ImageNet-trained models, arguing they are fundamentally misaligned with CLIP's training distribution. The work also builds on the data-centric robustness literature (Fang et al. [6], Mayilvahanan et al. [10]) but provides a counterpoint: while data quality matters, simply scaling data volume does not reduce spurious correlations in CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper challenges the widely-held narrative that CLIP's contrastive pre-training inherently resolves distribution shifts, showing instead that CLIP models remain vulnerable to realistic spurious correlations (background-object associations) that are invisible to ImageNet-oriented benchmarks. The CounterAnimal benchmark (45 classes, ~13K images) provides a practical tool for the community to evaluate CLIP-specific robustness, and the finding that model scale and data quality—but not data volume—mitigate spurious features offers concrete guidance for future pre-training. Limitations include the focus on animal classes and background-based spurious features only, leaving open whether the findings generalize to other object categories and types of spurious correlations (e.g., lighting, camera artifacts). The theoretical result that CLIP offers no robustness advantage over ERM is foundational but relies on simplified linearized assumptions that may not fully capture real-world CLIP dynamics.
