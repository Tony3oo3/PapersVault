## Problem

State-of-the-art OOD detection methods achieve superior detection performance by secretly sacrificing OOD generalization ability, with some SOTA methods suffering ~15% accuracy degradation under even slight noise. The paper theoretically identifies this as a 'sensitive-robust dilemma' where encouraging high uncertainty on semantic OOD inevitably induces high uncertainty on covariate-shifted OOD, and shows that the previously assumed trade-off between detection and generalization is not fundamental.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (64×64 for CIFAR experiments, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels, a binary IN/OUT OOD decision via thresholding on distributional uncertainty, and decomposed uncertainty estimates (distributional uncertainty for detection, overall predictive entropy for generalization). The CNN backbone outputs are reinterpreted as Dirichlet concentration parameters α, from which both the predicted class distribution and the differential entropy of the Dirichlet prior (measuring DU) are derived. The method operates as a fine-tuning procedure on a pre-trained classifier, requiring auxiliary unlabeled OOD data during fine-tuning but no covariate-shifted data.

## Method

Decoupled Uncertainty Learning (DUL) resolves the sensitive-robust dilemma by exploiting the Bayesian decomposition of overall uncertainty into distributional uncertainty (DU) and data/aleatoric uncertainty (AU). The key insight is that OOD detection only requires high DU, while generalization requires low overall uncertainty; since DU can be high even when overall uncertainty is low, the two objectives are decoupled rather than conflicting. Concretely, DUL fine-tunes a pre-trained classifier with a margin-based loss that pushes the differential entropy of the Dirichlet prior (DU) above a threshold on auxiliary OOD samples, combined with a KL-divergence term that constrains the final predicted distribution to remain close to its pre-finetuning state (preventing overall uncertainty from increasing). Theoretically, the paper proves via disparity discrepancy bounds (Theorem 1) that MSP and EBM detectors face an inherent lower bound on generalization error that worsens as OOD detection loss decreases, and shows that DUL's decoupled objective circumvents this bound by never forcing high overall entropy on OOD.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/ImageNet-RC, DUL achieves 88.01% OOD-Acc (matching the MSP baseline of 87.35%) while attaining 5.89% FPR95 and 98.47% AUROC — near-SOTA detection performance. In contrast, all other OOD detection methods suffer severe generalization degradation: Entropy drops to 72.57% OOD-Acc (−14.8%), POEM to 78.89% (−8.5%), and SCONE to 78.80% (−8.6%). On ImageNet-200/ImageNet-800, DUL achieves 75.59% OOD-Acc and 49.14% FPR95, outperforming both the MSP baseline (74.84% OOD-Acc, 58.23% FPR) and all finetuning-based competitors on both metrics simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUL is the only method that achieves near-SOTA OOD detection (FPR95, AUROC) without degrading OOD generalization. Compared to the MSP baseline (no OOD regularization), DUL improves FPR95 from 41.96% to 5.89% on CIFAR-10/ImageNet-RC while maintaining OOD-Acc at 88.01% (vs. 87.35%). Against Entropy [7], DUL recovers 15.4% OOD-Acc (88.01 vs. 72.57) at only a 0.76% FPR cost. Against POEM [11], DUL gains 9.1% OOD-Acc (88.01 vs. 78.89) with FPR of 5.89% vs. 3.32%. Against the most related SCONE [4], DUL improves OOD-Acc by 9.2% (88.01 vs. 78.80) while achieving comparable FPR (5.89% vs. 7.02%). Against WOODS [10], DUL gains 7.9% OOD-Acc (88.01 vs. 80.14) with slightly better FPR (5.89% vs. 7.12%). Against EBM (finetune) [8], DUL recovers 9.0% OOD-Acc (88.01 vs. 79.03) at a modest FPR cost (5.89% vs. 3.61%). Against DPN [9], DUL matches OOD-Acc (88.01 vs. 85.52) with competitive FPR (5.89% vs. 4.28%). Against Maxlogits [49] and Mahalanobis [18] (auxiliary-OOD-free methods), DUL substantially improves both FPR and OOD-Acc. The consistent pattern across all 9 baselines is that DUL is the sole method whose entire row shows no red (degraded) values relative to MSP, confirming the elimination of the sensitive-robust dilemma.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian uncertainty decomposition framework (aleatoric, epistemic, and distributional uncertainty) from prior networks [Malinin & Gales, 2018] and the transfer learning theory of disparity discrepancy [Ganin & Lempitsky, 2015; Zhang et al., 2019]. It departs from the most closely related work SCONE [Bai et al., 2023], which frames OOD detection and OOD generalization as an inherent trade-off requiring a balance, by arguing the conflict is not fundamental but arises from conflating distributional uncertainty (DU) with overall predictive uncertainty. The theoretical analysis also extends the energy-based OOD detection literature [Liu et al., 2020] by showing that gradient-based optimization of EBM regularization implicitly drives high-entropy predictions, connecting it to the same sensitive-robust dilemma proven for MSP detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled explanation for why SOTA OOD detection methods silently degrade under even minor noise (up to ~15% accuracy loss), a vulnerability that severely limits real-world deployment. The decoupled uncertainty learning approach is practical (a lightweight finetuning step applicable to any pretrained model) and achieves dual-optimal performance, but it still requires auxiliary OOD data during training—a cost the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Open questions include whether the dependency on auxiliary outliers can be eliminated (e.g., via virtual outlier synthesis) and whether the DU/overall-uncertainty decoupling principle generalizes to OOD detection in non-classification tasks like object detection or segmentation.
