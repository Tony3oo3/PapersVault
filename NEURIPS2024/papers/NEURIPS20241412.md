## Problem

Prior inverse constrained RL (ICRL) methods infer safety constraints from expert trajectories by nesting a constrained MDP solver (e.g., SAC-Lag) inside the IRL optimization, creating a tri-level optimization with a challenging landscape that leads to sub-optimal performance. The paper asks whether this constrained inner loop is actually necessary, or whether standard IRL techniques can recover equally good constraint functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241412/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert trajectories from a nominal (unconstrained) environment where the reward signal is directly observed, and produces a parameterized constraint function (a two-layer MLP with Leaky ReLU output) encoding safety boundaries. Although the constraint is trained as an additive penalty within an unconstrained MDP, it is transferable as a proper constraint in a downstream CMDP, where it would be rescaled by a new Lagrange multiplier for the specific task.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that when the constraint function class is a convex cone, the tri-level ICRL game is exactly equivalent to a standard bi-level MaxEnt IRL game, eliminating the need for a constrained MDP inner loop. Practically, they implement this as SAC-based MaxEnt IRL with three stabilizing modifications: (1) L2 regularization on the constraint output over expert trajectories combined with Leaky ReLU activation to softly enforce positivity while preserving the convex cone structure, (2) separate critics for reward and constraint via Q-decomposition to disentangle value learning from the changing constraint, and (3) periodic last-layer resets on the constraint critic to combat plasticity loss. The full combination (IRL-Plus) outperforms the Lagrangian-based ICRL baseline MECL in feasible rewards and violation rate across five MuJoCo continuous-control environments, including with sub-optimal expert data.

## Results

Across five MuJoCo environments (Ant, Half Cheetah, Walker2D, Swimmer, Inverted Pendulum), the paper evaluates constraint inference using two metrics: feasible rewards (total return up to first constraint violation, normalized by expert returns) and violation rate (percentage of episodes with ≥1 violation). IRL-Plus (IRL with L2 regularization, separate critics, and policy resets) outperforms the Lagrangian-based ICRL baseline MECL with statistical significance across all four summary statistics (IQM, Median, Mean, Optimality Gap) for feasible rewards and all but one for violation rate. On sub-optimal expert trajectories (20–80% unsafe), IRL-Plus maintains a feasible-reward advantage over MECL, though significance is only confirmed at the 20% corruption level due to high variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines from Liu et al. (2023a): MECL (MaxEnt ICRL with a Lagrangian inner loop, the canonical Lagrangian-based constraint inference method) and GACL (GAIL-based IRL with an additive log constraint term). IRL-Plus beats MECL on feasible rewards with statistical significance across all four aggregate statistics and on violation rate on all but one, while IRL-Base already matches or slightly exceeds MECL on violation rate. Against GACL, IRL-Plus outperforms with statistical significance on nearly all metrics for both feasible rewards and violation rate (the median being the sole exception), whereas IRL-Base is statistically indistinguishable from GACL on feasible rewards but still improves violation rate. On sub-optimal expert data (Half Cheetah, 20%/50%/80% unsafe trajectories), IRL-Plus clearly dominates GACL (which achieves no feasible rewards) and generally outperforms MECL, though the gap over MECL is only statistically significant at 20% corruption. Both IRL-Plus and MECL achieve near-zero violation rates in the sub-optimal setting, indicating the constraint is learned correctly even with noisy expert data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MaxEnt IRL framework (Ziebart et al., 2008) and the tri-level ICRL game formulation of Kim et al. (2023), showing that the Lagrangian inner loop in ICRL is theoretically redundant under convex cone constraint classes, reducing the problem to standard bi-level IRL. It draws on Q-decomposition (Russell & Zimdars, 2003) for the separate-critics modification and on plasticity-loss remedies (Lyle et al., 2023) for policy resets, while adapting the L2 regularization on expert trajectories from Malik et al. (2021). The key departure from prior ICRL work (Malik et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023a; Kim et al., 2023) is the elimination of the constrained MDP inner optimization entirely, replacing it with an unconstrained IRL objective over a learned cost function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving ICRL reduces to IRL under broad constraint classes, the paper opens the entire IRL literature—including offline IRL, adversarial IRL, and kernel-based methods—to the constraint inference problem, which was previously locked into the more complex Lagrangian formulation. Practically, this simplification removes a level of nested optimization, reducing hyperparameter sensitivity and implementation complexity. However, the empirical validation is limited to five MuJoCo environments with relatively simple binary position constraints, and the use of SAC (vs. PPO in baselines) introduces an algorithmic confound that prevents a clean claim of strict superiority. Reducing the high per-environment variance and extending to more challenging or real-world constraint settings remain open challenges.
