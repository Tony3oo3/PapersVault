## Problem

OKRidge, introduced at NeurIPS 2023, is a fast and optimal solver for k-sparse ridge regression that outperforms heuristic, exact, and MIP-based methods, yet it lacks any theoretical characterization of its estimation error. This absence of rigorous error analysis impedes large-scale deployment because practitioners cannot predict or bound the quality of the recovered sparse weight vector. The paper fills this gap by providing a precise high-dimensional asymptotic error bound for OKRidge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242419/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a Gaussian linear model y = Xβ* + ε where X ∈ R^{n×d} has i.i.d. N(0,1) entries, ε ~ N(0, σ²I), and β* is k-sparse with k/d held constant and n/d → δ ∈ (0,1). Given X, y, a regularization parameter λ > 0, and sparsity level k, OKRidge outputs a k-sparse estimate β̂. The theoretical contribution characterizes the Normalized Squared Error (NSE = ||β̂ − β*||²/σ²) as a deterministic function Δ(λ̂) of λ in the joint limit d → ∞ and σ → 0, where λ̂ is determined by the map equation map(τ) = λ/2.

## Method

The paper re-derives OKRidge's tight lower bound L_OKRidge(β) = ||y − Xβ||² + λ·SumTop_k(β⊙β) by eliminating the Fenchel dual variable c via ∂L/∂c = 0 (an equivalent but distinct route from the original paper's ∂L/∂β = 0). It then reframes the estimation error ||β̂ − β*||² as a Primary Optimization (PO) problem using the Fenchel-Moreau conjugate of the squared norm, and applies the Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT) to replace the PO with a simpler Auxiliary Optimization (AO) problem involving only i.i.d. Gaussian vectors g and h. The AO problem is further reduced via a first-order subdifferential approximation of the SumTop_k regularizer around β* and concentration of Lipschitz functionals to a deterministic two-scalar optimization over (α, η). Solving this scalar saddle-point problem yields the closed-form NSE limit Δ(λ̂) = D̄(λ̂)/(1 − D̄(λ̂)), where D̄ is the normalized Gaussian squared distance to the scaled subdifferential of SumTop_k at β*, and λ̂ is the unique root of the map equation.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.2) establishes that the Normalized Squared Error (NSE) of OKRidge satisfies lim_{d→∞} lim_{σ→0} ‖β̂−β*‖²₂/σ² →ᵖ Δ(λ̂) = D̄(λ̂)/(1−D̄(λ̂)), where λ̂ = λ_map is determined by the map(τ) = 0 equation. This implies that for a fixed regularization parameter λ, the NSE limit is a fixed constant, and lim_{d→∞} lim_{σ→0} ‖β̂−β*‖₂ →ᵖ 0, confirming that OKRidge's estimator is consistent. Numerical experiments (n/d=0.5, k/d=0.1, d=100, averaged over 10 trials) verify both aspects: NSE converges to a fixed value as σ→0 for each λ, and the empirical NSE curves converge to the theoretical Δ(λ̂) curve as σ→0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical analysis work and does not include empirical comparisons against competing algorithms (e.g., heuristic methods, optimal solvers, or MIP formulations). The original OKRidge paper [1] demonstrated that OKRidge outperforms heuristic algorithms, optimal algorithms, and Gurobi-solved MIP formulations in both speed and accuracy, but those comparisons are not replicated here. Instead, the 'comparison' in this paper is between the theoretical prediction Δ(λ̂) (the blue curve in Figure 2) and the empirically measured NSE under various σ values (σ² = 1, 0.1, 0.01, 0.001). The experiments confirm that as σ→0, the empirical NSE curves converge to the theoretical Δ(λ̂) curve, validating Theorem 5.2. Additional experiments in Appendix F.2 extend this verification to different n/d ratios (0.4, 0.5, 0.6), sparsity levels (k/d = 0.10, 0.15, 0.20), and dimensions (d = 200, 1000), all showing consistent agreement between theory and experiment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical error analysis for the OKRidge algorithm (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023), which was previously validated only empirically. It leverages the Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT) framework of Thrampoulidis et al. (2018), which has been successfully applied to analyze regularized M-estimators, and adapts it to the novel SumTop_k regularizer that defines OKRidge's objective. The paper also re-derives OKRidge's tight lower bound through an equivalent but distinct elimination of the Fenchel dual variable (via ∂/∂c = 0 rather than ∂/∂β = 0), connecting the perspective formulation literature to the CGMT analysis pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that the NSE of OKRidge converges to a deterministic constant Δ(λ̂) and that the estimation error vanishes as σ→0, this work removes the primary theoretical barrier to large-scale deployment of OKRidge, which does not require proprietary solvers like Gurobi. The main limitation is the reliance on Gaussian input and noise distributions; extending the analysis to non-Gaussian settings (e.g., via Fisher or Box-Cox transformations) is identified as future work. More broadly, the paper demonstrates that the CGMT framework can accommodate non-standard regularizers beyond the ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ norms previously analyzed, opening a path for theoretical analysis of other combinatorial-structured estimators.
