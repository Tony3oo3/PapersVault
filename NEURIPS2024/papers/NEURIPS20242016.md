## Problem

While score-based generative models are empirically robust to practical approximation errors, existing theoretical analyses either assume idealized conditions (absolute continuity, specific function spaces) or rely on bounding KL divergence, which yields vacuous bounds under the manifold hypothesis. No unified framework existed to quantify how multiple simultaneous sources of error—finite samples, early stopping, score-matching objective choice, neural net expressiveness, and reference distribution—collectively impact the generative distribution in a directly computable way.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a learned score function s_θ (a neural network) trained via the denoising score-matching objective with an early stopping parameter ε, initialized from a uniform distribution on a torus R𝕋^d. It produces a computable Wasserstein-1 (d₁) generalization bound that explicitly decomposes into contributions from each error source: the DSM objective value e_nn, a √ε early-stopping term, the d₁ distance between the empirical and true distributions, and an exponentially decaying reference-distribution term. Unlike prior bounds expressed through KL or χ² divergence, the resulting bound is directly in d₁ and remains finite even when the target distribution is supported on a low-dimensional manifold without a density.

## Method

The core tool is the Wasserstein Uncertainty Propagation (WUP) theorem, which maps an L² ball around the true score function to an IPM neighborhood around the true data distribution under Fokker-Planck evolution. The proof constructs test functions via the Kolmogorov backward equation, then applies Bernstein estimates from Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman PDE theory (obtained through a Hopf-Cole transform) to bound the gradient of these test functions without exponential time growth. The regularizing effect of the Laplacian—i.e., stochasticity—is the key mechanism: it allows bounding the stronger L¹/TV norm by the weaker d₁ norm and ensures the test function gradient remains bounded for all time. Applied to SGMs, the WUP theorem yields generalization bounds under minimal assumptions (no absolute continuity, no a priori manifold hypothesis) and directly connects the practical DSM objective to the ESM error needed for the bound, with a density lower bound assumption that can be removed in expectation via Jensen's inequality.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is the Wasserstein Uncertainty Propagation (WUP) theorem (Theorem 3.1), which shows that an L² neighborhood of radius ε₁ around the true score function maps to a d₁-neighborhood of radius C·R^(3/2)·(1+√‖∇b¹‖∞)·(d₁(m₁,m₂)+ε₂) around the true data distribution. The ESM generalization bound (Theorem 3.2) yields d₁(π, m_g(T)) ≤ C·R^(3/2)·(1+√‖∇s_θ‖∞)·(R·e^(−ωT/R²)·d₁(π, uniform) + √e_nn), while the DSM bound (Theorem 3.3) adds a √ε early-stopping term and a finite-sample correction involving |log δ|/√ε and T‖s_θ‖²_{C²}. Notably, the TV bound (Eq. 8) expresses the stronger L¹ distance in terms of the weaker d₁ metric, a consequence of the regularizing properties of the Fokker-Planck equation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but positions its theoretical bounds against prior analytical approaches. Unlike [5, 7, 11, 6], which derive d₁ or TV bounds by first bounding the KL divergence via the Girsanov theorem, Pinsker's inequality, or functional inequalities, the WUP theorem produces d₁ and TV bounds directly from PDE regularity, avoiding these intermediate steps. This distinction is critical under the manifold hypothesis: prior KL-based bounds become vacuous when the target distribution lacks a density, whereas the WUP framework remains valid for any ε > 0. Compared to [8], which also derives d₁ bounds directly, the present work does not assume the manifold hypothesis a priori and does not depend on a specific SDE discretization. The DSM generalization bound (Theorem 3.3) is a posteriori and computable from the DSM objective value, contrasting with prior results [5] that assume the learned score is already L²-close to the truth under the ESM objective. The approach also avoids the strong, uncheckable score-function regularity assumptions required by [21] for its Wasserstein-2 uncertainty propagation bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SGM framework of Song et al. (2020) and Ho et al. (2020), and extends the mean-field games connection to generative modeling established by Zhang & Katsoulakis (2023). It departs sharply from the dominant line of SGM generalization analyses (Chen et al. 2022, Lee et al. 2022, Oko et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023) that bound the KL divergence via Girsanov's theorem or Pinsker's inequality, instead deriving d_1 and TV bounds directly through PDE regularity theory—specifically Bernstein estimates for Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations and the Kolmogorov backward equation. Unlike De Bortoli (2022), the analysis is agnostic to the manifold hypothesis and does not require absolute continuity of the target distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WUP theorem provides a unified, computable framework that simultaneously quantifies how five distinct error sources (finite samples, early stopping, score-matching objective, network expressiveness, reference distribution) propagate to the generative distribution, offering practitioners a diagnostic tool for understanding and tuning SGM training. The identification of the Laplacian (stochasticity) as the key regularizing mechanism that enables bounding stronger norms by weaker ones is a conceptual shift from information-theoretic to PDE-theoretic reasoning. Limitations include the absence of discretization error analysis, reliance on a density lower bound in the pointwise DSM bound (removable only in expectation), and the fact that the bounds, while computable, may be loose in practice. Open questions include whether the Sobolev-space extensions hinted at in Section 6.1 can overcome the curse of dimensionality, and how the framework extends to non-linear SDEs and Schrödinger bridge formulations.
