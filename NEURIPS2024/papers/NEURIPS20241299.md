## Problem

Producing a family of LLMs at different deployment scales currently requires training each variant from scratch, which is extremely compute- and data-intensive. No prior work on structured LLM pruning has explored data-efficient retraining via knowledge distillation to minimize the cost of accuracy recovery after pruning, leaving practitioners without a practical guide for which axes to prune, in what order, and how to retrain efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully pretrained LLM (Nemotron-4 15B, 15.6B parameters), a small calibration set of 1024 samples for activation-based importance estimation, and a small fraction of the original pretraining data (as little as 1.8B tokens for lightweight retraining, up to ~94B for final models) as supervision. It produces compressed transformer models (MINITRON 8B at 8.27B params and MINITRON 4B at 4.19B params) with reduced embedding, MLP hidden, and attention head dimensions while retaining the full 32-layer depth, achieving 2–4× compression with up to 40× fewer training tokens than training from scratch.

## Method

The approach combines structured pruning across four axes (model depth, MLP neurons, attention heads, and embedding channels) with knowledge-distillation-based retraining. Importance is estimated purely from forward-pass activations on a small calibration set—using L2-norm over batch and mean over sequence for width axes, and perplexity or block importance for depth—avoiding expensive gradient computation. Width pruning is preferred over depth for models ≤15B, and pruned attention heads redistribute their residual information into surviving heads (an MHA analog of layer collapse). Retraining uses logit-based Kullback-Leibler divergence loss from the unpruned teacher (adding intermediate-state and embedding losses only when depth is significantly reduced), with a dynamically weighted combination. For aggressive compression (15B→4B), an iterative two-step strategy (15B→8B→4B) outperforms one-shot pruning by 12% on MMLU, and a lightweight architecture search over feasible width configurations with ~1.8B-token retraining stabilizes candidate rankings before final training.

## Results

MINITRON 8B (8.3B params, 94B training tokens) achieves 63.8 MMLU, 80.7 HellaSwag, and 31.6 HumanEval pass@1, using 40× fewer training tokens than Nemotron-3 8B (3.8T tokens) while outperforming it by 9.1 points on MMLU (63.8 vs 54.7). MINITRON 4B (4.2B params, 94B tokens) reaches 58.6 MMLU and 75.0 HellaSwag, surpassing Gemma2 2.6B (51.3 MMLU) and MiniCPM 2.7B (53.5 MMLU). Against state-of-the-art pruning baselines, MINITRON 8B achieves 63.8 MMLU vs ShortGPT's 54.7, LaCo's 45.9, SliceGPT's 37.1, and LLM-Pruner's 25.2; MINITRON 4B reaches 58.6 MMLU vs ShortGPT's 43.96 and Sheared LLaMa's 26.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINITRON 8B outperforms Nemotron-3 8B by 9.1 points on MMLU (63.8 vs 54.7) and 2.2 on HellaSwag (80.7 vs 78.5) while using 40× fewer training tokens (94B vs 3.8T), and substantially beats Llama-2 7B (63.8 vs 46 MMLU). It performs comparably to Mistral 7B (63.8 vs 64.1 MMLU), Gemma 7B (63.8 vs 64), and Llama-3 8B (63.8 vs 65.3), trailing by at most 1.5 points on MMLU. MINITRON 4B outperforms Gemma2 2.6B by 7.3 MMLU points (58.6 vs 51.3), MiniCPM 2.7B by 5.1 (58.6 vs 53.5), and Qwen2 1.5B by 2.1 (58.6 vs 56.5), while matching Phi-2 on WinoGrande (74.0 vs 74). Against pruning-specific baselines, MINITRON 8B leads ShortGPT by 9.1 MMLU points (63.8 vs 54.7) and 14.1 on HellaSwag (80.7 vs 66.6); MINITRON 4B leads ShortGPT by 14.6 MMLU points (58.6 vs 43.96) and Sheared LLaMa by 32.2 (58.6 vs 26.4). The paper also reports 1.8× compute savings for training the full 15B/8B/4B model family versus training all three from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of structured LLM pruning (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, Sheared LLaMA, ShortGPT, LaCo) and knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. 2015), but departs from prior work in three key ways: it uses purely activation-based importance estimation (forward passes only, no gradients) to simultaneously rank neurons, heads, embedding channels, and layers; it combines multiple pruning axes in a single pipeline rather than targeting one dimension; and it replaces conventional retraining with distillation from the unpruned teacher, requiring only a small fraction of the original training data. The head-pruning residual redistribution is explicitly framed as an MHA analog of Layer Collapse (Yang et al. 2024), and the depth-pruning metrics (PPL, Block Importance) are borrowed from Shortened LLaMA and ShortGPT respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× reduction in training tokens for deriving smaller models from a pretrained large model, combined with a 1.8× compute saving for training an entire model family, makes this a practically significant efficiency result for organizations that must serve multiple model sizes. The actionable list of ten best practices (aggregation metrics, width-over-depth preference, KLD-only distillation, single-shot importance, etc.) provides a concrete recipe that lowers the barrier to LLM compression. Key limitations include evaluation only on the Nemotron family and the requirement for full-model retraining (not parameter-efficient fine-tuning), leaving open whether the findings generalize to other architectures, larger scales (>15B), or multi-phase alignment pipelines.
