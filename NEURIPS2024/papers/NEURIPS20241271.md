## Problem

Traditional 3D reconstruction pipelines decompose the task into sequential stages—SfM for camera pose estimation, dense geometry reconstruction, and task-specific semantic modeling—each propagating errors and requiring extensive preprocessing. No prior method could perform dense 3D semantic reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and open-vocabulary segmentation directly from unposed images in a single feed-forward pass without relying on SfM or per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes two unposed RGB images with no camera intrinsics or poses; camera parameters are derived post-hoc from the predicted point maps via a Weiszfeld-based focal estimation and RANSAC-PnP. The output is a set of semantic anisotropic 3D Gaussians that can be differentially splatted to render novel-view RGB images, per-pixel depth maps, and open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation maps (using LSeg for language-driven queries) at real-time speed (~0.1s per scene on a single GPU).

## Method

LSM employs a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a Siamese ViT encoder-decoder with cross-view attention (initialized from DUSt3R) regresses pixel-aligned point maps at normalized scales, followed by a Point Transformer V3 module that performs local context aggregation over the point cloud with progressive multi-scale downsampling/upsampling. Cross-model attention fuses geometry-rich point features with semantic features from a frozen LSeg backbone and ViT encoder tokens, lifting 2D feature embeddings into a 3D-consistent semantic field. The scene is parameterized as anisotropic 3D Gaussians with learned semantic feature embeddings, rendered via differentiable splatting. Training is fully end-to-end with a combined loss of photometric (L1 + D-SSIM), semantic feature distillation (cosine similarity against LSeg features on novel views), and confidence-weighted depth supervision, requiring no per-scene optimization.

## Results

On ScanNet, LSM achieves a target-view mIoU of 0.5078 and accuracy of 0.7686 for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation, surpassing NeRF-DFF (0.4037 mIoU) and Feature-3DGS (0.4223 mIoU) while eliminating the 20.52 s SfM preprocessing step entirely. For depth estimation, LSM reports a relative error of 3.38 and inlier ratio of 67.77, substantially outperforming NeRF-DFF (rel 27.68, τ 9.61) and Feature-3DGS (rel 12.95, τ 21.07). For novel-view synthesis, LSM reaches 24.39 dB PSNR and 0.8072 SSIM in 0.108 s per scene, comparable to Feature-3DGS (24.49 dB, 18 min 36 s per scene) but slightly below pixelSplat (24.89 dB, 0.064 s). On the Replica dataset, LSM achieves 23.10 dB PSNR and 4.91 relative depth error without any SfM, while also providing 3D semantic segmentation (mIoU 0.51) that no other generalizable baseline offers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF-DFF, LSM wins decisively on every shared metric: target-view mIoU improves from 0.4037 to 0.5078, depth relative error drops from 27.68 to 3.38, and PSNR rises from 19.86 to 24.39 dB, while also removing the 1 min 2 s per-scene optimization. Against Feature-3DGS, LSM achieves higher mIoU (0.5078 vs. 0.4223) and far lower depth error (3.38 vs. 12.95), but trails slightly on PSNR (24.39 vs. 24.49 dB) and LPIPS (0.2506 vs. 0.2293); critically, LSM avoids the 18 min 36 s per-scene fitting. Against pixelSplat, LSM matches or exceeds depth accuracy (rel 3.38 vs. not reported) and adds full semantic segmentation, but pixelSplat achieves higher PSNR (24.89 vs. 24.39) and lower LPIPS (0.1641 vs. 0.2506) for pure rendering quality. Against the 2D LSeg baseline, LSM's target-view mIoU (0.5078) is close to LSeg's (0.5281) while providing 3D-consistent, view-invariant semantics and depth—capabilities LSeg lacks. On Replica, LSM is the only generalizable method that simultaneously reports depth (rel 4.91), PSNR (23.10 dB), and semantic mIoU (0.51) without SfM, whereas pixelSplat and Splatter Image require SfM and do not provide segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSM sits at the intersection of three research threads: pose-free geometry prediction (DUSt3R's pixel-aligned point maps), explicit radiance field rendering (3D Gaussian Splatting), and 2D-to-3D semantic feature lifting (Feature-3DGS, NeRF-DFF). It departs from prior work by replacing the multi-stage SfM → dense reconstruction → per-scene semantic optimization pipeline with a single feed-forward Transformer that jointly predicts geometry, appearance, and semantics from unposed stereo pairs. The point-level aggregation via Point Transformer V3 and the hierarchical cross-modal fusion with a frozen LSeg backbone are the key architectural contributions that bridge the gap between DUSt3R's geometry-only output and the semantic radiance fields previously requiring per-scene fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the SfM preprocessing step removes a major failure point and engineering bottleneck in 3D vision pipelines, making semantic 3D reconstruction viable in sparse-view or low-texture settings where COLMAP typically breaks. The real-time inference (~0.1s per scene vs. 18+ minutes for Feature-3DGS) opens the door to interactive applications in robotics, AR/VR, and digital twins. However, the method is currently limited to stereo image pairs and indoor scenes, relies on ground-truth depth for training (limiting internet-scale video applicability), and the semantic mIoU still trails the 2D LSeg baseline, leaving open questions about scalability to multi-view sequences, dynamic scenes, and outdoor environments.
