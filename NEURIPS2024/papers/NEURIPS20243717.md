## Problem

Existing RL algorithms for LTL and ω-regular objectives either require prior knowledge of unknown MDP parameters (such as ε-return mixing times or ε-recurrence times) or cannot guarantee convergence to provably optimal policies. It was an open problem whether optimal policies for these objectives could be learned at all in the model-free setting, since neither LTL nor limit-average reward objectives are PAC learnable in bounded time.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MDP with unknown transition probabilities, a label function mapping transitions to atomic propositions, and a DRA encoding the ω-regular objective. The translation produces a reward machine whose internal state tracks the DRA state, a status flag (⊤/⊥), and the set of product-MDP transitions discovered so far—ensuring the construction is fully independent of the unknown transition probabilities. The final output is a policy that is optimal for the original ω-regular/LTL objective, obtained by solving a standard limit-average RL problem on the reward machine.

## Method

The key insight is that memoryless reward functions provably cannot capture ω-regular objectives (Proposition 4), but reward machines with finite internal memory can. The translation constructs a product MDP synchronizing the MDP with the DRA, identifies accepting simple end components (ASECs), and builds a reward machine that assigns reward 1 when the run is in an ASEC following the correct action, and 0 otherwise, with a flag mechanism to handle the unknown transition structure by tracking discovered transitions. For learning, Algorithm 1 reduces the limit-average problem to a sequence of discounted-sum problems with γ_k = 1−1/k and ε_k = 1/k, leveraging PAC-learnable discounted RL as a black-box subroutine. A convergence proof (Theorem 16) shows that almost surely the policies become limit-average optimal after finitely many iterations, resolving the open problem of asymptotic learnability for ω-regular and LTL objectives.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental evaluation. The main contributions are: (1) a negative result (Proposition 4) proving that memoryless reward functions cannot provide optimality-preserving translations from ω-regular objectives to limit-average rewards; (2) a positive result (Theorem 12) establishing that such a translation exists when finite-memory reward machines are used; and (3) the first convergence proof (Theorem 16) of an RL algorithm for average rewards without assumptions on the induced Markov chains, resolving two open problems from [2].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines, as it contains no experiments. Instead, it positions its theoretical contributions relative to prior work: prior approaches (e.g., [15, 30]) require prior knowledge of MDP parameters such as ε-return mixing times or ε-recurrence times, which are unavailable in model-free settings. The paper's translation to limit-average reward machines (Theorem 12) removes this requirement entirely. The convergence result (Theorem 16) is noted as the first proof without assumptions on the induced Markov chains, applying to multichain MDPs with unknown transitions — a setting where prior average-reward RL algorithms (e.g., R-MAX [8]) either do not apply or lack guaranteed convergence. The paper also contrasts with [25], whose translation to limit-average rewards is only optimality-preserving for absolute liveness properties and requires the MDP to be communicating with known penalty magnitudes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reward machine framework of Icarte et al. (ICML 2018) as the vehicle for its translation, and directly resolves Open Problems 1 and 4 from Alur et al. (2022), who formalised the notion of optimality-preserving specification translation and proved impossibility for discounted rewards. The convergence proof for average rewards (Theorem 16) leverages the Blackwell-optimality-to-discounted-reward reduction of Grand-Clément & Petrik (NeurIPS 2023). The key departure from prior RL-with-LTL work (Wolff et al. 2012, Fu & Topcu 2014, Perez et al. 2024) is that those approaches either required prior knowledge of MDP parameters (ε-return mixing times, ε-recurrence times) or used non-optimality-preserving translations to discounted rewards; here, the translation to limit-average rewards is provably optimality-preserving and requires no knowledge of transition probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that optimality-preserving translation from ω-regular/LTL objectives to limit-average reward machines exists, the paper means that all current and future advances in average-reward RL (algorithms, convergence guarantees, sample complexity bounds) automatically carry over to the more explainable LTL/ω-regular setting. The first convergence proof for average-reward RL without assumptions on the induced Markov chain structure (applicable to multichain MDPs) is a standalone contribution. Limitations include the exponential size of the general translation, the asymptotic-only convergence guarantee (no finite-time bounds, consistent with the known non-PAC-learnability of average rewards), and the restriction to finite, fully observable MDPs. Open questions include whether the translation size can be made polynomial in the DRA size and whether the approach extends to partially observable settings.
