## Problem

Classical optimization algorithms designed for worst-case convergence guarantees often exhibit slow practical performance, while empirically tuned optimizers frequently lack provable convergence. This paper bridges the gap by proposing a systematic, circuit-theoretic methodology that yields new algorithms with built-in convergence guarantees, rather than relying on ad hoc analysis for each method.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard-form convex optimization problem (a closed, convex, proper function f: R^m → R∪{∞} and a selection matrix E defining n nets over m variables) together with a user-specified RLC circuit topology (resistor, inductor, capacitor values and connectivity via a reduced node incidence matrix A). It outputs a discrete-time algorithm with explicit update rules and a set of dissipativity parameters (η, h, α, β) that certify convergence; the circuit must satisfy an admissibility condition ensuring its equilibrium coincides with the KKT conditions of the original problem.

## Method

The methodology has two stages. First, the subdifferential operator ∂f is interpreted as an m-terminal nonlinear resistor, and an RLC circuit (the 'dynamic interconnect') is designed so that its continuous-time dynamics—governed by KCL, KVL, and component V-I relations—converge to a primal-dual solution; convergence is certified via an energy dissipation argument showing that capacitor and inductor energy is monotonically decreased by power dissipated in resistors and ∂f. Second, the continuous-time ODE is discretized using a two-stage Runge-Kutta scheme, and the discretization parameters (α, β, h) are found automatically by solving a performance estimation problem (PEP) formulated as a nonconvex QCQP (or SDP for common function classes), ensuring the discrete energy sequence satisfies a sufficient dissipativity condition. This recovers classical algorithms (Nesterov acceleration, decentralized ADMM, PG-EXTRA) as special cases and enables rapid exploration of new variants, such as the DADMM+C method demonstrated in experiments.

## Results

On a decentralized optimization problem with N=6 nodes, 7 edges, and x ∈ R^100 (a composite objective mixing L1 and L2 terms), the proposed DADMM+C algorithm reduces the relative error |f(x^k) − f*|/f* to 10^{-10} in 66 iterations. This is a 24% speedup over DADMM (87 iterations) and a 4.4× speedup over P-EXTRA (294 iterations). The speedup is attributed to adding a capacitor (momentum) to the DADMM circuit, which exploits the strong convexity of the local functions f_4 and f_5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed DADMM+C method against two established decentralized optimization baselines: DADMM (decentralized ADMM, Shi et al. 2014) and P-EXTRA (proximal gradient EXTRA, Shi et al. 2015). DADMM+C outperforms DADMM by reaching the 10^{-10} relative error threshold in 66 iterations versus 87, a 24% reduction in iterations. The improvement over P-EXTRA is more dramatic: 66 iterations versus 294, a 4.4× speedup. The key design insight is that adding a single capacitor to the DADMM circuit introduces momentum, which is known to accelerate convergence for strongly convex components (f_4 and f_5 in the test problem). The paper further provides a general convergence proof (Lemma I.1) for DADMM+C on any connected graph, establishing it as a new method family rather than a one-off tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the continuous-time dynamics perspective on optimization (Nesterov ODE, heavy ball, splitting methods) and the circuit-theoretic view of distributed optimization originating from Boyd's 2010 EE364b lecture [28]. While prior work [48, 163, 41, 153] used physical circuits to solve specific optimization problems, and PEP/QCQP [54, 150, 45] provided automated worst-case analysis for fixed algorithms, no prior work combined RLC circuit design with automated discretization to systematically generate new provably convergent algorithms. The paper positions itself as the first generalized framework for circuit-based algorithm design and the first to automate the discretization step using performance estimation programming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The methodology provides a principled, repeatable recipe for generating new optimization algorithms with convergence guarantees, lowering the barrier to algorithm design from ad-hoc construction to systematic circuit synthesis. The recovery of Nesterov acceleration, decentralized ADMM, and PG-EXTRA as special cases validates the framework's breadth, while the DADMM+C experiment demonstrates practical speedup (66 vs. 87 iterations). Key limitations include restriction to convex problems, reliance on solving potentially expensive QCQPs for discretization, and empirical validation on a single small-scale problem instance. Open questions include extension to stochastic and nonconvex settings, scalability of the automated discretization to larger circuits, and whether the framework can discover algorithms that outperform hand-designed methods on large-scale problems.
