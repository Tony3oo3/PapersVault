## Problem

Prior prompt-based UDA methods (e.g., DAPL, MPA) enforce invariant constraints on representation or prompt space but ignore the inherent gradient conflict between source and target objectives when updating shared prompts, potentially causing one domain's update to hurt the other. Additionally, fine-tuning large pre-trained vision-language models risks overfitting due to over-parameterization, especially in multi-task settings. The paper asks: can we foster consensus between per-domain gradient signals while simultaneously controlling prompt generalization, without complex gradient manipulation or extensive hyperparameter tuning?

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain images and unlabeled target-domain images, generating pseudo-labels for the target via CLIP's zero-shot predictions filtered by a confidence threshold τ. Only learnable prompt tokens (domain-agnostic shared prompts of K×M₁ class-specific tokens plus domain-specific prompts of M₂ tokens per domain) are optimized, totaling roughly 500k parameters versus ~2M for MPA. At inference, a single RGB image is encoded by CLIP's frozen image encoder and matched against prompt-augmented text embeddings to produce a class label.

## Method

PGA casts UDA as a multi-objective optimization problem where each source and the target domain define a separate cross-entropy objective over shared and domain-specific prompts. The key mechanism is gradient alignment: the shared prompt is temporarily shifted by a small step proportional to the normalized source gradient (and vice versa), and the target (or source) loss is re-evaluated at this perturbed position via a first-order Taylor expansion, yielding a practical gradient that implicitly maximizes the cosine similarity between source and target gradients without computing any Hessian. A gradient-norm penalty term is fused into the same perturbation to encourage flat minima and improve generalization. For multi-source UDA (MPGA), the target gradient is aligned with each source gradient individually, and the shared prompt is updated by summing all aligned source gradients weighted by λ plus the aligned target gradient. The entire procedure is end-to-end, requires no sequential per-pair training, and operates on the small prompt parameter space rather than full model gradients.

## Results

On ImageCLEF, PGA (source-combined) achieves 92.4% average top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best source-combined method (Simple Prompt, 88.4%) by 4.0% and MPA (91.3%) by 1.1%. In the multi-source setting, MPGA reaches 92.9% on ImageCLEF and 79.4% on Office-Home, outperforming MPA (91.3% / 75.4%) by 1.6% and 4.0% respectively. On DomainNet, MPGA achieves 56.2% average accuracy, exceeding MPA (54.1%) by 2.1%. Across all three benchmarks, PGA/MPGA consistently outperform zero-shot CLIP (88.1% / 71.4% / 50.5%) by 4–8% while using only ~500K trainable parameters versus 2M for MPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGA outperforms all source-combined baselines on both ImageCLEF and Office-Home, with the largest margins over DAPL (+5.3% avg on ImageCLEF, +4.0% on Office-Home) and DANN (+4.6% / +4.4%). In the multi-source setting, MPGA beats the strongest prompt-based competitor MPA by 1.6% on ImageCLEF and 4.0% on Office-Home, and by 2.1% on DomainNet (56.2% vs 54.1%). Against non-prompt multi-source methods, MPGA surpasses MFSAN by 5.3% on Office-Home and PFSA by 7.7% on DomainNet. On the 12 pairwise Office-Home settings (Table 4), PGA achieves 75.8% average, consistently beating DAPL (74.5%) across all 12 source-target pairs while using the same CoOp parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The only domain where prompt-based methods struggle is QuickDraw on DomainNet (11.6% for MPGA), where non-prompt methods like SIMplDA (18.9%) still lead, though MPGA remains the best prompt-based result.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prompt-learning-based UDA line initiated by DAPL (Ge et al., 2023), which introduced domain-agnostic and domain-specific prompts for CLIP, and MPA (Chen et al., 2022), which extended this to multi-source settings via auto-encoder-based prompt alignment. The key departure is reframing UDA as a multi-objective optimization problem where each domain loss is an objective, then aligning per-objective gradients in the (small) prompt space rather than manipulating representations or using contrastive/auto-encoder losses. The gradient norm penalization draws from sharpness-aware minimization and multi-task learning literature, and the theoretical bound connects to information-theoretic generalization analysis for SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, practical recipe (avoiding Hessian computation) for gradient alignment in prompt space that consistently outperforms prior prompt-based UDA methods by ~4% on Office-Home multi-source while using fewer trainable parameters (500k vs 2M for MPA). The information-theoretic bound formally justifies why minimizing gradient norms and aligning cross-domain gradients improves target-domain generalization, though the bound grows with training iterations and does not account for the KL divergence term that domain-alignment methods target. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CLIP backbones, whether combining gradient alignment with explicit distribution alignment (the second term in the bound) yields further gains, and whether the method's reliance on reliable pseudo-labels (threshold τ) limits its applicability to domains where zero-shot CLIP performance is weak (e.g., QuickDraw).
