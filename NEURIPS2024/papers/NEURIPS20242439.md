## Problem

Sparse tuning achieves state-of-the-art PEFT performance by adjusting only the most task-relevant weights, but it requires storing the entire pre-trained weight matrix as learnable parameters in the optimizer plus an additional binary mask of tunable weight indexes. This makes sparse tuning's memory footprint equal to or even exceeding full fine-tuning, negating its practical advantage for large-scale models. The paper asks whether sparse tuning's performance gains can be preserved while reducing memory to LoRA-level.

## Input / Output

SNELL operates on standard RGB images fed into pre-trained vision backbones (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14, Swin-B, ConvNeXt-Base) and outputs class labels for 24 downstream visual recognition tasks spanning fine-grained classification (FGVC) and transfer learning (VTAB-1k). The method is applied to all linear layers of the backbone, and a sparsity ratio hyperparameter s ∈ [0,1] controls the fraction of non-zero weights in the adaptation matrix, tuned per task. In an auxiliary NLP experiment, SNELL is applied to LLaMA-2-7B for commonsense reasoning, taking text prompts and producing text completions.

## Method

SNELL decomposes the tunable adaptation matrix into two small learnable low-rank matrices (rank r ≪ min(m,n)) that are merged via a nonlinear kernel function (default: piecewise linear) rather than a simple inner product, yielding a high-rank merged matrix that preserves the expressiveness needed for sparse tuning while keeping only the compact low-rank factors in the optimizer. For sparsification, a competition-based mechanism replaces the stored binary mask: a dynamic threshold is set to the ⌈smn⌉-th smallest absolute value in the merged matrix, and all weights below it are zeroed out via a soft-threshold function, so weights must compete end-to-end to survive without any explicit index storage. The kernelized merging is inspired by DyN's dynamical-systems interpretation of weight matrices and the kernel trick, allowing the merged matrix to approximate full-rank updates from low-rank factors. This design simultaneously achieves LoRA-level memory (only low-rank matrices stored in the optimizer, with the merged matrix recomputed on the fly during backpropagation) and sparse-tuning-level accuracy across FGVC, VTAB-1k, and LLM benchmarks.

## Results

On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained classification tasks) with supervised ViT-B/16, SNELL-32 achieves 91.8% mean top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best SPT-LoRA (90.1%) by 1.7% and SSF (90.7%) by 1.1%. On VTAB-1k (19 transfer tasks), SNELL-32 reaches 76.9% mean accuracy, outperforming SPT-LoRA (76.4%) and SSF (75.7%). SNELL achieves these gains with LoRA-level memory usage (~1673 MB for ViT-B/16 vs. ~2428 MB for GPS), and the memory advantage grows with model size, reaching ~50% of full fine-tuning memory on ViT-H/14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNELL-32 outperforms all reparameterization-based baselines on both benchmarks: it beats SPT-LoRA by 1.7% on FGVC (91.8% vs. 90.1%) and by 0.5% on VTAB-1k (76.9% vs. 76.4%), and surpasses SSF by 1.1% on FGVC and 1.2% on VTAB-1k. Against LoRA-8, SNELL-8 gains 5.5% on FGVC (91.5% vs. 86.0%) and 1.5% on VTAB-1k (76.4% vs. 74.9%), demonstrating that kernelized merging plus sparse tuning substantially recovers the expressiveness lost by low-rank constraints. Among addition-based methods, SNELL-32 matches SPT-Adapter on VTAB-1k (76.9% vs. 76.4%) while achieving 2.0% higher FGVC accuracy (91.8% vs. 89.8%), and it avoids the extra inference cost of adapter modules. On the larger-model experiments (ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14), SNELL-8 outperforms LoRA-8 by 1.6% (ViT-L) and 0.9% (ViT-H) on VTAB-1k mean accuracy. In the appendix, SNELL-32 matches GPS on FGVC (91.8% vs. 91.8%) but uses ~31% less memory (9692 MB vs. 16119 MB on ViT-H), and it outperforms VQT by 8.1% on VTAB-1k (76.9% vs. 68.8%). On LLaMA-2-7B commonsense reasoning, SNELL-32 beats LoRA-32 by 1.1% average (78.7% vs. 77.6%), confirming cross-domain applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNELL sits at the intersection of two PEFT threads: LoRA-style low-rank reparameterization [27] and sparse tuning that selects individual task-relevant weights [22, 64, 20]. Prior sparse tuning methods (SPT, GPS) achieved strong performance but required storing the full weight matrix in the optimizer plus binary index masks, negating any memory advantage over full fine-tuning. SNELL departs from this by sparsifying a kernel-merged low-rank adaptation matrix rather than the original weight matrix, and replaces explicit index storage with a competition-based soft-threshold mechanism inspired by neuronal competition in neuroscience [49]. The kernel-based rank expansion draws on DyN [45] and the classical kernel trick [32], repurposing them to overcome the expressiveness bottleneck of low-rank LoRA under sparsity constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNELL makes sparse tuning practically viable for large-scale models (ViT-H/14, LLaMA-2-7B) by cutting memory to roughly 50% of full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding SOTA PEFT accuracy, addressing a gap that limited prior sparse tuning to smaller backbones. The competition-based sparsification eliminates a non-trivial engineering overhead (index storage) and enables end-to-end, task-adaptive weight selection without pre-pruning heuristics. Open questions include whether the slight training-time overhead from ΔW recomputation can be eliminated via custom GPU kernels, whether the piecewise-linear kernel is optimal or whether other kernels unlock further gains, and how the method scales to multi-modal or diffusion-model settings where the linear-layer assumption may not hold.
