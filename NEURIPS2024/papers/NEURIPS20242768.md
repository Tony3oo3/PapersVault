## Problem

Dual-target drug design—creating a single ligand that simultaneously binds two distinct protein targets—lacks both a curated benchmark dataset and effective generative methods. Existing structure-based drug design models are trained on single-target protein-ligand complexes, and the scarcity of dual-target training data prevents direct application of powerful diffusion-based generators to this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242768/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two protein binding pockets (each a set of 3D atom coordinates and types) as input and produces a single 3D ligand molecule (atom coordinates in ℝ³ and one-hot atom type vectors) that can bind to both targets. Before generation, the two pockets are spatially aligned via a rotation and translation computed by docking a reference ligand to each pocket and matching the resulting poses, so that both pockets occupy a shared coordinate frame during the reverse diffusion process.

## Method

The core idea is to reprogram a pretrained single-target SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model (TargetDiff) for dual-target generation in a zero-shot manner by composing the drift terms from two conditional denoising processes. COMPDIFF builds two complex graphs (protein₁+ligand and protein₂+ligand) sharing the same ligand nodes, then averages the predicted ε_θ drifts for both 3D positions and categorical atom types at each reverse-diffusion step, equivalent to sampling from the product of two single-target energy functions via Langevin dynamics. DUALDIFF refines this by composing the SE(3)-equivariant messages (both hidden-embedding updates and coordinate updates) at every layer of the equivariant network rather than only at the output, yielding a more fine-grained interaction between the two target conditions. The alignment of dual pockets uses protein-ligand binding priors: a reference ligand is docked to each pocket, and the rotation/translation aligning the two docked poses defines the spatial transformation applied to the second pocket. No additional training is required—only the sampling procedure is modified.

## Results

On the curated dual-target dataset of 12,917 target pairs, DUALDIFF achieves the best Dual High Affinity (36.3%) and the strongest P-2 Vina Dock score (−8.48), indicating superior simultaneous binding to both targets. COMPDIFF and DUALDIFF substantially outperform single-target baselines on the second target: TargetDiff drops from −8.62 (P-1) to −6.89 (P-2), while DUALDIFF maintains −8.41/−8.48. DUALDIFF also achieves the best Max Vina Dock (−7.66), confirming that generated molecules bind both targets with minimal conformational change. In terms of molecular properties, LinkerNet leads in QED (0.61) and SA (0.72), while COMPDIFF/DUALDIFF offer better diversity (0.72/0.67 vs. 0.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUALDIFF outperforms all baselines on the key dual-target binding metrics: it achieves the best P-2 Vina Dock (−8.48 vs. −8.13 for LinkerNet, −6.89 for TargetDiff) and the highest Dual High Affinity (36.3% vs. 35.7% for LinkerNet, 29.0% for TargetDiff). Against the single-target baselines Pocket2Mol and TargetDiff, the gap on P-2 Vina Dock is dramatic (−8.48 vs. −4.63 and −6.89), confirming that single-target generative models cannot generalize to dual-target binding without reprogramming. Compared to the repurposed linker design baselines, DUALDIFF wins on binding affinity (Max Vina Dock −7.66 vs. −7.17 for LinkerNet, −5.87 for DiffLinker) and diversity (0.67 vs. 0.37 for LinkerNet, 0.52 for DiffLinker), while LinkerNet retains an edge in drug-likeness (QED 0.61 vs. 0.56) and synthesizability (SA 0.72 vs. 0.59). Notably, COMPDIFF and DUALDIFF operate without reference molecules, whereas DiffLinker and LinkerNet require them as input, making the generative methods more practical for de novo dual-target design. DUALDIFF also outperforms COMPDIFF on all binding affinity metrics, validating that composing SE(3)-equivariant messages at each network layer is more effective than composing only at the output level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generation framework of Liu et al. (ECCV 2022) from 2D visual generation to 3D molecular generation, adapting the drift-composition principle to SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models for structure-based drug design. It builds directly on TargetDiff (Guan et al., ICLR 2023), a pretrained single-target equivariant diffusion model, and reprograms it for dual-target design in a zero-shot manner by composing drift terms from two aligned pockets—either at the output level (COMPDIFF) or at each equivariant message-passing layer (DUALDIFF). The paper also repurposes linker design methods (DiffLinker, LinkerNet) as baselines by identifying binding-critical fragments from reference molecules, bridging the pharmacophore-combination tradition in medicinal chemistry with modern generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generative formulation of dual-target drug design and the first dataset curated specifically for this task from synergistic drug combinations, filling a gap that has hindered AI-driven progress in this area. The zero-shot reprogramming paradigm—requiring no dual-target training data and no fine-tuning—makes the framework broadly applicable to any pretrained SBDD generative model, which is practically significant given the scarcity of dual-target complex data. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation, the assumption of rigid protein structures, and roughly 2× the inference cost of single-target generation; open questions include extension to multi-target (≥3) scenarios, incorporation of protein flexibility, and whether the compositional framework generalizes to other equivariant generative architectures.
