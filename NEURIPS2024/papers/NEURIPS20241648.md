## Problem

Existing semantic segmentation methods handle either domain generalization (covariate shifts like weather changes) or anomaly detection (semantic shifts like novel objects), but not both simultaneously. Domain generalization techniques fail to flag unknown objects, while OOD detection methods assign excessive uncertainty to known objects under domain shifts. The paper asks whether a single model can generalize to covariate-shift regions while precisely identifying semantic-shift regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces two outputs: a pixel-wise (or mask-wise) segmentation prediction over known classes and a pixel-wise uncertainty map. The key design constraint is that the uncertainty map must be high only for semantic-shift regions (novel/unknown objects) while remaining low for covariate-shift regions (e.g., foggy, night, or rainy versions of known objects). The approach is architecture-agnostic, supporting both pixel-wise backbones (DeepLabv3+ with WideResNet38) and mask-wise backbones (Mask2Former with ResNet-50).

## Method

The core contribution is a Coherent Generative-based Augmentation (CG-Aug) pipeline that uses a pretrained ControlNet (semantic-to-image generation) to produce training images containing both covariate and semantic shifts in a single coherent image, rather than applying them separately. OOD object masks are pasted onto training labels, and text prompts specifying weather, time, and location guide the generation to create diverse domain shifts while the pasted objects introduce semantic shifts. A learnable semantic-exclusive uncertainty function (parameterizing the energy function or MSP) is trained with a relative contrastive loss that enforces large uncertainty gaps between unknown and known regions while keeping uncertainty similar between original and covariate-shifted known regions. A two-stage noise-aware training procedure first freezes the backbone to calibrate the uncertainty function, then fine-tunes the feature extractor with both the contrastive and segmentation losses, using pixel-wise small-loss sample selection to mitigate generation artifacts.

## Results

On the joint OOD detection and domain generalization benchmarks, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics. On ACDC-POC with DeepLabv3+, it reaches AP=82.41, FPR95=1.01 for anomaly detection and mIoU=54.12, mAcc=85.07 for known-class segmentation, outperforming the best prior OOD+DG combination (RPL) by 4.6 AP points and 7.8 mIoU points. On MUAD, it achieves AP=36.08 and mIoU=31.33 (DeepLabv3+), surpassing all baselines. With Mask2Former, the method further improves to AP=90.42 and FPR95=0.46 on ACDC-POC, and AP=45.65 on MUAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 21 baselines spanning three categories: OOD-only methods (PEBAL, RPL, Mask2Anomaly, RbA, M2F-EAM), DG-only methods (RobustNet, RuleAug, IBN, IterNorm, IW, ISSA, CMFormer), and combined OOD+DG approaches (OOD+RuleAug). On the joint ACDC-POC/MUAD benchmarks, the method decisively outperforms the strongest prior combined method RPL [31] by +4.6 AP and +7.8 mIoU on ACDC-POC (DeepLabv3+), and by +8.4 AP and +1.5 mIoU on MUAD. DG-only methods (RobustNet, RuleAug) improve known-class mIoU over baseline but catastrophically degrade OOD detection (AP drops to 2–4 vs. 82.41 for the proposed method), while OOD-only methods (PEBAL) achieve reasonable AP but sacrifice mIoU. On pure anomaly segmentation benchmarks (Table 1), the method surpasses RPL by +3.0 AP on RoadAnomaly and +4.6 AP on SMIYC-RA21 (DeepLabv3+), and outperforms Mask2Anomaly by +10.5 AP on RoadAnomaly (Mask2Former). The method also outperforms the naive OOD+RuleAug combination by +1.8 AP and +7.4 mIoU on ACDC-POC, demonstrating that coherent generative augmentation and decoupled training are more effective than simply combining existing OOD and DG pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—dense OOD detection (PEBAL, RPL, Mask2Anomaly) and domain generalization for segmentation (RobustNet, RuleAug, ISSA)—into a single training framework. It builds on ControlNet/Stable Diffusion for semantic-to-image generation (extending POC's inpainting-based approach to full-image coherent generation) and adapts the 'small loss' pixel-selection criterion from Arpit et al. for noise-aware training. The key departure from prior work is the observation that naively combining DG and OOD objectives interferes: DG methods degrade OOD detection and vice versa, motivating the coherent co-generation of both shift types and a decoupled two-stage training with a relative contrastive loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that domain generalization and OOD detection objectives actively interfere (rather than being orthogonal) is practically important for deploying segmentation in open-world settings like autonomous driving, where both novel objects and new weather/scene conditions occur simultaneously. The coherent generative augmentation strategy is a reusable recipe for creating training data with multiple coupled distribution shifts, applicable beyond driving. Key limitations include reliance on generative model quality (vegetation class degrades, small/remote objects fail) and performance saturation with more generated data, leaving open how to scale this approach to broader scene categories and larger datasets.
