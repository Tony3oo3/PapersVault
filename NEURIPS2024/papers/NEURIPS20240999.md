## Problem

Subquadratic sequence models such as Mamba SSMs have been pretrained with substantially less compute and data than the strongest Transformer models, leaving them underperforming despite their efficiency advantages. This paper asks whether the vast resources already invested in training large Transformers can be directly leveraged to produce stronger subquadratic models, rather than requiring them to be trained from scratch at comparable scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240999/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distillation pipeline consumes text token sequences from the C4 dataset at 2048 sequence length, using a pretrained Phi-1.5 Transformer as the teacher and a Mamba-2-based Phi-Mamba as the student. The student produces next-token logits matching the teacher's vocabulary, and the final model is evaluated on zero-shot commonsense reasoning benchmarks (WinoGrande, ARC, PIQA, HellaSwag, LAMBADA). The student architecture mirrors the teacher's block structure exactly, differing only in replacing the self-attention mixer with Mamba-2's structured state-space duality (SSD) mixer, so that all non-mixer components (embeddings, MLPs, layer norms, LM head) can be directly transferred.

## Method

MOHAWK is a three-phase progressive distillation that matches the teacher and student at increasing levels of granularity. Phase 1 (Matrix Orientation) minimizes the Frobenius distance between the teacher's materialized self-attention matrix and the student's SSD mixer matrix at each layer, treating both as sequence transformation matrices. Phase 2 (Hidden-State Alignment) then minimizes the L2 distance between the full block outputs (Mamba block vs. Attention block), done independently per block to preserve already-learned representations. Phase 3 (Weight-Transfer and Knowledge Distillation) transfers the teacher's remaining weights (token embeddings, MLPs, layer norms, LM head) into the student and fine-tunes end-to-end with a cross-entropy distillation loss on teacher logits. The key architectural insight is that Mamba-2's SSD mixer can be viewed as a causal linear attention with a learnable rolling multiplicative mask, making it a natural subquadratic substitute for softmax attention; the student's block structure is deliberately aligned with the teacher's so that MLP weights can even be frozen during distillation without significant performance loss, reducing trainable parameters by more than half.

## Results

Phi-Mamba-1.5B achieves 62.6% average accuracy across WinoGrande, ARC-E, ARC-C, PIQA, HellaSwag, and LAMBADA using only 3.0B training tokens — less than 1% of the data used by competing subquadratic models. This represents a 2.9-point improvement over the next-best open-source non-Transformer model (Mamba-1-1.4B at 59.7%) and comes within 2.3 points of the teacher model Phi-1.5-1.3B (64.9%). On individual benchmarks, Phi-Mamba reaches 71.7% on WinoGrande and 44.1% on ARC-Challenge, compared to 60.9% and 33.3% for Mamba-2-1.3B respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Phi-Mamba outperforms all six compared subquadratic baselines on average accuracy by a wide margin. Against Mamba-2-1.3B (59.6% avg), Phi-Mamba gains +3.0 points, with particularly large improvements on ARC-C (+10.8) and WinoGrande (+10.8). Against Mamba-1-1.4B (59.7%), the gap is +2.9 points on average. Phi-Mamba also surpasses xLSTM-1.4B (58.5%), RWKV4-1.5B (54.3%), and GLA-1.3B (51.0%) by 4.1, 8.3, and 11.6 points respectively. The only model that outperforms Phi-Mamba is the teacher Phi-1.5-1.3B (64.9%), which was trained on 150B tokens — 50× more data. Notably, Phi-Mamba achieves these results with 3.0B tokens, compared to 315B for Mamba-1/2, 300B for xLSTM, and 1.1T for Finch and Eagle, representing a >100× reduction in training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba-2 / Structured State Space Duality framework (Dao & Gu, ICML 2024), which established the theoretical equivalence between SSMs and a class of causal linear attention, and on the original Mamba selective state-space model (Gu & Dao, 2023). It departs from prior cross-architecture distillation work (e.g., Transformer→RNN, Transformer→linear attention) by introducing a three-stage progressive distillation (matrix orientation → hidden-state alignment → end-to-end knowledge distillation) and by targeting the more expressive Mamba-2 SSD mixer as the student. The key conceptual departure is treating both Attention and SSMs as matrix mixers over token sequences, enabling fine-grained, block-by-block alignment rather than only end-to-end logit matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOHAWK demonstrates that a competitive subquadratic SSM can be produced with only 3B tokens—less than 1% of the 315B tokens used to pretrain Mamba-1/2—by leveraging knowledge already embedded in a pretrained Transformer. This opens a practical pathway for the community to convert existing Transformer checkpoints into efficient SSMs without retraining from scratch, potentially democratizing access to subquadratic inference. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (7B+), whether other subquadratic architectures (xLSTM, RWKV, RetNet) benefit similarly, and whether the multi-stage recipe generalizes beyond the Phi-1.5 → Phi-Mamba pairing. The authors also note that 'trainability' and 'distillability' are distinct model properties, suggesting future work should tailor distillation strategies per architecture.
