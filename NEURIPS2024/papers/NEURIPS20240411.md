## Problem

Most non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for adaptive optimizers (Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam/AMSGRAD) assume unbiased gradient estimators, while existing biased-SGD analyses only cover non-adaptive steps. In practice, however, many important applications—reinforcement learning, MCMC-based generative modeling (e.g., IWAE), bilevel optimization, and conditional stochastic optimization—inherently produce biased gradients while simultaneously relying on adaptive step schedules. This paper closes the gap by providing the first convergence analysis that jointly handles both biased estimators and adaptive steps for non-convex smooth objectives.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes as input a differentiable objective V, a (potentially biased) stochastic update H_θ(X_{n+1}), and a symmetric positive-definite adaptive matrix A_n (instantiated as Adagrad, RMSProp, or AMSGRAD), and produces a high-probability or in-expectation bound on E[‖∇V(θ_R)‖²] of the form O(log n/√n + b_n), where b_n encodes the time-dependent bias. For the empirical validation, the method consumes CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) and FashionMNIST images through a CNN-based VAE/IWAE/BR-IWAE encoder-decoder (latent dim 100), and outputs negative log-likelihood on a held-out test set along with the gradient-squared norm trajectory across training epochs.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified Adaptive Stochastic Approximation recursion θ_{n+1} = θ_n − γ_{n+1} A_n H_{θ_n}(X_{n+1}) governed by four assumptions: PL condition (H1, optional), L-smoothness (H2), a combined biased-gradient/expected-smoothness condition (H3) that bounds both the inner-product alignment of the biased estimator with the true gradient and the second moment of the update, and a spectral-norm bound on A_n (H4). The key technical device is a recursive inequality (Lemma A.1) that absorbs the adaptive step's effect into an exponential decay factor, yielding a risk bound O(n^{−γ+2β+λ} + r_n) under PL and a gradient-norm bound via a randomized iterate R in the non-convex case. For Adagrad and RMSProp, the authors verify H4 by showing eigenvalues of A_n are bounded between (M²+δ)^{−1/2} and δ^{−1/2} under bounded gradients, and verify H3(i) by decomposing the conditional expectation into a standard SGD term plus a perturbation from the one-step change in A_n. For AMSGRAD, the proof exploits the coordinate-wise running maximum V̂_k = max(V̂_{k−1}, Diag(V_k)) to ensure A_k is decreasing, enabling telescoping sums over the momentum terms. The bias term b_n is explicitly characterized: if the estimator bias decays as O(n^{−α}), the overall rate is O(log n/√n) whenever α ≥ 1/4, matching the unbiased rate.

## Results

The paper establishes a non-asymptotic convergence rate of O(log n / √n + b_n) for Adagrad, RMSProp, and AMSGRAD with biased gradients on non-convex smooth functions, where b_n is the time-dependent bias term. Under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition, an improved linear convergence rate of O(n^{-γ+2β+λ} + r_n) is obtained. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 with a CNN VAE architecture (latent dim 100, 100 epochs), BR-IWAE consistently achieves lower negative log-likelihood than IWAE, which in turn outperforms standard VAE across all three optimizers (Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam). The gradient norm ‖∇V(θ_n)‖² in IWAE experiments converges at the predicted theoretical rates, with α=1/4 and α=1/2 matching O(log n/√n) and α=1/8 matching O(n^{-1/4}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three generative models (VAE [48], IWAE [12], BR-IWAE [14]) across three adaptive optimizers (Adagrad [27], RMSProp [72], Adam [47]) on CIFAR-10. IWAE consistently outperforms standard VAE in negative log-likelihood, confirming that the tighter importance-weighted ELBO yields better generative quality despite the biased gradient. BR-IWAE [14] further outperforms IWAE in all optimizer settings by reducing the O(1/k) bias of the self-normalized importance sampling estimator. Among optimizers, Adam achieves slightly better convergence than Adagrad and RMSProp, consistent with its momentum-based design, though all three converge at the same theoretical rate. The theoretical contribution extends prior unbiased adaptive results [27, 74, 19] and biased non-adaptive results [44, 21] by being the first to combine both biased gradients and adaptive steps, recovering the O(log n/√n) rate of [74, 79] in the unbiased limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in stochastic optimization theory: adaptive-step convergence analysis (Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam, AMSGRAD under unbiased gradients, as in Ward et al. 2020, Defossez et al. 2020, Reddi et al. 2018) and biased stochastic approximation with fixed steps (Karimi et al. 2019, Demidovich et al. 2024). The authors explicitly note that prior work covers either adaptive steps with unbiased estimators or biased estimators with non-adaptive steps, and their contribution is the first to handle both simultaneously. They extend the 'Expected Smoothness' assumption of Demidovich et al. to the adaptive setting and introduce a spectral-norm bound (H4) on the adaptive matrix that is naturally satisfied by Adagrad, RMSProp, and AMSGRAD under bounded gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant because many modern applications—reinforcement learning, MCMC/SMC-based generative modeling, bilevel optimization, and conditional stochastic optimization—necessarily use biased gradient estimators combined with adaptive optimizers, yet lacked non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. The O(log n/√n + b_n) rate shows that the bias term is additive and can be controlled by increasing Monte Carlo samples per iteration, providing concrete guidance for hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include the optimal allocation of Monte Carlo effort across iterations, whether the bounds are tight for specific architectures, and extending the analysis to non-smooth or non-convex settings beyond the PL condition.
