## Problem

Despite two decades of HSIC estimators (U-statistic, V-statistic, Nyström-based) all achieving an O_P(n^{-1/2}) convergence rate, it was unknown whether this rate is minimax optimal or whether faster estimators could exist. The existing MMD lower bound of Tolstikhin et al. (2016) was inapplicable to HSIC because it relied on radial kernels and adversarial distributions with product structure, which trivially yield zero HSIC.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes n i.i.d. samples from a Borel probability measure P on R^d and considers any estimator of HSIC_k(P) or the centered cross-covariance operator C_X. The kernel k must be a product of continuous bounded translation-invariant characteristic kernels (e.g., Gaussian kernels with common bandwidth γ), and the distribution class P must contain all d-dimensional Gaussians. The output is a distribution-free minimax lower bound of c/√n on the estimation error, with the constant c made explicit for Gaussian kernels (c = γ / (2(2γ+1)^{d/4}+1)) and shown to exist for the general translation-invariant case.

## Method

The proof applies Le Cam's two-point method: construct an adversarial pair of multivariate Gaussians N(μ₀, I_d) and N(μ₁, Σ(ρ_n)) that differ only in a single off-diagonal correlation entry ρ_n = 1/√n, then verify (i) the n-fold product measures are KL-close (KL ≤ 5/4) and (ii) their HSIC values differ by at least 2c/√n. For Gaussian kernels, HSIC is computed in closed form via determinant expressions (Lemma 1), and a monotonicity argument on a scalar function establishes the √n lower bound. For general translation-invariant kernels, Bochner's theorem rewrites MMD² as an L²(Λ_k) norm of the difference of characteristic functions; the mean value theorem applied to the exponential of the quadratic form, combined with the fact that the spectral measure Λ_k has full support (by the characteristic assumption), yields a strictly positive integral that scales as ρ_n² = 1/n. This construction avoids the product-structure pitfall that invalidated the Tolstikhin et al. MMD lower bound for the HSIC setting.

## Results

The paper proves that the minimax optimal rate of HSIC estimation on ℝ^d for Borel measures containing Gaussians with continuous bounded translation-invariant characteristic kernels is O(n^{-1/2}). For the specific case of Gaussian kernels with bandwidth γ, the explicit lower-bound constant is c = γ / (2(2γ+1)^{d/4} + 1). The same O(n^{-1/2}) lower bound extends to the estimation of the centered cross-covariance operator C_X in the RKHS norm (Corollary 1), recovering the covariance-operator result of Zhou et al. [2019] as a special case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new estimator; instead, it establishes a minimax lower bound that matches the known O_P(n^{-1/2}) upper bounds of the classical U-statistic and V-statistic HSIC estimators (Gretton et al. 2005; Quadrianto et al. 2009; Pfister et al. 2018) and the Nyström-based HSIC estimator (Kalinke and Szabó 2023), thereby certifying their minimax optimality on ℝ^d. The result is distinct from the MMD lower bound of Tolstikhin et al. [2016], which relies on radial kernels and independent Gaussian distributions whose adversarial pair has a product structure yielding zero HSIC difference, making it inapplicable here. It also generalizes the covariance-operator lower bound of Zhou et al. [2019] by noting that the centered covariance is a special case of the centered cross-covariance (a variable with itself). The proof handles the broader class of continuous bounded translation-invariant characteristic kernels via Bochner's theorem, going beyond the radial-kernel restriction of prior MMD minimax analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax estimation framework of Tsybakov (2009) and Le Cam's method to the specific problem of HSIC estimation, building on the characteristic-function representation of MMD for translation-invariant kernels (Sriperumbudur et al., 2010) and the theory of characteristic tensor product kernels (Szabó and Sriperumbudur, 2018). It departs from the closest prior lower-bound work of Tolstikhin et al. (2016) on minimax MMD estimation in two critical ways: replacing radial kernels with the broader class of translation-invariant kernels, and constructing adversarial Gaussian pairs with non-product covariance structure (so that HSIC is non-zero), since the product-structure adversarial pairs used for MMD yield zero HSIC and are inapplicable. The result also subsumes the minimax covariance operator lower bound of Zhou et al. (2019) as a special case via the cross-covariance operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving the O(n^{-1/2}) minimax lower bound, the paper settles a question that has been open since HSIC was introduced nearly two decades ago, confirming that the widely-used U-statistic, V-statistic, and Nyström-based estimators cannot be improved upon in the worst case for translation-invariant characteristic kernels on R^d. This closes the theoretical loop for practitioners: the standard estimators are provably optimal, so future work should focus on constant-factor improvements, finite-sample behavior, or extensions to settings where the translation-invariant assumption fails (e.g., non-Euclidean domains). A limitation is that the lower bound is established only over a class containing Gaussians, and the constant in the general translation-invariant case (Theorem 1(ii)) is not made explicit, leaving room for tighter characterizations.
