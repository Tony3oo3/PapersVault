## Problem

Unsupervised disentangled representation learning has traditionally relied on hand-crafted regularization losses (e.g., β-VAE, FactorVAE, InfoGAN) or complex architectural designs to encourage factor separation, yet Locatello et al. showed that such regularizations alone are insufficient. The paper asks whether diffusion models, as deep generative probabilistic models, inherently possess structural inductive biases that can drive disentanglement without any additional regularization terms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 64×64 RGB image and produces a set of N=20 concept tokens (each a 32-dim vector) that serve as disentangled factor representations, along with a reconstructed image generated by the diffusion decoder. The encoder outputs a scalar feature vector where each dimension encodes one factor; non-shared MLP layers then map each scalar to a concept token vector. The concept tokens condition the U-Net via cross-attention, and the final output is a pixel-space image reconstructed over 200 denoising steps in the VQ-VAE latent space.

## Method

EncDiff couples a standard CNN image encoder with a latent diffusion U-Net, bridged by cross-attention that treats the encoder's concept tokens as keys/values and the U-Net's spatial features as queries. The paper identifies two inductive biases that jointly drive disentanglement without any regularization: (1) the diffusion process exhibits time-varying information bottlenecks (the KL divergence between the reverse conditional q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0) and the Gaussian prior increases as t decreases), and (2) cross-attention enforces alignment between individual concept tokens and spatial regions, analogous to word-to-region alignment in text-to-image generation. A key theoretical result shows that the information bottleneck on the latent x_{t-1} is transferred to the learnable concept tokens S via invariance of KL divergence under differentiable mappings, so the diffusion training objective implicitly constrains the information capacity of S at each timestep. The entire system is trained end-to-end with only the standard ε-prediction diffusion loss, using a cosine β schedule and VQ-VAE latent space, achieving state-of-the-art DCI scores (0.969 on Shapes3D) without any disentanglement-specific loss terms.

## Results

On Shapes3D, EncDiff achieves a near-perfect FactorVAE score of 0.999 and DCI of 0.969, substantially outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DisDiff (0.902 / 0.723) and all VAE- and GAN-based methods. On MPI3D, EncDiff reaches 0.872 FactorVAE and 0.685 DCI, more than doubling DisDiff's DCI (0.337). On the real-world CelebA dataset, EncDiff attains the best TAD (0.638) and FID (14.8), surpassing DisDiff (0.305 / 18.2) and InfoDiffusion (0.299 / 23.6). On Cars3D, EncDiff achieves the highest DCI (0.279) but a lower FactorVAE score (0.773) than DisDiff (0.976), which the authors attribute to unreliable ground-truth labels for that dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EncDiff outperforms all 12 baselines on Shapes3D and MPI3D across both FactorVAE score and DCI, with the largest margins over DisDiff (the strongest prior diffusion-based method): +0.097 DCI on Shapes3D (0.969 vs 0.723) and +0.348 DCI on MPI3D (0.685 vs 0.337). On Cars3D, EncDiff wins on DCI (0.279 vs 0.232 for DisDiff) but trails DisDiff on FactorVAE score (0.773 vs 0.976); the authors note this metric is less reliable on Cars3D due to missing ground-truth labels for factors like color and shape. Among VAE-based methods, FactorVAE and β-TCVAE achieve moderate DCI on Shapes3D (~0.61) but drop sharply on MPI3D (~0.24), while GAN-based methods (InfoGAN-CR, LD, GS, DisCo) are consistently weaker. On the real-world CelebA dataset, EncDiff nearly doubles the TAD of the next-best method (0.638 vs 0.305 for DisDiff) and achieves the lowest FID (14.8 vs 18.2), demonstrating that the inductive-bias-driven disentanglement generalizes beyond synthetic data. Notably, EncDiff achieves these results without any additional regularization loss, whereas DisDiff relies on complex disentanglement losses and multiple decoder inferences for sub-gradient field prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM) architecture and the DDPM diffusion process, repurposing the cross-attention conditioning mechanism originally designed for text-to-image generation as an inductive bias for disentangled representation learning. It departs from the dominant paradigm in disentanglement research—where β-VAE, FactorVAE, InfoGAN, and DisDiff all rely on explicit regularization losses (KL divergence, total correlation, mutual information, sub-gradient fields)—by demonstrating that the diffusion process's time-varying information bottleneck combined with cross-attention is sufficient to drive disentanglement without any additional loss terms. The work also extends the AnnealVAE insight about dynamic information capacity allocation, showing that diffusion's reverse process naturally provides a schedule of varying bottleneck tightness across time steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that explicit regularization is necessary for disentanglement, showing that architectural inductive biases (diffusion + cross-attention) can substitute for tailored loss functions. This opens a new design space for disentangled representation learning where the generative model's structure, rather than the objective function, encodes the disentanglement prior. However, the method remains limited to relatively simple synthetic datasets and one real-world dataset (CelebA), and its multi-step sampling makes it slower than VAE/GAN alternatives. Open questions include whether this inductive bias generalizes to high-dimensional, complex real-world data and whether the information bottleneck analysis can be formalized into a tighter theoretical guarantee.
