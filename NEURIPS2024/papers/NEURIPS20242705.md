## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from slow sequential inference, and existing acceleration strategies either reduce sampling steps (degrading quality) or require computationally expensive distillation and retraining. This paper asks whether the sequential denoising process can be partially parallelized by exploiting structural redundancy within the UNet itself, without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in inference-time modification on pre-trained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v1.5, DeepFloyd-IF, DiT), accepting standard text prompts and optionally reference or conditional images (edges, scribbles) for downstream tasks. It produces generated images (256×256 to 512×512) or short videos (e.g., 8 or 16 frames) at the same resolutions as the base model, with no change to the model architecture or output format. The only additional requirement is a small amount of GPU memory to cache encoder features from key time-steps.

## Method

The paper empirically shows that UNet encoder features vary minimally across adjacent time-steps (MSE < 0.4) while decoder features vary substantially (MSE ~5), motivating 'encoder propagation': at non-key time-steps, the encoder is skipped and its features from the nearest preceding key time-step are reused as skip-connection inputs to the decoder. A non-uniform key time-step schedule (e.g., {50,49,48,47,45,40,35,25,15} for 50-step DDIM) places more key steps in the early denoising phase where encoder features change more. Because multiple adjacent non-key time-steps share the same encoder features, their decoder forward passes can be executed in parallel (parallel-batch), yielding up to 41% speedup for SD. A prior noise injection (z_t += 0.003·z_T for t < 25) compensates for texture detail loss. The entire approach is training-free and composable with existing solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, ToMe).

## Results

On Stable Diffusion (DDIM, 50 steps), encoder propagation with parallel decoding reduces sampling time from 2.42s to 1.42s per image (41% speedup) while improving FID from 21.75 to 21.08 and Clipscore from 0.773 to 0.783. On DeepFloyd-IF (DDPM, 225 steps), the method achieves a 24% time reduction (34.55s → 26.27s). On DiT (250 steps), the method accelerates sampling by 29% at 256×256 (5.13s → 3.62s) and 34% at 512×512 (26.25s → 17.35s) with minimal FID degradation (2.27→2.31 at 256, 3.04→3.25 at 512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard DDIM baseline, the proposed method wins on both speed (41% faster) and quality (FID improves from 21.75 to 21.08, Clipscore from 0.773 to 0.783) on SD. Compared to DeepCache (1.05s/image, FID 21.53) and CacheMe (1.30s/image), the proposed parallel encoder propagation is substantially faster at 0.56s/image with comparable FID (21.62), and uniquely supports multi-GPU parallelism (77% speedup vs. 56% for DeepCache and 44% for CacheMe). On DiT, the method achieves 29–34% speedup with only marginal FID increase (2.27→2.31 at 256px; 3.04→3.25 at 512px). For downstream tasks, the method consistently accelerates Text2Video-zero (23%), VideoFusion (32%), ControlNet (51% with parallel), Dreambooth (41%), and Custom Diffusion (41%) while preserving or slightly improving Clipscore. The method is orthogonal to step-reduction solvers (DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++) and token-merging (ToMe), stacking additional 41–48% UNet speedup on top of those techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model acceleration literature, which splits into step-reduction solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) and knowledge distillation (progressive distillation, LCM, ADD). Its closest conceptual neighbors are DeepCache and CacheMe, which also exploit feature similarity across time-steps, but both remain sequential and CacheMe requires fine-tuning. The key departure is the empirical observation that UNet encoder features are nearly static while decoder features evolve drastically, enabling the encoder to be skipped at non-key time-steps and the resulting decoder computations to be batched in parallel — a capability neither DeepCache nor CacheMe supports. The method is explicitly orthogonal to and composable with step-reduction solvers and token-merging (ToMe).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring no retraining and enabling multi-GPU parallel decoding, the approach makes high-quality diffusion sampling accessible to practitioners with limited compute budgets and on edge devices, a practical gap that distillation-based methods cannot fill. The validation across UNet (SD, DeepFloyd-IF) and transformer (DiT) backbones, and across text-to-image, text-to-video, personalized, and reference-guided tasks, suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the method degrades under very aggressive step reduction (the authors note quality loss at ~5 steps) and whether it can be combined with distillation pipelines, which the authors leave to future work.
