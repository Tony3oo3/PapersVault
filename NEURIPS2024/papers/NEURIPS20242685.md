## Problem

De novo design of co-crystals with predefined mechanical properties (tabletability) remains unsolved: existing AI methods can only predict properties of already-known co-crystal pairs or estimate co-crystallization probability, but cannot generate novel coformer molecules from scratch targeting specific tabletability profiles. This limits pharmaceutical co-crystal development to narrow, experimentally-driven screening campaigns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242685/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a drug molecule in SMILES form and binary target conditions for three plasticity parameters (unobstructed planes, orthogonal planes, H-bond bridging) as input. It outputs a ranked list of coformer SMILES candidates, each annotated with predicted tabletability probabilities and a CCGNet co-crystallization score. Generated molecules are filtered by synthetic accessibility (SA ≤ 3) and chemical validity via RDKit, and the final ranking is performed per generative model separately.

## Method

GEMCODE hybridizes three generative architectures (LSTM-GAN, transformer-based VAE, and transformer-based CVAE) pretrained on 1.75M ChEMBL molecules and fine-tuned on 4227 coformer structures, with a multi-objective evolutionary optimizer (MOEA/D via the GOLEM library) that mutates generated molecules to improve tabletability. The T-CVAE uses a 6-layer, 8-head Pre-Layer Normalization transformer with a 128-dim conditional Gaussian latent space and 512-dim token embeddings, where the condition vector encodes physicochemical property predictions from gradient-boosting classifiers trained on 6029 CSD co-crystal records. A pretrained CCGNet GNN (98.6% balanced accuracy) then ranks drug–coformer pairs by co-crystallization probability. The key architectural insight is that the CVAE condition vector is derived from the GB model's own predictions, creating a feedback loop that steers generation toward target properties, while the evolutionary stage further refines candidates using the same GB models as a multi-objective fitness function.

## Results

Among the three generative models, T-CVAE produces the highest percentage of target coformers (5.63%) with the desired tabletability profile, compared to GAN (2.23%) and T-VAE (1.68%), while T-VAE achieves the best validity (99.70%) and lowest duplicate rate (24.30%). Evolutionary optimization (MOEA/D) significantly improves H-bond bridging probability across all models (e.g., GAN: 0.62→0.69, p=1.05e-66) and equalizes performance between generative models. The ensemble of all three models generates 2.47× more unique target coformers than T-VAE alone. GEMCODE successfully recovered experimentally validated coformers for Nicorandil (fumaric acid), Rivaroxaban (malonic acid), and Paracetamol (naphthalene, trimethylglycine), and predicted 23 novel coformers for Nicorandil.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-objective GraphGA baseline (adapted from Guacamol), GEMCODE achieves superior mechanical property predictions: unobstructed planes 0.95 vs 0.938, orthogonal planes 0.72 vs 0.63, and H-bond bridging 0.18 vs 0.12, with more stable convergence. GEMCODE also generates 21.3% target molecules from the population during optimization versus 20.5% for GraphGA, and extends the coformer dataset by 2,452 molecules versus only 120 from GraphGA. In the language model comparison, GPT-2 (14.7M params) achieves 2.14% target coformers—comparable to T-VAE but well below T-CVAE (5.63%)—while Llama-3-8B (LoRA) improves validity to 98.3% and novelty to 85.4% but drops target coformers to just 0.34%, making it uncompetitive despite higher diversity. Among evolutionary schemes, MOEA/D marginally outperforms SPEA-2 for H-bond bridging (e.g., T-CVAE: 0.693 vs 0.682, p=0.013) but differences are small for other properties. Overall, the hybrid generative + evolutionary pipeline consistently outperforms any single component or baseline across all tabletability metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEMCODE extends the CVAE framework (Kingma et al., 2014) with a transformer encoder-decoder and physicochemical condition vectors, building on the attention-based molecular CVAE of Kim et al. (2021), but departs significantly by conditioning on gradient-boosting predictions of co-crystal mechanical properties rather than generic molecular descriptors. The pipeline integrates three previously separate research threads—deep generative molecular design (LSTM-GAN, T-VAE, T-CVAE), evolutionary graph optimization (GOLEM, MOEA/D), and GNN-based co-crystallization scoring (CCGNet)—into a unified de novo co-crystal design workflow. Unlike prior co-crystal property prediction work that only scored known molecular pairs, GEMCODE generates novel coformer structures and targets specific tabletability parameters, a task the authors explicitly frame as unaddressed in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end pipeline for de novo co-crystal design targeting mechanical properties (tabletability), with experimental validation showing GEMCODE recovers known coformers for three drugs and proposes 23 novel candidates for Nicorandil. Key limitations include the small coformer training set (4,227 structures), the absence of full wet-lab validation (synthesis + compression), and the unaddressed role of polymorphism in property prediction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond tabletability to other physicochemical properties, whether larger coformer datasets would resolve the orthogonal-planes prediction bias, and whether the computational cost of T-CVAE (45 min for 10k molecules on an A6000) limits practical adoption.
