## Problem

Black-box variational inference (BBVI) methods traditionally rely on stochastic gradient descent to optimize variational parameters, making them sensitive to learning rates, termination criteria, and other hyperparameters. Recent score-based BBVI methods (Gaussian Score Matching, Batch and Match) sidestep SGD for Gaussian variational families, but they cannot model non-Gaussian posterior structure. The paper asks whether score-based BBVI can be extended to expressive non-Gaussian variational families while still yielding closed-form, non-iterative optimization.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the score function (gradient of the log unnormalized target) evaluated at a batch of importance samples drawn from a user-specified proposal distribution, plus mean and covariance estimates (from Laplace, Gaussian VI, or domain knowledge) for a standardizing linear transform. It outputs a variational density in the form of a squared weighted sum of orthogonal basis functions, from which samples are drawn via sequential inverse-CDF sampling and low-order moments are computed analytically through one-dimensional integrals. The density is guaranteed nonnegative and normalized by construction, and the number of basis functions K directly controls the expressiveness-to-computation trade-off.

## Method

EigenVI constructs variational families by squaring weighted sums of orthonormal basis functions (e.g., weighted Hermite polynomials on ℝ^D), where the lowest-order term yields a Gaussian and higher-order terms systematically capture non-Gaussianity. The Fisher divergence between the variational and target distributions, estimated via importance sampling over a batch of B proposal samples, is shown to reduce exactly to a quadratic form αᵀMα in the expansion weights, where the K×K matrix M depends only on the basis functions, their gradients, and the target score at the sample points. Minimizing this quadratic form subject to the unit-norm constraint ∑α_k²=1 is a minimum eigenvalue problem, so the optimal variational parameters are given by the eigenvector of M's smallest eigenvalue—no iterative gradient steps are needed. A standardizing linear transform (z → Σ^{-1/2}(z−μ)) is applied before fitting to reduce the number of basis functions required, and the final density is obtained by reversing the change of variables.

## Results

On 8 posteriordb Bayesian models (dimensions 3–10), EigenVI achieves lower empirical Fisher divergence than all Gaussian baselines (ADVI, GSM, BaM) as the number of basis functions K increases, with the gap widening for more non-Gaussian targets. On 2D and 5D sinh-arcsinh synthetic targets, EigenVI with K=10–100 basis functions reduces forward KL(p;q) substantially below the Gaussian BaM and ADVI baselines, particularly for heavier-tailed or more skewed distributions. On the 8-schools model (D=10), EigenVI provides a marginal fit competitive with a real-NVP normalizing flow, while the Gaussian methods visibly underfit the tails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EigenVI outperforms all three Gaussian BBVI baselines on the Fisher divergence metric across all 8 posteriordb models: ADVI (ELBO-based, full-covariance Gaussian) [27], GSM (Gaussian score matching) [37], and BaM (regularized score-based Gaussian) [6]. The advantage grows with the number of basis functions K, confirming that the non-Gaussian higher-order terms capture structure the Gaussian family cannot. Against the real-NVP normalizing flow [12], EigenVI is competitive on 8-schools (D=10) and garch11 (D=4), matching the flow's ability to model heavy tails without requiring gradient-based optimization or architecture hyperparameter tuning. On the 2D synthetic targets (Gaussian mixture, funnel, cross), EigenVI with K=K₁K₂ basis functions achieves lower forward KL than the Gaussian BaM fit, with the gap most pronounced on the multimodal mixture and cross distributions. The paper notes that normalizing flow Fisher divergence estimates were unreliable due to score computation issues, so direct numerical comparison with NFs is limited to qualitative marginal plots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of score-based black-box variational inference (BBVI) work—Gaussian score matching (Modi et al., 2023) and Batch and Match VI (Cai et al., 2024)—from Gaussian variational families to a much broader class of non-Gaussian families constructed from orthogonal function expansions (Hermite, Legendre, Laguerre, Fourier). The key departure is a theoretical result: the specific combination of an orthogonal-expansion variational family, the Fisher divergence as objective, and an importance-sampling estimator causes the optimization to collapse to a minimum eigenvalue problem, eliminating the need for iterative gradient-based optimization that plagues both ELBO-based BBVI and prior score-matching methods. This connects to the quantum-mechanical tradition of representing wave functions as squared weighted sums of orthogonal basis functions, repurposed here for probabilistic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EigenVI offers a principled, non-iterative alternative to gradient-based BBVI that avoids the hyperparameter sensitivity (learning rates, termination criteria) that makes methods like ADVI brittle, while remaining more tractable than normalizing flows or energy-based models (closed-form moments, straightforward sampling). The main limitation is the exponential growth of basis functions with dimension under Cartesian products, which restricts practical use to moderate-dimensional problems; the authors identify low-rank structure and alternative orthogonal families as key directions for scaling. Open questions include adaptive importance sampling, iterative/subsampled versions for large-scale Bayesian inference, and whether the eigenvalue reduction extends to other divergence-estimator combinations.
