## Problem

Fully binarizing diffusion models (W1A1) causes severe generative degradation or complete collapse, as prior work has only achieved weight-only binarization (W1A4) or applied generic binarization methods not tailored to DMs. Two fundamental obstacles remain unaddressed: (1) DM activations exhibit highly dynamic, timestep-correlated ranges that fixed binary quantizers cannot capture, and (2) the discrete binary feature space makes it extremely difficult for generative models to match full-precision outputs during training.

## Input / Output

BiDM takes random Gaussian noise as input and produces RGB images via a fully binarized U-Net denoising network, evaluated on pixel-space DMs (CIFAR-10 at 32×32) and latent-space DMs (LSUN-Bedrooms, LSUN-Churches, FFHQ at 256×256). Because both weights and activations are 1-bit, all convolutions reduce to XNOR and bitcount operations, yielding 28.0× storage and 52.7× OPs savings over the FP32 baseline. The only additional floating-point operations during inference are a depth-1 3×3 convolution for the learnable scaling factor and a single pointwise multiply for the cross-timestep connection, keeping the overhead negligible.

## Method

BiDM introduces two complementary techniques. From a temporal perspective, the Timestep-friendly Binary Structure (TBS) modifies XNOR's activation quantizer by making the tiny 3×3 convolution kernel k learnable (instead of fixed uniform values), allowing the scaling factor to adapt to the highly dynamic activation ranges across denoising timesteps; it also adds cross-timestep feature connections with learnable per-block α coefficients that blend the current and previous timestep's U-Net outputs, exploiting the observed high-dimensional feature similarity between adjacent steps. From a spatial perspective, Space Patched Distillation (SPD) partitions intermediate features from both the binarized and full-precision models into p² spatial patches, computes per-patch attention maps (P·Pᵀ), and minimizes the L2 distance between normalized attention maps patch-by-patch, focusing the distillation signal on local spatial structure rather than a global L2 loss that overwhelms the limited representational capacity of binary features. Training uses quantization-aware training with the total loss combining the standard DM noise-prediction loss and the SPD distillation loss across all 2d+1 U-Net blocks.

## Results

On LDM-4 for LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256, BiDM achieves a FID of 22.74, sFID of 17.91, Precision of 33.54, and Recall of 19.90 at W1A1, compared to the full-precision model's FID of 2.99. On LDM-8 for LSUN-Churches, BiDM reaches FID 29.70, and on LDM-4 for FFHQ, FID 43.42. On pixel-space DDIM for CIFAR-10, BiDM achieves IS 5.18 and FID 81.65, the only W1A1 method producing visually acceptable samples. BiDM maintains identical inference efficiency to the XNOR baseline: 28.0× storage savings and 52.7× OPs savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiDM outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three LDM datasets. Against the strongest general binarization method ReSTE [62], BiDM reduces FID by 61.7% on LSUN-Bedrooms (22.74 vs. 59.44), 30.7% on LSUN-Churches (29.70 vs. 47.88), and 51.4% on FFHQ (43.42 vs. 144.37). The generative-model-specific baselines BBCU [63] and EfficientDM [15] also fail dramatically at W1A1, with FIDs of 236.07 and 194.45 on LSUN-Bedrooms respectively, confirming that existing quantization strategies for diffusion models cannot handle full binarization. XNOR++ [2] and DoReFa [78] produce completely invalid samples (Precision ≈ 0, Recall ≈ 0) on all LDM datasets, while ReActNet [33] and XNOR [49] yield only marginally better but still poor results. On CIFAR-10 (pixel-space DDIM), BiDM is the only W1A1 method achieving IS above 4 (5.18 vs. XNOR's 4.23), with all other baselines below 3.35 IS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binarization lineage from discriminative CNNs (XNOR-Net, ReActNet, ReSTE) to the generative diffusion model setting, specifically adapting XNOR's dynamically-computed activation scaling by making its tiny convolution learnable to handle the highly timestep-dependent activation ranges of DMs. It departs from prior DM quantization work (Q-DM, TDQ, EfficientDM, BinaryDM) that targeted 4-bit or W1A4 regimes, pushing to the extreme W1A1 limit where both weights and activations are binary. The two proposed mechanisms—Timestep-friendly Binary Structure (TBS) and Space Patched Distillation (SPD)—are novel architectural and loss-function modifications tailored to the temporal correlation and spatial locality unique to U-Net-based diffusion models, rather than general-purpose binarization techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first fully binarized diffusion model, BiDM demonstrates that W1A1 compression of DMs is feasible without complete generative collapse, opening a path toward deploying diffusion models on extremely resource-constrained hardware with 28× storage and 52.7× OPs savings. However, the absolute quality gap remains substantial (FID 22.74 vs. 2.99 full-precision on LSUN-Bedrooms), and the approach has only been validated on LDM-4/8 and DDIM at 256×256 or 32×32 resolutions, leaving open whether it scales to larger architectures (e.g., SDXL, video DMs) or higher resolutions. The increased training cost from cross-timestep connections (nearly doubling sampling steps) is a practical concern for adoption, and the paper acknowledges this as a limitation for future work.
