## Problem

The sample complexity of learning an ε-optimal policy in average-reward MDPs under a generative model remained unresolved: existing results either required the strong assumption of uniformly bounded mixing times for all policies (which can be infinite) or had suboptimal dependence on structural parameters. Moreover, no prior work addressed the general (multichain) setting where all optimal policies may be multichain and the optimal gain is not constant across states.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241055/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n independent samples per state-action pair from a generative model (simulator), along with the target accuracy ε and the complexity parameters H (span of the optimal bias function) and B (bounded transient time parameter) for general MDPs. It outputs a stationary policy π̂ satisfying ρ⋆ − ρ^π̂ ≤ ε·1 elementwise. Notably, H and B must be known a priori (or upper-bounded), and the algorithm internally constructs a perturbed empirical transition kernel and solves the resulting discounted MDP exactly via standard solvers in poly(1/(1−γ), S, A, log(1/δε)) time.

## Method

The approach reduces the average-reward problem to a discounted MDP with discount γ = 1 − ε/(12(B+H)), then solves the discounted problem using a perturbed empirical model-based planning algorithm (Algorithm 1). The key technical innovation is a multi-step variance Bellman equation that recursively decomposes the variance of cumulative discounted rewards over H steps, exploiting the fact that V_γ^{π⋆}(S_H) ≈ ρ⋆/(1−γ) + h⋆(S_H) to bound fluctuations by the span H rather than the effective horizon 1/(1−γ). This yields a quadratic (rather than cubic) dependence on 1/(1−γ) for a fixed MDP instance, circumventing the known minimax lower bound for discounted MDPs. For general multichain MDPs, a novel law-of-total-variance argument limits the total variance contribution from transient states, and a new reduction theorem (Theorem 6) handles the case where ρ⋆ is not constant by leveraging the bounded transient time parameter B.

## Results

For weakly communicating average-reward MDPs, the paper establishes a sample complexity of O~(SA·H/ε²) (Theorem 2), which is minimax optimal up to log factors and improves the prior best of O~(SA·H/ε³) [20]. For general (multichain) MDPs, the paper introduces a new transient time parameter B and proves a matching upper and lower bound of O~(SA·(B+H)/ε²) (Theorems 4 and 8). As a byproduct, the paper shows that O~(SA·H/((1-γ)²ε²)) and O~(SA·(B+H)/((1-γ)²ε²)) samples suffice for ε-optimal policies in weakly communicating and general discounted MDPs respectively, circumventing the known Ω~(SA/((1-γ)³ε²)) minimax lower bound by achieving quadratic rather than cubic dependence on the effective horizon 1/(1-γ) for a fixed MDP instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Theorem 2 (O~(SA·H/ε²)) strictly dominates all prior weakly communicating results: it improves the ε-dependence from ε⁻³ to ε⁻² over [20] (O~(SA·H/ε³)), and improves the H-dependence from H² to H over [26] (O~(SA·H²/ε²)). Against the uniform-mixing baselines [8, 9, 13, 22], the paper's bound is strictly stronger because H ≤ 8·τ_unif always holds, and there exist MDPs where H ≪ τ_unif or τ_unif = ∞ (e.g., periodic chains), making the H-based bound applicable in settings where τ_unif-based bounds are vacuous. The closest competitor is [22] (O~(SA·τ_unif/ε²)), which achieves the same ε-scaling but requires the stronger uniform mixing assumption; the paper explicitly notes that H ≤ 8·τ_unif makes their result strictly better. For general MDPs, no prior work provides any sample complexity bound, so the paper's O~(SA·(B+H)/ε²) result (Theorem 8) with its matching lower bound (Theorem 4) is entirely new. The discounted MDP results (Theorems 1 and 7) also improve on [22, 21] by replacing τ_unif with H (or B+H), again leveraging the H ≤ 8·τ_unif relationship.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reduction-to-discounted-MDP framework for average-reward MDPs initiated by Jin & Sidford [9] and refined by Wang et al. [20], using the perturbed empirical model-based planning algorithm of Li et al. [12] as the underlying solver. The key departure is a new multi-step variance Bellman equation analysis that replaces the standard law-of-total-variance bound, yielding instance-dependent variance parameters (span H, transient time B) instead of worst-case horizon bounds. The paper also builds on the value-function approximation result of Wei et al. [23] (Lemma 11) and, for general MDPs, introduces law-of-total-variance arguments for transient states that have no analogue in the weakly communicating setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves the sample complexity of weakly communicating average-reward MDPs with the first minimax-optimal bound in terms of the bias span H, and opens an entirely new line of study for general (multichain) MDPs where H alone is provably insufficient. The demonstration that a fixed MDP instance admits quadratic rather than cubic dependence on the effective horizon 1/(1−γ) in the discounted setting is a fundamental insight that reframes the role of the well-known minimax lower bound. A key open question is whether the requirement for prior knowledge of H and B can be removed; the paper shows (Theorem 3) that estimating H from samples is itself hard, and notes that in online settings, H-dependent regret bounds are conjectured to require H as input.
