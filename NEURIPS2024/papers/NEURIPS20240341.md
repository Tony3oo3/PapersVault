## Problem

Static close-ended benchmarks like MMLU overestimate LLMs' true knowledge capacity because they rely on fixed question formats that diverge from real-world usage and are vulnerable to data contamination during pre-training. Existing mitigations either detect contamination post-hoc or generate entirely new test data, discarding the valuable domain knowledge already encoded in expert-designed benchmarks. PertEval addresses this gap by restating existing benchmark questions through knowledge-invariant perturbations that preserve the underlying knowledge while altering surface form and format.

## Input / Output

The toolkit consumes static multiple-choice questions (question text, options, correct answer) from MMLU subsets and produces two types of perturbed samples: content-level paraphrases generated sentence-by-sentence by an LLM rewriter with a fixed expected similarity score of 0.6, and rule-based format-level transformations (option reordering, ID shifting, type conversion, position swapping). Evaluation outputs are structured as a suite of consistency metrics—ACC@Consist (requiring correctness on both original and most-complexly-perturbed versions), Performance Drop Rate (PDR), and Recall of Performance (ROP)—alongside response pattern breakdowns that categorize each answer into single-correct, single-wrong, invalid, multiple-with-correct, or multiple-without-correct selections.

## Method

PertEval's core mechanism is a two-tier knowledge-invariant perturbation pipeline. At the content level, an LLM rewriter paraphrases each sentence sequentially given its prerequisite context, controlled by a similarity score that permits sentence-structure-level changes while preserving semantic content, entity names, and equation meanings. At the format level, five deterministic strategies (OptionPerm, OptionForm, OptionCaesar, ChangeType, SwapPos) target specific biases: option ordering, ID format, selection character, question type, and global text position. The key evaluation metric, ACC@Consist, requires an LLM to answer correctly on both the original question and the most complex composite perturbation, effectively filtering out performance attributable to rote memorization. Knowledge invariance is verified through dual validation: human/LLM scoring against four explicit standards (semantic, reasoning, answer invariance, and statement clarity) and a behavioral test on questions that all evaluated LLMs answer correctly, where PDR near zero confirms the perturbation does not alter the knowledge being tested.

## Results

PertEval reveals a substantial overestimation of LLM knowledge capacity on static benchmarks: GPT-4-turbo shows a 25.8% absolute overestimation and Gemini-1.0-Pro a 38.8% overestimation on MMLU. The ACC@Consist metric (requiring correct answers on both original and most-complexly-perturbed versions) drops GPT-4-turbo to only 0.555, well above the 0.0625 random-guessing baseline but far below its raw accuracy. Across all six LLMs, the SwapPos perturbation causes the largest average performance drop (macro-PDR = −0.1637), followed by OptionForm (−0.1151) and ChangeType (−0.1083), with all perturbations yielding negative PDRs on average. Response pattern analysis shows GPT-4-turbo's rate of selecting extra incorrect options jumps from 12% to 21.7% under perturbation, indicating rote memorization rather than genuine knowledge mastery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PertEval's knowledge-invariant perturbations against PromptAttack (Xu et al., ICLR 2024) as the sole baseline for perturbation quality. In human-rated knowledge invariance scoring (Table 2), PertEval consistently outperforms PromptAttack across all four MMLU subjects, with scores mostly exceeding 4.0 (the acceptability threshold) versus PromptAttack's scores frequently below 3.0 (e.g., 1.3–2.8 on P-Psychology vs. 4.3–4.7 for PertEval). LLM-based scoring (Table 3) confirms this gap: PertEval achieves 3.8–4.4 average scores while PromptAttack ranges from 3.2–4.3, with PertEval leading on C-Math (3.8 vs. 3.2) and W-History (4.0 vs. 3.2). PertEval also achieves higher edit distances from the original text, confirming it produces more substantial surface-level changes while preserving knowledge, whereas PromptAttack's smaller perturbations correlate with lower knowledge invariance scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PertEval synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) knowledge capacity evaluation via close-ended benchmarks (MMLU, C-Eval, ARC), (2) data contamination mitigation (detection methods and new-data generation), and (3) LM consistency evaluation (Elazar et al., Rajan et al.). Its key conceptual departure is repurposing consistency from a property to be measured into a measurement instrument for probing genuine knowledge capacity, and repurposing existing benchmark knowledge through knowledge-invariant perturbations rather than discarding it for contamination concerns. The perturbation strategies draw on adversarial text attack literature (e.g., PromptAttack) but are explicitly designed to preserve knowledge rather than disrupt it, and the format-level perturbations (option permutation, Caesar cipher, position swapping) are inspired by human educational assessment practices like item personalization and distractor design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that static benchmarks overestimate GPT-4's knowledge capacity by 25.8% (and Gemini-1.0-Pro by 38.8%) on MMLU, combined with the response pattern evidence of rote memorization of correct options, challenges the reliability of widely-adopted evaluation protocols and provides practitioners with concrete metrics (ACC@Consist, PDR, ROP) to audit their own evaluations. Key limitations include applicability only to close-ended benchmarks, evaluation on just four MMLU subjects due to budget constraints, and the inherent lack of fine-grained controllability in LLM-based paraphrasing. Open questions include adapting knowledge-invariant perturbation to open-ended evaluation, achieving continuous and nuanced perturbation control, and defending against adaptive optimization against the open-sourced toolkit.
