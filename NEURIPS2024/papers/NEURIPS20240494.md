## Problem

Scaling Gaussian process hyperparameter optimisation to large datasets requires iterative linear system solvers (conjugate gradients, alternating projections, SGD) whose convergence is slow under poor conditioning. While these solvers dominate training time, key acceleration techniques—pathwise gradient estimation, warm starting, and early stopping—were important to prior methods' success but never received unified analysis or cross-solver validation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular regression data (inputs x ∈ R^{n×d}, targets y ∈ R^n) and kernel hyperparameters (Matérn-3/2 length scales per dimension, signal scale, noise scale), and produces optimised hyperparameters plus s=64 posterior function samples as a byproduct of gradient computation. The linear systems are solved either to a relative residual norm tolerance of 0.01 or within a fixed compute budget of 10–50 solver epochs, with the pathwise estimator's probe vector solutions directly yielding posterior samples via pathwise conditioning without additional linear solves.

## Method

The paper introduces three composable improvements to iterative GP marginal likelihood optimisation. First, a pathwise gradient estimator absorbs H_θ^{-1} into the probe vector distribution (sampling ξ ~ N(0, H_θ) and solving H_θ ẑ = ξ), which provably reduces the initial RKHS distance to the solution from tr(H_θ^{-1}) to the constant n, accelerating solver convergence and simultaneously producing posterior samples via pathwise conditioning. Second, warm starting reuses the previous outer-loop solution to initialise the inner-loop solver, reducing iterations to tolerance by up to 72× for AP with negligible bias (formally bounded via sub-Gaussian concentration). Third, under a limited compute budget, warm starting allows solver progress to accumulate across marginal likelihood steps, decreasing average residual norms by up to 7×. All three techniques are shown to be solver-agnostic, applicable to CG, AP, and SGD, with AP and SGD benefiting most from warm starting while CG is more sensitive to early stopping.

## Results

Across five UCI regression datasets (n < 50k), combining the pathwise gradient estimator with warm starting yields average speed-ups of 2.1× (CG), 18.9× (AP), and 5.1× (SGD) relative to the standard estimator without warm starting, with a peak speed-up of >72.1× for AP on POL. Test log-likelihoods remain essentially unchanged (e.g., 1.27 on POL, −0.39 on ELEVATORS, 2.15 on BIKE) across all solver configurations, confirming no loss in predictive performance. Under a limited compute budget (10 solver epochs), warm starting reduces the average relative residual norm by up to 7× compared to the baseline, and the pathwise estimator reaches lower residual norms than the standard estimator at the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external GP methods (e.g., sparse GPs or other iterative GP formulations); instead, it ablates three internal techniques—pathwise gradient estimation, warm starting, and early stopping—across three linear system solvers (CG, AP, SGD). The baseline is the standard Hutchinson trace estimator with solvers initialised at zero. Warm starting alone provides the largest gains for AP (18.9×) and SGD (5.1×), while the pathwise estimator alone gives modest improvements (1.2× for CG, >5.4× for AP, 2.1× for SGD). Combining both techniques yields the best overall speed-ups (1.9× CG, >72.1× AP, 7.2× SGD) without any degradation in test log-likelihood. CG benefits least from warm starting (only ~2×) because its line-search mechanism is more sensitive to descent direction than initial distance. Under limited compute budgets, AP is the most robust solver, achieving good predictive performance even at 10 epochs without reaching the τ=0.01 tolerance, whereas CG is brittle and degrades significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates and unifies three techniques—pathwise gradient estimation (derived from Antorán et al.'s sampling-based inference [2] and Wilson et al.'s pathwise conditioning [31,32]), warm starting of linear system solvers (from Lin et al. [17]), and early stopping under limited compute budgets—that were individually important to prior iterative GP methods but had not been systematically analysed together. Rather than proposing a new solver, the paper demonstrates that these amortisation techniques are broadly applicable across conjugate gradients, alternating projections, and stochastic gradient descent, and provides theoretical justification (Theorem 1) that warm starting introduces negligible bias. The hierarchical bilevel optimisation framing (outer-loop hyperparameter optimisation, gradient estimator, inner-loop solver) serves as the organising principle that makes cross-solver comparisons tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The up-to-72× speed-ups (particularly for alternating projections) make iterative GPs substantially more practical for large-scale regression, and the pathwise estimator's ability to produce posterior samples as a byproduct of gradient computation eliminates redundant linear solves for prediction. A genuinely open question the paper surfaces is the weak empirical relationship between relative residual norm and downstream predictive performance—AP achieves good predictions even when far from the τ=0.01 tolerance—suggesting the field's reliance on residual norms as a convergence criterion may be misguided. Limitations include the dependence on random features for prior sampling (which introduces small deviations from exact optimisation) and the fact that CG remains brittle under early stopping, limiting the generality of the compute-budget results.
