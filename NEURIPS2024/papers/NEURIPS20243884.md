## Problem

Standard centering and whitening operations applied to word embedding spaces implicitly assume uniform word frequency, treating each word type as if it occurred equally often. In reality, word frequencies follow Zipf's law, so the unweighted mean and covariance used in PCA whitening are miscalculated relative to the true distribution over word types. This paper identifies the type-token distinction as the root cause: word vectors represent types (classes), not tokens (instances), and frequency information is absent from the embedding matrix itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained word embedding matrix (e.g., 300-dim GloVe, Word2Vec, or fastText) and an empirical word frequency distribution p(w) over the vocabulary, which can be sourced from a reference corpus (enwiki), the evaluation test set itself, or a language-specific frequency list (jawiki for Japanese). It outputs a post-processed embedding matrix where each word vector has been centered and whitened under the Zipfian prior, satisfying zero-mean and isotropic conditions with respect to the frequency-weighted expectation. For dynamic embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa), the method operates on token-level embeddings aggregated via first-last average pooling, where the token multiplicity implicitly encodes the Zipfian type frequency.

## Method

Zipfian whitening modifies standard PCA whitening by weighting every expectation calculation with the empirical word frequency. The algorithm proceeds in two steps: (1) Zipfian centering computes the frequency-weighted mean μ̂ = Σ p(wᵢ)wᵢ and subtracts it from each vector; (2) Zipfian decorrelation and standardization performs SVD on the matrix whose rows are √p(wᵢ)·w̄ᵢᵀ, then applies VΣ⁻¹ to the centered vectors. The theoretical contribution frames both uniform and Zipfian whitening as making the partition function of an exponential-family generative model constant up to second order, with the Zipfian variant corresponding to the Levy-Goldberg formula and naturally emphasizing low-frequency (informative) words through their vector norms. The paper further shows that existing methods like skip-gram negative sampling, WhiteningBERT, and headless language models succeed precisely because their embeddings encode a Zipfian prior, unifying them under this exponential-family framework.

## Results

On STS-B, Zipfian whitening achieves 66.92 (GloVe) and 66.50 (Word2Vec), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SIF+CCR (58.70 and 63.04 respectively) by 8.2 and 3.5 points. The improvement over raw embeddings is even more dramatic: 20.75 points for GloVe (46.17 → 66.92) and 9.52 points for Word2Vec (56.98 → 66.50). For symmetry evaluation, the Zipfian 2nd-moment metric correlates with STS-B performance at Spearman ρ = 0.90, compared to 0.07 for IsoScore and −0.07 for Ave. Cos., demonstrating that frequency-aware symmetry scores are far more predictive of downstream task quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zipfian whitening decisively outperforms both post-processing baselines on STS-B: it beats ABTT [39] by 12.6 points on GloVe (66.92 vs. 54.28) and 9.5 points on Word2Vec (66.50 vs. 56.98), and beats SIF+CCR [7] by 8.2 points on GloVe (66.92 vs. 58.70) and 3.5 points on Word2Vec (66.50 vs. 63.04). The advantage is consistent across all seven STS/SICK datasets and both English and Japanese (JSTS), and holds whether using enwiki or test-set word frequencies. For symmetry evaluation, the Zipfian metrics dramatically outperform the uniform-frequency baselines: the Zipfian 2nd-moment score achieves Spearman ρ = 0.90 with STS-B performance, while Ave. Cos. [21] shows ρ = −0.07 and IsoScore [49] shows ρ = 0.07, indicating that existing symmetry metrics are essentially uncorrelated with actual task quality. The paper further shows that uniform whitening of dynamic token embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa) is empirically inferior to the equivalent Zipfian-type whitening, confirming that the frequency-weighted approach generalizes beyond static embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on post-processing word embeddings for improved symmetry (ABTT, SIF+CCR, WhiteningBERT) by identifying a fundamental oversight: all existing centering/whitening methods implicitly assume uniform word frequency, treating type embeddings as if they were token embeddings. The theoretical framework unifies these methods through exponential families, showing that uniform-prior methods (Arora et al.'s latent variable model, softmax cross-entropy) and Zipfian-prior methods (Levy-Goldberg formula, SGNS) correspond to different base measures, and that Zipfian whitening is the frequency-weighted generalization of ABTT. The paper also refines Oyama et al.'s norm-information theorem to the Zipfian setting and provides a unified explanation for why WhiteningBERT, headless language models, and contrastive sentence encoders work well: they implicitly encode Zipfian priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that expectations over word vectors should be weighted by empirical frequency—is deceptively simple yet has broad implications for how the community thinks about embedding geometry, and it provides a principled lens for understanding why many ad-hoc NLP tricks work. The finding that the widely-used Ave. Cos. symmetry metric has near-zero correlation (0.04) with downstream performance while the Zipfian-weighted version achieves 0.83 challenges a standard evaluation practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-order moments, whether it can serve as a regularization term for large language models, and whether the numerical instability from small singular values limits its applicability to very large or poorly-converged vocabularies.
