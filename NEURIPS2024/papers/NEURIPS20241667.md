## Problem

The overfitting behavior of minimum norm interpolating solutions of Gaussian kernel ridgeless regression was previously understood only for fixed bandwidth in fixed dimension (catastrophic) or polynomial scaling of dimension with sample size. This paper addresses two gaps: what happens when the bandwidth is varied or tuned with sample size in fixed dimension, and what happens for arbitrary (including sub-polynomial) scaling of the input dimension with sample size.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes training pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a distribution on the (d−1)-sphere with a target function plus independent noise, a Gaussian kernel with bandwidth τ_m that may vary with sample size m, and an input dimension d = d(m) that may scale arbitrarily with m. The output is upper and lower bounds on the predicted test risk of the minimum norm interpolant, expressed in terms of kernel eigenvalue multiplicities and effective ranks, which classify the overfitting as benign (risk → Bayes error), tempered (risk → finite constant), or catastrophic (risk → ∞). All results are derived under the Gaussian universality ansatz and the eigenframework's closed-form risk prediction.

## Method

For fixed dimension, the paper exploits the closed-form eigenvalues of the Gaussian kernel on the sphere (expressed via modified Bessel functions) and the multiplicities of spherical harmonics to derive sharp bounds on the effective rank r_k and the amplification factor E_0, showing that for any bandwidth scaling the interpolant is inconsistent and typically worse than the null predictor. For increasing dimension, the paper introduces a generic framework using lower and upper indices (L_m, U_m) defined from cumulative eigenvalue multiplicities to bound the test risk from above and below for any kernel with bounded eigenvalue sum. This generic bound is then instantiated for dot-product kernels on the sphere, recovering the known polynomial-scaling results (benign for non-integer exponent, tempered for integer) and yielding two new results: inconsistency for logarithmic dimension scaling, and the first example of benign overfitting with sub-polynomial scaling d = exp(√log m) using the Gaussian kernel.

## Results

For fixed dimension with Gaussian kernel, the minimum norm interpolating solution is never consistent regardless of bandwidth scaling: when τ_m = ω(m^{-1/(d-1)}), the test risk diverges to infinity (catastrophic); when τ_m = o(m^{-1/(d-1)}) or Θ(m^{-1/(d-1)}), the test risk is finite but always at least as large as the null predictor risk R(0) = σ² + ‖f*‖². For polynomially scaling dimension d^α/m = Θ(1), the paper recovers the known result that overfitting is benign for non-integer α and tempered for integer α. The headline new result (Corollary 13) is the first demonstration of benign overfitting with sub-polynomial dimension scaling: with the Gaussian kernel and d = exp(√(log m)), the test risk converges to the Bayes error σ², with an explicit bound R̃(f̂₀) ≤ (1 - 1/log m)^{-1}(1 - exp(-0.89√(log m)))^{-1}σ² + 2B²/m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against reference predictors rather than competing algorithms. The primary baseline is the null predictor (f ≡ 0) with risk R(0) = σ² + ‖f*‖², and the ideal reference is the Bayes optimal predictor with risk σ². In fixed dimension, the minimum norm interpolant is shown to be strictly worse than the null predictor in all three bandwidth regimes (Theorem 3), meaning interpolation provides no benefit over predicting zero. For polynomially scaling dimension, the paper recovers and unifies prior results from Ghorbani et al. [24], Mei et al. [38], Misiakiewicz [40], Barzilai and Shamir [4], and Zhang et al. [64], confirming benign overfitting for non-integer exponents and tempered overfitting for integer exponents. The key new contribution is Corollary 13, which shows that with d = exp(√(log m)) and the Gaussian kernel, the interpolating solution achieves the Bayes error—something impossible with any polynomial or logarithmic scaling. Corollary 12 further establishes that no dot-product kernel on the sphere can achieve benign overfitting at d = log m, providing a negative result that delineates the boundary between possible and impossible benign regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overfitting analysis of kernel ridgeless regression from Mallinar et al. [37] and the eigenframework of Zhou et al. [66] to two new regimes: (1) fixed dimension with arbitrary varying or adaptively chosen bandwidth, and (2) increasing dimension with arbitrary (including sub-polynomial) scaling. Prior work on increasing dimension [4, 64, 24, 38, 40] was restricted to polynomial scaling d ∝ m^α, and fixed-dimension results [37, 15] assumed a fixed bandwidth. The paper's generic upper and lower bounds on test risk in terms of eigenvalue multiplicities (L_m, U_m) unify and recover the polynomial-scaling results while enabling new sub-polynomial and logarithmic scaling analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a more complete taxonomy of when Gaussian kernel ridgeless regression overfits benignly, temperedly, or catastrophically, showing that in fixed dimension no bandwidth choice rescues consistency and that the first sub-polynomial dimension scaling (d = exp(√log m)) achieving benign overfitting exists. Key limitations include reliance on the Gaussian universality ansatz and non-rigorous eigenframework risk predictions, and the open question of whether small noise in the Θ(m^{-1/(d-1)}) bandwidth regime could make interpolation beat the null predictor. For practitioners, the fixed-dimension negative result reinforces that ridgeless Gaussian KRR is fundamentally unreliable in low dimensions regardless of bandwidth tuning, while the sub-polynomial positive result suggests that high-dimensional regimes are where kernel interpolation can succeed.
