## Problem

Probabilistic integral circuits (PICs) with continuous latent variables have only been explored in tree-shaped form, limiting their expressiveness to latent tree models. Training PICs via numerical quadrature is memory-intensive at scale because each integral unit requires its own MLP and the full quadrature PC is materialized in one shot. The paper addresses both gaps by extending PICs to DAG-shaped hierarchies and introducing scalable training techniques.

## Input / Output

The method consumes grayscale (28×28) or RGB (32×32, 64×64) images, treating each pixel as a categorical variable with 256 (grayscale) or 768 (RGB) categories. It outputs a full probability distribution over the image, evaluated as bits-per-dimension (bpd) on test sets. The QPC materialization uses K quadrature points (up to 512) over bounded domains [-1,1], and the PIC functions are parameterized by multi-headed MLPs whose size M is independent of K, decoupling model capacity from quadrature resolution.

## Method

The paper presents a three-stage pipeline: (1) building DAG-shaped PICs from arbitrary region graphs (quad-trees or quad-graphs) via a bottom-up algorithm that merges units using Tucker-merge (for distinct latent variables) or CP-merge (for shared latent variables); (2) materializing the PIC as a tensorized quadrature PC (QPC) by hierarchically applying numerical quadrature, where integral units become sum layers parameterized by matricized quadrature tensors and product units become Hadamard or Kronecker product layers, exactly recovering Tucker and CP layer architectures; (3) applying neural functional sharing—F-sharing (identical functions across a group) and C-sharing (shared inner MLP with per-unit heads)—to parameterize groups of PIC units with a single multi-headed MLP augmented with Fourier features, reducing trainable parameters by up to 99% while requiring comparable GPU memory and time to standard PCs.

## Results

On the MNIST-family (6 grayscale datasets), QPCs achieve the best bits-per-dimension (bpd) on 5/6 datasets, with headline results of 1.11 bpd on MNIST and 3.16 bpd on Fashion-MNIST, consistently outperforming standard PCs (1.17 and 3.32 respectively). On RGB datasets with YCoCg preprocessing, QPCs achieve 4.48 bpd on CIFAR-10, 4.46 on ImageNet32, and 4.42 on ImageNet64, beating standard PCs (4.85, 4.63, 4.59) and HCLT (4.61, 4.82, 4.67). QPCs are competitive with latent variable distillation methods (LVD: 4.37, LVD-PG: 3.87 on CIFAR) that require pre-trained deep generative models, while PIC training is end-to-end and self-contained. Functional sharing reduces trainable parameters by up to 99% (e.g., ~387K vs ~262M for a folded CP-layer with F=500, K=512) while requiring comparable GPU memory and time to standard PCs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPCs outperform standard PCs on 5/6 MNIST-family datasets (losing only on EMNIST-BY where PC achieves 1.47 vs QPC 1.53) and on all 4 RGB datasets (e.g., CIFAR: 4.48 vs 4.85 bpd). Against HCLT [31], which uses EM-based training and patch-wise methods, QPCs win on all 6 MNIST-family datasets and all 3 RGB datasets where both are reported (CIFAR: 4.48 vs 4.61, ImageNet32: 4.46 vs 4.82, ImageNet64: 4.42 vs 4.67). QPCs also beat the pruning-and-growing scheme Sp-PC [10] on 5/6 MNIST-family datasets and substantially outperform RAT-SPN [43], IDF [21], BitSwap [25], BBans [50], and McBits [46] across all datasets. On RGB datasets, QPCs (4.48 bpd on CIFAR) approach but do not match LVD [32] (4.37) and LVD-PG [33] (3.87), which require pre-trained deep generative models, multiple heuristics, and a final EM/SGD fine-tuning stage, whereas PIC training is fully differentiable and self-contained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Probabilistic Integral Circuits (PICs) from their original tree-shaped formulation to DAG-shaped hierarchies of continuous latent variables, and shows that their quadrature-based approximations (QPCs) exactly recover known tensorized circuit architectures: Tucker layers correspond to RAT-SPNs and EiNets, while CP layers correspond to HCLT. The key new ingredient is neural functional sharing (F-sharing and C-sharing), adapted from the module networks literature, which parameterizes groups of PIC units with multi-headed MLPs featuring Fourier-feature layers. This positions the work at the intersection of probabilistic circuits, tensor decompositions, and neural parameterization, providing a unified pipeline (region graph → PIC → QPC → folded QPC) that subsumes several previously independent architecture proposals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a simple, end-to-end differentiable pipeline for training tractable probabilistic models that rivals more sophisticated alternatives (latent variable distillation, pruning-and-growing) without requiring pre-trained deep generative models or complex heuristics, while using up to 99% fewer trainable parameters. This is practically significant for the tractable modeling community, as it removes a major scalability bottleneck. However, limitations remain: sampling from PICs is currently infeasible, the method is less scalable than VAEs/flows/diffusion models, and QPCs still trail the best PC results from latent variable distillation (e.g., 4.48 vs. 3.87 bpd on CIFAR). Open questions include whether variational inference or direct PIC lower-bound optimization could further close this gap, and whether the DAG-shaped expressiveness advantage generalizes beyond image density estimation.
