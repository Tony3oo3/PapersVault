## Problem

All existing (ε,δ)-PAC best policy identification algorithms for average-reward MDPs require prior knowledge of the MDP's complexity (e.g., the optimal bias span H or mixing time), and the paper proves that estimating H to sufficient accuracy is intractable—no bound on the required samples depends solely on S, A, H, δ, and the desired precision. In the online setting, the paper further establishes that no algorithm can achieve sample complexity polynomial in S, A, and H, even with H known.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an MDP with S states and A actions (unknown transition kernel, known mean rewards in [0,1]), an accuracy parameter ε ∈ (0,1), and a confidence level δ ∈ (0,1). It operates under either a generative model (sampling any (s,a) pair freely) or an online model (only choosing actions given the current state). The output is an ε-optimal policy π̂ satisfying g* − g_{π̂}(s) ≤ ε for all states s with probability ≥ 1−δ, along with a diameter upper bound D̂ satisfying D ≤ D̂ ≤ 4D. No prior knowledge of H, D, or mixing time is required.

## Method

The main algorithm, Diameter Free Exploration (DFE), combines two components: (1) a diameter estimation sub-routine (Algorithm 2) that uses an Extended Value Iteration scheme for Stochastic Shortest Path to output D̂ ∈ [D, 4D] with O~(D²SA log(1/δ)) samples, and (2) the state-of-the-art algorithm of Zurek & Chen [27] that finds an ε-optimal policy given an upper bound on H, here instantiated with D̂ as the (loose) upper bound on H via H ≤ D. This yields a near-optimal O~(SAD/ε² · log(1/δ)) sample complexity in the generative model setting. For the online setting, an Online-DFE variant replaces the generative model with GOSPRL-based sample collection, achieving O~(SAD²/ε²). Additionally, the paper introduces a novel data-dependent stopping rule inspired by value iteration for AR-MDPs: it monitors the span of upper/lower confidence bounds on the Bellman residual I_{s,a}(n) = r̄_{s,a} + p_{s,a}b_n − b_n(s) and stops when this span drops below ε, yielding a PAC guarantee for any sampling rule without requiring prior knowledge of the MDP's complexity.

## Results

In the generative model setting, DFE achieves a sample complexity of Õ([SAD/ε² + D²SA] log(1/δ) + D²S²A), which in the small-ε regime reduces to Õ(SAD/ε² log(1/δ)) — matching the worst-case lower bound Ω(SAD/ε² log(1/δ)) from [22] up to logarithmic factors. In the online setting, Online-DFE achieves Õ_δ(SAD²/ε² + S²AD³), introducing an extra factor of D over the generative-model bound. The paper also proves a negative result: no algorithm can estimate the optimal bias span H within a prescribed accuracy using a number of samples bounded by any function of S, A, and H alone (Theorem 1), and a lower bound showing that online BPI cannot achieve sample complexity polynomial in S, A, and H (Theorem 3, with E[τ] = Ω(A^{S-1}/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The key comparison is against [27] (Zurek & Chen), which achieves the optimal Õ(SAH/ε²) bound but critically requires H as an input. DFE replaces this with a diameter estimate D̂ (satisfying D ≤ D̂ ≤ 4D), yielding Õ(SAD/ε²) in the small-ε regime — optimal up to log factors since H ≤ D. Against [22] (Wang et al.), which gives Õ(SAH/ε³) with H known, DFE improves the ε-dependency from ε⁻³ to ε⁻² at the cost of using D instead of H. Against [24] (Wang et al.), which achieves Õ(SA·t_mix,D*/ε²) with the mixing time known, DFE avoids requiring any MDP-specific parameter. The online variant (Online-DFE) incurs an extra factor of D over the generative-model bound (SAD²/ε² vs. SAD/ε²), a gap the authors attribute to the GOSPRL-based generative-to-online conversion. The paper's negative results (Theorems 1 and 3) establish that no algorithm can simply plug in an estimated H to recover the [27] bound, and that no online algorithm can achieve polynomial-in-H sample complexity, making DFE's diameter-based approach the first viable path to prior-knowledge-free BPI in AR-MDPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the span-based algorithm of Zurek & Chen [27], which achieves the optimal O~(SAH/ε²) sample complexity for ε-optimal policy identification in average-reward MDPs but requires the optimal bias span H as input. It also draws on the diameter estimation machinery from the Stochastic Shortest Path literature [21, 20] and the discounted-to-average-reward reduction of Wang et al. [22]. The key departure is a negative result showing that H cannot be estimated with sample complexity polynomial in S, A, and H, which rules out the natural 'plug-in an estimate of H' approach and motivates the use of the diameter D as a proxy instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first (ε,δ)-PAC policy identification algorithms for average-reward MDPs that require no prior knowledge of the MDP's complexity, closing a gap where all existing algorithms assumed H, mixing time, or other parameters were known. The negative result on estimating H and the online lower bound (ruling out polynomial-in-H sample complexity) are important conceptual contributions that reshape how the field should think about complexity measures in AR-MDPs. Open questions include whether the online gap (SAD²/ε² vs. the generative lower bound SAD/ε²) can be closed, and whether the proposed adaptive value-iteration stopping rule combined with a suitable online sampling rule can achieve the optimal scaling.
