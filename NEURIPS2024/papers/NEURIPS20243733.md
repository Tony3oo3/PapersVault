## Problem

Existing image generation models condition on text or single reference images but cannot exploit the combinatorial, relational structure of real-world entity graphs. This paper formulates the Graph2Image task: synthesizing an image for a node in a Multimodal Attributed Graph (MMAG) by conditioning on both its text description and its graph connectivity to neighboring entities, a setting grounded in applications like virtual artwork creation and product recommendation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243733/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a target node in an MMAG, the method consumes its text description (e.g., artwork title or product name), the graph edge structure, and the images of K sampled neighbor nodes (selected via semantic PPR-based sampling). The neighbor images are encoded into a fixed-length set of graph prompt tokens that are concatenated with CLIP text prompt tokens to condition the diffusion denoising process. Output is a 256×256 RGB image for the target node, with test-time controllability over text vs. graph guidance strength and independent weighting of multiple graph edges.

## Method

INSTRUCTG2I extends Stable Diffusion 1.5 by introducing an auxiliary set of graph conditioning tokens alongside the standard text tokens. Neighbor selection uses a two-stage semantic PPR-based sampling: Personalized PageRank identifies structurally relevant nodes, then CLIP-based text-image similarity reranks them to retain semantically aligned neighbors. A Graph-QFormer encoder (self-attention layers for image-image dependency + cross-attention layers guided by the text prompt for text-image dependency) compresses the sampled neighbor images into fixed-capacity graph prompt tokens, initialized from CLIP text encoder weights. At inference, a graph classifier-free guidance formulation decomposes the noise prediction into unconditional, text-conditional, and graph-conditional components with separate scales s_T and s_G (or per-edge scales s_G^(k) for multiple graph conditions), enabling smooth, plug-and-play control over content vs. style and blending of multiple artist/product influences without retraining.

## Results

INSTRUCTG2I consistently outperforms all baselines across three datasets (ART500K, Amazon, Goodreads) on both CLIP and DINOv2 instance-level similarity scores. On ART500K, it achieves 73.73 CLIP and 46.45 DINOv2, surpassing the best baseline (SD-1.5 FT) by +7.18 and +11.80 respectively. On Amazon, it reaches 68.34 CLIP and 51.70 DINOv2 (+3.04 and +10.18 over SD-1.5 FT), and on Goodreads it attains 50.37 CLIP and 25.54 DINOv2 (+3.07 and +4.60 over InstructPix2Pix). The paper also reports that INSTRUCTG2I achieves the best trade-off between DINOv2 (↑) and FID (↓) scores, though InstructPix2Pix obtains a slightly lower FID due to its in-distribution image input constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSTRUCTG2I outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the text-to-image baselines (SD-1.5 and SD-1.5 FT), the gains are most pronounced on DINOv2 scores, where graph conditioning adds 11.80 points on ART500K and 10.18 on Amazon over the fine-tuned SD-1.5 FT, indicating that graph structure captures style and visual consistency that text alone cannot. Against the image-to-image baselines (InstructPix2Pix and ControlNet), which use the single most relevant neighbor image as input, INSTRUCTG2I still leads by 8.07 CLIP on ART500K and 3.07 CLIP on Goodreads, showing that leveraging multiple sampled neighbors with semantic PPR-based selection is superior to relying on a single reference image. The one caveat is that InstructPix2Pix achieves a lower FID than INSTRUCTG2I, which the authors attribute to its in-distribution image input constraining outputs closer to the original distribution. Overall, the results support the paper's central claim that graph-structured relationships provide complementary information beyond what text or single-image conditioning can capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSTRUCTG2I extends Stable Diffusion's text-conditioned latent diffusion framework by introducing a parallel graph-conditioning pathway, drawing on the classifier-free guidance mechanism of Ho & Salimans to handle dual (text + graph) conditions with independent guidance scales. The Graph-QFormer encoder is architecturally inspired by BLIP-2's QFormer, repurposing self-attention for image-image dependency and cross-attention for text-image dependency among sampled neighbors. For neighbor selection, the paper combines Personalized PageRank (from graph representation learning) with CLIP-based semantic reranking, bridging structural graph analytics and vision-language models in a way that prior diffusion or GNN literature has not attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Graph2Image, the paper opens a new axis of controllability for generative models: graph edges encode relational semantics (shared artist, co-purchase, genre) that neither text prompts nor single reference images can express, enabling applications like virtual artwork creation and generative product recommendation. The graph classifier-free guidance mechanism offers a plug-and-play test-time control over how strongly different graph edges influence generation, which is practically useful for multi-style blending. Key limitations include the restriction to homogeneous graphs, reliance on SD 1.5 (not SDXL or later), and the absence of joint text-image generation; open questions include scaling to heterogeneous graphs and whether the approach transfers to 3D or video generation.
