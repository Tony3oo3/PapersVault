## Problem

StyleGAN-based hair editing methods struggle with multi-color hair (e.g., ombre, split-color) due to the scarcity of such examples in facial training datasets and the limited spatial distribution of StyleGAN's latent space, leading to poor generation of intricate color structures and loss of facial attributes during editing. Applying diffusion models to this task introduces additional challenges: the absence of tailored inpainting masks for hair regions, insufficient control mechanisms for faithful color transfer or retention, and limited CLIP understanding of hair-specific color terminology.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source face image (processed at 1024×1024), optional text prompts for hairstyle and/or hair color, optional reference images for style and/or color, and optional stroke maps, supporting any combination of these modalities. It outputs a single edited face image where the hair region is modified while identity, background, clothing, and accessories are preserved. The pipeline internally constructs two distinct hair-agnostic masks (M_a for the style stage retaining face/neck, and M_c for the color stage covering the union of source and style-proxy hair regions) to frame hair editing as a two-stage inpainting problem.

## Method

HairDiffusion operates a two-stage Stable Diffusion inpainting pipeline. In stage one, a style proxy P^s is generated by inpainting the source image's hair region using a hair-agnostic mask M_a, guided by pose keypoints via ControlNet and conditioned on a text prompt or a reference image encoded through a textual-inversion adapter. In stage two, a color proxy P^c is produced by warping the reference hair (using an HR-VITON-based warping network conditioned on DensePose and hair-agnostic segmentation) onto the target hairstyle region, followed by bilateral filtering to retain color structure while removing texture, and PatchMatch to fill gaps. The key mechanism is Multi-stage Hairstyle Blend (MHB): at a chosen intermediate timestep τ during denoising, the noised color-proxy latent is spatially blended with the masked-image latent only within the hair mask, so that early denoising steps are guided by the color proxy while later steps rely on context-aware inpainting to restore occluded face and background. A CLIP text encoder fine-tuned on ~4,600 multi-color hair image-text pairs (with rotation/symmetry augmentation) enables precise text-driven control over color structures like 'ombre' or 'split color.' Canny edges from the style proxy or source image are fed through ControlNet throughout denoising to lock the hairstyle structure.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ test set (2,000 images), HairDiffusion achieves the best identity preservation with IDS 0.94, PSNR 33.1, and SSIM 0.95, outperforming HairCLIPv2 (0.84/29.5/0.91), HairCLIP (0.45/21.6/0.74), TediGAN (0.16/22.5/0.74), and DiffCLIP (0.71/26.8/0.86). In the user study, HairDiffusion leads in accuracy across all three editing categories (text-driven: 42.9%, color transfer: 64.5%, cross-model: 68.3%). For self-transfer reconstruction, HairDiffusion achieves the best FID (5.41), FID_CLIP (0.68), and PSNR (31.66), though it trails HairFastGAN (3.00) and CtrlHair (3.62) on single-color transfer FID_CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairDiffusion dominates all five text-driven baselines on identity preservation (Table 1), with the largest gap over HairCLIP (IDS 0.94 vs. 0.45) and TediGAN (0.94 vs. 0.16), and a clear margin over the strongest competitor HairCLIPv2 (0.94 vs. 0.84 IDS; 33.1 vs. 29.5 PSNR). In the user study (Table 2), HairDiffusion wins accuracy by a wide margin in all three settings (text-driven 42.9% vs. 21.3% for HairCLIPv2; color transfer 64.5% vs. 10.8% for Barbershop; cross-model 68.3% vs. 21.7% for HairCLIPv2), though it trails PowerPaint [47] slightly on preservation (24.5% vs. 25.1%) and SYH [19] on naturalness (27.8% vs. 28.3%). For single-color transfer (Table 4), HairDiffusion achieves the best reconstruction FID (5.41) and PSNR (31.66) but is outperformed by CtrlHair (FID 19.65) and HairFastGAN (FID_CLIP 3.00) on single-color FID_CLIP (5.96), indicating that the warping-based color proxy introduces some color fidelity loss in simple single-color scenarios. Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced in multi-color hair editing and attribute preservation, where StyleGAN-based baselines (HairCLIP, HairCLIPv2, Barbershop, HairFastGAN) are fundamentally constrained by their limited latent-space distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant StyleGAN-based hair editing paradigm (HairCLIP, HairCLIPv2, Barbershop, HairFastGAN) by reframing hair editing as a latent diffusion inpainting task, leveraging Stable Diffusion's inpainting pipeline as the backbone. It integrates ControlNet for hairstyle structure control (Canny edges, pose keypoints) and repurposes the HR-VITON warping architecture from virtual try-on to align reference hair color onto target regions — a cross-domain transfer not previously attempted in hair editing. The Multi-stage Hairstyle Blend (MHB) mechanism, which spatially blends color-proxy and masked-image latents at a specific denoising timestep, is the paper's core architectural novelty, enabling independent control of hair color and hairstyle within a single diffusion process. A fine-tuned CLIP text encoder trained on a curated multi-color hair dataset addresses the semantic gap in describing complex color structures (ombre, split-color) that standard CLIP cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based framework for hair editing, and it demonstrates that the multi-color hair editing problem — which StyleGAN-based methods fundamentally struggle with due to limited latent space coverage — can be addressed through inpainting with explicit color proxies. The warping module's successful transfer from virtual try-on to hair color alignment suggests broader applicability of cross-domain warping techniques. However, the method remains heavily dependent on segmentation quality (BiSeNET), and the warping module fails on large pose/length discrepancies, limiting practical robustness. The single-color transfer FID (20.83) is actually worse than several StyleGAN baselines, indicating that the diffusion advantage is concentrated in multi-color and structural editing rather than simple color swaps. Open questions include whether more advanced diffusion backbones (SDXL, Flux) would close this gap and whether the MHB blending strategy generalizes to other attribute-editing tasks beyond hair.
