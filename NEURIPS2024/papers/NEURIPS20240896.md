## Problem

The spiked tensor model exhibits a large computational-statistical gap: weak recovery is information-theoretically possible at constant SNR, yet the best polynomial-time classical algorithms require SNR scaling as n^((q-2)/4). Whether the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), a realistic near-term quantum algorithm with asymptotic convergence guarantees, can bridge or even surpass this classical threshold is an open question. This paper provides the first rigorous analysis of QAOA applied to a statistical inference problem, characterizing its exact recovery threshold and overlap distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy q-tensor Y ∈ R^{n^q} (a planted Rademacher signal u^{⊗q} plus i.i.d. Gaussian noise scaled by SNR λ_n) and QAOA parameter vectors (γ, β) ∈ R^p × [0,2π]^p, and produces a bit-string z ∈ {±1}^n sampled from the p-step QAOA quantum state. The cost function optimized is the log-likelihood C(z) = ⟨Y, z^{⊗q}⟩ / n^{(q-2)/2}, which has all-to-all connectivity across n qubits. The primary output metric is the overlap R_QAOA = z^T u / n ∈ [-1,1], whose asymptotic distribution (a sine-Gaussian law) is the central analytical result.

## Method

The paper derives the asymptotic overlap distribution of p-step QAOA by computing the expected moment-generating function of the overlap in the configuration basis, then taking the n→∞ limit. The key technical novelty is using discrete Fourier transforms to decouple nonlinear polynomial terms in the combinatorial sums, replacing them with dual variables that yield linear exponents amenable to the central limit theorem; this produces a sine-Gaussian limiting law distinct from the sine-arctan-Gaussian law of tensor power iteration. For p=1, the analysis is fully rigorous, proving the weak recovery threshold is λ_n = Θ(n^{(q-1)/2}), matching 1-step power iteration. For p>1, a physics-style heuristic derivation extends the threshold to λ_n = Θ(n^{(q-2+ε_p)/2}) with ε_p = (q-2)/[(q-1)^p - 1], and numerical optimization of the QAOA parameters reveals a constant-factor quantum enhancement over power iteration for certain (p,q) pairs. A tensor-unfolding variant of the QAOA is shown to recover the classical Θ(n^{(q-2)/4}) threshold as p→∞.

## Results

The paper proves that 1-step QAOA achieves the same weak recovery threshold as 1-step tensor power iteration, λ_n = Θ(n^((q-1)/2)), and that p-step QAOA matches p-step tensor power iteration at λ_n = Θ(n^((q-2+ε_p)/2)) where ε_p = (q-2)/[(q-1)^p − 1]. In the small-SNR regime, 1-step QAOA achieves a constant-factor overlap improvement of √(q/e) over power iteration (Eq. 3.7), which exceeds 1 for all q ≥ 3. The quantum enhancement factor |a_p b_p|^ε_p (Table 1) reaches up to ~1.6 for (p=1, q=7) and ~1.07 for (p=7, q=2), indicating modest but non-trivial constant-factor advantages. The overlap distribution follows a novel sine-Gaussian law, distinct from the sine-arctan-Gaussian law of power iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is p-step tensor power iteration (Wu & Zhou, JMLR 2024 [33]). QAOA matches the asymptotic SNR threshold of power iteration exactly for all p and q, meaning neither algorithm can recover the signal below λ_n = Θ(n^((q-2+ε_p)/2)). However, in the small-SNR regime (Λ ≪ 1), QAOA achieves a constant-factor improvement in overlap: the enhancement factor |a_p b_p|^ε_p exceeds 1 for all q ≥ 3 at p = 1 (reaching √(q/e) ≈ 1.60 for q = 7), and for q ≥ 4 at all p ≥ 2. For q = 2, the advantage only appears at p ≥ 3 (factor ~1.02–1.07). The paper also notes that QAOA with tensor unfolding can reach the classical computation threshold Θ(n^((q-2)/4)) as p → ∞, but cannot surpass it, matching the best known polynomial-time classical algorithms (spectral methods, sum-of-squares). The overlap distribution is qualitatively different: QAOA follows a sine-Gaussian law while power iteration follows a sine-arctan-Gaussian law, and QAOA's overlap does not concentrate at ±1 as SNR → ∞.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the QAOA (Farhi et al., 2014) to the spiked tensor model, a canonical statistical estimation problem with a large computational-statistical gap. The analytical framework builds on the moment-generating function and configuration-basis techniques developed for QAOA on spin-glass models (Farhi et al., 2022; Basso et al., 2022), but departs from them by introducing discrete Fourier transforms to decouple nonlinear polynomial exponents in combinatorial sums—a technique unnecessary in the spin-glass setting due to the planted signal structure. The primary classical baseline is tensor power iteration (Wu & Zhou, 2024), with which the paper compares both thresholds and overlap distributions. The authors position this as the first analytical study of a polynomial-time quantum algorithm on a statistical inference problem, as opposed to the combinatorial optimization problems (MaxCut, spin glasses, CSPs) that dominate prior QAOA theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that constant-depth QAOA achieves the same asymptotic SNR threshold as tensor power iteration for weak recovery, meaning it cannot close the large computational-statistical gap in the spiked tensor model without super-logarithmic depth. The discovery of a sine-Gaussian overlap law (distinct from power iteration's sine-arctan-Gaussian) and a constant-factor quantum enhancement for certain (p,q) pairs provides a nuanced picture of where modest quantum advantages may exist. A key open question is whether QAOA with depth growing with n can surpass the Θ(n^{(q-2)}/4) classical threshold, and whether the observed anti-concentration (over measurements rather than instances) invalidates existing QAOA limitation proofs. The Fourier-transform methodology for handling planted-structure QAOA analysis is of independent interest for future quantum algorithm studies on other inference models.
