## Problem

Prior neural network approaches to learning contracting dynamical systems either guarantee contractivity only locally around training data, restrict the vector field to negative gradient flows (symmetric Jacobians), or enforce contractivity only on a lower-dimensional submanifold. No existing method provides a neural network-based dynamical system with *global* contractivity guarantees in arbitrary metrics while retaining sufficient expressiveness to capture rich nonlinear behaviors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242115/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of state-velocity demonstration pairs {(x_i, xdot_i)} where x_i ∈ R^d are states and xdot_i ∈ R^d are corresponding velocities, standardized to zero mean and unit variance. It produces a globally contracting vector field f: R^d → R^d with a unique equilibrium x* (either fixed or learned), such that evaluating f at any state yields the instantaneous velocity. The contraction metric is implicitly defined by the learned diffeomorphism and need not be explicitly computed during training or inference.

## Method

ELCD factorizes the vector field via extended linearization as f(x) = A(x, x*)(x − x*), where A is parametrized as −P_s^T P_s + P_a − P_a^T − αI with P_s and P_a as neural networks, guaranteeing the symmetric part of A is negative definite everywhere. This structure ensures global exponential stability and equilibrium contraction via partial contraction theory, and global contractivity in some metric via a converse contraction theorem. To capture arbitrary contraction metrics, the method learns a diffeomorphism (composed of coupling layers with rational-quadratic splines) between the data space and an equal-dimensional latent space, enforcing the ELCD structure in latent space. Critically, the diffeomorphism and dynamics are trained jointly rather than sequentially, and the skew-symmetric component P_a allows representing non-gradient flows with asymmetric Jacobians—overcoming the expressiveness limitation of NCDS, which is restricted to negative gradient flows.

## Results

ELCD achieves the lowest mean Dynamic Time Warping Distance (DTWD) across all eight dataset configurations, ranging from 0.03±0.01 on 4D Pendulum to 2.57±0.09 on 16D Rosenbrock. On the 2D LASA dataset, ELCD achieves 0.12±0.11 DTWD, outperforming the next-best baseline (SDD at 0.37±0.32) by over 3×. On the high-dimensional 8D LASA dataset, ELCD reaches 1.52±0.61, compared to 5.26±0.50 for SDD and 5.04±0.77 for NCDS. SDD fails to converge (NaN) on both Rosenbrock datasets due to instability during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELCD outperforms all three baselines on every dataset configuration. Against NCDS [6], ELCD achieves substantially lower DTWD, with the largest gaps on Pendulum-8D (0.14 vs. 2.88) and LASA-8D (1.52 vs. 5.04), reflecting NCDS's limitation to contracting only on a lower-dimensional submanifold. Against EFlow [32], ELCD wins on all datasets, with the closest margin on Pendulum-16D (0.44 vs. 0.45) and the largest on LASA-2D (0.12 vs. 1.05). Against SDD [30], ELCD dominates across the board and is the only method that successfully trains on the stiff Rosenbrock dynamics, where SDD diverges (reported as NaN). The paper attributes ELCD's advantage on pendulum dynamics to the skew-symmetric component of its parametrization, which captures the oscillatory behavior arising from complex eigenvalues in the Jacobian.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELCD builds directly on Neural Contractive Dynamical Systems (NCDS, Beik-Mohammadi et al., ICLR 2024), the first neural network model with explicit contractivity enforcement, but departs from it in three key ways: it parametrizes the vector field via extended linearization rather than integrating a symmetric Jacobian, it uses a same-dimensional latent space with diffeomorphisms (coupling layers) to achieve global contractivity in arbitrary metrics rather than contractivity only on a decoder submanifold in the identity metric, and it allows asymmetric Jacobians through a skew-symmetric component. The theoretical machinery draws on partial contraction theory (Wang & Slotine) and a converse contraction theorem (Giesl, Hafstein, Mehrabinezhad) to show that negative definiteness of the symmetric part of the extended linearization matrix alone suffices for global contractivity. The broader context includes stable-but-not-contracting dynamics learners (SDD, EFlow, SEDS) that provide Lyapunov-based guarantees but lack the robustness properties of contraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first global contractivity guarantee for a neural network-based dynamical system is practically significant for robotics and imitation learning, where robustness to perturbations away from demonstration trajectories is essential and local guarantees are insufficient. The paper demonstrates consistent superiority over NCDS, SDD, and EFlow across LASA, multi-link pendulum, and Rosenbrock datasets, with particularly large gains on oscillatory (pendulum) and stiff (Rosenbrock) dynamics where the skew-symmetric component and diffeomorphism flexibility matter most. Key limitations remain: the method assumes the underlying dynamics are contracting, is restricted to R^n (not general manifolds), and currently handles only single-equilibrium trajectories. Open questions include extending to richer dynamical behaviors (limit cycles, chaotic attractors) via weaker contraction notions, handling multiple time scales more effectively, and scaling to higher-dimensional real-world robotic tasks.
