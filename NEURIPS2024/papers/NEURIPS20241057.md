## Problem

Answering Most Probable Explanation (MPE) queries in large probabilistic models is NP-hard, and existing approximate solvers are either too slow (exact methods) or lack accuracy (heuristic search). Prior neural-network-based approaches (Arya et al., 2024) required a fixed evidence/query partition known before training and were restricted to probabilistic circuits, leaving the more general 'any-MPE' task—where the evidence set is arbitrary at query time—unaddressed across the broader class of probabilistic models including Bayesian networks, Markov networks, and neural autoregressive models.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 2n-dimensional binary input vector encoding an arbitrary MPE query: for each of the n variables, two input nodes indicate whether the variable is evidence (one node set to 1, the other to 0) or query (both set to 0), making the mapping from queries to inputs injective. The network produces n sigmoid outputs, one per variable; outputs corresponding to evidence variables are ignored, and query-variable outputs are thresholded at 0.5 to recover the binary MPE assignment. This fixed-size encoding allows a single trained network to answer any arbitrary evidence/query partition over the model.

## Method

The approach has three key components. First, a tractable self-supervised loss ℓ^c(q^c, e) + ℓ_E(q^c, α) extends the discrete negative log-likelihood to continuous sigmoid outputs by substituting binary variables with their continuous counterparts in the model's multilinear polynomial (for PCs/PGMs) or neural network (for NAMs), augmented with an entropy penalty that encourages binary solutions and tightens the lower bound on the discrete optimum. Second, ITSELF performs inference-time optimization: at test time, gradient descent on this self-supervised loss iteratively updates the network parameters for a given query, yielding anytime-improving MPE solutions without requiring labeled data or an external solver. Third, GUIDE is a two-phase teacher-student training procedure in which the teacher network overfits a mini-batch via ITSELF to produce near-optimal pseudo-labels, and the student network learns from these labels via binary cross-entropy; the student then serves as a strong initialization for ITSELF at test time, drastically reducing the number of gradient iterations needed for convergence.

## Results

Across 20 TPM datasets and 6 query ratios (120 test configurations) for PCs, GUIDE + ITSELF achieves the highest log-likelihood in 108/120 cases vs. MAX, 115/120 vs. ML, 113/120 vs. Seq, and 107/120 vs. SSMP. For NAMs (80 test sets across 4 query ratios), GUIDE + ITSELF outperforms the hill-climbing baseline in the majority of cases, with >50% LL improvement on larger datasets. For PGMs (16 test sets across 4 high-treewidth grid networks), GUIDE + ITSELF consistently outperforms AOBB (60-second anytime solver). In the gap analysis against exact solutions on small PGMs (Table 2), GUIDE + ITSELF achieves gaps as low as 0.000–0.007 relative to AOBB, compared to 0.02–0.08 for SSMP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUIDE + ITSELF dominates all polynomial-time PC baselines: it beats MAX (Poon & Domingos 2011) in 108/120 cases, ML (Park & Darwiche 2004) in 115/120, and Seq in 113/120. Against the prior neural baseline SSMP (Arya et al. 2024), GUIDE + ITSELF wins in 107/120 cases, while even SSMP + ITSELF (adding inference-time optimization to the older training) beats plain SSMP in 104/120. For NAMs, GUIDE + ITSELF surpasses the 60-second hill-climbing (HC) baseline in most of 80 test configurations, with the heatmap showing >50% LL improvement on larger datasets (e.g., Ad, BBC, 20 NewsGroup). For PGMs, both GUIDE and SSMP outperform the AOBB anytime solver (Otten 2012, 60-second limit) in at least 50% of 16 test sets, with GUIDE + ITSELF winning in the majority. The gap analysis on small PGMs confirms GUIDE + ITSELF closes the gap to exact solutions most tightly (e.g., 0.007 vs. 0.024 for SSMP on Grids-17 at qr=0.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Arya et al. (AAAI 2024), which trained neural networks to solve MPE in Probabilistic Circuits under a fixed evidence/query partition. The key conceptual departure is the 'any-MPE' formulation, where the partition is arbitrary and unknown at training time, requiring a new input encoding (2n nodes with a three-state scheme) and a single network to handle all possible evidence subsets. The paper also generalizes the target model class from PCs alone to BNs, MNs, and NAMs (MADE/NADE), and introduces two novel training/inference mechanisms—ITSELF (inference-time gradient updates with a self-supervised loss) and GUIDE (a teacher-student distillation loop)—that go beyond the single-pass or fixed-loss training of prior work. The self-supervised loss with entropy penalty builds on Arya et al.'s formulation but is made tractable across all four PM families via continuous relaxation of the log-likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPE is NP-hard and exact solvers (AOBB, branch-and-bound) become intractable at scale, while existing polynomial-time approximations (MAX, ML, Seq) sacrifice accuracy. This paper shows that a modest feedforward network trained with GUIDE and refined with ITSELF can consistently match or exceed these baselines across 20+ datasets and four PM families, offering a practical, model-agnostic inference path. Limitations include restriction to binary variables (extensions to multi-valued/continuous are discussed but not fully validated), dependence on polynomial-time likelihood evaluation in the PM, and the non-convexity of the self-supervised loss that necessitates the teacher-student workaround. Open questions include scalability to very large models, handling constrained MPE, and whether the approach transfers to settings where the PM itself is learned jointly with the inference network.
