## Problem

Video point tracking models require brute-force correspondence matching across every spatial location in every frame, making full-resolution processing computationally infeasible. Existing models down-sample frame resolutions to meet computation budgets, which truncates high-frequency visual details and causes tracking failures on intricate object parts. The paper addresses this by pruning the search space to only the regions occupied by the queried object instance, allowing models to operate at full resolution without increasing computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (resized so the shorter side is 1080 pixels) and a single query point in the initial frame, and produces per-frame 2D trajectory coordinates, per-frame visibility probabilities, and instance segmentation masks. Internally, it samples 32 semantic neighbor points from the SAM-predicted instance mask and tracks them jointly; the pruning window sizes (e.g., 960×960 and 384×384 for TAPIR) are chosen to match the tracking model's native input resolution so that internal feature resolutions and FLOP counts remain unchanged. The framework is a plug-in that wraps any existing tracker without retraining.

## Method

TrackIME estimates an instance motion trajectory by prompting SAM with the query point to obtain an instance mask, sampling 32 points on the mask (weighted by distance transform to favor contour regions), tracking all of them, and aggregating their per-frame velocities weighted by visibility confidence. To handle occlusions, it re-samples invisible points from a mixture of segmentation distributions built by aggregating masks queried at the remaining visible points. The aggregated trajectory then guides search-space pruning: each frame is cropped to a window centered on the trajectory at a size matching the tracker's native resolution, so the model processes only the relevant region at full detail. A progressive inference stage runs two TrackerHD passes with different pruning sizes (960×960 then 384×384), where the first pass's trajectory refines the second pass's crop, and the final trajectory is a visibility-weighted average of both. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic and has been validated as a drop-in improvement for TAPIR, CoTracker, TAPNet, PIPS2, and OmniMotion.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark (First Query), TrackIME achieves 65.3 AJ and 78.6 δ_avg^x, surpassing the strongest baseline CoTracker (60.8 AJ, 76.1 δ_avg^x) by 4.5 and 2.5 points respectively. Under the harsh 1-pixel threshold, TrackIME reaches 35.4 J_1 and 48.2 δ_1^x, a 25.1% relative improvement over CoTracker (28.3 J_1). When incorporated with TAPIR, TrackIME yields a 13.5% relative AJ improvement (57.5→65.3) on DAVIS, and consistent gains across all five baseline trackers on DAVIS, RGBStacking, and Kinetics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrackIME consistently outperforms all five point tracking baselines. Against TAPIR [6] (its primary backbone), TrackIME improves AJ from 57.5 to 65.3 (+13.5% relative) and δ_1^x from 34.3 to 48.2 on DAVIS-F. Against CoTracker [7], the strongest baseline, TrackIME gains 4.5 AJ points (60.8→64.5) and 7.1 J_1 points (28.3→35.4). Against TAPNet [11], PIPS2 [9], and OmniMotion [8], improvements range from 3.7 to 13.6 AJ points depending on the dataset. For zero-shot video segmentation (Table 4), TrackIME (79.6 J&F_m) narrowly beats SAM-PT [30] (78.8 J&F_m) on DAVIS-2017-val, and substantially outperforms all class-guided baselines including EntitySeg [29] (73.4), Propose-Reduce [27] (70.4), and PDB [23] (55.1). The universality study (Table 2) confirms gains across all trackers: TAPNet +5.3 AJ, PIPS2 +3.7 AJ, CoTracker +3.7 AJ, OmniMotion +1.5 AJ, and TAPIR +7.8 AJ on DAVIS.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrackIME positions itself as a plug-in enhancement for existing point tracking models (TAPIR, CoTracker, TAPNet, PIPS2, OmniMotion) rather than a new tracker architecture. Its core departure from prior work is replacing the standard down-sampling strategy (e.g., 1080p → 256×256) with instance-aware search space pruning, guided by an estimated instance trajectory obtained by aggregating trajectories of multiple semantic neighbors sampled from a SAM segmentation mask. The progressive inference structure, which cascades TrackerHD models at different pruning scales and aggregates their outputs, draws on multi-scale feature fusion ideas from detection literature but applies them to the point tracking setting. The joint tracking-segmentation framework with mutual synergy (tracking informs re-sampling for occlusion; segmentation provides masks for pruning) is a conceptual contribution that distinguishes it from SAM-PT, which uses tracking to improve segmentation rather than the reverse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 13.5% AJ improvement across all five baselines demonstrates that the down-sampling bottleneck is a genuine and addressable limitation in current point tracking, and the plug-in nature makes the technique broadly adoptable without retraining. However, the method increases FLOPs by ~4.5× over vanilla TAPIR (2789G vs 612G), and the occlusion accuracy (OA) shows only marginal gains or slight trade-offs, reflecting an inherent tension between focusing on one instance and modeling inter-object occlusions. The paper acknowledges that TrackIME relies on pre-trained trackers (often trained on synthetic data) and SAM (trained on real images), leaving open the question of whether end-to-end training on real video scenes could further close the gap. A key open direction is whether the pruning-and-aggregation paradigm can be integrated into the training loop rather than applied purely at inference time.
