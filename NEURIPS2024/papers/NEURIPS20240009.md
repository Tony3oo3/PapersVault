## Problem

Prior to this work, no polynomial-time algorithm was known to achieve error o(√ε) for robust sparse regression with non-isotropic (unknown covariance) designs, even in the special case of Gaussian designs and Gaussian noise. The paper addresses the simultaneous presence of two adversaries—an oblivious adversary choosing the noise and an adaptive adversary corrupting an ε-fraction of samples—in the high-dimensional sparse regime where n < d².

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the ε-corrupted design matrix X ∈ ℝ^{n×d}, response vector y ∈ ℝ^n, sparsity parameter k, corruption fraction ε, and noise scale σ, and outputs a coefficient estimate β̂ ∈ ℝ^d. The error is measured in the anisotropic norm ‖Σ^{1/2}(β̂−β*)‖, which accounts for the unknown covariance structure. The noise assumption is deliberately weak: only a constant fraction (≥0.01) of noise entries need to be bounded by σ, accommodating heavy-tailed distributions like Cauchy noise.

## Method

The estimator combines three components: (1) a truncation step for heavy-tailed designs that clips entries of X at a threshold τ chosen so that only an ε-fraction of samples are affected, treating those as additional adversarial outliers; (2) a covariate filtering algorithm that uses sum-of-squares relaxations with elastic ball constraints (rather than standard sparsity constraints) to identify and down-weight corrupted samples, ensuring the filtered set satisfies bounded higher-moment conditions; and (3) weighted ℓ₁-penalized Huber loss minimization on the filtered samples. The key technical insight is that elastic ball constraints in the SoS relaxation guarantee containment of all k-sparse vectors, enabling Hölder's inequality with 2t-th moments to yield ε^{1−1/(2t)} error (e.g., ε^{3/4} for t=2) instead of the √ε barrier from Cauchy-Schwarz. For Gaussian designs with certifiably bounded fourth moments, this achieves O(ε^{3/4}) error with Õ(k⁴/ε³) samples, and for log-concave distributions the error becomes o(√ε) when ε ≤ 1/polylog(d).

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees for robust sparse regression with non-isotropic designs under two adversaries (oblivious noise + adaptive corruption). Theorem 1.4 achieves error O(σ√ε) with n ≳ k²log(d)/ε samples, requiring only M-bounded 3rd moment and entrywise ν-bounded 4th moment of the design. Theorem 1.7 achieves the stronger error O(M·σ·ε^{3/4}) with n ≥ M⁴·k⁴log(d)/ε³ samples under ℓ-certifiably bounded 4th moment and entrywise ν-bounded 8th moment; for Gaussian designs this gives O(ε^{3/4}) error, and for all log-concave distributions the error is O(σ√(log d)·ε^{3/4}), which is o(√ε) when ε ≤ o(1/log²(d)). SQ lower bounds show that k² samples are likely necessary for o(1) error and k⁴ samples for o(√ε) error, suggesting near-optimal sample complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with [Sas22], which also achieves O(σ√ε) error but with sample complexity that depends polynomially on ‖β*‖₁ (specifically Õ(k² + ‖β*‖₁⁴/k²)), making it unsuitable in the sparse regime when ‖β*‖ is large. This paper removes that dependence entirely, requiring only Õ(k²/ε) samples, and weakens the moment assumptions from bounded 4th + entrywise 8th to M-bounded 3rd + entrywise 4th moment. Compared to [SF23], which achieves O(√ε) with Õ(k²) samples for sub-Gaussian designs, this paper handles heavy-tailed designs and provides a stronger error bound of O(ε^{3/4}) (beyond √ε) for Gaussian and log-concave designs. Most significantly, prior to this work no polynomial-time algorithm was known to achieve o(√ε) error with unknown Σ even in the Gaussian design + Gaussian noise case; Theorem 1.7 is the first such result, achieving O(ε^{3/4}) for Gaussian designs and o(√ε) for all log-concave distributions when ε ≤ 1/polylog(d). The SQ lower bounds (Propositions 1.10–1.11) indicate that the k⁴ sample complexity for o(√ε) error is likely information-theoretically necessary for polynomial-time algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the covariate-filtering-plus-Huber-loss paradigm introduced by Pensia et al. (2020) for dense robust regression and adapted to sparse settings by Sasai (2022) and Sasai & Fujisawa (2023), but departs from them in two key ways: it removes the polynomial dependence on ‖β*‖ from the sample complexity via a novel truncation strategy that absorbs thresholded samples into the adversarial corruption budget, and it replaces sparse-constraint sum-of-squares relaxations (used in Diakonikolas et al., 2022 for sparse mean estimation) with elastic-ball constraints that are provably valid for the broader elastic ball geometry needed in regression. The oblivious-adversary analysis builds directly on d'Orsi et al. (2021), and the SQ lower bounds follow the reduction template of Diakonikolas, Kane & Stewart (2017, 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time algorithm to break the √ε error barrier for non-isotropic robust sparse regression, even in the Gaussian case, closing a gap that persisted despite extensive prior work. The SQ lower bounds (k² samples for o(1) error, k⁴ for o(√ε)) provide strong evidence of near-optimality, sharpening the information-computation gap picture. A key open problem identified is whether the nearly-optimal O(ε) error achievable in sparse mean estimation (Diakonikolas et al., 2022) can be transferred to regression; the authors show that naive elastic-ball relaxations fail for this goal, suggesting a genuine algorithmic barrier. The dependence on the KLS conjecture for log-concave distributions means the full O(ε^{3/4}) guarantee for all log-concave designs remains conditional on a major open problem in convex geometry.
