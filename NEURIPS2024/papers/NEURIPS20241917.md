## Problem

Training AI agents to coordinate zero-shot with novel human partners is hindered by a dual data challenge: human cooperation data is scarce and expensive, while synthetic self-play populations fail to cover the diverse strategies and styles real humans employ. Existing approaches that train against fixed populations of simulated agents or behavior-cloned human proxies each suffer from insufficient coverage of the human strategy space, leading to poor generalization at test time.

## Input / Output

The generative model consumes joint coordination trajectories (sequences of state, agent action, and partner action pairs, truncated to length 100 for optimization) and produces a 16-dimensional latent variable z encoding the partner's style, skill, or strategy. The decoder then takes z plus the Cooperator's own past experience (a GRU-processed history of states and actions) to predict the partner's next action. For human-adaptive sampling, a small set of human trajectories (20–50) is encoded to estimate a shifted latent Gaussian mean, which replaces the standard prior during Cooperator training.

## Method

GAMMA trains a trajectory-level VAE on coordination data (simulated or human) where the encoder maps a full trajectory to a latent z and the decoder predicts partner actions conditioned on z and the agent's own history, optimized via an ELBO objective with linearly scheduled KL penalty. The Cooperator is then trained with PPO against partners sampled from the generative model's latent space, treating each sampled z as a distinct MDP environment. The key differentiator is Human-Adaptive (HA) sampling: a small human dataset is encoded to estimate a human-centered latent Gaussian p_h(z), and the Cooperator is trained by sampling z from p_h rather than the standard prior, optionally combined with decoder-only or full fine-tuning of the VAE on human data. This allows the model to leverage large-scale synthetic data for robustness while steering generation toward the region of latent space relevant to real human behavior.

## Results

In a user study with 80 real human players, GAMMA HA (combining simulated population data with human-adaptive sampling) achieves the highest cooperative scores: 91.11±2.96 on Counter Circuit and 93.09±3.19 on Multi-strategy Counter. This represents a 60% improvement over the strongest simulated-population baseline (MEP: 76.19) on Counter Circuit and a 43% improvement over PPO+BC (85.26) on Multi-strategy Counter. Even without human data, MEP+GAMMA reaches 88.30 on Multi-strategy Counter, a 38% improvement over MEP alone (64.02). All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05) except GAMMA HA vs. PPO+BC on Multi-strategy Counter (p=0.426).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAMMA HA decisively outperforms all four baselines on both layouts. Against FCP [29], GAMMA HA improves scores from 26.24 to 91.11 on Counter Circuit (p=4.21×10⁻¹¹³) and from 34.35 to 93.09 on Multi-strategy Counter (p=1.10×10⁻⁶³). Against CoMeDi [24], the gains are 56.87→91.11 and 27.11→93.09, with CoMeDi performing particularly poorly on Multi-strategy Counter because its population fails to discover tomato-soup strategies. Against MEP [40], the strongest simulated-population baseline, GAMMA HA improves from 76.19 to 91.11 (Counter Circuit, p=1.43×10⁻⁴) and from 64.02 to 93.09 (Multi-strategy Counter, p=2.92×10⁻¹³). Against PPO+BC [1], the human-data baseline, GAMMA HA wins on Counter Circuit (53.51→91.11, p=3.14×10⁻³²) but the advantage on Multi-strategy Counter (85.26→93.09) is not statistically significant (p=0.426), suggesting PPO+BC is already competitive on that layout due to the limited human data being well-matched to the simpler strategy space.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAMMA unifies two previously separate lines of work on zero-shot coordination: training against diverse simulated populations (FCP, CoMeDi, MEP) and training with human cooperation data (PPO-BC). Rather than treating these as competing data sources, the paper repurposes a VAE as a continuous generative model of partner strategies, enabling interpolation and composition of behaviors beyond what any discrete population or small human dataset can provide. The human-adaptive sampling technique is a novel contribution that steers the latent prior toward human-relevant regions using only a handful of human trajectories, bridging the gap between the two data regimes without requiring large-scale human data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that human-data-trained methods (PPO-BC) outperform simulated-population methods on complex layouts directly contradicts prior published results (Strouse et al., 2021), suggesting that the sufficiency of synthetic data may depend on task complexity in ways not previously appreciated. The generative-model framing offers a principled path to combining both data sources, but the approach is sensitive to training data quality (CoMeDi+GAMMA fails on Multi-strategy Counter because the underlying population lacks tomato strategies). Open questions include scaling beyond two players, conditioning the Cooperator policy on the inferred partner latent z for online adaptation, and whether the benefits persist with larger, more diverse human datasets.
