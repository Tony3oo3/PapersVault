## Problem

Most semi-supervised learning methods rely on heuristics or predefined rules for generating pseudo-labels and are confined to standard differentiable loss functions and regularization terms. This paper asks whether reinforcement learning, with its non-differentiable reward signals and adaptive exploration, can be formulated to guide the pseudo-labeling process in SSL and improve generalization beyond what conventional SSL losses achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244352/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 96×96 for STL-10) partitioned into a small labeled subset and a large unlabeled subset, and outputs a C-dimensional class probability vector per image. Unusually, the prediction network doubles as a policy function in a one-armed bandit formulation: its output on unlabeled data constitutes the 'action' (soft pseudo-labels), and a mixup-based reward computed over interpolated labeled–unlabeled pairs serves as the non-differentiable feedback signal that drives policy gradient updates.

## Method

RLGSSL formulates SSL as a one-armed bandit problem where the state is the full training set, the action is the teacher model's soft pseudo-labels on unlabeled data, and the reward is the negative MSE between the student's predictions on inter-mixup samples (linearly interpolating labeled and pseudo-labeled points) and the corresponding mixup labels. The RL loss is a KL-divergence-weighted negative reward that treats the reward as a non-differentiable constant (via stop-gradient), so the gradient flows only through the policy output, encouraging discriminative predictions while maximizing reward. A teacher-student framework with EMA updates stabilizes action generation, and the total objective combines the RL loss with a supervised cross-entropy loss on labeled data and a KL consistency loss between student and teacher on unlabeled data, weighted by hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂. The model is pre-trained for 50 epochs with Mean Teacher before switching to the full RLGSSL training loop for 400 epochs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with CNN-13, RLGSSL achieves test errors of 9.15%, 6.90%, and 6.11% with 1,000, 2,000, and 4,000 labeled samples respectively, outperforming all 12 compared baselines. On CIFAR-100, RLGSSL reaches 36.92% and 29.12% test error with 4,000 and 10,000 labels, beating the second-best method ICT by 3.15% and 3.12% margins. With WRN-28-2/WRN-28-8 backbones, RLGSSL achieves 3.52% error on CIFAR-10/4000 and 20.15% on CIFAR-100/10000, surpassing MarginMatch and Meta Pseudo-Labels. On STL-10/1000 with WRN-37-2, RLGSSL attains 6.12% test error, outperforming UDA (7.66%) and MixMatch (10.41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLGSSL outperforms all 15 compared baselines across nearly all settings. On CIFAR-10 with CNN-13, it beats the strongest competitor ICT by 1.79% (2,000 labels) and 1.07% (4,000 labels), and surpasses Learning to Reweight by 2.59% at 1,000 labels. On CIFAR-100, the margin over ICT is 3.15% (4,000 labels) and 3.12% (10,000 labels). With WRN-28-2/WRN-28-8 backbones, RLGSSL edges out MarginMatch on CIFAR-100 (23.18% vs. 23.71% at 2,500 labels; 20.15% vs. 21.39% at 10,000 labels) and SVHN (1.92% vs. 1.93%), and beats Meta Pseudo-Labels on CIFAR-10/4000 (3.52% vs. 3.89%). The sole exception is CIFAR-10/250 with WRN-28-2, where MarginMatch (4.73%) slightly outperforms RLGSSL (5.01%), though RLGSSL still beats FixMatch (5.07%) and ReMixMatch (5.44%) in that extreme low-label regime. On STL-10/1000, RLGSSL improves over UDA by 1.54% and over MixMatch by 4.29%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLGSSL extends the Mean Teacher framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) by repurposing the teacher-student EMA mechanism to stabilize pseudo-label generation within a reinforcement learning loop, and borrows the mixup data interpolation strategy (Zhang et al., 2018) to construct its reward signal. Conceptually, it departs from the standard SSL paradigm—where pseudo-labels are optimized via differentiable losses (cross-entropy, KL-divergence)—by formulating the prediction model as a policy in a one-armed bandit problem, drawing an analogy to RLHF's single-step reward optimization for LLMs. This positions the work at the intersection of RL-based fine-tuning and semi-supervised pseudo-labeling, treating the non-differentiable reward as a guided signal rather than a direct gradient target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a non-differentiable, reward-based optimization signal can effectively guide pseudo-label generation in SSL, offering an alternative to the standard differentiable loss landscape. However, the approach is validated only on small-scale image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, STL-10) with modest architectures, and the authors acknowledge the same-distribution assumption between labeled and unlabeled data as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the one-armed bandit formulation scales to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), whether the RL reward provides advantages beyond what well-tuned consistency or pseudo-labeling losses already capture, and how the method behaves under distribution shift between labeled and unlabeled data.
