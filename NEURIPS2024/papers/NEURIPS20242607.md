## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods either produce only small-scale object compositions without rich room semantics, or rely on panoramic representations that restrict layouts to single rooms with fixed camera viewpoints. None support user-specified free-form multi-room layouts with arbitrary camera trajectories while maintaining 3D geometric consistency and diverse textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242607/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a short style prompt (e.g., 'This is one view of a bedroom in Van Gogh painting style'), a bounding-box scene (BBS) where each object is a union of one or more axis-aligned or oriented bounding boxes with a semantic category label, and a free camera trajectory defined in BBS space. The BBS is rasterized into per-view 'bounding-box images' (BBI) at 512×768 resolution containing a one-hot semantic category map and a depth map. The output is a Nerfacto (NeRF) scene representation renderable from arbitrary viewpoints, with intermediate high-fidelity multi-view RGB images produced by the 2D diffusion model.

## Method

SceneCraft is a two-stage framework. First, SceneCraft2D is trained by finetuning Stable Diffusion with two ControlNets (one injecting the BBI semantic map, one the BBI depth map) on ScanNet++ and HyperSim using a generic base prompt to avoid overfitting to specific content. Second, the 3D scene is generated via an IN2N-style SDS-equivalent distillation loop where SceneCraft2D iteratively produces new views that replace a multi-view training set used to fit a Nerfacto representation. Key mechanisms include: (1) an annealing schedule using SDEdit that gradually reduces the similarity between generated images and the current scene, transitioning from free generation to refinement; (2) a layout-aware depth loss with a soft threshold δ that anchors early geometry to the BBS before being disabled; (3) periodical migration between a frozen coarse scene S_c and a fresh fine scene S_f to eliminate flocs and Janus artifacts; and (4) VGG perceptual and stylization losses for texture consolidation, eliminating the need for explicit mesh export.

## Results

SceneCraft achieves the best CLIP Score (24.34), 3D consistency (3.71/5), and visual quality (3.56/5) among all compared methods in the layout-guided scene generation task. The 3D metrics are from a user study with 32 participants rating on a 1–5 scale. SceneCraft's Inception Score (3.54) is lower than Text2Room (4.20) and MVDiffusion (4.36), which the authors attribute to the fixed category set used during finetuning slightly limiting generation diversity. The method is evaluated on HyperSim and ScanNet++ room layouts with free camera trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneCraft outperforms all three baselines on CLIP Score, 3D consistency, and visual quality. Against Text2Room (inpainting-based), SceneCraft gains +1.36 CS, +0.60 3DC, and +0.50 VQ, while Text2Room's iterative generation produces repetitive or contradictory frames (e.g., generating four beds in a bedroom). Against MVDiffusion (panorama-based), SceneCraft leads by +0.49 CS, +0.51 3DC, and +0.21 VQ; MVDiffusion's panorama constraint prevents it from modeling irregular room shapes like L- or S-shaped layouts. Against Set-the-scene (NeRF-composition), SceneCraft achieves +3.02 CS, +0.18 3DC, and +1.15 VQ; Set-the-scene struggles with objects of significantly different sizes and cannot generate wall-mounted items like blinds or televisions. SceneCraft's only metric loss is Inception Score versus Text2Room (−0.66) and MVDiffusion (−0.82), which the authors note reflects reduced diversity from category-constrained finetuning rather than a quality deficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneCraft extends the SDS-based distillation paradigm from DreamFusion and the IN2N-style pipeline to the layout-guided scene generation setting, replacing the text-only conditioning with a dual ControlNet architecture (semantic map + depth map) that encodes a user-specified 3D bounding-box scene rendered into 2D proxy images. It departs from concurrent panorama-based methods (ControlRoom3D, Ctrl-Room, MVDiffusion) by eliminating the panoramic representation constraint, enabling free multi-view camera trajectories and multi-room generation. The annealing strategy borrows from SDEdit to progressively tighten the gap between generated and rendered views, while the layout-aware depth constraint and periodical migration for floc removal are novel engineering contributions specific to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneCraft is the first framework to generate complex multi-room indoor scenes (e.g., three-story apartments with irregular layouts) under free camera trajectories, removing the single-room and panorama constraints that limited prior layout-guided methods. The bounding-box scene format offers a practical, Minecraft-like interface for non-expert users to specify spatial arrangements. Key limitations include struggles with extremely dense or overlapping object layouts, sensitivity to prompt-layout mismatches, and residual blurriness on objects with irregular geometry (hollowed chairs, blinds). Open questions include extending the framework to outdoor scenes, developing fair evaluation metrics for 3D scene generation, and automating layout and trajectory creation via LLMs or transformers.
