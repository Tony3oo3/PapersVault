## Problem

Semantic Scene Completion is inherently ill-posed: filling in unseen regions relies heavily on structural priors learned from training data, making pre-trained models vulnerable to the diverse scene structures encountered in real driving. While test-time adaptation (TTA) has been explored in other 3D perception tasks, it has been scarcely applied to SSC, and no prior work exploits the temporal structure of consecutive LiDAR observations as a source of self-supervision for online adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242360/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous sequence of LiDAR point clouds (voxelized at 256×256×32) along with inter-frame transformation matrices, operating online at each timestep. At moment i, it uses the transformed point cloud from a past moment j (and, via the dual scheme, a future moment k) to construct self-supervision signals. The output is a completed 3D voxel grid with per-voxel semantic class probabilities, produced by unifying predictions from two adapted models (moment-wise and gradual), where the gradual model's output is trusted only for static categories.

## Method

TALoS exploits the LiDAR line-of-sight property: an observed point at a location confirms both occupancy at that voxel and emptiness along the ray from the sensor to that point, identified via Bresenham's algorithm. This yields a binary completion map (occupied/empty/ignore) used as self-supervision with cross-entropy and Lovász-softmax losses. For semantic adaptation, the method thresholds Shannon-entropy-based reliability to extract confident pseudo-labels, then aggregates them across moments to build a consensus pseudo-GT. A dual optimization scheme employs two models: a moment-wise model F^M (reinitialized each step, updated instantly with past observations) and a gradual model F^G (continuously updated, but its update at moment i is delayed until future observation k becomes available), enabling exploitation of future information without violating causality. Only the last few layers of the pre-trained SCPNet are updated, with a 3D convolution layer added to expand the sparse tensor computation region so distant voxels participate in adaptation.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI test set, TALoS achieves 37.9% mIoU and 60.2% cIoU, improving over the SCPNet baseline (36.7% mIoU, 56.1% cIoU) by +1.2 and +4.1 points respectively. On the validation set, the full TALoS framework (dual optimization with both completion and semantic losses) reaches 39.29% mIoU and 56.09% cIoU, compared to 37.56% and 50.24% for the unmodified baseline. Cross-dataset evaluation on SemanticPOSS shows TALoS gains over 10 points in cIoU over the baseline under severe domain shift. The method requires only 3 optimization iterations per frame and adds ~194% computational overhead (6.65s vs 2.26s per step) relative to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TALoS outperforms all compared SSC architectures on the SemanticKITTI test set: it beats SCPNet (the strongest baseline) by +1.2 mIoU and +4.1 cIoU, and surpasses SSA-SC, JS3C-Net, and S3CNet by 8–14 mIoU points. Against existing TTA methods on the validation set, TALoS (39.29% mIoU, 56.09% cIoU) clearly dominates TENT (37.92%, 49.86%) and CoTTA (36.55%, 50.61%), with TENT actually degrading cIoU below the baseline. The paper also validates generality across architectures: on KITTI-360 with SSCNet, TALoS gains +0.4 mIoU over baseline while TENT shows no improvement, and on SemanticKITTI with SSA-SC, TALoS gains +0.8 mIoU versus +0.2 for TENT. Notably, SCPNet already uses multi-scan distillation during training, yet TALoS's online TTA still yields a larger cIoU gain (+4.1), suggesting that test-time adaptation is more effective than training-time distillation for leveraging temporal information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Test-time Adaptation (TTA) to the Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) field, a task previously addressed almost exclusively through architectural innovations (SSA-SC, JS3C-Net, SCPNet, etc.). While TTA has been explored for image classification (TENT, CoTTA) and point cloud registration (MATE, Point-TTA), its application to SSC is novel here. The method departs from prior TTA approaches that rely on entropy minimization or pseudo-labeling by exploiting the temporal structure of driving sequences: LiDAR observations at one moment provide geometric (Line-of-Sight emptiness) and semantic (confident predictions) self-supervision for another moment. The dual optimization scheme, which delays model updates until future observations become available, is a new mechanism not seen in prior TTA literature, and contrasts with SCPNet's training-time distillation from multi-scan models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TALoS opens a practical direction for SSC in autonomous driving where the model must adapt online to unseen environments without retraining, and the LoS-based self-supervision is a natural exploitation of LiDAR physics that prior SSC methods ignored. However, the approach is limited to point-cloud inputs, requires sequential observations (not applicable to single-frame settings), and incurs a ~194% computational overhead per step. The cross-dataset results (SemanticKITTI→SemanticPOSS) show that under severe domain gaps, the absolute performance remains low, suggesting TTA alone cannot substitute for domain-specific pre-training. Extending the philosophy to image-based SSC and combining it with domain adaptation techniques are natural next steps.
