## Problem

Generative restoration models produce increasingly realistic outputs but simultaneously generate more hallucinations—details absent from the ground truth—yet the relationship between perceptual quality and prediction uncertainty remained theoretically uncharacterized. This paper asks: what is the fundamental, distribution-agnostic lower bound on uncertainty as a function of perceptual quality, and how does it relate to the well-known distortion-perception tradeoff?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For super-resolution, the input is a 4× bicubic-downsampled low-resolution image from BSD100; for inpainting, it is a partially masked image from the SeeTrue dataset. The method produces restored images alongside a quantitative uncertainty-perception plane, where uncertainty is measured via the entropy power of the conditional recovery error (practically approximated by the geometric mean of singular values of the error covariance) and perception is measured via conditional Rényi divergence (order 1/2) between the true and recovered image distributions, complemented by learned metrics (HyperIQA, LIQE, Q-ALIGN) and distortion metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM).

## Method

The paper formulates restoration as a Bayesian inverse problem X→Y→X̂ and defines the inherent uncertainty U_Inherent = N(X|Y) (entropy power of the conditional distribution) as a fundamental lower bound on any estimator's uncertainty. It then defines the uncertainty-perception (UP) function U(P) as the minimum conditional entropy power of the recovery error subject to a conditional Rényi divergence constraint, and proves (Theorem 3) that U(P) is universally bounded by η(P)·N(X|Y) ≤ U(P) ≤ η(P)·N(X_G|Y), where η(P) is an explicit convex function ranging from 1 to 2, meaning perfect perception requires at least twice the inherent uncertainty. Theorem 4 further shows that the UP tradeoff induces the classical distortion-perception tradeoff by proving that per-dimension MSE lower-bounds the conditional entropy power of the error. Empirically, the framework is validated by plotting diverse SR and inpainting algorithms (EDSR, ESRGAN, SinGAN, SANGAN, DIP, SRGAN, EnhanceNet, LDMs) on the uncertainty-perception and uncertainty-distortion planes, confirming the predicted anti-correlation and the existence of an impossible region where no estimator can simultaneously achieve low uncertainty and high perception.

## Results

The paper's experiments confirm the theoretical uncertainty-perception tradeoff: across all evaluated super-resolution and inpainting algorithms, no model simultaneously achieves both low uncertainty and high perceptual quality, with an unattainable blank region in the lower-right of the uncertainty-perception plane. An anti-correlation emerges near this region, where modest improvements in perceptual quality (measured via HyperIQA, LIQE, Q-ALIGN) translate to dramatic increases in uncertainty (measured via entropy power upper bound). The uncertainty-distortion plane further shows a strong positive relationship: any increase in uncertainty leads to significant rises in MSE, PSNR, and SSIM distortion. These trends hold consistently across BSD100 (4× bicubic super-resolution) and SeeTrue (inpainting), and are reinforced by direct high-dimensional entropy estimation using the Kozachenko-Leonenko estimator on 9×9 patches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new restoration method but instead evaluates eight existing algorithms to validate its theoretical predictions. On the uncertainty-perception plane (Figures 5, 6, 8), deterministic methods like EDSR [50] and SRResNet [47] occupy the low-perception, low-uncertainty region, while generative models like ESRGAN [81], SinGAN [71], SANGAN [39], and EnhanceNet [66] shift toward higher perception at the cost of substantially higher uncertainty. Latent Diffusion Models [65] with varying β (interpolating between DDIM [32] at β=0 and DDPM [73] at β=1) trace a continuous trajectory across the plane, with β=1 (DDPM) achieving the highest perception but also the highest uncertainty and distortion. DIP [77] falls in an intermediate region. The key finding is that no algorithm dominates: moving from EDSR to ESRGAN to LDM(β=1) consistently increases both perceptual quality and uncertainty/distortion, confirming the theoretical bound that perfect perception requires at least twice the inherent uncertainty U_Inherent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the perception-distortion tradeoff framework of Blau and Michaeli (2018) and its extensions (Freirich et al., 2021; Ohayon et al., 2023), but introduces a fundamentally different axis: uncertainty (measured via entropy power of the recovery error) rather than distortion (MSE). The key conceptual departure is showing that the well-known distortion-perception tradeoff is not a primitive phenomenon but rather a *consequence* of a deeper uncertainty-perception tradeoff (Theorem 4). The mathematical toolkit draws on classical information theory—entropy power inequalities, Rényi divergence, and the maximum entropy principle—applied in a novel way to the Bayesian restoration setting, while the empirical validation leverages the same generative restoration algorithms (ESRGAN, SinGAN, LDMs, etc.) studied in prior perception-distortion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental, distribution-agnostic lower bound: no generative restoration model can simultaneously achieve perfect perceptual quality and low uncertainty, with the minimal uncertainty at perfect perception being at least 2× the inherent uncertainty of the problem. The uncertainty-perception plane offers a practical diagnostic tool for practitioners to evaluate where their model sits relative to this fundamental limit, potentially guiding decisions in safety-critical applications (healthcare, autonomous systems). Key limitations include the difficulty of estimating high-dimensional entropy in practice (the paper uses a tractable upper bound), the empirical validation being limited to super-resolution and inpainting, and the open question of whether the specific form of η(P) generalizes cleanly to all divergence measures. Future work could incorporate the uncertainty bound as a training objective to explicitly control the tradeoff.
