## Problem

Existing pure-exploration methods for multi-objective bandits either identify arms only approximately close to the Pareto front, rely on gap-based lower bounds valid for narrow parameter ranges or fixed preference cones, or lack a KL-divergence-based characterization of hardness. This paper asks: what is the exact sample-complexity lower bound for identifying the complete Pareto Optimal Set under a general polyhedral preference cone, and can a computationally tractable algorithm match it?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240550/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives K arms, each producing an L-dimensional reward vector with unknown mean and known diagonal covariance, plus a polyhedral preference cone C specified by its half-space representation Ax ≥ 0 and a confidence parameter δ. The output is the exact Pareto Optimal Set of arms (equivalently, a policy in the simplex supported on those arms), identified with probability at least 1−δ. Unlike scalar BAI, the cone induces a partial order so multiple arms can be simultaneously optimal, and the geometry of C directly determines which arms are Pareto optimal for the same underlying means.

## Method

The paper derives a KL-divergence-based lower bound via a change-of-measure argument where the alternating set is defined through the preference cone and the most confusing instances provably lie on the boundary of the polar cone C°; for Gaussian rewards this reduces to a bilinear projection of the mean matrix onto preferences and policy gaps. Because the alternating set is piece-wise polyhedral (non-convex), the authors apply disjunctive programming (Balas, 1985) to obtain a convex relaxation that is tractable as a linear program. The PreTS algorithm is a Track-and-Stop procedure that tracks this convexified lower bound: it samples arms to match the optimal allocation, uses a new Chernoff-type stopping rule that accounts for preference-aligned suboptimality gaps, and returns the empirical Pareto front. An upper bound matching the convexified lower bound asymptotically is established via a novel distance metric between Pareto fronts and a concentration inequality for vector-valued sub-Gaussian rewards.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The main contributions are: (1) a novel KL-divergence-based lower bound on sample complexity for identifying the Pareto Optimal Set with confidence 1−δ (Theorem 3.1), showing E[τ] ≥ T_{M,C} · log(1/(2.4δ)); (2) a closed-form characterization for Gaussian bandits (Theorem 3.2) where the hardness depends on the bilinear projection (z^T M(π*−π))² / (2·Tr(Σ)·‖π*−π‖²₂); and (3) the PreTS algorithm whose expected stopping time asymptotically matches the convexified lower bound: lim_{δ→0} E[τ]/log(1/δ) ≤ T̄_{M,C} (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not conduct empirical comparisons against baselines; it is a theoretical contribution. However, it positions itself relative to existing theoretical bounds: the proposed lower bound generalizes the classical BAI lower bound of Kaufmann et al. (2016) (recovered when the cone is ℝ₊), the pure exploration bound under linear constraints of Carlsson et al. (2024) (recovered when L=1), and subsumes the gap-dependent bounds of Ararat and Tekin (2023) and Kone et al. (2023a) which hold only for narrow ranges of mean vectors or fixed preference cones. The PreTS algorithm is the first Track-and-Stop style algorithm for exact Pareto front identification under a general preference cone, whereas prior work (Auer et al. 2016; Kone et al. 2023a; crepon et al. 2024) either targets ε-approximate identification or assumes a fixed known cone. The convex relaxation via disjunctive programming (Balas, 1985) introduces a computational cost: for non-Gaussian rewards, the convexified characteristic time can be larger than the true lower bound, representing a statistical-computational trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Track-and-Stop paradigm of Garivier and Kaufmann (2016) from scalar-reward Best Arm Identification to the vector-valued, preference-cone-ordered setting, deriving a KL-divergence-based lower bound that generalizes both the classical BAI bound (Kaufmann et al., 2016) and the constrained pure exploration bound of Carlsson et al. (2024). It departs from prior Pareto front identification work (Auer et al., 2016; Ararat and Tekin, 2023; Kone et al., 2023a) in two key ways: (i) it targets exact identification of the Pareto set under a general polyhedral preference cone rather than ε-approximation or the fixed positive orthant, and (ii) it uses a Track-and-Stop algorithmic paradigm rather than gap-based elimination. The convex relaxation of the non-convex alternating set via disjunctive programming (Balas, 1985) is a novel technical bridge that makes the lower bound tractable for algorithm design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first asymptotically tight (up to convexification) sample complexity characterization for exact Pareto front identification under general preference cones, filling a gap between gap-based bounds that only hold for narrow parameter regimes and the Track-and-Stop paradigm that was previously unavailable for this problem. The geometric insight that hardness depends on a bilinear projection of the mean matrix onto the preference cone and policy gaps offers a new lens for understanding multi-objective bandit complexity. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the convexification introduces a potential optimality gap that is acknowledged but not quantified. Open questions include adapting other asymptotically optimal strategies (gamified explorers, top-two) to this setting, studying the computational-statistical trade-off of convexification, and validating results on real-world multi-objective data such as clinical trial endpoints.
