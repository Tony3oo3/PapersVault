## Problem

Existing 3D radiance representations for generative modeling face a fundamental trade-off: implicit NeRF-based methods require a shared feature decoder across all objects, degrading expressiveness, while fully explicit representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting are spatially unstructured, making them incompatible with standard 3D convolutional diffusion architectures. No prior representation simultaneously offers spatial structure, full explicitness, real-time rendering, and compact parameterization.

## Input / Output

During the representation construction stage, the pipeline consumes multi-view renderings (100–300 views per object) and produces a fixed-size 32×32×32×14 GaussianCube, where each voxel stores a Gaussian's position offset from the voxel center, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and RGB color. For generation, the 3D diffusion model takes isotropic Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label via AdaGN, a 512×512 portrait image encoded by DINO ViT-B/16 into 1025×768 tokens, or a text prompt encoded by CLIP-L/14 into 77×768 tokens) and outputs a denoised GaussianCube that can be rasterized into novel-view images at 512×512 resolution in real time.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage representation construction pipeline. First, a densification-constrained 3DGS fitting algorithm retains the original pruning step but caps the total Gaussian count at N_max=32,768 by selecting only the highest-gradient candidates for densification, yielding high-fidelity fitting with a fixed parameter budget (0.46M params vs. 67M for voxel grids at comparable quality). Second, the fitted Gaussians are assigned to a 32×32×32 voxel grid by solving a linear assignment (Optimal Transport) problem via the Jonker-Volgenant algorithm, minimizing total squared transport distance between Gaussian centers and voxel centers while preserving spatial correspondence. The resulting structured, explicit grid plugs directly into a standard 3D U-Net diffusion model (ADM architecture with 3D convolutions and full attention at 8³ and 4³ resolutions), trained with a simple x₀-prediction loss plus VGG-based image-level rendering supervision, eliminating the need for specialized network designs or shared implicit decoders.

## Results

On unconditional 3D generation, GaussianCube achieves FID-50K of 13.01 and KID-50K of 8.46% on ShapeNet Car, and 15.99 / 9.95% on ShapeNet Chair, substantially outperforming EG3D (30.48 / 20.42%), GET3D (17.15 / 9.58%), and DiffTF (51.88 / 41.10%). On the large-vocabulary OmniObject3D dataset, it reaches FID-50K of 11.62 and KID-50K of 2.78%, far surpassing DiffTF (46.06 / 22.86%). For image-conditioned avatar creation, GaussianCube attains FID-5K of 8.32 and identity CSIM of 0.7821, beating Rodin (20.02 / 0.6466). For text-to-3D, it achieves a CLIP score of 30.56 with 2.30 s inference, matching Shap-E (30.52) while being ~2× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianCube wins decisively on all generation benchmarks. Against EG3D (Triplane GAN) [8], it improves FID-50K from 30.48 to 13.01 on ShapeNet Car and from 27.98 to 15.99 on Chair, while also using 13.7× fewer parameters in the underlying representation. Against GET3D [19], the gains are more modest (FID 13.01 vs 17.15 on Car) but consistent across both ShapeNet categories. Against DiffTF [5], the advantage is overwhelming: FID drops from 51.88 to 13.01 on Car and from 46.06 to 11.62 on OmniObject3D, reflecting the benefit of a fully explicit structured representation over a shared implicit decoder. For avatar creation, GaussianCube outperforms Rodin [59] on every metric (FID 8.32 vs 20.02, CSIM 0.7821 vs 0.6466, PSNR 21.87 vs 18.59), even though Rodin employs a 2D super-resolution refiner. In text-to-3D, GaussianCube matches Shap-E [28] on CLIP score (30.56 vs 30.52) while being ~2× faster (2.30 s vs 4.42 s), and clearly beats DreamGaussian [53] (CLIP 30.56 vs 26.38, 2.30 s vs ~120 s) and VolumeDiffusion [57] (30.56 vs 24.41). Against LGM [54], GaussianCube edges out on CLIP (30.56 vs 30.06) at a small inference-time cost (2.30 s vs 1.55 s). In the fitting comparison (Table 2), GaussianCube slightly trails raw 3DGS [30] in PSNR (34.94 vs 35.32) but is the only method that is simultaneously spatially structured, fully explicit, and real-time, with 4× fewer parameters than 3DGS and 26.6× fewer than Instant-NGP [40].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [Kerbl et al., 2023] but addresses its two key limitations for generative modeling: the variable number of Gaussians and the lack of spatial structure. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based 3D generation line (EG3D, GET3D, DiffTF, Rodin) that relies on implicit feature decoders shared across objects, which the authors argue degrades representation power. The conceptual contribution is combining a densification-constrained fitting strategy (retaining pruning but capping densification) with Optimal Transport-based voxel grid organization, yielding a representation that is simultaneously structured, fully explicit, and compact—properties no prior 3D representation achieved together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–2 order-of-magnitude parameter reduction (0.46M vs. 67M for Voxels at comparable quality) directly reduces the modeling difficulty for 3D diffusion, which recent work has shown struggles with high-dimensional distributions. This makes GaussianCube a practical and versatile representation likely to be adopted across 3D generative tasks. Open questions include scaling the ~5-minute-per-object construction pipeline to very large datasets, and whether the fixed N³ grid structure generalizes well to highly irregular or open geometries beyond the tested object categories.
