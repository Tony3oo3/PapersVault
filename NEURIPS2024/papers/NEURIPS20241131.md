## Problem

No single post-hoc OOD detection method consistently outperforms all others across benchmarks, with particular difficulty in 'near OOD' settings where OOD samples closely resemble the training distribution. Existing confidence-based methods operate only on the C-dimensional logit space, while distance-based methods work in the penultimate layer without leveraging the class-discriminative structure learned by the final layer's weight matrix.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241131/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

WeiPer is a post-hoc method applied to any pretrained classifier (ResNet18, ResNet50, ViT-B/16) on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet). It takes the penultimate-layer feature vector z ∈ ℝ^K and the final layer weight matrix W_fc ∈ ℝ^{C×K}, then produces a scalar OOD score by projecting z onto r·C perturbed vectors, expanding the effective output dimension from C to r·C. The score is thresholded to yield a binary ID/OOD decision, with AUROC and FPR95 as evaluation metrics.

## Method

WeiPer constructs a cone of perturbed weight vectors around each class projection w_j by adding normalized Gaussian noise scaled by a factor δ, so that each perturbed vector sits at a fixed angle α ≈ arctan(δ) from the original. The penultimate activations are projected onto all r·C perturbed vectors, creating an augmented 'WeiPer space' that captures more of the penultimate layer's distributional structure than the C class projections alone, motivated by the Cramér-Wold theorem. Two scoring functions exploit this space: MSP_WeiPer averages the maximum softmax probability over all r perturbed logit vectors, and WeiPer+KLD treats the K penultimate activations and r·C WeiPer projections as samples from a single per-sample distribution, then measures the symmetric KL divergence between each sample's histogram (smoothed with a uniform kernel) and the training-set mean distribution. The KLD score combines both the penultimate-space and WeiPer-space divergences with a weighted MSP_WeiPer term, requiring only 6 hyperparameters tuned on validation sets.

## Results

WeiPer+KLD achieves the best AUROC on CIFAR-100 near (81.37) and ImageNet/ResNet50 near (80.05), outperforming the strongest competitor ASH by 1.88% AUROC on the most challenging near-OOD benchmark. Across all four near-OOD settings, WeiPer+KLD attains the highest mean relative score (S_rel = 0.988) in Table 3, establishing a new state of the art for post-hoc OOD detection on near datasets. On far-OOD benchmarks, WeiPer+KLD reaches 95.54 AUROC on ImageNet/ResNet50 (second only to ASH at 95.74) and 93.12 on CIFAR-10 (second to NAC at 94.60). WeiPer+MSP improves over vanilla MSP by 1–4.1% AUROC across all benchmarks, and WeiPer+ReAct consistently outperforms ReAct with an energy-based score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeiPer+KLD outperforms ASH (78.17) by 1.88% AUROC on ImageNet/ResNet50 near-OOD (80.05), the most challenging near benchmark, and beats GEN (81.31) and WeiPer+MSP (81.32) on CIFAR-100 near-OOD (81.37). On far-OOD, NAC dominates (94.60 on CIFAR-10, 86.98 on CIFAR-100, 95.29 on ImageNet/R50, 93.16 on ImageNet/ViT), with WeiPer+KLD placing second on CIFAR-10 (93.12) and ImageNet/R50 (95.54 vs. ASH's 95.74). WeiPer+MSP consistently improves over vanilla MSP by 1–4.1% AUROC across all eight benchmark settings, and WeiPer+ReAct outperforms ReAct+Energy on every benchmark. On ViT-B/16, WeiPer+KLD underperforms RMDS (80.09 vs. 75.00 near; 92.60 vs. 90.32 far), which the authors attribute to the narrow penultimate layer compressing class clusters. KNN remains competitive on CIFAR-10 near (90.64 vs. 90.54) but degrades sharply on ImageNet (71.10 near), where WeiPer+KLD leads by 8.95% AUROC.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeiPer builds directly on confidence-based OOD detection (MSP) and activation-shaping methods (ReAct), but introduces a structured perturbation of the final layer's class-projection weight vectors to create an augmented representation space. The theoretical grounding draws on the Cramér-Wold theorem, which guarantees that a sufficient set of one-dimensional projections can reconstruct a multivariate distribution, motivating why multiple perturbed projections around class directions should capture more of the penultimate-layer structure than the single logit vector alone. The paper departs from prior random-projection approaches (e.g., Kuan & Mueller, 2022) by showing that unstructured Gaussian projections fail to separate ID from OOD, while perturbations anchored to the learned class directions succeed—linking the method to the neural collapse literature (Papyan et al., 2020) that identifies class-specific directions as the informative subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WeiPer+KLD establishes a new state of the art on near-OOD benchmarks (the harder regime where OOD samples are close to the training distribution), outperforming the strongest competitor ASH by ~1.9% AUROC on ImageNet/ResNet50. The 'fingerprint' observation—that ID samples share a characteristic activation distribution across dimensions, which OOD samples violate—offers a new lens for understanding why density-based scores work in the perturbed space. Key limitations include degraded performance on ViT-B/16 (narrower penultimate layer), six tunable hyperparameters, and memory scaling with the number of perturbations r. An open question is whether the method benefits from higher-dimensional latent spaces, which would determine its applicability to modern transformer architectures.
