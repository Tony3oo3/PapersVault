## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods typically suppress low-confidence pixels but fail to identify which regions carry higher learning value or to design region-specific training strategies for them. This paper shows empirically that the reliable unstable region (where one network is confident and the other is not) and the unreliable stable region (where both networks are uncertain) are the most valuable learning regions, yet prior methods apply uniform or binary confidence-based losses that do not exploit the distinct characteristics of these regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical volumes (H×W×Z) from MRI (ACDC cardiac, Brats brain tumor) or CT (AbdomenCT-1K abdominal organs) in a semi-supervised setting with a small labeled set (5–20%) and a large unlabeled set. Each input volume is processed by two independently initialized CNNs (U-Net or V-Net), where each network's decoder receives both the original encoded feature and a perturbed version (random noise or dropout), yielding two prediction maps per network. The output is a per-voxel class probability map (3 classes for ACDC, 4 for AbdomenCT-1K, 3 for Brats) from which a segmentation mask is derived.

## Method

ALHVR builds on the CPS dual-branch architecture and introduces an adaptive confidence threshold (mean of the lowest β·N pixel confidences from both networks' perturbed predictions) to partition pixels into three regions: reliable stable (Ω₁), reliable unstable (Ω₂), and unreliable stable (Ω₃). For Ω₂, the CG-CPCL module constructs per-class prototypes from one network's original decoder features, computes cosine similarity between the other network's perturbed features and those cross-prototypes, and applies a confidence-guided mask so that only low-confidence pixels learn from the high-confidence counterpart's pseudo-labels, combined with a cosine similarity loss to tighten intra-class clustering. For Ω₃, the DTCT module performs pixel-wise confidence comparison between the two networks' unperturbed predictions to competitively select the more reliable prediction as a teacher, sharpens it via a temperature-based probability sharpening function, and supervises the perturbed predictions with MSE loss plus an entropy regularization term to reduce uncertainty. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy + Dice on labeled data with the region-specific unsupervised losses, weighted by a Gaussian warm-up schedule.

## Results

On the ACDC dataset with 10% labeled data, ALHVR achieves 90.56% Dice, 83.21% Jaccard, 2.57 95HD, and 0.78 ASD, surpassing the full-supervision U-Net baseline (91.10% Dice) by only 0.54%. With 20% labeled data, it reaches 91.09% Dice, essentially matching the full-supervision upper bound. On AbdomenCT-1K (5% labeled), ALHVR achieves 84.75% Dice (vs. 67.39% U-Net baseline), and on Brats (10% labeled), it reaches 85.14% Dice (vs. 79.23% V-Net baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALHVR consistently outperforms all nine compared methods across all three datasets and all four metrics. On ACDC with 10% labeled data, it surpasses the second-best method EVIL (87.25% Dice) by 3.31% and MLRP (87.10%) by 3.46%, while also achieving the best edge-sensitive metrics (2.57 95HD vs. 4.91 for MLRP; 0.78 ASD vs. 1.30 for MLRP). On AbdomenCT-1K with 5% labeled data, the margin is even larger: ALHVR (84.75% Dice) beats MC-Net (80.97%) by 3.78% and MLRP (80.30%) by 4.45%. On Brats with 10% labeled data, ALHVR (85.14% Dice) exceeds MLRP (83.96%) by 1.18% and UG-MCL (82.85%) by 2.29%, while also achieving the lowest 95HD (7.79 vs. 11.12 for UG-MCL). The method also dramatically outperforms the supervised U-Net/V-Net baselines trained on the same small labeled subset, with gains of 8.97% (ACDC 10%), 17.36% (AbdomenCT-1K 5%), and 5.91% (Brats 10%) in Dice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CPS (Cross Pseudo Supervision) dual-branch framework by introducing a three-way pixel classification scheme (reliable stable, reliable unstable, unreliable stable) derived from confidence thresholds and inter-network agreement. The prototype consistency mechanism borrows from UPCoL [15], while the confidence-guided selective supervision and dynamic teacher selection depart from prior dual-network methods (MCF, MLRP) that apply uniform or binary reliability criteria. The key conceptual departure is treating the two types of 'difficult' regions as fundamentally different learning problems requiring distinct loss formulations, rather than a single confidence-based filtering strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that masking the reliable stable region during training yields the best performance (Table 1) provides a useful diagnostic for understanding where semi-supervised models actually gain from unlabeled data. However, the method is tightly coupled to the CPS dual-branch architecture with feature perturbation, limiting direct transfer to mean-teacher or single-network paradigms. Open questions include whether the three-region taxonomy generalizes to 2D natural image segmentation or to settings with more than two networks, and whether the adaptive threshold (controlled by β) can be made fully data-driven rather than requiring per-dataset tuning.
