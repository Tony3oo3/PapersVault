## Problem

Standard 3D human pose estimation benchmarks rely on root-centering, which normalizes poses relative to the pelvis and discards absolute root position information essential for real-world applications like motion tracking and AR/VR. Naively augmenting root-centered models to also predict root position (the 'Root+' approach) degrades overall pose accuracy due to a fundamental conflict between learning relative joint relationships and predicting absolute positions. The paper addresses how to recover absolute root position robustly without sacrificing pose accuracy or requiring architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 2D joint detections (from a CPN detector) and camera intrinsic focal lengths (fx, fy), combined with root-centered 3D joint coordinates predicted by any existing 3D HPE model. It outputs the absolute 3D root position (pelvis center in global space), the reconstructed absolute 3D pose (root-centered coordinates plus estimated root offset), and a support set identifying which joints are reliable. A key design constraint is that ZS-anchor operates purely as a post-processing step on the predicted root-centered pose and 2D detections, requiring no retraining or additional network parameters.

## Method

PoseAnchor formulates root position estimation as a linear system Ar = b derived from camera projection constraints across all J joints, then solves it via Iterative Hard Thresholding Robust Least Squares Regression (ITRR). ITRR initializes with standard least squares, then iteratively computes residuals, selects a support set of joints with the smallest residuals via hard thresholding, and re-solves the least-squares problem using only inlier joints until convergence. The support set is determined purely from spatial and projection consistency, requiring no ground-truth 2D labels. For the full PoseAnchor training variant, the support set is further used to compute the pose loss exclusively over high-confidence joints, filtering out noisy supervision signals. The approach provides a theoretical geometric convergence guarantee under Subset Strong Convexity/Smoothness conditions, and is model-agnostic, demonstrated across MLP, CNN, GCN, and Transformer backbones.

## Results

On Human3.6M, PoseAnchor achieves an average MPJPE of 40.3 mm and P-MPJPE of 32.1 mm, outperforming the MixSTE baseline (40.9 / 32.6) and matching or exceeding STCFormer-L (40.5 / 31.8). For absolute pose metrics, PoseAnchor attains the best Abs-MPJPE (103.3 mm) and MRPE (96.7 mm), surpassing Ray3D (109.5 / 99.6) and RIE (138.4 / 129.6). On MPI-INF-3DHP, PoseAnchor achieves the highest PCK (99.3%) and AUC (88.1%), while its MPJPE of 17.2 mm is marginally behind MotionAGFormer (16.2 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On H36M, PoseAnchor (40.3 mm MPJPE) outperforms the MixSTE baseline (40.9) and MotionAGFormer-L (41.6), and edges out STCFormer-L (40.5) by 0.2 mm; on P-MPJPE it beats MixSTE (32.6) but trails STCFormer-L (31.8) by 0.3 mm. For absolute pose, PoseAnchor dominates all compared methods: it beats Ray3D by 6.2 mm on Abs-MPJPE (103.3 vs 109.5) and by 2.9 mm on MRPE (96.7 vs 99.6), and outperforms RIE by over 35 mm on Abs-MPJPE. On 3DHP, PoseAnchor wins PCK (99.3% vs 98.2%) and AUC (88.1% vs 85.3%) over MotionAGFormer, though it concedes 1.0 mm on MPJPE (17.2 vs 16.2). In the model-agnostic ablation (Table 3), PoseAnchor consistently improves over Root+ across all four architectures (e.g., MixSTE: 103.3 vs 125.2 Abs-MPJPE; GLA-GCN: 113.8 vs 128.5), while ZS-anchor already surpasses Root+ without retraining. Among RLSR methods (Table 6), ZS-anchor achieves the lowest Abs-MPJPE (107.7) and MRPE (100.5), beating TORRENT (109.4 / 102.2), M-estimation (110.4 / 103.1), LMS (111.0 / 103.7), and RANSAC (123.8 / 117.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the iterative hard thresholding robust regression framework of Bhatia et al. (TORRENT, NeurIPS 2015) to the specific problem of 3D root position estimation, a domain where robust regression had not previously been applied. It departs from the dominant root-centering paradigm in 3D HPE (VPose, MixSTE, STCFormer) that discards absolute position, and contrasts with prior absolute-pose methods (RootNet, LCR-Net, SMAP, Ray3D) that require additional supervision, architectural modifications, or camera-ray formulations. The key conceptual move is reframing root position recovery as a robust linear regression over projection constraints, enabling a model-agnostic, zero-shot solution that requires no retraining of the underlying pose estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot root localization capability is practically significant: any existing root-centered 3D HPE model can be upgraded to produce absolute poses without retraining or architectural changes, lowering the barrier to real-world deployment in motion tracking and AR/VR. The finding that support-set-guided training actually improves root-relative pose accuracy (rather than degrading it, as the naive Root+ approach does) challenges the assumption that adding absolute position learning conflicts with relative pose learning. Open questions include generalization to multi-person scenarios, performance under severe occlusion beyond Gaussian noise, and whether the SSC/SSS theoretical conditions hold reliably in practice across diverse camera geometries.
