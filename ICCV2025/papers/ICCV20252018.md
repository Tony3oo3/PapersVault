## Problem

Traditional 4D CT reconstruction relies on phase-binning, which discretizes temporal resolution into a fixed number of respiratory phases and requires external gating devices, introducing motion misalignment, temporal inconsistencies, and hardware dependencies. No prior method achieves truly continuous-time 4D CT reconstruction from sparse projections while simultaneously eliminating the need for external respiratory gating.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252018/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 300 X-ray projections with corresponding view matrices and timestamps per patient (simulated via TIGRE at a 3-second respiratory cycle), substantially fewer than the several thousand projections used clinically. It outputs a continuous-time 4D CT volume represented as a deformable set of radiative Gaussians whose position, rotation, and scale are functions of an arbitrary query timestamp, along with a learned patient-specific respiratory period. Evaluation is performed by rendering 10 phase-aligned 3D volumes and computing PSNR over the full volume and SSIM averaged across axial, coronal, and sagittal 2D slices.

## Method

X²-Gaussian extends radiative Gaussian splatting into the temporal domain via a dynamic deformation field D(μᵢ, t) = F∘E, where E is a spatiotemporal encoder that projects 4D coordinates (x,y,z,t) onto six multi-resolution K-Plane feature grids (three spatial, three temporal) with bilinear interpolation, and F is a lightweight multi-head decoder that predicts per-Gaussian deformation offsets (Δμ, ΔR, ΔS) at any queried time. To remove external gating, a self-supervised periodic consistency loss enforces I(t) ≈ I(t+nT) with n restricted to {−1,1} (bounded cycle shifts) and the period parameterized as T̂=exp(τ̂) (log-space), allowing the breathing period to be learned end-to-end from projections alone. Training is progressive: a static 3D radiative Gaussian model is first optimized for 5000 iterations, then the full 4D framework (Gaussian parameters, encoder/decoder, and learnable period τ̂) is jointly optimized with a compound loss combining rendering fidelity (L1 + D-SSIM), periodic consistency, 3D total variation, and 4D grid total variation regularization.

## Results

On the DIR dataset (5 patients, 300 projections), X²-Gaussian achieves an average PSNR of 39.34 dB and SSIM of 0.955, outperforming the strongest baseline R²-GS (37.09 dB / 0.943) by 2.25 dB and the traditional FDK method (29.41 dB / 0.727) by 9.93 dB. On 4DLung, the method reaches 38.61 dB PSNR and 0.957 SSIM, and on the more challenging SPARE dataset (sparse-view, 1-min scan) it achieves 32.24 dB PSNR and 0.922 SSIM, again surpassing R²-GS (31.12 dB / 0.908). The self-supervised respiratory period estimation attains a mean error of only 5.2 ms, and the method extracts clinically relevant parameters (tidal volume 370 ml, minute ventilation 7.4 L/min) directly from the reconstructed 4D volume.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X²-Gaussian outperforms all nine baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across every dataset. Against the closest competitor R²-GS [59] (the prior best Gaussian-splatting CT method), the gain is 2.25 dB on DIR, 1.30 dB on 4DLung, and 1.12 dB on SPARE. Compared to the best NeRF-based method SAX-NeRF [7], the improvement is 4.76 dB on DIR. Against the traditional FDK analytic reconstruction [41], the gain reaches 9.93 dB on DIR and 24.36 dB on SPARE, where FDK degrades severely (14.25 dB). The other Gaussian-splatting baselines (3D-GS [28], X-GS [6]) lag substantially, with X-GS trailing by 8.48 dB on DIR, indicating that simply applying static 3DGS to each phase is insufficient for dynamic reconstruction. On the SPARE dataset, which simulates a clinically realistic 1-minute sparse scan, X²-Gaussian maintains a 1.12 dB lead over R²-GS while FDK drops to 14.25 dB, highlighting the robustness of the continuous-time formulation under extreme projection scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Radiative Gaussian Splatting (R²-GS) from static 3D CT reconstruction to the temporal domain, introducing a dynamic deformation field parameterized by K-Planes-style multi-resolution feature planes for spatiotemporal encoding. It departs fundamentally from the established phase-binning paradigm (FDK, NeRF-based, and prior GS-based 4D CT methods) by formulating reconstruction as a continuous-time problem rather than a sequence of independent 3D reconstructions at discrete phases. The self-supervised respiratory period learning via periodic consistency loss is a novel addition not present in prior 4D CT work, which universally relied on external respiratory gating devices for phase assignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating external respiratory gating devices and enabling arbitrary temporal resolution addresses two major practical barriers in clinical 4D CT: hardware dependency/patient discomfort and limited temporal sampling. The ability to extract clinical parameters (tidal volume, minute ventilation, I:E ratio) directly from the continuous reconstruction opens a path toward automated radiomic analysis for radiotherapy planning. However, all experiments use TIGRE-simulated projections with only 300 views and 13 patients, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real clinical data with noise, metal artifacts, and longer scan durations. The method's reliance on a quasi-periodic breathing assumption also limits applicability to irregular respiratory patterns (e.g., in COPD patients).
