## Problem

Residual neural networks rely on skip connections (adding input to hidden-layer output), an operation that cannot be expressed by the unitary gates of digital quantum computers. While neural ODEs reformulate ResNets as continuous-time dynamics, no framework existed to implement them on analog quantum hardware. This paper identifies that Rydberg atom quantum computers, which natively evolve under time-dependent Hamiltonians, are uniquely suited to realize this continuous residual structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251738/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Input images (28×28 grayscale MNIST/FashionMNIST) or tabular health features (Pima Indian Diabetes) are first reduced to 5 PCA components, which are then MinMax-scaled into hardware-allowed ranges and embedded directly as scaling factors in the Hamiltonian's pulse amplitudes (3 features into global Rabi/detuning pulses) and local detuning couplings (2 features into site-dependent h_i values). The output is a single binary class label derived from the average probability of measuring |1⟩ across all qubits after Hamiltonian evolution, with a soft probability in [0,1] used for loss computation and a 0.5 threshold for hard classification.

## Method

RESQ encodes a neural ODE ResNet into the time-dependent Hamiltonian of a 4-atom Rydberg system arranged in a configurable grid (chain, ring, square, or triangle). The key parameterization uses piecewise-linear pulses (3 intervals) where each interval's holding value is θ_j·ω_i + θ_{j+1}, coupling a learned parameter θ to an input feature ω_i, yielding 3 + N/2 input channels and 6M + N/2 trainable parameters that scale linearly with qubit count. Training uses the stochastic pulse gradient method (20 samples per gradient) with binary cross-entropy loss and Adam optimization over 75 iterations, all simulated classically via Bloqade.jl. The approach achieves constant execution time independent of problem size and demonstrates noise resilience on the 256-qubit QuEra Aquila hardware, with accuracy within 1% of ideal simulation under realistic Gaussian pulse perturbations.

## Results

Across all classification tasks (MNIST, FashionMNIST, and Pima Indian Diabetes), RESQ achieves a 50% average accuracy improvement over similarly-sized classical baselines, with a 56% improvement over C-NN, 57% over C-ResNet, and 36% over C-NODE. Per-dataset, the improvement over similarly-sized classical models is 52% on PID, 61% on MNIST, and 37% on FashionMNIST. RESQ also rivals or exceeds models with 10× and 100× more parameters in most tasks, and maintains accuracy and F1 scores within 1% of ideal simulation when run on noisy QuEra Aquila hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESQ is compared against three classical baselines at matched (×1), 10×, and 100× parameter counts: a feedforward network (C-NN), a residual network (C-ResNet, following He et al. [16]), and a neural ODE classifier (C-NODE, following Dupont et al. [14]). Against the ×1-parameter versions of all three baselines, RESQ wins on every task, with the largest margins on MNIST (61% average improvement) and PID (52%). Against the ×10 and ×100 parameter versions, RESQ remains competitive in most tasks but is outperformed on the FashionMNIST trousers/boots task, where the larger classical models' extra capacity gives them an edge. On F1 scores (Figure 7), RESQ maintains its advantage over similarly-sized models and rivals the larger classical models in most classification tasks, indicating robust precision-recall balance. On real QuEra Aquila hardware, RESQ's predictions are largely stable, with only samples near the 0.5 decision boundary showing class flips due to hardware noise, and simulated-noise accuracy stays within 1% of the ideal simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESQ builds on the neural ODE formulation of residual networks (Chen et al., 2018) and the stochastic pulse gradient method for training analog quantum programs (Leng et al., 2022; Banchi & Crooks, 2021), but departs from prior QML work that attempts to implement residual connections via discrete gate-based circuits (Wen et al., 2024; Kashif & Al-Kuwari, 2024) or hybrid quantum-classical architectures (Liang et al., 2021). It also distinguishes itself from the only prior Rydberg-atom classification work [27], which relied on a hybrid digital-gate-plus-analog-evolution approach incompatible with current commercial hardware. RESQ is positioned as the first fully quantum-native analog implementation of ResNets, encoding both data and parameters directly into the time-dependent Hamiltonian of a Rydberg atom array.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of ResNet-based classification on a real analog quantum computer (QuEra Aquila), showing that the continuous-time Hamiltonian dynamics naturally align with the ODE structure of residual networks and that the resulting model is remarkably noise-resilient (within 1% of ideal simulation). However, the evaluation is constrained to N=4 atoms, binary classification, and small datasets (1000 training samples), leaving open whether the approach scales to multi-class problems, larger feature spaces, or deeper network equivalents. A key open question is whether the parameter-efficiency advantage persists as problem complexity grows, and whether the piecewise-linear pulse parameterization can be replaced by more expressive continuous controls as hardware matures.
