## Problem

While unified image generation and editing models (e.g., ACE, OmniGen) have emerged, video synthesis lacks an equivalent all-in-one framework because of the stringent requirements for spatiotemporal consistency across diverse input modalities. Existing video models are single-task, single-model systems, forcing users to deploy and chain multiple specialized models for complex creative workflows. Additionally, no comprehensive multi-task benchmark exists for systematically evaluating video generation and editing models across their full task spectrum.

## Input / Output

All inputs are organized into a Video Condition Unit V = [T; F; M], where T is a text prompt, F is a sequence of n context frames (h×w, RGB normalized to [-1,1]), and M is an aligned binary mask sequence indicating which pixels to regenerate. Different tasks are encoded by varying F and M: T2V uses zero frames with all-ones masks, R2V prepends reference frames with zero masks, V2V feeds the source video with all-ones masks, and MV2V uses the source video with arbitrary spatiotemporal masks. The model accepts arbitrary resolutions, dynamic durations, and variable frame rates, and produces a single generated video as output.

## Method

VACE restructures a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (LTX-Video-2B or Wan-T2V-14B) to consume VCU inputs via three key mechanisms. First, Concept Decoupling splits context frames into reactive (F_c = F×M, pixels to change) and inactive (F_k = F×(1−M), pixels to retain) sequences using the mask, explicitly separating what the model should modify versus preserve. Second, a Context Embedder concatenates F_c, F_k, and M along the channel dimension and tokenizes them, with F_c/F_k weights copied from the original video embedder and M weights zero-initialized. Third, a Context Adapter Tuning strategy (inspired by Res-Tuning) inserts distributed, cascade-type Transformer blocks that process context and text tokens in a bypass branch, injecting additive signals into frozen DiT blocks—keeping the backbone frozen while training only the Context Embedder and Context Blocks for faster convergence and pluggability.

## Results

On VACE-Benchmark, VACE (LTX-Video-2B) achieves the highest Normalized Average score across six of seven evaluated tasks: I2V (74.38% vs. 73.66% CogVideoX-I2V), Inpaint (72.05% vs. 70.15% ProPainter), Outpaint (74.25% vs. 73.16% M3DDM), Depth (74.99% vs. 72.35% Control-A-Video), Pose (76.13% vs. 72.45% ControlVideo), and Flow (75.90% vs. 74.42% FLATTEN). In user studies, VACE leads on I2V (3.24 vs. 2.92), Outpaint (3.80 vs. 3.29), Depth (3.23 vs. 2.40), and Pose (3.18 vs. 2.06). On the R2V task, VACE (76.76% normalized, 3.40 user study) trails commercial models Kling 1.6 (78.81%, 4.04) and Pika 2.2 (77.87%, 3.91) but is comparable to Vidu 2.0 (76.47%, 3.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VACE outperforms all open-source baselines on six of seven tasks. On I2V, it surpasses CogVideoX-I2V by 0.72% normalized average and leads in user study (3.24 vs. 2.92). For inpainting, VACE beats ProPainter by 1.9% on normalized average, though ProPainter edges it slightly in user study (3.11 vs. 3.00). On outpainting, VACE leads M3DDM by 1.09% and Follow-Your-Canvas by 2.71%, with a clear user-study advantage (3.80 vs. 3.29). In controllable generation, VACE dominates: it exceeds Control-A-Video by 2.64% on depth and ControlVideo by 3.68% on pose, with user-study margins of 0.83 and 1.19 points respectively. On optical flow, VACE narrowly beats FLATTEN (75.90% vs. 74.42% normalized; 3.08 vs. 3.03 user study). The sole task where VACE trails is R2V, where commercial models Kling 1.6 (78.81%, 4.04) and Pika 2.2 (77.87%, 3.91) outperform VACE (76.76%, 3.40), though VACE remains competitive with Vidu 2.0 (76.47%, 3.84) and benefits from being a 2B open-source model versus proprietary systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

VACE extends the image-domain unified generation/editing paradigm (ACE, OmniGen, UniControl) to the video setting, where temporal-spatial consistency makes unification substantially harder. Architecturally, it builds on pre-trained video DiTs (LTX-Video, Wan2.1) and adapts the Res-Tuning paradigm into a pluggable Context Adapter that injects task-specific conditioning without modifying the frozen backbone. The Video Condition Unit (VCU) formalizes diverse inputs—reference frames, control signals, masks—into a single (text, frame-sequence, mask-sequence) interface, while Concept Decoupling explicitly separates reactive from inactive pixels, a design choice absent in prior task-specific video editors like Video-P2P, MagicEdit, or Phantom.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By consolidating 12+ video tasks into one inference call, VACE reduces deployment overhead and, more importantly, enables compositional workflows (e.g., reference-guided inpainting with temporal extension) that no single prior model supports. The VACE-Benchmark addresses a real evaluation gap for non-T2V video tasks. Key limitations include a measurable quality gap versus commercial R2V systems (Kling, Pika, Vidu) at the 2B scale, and the framework's reliance on RGB-representable control signals, which may not generalize to arbitrary conditioning modalities. Open questions include whether the unified approach degrades as task count grows further and whether the Context Adapter scales efficiently to 14B+ backbones.
