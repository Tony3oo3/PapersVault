## Problem

G-PCC geometry compression introduces coordinate quantization distortion that simultaneously causes point reduction and geometry displacement, a combined degradation that existing upsampling, completion, and denoising methods cannot address because they assume the latent surface structure is preserved. Moreover, prior quantization-specific enhancement methods are restricted to either small-scale or large-scale point clouds and rarely handle sparse LiDAR scene data, leaving no unified solution for omni-scene point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a G-PCC reconstructed point cloud (with known quantization step Q_step) as input and produces a geometry-enhanced point cloud approximating the original. For small-scale objects (1k–4k points), the compressed cloud is replicated to a fixed tensor size before processing; for large-scale objects and scenes (>1000k points), Minkowski sparse convolution avoids patch partitioning. The quantization step parameter is explicitly fed into the loss function as a geometric prior, and the output point count matches the raw cloud size.

## Method

The core contribution is a rooting-growing-pruning (RGP) paradigm that decomposes quantization distortion removal into three sequential stages: rooting pulls displaced points back toward the latent surface, growing generates candidate points to compensate for point reduction, and pruning refines geometry positions. For small-scale clouds, each stage uses global/local feature extraction (point transformer units) followed by MLP-based offset prediction; for large-scale clouds, a sparse-convolution U-Net handles local geometry while a pre-trained small-scale model provides global context via random sampling. A novel loss term L_q constrains the predicted coordinate offset magnitude to at most Q_step/2, encoding the known quantization bound, and a repulsion loss L_r enforces minimum inter-point spacing. Small-scale stages are jointly optimized end-to-end with CD/EMD losses, while large-scale stages are trained separately with EMD, BCE, and CD losses respectively.

## Results

On small-scale point clouds (ModelNet40, ShapeNet, ScanObject), the proposed method achieves the best CD and D1 PSNR across all three quantization levels. For example, on ModelNet40 at the most severe distortion (r1, Q_step=8), it reaches 4.47×10⁻² CD / 36.86 dB D1 PSNR, outperforming the best quantization-specific baseline DGPP (6.71/30.82) and the best completion method DcTr (4.77/35.81). On large-scale 8iVFB at r1, the method yields a ΔD1 PSNR gain of 10.39 dB and ΔCD of 4.74×10⁻², surpassing Grnet-ER (7.84/4.35) and DGPP (8.18/4.47). Downstream, enhanced point clouds recover 91.13% classification accuracy on ModelNet40 (vs. 89.87% for G-PCC and 91.69% raw) and 88.2/76.9/72.7 AP for car detection on KITTI (vs. 86.7/75.5/70.1 for G-PCC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 17 baselines on every reported metric across all datasets and quantization levels. Among upsampling methods, NP-Net [12] is the strongest (e.g., 2.98/40.56 on ModelNet40 r3) but still trails the proposed method by 0.60 CD and 2.46 dB D1 PSNR; RepKPU [32] and PUDM [30] are competitive at mild distortion (r3) but degrade sharply at r1. Completion methods such as DcTr [11] and GRNet [43] achieve reasonable results at r3 (2.84/40.31 and 3.03/39.09 on ModelNet40) but fall well behind at r1 (4.77/35.81 and 4.77/35.55 vs. 4.47/36.86 for Ours). The denoising baseline DMR-Net [25] performs poorly across all settings, confirming that quantization distortion is fundamentally different from noise. Among quantization-specific methods, PU-Dense [2] and Geo-Net [47] are only evaluated at r3 and are outperformed (2.55/40.21 and 2.59/39.46 vs. 2.32/43.02); DGPP [10] and Grnet-ER [22] are the closest competitors at severe distortion but still lag by 1.5–2.2 dB in D1 PSNR on 8iVFB. Notably, the proposed method is the only one that maintains strong performance uniformly across all three distortion levels and both small-scale and large-scale point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of G-PCC geometry quantization distortion removal (PU-Dense, DGPP, Geo-Net, Grnet-ER) by proposing a unified rooting-growing-pruning paradigm that handles both small-scale object and large-scale scene point clouds within a single framework, whereas prior methods were limited to one scale or the other. For the large-scale pipeline, it borrows the U-Net sparse convolution structure from Liu et al. (AAAI 2024) and augments it with a pre-trained small-scale global feature extractor to compensate for sparse convolution's limited spatial modeling. The method departs from prior work by introducing a quantization-step-aware loss (L_q) that encodes the bounded-offset property of uniform quantization, and by explicitly targeting scene point clouds (KITTI), which no prior quantization-distortion-removal method had addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework is practically valuable for G-PCC deployment pipelines that must handle heterogeneous content (objects and scenes) without separate models, and the quantization-step-aware loss provides a reusable inductive bias for any uniform-quantization post-processing task. However, the large-scale pipeline requires separate per-stage training (no end-to-end optimization), and the scene point cloud evaluation covers only four SemanticKITTI sequences, leaving open whether the gains hold across more diverse driving scenarios. A key open question is whether the rooting-growing-pruning decomposition generalizes to other compression artifacts (e.g., octree truncation, attribute quantization) or to dynamic point cloud sequences where temporal consistency matters.
