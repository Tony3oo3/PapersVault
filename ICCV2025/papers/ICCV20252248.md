## Problem

Current audio description (AD) generation methods and VideoLLMs treat video as a sequence of frames without awareness of the shot, which is the fundamental unit of edited video. This limits their ability to interpret multi-shot scenes where film grammar devices—thread structure (interleaved camera viewpoints) and shot scale (close-up vs. long shot)—carry critical semantic information about character relationships and scene context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip with an AD interval [t_A, t_B] and extends it to include up to two past and two future shots, sampling 32 frames total (16 densely within the AD interval, 16 sparsely in context). Each frame is visually annotated with its shot number at the top-left. The pipeline produces a dense multi-shot description (Stage I) and a concise AD-style narration (Stage II), with intermediate outputs including thread cluster assignments and a five-class shot scale label per shot.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage training-free pipeline: Stage I uses a VideoLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B) to generate dense descriptions guided by three film-grammar-aware prompting strategies—(i) shot-based temporal context via dynamic frame sampling and shot-number referral, (ii) thread structure injection where DINOv2 dense feature matching (cost volume with spatial neighbourhood constraint) identifies shared-camera shot groups and the VideoLLM is prompted to explain the thread via chain-of-thought, and (iii) scale-dependent factor composition where a fine-tuned DINOv2 shot-scale classifier determines which descriptive factors (facial expression, environment, objects) to include in the prompt. Stage II uses an LLM (LLaMA3-8B) to extract and format the final AD narration. No VLM weights are updated; all film-grammar knowledge is injected through prompt engineering and add-on prediction modules.

## Results

On CMD-AD, the proposed training-free framework (Qwen2-VL-7B + LLaMA3-8B) achieves CIDEr 26.3, CRITIC 47.8, Action score 28.4, and R@1/5 33.0, outperforming the best prior training-free method AutoAD-Zero (CIDEr 21.9, CRITIC 44.3, Action 26.9, R@1/5 30.8) and surpassing fine-tuned AutoAD-III on CIDEr (26.3 vs 25.0) and CRITIC (47.8 vs 32.7). With GPT-4o as the backbone, the method reaches CIDEr 26.1, CRITIC 49.1, Action 32.5, and R@1/5 36.5 on CMD-AD, and CIDEr 34.2, CRITIC 46.5, Action 27.4, R@1/5 36.6 on TV-AD, surpassing all fine-tuned baselines. On TV-AD with Qwen2-VL, the method achieves CIDEr 31.1, CRITIC 42.2, and Action 23.9, improving over AutoAD-Zero (CIDEr 26.4, CRITIC 26.4, Action 22.1). This is the first training-free approach to exceed fine-tuned methods on multiple benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest training-free baseline AutoAD-Zero with the same Qwen2-VL-7B backbone, the proposed method improves CIDEr by +4.4 (26.3 vs 21.9), CRITIC by +3.5 (47.8 vs 44.3), Action by +1.5 (28.4 vs 26.9), and R@1/5 by +2.2 (33.0 vs 30.8) on CMD-AD, with similar gains on TV-AD (CIDEr 31.1 vs 26.4, CRITIC 42.2 vs 26.4). Against fine-tuned AutoAD-III, the method wins on CIDEr (26.3 vs 25.0), CRITIC (47.8 vs 32.7), and R@1/5 (33.0 vs 31.2) on CMD-AD, though AutoAD-III retains an edge on Action score (31.5 vs 28.4). Against fine-tuned DistinctAD, the method achieves higher CIDEr (26.3 vs 22.7) on CMD-AD. With GPT-4o, the method dominates all baselines: it outperforms AutoAD-Zero (GPT-4o) by +3.7 CIDEr and +4.0 CRITIC on CMD-AD, and by +3.3 CIDEr and +2.1 CRITIC on TV-AD, while also surpassing fine-tuned AutoAD-III on every reported CMD-AD metric. The weaker general-purpose VideoLLMs (Video-LLaMA, VideoBLIP) are far behind, scoring CIDEr below 6 on CMD-AD, confirming the necessity of the two-stage VideoLLM+LLM pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AutoAD-Zero two-stage pipeline (VideoLLM for dense description → LLM for AD summarisation) by injecting film-grammar knowledge—shot-based temporal context, thread structure, and shot scale—through training-free prompting. The thread structure prediction borrows the cost-volume dense matching formulation from Jabri et al. (NeurIPS 2020), adapted with a spatial neighbourhood constraint for shot-pair comparison. Unlike prior fine-tuned AD methods (AutoAD-II/III, DistinctAD, UniAD) that learn from annotated ADs, or earlier training-free approaches that ignore shot boundaries, this work treats shots as the fundamental unit of temporal context and uses off-the-shelf classifiers (DINOv2-based shot scale, DINOv2 feature matching for threads) as plug-in modules that require no VLM retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a training-free framework can match or exceed fine-tuned models on CMD-AD and TV-AD is practically significant: it removes the need for large-scale AD annotations and per-backbone fine-tuning, making the approach immediately adaptable to new VLMs. The introduced action score and assisted-generation protocol (multiple candidates with automatic selection) address real gaps in AD evaluation—character-free action assessment and the inherent subjectivity of AD content selection. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by the base VideoLLM's hallucination rate, and story-level narrative context is not incorporated, leaving a gap for long-form coherence across scenes.
