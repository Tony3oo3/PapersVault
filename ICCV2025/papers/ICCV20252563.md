## Problem

Generating 4D light fields from a single image remains challenging because existing single-view synthesis methods produce misaligned correspondences across angular views, leading to blurry artifacts that prevent realistic photographic effects like refocusing and shallow depth of field. While multi-view approaches (NeRF, 3DGS, light field cameras) achieve angular consistency, they require dense captures or specialized hardware, and single-image methods that rely on explicit 3D geometry or visual feature aggregation struggle with unseen content quality and generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (any resolution) as input, along with disparity inferred from a depth foundation model (DepthAnythingV2) refined by learned scale/shift parameters. It outputs a full two-plane-parameterized 4D light field on a regular grid of sub-aperture images (e.g., 11×11, 17×17, or 9×9), from which arbitrary novel views, digital refocusing, and specularity simulations can be rendered. The pipeline operates pixel-wise, so it handles any input image without scene-specific retraining.

## Method

iIBRnet formulates light field generation as the inverse of image-based rendering: rather than blending colors from multiple source views into one target, it reconstructs continuous light flows from a single image's pixels by casting each pixel as a 2D ray in epipolar-plane-image (EPI) space. A ray transformer takes only Plücker coordinates of source and target rays (no visual features) plus disparity-based positional encoding to predict attention weights that aggregate colors along angular dimensions, enabling sub-pixel-accurate, anti-aliased ray rendering with unlimited angular super-resolution. Occluded regions are detected via entropy of the attention weights, inpainted with a latent diffusion model, and iteratively fed back as new source rays to ensure angular consistency. Training uses a composite loss combining L2 color error, an entropy loss that enforces dominant-ray selection (preventing foreground-background blending), and an EPI structure tensor loss that encourages straight linear structures in rendered epipolar slices.

## Results

iIBR achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis across all four datasets in a zero-shot setting (trained only on synthetic Pov-ray). On Pov-ray, iIBR zero-shot reaches 28.41 PSNR / 0.931 SSIM / 0.037 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline TMPI (23.62 PSNR) by nearly 5 dB. On the real-world NeRF LLFF dataset, iIBR zero-shot achieves 24.91 PSNR / 0.810 SSIM / 0.183 LPIPS, surpassing the next-best method TMPI (17.56 PSNR) by over 7 dB. Fine-tuning on each dataset yields modest additional gains, e.g., 25.53 PSNR on LLFF and 28.38 PSNR on 4DLF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iIBR decisively outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and every metric, even in the zero-shot setting where it is only pretrained on synthetic Pov-ray scenes. Against TMPI (the strongest baseline), iIBR zero-shot gains approximately 4.8 dB PSNR on Pov-ray (28.41 vs. 23.62), 3.3 dB on 4DLF (28.10 vs. 24.83), 3.9 dB on Stanford (27.89 vs. 24.01), and 7.3 dB on the real-world LLFF dataset (24.91 vs. 17.56). SinMPI, which uses expanded multi-plane images with a latent diffusion inpainter, trails iIBR by 6 dB on Pov-ray and 7.8 dB on LLFF, and additionally suffers from depth-scale misalignment in out-painted regions. ZeroMVS and NVIST, which rely on text-to-image generation priors or large-scale training data, show the weakest performance, with NVIST dropping to 15.34 PSNR on LLFF and ZeroMVS to 11.21 PSNR, indicating that independent view generation introduces significant correspondence inconsistencies that iIBR's iterative ray-update strategy avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical image-based rendering (IBR) paradigm in reverse: rather than blending multiple source views into one target image, iIBRnet propagates light flows from a single input image outward to reconstruct a full 4D light field. Conceptually it draws on two-plane light field parameterization and EPI structure (Levoy & Hanrahan, Ng et al.), but departs from prior single-image NVS methods (SinMPI, NViST, ZeroNVS) that estimate 3D geometry or multi-plane images and then project; instead, it treats each pixel as a ray in 4D Plücker-coordinate space and uses a Transformer to compute inter-ray relationships directly. The inpainting of occluded regions borrows from latent diffusion models, and the disparity-based positional encoding is inspired by GPNR's epipolar-aware encoding, but the overall pipeline—ray transformer predicting aggregation weights from ray coordinates alone, without visual features—is a new architectural choice within the neural rendering family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate a structured 4D light field from a single photograph without specialized hardware opens the door to computational photography effects (refocusing, variable DoF) on consumer devices, and the zero-shot transfer from synthetic training to real scenes (PSNR ~25 on LLFF without fine-tuning) suggests the EPI-slope formulation captures scene-agnostic geometric structure. Key open questions remain: temporal consistency for moving objects is unaddressed, and the two-plane parameterization limits the method to near-viewpoint synthesis, making 360° rendering infeasible without a two-sphere extension. The reliance on an external depth foundation model (DepthAnythingV2) also introduces a dependency whose errors propagate into ray direction estimation, a vulnerability the ablation (12 dB drop without disparity encoding) confirms is critical.
