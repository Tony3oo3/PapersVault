## Problem

Sparse-view 3D scene reconstruction and novel view synthesis remain challenging because occluded regions are unobserved, geometric cues are insufficient, and existing diffusion-based approaches lack precise camera control and 3D consistency at the scene level. Additionally, joint training across multiple scene datasets suffers from scale ambiguity, causing performance degradation and difficulty generating large-motion videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252560/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 to 9 sparse RGB views with camera poses (estimated via VGGT when not provided) and produces two outputs: (1) a temporally coherent video of novel views generated by a camera-controlled diffusion model (25 frames at 576×1024), and (2) a 3D Gaussian splatting representation of the scene with per-pixel 12-channel parameters (RGB, 3D scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, ray distance). The reconstruction is formulated as a video frame interpolation problem conditioned on boundary frames in both latent and CLIP semantic space.

## Method

SpatialCrafter operates in two stages. First, a camera-controlled video diffusion model (built on SVD) generates plausible additional views: camera trajectories are parameterized as ray embeddings or depth-warped frames and injected via a trainable conditional encoder, while an epipolar attention mechanism restricts cross-frame attention to geometrically valid regions defined by the fundamental matrix, and a unified scale estimator (using Metric3D inter-percentile range ratios) resolves scale ambiguity for multi-dataset training. Second, a feed-forward reconstruction module fuses video latent tokens, ray-embedding pose tokens, and monocular depth tokens into a unified sequence processed by a hybrid Mamba-Transformer architecture (bi-directional SSM for O(L) complexity), then decodes per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives via 3D-DeConv layers. Training uses a composite loss combining photometric MSE, perceptual, and depth consistency terms, with a three-stage curriculum from smooth to arbitrary camera trajectories.

## Results

On the out-of-domain sparse-view reconstruction benchmark (Table 2), SpatialCrafter achieves 27.92 PSNR on DTU (3-view) and 29.03 PSNR (9-view), substantially outperforming the next-best method CAT3D (22.02 and 25.92 PSNR respectively). On Mip-NeRF360, it reaches 20.19 PSNR at 9 views, edging out ReconX (20.13) and CAT3D (18.67). For single-view novel view synthesis (Table 5), the method attains 22.78 PSNR on DL3DV and 22.33 PSNR on Tanks-and-Temples, surpassing ViewCrafter by ~1.4 dB. In the video generation component (Table 1), it achieves the lowest FVD (18.21–20.17) and translation error (0.103–0.175) across RealEstate10K, DL3DV, and Tanks-and-Temples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialCrafter consistently outperforms all 20 baselines across the majority of metrics and datasets. In video generation (Table 1), it beats MotionCtrl [64] by 2–10 FVD points and reduces translation error by 50–85% (e.g., 0.103 vs 0.798 on RealEstate10K); it also surpasses CameraCtrl [19] and ViewCrafter [77] on FVD, rotation/translation error, and LPIPS across all three video benchmarks, though it trails CameraCtrl by 0.12 dB PSNR on Tanks-and-Temples. In end-to-end sparse-view reconstruction (Table 2), the gains are most dramatic on DTU, where SpatialCrafter achieves 27.92 PSNR (3-view) versus CAT3D's 22.02 and ReconFusion's 20.74 — a 5–7 dB margin — and on Mip-NeRF360 where it leads ReconX [34] by 0.16 dB at 9 views. For two-view reconstruction (Table 3), it edges out ReconX [34] (28.35 vs 28.31 PSNR on RealEstate10K) and GS-LRM [79] (28.35 vs 28.10), while decisively beating ViewCrafter [77] by 4.1 dB. In single-view synthesis (Table 5), it surpasses ViewCrafter [77] by 1.0–1.4 dB PSNR and MotionCtrl [64] by 7 dB, with the largest gap over LucidDreamer [9] and ZeroNVS [45] (~7–8 dB). The only minor losses are on DTU 6-view SSIM (0.895 vs ReconFusion's 0.904) and LLFF 6-view SSIM/LPIPS (0.814/0.124 vs CAT3D's 0.833/0.121), where the gap is within 0.02.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialCrafter sits at the intersection of camera-controlled video generation (CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter) and diffusion-prior-based 3D reconstruction (ReconFusion, ReconX, CAT3D). It builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion as its generative backbone and adopts ray embeddings and depth-warped frames from CameraCtrl and ViewCrafter for camera conditioning, but departs by (1) introducing an epipolar attention mechanism to enforce explicit geometric constraints across generated frames, (2) proposing a unified scale estimator to resolve cross-dataset scale ambiguity during joint training, and (3) replacing the typical per-scene optimization or image-diffusion inpainting pipeline with a feed-forward Mamba-Transformer module that regresses 3D Gaussians directly from video latents fused with monocular depth and ray embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video diffusion models, when properly conditioned on camera trajectories, can serve as a practical 'scene imagination' engine that fills in occluded and unseen regions, substantially reducing the ambiguity that plagues sparse-view reconstruction. The unified scale alignment finding (COLMAP error dropping from 14.3% to 4.2%) is a useful practical insight for anyone doing multi-dataset video diffusion training. However, the method still requires a two-stage pipeline (generate video, then reconstruct), does not handle dynamic scenes, and its gains over the strongest baseline (ViewCrafter) are most pronounced in the hardest settings (single-view, low overlap) but more modest in well-constrained scenarios, leaving open whether the added generative stage is always worth its computational cost.
