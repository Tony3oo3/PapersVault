## Problem

Deep neural networks trained on biased datasets over-rely on spurious correlations between target and bias attributes, degrading performance on underrepresented groups. Existing fairness methods that enforce independence between predictions and bias attributes rely on adversarial learning or regularization terms, which are unstable in practice and require careful hyperparameter tuning. This paper seeks a simple, hyperparameter-free mechanism to remove bias without these fragile optimization procedures.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces a class label as output via a linear classifier on whitened target features. A key requirement is access to bias attribute labels (e.g., gender, age) during training, which are used to compute the re-weighted covariance matrix and train the auxiliary bias encoder. The target encoder is frozen (pretrained on the same biased dataset), so only the bias encoder and two linear classifiers are updated during the debiasing phase.

## Method

The core mechanism is Controllable Feature Whitening (CFW): target features z_t (from a frozen biased encoder) and bias features z_b (from a trainable encoder) are concatenated, then whitened by multiplying with the inverse square root of a blended covariance matrix Σ_λ = λ·Σ_u + (1−λ)·Σ_b, where Σ_b is computed over the biased training distribution and Σ_u approximates an unbiased distribution by over-weighting rare (bias-conflicting) groups. The whitened features are split into z_wt and z_wb, which are linearly independent by construction, and separate linear classifiers are trained on each. Because z_wb is trained to predict the bias attribute, z_wt is rendered incapable of linearly encoding bias information, effectively forcing the target prediction to 'forget' the bias shortcut. The coefficient λ smoothly interpolates between demographic parity (λ=0) and equalized odds (λ=1), and the authors show that λ=0.25 consistently yields strong performance across four datasets, making the method practically hyperparameter-free. The matrix inverse square root is computed via coupled Newton-Schultz iterations (T=5), chosen for numerical stability over ZCA-whitening or Cholesky decomposition.

## Results

On Corrupted CIFAR-10, Ours+S (using a SelecMix-pretrained encoder) achieves 42.51% unbiased accuracy at the 0.5% bias-conflicting ratio, outperforming the best baseline SelecMix+L (37.02%) by 5.5 points, and 59.05% at 5.0% ratio vs. 53.47% for SelecMix+L. On Biased FFHQ (0.5% bias-conflicting), Ours+S reaches 82.77% bias-conflicting accuracy, surpassing the next-best method DisEnt (63.87%) by nearly 19 points. On Celeb-A with ResNet-50, the method achieves 92.33% unbiased, 93.24% bias-conflicting, and 91.02% worst-group accuracy, setting new state-of-the-art across all three metrics. The method is effectively hyperparameter-free, with λ=0.25 consistently yielding strong results across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed CFW method outperforms all 23 baselines on the primary metrics across four datasets. On Corrupted CIFAR-10, Ours+S beats the strongest baseline SelecMix+L (w/ GT) by 5.5–5.6 points across all bias ratios (e.g., 42.51% vs. 37.02% at 0.5%), and surpasses DisEnt by 12.6 points at 0.5%. On Biased FFHQ, the 19-point margin over DisEnt (82.77% vs. 63.87% bias-conflicting accuracy) is the largest gap in the table, and even Ours+V (79.80%) exceeds the previous best SelecMix+L (75.00%) by 4.8 points. On Celeb-A (ResNet-50), the method achieves 91.02% worst-group accuracy, exceeding the next-best SSA (89.8%) and LISA (89.30%), while also attaining the highest unbiased accuracy (92.33%). In the Celeb-A Δ_EO comparison (Table 3), CFW achieves the lowest equalized-odds violation in every target/sensitive attribute pair (e.g., 4.2 vs. 24.9 for GRL on attractiveness/male) while maintaining or exceeding their accuracy, confirming that adversarial-learning baselines (GRL, LNL) are both less fair and less accurate. The method also improves upon the no-bias-label baseline SelecMix+L (w/o GT) by 3.1 points on CIFAR-10 at 0.5% and 11.97 points on bFFHQ, demonstrating the value of explicit bias labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes feature whitening—a standard normalization technique from representation learning and GANs—as a fairness mechanism, departing from the dominant paradigm of adversarial learning (GRL, LNL) and intractable statistical dependency estimation (mutual information, HSI, HGR). It builds on the observation that last-layer retraining suffices for debiasing and the theoretical link between zero covariance and linear independence, but the key conceptual move is showing that enforcing linear independence between target and bias features at the final classifier is sufficient for meaningful fairness gains without modeling higher-order dependencies. The controllable covariance blending (interpolating between biased and unbiased statistics) is a novel extension of re-weighting strategies, reframing the demographic parity vs. equalized odds trade-off as a single scalar parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: no adversarial min-max games, no regularization hyperparameters, and a single coefficient (λ=0.25) that transfers across four diverse datasets, making the method genuinely easy to deploy. The conceptual finding that linear independence alone—without full statistical independence—yields substantial fairness improvements challenges the field's reliance on intractable dependency measures. Key limitations include the requirement for explicit bias labels, the assumption that the last linear classifier captures the relevant decision boundary, and the observation that pure unbiased whitening (λ=1) overfits due to sample diversity imbalance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unknown-bias settings, multi-attribute biases, or non-linear decision boundaries.
