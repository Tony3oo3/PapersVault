## Problem

Multimodal LLMs handle 2D spatial relations well but remain weak at 3D perception tasks such as relative depth ordering, metric distance/size estimation, and 3D bounding box grounding. Existing 3D spatial datasets and benchmarks cover only subsets of these tasks, lack high-quality 3D ground truth, and do not systematically evaluate the role of depth maps or multi-view inputs. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a comprehensive data generation pipeline, a new SFT dataset (CA-VQA), and a challenging evaluation benchmark for indoor 3D spatial understanding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a reference RGB image at 672×672 (with 4 static sub-image splits for higher effective resolution), optionally up to 4 preceding support frames with their camera intrinsics and relative poses provided as interleaved JSON strings, and a text question. Depth can be supplied either as a full colorized depth map encoded through a dedicated connector, or as per-object metric depth values retrieved via a tool-use function call at inference time. Outputs span spatial relationship judgments, metric distance/size estimates (in meters), 2D and 3D bounding boxes, object counts, multi-choice selections, and chain-of-thought responses that explicitly predict intermediate depth values before producing the final answer.

## Method

MM-Spatial is built on the MM1.5-3B architecture (DFN-CLIP image encoder, C-Abstractor connector, decoder-only LLM) and is fine-tuned via supervised learning on ~10M QA pairs generated from the CA-1M dataset, which provides gravity-aligned 7-DOF 3D bounding boxes and open-set labels for every object in ARKitScenes. The data pipeline automatically produces template-based QA pairs across seven spatial task categories (counting, viewpoint-dependent relations, metric regression, 2D/3D grounding, binary, multi-choice, and external benchmark templates), with a blind-filtering step that removes examples solvable by text-only judges to reduce language-prior bias. The key architectural insight is that a simple tool-use mechanism—where the model predicts 2D boxes, calls a depth function to retrieve median depth within each box, and then reasons over the returned metric values—outperforms full depth-map encoding, which is limited to relative (normalized) depth. The model is trained as a generalist by mixing CA-VQA with general VQA, knowledge, text-rich, and 2D grounding data, and the paper demonstrates that SFT on high-quality 3D annotations alone enables the model to achieve monocular metric depth estimation accuracy (δ₁ = 89.4, AbsRel = 11.0) comparable to the dedicated DepthPro model, without any explicit depth supervision.

## Results

On the CA-VQA benchmark, MM-Spatial-3B achieves an average score of 47.0, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (22.8), LLaVA-OneVision-7B (24.6), and SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (23.9). With multi-view input and ground-truth depth via tool-use, the best configuration reaches 52.4 average (55.3 for the specialist variant). On CV-Bench, MM-Spatial-3B scores 91.2 average (93.1 with monocular depth tool-use), surpassing the much larger Cambrian-1-34B (76.9) and Mini-Gemini-HD-34B (75.4). On SpatialRGPT-Bench, MM-Spatial-3B trained on CA-VQA*+OSD with monocular depth achieves 81.0 average, outperforming SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (74.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Spatial-3B (47.0 avg) outperforms all commercial and open-source baselines on CA-VQA by a wide margin: +24.2 over GPT-4o (22.8), +22.4 over LLaVA-OneVision-7B (24.6), and +23.1 over SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (23.9), despite being a 3B-parameter model. The gap is most dramatic on 3D grounding (20.7 AP@15 vs. 0.0 for all baselines) and metric regression (40.0 Ego-Distance vs. 11.7 for GPT-4o). On CV-Bench, MM-Spatial-3B (91.2) surpasses the 34B Cambrian-1-34B (76.9) by 14.3 points, and with monocular depth tool-use reaches 93.1. On SpatialRGPT-Bench, MM-Spatial-3B trained on CA-VQA*+OSD with monocular depth (81.0) beats SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (74.4) by 6.6 points, and the specialist variant reaches 82.2. The paper notes that SpatialRGPT underperforms on CA-VQA due to its use of axis-aligned 3D boxes and relative-only depth encoding, while MM-Spatial benefits from high-quality yaw-oriented 3D ground truth and metric depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MM1.5 architecture (DFN-CLIP encoder, C-Abstractor connector, and SFT mixture) as its model backbone, and follows SpatialRGPT's depth connector design for its full-encoding depth variant. It departs from prior 3D spatial MLLM work (Spatial-VLM, Cube-LLM, SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot) in three key ways: (1) grounding all annotations in high-quality 3D ground truth from CA-1M/ARKitScenes rather than pseudo-annotations or synthetic data, (2) uniquely providing multi-view images and multiple depth sources (sensor-based FARO, LiDAR-fused ARKit, and monocular DepthPro) as input signals, and (3) covering a broader task spectrum including 3D grounding alongside relationship prediction and metric estimation. The tool-use approach for depth (querying per-object depth values as text) is a deliberate departure from SpatialRGPT's full depth-map encoding, which the paper shows to be substantially less effective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CA-VQA fills a clear gap as the first 3D spatial dataset combining high-quality GT, multi-view, and multiple depth modalities, and its benchmark is shown to be less susceptible to language priors than existing ones (validated via blind filtering). The finding that a 3B MLLM trained purely via SFT can rival a dedicated SOTA monocular depth estimator (DepthPro) on metric depth is a notable conceptual result suggesting MLLMs can acquire perceptual capabilities through data curation alone. Key limitations include the indoor-only scope (outdoor generalization requires scale augmentation), the underperformance of full depth encoding (suggesting the architecture for depth integration remains unsolved), and the fact that the depth estimation comparison is on the authors' own indoor benchmark. Open questions include whether the depth estimation ability transfers to outdoor scenes, how to better architect depth map encoding for MLLMs, and whether the tool-use paradigm generalizes to other sensor modalities.
