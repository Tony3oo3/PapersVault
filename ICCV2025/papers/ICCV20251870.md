## Problem

Cropping-based high-resolution MLLMs suffer from two coupled issues: visual redundancy, where the token count grows quadratically with resolution and overwhelms the LLM, and visual fragmentation, where non-overlapping sub-image cropping severs semantic continuity (e.g., splitting a word or object across crops). Existing remedies either bolt on expensive compression modules (QFormer, Abstractor) that demand massive pretraining data, or apply local window attention that cannot restore global coherence.

## Input / Output

FALCON accepts a high-resolution image (up to 16 sub-images of 384×384 px each, plus a full-image resize for global layout) and a text prompt. Rather than forwarding all patch tokens to the LLM, only 64 learnable visual-register tokens per sub-image (1024 total for 16 crops) are projected through a two-layer MLP and fed to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, yielding a 9× reduction in visual tokens. The model outputs a free-form text response for VQA, OCR, document understanding, and reasoning tasks.

## Method

FALCON introduces a visual register technique with two coupled mechanisms. ReCompact appends 64 learnable register tokens to each sub-image's patch tokens before the ViT; during self-attention the registers adaptively aggregate salient visual information while discarding background redundancy, so only the 64 register outputs (not all 576 patch tokens) are passed downstream, eliminating any separate compression module. ReAtten inserts a cross-ViT self-attention layer at every ViT depth that operates on the concatenated register states from all sub-images, enabling global information exchange across crops and restoring semantic continuity without the quadratic cost of full-image attention. The model is trained with a four-stage progressive pipeline (frozen-alignment → coarse low-res tuning → refined high-res tuning → high-res instruction tuning with ReAtten), using SigLIP-L/16-384px as the vision encoder and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the LLM backbone, with ReAtten parameters initialized from the corresponding ViT self-attention weights for stable convergence.

## Results

On MME-RealWorld, FALCON achieves a perception average of 50.3 (Avg-C: 48.0) and a reasoning average of 40.7 (Avg-C: 43.4), using only 64 visual tokens per sub-image (384²×16), a 9× compression over standard token counts. On high-resolution benchmarks, FALCON scores 64.4 on V*, 74.4 on DocVQA, 66.5 on TextVQA, and 68.8 on ChartQA, setting new state-of-the-art on all four. On broader-domain benchmarks, FALCON reaches 88.8 on ScienceQA, 88.0 on POPE, 76.1 on MMBench, and 73.3 on SEED-Bench, outperforming all open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALCON (64 tokens/sub-image) outperforms CogVLM2 (2304 tokens) by +4.5 on perception Avg-C and +3.6 on reasoning Avg-C, and surpasses IXC2.5 (up to 9600 tokens) by +6.1 on reasoning Avg-C, demonstrating that aggressive token compression does not sacrifice accuracy. Against commercial models, FALCON beats GPT-4o by +6.1 on perception Avg-C and +1.1 on reasoning Avg-C, and outperforms GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-pro by large margins (e.g., +15.2 perception Avg-C over Gemini-1.5-pro). FALCON also exceeds MiniCPM-V 2.5 (864 total tokens) by +4.0 on perception and +7.4 on reasoning Avg-C. The only open-source model that surpasses FALCON is LLaVA-OneVision (6561 tokens), which leads by +7.8 on perception Avg-C and +0.8 on reasoning Avg-C; Claude 3.5 Sonnet also edges ahead on both metrics. On the high-resolution benchmarks (Table 2), FALCON leads all baselines by substantial margins, e.g., +9.0 on DocVQA over Monkey and +14.5 on ChartQA over LLaVA-Next-Vicuna7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON builds directly on the observation from Darcet et al. (ICLR 2024) that pretrained ViTs naturally aggregate global information into specific register tokens via self-attention, repurposing this mechanism as a compression tool rather than merely an artifact-absorbing device. It departs from the dominant cropping-based high-resolution MLLM paradigm (LLaVA-HD, Monkey, TextMonkey) in two ways: (1) ReCompact eliminates the need for a post-encoding compression module (QFormer, ReSampler, pooling) by letting the ViT itself produce a compact representation through shared visual registers, and (2) ReAtten replaces local-window or redundant-pyramid strategies for cross-sub-image continuity with a lightweight cross-ViT attention over the compact register tokens. The progressive four-stage training pipeline is positioned as a practical enabler, requiring only 3.5M pretraining samples versus the 129M–1.4B used by Abstractor-like modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× visual token reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA on high-resolution benchmarks (MME-RealWorld, DocVQA, ChartQA) offers a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying MLLMs in latency- and memory-constrained settings. The finding that a simple register-based mechanism outperforms more parameter-heavy Abstractor modules under limited training data challenges the assumption that complex cross-attention compressors are necessary, potentially reshaping how future MLLMs handle token budgets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video inputs, whether the 64-register sweet spot holds at substantially higher resolutions (beyond 16 sub-images), and whether the progressive training pipeline can be simplified or replaced by a single-stage recipe.
