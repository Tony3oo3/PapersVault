## Problem

End-to-end driving methods predict trajectories but lack effective online evaluation to ensure safety. Existing evaluation approaches are either rule-based (sensitive to perception errors and not end-to-end optimizable) or model-free (assess trajectories using only the current state without anticipating future consequences). The paper addresses the gap that trajectory evaluation fundamentally requires predicting the future outcomes of each candidate trajectory, a capability absent from prior end-to-end frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (front view concatenated with center-cropped front-left and front-right views at 256×1024) and a 64m×64m LiDAR point cloud, encoded into a compact BEV feature map of spatial size 8×8. It outputs a single selected driving trajectory chosen from 256 K-Means-initialized candidates, along with intermediate per-trajectory rewards (one imitation reward plus five simulation rewards: NC, DAC, TTC, Comfort, Ego Progress) and predicted future BEV states at t+2s and t+4s. The final trajectory is selected by maximizing a weighted combination of log-rewards defined in Eq. 4.

## Method

WoTE comprises four modules: a TransFuser-style BEV encoder producing the current state, a trajectory refinement head using cross-attention between 256 K-Means anchors and the BEV state, a BEV World Model (2-layer transformer encoder) that recurrently predicts future BEV states given state-action pairs, and a Reward Model (2D convolutions + global pooling + MLP) that scores each trajectory from its full temporal sequence of BEV states. The key architectural choice is operating the world model in BEV space rather than image space: the 8×8 spatial resolution enables a single-step feed-forward prediction (18.7 ms total latency) instead of multi-step diffusion denoising, and the compact representation allows direct supervision from off-the-shelf BEV-space traffic simulators (nuPlan) that generate ground-truth future semantic maps and rule-based rewards for each trajectory candidate. Training uses Focal Loss on decoded BEV semantic maps, BCE on simulation rewards, Cross Entropy on imitation rewards, and winner-take-all L1 loss on the single best trajectory, all jointly optimized with a ResNet34 backbone on 8 GPUs.

## Results

On the NAVSIM test set, WoTE achieves a PDMS of 88.3, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Hydra-MDP (86.5) by 1.8 points. Key sub-metrics include NC 98.5, DAC 96.8, EP 81.9, TTC 94.9, and Comfort 99.9. On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark (CARLA), WoTE achieves a Driving Score of 61.71 and a Success Rate of 31.36%, surpassing the TCP baseline (59.90 / 30.00%) and significantly outperforming UniAD (45.81 / 16.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WoTE outperforms all 10 baselines on NAVSIM PDMS. Against the closest competitor Hydra-MDP (model-free evaluation, 8192 trajectories), WoTE gains +1.8 PDMS (88.3 vs. 86.5) while using only 256 trajectories, demonstrating that model-based evaluation with future states is more effective than simply scaling trajectory count. Compared to DRAMA (85.5), the best single-trajectory method, WoTE improves by +2.8 PDMS, with the largest gains in DAC (+3.7) and EP (+1.8). Against TransFuser (84.0), the trajectory-prediction-only baseline, the improvement is +4.3 PDMS, confirming the value of the trajectory evaluation module. On Bench2Drive, WoTE improves Driving Score by +1.81 over TCP (61.71 vs. 59.90) and by +15.9 over UniAD (61.71 vs. 45.81), with a Success Rate of 31.36% versus 30.00% for TCP. Notably, WoTE achieves the best NC (98.5) and DAC (96.8) among all methods, indicating superior safety and drivable-area compliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

WoTE builds directly on TransFuser's multi-modal BEV encoder for sensor fusion and on Hydra-MDP's multi-trajectory K-Means anchor framework with dual imitation/simulation rewards. The key conceptual departure is replacing Hydra-MDP's model-free (current-state-only) trajectory evaluation with a model-based approach that uses a BEV-space world model to predict future states before scoring trajectories. This draws on the model-based RL literature (PPUU, Rails, MARL-CCE) but adapts it to the end-to-end driving setting by operating in compact BEV space rather than image space, and by leveraging off-the-shelf BEV traffic simulators (nuPlan) to generate multi-future supervision that is otherwise unavailable in real driving logs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating predicted future states into trajectory evaluation yields a consistent ~2.4 PDMS gain over current-state-only evaluation, and that the BEV-space world model achieves this at only 18.7 ms latency—making it viable for real-time deployment where image-level diffusion world models are not. The generalization finding (1024 unseen trajectory anchors outperforming 256 trained ones) suggests the learned world model captures transferable dynamics rather than memorizing specific trajectory patterns. Open questions include whether the 8×8 BEV representation is sufficient for long-horizon or highly reactive scenarios, whether the approach extends to truly interactive (reactive) closed-loop settings beyond Bench2Drive's non-reactive agents, and whether the nuPlan-based supervision transfers cleanly to other driving distributions.
