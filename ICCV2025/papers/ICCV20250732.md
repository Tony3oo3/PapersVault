## Problem

Existing visual object trackers trained on well-conditioned daytime data suffer severe performance degradation under adverse weather conditions such as nighttime, fog, and rain. Prior domain adaptation approaches are designed for a single weather condition and require redundant per-domain parameters, making them unable to generalize across multiple target domains within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a video frame sequence with an initial bounding box annotation on the first frame. Template and search regions are cropped to 128×128 and 256×256 pixels (2× and 4× the target box area) and processed by a ViT-Base backbone with 16×16 patches. The template feature is computed once from the first frame and cached for all subsequent frames, enabling 138 FPS inference on a single RTX 3090. The output is a predicted bounding box per frame via a lightweight FCN head with focal classification, L1, and GIoU regression losses.

## Method

UMDATrack combines three modules in a student-teacher framework. First, a Controllable Scenario Generator (CSG) uses Stable Diffusion-Turbo with ControlNet-style skip connections to synthesize unlabeled video frames in arbitrary weather conditions (dark, foggy, rainy) from source daytime frames guided by text prompts, requiring fewer than 2% of source domain frames. Second, a Domain-Customized Adapter (DCA) inserts a lightweight cross-attention block with a learnable token bank between the frozen ViT-Base encoder and the localization head, allowing rapid adaptation to each weather condition by training only the adapter for 50 epochs without retraining the backbone. Third, a Target-Aware Confidence Alignment (TCA) module formulates a position-sensitive optimal transport problem over the student and teacher response maps, combining confidence and spatial position costs solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm to enforce localization consistency across domains. Training proceeds in two stages: a 250-epoch backbone stage with joint target supervision and PSOT loss, followed by a 50-epoch DCA fine-tuning stage, with teacher weights updated via EMA (α=0.99) each epoch.

## Results

On the real-world NAT2021 nighttime dataset, UMDATrack achieves 54.58 AUC and 70.78 Precision, outperforming the second-best tracker ARTrackV2 (53.13 AUC) by 1.45 points. On UAVDark70, it reaches 60.05 AUC and 73.35 Precision, surpassing the runner-up by 1.83 AUC and 1.4 Precision. On the multi-weather AVisT benchmark, UMDATrack leads with 60.50 AUC and 59.01 Precision. On synthetic datasets (e.g., DTB70-Dark), UMDATrack achieves 66.07 AUC and 85.72 Precision, exceeding the second-best by 3.06% AUC and 4.15% Precision, while running at 138 FPS with only 18G MACs and 65M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMDATrack outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against single-domain cross-domain trackers (UDAT-CAR, SAM-DA, MLKD-Track), the gains are most dramatic: on AVisT, UMDATrack achieves 60.50 AUC versus 38.91 (UDAT-CAR) and 37.36 (SAM-DA), confirming the limitation of single-weather adaptation. Among generic trackers, UMDATrack leads ARTrackV2 by 1.45 AUC on NAT2021 and 1.83 AUC on UAVDark70, while also running 43 FPS faster (138 vs. 95 FPS) with fewer parameters (65M vs. 126M). On synthetic foggy benchmarks (DTB70-Foggy), UMDATrack surpasses the second-best DCPT by 3.96% AUC and 5.90% Precision. For synthetic data generation quality (Table 7), the text-guided CSG module outperforms CycleGAN (SSIM 0.920 vs. 0.895) and UNIT (SSIM 0.920 vs. 0.875) while yielding the highest downstream AUC (54.58 vs. 51.10 and 50.23).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the OSTrack ViT-based template-search tracking paradigm and extends it with a student-teacher domain adaptation pipeline, but departs from prior single-domain trackers (UDAT, DCPT, SAM-DA) by unifying multiple adverse weather conditions (fog, dark, rain) under one framework. For data synthesis, it replaces GAN-based or manual enhancement approaches with a text-conditioned Stable Diffusion-Turbo generator (leveraging ControlNet-style skip connections), and introduces optimal transport (Sinkhorn) for confidence alignment between teacher and student networks—a repurposing of OT from general domain adaptation to tracking-specific localization consistency. The Domain-Customized Adapter (DCA) draws on learnable token bank / cross-attention mechanisms to enable per-domain adaptation without retraining the frozen backbone, distinguishing it from methods that require separate feature alignment per weather condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is notable: synthesizing fewer than 2% of source frames and training all weather-condition adapters in roughly 1.5 days, while achieving 138 FPS inference with only 65M parameters, makes this approach deployable on resource-constrained platforms like UAVs where prior cross-domain trackers struggle. However, the 'unified' framing is somewhat limited—the DCA is still instantiated per weather condition, and the framework has only been validated on three specific domains (fog, dark, rain), leaving open how it scales to more diverse or compound conditions (e.g., nighttime fog, heavy rain with glare). A key open question is whether the diffusion-synthesized data quality remains a bottleneck as domain complexity increases, and whether the optimal transport alignment generalizes beyond the small-batch confidence distributions tested here.
