## Problem

Existing multi-view image generation methods require invasive modifications to pre-trained T2I diffusion models and full fine-tuning on scarce 3D data, causing high computational costs and quality degradation. Adapter-based alternatives like NVS-Adapter train new modules from scratch, lacking the fundamental image priors needed for effective multi-view consistency, and are restricted to single-image input without support for text prompts or geometry guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adapter accepts text prompts, reference images (encoded via a frozen U-Net at t=0), camera raymaps, or concatenated position+normal maps as conditioning, and produces multi-view consistent images at 512×512 (SD2.1) or 768×768 (SDXL) resolution. It is plug-and-play: the adapter can be inserted into personalized (LoRA, DreamBooth), few-step distilled, or ControlNet-augmented T2I models without retraining them. Downstream, the generated views feed into NeuS for 3D mesh reconstruction, back-projection for UV texture maps, or iterative multi-round generation for arbitrary view synthesis.

## Method

MV-Adapter duplicates the pre-trained U-Net's spatial self-attention layers to create new multi-view attention and optional image cross-attention layers, organized in a parallel (not serial) architecture so that the new layers receive the same input features as the original self-attention and can be initialized with its learned weights, inheriting image priors without relearning. A unified condition guider (a lightweight convolutional encoder) maps camera raymaps or geometry maps (position + normal) into multi-scale spatial features added to the U-Net at corresponding resolutions. For image-conditioned generation, the frozen pre-trained U-Net encodes the reference image at timestep 0, and its multi-scale self-attention features are injected via the decoupled image cross-attention layers. Different multi-view attention strategies are selected per application: row-wise attention for 3D object generation at 0° elevation, row-wise plus column-wise for texture generation with top/bottom views, and full self-attention for arbitrary view generation.

## Results

On image-to-multi-view generation (GSO dataset), MV-Adapter with SDXL and parallel attention achieves PSNR 22.13, SSIM 0.882, and LPIPS 0.100, outperforming all 7 baselines. On text-to-multi-view generation (Objaverse), the SDXL variant attains FID 29.71, IS 16.38, and CLIP Score 33.17, surpassing MVDream (FID 32.15) and SPAD (FID 48.79). For 3D texture generation, SDXL-Image achieves the best FID (27.28) and KID (29.47) while running in 33 s, faster than all five texture baselines. In 3D reconstruction, the SDXL adapter reaches Chamfer Distance 0.0206 and Volume IoU 0.568 with only 490 M trainable parameters, versus Era3D's 3.1 B parameters and CD 0.0329.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-Adapter (SDXL, parallel attention) wins on every metric against all 14 baselines across the four evaluation settings. In image-to-multi-view generation, it surpasses the strongest full-tuning baseline Era3D by +1.24 PSNR and −0.020 LPIPS, and dramatically outperforms the only other adapter-based method NVS-Adapter by +4.89 PSNR and −0.047 LPIPS, confirming that inheriting pre-trained self-attention priors is critical. In text-to-multi-view generation, it beats MVDream by 2.44 FID and SPAD by 19.08 FID, with the gap widening at SDXL resolution. For texture generation, the SDXL-Image model achieves FID 27.28 vs. the best baseline SyncMVD (36.13) while running 1.5× faster (33 s vs. 50 s), and is 12.8× faster than Text2Tex (421 s). In 3D reconstruction, the SDXL adapter reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0329 (Era3D-SD2.1) to 0.0206 and improves Volume IoU from 0.5118 to 0.5682, while using only 490 M trainable parameters versus Era3D's 3.1 B — a 6.3× reduction in training parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV-Adapter departs from the dominant paradigm of multi-view diffusion (MVDream, SPAD, Era3D, Zero123++) that invasively modifies the base T2I model's self-attention layers and requires full fine-tuning, instead duplicating those layers into a parallel adapter that preserves the original feature space. The row-wise attention mechanism is inherited from Era3D, and the adapter-based formulation follows NVS-Adapter, but the key architectural departure is the parallel (rather than serial) arrangement of new attention layers, which enables effective weight initialization from pre-trained self-attention and avoids the domain-shift problem that plagues serial stacking. This design choice is what makes the adapter compatible with the broader T2I derivative ecosystem (LoRA, DreamBooth, ControlNet, distilled models) without retraining those components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing trainable parameters from 3.1B (Era3D on SDXL) to 490M while achieving superior 3D reconstruction quality (CD 0.0206 vs. infeasible for Era3D-SDXL), MV-Adapter makes high-resolution multi-view generation accessible on consumer hardware and compatible with the existing T2I toolchain. The unified condition encoder supporting both camera and geometry guidance opens a path toward a single model handling texturing, 3D generation, and arbitrary view synthesis. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video-based multi-view generation, whether the Objaverse training distribution limits real-world performance, and whether the parallel architecture insight transfers to other adapter-based extensions beyond multi-view consistency.
