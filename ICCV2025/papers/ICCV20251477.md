## Problem

State-of-the-art video embedders like MAGVIT-v2 are limited to 4× temporal compression because the conventional encoder-decoder (VAE) objective demands pixel-faithful reconstruction, creating a fundamental trade-off between compression ratio and fidelity. This paper argues that for latent diffusion models, the embedder only needs to produce visually plausible reconstructions rather than exact copies, and that this relaxed criterion can unlock substantially higher compression without degrading downstream generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251477/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes video chunks of k+1 frames (where k is the temporal compression ratio, up to 32) at arbitrary H×W resolution and produces two 8-channel latent frames: a content latent z_c (encoding only the first frame due to causal 3D convolutions) and a motion latent z_m (compressing the remaining k frames), each at H/8 × W/8 spatial resolution. The DiT decoder operates in pixel space with patch size 8 and reconstructs the full video from these two latents, supporting not only reconstruction but also temporal interpolation and extrapolation by redefining the target. The content-aware positional encoding allows decoding at resolutions and aspect ratios unseen during training.

## Method

REGEN replaces the conventional VAE-based encoder-decoder with an encoder-generator framework: a 3D causal convolutional encoder (same architecture as MAGVIT-v2) paired with a 24-block DiT decoder trained end-to-end under a diffusion noise-prediction objective. The key architectural contribution is a latent conditioning module C_e that generates content-aware positional embeddings from the latents, replacing DiT's fixed positional encoding. Specifically, a SIREN network M_t maps the temporal coordinate t_f into a feature vector modulated by the motion latent z_m at each spatial location, which is channel-concatenated with the content latent z_c value and projected to the DiT hidden dimension via a linear layer with RMSNorm. This design eliminates the need for fixed spatial/temporal PE, enabling generalization to arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios, and provides a strong conditioning signal that allows the decoder to perform high-quality reconstruction with as few as one sampling step. The model is trained jointly on reconstruction, interpolation, and extrapolation objectives for ~100K iterations per compression ratio.

## Results

At the standard 4× temporal compression (8×8×4), REGEN achieves 32.94 PSNR / 0.857 SSIM / 22.40 rFVD on MCL-JCV and 30.25 PSNR / 0.801 SSIM / 48.38 rFVD on DAVIS 2019 (512×512), outperforming all SOTA 8-channel video embedders. At the paper's headline 32× temporal compression (8×8×32), REGEN reaches 26.05 PSNR / 0.695 SSIM / 265.96 rFVD on MCL-JCV 256×256, while MAGVIT-v2 drops to 22.97 PSNR / 0.573 SSIM / 536.01 rFVD and suffers out-of-memory at 512×512. The performance gap between REGEN and MAGVIT-v2 widens monotonically as temporal compression increases from 8× to 32×, confirming that the generative decoder bypasses the conventional compression–reconstruction trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REGEN outperforms all four baselines on every metric at the standard 4× temporal compression. On MCL-JCV, REGEN leads VidTok (the strongest baseline) by +0.88 PSNR and −16.45 rFVD, and leads MAGVIT-v2 by +1.45 PSNR and −6.23 rFVD. On DAVIS 2019, the margins are even larger: REGEN beats VidTok by +1.58 PSNR and −18.86 rFVD. At the paper's primary focus of high temporal compression (Table 1), REGEN's advantage over MAGVIT-v2 grows with compression: at 8× temporal the rFVD gap is 20.35 (85.37 vs 105.72 on MCL-JCV 256×256), at 16× it widens to 31.46 (174.29 vs 205.75), and at 32× it reaches 270.05 (265.96 vs 536.01). MAGVIT-v2 additionally fails entirely at 32× compression on 512×512 inputs due to GPU memory exhaustion in its 3D-convolution decoder, whereas REGEN completes evaluation. OmniTokenizer, WF-VAE, and VidTok are only compared at 4× compression and are not extended to higher ratios, so REGEN's generative-decoder advantage is most clearly demonstrated against MAGVIT-v2 at aggressive compression settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

REGEN departs from the standard encoder-decoder (VAE) paradigm for video tokenizers by replacing the convolutional decoder with a diffusion transformer (DiT) that synthesizes plausible video content from compact latents, drawing on the diffusion autoencoder concept previously explored in the image domain (Preechakul et al., Betker et al., Hudson et al.). The encoder architecture follows MAGVIT-v2's causal 3D convolution design, while the decoder leverages DiT's superior video modeling capability as demonstrated in Sora, Snap Video, and CogVideoX. A key technical departure is the content-aware positional encoding module, which uses SIREN-based implicit neural representations to generate spatiotemporal conditioning from the latents themselves, eliminating fixed positional embeddings and enabling arbitrary-resolution generalization. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to explore diffusion transformer autoencoders for learning highly compact video latent spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 32× temporal compression (8× beyond SOTA embedders) with competitive reconstruction quality directly translates to a ~5× reduction in latent frames for text-to-video generation, substantially lowering training and inference costs for latent diffusion models. The content-aware PE mechanism solving the arbitrary-resolution generalization problem and the ability to perform one-step sampling without external distillation are practically valuable properties for deployment. However, the T2V validation is limited to a small-scale 5B model with modest training iterations, leaving open whether the ultra-compact latent space scales to frontier video generation quality. Remaining challenges include the high training cost of diffusion-based decoders, residual chunking artifacts, and the quality trade-off of operating in pixel space with a large patch size (8).
