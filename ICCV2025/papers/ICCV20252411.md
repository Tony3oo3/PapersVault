## Problem

VLMs perform well on static visual understanding and general video comprehension but fundamentally lack the ability to reason about dynamic spatiotemporal interactions—tracking object motion, rotations, and perspective shifts in 4D (3D space + time). No existing benchmark specifically probes this 4D spatiotemporal reasoning capability, leaving the gap between current VLMs and human-level dynamic scene understanding unquantified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252411/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs a short video clip (3–8 s for real footage, ~5 s for synthetic) with a multiple-choice question targeting one of four spatiotemporal dimensions: translational direction, rotational direction, spatiotemporal counting, or false-positive detection of nonexistent events. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot under two prompting regimes—direct output (DO) and chain-of-thought (CoT)—with answers graded by LLM-as-Judge (GPT-o3 and o4-mini) to handle variability in CoT reasoning traces rather than relying on string matching.

## Method

VLM4D is constructed from 1,000 videos (real egocentric from Ego4D, real exocentric from DAVIS/YouTube-VOS, and synthetic from Cosmos with Mojito-style trajectory control) annotated with 1,800+ human-verified QA pairs across four spatiotemporal categories. The paper evaluates 23 state-of-the-art VLMs (proprietary and open-source, 2B–72B parameters) in zero-shot, revealing a large gap between the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 62%) and human performance (98.8%). To probe solutions, the authors explore two directions: (1) spatiotemporal supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL on a real-data split, yielding ~12–13 point accuracy gains, and (2) lifting InternVideo2's 2D feature space into a 4D Gaussian feature field via Feature4X, which improves accuracy over both original 2D video and rendered global-view video inputs by providing structured scene-level representations during inference.

## Results

On the VLM4D benchmark (1000 videos, 1800+ QA pairs), the best-performing model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 62.0% overall accuracy, followed by GPT-4o at 57.5%, while human performance reaches 98.8%—a gap of over 36 percentage points. Among open-source models, Llama-4-Scout-17B (54.1%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.0%) lead, with the weakest model (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B) scoring only 30.1%. Performance varies substantially across sub-categories: egocentric vs. exocentric real videos and directional vs. false-positive synthetic questions, indicating that no current VLM generalizes well across spatiotemporal reasoning dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro leads all 23 evaluated VLMs at 62.0% overall, outperforming GPT-4o (57.5%) by 4.5 points and Claude-Sonnet-4 (51.3%) by 10.7 points. Among open-source models, Llama-4-Scout-17B (54.1%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.0%) are the strongest, with the former even surpassing GPT-4o on the synthetic directional sub-task (53.3% vs. 49.5%). Proprietary models consistently outperform open-source counterparts on real exocentric videos (Gemini: 62.9% vs. best open-source Llama-4-Scout: 56.2%), but the gap narrows on synthetic data where Llama-4-Scout-17B (55.5%) edges out GPT-4o (49.9%). All models remain far below the 98.8% human baseline, with the largest deficits on egocentric real videos (best model: 64.6% vs. 99.6% human) and false-positive detection, where several models (e.g., Phi-4-Multimodal at 11.1%, InternVideo2-8B at 0.0%) perform at or near chance level. The 4D feature field reconstruction experiment (Table 3) shows that augmenting InternVideo2-8B with global feature fields improves accuracy from 36.0% to 37.4% (CoT) and 24.3% to 29.0% (DO), a modest but consistent gain over raw 2D video input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of video understanding benchmarks and 3D/4D spatial intelligence evaluation. Prior video QA benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, Video-Bench) assess temporal coherence and event comprehension but do not explicitly probe 4D spatiotemporal reasoning (3D space + time), while recent 3D spatial intelligence benchmarks [87] are restricted to static scenes. The paper builds on Feature4X [99] for its 4D feature field reconstruction experiments and on Mojito [32] for motion-controlled synthetic video generation via Cosmos, but its core contribution is a new evaluation axis rather than a new method. It departs from the LLM-generated benchmark paradigm by emphasizing human-annotated QA pairs, motivated by observed unreliability of automated spatiotemporal labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap—best VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro, 62%) vs. human (98.8%)—that is unlikely to close with scale alone, given that CoT prompting provides no consistent benefit and that spatiotemporal labels in popular SFT datasets are accurate in fewer than 10% of cases. The 4D feature field and targeted SFT directions show promise but remain preliminary: feature fields require per-scene optimization, and SFT gains are modest (~12–16 points on a 20% test split). Open questions include whether native 4D representations (rather than post-hoc feature lifting) can be integrated into VLM pretraining, and whether the benchmark's 1000-video scale is sufficient to capture the full breadth of real-world spatiotemporal reasoning.
