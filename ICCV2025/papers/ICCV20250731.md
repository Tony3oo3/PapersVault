## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks on MLLMs rely on complex adversarial optimization or carefully crafted image-text prompts, yet achieve low success rates against commercial closed-source models that deploy additional outer safety guardrails. This paper identifies an unexplored vulnerability: MLLMs exhibit a 'Shuffle Inconsistency' where their comprehension ability remains intact for shuffled harmful instructions while their safety alignment mechanisms fail to detect the shuffled content, creating a gap that can be exploited with minimal perturbation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-existing harmful text-image pair (from benchmarks like MM-safetybench, HADES, or SafeBench) as the starting point. The text is split into individual words and the image is divided into 4 patch blocks; both are then randomly shuffled. The output is the selected shuffled text-image pair that maximizes the toxic score (1–5 scale, judged by ChatGPT-3.5) of the target MLLM's response, with attack success defined as a toxic score ≥ 4. The entire optimization is black-box, requiring only query access to the target model and the judge model, with a maximum of 10 iterations.

## Method

SI-Attack exploits the Shuffle Inconsistency by jointly shuffling the harmful text at word level (Shuffle_w) and the harmful image at patch level (Shuffle_p, 4 blocks), then applying a query-based black-box optimization loop. In each iteration, a new random shuffle is generated, the target MLLM is queried, and a toxic judge model scores the response against the original harmful intent. The algorithm retains the shuffled pair with the highest toxic score and terminates early if the score exceeds the success threshold S_τ = 4, otherwise returning the best pair after max 10 iterations. Ablations confirm that both text and image shuffling contribute (text shuffling alone yields higher scores than image shuffling alone), and that the query-based selection is essential—random shuffling without optimization achieves only 28.87% ASR versus 80.41% with optimization. The approach requires no gradient access, no adversarial training, and no modification of the harmful content itself, making it applicable to both open-source and closed-source MLLMs.

## Results

On the primary MM-safetybench benchmark, SI-Attack achieves ASR of 68.57% (toxic 3.85) on GPT-4o, 47.20% (toxic 3.21) on Claude-3.5-Sonnet, 71.25% (toxic 3.96) on Gemini-1.5-Pro, and 68.63% (toxic 3.82) on Qwen-VL-Max, compared to QR-Attack's 20.77%, 7.50%, 21.07%, and 33.04% respectively. On open-source models, SI-Attack reaches 62.68% ASR on LLaVA-NEXT, 62.44% on MiniGPT-4, 71.01% on InternVL-2, and 40.77% on VLGuard (a safety fine-tuned model). On SafeBench against Figstep, SI-Attack improves ASR from 11.80% to 59.20% on GPT-4o and from 50.60% to 80.20% on Gemini-1.5-Pro, using only 10 queries per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SI-Attack consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model and benchmark. Against QR-Attack (MM-safetybench), SI-Attack improves ASR by 47.80 pp on GPT-4o (20.77→68.57%), 39.70 pp on Claude-3.5-Sonnet (7.50→47.20%), 50.18 pp on Gemini-1.5-Pro (21.07→71.25%), and 35.59 pp on Qwen-VL-Max (33.04→68.63%); on open-source models the gains are 18.69–35.24 pp. Against HADES (HADES benchmark), SI-Attack lifts GPT-4o ASR from 5.33% to 60.53% and Qwen-VL-Max from 10.27% to 82.67%, while HADES is nearly ineffective on Claude-3.5-Sonnet (0.40% ASR) where SI-Attack still reaches 10.27%. Against Figstep (SafeBench), SI-Attack surpasses Figstep on all eight models, with the largest margin on GPT-4o (11.80→59.20% ASR) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (50.60→80.20% ASR). Notably, SI-Attack also breaks through VLGuard, a model specifically fine-tuned for jailbreak defense, achieving 40.77% ASR versus QR-Attack's 9.52%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM jailbreak paradigm of adversarial perturbations (Bailey et al., Niu et al., Qi et al.) and typography-based attacks (Figstep, Liu et al.) by exploiting a structural property of the models themselves: Shuffle Inconsistency. It builds on prior observations that BERT and vision-language models retain comprehension under token/patch shuffling (Hessel et al., 2021; Hessel et al., 2022), but is the first to systematically demonstrate that this preserved comprehension creates a safety gap specifically for harmful instructions. The query-based black-box optimization loop is conceptually similar to prior query-based attacks (Li et al., Zhao et al.) but is applied to selecting among random shuffles rather than optimizing continuous perturbations, making it far simpler in design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivial, non-adversarial transformation (word/patch shuffling) reliably bypasses safety alignment in both open-source and commercial closed-source MLLMs—including the safety fine-tuned VLGuard—raises urgent questions about the robustness of current safety training, which appears to rely on surface-level pattern matching rather than deep semantic understanding of harmful intent. Open questions include whether defenses can be made invariant to such permutations, whether the effect generalizes beyond English and specific model architectures, and whether the moderate ASR on the most robust closed-source models (e.g., ~47% on Claude-3.5-Sonnet) represents a fundamental ceiling or can be pushed higher with more queries. The paper also shows other mutations (replacement, deletion, masking) produce similar but weaker effects, suggesting a broader class of vulnerabilities in safety generalization.
