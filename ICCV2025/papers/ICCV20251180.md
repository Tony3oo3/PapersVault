## Problem

Existing streaming 3D reconstruction methods like Spann3R struggle with long input sequences because their memory is attended only once per iteration, becomes spatially redundant as frames accumulate, and their training strategy does not support adaptation to longer contexts. This limits real-time reconstruction to short sequences, whereas practical applications (robotics, autonomous driving) require processing tens to hundreds of frames with near-constant memory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming sequence of RGB images at 224×224 resolution, processing one frame at a time in a recurrent fashion with near-constant GPU memory (~22 FPS on an RTX 3090). Each new frame produces a per-pixel 3D pointmap via a DPT head, and camera poses can be derived from the predicted geometry. The key constraint is that the model must maintain a fixed-size memory (10-frame short-term window + 3000-token long-term 3D spatial memory) regardless of how many frames have been observed.

## Method

LONG3R uses a ViT-Large encoder (initialized from DUSt3R) followed by a Coarse Decoder of PairwiseBlocks that interacts the current frame with the previous frame's refined features. An attention-based memory gating module then filters the spatio-temporal memory using a threshold (τ=5×10⁻⁴) on cross-attention weights, retaining only tokens relevant to the current observation. A Dual-Source Refined Decoder alternates between PairwiseBlocks (interacting with next-frame coarse features) and MemoryBlocks (interacting with filtered memory) to enable coarse-to-fine refinement. The long-term 3D spatial memory uses an adaptive voxel size (computed from predicted 3D positions) and prunes to one token per voxel by accumulated attention weight, preventing spatial redundancy. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: first 5-frame sequences with full training, then 10- and 32-frame sequences with the ViT encoder frozen, progressively building long-sequence capability.

## Results

On the Replica_200 long-sequence benchmark (200 frames), LONG3R achieves 11.93 cm mean accuracy and 2.73 cm mean completeness, substantially outperforming Spann3R (16.29/4.02) and CUT3R (28.3/6.61) while matching the offline MV-DUSt3R+ (11.79/5.64) at ~21 FPS versus ~1 FPS. On 7Scenes, LONG3R attains 2.57 cm mean accuracy and 2.08 cm completeness, the best among all streaming methods and competitive with offline approaches. For camera pose estimation, LONG3R reduces ATE to 8.72 cm on 7Scenes and 6.44 cm on ScanNet, outperforming Spann3R (12.64/9.83) and CUT3R (12.40/14.27) by 30–55%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary streaming baseline Spann3R, LONG3R wins on all long-sequence metrics: on Replica₂₀₀ it reduces accuracy error from 16.29 to 11.93 cm (−27%) and completeness from 4.02 to 2.73 cm (−32%), while maintaining the same ~21 FPS. Against CUT3R, the advantage is even more pronounced on long sequences—CUT3R's accuracy degrades to 28.3 cm on Replica₂₀₀ versus LONG3R's 11.93 cm, a 2.4× gap, reflecting CUT3R's drift accumulation. On shorter 7Scenes sequences, LONG3R (2.57 cm Acc) slightly beats Spann3R (3.42 cm) and substantially beats CUT3R (7.73 cm), while remaining competitive with offline methods like MV-DUSt3R+ (2.93 cm) at 7× the speed. Against offline baselines DUSt3R and MASt3R, LONG3R trades a small accuracy margin (DUSt3R: 4.99 cm vs. 11.93 cm on Replica₂₀₀) for real-time streaming capability (~21 FPS vs. ≤3 FPS). MV-DUSt3R+ achieves the best offline accuracy (11.79 cm on Replica₂₀₀) but at only ~1 FPS, making LONG3R the best accuracy-to-speed tradeoff among all compared methods. For camera pose estimation, LONG3R outperforms both Spann3R and CUT3R on 7Scenes (ATE 8.72 vs. 12.64/12.40) and ScanNet (6.44 vs. 9.83/14.27), while remaining competitive on the dynamic TUM dataset (5.40 vs. 5.66/6.25).

## Relation to Prior Work

LONG3R extends the streaming 3D reconstruction paradigm initiated by Spann3R (which introduced recurrent processing with a spatial memory bank) and the end-to-end pointmap prediction framework of DUSt3R/MASt3R. It departs from Spann3R by addressing three specific architectural limitations: single-pass memory attention, spatial redundancy in accumulated memory, and a training strategy that does not scale to long sequences. The 3D spatio-temporal memory with adaptive voxel-size pruning and the dual-source refined decoder represent a conceptual shift from Spann3R's flat token memory toward a geometrically-aware, resolution-adaptive storage scheme, while the two-stage curriculum training strategy is borrowed from general deep learning practice but applied here specifically to progressive sequence-length adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LONG3R demonstrates that real-time (≈22 FPS) streaming 3D reconstruction can handle 100–200 frame sequences without the catastrophic drift that plagues prior streaming methods, which is practically important for robotics, autonomous driving, and AR applications where loop closure and post-optimization are infeasible. The ablation showing that removing the 3D spatial memory causes a ~5× accuracy degradation on long sequences (65.75 vs 11.93 Acc on Replica₂₀₀) underscores that geometrically-structured memory is not merely an optimization but a necessity for long-horizon consistency. Key limitations remain: predictions are anchored to the first frame (degrading under large viewpoint changes), and the model struggles with highly dynamic scenes due to the absence of dynamic training data. Open questions include whether the adaptive voxel mechanism generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes and whether the framework can incorporate loop-closure or global alignment without sacrificing streaming latency.
