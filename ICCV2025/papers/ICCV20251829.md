## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: relative and scale-invariant depth models generalize well but produce per-frame scale/shift ambiguity that causes flickering and 3D inconsistency in video, while metric depth models provide real-world measurements but struggle with cross-domain generalization. Video-based approaches like DepthCrafter achieve temporal consistency but require processing entire sequences at only 0.45 FPS, making them impractical for online applications. The paper identifies scene-consistent and scale-invariant (SCSI) depth as the missing paradigm that combines per-scene scale consistency with cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a per-pixel depth map in SCSI space (range 0–1 via sigmoid activation, scaled by a predefined maximum depth for metric interpretation). Inference is fully online at 5.77 FPS on an H20 GPU at 1920×1080 resolution with FP16, requiring no access to past or future frames. At evaluation time, each scene's predictions are aligned to ground truth using a single RANSAC-determined scale factor with zero shift, enforcing the SCSI constraint across all frames in a video.

## Method

StableDepth employs a shared ViT-L encoder with two independent DPT decoders: the primary decoder (D1, sigmoid activation) predicts metric-scale depth supervised by SiLogLoss on labeled Hypersim data, while the auxiliary decoder (D2, ReLU activation) predicts disparity-space depth aligned to pseudo-labels generated by DepthCrafter (a video diffusion model fine-tuned on Stable Video Diffusion) over unlabeled SA-V video. The key training mechanism is scene-level normalization: both pseudo-labels and model predictions for an entire video clip are normalized by the video's median (shift) and mean absolute deviation (scale), then supervised with an L1 alignment loss, ensuring a unique scale/shift per scene rather than per frame. The combined loss weights the unlabeled video term with a cosine-scheduled coefficient γ, and the dual-decoder design decouples depth accuracy from temporal consistency learning, avoiding the accuracy degradation observed when a single decoder must serve both objectives. This architecture achieves MTD scores comparable to DepthCrafter (0.003482 vs 0.003479) while running 13× faster with online frame-by-frame inference.

## Results

On the standard frame-interval-1 evaluation, StableDepth achieves AbsRel of 0.280 (δ1=0.617) on Sintel, 0.063 (δ1=0.979) on Bonn, 0.094 (δ1=0.921) on ScanNet v2, and 0.112 (δ1=0.900) on KITTI, outperforming DepthCrafter by 13.2%, 86.8%, 39.3%, and 8.2% in AbsRel respectively. Performance remains stable across frame intervals (1, 4, 8), with less than 7% degradation at interval 8. On the DAVIS temporal consistency benchmark, StableDepth achieves MTD=0.003482—nearly identical to DepthCrafter's 0.003479—while running at 5.77 FPS (13× faster) with online, frame-by-frame inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DepthCrafter (the primary video-consistency baseline), StableDepth wins on all four datasets and both metrics, with the largest gap on Sintel (AbsRel 0.280 vs. 2.959) and Bonn (0.063 vs. 0.311), while also matching its temporal consistency (MTD 0.003482 vs. 0.003479) at 13× the inference speed (5.77 vs. 0.45 FPS) and with online capability. Against ZoeDepth, StableDepth dominates across all datasets, e.g. reducing Sintel AbsRel from 0.454 to 0.280 and KITTI AbsRel from 0.216 to 0.112. Against Metric3D, the picture is more nuanced: Metric3D achieves lower AbsRel on Bonn (0.055 vs. 0.063), ScanNet (0.059 vs. 0.094, though it was trained on ScanNet), and KITTI (0.067 vs. 0.112), but StableDepth edges it out on Sintel (0.280 vs. 0.372) and, critically, provides scene-level temporal consistency that Metric3D, as a single-frame model, does not offer. The paper's core claim is therefore not per-frame accuracy superiority over metric depth models, but achieving comparable accuracy while adding the scene-consistent, scale-invariant property essential for 3D reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableDepth builds directly on the Depth Anything V2 (DAv2) architecture (ViT-L encoder + DPT decoder) and repurposes DepthCrafter, a video diffusion model, as a pseudo-label generator rather than a direct depth estimator. Conceptually, it departs from both the relative-depth line (per-frame scale/shift invariance, e.g., DAv2, MiDaS) and the metric-depth line (absolute scale, e.g., Metric3D, UniDepth) by introducing the SCSI formulation: scale-invariant within a scene but consistent across frames. The dual-decoder design—where one DPT head handles supervised metric regression and a second DPT head aligns to video-diffusion pseudo-labels in disparity space—is the key architectural departure from prior single-decoder depth networks, motivated by the observed conflict between accuracy and temporal smoothness when both objectives share one head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching video-diffusion temporal consistency (MTD ≈ 0.0035) at 13× the inference speed with online, frame-by-frame processing removes the primary deployment barrier for video-consistent depth in robotics, AR, and 3D reconstruction pipelines. A notable limitation is the dependence on DepthCrafter pseudo-labels, which degrade on distant objects, forcing the authors to supplement with additional labeled datasets (VKITTI2, LightWheelOcc, MatrixCity) to recover accuracy—raising the question of whether the pseudo-label source can be replaced or improved without retraining. Open questions include whether the SCSI formulation generalizes to longer sequences beyond the 16-frame training clips, whether the dual-decoder overhead can be reduced, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-view or stereo settings.
