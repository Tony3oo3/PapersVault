## Problem

Sparse-view 3D surface reconstruction remains challenging when input views have small overlaps or low texture. Generalization-based methods fail on unseen view configurations, while overfitting-based methods still produce rough or incomplete surfaces because existing geometric priors (monocular depth, on-surface feature consistency) are either inaccurate or fail to converge when surface positions are poorly constrained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252579/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 sparse RGB images (1600×1200 on DTU, 768×576 on BlendedMVS) with known camera poses, plus a COLMAP sparse point cloud used solely for calibrating monocular depth priors. It produces a per-scene neural implicit SDF that is extracted into a 3D mesh via standard marching cubes. The optimization is per-scene (overfitting-based), requiring ~100K iterations on a single GPU, and relies on a pre-trained Vis-MVSNet for feature extraction and a pre-trained monocular depth network for depth priors.

## Method

Built on the NeuS volume-rendering SDF framework, SparseRecon introduces two key losses. First, a volume rendering-based feature consistency loss: instead of comparing features only at estimated surface intersection points (as in MVSDF, NeuSurf, FatesGS), it computes cosine similarity between reference-image features and source-image features at every sampled point along each ray, then accumulates these similarities using volume rendering weights. This provides dense, well-conditioned supervision without requiring accurate surface localization. Second, an uncertainty-guided depth constraint: monocular depth priors are calibrated against COLMAP sparse points (rather than rendered depth as in MonoSDF), and the resulting depth loss is applied only where rendered depth confidence is low (computed via forward-backward projection consistency), preventing prior errors from corrupting well-constrained regions. A standard color consistency loss (pixel + SSIM patch warping from NeuralWarp) and Eikonal regularization complete the training objective.

## Results

On the DTU dataset with 3 small-overlapping views, SparseRecon achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.11, outperforming the best generalization-based method UFORecon (1.39) by 20% and the best overfitting-based methods S-VolSDF (1.91), FatesGS (2.22), and NeuSurf (2.26) by 42–51%. On BlendedMVS with 3 small-overlapping views, all generalization-based methods completely fail, while SparseRecon produces the most complete and detailed meshes among overfitting-based methods. The method also outperforms dense-view baselines (NeuS: 3.98, NeuralWarp: 2.93) under the sparse-view setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseRecon (mean CD 1.11) outperforms all 14 baselines on DTU with 3 small-overlapping views. Among generalization-based methods, it beats the strongest competitor UFORecon (1.39) by 20%, while other generalization-based methods (ReTR 2.72, VolRecon 2.75, GenS 5.67) degrade significantly under small-overlap conditions. Among overfitting-based methods, it surpasses S-VolSDF (1.91) by 42%, FatesGS (2.22) by 50%, NeuSurf (2.26) by 51%, and SparseCraft (2.16) by 49%. Dense-view methods (NeuS 3.98, NeuralWarp 2.93, MVSDF 3.03) also underperform, confirming that the sparse-view setting is fundamentally harder. On BlendedMVS, all generalization-based methods fail entirely, and SparseRecon produces visibly more complete meshes than S-VolSDF, NeuSurf, and FatesGS, which all exhibit significant surface defects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuS volume-rendering framework for neural implicit surface reconstruction by introducing two new loss terms: a volume rendering-based feature consistency loss (replacing the on-surface feature consistency used in MVSDF, NeuSurf, and FatesGS) and an uncertainty-guided depth prior constraint (improving upon MonoSDF's rendered-depth calibration). It also adopts NeuralWarp's pixel and patch warping losses for color consistency. Conceptually, the key departure from prior overfitting-based sparse-view methods is shifting feature consistency from a single surface intersection point to all sampled points along a ray, which the authors argue addresses convergence failures in small-overlap scenarios where surface point estimates are unreliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: reconstructing complete, smooth 3D surfaces from only 3 views with small overlaps, a setting where generalization-based methods fail entirely and prior overfitting-based methods produce rough or defective meshes. The volume rendering-based feature consistency is a natural but effective reformulation that provides denser supervision to the implicit field. However, the method still depends on accurate COLMAP camera poses and a pre-trained Vis-MVSNet feature extractor, and struggles with specular objects, leaving open the question of whether it can handle truly unstructured captures without reliable SfM initialization.
