## Problem

Zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition (ZSAR) requires aligning skeleton features with text features of action labels to predict unseen actions, but the inherent modality gap between skeletal motion patterns and high-level textual semantics severely limits generalization. Previous methods attempt direct alignment between separate skeleton and text latent spaces, which struggles to produce robust cross-modal representations. No prior work has exploited the cross-modal alignment power of diffusion models—where conditioning signals are naturally fused into a shared latent space during denoising—for this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence of shape T×V×M×C_in (temporal frames, joints, actors, joint dimensions) and text prompts derived from action labels as input, producing a predicted action class label for unseen categories. The skeleton encoder (Shift-GCN or ST-GCN) is pre-trained with cross-entropy and frozen, while the text encoder is a frozen CLIP model that yields both a global sentence-level feature and token-level local features. Inference is a single forward pass at a fixed timestep (t_test=25) with a fixed Gaussian noise sample, scoring each candidate label by the L2 distance between that noise and the DiT's predicted noise, rather than iteratively denoising.

## Method

TDSM repurposes a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) not for generation but as a conditional denoiser that implicitly fuses skeleton and text features in a unified latent space: the forward process adds Gaussian noise to the skeleton embedding, and the reverse process predicts the noise conditioned on both global and local CLIP text features via cross-attention. The key training signal is a novel triplet diffusion (TD) loss that minimizes the noise-prediction error for the correct (positive) text prompt while maximizing it for a randomly sampled incorrect (negative) prompt, with a margin term, thereby making the fused skeleton-text latent space discriminative. The total objective combines the standard diffusion denoising loss with the TD loss (weighted by λ=1.0), and the stochastic noise at each training step acts as a natural regularizer. At inference, a single denoising step at a fixed timestep suffices: the label whose text prompt yields the smallest noise-prediction error relative to a fixed test noise is selected, enabling efficient zero-shot classification without iterative sampling.

## Results

On the SynSE and PURLS benchmarks, TDSM achieves top-1 accuracy of 86.49% (NTU-60 55/5), 56.03% (NTU-60 48/12), 74.15% (NTU-120 110/10), and 65.06% (NTU-120 96/24), outperforming the previous best by 2.36–13.05 percentage points across all splits. On the SMIE benchmark, TDSM reaches 88.88% on NTU-60 (55/5), 69.47% on NTU-120 (110/10), and 70.76% on PKU-MMD (46/5), again leading all baselines. The largest margin (13.05 pp) appears on NTU-120 96/24, where TDSM (65.06%) surpasses PURLS (52.01%), demonstrating strong scalability to larger unseen-class sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDSM outperforms all eight baselines across every reported split. Against the strongest recent competitors, TDSM beats SA-DVAE (ECCV 2024) by 4.12 pp on NTU-60 55/5 (86.49 vs 82.37) and by 18.80 pp on NTU-120 110/10 (74.15 vs 68.77). Against PURLS (CVPR 2024), the margin ranges from 2.20 pp (NTU-120 110/10) to 13.05 pp (NTU-120 96/24: 65.06 vs 52.01), with the largest gains on splits with more unseen classes. Against STAR (ACM MM 2024), TDSM leads by 5.09 pp on NTU-60 55/5 and 10.76 pp on NTU-120 96/24. On the SMIE benchmark (Table 2), TDSM achieves 88.88% on NTU-60 (vs. SA-DVAE's 84.20%) and 69.47% on NTU-120 (vs. SA-DVAE's 50.67%), while on PKU-MMD it edges out STAR by 0.16 pp (70.76 vs 70.60). Older baselines (ReViSE, JPoSE, CADA-VAE, SynSE) are surpassed by 10–30+ pp, confirming that diffusion-based alignment substantially narrows the skeleton-text modality gap compared to direct VAE or contrastive alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDSM departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition, where prior methods (CADA-VAE, SynSE, SMIE, PURLS, STAR, SA-DVAE) attempt direct alignment between separate skeleton and text latent spaces. Instead, it repurposes the conditional denoising mechanism of diffusion models—specifically the DiT architecture and the latent-space formulation from LDMs/DDPM—as an implicit alignment tool, where text prompts condition the reverse diffusion process to fuse skeleton and text features into a unified space. The triplet diffusion loss is a novel reformulation of the classic triplet loss in the noise-prediction space, and the CLIP text encoder provides both global and local semantic features that guide denoising. This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion model conditioning and cross-modal metric learning, rather than generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that diffusion models can serve as pure alignment/discrimination engines for zero-shot recognition without any generative output, and the one-step inference at a fixed timestep makes it computationally practical. The large and consistent margins (up to 13.05 points) across NTU-60, NTU-120, and PKU-MMD suggest that the modality gap problem in ZSAR may be more tractable through implicit fusion than through explicit space mapping. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modality pairs (e.g., audio-text, point-cloud-text), whether the fixed single-step inference can be replaced by adaptive multi-step refinement for harder splits, and whether the reliance on a frozen GCN encoder limits ceiling performance. The stochastic noise as implicit regularization is an elegant side benefit that may generalize to other alignment tasks.
