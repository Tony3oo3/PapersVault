## Problem

The prior diffusion-based packing method GFPack lacks continuous rotation support, produces frequent polygon collisions (overlap ratios up to 1.43%), and has limited adaptability to arbitrary container boundaries. Its reliance on positional-only encoding and PointNeXt-based geometry extraction fails to capture orientation, topological, and local geometric features essential for accurate gradient field learning, while its distance-based graph must be rebuilt at every sampling step, causing high computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250979/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of 2D irregular polygons (each defined by an ordered sequence of contour vertices with computed internal angles) and a polygonal container boundary, typically an infinite horizontal strip of fixed height. It outputs SE(2) rigid transformations (translation and continuous rotation) for every polygon, producing a collision-free packing arrangement. During inference, the model generates multiple candidate solutions in parallel (batch sizes of 128 or 512) and selects the collision-free solution with the highest space utilization; rotations are encoded as (x, y, 1000·cos θ, 1000·sin θ) to maintain uniform numerical scale between translation and rotation spaces.

## Method

GFPack++ formulates packing as a score-based diffusion process (VE-SDE) that learns a gradient field over polygon transformations, but replaces GFPack's distance-based graph with two attention-based encoders: a 4-layer GCN with residual connections and attention pooling that extracts per-polygon geometric features from ordered vertex sequences, and an 8-layer Transformer (encoder-decoder) that encodes three types of relationships—geometric, spatial (position + orientation), and boundary—via dedicated attention modules without explicit graph construction. Training uses Denoising Score Matching with a sigmoid weighting function that up-weights high-utilization teacher samples, and inference solves the reverse-time RSDE over 128 Euler-Maruyama steps. In the later sampling stages (steps > 96), a collision potential gradient computed via the Separating Axis Theorem is injected into the velocity field to enforce collision-free configurations, followed by a binary-search-based gap-elimination enhancement to further tighten the packing.

## Results

GFPack++ (b=512) achieves average packing ratios of 77.04% (Garment), 77.53% (Dental), 95.12% (Puzzle square), 80.16% (Atlas building), and 67.47% (Atlas object), outperforming all three baselines by 4–32% in space utilization. Inference time ranges from 10.5 s (Garment) to 65.4 s (Atlas object), which is roughly one order of magnitude faster than SVGnest (80–402 s) while simultaneously achieving higher utilization. Compared to the prior diffusion-based method GFPack, GFPack++ improves utilization by 5–7% (with enhancement), reduces overlap from 0.85% to 0.08% of total area, and runs ~10× faster (10.5 s vs 124 s on Garment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFPack++ (b=512) outperforms all three traditional baselines on every dataset. Against XAtlas, it gains 7.5–28.6 percentage points in average utilization (e.g., 77.04% vs 69.53% on Garment, 95.12% vs 66.48% on Puzzle square). Against NFP, the margin is even larger, reaching 32.6 points on Puzzle square (95.12% vs 62.53%). Against SVGnest—the teacher algorithm and strongest traditional baseline—GFPack++ still leads by 4.3–27.9 points in utilization while being 8–6× faster (e.g., 10.5 s vs 80.21 s on Garment; 65.4 s vs 402.2 s on Atlas object). Compared to the prior diffusion method GFPack, GFPack++ improves utilization by 5–7% with enhancement, reduces overlap from 0.85% to 0.08% (Garment) and 1.43% to 0.00% (Dental), and cuts inference time by roughly 10× (10.5 s vs 124 s). Against the RL-based method of Yang et al. [40] on Atlas data, GFPack++ achieves comparable or slightly better average ratios (80.16% vs 82.7% on building; 67.47% vs 68.7% on object) with a small gap attributed to using SVGnest-generated training data rather than the original [40] dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GFPack++ is a direct architectural successor to GFPack [39], which first demonstrated that score-based diffusion can learn a gradient field guiding simultaneous polygon motion for 2D irregular packing. The key departure is replacing GFPack's distance-based graph (rebuilt at every sampling step) and PointNeXt geometry encoder with an attention-based relation encoder and a GCN-plus-attention-pooling geometry encoder, respectively, eliminating the per-step graph reconstruction cost and capturing orientation and edge topology. The collision-free constraint via a Separating-Axis-Theorem potential field and the sigmoid utilization weighting on the DSM loss are practical additions absent from the original formulation. The broader lineage connects to VE-SDE score matching [28], Transformer encoders [31], and the NFP/heuristic packing literature [3, 24] that supplies the teacher data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup over GFPack (≈10 s vs ≈124 s on Garment) combined with a 5–7 pp utilization gain and near-zero overlap makes diffusion-based packing practically viable for manufacturing-scale strip packing, where SVGnest's minutes-per-instance runtime is prohibitive. The demonstrated generalization to arbitrary boundaries and continuous rotation opens the door to applications (texture atlases, garment nesting) that prior gradient-field methods could not handle. Open questions include whether the self-improvement loop (feeding generated solutions back as teacher data) converges, whether a single unified model can cover diverse shape distributions, and how the approach extends to 3D packing where the collision potential and rotation space grow substantially.
