## Problem

Information Pursuit (IP) provides interpretable-by-design image classification by sequentially selecting informative natural-language queries, but its accuracy critically depends on a task-relevant query dictionary that has previously been handcrafted or generated via LLM prompting. These curated dictionaries may contain irrelevant, redundant, or insufficient queries, creating a performance gap between IP and black-box predictors. The paper asks how to learn a compact, task-sufficient query dictionary directly from data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251172/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label along with a sequential explanation: an ordered list of binary (yes/no) query-answer pairs, where each query is a natural-language visual concept (e.g., "wings", "four-legged animal"). Queries are selected greedily by information gain until the posterior entropy over class labels drops below a user-defined threshold or a fixed query budget is exhausted. The answering mechanism is CLIP (ViT-L/14), which computes min-max-normalized dot products between the query's text embedding and the image embedding, then thresholds at 0.5 to yield hard binary answers.

## Method

The paper parameterizes the IP query dictionary as K learnable vectors in CLIP's text-embedding space, where each learnable query is projected to its nearest neighbor in a large query universe (~300K CLIP text embeddings) to guarantee interpretability. Training alternates between (1) t gradient steps on the V-IP querier and classifier networks (two-layer MLPs) with the dictionary frozen, and (2) a single gradient step on the dictionary embeddings with the V-IP networks frozen, mirroring the sparse-coding/dictionary-update cycle of K-SVD. Straight-through estimators handle the non-differentiable nearest-neighbor projection and binary thresholding. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that minimizing the dictionary-augmented V-IP objective with biased sampling is equivalent to a sparse dictionary learning objective where KL-divergence replaces the ℓ₂ reconstruction loss and the IP explanation sequence plays the role of the sparse code.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, K-Learned V-IP achieves 88.55% top-1 accuracy at a query budget of 128, outperforming all four SOTA CBM baselines (PCBM 82.08%, LaBo 87.52%, Label-free 86.77%, Res-CBM 88.03%). On CIFAR-100, K-Learned reaches 68.02% at budget 823, again surpassing every CBM competitor. Across all six image classification benchmarks (RIVAL-10, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, Stanford-Cars, ImageNet-100), the learned query dictionary consistently outperforms the LLM-prompted K-LLM initialization and narrows the accuracy gap to the black-box CLIP+MLP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the K-LLM baseline (Chattopadhyay et al., ICLR 2023), which uses LLM-prompted query dictionaries with V-IP, K-Learned outperforms across all six datasets and all three initializations (K-LLM, K-Random, K-Medoids), with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets like CUB-200 and Stanford-Cars. In the CBM comparison (Table 2), K-Learned wins on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100: it beats Res-CBM (Shang et al.) by 0.52 and 0.11 points respectively, LaBo (Oikarinen et al.) by 1.03 and 0.66 points, and PCBM (Yuksekgonul et al.) by a substantial 6.47 and 12.02 points. The margin over Res-CBM is the smallest, indicating that the two methods are closely competitive on CIFAR-100, while the gap over PCBM is the largest, reflecting PCBM's reliance on post-hoc concept discovery. The ablation (Table 1) further confirms that alternating optimization is critical: joint optimization drops accuracy by up to 8.12 points on CIFAR-10 (87.00% vs 95.12%), validating the design choice of decoupling dictionary and network updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Pursuit (IP) and Variational IP (V-IP) framework by making the query dictionary learnable rather than fixed. Prior IP work relied on handcrafted concept dictionaries (e.g., CUB-200 annotations) or LLM-prompted query sets, and the CLIP-based V-IP pipeline of Chattopadhyay et al. (ICLR 2023) still assumed a static dictionary. The key conceptual departure is parameterizing queries as learnable embeddings in CLIP's text-encoder latent space and training them via an alternating optimization whose structure mirrors K-SVD sparse dictionary learning: the V-IP update plays the role of sparse coding, and the dictionary update refines atoms to reduce classification error. The paper also draws a parallel to Prototype Networks, where visual prototypes are learned from data rather than handcrafted, but retains IP's natural-language interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in interpretable-by-design classification: the quality of the query dictionary was previously limited by human curation or prompt-engineering heuristics, and learned dictionaries close part of the accuracy gap to black-box models across six benchmarks. However, the method remains tightly coupled to CLIP as the query-answering mechanism, and the paper itself notes that CLIP's noisy answers (e.g., affirming 'anemone' for a jellyfish) cap both accuracy and explanation quality. A key open question is whether the dictionary-learning objective transfers to more reliable or multimodal answer mechanisms, and whether the learned queries remain semantically meaningful at scale beyond the ~300K-query universe used here.
