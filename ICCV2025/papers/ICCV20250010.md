## Problem

Classification models trained with empirical risk minimization (ERM) exploit spurious correlations between non-target attributes and labels, leading to poor generalization when those correlations are absent at test time. Existing debiasing methods—whether relying on full attribute annotations, reweighting, or resampling—still struggle when the number of samples lacking spurious correlations is very limited. This paper addresses the problem from a model optimization perspective, seeking a principled way to steer gradient updates away from spurious features without requiring explicit attribute labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard classification inputs (RGB images for vision benchmarks like Waterbirds, CelebA, C-MNIST, C-CIFAR-10, bFFHQ; text for CivilComments) and outputs a class label. The key operational detail is that each training step requires two differently-sampled mini-batches: a biased batch reflecting the dataset's natural attribute distribution and a less biased batch with a more balanced conditional attribute distribution controlled by hyperparameter c. In the unknown-attribute setting, pseudo-attributes are derived from a pre-trained ERM model's softmax confidence scores, partitioning each class into 'easy' (high-confidence) and 'hard' (low-confidence) subsets via a threshold t.

## Method

GERNE computes the loss on a biased batch B_b and a less biased batch B_lb, then defines the target gradient as a linear extrapolation: ∇L_ext = ∇L_lb + β(∇L_lb − ∇L_b), where β is the extrapolation factor that modulates the degree of debiasing. The paper proves that minimizing L_ext is equivalent to training on class-balanced batches with a synthetic conditional attribute distribution p_ext(a|y) = α_ya + c(β+1)(1/A − α_ya), which unifies ERM (β=−1) and Resampling (c=1, β=0) as special cases. Theoretical upper and lower bounds on β are derived to keep p_ext within [0,1], and tuning β beyond 1/c − 1 shifts weight toward the worst-case group's loss, enabling optimization of either Group-Balanced Accuracy or Worst-Group Accuracy. For unknown attributes, GERNE constructs pseudo-groups from a pre-trained model's predictions and shows (Proposition 1) that the extrapolated gradient can simulate batch distributions beyond what direct sampling from pseudo-groups can achieve.

## Results

On the Datasets-1 benchmarks (C-MNIST, C-CIFAR-10, bFFHQ), GERNE achieves GBA of 77.79% (C-MNIST, 0.5% minority) and 85.20% minority-group accuracy on bFFHQ, outperforming Resampling (72.13%) by over 13 points and Group DRO by up to 16 points. On Datasets-2 (Waterbirds, CelebA, CivilComments), GERNE attains the best worst-group accuracy on CelebA (91.98%) and CivilComments (74.65%) under known attributes, and on Waterbirds (90.21%) and CivilComments (71.00%) under unknown-training/known-validation attributes. Under the most challenging unknown/unknown setting, GERNE still leads on Waterbirds (89.88%) and CelebA (74.24%), surpassing the next-best methods by 10+ points on Waterbirds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GERNE outperforms Group DRO [39] by a wide margin on C-MNIST (up to +16 pts at 1% minority) and C-CIFAR-10 (up to +5 pts), and beats Resampling [16] by 13 pts on bFFHQ (85.20 vs 72.13) while matching or exceeding it on C-MNIST/C-CIFAR-10. In the unknown-attribute setting on Datasets-1, GERNE surpasses JTT [28], LfF [31], DFA [27], LC [29], and DeNetDM [44] across nearly all minority ratios, with the sole exception of LC on C-MNIST at 5% minority (91.16 vs 90.98). On Datasets-2 with known attributes, GERNE beats DFR [18] on CelebA (+1.58) and CivilComments (+5.05) but trails it on Waterbirds (90.20 vs 91.00); it also outperforms LISA [51], ReWeight [19], ReSample [19], CBLoss [7], and BSoftmax [36] on all three datasets. Under unknown-training/known-validation, GERNE leads on Waterbirds (90.21 vs DFR 89.00) and CivilComments (71.00 vs CnC 68.90) while remaining competitive on CelebA (86.28 vs CnC 88.80). In the hardest unknown/unknown setting, GERNE achieves the best Waterbirds score (89.88, +10.88 over DFR) and CelebA score (74.24, +0.54 over DFR), though it does not lead on CivilComments (63.10 vs SqrtReWeight [50] 68.60), likely due to the use of worst-class rather than worst-group accuracy as the selection metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

GERNE positions itself within the debiasing literature by reframing the problem at the gradient-optimization level rather than the loss-objective level. It builds on the pseudo-attribute construction from JTT [28] and related works [31, 54] for the unknown-attribute case, but departs from prior methods (Group DRO, DFR, LISA) by defining a debiasing direction as the linear extrapolation between gradients of two differently-biased batches. The paper's key conceptual contribution is showing that ERM (β=−1) and Resampling (c=1, β=0) are special cases of a single gradient-extrapolation framework, unifying previously separate debiasing strategies under one parameterized family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bounds on the extrapolation factor β and its direct connection to worst-group risk provide practitioners with principled guidance for tuning, and the unification of ERM/Resampling as limiting cases offers a cleaner mental model for the debiasing landscape. However, the method's practical utility is tempered by β's narrow feasible range (divergence just above the upper bound) and the continued reliance on pseudo-attributes from a biased ERM model in the unknown-attribute setting. Open questions include whether dynamic adaptation of β during training could reduce sensitivity, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-attribute or hierarchical group structures beyond the binary pseudo-attribute split used here.
