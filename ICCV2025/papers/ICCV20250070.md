## Problem

Existing step-distillation methods for diffusion models face a fundamental tradeoff: GAN-based approaches suffer training instability and mode collapse, VSD variants impose heavy GPU memory overhead, and consistency models degrade in quality at ultra-few steps (<4). Additionally, continuous-time consistency models (sCM) require the TrigFlow formulation, but most pretrained models (SANA, FLUX, SD3) use flow matching, forcing practitioners to either design separate algorithms or pretrain expensive dedicated TrigFlow models.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in 1–4 inference steps using a single unified model (no step-specific checkpoints). For the ControlNet extension, an HED scribble map extracted from a reference image is fed as additional structural conditioning alongside the text prompt, enabling real-time interactive generation at ~250 ms latency on H100. The 0.6B and 1.6B variants achieve 0.10–0.21 s latency on H100 and 0.31 s on an RTX 4090 for single-step 1024×1024 synthesis.

## Method

SANA-Sprint introduces a training-free, lossless mathematical transformation (Proposition 3.1) that converts a pretrained flow-matching model into TrigFlow by remapping the time variable (t_FM = sin(t)/(sin(t)+cos(t))) and rescaling the noisy input/output, enabling direct use of the sCM training pipeline without retraining the teacher. The hybrid distillation objective combines the sCM consistency loss (which preserves teacher alignment and diversity) with LADD's latent adversarial loss (which provides global cross-timestep supervision and sharpens single-step fidelity), weighted at λ=0.5. Training stability at scale is ensured by replacing the 1000× time-embedding scaling with a dense c_noise(t)=t schedule and adding RMS QK-normalization to self- and cross-attention. An additional max-time weighting (50% probability of sampling t=π/2) further boosts one-step generation quality.

## Results

SANA-Sprint 0.6B achieves FID 7.04 and GenEval 0.72 in a single step with 0.21s latency on A100, while SANA-Sprint 1.6B reaches GenEval 0.76 at the same step count. At 4 steps, SANA-Sprint 0.6B attains FID 6.48 and GenEval 0.76 (0.32s latency), and SANA-Sprint 1.6B reaches GenEval 0.77 (0.31s). Throughput reaches 7.22 samples/s (0.6B) and 6.71 samples/s (1.6B) at 1 step, compared to 1.58 samples/s for FLUX-schnell (12B) at the same step count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANA-Sprint 0.6B outperforms FLUX-schnell (12B) at every step count: at 1 step it achieves FID 7.04 vs 7.26 and GenEval 0.72 vs 0.69 while being 3.2× faster (0.21s vs 0.68s); at 4 steps it reaches FID 6.48 vs 7.94 and GenEval 0.76 vs 0.71 with a 6.6× speed advantage (0.32s vs 2.10s). Against SDXL-DMD2, SANA-Sprint 0.6B wins on FID at 1 step (7.04 vs 7.10) and 4 steps (6.48 vs 6.82), and on GenEval at all step counts (0.72 vs 0.59 at 1 step; 0.76 vs 0.60 at 4 steps). SANA-Sprint dramatically outperforms LCM-based methods (SDXL-LCM, PixArt-LCM) and SD3.5-Turbo at 1–2 steps, where those methods suffer severe quality degradation (FID 50–73 for LCM, 51–52 for SD3.5-Turbo at 1 step) while SANA-Sprint maintains FID below 7.7. The 1.6B variant trades a small FID increase (7.69 vs 7.04 at 1 step) for a GenEval gain (0.76 vs 0.72), matching or exceeding FLUX-schnell's GenEval at every step count. Overall, SANA-Sprint establishes a new Pareto frontier: it is the only sub-1B model to simultaneously beat the 12B FLUX-schnell on both FID and GenEval at 1 step while running 3–6× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two distillation paradigms for diffusion models: trajectory-based consistency distillation (sCM, which requires TrigFlow parameterization) and distribution-based adversarial distillation (LADD). The key conceptual contribution is a training-free, lossless mathematical transformation that converts any pre-trained flow-matching model (SANA, FLUX, SD3) into TrigFlow form, thereby unlocking the sCM training pipeline without retraining a dedicated TrigFlow teacher. The hybrid objective (sCM + LADD) is a straightforward combination, but the stabilization tricks—QK-norm in attention and dense time embeddings—are practical engineering contributions that make continuous-time distillation viable at 1.6B scale and 1024×1024 resolution, where prior work reported training collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.6B model generating 1024×1024 images in 0.1s on H100 (0.31s on RTX 4090) with competitive FID/GenEval opens the door to real-time interactive generation (ControlNet at 250ms) and consumer-grade AIPC applications that were previously infeasible. The training-free flow-matching-to-TrigFlow transformation is broadly applicable and could benefit any existing flow-matching model without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the hybrid sCM+LADD objective is truly necessary at larger scales (the 0.6 FID gap over sCM-alone is modest), whether the approach generalizes cleanly to video or 3D generation, and whether the QK-norm/dense-embedding stabilization is specific to the SANA architecture or universally needed for continuous-time distillation.
