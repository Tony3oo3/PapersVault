## Problem

Training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVS) is constrained by either the weak pixel-level supervision of vision-language models (limiting attention-based methods) or the suboptimal quality of synthetic reference sets (limiting retrieval-based methods). The paper identifies that data quality in retrieval-based OVS has been largely overlooked: existing methods either generate synthetic images with diffusion models or design complex retrieval strategies to tolerate noisy data, rather than directly addressing the quality of the reference set itself.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB test image and an arbitrary set of target text class labels, producing a pixel-level segmentation mask where each pixel is assigned to one of the target classes. The reference set is pre-constructed offline from 118k COCO-2017 images using only images as input (no ground-truth captions, classes, or masks), yielding approximately 1 million segment-text pairs stored in 4 GB. The class-agnostic segmenter (Felzenszwalb superpixels by default, or SAM/SAM2 optionally) operates at the native image resolution, and the final mask is produced by argmax over per-pixel class probabilities aggregated from segment-level logits.

## Method

ReME constructs a high-quality reference set through a two-phase data pipeline: (1) initial pairing, where LLaVA generates image descriptions, noun phrases are extracted as candidate labels, Felzenszwalb superpixels provide class-agnostic segments, and CLIP performs initial segment-label matching; and (2) data enhancing, which applies group-based filtering (grouping segments by root noun, computing the median visual feature center via DINOv2, and removing the bottom δ_filter% outliers by intra-modal similarity) and semantic enriching (identifying synonym pairs among label embeddings and augmenting each label with its k most similar alternatives). The key insight is that intra-modal visual features are more discriminative for detecting misalignments than cross-modal CLIP scores, which tend to remove correct pairs while leaving errors unaddressed. At inference, a simple similarity-based retrieval computes affinities between test segments and reference segments (DINOv2 features), multiplies by the binary label-assignment matrix, then multiplies by affinities between reference labels and test class labels (CLIP text features), and aggregates per-segment logits into pixel-wise predictions via the segment masks.

## Results

ReME achieves the best mIoU across all 10 training-free OVS benchmarks without SAM, reaching 92.3 on VOC-20, 50.4 on Cityscapes, 44.9 on PC-59, 45.5 on COCO Object, 33.1 on COCO Stuff, 26.1 on ADE-150, 14.1 on PC-459, and 8.4 on ADE-847. With SAM as the segmenter, gains are further amplified (e.g., 59.0 on Cityscapes, 53.1 on PC-59, 48.2 on COCO Object). The method requires no post-processing (e.g., DenseCRF) and uses only a simple similarity-based retrieval over a ~1M-pair reference set, yet outperforms methods that rely on complex attention modifications or iterative multi-model inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReME outperforms all 14 training-free baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest SAM-free baselines, ReME leads by 0.9 mIoU over CaR on VOC-20 (92.3 vs. 91.4), by 13.7 mIoU on Cityscapes (50.4 vs. 36.7 for FreeDA), and by 3.7 mIoU on PC-459 (14.1 vs. 10.4 for OVDiff). Among SAM-involved methods, ReME w/SAM surpasses CorrCLIP by 1.6 mIoU on VOC-20 (93.2 vs. 91.6) and by 11.3 mIoU on Cityscapes (59.0 vs. 47.7). The retrieval-based FreeDA, which uses 5× more input samples and a 17 GB synthetic reference set, is outperformed by ReME's 4 GB real-image reference set on all benchmarks (e.g., 33.1 vs. 28.8 on COCO Stuff, 26.1 vs. 22.4 on ADE-150). Notably, ReME achieves these gains without any mask post-processing (DenseCRF), whereas most competing methods (SCLIP, CaR, FreeDA, NACLIP) rely on such refinements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in training-free OVS: (1) attention-based methods (SCLIP, MaskCLIP, ProxyCLIP) that extract dense predictions from CLIP's self-attention, and (2) retrieval-based methods (FreeDA, ReCo, RIM) that construct reference sets from synthetic data or GT annotations. Rather than improving the attention mechanism or designing more sophisticated retrieval algorithms, ReME reframes the problem as a data-quality challenge, repurposing the retrieval paradigm with a pipeline that uses LLaVA for rich descriptions, CLIP for initial pairing, and DINOv2 intra-modal features for group-based filtering and semantic enriching. The key conceptual departure is the finding that intra-modal visual similarity is more discriminative than cross-modal CLIP scores for detecting segment-text misalignments, inverting the common practice of using CLIP scores for data filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data quality, not algorithmic sophistication, is the primary bottleneck for training-free OVS: a simple similarity-based retrieval over a well-constructed reference set from real images outperforms 14 baselines including those using SAM, diffusion models, and iterative CLIP querying. This shifts the research focus from model-specific attention hacks to data-centric design principles that could generalize to other dense prediction tasks. Limitations include dependence on the quality of pre-trained encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, LLaVA), the computational cost of constructing the reference set, and the fact that the approach still cannot surpass the inherent ceiling of weakly-supervised VLMs for truly novel or rare concepts. Open questions include whether the intra-modal filtering principle extends to other multi-modal data curation tasks and whether the reference set can be made more compact or domain-adaptive.
