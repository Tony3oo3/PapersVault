## Problem

Neuron segmentation from high-resolution EM volumes is hindered by high noise, anisotropic voxel dimensions, and ultra-long spatial dependencies spanning thousands of patches. Existing self-supervised vision methods (MAE, contrastive) lack favorable scaling laws, while autoregressive approaches suffer from O(K) error accumulation that degrades performance on long visual sequences. The paper addresses how to adapt autoregressive pretraining to long-sequence 3D EM data while mitigating cumulative prediction errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D EM image volumes (e.g., 1250×1250×125 voxels per region) with anisotropic resolution (coarser z-axis), partitioned into non-overlapping patches and tokenized into sequences of K tokens. The segmentation network outputs a 3-channel affinity map encoding connectivity probabilities along three spatial directions, which is then post-processed via seeded watershed and structure-aware region agglomeration (LMC) to yield final neuron instance segmentations.

## Method

TokenUnify is a hierarchical predictive coding framework built on the Mamba state-space architecture for linear-time sequence modeling. It unifies three complementary objectives—random token prediction (position-invariant spatial patterns), next-token prediction (sequential morphological continuity), and next-all token prediction (global structural context)—within a single information-theoretic formulation, reducing cumulative autoregressive error from O(K) to O(√K). A multi-resolution curriculum schedules task weights from local to global dominance over training, and a Perceiver Resampler converts variable-size feature maps into fixed-length token sequences. For fine-tuning, the pre-trained Mamba encoder is paired with an adapted SegMamba decoder (EMmamba) that handles anisotropic EM features, trained with MSE loss on affinity maps.

## Results

On the large-scale MEC dataset (Wafer4, Waterz post-processing), TokenUnify achieves VOI=0.9951 and ARAND=0.0509, representing a 44% improvement over training from scratch (VOI=1.7839) and a 25% improvement over MAE pretraining (VOI=1.3248). On Wafer36-2, TokenUnify similarly leads with VOI=0.4848 and ARAND=0.0650. On the smaller AC3/4 dataset, TokenUnify achieves VOI=0.9918, outperforming MAE (VOI=1.1131) by 11% and approaching supervised PEA (VOI=1.0502) with only self-supervised pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenUnify outperforms all self-supervised baselines across both MEC wafers and all four metrics. Compared to MAE, the closest general-purpose baseline, TokenUnify reduces VOI from 1.3248 to 0.9951 (25% improvement) on Wafer4 and from 0.6161 to 0.4848 (21% improvement) on Wafer36-2. Against EM-specific methods dbMIM and MS-Con-EM, TokenUnify achieves 11% and 14% VOI reductions respectively on Wafer4. Versus the no-pretraining baseline (Random), the 44% VOI reduction confirms the substantial value of the unified pretraining objective. Among supervised methods, TokenUnify's self-supervised VOI of 0.9951 on Wafer4 surpasses PEA (1.2658) and approaches Superhuman (1.4587) and MALA (2.0375), demonstrating that the Mamba backbone with TokenUnify pretraining can match or exceed supervised approaches with far fewer labeled examples. On AC3/4, TokenUnify (VOI=0.9918) also beats all self-supervised baselines and slightly outperforms supervised PEA (VOI=1.0502), highlighting its effectiveness in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenUnify bridges the gap between LLM-style autoregressive pretraining (AIM, LVM) and vision-specific self-supervised methods (MAE, BYOL) by proposing a unified three-task predictive coding framework for long visual token sequences. It builds directly on SegMamba's Mamba-based 3D segmentation architecture, adapting it into EMmamba with an enhanced decoder for anisotropic EM features, while the pretraining strategy departs from both pure masked reconstruction and pure next-token prediction. The theoretical framing—showing that random, next, and next-all token prediction capture complementary mutual information channels and reduce cumulative error from O(K) to O(√K)—is a novel contribution not present in prior autoregressive or masked-prediction literature for vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.2-billion-voxel MEC dataset across six mouse brain regions is one of the largest manually annotated EM segmentation resources to date, providing a critical testbed for long-sequence visual modeling in connectomics. The demonstrated scaling properties of the Mamba-based TokenUnify framework (100M to 1B parameters) suggest a viable path toward large-scale visual foundation models for biological imaging, an area where Transformer-based approaches face quadratic complexity barriers. Open questions include whether the three-task combination generalizes beyond EM to other long-sequence 3D modalities, whether the O(√K) error reduction holds empirically at all sequence lengths, and whether the Mamba advantage over Transformers persists as model scale and data volume grow further.
