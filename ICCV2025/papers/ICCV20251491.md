## Problem

Flat minima are well-established to improve generalization and robustness in supervised learning, but their role in generative models—particularly diffusion models—remains largely unexplored. A key open question is whether flatness in a generative model contradicts the goal of sample diversity, and if it is beneficial, what theoretical guarantees link flatness to improved generative performance and robustness against practical challenges like exposure bias and model quantization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes random Gaussian noise as input and iteratively denoises it over T timesteps to produce RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (LSUN Tower, FFHQ). Evaluation uses reduced respaced timesteps (T′=20 or 100) to stress-test exposure bias, and the paper additionally examines performance under direct 32-bit-to-8-bit parameter quantization without fine-tuning, as well as under adversarial perturbations to the initial noise.

## Method

The paper provides a theoretical framework showing that parameter perturbation in a score-based generative model translates into a perturbation of the target noise distribution (Theorem 1/Corollary 1), and that a Δ-flat minimum upper-bounds the distributional gap the model can tolerate (Theorem 2/Corollary 2), thereby linking flatness to robustness against exposure bias and quantization. Empirically, the authors apply Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to the ADM baseline and discover that diffusion models are inherently much flatter than discriminative models, requiring significantly stronger SAM regularization (ρ tuned over [1e-1, 1e-2, 1e-3]) to induce meaningful additional flatness. They show that SAM explicitly reshapes the loss surface (lowest LPF scores), whereas ensemble-based methods (SWA, EMA) and Input Perturbation (IP) improve FID without significantly enhancing flatness. The practical benefits are demonstrated through reduced exposure bias (smaller ‖ε_θ‖² gap between training and sampling) and near-zero FID degradation under 8-bit quantization, where +SAM+EMA achieves FID 7.00→7.20 versus ADM's 34.47→48.02.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 20 respaced timesteps, SAM reduces FID from 34.47 (ADM baseline) to 9.01, and SAM+SWA further improves to 7.27. The LPF flatness metric drops from 0.097 (ADM) to 0.063 with SAM, while SWA and EMA leave it essentially unchanged (0.099). Under 8-bit quantization, SAM+EMA degrades FID by only +0.2 (7.00→7.20) versus +13.65 for ADM (34.47→48.02), demonstrating that flat minima preserve generative quality under aggressive precision reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ADM baseline [Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021], SAM dramatically improves FID across all datasets and timestep settings (e.g., 34.47→9.01 on CIFAR-10 at 20 steps), confirming that explicit flatness optimization benefits diffusion models. Against SWA [Izmailov et al., 2018], SAM clearly wins on the flatness metric: LPF drops to 0.063 with SAM versus 0.099 with SWA, showing that weight averaging does not actually flatten the loss landscape in diffusion models despite improving FID. Against IP (Input Perturbation) [Ning et al., 2023], SAM is substantially better at short sampling horizons (9.01 vs. 20.11 FID on CIFAR-10 at 20 steps), because IP lacks a principled noise direction and converges to suboptimal minima, though IP+SWA (9.04) approaches SAM+SWA (7.27) at 20 steps. In the quantization setting, SAM+EMA degrades by only +0.2 FID under 8-bit precision while ADM degrades by +13.65, a gap of over 60×, directly validating the theoretical claim that flat minima are robust to parameter perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied flat-minima literature from supervised learning (where SAM, SWA, and EMA are established tools for improving generalization) to the fundamentally different setting of diffusion-based generative models. It builds directly on SAM's sharpness-aware objective and on the exposure-bias mitigation via input perturbation (IP), but reframes both through a unified theoretical lens: Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 translate parameter-space perturbations into perturbed noise distributions, and Theorem 2 upper-bounds the distributional gap that a flat minimum can tolerate. The paper departs from prior flatness work by showing that diffusion models are inherently flatter than discriminative models, and that ensemble-averaging methods (SWA, EMA) fail to induce additional flatness in this setting, unlike in classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic theoretical and empirical treatment of loss-landscape flatness in generative models, providing a principled explanation for why flat diffusion models resist exposure bias and quantization degradation. The practical takeaway—that SAM-trained diffusion models retain near-full-precision FID under 8-bit quantization while baselines degrade severely—could influence deployment strategies for real-time generative applications. However, all experiments are limited to 32×32 and 64×64 images with relatively small ADM architectures, leaving open whether these findings hold at the scale of modern large diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DiT). The authors also flag broader open questions: cross-domain generation, few-shot personalization, memorization mitigation, and adversarial robustness as potential downstream benefits of flatness that remain unexplored.
