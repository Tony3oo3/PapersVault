## Problem

Open-ended visual trend discovery from massive image collections (tens of millions of images) without labeled training data remains unsolved: prior methods require predetermined target subjects or supervised labels, while MLLMs—capable of the needed holistic semantic understanding—cannot ingest such data due to context-window limits. The paper tackles how to decompose this intractable problem into sub-tasks that individual MLLM calls can handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250940/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 20M posed, timestamped Street View images per city (NYC, SF; 2011–2023), organized into ~9M locations with ~20–23 chronologically sorted images each. It produces open-ended text descriptions of visual trends (e.g., 'the overpass was painted blue'), each backed by hundreds of before/after image pairs with timestamps, plus intermediate local change descriptions. Queries can be open-ended ('what changed?'), time-conditioned ('what changed since 2020?'), or subject-conditioned ('what happened to retail stores?'), all without any predefined category list.

## Method

Visual Chronicles is a two-step, training-free hierarchical pipeline built entirely on Gemini-1.5 Pro. Step 1 (local change detection) feeds each location's sorted image sequence (~20–40 images) to the MLLM, which identifies semantic changes, describes them in text, and grounds each to specific image pairs while ignoring trivial variations (time of day, season). Step 2 (trend discovery) first embeds all ~3M change descriptions with text embeddings and applies Canopy Clustering to propose trend clusters, then verifies each proposal with a hybrid algorithm: text-embedding similarity ranks all changes against the trend, and the MLLM verifies only the top-k nearest neighbors (k=1500 for N=500), confirming the trend if ≥N changes match. This hybrid reduces MLLM inference cost by 2000× over exhaustive verification while achieving 93.9–98.3% accuracy on trend classification, far exceeding embedding-only baselines.

## Results

On the local visual change detection task (200 locations, 3036 images), Gemini-1.5 Pro achieves 76.56% Average Precision, outperforming the best embedding-based baseline (CLIP at 26.52%) by over 50 AP points, with 81.34% precision and 89.87% recall on detected changes. For trend verification, the MLLM reaches 86.63% AP versus 73.13% for the best text-embedding baseline (NV-Emb). The final hybrid verification algorithm achieves 93.9% accuracy at N=50 and 98.3% at N=200, compared to 77.9% and 81.8% for the best scalable alternative (NV-Emb thresholding).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning image features, remote sensing, and semantic embeddings. On change detection (Table 1), Gemini (76.56% AP) decisively outperforms all baselines: HoG (16.44%), C-Hist (16.76%), R-Sensing (18.51%), CLIP (26.52%), and NV-Emb (23.75%), with a margin exceeding 50 AP points over the strongest baseline. On trend discovery (Table 2), Gemini (86.63% AP) beats CLIP (54.78%) by 32 points and NV-Emb (73.13%) by 13 points. On the final change-trend classification task (Table 3), the proposed hybrid algorithm (93.9% Acc@50, 98.3% Acc@200) substantially outperforms NV-Emb thresholding (77.9%, 81.8%) and random MLLM sampling (31.8%, 74.9%), while approaching the accuracy of exhaustive MLLM verification (AllTrue: 72.7%, 28.9%) at a 2000× lower inference cost. The paper does not report any comparison where the proposed method underperforms a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of large-scale image analysis (Doersch et al., Gebru et al.) and temporal visual change detection (Martin-Brualla et al., Naik et al., Ginosar et al.), all of which require either predetermined target categories or labeled training data. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes off-the-shelf MLLMs (Gemini-1.5 Pro) within a novel bottom-up system that decomposes massive-scale temporal trend discovery into local change detection and global trend verification. The hybrid verification algorithm—combining text-embedding retrieval with MLLM-based confirmation—repurposes Canopy Clustering and embedding similarity in a way that prior visual analysis tools (CLIP, NV-Embed, HoG, color histograms) could not achieve, since those methods lack the semantic reasoning needed for open-ended change interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that MLLMs can be applied to image collections exceeding one million images, opening a new paradigm for label-free, open-ended visual analysis that was previously infeasible. The 2000× cost reduction from the hybrid verification step makes the approach practically deployable, and the discovered trends (blue overpass, outdoor dining expansion, solar panel additions) illustrate the kind of evidence-grounded urban insight that would require enormous manual effort otherwise. Key limitations remain: the authors acknowledge sampling biases from Street View geometry, the absence of a rigorous statistical framework for drawing conclusions, and the proof-of-concept nature of the system. Open questions include extending to video, incorporating uncertainty quantification, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Street-View collections with less structured temporal coverage.
