## Problem

Person re-identification has been extensively studied, but it remains unclear which of its techniques generalize to animal re-identification, a task complicated by species diversity, varied environments (terrestrial vs. aquatic), species-specific poses, and limited data. No dedicated codebase or strong baseline existed for animal re-identification, making systematic comparison and ablation studies difficult.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB animal images at a square 384×384 resolution (deliberately avoiding the portrait-oriented inputs like 256×128 used in person re-ID, since animals exhibit diverse poses such as quadrupedal locomotion and swimming). At test time, a query image is matched against a gallery of images by computing Euclidean distance between concatenated global and part-level embeddings, producing a ranked identity list evaluated via rank-1 accuracy and mean Average Precision.

## Method

ARBase builds on a ResNet-50 backbone with three key animal-oriented modifications: (1) Instance-Batch Normalization (IBN) replaces standard BN to balance appearance invariance (via IN) with content preservation (via BN); (2) the last stride is set to 1 for higher-resolution feature maps; and (3) a multi-branch architecture extracts global, 2-part, and 3-part features simultaneously, inspired by MGN. The head applies Global Average Pooling, a Linear layer, and BNNeck to separate features for triplet loss (pre-BN) and cross-entropy loss with label smoothing (post-BN). Training uses Adam with cosine annealing, and data augmentation is limited to random horizontal flips (0.5 probability), deliberately omitting Random Erasing and AutoAug which the revisiting experiments showed to be harmful or neutral for animals. The paper also systematically revisits BoT, AGW, SBS, and MGN to identify which person re-ID components transfer, finding that label smoothing, last stride, BNNeck, cosine annealing, and multi-granularity features generalize well while random erasing, non-local attention, generalized-mean pooling, freeze training, and AutoAug do not.

## Results

ARBase achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four animal re-identification benchmarks, with rank-1 accuracy of 73.18% on HyenaID, 64.34% on LeopardID, 86.92% on SeaTurtleID, and 62.44% on WhaleSharkID. The corresponding mAP values are 44.87%, 37.08%, 55.99%, and 29.45%, respectively. Compared to the strongest person re-identification baseline on each dataset, ARBase improves rank-1 accuracy by 14.54% (HyenaID), 9.42% (LeopardID), 0.87% (SeaTurtleID), and 9.90% (WhaleSharkID), with mAP gains of 7.98–9.91 percentage points across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARBase outperforms all four person re-identification baselines (BoT, AGW, SBS, MGN) on every benchmark and every metric. BoT is the strongest baseline in rank-1 accuracy on HyenaID (58.64%), LeopardID (54.92%), and WhaleSharkID (52.54%), while MGN leads on SeaTurtleID (86.05% R1). ARBase surpasses the best baseline by a large margin on terrestrial species (14.54% on HyenaID, 9.42% on LeopardID) and aquatic species (9.90% on WhaleSharkID), with a smaller but consistent gain on SeaTurtleID (0.87% R1, 9.32% mAP). The mAP improvements are uniformly substantial (7.98–9.91 points), indicating that ARBase not only retrieves the correct identity more often but also ranks it higher among all candidates. Notably, the person re-identification methods that perform best on their own domain (e.g., MGN with multi-granularity features) do not transfer well to animals, with SBS performing worst overall, underscoring the domain gap the paper highlights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FastReID codebase (He et al., ACM MM 2023) and the WildLifeDatasets toolkit (Čermák et al., WACV 2024) to construct OpenAnimals, a unified framework for animal re-identification. Conceptually, it departs from the implicit assumption in the person re-ID literature that widely adopted techniques (Random Erasing, Non-local Attention, Generalized-mean Pooling, Weighted Triplet Loss, AutoAug) transfer across re-identification domains. By systematically ablation-testing BoT, AGW, SBS, and MGN on four animal benchmarks, the paper shows that many of these components are neutral or harmful for animals, and then synthesizes the surviving effective components (BNNeck, last-stride-1, label smoothing, multi-branch granularity, IBN, cosine annealing) into the ARBase model, which is architecturally closest to MGN but with animal-specific data and normalization choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Animal re-identification is critical for wildlife conservation and population monitoring, yet the field has lacked a rigorous, reproducible baseline and a clear understanding of which person re-ID techniques actually transfer. The negative-transfer findings (e.g., Random Erasing degrading performance because it destroys the subtle spot/scar patterns that distinguish individuals) save practitioners from blindly porting person re-ID recipes. Open questions remain: whether ARBase's ResNet-50-centric design scales to transformer backbones or video inputs, how it performs on the 20+ other species benchmarks mentioned but not evaluated, and whether the limited per-identity sample counts (~10) constrain further gains. The paper's scope is also limited to four species and a single backbone, so broader generalization claims are tentative.
