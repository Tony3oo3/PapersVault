## Problem

Sparse-view 3D reconstruction from extremely few images (e.g., 3 views of a 360° scene) remains challenging: existing 3DGS-based methods overfit to input views and produce blurry or noisy content in occluded regions, while diffusion-prior approaches (ReconFusion, CAT3D) overblur details because their synthesized views lack 3D consistency. The paper identifies that visible-region reconstruction and missing-region hallucination are fundamentally different tasks that benefit from separate, specialized models rather than a single unified diffusion prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (as few as 3) with known camera poses and produces a complete 3D Gaussian scene representation renderable from arbitrary novel viewpoints. Input images are resized so the smallest dimension is 512 pixels to match the latent diffusion model resolution, followed by random 512×512 cropping for fine-tuning. The 3DGS is initialized with one Gaussian per pixel per input image, projected into 3D using a combined depth map, and the final output is a dense set of 3D Gaussians with per-Gaussian position, opacity, covariance, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients.

## Method

RI3D separates sparse view synthesis into two tasks handled by two personalized diffusion models: a repair model (ControlNet fine-tuned via a leave-one-out strategy on corrupted-to-clean image pairs) that converts rendered images into pseudo ground truth to constrain 3DGS optimization, and an inpainting model (Stable Diffusion inpainting fine-tuned with random masking on the input images) that hallucinates content in unobserved regions. Gaussian initialization combines DUSt3R's 3D-consistent but smooth depth with Depth Anything V2's detailed but relative monocular depth through a Poisson blending objective that enforces gradient matching everywhere while anchoring absolute depth in high-confidence regions. A two-stage optimization follows: Stage 1 reconstructs only visible areas using the repair model at M novel cameras with an opacity penalty in missing regions, and Stage 2 iteratively inpaints a subset of K non-overlapping novel views, projects the inpainted content into 3D via depth, and continues optimization with the repair model to seamlessly integrate hallucinated details into the scene.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, RI3D achieves the best LPIPS across all sparsity levels: 0.505 (3-view), 0.462 (6-view), and 0.415 (9-view), indicating superior texture detail preservation. On PSNR and SSIM, RI3D (15.74/0.342 at 3-view) is slightly behind CAT3D (16.62/0.377) and ReconFusion (15.50/0.358), which the authors attribute to these metrics favoring the smoother, blurrier outputs of those methods. RI3D substantially outperforms all 3DGS-based baselines (FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian) on every metric, e.g., improving LPIPS from 0.578 (FSGS) to 0.505 at 3-view.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RI3D achieves the best LPIPS (perceptual quality) across all three sparsity levels, beating the closest competitor CAT3D by 0.010 (3-view), 0.020 (6-view), and 0.045 (9-view), with the gap widening as fewer views are available. On PSNR and SSIM, RI3D trails CAT3D by ~0.9 dB and ReconFusion by ~0.2 dB at 3-view, which the authors explain by noting these pixel-wise metrics reward the smooth, blurry textures that ReconFusion and CAT3D produce in missing regions. Against 3DGS-based baselines, RI3D leads decisively: at 3-view it improves LPIPS over FSGS (0.578→0.505), CoR-GS (0.633→0.505), and DNGaussian (0.665→0.505), while also gaining 2.6 dB PSNR over FSGS. The NeRF-based baselines (DiffusioNeRF, FreeNeRF, SimpleNeRF, Zip-NeRF, ZeroNVS) are all outperformed on every metric by a wide margin. The paper also notes that ReconFusion and CAT3D use NeRF (slow rendering) whereas RI3D uses 3DGS for real-time inference, and that visual comparisons in the supplementary video show RI3D producing sharper, more detailed textures in occluded regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3DGS as its 3D representation and departs from prior diffusion-based sparse view synthesis methods (ReconFusion, CAT3D) that use a single view-synthesis diffusion model, which the authors argue produces 3D-inconsistent reconstructions leading to overblurring. The key conceptual departure is splitting the problem into two specialized sub-tasks—repairing visible regions (following GaussianObject's ControlNet-based approach) and inpainting missing regions (using a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion inpainting model)—and integrating them through a two-stage optimization. The Gaussian initialization strategy, which combines DUSt3R's 3D-consistent but smooth depth with monocular depth's fine detail via a Poisson-blending formulation, is a novel practical contribution that addresses the brittleness of sparse-view 3DGS optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of view synthesis into 'reconstruct what you can see' and 'hallucinate what you can't' provides a principled framework that could inform future sparse-view methods, and the LPIPS advantage over ReconFusion/CAT3D (while trailing in PSNR/SSIM) highlights that perceptual quality and pixel accuracy diverge significantly in this regime. However, the per-scene fine-tuning of both diffusion models (via leave-one-out for repair, random masking for inpainting) limits scalability and practical deployment, and the method cannot handle single-image inputs. Open questions include whether the repair model can be trained offline at scale to eliminate per-scene fine-tuning, and whether the two-stage strategy generalizes to even sparser settings or dynamic scenes.
