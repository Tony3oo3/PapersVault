## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models produce static appearances throughout a video and cannot generate gradual attribute transitions (e.g., day-to-night, sunny-to-rainy). Prompt interpolation methods designed for motion transitions fail on attribute transitions because the subtle, multi-frame changes amplify inconsistencies, causing objects to distort or shift abruptly across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252229/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two full descriptive text prompts specifying the initial and final attribute states of a scene (e.g., 'a bustling cityscape at midday, bathed in bright sunlight' → 'a nighttime cityscape under heavy rain') and produces a 32-frame video at the resolution supported by the VideoCrafter2 backbone. A neutral prompt is automatically derived by extracting the shared motion description from both prompts, and the transition is guided over 50 denoising steps with the first τ=5 steps reserved for structural layout via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

The approach is training-free and operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained T2V diffusion model (VideoCrafter2). It first crafts a data-dependent transitional direction in the ε-space by normalizing the difference between denoising scores conditioned on the final and initial prompts, yielding a local direction that increases the likelihood of the final attribute while decreasing the initial one. During denoising, each frame's score is adjusted along this direction with a frame-dependent scale factor, but to avoid the 'distance effect' (where scores drift too far from the attribute boundary hyperplane and cause distortions), the middle frame is anchored to a neutral prompt containing only motion information, and preceding/following frames are guided against/along the direction symmetrically. Classifier-free guidance is applied on top of the modified scores to preserve sample quality. The method also introduces CAT-Bench (120 prompt pairs across 8 attribute categories) and two CLIP-based metrics—Wholistic and Frame-wise Transition Scores—to evaluate transition correctness and smoothness.

## Results

On CAT-Bench, the proposed method achieves an Overall Wholistic Transition Score of 0.1486, outperforming the best multi-prompt baseline Gen-L (0.1166) by 27% and Free-Bloom (0.1077) by 38%. On the Frame-wise Transition Score (smoothness), the method scores 0.0201 versus 0.0135 for Gen-L and −0.0020 for Free-Bloom, indicating substantially smoother transitions. On TC-Bench-T2V, the method achieves TCR of 13.47 (Attribute) and 35.88 (Background) with TC-Scores of 0.6889 and 0.8043, respectively, surpassing the TC-Bench baseline. In the user study, the method is preferred by 46.19% of participants for overall performance, nearly double the next-best method (Gen-L at 28.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on both Wholistic and Frame-wise Transition Scores across all eight attribute categories. Among single-prompt baselines (AnimateDiff, ModelScope, Latte, VideoCrafter2), all achieve near-zero or negative scores (Overall ≤ 0.0082), confirming they cannot handle attribute transitions without retraining. Among multi-prompt baselines, Gen-L (0.1166) and Free-Bloom (0.1077) are the strongest competitors on Wholistic Score, but both suffer from poor smoothness: Free-Bloom's Frame-wise Score is negative (−0.0020), indicating frames frequently move against the intended transition direction, while Gen-L achieves only 0.0135. FreeNoise (0.0578) and VideoTetris (0.0134) perform notably worse, with FreeNoise particularly struggling on binary attributes like beard (0.0003). On TC-Bench-T2V, the method edges out the TC-Bench baseline (TCR 13.47 vs. 13.08 for Attribute; 35.88 vs. 35.43 for Background) and substantially outperforms FreeBloom (6.32/24.02 TCR) and VideoCrafter2 (4.25/7.06 TCR). In the user study, the method is preferred over Gen-L by a margin of ~17–21 percentage points across all five criteria, with the largest gap on Transition Correctness (49.12% vs. 28.91%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the score-based guidance framework from text-to-image diffusion (classifier-free guidance, semantic score guidance) for the video domain, but departs from the standard multi-prompt video generation paradigm (GenL, FreeNoise, FreeBloom, VideoTetris) that relies on prompt interpolation. The key conceptual shift is replacing prompt interpolation with a data-dependent transitional direction in the ε-space, applied frame-wise, motivated by the observation that interpolation handles discrete motion changes but fails for gradual attribute shifts. The neutral prompt anchoring mechanism is a novel stabilization technique that keeps denoising scores near the attribute boundary hyperplane, addressing a 'distance effect' not previously identified in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a concrete gap in video generation: gradual attribute transitions (color, weather, appearance) are fundamentally different from motion transitions and require a distinct mechanism, a distinction prior multi-prompt methods did not draw. CAT-Bench fills an evaluation gap by incorporating motion dynamics alongside attribute changes, which TC-Bench lacks. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the backbone model's representational capacity, and the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (VideoCrafter2) with 32-frame clips, leaving open questions about scalability to longer videos, more complex multi-attribute scenes, and different architectures (transformer-based vs. UNet-based).
