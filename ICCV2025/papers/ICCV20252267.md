## Problem

Discrete visual tokenizers must simultaneously learn to compress continuous visual signals and discretize them into a fixed codebook, a joint objective that causes unstable training, severe codebook under-utilization, and reconstruction quality far below what continuous VAEs achieve. The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that continuous VAEs and discrete tokenizers are mutually exclusive, asking whether a well-optimized continuous VAE can be directly adapted into a high-fidelity discrete tokenizer by focusing training effort solely on discretization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252267/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 256×256 RGB image and produces a sequence of discrete tokens structured as 10 levels of residual quantization codes (each level selecting one index from a codebook of up to 65,536 entries), operating at either 8× or 16× spatial compression. The decoder reconstructs the image from these multi-level tokens, and when paired with MaskGIT, the same token sequence serves as the generation target, with text prompts conditioning text-to-image synthesis on MS-COCO. A key structural detail is that each spatial position in the latent grid is represented by L=10 discrete codes rather than a single code, exponentially increasing representational capacity.

## Method

CODA decouples compression from discretization by freezing a pretrained continuous VAE (MAR 16× or FLUX 8×) as the compression backbone and learning only the discretization layer on top. Three mechanisms address the discretization challenges: (1) residual quantization with 10 levels progressively refines the approximation of each continuous latent vector, analogous to spline interpolation, reducing quantization error from 0.181 to 0.094; (2) an attention-based quantizer projects features and codebook entries onto a normed hypersphere via RMSNorm and computes softmax attention scores, enforcing sparse code assignment that achieves 100% codebook utilization (vs. 5.9% for VQGAN) by introducing a push-pull training dynamic; (3) LoRA adapters (rank 32) on the VAE's convolution blocks allow the encoder-decoder to adapt to the discretization-induced distribution shift, reducing rFID from 7.06 to 1.34. Training requires only 50K iterations at batch size 512—roughly 6× less compute than standard VQGAN—while achieving rFID of 0.43 (8×) and 1.34 (16×) on ImageNet.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, CODA achieves reconstruction FID (rFID) of 1.34 at 16× compression (MAR VAE base) and 0.43 at 8× compression (FLUX VAE base), with 100% codebook utilization at a codebook size of 65,536. This requires only 50K training iterations, a 6× reduction compared to VQGAN's 300K iterations. When paired with MaskGIT for image generation, CODA achieves FID 2.66 (32 steps) and 3.17 (8 steps), outperforming LDM-4-G (3.60), LlamaGen (3.07), and VAR (3.30) while using fewer sampling steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODA decisively outperforms all discrete tokenizer baselines on reconstruction quality: at 16× compression, CODA MAR achieves rFID 1.34, beating the closest competitor MaskBit (1.61) by 0.27 and LlamaGen (2.19) by 0.85, while using 27× fewer training iterations than MaskBit (50K vs. 1350K) and 6× fewer than VQGAN (50K vs. 300K). At 8× compression, CODA FLUX (rFID 0.43) surpasses OmniTokenizer (1.11), VQGAN† (1.14), and ViT-VQGAN (1.28) by a wide margin. Codebook utilization is a key differentiator: CODA achieves 100% utilization at 65,536 codes, whereas VQGAN drops to 5.9% at 16,384 codes and LlamaGen reaches only 97%. For image generation, CODA MaskGIT-L (32 steps, FID 2.66) beats LDM-4-G (3.60), LlamaGen-L-384 (3.07), and VAR (3.30), while the 8-step variant (FID 3.17) still outperforms ENAT (3.53) and VAR (3.30) with fewer sampling steps. On MS-COCO text-to-image, CODA MaskGIT-S achieves FID 5.44, improving over U-ViT-S (5.95) and U-Net (7.32) baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODA departs from the standard VQGAN paradigm, where compression and discretization are learned jointly from scratch, by decoupling the two: it starts from pretrained continuous VAEs (MAR for 16× and FLUX for 8×) that are already optimized for perceptual compression, and learns only the discretization layer on top. The quantization mechanism draws on residual quantization (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) to handle representational capacity, but introduces a novel attention-based assignment strategy that frames code selection as a retrieval task over a normed hypersphere, replacing the standard Euclidean nearest-neighbor lookup. For downstream generation, CODA integrates with MaskGIT (Chang et al., CVPR 2022), positioning itself as a drop-in tokenizer improvement rather than a new generation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CODA achieves near-continuous-VAE reconstruction quality (rFID 1.34 vs. 0.69 for the underlying MAR VAE) with 6× less training compute than VQGAN and 100% codebook utilization at 65536 codes, compared to VQGAN's 5.9% at 16384. This suggests that the long-standing instability and codebook collapse problems in discrete tokenizers may be largely artifacts of jointly learning compression and discretization, and that leveraging pretrained continuous representations is a more efficient path forward. Open questions include whether this approach scales to video tokenization, larger codebooks beyond 65536, and whether the LoRA-based VAE adaptation generalizes to architectures without a clear encoder-decoder split. The paper is limited to ImageNet 256×256, so claims about broader applicability remain untested.
