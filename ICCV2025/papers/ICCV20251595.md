## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving typically depends on costly perception annotations (3D bounding boxes, HD maps) to extract scene information, limiting scalability. The key challenge is building an informative driving world model that enables perception annotation-free planning via self-supervised learning. Prior latent world models like LAW extract single-modal features from raw images, which fail to capture spatial-semantic scene structure and multi-modal driving intention uncertainty, leading to slow convergence and suboptimal planning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images (360×640 on nuScenes) or a stitched 3-view image (256×1024 on NavSim) together with a predefined trajectory vocabulary of 8192 trajectories (each with S waypoints). The intention encoder applies k-means clustering on trajectory endpoints to derive 3 command types × 6 intentions per type, producing K multi-modal trajectory candidates. The final output is a single ego planning trajectory selected by the world model selector from among the K candidates, sampled at 2 Hz over a 3-second (nuScenes) or 4-second (NavSim) horizon.

## Method

World4Drive couples two modules: a Driving World Encoding stage and an Intention-aware World Model. The encoding stage enriches image features with spatial priors (forward-projected 3D positions from Metric3D v2 depth maps, encoded via sinusoidal positional encoding + MLP) and semantic priors (pseudo-labels from Grounded-SAM with cross-entropy supervision), then aggregates temporal context via cross-attention between consecutive frames. The intention encoder extracts multi-modal driving intentions from the trajectory vocabulary via k-means clustering and self-attention with an ego query. The world model predicts future latent representations for each intention modality using multi-layer cross-attention between a learnable future query and concatenated action tokens + current latents. A World Model Selector computes MSE distances between predicted and actual future latents to pick the best modality, while a ScoreNet trained with focal loss learns to rank modalities directly at inference. Training is self-supervised: the reconstruction loss aligns predicted latents with actual future-frame latents, supplemented by L1 trajectory loss, semantic cross-entropy, and focal scoring loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes open-loop benchmark, World4Drive achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.50 m and an average collision rate of 0.16%, representing an 18.0% relative reduction in L2 error and a 46.7% reduction in collision rate over the prior SOTA perception-free method LAW (0.61 m / 0.30%). On the NavSim closed-loop benchmark, World4Drive attains a PDMS score of 85.1, surpassing LAW (83.8) and all other camera-only baselines, though trailing DiffusionDrive (88.1) which uses LiDAR. The method also converges 3.75× faster than LAW during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

World4Drive outperforms the primary perception-free baseline LAW [21] on both benchmarks: on nuScenes it reduces average L2 error from 0.61 m to 0.50 m (−18.0%) and average collision rate from 0.30% to 0.16% (−46.7%), while on NavSim it improves PDMS from 83.8 to 85.1. Against perception-annotated methods, World4Drive matches or exceeds VAD (L2 0.50 vs 0.72 m; CR 0.16% vs 0.23%), UniAD (L2 0.50 vs 1.03 m; CR 0.16% vs 0.31%), PPAD (L2 0.50 vs 0.58 m), and GenAD (L2 0.50 vs 0.52 m) on nuScenes, all without any 3D bounding-box or HD-map supervision. Compared to the perception-based variant of LAW (L2 0.49 m, CR 0.19%), World4Drive is within 0.01 m on L2 while achieving a lower collision rate (0.16% vs 0.19%). On NavSim, World4Drive (PDMS 85.1) surpasses Transfuser (84.0), PARA-Drive (84.0), UniAD (83.4), VADv2 (80.9), and Hydra-MDP (83.0), but trails DiffusionDrive (88.1), which leverages LiDAR input. The method also demonstrates superior robustness in adverse conditions, reducing collision rates by 63.7% (night) and 68.8% (rainy) relative to LAW.

## Relation to Prior Work

World4Drive extends the latent world model paradigm introduced by LAW [21], which first demonstrated that end-to-end driving can be trained without perception annotations via temporal self-supervision on latent features. The key departures are threefold: (1) replacing LAW's single-modal latent prediction with multi-modal intention-conditioned future latents, (2) enriching the physical latent encoder with spatial priors from a metric depth model (Metric3D v2) and semantic priors from a vision-language model (Grounded-SAM), and (3) introducing a world model selector that scores and ranks trajectory candidates rather than relying solely on reconstruction distance. The trajectory vocabulary and ego-query mechanism are inherited from VADv2 [3], and the 3D positional encoding adapts PETR's [28] post-projection meshgrid approach into a forward-projection depth-based formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA planning performance without any manual perception annotations (3D boxes, HD maps), World4Drive addresses a major scalability bottleneck in end-to-end driving research, where annotation costs have limited dataset size and diversity. The 3.75× faster convergence and 46.7% collision-rate reduction over the prior annotation-free SOTA (LAW) are practically meaningful for teams with limited labeling budgets. However, the reliance on two external vision foundation models (Grounded-SAM and Metric3D v2) introduces additional inference latency and engineering complexity that the paper does not fully quantify, and the closed-loop NavSim results, while competitive, still trail the strongest perception-annotated methods (DiffusionDrive). Open questions include whether the world model selector generalizes to truly novel or adversarial scenarios beyond the training distribution, and whether the annotation-free paradigm can close the remaining gap to fully supervised approaches as datasets scale.
