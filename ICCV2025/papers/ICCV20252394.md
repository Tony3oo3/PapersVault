## Problem

Current BEV-based multi-UAV collaborative perception relies on bounding-box representations that fail to capture complete geometric and semantic scene information, and degrades significantly on occluded or undefined objects. Extending 3D occupancy prediction to multi-UAV collaboration introduces a new challenge: UAVs operate at altitudes 10× higher than ground vehicles, vastly expanding the feature space and making direct transmission of 3D occupancy features infeasible for real-time communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each UAV ingests a single RGB image (resolutions range from 800×450 in virtual scenes to 5472×3648 in the real-world GauUScene dataset) and produces a 3D semantic occupancy voxel grid with seven categories (free, others, ground, building, vegetation, vehicle, urban road) at 0.4 m³ or 2 m³ voxel resolution. For inter-UAV communication, the full 3D occupancy feature is compressed into a 2D BEV representation of only 0.23 MB per transmission, compared to 17.50–19.14 MB for baseline collaborative methods, while the ego UAV ultimately outputs a fused 3D occupancy segmentation.

## Method

MCOP comprises four modules: (1) a Spatial-Aware Feature Encoder that uses a ResNet101-DCN/FPN backbone followed by Voxel-Image Attention (deformable attention linking 3D voxel queries to image features) and Cross-Voxel Attention to produce dense 3D occupancy features without depth sensors; (2) Altitude-Aware Reduction, which compresses 3D features to 2D BEV by computing a mask-weighted average along the Z-axis and concatenating a per-pillar altitude encoding (mean valid altitude normalized to [0,1]), then applying a 2D convolution; (3) Dual-Mask Perceptual Guidance, which assigns each BEV grid a quality score from distance and feature gradient, generating a support mask (high-confidence) and request mask (low-confidence) so that the ego UAV broadcasts its request mask and connected UAVs transmit only features in the intersection of the request mask and their projected support mask; and (4) Cross-Agent Feature Integration, which upsamples received 2D features back to 3D, concatenates with the ego's local 3D features, and refines them through residual 3D convolutions before an MLP head predicts semantic occupancy. Training uses focal loss for segmentation plus an L1 communication-constraint loss to minimize transmitted data volume.

## Results

MCOP achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all three datasets while reducing communication volume to 0.23 MB per transmission (vs. 17.50 MB for BEVDet and 19.14 MB for PanoOcc). On Air-to-Pred-Occ, MCOP reaches 46.41 mIoU, surpassing collaborative PanoOcc (41.96) by 4.45 points and collaborative BEVDet (12.29) by 34.12 points. On UAV3D-Occ, MCOP achieves 47.89 mIoU (+3.16 over PanoOcc), and on the real-world GauUScene-Occ, it reaches 42.92 mIoU (+0.23 over PanoOcc). The Altitude-Aware Reduction module compresses 3D features to 2D with only 0.75% mIoU loss, and the Dual-Mask Perceptual Guidance at threshold 0.8 reduces communication by ~80% with negligible accuracy cost (0.01% loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCOP is compared against two adapted autonomous-driving baselines: BEVDet (with FlashOcc for occupancy conversion) and PanoOcc (direct occupancy prediction), each with and without a Where2comm-based collaboration module. Against collaborative PanoOcc, MCOP wins on all three datasets: +4.45 mIoU on Air-to-Pred-Occ (46.41 vs. 41.96), +3.16 on UAV3D-Occ (47.89 vs. 44.73), and +0.23 on GauUScene-Occ (42.92 vs. 42.69), while using only 0.23 MB of communication versus 19.14 MB (~83× less). Against collaborative BEVDet, the margin is far larger (+34 to +36 mIoU), reflecting BEVDet's inherent weakness in 3D occupancy representation. The key advantage of MCOP is that its altitude-aware 2D compression preserves enough 3D geometric and semantic information to outperform direct 2D BEV transmission, achieving both higher accuracy and dramatically lower bandwidth simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCOP builds on the voxel-query occupancy prediction paradigm from PanoOcc (3D voxel queries with deformable attention) and the BEVFormer querying mechanism for perspective-to-voxel feature transfer, repurposing both for aerial viewpoints. It departs from the dominant BEV-based collaborative perception line (BEVDet, Where2comm, CoBEVT) by replacing 2D BEV feature sharing with compressed 3D occupancy features, and extends Where2comm's single spatial-confidence map into a dual-mask (support + request) protocol tailored to the high-overlap, altitude-varied geometry of UAV swarms. The Altitude-Aware Reduction module is a novel compression strategy with no direct analogue in the autonomous-driving collaborative perception literature, motivated by the 10× higher operating altitude of UAVs relative to ground vehicles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap by releasing three occupancy-annotated UAV datasets (two CARLA-based, one real-world) that did not previously exist, providing the first benchmark for multi-UAV collaborative semantic occupancy prediction. The 76× communication reduction (76.56 MB → 0.23 MB) with negligible accuracy loss (0.75%) makes real-time inter-UAV collaboration practically feasible, which is critical for bandwidth-constrained aerial platforms. Open questions include scalability to larger swarms or dynamic scenes, the limited seven-class taxonomy, and whether the altitude-aware compression generalizes to non-uniform altitude distributions or multi-floor indoor environments. The real-world evaluation relies on a single 0.9 km² subset treated as a static four-UAV cluster, leaving robustness to motion and communication latency untested.
