## Problem

Existing vision-language-action models rely on compact action heads (small MLPs or shallow DiT decoders) to predict discretized or continuous actions, which constrains adaptability to the heterogeneous action spaces and diverse camera views found in large-scale cross-embodiment datasets. Conditioning denoising on early-fused embeddings from a causal Transformer further limits the model's ability to capture fine-grained visual nuances—such as action deltas across historical observations—needed for long-horizon and multi-step manipulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a frozen CLIP tokenization of the language instruction and 2-frame third-person RGB images (processed by a jointly-optimized DINOv2 encoder followed by a Q-Former with FiLM conditioning for instruction-aware feature selection) as input. It outputs a continuous 7D end-effector action chunk (3D translation, 3D rotation, 1D gripper) of length 16, where the action vector is zero-padded to match the token dimensionality of image and language features. Notably, the model operates with only a single third-person camera and no proprioceptive or wrist-camera input, keeping the architecture at 334M parameters.

## Method

Dita replaces the conventional small diffusion action head with a full DiT-style causal Transformer that denoises the entire action chunk in an in-context conditioning manner: language tokens, DINOv2 image patch features, timestep embeddings, and the noisy action tokens are concatenated into a single sequence and processed jointly, so that denoising is conditioned directly on raw visual patches rather than on a single fused embedding. This design lets the model attend to subtle spatial and temporal changes across historical observations (e.g., action deltas) without an intermediate bottleneck. Training uses the standard DDPM objective (1000 timesteps, MSE on predicted noise) with AdamW, while inference accelerates to 20 DDIM steps. The model is pretrained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset across 32 A100 GPUs (batch size 8192, 100K steps) and adapted to new real-robot tasks via 10-shot LoRA finetuning, demonstrating that the in-context conditioning mechanism outperforms both discretization heads and compact diffusion heads on long-horizon and camera-view generalization benchmarks.

## Results

On LIBERO, Dita achieves an 82.4% average success rate across four sub-datasets, outperforming OpenVLA (76.5%), Octo (75.1%), and Diffusion Policy (72.4%), with a particularly strong 63.8% on LIBERO-LONG (vs. 53.7% for OpenVLA). On SimplerEnv (zero-shot), Dita reaches 83.7% on coke_can (match) and 76.0% on move_near (match), substantially exceeding RT-1-X (56.7%, 31.7%) and OpenVLA (16.3%, 46.2%). On CALVIN (ABC→D, single RGB camera), Dita attains an average success length of 3.61, competitive with GHIL-Glue (3.69) and surpassing all other single-RGB methods. In real-robot 10-shot finetuning, Dita achieves a 63.8% success rate on two-step tasks, with the second stage contributing nearly half of overall success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dita decisively outperforms Octo [72] across all benchmarks: on SimplerEnv it achieves 83.7% vs. 17.0% on coke_can (match), and on LIBERO it gains 7.3 points in average success rate (82.4% vs. 75.1%). Against OpenVLA [32], Dita wins on LIBERO average (82.4% vs. 76.5%) and especially on the long-horizon LIBERO-LONG subset (63.8% vs. 53.7%), though OpenVLA edges ahead on SimplerEnv drawer-variant (17.7% vs. 37.5% — actually Dita wins here too at 37.5%). On SimplerEnv, Dita surpasses RT-1-X [8] on coke_can (83.7% vs. 56.7%) and move_near (76.0% vs. 31.7%) but trails on drawer-match (46.3% vs. 59.7%). On CALVIN, Dita (3.61 avg. length, S-RGB only) beats RoboFlamingo [36] (2.47), GR-1 [78] (3.06), 3D Diffuser [30] (3.27), and SuSIE [4] (2.69), but falls short of GR-MG [34] (4.04) which uses richer inputs (RGB-D, gripper, proprioception). Dita is nearly tied with GHIL-Glue [4,24] (3.61 vs. 3.69) using only a single static RGB camera, while GHIL-Glue relies on further finetuned generative models. Against the from-scratch Diffusion Policy [17] baseline, Dita gains 10 points on LIBERO average (82.4% vs. 72.4%), confirming the benefit of cross-embodiment pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dita repurposes the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture originally designed for image generation [56] as a unified action denoiser, departing from the dominant paradigm in generalist VLA models (Octo, π0) where a large causal Transformer produces a single fused embedding that conditions a small MLP or DiT diffusion head. The key architectural departure is in-context conditioning: rather than compressing visual and linguistic information into a bottleneck embedding before denoising, Dita concatenates raw DINOv2 image patch tokens, CLIP language tokens, timestep embeddings, and noisy action tokens into a single causal Transformer sequence, allowing the denoiser to attend directly to fine-grained visual details. This positions Dita between the discretized-action approach of RT-1/OpenVLA and the small-diffusion-head approach of Octo, arguing that the Transformer's native scalability is better exploited when the denoising process itself is the Transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dita provides a clean, lightweight (334M parameter), fully open-source baseline that demonstrates a 334M model can match or exceed 7B-parameter VLA models on zero-shot simulation benchmarks, suggesting that architectural design (in-context conditioning) may matter as much as model scale for robot policy learning. The 10-shot real-robot finetuning with only a third-person camera achieving 63.8% on multi-step long-horizon tasks is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-robot or bimanual settings, whether the single-camera constraint is a fundamental limitation or merely a design choice, and whether the in-context conditioning advantage persists at much larger model scales where fused-embedding approaches might close the gap.
