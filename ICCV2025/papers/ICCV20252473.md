## Problem

Infrared small targets occupy only a few pixels and lack texture, so existing visual-only detection methods struggle to discriminate targets from clutter in complex scenes. While text-image models have advanced generic object detection, they cannot be directly applied to IRSTD because infrared targets have ambiguous categories (e.g., a bright spot that cannot be identified as a specific object), which invalidates standard text-image semantic matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 infrared image and a fuzzy semantic text prompt (e.g., "A photo of a sky target in the sky and ground background") as dual inputs. The text prompt deliberately avoids specifying a concrete object category, using [Interested Region] and [Scene] placeholders instead; for unseen scenes a default prompt ("A photo of a small target in the background") is used. The output is a pixel-level detection map produced by a 1×1 convolution followed by a sigmoid activation on the decoder output.

## Method

Text-IRSTD pairs a ResNeSt image encoder (four groups of blocks) with a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32 text encoder, feeding both into a Progressive Cross-modal Semantic Interaction Decoder (PCSID) composed of three stacked TGFA+TGSI block pairs. The TGFA block converts text features into two modulation maps via an MLP, element-wise multiplying them with low-level and high-level image features respectively; the low-level path then passes through a Detail Perception Module (multi-scale 3×3/5×5 depthwise convolutions + pointwise context aggregation) while the high-level path uses channel attention to suppress redundant features. The TGSI block maps text features through an FFN into a gain parameter α_t and an offset parameter β_t, which modulate image features as M̂ = (1+α_t)⊗M + β_t, enabling simultaneous region-of-interest focusing and fine-grained contour recovery. The key design insight is that fuzzy semantic prompts establish robust text-image associations for ambiguous IR targets, and the dual-perspective (gain + offset) modulation captures complementary aspects of text-image interaction that simple concatenation or single-parameter modulation cannot.

## Results

On NUDT-SIRST, Text-IRSTD achieves the best IoU of 95.84%, Pd of 99.73%, Fa of 1.032×10⁻⁶, and F1 of 97.95%, outperforming the previous best (SCTrans: IoU 94.83%, Fa 2.065×10⁻⁶). On the more challenging IRSTD-1k, Text-IRSTD reaches IoU 69.57% and Pd 92.59%, surpassing the best visual-only baseline DNANet (IoU 64.51%) by over 5 percentage points in IoU. In the unseen-scenario test (trained on NUDT-SIRST, tested on NUAA-SIRST), Text-IRSTD also leads with IoU 54.51% and Fa 29.86×10⁻⁶, compared to UIUNet's 53.32% IoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text-IRSTD outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across both NUDT-SIRST and IRSTD-1k. On NUDT-SIRST, the closest competitor is SCTrans (IoU 94.83%, Fa 2.065×10⁻⁶), which Text-IRSTD surpasses by 1.01% in IoU and halves the false alarm rate. On the harder IRSTD-1k, the gap widens: DNANet achieves the best visual-only IoU of 64.51%, while Text-IRSTD reaches 69.57%—a 5.06-point improvement—attributed to text-guided cross-modal interaction. Among traditional methods, IPI [4] is the strongest (IoU 38.15% on NUDT-SIRST) but remains far below all deep learning baselines. In the unseen-scenario experiment (NUDT-SIRST → NUAA-SIRST), Text-IRSTD leads with IoU 54.51% and Fa 29.86×10⁻⁶, beating UIUNet (53.32%, 35.15×10⁻⁶) and SCTrans (50.97%, 37.71×10⁻⁶), confirming better generalization. Notably, even the text-free variant (Text-IRSTD w/o text) already exceeds all visual-only baselines, indicating the architectural design itself contributes meaningfully beyond the text prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established IRSTD literature, which has focused exclusively on visual feature mining (filter-based, saliency-based, and deep learning approaches like ACMNet, AGPCNet, DNANet, and SCTrans), by introducing semantic text as an additional modality to guide detection. It repurposes the CLIP text encoder from generic vision-language tasks (RegionCLIP, ViLD, CORA) but addresses a fundamental incompatibility: IR small targets are too ambiguous in category for standard explicit text prompts to work, motivating the novel fuzzy semantic text prompt. The PCSID decoder (combining TGFA and TGSI blocks) is a new cross-modal fusion architecture specifically designed for the low-information regime of IR small targets, rather than adapting existing text-image detection pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a genuinely new research direction—text-guided IRSTD—and provides the first text-image bimodal benchmark (FZDT, 2,755 images) to support future work in this space. The demonstrated generalization to unseen scenarios (NUAA-SIRST) suggests the text-guided approach may be particularly valuable in operational settings where deployment conditions differ from training. However, the text prompts remain relatively simple template-based descriptions, the CLIP encoder is frozen, and evaluation is limited to three datasets; open questions include whether richer or learned text prompts, video-level temporal context, or more diverse scene categories would further unlock the potential of this paradigm.
