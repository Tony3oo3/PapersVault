## Problem

Open-world segmentation has developed along two isolated paradigms—open-vocabulary segmentation (text-driven) and in-context segmentation (image-driven)—that differ in architecture, learning objectives, and representation strategies. No prior work unifies both within a single model, leaving the complementary strengths of textual and visual prompts unexploited and preventing synergistic generalization across segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

COSINE takes a target RGB image together with one or more prompt types: text prompts (category names or referring expressions) and/or image prompts (example images with binary masks). Prompts can be supplied individually or jointly, and the model outputs segmentation masks at multiple granularities (semantic, instance, panoptic) along with per-query classification scores. The frozen foundation models in the Model Pool convert all inputs into standardized token sequences, so the SegDecoder operates on a unified representation regardless of prompt modality.

## Method

COSINE consists of a frozen Model Pool (DINOv2 and CLIP vision encoders plus CLIP text encoder) that extracts image and prompt features, and a lightweight trainable SegDecoder. The SegDecoder first standardizes features via adapters (Feature Blender for multi-model image features, V-Adapter and T-Adapter for visual and text prompts), then applies an Image-Prompt Aligner (stacked self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN layers) to reduce the modality gap between image and prompt representations. A Pixel Decoder upsamples blended image features to high resolution, while a Multi-Modality Decoder (dual-path self- and cross-attention) jointly refines object queries and both prompt types to produce classification scores and mask predictions. Only the SegDecoder is trained end-to-end with bipartite matching, cross-entropy, binary mask, and Dice losses, keeping training overhead low. A key empirical finding is that co-training both visual and textual branches—and optionally fusing both prompt types at inference—yields significant gains over either modality alone, demonstrating synergistic cross-modal generalization.

## Results

On few-shot semantic segmentation (LVIS-92i), COSINE achieves 35.2 mIoU (1-shot) and 40.7 mIoU (5-shot), surpassing the best in-context baseline Matcher (33.0/40.0) and the few-shot specialist DiffewS (31.4/35.4). For open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation on ADE20K, COSINE reaches 31.0 PQ and 21.1 AP, outperforming FC-CLIP (26.8 PQ) and ODISE (23.4 PQ). On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, COSINE attains 15.6 mIoU on A-847 and 19.2 mIoU on PC-459, exceeding all universal models. For referring segmentation, COSINE achieves 77.2 cIoU on refCOCO val, surpassing LISA (74.9) and ReLA (73.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSINE outperforms all in-context segmentation baselines on few-shot semantic segmentation, beating Matcher by 2.2 mIoU (1-shot) and 0.7 mIoU (5-shot), and SINE by 4.0 mIoU (1-shot). On few-shot instance segmentation (LVIS), COSINE's 20.3 AP substantially exceeds DINOv (15.4) and SINE (8.6), with a 25.8 APr on rare categories. For open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation on ADE20K, COSINE leads FC-CLIP by 4.2 PQ and ODISE by 7.6 PQ; however, on Cityscapes, FC-CLIP retains a slight edge (44.0 vs. 42.0 PQ). On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, COSINE surpasses FC-CLIP (15.6 vs. 14.8 on A-847; 19.2 vs. 18.2 on PC-459) and all universal models, though it trails the specialized SED model (13.9/22.6) on A-847/PC-459. For referring segmentation, COSINE wins on refCOCO val (77.2 vs. LISA 74.9) and refCOCO+ testA (73.2 vs. 70.8), but LISA remains stronger on refCOCO testB (72.3 vs. 71.1) and refCOCOg test (70.6 vs. 68.5). Among non-video VOS models, COSINE is competitive with SINE (76.7 vs. 77.0 on DAVIS; 66.0 vs. 66.4 on YT-VOS), while the fine-tuned variant COSINE-FT (80.2/70.0) clearly surpasses all non-video baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSINE unifies two previously isolated paradigms—open-vocabulary segmentation (ODISE, FC-CLIP, SED) and in-context segmentation (SegGPT, SINE, Matcher)—into a single decoder-only framework. It builds directly on SINE's dual-path Multi-Modality Decoder design and extends the frozen-foundation-model philosophy of Matcher and DINOv by adding CLIP's text encoder to the Model Pool. The key architectural departure is the Image-Prompt Aligner, which explicitly harmonizes heterogeneous representations from different modalities before joint decoding, addressing the architectural discrepancies and divergent learning objectives that have kept the two paradigms separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that visual and textual prompts synergistically improve each other's performance both during training and inference—provides a concrete argument for multi-modal prompt collaboration in future universal segmentation models. However, COSINE still trails specialized models on individual tasks (e.g., SED on open-vocabulary semantic segmentation), and the synergy results are demonstrated on a single architecture with limited dataset coverage. Open questions include whether the synergy generalizes to other foundation model combinations, whether it holds at larger scale, and whether the 1:1 co-training ratio is optimal or task-dependent.
