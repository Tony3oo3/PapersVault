## Problem

Existing pansharpening methods struggle to simultaneously capture intricate spatial-spectral interactions while preserving inter-channel dependencies, because conventional real-valued networks handle multi-channel inputs by stacking and mapping to single output channels, causing information loss. This limits both spectral fidelity and spatial detail in the fused output, as the network cannot effectively model the latent relationships among spectral bands or local spatial structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250818/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a panchromatic image P ∈ R^{H×W×1} and a low-resolution multispectral image L ∈ R^{H/r×W/r×C} (where r is the resolution ratio, typically 4), bicubic-upsamples L by factor r, and produces a high-resolution multispectral image of size H×W×C. During training, inputs are cropped into 32×32 MS patches paired with 128×128 PAN patches, and the network is supervised with a joint spatial L1 loss plus a quaternion Fourier-domain L1 loss (weighted λ=0.1) against ground-truth HRMS images.

## Method

QuatPanNet is a CNN-based framework built from 5 stacked Quaternion Spatial-Spectral Interaction Blocks (QSSIBs), each containing three branches operating in quaternion space. The global spectral branch applies a left-sided Quaternion Fourier Transform to channel-quaternion-represented features, decomposing them into amplitude and three phase components, then fuses PAN and MS spectra via quaternion convolution before inverse QFT, preserving inter-channel dependencies through the quaternion structure. The local spatial branch uses a spatial quaternion representation (2×2 pixel windows as quaternion components) combined with a shifted-window strategy (inspired by Swin Transformer) to maintain local structural dependencies while enabling cross-window spatial interactions. The spatial-spectral interaction branch integrates both pathways by combining their outputs into a unified quaternion for global fusion and by injecting spatial features into each component of the channel-dimension quaternion for channel-aware spatial injection. The entire network uses only 0.0387M parameters, leveraging quaternion parameter-sharing to achieve compact cross-resolution, multi-band information integration.

## Results

QuatPanNet achieves the best performance across all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ERGAS) on all three benchmark datasets. On WorldView-II, it reaches 42.48 dB PSNR and 0.9738 SSIM; on WorldView-III, 31.18 dB PSNR and 0.9332 SSIM; and on GaoFen-2, 49.14 dB PSNR and 0.9901 SSIM. The method uses only 0.0387M parameters, the fewest among all compared deep learning methods, while outperforming the next-best method (FAME-Net, 0.1408M params) by 0.25–1.47 dB in PSNR depending on the dataset. On full-resolution no-reference evaluation, QuatPanNet also leads on all three metrics (D_λ=0.0566, D_S=0.1055, QNR=0.8447).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuatPanNet outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest deep learning competitors, it beats FAME-Net (0.1408M params) by 0.46 dB PSNR on WV2, 0.19 dB on WV3, and 1.47 dB on GF2, while using roughly 3.6× fewer parameters. It also surpasses HFIN by 0.25 dB (WV2), 0.56 dB (WV3), and 0.26 dB (GF2) in PSNR. On spectral fidelity metrics, QuatPanNet achieves the lowest SAM (0.0208/0.0521/0.0090) and ERGAS (0.8554/2.5761/0.4474) across all datasets, indicating superior spectral preservation. On full-resolution no-reference evaluation (Table 2), it leads all methods on D_λ (0.0566 vs. 0.0674 for FAME-Net), D_S (0.1055 vs. 0.1098 for HFIN), and QNR (0.8447 vs. 0.8291 for FAME-Net), confirming generalization to real-world scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes quaternion neural network operations (quaternion convolution, Quaternion Fourier Transform) from speech and color-image processing into the pansharpening domain, where all prior deep-learning methods operate in real-valued space. It draws on the frequency-domain pansharpening line (HFIN, FAME-Net, FourMer) but replaces per-channel DFT with a holistic QFT that preserves inter-channel dependencies, and adapts the Swin Transformer's shifted-window strategy into a spatial quaternion representation. The key conceptual departure from prior pansharpening CNNs is treating multi-band features as a unified quaternion entity with parameter sharing rather than stacking channels and mapping them independently, which the authors argue reduces information loss in cross-channel interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter efficiency is the most practically relevant finding: QuatPanNet achieves SOTA with 0.0387M parameters, roughly half to a third of competing methods (0.07–0.14M), suggesting quaternion parameter sharing is a viable route to lighter pansharpening models. However, the performance margins over the strongest baselines (HFIN, FAME-Net) are modest (0.18–0.27 dB PSNR), and the quaternion channel grouping (splitting C channels into four equal parts) is somewhat ad-hoc and may not generalize cleanly to hyperspectral data with many more bands. Open questions include whether the QFT-based global branch scales efficiently to very large images, whether the shifted-window quaternion strategy offers advantages over standard attention-based spatial modeling, and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond pansharpening (e.g., hyperspectral super-resolution).
