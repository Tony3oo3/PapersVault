## Problem

Unsupervised temporal action segmentation has been extensively studied for RGB video but remains largely unexplored for skeleton sequences, despite their robustness, privacy-preserving nature, and structural richness. Existing video-based unsupervised methods fail on skeleton data because they do not exploit the inherent joint structure, while prior skeleton-based approaches are either fully supervised or limited to short, single-action sequences for recognition rather than long untrimmed sequences containing multiple actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250576/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed skeleton sequences of shape N×C×T×V (batch × per-joint channels × frames × joints), where C encodes joint positions, orientations, or IMU readings depending on the dataset (e.g., 6 IMU channels at 60 Hz for HuGaDB, 22 joints at 50 fps for LARa, 25 SMPL joints at 30 fps for BABEL). It produces per-frame action segment labels Y ∈ {1,…,K}^T derived directly from patch-level quantization assignments, along with a learned codebook of K skeleton motion words that serve as discrete prototypes for the discovered actions.

## Method

SMQ is a sequence-to-sequence temporal autoencoder with a joint-disentangled embedding space and patch-based vector quantization. The encoder is a dilated TCN that processes each joint independently (reshaping input to (N·V)×C×T), so that joint information remains disentangled in the latent space rather than being dominated by a subset of joints. Latent representations are divided into non-overlapping temporal patches of fixed size P (1 second of motion), and each patch is quantized to its nearest entry in a learned codebook of K skeleton motion words via Euclidean distance, with codebook updates performed using exponential moving averages. The decoder mirrors the encoder to reconstruct the input from quantized patches, and training uses a translation/rotation-invariant inter-joint distance MSE loss combined with a VQ-VAE commitment loss. The segmentation is obtained directly from the patch-to-codebook assignment, requiring no post-processing such as Viterbi decoding.

## Results

On HuGaDB, SMQ achieves a MoF of 42.0 and Edit Score of 36.1, surpassing the best unsupervised baseline (CTE+Viterbi) by 2.8 MoF and 14.4 Edit. On LARa, SMQ reaches MoF 37.4, Edit 39.4, and F1@50 16.4, outperforming the strongest unsupervised method ASOT (MoF 22.9, Edit 23.4, F1@50 5.7) by a large margin. On BABEL, SMQ outperforms all unsupervised baselines on Subset-2 (MoF 49.1 vs. 43.1 for ASOT) and Subset-3 (MoF 40.6 vs. 38.0), while ASOT edges ahead on a few Subset-1 metrics. SMQ also consistently outperforms self-supervised skeleton representation learning methods (Predict&Cluster, AimCLR, ActCLR) across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMQ outperforms all unsupervised temporal action segmentation baselines (CTE, TOT, ASOT) on both HuGaDB and LARa by substantial margins: on LARa, SMQ's F1@50 of 16.4 exceeds ASOT's 5.7 by more than 10 points, and its Edit Score of 39.4 nearly doubles ASOT's 23.4. On BABEL, SMQ wins on Subset-2 (MoF 49.1 vs. ASOT's 43.1) and Subset-3 (MoF 40.6 vs. 38.0), though ASOT retains a slight edge on a few Subset-1 metrics (e.g., Edit 43.1 vs. 38.5). Against self-supervised skeleton representation learning methods (Predict&Cluster, AimCLR, ActCLR), SMQ achieves the highest scores across all three datasets, with the largest gap on LARa (MoF 37.4 vs. 22.0 for AimCLR). Supervised methods (MS-GCN, MS-TCN) still achieve substantially higher scores (e.g., MoF 90.4 on HuGaDB), but they require full frame-level annotations, whereas SMQ operates entirely without labels. The qualitative analysis shows that CTE and TOT produce overly fragmented segments requiring Viterbi post-processing, while ASOT tends toward overly large segments; SMQ avoids both failure modes and better captures re-occurring actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMQ adapts the VQ-VAE quantization mechanism (codebook assignment, EMA updates, commitment loss) from Van Den Oord et al. to the skeleton domain, but introduces two key departures: a joint-disentangled TCN encoder that processes each joint independently, and patch-based (rather than frame-wise) quantization to capture short-term temporal motion patterns. It explicitly departs from prior unsupervised temporal action segmentation methods (CTE, TOT, ASOT), which were designed for RGB video and rely on Viterbi decoding or optimal transport post-processing that fails on skeleton sequences. It also differs from self-supervised skeleton representation learning (AimCLR, ActCLR, Predict&Cluster), which operate on short single-action sequences and require annotated data for downstream evaluation, whereas SMQ discovers and segments actions jointly during training on long untrimmed sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap by showing that video-based unsupervised TAS methods degrade substantially on skeleton data, motivating skeleton-specific architectures. The validated design choices—joint-disentanglement and one-second patching—provide practical guidance for future skeleton sequence modeling. Key limitations remain: action transitions are restricted to patch boundaries, and the method still struggles with precise boundary detection (e.g., confusing standing up vs. sitting down). The authors identify hierarchical SMQ and improved boundary detection as natural next steps, and the approach could serve as a foundation for privacy-preserving, annotation-free action understanding in real-world deployment.
