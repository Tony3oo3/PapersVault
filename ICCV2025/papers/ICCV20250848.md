## Problem

Existing egocentric 3D full-body pose estimation methods rely exclusively on frontal HMD cameras, leaving the back of the body unobserved and causing failures under common motions like head tilting due to self-occlusion and limited field-of-view. Moreover, naively appending rear views to frontal inputs does not improve—and can even degrade—accuracy in current methods, because they depend on per-view 2D joint detectors without effective multi-view integration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four egocentric fisheye RGB images (two front, two rear) at 872×872 pixels from a helmet-mounted HMD prototype with 12 cm inter-camera spacing and 37 cm front-to-rear distance. It outputs refined 2D joint heatmaps (15 joints at 64×64 resolution) and 3D pose (16 SMPL joints), with the refined heatmaps and features serving as drop-in replacements for existing 2D-to-3D lifting modules such as EgoPoseFormer or EgoTAP.

## Method

The core contribution is a transformer-decoder refinement module that generates per-view offset features to correct initial 2D joint heatmaps using multi-view context. View-specific joint queries interact with heatmap features from all four views via deformable attention centered on 2D joint anchors (extracted as argmax of initial heatmaps), rather than attending over full spatial dimensions. Joint queries are further enriched with MLP-projected embeddings of the initial heatmaps and RGB backbone features to propagate the estimation state, and an uncertainty-aware binary mask (threshold 0.5 on heatmap peak values) zeros out queries for low-confidence views so that self-attention prioritizes reliable features. The resulting offset features are added element-wise to initial heatmap features and passed through a regression layer to produce refined heatmaps. The module is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on heatmaps and is architecture-agnostic, plugging into any existing 2D-to-3D lifting pipeline.

## Results

On Ego4View-RW with 2 front + 2 rear views, the proposed method (EgoPoseFormer + Ours) achieves 63.38 mm MPJPE and 56.94 mm PA-MPJPE, a 18.7% improvement over EgoPoseFormer with only 2 front views (77.95 mm MPJPE). On Ego4View-Syn, the same configuration yields 20.20 mm MPJPE and 19.25 mm PA-MPJPE, a 26.2% reduction from the 2-front-view baseline (27.36 mm). Per-joint analysis shows the largest gains on end-effectors: hands improve by 20.2% (60.35→48.17 mm) and feet by 10.3% (115.40→103.46 mm) on Ego4View-RW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two multi-view egocentric pose estimation baselines: EgoPoseFormer (ECCV 2024, current SOTA) and EgoTAP (CVPR 2024, second-best). With 2 front + 2 rear views on Ego4View-RW, the proposed method (EPFormer+Ours) achieves 63.38 mm MPJPE, outperforming EgoPoseFormer with only 2 front views (77.95 mm) by 18.7% and EgoTAP with 2 front views (91.23 mm) by 30.5%. On Ego4View-Syn, the same configuration yields 20.20 mm MPJPE vs. 27.36 mm (EPFormer, 2 front) and 32.56 mm (EgoTAP, 2 front). Importantly, the paper demonstrates that naively adding rear views to EgoPoseFormer without the proposed refinement module can actually degrade accuracy in certain scenarios (e.g., 5:58 in the supplementary video), whereas the proposed multi-view heatmap refinement consistently improves results. The per-joint evaluation further shows that the refinement is most impactful on end-effectors (hands: 20.2% improvement, feet: 10.3% on Ego4View-RW), where occlusion is most severe in rear views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the egocentric 3D pose estimation line of work (EgoPoseFormer, EgoTAP, UnrealEgo) by introducing rear-facing cameras on HMDs, a configuration entirely absent from prior methods that rely exclusively on frontal or body-facing cameras. The proposed transformer-based refinement module builds on deformable attention (from object detection) to perform multi-view 2D heatmap correction before 3D lifting, departing from prior approaches that treat each view's 2D joint detection independently. The method is designed to be plug-and-play with existing 2D-to-3D lifting backbones, positioning it as a complementary refinement layer rather than a full pipeline replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of rear cameras for egocentric full-body tracking, and the two new large-scale datasets (Ego4View-Syn with realistic cloth deformation via BEDLAM, and Ego4View-RW with 25 identities and loose clothing) fill a critical gap in the egocentric pose estimation benchmark landscape. The finding that naively adding rear views can degrade accuracy challenges the common assumption that more views monotonically improve estimation, highlighting the need for principled multi-view integration. Open questions include whether physics-based motion priors can resolve remaining failures under extreme self-occlusion, and whether the HMD design insights generalize to other body-worn sensing tasks like avatar reconstruction or human-object interaction.
