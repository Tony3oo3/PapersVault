## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting head avatar methods entangle geometry and appearance in per-Gaussian color attributes, making real-time texture editing infeasible. While mesh-based approaches offer explicit UV textures, they cannot capture fine-grained non-Lambertian details like hair and lips. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a hybrid representation that explicitly disentangles global appearance into editable texture images while retaining the photorealistic rendering quality of 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250462/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view head videos (16 calibrated viewpoints, downsampled to 802×550) along with tracked FLAME shape, expression, and pose parameters as input. It produces a rigged 3D Gaussian head avatar comprising surface and volumetric Gaussians, an explicit diffuse texture image, and an expression-conditioned dynamic texture image. The avatar supports photorealistic novel-view and novel-expression rendering at ~75 fps, FLAME-driven animation, and real-time appearance editing by directly painting on the diffuse texture map.

## Method

SVG-Head introduces two complementary Gaussian types bound to a FLAME mesh: surface Gaussians (surf-GS) constrained to the mesh surface via learnable barycentric coordinates with rotations fixed to face normals, sampling colors from two learnable texture images (diffuse + expression-dependent dynamic) through a mesh-aware UV mapping that projects ray-Gaussian intersections onto triangles and condenses the lookup into a single precomputed affine transform; and volumetric Gaussians (vol-GS) free to move around the mesh with full per-point SH colors to model non-Lambertian regions like hair and lips. A differential hybrid rendering pipeline unifies both types under a single alpha-blending pass, where surf-GS colors combine texture sampling with high-degree SH residuals and vol-GS use standard SH evaluation. A two-stage hierarchical optimization first trains surf-GS alone with an additional diffuse-only photometric loss to produce sharp, editable textures, then introduces vol-GS with selective parameter updates (only surf-GS opacity and dynamic texture remain trainable) plus position and alpha regularization losses to prevent drift and preserve editing fidelity.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset, SVG-Head achieves 30.3 PSNR / 0.931 SSIM / 0.078 LPIPS for novel-view synthesis and 26.0 PSNR / 0.910 SSIM / 0.087 LPIPS for novel-expression synthesis. Among editable methods, SVG-Head is the clear winner, outperforming MeGA by 15.1 PSNR on novel-view (30.3 vs. 15.2). Against the strongest non-editable baseline GaussianAvatars, SVG-Head is within 1.3 PSNR on novel-view (30.3 vs. 31.6) and ties exactly on novel-expression PSNR (26.0), while uniquely supporting real-time texture editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVG-Head dominates the only other editable baseline, MeGA, by a wide margin (30.3 vs. 15.2 PSNR on novel-view; 26.0 vs. 17.4 PSNR on novel-expression), which the authors attribute to MeGA's inability to model shoulder regions. Against the strongest non-editable method, GaussianAvatars, SVG-Head trails by 1.3 PSNR on novel-view (30.3 vs. 31.6) but matches it exactly on novel-expression PSNR (26.0) and SSIM (0.910), while adding real-time texture editing capability. SVG-Head substantially outperforms PointAvatar (25.8 PSNR) and Gaussian Head Avatar (29.5 PSNR) on novel-view synthesis. In qualitative editing comparisons (Fig. 4), SVG-Head supports fine-grained, real-time texture editing by directly painting on the diffuse texture map, whereas MeGA requires minutes-to-hours of optimization-based editing and GaussianAvatar-Editor relies on text prompts and also needs several minutes. In the mesh-vs-surface-Gaussian ablation (Table 3), surf-GS with diffuse texture achieves 31.3 PSNR versus 29.8 PSNR for the equivalent mesh-based VHAP_rgb rendering, and adding SH coefficients to surf-GS further boosts it to 33.3 PSNR at comparable memory (~2100 MB vs. ~14200 MB for VHAP_SH).

## Relation to Prior Work

SVG-Head extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for head avatars (GaussianAvatars) by introducing a hybrid surface-volumetric decomposition: surface Gaussians bound to a FLAME mesh sample colors from explicit learnable texture images via a mesh-aware UV mapping (inspired by Texture-GS's static-scene texture disentanglement), while volumetric Gaussians handle non-Lambertian regions like hair. This departs from prior Gaussian head avatar methods (GaussianAvatars, MeGA, Gaussian Head Avatar) that store colors per-Gaussian and thus lack a global, editable appearance representation. The mesh-aware UV mapping specifically addresses the inconsistent-UV-coordinate problem that would arise when a ray intersects multiple unconstrained Gaussians, a challenge absent in Texture-GS's static setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to extract explicit, editable texture images from a 3DGS-based head avatar, enabling real-time appearance editing (drawing on a texture map) versus the minutes-to-hours optimization-based editing of MeGA or the text-prompt-only editing of GaussianAvatar-Editor. The practical impact for AR/VR, gaming, and film production is significant, as artists can now interactively modify head appearance without re-optimization. Key limitations remain: non-Lambertian regions (hair) still resist texture-based editing, and reconstruction quality lags behind methods dedicated solely to rendering fidelity. Open questions include whether novel UV mapping functions or learned texture spaces could close the gap with reconstruction-only methods while preserving editability.
