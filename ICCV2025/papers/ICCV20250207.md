## Problem

In federated learning, feature drift—where the same class exhibits different feature distributions across clients due to varying data collection methods, devices, or environments—remains largely unexplored compared to label heterogeneity. Existing approaches either align feature spaces at the cost of losing class-discriminative information or rely on sharing raw features/partial data, introducing privacy risks. FedPall addresses this gap by simultaneously unifying feature spaces and reinforcing class boundaries without exposing raw data.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images through a shared ResNet-50 backbone (2048-dim embeddings) and produces class labels. Rather than uploading full model parameters as in FedAvg, clients transmit Bernoulli-masked prototype-mixed features (a weighted fusion of local features and server-provided global prototypes) to the server, which trains a global classifier and an amplifier. The final output is a class label produced by a globally trained classifier that is deployed back to each client and fine-tuned on local data.

## Method

FedPall integrates two complementary mechanisms: (1) adversarial learning between client and server, where a server-side amplifier (3-layer MLP) amplifies client-specific heterogeneity in features while the client minimizes KL divergence between amplified features and a uniform distribution, forcing the feature encoder to discard client-specific information; and (2) collaborative learning via global prototypes, where the server aggregates weighted local prototypes and sends them to clients, which then apply an InfoNCE contrastive loss to pull local features toward their corresponding global prototype and push them away from others. The total local loss is L_CE + μ·L_KL + δ·L_InfoNCE. For server-side training, clients upload Bernoulli-masked mixed features (α·z + (1−α)·G^k) to train a global classifier on class labels and the amplifier on client IDs, after which the global classifier is decentralized to each client for local fine-tuning.

## Results

FedPall achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy on all three feature-drifted benchmarks: 67.5% average on Office-10, 88.7% on Digits, and 60.6% on PACS. On Office-10, FedPall outperforms the second-best method ADCOL by 3.0 percentage points and FedBN by 31.5 points. On Digits and PACS, FedPall leads FedBN (the closest competitor) by 1.1 and 1.1 percentage points respectively, while also surpassing FedProto and FedHEAL by 2.8 and 7.5 points on Digits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPall outperforms all 10 baselines on the average accuracy of all three datasets. Against the closest competitor ADCOL (the prior adversarial-learning approach for feature drift), FedPall gains +3.0 pts on Office-10 (67.5 vs 64.5), +2.1 pts on Digits (88.7 vs 86.6), and +2.0 pts on PACS (60.6 vs 58.6). FedBN, which compresses feature space via batch normalization, is competitive on Digits (87.6) and PACS (59.5) but collapses to 36.0% on Office-10, where FedPall leads by 31.5 points—highlighting FedBN's fragility under severe real-world feature drift. FedProto (prototype contrastive learning) trails FedPall by 6.1, 2.8, and 2.2 points on Office-10, Digits, and PACS respectively, confirming that prototype-based collaboration alone is insufficient without adversarial alignment. FedHEAL (fair aggregation) is the weakest among the domain-specific baselines, lagging FedPall by 7.9, 7.5, and 6.4 points on the three datasets. Notably, FedPall also surpasses the single-client baseline (SingleSet) on all three averages, demonstrating that the federated adversarial-collaborative framework adds value even when each client could train independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPall extends the adversarial feature-alignment mechanism of ADCOL (Li et al., ICML 2023), which uses a server-side amplifier and KL divergence to unify heterogeneous client feature spaces, by adding a collaborative learning component that reinforces class information via prototype-based contrastive loss. It also builds on the prototype-driven FL paradigm of FedProto (Tan et al., AAAI 2022), repurposing global prototypes not only for local contrastive training but also as a mixing ingredient for a privacy-preserving global classifier. The key departure from both lines is the hierarchical integration: adversarial learning handles inter-client feature alignment while collaborative learning handles intra-client class separation, and the prototype-mixed features bridge the two to train a global-view classifier—something neither ADCOL nor FedProto achieves in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Feature drift is a practically important and still under-addressed form of data heterogeneity in FL, and FedPall provides a clean, modular framework that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes beyond adding an MLP amplifier. The ablation finding that InfoNCE alone can *amplify* drift in the absence of KL alignment is a useful caution for anyone combining contrastive and alignment losses. However, the framework is currently limited to image classification with ResNet-50 backbones, and the authors acknowledge that extension to NLP or time-series would require substantial redesign of both the prototype definitions and the loss functions, leaving cross-domain generalization as an open question.
