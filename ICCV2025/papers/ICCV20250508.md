## Problem

Existing bit-level sparsity quantization methods (BSQ, CSQ) achieve mixed-precision quantization by instantiating a separate trainable variable for every bit in each weight, inflating trainable parameters by up to 8× and dramatically increasing GPU memory and training time. This makes bit-level quantization impractical for larger models such as Vision Transformers. The paper asks whether bit-level sparsity and precision reduction can be achieved without explicit bit-level parameter splitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet) and produces class labels, applied to both CNNs (ResNet-20/18/50, MobileNetV3-Large) and Vision Transformers (DeiT-T/S, Swin-T). During training, the method consumes the floating-point weight tensor of each layer to compute its least-significant bits (LSBs) on the fly, and the final output is a mixed-precision quantized model where each layer retains a different integer bit-width determined by the pruning schedule. Activations are kept at full precision for ImageNet CNN experiments or quantized to 8-bit for ViT experiments.

## Method

MSQ introduces a RoundClamp quantizer that replaces DoReFa's (2ⁿ−1) scaling with 2ⁿ scaling plus clamping, aligning quantization bin boundaries across precisions so that LSBs can be computed as B_k = W_n − 2^k·W_{n−k} directly from the original weight without any bit-level parameter splitting. An ℓ₁ regularization on the computed LSBs drives them toward zero during QAT, inducing bit-level sparsity and enabling precision reduction. To control the per-layer pruning speed, MSQ incorporates Hessian-aware aggressive pruning: the Hessian trace of each layer (following HAWQ-V2) determines whether that layer prunes 1 bit (sensitive) or 2 bits (less sensitive) at each pruning interval, accelerating convergence on insensitive layers while protecting sensitive ones. This eliminates the multiplied bit-level parameters entirely, reducing trainable parameters by up to 8× and training time by up to 86% compared to BSQ/CSQ while maintaining competitive accuracy-compression tradeoffs.

## Results

On ImageNet, MSQ achieves 69.74% top-1 accuracy at 11.84× compression on ResNet-18 and 75.32% at 10.89× on ResNet-50, matching or exceeding prior mixed-precision methods (CSQ, BSQ, HAQ) while using up to 8× fewer trainable parameters. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, MSQ reaches 92.15% accuracy at 20.13× compression (2-bit activations), surpassing BSQ (91.87% at 19.24×) and CSQ (90.33% at 16.41×). For Vision Transformers on ImageNet, MSQ achieves 74.74% on DeiT-T, 80.64% on DeiT-S, and 81.38% on Swin-T at ~10× compression, outperforming all prior ViT quantization baselines. Training efficiency gains are substantial: MSQ reduces training time by up to 86% (5.3× speedup over BSQ, 14.2× over CSQ on ResNet-50) and enables 4× larger batch sizes before OOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSQ outperforms all uniform-precision baselines (DoReFa, PACT, LQ-Nets) by a wide margin on ImageNet: on ResNet-50, MSQ reaches 75.32% at 10.89× vs. DoReFa's 69.90% at 10.67× and PACT's 75.30% at 10.67×. Against the closest mixed-precision competitor CSQ, MSQ ties on ResNet-18 (69.74% vs. 69.73%) and is 0.15% below on ResNet-50 (75.32% vs. 75.47%), but achieves higher compression (10.89× vs. 10.67×) with 8× fewer trainable parameters and 14.2× less training time. MSQ beats BSQ on both accuracy and compression for ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 (92.15% at 20.13× vs. 91.87% at 19.24×) and on ResNet-50/ImageNet (75.32% at 10.89× vs. 75.16% at 13.90×). For Vision Transformers, MSQ surpasses all four ViT-specific baselines (LSQ, Mix-QViT, Q-ViT, OFQ-3bit) on DeiT-T, DeiT-S, and Swin-T, e.g., 74.74% vs. OFQ's 72.72% on DeiT-T at comparable ~10× compression. The main trade-off is that MSQ is slightly below CSQ on ResNet-50 accuracy (−0.15%) and below OFQ-4bit on DeiT-T (74.74% vs. 75.46%), but MSQ achieves these results with far lower training cost and without explicit bit-level parameter splitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSQ directly extends the bit-level sparsity quantization line initiated by BSQ [27] and refined by CSQ [26], which treat each bit of a quantized weight as an independent trainable variable to discover mixed-precision schemes during training. The key departure is eliminating explicit bit-level parameter splitting: instead of instantiating separate variables per bit, MSQ computes the least significant bits (LSBs) directly from the original weight via a novel RoundClamp quantizer (a modified DoReFa [31] with a 2^n scaling factor) and applies L1 regularization to those computed LSBs. The Hessian-aware aggressive pruning strategy borrows the Hessian-trace sensitivity metric from HAWQ-V2 [6] to adaptively control per-layer bit reduction speed, and the continuous sparsification mechanism [21] underpins the L1 sparsity-inducing regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× reduction in trainable parameters and up to 86% training-time reduction make bit-level mixed-precision quantization practical on resource-constrained hardware, and the extension to Vision Transformers (DeiT, Swin) addresses a gap where prior bit-level methods were infeasible due to memory overhead. A notable limitation is that activation quantization remains at 8-bit (ViTs) or full precision (ImageNet CNNs), so the full end-to-end low-precision deployment story is incomplete. Open questions include whether the RoundClamp quantizer and Hessian-guided pruning generalize to even larger models (e.g., LLMs or billion-parameter ViTs) and whether the heuristic thresholding for k=1 vs. k=2 pruning can be made fully differentiable or learned.
