## Problem

Full-parameter fine-tuning of large-scale foundation models (e.g., BEiT-L with 307M parameters) for binary object segmentation incurs prohibitive computational cost, while existing prompt-based fine-tuning methods embed trainable prompts that lack inherent semantic priors, resulting in significantly lower segmentation accuracy. The paper addresses how to efficiently adapt frozen large-scale models to challenging segmentation tasks with minimal trainable parameters while preserving the powerful universal representations of the pre-trained backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252340/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 512×512 as input and produces binary segmentation masks via a lightweight Mask2Former-style decoder (1.08M parameters). The frozen foundation model (BEiT-L or UniPerceiver-L) encodes task-universal features at H/16×W/16 resolution, while the trainable DMLP extractor generates task-specific features at four spatial scales (H/4×W/4 through H/32×W/32). Only 23.4M parameters are updated during training, compared to 326–328M for full-parameter fine-tuning of the same backbone.

## Method

Controllable-LPMoE introduces a dynamic priors-based fine-tuning paradigm with two core components. First, a Dynamic Mixed Local Priors (DMLP) extractor uses four heterogeneous convolutions (depthwise separable, atrous, asymmetric, wavelet) with different receptive fields to generate diverse local priors, which are then dynamically weighted and fused via a Mixture-of-Experts gating network to produce task-specific features enriched with semantic knowledge. Second, a Bi-directional Interaction (BDI) adapter performs iterative information exchange between frozen and trainable features across four encoding blocks using cosine-aligned deformable attention (CDA) for semantic-aligned cross-attention and channel-oriented adaptive scale enhancement (CASE) for multi-scale channel refinement. The CDA incorporates cosine similarity into attention weights and uses deformable sampling offsets, while CASE decomposes features and adaptively fuses channel and reverse attention outputs through a second MoE gating mechanism. This bidirectional design ensures frozen features gain task-specific attributes while trainable features inherit universal representations, achieving SOTA performance with only ~1/14 the trainable parameters of full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the most challenging COD task, Controllable-LPMoE (BEiT-L backbone) achieves IoU of 0.863 on CHAMELEON, 0.834 on CAMO, 0.817 on COD10K, and 0.842 on NC4K, outperforming the best full-parameter fine-tuning baseline ZoomXNet by 3.4–7.8% IoU. The method uses only 23.4M trainable parameters (vs. 326.7M for full fine-tuning of UniPerceiver-L), reducing training memory by 35–56% while maintaining competitive accuracy. Across all six binary segmentation tasks (COD, SOD, PS, SLS, SD, GD) on 18 benchmark datasets, the method consistently achieves the best IoU, Dice, Fm^w, and M scores among 31 compared SOTA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Controllable-LPMoE outperforms all 31 baselines across every task. On COD (the hardest task), it surpasses the strongest full-parameter fine-tuning method ZoomXNet [52] by 3.4–7.8% IoU (e.g., 0.817 vs. 0.758 on COD10K) and FSEL [58] by 4.6–11.2% IoU. Among prompt-based methods, it beats VSCode [46] by 7.7% IoU on COD10K (0.817 vs. 0.711) and EVP [44] by 15.4% IoU, demonstrating that dynamic local priors with MoE gating substantially outperform static prompt embeddings. On SOD, it exceeds VSCode [46] by 2.3–2.7% IoU (e.g., 0.870 vs. 0.847 on DUTS-TE) and VST++ [43] by 5.7–6.0% IoU. On polyp segmentation, it leads CFANet [85] by 1.7–2.1% IoU and LSSNet [68] by 2.4–3.4% IoU. On shadow detection, it surpasses FSEL [58] by 2.2–3.3% IoU (e.g., 0.941 vs. 0.908 on ISTD), and on glass detection it beats FSEL [58] by 3.4–3.8% IoU (e.g., 0.931 vs. 0.892 on Trans10k). Notably, the SAM [36] foundation model performs poorly on COD (IoU 0.522–0.616), highlighting the difficulty of these tasks and the value of task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a third fine-tuning paradigm between full-parameter fine-tuning (dominant in binary segmentation) and prompt-based fine-tuning (VPT, EVP, VSCode), which the authors argue fails because directly-generated prompts lack semantic priors. Architecturally, it builds on Deformable DeTR's reference-point and sampling-offset mechanism for its cosine-aligned deformable attention, employs a Mask2Former-style decoder, and repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts gating strategy to dynamically weight heterogeneous convolutional local priors (depthwise-separable, atrous, asymmetric, wavelet). The key departure from prior prompt-based work is that the 'priors' are not static learnable embeddings but are extracted per-image through a lightweight CNN branch and dynamically reweighted, giving them input-dependent semantic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing trainable parameters from ~300M (full fine-tuning of BEiT-L/UniPerceiver-L) to 23.4M while matching or exceeding the accuracy of full fine-tuning methods on 18 benchmark datasets across six segmentation tasks, with 35–55% memory savings. This makes large-scale foundation models viable for resource-constrained segmentation pipelines. Open questions include whether the specific choice of four heterogeneous convolutions is optimal, whether the approach extends to multi-class or instance segmentation, and whether the gains persist when the frozen backbone is scaled further (e.g., BEiT-H). The paper does not explore failure modes or cases where the dynamic gating collapses to uniform weights.
