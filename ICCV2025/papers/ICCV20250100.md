## Problem

The MSDeformAttn-based pixel decoder in Mask2Former and Mask DINO accounts for over 50% of computational cost in efficient segmentation networks, yet its low compute-to-memory-access ratio and suboptimal hardware implementation make it a significant runtime bottleneck. Beyond speed, the paper identifies that MSDeformAttn fails to exploit locality bias: lower-scale pixels (which constitute over 75% of total pixels) allocate substantial attention weight to distant global information rather than focusing on local features, making the information distribution inefficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250100/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pixel decoder receives multi-scale feature maps (4 or 5 scales) from a backbone (ResNet-50 or SwinL) and outputs processed multi-scale feature maps with completed cross-scale information interaction, which are then consumed by the transformer decoder to produce per-pixel embeddings. For COCO experiments, input images are resized to an average of 800×1120 pixels. The full model outputs panoptic segmentation masks (with thing/stuff labels), instance masks, semantic class maps, and bounding boxes depending on the task configuration.

## Method

HyPiDecoder replaces a subset of MSDeformAttn blocks in the pixel decoder with convolution-based FPN blocks called CSPRepLayer (CSP connections with RepVGG-style 3×3 convolutions) or CSPSepLayer (CSP with separable convolutions for parameter efficiency), introducing explicit locality awareness into the multi-scale feature fusion. The architecture is configurable via a notation like 3d3r (3 MSDeformAttn blocks followed by 3 CSPRep blocks), allowing flexible mixing of attention and convolution layers. The paper also proposes MSLinearAttn, a multi-scale linear attention module with O(ND²) complexity that flattens and concatenates multi-scale features, applies linear attention with 2D sinusoidal and scale-dependent positional embeddings, and splits the output back into per-scale feature maps. Ablations show that the 3l3r configuration (3 MSLinearAttn + 3 CSPRep) achieves both higher speed and accuracy without any MSDeformAttn, and that placing attention blocks before convolution blocks yields the best performance.

## Results

On COCO panoptic segmentation (val2017), Mask DINO with HyPiDecoder (3d3r) and SwinL backbone achieves 58.8 PQ, a new SOTA for SwinL-scale models without extra data, with a 127% inference speedup (9.8 FPS vs. 4.3 FPS) over the original Mask DINO. On the ResNet-50 backbone, the same configuration yields 53.7 PQ (+0.7 over default) with a 27% speedup. For instance segmentation, HyPiDecoder matches the original Mask DINO at 52.3 Mask AP while delivering a 66% FPS improvement. On semantic segmentation, HyPiDecoder improves mIoU by +0.4 on ADE20K (47.6 single-scale) and +0.7 on Cityscapes (81.1 single-scale) over the Mask2Former baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary segmentation baseline Mask2Former [8], HyPiDecoder consistently improves PQ by +0.5 (58.1 vs. 57.6 on SwinL; 47.5 vs. 47.0 on R50) while increasing FPS by 23–32%, and also improves mIoU on ADE20K (+0.4) and Cityscapes (+0.7). Against the unified detection-segmentation baseline Mask DINO [23], HyPiDecoder achieves +0.5 PQ on SwinL (58.8 vs. 58.3) with a dramatic 127% speedup, and +0.7 PQ on R50 (53.7 vs. 53.0) with a 27% speedup; on instance segmentation it matches the 52.3 Mask AP of the original Mask DINO while delivering a 66% FPS gain. Compared to OneFormer [19] (57.9 PQ, SwinL, 100 epochs), HyPiDecoder-equipped Mask DINO achieves 58.8 PQ in only 50 epochs. On the R50 backbone, HyPiDecoder-equipped Mask DINO (53.7 PQ) surpasses MaskConver [31] (53.6 PQ, 300 epochs), kMaX-DeepLab [37] (53.0 PQ, 81 epochs), Panoptic SegFormer [24] (49.6 PQ), and DETR [4] (43.4 PQ) by a wide margin. Notably, the 3l3r configuration (using MSLinearAttn instead of MSDeformAttn) also outperforms the default pixel decoder, demonstrating that the speed-accuracy gains are not solely attributable to removing deformable attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-established design paradigms: attention-based pixel decoders (MSDeformAttn from Deformable DETR, as used in Mask2Former and Mask DINO) and convolution-based feature pyramid networks (FPN, PAFPN, BiFPN). The core architectural move is to replace some MSDeformAttn blocks in the pixel decoder with convolution-based CSP/RepVGG FPN blocks, borrowing the top-down/bottom-up pathway structure from PAFPN and the reparameterization trick from RepVGG. The paper also introduces a multi-scale linear attention module (MSLinearAttn) built on the Katharopoulos et al. formulation, demonstrating that the hybrid decoder can function without any MSDeformAttn at all. Conceptually, the paper argues that MSDeformAttn's learned attention weights fail to exploit the locality bias expected in visual tasks, and that explicit convolutional locality is both more efficient and more effective for multi-scale feature fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying Mask2Former or Mask DINO: a 127% inference speedup on SwinL-scale models while simultaneously improving panoptic PQ by 0.5 points makes this a drop-in replacement with clear deployment benefits. The empirical observation that MSDeformAttn's attention weights allocate substantial mass to non-local scales (especially at 1/4 resolution) provides a concrete justification for the architectural change beyond mere speed. However, the paper's scope is limited: it does not extend to pure detection models (DINO), universal segmentation models (OneFormer), or larger backbones/datasets/epochs, and the FLOPS actually increase (the speedup comes from hardware efficiency of convolutions, not reduced computation). Open questions include whether the hybrid design generalizes to other attention-based encoders and whether the locality argument holds for higher-resolution or video segmentation settings.
