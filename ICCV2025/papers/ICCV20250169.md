## Problem

Intention-driven visual perception requires models to dynamically select relevant image regions based on a query, but existing approaches like Visual CoT rely on supervised training with dense bounding box annotations for every query-image pair. Since the same image can correspond to vastly different focus regions depending on the request, annotation complexity grows combinatorially, making scalable coverage infeasible. The paper asks whether the visual reasoning process can be optimized using only task-level reward signals, eliminating the need for region annotations entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural language query as input and produces a two-step structured output: first a bounding box in coordinate format identifying the region of interest, then a text response generated by conditioning on both the original image and the cropped sub-image. The bounding box is not a final deliverable but an intermediate reasoning step that guides the model's attention before the final answer is produced, mirroring a human's process of zooming into a relevant area before responding.

## Method

VisRL applies step-level DPO to the Visual CoT two-stage pipeline (predict bounding box → answer from crop) in an iterative self-evolution loop. During data generation, the current model samples multiple bounding boxes and responses per query, a diversity controller (IoU-based rejection) ensures spatial coverage, and the pre-SFT model acts as a critic to score both boxes and responses, constructing win-lose preference pairs without any external annotations. Optimization proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 optimizes bounding box selection alone via DPO, then Stage 2 jointly optimizes the box and the final response using a weighted combination of both preference losses. The entire data-generation-and-optimization cycle repeats iteratively, with each improved model producing higher-quality preference data for the next round, progressively increasing the proportion of valid positive pairs from 64.6% to 70.1% over three iterations.

## Results

On the LLaVA-1.5-7B backbone, VisRL achieves 1526.3 on MME, 70.1 on MMBench, and 87.5 on POPE, outperforming the supervised Visual CoT baseline (1453.6 / 67.9 / 86.0) across all three benchmarks. After one RL iteration (VisRL-Iter1), scores further improve to 1560.0 / 71.7 / 88.8. On the Visual CoT benchmark (11-dataset average), VisRL yields improvements of 23.8%–49.1% over the base model across seven different LMMs (e.g., PaliGemma2-10B: 0.377→0.562; Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 0.640→0.796). For bounding-box detection accuracy (IoU@0.5), VisRL reaches 0.437 average, surpassing Visual CoT's 0.378 despite using only 30k annotated samples versus 438k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary supervised baseline VisCoT [48], VisRL wins on all three benchmarks with the LLaVA-1.5 backbone (MME +5.0%, MMBench +2.2 pts, POPE +1.5 pts) while using only 30k annotated samples versus VisCoT's 376k. Compared to the base LLaVA-1.5 model [33], VisRL improves MMBench by 5.8 points (70.1 vs 64.3) and POPE by 1.7 points. Against tool-usage methods, VisRL outperforms SEAL [61] on MME and MMBench but trails on POPE (78.2 vs 82.4), and it surpasses Insight-V-LLaVA [15] on MME (1619.2 vs 1583.9) though Insight-V leads on MMBench (81.7 vs 78.8). In the direct-response ablation (Table 5), VisRL-Full (0.796 avg) substantially outperforms DPO (0.656), KTO (0.658), and PPO (0.661), confirming that the two-stage CoT-structured RL is critical. On the Visual CoT benchmark, VisRL generalizes across seven LMMs (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Llama-3.2-V, MiniCPM-o, PaliGemma2, Yi-VL, Qwen2.5-VL), with the largest relative gain on PaliGemma2-10B (+49.1%) and the smallest on Llama-3.2-V-11B (+23.8%), demonstrating broad applicability beyond the LLaVA family.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisRL sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the Visual CoT paradigm (Shao et al., 2024), which introduces a two-step reasoning pipeline of predicting a focus bounding box and then answering from the cropped region; (2) step-level DPO (Lai et al., 2024), which decomposes preference optimization into per-step objectives; and (3) DPO itself (Rafailov et al., 2024) as the underlying pairwise preference optimizer. The key departure from prior intention-driven perception methods (VisCoT, SpatialCoT, V*/SEAL, SegLLM) is the replacement of supervised fine-tuning on densely annotated bounding boxes with a self-evolution RL loop that generates its own preference data and rewards, thereby removing the combinatorial annotation bottleneck that has limited scalability of all prior approaches in this space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 30k annotated samples plus 180k unlabeled QA pairs with iterative RL can match or exceed 438k densely-annotated SFT training (VisCoT), VisRL offers a practical path to scaling intention-driven visual perception without the infeasible annotation cost of intention-region pairs. The consistent gains across seven heterogeneous LMMs (from 6B to 11B, spanning LLaVA, Qwen, Llama, PaliGemma, MiniCPM, Yi) suggest the technique is architecture-agnostic and likely to be adopted as a post-training recipe. Open questions include whether the self-critic (pre-SFT model) remains a reliable reward signal as the policy improves, whether online RL or larger iteration counts yield further gains, and whether the two-step CoT structure generalizes to more complex multi-step visual reasoning chains.
