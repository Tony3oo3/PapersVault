## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for mitigating hallucinations in LVLMs (e.g., VCD, M3ID) require two or more forward passes per token—processing both original and perturbed inputs—resulting in 2× or greater inference latency and higher GPU memory. This efficiency cost makes them impractical for real-time deployment, yet no prior approach achieves competitive hallucination reduction with a single query and minimal overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in decoding intervention on standard LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL), taking the same RGB image and text query as the base model and producing a textual response in the usual autoregressive format. No additional training data, fine-tuning, or input perturbation is required; the only change is a single-layer attention modification during the forward pass, adding approximately 0.07× inference time and negligible GPU memory over the base model.

## Method

ONLY introduces a training-free, single-layer intervention that selectively amplifies textual information during decoding. At a chosen Transformer layer, it computes a Text-to-Visual Entropy Ratio (TVER) for each attention head by comparing the entropy of attention weights over text tokens versus visual tokens, then zeros out heads below the layer-average TVER to produce a Textual-Enhanced MHA (TE-MHA) output. This TE-MHA output is injected via a residual connection into the final layer, yielding textual-enhanced logits with only one extra attention computation. Finally, an adaptive decoding step measures the Manhattan distance between original and TE logits: if the distance is below a threshold γ, the two are combined collaboratively (additive); otherwise, they are contrasted (subtractive), allowing the method to either reinforce or suppress language bias depending on the degree of divergence.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (MS-COCO, Random, LLaVA-1.5), ONLY achieves 89.10% F1, surpassing VCD (87.15%) and M3ID (87.52%) by 1.95 and 1.58 points respectively. On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5, 128 tokens), ONLY reduces CHAIR_S to 49.8 and CHAIR_I to 14.3, outperforming VCD (54.4/16.6) and M3ID (56.6/15.7). On MME-Hallucination, ONLY scores 635.55, exceeding the second-best M3ID (598.11) by 37.44 points. Critically, ONLY achieves these gains with only a 1.07× inference time overhead and negligible GPU memory increase (14951 MB vs. 14945 MB baseline), compared to 2.01–7.12× time and 1.04–1.54× memory for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONLY outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark evaluated. Against VCD (the most direct contrastive-decoding competitor), ONLY gains ~2% F1 on POPE, ~5 points on CHAIR_S, and ~55 points on MME, while running 1.9× faster (1.07× vs. 2.01× latency). Against M3ID, the margin is similar: +1.6% POPE F1, +6.8 CHAIR_S reduction, and +37.4 MME points, again at roughly half the inference cost. Against the heavier methods OPERA (7.12× latency) and HALC (6.52×), ONLY matches or exceeds their POPE and MME scores while being 6–7× faster and using ~50% less GPU memory. Woodpecker, which uses a separate correction model, is slower (3.08×) and actually worsens CHAIR_S (57.6 vs. 55.0 baseline), whereas ONLY improves it to 49.8. DoLa, a layer-contrast method from the LLM literature, degrades MME performance (522.22 vs. 562.50 baseline) on LLaVA-1.5, highlighting that naive layer contrast is not directly transferable to LVLMs. On MMBench and MM-Vet (general multi-modal understanding), ONLY also leads all baselines (65.0 and 32.8 respectively), indicating that hallucination mitigation does not come at the cost of general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding lineage for LVLM hallucination mitigation (VCD, M3ID, HALC, OPERA) by shifting the intervention target from the visual modality to the textual modality. Rather than requiring a second forward pass on a distorted image to extract language bias, ONLY identifies attention heads with high text-to-visual entropy ratios within a single forward pass and selectively amplifies their outputs through a one-layer residual intervention. This departs from prior contrastive methods that treat hallucination as primarily a visual-grounding failure, reframing it as a problem of excessive language prior dominance that can be corrected by modulating which attention heads contribute to the next-token prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.07× inference overhead (versus 2×–7× for competing methods) with negligible memory cost makes hallucination mitigation practically deployable in real-time settings, which prior contrastive decoding approaches could not support. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means the method can be applied to any LVLM without retraining, lowering the barrier for practitioners. Open questions include whether the text-to-visual entropy ratio principle generalizes to next-generation LVLMs with different attention architectures (e.g., mixture-of-experts, sliding-window attention), and whether the single-layer intervention remains sufficient as models scale or as hallucination manifests in more complex reasoning chains rather than simple object presence.
