## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods learn temporal deformation of Gaussians via a single deformation field, but this is inadequate for newly introduced dynamic objects in autonomous driving scenes that lack prior-frame information. The limited and trajectory-constrained data typical of driving captures further compounds the difficulty of accurately predicting Gaussian deformations at novel time steps, leading to degraded rendering of moving objects like pedestrians and vehicles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251576/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images captured along simple (e.g., linear) vehicle trajectories, LiDAR point clouds from all frames for Gaussian initialization (replacing SfM), camera poses, and per-frame time stamps. Semantic supervision is self-supervised: a 2D foundation model (LSeg or Meta-SAM) provides high-dimensional feature maps that are distilled onto Gaussians via a rasterizer, requiring no manual annotations. Beyond rendered novel views, the deformed semantic features attached to each Gaussian enable prompt-based 4D semantic segmentation, bounding-box-free instance decomposition, and 4D scene reconstruction as downstream outputs.

## Method

CoDa-4DGS extends vanilla 4DGS (HexPlane spatiotemporal encoding + MLP deformation decoder) by introducing a Deformation Compensation Network (DCN) that refines the predicted Gaussian deformation at each time step. The DCN aggregates three feature streams: a sinusoidal time embedding, the temporal deformation awareness (the raw HexPlane-predicted deformation ΔG fed back as input), and context awareness (128-dim semantic features distilled from a 2D foundation model that co-deform with the Gaussians). Internally, the DCN uses an MLP channel (φ_p) to predict a per-Gaussian deformation offset and a sigmoid-gated linear channel (φ_s) that acts as a learned mask to suppress noise from irrelevant Gaussian motions (e.g., sky or ground). The two channels are combined via element-wise multiplication and added to the HexPlane deformation, yielding a compensated 4D Gaussian set. Training is self-supervised with a composite loss (L1, DSSIM, grid-based total variation, depth, and cosine-similarity feature loss), and the entire pipeline operates without bounding-box annotations for dynamic objects.

## Results

On the Waymo Open dataset, CoDa-4DGS achieves 33.65 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM / 0.078 LPIPS for 4D reconstruction and 28.66 PSNR / 0.900 SSIM / 0.058 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming the strongest baseline S3Gaussian (32.16 / 0.915 / 0.101 and 27.51 / 0.862 / 0.100) by 1.49 dB PSNR and 0.038 LPIPS in reconstruction, and 1.15 dB PSNR and 0.042 LPIPS in novel view synthesis. On KITTI, the gains over S3Gaussian are 1.24 dB PSNR (reconstruction) and 1.02 dB PSNR (novel view synthesis). On the NOTR Dynamic-32 benchmark, CoDa-4DGS leads on dynamic-object-specific metrics (PSNR* 29.25, SSIM* 0.866) over S3Gaussian (28.39, 0.850).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDa-4DGS outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both Waymo and KITTI. The closest competitor is S3Gaussian [13], which CoDa-4DGS surpasses by 1.49 dB PSNR on Waymo 4D reconstruction (33.65 vs. 32.16) and by 1.15 dB on novel view synthesis (28.66 vs. 27.51); the gap narrows slightly on KITTI (1.24 dB reconstruction, 1.02 dB NVS). The vanilla 4DGS [53] baseline trails by 2.63 dB PSNR on Waymo reconstruction, confirming that the Deformation Compensation Network and awareness features provide substantial gains over the HexPlane-only deformation. NeRF-based methods (StreetSurf, NSG, MARS, SUDS, EmerNeRF) are consistently 5–9 dB behind the Gaussian-splatting methods, with NSG [33] being the weakest at 24.11 PSNR on Waymo reconstruction. Static 3DGS [17] achieves 26.97 PSNR on Waymo reconstruction, well below the dynamic methods, highlighting the importance of temporal modeling. On the NOTR Dynamic-32 benchmark, CoDa-4DGS leads S3Gaussian by 0.86 dB on dynamic-object PSNR* (29.25 vs. 28.39), demonstrating particular strength in rendering moving objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDa-4DGS extends 4DGS (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), which uses HexPlane encoding to predict temporal Gaussian deformations, by adding a Deformation Compensation Network (DCN) that refines those predictions using two new signals: context awareness distilled from a 2D foundation model (LSeg/Meta-SAM, following Feature3DGS) and explicit temporal deformation awareness fed back from the HexPlane output itself. This departs from prior driving-scene methods like StreetGaussian (which requires bounding-box annotations) and S3Gaussian (which decomposes static/dynamic elements) by operating fully self-supervised and treating deformation as a two-stage process—coarse HexPlane prediction followed by fine-grained neural correction. The sigmoid-based masking within the DCN is a design choice not seen in comparable 4DGS extensions like DN-4DGS, which instead uses separate MLPs across training stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play DCN module offers a practical path to improving existing 4DGS pipelines for autonomous driving without retraining from scratch, and the bounding-box-free self-supervised approach removes a key scalability bottleneck compared to annotation-dependent methods. However, the 2.1× computational overhead and ~4.1 GB additional memory for 1M Gaussians limit real-time deployment, and the method has only been validated on three driving datasets with relatively short sequences. Open questions include whether the dual-awareness mechanism generalizes to non-driving dynamic scenes, how it behaves over longer temporal horizons where HexPlane extrapolation degrades, and whether the added parameter cost can be reduced without sacrificing the dynamic-object rendering gains.
