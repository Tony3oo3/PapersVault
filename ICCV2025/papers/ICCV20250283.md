## Problem

Existing neural field methods for polarimetric parameters (TI, DoP, AoP) treat them as independent quantities, ignoring their physical interdependencies through Stokes parameters. This leads to fitting artifacts from wide spatial-frequency ranges, scene-dependent manual band tuning in multi-band solutions, and unnecessarily large network capacity. The paper asks whether a physics-grounded representation that explicitly encodes these interdependencies can enable tuning-free, artifact-free continuous reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes four discrete polarized images captured at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° (resized to 1024×1024), from which Stokes parameters S₀, S₁, S₂ and hence discrete TI, DoP, and AoP are computed as supervision targets. The output is a continuous complex-valued neural field ψ = I·p·e^(i2θ) that can be queried at arbitrary spatial coordinates and resolutions, enabling smooth polarized image rendering at any polarizer angle via Malus' law and 2× super-resolution querying (1024→2048) without retraining.

## Method

The core representation maps the three polarimetric parameters into a single complex-valued wave ψ = S₁ + iS₂ = I·p·e^(i2θ), whose amplitude encodes the fully polarized component and whose phase encodes AoP, making it consistent with the additive superposition rules of Stokes parameters. The fitting strategy models the network as a communication system: a hierarchical transmitter encoder (wave → message + carrier → k + θ branches) separates shared structural features from parameter-specific details, while a parallel three-branch receiver decoder applies demodulation-like operations to recover I (real), k (complex, from which p = ‖k‖), and θ (using both F_k and F_θ features). The architecture uses a lightweight complex-valued network with cMLP + cSine layers for modulators and a custom c2rMLP layer (with alternating merging of real/imaginary parts) for demodulators, keeping the parameter count at 0.66M—comparable to direct-fitting baselines but far smaller than multi-band pCON (1.34M). Training is a single supervised pass with ℓ₁+ℓ₂ losses on I, k, and θ, requiring no per-scene hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On the pCON dataset (7 scenes, 1024×1024), the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM on both TI and DoP, with TI PSNR ranging from 43.56 to 53.54 dB (vs. 35.98–42.94 for pCON, the strongest baseline) and DoP PSNR from 29.23 to 41.35 dB (vs. 26.84–38.49 for pCON). On AoP, the method achieves comparable PSNR to pCON (e.g., 21.33 vs. 21.28 on Building; 28.20 vs. 28.18 on Sunroom) but with slightly lower SSIM on several scenes. The method uses only 0.66M parameters (5.05 MB), matching FINER's parameter count while outperforming pCON (1.34M params, 5.14 MB) in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: pCON (multi-band, CVPR 2023), FINER (direct fitting with variable-periodic activations, CVPR 2024), and S-INR (superpixel-informed INR, ECCV 2024). On TI, the proposed method dominates all baselines by a large margin, achieving 8–15 dB higher PSNR than pCON (e.g., 53.54 vs. 39.95 on Firewood) and 15–26 dB higher than FINER. On DoP, the method consistently outperforms pCON by 2–3 dB PSNR (e.g., 36.01 vs. 33.66 on Firewood) and FINER by 2–5 dB, while also achieving substantially higher SSIM (0.767–0.952 vs. 0.543–0.919). On AoP, the method achieves the best PSNR on 6 of 7 scenes (marginally beating pCON by 0.02–0.46 dB) but records lower SSIM than pCON on 4 scenes (e.g., 0.622 vs. 0.710 on Building), indicating comparable but not clearly superior angular reconstruction. In terms of efficiency, the method uses 0.66M parameters—half of pCON's 1.34M—while delivering higher accuracy, and its 5.05 MB model size is comparable to pCON's 5.14 MB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in polarimetric neural fields (pCON, FINER, S-INR) that treats total intensity, degree of polarization, and angle of polarization as independent scalar fields to be fitted concurrently or sequentially. Instead, it repurposes the complex-valued signal representation from communications physics—modeling the three parameters as a single modulated wave ψ = I·p·e^{i2θ}—and shows this is mathematically equivalent to the Stokes parameter formulation S₁ + iS₂. The tuning-free fitting strategy borrows the transmitter/receiver and modulation/demodulation vocabulary from communication systems to structure a hierarchical encoder-decoder, while the lightweight complex-valued network (cSine, c2rMLP) is a novel architectural contribution tailored to this wave formulation rather than a direct port of existing complex-valued NN designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables continuous, differentiable polarimetric parameter reconstruction that supports smooth polarized image rendering at arbitrary angles and flexible super-resolution querying—capabilities that discrete polarimetric imaging fundamentally cannot provide. The tuning-free design removes the per-scene band-threshold tuning that plagued pCON, making the pipeline more automatable. However, the method is restricted to linear polarization (not circular), and evaluation is limited to a single 7-scene dataset, leaving open whether the wave representation's advantages generalize to more diverse scenes or to circular polarization. The complex-valued network design and the physics-grounded representation could potentially transfer to other multi-parameter reconstruction tasks where parameters share physical interdependencies.
