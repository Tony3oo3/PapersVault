## Problem

Current 3D Large Language Models are trained exclusively on positive vision-language pairs with fixed template-based instructions, resulting in poor discriminative power (models hallucinate positive responses regardless of input) and limited generalization to diverse language styles. No prior work has systematically introduced adversarial/negative samples or linguistic diversity into 3D instruction-tuning data at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252675/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 150 object-centric 3D features per scene (extracted via Mask3D + Uni3D), corresponding position embeddings, and 2D features from DINO v2 over multi-view RGB images, all interleaved with special object ID tokens (<OBJ_i>) in the LLM input sequence. Outputs are structured as natural language responses that may include object ID tokens for grounding, free-text captions, or yes/no judgments, with the post-vision order placing all vision tokens immediately before the generated answer tokens to minimize attention deviation.

## Method

Robin3D introduces the Robust Instruction Generation (RIG) engine, which produces 1M instruction samples: 344K adversarial samples across four novel tasks (HOPE, HROC, PF-3DVG, 3DFQA) that mix negative and positive pairs to decouple memorized associations, and 508K diverse samples rephrased by GPT-4o with temperature-sampled prompts to expand linguistic coverage. Architecturally, a Relation-Augmented Projector (RAP) concatenates L2-normalized Uni3D object features with Mask3D scene-level features and adds MLP-projected position embeddings, preserving spatial relationships lost in object-centric normalization. A dual-ID wrapping scheme (two identical ID tokens bracketing each object's features) plus a post-vision token order strengthens the ID-to-feature binding under causal attention. The full model (Vicuna-7B backbone) is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16) in two stages: 2 epochs on RIG data followed by 2 epochs on benchmark data.

## Results

Robin3D achieves state-of-the-art results across all 12 benchmark metrics without task-specific fine-tuning. Headline numbers include 60.8% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, 64.9% F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer, 87.2 CIDEr@0.5 on Scan2Cap, 19.2 METEOR on ScanQA, and 56.9% EM on SQA3D (test). Compared to the direct predecessor Chat-Scene, Robin3D improves Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 by 7.8% (64.9 vs. 57.1), Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 by 10.1% (87.2 vs. 77.1), and ScanRefer Acc@0.25 by 5.3% (60.8 vs. 55.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robin3D outperforms all 19 compared baselines on every metric where a fair comparison is possible. Against its direct architectural predecessor Chat-Scene [19], Robin3D gains +5.3 on ScanRefer Acc@0.25 (60.8 vs. 55.5), +7.8 on Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 (64.9 vs. 57.1), and +10.1 on Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 (87.2 vs. 77.1), with consistent 2–3 point improvements on ScanQA and SQA3D. Against LEO [21], the strongest prior joint-training 3DLLM on QA tasks, Robin3D surpasses it on Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 (87.2 vs. 72.4) and SQA3D test EM (56.9 vs. 50.0), though LEO reports a slightly higher ScanQA ROUGE (49.2 vs. 44.0). Against Grounded-3DLLM [9], which introduces referent tokens for grounding, Robin3D leads by +12.9 on ScanRefer Acc@0.25 (60.8 vs. 47.9) and +19.7 on Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 (64.9 vs. 45.2). Against PQ3D [52], the strongest prior method on Scan2Cap, Robin3D improves CIDEr@0.5 by 6.9 points (87.2 vs. 80.3) while also matching or exceeding its grounding scores. The largest relative gains appear on Multi3DRefer, which includes zero-target cases requiring the model to correctly say 'No'—precisely the discriminative capability targeted by the adversarial training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Robin3D builds directly on Chat-Scene (NeurIPS 2024), inheriting its Mask3D + Uni3D + DINO v2 feature extraction pipeline and object-ID-based referring/grounding mechanism from Chat-3D v2. The key conceptual departure is in training data: whereas Chat-Scene and prior 3DLLMs (LEO, Scene-LLM, Grounded-3D-LLM) train exclusively on positive vision-language pairs with template-based instructions, Robin3D introduces the RIG engine to generate adversarial (mixed positive/negative) and linguistically diverse instruction data, drawing inspiration from the 2D POPE hallucination benchmark. Architecturally, the Relation-Augmented Projector and post-vision sequence organization are targeted modifications to address the spatial-relationship loss in Uni3D's object-centric normalization and the weak ID-feature binding in prior token layouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in 3DLLMs—overfitting to positive pairs that causes hallucinated affirmative responses—which is critical for embodied AI and robotic agents that must reliably say 'No' or correct partial information. The 1M-sample RIG dataset and the four adversarial task formulations (HOPE, HROC, PF-3DVG, 3DFQA) provide a reusable resource for the community. However, the work remains confined to ScanNet indoor scenes, relies on GPT-4o for instruction diversification (raising reproducibility and cost concerns), and does not evaluate open-vocabulary capabilities, leaving significant gaps for real-world deployment. The finding that adversarial data improves captioning by 8.9% despite containing no captioning examples suggests discriminative object understanding is a transferable bottleneck, but this insight awaits validation beyond a single architecture and scene dataset.
