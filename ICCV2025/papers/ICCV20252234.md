## Problem

Existing cross-subject fMRI mind decoding frameworks suffer from two sequential error sources: unidirectional mappings produce partially inaccurate image representations due to inter-subject cognitive variability, and these errors compound when fed uncorrected into diffusion models, degrading reconstruction fidelity. No prior method simultaneously improves representation prediction accuracy and makes the downstream decoding stage robust to residual representation errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI voxel signals (resized to 8,192 dimensions via adaptive max pooling) from any subject as input and produces a reconstructed RGB stimulus image. Internally, the fMRI signal is mapped to three disentangled representations—a CLIP text-encoder semantic embedding, a PidiNet edge map, and a 64×-downsampled color palette—which are then decoded through a frozen Stable Diffusion V1.5 pipeline with two ControlNets (one for edges, one for color) and a DDIM sampler at 20 steps.

## Method

The core contribution is the Bidirectional Autoencoder Intertwining (BAI) framework, which uses two autoencoders (one for fMRI voxels, one for image representations) and achieves bidirectional mapping by swapping decoders, with cycle-consistency losses providing additional unsupervised supervision. A Subject Bias Modulation Module (SBMM) applies per-subject MLP-based mean and variance modulation to fMRI features, yielding subject-invariant latent codes and enabling adaptation to new subjects by training only the SBMM. To tolerate residual representation errors during decoding, a transformer-based Semantic Refinement Module (SRM) refines predicted CLIP embeddings, and a Visual Coherence Module (VCM) predicts spatially-varying weights (α_e, α_c) that optimally blend edge and color features into the ControlNet UNet decoder rather than using fixed addition, reducing dependence on pixel-accurate representations.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD) with 4 subjects, the proposed cross-subject framework achieves the best or tied-best scores on 7 out of 8 metrics: PixCorr 0.356, SSIM 0.356, AlexNet(2) 97.3%, AlexNet(5) 98.8%, Inception 96.7%, CLIP 97.5%, and SwAV 0.345. The closest cross-subject competitor, Psychometry, trails by 0.059 on PixCorr (0.297) and 0.7% on CLIP (96.8%). The method also outperforms all subject-specific baselines (e.g., MindEye1, DREAM) on every metric, demonstrating that a single unified model can match or exceed per-subject models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms the strongest cross-subject baseline, Psychometry [28], on 6 of 8 metrics, with the largest gaps on PixCorr (+0.059) and CLIP (+0.7%). Against NeuroPictor [10], the method wins on PixCorr (+0.127), CLIP (+4.2%), and Inception (+2.2%), but NeuroPictor retains a slight edge on SSIM (0.375 vs 0.356). Compared to the subject-specific DREAM [43], the cross-subject framework surpasses it on every reported metric (e.g., PixCorr 0.356 vs 0.288, CLIP 97.5% vs 95.2%), confirming that the bidirectional mapping with SBMM captures subject-invariant representations effectively. On EffNet-B (lower is better), Psychometry achieves a marginally lower value (0.628 vs 0.639), while the method ties NeuroPictor at 0.639. In the new-subject adaptation setting (Table 2), the method with only 500 novel samples and SBMM-only fine-tuning outperforms MindBridge's adapted version on all 8 metrics (e.g., PixCorr 0.145 vs 0.112, CLIP 89.1% vs 86.7%), and also beats MindBridge trained from scratch on the same 500 samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-subject fMRI-to-image decoding line initiated by MindBridge and MindEye2, which introduced shared-subject models but relied on unidirectional mappings from voxels to representations. It builds on DREAM's three-representation decomposition (semantic embedding, edge map, color palette) and integrates ControlNet and Stable Diffusion as the generative backbone, while borrowing the cycle-consistency principle from CycleGAN to enforce bidirectional mapping between fMRI and representation spaces. The key conceptual departure is explicitly modeling and correcting for the two sequential error sources—imprecise representation prediction and error propagation into the diffusion decoder—via the SRM and VCM modules, rather than assuming representations are accurate enough for direct conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-subject generalization is a critical bottleneck for practical brain-computer interfaces, and this paper demonstrates that a single model can match subject-specific performance while adapting to new subjects with as few as 500 fMRI-stimuli pairs, substantially reducing the data burden for deployment. The two-stage error-tolerance design (bidirectional mapping for better prediction + SRM/VCM for robust decoding) offers a reusable pattern for any pipeline where intermediate representations are inherently noisy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the NSD dataset and 4-subject setup, whether it extends to video or other brain imaging modalities (EEG, MEG), and whether the reliance on pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1.5 limits fidelity for novel or out-of-distribution stimuli.
