## Problem

Text-only guided diffusion models can generate diverse synthetic data for data augmentation, but they cannot control the visual proximity of generated images to the originals, producing out-of-distribution samples that hurt downstream classification. This paper asks how to generate a controlled spectrum of synthetic-to-real interpolations and schedule their use across training stages to effectively learn from hard data in long-tail and low-quality image settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250413/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes hard-sample RGB images (identified by low ground-truth confidence or tail-class membership) and text prompts (class names) as input. It produces a full spectrum of synthetic images parameterized by an image-guidance level λ ∈ [0,1), where λ=0 yields text-only generations and λ→1 recovers the original image. Low-fidelity images are filtered out via a CLIPScore threshold before training. The trained classifier then outputs standard class labels on balanced test sets.

## Method

DisCL operates in two phases. Phase 1 identifies hard samples and generates a syn-to-real spectrum by modifying the initial denoising step in Stable Diffusion to t(λ) = ⌊(1−λ)T⌋, so that varying λ smoothly interpolates between prototypical text-guided images and the original real image. Phase 2 applies a curriculum strategy over this spectrum: for long-tail classification, a non-adaptive 'Diverse-to-Specific' schedule progressively increases λ from near-zero (high diversity, prototypical features) toward one (task-specific, real-like features); for low-quality data, an adaptive strategy selects λ at each epoch by measuring ground-truth class confidence improvement on validation subsets corresponding to each guidance level. CLIPScore-based filtering removes synthetic images with poor text-image alignment before training begins.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT (ResNet-10, CE loss), DisCL with the Diverse-to-Specific curriculum improves few-class accuracy from 4.40% (CE baseline) to 23.64% and overall accuracy from 35.80% to 39.82%. With Balanced Softmax loss, DisCL further boosts overall accuracy to 41.33% and few-class accuracy to 21.36%. On iWildCam (CLIP ViT-B/16, FLYP fine-tuning), DisCL with the Adaptive curriculum achieves 38.1 OOD macro-F1 and 52.8 ID macro-F1, outperforming the strongest baseline (FLYP+ALIA) by 1.2 and 0.2 points respectively. Scaling to CLIP ViT-L/14, DisCL reaches 38.7 OOD and 54.6 ID macro-F1 with Weighted Ensembling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-LT, DisCL (CE, Diverse-to-Specific) outperforms CE+LDMLR by 16.34% on few-class accuracy (23.64% vs. 7.30%) and 2.62% overall (39.82% vs. 37.20%), and beats CE+CUDA by 17.06% on few-class (23.64% vs. 6.58%) and 3.52% overall. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline BS (Balanced Softmax), DisCL with CE loss matches overall accuracy (39.82% vs. 39.80%) while substantially improving few-class accuracy (23.64% vs. 19.29%); with BS loss, DisCL adds 1.53% overall (41.33% vs. 39.80%). On iWildCam, DisCL (FLYP+Adaptive) surpasses FLYP+ALIA (the nearest diffusion-based baseline) by 1.2 OOD F1 (38.1 vs. 36.9) and 0.2 ID F1 (52.8 vs. 52.6), and outperforms FLYP by 2.6 OOD and 0.6 ID F1. DisCL also beats LP-FT by 3.4 OOD and 3.1 ID F1, confirming gains over both linear-probing and contrastive fine-tuning strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-guided diffusion mechanism from SDEdit (Meng et al.) by repurposing the initial denoising step t as a continuous 'image guidance level' λ that parameterizes a full spectrum from text-only synthetic images to near-real images. While prior diffusion-based augmentation works (Dunlap et al., Hemmat et al., LDMLR) generate synthetic data at a single fixed fidelity for one-shot or multi-stage training, DisCL treats the guidance level itself as a curriculum variable, selecting which point on the syn-to-real spectrum to train on at each stage. It also builds on the curriculum learning tradition (Bengio et al., Zhou et al.) but applies it to a generative data axis rather than sample difficulty, and on long-tail/low-quality classification baselines (CUDA, Balanced Softmax, FLYP, ALIA) as the task setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a simple, interpretable knob (λ) that lets practitioners dial the diversity-vs-fidelity tradeoff of synthetic data and schedule it across training, which is broadly applicable to any diffusion-augmented classification pipeline. Limitations noted by the authors include dependence on the base diffusion model's quality, reliance on LLM-generated class-name prompts (rather than image captions), and residual position/size mismatches between real and synthetic objects that widen the distribution gap. Open questions include whether the curriculum principle generalizes beyond ResNet-10/CLIP-scale models, whether richer prompts or object-aware cropping close the remaining gap, and whether the adaptive strategy can be made more sample-efficient when validation data for tail classes is extremely scarce.
