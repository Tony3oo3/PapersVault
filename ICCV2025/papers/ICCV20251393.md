## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting requires millions of gaussian primitives, consuming several gigabytes of storage that preclude deployment on resource-constrained devices. Existing compression methods reduce gaussian count via pruning or quantize attributes, but they neglect spatial redundancies within the primitives themselves. Standard video codecs, which excel at exploiting spatial correlations in 2D signals, have not been applied to 3DGS because unstructured gaussian primitives lack the organized spatial layout these codecs expect.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained 3DGS point cloud (positions plus per-gaussian attributes: color, scale, rotation, opacity) and produces two compressed artifacts: (1) feature planes of 512×512 resolution with 32 channels (8 per attribute), concatenated into 32 frames of 16-bit grayscale and encoded with a standard HEVC codec at QP=1, and (2) Morton-order-sorted point positions quantized to 16-bit and coded losslessly. At inference, the decoded planes are fed through a small MLP decoder to reconstruct all gaussian attributes, which are then rendered via the original differentiable 3DGS rasterizer with no additional overhead.

## Method

The core architecture replaces explicit per-gaussian attributes with a tri-plane (k-planes) representation that implicitly predicts all attributes from 3D positions via a decoder MLP. Training is two-phase: 15k iterations of standard 3DGS (including densification) followed by feature-plane training with progressive channel masking, where only the first L_i channels receive gradients at each stage (L_i = {2,4,6,8}), yielding a multi-level representation from coarse to fine. The key technical contribution is a frequency-domain entropy loss: a block-wise 4×4 DCT is applied to each plane channel, and the model minimizes the entropy of the transformed coefficients (via a straight-through estimator with uniform noise), directly aligning the learned representation with the DCT-based compression of standard video codecs. A channel-importance score, computed as the gradient of rendering energy with respect to each plane channel, determines per-channel weights in the entropy loss, automatically allocating fewer bits to less visually important channels. Unbounded scenes are handled via piecewise-projective contraction to enhance spatial correlation on the planes.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, the method achieves 27.30 dB PSNR / 0.810 SSIM / 0.236 LPIPS at only 9.78 MB, a 76× compression over the 745 MB 3DGS baseline with just 0.19 dB PSNR loss. On DeepBlending, it surpasses the original 3DGS in both PSNR (29.82 vs. 29.42) and SSIM (0.907 vs. 0.899) while compressing from 664 MB to 8.62 MB. On Tanks & Temples, it reaches 23.63 dB PSNR at 7.46 MB, within 0.06 dB of the uncompressed 3DGS (23.69 dB at 431 MB). Across all three datasets, the method achieves the smallest or near-smallest file size among all compression baselines while maintaining competitive rendering quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest Scaffold-GS-based baselines, HAC achieves the highest PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (27.53 vs. 27.30) and Tanks & Temples (23.70 vs. 23.63), but the proposed method matches or exceeds HAC in SSIM on Mip-NeRF360 (0.810 vs. 0.807) and LPIPS on DeepBlending (0.251 vs. 0.262), while being slightly smaller in size. Compared to CompGS, the proposed method wins on PSNR (27.30 vs. 27.26), SSIM (0.810 vs. 0.802), and LPIPS (0.251 vs. 0.280) on DeepBlending, and is substantially smaller on Mip-NeRF360 (9.78 vs. 16.5 MB). Against pruning/quantization-based methods (LightGaussian, C3DGS, RDOGaussian, Compact3DGS, EAGLES), the proposed method consistently achieves higher PSNR (by 0.25–0.32 dB on Mip-NeRF360) and smaller file sizes (9.78 MB vs. 23–69 MB). On DeepBlending, the method surpasses the original 3DGS in PSNR and SSIM, a result shared only by Scaffold-GS and HAC among the baselines. The key trade-off is that HAC and CompGS (built on Scaffold-GS) achieve marginally higher PSNR on some datasets, but the proposed method offers the best overall size-quality balance and, critically, integrates directly with standard non-differentiable video codecs (HEVC/VVC) for hardware-accelerated deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DGS compression paradigm (pruning + vector quantization of individual gaussian attributes) by instead replacing explicit per-point storage with an implicit tri-plane architecture that predicts all attributes from continuous spatial coordinates. It builds directly on the K-Planes/Tri-plane formulation for attribute prediction and the piecewise-projective contraction for unbounded scenes, while drawing on VideoRF and TeTriRF's insight that standard video codecs can compress structured 2D feature grids — a connection those prior works could not extend to unstructured 3DGS data. The key conceptual departure is the DCT-domain entropy loss, which aligns the learned representation with the internal transform of HEVC/VVC, something prior entropy-modeling approaches for 3DGS (RDOGaussian, CompGS) did not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling 3DGS deployment on hardware with built-in video codec support (mobile SoCs, HMDs) without requiring custom decoders, since the compressed representation is a standard HEVC bitstream. The ~146× compression (1.4 GB → 9.8 MB) with <0.2 dB PSNR loss is compelling for storage-constrained applications, though the more relevant comparison against state-of-the-art compression (HAC at 15.3 MB, CompGS at 16.5 MB on Mip-NeRF360) shows a more modest 1.5–1.7× size advantage. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the ~90-minute training overhead is acceptable for production pipelines, and whether further gains are possible as next-generation codecs (VVC, ECM) introduce richer transforms.
