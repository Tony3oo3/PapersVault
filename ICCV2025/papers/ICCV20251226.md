## Problem

State-of-the-art cross-view localization methods require precise ground-truth pose annotations that are costly to collect at scale, and existing weakly supervised alternatives rely on image-level metric learning that cannot provide strong supervision for the discriminative local features needed for accurate pose estimation. Additionally, prior weakly supervised approaches lack the ability to estimate relative orientation between ground and aerial views, limiting them to 2-DoF translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251226/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-level image (360° panorama on VIGOR or limited-FoV pinhole image on KITTI) and a geo-referenced aerial image (70m×70m or 40m×40m patch) as input, with only a coarse GPS location needed to retrieve the matching aerial tile. It produces a 2D planar position via argmax over a spatial heat map and a yaw orientation angle predicted as a discrete classification over one-degree bins. The heat map encodes spatial uncertainty, and the orientation is estimated before location via a BEV-projected ground image compared against the aerial view.

## Method

GeoDistill is a two-stage pipeline: an orientation estimator projects the ground panorama into a Bird's-Eye-View representation and uses unshared feature extractors with an MLP classifier to predict yaw, followed by a location estimator enhanced by geometry-guided self-distillation. In the distillation stage, a teacher model receives the full panorama while a student model receives a FoV-masked version (randomly sampled 180°–240°) of the same scene; both process the same satellite image and the student is trained to match the teacher's heat map via cross-entropy on temperature-sharpened (τ=0.06) softmax distributions. This forces the student to extract discriminative local features (e.g., lane markings) rather than relying on global scene context. A bidirectional EMA update (α=0.9) progressively refines the teacher with the student's improved weights, and the refined teacher is used at inference. The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been validated on both CNN (VGG) and ViT (DINOv2) backbones across G2SWeakly and CCVPE base models.

## Results

On VIGOR Cross-Area with 0° orientation noise, GeoDistill with a DINOv2 backbone achieves a mean localization error of 2.68 m and median of 1.20 m, surpassing the fully supervised SOTA HC-Net (3.35 m mean, 1.59 m median). With ±45° orientation noise, GeoDistill (DINO) reports 4.20 m mean / 2.57 m median localization and 2.72° mean / 1.35° median orientation error, matching HC-Net's orientation accuracy while remaining strictly weakly supervised. On KITTI Cross-Area, applying GeoDistill to G2SWeakly (DINO) reduces mean error from 12.61 m to 11.85 m (↓6.0%), and on VIGOR the same paradigm improves CCVPE by 18.5% and G2SWeakly (DINO) by 25.1% in mean localization error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDistill (DINO) decisively outperforms all fully supervised baselines on VIGOR Cross-Area with 0° noise: it achieves 2.68 m mean localization error versus HC-Net's 3.35 m, CCVPE's 4.97 m, Boosting's 5.16 m, DenseFlow's 5.01 m, SliceMatch's 5.53 m, and CVR's 9.45 m. Under ±45° orientation noise, GeoDistill (VGG) beats HC-Net on median localization (1.33 m vs. 1.60 m) and matches it on orientation (2.72° vs. 3.00° mean), while GeoDistill (DINO) trails HC-Net on mean localization (4.20 m vs. 3.46 m) but still surpasses CCVPE (5.16 m). As a plug-and-play enhancement, GeoDistill improves CCVPE by 18.5% (4.97→4.05 m) and G2SWeakly (DINO) by 25.1% (3.58→2.68 m) on VIGOR Cross-Area without any architectural changes. On KITTI Cross-Area, gains are more modest (3–6% for G2SWeakly variants) due to the weaker baseline performance providing a noisier distillation signal, but CCVPE still benefits by 21.8% in mean error. Overall, the weakly supervised GeoDistill matches or exceeds the fully supervised SOTA (HC-Net) on median localization and orientation, while requiring no ground-truth position labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDistill sits at the intersection of weakly supervised cross-view localization (G2SWeakly, [39]) and self-distillation (Born Again Networks, [9]). While prior weakly supervised methods rely on image-level metric learning or require in-domain adaptation data, this paper repurposes the teacher-student self-distillation paradigm with a geometry-specific twist: FoV-based masking creates an information asymmetry between teacher (full panorama) and student (partial view), forcing the student to extract discriminative local features. The orientation estimation module builds on the spherical-to-BEV projection from HC-Net [36] but formulates it as a classification problem that operates without precise planar position ground truth, filling a gap left by prior weakly supervised approaches that could not estimate orientation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for precise GT pose annotations while matching or surpassing fully supervised SOTA (HC-Net) in the challenging cross-area setting, GeoDistill addresses a key scalability bottleneck for real-world deployment of cross-view localization. The plug-and-play nature—applicable to both weakly and fully supervised base models without architectural changes—makes it broadly adoptable. However, the method still depends on coarse GPS for pairing, has been validated on only two datasets (VIGOR, KITTI), and the orientation and localization stages remain decoupled, leaving open whether a fully end-to-end weakly supervised 3-DoF pipeline can match the current two-stage design at scale.
