## Problem

The nuScenes dataset, the de facto benchmark for end-to-end autonomous driving, suffers from insufficient scale (~40k frames), simple scenes (34.63 objects/frame), homogeneous driving behaviors (87.8% straight driving), and outdated 32-beam LiDAR at 2Hz, collectively capping the upper bound of E2E-AD algorithm research. This paper introduces TAD-E2E, a 1M-frame, 10Hz dataset with 128-beam LiDAR, 60-second sequences, and diverse urban driving scenarios, and demonstrates that SOTA methods degrade substantially on it, confirming the dataset's higher research ceiling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6 surround-view RGB images (1920×1080, 10Hz) and a merged 5-LiDAR point cloud (2×128-beam + 3×64-beam, 10Hz) synchronized via PTP, along with ego vehicle state and navigation commands. Temporal context is provided by fusing features from the previous 20 frames. Outputs span the full E2E pipeline: 3D bounding boxes with tracking IDs, surrounding-vehicle 60-frame trajectory predictions, vectorized map elements (lanes, boundaries), and a 30-frame ego driving trajectory, all supervised by modular ground truth at 10Hz over 60-second clips.

## Method

SparseFusion-E2E (SPF-E2E) is a multimodal sparse end-to-end network that merges SparseDrive's symmetric sparse scene representation (avoiding dense BEV feature maps) with FusionAD's LiDAR-camera fusion, enabling joint learning across detection, tracking, prediction, mapping, and planning modules. The architecture processes camera and LiDAR modalities through separate sparse query branches that are fused at the instance level, with a temporal fusion module aggregating features from the prior 20 frames. Training is supervised with modular losses at each stage; a confidence threshold of 0.2 gates gradient backpropagation for detection targets to stabilize optimization. The model is trained with batch size 64 on 8× L40 (48 GB) GPUs for approximately ten days. Ablations confirm that each module's ground truth supervision is indispensable—removing perception supervision causes complete training failure—while LiDAR fusion yields substantial gains over pure vision in complex urban scenes.

## Results

On the TAD-E2E dataset, SparseFusion-E2E achieves 0.659 detection mAP, 0.628 AMOTA, 0.385 mapping mAP, 0.473 prediction minADE, and 0.70 m average planning L2 error with 0.45% collision rate, substantially outperforming SparseDrive (0.226 mAP, 1.63 m L2) and UniAD (0.214 mAP, 2.17 m L2). On nuScenes, SparseFusion-E2E also leads with 0.618 detection mAP and 0.673 NDS versus SparseDrive's 0.415/0.526. Notably, AD-MLP (which uses only ego state) achieves 0.29 m planning L2 on nuScenes but degrades to 4.37 m on TAD-E2E, confirming that nuScenes' simple scenes mask the true difficulty of planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseFusion-E2E dominates all three baselines on TAD-E2E across every module: it improves detection mAP by 433 points over UniAD (0.659 vs 0.214) and 292 points over SparseDrive (0.659 vs 0.226), and reduces planning L2 error to 0.70 m versus 1.63 m (SparseDrive) and 2.17 m (UniAD). On nuScenes, SparseFusion-E2E similarly leads in detection (0.618 mAP vs 0.415 for SparseDrive), tracking (0.598 AMOTA vs 0.373), mapping (0.641 vs 0.556), and prediction (0.472 minADE vs 0.634), while matching SparseDrive's planning L2 (0.66 vs 0.69 m). The AD-MLP comparison is particularly revealing: it achieves 0.29 m planning L2 on nuScenes (better than all full-pipeline methods) but collapses to 4.37 m on TAD-E2E, demonstrating that nuScenes' homogeneous driving behaviors allow trivial baselines to appear competitive. The LiDAR-camera fusion in SparseFusion-E2E is critical on TAD-E2E, where pure-vision SparseDrive suffers from missed detections of occluded and truncated vehicles, leading to collision-prone trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct response to the dominance of nuScenes in end-to-end autonomous driving research, arguing that its 2019-era sensors, limited scale (40k frames), simple scenes, and homogeneous driving behaviors cap the upper bound of E2E-AD algorithms. Methodologically, SparseFusion-E2E (SPF-E2E) merges the sparse query-centric architecture of SparseDrive with the LiDAR-camera multimodal fusion strategy of FusionAD, creating a unified sparse representation that processes both modalities without relying on dense BEV feature maps. The dataset construction follows the cold-start + offline pre-annotation paradigm established by nuPlan and H-V2X, using an ensemble of BEVFusion models with Weighted Boxes Fusion for scalable 3D annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TAD-E2E dataset (1M frames, 10Hz, 128-beam LiDAR, 60s sequences) is poised to become the next standard open-loop E2E-AD benchmark, and the demonstrated 3–10× performance degradation of SOTA methods on it signals that the field has been overfitting to nuScenes' simplicity. The finding that a perception-free MLP (AD-MLP) achieves competitive planning on nuScenes but collapses on TAD-E2E is a strong diagnostic that prior open-loop results may not reflect genuine driving competence. Open questions include whether the dataset's pre-annotated (rather than fully manual) ground truth introduces systematic biases, how results transfer to closed-loop simulation, and whether the modular supervision signals (detection, tracking, mapping) can eventually be eliminated to reduce annotation cost.
