## Problem

Existing 3D avatar reconstruction methods require per-ID training on multi-view or monocular videos, making them slow and non-generalizable, while 2D diffusion-based animation methods struggle with identity consistency under large pose changes and cannot support real-time rendering. No prior work achieves fast, generalizable, feed-forward reconstruction of an expressive upper-body 3D avatar (with detailed facial expressions and hand gestures) from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251589/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input, along with EHM tracking parameters (body shape/pose, hand pose, facial shape/expression/jaw) obtained via a two-stage pipeline (coarse estimation with PIXIE/TEASER/HaMeR followed by keypoint-based refinement). It outputs Ubody Gaussians in canonical space (template + UV Gaussians, typically <150K valid Gaussians) that can be deformed into arbitrary pose space for animation, rendered at 512×512 at ~50 FPS, with the full reconstruction completing in ~98 ms.

## Method

GUAVA introduces EHM, which replaces the SMPLX head with FLAME to improve facial expressiveness, and a two-stage tracking method that optimizes parameters in parallel across frames using 2D keypoint losses. The reconstruction model has two branches: a Template branch that projects EHM vertices into screen space to sample appearance features (from a DINOv2 encoder) and predicts per-vertex Gaussian attributes via MLPs, and a UV branch that uses inverse texture mapping to explicitly warp screen-space features into UV texture space, then predicts per-pixel Gaussians rigged to mesh triangles via barycentric coordinates. A StyleUNet-based neural refiner decodes a coarse feature map (rendered by splatting latent Gaussian features) into the final image, compensating for Gaussian sparsity. The model is trained in a feed-forward manner on 26K+ monocular upper-body video clips using random source-target frame pairs, enabling generalization to unseen identities at inference time.

## Results

On self-reenactment against 3D-based methods, GUAVA achieves PSNR 25.70, L1 0.0168, SSIM 0.8976, and LPIPS 0.0836, outperforming all baselines while reconstructing from a single frame in ~98 ms (vs. 7 min to 2.4 h for 3D baselines). Against 2D diffusion-based methods, GUAVA reaches PSNR 25.87 and 52.21 FPS, compared to 21.25–24.46 PSNR and 0.12–0.53 FPS for MagicPose, Champ, and MimicMotion. On cross-reenactment, GUAVA attains an Identity Preservation Score (IPS) of 0.5554, substantially exceeding MimicMotion (0.1310), Champ (0.3677), and MagicPose (0.3277).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D-based methods, GUAVA wins on all four image-quality metrics (PSNR, L1, SSIM, LPIPS) while requiring only a single input frame and ~98 ms of reconstruction time, compared to GART (24.46 PSNR, ~7 min), ExAvatar (24.09 PSNR, ~2.4 h), and GaussianAvatar (23.62 PSNR, ~1.3 h), which all require half a video for per-ID training. Against 2D diffusion-based methods, GUAVA achieves the highest PSNR (25.87 vs. 24.46 for MimicMotion, 22.01 for Champ, 21.25 for MagicPose) and is roughly 100× faster in animation/rendering (52.21 FPS vs. 0.12–0.53 FPS). On cross-reenactment identity preservation, GUAVA's IPS of 0.5554 far exceeds Champ (0.3677), MagicPose (0.3277), and MimicMotion (0.1310), confirming that the 3D Gaussian representation maintains identity consistency under large pose changes where 2D methods degrade. GUAVA does not lose to any baseline on any reported metric; the closest competitor is GART on PSNR (24.46 vs. 25.70), a gap of ~1.2 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) parametric human models (SMPLX, FLAME, MANO) used as geometric priors for avatar reconstruction, (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a real-time rendering and representation backbone (building on GaussianAvatars and GART for rigged Gaussians), and (3) generalizable single-image avatar synthesis (building on GAGAvatar's feed-forward head reconstruction). The key departure is extending the generalizable single-image paradigm from head-only avatars to the upper body, while introducing EHM (SMPLX+FLAME fusion) to overcome SMPLX's limited facial expressiveness, and using inverse texture mapping to populate UV-space Gaussians from screen-space features—neither of which appears in prior 3DGS avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving sub-second feed-forward reconstruction and ~50 FPS rendering from a single image, GUAVA removes the per-ID training bottleneck that has limited 3D avatar methods to controlled multi-view or monocular video settings, making expressive upper-body avatars practical for real-time applications like virtual meetings and interactive media. However, the paper acknowledges significant open problems: geometric fidelity is constrained by training data diversity (clothing wrinkles, loose garments, hair), the mesh prior limits reconstruction of regions far from the template (e.g., large hairstyles), clothing deformation is not modeled, and the frontal-view training data prevents full 360° synthesis. Extending the framework to full-body, multi-view, and physics-based clothing remains important future work.
