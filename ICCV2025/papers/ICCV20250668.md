## Problem

Current image generation capabilities are fragmented across task-specific models (T2I, inpainting, instruction-based editing, ID-preserving generation, etc.), each requiring separate architectures, training pipelines, and parameter sets. While generalist models like OmniGen and OneDiffusion have demonstrated feasibility, they lack systematic ablation studies on critical design choices such as input encoding, data mixing, and training schedules, leaving the community without clear best practices for building unified diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250668/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

All visual inputs (latent noise, input image, binary mask) are encoded via an 8-channel VAE and concatenated along the channel dimension into a single equal-length sequence, so that generation and editing share identical computational cost. External conditions (e.g., CLIP face embeddings for ID preservation, or layout bounding boxes) are injected by replacing pre-designed placeholder tokens in the prompt embedding sequence, allowing arbitrary-length conditioning without architectural changes. The model operates at 512×512 resolution and always outputs a decoded image, with auxiliary tasks (depth, pose, segmentation) producing their visualizations directly as generation targets rather than structured outputs.

## Method

UniVG builds on a minimally modified MM-DiT (38 layers, 2432 hidden dim, 3.7B params) with a flow-matching objective, where the key architectural choice is channel-wise concatenation of noise, image, and mask latents rather than sequence-wise concatenation (as in OmniGen), preserving a fixed sequence length and enabling 3.5× faster editing inference. External conditions are injected via embedding replacement of placeholder tokens, decoupling the conditioning mechanism from the backbone. Training follows a three-stage progressive recipe: (I) T2I pretraining from scratch, (II) multi-task training mixing T2I, in/outpainting, instruction-based editing, auxiliary tasks, and layout-guided generation, and (III) a dedicated ID-preserving fine-tuning stage at 1:1 ratio with all other tasks to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Ablations reveal that T2I and editing coexist without trade-off, auxiliary perception tasks (depth, pose, segmentation) measurably improve editing quality, and the channel-concatenation design is critical for maintaining efficiency across all tasks.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, UniVG (3.7B params) achieves a GenEval score of 0.70, matching OmniGen and outperforming FLUX.1-dev (0.66), while reaching DSG 0.75 and T2I-CompBench 0.48, closely approaching the task-specific SD3 (0.76, 0.49) with half the parameters. On instruction-based editing, UniVG attains the highest CLIP-T of 29.5 on MagicBrush and 25.9 on EmuEdit, surpassing all task-specific editors (InsP2P, MGIE, EmuEdit) and unified baselines. For ID-preserving generation, UniVG scores 0.329 ID similarity on Unsplash-50, outperforming unified models OneDiff (0.283) and OmniGen (0.294), though trailing task-specific methods InstantID (0.648) and PuLID (0.654).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified generalist models, UniVG clearly dominates: it beats OneDiff on all four T2I metrics (e.g., GenEval 0.70 vs 0.65, DSG 0.75 vs 0.68) and OmniGen on CompBench (0.48 vs 0.46), DSG (0.75 vs 0.66), and HPSv2 (28.2 vs 27.7), while tying on GenEval (0.70). On instruction-based editing, UniVG achieves the best CLIP-T across all baselines (29.5 MagicBrush, 25.9 EmuEdit), outperforming task-specific editors like InsP2P (24.5/21.9) and MGIE (26.4/22.4), though it trails EmuEdit (89.7) and OneDiff (88.5) on CLIP-I input preservation. For ID-preserving generation, UniVG (ID 0.329) surpasses both unified baselines but falls well short of task-specific InstantID (0.648) and PuLID (0.654), which leverage dedicated face encoders. Compared to the task-specific SD3 (8B params), UniVG (3.7B) is within 0.01–0.02 on GenEval/CompBench/DSG and 0.7 on HPSv2, demonstrating that the generalist design incurs minimal T2I quality cost. In inference efficiency, UniVG matches OneDiff's editing time (10.4s vs 10.8s) while OmniGen requires 36.8s for the same task, thanks to channel-dimension concatenation keeping sequence length constant.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVG builds directly on the MM-DiT architecture and rectified flow formulation from SD3/Flux, extending them into a multi-task generalist setting. It is positioned explicitly against OmniGen and OneDiffusion as the two closest prior generalist diffusion models, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) a minimalist channel-dimension concatenation of visual inputs (noise, image, mask) rather than sequence-dimension concatenation, and (2) a carefully ablated three-stage progressive training recipe that the prior works left underspecified. The embedding-replacement mechanism for injecting external conditions (e.g., CLIP face features) follows the broader adapter/conditioning paradigm seen in ControlNet and IP-Adapter but integrates it natively into the MM-DiT prompt stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic ablation study of how to train a generalist diffusion model, showing that T2I and instruction-based editing coexist without trade-offs and that auxiliary perception tasks (depth, segmentation) synergistically improve editing quality—insights that were absent from prior generalist-model papers. The channel-concatenation design yields a 3.5× speedup in editing inference over OmniGen at comparable model size, making unified models practically deployable. Open questions include whether the progressive training recipe generalizes to even more tasks, whether the model can be scaled further (the paper shows monotonic improvement up to 3.7B), and whether the gap to task-specific models on ID preservation (UniVG 0.329 vs. PuLID 0.654) can be closed without sacrificing generality.
