## Problem

Current gait recognition methods learn identity by contrasting samples under varying conditions (cross-view, cross-clothing, occlusion), which causes two issues: semantic inconsistency (representations under different conditions exhibit large gaps) and semantic uniformity (representations collapse into a narrow space to remain usable across all covariates). The paper asks whether a generative paradigm—where all gait samples are produced from a single unified template—can regularize representations into a consistent yet diverse semantic space that captures accurate identity differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251474/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gait silhouette sequence of 30 frames resized to 64×44 pixels, processed by a ResNet-based visual encoder followed by a Horizontal Partition that splits the body into P parts. For each part, the latent representation (dimension C4 × S) is fed into Origins, which produces both a generated representation (for the MSE generative loss) and a compressed one-token feature F (for the Recognition Head). The final output is an identity prediction via Triplet Loss and Cross-Entropy Loss, with the unified template T ∈ R^{1×C4} serving as the shared generative and discriminative backbone.

## Method

Origins introduces a learnable unified template T that is diffused into N timestep templates via sinusoidal time encoding and an MLP, then used in two complementary attention mechanisms. In Diffusing Timestep Templates, each gait sample randomly selects one timestep template as (K, V) while the gait representation acts as Q; the generated output is trained with MSE loss against the detached input, regularizing all representations into a consistent and diverse space without adding explicit noise. In Transferring Unified Template, T itself acts as Q attending over the gait sequence (K, V), compressing S frames into a single token F for recognition. The two objectives are jointly trained end-to-end with L = L_mse + L_triplet + L_ce. Unlike standard diffusion models, Origins uses random timestep sampling (implicit relations rather than a Markov chain), operates entirely in latent space without pixel-level noise, and imposes no identity constraint on the generative process—only the unified template and real samples are used for identification.

## Results

Origins achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six gait recognition benchmarks. On CASIA-B, Origins-T reaches 95.7% mean Rank-1 (99.3% NM, 97.4% BG, 90.3% CL), surpassing the previous best (CLTD, 94.8%). On the in-the-wild Gait3D dataset, Origins-M attains 75.8% Rank-1, 86.8% Rank-5, and 67.0% mAP. On the large-scale GREW benchmark, Origins-L achieves 80.8% Rank-1 and 92.1% Rank-10. On CCGR-MINI, Origins-M reaches 41.45% Rank-1 and 24.71% mINP, outperforming the prior best by 2.1% and 7.9% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Origins outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every dataset. The closest competitor is DeepGaitV2 [12] (TPAMI 2025), which Origins surpasses by 4.6% Rank-1 on SUSTech1K (86.9% vs. 82.3%), 1.4% Rank-1 on Gait3D (75.8% vs. 74.4%), 3.1% Rank-1 on GREW (80.8% vs. 77.7%), and 2.1% Rank-1 on CCGR-MINI (41.45% vs. 39.37%). Among appearance-based methods, VPNet [35] (CVPR 2024) is the strongest single competitor, beaten by 0.8% on GREW and 0.4% on Gait3D Rank-1. Model-based methods (GaitGraph2 [47], Gait-TR [68], SkeletonGait [11], MSGG [40]) trail Origins by 15–50+ points on in-the-wild datasets, confirming the advantage of the unified template's diverse semantic space over structural priors. Earlier baselines like GaitSet [4] and GaitPart [9] are outperformed by 20–40 points on Gait3D, while recent ECCV 2024 methods (CLTD [63], GaitMoE [25], Free Lunch [53]) are surpassed by 5–6% Rank-1 on Gait3D. Notably, Origins-M on Gait3D also beats the parameter-heavy GaitMoE [25] by 2.1% Rank-1, suggesting the gains come from meaningful semantic regularization rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Origins departs from the dominant gait recognition paradigm of learning invariant representations by contrasting samples under varying conditions (cross-view, cross-clothing, occlusion), instead proposing that all gait representations should be generated from a single learnable unified template. Conceptually, it borrows the step-by-step diffusion mechanism from DDPM/LDM (sinusoidal timestep encoding, random timestep sampling, latent-space generation) but explicitly rejects three core diffusion assumptions: no noise is added, timestep relations are learned implicitly rather than following a Markov chain, and no identity constraint is imposed during generation. Among diffusion-based representation learning methods (l-DAE, ADDP, HybirdViT), Origins is the first to apply a unified generative-discriminative template specifically to gait recognition, combining it with the standard Horizontal Partition and ResNet-based backbone from the OpenGait/GaitBase lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single learnable template, diffused into timestep templates for generation and transferred for recognition, can simultaneously address semantic inconsistency (different conditions mapping to different subspaces) and semantic uniformity (representations collapsing under multiple covariates), yielding consistent SOTA across six benchmarks of varying scale and complexity. A key open question is whether this representation-space generative learning can be extended to pixel-level gait synthesis for pretraining or data augmentation, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The finding that a simple ResNet backbone with the template mechanism outperforms more complex architectures (e.g., GaitMoE with mixture-of-experts) suggests the template's regularization effect may be more important than architectural sophistication, though this claim would benefit from more controlled ablations across backbone families.
