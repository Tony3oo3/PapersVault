## Problem

Existing dToF depth enhancement methods assume ideal RGB-dToF calibration and reliable sensor signals, but real-world devices exhibit calibration drift (up to 2 dToF pixels of misalignment) and inherent signal anomalies (non-Lambertian reflections, low-reflectivity loss, long-distance failures). These assumptions break down in practice, causing existing super-resolution-based approaches that depend on precise region correspondences to degrade significantly on real mobile phone data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252344/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a dToF sparse depth map (projected from raw dToF signals into high-resolution sparse points using device intrinsic/extrinsic parameters, rather than used as a low-resolution dense grid) plus inverse and relative depth maps generated by a pre-trained Depth Anything V2 model. The output is a full-resolution dense depth map. On ZJU-L5 the dToF sparsity is ~0.02% of pixels; on their Honor Magic6 Ultra data it is ~0.18%, making the input extremely sparse compared to conventional depth completion benchmarks.

## Method

DEPTHOR reformulates dToF enhancement as depth completion (not super-resolution) and introduces two key components. First, a noise-robust training strategy simulates real-world dToF characteristics on the Hypersim synthetic dataset by modeling overall FoV distribution, specific abnormal regions (non-Lambertian surfaces, low-reflectivity areas, long-distance signal loss), random anomalies (~5% noise + 5% blank points), and calibration-induced regional shifts (0–2 dToF pixels for background points). Second, the network has two stages: a multimodal fusion encoder-decoder (BPNet-based RGB encoder, depth encoder with large-kernel dilated convolutions and CBAM, AdaBins-style depth head producing half-resolution initial depth) followed by a refinement stage using mixed affinity propagation (based on CSPN++) that jointly computes affinity from both the UNet decoder features and MDE features to correct errors in ill-posed regions. Notably, the conventional point-embedding step in affinity propagation is removed because dToF measurements are not fully accurate, and the MDE features provide global depth relationships that compensate for missing or erroneous sparse measurements.

## Results

On the ZJU-L5 dataset, DEPTHOR-Large achieves δ₁=0.933, Rel=0.075, RMSE=0.350, and log₁₀=0.034, improving Rel and RMSE by 27% and 18% respectively over the previous SOTA depth super-resolution method CFPNet (Rel=0.103, RMSE=0.431). On the authors' more challenging real-world dToF samples (evaluated against stereo-matching GT), DEPTHOR achieves Rel=0.155 and RMSE=0.226, outperforming the best baseline PENet* (Rel=0.202, RMSE=0.327) by 23% and 22%. The lightweight DEPTHOR-Small variant (6M+24M params, 26G+13G FLOPs) also surpasses all baselines, achieving Rel=0.080 and RMSE=0.379 on ZJU-L5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPTHOR-Large outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric for ZJU-L5. Against the strongest depth super-resolution baseline CFPNet, it improves Rel from 0.103 to 0.075 (−27%) and RMSE from 0.431 to 0.350 (−18%), while also leading on δ₁ (0.933 vs 0.883) and log₁₀ (0.034 vs 0.047). Among depth completion methods, it surpasses the SOTA 2D method CompletionFormer (Rel 0.075 vs 0.103) and the 3D method BPNet (RMSE 0.350 vs 0.671), as well as the generalizable OMNI-DC (Rel 0.075 vs 0.099). Notably, even the lightweight PENet* retrained with DEPTHOR's simulation strategy (Rel=0.093, RMSE=0.447) already matches or exceeds CFPNet on several metrics, demonstrating that the training strategy alone closes much of the gap between completion and super-resolution. On the real-world Honor Magic6 Ultra samples, DEPTHOR leads all baselines including DAv2-RS (RMSE 0.226 vs 0.292) and PENet* (RMSE 0.226 vs 0.327), with the largest margin over OMNI-DC (RMSE 0.226 vs 0.643). Monocular depth estimation methods (BTS, AdaBins, DAv2-ML/RS) consistently underperform on Rel and log₁₀, confirming that dToF signals provide essential scale information that MDE alone cannot recover.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPTHOR departs from the dominant depth super-resolution framing for dToF enhancement (Deltar, CFPNet, DVSR) by reformulating the problem as depth completion, motivated by the observation that real-world calibration errors and signal anomalies make precise RGB-dToF region correspondences unreliable. Architecturally, it builds on BPNet's encoder and adaptive-bin depth head, CSPN++'s affinity propagation for refinement, and integrates Depth Anything V2 as a frozen MDE backbone to supply global depth context. The key conceptual contribution is a noise-robust training strategy that simulates real-world dToF imperfections (calibration drift, non-Lambertian signal loss, random anomalies) on synthetic Hypersim data, enabling standard completion architectures to be trained without access to real dToF ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a lightweight completion model trained with the proposed dToF simulation strategy matches or exceeds SOTA super-resolution methods (which require precise region correspondences) suggests that robustness to sensor imperfections can be addressed at the data level rather than through increasingly complex architectures. This is practically significant for mobile dToF deployment where calibration drift is inevitable. However, the method is not fast enough for real-time inference, and the real-world evaluation relies on stereo-matching GT that the authors acknowledge is imperfect, leaving open questions about true performance in the most challenging regions. Extending the approach to other depth sensors and achieving real-time speed remain important open problems.
