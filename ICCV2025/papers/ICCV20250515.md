## Problem

Existing Tracking Any Point (TAP) methods rely heavily on tracking-specific inductive biases—cost volumes, iterative refinement, windowed inference, and temporal smoothness priors—that limit generality and scalability. The paper asks which of these architectural and algorithmic design decisions can be removed to build a simpler, more scalable model that still achieves state-of-the-art tracking performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250515/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (256×256 or 384×512) partitioned into 8×8 patches, along with Q query points specified as (t, x, y) tuples. At the query frame, the point token is initialized with positional embeddings of the query coordinates; at all other frames, point tokens are set to a learned mask value, so tracking is framed as imputing masked tokens. The model outputs per-frame (x, y) coordinates parameterized as a 256-bin classification per axis (yielding continuous predictions via expected value) and a binary visibility flag, all produced causally in a single forward pass per frame with no iterative refinement or windowed inference.

## Method

TAPNext adapts the TRecViT architecture, interleaving RecurrentGemma SSM blocks (causal linear recurrence over the temporal dimension, treating spatial tokens as batch) with ViT blocks (full self-attention across image patches and point track tokens per frame). The key reformulation casts tracking as masked token decoding: image tokens and point track tokens are concatenated along the spatial axis and processed jointly, with the model learning to propagate the single known query coordinate through the recurrent state to all subsequent timesteps. Coordinate prediction uses a classification head (256 bins per axis) trained with combined softmax cross-entropy and Huber loss, enabling multimodal uncertainty representation. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised learning on 500K synthetic Kubric videos (48 frames, 256 queries each), followed by BootsTAP-style self-supervised fine-tuning on ~15M real video clips with a student-teacher setup. Losses are applied at every layer with equal weights, and the model requires no cost volumes, iterative refinement, local search windows, or temporal positional embeddings.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid benchmark (256×256), BootsTAPNext-B achieves state-of-the-art performance on 10 of 12 metrics across DAVIS and Kinetics, with headline numbers of AJ 65.2 / δ^avg 78.5 / OA 91.2 on DAVIS First and AJ 62.2 / δ^avg 75.1 / OA 89.0 on Kinetics Strided. Crucially, BootsTAPNext-B operates at frame-level latency (per-frame causal inference), outperforming all prior online methods by large margins (e.g., +5.5 AJ over Online BootsTAP on DAVIS First) while also surpassing windowed and video-level methods. The model generalizes to 250-frame Kinetics videos despite being trained on only 48-frame clips, and achieves the lowest latency (5.29 ms on H100) and memory footprint (2.1 GB) among all compared trackers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BootsTAPNext-B (frame latency) outperforms all prior online trackers by substantial margins: +5.5 AJ and +6.2 δ^avg over Online BootsTAP on DAVIS First, and +7.2 AJ over Track-On on Kinetics First. Against the strongest windowed methods, BootsTAPNext-B surpasses TAPIR by +6.7 AJ on DAVIS First and +7.7 AJ on Kinetics First, and beats TAPTRv3 by +2.0 AJ on DAVIS First and +2.8 AJ on Kinetics First. It also exceeds the video-level LocoTrack-B by +2.2 AJ on DAVIS First and +2.7 AJ on Kinetics Strided, despite requiring only one frame of input versus the entire video. The only metrics where BootsTAPNext-B does not achieve the best score are OA on Kinetics First (87.4 vs. TAPTRv3's 88.2) and OA on Kinetics Strided (89.0 vs. BootsTAP's 89.7), both narrow gaps. Notably, BootsTAPNext-B matches or exceeds CoTracker3 (window latency) on all reported metrics at 256×256 while operating at per-frame latency, and achieves the lowest inference latency (5.29 ms on H100) and memory usage (2.1 GB) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPNext departs from the dominant TAP paradigm (TAPIR, CoTracker, TAPTR, PIPs) that relies on cost-volume matching, iterative refinement, and windowed inference by reframing tracking as masked token decoding over a shared spatio-temporal token bank. Architecturally, it adapts TRecViT [40], which interleaves RecurrentGemma SSM blocks (for temporal recurrence) with ViT blocks (for spatial attention), and borrows the coordinate-classification head from Farebrother et al. [16]. The BootsTAPNext variant follows the self-supervised student-teacher fine-tuning procedure from BootsTAP [13], but replaces TAPIR's coordinate loss with the proposed classification-based loss. Conceptually, the paper positions TAP as an instance of multi-modal masked imputation (à la MultiMAE), where image patches and point trajectories are two modalities processed jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPNext demonstrates that a simple, bias-free SSM+ViT architecture can match or exceed state-of-the-art trackers while operating with per-frame latency and minimal memory, making it directly applicable to real-time robotics and autonomous driving. The observation that cost-volume-like attention, motion clustering, and coordinate-based readout emerge organically from end-to-end training challenges the field's assumption that tracking-specific inductive biases are necessary to bridge the sim-to-real gap. A key open limitation is temporal generalization: the SSM degrades significantly beyond ~150 frames (trained on 48), and the authors identify this as the primary bottleneck for further improvement. The framework's generality (masked decoding over arbitrary token types) suggests natural extensions to other video correspondence and reconstruction tasks.
