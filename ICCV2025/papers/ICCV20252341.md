## Problem

The self-attention mechanism in Vision Transformers acts as a low-pass filter, and stacking L such layers causes high-frequency components to be exponentially suppressed (frequency vanishing), leading to blurred features and representation collapse. This severely limits performance on dense prediction tasks requiring fine-grained spatial details. Existing anti-oversmoothing methods (AttScale, FeatScale, NeuTRENO) apply static high-frequency boosts but lack dynamic, full-spectrum frequency control that adapts across layers and spatial regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252341/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FDAM is a plug-and-play module inserted into existing ViT backbones (SegFormer, DeiT, MaskDINO, Spatial Mamba, LSKNet) that operates on internal feature maps and attention matrices at each transformer layer. It takes the standard attention matrix A ∈ R^{HW×HW} and feature maps X ∈ R^{C×H×W} as inputs, and outputs a modulated attention matrix (a spatially-weighted blend of low-pass and inverted high-pass filters) and frequency-scaled feature maps. The module adds minimal overhead (e.g., 0.5M params / 0.3G FLOPs for SegFormer-B0, 1M / 3G for MaskDINO) while preserving the original input image format and output prediction format of the host architecture.

## Method

FDAM comprises two complementary techniques. Attention Inversion (AttInv) computes the DFT of each attention filter, subtracts it from an all-pass filter to obtain a complementary high-pass filter, and then dynamically blends the original low-pass and inverted high-pass filters using spatially-varying weights predicted by a lightweight convolutional layer; cascading L such layers yields 2^L distinct filter combinations, breaking the monotonic high-frequency decay of standard attention. Frequency Dynamic Scaling (FreqScale) generates input-dependent frequency band weights via an MLP with tanh activation, reassembles them with learnable static weights in a group-wise manner (C/g × b×b per group), and modulates the feature spectrum by element-wise multiplication in the Fourier domain across b×b frequency bands. Together, AttInv provides structural full-spectrum representation while FreqScale offers fine-grained, per-band amplification of suppressed high-frequency components, and both are differentiable and require no architectural redesign of the host model.

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation, FDAM improves SegFormer-B0 by +2.4 mIoU (37.4→39.8 SS) and DeiT-S by +1.4 mIoU (42.9→44.3 SS), with consistent gains across DeiT-B (+1.1), ViT-B (+1.2), DeiT3-B (+0.8), and DeiT3-L (+0.6). On COCO, Mask DINO+FDAM achieves 47.1 AP_box (+1.6) and 42.6 AP_mask (+1.4) for object/instance detection, and 49.6 PQ (+0.9) for panoptic segmentation. On DOTA remote sensing detection, LSKNet-S+FDAM reaches 78.61 mAP (+1.12), setting a new state-of-the-art under single-scale settings. All improvements come with minimal overhead (≤1M params, ≤3G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against anti-oversmoothing baselines on ADE20K (Table 1), FDAM (38.3 SS mIoU) outperforms AttScale (36.8), FeatScale (37.0), and NeuTRENO (37.2) by 1.1–1.5 mIoU, confirming its superiority in addressing frequency vanishing. In semantic segmentation (Table 2), FDAM consistently improves every backbone it is plugged into—SegFormer-B0, DeiT-S/B, ViT-B, DeiT3-B/L, and Spatial Mamba-B—by 0.5–2.4 mIoU, and the FDAM-enhanced DeiT3-B (52.6 SS) surpasses non-FDAM baselines like ConvNeXt-B (49.1), Focal-B (49.0), and DAT-B (49.4) by 3+ mIoU. For COCO detection (Table 3), Mask DINO+FDAM (47.1 AP_box, 42.6 AP_mask) beats ViTDet-L (46.8 AP_box) with 6× fewer parameters (53M vs. 308M) and 5× fewer FLOPs (289G vs. 1542G), and outperforms META-B (45.4) and ViT-Adapter-B (47.0 AP_box). On DOTA (Table 5), LSKNet-S+FDAM (78.61 mAP) surpasses PKINet-S (78.39), GRA (77.65), and O-RCNN (77.35), achieving the best single-scale result among all listed methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Fourier-domain analyses of ViT attention (Wang et al., ICLR 2022; Nguyen et al., NeurIPS 2023; Dong et al., ICML 2021) that established self-attention as a low-pass filter causing frequency vanishing. It departs from prior anti-oversmoothing methods (AttScale, FeatScale, NeuTRENO) by borrowing a circuit-theory perspective: rather than merely scaling or regularizing high-frequency components, it literally inverts the attention matrix in the frequency domain to synthesize a complementary high-pass filter, then dynamically blends the two per spatial location. The FreqScale component adds per-band fine-grained control that prior global-scaling approaches lack, and the 2^L combinatorial argument for cascaded layers provides a theoretical justification for why the stacked architecture can learn arbitrary frequency responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDAM offers a principled, plug-and-play remedy for the frequency vanishing problem that affects all ViT-based dense prediction models, with minimal parameter and FLOP overhead. The consistent gains across heterogeneous backbones (SegFormer, DeiT, MaskDINO, and even Spatial Mamba) suggest the low-pass bottleneck is a general architectural limitation rather than model-specific. However, the diminishing returns on larger models (+0.5–0.6 mIoU on DeiT3-L vs. +2.4 on SegFormer-B0) raise the open question of whether the benefit saturates at scale, and whether the Fourier-domain operations become a bottleneck at very high resolutions. The circuit-theory framing also opens the door to more sophisticated filter topologies (band-pass, notch) that the current binary low/high split does not exploit.
