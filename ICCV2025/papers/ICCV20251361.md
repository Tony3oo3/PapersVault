## Problem

SAM, a prompt-driven foundation model for natural image segmentation, cannot be directly applied to medical image segmentation because it (1) produces binary masks without semantic labels, (2) requires user-provided prompts that are impractical for automated multi-organ segmentation, and (3) performs suboptimally on volumetric medical images due to the 2D-to-3D domain gap. No prior SAM adaptation simultaneously resolves all three limitations while preserving SAM's full architecture and pre-trained weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D volumetric medical image (CT or MRI) as input, which is first converted from its native modality channels to a standardized 3-channel format via a convolutional adapter. It produces N binary segmentation masks (where N exceeds the maximum number of object classes in the dataset), each paired with a predicted semantic class label from K+1 categories (K organs plus a 'no object' class). A dataset mapping pipeline converts multi-class ground truth into per-class binary masks, and bipartite matching aligns the N predictions to the variable number of ground truth segments per volume.

## Method

MaskSAM retains SAM's full ViT image encoder and mask decoder with frozen weights, inserting lightweight adapters at each transformer block: a 3D depth-convolution adapter (DConvAdapter) for image embeddings that adds a depth-wise convolution with skip connection, and a 3D depth-MLP adapter (DMLPAdapter) for prompt embeddings using an inverted bottleneck along the depth dimension. A prompt generator, fed by multi-level feature maps from the image encoder, produces auxiliary binary masks, bounding boxes (averaged with mask-derived boxes), and classifier tokens that replace all external prompts. Semantic label prediction is enabled by summing auxiliary classifier tokens with learnable global classifier tokens in the mask decoder, following the DETR/MaskFormer set-prediction paradigm with bipartite matching. Learnable depth positional embeddings are added to both encoder and decoder to capture the volumetric dimension, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with combined classification, mask (BCE+Dice), and box (L1+GIoU) losses.

## Results

MaskSAM achieves a Dice score of 90.52% on AMOS2022 (16 abdominal organs), 93.41% on ACDC (cardiac structures), and 87.30% on Synapse (8 abdominal organs), all evaluated via 5-fold cross-validation without ensembles. These results surpass nnUNet by 2.7%, 1.8%, and 1.0% DSC respectively, and outperform the strongest prompt-free SAM-based baselines (SAMed, SAM3D) by 13–15% on AMOS2022. When given GT bounding-box prompts, MaskSAM further reaches 94.70% DSC on Synapse and 94.30% on ACDC, exceeding nnUNet by 8.5% and 2.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskSAM outperforms all 12 baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest non-SAM baselines, it beats nnUNet by 2.7% DSC on AMOS2022 (90.52 vs 87.80), 1.8% on ACDC (93.41 vs 91.61), and 1.0% on Synapse (87.30 vs 86.21), and surpasses nnFormer by 1.5%, 1.3%, and 0.7% respectively. Against prompt-free SAM-based methods, the gains are substantial: MaskSAM exceeds SAMed by 13.3% on AMOS2022 (90.52 vs 77.20) and 5.4% on Synapse (87.30 vs 81.88), and outperforms SAM3D by 15.4% on AMOS2022 (90.52 vs 75.10) and 7.7% on Synapse (87.30 vs 79.56). Against SAM and MedSAM with GT bounding-box prompts, MaskSAM (prompt-free) still leads by 36.7% on AMOS2022 (90.52 vs 53.80 for SAM) and 30.3% on Synapse (87.30 vs 57.00 for SAM), demonstrating that the prompt generator eliminates the accuracy penalty of external prompt dependency. On ACDC, MaskSAM (93.41%) also beats SAM3D (90.41%) by 3.0% and MedSAM with GT bbox (86.17%) by 7.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskSAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: SAM-based medical segmentation adaptations, the mask classification set-prediction paradigm from DETR and MaskFormer, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via adapters. Unlike prior SAM adaptations (SAMed, SAM3D, MedSAM) that either require external prompts or restructure SAM's architecture, MaskSAM retains SAM's full pre-trained structure and introduces a prompt generator that produces auxiliary masks, bounding boxes, and classifier tokens directly from the image encoder. The 3D depth-aware adapters (DConvAdapter, DMLPAdapter) extend the standard bottleneck adapter with depth-dimension operations to bridge the gap between 2D natural-image pre-training and volumetric medical data, a design choice distinct from prior 3D-SAM works that modify the backbone more invasively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskSAM demonstrates that a prompt-free, parameter-efficient adaptation of SAM can surpass the long-dominant nnUNet baseline on three major volumetric segmentation benchmarks, suggesting that foundation-model-based approaches are now competitive for clinical multi-organ segmentation. The practical significance is high: eliminating manual prompts and retaining SAM's architecture makes the pipeline more deployable in clinical settings where per-prompt annotation is infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 2D medical imaging, other modalities (ultrasound, pathology), and larger-scale datasets, and whether the modest per-component gains in the ablation (0.1–1.9%) would hold under different training regimes or dataset scales.
