## Problem

Once graphic designs are composited into raster images, the layer structure is lost, preventing designers from re-editing individual elements. Existing image decomposition methods tuned for natural scenes fail on graphic designs because they over-segment photo content embedded in designs or introduce artifacts on flat vector elements, and the task is inherently ill-posed since multiple valid layer granularities exist for the same image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB graphic design image (resized to 512 px on the shorter side) and produces an ordered sequence of RGBA layers (layer 0 = background, layers 1–K = foreground) that can be re-composited via standard alpha blending to reconstruct the input. The number of layers is determined adaptively at inference time by iterating until no significant top-layer alpha remains, with a practical cap of 3 iterations. Transparent layers are excluded from evaluation since neither LayerD nor baselines focus on accurate transparency estimation.

## Method

LayerD formulates decomposition as iterative top-layer matting and background completion: a BiRefNet (Swin-L backbone) matting model extracts all unoccluded foreground layers simultaneously, LaMa inpaints the revealed background, and foreground RGB is recovered via inverse alpha blending rather than naive mask replacement. A key differentiator is palette-based refinement that exploits the domain prior that graphic designs contain flat, texture-less regions—surrounding color gradients identify flat areas, and dominant palette colors in Lab space snap completed pixels to eliminate inpainting artifacts on both background and foreground. The matting model is trained on Crello with a composite BCE + IoU + SSIM loss, and training data is augmented with inpainting artifacts to close the train-inference gap. For evaluation, the paper introduces a DTW-based layer alignment metric with a layer-merge edit operation to handle the ill-posed nature of ground-truth granularity.

## Results

LayerD is evaluated on the Crello test set (1,971 samples) using RGB L1 distance and Alpha IoU as visual quality metrics, reported across increasing maximum allowed layer-merge edits (0–5). LayerD achieves the best performance in all settings—both with and without text layers—requiring fewer edits to reach comparable visual quality than the YOLO and VLM baselines. Notably, LayerD alone outperforms LayerD + Hi-SAM (which replaces the first iteration with a dedicated text segmenter), and training with text layers yields slightly better results even when evaluating on text-free designs, suggesting text acts as a useful proxy for vector-shape decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LayerD against two baselines: a YOLO-based pipeline (fine-tuned YOLOv11 for detection + SAM2 for segmentation + LaMa inpainting) and a VLM-based pipeline following Accordion [5] (PaliGemma2 for decomposition planning + Hi-SAM for text + SAM2 for other elements + LaMa inpainting). LayerD outperforms both baselines on all metrics (RGB L1 and Alpha IoU) across all edit budgets, in both the all-layers and no-text-layers settings. The VLM baseline struggles when bounding-box detection fails or when boxes overlap (e.g., donut-shaped elements whose bounding boxes encompass the entire image), while the YOLO baseline suffers from false negatives leading to incomplete decomposition. LayerD's unified iterative matting approach avoids these failure modes by directly extracting layers without relying on bounding boxes. Additionally, LayerD outperforms the untrained BiRefNet backbone, confirming the importance of domain-specific training on top-layer matting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerD departs from the dominant stacked-pipeline paradigm for image decomposition (detection → segmentation → ordering → inpainting, as in MULAN or Accordion) by unifying these subtasks into a single iterative top-layer matting objective, reducing error accumulation. Architecturally, it builds directly on BiRefNet for the matting backbone and LaMa for background completion, but the key conceptual shift is treating decomposition as repeated trimap-free matting on progressively revealed backgrounds rather than a one-shot multi-stage process. The palette-based refinement step is a domain-specific departure, exploiting the flat-color prior of graphic design in a way that prior natural-image decomposition methods do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LayerD makes raster graphic designs re-editable at the layer level, which is a practical bottleneck in creative workflows where designers lose layer information upon export. The DTW-based evaluation protocol with layer-merge edits is a useful contribution for an inherently ill-posed task where ground-truth granularity is inconsistent even among humans. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond template-based Crello designs to more complex or photorealistic compositions, and whether the decomposed layers can serve as a reliable preprocessing step for vectorization or layered generative models as the authors suggest.
