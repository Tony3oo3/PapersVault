## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstruction methods from motion-blurred images (e.g., Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, BAD-Gaussians) predict camera trajectories using simple spline interpolations or piecewise functions that do not enforce temporal continuity of camera motion. Since motion blur physically arises from continuous integration of scene radiance over the camera's actual movement during exposure, these discontinuous trajectory models produce inaccurate blur approximations and fail to capture complex, smoothly varying camera motions, resulting in suboptimal deblurring and reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252291/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes camera motion-blurred RGB images, their calibrated camera poses (assumed to correspond to the midpoint of the exposure interval), and a sparse point cloud from COLMAP/SfM as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that can render sharp novel views at real-time speed, along with a continuous camera motion trajectory estimated per image. During training, N rendered images along the estimated trajectory are combined via a pixel-wise weighted sum (computed by a shallow CNN with softmax) and blended with the sharp render at the calibrated pose using a per-pixel scalar mask to form the final predicted blurry image for supervision.

## Method

CoMoGaussian models the continuous camera trajectory within the exposure time using neural ODEs in a latent space, solved with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator. The trajectory is composed of two per-timestep transformations: (1) a rigid body transformation parameterized by a screw axis (unit rotation axis ω̂, rotation angle θ, and translation v), where ω̂ and θ are decoded independently to avoid the coupling present in prior work, and (2) a Continuous Motion Refinement (CMR) transformation that learns an unconstrained rotation offset (initialized near identity) and translation to compensate for the inherent discreteness of numerical integration. Both transformations are driven by separate neural ODEs with per-image latent states initialized from embedded image indices. The rendered N images are aggregated via a pixel-wise weighted sum and a sparsity-regularized scalar mask, and the full 3DGS scene is optimized with Mip-Splatting as the rasterization backbone using an L1 + D-SSIM loss plus orthogonality and mask-sparsity regularizers.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF synthetic dataset, CoMoGaussian achieves 31.02 PSNR, 0.9167 SSIM, and 0.0492 LPIPS, outperforming the best prior method (DP-NeRF) by 1.79 dB in PSNR and 0.049 in SSIM. On the Deblur-NeRF real-world dataset, it reaches 27.85 PSNR / 0.8431 SSIM / 0.0822 LPIPS, surpassing BAGS (26.70 / 0.8237 / 0.0956) by 1.15 dB in PSNR. On the challenging ExbluRF extreme-blur dataset, CoMoGaussian attains 30.15 PSNR and 0.7559 SSIM, exceeding the next-best Deblurring 3DGS (27.36 / 0.6795) by 2.79 dB in PSNR. On the sharp-image NeRF-LLFF dataset, CoMoGaussian (27.56 PSNR) is within 0.15 dB of Mip-Splatting (27.71), confirming generalization beyond blurred inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMoGaussian outperforms all eight baselines on every blur dataset across all three metrics. Against the strongest ray-tracing baseline DP-NeRF, it gains 1.79 dB PSNR on synthetic and 1.94 dB on real-world Deblur-NeRF scenes, while also halving LPIPS (0.0492 vs. 0.1184 synthetic). Among rasterization-based methods, it beats Deblurring 3DGS by 2.79 dB PSNR on ExbluRF (30.15 vs. 27.36) and BAGS by 1.15 dB on Deblur-NeRF real-world. BAD-Gaussians, despite competitive LPIPS on ExbluRF (0.3221 vs. 0.3107), lags significantly in PSNR (26.83 vs. 30.15) and SSIM (0.6625 vs. 0.7559), indicating poorly optimized camera poses. On the sharp-image NeRF-LLFF generalization test, CoMoGaussian (27.56 PSNR) is within 0.15 dB of Mip-Splatting (27.71) and substantially ahead of Deblurring 3DGS (17.42) and BAGS (26.35), confirming that the method does not overfit to blur-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMoGaussian extends the 3D scene deblurring line initiated by Deblur-NeRF and advanced by DP-NeRF, BAD-NeRF, and BAD-Gaussians, all of which model camera motion during exposure via discrete interpolation (spline-based or endpoint-based) in physical 3D space. The key departure is the use of neural ODEs to model camera trajectories as continuous functions in a learned latent space, combined with a novel CMR transformation that compensates for the inherent discretization error when approximating continuous integration with a finite number of rendered frames. The rendering backbone is Mip-Splatting, placing the method firmly in the 3DGS family rather than the ray-tracing NeRF family that dominated earlier deblurring work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Camera motion blur is a pervasive practical limitation in 3D reconstruction, and this paper demonstrates that physically faithful continuous trajectory modeling yields substantially better deblurring than the discrete interpolation approaches that have been standard. The ablation showing neural ODEs consistently outperforming MLP and GRU alternatives in latent space provides a useful empirical guideline for future continuous-motion modeling tasks. An open question the authors identify is adaptive selection of the number of warped poses based on blur severity, which could improve efficiency and reduce overfitting on mild-blur scenes. The method's generalization to sharp images (NeRF-LLFF) suggests the continuous trajectory machinery does not introduce unnecessary inductive bias.
