## Problem

Existing PBR texture generation methods suffer from two critical failures: illumination from reference images gets incorrectly baked into albedo outputs, and slight camera pose perturbations produce dramatically different material results. Additionally, albedo and metallic-roughness maps generated by separate or shared channels are frequently misaligned, causing visible artifacts and shadows on textured meshes. No prior method simultaneously addresses illumination invariance, multi-view consistency, and cross-channel material alignment in a single end-to-end framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (encoded as normal and position maps, concatenated channel-wise with noise latent as U-Net input) and a single reference image as prompt. It produces multi-view PBR textures at 512×512 resolution: an RGB albedo map and a combined metallic-roughness (MR) map, both free from lighting information and spatially aligned with each other. The reference image is consumed through a dedicated reference attention branch rather than being directly cross-attended into the MR channel, which avoids a distributional mismatch between RGB and material latents.

## Method

MaterialMVP extends a Stable Diffusion 2.1 (ZSNR) U-Net into a multi-view PBR diffusion model with a Dual-Channel architecture that generates albedo and MR maps in separate channels. A Multi-Channel Aligned Attention (MCAA) module synchronizes the two channels by feeding albedo attention outputs into the MR channel via residual connections, while the MR channel deliberately discards direct reference-image conditioning to avoid the RGB-to-material distributional gap. Consistency-Regularized Training conditions the model on pairs of reference images with subtle camera pose and lighting perturbations, enforcing identical denoised outputs through an L2 consistency loss (weighted λ=0.1) that disentangles illumination from material properties. Learnable 16×1024 material embeddings are injected per channel via cross-attention to capture the distinct statistical characteristics of albedo versus MR textures. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in one stage on 70,000 Objaverse/Objaverse-XL assets, avoiding the error accumulation inherent in two-stage approaches that first generate RGB views and then estimate PBR properties.

## Results

On a 176-object subset of Objaverse, MaterialMVP achieves the best scores across all five metrics: CLIP-FID 24.78, FID 168.5, CMMD 2.191, CLIP-I 0.9207, and LPIPS 0.1211. Compared to the strongest image-conditioned baseline (Paint3D), it improves CLIP-FID by 2.08 points, FID by 8.4, CMMD by 0.209, CLIP-I by 0.0336, and LPIPS by 0.005. Against the best text-conditioned method (SyncMVD), the gains are even larger: CLIP-FID drops from 29.93 to 24.78 and FID from 189.2 to 168.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaterialMVP outperforms all five quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest image-conditioned competitor, Paint3D (image mode), it reduces CLIP-FID from 26.86 to 24.78 and FID from 176.9 to 168.5, while also improving semantic alignment (CLIP-I 0.9207 vs. 0.8871) and perceptual similarity (LPIPS 0.1211 vs. 0.1261). TexGen, which uses both text and image prompts, is surpassed on all five metrics, with the largest gap in CMMD (2.191 vs. 2.447), indicating richer texture detail. Among text-only methods, SyncMVD is the strongest baseline (FID 189.2), yet MaterialMVP still leads by 20.7 FID points. SuperMat [16] is compared qualitatively in the ablation study (Fig. 8a) as a two-stage baseline; MaterialMVP's one-stage approach avoids the accumulated errors that cause SuperMat to misestimate materials (e.g., rendering glass or metal with a plastic-like appearance). Overall, the paper demonstrates consistent wins across both text-conditioned and image-conditioned baselines, with the most pronounced advantages in illumination invariance and multi-view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaterialMVP extends the multi-view latent diffusion framework (building on LDM and ImageDream-style multi-view attention) from RGB image synthesis to PBR material generation, introducing a dual-channel architecture that jointly produces albedo and metallic-roughness maps. It departs from optimization-based PBR methods (Text2Tex, Paint-it, Texturedreamer) by being a one-stage end-to-end model, and from single-view PBR generators (SuperMat, IntrinsicAnything) by operating across multiple views simultaneously. The closest prior work, CLAY, also generates multi-view PBR textures with image prompts via IP-Adapter, but MaterialMVP differentiates itself through Consistency-Regularized Training for illumination invariance and Multi-Channel Aligned Attention to resolve the distributional mismatch between reference images and MR textures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D asset pipelines: generating physically accurate, illumination-invariant PBR textures from a single reference image without expensive per-shape optimization. The consistency-regularized training strategy and MCAA module offer reusable techniques for any multi-channel diffusion task where channels have different statistical properties. Limitations include dependence on a pre-existing 3D mesh, training cost (~180 GPU days on 70K Objaverse assets), and evaluation limited to a 176-object subset; open questions include generalization to transparent or anisotropic materials, real-time inference, and whether the approach scales to scene-level texture generation.
