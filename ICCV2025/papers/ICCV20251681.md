## Problem

Targeted adversarial attacks suffer from significantly lower transferability than non-targeted attacks due to the substantial shift in decision boundaries required to redirect predictions to a specific class. Existing competition-based and transformation-based methods have not adequately addressed this gap, leaving targeted AEs with poor cross-model success rates that limit their practical utility in scenarios like CAPTCHA-based verification where the output class must be controlled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251681/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (224×224 from ILSVRC2012 validation set), a randomly selected target class label (from the 999 non-true classes), and n=3 randomly drawn target-class samples as auxiliary inputs. The perturbation is constrained to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε=16, and the output is a single targeted adversarial example crafted over T=100 iterations with step size α=1.6. The siamese framework simultaneously optimizes both the targeted AE (on the source image) and non-targeted AEs (on the target-class samples) within the same loop.

## Method

ITDS combines two mechanisms within a siamese-network-like optimization loop. First, Inverse Target Gradient Competition (ITC) generates non-targeted AEs from target-class samples using Logit loss, then injects the raw (unnormalized, non-momentum) inverse target gradients as competitive perturbations into the targeted AE's gradient computation at each step, with a competition ratio β=1.5 and scaling levels m=5. Second, Spatial Distance Stretching (SDS) replaces the standard CE/Logit objective with a top-k distance loss that either maximizes the gap between the target logit and the mean of the top-k non-target logits (S1) or normalizes this gap by the gradient norm to emulate a point-to-hyperplane distance (S2, inspired by DeepFool). Two key design choices distinguish the optimization: omitting N-norm normalization to allow more aggressive gradient exploration, and using raw inverse target gradients (rather than their momentum-accumulated versions) as the competitive signal to preserve feature integrity.

## Results

On ImageNet (ILSVRC2012 validation, 2,000 images, ε=16, 100 steps), ITDS-DIM achieves average black-box targeted attack success rates (BAvg) of 61.7% (RN50 surrogate), 20.9% (VGG16), 36.0% (MN-v3), and 63.2% (RegN), surpassing the strongest competition-based baseline CFM-DIM by 15.7%, 13.7%, 9.5%, and 15.3% respectively. The maximum average black-box TASR gap over CFM-DIM reaches 16.1% on CNNs (RN50) and 21.4% on ViTs (RegN). Against the strongest transformation-based baseline SIA, ITDS-DIM gains 15.9% and 21.9% on MN-v3 and RegN surrogates. Against defenses, ITDS-DIM achieves 91.3% TASR under Score-Opt (vs. 51.5% for CFM-DIM) and 50.2% against the ℓ∞-4 adversarially trained model (vs. 25.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITDS-DIM outperforms all six baselines across every surrogate model. Against the strongest competition-based method CFM-DIM, ITDS-DIM gains 15.7% BAvg on RN50 (61.7% vs. 46.0%), 13.7% on VGG16 (20.9% vs. 7.2%), 9.5% on MN-v3 (36.0% vs. 26.5%), and 15.3% on RegN (63.2% vs. 47.9%). Against the strongest transformation-based method SIA, ITDS-DIM leads by 6.0% on RN50 (61.7% vs. 55.7%), 1.6% on VGG16 (20.9% vs. 19.3%), 15.9% on MN-v3 (36.0% vs. 20.1%), and 21.9% on RegN (63.2% vs. 41.3%). Admix-DIM and BSR are substantially weaker, with BAvg values at most 34.3% and 51.4% respectively, well below ITDS-DIM. ODIM and DIM are the weakest baselines, with BAvg values ranging from 5.2% to 35.3%, confirming that simple input-diversity transformations alone are insufficient for targeted attack transferability. The ViT-specific gap is particularly pronounced: on RegN, ITDS-DIM reaches 56.5% on ViT vs. 22.5% for CFM-DIM and 19.7% for SIA, a 21.4% advantage over the best competition-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the competition-based adversarial attack paradigm (Admix, CFM) by reframing the competitive force as the inverse gradient of target-class samples rather than random feature mixup or adversarial/friendly perturbations. It introduces a siamese-network-like architecture that jointly optimizes targeted and non-targeted AEs, a structural departure from prior methods that handle only one attack type per optimization loop. The SDS loss, inspired by DeepFool's point-to-hyperplane distance, provides a principled top-k margin objective that complements the ITC mechanism, positioning the work at the intersection of competition-based and geometric-optimization approaches to transferable targeted attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Targeted adversarial attacks have historically lagged far behind non-targeted attacks in transferability, limiting their practical utility in scenarios like reCAPTCHA-style verification where the output class must be controlled. The 16–21% absolute gains over SOTA on both CNNs and ViTs, combined with strong defense-breaking (99.4% on JPEG, 91.3% on Score-OPT), make targeted black-box attacks substantially more viable. Open questions include whether the siamese framework and inverse-gradient competition generalize to non-ImageNet domains or to attacks against ensembles, and whether the computational overhead of maintaining multiple target-class samples and scaling levels limits real-time deployment.
