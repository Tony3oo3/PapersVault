## Problem

Existing VLN methods assume panoramic (360°) observations, but practical robots are equipped with monocular RGB-D cameras whose limited field of view creates intrinsic information incompleteness. Prior monocular VLN approaches (3DFF, g3D-LF) reconstruct 3D feature fields to render panoramic views but ignore that rendered views contain large unobserved (black) regions, which prevents visual encoders from extracting comprehensive features and leads to suboptimal navigation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a monocular RGB image (90° FOV on R2R-CE, 63° on RxR-CE), a corresponding depth image, a natural language instruction, and its 4D pose (x, y, z, θ) at each step. It outputs a discrete action from {turn left 15°, turn right 15°, move forward 0.25 m, stop}, with success defined as stopping within 3 m of the target. Internally, the framework produces a 192×192 BEV feature map (5 cm per pixel) for waypoint prediction and 12 panoramic feature maps (30° apart) for topological mapping; the UAP strategy may insert additional rotation-only steps before committing to a move.

## Method

monoVLN incrementally builds a training-free 3DGS feature field by extracting MobileSAM features from each monocular RGB frame, back-projecting via depth, and transferring point attributes directly to Gaussians (fixed 2.5 cm scale, opacity 1, no rotation). Two complementary modules address information incompleteness: (1) an Implicit Partial Completion (IPC) module trained in an MAE-style paradigm where a transformer view encoder takes partially rendered feature maps (with randomly masked tokens) and a decoder reconstructs the full map, followed by CLIP-alignment fine-tuning to match the planner's expected feature space; and (2) an Uncertainty-aware Active Perception (UAP) strategy that, when the planner's class-normalized entropy exceeds a threshold and the predicted direction is novel (outside ±30° of previously viewed sectors), inserts a rotation step toward the predicted node to acquire new visual evidence before replanning. A BEV-based waypoint predictor (two U-Nets: one for inpainting, one for heatmap prediction with NMS) replaces panoramic-depth-based predictors, and the overall planner follows the PRET topological-map architecture trained with cross-entropy on next-node labels.

## Results

On R2R-CE (Val Unseen), monoVLN achieves 54.8% SR, 62.4% OSR, 44.4% SPL, and 4.61m NE, outperforming the best prior monocular method g3D-LF (47.2% SR) by 7.6% and 3DFF (44.9% SR) by 9.9%. On Test Unseen, it reaches 53.6% SR and 44.9% SPL. On RxR-CE (Val Unseen), monoVLN achieves 31.8% SR and 26.8% SPL, improving over 3DFF (25.5% SR) by 6.3%. The gap to the best panoramic methods (BEVBert/PRET at ~59% SR on R2R-CE) is narrowed from 12% to 4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

monoVLN outperforms all monocular baselines on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE. On R2R-CE Val Unseen, it surpasses g3D-LF [46] by 7.6% SR (54.8 vs 47.2) and 3DFF [50] by 9.9% SR (54.8 vs 44.9), while also improving SPL by ~10 points over both. Against older monocular methods (CM2, WS-MGMap, NaVid), the gains are even larger (15–20% SR). On RxR-CE, monoVLN leads 3DFF by 6.3% SR (31.8 vs 25.5) and 8.7% SPL (26.8 vs 18.1). Compared to panoramic methods (BEVBert, PRET, ETPNav), monoVLN narrows the SR gap from ~12% to ~4% on R2R-CE, though it still trails by 4–5% SR. The paper attributes the remaining gap to the inherent information loss from monocular FOV, which its IPC and UAP modules partially but not fully resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the monocular VLN line initiated by 3DFF (NeRF-based feature fields) and g3D-LF (improved feature field quality) by replacing NeRF with training-free 3D Gaussian Splatting for feature field construction, and by explicitly addressing the information incompleteness that prior monocular methods overlooked. The Implicit Partial Completion module repurposes the MAE masked-reconstruction paradigm from image classification to infer missing regions in rendered panoramic feature maps, while the Uncertainty-aware Active Perception strategy draws on the agent's subjective perspective to selectively acquire observations. The overall navigation pipeline builds directly on the PRET panoramic planner, transferring it to the monocular setting via the 3DGS-rendered features and a novel BEV-based waypoint predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the monocular-to-panoramic success rate gap from 12% to 4% on R2R-CE, this work demonstrates that the observation modality gap in VLN is largely an information-completeness problem rather than a fundamental architectural one, opening the door to deploying powerful panoramic planners on practical monocular-camera robots. The finding that a full 360° rotation at every step underperforms selective uncertainty-triggered observation suggests that current panoramic agents may also benefit from eliminating redundant views, pointing to a broader efficiency principle. However, the acknowledged domain gap between simulator training and real-world deployment (noted on Turtlebot4) remains a significant open question for practical adoption, and the method's reliance on MobileSAM features and a specific planner backbone limits immediate generalizability.
