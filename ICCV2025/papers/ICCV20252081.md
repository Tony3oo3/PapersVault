## Problem

Existing fMRI decoding methods are largely confined to reconstructing visual stimuli or generating captions, without extending to creative content generation such as predicting future actions or producing videos. Moreover, cross-subject decoding remains difficult because prior approaches rely on a single 'expert' model operating in latent space, making it hard to simultaneously capture shared neural representations and preserve individual subject-specific differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252081/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI voxel signals (1×N dimensional per trial) and optional text interaction prompts as input, and produces reconstructed images, text captions, answers to interaction questions, and creation videos that predict the next action in a scene. The creation task specifically extracts information from higher visual cortex ROIs (visual working memory) to generate action-prediction prompts, which are then fed into a diffusion-based video generator. fMRI is first adapted via BEA into a shared embedding space before being projected into the LLM's textual representation space.

## Method

NeuroCreat comprises three stages: (1) Brain Expert Adaption (BEA), a Mixture-of-Experts module where a shared expert learns cross-subject commonalities and routed experts capture individual neural differences, trained with a CLIP-style contrastive loss and a MixCo voxel-mixing alignment loss; (2) Prompt Variant Alignment, which decomposes the decoding prompt into fMRI (P^F), text (P^T), and creation (P^C) components, extracting creation information from visual working memory ROIs in higher visual cortex and aligning them via KL-divergence and cross-entropy losses; (3) multimodal task execution using LLaMA 3-8B (fine-tuned with LLaMA Adapter) to integrate fMRI-visual-semantic features, and SDXL-Turbo with IP-Adapter for high-fidelity image reconstruction and video generation. The key insight is that separating prompt variants at fine-grained levels and routing subject-specific information through dedicated experts enables both accurate reconstruction and creative extrapolation from a single unified model.

## Results

On the NSD dataset, NeuroCreat (cross-subject) achieves SSIM 0.448, CLIP 97.80%, AlexNet-5 98.90%, and fMRI/image retrieval of 98.80%/98.90%, setting new SOTA across nearly all reconstruction and retrieval metrics. On the GOD dataset, NeuroCreat reaches SSIM 0.501, CLIP 87.30%, and image retrieval 90.20%, substantially outperforming the previous best (CMVDM: SSIM 0.455, CLIP 82.10%). For the novel creation video task, NeuroCreat achieves FVD 49.08, DOVER 0.840, and GenVideo-RefVideo CLIP 0.819 on NSD, significantly surpassing the UMBRAE+AIGC cascade baseline (FVD 58.77, DOVER 0.664, CLIP 0.647).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NSD, NeuroCreat outperforms the strongest reconstruction baselines MindEye2 (SSIM 0.431, CLIP 93.00%) and PSYCHOMETRY (SSIM 0.340, CLIP 96.80%) by a clear margin on SSIM (+0.017 over MindEye2) and CLIP (+4.8 pp over MindEye2, +1.0 pp over PSYCHOMETRY). Neuro-V achieves the highest Inception score (96.80%) but trails NeuroCreat on SSIM (0.357 vs 0.448) and CLIP (97.50% vs 97.80%). On GOD, NeuroCreat dominates all baselines, improving SSIM by +0.046 over CMVDM and CLIP by +5.2 pp, with retrieval accuracy jumping from 75.40% (CMVDM) to 90.20%. For captioning (Table 3), NeuroCreat leads all fMRI-based methods (B@1 60.01, CIDEr 69.93, CLIP-S 69.31) but remains below the BLIP-2 upper bound (B@1 73.79, CIDEr 98.23) that uses direct image input. On the creation video task, NeuroCreat substantially outperforms the UMBRAE+AIGC cascade on all quality metrics (FVD 49.08 vs 58.77, DOVER 0.840 vs 0.664, GenVideo CLIP 0.997 vs 0.788), demonstrating that end-to-end brain-guided generation surpasses the two-stage reconstruction-then-animate pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroCreat extends the fMRI-to-image reconstruction line (MindEye, MindEye2, DREAM, PSYCHOMETRY) and the brain-based LLM decoding line (BrainCap, OneLLM, UMBRAE, Neuro-V) by introducing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture (shared + routed experts) for cross-subject adaptation, departing from prior methods that rely on latent-space alignment or single-expert models. It also repurposes the visual working memory concept from cognitive neuroscience as a computational signal for a new 'creation' task (action prediction and video generation), a framing not present in prior brain decoding work. The Prompt Variant Alignment module draws on CLIP-style contrastive learning and InstructBLIP-style instruction tuning but applies them at the fMRI-visual-semantic interface in a way that prior brain decoding papers have not attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper pushes brain decoding beyond passive reconstruction toward active creative generation, which could open new avenues for brain-computer interfaces that go beyond reading out perceived content. However, the evidence for the creation task is thin: only one cascade baseline (UMBRAE + Animate Anyone) is compared, and the creation results on GOD show inflated CLIP scores (>1.0) that raise questions about metric validity. Open questions include whether the VWM-based creation signal generalizes beyond the two small datasets used, whether the MoE cross-subject approach scales to larger subject pools, and whether the 'creation' output is genuinely grounded in neural signals or largely driven by the LLM's prior knowledge of common object actions.
