## Problem

Standard Multi-Head Attention in Transformer-based image restoration assigns all heads uniform-size subspaces containing similar information, causing heads to redundantly focus on the same spatial regions while neglecting degraded areas. This redundancy limits the model's capacity to capture diverse contextual features needed for high-quality restoration across multiple degradation types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251713/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HINT takes a degraded RGB image of arbitrary spatial resolution (H×W×3) and outputs a restored RGB image via residual learning (restored = input + predicted residual). The model uses an asymmetric encoder-decoder with 4 levels, embedding dimension 48, and 4 attention heads with a dimensional ratio of [1,2,2,3]. Training is supervised with paired degraded/reference images, and the same architecture is applied across five tasks (low-light enhancement, dehazing, desnowing, denoising, deraining) with task-specific training data.

## Method

HINT introduces two mechanisms to replace vanilla MHA. First, Hierarchical Multi-Head Attention (HMHA) reranks channels by Pearson correlation similarity before splitting them into subspaces of increasing size (C₁≤C₂≤…≤Cₕ), so each head operates on a differently-sized, information-independent subspace and learns distinct contextual features. Second, the Query-Key Cache Updating (QKCU) module adds intra-layer and inter-layer interactions: the intra-layer path applies a GELU gating on the sum of a running Q+K cache and the HMHA output, then compresses and rebuilds the feature; the inter-layer path computes scale and shift components from a historical attention-score cache (updated via exponential moving average with α=0.9) to modulate each head's attention map. The overall pipeline follows a 4-level encoder-decoder with asymmetric design (FFN-only encoder blocks, HMHA+FFN decoder blocks), skip connections, and a 4-block refinement stage, trained with AdamW and standard L1+LPIPS losses.

## Results

On LOL-v2 (low-light enhancement), HINT achieves 25.14 dB PSNR and 0.917 SSIM (averaged over real and synthetic subsets), surpassing the previous best MambaLLIE by 0.73 dB. On Snow100K (desnowing), HINT reaches 34.14 dB PSNR, a 1.64 dB gain over AST. On SOTS (dehazing), HINT attains 32.24 dB PSNR and 0.981 SSIM, outperforming all 11 compared methods. On Rain100L (deraining), HINT scores 40.04 dB PSNR and 0.986 SSIM, exceeding FPro by 0.84 dB. On BSD68/Urban100 (denoising), HINT delivers the best PSNR at all noise levels (σ=15/25/50), with an average 0.2 dB gain over Restormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HINT consistently outperforms all 33 compared baselines across all five restoration tasks. In low-light enhancement, HINT surpasses the strongest Transformer-based competitor RetinexFormer by 0.9 dB PSNR and the Mamba-based MambaLLIE by 0.73 dB, while also beating CNN-based methods (MIRNet, Sparse) by over 4 dB. For desnowing on Snow100K, HINT leads the general restoration pipeline AST by 1.64 dB and the dedicated desnowing methods (JSTASR, HDCW, DesnowNet) by 2.6–11 dB. In dehazing on SOTS, HINT exceeds the all-in-one methods (AdaIR, PromptIR, NDR-Restore) by 0.28–0.93 dB and the dehazing-specific DehazeFormer by 0.46 dB. For deraining on Rain100L, HINT achieves a 0.84 dB margin over FPro and 1.14 dB over AdaIR. In denoising, HINT edges out Restormer by ~0.2 dB average and the all-in-one methods (AirNet, PromptIR, NDR-Restore) by at least 0.12 dB across all noise levels on both BSD68 and Urban100.

## Relation to Prior Work

HINT builds directly on the Restormer encoder-decoder architecture and its MDTA attention mechanism, replacing the uniform multi-head subspace split with a hierarchical one (HMHA) that assigns heads to subspaces of varying sizes after a Pearson-correlation-based channel reranking. The QKCU mechanism extends the line of work on dynamically modulating attention scores (Nguyen et al., NeurIPS 2022; Xiao et al., ICML 2024) by introducing both intra-layer gating and inter-layer cache-based scale/shift modulation, moving beyond static projection-based approaches. The bottleneck unification at the deepest level follows Uformer's design, and the overall framing positions HINT as a targeted fix for the head-redundancy problem identified in NLP literature (Voita et al., 2019; Michel et al., 2019) but previously underexplored in restoration Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eigenvalue analysis providing concrete evidence that vanilla MHA concentrates >90% of attention energy in only ~30 eigenvalues (vs. ~200 for HINT) gives the field a useful diagnostic for quantifying head redundancy in restoration models. The hierarchical subspace + reranking design is simple, adds minimal parameters (~16.5%), and generalizes across five restoration tasks without task-specific tuning, making it a practical drop-in replacement for MDTA in existing pipelines. The acknowledged limitation—failure under extremely low-light conditions due to synthetic-to-real domain gap—highlights that architectural improvements alone cannot close the distribution shift, pointing toward the need for larger real-world training data or domain-adaptive strategies.
