## Problem

Existing portrait style transfer methods either fail to generalize across diverse domains (photo, cartoon, sketch, animation) or rely on semantic alignment that breaks down when structural differences between input and reference portraits are large. Diffusion-based style transfer methods largely ignore semantic correspondence, making them unsuitable for portrait tasks that require region-specific stylization (skin, lips, hair, eyes) while preserving identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251710/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 512×512 RGB portrait images — a content (input) portrait and a style (reference) portrait — and produces a single stylized portrait at the same resolution. The content image is consumed through three parallel pathways: Haar DWT high-frequency extraction feeding ControlNet, DDIM inversion for latent initialization, and feature extraction for correspondence. The reference image is warped via learned dense correspondence before being fed to the style adapter (CLIP penultimate-layer features) and DDIM inversion. A fixed text prompt ('a photo of a portrait') also conditions the generation.

## Method

The framework has three core components. First, a semantic adapter (frozen SD U-Net + CLIP encoder) extracts features from both portraits to compute a correlation matrix for dense correspondence, which warps the reference toward the input; this adapter is trained with mask warping loss and cyclic warping consistency loss to enforce semantic alignment. Second, a dual-conditional diffusion model combines ControlNet (fed with Haar DWT high-pass components of the input for structure) and a style adapter (decoupled cross-attention injecting CLIP image features of the warped reference) into the denoising U-Net. Third, an AdaIN-Wavelet latent initialization blends the low-frequency content of the warped reference's DDIM-inverted latent with the high-frequency content of the AdaIN-normalized input latent, balancing color/style transfer against detail preservation. Training is two-stage: the semantic adapter is trained first (500K iterations with noise prediction + mask + cyclic losses), then ControlNet and style adapter are trained jointly (300K iterations) on CelebAMask-HQ only.

## Results

On CelebAMask-HQ, the proposed method achieves the best LPIPS (0.116) and Identity Distance (0.057), significantly outperforming the next-best baselines (Wang et al. [38]: LPIPS 0.181, ID 0.106; Shih et al. [28]: ID 0.093), while its Gram loss (0.274) is comparable to the best baseline (Wang et al. [38]: 0.208). On a mixed multi-domain dataset (CelebAMask-HQ + FFHQ + AAHQ), the method achieves the lowest Gram loss (0.657), LPIPS (0.083), and ID (0.087) across all three metrics, demonstrating strong domain generalizability. Inference time is 6.97 seconds per 512×512 image on an RTX 4090, faster than all compared diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four conventional portrait style transfer methods (Shih et al., Chen et al., Zhou et al., Wang et al.) and four diffusion-based methods (IP-Adapter+ControlNet, Z*, StyleID, InstantStyle+). On CelebAMask-HQ, the proposed method achieves the best LPIPS (0.116 vs. 0.156 for the next-best Chen et al.) and ID (0.057 vs. 0.093 for Shih et al.), but trails Wang et al. [38] on Gram loss (0.274 vs. 0.208), suggesting slightly weaker global style matching on the in-domain dataset. On the mixed multi-domain dataset, the method wins all three metrics: Gram loss 0.657 (vs. 0.723 for InstantStyle+), LPIPS 0.083 (vs. 0.119 for Wang et al.), and ID 0.087 (vs. 0.096 for Wang et al.), confirming its domain-generalization advantage. The diffusion-based baselines (IP-Adapter+ControlNet, Z*, StyleID) suffer substantially worse ID scores (0.222–0.774), indicating they alter facial identity, while the proposed method preserves identity most faithfully. Wang et al. [38] is the closest competitor on in-domain Gram loss but degrades more on the mixed dataset, highlighting the gap in cross-domain robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's U-Net architecture and extends it with two conditioning pathways: a ControlNet fed with Haar wavelet high-frequency components for structural guidance, and an IP-Adapter-style decoupled cross-attention module for style guidance. For correspondence extraction, it departs from the emergent-correspondence approach of Tang et al. (NeurIPS 2023) by introducing a semantic adapter trained with mask-warping and cyclic-warping-consistency losses to enforce region-level alignment. The AdaIN-Wavelet latent initialization is a novel contribution that blends DDIM-inverted warped-reference latents (for color transfer) with input latents (for detail preservation), addressing a tradeoff not explicitly handled in prior diffusion-based style transfer methods like StyleID or Z*.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generalize from 30K real CelebAMask-HQ portraits to cartoon, sketch, and animation domains without domain-specific training is practically valuable for applications like colorizing sketches and modernizing archival photos. The semantic-aware warping ensures style is applied to the correct facial regions (hair-to-hair, lips-to-lips), which prior diffusion-based methods largely ignore. Limitations include the 512×512 resolution constraint, dependence on pre-trained semantic masks during training, and the fact that the approach is still portrait-specific; whether the semantic-correspondence + wavelet-blending strategy transfers to non-portrait or multi-object style transfer remains an open question.
