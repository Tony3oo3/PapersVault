## Problem

Existing clothed avatar generation methods either represent the human body, hair, and clothes as a single entity (preventing component-level editing like cloth transfer), or rely on optimization-based pipelines that take hours per avatar. No prior feed-forward method achieves full component disentanglement while maintaining high generation quality, particularly for the severely occluded inner body layer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252626/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes Gaussian noise as input and generates a 3-layer UV feature plane (body, hair+shoes, clothes) that is decoded into per-component 3D Gaussian primitives. Training uses multi-view RGB images (54 or 158 views) with Sapiens-predicted semantic segmentation masks to derive per-component silhouette masks, eliminating the need for separate per-component meshes. The output avatar supports LBS-based animation to arbitrary poses, novel-view rendering, and direct component transfer between generated samples.

## Method

LayerAvatar introduces a layered UV feature plane where 3D Gaussians initialized on subdivided SMPL-X-based templates are projected into three separate 2D UV layers (innermost body, hair+shoes, top+bottom clothes), each with semantic labels. Two shared lightweight MLP decoders (geometry: position offset and opacity; texture: color, rotation, scale) decode the UV features, with per-Gaussian attributes recovered via bilinear interpolation and residual offsets from template initial values. A single-stage diffusion model with v-prediction is trained jointly with UV feature plane fitting, using reconstruction losses on both individual components and the compositional avatar, plus constraint terms (body silhouette RElU loss, occluded-region skin color matching, semantic segmentation depth-ordering loss) to handle severe body occlusion. The layered structure provides each component an independent feature space, enabling clean disentanglement without identity shifting, while SMPL-X-based templates support shape/pose/expression deformation via LBS and volumetric skinning for exterior components.

## Results

On THuman2.0, LayerAvatar achieves a FID of 12.50 for holistic generation quality, substantially outperforming EVA3D (124.54), StructLDM (25.22), and E3Gen (15.78). For layer-wise generation on Tightcap, it reaches FID 17.37 and L-PSNR >40, compared to HumanLiff's FID 54.39 and L-PSNR 28.57. In a user study against optimization-based methods, LayerAvatar scores 58.14/100 on disentanglement and 77.14/81.43 on component/overall quality preference, while generating each avatar in ~2 seconds versus 1.5–6 hours for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerAvatar outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. Against feed-forward GAN/diffusion methods, it reduces FID from 124.54 (EVA3D) and 25.22 (StructLDM) to 12.50 on THuman2.0, and beats the closest feed-forward baseline E3Gen/LGM [80] (15.78) by ~3.3 FID points. For layer-wise disentanglement on Tightcap, it achieves FID 17.37 and L-PSNR >40, far exceeding Rodin [65] (FID 56.57, L-PSNR 18.12) and HumanLiff [23] (FID 54.39, L-PSNR 28.57), indicating both higher generation quality and more complete component separation without identity shifting. In the user study against optimization-based methods, LayerAvatar dominates on all three axes: disentanglement score (58.14 vs. 13.02 for SO-SMPL [63], 9.77 for LAGA [18], 19.07 for TELA [15]), quality preference (77.14/81.43 vs. 3.57/8.57, 5.00/1.43, 14.29/8.57), and generation time (2 s vs. 5 h, 1.5 h, 6 h respectively). No baseline matches or exceeds LayerAvatar on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D diffusion generation (Single-Stage Diffusion NeRF, E3Gen) and compositional avatar representation (HumanLiff, EVA3D, COAP). It departs from prior feed-forward methods (EVA3D, StructLDM, E3Gen) that treat the clothed avatar as a monolithic entity by introducing a layered UV feature plane where body, hair, shoes, and clothes occupy separate UV layers with semantic labels. It also departs from optimization-based disentanglement methods (SO-SMPL, LAGA, TELA) that require hours per subject by learning a single-stage diffusion model directly on the layered representation, and from HumanLiff's sequential layer-wise generation by performing all components simultaneously in one forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-second generation time (vs. 1.5–6 hours for optimization-based baselines) makes fully disentangled clothed avatar generation practically viable for interactive applications like VR/AR and virtual try-on, where per-subject optimization is prohibitive. The layered UV feature plane is a clean, reusable representation that could inform future work on editable 3D humans. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex multi-layer clothing (e.g., jackets over shirts), whether text or image conditioning can be added for controllable generation, and how well the SMPL-X-based templates handle extreme body shapes or non-standard topologies beyond the training distribution.
