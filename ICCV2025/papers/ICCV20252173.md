## Problem

Existing multi-modal fusion methods apply static frame-based image fusion techniques directly to video, neglecting inherent temporal dependencies and producing flickering, temporally inconsistent results. No prior framework jointly models temporal dynamics, visual fidelity, and semantic accuracy in multi-modal video fusion, leaving a gap between the quality of individual frames and the coherence of the full sequence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded visible RGB video (T×H×W×3) and a degraded infrared video (T×H×W×1) at 640×480 resolution, where degradation is simulated as blur on visible frames and stripe noise on infrared frames. Training uses 10-frame clips while testing uses 30-frame clips; longer sequences are processed via overlapping concatenation to maintain temporal continuity. The output is a fused RGB video (T×H×W×3) that simultaneously recovers weak information lost to degradation and preserves inter-frame coherence.

## Method

TemCoCo replaces all 2D convolutions with 3D separable convolutions to enable spatio-temporal interaction throughout the network. A Visual-Semantic Interaction Module (VSIM) splits low-scale features into parallel semantic and visual branches, each guided by knowledge distillation from Dinov2 and VGG19 respectively, then fuses them via SwinTransformer-based cross-modality attention and a bidirectional semantic-visual attention. A Temporal Cooperative Module (TCM) at medium and high scales uses deformable convolutions to align two preceding frames as conditional references for weak information recovery, unifying video enhancement and fusion in one pipeline. Temporal consistency is enforced by a dedicated loss that constrains the cosine direction of frame-to-frame variations in the fused video to match those of the source videos, complemented by temporal attention within VSIM. The authors also introduce two evaluation metrics—flowD (optical-flow-warping error) and feaCD (ResNet-18 feature-direction consistency)—to quantify temporal coherence at the image and feature levels.

## Results

On the M3SVD dataset (220 sequences), TemCoCo achieves the best scores across all four metrics: MI=2.4219, SD=50.5400, flowD=3.2847, and feaCD=1.8049. On the HDO dataset (26 sequences), it similarly leads with MI=1.3373, flowD=5.1147, and feaCD=1.7336, though MMIF-EMMA edges it out on SD (60.44 vs 59.40). The temporal consistency gains are particularly pronounced: flowD improves by 13–36% over the best baseline, and feaCD improves by 1–11%, confirming the effectiveness of the temporal-enhanced mechanism and temporal loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TemCoCo outperforms all eight baselines on MI (information fidelity) and both temporal metrics (flowD, feaCD) on both M3SVD and HDO. On M3SVD, the closest competitor is MMIF-EMMA (MI=2.3140, flowD=4.3144), which TemCoCo surpasses by 4.7% in MI and 24% in flowD. RCVS, the only other video-specific baseline, achieves the best feaCD among baselines (1.8160) but trails TemCoCo (1.8049) and lags significantly in MI (1.1548 vs 2.4219). On HDO, the temporal consistency advantage is even more dramatic: TemCoCo's flowD of 5.1147 beats the next-best DDFM (6.9643) by 27%, and feaCD of 1.7336 beats CDDFuse (1.9460) by 11%. The sole metric where TemCoCo does not lead is SD on HDO, where MMIF-EMMA scores 60.44 vs TemCoCo's 59.40, a marginal 1.7% gap. Overall, the results confirm that explicit temporal modeling and visual-semantic collaboration yield consistent gains over frame-by-frame fusion methods across both image quality and temporal coherence dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of applying static frame-based fusion methods (CrossFuse, SwinFusion, Diff-IF, CDDFuse) directly to video sequences, which the authors argue neglects inter-frame dependencies. It also moves beyond preliminary video fusion attempts (DFTP, RCVS) that either apply varying per-frame rules or focus on registration without explicit temporal modeling. Conceptually, it repurposes foundation models (Dinov2, VGG19) as distillation teachers for fusion—a use not previously explored in multi-modal fusion—and borrows the deformable-alignment and temporal-attention machinery from video super-resolution (BasicVSR lineage) to enforce frame-to-frame coherence. The unification of degradation recovery (deblurring, destriping) within the fusion pipeline is presented as a first, though the individual degradation tasks are well-studied in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing temporal consistency as an explicit, measurable objective in multi-modal video fusion, this work provides both a design template (3D convolutions + temporal attention + temporal loss) and evaluation tools (flowD, feaCD) that the field lacked. The practical implication is that fused video outputs become more reliable for downstream tasks like video segmentation, where frame-to-frame flicker compounds errors. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with limited degradation types (blur + stripe noise), a very small manually annotated segmentation set (5 sequences × 50 frames), and no real-time or long-video scalability analysis. Open questions include whether the temporal cooperative module generalizes to more complex degradations (rain, fog, motion blur) and whether the foundation-model distillation overhead is justified at deployment scale.
