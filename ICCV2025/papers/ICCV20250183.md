## Problem

Foundational segmentation models like SAM underperform on unconventional domains (camouflage, medical, thermal, industrial), and fine-tuning is impractical due to limited labeled data and computational cost. Existing image-augmentation-based adaptation methods rely almost exclusively on rule-based transforms, capping their effectiveness. When learning-based augmentations (e.g., depth or normal estimation) are added alongside rule-based ones, the two types are heterogeneous—rule-based methods are degree-describable while learning-based methods are not—creating a parameter misalignment problem that destabilizes joint optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target-domain image plus a small set of 5 or 10 labeled samples (image–mask pairs) from that domain. It outputs a domain-specific 3-step augmented policy (a sequence of operations drawn from 32 methods, each optionally paired with a magnitude level 0–9) that is applied to new images before they enter a frozen SAM (ViT-B or ViT-H). The final segmentation mask is produced by SAM on the augmented image; the policy itself is the learned artifact, and a distilled pix2pix generator can replace the multi-model augmentation pipeline at inference time.

## Method

The core mechanism is a PPO-based reinforcement-learning agent that searches a multi-discrete action space of shape {32, 10, 32, 10, 32, 10}, encoding a 3-step augmentation policy where each step selects one of 32 methods (22 rule-based + 10 learning-based) and a magnitude (effective only for rule-based methods, null for learning-based), thereby resolving the parameter misalignment between the two augmentation families. The reward is the mean IoU between SAM's prediction on the augmented image and the ground-truth mask, averaged over the current image plus two additional randomly sampled training images to reduce sensitivity to atypical few-shot samples. After training, the best policy found during validation is distilled into a single pix2pix GAN generator trained on original-to-augmented image pairs, replacing the sequential invocation of multiple learning-based models with one forward pass for efficient deployment.

## Results

The proposed SAM-IA-32 method is evaluated on 9 datasets across 11 experimental setups using mIoU as the metric, with both ViT-B and ViT-H SAM backbones under 5-shot and 10-shot settings. Headline improvements are substantial: on NJU2K (common scenes, ViT-B, 5-shot), mIoU jumps from 41.35 (SAM zero-shot) to 72.61 (+31.26); on CAMO (camouflage, ViT-B, 5-shot), from 37.92 to 62.70 (+24.78); and on Kvasir-SEG (endoscopy, ViT-B, 5-shot), from 62.13 to 70.56 (+8.43). Gains are more modest in industrial and thermal domains (e.g., +1.17 on MTSD, +2.34 on VT1K-T with ViT-B), and the method slightly underperforms SAM-FT on KoletorSDD2 (51.77 vs. 52.09, ViT-B 5-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: SAM zero-shot [Kirillov, ICCV 2023] and SAM-FT (fine-tuned SAM) [Hu, ACM MM 2024]. Against SAM zero-shot, SAM-IA-32 wins in 19 out of 20 dataset-backbone combinations (5-shot), with the largest margins in common scenes (+31.26 on NJU2K, ViT-B) and camouflage (+24.78 on CAMO, ViT-B). Against SAM-FT, the proposed method also dominates in most domains: +27.63 on NJU2K, +24.30 on CAMO, +9.61 on Kvasir (all ViT-B, 5-shot). However, SAM-IA-32 loses to SAM-FT on KoletorSDD2 (51.77 vs. 52.09, ViT-B) and BUSI (56.42 vs. 56.59, ViT-H), and slightly underperforms SAM zero-shot on MTSD with ViT-H (58.21 vs. 59.24). The 10-shot results (Table 2) show similar patterns with slightly larger margins, e.g., NJU2K reaches 80.69 (ViT-H) and CAMO reaches 64.34 (ViT-H), confirming that the augmentation policy generalizes well with a few more labeled samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of augmentation-based adaptation for foundational segmentation models (SAM-Adapter [6], Prompting-to-Adapt [11], Medical SAM Adapter [30]), which have relied almost exclusively on rule-based image transformations. It departs from this lineage by introducing learning-based augmentation methods (depth estimation, normal surface estimation, albedo estimation, deblurring, etc.) into the search space alongside rule-based ones, and by formulating the policy search as a reinforcement learning problem (PPO) over a multi-discrete heterogeneous action space. The parameter-misalignment problem—where rule-based methods accept degree parameters but learning-based methods do not—is addressed through a structured mapping that nullifies magnitude slots for learning-based actions, a design choice not present in prior augmentation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical alternative to fine-tuning SAM for atypical domains (camouflage, medical, industrial, thermal) using only 5–10 labeled samples, preserving the original pre-trained weights and enabling rapid domain switching. However, the distillation step (pix2pix) sometimes degrades performance substantially (e.g., CAMO drops from 65.05 to 56.79 mIoU), and the 3-step policy length limits expressiveness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SAM to other foundational models, whether longer policies or continuous action spaces would help, and whether the RL component provides meaningful gains over well-designed random search given the noisy best-of-N selection observed in Table 6.
