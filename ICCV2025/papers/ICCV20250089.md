## Problem

Existing federated prompt tuning methods for black-box VLMs (e.g., FedBPT) rely on prediction logits for zero-order gradient estimation, exposing them to inversion attacks that can recover model parameters. Additionally, soft-prompt approaches suffer from poor interpretability and high communication overhead in federated settings, while discrete prompt tuning has not been adapted to the federated VLM scenario.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250089/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, variable for VQA) paired with a discrete natural-language prompt, and queries a black-box VLM API (Qwen2-VL-72B, InternVL2-76B, TeleMM, or GPT-4o) that returns only text outputs—no logits or gradients are accessed. The optimization artifact is a short sequence of discrete vocabulary tokens (150 parameters total) that, when prepended to the image/question, steers the VLM toward correct class labels or answers. For VQA, predictions are constrained to the available label set and scored by accuracy.

## Method

FDPT operates in two federated stages. On the client side, an LLM-driven agent iteratively mutates discrete prompt tokens: it generates mutants, evaluates them against the local dataset via the VLM's text output, maintains a priority queue, and uses a difference-matrix-based importance estimator (S = M⁻¹V) to target high-impact token positions for further mutation, guided by chain-of-thought and feedback mechanisms. On the server side, a semantic-similarity-guided evolutionary computation aggregates client prompts: each token is embedded via a pre-trained RoBERTa encoder, tokens are clustered by embedding distance, and within each cluster a representative is elected by a 'reputation' score (sum of cosine-similarity weights to all other tokens), mimicking a majority-rule voting process. Single-token clusters are discarded to filter client-specific noise. This design eliminates the need for logits (preventing inversion attacks), reduces communication to 150 discrete tokens versus 614k soft-prompt parameters, and yields interpretable, transferable prompts.

## Results

On the GPT-4o black-box backbone, FDPT achieves 76.1% average accuracy across all eight datasets, outperforming the strongest black-box baseline FedBPT (69.2%) by 6.9 percentage points and the best white-box method FedPrompt (not available for GPT-4o) by a wide margin. On Qwen2-VL-72B, FDPT reaches 72.4% average, surpassing FedBPT (64.3%) by 8.1 points and even exceeding the white-box FedPrompt (74.1%) on several individual datasets. In VQA, FDPT gains 11.1% and 7.4% over FedBPT on the Art and Chemistry categories of MMMU under GPT-4o. Communication overhead is reduced to 150 parameters versus 40.8k–1796k for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDPT consistently outperforms all black-box baselines across every backbone and dataset. Against FedBPT (the closest black-box competitor), FDPT gains 6.9 points in average accuracy under GPT-4o (76.1% vs. 69.2%), 8.1 points under Qwen2-VL-72B (72.4% vs. 64.3%), and 6.4 points under TeleMM (68.9% vs. 62.5%). Fed-BPTVLM and FedAvg-BBT trail FedBPT by 0.7–2.2 points on average, and FDPT surpasses them by 7–10 points. Among white-box methods, FedPrompt is the strongest (74.1% avg on Qwen2-VL-72B), and FDPT as a black-box method narrows the gap to just 1.7 points while requiring only 150 parameters versus 614k. On VQA specifically, FDPT's advantage over FedBPT is largest on MMMU-Art (+11.1%) and MMMU-Chemistry (+7.4%) under GPT-4o, indicating that discrete prompt optimization is particularly effective for knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

FDPT extends the federated black-box prompt tuning paradigm established by FedBPT (which uses zero-order optimization on soft prompts for LLMs) and FedDTPT (which introduces discrete prompt tuning for LLMs) to the vision-language model setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing gradient-free numerical search with an LLM-agent-driven token mutation process on the client side, and replacing weighted averaging of soft embeddings with a semantic-similarity-guided evolutionary election on the server side. By operating exclusively on discrete text tokens and VLM text outputs rather than logits or continuous embeddings, FDPT simultaneously addresses model-privacy (inversion attacks) and communication-efficiency concerns that prior federated prompt tuning methods leave unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 150-parameter communication footprint (versus 614k for soft-prompt baselines) makes federated prompt tuning for VLMs practically viable in bandwidth-constrained or multi-party settings, and the near-parity with white-box methods (e.g., 72.4% vs. 74.1% average on Qwen2-VL-72B) suggests discrete prompts are a viable substitute for continuous ones in federated contexts. Open questions include whether the LLM-agent mutation loop scales to larger client populations or longer prompts without prohibitive API costs, whether the RoBERTa-based semantic clustering generalizes to non-English or domain-specific vocabularies, and whether the evolutionary election degrades when client data distributions are highly non-IID beyond the Dirichlet settings tested. The framework also raises the broader question of whether agent-driven optimization can replace zero-order methods in other black-box federated settings beyond prompt tuning.
