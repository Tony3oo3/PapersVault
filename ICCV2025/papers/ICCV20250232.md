## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors are black-box classifiers that cannot provide human-verifiable explanations for their decisions, and they fail to generalize to rapidly evolving generation technologies (e.g., autoregressive models like VAR and FLUX). A second barrier is training data scarcity: no existing dataset offers the instruction-tuning explanations and human-aligned preference pairs needed to fine-tune multimodal large language models for explainable detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250232/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces two outputs: a binary real/fake classification and a free-form natural language explanation. The explanation spans both high-level semantic dimensions (anatomical errors, physical law violations, commonsense inconsistencies, text rendering flaws) and low-level artifacts (texture, hue, edge quality), making it verifiable by human experts. At inference, the final classification is determined by a collaborative decoding step that fuses the MLLM's token logits with the CLIP and NPR visual expert logits using fixed weights (α=1, β=1, γ=0.2).

## Method

AIGI-Holmes augments LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B with an NPR-based ResNet visual expert, then trains it through a three-stage Holmes Pipeline: (1) Visual Expert Pre-training, where CLIP-ViT-L/14 (LoRA r=4) and the first two ResNet layers are fine-tuned for binary classification on Holmes-SFTSet; (2) Supervised Fine-Tuning, where the full MLLM (LoRA rank=128) learns to generate structured explanations on 65K annotated images; and (3) Direct Preference Optimization, where contrastive explanation pairs from Holmes-DPOSet reshape the model's reasoning to align with human judgment. The training data is produced by a Multi-Expert Jury system that cross-evaluates annotations from four open-source MLLMs (Qwen2VL-72B, InternVL2-76B, InternVL2.5-78B, Pixtral-124B) and applies expert-guided filtering. At inference, Collaborative Decoding linearly combines the MLLM's 'real'/'fake' token logits with the CLIP and NPR expert classification logits, preventing overfitting to known forgery types and improving generalization to unseen generators.

## Results

On the most challenging Protocol-III benchmark (P3) with unseen state-of-the-art generators (Janus, VAR, FLUX, SD3.5, etc.), AIGI-Holmes achieves a mean detection accuracy of 99.2% and mean A.P. of 99.9%, outperforming the previous best methods AIDE (97.0% Acc), RINE (96.2% Acc), and NPR (84.0% Acc) by 2.2%, 3.0%, and 15.2% respectively. On the explanation quality front (Table 3), AIGI-Holmes with DPO achieves a BLEU-1 of 0.622 and a human ELO rating of 11.420, surpassing GPT-4o (BLEU-1 0.433, ELO 10.271) by 0.189 and 1.149 points respectively. Under robustness perturbations (JPEG QF=70, Gaussian blur σ=2, resize ×0.5), AIGI-Holmes retains 95.9–99.0% accuracy, far exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIGI-Holmes achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 12 baselines on both detection accuracy and explanation quality. Among detection baselines, the gap is largest against NPR (84.0% → 99.2%, +15.2% Acc) and UnivFD (83.6% → 99.2%, +15.6% Acc), while the closest competitor AIDE (97.0% Acc) is still surpassed by 2.2% in accuracy and 0.2% in A.P. On the autoregressive generator VAR specifically, AIGI-Holmes (99.6%) outperforms the next best LaRE (98.8%) by 0.8% and AIDE (93.6%) by 6.0%. For explanation quality, AIGI-Holmes (w/ DPO) dominates all MLLM baselines: it beats GPT-4o by 0.189 BLEU-1 and 1.149 ELO points, and outperforms the strongest open-source MLLM Pixtral-124B (BLEU-1 0.428) by 0.194. Under robustness perturbations, AIGI-Holmes maintains 95.9–99.0% accuracy versus AIDE's 89.2–92.8% and RINE's 91.1–94.2%, confirming superior resilience to real-world degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA multimodal architecture by integrating an NPR-based low-level visual expert and a three-stage training pipeline (visual expert pre-training, SFT, DPO) specifically for AI-generated image detection. It departs from prior MLLM-based detection work (AntiFakePrompt, Bi-Lora, FakeBench, LOKI) that relied solely on SFT with limited explanation datasets, by introducing a human-aligned preference optimization stage and a collaborative decoding strategy that fuses visual expert logits with MLLM semantic reasoning. The data annotation pipeline (Multi-Expert Jury) builds on the MLLM-as-a-judge paradigm but applies it to AIGI forensics with expert-guided defect filtering, addressing the annotation scarcity that limited prior explanation datasets to only 3K–6K samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Holmes-Set dataset (65K+4K images with explanations and preference pairs) is the first large-scale, explanation-rich resource for AIGI detection, potentially enabling broader research into interpretable synthetic media forensics. The collaborative decoding strategy offers a practical, lightweight mechanism for combining low-level artifact detection with high-level semantic reasoning, which could be adapted to other detection tasks. However, the approach's reliance on a fixed set of 18 training generators and the computational cost of the multi-expert annotation pipeline raise questions about scalability to rapidly evolving generation models (e.g., next-generation autoregressive or video models) and whether the DPO alignment generalizes beyond the specific explanation style of the annotators.
