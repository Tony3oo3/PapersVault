## Problem

Diffusion models struggle with HDR image generation due to two fundamental barriers: pretrained SDR auto-encoders irreversibly clip high-bit-depth (16-bit) linear HDR distributions into 8-bit latent spaces, and no large-scale text-HDR datasets exist for supervised training. Existing approaches like GlowGAN are limited to narrow scene categories, low resolutions (256×256), and lack controllability.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts and optionally additional spatial conditions (canny edge maps, depth maps via ControlNet) or a source SDR image for up-conversion. It outputs a decomposed HDR representation consisting of an SDR image and a pixel-aligned three-channel Gain Map, which are combined via a closed-form equation to reconstruct the full HDR image. For practical SDR-to-HDRTV up-conversion, the GM can be stored at 1/4 or 1/16 of native resolution, enabling 4K (3840×2160) processing while training operates at 512×512.

## Method

The core insight is decomposing HDR generation into two VAE-compatible sub-problems: SDR image synthesis (handled by pretrained Stable Diffusion) and Gain Map prediction (handled by an adapted UNet). Training proceeds in two stages: first, a LoRA-fine-tuned VAE learns SDR→GM mapping using an unsupervised degradation pipeline that applies log-based brightness-aware compression (Eq. 2) and BT.2020→BT.709 gamut-constrained reduction with hard clipping to create supervisory signals from MSCOCO; second, a Stable Diffusion UNet is fine-tuned with concatenation-based conditioning (SDR latent concatenated with noisy GM latent along channels) to achieve pixel-aligned GM generation. At inference, two UNets run in parallel during reverse diffusion: the base SD model predicts SDR noise, recovers the clean SDR latent via X₀-prediction, and feeds it into the adapted UNet to predict the corresponding GM noise, yielding a pixel-aligned SDR-GM pair after T timesteps.

## Results

On the HDR image generation task (4,000 landscape samples), the proposed method achieves the best scores across all HDR/WCG metrics: FHLP 0.6318, EHL 0.0900, FWGP 0.0084, and EWG 0.0031, while also attaining the lowest BRISQUE (15.08) and NIQE (3.70) among all methods, indicating superior tone-mapped visual quality. On the SDR-to-HDRTV up-conversion task (HDRTV1K), the method achieves FHLP 0.4441, EHL 0.0840, FWGP 0.0386, and EWG 0.0295, representing up to 3.77× and 6.02× improvements in luminance range extension and color gamut coverage over the strongest baseline (KUNet). The method also obtains the best non-reference SDR quality scores (BRISQUE 45.47, NIQE 5.26) on HDRTV1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines across two tasks. For HDR image generation (Table 1), the proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every HDR/WCG metric; the closest competitor is the SD+KUNet hybrid pipeline (FHLP 0.5764 vs. 0.6318, EHL 0.0575 vs. 0.0900), while GlowGAN alone produces negligible dynamic range (EHL 0.0001) and the SD+GlowGAN hybrid is even worse. For SDR-to-HDRTV up-conversion on HDRTV1K (Table 2), the method dominates all five regression-based baselines (HDRTVNet, FMNet, HDCFM, KUNet, HDRTVDM) on all four HDR/WCG metrics, with the largest margins on EHL (0.0840 vs. 0.0223 for KUNet, a 3.8× gap) and EWG (0.0295 vs. 0.0006 for KUNet, a ~49× gap). The method also achieves the best BRISQUE and NIQE scores on HDRTV1K, though reference-based metrics (PU21-VSI, HDRVDP3) are noted as inapplicable to the unsupervised approach. Notably, GlowGAN is excluded from the HDRTV1K comparison due to its inability to handle 4K-resolution inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (Stable Diffusion) and the double-layer HDR image format (Adobe/Apple/Google gain map specifications) to reformulate HDR generation as a decomposition problem. It departs from prior HDR generation approaches—GlowGAN's unpaired adversarial learning, ExposureDiffusion's multi-exposure denoising, and LEDiff's latent-space fusion—by avoiding the need for HDR-specific VAEs or multi-exposure synthesis entirely. The unsupervised data construction pipeline (brightness-aware compression + gamut-constrained reduction on MSCOCO) is a novel contribution that sidesteps the fundamental data scarcity problem that constrained all prior methods. The integration with ControlNet for conditional generation and the LoRA-based VAE fine-tuning follow established patterns from the broader diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gain map decomposition elegantly resolves the incompatibility between pretrained SDR autoencoders and high-bit-depth HDR images, making the full ecosystem of pretrained diffusion models directly applicable to HDR generation without retraining the VAE. The unsupervised triplet construction from MSCOCO is a practical solution to the HDR data scarcity problem, though its quality ceiling is bounded by the SDR source images. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video HDR generation, whether the 512×512 resolution limit can be lifted without degrading gain map quality, and whether the unsupervised degradation pipeline captures the full diversity of real HDR content (e.g., extreme outdoor scenes beyond MSCOCO's scope). The practical SDR-to-HDRTV up-conversion at 4K resolution demonstrates immediate applicability for content creators and display manufacturers.
