## Problem

Language-assisted Image Clustering (LaIC) leverages textual semantics to improve visual representation discriminability, but the core challenge of filtering positive nouns from unlabeled wild corpus data (e.g., WordNet) has lacked a rigorous theoretical foundation. Existing strategies (SIC, TAC) rely on intuitive CLIP feature-space heuristics without provable error bounds or a unified formalization of when and why positive semantics are separable from negative ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled image set (processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder to obtain unit-norm d-dimensional features) and a large pool of unlabeled wild text nouns from WordNet (formatted with a prompt template like 'A photo of [CLASS]' and encoded by CLIP's text encoder). It produces final image cluster assignments via k-means on concatenated image-text features, along with the intermediate set of candidate positive text semantics selected per cluster. The number of clusters C is adaptively set as N/600 for large-average-cluster datasets or 3K otherwise, and the selection threshold T_k is the β-th smallest gradient score within each cluster's candidate pool.

## Method

GradNorm trains a single-layer linear classifier h(·;W) on CLIP image features using pseudo-labels from k-means, minimizing cross-entropy with a temperature-scaled softmax. It then forwards each wild text feature through this classifier and computes the Frobenius norm squared of the gradient of the cross-entropy loss with respect to W, yielding a per-text score S(t̃_i) = τ²(Σ_k π̃_ik² + 1 − 2 max_j π̃_ij). Texts whose score falls below an adaptive per-cluster threshold are retained as candidate positive semantics, and Theorem 1 provides a rigorous upper bound on the false-negative rate proportional to the optimal expected risk. The paper further proves that TAC's maximum-softmax-probability score and SIC's cosine-similarity score are degenerate special cases of GradNorm under τ→0 and self-normalization assumptions, respectively. Finally, a text counterpart v_i is constructed per image via an attention-weighted (deep set) aggregation over the selected positive text features, and k-means is applied to the concatenated [e_i; v_i] ∈ ℝ^{2d} for the final clustering.

## Results

On five classic benchmarks, GradNorm achieves 98.3% ACC on STL-10, 91.1% ACC on CIFAR-10, 60.6% ACC on CIFAR-20, 99.4% ACC on ImageNet-10, and 81.2% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs, outperforming the strongest LaIC baseline TAC by up to 6.1% ACC (ImageNet-Dogs) and 7.3% ARI (ImageNet-Dogs). On three challenging datasets with larger cluster counts, GradNorm leads with 50.9% ACC on DTD, 62.7% ACC on UCF-101, and 52.6% ACC on ImageNet-1K, surpassing TAC by an average of 3.2% ACC, 1.7% NMI, and 2.4% ARI across the three. Notably, GradNorm also exceeds the zero-shot CLIP baseline on all five classic datasets, with the largest margin on CIFAR-20 (+2.3% ACC) and ImageNet-Dogs (+8.4% ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GradNorm consistently outperforms TAC (ICML'24), the most recent LaIC method, across all eight datasets, with the largest margins on ImageNet-Dogs (+6.1% ACC, +7.3% ARI) and CIFAR-20 (+4.8% ACC). Against SIC (AAAI'23), GradNorm wins on four of five classic datasets but is slightly behind on CIFAR-10 (91.1% vs. 92.6% ACC; 81.5% vs. 84.4% ARI), where SIC's more sophisticated training strategy with additional trainable parameters provides a small edge. On the three challenging datasets (DTD, UCF-101, ImageNet-1K), GradNorm surpasses both TAC and SIC on all metrics, achieving 5.0% higher ACC and 5.2% higher ARI than TAC on DTD. Compared to pure deep clustering baselines (TCC, RPSC, SPICE, etc.), GradNorm substantially outperforms them on all datasets, confirming the benefit of language-assisted supervision. Interestingly, on ImageNet-1K the zero-shot CLIP baseline (63.6% ACC) still exceeds GradNorm (52.6% ACC), suggesting that with 1000 fine-grained classes the gradient-based filtering may struggle to select sufficiently discriminative nouns from WordNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

GradNorm builds directly on TAC [32] for the post-hoc text-image collaboration step (deep set representation) and on SeCu [48] for the stable classifier training objective, but departs fundamentally in the filtering stage: where TAC uses maximum softmax probability (MSP) and SIC uses cosine similarity to select positive nouns from WordNet, GradNorm introduces a gradient-norm-based score with a provable error bound. The paper's key conceptual move is showing that both MSP and cosine-similarity filtering emerge as degenerate special cases (τ→0) of the gradient-norm score, thereby unifying prior ad-hoc strategies under a single theoretical framework grounded in the Huber contamination model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a rigorous error bound quantifying the separability of truly positive semantics in Language-assisted Image Clustering, filling a gap that prior empirical-only approaches (SIC, TAC) left open. The unification result—showing that two apparently distinct filtering heuristics are limiting cases of one principled score—gives practitioners a clearer understanding of when and why those heuristics work or fail (e.g., the overconfidence vulnerability of MSP noted in Remark 4). Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantee tightens with larger CLIP backbones in practice, whether the single-layer classifier assumption limits scalability to very fine-grained datasets, and whether the gradient-norm idea transfers to other vision-language pre-training paradigms beyond CLIP-style contrastive models.
