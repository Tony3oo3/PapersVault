## Problem

Training tooth arrangement neural networks requires large quantities of paired pre- and post-orthodontic 3D teeth models, but acquiring such data is labor-intensive (multi-year patient treatments) and constrained by privacy concerns, with only one small public dataset exhibiting uneven malocclusion distributions. Existing 3D shape generation methods target single-object synthesis and cannot handle the multi-instance, anatomically structured nature of teeth models comprising 24–32 tightly integrated segmented teeth with strict morphological consistency requirements between paired models.

## Input / Output

Stage I takes Gaussian noise and produces an aggregated post-orthodontic point cloud of shape [32, 128, 3] (32 teeth × 128 FPS-sampled points × 3D coordinates) plus a validity mask, organized in a 2×2×8 spatial grid following the FDI numbering system. Stage II takes the Stage I output together with an arbitrary style model (a pre-orthodontic point cloud) and produces the corresponding pre-orthodontic point cloud by applying per-tooth rigid transformations (3D translation + 6D rotation), ensuring identical tooth morphology and point correspondence between the pair. The voxel resolution is r=4 with 64-dimensional latent codes per voxel.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage I trains a VQ-VAE with a 3D U-Net backbone (built on PVCNN's point-voxel convolution paradigm) to encode the 2×2×8 tooth grid into discrete latent tokens, then trains a latent diffusion model to learn the distribution of these tokens, enabling diverse post-orthodontic shape generation. Stage II employs a 12-block Transformer (8 attention heads) that receives per-tooth style features (extracted via localized voxel partitioning with mean+standard pooling) as input tokens and global shape features (extracted via whole-model voxel partitioning with max pooling) as cross-attention conditioning, outputting per-tooth transformation parameters (x ∈ R^{K×9}) rather than raw point clouds. This parameter-based approach inherently preserves tooth morphology and point correspondence, while a collision-avoidance loss (12th-order polynomial on inter-tooth distance) prevents overlapping teeth. The spatial grid structure explicitly encodes bilateral symmetry and occlusal relationships, and the separation of style (local) vs. shape (global) information is validated by ablation showing that swapping the voxelization strategies degrades style fidelity.

## Results

On the 3D Dental Model Dataset (720 generated samples), TeethGenerator achieves the best performance across all three metrics: 1-NNA CD of 69.50%, 1-NNA EMD of 71.88%, and Uniqueness (U_CD) of 96.25%. The closest baseline on CD is PVD (84.87%), and on EMD is DPM (74.50%), while on uniqueness DPM leads among baselines at 75.69%. Additionally, when synthetic data is combined with real data to train TANet for tooth arrangement, performance improves monotonically up to 10× the real data volume before converging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeethGenerator outperforms all five general 3D shape generation baselines on every metric. On 1-NNA CD (lower is better), it achieves 69.50%, a 15.4-point improvement over the best baseline PVD (84.87%) and a 28.1-point margin over PointFlow (97.62%). On 1-NNA EMD, it reaches 71.88%, beating DPM (74.50%) by 2.6 points and PVD (78.12%) by 6.2 points. On uniqueness (U_CD, higher is better), TeethGenerator scores 96.25%, substantially exceeding DPM (75.69%), PointFlow (62.22%), LION (52.78%), PVD (49.58%), and DiT-3D (34.03%). The large uniqueness gap indicates that general-purpose 3D generators produce highly repetitive teeth models, whereas the domain-specific VQ-VAE + diffusion pipeline captures the true morphological diversity of real orthodontic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE framework for discrete latent representations and the PVCNN paradigm for point-voxel feature extraction, combining them with latent diffusion to generate 3D teeth point clouds. It departs from general 3D shape generation methods (PointFlow, DPM, LION, PVD, DiT-3D), which target single-object synthesis, by introducing a domain-specific two-stage pipeline: a shape generation stage using VQ-VAE + diffusion in a structured 2×2×8 FDI grid, and a style transfer stage using a Transformer conditioned on per-tooth style features. The approach is conceptually inverse to existing tooth arrangement networks (TANet, TADPM, Tooth Motion Diffusion), which predict post-orthodontic targets from pre-orthodontic inputs, whereas here the post-orthodontic model is generated first and the pre-orthodontic model is synthesized via predicted transformation parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical data bottleneck in digital orthodontics where paired pre/post-orthodontic 3D scans are expensive, privacy-sensitive, and scarce (only one public dataset exists with uneven malocclusion distribution). The demonstration that synthetic data improves downstream tooth arrangement performance (TANet) up to 10× the real data volume before converging provides a practical path to augmenting limited clinical datasets. However, the evaluation is confined to a single 720-sample dataset, and open questions remain about whether synthetic data can fully replace real data, how the method generalizes to other dental applications (implants, prosthetics), and whether the style transfer stage can handle extreme malocclusions not well-represented in training.
