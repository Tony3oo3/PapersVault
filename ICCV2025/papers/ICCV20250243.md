## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction requires thousands of ADAM iterations (up to an hour) to converge, and existing acceleration methods focus on faster rasterizers or fewer Gaussians without addressing the optimizer itself. This paper tackles the gap by replacing the first-order ADAM optimizer with a tailored Levenberg-Marquardt solver that achieves the same quality in far fewer iterations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250243/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense set of posed RGB images and their SfM point cloud as input, and produces optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scaling, opacity, and 3rd-order spherical harmonics) that enable real-time novel view rendering. During the LM stage, images are processed in batches of 25–70 (up to 4 batches per iteration) to control the gradient cache size, which consumes ~53 GB GPU memory on average versus 6–11 GB for ADAM-based baselines. The two-stage pipeline first runs ADAM for 20K iterations (completing densification at 15K), then only 5 LM iterations with 8 PCG sub-iterations each are needed to converge.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing ADAM with a Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer that solves the normal equations (J^T J + λ_reg·diag(J^T J))Δ = −J^T F(x) in a matrix-free fashion via preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) implemented in custom CUDA kernels. The key enabling mechanism is a gradient cache that stores intermediate α-blending quantities (transmittance T_s, ∂c/∂α_s, ∂c/∂c_s) once per pixel using the original per-pixel parallelization, then reuses them across all PCG iterations under a new per-pixel-per-splat parallelization for the Jacobian-vector products (applyJ and applyJT). To handle high-resolution dense captures, images are split into independent batches whose normal-equation solutions are combined via a weighted mean using diag(J_i^T J_i) as weights. The L1+SSIM rendering loss is reformulated as sum-of-squares residuals by taking the square root of each term, preserving the identical objective while enabling the LM formulation. A line search on a 30% image subset determines the step size, and the regularization parameter is adapted per iteration following the CERES solver strategy.

## Results

Across three datasets (MipNeRF360, Tanks&Temples, Deep Blending) and four 3DGS implementations, the LM optimizer achieves the same reconstruction quality (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS within ±0.01) while reducing optimization time by approximately 20% on average. For example, on Deep Blending, 3DGS+LM reaches PSNR 29.72 in 951 s versus 29.51 in 1222 s for vanilla 3DGS. The speedup is consistent across all baselines: 23.5% faster on MipNeRF360 (3DGS), 20.9% on DISTWAR, 23.1% on gsplat, and 20.0% on Taming-3DGS. The method requires only 5 LM iterations (each with 8 PCG sub-iterations) after a 20K-iteration ADAM initialization to converge, versus 10K additional ADAM iterations for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not introduce a new scene representation but replaces the ADAM optimizer with a tailored Levenberg-Marquardt solver, so the comparison is framed as 'baseline + Ours' versus 'baseline alone.' Against vanilla 3DGS [23], the LM optimizer matches SSIM/PSNR/LPIPS within noise (e.g., PSNR 27.39 vs. 27.40 on MipNeRF360) while cutting runtime from 1271 s to 972 s (23.5% faster). Against DISTWAR [12], the same pattern holds: quality is identical (PSNR 27.42 on MipNeRF360) with a 20.9% speedup (966 s → 764 s). Against gsplat [48], the speedup is largest on Tanks&Temples (646 s → 414 s, 35.9% faster) with PSNR actually improving from 23.50 to 23.68, while on MipNeRF360 the gain is 23.1% (1064 s → 818 s) at matched quality. Against Taming-3DGS [32] (the fastest baseline in absolute time), the LM optimizer still yields a 15–22% reduction (e.g., 447 s → 347 s on Deep Blending) with PSNR improving slightly (29.84 → 29.91). In all cases the method trades ~53 GB GPU memory (for the gradient cache) against the 6–11 GB used by baselines, representing a runtime–memory tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), retaining its Gaussian representation, differentiable rasterizer, and L1/SSIM loss, but replaces the ADAM optimizer with a tailored Levenberg-Marquardt solver. It departs from prior 3DGS acceleration work (DISTWAR, gsplat, Taming-3DGS) that improves the rasterizer or densification, by targeting the optimizer itself, and is explicitly orthogonal to those methods. The LM/Gauss-Newton lineage in 3D reconstruction (BundleFusion, OPT, PERF for NeRFs) informs the approach, but this paper is the first to exploit the explicit Gaussian primitives of 3DGS for data-parallel Jacobian-vector products via a gradient cache, enabling PCG to run efficiently at 3DGS scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20% runtime reduction is modest in isolation, but the key contribution is demonstrating that quasi-second-order optimization is practical for 3DGS at scale (millions of Gaussians, hundreds of high-res images) through a cache-driven per-pixel-per-splat parallelization. This opens the door to further optimizer-level improvements (e.g., true second-order methods like 3DGS2) and makes 3DGS reconstruction more practical for time-sensitive applications. Significant limitations remain: the 53 GB memory footprint (vs. 6–11 GB for baselines) restricts deployment on consumer hardware, and the two-stage design still depends on ADAM for densification, which is not easily integrated into the 5–10 LM iterations. Whether densification can be reformulated for LM, or the cache further compressed without quality loss, are open questions.
