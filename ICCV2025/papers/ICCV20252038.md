## Problem

Existing neural symmetry detection methods treat the task as per-pixel heatmap prediction, making it difficult to recover precise geometric parameters of symmetry axes. Additionally, they either fail to incorporate symmetric structure explicitly into feature representations or lack dedicated matching mechanisms, leading to inconsistent outputs under image rotations and reflections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252038/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces axis-level geometric primitives: reflection axes as line segments parameterized by midpoint probability, orientation offset, and length (using center-angle-length representation rather than endpoints), and rotation centers as points with a multi-class fold label. Each spatial location carries N/2 orientation-specific anchor predictions (for D_8, that is 4 anchors), each predicting an orientation offset within ±π/N of its base angle, enabling disambiguation of overlapping axes at different orientations.

## Method

The architecture is a D_8-equivariant ResNet34 backbone feeding two specialized branches. The reflection branch introduces orientational anchor expansion: it aggregates each (r^i, br^i) reflection pair via a learnable transformation (addition for midpoint/length, subtraction for orientation) to obtain C_N-equivariant features, then combines opposite orientations (α, α+N/2) to resolve the θ/θ+π line ambiguity, yielding N/2 orientation-specific anchors. An equivariant reflectional matching module computes cosine similarity between rotated and rotated-then-reflected feature fibers across all N rotation angles, extended to multi-scale spatial neighborhoods (k=0,1,2), producing features that are D_N-equivariant yet reflection-invariant. The rotation branch applies an invariant rotational matching module that compares features at floor(N/2) fixed angular separations, yielding fully D_N-invariant similarity scores for fold classification. Training uses weighted binary cross-entropy for midpoint and fold classification plus Smooth-L1 and L1 losses for length and orientation regression, with orientation loss weighted 150× to account for radian scale.

## Results

On the DENDI dataset, the proposed method achieves reflection sAP of 18.7/22.7/24.7 and rotation center sAP of 36.8/39.1/40.0 at 5/10/15-pixel thresholds, with fold sAP of 26.6/28.3/28.9. In pixel-level F1 comparisons (Table 3), the method attains 68.3% on SDRW, 43.4% on LDRS, and 37.2% on DENDI for reflection symmetry, and 26.8% on DENDI for rotation symmetry. The method outperforms the strongest prior baseline (EquiSym) on LDRS (+3.4), DENDI reflection (+0.5), and DENDI rotation (+4.4), while being marginally below PMCNet on SDRW (68.3 vs. 68.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior neural methods: PMCNet (Seo et al., ICCV 2021) and EquiSym (Seo et al., CVPR 2022). Against EquiSym, the proposed method wins on every reported metric: LDRS reflection F1 improves from 40.0 to 43.4 (+3.4), DENDI reflection F1 from 36.7 to 37.2 (+0.5), DENDI rotation F1 from 22.4 to 26.8 (+4.4), and SDRW reflection F1 from 67.5 to 68.3 (+0.8). Against PMCNet, the method is slightly lower on SDRW (68.3 vs. 68.8) but substantially higher on LDRS (43.4 vs. 37.3, +6.1) and DENDI reflection (37.2 vs. 32.6, +4.6); PMCNet does not support rotation symmetry detection. The authors note that the small SDRW gap versus PMCNet reflects the inherent difference between heatmap-based segmentation and axis-level detection, and that under stricter evaluation (smaller axis padding) the axis-level approach actually surpasses PMCNet due to more precise localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dihedral-group equivariant CNN framework of Cohen & Welling (2016) for feature extraction, but departs from prior deep symmetry detection methods (PMCNet, EquiSym) that frame the problem as pixel-level heatmap prediction. The key conceptual shift is treating symmetry axes as explicit geometric primitives—line segments for reflection and points for rotation—rather than dense per-pixel scores, a direction previously explored only for isolated objects without background context. The orientational anchor expansion mechanism is novel in integrating the group's rotation dimension into the detection head, analogous to how anchor boxes parameterize scale and aspect ratio in object detection, while the reflectional and rotational matching modules provide the first equivariant/invariant feature-comparison operators specifically designed for symmetry validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The axis-level formulation enables precise recovery of geometric parameters (axis length, orientation, fold number) that heatmap-based methods cannot provide, which is critical for downstream applications like 3D reconstruction or symmetry-guided generation. The equivariant design offers a principled solution to the rotation-robustness problem that plagues prior methods, as validated by the RMSE analysis. However, absolute performance remains modest (sAP@5 of ~19% for reflection, ~37% for rotation), fold misclassification between 2-fold and 4-fold symmetries persists, and the approach is limited to discrete dihedral groups in 2D. Extending to continuous rotation groups, 3D scenes, and varying viewpoints are natural but non-trivial next steps.
