## Problem

Existing diffusion-based detectors rely on reconstruction error to distinguish real from generated images, but this signal conflates aleatoric uncertainty (inherent data noise) with epistemic uncertainty (model ignorance of unfamiliar patterns). Aleatoric uncertainty inflates prediction ambiguity and does not increase for out-of-distribution samples, undermining detection generalizability. The paper asks whether isolating the epistemic component of diffusion uncertainty yields a more reliable and generalizable detection signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, encodes it into latent space via a pretrained VAE, and runs a single-step DDIM reconstruction (t=200) through Stable Diffusion V1.5 with M parameter samples and N noise samples to compute spatially-aligned epistemic uncertainty maps. These uncertainty maps are fused with CLIP-pretrained ResNet50 visual features (also at 224×224) via multi-head attention, and the concatenated global features feed a binary classifier that outputs a real/fake probability.

## Method

DEUA is a two-stage framework. In the estimation stage, the last-layer Laplace approximation (LLLA) with diagonal factorization is applied to a pretrained diffusion model's final layer, converting it into a Bayesian network; epistemic uncertainty is then computed as the Monte Carlo variance over parameter samples of the denoised prediction at each spatial location (Eq. 13). In the training stage, the spatial uncertainty map serves dual roles: as a query/key for multi-head attention to refine CLIP visual features, and as a global feature via attention pooling, which is concatenated with the refined visual feature for classification. The classifier is trained with an asymmetric contrastive loss that imposes a smaller similarity margin on the real class (m⁰=0.6) than on the fake class (m¹=1), counteracting the 'sink class' effect where the diverse real-image distribution causes a skewed decision boundary. This combination of epistemic-uncertainty-based features and asymmetric margins yields state-of-the-art accuracy on GenImage and DRCT-2M with strong cross-generator and cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On GenImage (without DRCT DR), DEUA achieves 85.6% average ACC across eight generators, outperforming the strongest baseline LaRE² (79.1%) by 6.5 percentage points. With DRCT DR training data, the method reaches 91.5% average ACC, surpassing DRCT/UniFD (89.1%) by 2.4 points. On DRCT-2M, DEUA achieves 90.5% average ACC without DRCT DR (vs. LaRE²'s 88.0%) and 98.8% with DRCT DR (vs. DRCT/Conv-B SDv2's 96.6%), demonstrating consistent gains across both large-scale benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEUA outperforms all six baselines on both GenImage and DRCT-2M. Against frequency- and texture-based methods (F3Net at 64.6%, GramNet at 64.7% avg ACC on GenImage), the gap is largest (~21 points), reflecting the limited generalizability of handcrafted spectral cues. CLIP-based UnivFD (69.7%) and diffusion-reconstruction-error methods DIRE (67.2%) and LaRE² (79.1%) are closer competitors, but DEUA still leads by 15.9, 18.4, and 6.5 points respectively. The most competitive comparison is against DRCT/UniFD (89.1% with DRCT DR), where DEUA achieves 91.5%—a 2.4-point margin. On DRCT-2M, DEUA (90.5% w/o DRCT DR) exceeds LaRE² (88.0%) by 2.5 points, and with DRCT DR the method reaches 98.8% (SDv2 variant) versus DRCT/Conv-B's 96.6%. Notably, DEUA's advantage is most pronounced on unseen or substantially different generators (e.g., SDXL, LCM-SDXL, BigGAN), where baselines degrade sharply while DEUA maintains near-perfect accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-reconstruction-error paradigm (DIRE, LaRE², SeDID) by arguing that the forward diffusion process introduces aleatoric uncertainty that inflates prediction ambiguity, making raw reconstruction error a noisy discriminative signal. It repurposes the last-layer Laplace approximation (Daxberger et al., Kristiadi et al.)—previously applied to standard classifiers—to estimate epistemic uncertainty specifically within the diffusion denoising network, isolating the component that actually tracks distance from the generated-image manifold. The asymmetric contrastive loss addresses the 'sink class' phenomenon identified in UnivFD, and the overall pipeline fuses this uncertainty signal with CLIP visual features in a manner consistent with DRCT's multi-signal design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uncertainty decomposition provides a principled explanation for why reconstruction-error-based detectors degrade on unseen generators, and the resulting epistemic-uncertainty feature offers a more robust, source-agnostic signal that generalizes substantially better across datasets (89.5% vs. 53.7% cross-dataset ACC vs. LaRE²). However, the method still struggles with GAN-generated images (BigGAN subset) and DR (inpainting) variants without additional training data, and the Laplace approximation is applied only to the last layer, leaving open whether full Bayesian treatment or alternative uncertainty estimators (deep ensembles, MC dropout) would yield further gains. Broader questions remain about whether this uncertainty-based framing extends to video, 3D, or audio generation detection.
