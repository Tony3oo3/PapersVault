## Problem

Existing diffusion-based missing-modality recovery methods suffer from modality generation bias: certain modalities (e.g., text) are synthesized with high fidelity while others (e.g., video) remain difficult to reconstruct due to intrinsic modality gaps. This bias leads to imbalanced training where models perform well under some missing-modality conditions but degrade sharply under others, and no prior work explicitly addresses the unequal difficulty of generating different modalities within a unified recovery framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251867/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-encoded modality features as input: 768-dim BERT hidden states for text, 35-dimensional facial action unit vectors (via Facet) for video, and 74-dimensional COVAREP features for audio, with at least one modality guaranteed available per sample. During training all modalities are complete; missing modalities are simulated only at inference. The output is the recovered missing modality in the same latent space, which is then concatenated with available features and passed through a multimodal Transformer and MLPs to produce a 7-class or binary sentiment prediction.

## Method

MD²N decomposes the VP-SDE diffusion process into three sequential stages—global structure generation, modality transfer, and local cross-modal refinement—within a duplex architecture where both the available and missing modalities simultaneously denoise each other in an intersecting manner. The key mechanism is a modality transfer module operating in the middle stage (t₁, t₂], which injects a cross-modal correction term Φ(x₀ᵐ − x₀ᵃ) into the forward and reverse SDEs, enabling bidirectional knowledge flow that preserves modality-invariant representations while transferring modality-variant information. A time-step-dependent variance function β(t) dynamically adjusts noise intensity at each diffusion step to stabilize generation. Training minimizes a combined score-matching loss (applied to both modalities' score networks) plus an L2 reconstruction loss, and the full pipeline is optimized end-to-end with a cross-entropy task loss weighted by a balancing coefficient γ.

## Results

On CMU-MOSEI under fixed missing scenarios, MD2N achieves 88.4/88.3/55.2 (ACC2/F1/ACC7) when only text is available, 69.7/69.7/43.5 when only audio is available, and 70.1/70.0/44.7 when only video is available. On CMU-MOSI, the corresponding scores are 87.4/87.3/45.9, 68.8/68.8/27.5, and 67.1/67.0/27.2. MD2N outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baseline IMDer by 3.9–6.3 points in ACC2 across all single-modality-available settings on CMU-MOSEI, and by 2.6–6.8 points on CMU-MOSI. Under two-modality-available settings (e.g., {L,A}), MD2N reaches 88.5/88.4/56.5 on CMU-MOSEI, surpassing IMDer (85.1/85.1/53.1) by 3.4 points in ACC7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD2N consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every missing-modality configuration on both datasets. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor IMDer [52], MD2N gains 2.6–6.8 points in ACC2 on CMU-MOSI and 3.9–6.3 points on CMU-MOSEI, with the largest margins appearing when audio or video is the sole available modality (e.g., 69.7 vs. 63.8 ACC2 for {A} on CMU-MOSEI). The second-strongest baseline DiCMoR [51] is surpassed by 2.9–6.8 points in ACC2, confirming that the duplex multi-stage design adds substantial value over single-direction diffusion recovery. Non-recovery baselines DCCA [3] and DCCAE [50] trail by 9–16 points, underscoring the benefit of explicit modality reconstruction. Among earlier recovery methods, MCTN [40] and TransM [53] lag by 5–10 points, while ICDN [63], MMIN [65], and GCNet [31] are beaten by 4–10 points. Notably, MD2N's advantage is most pronounced in the hardest scenarios (single-modality-available, especially {A} and {V}), where prior methods exhibit the largest performance drops due to modality generation bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based missing-modality recovery line initiated by IMDer (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023) and DiCMoR (Wang et al., ICCV 2023), which use score-based diffusion to reconstruct absent modalities from Gaussian noise conditioned on available ones. The key departure is the introduction of a 'duplex' architecture where both available and missing modalities are simultaneously diffused and generate each other through a dedicated modality-transfer stage, rather than the unidirectional available→missing generation used by prior methods. The multi-stage temporal decomposition (global structure → modality transfer → local refinement) draws on the VP-SDE framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and variance-preserving noise scheduling (Wang et al., 2024), but restructures the diffusion timeline to explicitly allocate a middle phase for cross-modal knowledge exchange.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'modality generation bias'—where diffusion-based recovery systematically underperforms on harder-to-generate modalities like video—provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners deploying incomplete multimodal systems. The duplex mechanism offers a principled way to balance generation quality across modalities without task-specific tuning, and the ablation showing ~2-point gains when the module is grafted onto MCTN suggests broad applicability. However, the evaluation is confined to two small sentiment-analysis datasets with three modalities, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger modality sets, higher-dimensional data (e.g., full video frames), or non-sentiment tasks. The paper itself flags extension to >3 modalities and adaptive stage scheduling as future directions.
