## Problem

General MLLMs relying solely on CLIP visual tokens struggle in autonomous driving VQA because CLIP tokens lack token-wise affinity (instance-level spatial structure), underrepresent sparse driving-critical elements like distant vehicles and traffic signs, and cannot adapt visual features to the specific question being asked. Existing multi-encoder fusion approaches are complex and do not address the core challenge of rapid domain adaptation with limited driving-specific data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a driving scene image (encoded by CLIP into a 24×24 grid of 576 visual tokens) and a free-form text question, and produces a natural-language answer. Beyond the standard CLIP tokens, the pipeline additionally extracts 576 DINOv2 tokens (or their pairwise similarity matrix) for the Affinity hint, top-K sparse object queries from Mask2Former (typically 16–32 tokens) augmented with class embeddings for the Semantic hint, and the LLM's own input-embedding vectors of the question tokens for the Question hint. The fused visual representation is passed through a linear adapter into a Vicuna-7B LLM that autoregressively generates the answer.

## Method

HoP introduces three hierarchical hint token sets—Affinity (DINOv2 tokens preserving instance-level token-wise structure), Semantic (sparse Mask2Former queries with category embeddings capturing driving-specific objects), and Question (LLM text embeddings of the query for context-aware focus)—and fuses them into the original CLIP visual tokens via a single Joint Cross-Attention layer: P_f = P + CA(P, [P, H_A, H_S, H_Q]). This design outperforms concatenation, self-cross-attention, sequential, and parallel cross-attention strategies while using only 8.7M parameters and 3.8 GFLOPs. The fused tokens are aligned by a linear adapter and fed to a Vicuna-7B LLM, with the visual encoder frozen and remaining modules fine-tuned for one epoch. An Efficient HoP variant replaces DINOv2 and Mask2Former with lightweight distilled heads (a 4-layer ViT-like decoder and a ViTDet+Mask2Former neck) trained on LingoQA and Cityscapes respectively, cutting visual-side parameters from 832M to 385.8M and inference latency from 956 ms to 661 ms while retaining most of the performance gain.

## Results

On LingoQA, HoP achieves a Lingo-Judge score of 67.8, BLEU-4 of 15.8, METEOR of 20.3, and CIDEr of 70.9, outperforming the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline (63.2 / 14.1 / 19.3 / 63.7) by 4.6 points on Lingo-Judge and 7.2 on CIDEr. On DRAMA, HoP reaches BLEU-1 76.2, BLEU-4 56.3, METEOR 41.7, ROUGE 79.8, and SPICE 59.1. On BDD-X (All split), HoP attains CIDEr 102.20, BLEU-4 17.91, and ROUGE 45.79, leading all difficulty levels. The lightweight Efficient HoP variant retains most of these gains (Lingo-Judge 66.8) while reducing inference latency from 956 ms to 661 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoP outperforms all nine baselines on LingoQA across every metric, with the largest margins over GPT-4V zero-shot (+8.2 Lingo-Judge, +28.1 CIDEr) and the LingoQA original baseline (+7.2 Lingo-Judge, +5.3 CIDEr). Against the closest competitors—QA-ViT (63.6) and VTS (64.2)—HoP gains 4.2 and 3.6 Lingo-Judge points respectively, while also surpassing VTS on CIDEr by 14.0 points (70.9 vs 56.9); the only metric where HoP trails is METEOR vs VTS (20.3 vs 20.5, a 0.2 gap). On DRAMA, HoP leads on BLEU-1, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE, and SPICE but is marginally behind LLaVA-v1.5 on CIDEr (2.8 vs 2.9). On BDD-X, HoP dominates all difficulty splits, achieving CIDEr 102.20 (All) versus DriveGPT4's 99.10 and LLaVA-v1.5's 96.51, with the largest gap on the Hard split (60.37 vs 57.29 for DriveGPT4). The A-MoF multi-encoder fusion approach (64.2) and the DINOv2-swap variant (62.8) both underperform HoP, confirming that the structured hint-token design is more effective than simply adding another visual encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-v1.5 MLLM architecture and departs from prior multi-encoder fusion approaches (e.g., MOME, A-MoF) that combine CLIP, DINOv2, and Pix2Struct features through complex strategies. Instead of replacing or heavily modifying the visual encoder, HoP repurposes outputs from existing models—DINOv2 token similarities, Mask2Former queries, and LLM text embeddings—as lightweight 'hint tokens' fused via a single Joint Cross-Attention layer. This positions the work between QA-ViT's question-aware visual features and Hint-AD's holistic alignment, but with a more modular, backbone-agnostic design that avoids the intricate fusion pipelines of prior multi-encoder methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-efficiency result—25% training data surpassing the full-data LLaVA-v1.5 baseline—suggests that structured, domain-informed hints can substitute for large volumes of driving-specific visual-language pairs, which are expensive to collect. The Efficient HoP variant (distilled heads + AWQ quantization at 281 ms) points toward practical deployment, though it still trails the full HoP by ~1 Lingo-Judge point. Open questions include whether the hint-token paradigm generalizes beyond driving to other domain-specific VQA, whether the reliance on external detectors (Mask2Former) for Semantic hints limits end-to-end trainability, and whether the gains persist as base LLMs improve in spatial reasoning natively.
