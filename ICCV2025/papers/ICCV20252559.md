## Problem

Instruction-based video editing requires large-scale training triplets (source video, edited video, instruction), but existing datasets are limited to low resolution (256×256 or 512×512), short duration (8–16 frames), and unsatisfactory editing quality due to reliance on random-parameter synthesis or time-consuming one-shot methods. This data bottleneck prevents training robust models that can handle real-world, high-resolution video editing from text instructions alone.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source video (1024×576, 25 frames) and a free-form text instruction, and outputs an edited video at the same resolution and frame count. During training, videos are randomly cropped to 720×480, and the source video is encoded into a latent representation that is concatenated with the text embedding before being fed to the DiT backbone. At inference, a dual-condition classifier-free guidance scheme applies separate CFG scales (s_V for the source video, s_T for the instruction) to balance fidelity to the original video against adherence to the editing instruction.

## Method

The paper contributes both a 1M-triplet dataset (InsViE-1M) and a video editing model (InsViE). For dataset construction, a two-stage editing-filtering pipeline is used: Stage 1 generates six edited first frames via CosXL at CFG values 3–8, then GPT-4o selects the best candidate based on instruction adherence, natural integration, artifact absence, and subject consistency; Stage 2 propagates the selected first frame to all 25 frames using SVD inversion, then filters the full video using GPT-4o scoring (six criteria including cross-frame coherence) and GMFlow optical-flow endpoint error. The model is built by LoRA-finetuning (rank 128) CogVideoX-2B, a video generation model rather than an image model, to inherently preserve temporal consistency. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: Stage 1 learns general editing on the full 1M set, Stage 2 refines on a higher-quality 227k subset, and Stage 3 augments with 680k static-video pairs (5:1 static-to-real ratio) and adds LPIPS loss in pixel space to improve visual fidelity.

## Results

On the 100-video test set (DAVIS, YoutubeVOS, Pexels), InsViE achieves the best score across all eight evaluation metrics: CLIP temporal consistency of 0.956, optical flow EPE of 4.84 (lower is better), CLIP textual alignment of 19.37, Pick Score of 18.91, and DOVER video quality of 0.567. On TGVE/TGVE+, InsViE also leads with Pick Score 20.90/20.97, TC CLIP 0.941/0.945, ViCLIP_dir 0.242/0.201, and ViCLIP_out 0.278/0.270. The closest competitor, InsV2V, trails by 0.36 in CLIP textual alignment (19.01 vs 19.37) and 0.008 in DOVER (0.559 vs 0.567).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsViE outperforms all six baselines on every metric in the main comparison. The closest competitor is InsV2V [6], which trails by 0.36 in CLIP textual alignment (19.01 vs 19.37), 0.16 in Pick Score (18.75 vs 18.91), and 0.008 in DOVER (0.559 vs 0.567), while InsViE also achieves lower OF EPE (4.84 vs 4.97). Among training-free methods, Videohop [9] is the strongest on temporal consistency (GPT score 3.81) but falls behind InsViE on textual alignment (18.92 vs 19.37) and video quality (DOVER 0.501 vs 0.567). FateZero [26] and RAVE [17] show the weakest performance overall, with FateZero's OF EPE of 13.07 being nearly 3× worse than InsViE's 4.84, reflecting poor motion preservation. On TGVE/TGVE+, InsViE also beats EVE [30] (Pick 20.76/20.88, ViCLIP_dir 0.221/0.198) and the retrained InsV2V* (Pick 20.83/20.91, ViCLIP_dir 0.237/0.191), confirming that the advantage is not solely due to dataset quality but also to the video-generation-based architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based video editing line initiated by InstructVid2Vid and InsV2V, which relied on image editing models and random or one-shot data generation. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the model is built on a video generation backbone (CogVideoX) rather than an image model, addressing the temporal consistency failures of image-based approaches; and (2) the dataset construction replaces random-parameter synthesis with a two-stage GPT-4o + optical-flow filtering pipeline that explicitly screens for editing quality. The dual-condition CFG sampling strategy is inherited from InstructPix2Pix, and Stable Video Diffusion is used as the propagation engine in the data pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-triplet dataset at 1024×576 × 25 frames addresses the most cited bottleneck in instruction-based video editing—insufficient high-quality training data—and the two-stage filtering pipeline (GPT-4o scoring + GMFlow EPE) offers a reusable recipe for quality control in synthetic data generation. However, the authors acknowledge that GPT-4o's visual understanding caps filtering quality, the 25-frame clips remain short for real-world applications, and the model degrades on noisy or verbose instructions. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to longer videos, whether the 5:1 static-to-real ratio holds at scale, and whether the approach transfers to other base video models beyond CogVideoX.
