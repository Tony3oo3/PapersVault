## Problem

Existing Single-Domain Generalized Object Detection (Single-DGOD) methods assume that training and testing domains share the same label space, but real-world deployments frequently encounter both unseen domains and previously unknown object categories simultaneously. This dual challenge of domain shift and class shift—termed Single-domain Generalizable Open-Set Object Detection (SG-OSOD)—remains largely unexplored, with no principled framework that jointly addresses both.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a single labeled source domain (e.g., Cityscapes or Pascal VOC) and produces bounding boxes annotated with either one of |C_s| known class labels or a single 'unknown' class label, yielding |C_s| + 1 output categories. Internally, the Deformable DETR backbone (ResNet-50 + DINO pretraining) generates 100 query embeddings of dimension 256 per image, which are partitioned via bipartite matching into matched (known) and unmatched (potential unknown/background) sets before the ASGS modules operate on them.

## Method

ASGS builds on Deformable DETR and introduces two complementary modules. The Subgraph-wise Unknown-class Learning (SUL) module first identifies boundary known-class samples via L2 distance from EMA-updated class prototypes, then uses KNN to locate nearby unmatched embeddings, and finally applies Adaptive Subgraph Searching (ASS) to construct variable-order subgraphs by connecting known-boundary and unmatched embeddings whose cosine similarity exceeds a threshold δ; the mean feature of each subgraph trains an unknown-class classifier. The Class-wise Embedding Compaction (CEC) module applies a supervised InfoNCE contrastive loss where each class prototype serves as a query, same-class matched embeddings are positives, and all other-class prototypes and embeddings are negatives, pulling intra-class features into compact clusters while preserving inter-class separation. The key insight is that high-order structural relationships captured by subgraphs are more domain-invariant than individual sample embeddings, and the contrastive compaction prevents unknown-class activation from degrading known-class detection under domain shift.

## Results

On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes (het-sem, 3 unknown classes), ASGS achieves 41.09% mAP_k and 2.87% AR_u, outperforming the strongest baselines (D-DETR: 37.69% mAP_k; OpenDet: 37.12% mAP_k, 2.32% AR_u) by 3.4 and 0.55 points respectively. On Pascal VOC → Clipart1K (6 unknown classes), ASGS reaches 21.66% mAP_k and 25.10% AR_u, surpassing the next-best method (OW-DETR: 16.67% mAP_k, 21.78% AR_u) by over 5 mAP_k points. On Cityscapes → BDD100K (het-sem, 3 unknown classes), ASGS obtains 13.57% mAP_k and 1.81% AR_u, edging out D-DETR (13.24% mAP_k) and matching PROB's AR_u (1.83%) while achieving the lowest WI (0.126) and AOSE (600).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASGS consistently outperforms all six baselines on mAP_k across every evaluated setting and dataset. The margin is most pronounced on Pascal VOC → Clipart1K, where ASGS leads the next-best method (OW-DETR) by 4.99 mAP_k points (21.66 vs 16.67) with 6 unknown classes, indicating that the adaptive subgraph mechanism is particularly effective under large domain gaps. On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, ASGS improves mAP_k over D-DETR by 3.4 points (het-sem, 3 unknown) while simultaneously achieving the highest AR_u (2.87 vs 2.32 for OpenDet), demonstrating that unknown-class detection does not come at the cost of known-class accuracy. PROB achieves the lowest WI and AOSE in some settings (e.g., WI=0.195, AOSE=20 on het-sem/3), but at the expense of substantially lower mAP_k (36.67 vs 41.09), suggesting PROB sacrifices known-class precision for conservative unknown rejection. On Cityscapes → BDD100K, the performance gap narrows (ASGS 13.57 vs D-DETR 13.24 mAP_k), yet ASGS still achieves the best AR_u among methods that maintain competitive mAP_k, and the lowest WI (0.126) and AOSE (600) overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Single-Domain Generalized Object Detection (Single-DGOD) and Open-Set Object Detection (OSOD), two lines of work that have been studied largely in isolation. It builds on Deformable DETR as the detection backbone and adapts the InfoNCE contrastive framework for class-wise embedding compaction, but its core departure is the Adaptive Subgraph Searching (ASS) mechanism, which replaces the single-sample embedding paradigm used by prior OSOD methods (OpenDet, OW-DETR, PROB, CAT) with high-order structural relationships between known and unknown instances. The paper explicitly positions SG-OSOD as filling a gap: prior DGOD assumes a shared label space, and prior OSOD assumes a shared domain, but no prior detection method jointly addresses both domain shift and class shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SG-OSOD formulation is practically motivated—real-world deployment (e.g., autonomous driving) inevitably encounters both environmental changes and novel object categories simultaneously, yet this combined challenge had no dedicated benchmark or method before this work. The subgraph-based approach offers a principled argument that relational structure is more domain-invariant than individual visual features, a design choice that could inform other open-set or OOD detection settings. However, absolute unknown-class recall (ARu) remains low (single digits to ~11%), and evaluation is limited to three benchmarks with small class spaces (8–20 classes), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse taxonomies. The paper does not explore computational overhead of the subgraph construction at inference time or how the method behaves when the number of unknown classes grows substantially.
