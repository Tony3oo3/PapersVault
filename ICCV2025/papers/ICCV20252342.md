## Problem

Deep learning-based pansharpening models achieve strong performance on their training satellite but suffer substantial degradation when deployed on unseen sensors due to distributional discrepancies in spectral signatures, noise levels, and sensor conditioning. Existing remedies—retraining, fine-tuning, or architectural redesign—are impractical for real-world deployment across diverse satellite platforms. The paper identifies the root cause as the gap between training and testing pixel distributions and argues that bridging this gap is sufficient to restore generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a low-resolution multispectral (LRMS) image and a high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image, both of which are first passed through a per-band distribution transformation fitted on the training set before entering the network. The model predicts a residual that is added to the bicubic-upsampled LRMS to yield the final high-resolution multispectral (HRMS) fused image. Training follows the Wald protocol with an l1 loss, and the transformation is refitted per satellite to avoid test-set leakage.

## Method

UniPAN is a model-agnostic, parameter-free preprocessing that maps pixel values from any source distribution to a unified target distribution (standard normal or uniform) via inverse transform sampling: T(x) = F_T^{-1}(F_S(x)), where F_S is the empirical CDF estimated from training pixels and F_T^{-1} is the inverse CDF of the target. The authors prove this mapping is exactly the Monge optimal transport map in the one-dimensional case, grounding the heuristic in optimal transport theory. In practice, the empirical CDF is approximated by m quantiles with bidirectional linear interpolation to handle repeated values and sparse regions, and the transformation is applied independently per spectral band. Because the same transformation is applied to both training and testing data, the network always operates on a consistent distribution, enabling a 'train once, deploy forever' workflow without any architectural modification or additional trainable parameters.

## Results

Across 12 pansharpening models and 6 satellite sensors (GF1, IK, QB, WV2, WV3, WV4) on NBUPansharpRSData, UniPAN with ρ_t = U(0,1) yields the strongest cross-satellite generalization. The headline improvement is a QNR gain of +0.084 (from 0.729 to 0.813) when generalizing from WV3 to QB, averaged over all 12 models. Spatial metrics D_S and D_ρ show consistent reductions (e.g., D_S drops from 0.167 to 0.117 for WV3→QB), while spectral distortion D_λ exhibits only marginal increases in rare cases. The uniform target distribution outperforms the Gaussian target across most sensor pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 12 widely-used pansharpening architectures (FusionNet, PanNet, MSDDN, GPPNN, MSDCNN, FeINFN, PNN, LAGConv, SFIIN, PreMix, UAPN, MDCUN) each in two configurations: baseline (w/o UniPAN) and UniPAN-enhanced. UniPAN with U(0,1) wins on QNR in the vast majority of 30 cross-satellite transfer scenarios, with the largest gains on the hardest transfers (e.g., WV3→QB: +0.084, QB→QB: +0.082, IK→QB: +0.070). The only notable QNR regression is WV2→IK (−0.006), suggesting that when source and target sensors are already spectrally similar, the transformation offers little benefit. On spectral distortion D_λ, UniPAN produces small degradations in a few cases (e.g., WV3→GF1: +0.010) but reduces distortion in most others, confirming that the per-band quantile mapping preserves relative spectral structure. Spatial metrics D_S and D_ρ show near-universal improvements, with D_S reductions up to 0.072 (WV3→QB). The uniform target consistently outperforms the Gaussian target, particularly for WV2 and WV3 training sets, likely because the peaked Gaussian distribution hinders training while the uniform provides smoother normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes inverse transform sampling—a classical probability technique—and connects it to the Monge optimal transport problem in 1D, showing the two are exactly equivalent. It departs from prior generalization efforts in pansharpening (unsupervised learning, generalized module design, spatial data augmentation) by intervening in data space rather than model space, positioning itself as a non-linear, distribution-aware alternative to standard min-max or z-score normalization. The approach is explicitly model-agnostic and introduces no trainable parameters, distinguishing it from architecture-specific or retraining-based solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'train once, deploy forever' capability is practically significant for operational remote sensing pipelines where new satellite sensors arrive continuously and per-sensor retraining is infeasible. The method's simplicity (a one-time quantile fitting step) and zero-parameter overhead make it easy to adopt as a preprocessing layer for any existing or future pansharpening architecture. Key open questions include extending the 1D per-band transformation to capture inter-band spectral correlations via multidimensional optimal transport, and whether adaptive target distribution selection can further close the residual performance gap. The slight spectral distortion observed in D_λ metrics suggests the per-band independence assumption has a measurable cost that multidimensional formulations may address.
