## Problem

Standard causal self-attention in LVLMs treats visual and textual tokens homogeneously, creating two issues: positional encodings bias text-to-visual attention toward spatially closer visual tokens (due to row-major flattening), and visual-to-visual attention incurs O(|V|²) cost despite visual tokens already encoding pairwise correlations from bidirectional vision encoders. Existing cross-attention architectures offer flexibility but break the integrity of pre-trained LLMs, degrading language capability.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image encoded by a pre-trained SigLip vision encoder into a sequence of visual tokens, concatenated with a text question or prompt, and produces a free-form text response. A key practical detail is that V2V diagonalization allows the model to process up to 74k visual tokens during training (versus ~9k for the S-Attn baseline) before hitting GPU memory limits, enabling high-resolution image and multi-frame video inputs. The architecture is built on LLaVA with a two-layer MLP adapter (with RMSNorm) bridging the vision encoder to either Mistral v0.3 7B or Gemma 2 9B.

## Method

D-Attn mathematically decomposes the 1-D causal self-attention of an LVLM into three independent components: visual-to-visual self-attention (V2V SA), textual-to-visual cross-attention (T2V XA), and textual-to-textual self-attention (T2T SA). Two key modifications exploit this decomposition: (1) debiased positional encodings set all visual token positions to the same value in T2V XA, eliminating the row-major flattening bias so text tokens attend uniformly to all visual tokens; (2) V2V diagonalization replaces the softmax attention matrix with an identity matrix, reducing visual-to-visual computation from O(|V|²) to O(|V|) by forcing each visual token to attend only to itself. The decomposed outputs are merged via an analytically derived α-weighting strategy where α_V = Sigmoid(S_V − S_T) and α_T = 1 − α_V, which is provably equivalent to the original causal self-attention, thereby preserving the pre-trained LLM's full capability without introducing additional parameters or architectural changes. Training follows a three-stage supervised fine-tuning protocol (modality alignment on 58k pairs, dense caption tuning on 1.25M samples, instruction tuning on 767k examples) completing in 24 hours on 32 H100 GPUs.

## Results

D-Attn (Gemma 2 9B) achieves 84.3 on VQAv2, 65.9 on GQA, 75.5 on SQA-I, 70.7 on VQA-T, 1636.7 on MME, 76.5 on MMB, 74.6 on SEED-I, 79.7 on LLaVA-W, 45.3 on MMVP, and 45.0 on MMStar, consistently outperforming its S-Attn counterpart (e.g., +2.5 on VQAv2, +2.9 on GQA, +130.1 on MME, +16.0 on MMVP). D-Attn (Mistral 0.3 7B) similarly beats S-Attn on all ten benchmarks (e.g., 82.9 vs 80.3 on VQAv2, 1598.6 vs 1533.1 on MME). The V2V diagonalization enables processing up to 74k visual tokens (vs 9k for S-Attn) and trains 5× faster (2.24 s/it vs 11.25 s/it) without sacrificing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is D-Attn vs. its S-Attn counterpart (same LLM backbone, same data, same training recipe): D-Attn wins on all ten benchmarks for both Mistral 0.3 7B and Gemma 2 9B, with the largest gains on MMVP (+16.0 for Gemma 2) and MME (+130.1 for Gemma 2). Against LLaVA-1.6 (Mistral 0.2 7B, 1.4M data), D-Attn (Mistral 0.3 7B, 2.5M data) leads on VQA-v2 (+0.7), SQA-I (+2.9), VQA-T (+2.6), MME (+100.6), and MMVP (+2.0), but trails on LLaVA-W (79.8 vs 83.2) and GQA (64.4 vs 64.8). Against CuMo (Mistral 0.2 7B, 2.9M data), D-Attn wins on VQA-v2, SQA-I, VQA-T, MME, and SEED-I but loses on GQA (64.4 vs 64.9), MMB (71.3 vs 73.0), and LLaVA-W (79.8 vs 85.7). Against larger-data models like Qwen2-VL and Intern-XC 2.5 (both with undisclosed or larger data), D-Attn (Gemma 2 9B) trails on MMB (76.5 vs 83.0/82.2) and MMStar (45.0 vs 60.7/59.9), but the authors note these models use substantially more training data and more advanced training techniques. Overall, D-Attn demonstrates that the architectural change alone (decomposed attention + debiased positional encoding + V2V diagonalization) yields consistent gains over the standard self-attention baseline while remaining competitive with SoTA models trained on comparable or larger datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a bridge between two dominant LVLM paradigms: decoder-only self-attention models (LLaVA, Qwen2-VL) that concatenate visual and textual tokens but offer no flexibility for visual-specific processing, and cross-attention models (Flamingo, IDEFICS, LLaMA 3) that allow tailored visual operations but break the pre-trained LLM's integrity. Building directly on LLaVA's architecture, D-Attn mathematically decomposes causal self-attention into V2V, T2V, and T2T components, provably recovering the original attention output via an analytically derived α-weighting merge. This decomposition grants cross-attention-level design freedom (debiased positional encodings, V2V diagonalization) while remaining exactly equivalent to the LLM's native self-attention, avoiding the architectural modifications that prior X-Attn approaches require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: V2V diagonalization enables 5× faster training and 8× more visual tokens (9k→74k), making high-resolution image and video processing significantly more tractable without performance loss. The conceptual finding that visual tokens from bidirectional encoders do not benefit from pairwise re-attention within the LLM challenges a default assumption in LVLM design and could simplify future architectures. Limitations include evaluation only on image benchmarks (no video), models capped at 9B parameters, and reliance on supervised fine-tuning without RLHF/DPO, leaving open whether the diagonalization and debiasing strategies transfer to video understanding, larger models, or preference-optimized training regimes.
