## Problem

Existing continual test-time adaptive object detection methods fine-tune a small subset of parameters (e.g., BatchNorm layers or lightweight adapters) on unlabeled target data, but this direct optimization can trap the model in local optima as the domain shifts continually, and the updated parameters may interfere with the fixed backbone weights, causing performance degradation. The paper asks whether the adaptation process can be reframed as a parameter *generation* problem rather than a parameter *optimization* problem, so that robust, diverse adapter weights are synthesized from scene context instead of being incrementally tuned.

## Input / Output

At test time the method consumes a streaming sequence of unlabeled RGB images from dynamically shifting target domains (weather, corruption, or time-of-day changes) together with a frozen pre-trained Faster R-CNN detector and the current adapter parameter matrices. It outputs standard object-detection results (bounding boxes and class labels) per frame, and additionally produces a new set of adapter weight matrices at each adaptation step via the conditional diffusion generator, which are then injected into the backbone's LoRA adapter blocks for the next forward pass.

## Method

The approach has three coupled components. First, a dual-path LoRA adapter is inserted into multiple backbone blocks, decomposing each weight update into a domain-invariant low-rank term (A_inv·B_inv) and a domain-specific low-rank term (A_sp·B_sp); the difference F_inv − F_sp is added to the original weight, and an orthogonal loss plus an HSIC loss enforce statistical independence between the two feature subspaces. Second, a conditional diffusion model—pre-trained offline on source-domain data together with an autoencoder that compresses adapter matrices into a low-dimensional latent code—generates robust adapter parameters at test time by denoising a latent vector conditioned on the current target-domain image features, thereby injecting diversity that prevents the unsupervised optimization from converging to local optima. Third, a class-centered optimal transport alignment loss transports each target-domain instance feature toward the corresponding source-domain class center, preserving source knowledge and mitigating catastrophic forgetting across the continual stream. During testing the diffusion generator is frozen and used only for inference, while the adapter parameters and the OT alignment are the only quantities optimized per step.

## Results

On Diverse-Weather (ResNet-101), the proposed method achieves 30.7% average mAP across four target weather domains, outperforming the strongest baseline SKIP (29.2%) by 1.5 points and Direct-Test (27.9%) by 2.8 points. On COCO→COCO-C (severity 5), the method reaches 23.0% average mAP with ResNet-50 and 22.5% with Swin-T, surpassing SKIP by 0.8% and 0.9% respectively, while also retaining 42.4%/42.3% source-domain mAP (vs. 41.8%/41.2% for SKIP), indicating reduced catastrophic forgetting. On SHIFT, the method achieves 39.5%/41.0% (Discrete) and 22.2%/24.9% (Continuous) average mAP with ResNet-50/Swin-T, consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary SOTA baseline SKIP [44] is outperformed on every dataset: by 1.5% on Diverse-Weather (30.7 vs. 29.2), by 0.8–0.9% on COCO→COCO-C, and by 0.5–1.0% on SHIFT, while also preserving higher source-domain accuracy (42.4% vs. 41.8% on ResNet-50 COCO), confirming better resistance to catastrophic forgetting. Among domain-generalization baselines on Diverse-Weather, IBN-Net (25.5%), IterNorm (23.4%), SW (26.1%), and ISW (26.3%) all trail the proposed method by 4.4–7.3 points, reflecting their inability to adapt online to unseen weather conditions. On COCO→COCO-C and SHIFT, the normalization-based TTA methods NORM (17.5%/37.7%) and DUA (17.1%/27.3%) underperform the proposed method by 5.5–15.9 points, while ActMAD (10.5%/34.1%) and Mean-Teacher (14.5%/37.1%) show even larger gaps, particularly on severe corruptions like motion blur and digital distortions where the proposed method's parameter generation provides the most substantial gains (e.g., 12.1% vs. 3.2% on motion blur with ResNet-50). The method wins on all 16 corruption types in COCO-C and all 7+2 SHIFT scenarios, with the largest margins appearing on blur and digital corruptions where local-optima avoidance via diffusion-based parameter generation is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual test-time adaptation (CTTA) line of work—exemplified by CoTTA, EcoTTA, and SKIP—by replacing the standard fine-tuning paradigm with a generative one: a conditional diffusion model synthesizes adapter parameters rather than the model optimizing them in place. The adapter itself is a dual-path extension of LoRA, splitting the low-rank update into domain-invariant and domain-specific branches regularized by orthogonality and HSIC losses, a design not present in prior CTTAOD methods. The parameter-generation component draws on the broader hypernetwork/diffusion-for-weights literature (HyperStyle, Peebles et al., P-diff) but is the first to apply conditional diffusion to generate LoRA adapter weights for object detection under domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core idea—using a generative model to propose adapter parameters and thereby escape local optima during unsupervised continual adaptation—is conceptually appealing and could generalize to other parameter-efficient fine-tuning settings. However, the practical gains are modest (≈1–1.5 mAP over SKIP) while the test-time pipeline now requires running a diffusion model plus an autoencoder at every adaptation step, raising open questions about computational cost and whether the generation step is truly necessary versus simpler stochastic restarts. The paper does not report inference latency or a comparison against random-restart baselines, leaving the causal contribution of the diffusion component somewhat under-validated.
