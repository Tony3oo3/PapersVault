## Problem

MMDiT architectures (e.g., SD3, FLUX) use joint self-attention over concatenated text and image tokens, creating attention patterns that existing DiT compression methods like DiTFastAttn cannot handle: visual tokens show strong diagonal locality while text interactions are semantically variable, and attention heads within the same layer exhibit dramatically different redundancy across timesteps. Additionally, prior compression plan search requires 10+ hours of full denoising evaluations, making per-model configuration impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a post-training compression applied to pre-trained MMDiT models (Stable Diffusion 3, FLUX.1-dev) that takes a text prompt and produces a generated image at 1024×1024 or 2048×2048 resolution over 50 denoising steps. A per-model compression plan is searched offline using 8 calibration prompts from MS-COCO 2014, then applied at inference time to reduce attention FLOPs by up to 68% while preserving visual fidelity.

## Method

DiTFastAttnV2 introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) Head-wise Arrow Attention, where each attention head independently selects between full attention and a sparse 'arrow' pattern that applies local windowed attention only in the visual-to-visual region while preserving full attention in all text-involving sub-regions (visual-to-language, language-to-visual, language-to-language); (2) Head-wise Caching, which skips attention recomputation for heads whose outputs are highly similar to the previous timestep, determined per-head rather than per-layer; and (3) a fused GPU kernel built on FlashAttention2 that executes all three modes (full, cached, arrow) in a single pass using block-based sparsity to minimize irregular memory access. The compression plan is searched via a single-layer Relative Squared Error (RSE) calibration metric combined with an ILP-based head-wise optimizer that includes a constraint coefficient preventing any single head from absorbing the entire error budget, reducing search time from 10+ hours to ~15 minutes.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 3 (1024×1024), DiTFastAttnV2 at δ=0.4 achieves 55% attention sparsity with LPIPS 0.238, SSIM 0.644, HPSv2 0.2935, and CLIP 0.3236, matching or exceeding the original model's HPSv2 (0.2926) and CLIP (0.3254) scores. On FLUX 2K (2048×2048) generation, the method achieves up to 68% attention FLOPs reduction and 1.5× end-to-end speedup on a single A100 GPU. The compression plan search time is reduced from over 10 hours (DiTFastAttn) to approximately 15 minutes using the proposed single-layer RSE calibration and ILP-based head-wise optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTFastAttnV2 (δ=0.4) outperforms all baselines on every metric while achieving the highest attention sparsity (55% vs. 32% for DiTFastAttn, 44% for DiTFastAttn+Arrow, and 50% for layer-wise caching). Specifically, it achieves the best LPIPS (0.238 vs. 0.297–0.323) and SSIM (0.644 vs. 0.567–0.616), indicating closer pixel-level fidelity to the original output. In terms of perceptual quality, DiTFastAttnV2's HPSv2 (0.2935) and CLIP (0.3236) scores match or slightly exceed the uncompressed original (0.2926, 0.3254), whereas DiTFastAttn drops HPSv2 by ~3% (0.2839) and IS by ~5% (21.185 vs. 22.346). The layer-wise caching baseline maintains competitive HPSv2 (0.2908) but suffers the worst SSIM (0.567) and LPIPS (0.323), and the authors note it occasionally produces incomplete objects. At the more aggressive δ=0.6 setting (63% sparsity), DiTFastAttnV2 still retains HPSv2 of 0.2933 and CLIP of 0.3246, remaining close to the original, while no baseline achieves comparable sparsity without significant quality degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to DiTFastAttn, which introduced windowed attention, cross-timestep caching, and CFG-based attention sharing for vanilla DiT architectures. The key conceptual departure is the shift from layer-wise uniform strategies to head-wise fine-grained compression, motivated by the architectural transition to MMDiT where text and image tokens share a joint self-attention mechanism rather than separate cross-attention. The arrow attention pattern (local visual attention + full text attention) and the ILP-based compression plan search are novel methodological contributions that address the specific heterogeneity observed in MMDiT's attention landscape, building on the broader sparse attention literature from ViTs and LLMs (Swin, StreamingLLM, Minference) but adapting it to the diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With MMDiT now the dominant architecture behind widely deployed models (SD3, FLUX, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo), efficient attention compression for this specific design is practically critical for real-time and high-resolution generation. The reduction of calibration search from 10+ hours to ~15 minutes makes per-model compression configuration feasible for practitioners. However, the 1.5× end-to-end speedup is modest since attention is only a fraction of total inference cost, and the method has been validated only on two image-generation models at up to 2K resolution; whether the head-wise patterns and caching strategies transfer to video MMDiTs or require re-calibration remains an open question. Additionally, the reliance on a per-model calibration step (even if fast) limits the method's plug-and-play applicability compared to architecture-agnostic approaches.
