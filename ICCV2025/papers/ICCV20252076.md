## Problem

Stochastic Segmentation Networks (SSNs) model spatially correlated aleatoric uncertainty by placing a low-rank Gaussian over logit space, but the assumed rank (typically ~10) is almost surely underspecified for high-dimensional pixel-wise covariances, and the method suffers from training instabilities and the need for expensive mean pre-training. This paper proves that the effective rank of the induced pixel-space covariance grows only sublinearly with the assumed logit-space rank, establishing a fundamental expressivity limitation of the low-rank parameterisation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single medical image (128×128 CT patches for LIDC-IDRI, 256×256 fundus images for REFUGE2) and produces a family of stochastic segmentation masks by sampling from a learned conditional distribution, along with pixel-wise uncertainty maps derived from the sample ensemble. Unlike deterministic segmenters that output a single mask, Flow-SSN generates M independent samples (16–512) per image, each a full k×d one-hot label map, enabling direct comparison against multiple expert annotations via GED, Dice, and HM-IoU metrics.

## Method

Flow-SSN decomposes the logit-space distribution into two components: (i) an expressive, pixel-wise independent conditional base distribution p_{U|X}(u|x;λ) parameterised by a large UNet encoder-decoder that outputs per-pixel means and variances, and (ii) a lightweight flow φ that transforms the base samples into logits with full covariance structure. The discrete-time variant uses a single Inverse Autoregressive Flow layer (MADE-style masking) trained via expected categorical likelihood, while the continuous-time variant defines a linear interpolation path y_t = (1−t)u + ty and trains a small velocity-field network via a variational Flow Matching objective. Crucially, the Markovian projection of the ODE preserves marginals, so the flow network only needs to approximate E[y|y_t] with a network ~100× smaller than the prior, making ODE-based sampling ~10× cheaper than diffusion-based segmentation. This architecture allocates most model capacity to the learned prior rather than the flow, enabling arbitrarily high-rank pixel-wise covariances without storing distributional parameters or assuming a rank a priori.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI (128×128), the continuous-time Flow-SSN (Euler, T=250) achieves the best D_GED²(M) of 0.181, Dice of 0.611, and HM-IoU of 0.873, outperforming all baselines while using only 14M parameters (vs. 42M for MoSE and 63M for PHiSeg). On REFUGE-MultiRater (256×256), the discrete-time Flow-SSN with M=512 MC samples achieves D_GED²=0.081 and HM-IoU=0.881, setting new stochastic segmentation benchmarks. The continuous-time variant is ~10× more efficient than CCDM at inference, and can perform competitively with as few as 10 ODE solver steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow-SSN∞ (continuous-time) dominates all stochastic segmentation baselines on LIDC-IDRI: it improves D_GED²(M) over MoSE (0.181 vs. 0.189) and JProbUNet (0.181 vs. 0.206), and achieves a substantially higher Dice (0.611 vs. 0.511 for JProbUNet, 0.486 for PHiSeg) and HM-IoU (0.873 vs. 0.679 for P²SAM, 0.647 for JProbUNet). The discrete-time Flow-SSNΔ (single-step IAF) matches the continuous variant on HM-IoU (0.879) and is ~200× faster to sample from, though it lags on D_GED² and Dice. Compared to the original SSN [55], Flow-SSN improves D_GED²(M) from 0.225 to 0.181 and Dice from 0.436 to 0.611, while using fewer parameters (14M vs. 41M). On REFUGE-MultiRater, Flow-SSN outperforms deterministic baselines (TransUNet, SAM, MedSAM, MSA) by a considerable margin on Dice using only 15M parameters, and the discrete-time variant surprisingly outperforms the continuous-time one on this dataset, enabling evaluation with up to M=512 MC samples for further gains. The paper also shows that simply increasing the assumed rank in SSN collapses it to a deterministic model on real-world data, whereas Flow-SSN makes no rank assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow-SSN directly extends the Stochastic Segmentation Network (SSN) framework of Monteiro et al. by replacing the low-rank Gaussian over logit space with a two-component flow architecture: an expressive conditional base distribution (prior) and a lightweight flow to model pixel-wise dependencies. The continuous-time variant is trained via Flow Matching (Lipman et al.), while the discrete-time variant leverages Inverse Autoregressive Flows for fast sampling. The paper departs from diffusion-based segmentation (e.g., CCDM) by allocating most model capacity to the prior rather than the velocity field, and from VAE-based approaches by avoiding latent variable assumptions entirely. A key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof that the effective rank of SSN covariances grows only sublinearly with the assumed rank, establishing a fundamental expressivity limitation of the low-rank parameterisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flow-SSN addresses a practical bottleneck in stochastic segmentation: the inability of SSNs to model high-rank pixel-wise covariances without prohibitive compute, and the high inference cost of diffusion-based alternatives. The ~10× sampling efficiency over CCDM and the ability to use foundation models as priors open a path toward real-time uncertainty-aware segmentation in clinical settings. Open questions include whether the discrete-time > continuous-time result on REFUGE generalises, how the approach scales to 3D volumetric data, and whether the lightweight flow component can be further simplified or replaced by learned priors alone. The effective-rank theorem may also inform other low-rank approximation techniques beyond segmentation.
