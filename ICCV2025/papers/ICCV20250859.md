## Problem

Existing radar-based neural scene representations, such as Radar Fields, lack explicit noise modeling and can only synthesize preprocessed, noise-excluded radar images, making them unreliable under the multipath reflections, receiver saturation, and speckle noise that are ubiquitous in real-world autonomous driving radar data. This limits their ability to generate realistic synthetic radar data or produce clean 3D geometry estimates from raw sensor inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250859/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D polar range-power images from a Navtech 360° CIR304-H scanning radar (1.8° azimuth beamwidth, 0.9° scan resolution) along with GNSS/IMU-derived radar poses. It produces synthetic radar images from novel viewpoints, a 3D Gaussian scene representation, a binary occupancy map, and a radar inverse rendering decomposition that separates real target returns, multipath ghost reflections, and residual noise (speckle + saturation). LiDAR point clouds are used exclusively as ground-truth geometry for evaluation, not as a training input.

## Method

RadarSplat represents the scene as 3D Gaussians whose power return ratio σ is decomposed into reflectance (view-dependent via spherical harmonics), occupancy probability α, and a novel noise probability η, enabling the model to simultaneously render realistic noise and extract clean occupancy. An FFT-based detection pipeline identifies multipath and receiver-saturation beams, and a multipath source map stores each source's position, reflectivity, and exponential attenuation so that ghost reflections can be reconstructed from arbitrary novel views. The rendering pipeline projects Gaussians through elevation and azimuth antenna gain profiles and models spectral leakage with a Gaussian kernel to reproduce the characteristic range blur of FMCW radar. A dedicated denoising step (Gaussian smoothing + decay-region mask) produces a noise-free occupancy map that supervises training, while a regularization loss constrains α + η ≤ 1 to keep the two probabilities disentangled.

## Results

On the Boreas dataset, RadarSplat achieves 26.06 PSNR and 0.51 SSIM for radar image synthesis, outperforming Radar Fields (22.66 PSNR, 0.20 SSIM) by +3.4 PSNR and 2.6× SSIM. For 3D scene reconstruction against LiDAR ground truth, RadarSplat reduces RMSE from 3.03 m to 1.81 m (−40%) and improves occupancy accuracy from 0.59 to 0.91 (1.5×). The Relative Chamfer Distance drops from 0.29 to 0.04, indicating substantially tighter geometric alignment. Rendering speed reaches 4.5 FPS on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Radar Fields [Borts et al., SIGGRAPH 2024], a NeRF-based method for scanning radar that uses a dynamic threshold to remove noise during preprocessing. RadarSplat wins on every reported metric: +3.4 PSNR and 2.6× SSIM for image synthesis, and −40% RMSE with 1.5× occupancy accuracy for scene reconstruction. The gap is particularly large on R-CD (0.04 vs 0.29), suggesting Radar Fields' geometry is substantially misaligned, likely due to ghost artifacts from unmodeled multipath and saturation noise. Ablation results confirm that the noise-probability term (η) contributes the largest single improvement in synthesis quality (PSNR drops from 26.06 to 23.52 when removed), while multipath modeling adds a smaller but visually significant gain. The proposed denoising-based occupancy map also nearly halves RMSE compared to Radar Fields' dynamic-threshold + Bayesian grid approach (1.81 m vs 3.40 m in the ablation), indicating that the preprocessing pipeline itself is a major source of the overall improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RadarSplat is the first 3D Gaussian Splatting formulation for scanning radar in autonomous driving, building directly on the GS rendering pipeline of Kerbl et al. [26] and the spherical-coordinate projection strategy from LiHi-GS [33]. It departs from the closest prior work, Radar Fields [5] (a NeRF-based method), by replacing the implicit neural representation with explicit Gaussians and, critically, by modeling radar noise (multipath, receiver saturation, speckle) rather than removing it via a dynamic threshold. The radar rendering equation incorporates azimuth/elevation antenna gain profiles and a Gaussian approximation of spectral leakage, grounding the synthesis in the physical radar equation rather than learned image statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By synthesizing realistic noisy radar images from novel views, RadarSplat enables data augmentation for radar perception models without additional data collection—a significant practical benefit given radar's role in adverse-weather driving. The radar inverse rendering capability (decomposing signal, multipath, and noise) is a new diagnostic tool for understanding radar scene structure. However, the method is limited to static scenes, evaluated on a single dataset (Boreas) with a single baseline, and the 4.5 FPS rendering speed, while practical, is far below real-time. Extending to dynamic scenes and validating on additional radar datasets would strengthen the contribution considerably.
