## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks for autonomous driving rely on coarse-grained task categories (perception, prediction, planning), open-form QA with unreliable language-based metrics, and limited coverage of dynamic temporal reasoning. This makes it impossible to pinpoint specific cognitive weaknesses of VLMs in safety-critical driving scenarios or to validate whether domain-specific training genuinely improves driving-relevant capabilities without sacrificing generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes single-frame driving images (2,000 static scenes) and multi-frame video sequences (3,000 dynamic scenarios) alongside text questions, with optional visual prompts (bounding boxes or coordinate tuples) and descriptive tips for targeted perception tasks. Most questions are close-form multiple choice with 4–10 semantically similar options to enforce zero-tolerance accuracy; a subset requires structured outputs including detection boxes, traffic element relation graphs, and 6-waypoint 3-second trajectories conditioned on ego status and 4 historical waypoints.

## Method

VLADBench is constructed by manually curating 12,000 close-form QA pairs from 12 public driving datasets, organized hierarchically into 5 domains (Traffic Knowledge Understanding, General Element Recognition, Traffic Graph Generation, Target Attribute Comprehension, Ego Decision-Making and Planning), 11 aspects, and 29 tasks, with a rule-based filter and weighted accuracy/instruction-compliance scoring. For the empirical study, the authors start from InternVL2-4B and train domain-specific variants on ~1.4M QA pairs (categorized into the five domains by GPT-4) plus 1.3M general QAs, then evaluate all 20+ general and DS VLMs on VLADBench. The key experimental finding is that the five domains exhibit strong cross-domain synergy: training on one domain's data improves performance in others, and joint training yields up to 67% higher gains than single-domain training, while biased DS data degrades generalization in unrelated tasks. The paper further shows that upgrading the vision encoder (e.g., InternVL2's 6B-parameter encoder) is more impactful for AD performance than scaling the language model alone.

## Results

On VLADBench, only Qwen2.5-VL-72B surpasses a score of 60 (61.03 overall), while GPT-4o achieves 58.92 and the best small-scale open-source model (LLaVA-Video-7B) reaches 53.63. The paper's own domain-specific model (Ours-4B, built on InternVL2-4B) achieves 59.45 overall, outperforming all other small-scale models. Across the 10 secondary aspects, the most challenging tasks are Signal Element Relation (best: 49.56 by GPT-4o), Intention Judgment (best: 68.23 by Qwen-VL-7B), and Meta Decision-Making (best: 58.33 by InternVL2-76B), indicating substantial gaps in spatial reasoning, temporal prediction, and tactical driving decisions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 19 evaluated models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (61.03) is the only model to exceed 60, narrowly beating GPT-4o (58.92) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (57.19). The paper's Ours-4B model (59.45) outperforms all small-scale open-source baselines (best: LV-7B at 53.63) and all domain-specific models (best: DriLM-B-4B at 51.32), demonstrating the benefit of multi-domain training. Domain-specific models show task-level strengths—Dolphins-9B ranks 1st on vehicle cut-in (86.70) and DriveLM-4B ranks 1st on traffic light recognition (75.14)—but suffer significant generalization losses in other domains (e.g., DriveLM-4B drops sharply in TAC). Scaling the language model alone does not guarantee improvement: OV-72B and LV-72B show only marginal gains or even declines over their 7B counterparts, whereas InternVL2-76B (with a 6B vision encoder) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (with dynamic-resolution vision encoder) achieve the strongest results, suggesting vision encoder quality is more critical than LLM scale for AD tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against existing AD VLM benchmarks (DriveLM, DriveBench, nuScenes-QA, LingoQA, IDKB) that rely on coarse-grained task categories (perception/prediction/planning) and open-form VQA, arguing these are insufficient for assessing nuanced cognitive and reasoning abilities. It also departs from general VLM evaluation suites (MME, VideoMME, MMBench) that lack driving-specific elements. The key conceptual departure is the hierarchical close-form evaluation structure (5 domains → 11 aspects → 29 tasks) combined with a domain-specific training data exploration (1.4M QAs) to study inter-domain interactions, which no prior AD benchmark has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that only one model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) exceeds 60/100, exposing a substantial gap between current VLMs and human-level driving comprehension, particularly in traffic graph generation, intention judgment, and meta decision-making. The finding that upgrading the vision encoder (e.g., InternVL2's 6B encoder) is more impactful than scaling the language model challenges the prevailing assumption that LLM scale drives VLM performance in AD. Limitations include single-view evaluation only and straightforward DS training without data sampling optimization, leaving open questions about multi-view 3D spatial perception and scalable domain-specific fine-tuning strategies.
