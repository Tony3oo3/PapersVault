## Problem

Neural surface reconstruction faces three unresolved conflicts: strong reflections and indirect illumination violate photometric consistency assumptions causing geometric artifacts, sparse visual cues in textureless regions lead to over-regularization that erodes valid structures, and uniform Eikonal constraints prioritize smoothness over high-frequency details. Existing methods address these challenges in isolation—reflection-aware techniques introduce noise in low-texture areas, surface regularization oversmooths details, and high-fidelity methods struggle with view-dependent effects—leaving no unified framework that resolves all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces a continuous signed distance field (extractable as a watertight mesh via zero-level-set), novel view renderings, and—when extended to inverse rendering—decomposed BRDF material properties and HDR environment maps. The SDF network uses progressive multiresolution hash encoding from 32³ to 2048³ resolution with 64 stratified ray samples and 128 proposal updates per ray, while the radiance and feature MLPs condition on view direction via positional encoding.

## Method

HiNeuS unifies three mechanisms within a single SDF-based volume rendering pipeline. First, SDF-guided multi-view consistency computes per-view visibility factors by evaluating the SDF along rays from other cameras to a surface point (continuous occlusion without mesh tessellation), then uses a Mahalanobis-distance-based ambiguity factor to down-weight RGB supervision for rays affected by reflection. Second, ray-aligned planar regularization enforces local SDF linearity in textureless regions, with adaptive per-sample weights inversely proportional to radiance feature differences so that sharp edges are preserved while flat areas are regularized. Third, a rendering-prioritized Eikonal relaxation modulates the standard |∇f|−1 penalty by an exponential of the local rendering error multiplied by a planarity factor, automatically relaxing geometric constraints where appearance fidelity demands detail and tightening them in stable planar regions. The total loss L_rgb + L_planar + L_eikonal is jointly optimized, with the SDF-to-density mapping using a Laplace CDF and a separate reflection MLP compensating for indirect specular paths via physics-constrained secondary ray tracing.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark, HiNeuS achieves 0.43 mm average Chamfer distance, outperforming the strongest baseline RaNeuS (0.51 mm) by 15.7% and Neuralangelo (0.61 mm) by 29.5%. On NeRF-Synthetic, the method reaches 35.00 dB average PSNR, surpassing 3D Gaussian Splatting (32.68 dB) by 2.32 dB and Mip-NeRF (34.96 dB) by 0.04 dB. On Mip-NeRF 360, HiNeuS attains 29.50 dB average PSNR, a 1.15 dB improvement over RaNeuS (28.35 dB) and 1.81 dB over Mip-NeRF 360 (27.69 dB). On GlossySynthetic, the method reduces Chamfer distance to 0.0038 mm, a 21.4% improvement over RefNeuS (0.0048 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiNeuS achieves the best average Chamfer distance across all 15 DTU scenes (0.43 mm), beating the closest competitor RaNeuS (0.51 mm) by 15.7% and Neuralangelo (0.61 mm) by 29.5%, with per-scene wins in 14 out of 15 scenes (losing only scene 40 to RaNeuS by 0.02 mm). On NeRF-Synthetic, HiNeuS (35.00 dB) narrowly edges out Mip-NeRF (34.96 dB) by 0.04 dB while substantially outperforming 3DGS (32.68 dB) by 2.32 dB and all SDF-based methods (VolSDF 26.20 dB, NeuS 27.44 dB). On Mip-NeRF 360, the method leads RaNeuS (28.35 dB) by 1.15 dB and Mip-NeRF 360 (27.69 dB) by 1.81 dB, with the largest per-scene gain on bonsai (+0.69 dB over RaNeuS). On GlossySynthetic, HiNeuS (0.0038 mm) outperforms RefNeuS (0.0048 mm) by 21.4% and NeRO (0.0042 mm) by 9.5%, with the horse scene showing the largest margin (0.0045 vs 0.0062 mm over RefNeuS). The method consistently wins on both geometric (Chamfer) and photometric (PSNR) metrics across all four quantitative benchmarks, with no losses to any baseline on average scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiNeuS sits at the intersection of SDF-based neural surface reconstruction (NeuS, VolSDF) and reflection-aware methods (Ref-NeuS, NeRO), but departs from the prevailing pattern of addressing reflection, textureless-region regularization, and Eikonal over-smoothing as separate modules. Its key conceptual departure is replacing mesh-based visibility checks (Ref-NeuS) and learned reflection probabilities (NeRO) with continuous SDF-guided ray tracing for occlusion reasoning, and coupling an adaptive Eikonal weight to rendering error rather than applying it uniformly. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in that can be integrated into NeuS2, HF-NeuS, and Neuralangelo, positioning it as a general-purpose regularization layer rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of reflection ambiguity, textureless-surface collapse, and detail-geometry conflict in a single joint optimization is practically important for real-world scenes (urban environments, vehicles, metallic objects) where all three issues co-occur. The demonstrated extension to inverse rendering (BRDF decomposition, relighting) suggests the learned SDF + material pipeline could serve as a foundation for downstream tasks beyond geometry. However, the authors acknowledge limitations with occluded structures under limited views and deformable scenes, and the method's reliance on dense multi-view supervision restricts applicability to sparse-view or dynamic settings. Whether the SDF-based visibility mechanism scales efficiently to very large scenes (e.g., city-scale) remains an open question given the per-ray sampling cost.
