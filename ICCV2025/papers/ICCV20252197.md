## Problem

Existing 3D human reconstruction methods produce misaligned textures where colors bleed across part boundaries (e.g., jacket texture appearing on pants), because they rely on global image context without explicitly identifying local part semantics. No prior method explicitly infers dense 3D part segmentations and uses them as structural guidance for texture reconstruction, leaving the part-alignment problem largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view RGB image of a clothed person and produces a fully textured 3D human surface (as an MLP predicting RGB from 3D points) along with a 3D part-segmented surface where each vertex carries one of five semantic labels (face & hair, upper-clothes, lower-clothes, footwear, others). Geometry is obtained off-the-shelf via TeCH, so the pipeline's novel contributions operate on the textureless mesh: part labels are assigned per-vertex in 3D space, and textures are optimized per-part using score distillation sampling from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

PARTE decomposes reconstruction into two specialized stages. PartSegmenter renders normal maps from 30 uniformly sampled viewpoints of the textureless mesh and feeds them, alongside Sapiens-segmented front-view part maps, into SegmentNet—a dual-branch Transformer (10 layers, Sapiens-initialized and frozen) that fuses geometric and semantic cues via cross-branch self-attention to predict 2D part segments, which are then voted into a consistent 3D label field. PartTexturer optimizes an MLP color network using an SDS loss whose diffusion backbone is PartDiffusion: a StableDiffusion network (frozen) augmented with a fusion layer that injects three conditioning signals—rendered part segments at the current viewpoint, per-part image patches extracted from the front view via Sapiens, and per-part text prompts from BLIP—through self-attention, ensuring textures are structurally aligned to part boundaries while remaining visually consistent with the input image.

## Results

On THuman2.1, PARTE achieves PSNR 22.175, LPIPS 0.096, and Part IoU 0.641 for 3D texture reconstruction, outperforming the best optimization-based baseline TeCH (PSNR 21.089, LPIPS 0.108, Part IoU 0.588) and the best regression-based baseline SiTH (PSNR 20.692, LPIPS 0.120, Part IoU 0.535). On HuMMan, PARTE reaches PSNR 22.076, LPIPS 0.083, and Part IoU 0.589, with a particularly large Part IoU gain over TeCH (0.437) and SIFU (0.437). Geometry metrics (P2S, CD) are identical to TeCH since PARTE uses TeCH as its geometry backbone, confirming that the gains are purely from improved texturing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARTE matches TeCH exactly on geometry (P2S 2.984, CD 3.008 on THuman2.1; P2S 2.718, CD 2.667 on HuMMan) because it reuses TeCH as its geometry backbone, so all texture gains are attributable to the part-guided texturing pipeline. On texture quality, PARTE surpasses the strongest optimization-based baseline TeCH by +1.09 dB PSNR and +0.053 Part IoU on THuman2.1, and by +0.43 dB PSNR and +0.152 Part IoU on HuMMan. Against the best regression-based method SiTH, the PSNR gap is even larger (+1.48 dB on THuman2.1). The Part IoU metric, which directly measures part alignment, shows the most dramatic improvement on HuMMan: PARTE reaches 0.589 versus 0.437 for both TeCH and SIFU, indicating that explicit 3D part segmentation is especially critical for generalization to in-the-wild poses. Among the ablation baselines, PartDiffusion outperforms vanilla StableDiffusion (PSNR 19.276, Part IoU 0.472) and InstanceDiffusion (PSNR 20.860, Part IoU 0.527) in 3D texturing, confirming that jointly conditioning on part segments, front-view image, and text prompts is necessary for aligned reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARTE extends the optimization-based 3D human reconstruction paradigm (SDS loss, as in DreamFusion and TeCH) by inserting an explicit 3D part segmentation stage before texturing. It departs from prior methods (PIFu, SiTH, TeCH, SIFU, HumanRef) that rely on global image context or text prompts alone, by conditioning a modified StableDiffusion (PartDiffusion) on dense 3D part labels rendered from multiple viewpoints. The PartSegmenter module itself builds on the 2D-to-3D aggregation strategy and leverages Sapiens for 2D part segmentation, while the PartTexturer's fusion of part segments, front-view image patches, and BLIP text prompts via self-attention is a novel architectural combination not present in InstanceDiffusion or LayoutDiffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, visually obvious failure mode (texture bleeding across clothing boundaries) that prior SOTA methods still exhibit, and the two-stage decomposition (segment parts, then texture per-part) offers a clean conceptual framework that could transfer to other structured 3D reconstruction tasks. Limitations include dependence on TeCH for geometry (bounding overall quality), reliance on off-the-shelf Sapiens for 2D segmentation, and the computational cost of rendering 30 viewpoints for part voting. Open questions include whether end-to-end joint training of segmentation and texturing could further improve results, and whether the approach generalizes beyond humans to other articulated or multi-part 3D objects.
