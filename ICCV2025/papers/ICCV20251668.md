## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks (ADE-847, PC-459, VOC) exhibit high semantic similarity to the COCO training set (mean similarity 0.79–0.97), making them poor measures of true open-vocabulary generalization. This similarity also explains the counterintuitive finding that fine-tuning CLIP improves performance on these benchmarks, despite reducing the generalization that open-vocabulary tasks require. The paper addresses both the evaluation gap and the need for methods that genuinely handle unseen semantics.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image resized and cropped to 1024×1024 along with a set of text category names (ranging from 20 to 847 categories depending on the benchmark). It outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask assigning each pixel to one of the prompted categories or to an 'others/background' class. Notably, the proposed OpenBench explicitly includes 'others' pixels, forcing the model to reason about relative class relationships rather than simply matching the closest visual-linguistic correspondence, which existing datasets omit.

## Method

OVSNet encodes images and text with a frozen ConvNeXt-CLIP backbone and uses a Mask2Former decoder to produce masks and query embeddings. The key architectural contribution is Gradient-Free Aggregation (GFA), which fuses query embeddings with mask-pooled CLIP features via an iterative Random-Walk-inspired update solved in closed form using a Neumann series approximation, avoiding the overfitting to seen semantics that learning-based fusion (e.g., cross-attention) introduces. For training, Proxy Calibration (PC) expands the effective semantic space at zero additional cost by generating proxy class embeddings as random convex combinations of in-vocabulary embeddings (sampled from a Beta(γ,γ) distribution) and applying cosine-distance supervision on them. The final loss combines dice and cross-entropy segmentation losses, cross-entropy classification loss, and proxy supervision losses on both query and CLIP features.

## Results

OVSNet achieves state-of-the-art average mIoU of 44.8 (base CLIP) and 47.4 (large CLIP) across six benchmarks, outperforming the strongest prior method MAFT+ (43.5 / 46.0). On the proposed OpenBench, OVSNet reaches 44.9 mIoU (base) and 48.6 mIoU (large), surpassing all baselines by 1.2–6.2 points. The largest absolute gains appear on PC-459 (19.1 vs. 16.2 for MAFT+ with base CLIP) and ADE-847 (14.5 vs. 13.8), while on Pascal VOC the method trails SCAN by ~1.3 mIoU due to VOC's near-complete overlap with the training vocabulary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVSNet outperforms all nine baselines on five of six benchmarks with both base and large CLIP backbones, with the most consistent margin over MAFT+ (the strongest prior one-stage method): +1.2 mIoU on ADE-150, +2.9 on PC-459, and +1.2 on OpenBench (base CLIP). The sole exception is Pascal VOC, where SCAN (97.0/97.1) edges out OVSNet (95.7/96.9) by ~1.3 mIoU, which the authors attribute to VOC's near-complete semantic overlap with the COCO training set favoring CLIP-finetuning approaches. On the new OpenBench, OVSNet leads the next-best method (SCAN at 49.4 with large CLIP) by only 0.8 mIoU, but surpasses all other methods by 2.3–19.2 points, confirming that the benchmark exposes weaknesses in prior approaches. Two-stage methods (SimSeg, OVSeg) lag substantially behind one-stage methods across all benchmarks, with SimSeg scoring 19.4 mIoU on OpenBench versus OVSNet's 44.9. The gradient-free aggregation and proxy calibration components each contribute 1.4–2.9 mIoU individually and 2.6–4.8 mIoU combined over the Mask2Former baseline, with the largest gains on the more open-vocabulary sets (PC-459, OpenBench).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads in open-vocabulary segmentation: the architectural lineage from two-stage pipelines (SimSeg, OVSeg) through one-stage CLIP-integrated methods (SAN, FCCLIP, MAFT) to CLIP-fine-tuning approaches (CATSeg, SED, SCAN), and the evaluation methodology question. It builds directly on CLIP's pretrained vision-language alignment and Mask2Former's query-based decoder, but departs from the prevailing trend of fine-tuning CLIP by arguing that such adaptation narrows the semantic space and undermines true open-vocabulary generalization. The Gradient-Free Aggregation draws on Random Walk theory as an alternative to attention-based feature fusion, while Proxy Calibration borrows the convex-combination idea from ProxyDet for open-vocabulary detection and adapts it to the segmentation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OpenBench benchmark addresses a genuine gap in the OVS evaluation landscape: existing test sets are so semantically close to COCO training categories that they cannot distinguish between models that truly generalize and those that merely memorize training semantics. The finding that CLIP fine-tuning consistently degrades performance on OpenBench while improving on standard benchmarks reframes how the community should interpret prior results and motivates preserving CLIP's original alignment. Limitations include the benchmark being curated from existing datasets rather than collected fresh, and the 'others' category handling, which adds complexity but may not fully capture real-world ambiguity. Open questions include whether the gradient-free fusion and proxy calibration strategies transfer to other vision-language tasks and whether a truly open evaluation (with unbounded category sets) is feasible.
