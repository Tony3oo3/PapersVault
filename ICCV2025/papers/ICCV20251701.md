## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud classifiers are largely black boxes that provide only predictive probabilities without explaining which spatial regions drive a decision. In biomedical applications such as drug-response morphological profiling, this lack of interpretability prevents researchers from linking classification to biologically meaningful shape features. Most available interpretability methods for point clouds are post hoc (gradient-based or surrogate models), which have been shown to produce conflicting or unreliable explanations, while inherently interpretable approaches tend to be global rather than point-level.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered 3D point cloud of N points in Cartesian space (x, y, z) along with optional d-dimensional per-point features (typically surface normals, or the coordinates themselves when no features exist). Evaluation uses 1024 points per shape. The output is a class label coupled with a scalar importance score for every point in the cloud, where the score is class-specific for Instance, Additive, and Conjunctive pooling (obtained via softmax over the class dimension) but a general, class-agnostic importance weight for Attention pooling. Because interpretation is produced by the same forward pass as classification, no separate analysis step is required.

## Method

POINTMIL replaces the standard global-pooling + classifier head of point cloud backbones with a two-stage pipeline: (1) a point-level feature extractor (Transformer block adapted from 3DMedPT without farthest-point sampling, or PointNet/DGCNN/CurveNet/PointMLP with sampling removed) that retains a d-dimensional feature vector for every input point, and (2) one of four MIL pooling strategies—Instance, Attention, Additive, or Conjunctive—that simultaneously produce the bag-level class prediction and per-point importance scores. A contextual attention module further smooths attention weights by averaging over k-NN neighbourhoods (Eq. 6), addressing the sparsity of raw attention and yielding more spatially coherent explanations. The best configuration (Conjunctive pooling + Transformer backbone + contextual attention with k=12) achieves 97.3% mACC and 97.5% F1 on IntrA while localising aneurysm regions, drug-induced morphological changes, and object-specific shape motifs without any post hoc analysis.

## Results

On the IntrA biomedical dataset, POINTMIL with the Transformer backbone and Conjunctive pooling achieves state-of-the-art 97.3% mACC and 97.5% F1, outperforming the best prior method (3DMedPT at 92.2% mACC) by over 5%. On the RBC dataset, POINTMIL(Trans.) reaches 92.6% mACC, surpassing the best baseline (PointMLP at 91.8%) by 11.3% in mACC. For interpretability on IntrA, POINTMIL (Additive, Transformer) achieves 18.16 AOPCR and 0.613 NDCG@n, substantially exceeding post-hoc methods CLAIM (14.02/0.593) and PSM (6.52/0.320). On standard benchmarks, POINTMIL(PointNet) improves ScanObjectNN oACC from 68.2% to 73.8%, while POINTMIL(Trans.) achieves competitive but slightly lower scores than the strongest baselines (e.g., 92.7% vs. 94.1% oACC on ModelNet40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the biomedical datasets, POINTMIL(Trans.) dominates all baselines: it leads IntrA by 5.1% mACC over 3DMedPT (97.3% vs. 92.2%), RBC by 0.8% mACC over PointMLP (92.6% vs. 91.8%), and ATLAS-1 by 0.4% mACC over PointNeXt (84.0% vs. 83.6%). For interpretability on IntrA, POINTMIL (Additive, Transformer) achieves 18.16 AOPCR and 0.613 NDCG@n, beating the best post-hoc method CLAIM (14.02/0.593) and PSM (6.52/0.320) by large margins. On ModelNet40, POINTMIL(Trans.) trails the strongest baselines slightly (92.7% oACC vs. PointMLP's 94.1%), though POINTMIL(DGCNN) and POINTMIL(CurveNet)† remain within 0.6% of the best. On ScanObjectNN, POINTMIL(Trans.) scores 83.2% oACC, below PointNeXt (87.7%) and PointMLP (85.4%), but POINTMIL(PointNet) improves over vanilla PointNet by 5.6% oACC (73.8% vs. 68.2%), and POINTMIL(DGCNN) is within 0.4% of DGCNN. The paper notes that small decreases for DGCNN and PointMLP on ScanObjectNN fall within the standard deviations reported in the original papers (±0.5–0.8% oACC).

## Relation to Prior Work

POINTMIL reframes point cloud classification as a Multiple Instance Learning problem, where each point is an 'instance' and the full point cloud is a 'bag,' thereby inheriting point-level interpretability from MIL pooling rather than relying on post-hoc saliency methods (CLAIM, PSM) or global prototype-based approaches (XPCC, Interpretable3D). The feature extractor follows the Transformer block of 3DMedPT [64], while the contextual attention mechanism that smooths attention weights via k-NN neighbourhoods is adapted from CAMIL [19]. The four MIL pooling strategies (Instance, Attention, Additive, Conjunctive) are directly adopted from prior MIL literature [54, 24, 25, 15], making the core contribution a unification and adaptation of existing components into a coherent, inherently interpretable 3D classification pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that interpretability (MIL pooling) consistently improves classification accuracy across all tested backbones—rather than imposing a trade-off—this work challenges the assumption that explainability must come at a performance cost. The introduction of ATLAS-1, a dataset of drug-treated 3D cancer cells with biologically grounded morphological differences, provides a concrete benchmark for interpretable 3D classification in morphological profiling. Key open questions remain: linking highlighted morphological regions to specific molecular mechanisms, extending the framework to segmentation for richer point-level representations, and addressing the O(N²) computational cost of k-NN graph construction that limits scalability to very large point clouds.
