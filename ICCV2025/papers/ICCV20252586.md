## Problem

Existing 3D human digitization methods suffer from slow per-instance optimization, unstable cascade generation, or the ill-posed mapping from sparse 2D views to 3D geometry with inherent occlusion and invisibility. Compounding these architectural limitations, publicly available 3D human assets number only in the thousands, far too few to support the large-scale training that modern generative models require.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single 512×512 RGB image (front or arbitrary view) or a text prompt as conditioning, and produces a canonical 3D human Gaussian Splatting parameterized by position offsets, scale offsets, rotation, RGB color, and opacity on SMPL-X UV coordinates. The canonical Gaussian is then deformed to any target pose via differentiable LBS, enabling free-view rendering and pose-driven animation without re-optimization. During VAE training, 90 rendered views (30 horizontal, 30 upward, 30 downward) at 512×512 serve as supervision, with 6 input views and 10 randomly chosen target views per iteration.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: a UV-structured VAE and an MM-DiT diffusion generator. The VAE encoder takes multi-view renderings with Plücker ray embeddings, processes them through 3D convolutions, and uses learnable tokens defined in SMPL-X UV space (initialized via UV-map projection) to query multi-view contexts via cross-attention, followed by Conv-Atten dual-branch blocks that capture both global structure and fine-grained local details. The decoder upsamples the 16-channel latent into per-vertex Gaussian attributes on the UV grid, back-projects onto the SMPL-X T-pose template, and applies LBS for posing. The generator is an MM-DiT (up to 2B parameters) using DDPM v-prediction, which treats the conditional sequence (Sapiens features for image, T5-XXL for text) and noise as a single sequence with full attention, RoPE, and RMSNorm, learning the distribution shift from conditional features to structured latents rather than the direct 2D-to-3D mapping. This transforms the ill-posed reconstruction problem into a tractable conditional generation task, and the large-scale HGS-1M dataset (1M Gaussians from multi-view optimization of six human datasets plus 100k synthetic assets) provides the data volume needed for effective training.

## Results

On the image-to-3D human Gaussian generation task (evaluated on a 300k-subset of HGS-1M with 330 test IDs), SIGMAN achieves PSNR 25.45, LPIPS 0.044, and SSIM 0.939, substantially outperforming all baselines. When trained on the full HGS-1M dataset (1M assets), performance further improves to PSNR 26.14, LPIPS 0.040, and SSIM 0.943, demonstrating the benefit of large-scale data. The VAE reconstruction (Table 2a) also shows consistent gains, reaching PSNR 29.32 (30.53 with full data) versus 24.38–28.61 for alternative compression strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGMAN outperforms all three baselines by a wide margin across every metric. Against SIFU (per-instance optimization), the improvement is dramatic: +11.13 dB PSNR and a 2.8× reduction in LPIPS, reflecting the speed-quality tradeoff of optimization-based methods. Against the cascade method LGM, SIGMAN gains +4.58 dB PSNR and halves the LPIPS (0.044 vs. 0.086), showing that avoiding the unstable multi-view generation stage eliminates a major quality bottleneck. Against the feed-forward regression baseline GHG, the gap is +4.42 dB PSNR and a 40% LPIPS reduction (0.044 vs. 0.074), attributed to the latent-space paradigm avoiding the ill-posed 2D-to-3D mapping. Scaling from 300k to 1M training assets yields an additional +0.69 dB PSNR, confirming that the large-scale HGS-1M dataset provides meaningful gains beyond what smaller datasets can support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for 3D human digitization—per-instance optimization, feed-forward regression, and cascade multi-view generation—by introducing a latent-space generation pipeline (UV-structured VAE + MM-DiT) that reframes the ill-posed 2D-to-3D mapping as a conditional distribution shift, analogous to how Stable Diffusion operates in 2D. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the geometric representation, SMPL-X as the parametric prior for UV-structured latents, and the MM-DiT architecture from large-scale image/video generation, while leveraging AnimatableGaussians to construct training data from multi-view captures. The key architectural departure is the UV-structured latent with learnable tokens that query multi-view contexts via cross-attention, avoiding explicit geometry learning while decoupling appearance from pose for LBS-driven animation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HGS-1M dataset (1 million 3D human Gaussians with free-view rendering) is a major practical contribution that fills a critical gap—prior 3D human asset collections were limited to a few thousand IDs—and will likely become a standard benchmark for the field. The finding that loose clothing deformation (e.g., skirts) emerges implicitly from large-scale data without explicit physics modeling suggests a data-driven path to a long-standing challenge in human digitization. However, the model is limited to single-frame generation without temporal consistency, and the high compute cost (2B parameters, 48 A800 GPUs) may limit accessibility; future work on temporal Gaussians and multimodal control is identified as the natural next step.
