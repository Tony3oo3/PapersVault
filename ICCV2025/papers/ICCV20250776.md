## Problem

Reconstructing global surface CO2 from sparse ground-based observations (covering only 0.7% of the globe) traditionally relies on inversion models that are computationally expensive and depend on uncertain prior estimates of fluxes and emissions. While data-driven weather forecasting has advanced rapidly, whether such models can achieve CO2 reconstruction accuracy comparable to inversion models—without extensive priors—remains unexplored, particularly given the unique challenges of complex spatio-temporal transport dynamics, strong periodic patterns, and extreme observation sparsity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatio-temporal tensor of shape B×(K+1+E)×Δt×H×W at 2°×3° resolution, where channels include sparse CO2 spot observations (restricted to ground-based locations Z⊂Ω), densely sampled auxiliary variables (wind UAS/VAS, temperature, humidity, pressure, geopotential), and wind-derived periodic embeddings. The output is a full global CO2 concentration field in μmol/mol over the entire sphere Ω×[0,T], effectively performing spatio-temporal inpainting from the sparse observation mask to a complete field. On CarbonTracker the temporal resolution is 3-hourly, while CMIP6 uses monthly averages over a 165-year span.

## Method

CO2-Net formulates reconstruction as solving a constrained advection-diffusion PDE and decomposes the solution into a time-invariant particular solution (modeled by a spatial ViT expert processing even frames for intra-frame correlations) and a time-varying homogeneous solution (modeled by a temporal ViT expert tokenizing 2×p×q tubelets for inter-frame dynamics), with the two streams connected via B-CAST layer-wise cross-attention and fused by a three-layer MLP. Wind-based spatio-temporal embeddings are derived from a theorem proving the homogeneous solution is a unique series of spherical-harmonic × temporal-wave basis functions, guiding periodic embedding design that captures zonal/meridional transport. A semi-supervised wind-reconstruction loss—justified by a theorem showing the wind field is uniquely recoverable from the homogeneous solution—applies a linear probe to the temporal expert's hidden representations, enforcing physical consistency and mitigating CO2 observation sparsity. The model is instantiated in three sizes (S: 38M/1-layer, B: 95M/4-layer, L: 247M/12-layer ViT blocks) and trained with AdamW plus cosine annealing for 250 epochs.

## Results

On CMIP6 reanalysis data, CO2-Net (L) achieves a global RMSE of 5.31 μmol/mol and ACC of 0.99, reducing RMSE by 71% compared to the best data-driven baseline (ViT at 18.18). On CarbonTracker, CO2-Net (L) achieves 3.36 μmol/mol RMSE and 0.85 ACC, outperforming the best data-driven baseline (CycleGAN at 4.70). On real GLOBALVIEWplus observations, CO2-Net (L) reaches 7.81 μmol/mol RMSE, matching the inversion model 4D-Var (7.80) while outperforming all data-driven baselines by 6.7–21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CO2-Net (L) outperforms all seven baselines on both reanalysis datasets. On CMIP6, it reduces global RMSE by 71% versus ViT (18.18 → 5.31) and by 90% versus CycleGAN (51.48 → 5.31); even the smallest variant CO2-Net (S) at 38M parameters beats ViT by 11%. On CarbonTracker, CO2-Net (L) (3.36) surpasses the best data-driven baseline CycleGAN (4.70) by 28% and SwinLSTM (5.31) by 37%. On real GLOBALVIEWplus observations, CO2-Net (L) achieves 7.81 μmol/mol RMSE, essentially matching the 4D-Var inversion model (7.80) and beating COLA (7.94), while all data-driven baselines (ViT 9.10, SwinLSTM 8.34, CycleGAN 9.45) remain 6.7–21% worse. Kriging interpolation methods (Spherical, Exponential) perform poorly on CMIP6 (RMSE ~7.4) due to their static nature, and Senseiver, despite its low parameter count, struggles with spatio-temporal dynamics (RMSE 36.47 on CMIP6). The ablation confirms that the wind-based embedding and semi-supervised loss are specifically effective for CO2-Net's factorized architecture but degrade or have no effect on baseline models, indicating tight coupling between architecture and auxiliary design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

CO2-Net adapts the spatio-temporal factorization architecture from CAST (video action recognition) and the physics-informed embedding philosophy from ClimODE (weather forecasting), but re-derives both from the advection-diffusion PDE governing atmospheric transport: the spatial expert models the time-invariant particular solution, while the temporal expert models the time-varying homogeneous solution. Unlike ClimODE's generic daily/yearly periodic embeddings, CO2-Net's wind-based embeddings are analytically derived from the PDE's eigenstructure (spherical harmonics with zonal-wind phase shifts). The paper positions itself against traditional inversion models (4D-Var, COLA, Kalman filters) that require uncertain flux/emission priors, and against generic data-driven inpainting approaches (ViT, CycleGAN, SwinLSTM) that lack physical grounding for atmospheric dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical result is that a purely data-driven model matches inversion-model accuracy (7.81 vs 7.80 μmol/mol RMSE) on real GLOBALVIEWplus observations without requiring uncertain prior estimates of surface fluxes or emissions, which could enable faster, more scalable CO2 monitoring. The ablation revealing that wind-based embeddings and the semi-supervised wind-reconstruction loss only improve performance when paired with the PDE-derived spatio-temporal factorization (and actively hurt generic baselines) highlights a non-trivial architectural entanglement that may inform future physics-informed model design. Limitations include the absence of explicit conservation-law constraints, dependence on dense auxiliary meteorological data, and validation primarily on two reanalysis datasets plus one real-observation set; extending the framework to other atmospheric tracers (CH4, NO2) is suggested but not yet demonstrated.
