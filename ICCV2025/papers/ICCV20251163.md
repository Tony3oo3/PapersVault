## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: regression-based feed-forward approaches produce blurry results in unobserved regions, while optimization-based pipelines (e.g., CAT3D) require generating hundreds of synthetic views and then fitting a 3D representation, taking minutes to hours per scene. Additionally, high-quality 3D training data is extremely scarce compared to 2D image data, limiting the scalability of diffusion-based 3D generators. Bolt3D addresses both challenges by enabling fast feed-forward generation of full 360° scenes in under 7 seconds while training on a large-scale synthetic 3D dataset.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 1–4 posed RGB images at 512×512 resolution along with a set of target camera poses, and outputs 16 Splatter Images—pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians (color, 3D position, opacity, covariance) per view—that together form a complete renderable 3D Gaussian scene. Unlike prior methods that only reconstruct observed regions, Bolt3D generates content for unobserved regions by producing more output views than input views. Inference completes in ~6.25 s on a single H100 GPU (15 s on A100), compared to ~5 GPU-minutes for CAT3D.

## Method

Bolt3D factorizes 3D Gaussian generation into two stages: a multi-view latent diffusion model (initialized from CAT3D, using v-prediction) jointly denoises per-view color latents and geometry latents in a shared 64×64 latent space, and a multi-view U-ViT Gaussian head then regresses opacity, covariance, and refined color from the decoded images and pointmaps using photometric rendering losses. A key architectural contribution is a Geometry VAE (convolutional encoder + transformer decoder) trained from scratch to encode/decode pointmaps and camera raymaps, since the authors show that pre-trained image VAEs fail catastrophically on unbounded real-world geometry. To overcome 3D data scarcity, the authors construct ~300k multi-view-consistent 3D scenes by running MASt3R dense reconstruction with bundle adjustment over CO3D, MVImgNet, RE10K, and DL3DV-7K, supplemented with synthetic Objaverse renders. The diffusion model is trained in three stages (Geometry VAE → Gaussian head → latent diffusion) with progressive resolution (256→512) and view count (8→16) scaling.

## Results

On 1-view RealEstate10K, Bolt3D achieves 21.03 PSNR and 0.257 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the regression baseline Flash3D (17.40 PSNR, 0.419 LPIPS). Against the optimization-based CAT3D on 3-view RealEstate10K, Bolt3D reaches 27.00 PSNR vs. CAT3D's 29.56 PSNR while requiring only 0.25 GPU-minutes of inference versus CAT3D's 77.28 GPU-minutes — a 300× speedup. On 2-view DL3DV, Bolt3D achieves 17.75 PSNR and 0.392 LPIPS, beating DepthSplat (16.25 PSNR, 0.465 LPIPS). The largest quality gains over regression baselines appear in the 1-view setting where scene ambiguity is greatest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against regression-based methods, Bolt3D consistently wins: on 1-view RE10K it improves PSNR from 17.40 (Flash3D) to 21.03 and LPIPS from 0.419 to 0.257; on 2-view DL3DV it beats DepthSplat by 1.5 PSNR (17.75 vs 16.25) and 0.073 LPIPS. Against feed-forward generative methods, Bolt3D outperforms LatentSplat on PSNR (23.13 vs 22.62), SSIM (0.806 vs 0.777), and LPIPS (0.166 vs 0.196) on 2-view RE10K, though LatentSplat achieves a slightly better FID (2.79 vs 3.66), which the authors attribute to its adversarial training. Bolt3D also beats Wonderland on 1-view RE10K (21.54 vs 17.15 PSNR, 0.234 vs 0.292 LPIPS). Against the optimization-based CAT3D, Bolt3D trades roughly 2–3 PSNR for a 300× reduction in inference cost (0.25 vs ~77 GPU-minutes), and qualitatively produces sharper details in backgrounds and fine structures where CAT3D's optimization regresses to the mean. Qualitatively, Bolt3D also outperforms RealmDreamer on the shared test scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bolt3D sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: multi-view latent diffusion for 3D generation (CAT3D, which it initializes from) and pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian representations (Splatter Image, which it extends from single-view to multiple generated views). It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm—generate many views with a diffusion model, then optimize a 3D representation—by making the 3D scene a direct output of the diffusion process, eliminating the optimization stage entirely. Compared to prior feed-forward generative methods (LatentSplat, Wonderland), Bolt3D generalizes to arbitrary scenes and any number of input views (1–4) rather than single objects or fixed 2-view settings, and replaces the VAE-GAN framework with a latent diffusion model plus a dedicated Geometry VAE trained from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 300× reduction in inference cost (7 seconds vs. ~30 minutes) makes interactive, on-demand 3D scene generation practical for the first time, opening the door to real-time 3D content creation pipelines. The finding that pre-trained image VAEs fail catastrophically on unbounded geometry—requiring a purpose-built Geometry VAE with a transformer decoder—is a useful negative result for anyone encoding 3D structure in latent spaces. However, Bolt3D still trails CAT3D in reconstruction quality (e.g., 27.0 vs. 29.6 PSNR on RE10K), and it requires posed input images, leaving open whether the approach can handle unposed or single-image inputs without explicit camera parameters. Scaling beyond 16 generated views and closing the quality gap with optimization-based methods remain key challenges.
