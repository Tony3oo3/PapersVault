## Problem

Fast Adversarial Training (FAT) using single-step FGSM is highly susceptible to Catastrophic Overfitting, where multi-step robust accuracy collapses to zero during training. While prior work identified that single-step perturbations embed label information that models overfit to, the paper uncovers that this label information is remarkably transferable across different samples post-CO, and that adversarial initialization strategies work by inadvertently removing this label information rather than simply improving perturbation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251055/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels, evaluated under L∞ perturbation budgets of 8/255 or 16/255 (12/255 for Tiny-ImageNet). Unusually, the training procedure also consumes a fixed uniform gray image (all pixel values 0.5) to precompute class-specific label information perturbations, requiring only extra memory proportional to the number of classes rather than the full training set size.

## Method

LIET (Label Information Elimination Training) proactively removes transferable label information from single-step adversarial perturbations during training. It first generates class-specific FGSM perturbations from a uniform gray image (value 0.5), which empirically carry strong transferable label information; these perturbations are then added or subtracted as initialization before the standard FGSM step on each training sample, ensuring the final adversarial example is unlikely to retain the label information that causes CO. The training objective combines standard cross-entropy on the adversarial example with a Jensen-Shannon divergence term that enforces prediction consistency between clean and perturbed inputs, smoothing the loss landscape. A Non-uniform Label Smoothing (NLS) trick further obscures label information by distributing the smoothing probability α across incorrect classes with random weights rather than uniformly, adding stochasticity that makes it harder for perturbations to exploit residual label cues.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), LIET achieves 56.08% PGD-50 robust accuracy at ε=8/255 and 33.10% at ε=16/255, the best among all FAT methods, approaching the multi-step PGD-AT-WA (54.08%/31.17%). Under AutoAttack, LIET reaches 50.01%/25.22% (ε=8/255/16/255), matching or exceeding all FAT baselines. On CIFAR-100, LIET attains 32.74% PGD-50 and 27.05% AA at ε=8/255; on Tiny ImageNet, 26.54% PGD-50 and 19.79% AA at ε=8/255. Training cost is 101.29 min / 10.93 PFLOPs on CIFAR-10, roughly 1.25× faster than GAT and NuAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIET outperforms all FAT baselines on PGD-50 robust accuracy at both perturbation budgets. At ε=16/255 on CIFAR-10, LIET (33.10%) surpasses the next-best FAT method N-FGSM (31.65%) by 1.45 points and dramatically exceeds FGSM-PGI (8.78%), FGSM-UAP (6.03%), and GAT (4.44%), which all suffer severe catastrophic overfitting at this budget. Under AutoAttack at ε=8/255, LIET (50.01%) edges out NuAT (50.04%—essentially tied) and FGSM-PGI (49.73%), while at ε=16/255 it leads by a wide margin (25.22% vs. 0.13% for NuAT and 0.11% for FGSM-PGI). On CIFAR-100, LIET achieves 32.74% PGD-50 and 27.05% AA at ε=8/255, beating FGSM-PGI (32.31%/26.76%) and FGSM-UAP (31.81%/26.29%). On Tiny ImageNet, LIET leads all FAT methods with 26.54% PGD-50 and 19.79% AA at ε=8/255, outperforming FGSM-PGI (26.39%/19.52%) and N-FGSM (25.10%/18.76%). Overall, LIET's advantage is most pronounced at ε=16/255 where most FAT methods collapse due to CO, and it approaches multi-step PGD-AT-WA performance at roughly 3.5× lower training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fast adversarial training (FAT) literature by building on the decoupling of single-step perturbations into data-information and self-information components (He et al., 2023) and the adversarial initialization strategies of FGSM-PGI and FGSM-UAP. Rather than treating CO as a purely optimization or overfitting problem, the authors reframe it through the lens of transferable label information embedded in FGSM perturbations, proposing that adversarial initialization works by inadvertently removing this information. The method (LIET) combines this label-information-elimination initialization with Jensen-Shannon divergence regularization and non-uniform label smoothing, drawing on regularization-based FAT (Grad-Align, NuAT) and label smoothing for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a mechanistic explanation for CO that is more specific than prior 'overfitting to early adversarial patterns' accounts, and the label-information transferability finding (a single gray-image perturbation shifting 10%→54% of test images to a target class) provides a concrete diagnostic for practitioners. However, the practical gains are modest: LIET matches or slightly exceeds the best FAT baselines (N-FGSM, FGSM-PGI) but remains well below multi-step PGD-AT, and experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet-18/PreResNet-18. Open questions include whether the label-information-elimination principle generalizes to larger architectures, higher-resolution datasets, or non-FGSM single-step attacks, and whether the non-uniform label smoothing component contributes meaningfully beyond standard label smoothing at scale.
