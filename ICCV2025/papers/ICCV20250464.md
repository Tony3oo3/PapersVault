## Problem

Existing retrieval-based human motion video systems (GVR, TANGO) are restricted to co-speech gesture generation and rely on linear motion blending for frame interpolation, which fails for complex dynamic motions like dancing (only 17% of dance gestures are well-approximated by linear blending vs. 78% for speech gestures). There is no comprehensive retrieval-plus-generation framework that handles general human motion with diverse conditional signals such as music, motion tags, and text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250464/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a reference video (converted into a motion graph) plus a conditional signal—music audio, motion tags, text prompt, or sparse keyframes—and outputs a temporally coherent human motion video at 256×256 resolution. The core VFI module (HMInterp) specifically takes a start and end RGB frame pair and generates 12 interpolated frames (0.5 s at 24 FPS), with an optional background reorganization stage for dynamic scenes. Path searching supports both offline dynamic programming and real-time beam search, enabling applications from offline editing to real-time video game generation.

## Method

HMInterp is a dual-branch diffusion-based VFI model built on AnimateDiff that fuses a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) for non-linear 2D skeleton interpolation with a UNet-based VFI backbone for pixel-level frame synthesis. The MDM replaces the vanilla 8-layer transformer with a UNet-like architecture using skip connections and feature concatenation to produce accurate non-linear motion trajectories, while the VFI backbone incorporates ReferenceNet for hierarchical latent feature injection from start/end frames and an improved Reference Decoder (initialized from Stable Video Diffusion's temporal decoder) that uses duplicated reference frames instead of zero-padding to preserve fine details. A key training innovation is condition progressive training: the model first trains with identity-strong image conditions (seed images) for 100k iterations, then fine-tunes with identity-weak pose conditions for only 8k iterations, preventing the appearance inconsistencies that arise from joint or pose-first training. The full Video Motion Graphs system wraps HMInterp in a four-stage pipeline—graph initialization with pose-distance edges, task-specific rule-based path searching, HMInterp frame interpolation, and optional background removal/regeneration—supporting four generation tasks: motion-tag action, text-to-motion, music-driven dance, and speech-driven talking avatar.

## Results

On the full-system human motion video generation task (Table 1), Video Motion Graphs with f=32 masked frames achieves PSNR 42.91, LPIPS 0.009, MOVIE 37.31, and FVD 0.180, substantially outperforming the best pose-driven baseline MimicMotion (PSNR 36.30, FVD 1.078) by a wide margin on all four metrics. Even under the most challenging setting (f=216 frames to interpolate), the method retains PSNR 39.75 and FVD 0.799, still surpassing every generative baseline. In the user study (Table 2), the system achieves 84.99% overall preference for dance, 70.24% for gesture, and 61.05% for action generation, with texture quality being the primary driver of the win.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four pose-driven generative baselines (AnimateAnyone, MagicPose, UniAnimate, MimicMotion), Video Motion Graphs wins decisively on all four metrics at every masking level; the gap is largest in FVD (0.180 vs. 1.078–1.369) and LPIPS (0.009 vs. 0.042–0.048), reflecting the retrieval-based approach's advantage in preserving real video texture. In the VFI module comparison (Table 4), HMInterp (s=1) outperforms non-diffusion methods FILM (PSNR 37.57, FVD 1.303) and VFIFormer (PSNR 36.29, FVD 1.390), as well as diffusion-based DCInterp (PSNR 36.73, FVD 1.374) and ACInterp (PSNR 37.57, FVD 1.280), achieving the best PSNR (39.53), LPIPS (0.034), and FVD (1.210). In the user study, the system beats DanceAnyBeat [48] on dance (84.99% preference), S2G-Diffusion [14] on gesture (70.24%), and Text2Performer [21] on action (61.05%), with the largest margin in cross-modal alignment for dance (88.39%). The method's advantage is most pronounced in texture quality (69–82% preference) rather than motion alignment, where it is competitive but not dominant for gesture and action tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrieval-based motion graph framework from GVR and TANGO (designed exclusively for co-speech gesture videos) to general human motion (dancing, kung fu, etc.), with the critical departure being the replacement of linear motion blending with a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) for non-linear skeletal interpolation. The VFI backbone is built on AnimateDiff, augmented with ReferenceNet from AnimateAnyone/MagicAnimate for identity preservation and a modified Reference Decoder from ToonCrafter for low-level detail retention. The condition progressive training strategy—sequentially training identity-strong (image) before identity-weak (pose) conditions—is a novel training recipe not present in prior pose-to-video or VFI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retrieval-plus-interpolation can substantially outperform pure generative models on human motion video quality (PSNR 42.9 vs ~36 for pose-driven generators), with a 100-second reference database already surpassing generative baselines—a practical threshold for real-world deployment. The 78% vs 17% statistic quantifying the inadequacy of linear blending for dance vs. speech gestures provides clear motivation for the MDM-based approach. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, handling of multi-person scenes, and whether the condition progressive training insight generalizes beyond the specific AnimateDiff backbone. The system's dependence on a curated reference video and separate background reorganization pipeline limits its end-to-end autonomy.
