## Problem

Continual MIL for histopathological WSI analysis has been explored primarily for bag-level classification, but instance-level localization under sequential task shifts remains largely unaddressed. Existing continual localization methods for natural images rely on global relationships among small patches (e.g., 16×16) via pre-trained models, which is infeasible for MIL where ~10^5 large patches (256×256) lack clear global spatial structure. This paper tackles the dual challenge of maintaining both bag and instance classification performance across continually arriving organ/tumor tasks while minimizing catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes histopathological WSIs as bags of up to 10^5 patches (256×256 at 20× magnification), with features extracted by a frozen UNI foundation model and only bag-level weak labels available during training. It produces a bag-level class prediction, per-instance pseudo-labels for tumor localization, and attention maps indicating critical regions. Under the continual setup, the model sequentially processes five organ datasets (SLN, liver, prostate, pancreas, colon) or four tumor subtypes (NSCLC, BRCA, RCC, ESCA) without access to prior task data.

## Method

CoMEL combines three components: (1) Grouped Double Attention Transformer (GDAT) reduces attention complexity from O(M²) to O(mM) by grouping instances into regional tokens and applying two sequential efficient attentions, with a residual value connection to preserve instance diversity; (2) Bag Prototypes-based Pseudo-Labeling (BPPL) normalizes attention into class probabilities, constructs positive and negative bag prototypes via weighted instance aggregation, computes instance pseudo-labels through cosine similarity to residual features relative to the prototype midpoint, and adaptively filters low-confidence labels using a threshold tied to current bag-classification accuracy; (3) Orthogonal Weighted Low-Rank Adaptation (OWLoRA) is a rehearsal-free continual learning strategy that decomposes each task's weight update into low-rank matrices with orthonormal bases, enforcing intra-orthogonality via a Frobenius-norm loss and inter-orthogonality by projecting gradients onto the subspace orthogonal to all previously learned bases, thereby preventing task interference without storing prior data.

## Results

On the continual instance classification task (tumor detection across 5 sequential organ datasets from CAMELYON16 and PAIP), CoMEL achieves 72.64% instance accuracy, 41.87% IoU, 51.44% Dice, and 62.96% bag accuracy, with only 14.00% forgetting. This outperforms the strongest LoRA-based baseline InfLoRA (70.17% ACC_inst, 31.87% IoU, 40.84% Dice) by 2.47% in instance accuracy and 10.00% in IoU. On continual bag classification (tumor subtyping on TCGA), CoMEL reaches 87.94% ACC_bag with 10.12% forgetting, surpassing the best rehearsal-free baseline QPMIL-VL (86.53%, 12.17%). The upper bound (joint training with full labels) is 90.94% ACC_inst and 67.35% IoU, indicating CoMEL closes a substantial portion of the gap under the weakly-supervised continual setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMEL outperforms all 15 baselines across every metric in both continual instance and bag classification. Against the strongest rehearsal-based methods (ER/100, ConSlide/30), CoMEL gains 4.01–6.13% in ACC_inst and 21.84–24.64% in IoU, while also achieving higher ACC_bag (62.96% vs. 50.43% and 56.73%), demonstrating that rehearsal-free OWLoRA surpasses memory-based approaches without storing prior-task data. Compared to the prompt-tuning baseline QPMIL-VL, CoMEL improves ACC_inst by 3.89%, IoU by 16.00%, and ACC_bag by 3.68%, showing that orthogonal low-rank adaptation is more effective than task-specific prompts for MIL localization. Among LoRA-based methods, CoMEL extends InfLoRA by 2.47% in ACC_inst and 10.00% in IoU, with the gap widening on Dice (51.44% vs. 40.84%), confirming that the intra- and inter-orthogonality constraints in OWLoRA reduce task interference more effectively than InfLoRA's interference-free design alone. On continual bag classification (TCGA), CoMEL achieves 87.94% ACC_bag and 10.12% forgetting, beating QPMIL-VL (86.53%, 12.17%) and ConSlide (84.88%, 14.35%), while regularization-based methods (EWC, LwF) suffer severe forgetting (>61% Forget_bag). Notably, CoMEL remains robust across different feature extractors (UNI, ResNet50, PLIP, CONCH), consistently outperforming all CL baselines in Dice score regardless of backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMEL sits at the intersection of attention-based MIL for WSI analysis (building on ABMIL [24] and the grouping strategy from R²T-MIL [56]) and LoRA-based continual learning (extending InfLoRA [37] and Qiao & Mahdavi [47] with explicit intra- and inter-orthogonality constraints). It departs from rehearsal-based continual MIL (ConSlide [22]) by being rehearsal-free, addressing privacy concerns in clinical settings, and from prompt-tuning approaches (QPMIL-VL [17]) by not requiring vision-language foundation models. The key conceptual departure from prior continual MIL work is the explicit treatment of instance-level localization as a first-class objective alongside bag classification, rather than evaluating only slide-level accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: WSI data volumes grow rapidly (hundreds of TB per year in large hospitals), necessitating models that adapt to new organs and tumor subtypes without retraining from scratch, while preserving diagnostic localization quality. The finding that rehearsal-based CL methods effective for bag classification still suffer severe forgetting in instance localization (e.g., ER/100 achieves 50.4% ACC_bag but only 20.0% IoU) highlights that bag-level and instance-level forgetting are distinct failure modes requiring different solutions. Open questions include scalability beyond 5–6 sequential tasks, generalization to other foundation models beyond UNI, and whether the orthogonality-based approach transfers to other continual learning domains with dense prediction objectives.
