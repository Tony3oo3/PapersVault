## Problem

MLLMs used as judges for text-to-image generation suffer from overconfidence, bias, and inconsistent calibration across diverse image domains. While Bayesian Prompt Ensembles (BPE) improve calibration for text-only LLM judges by learning prompt weights via variational inference, standard ensembling methods fail to generalize to multimodal TTI tasks because the optimal prompt for a given judgment can depend on the visual content of the image. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to condition prompt ensemble weights on image characteristics to achieve reliable, calibrated multimodal evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of TTI-generated images along with their shared text caption, and queries a frozen black-box MLLM (GPT-4o) with N semantically equivalent task prompts to obtain per-prompt class probabilities. An additional unlabeled support set of images (256×K samples) is embedded via CLIP ViT-B16 and clustered to define the group structure. The output is a single calibrated preference probability per image pair, computed as a weighted mixture over prompts and image groups, enabling downstream selective classification or deferral of low-confidence judgments to human reviewers.

## Method

MMB (Multimodal Mixture-of-Bayesian Prompt Ensembles) generalizes BPE by introducing a soft image-grouping layer: images are embedded with CLIP ViT-B16, partitioned into K clusters via spherical k-means (FAISS, cosine distance), and each test image receives a soft assignment p(z|x) ∝ exp(sim(φ_I(x), g_z)/τ) over cluster centroids. The variational objective then learns group-specific prompt weights w_za by maximizing a per-group log-likelihood on a small validation set, weighted by the soft group assignment, minus a per-group entropy regularizer that prevents collapse onto a single prompt. At inference, the final probability is an expectation over both group assignments and prompts: p(y|x) ≈ Σ_z p(z|x) Σ_a w_za* p(y|x,a). The temperature τ interpolates between independent per-group BPE (τ→0) and a single global BPE (τ→∞), and the method reduces to BPE when K=1 or to a uniform average ensemble when no validation data is available.

## Results

On HPSv2 (10 prompts, 20 validation samples), MMB achieves an ECE of 0.091, a 20% reduction over BPE (0.114) and 32% over the unweighted average (0.133), while also attaining the best F1 (0.778), Kappa (0.627), and NLL (0.430) among all methods. MMB's advantage is consistent across the full factorial design: at 20 prompts and 50 samples, ECE drops to 0.076 and MCE to 0.154, both significantly better than BPE (0.113 / 0.275). On MJBench-Bias, MMB achieves the second-lowest average confidence (0.703–0.705) on the synthetic no-preference split, balancing fairness with strong discriminative performance, whereas the unweighted average is most conservative (0.654–0.683) but underperforms on HPSv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMB outperforms all four baselines on HPSv2 across every reported metric. Against the SOTA BPE [Tonolini et al., 2024], MMB reduces ECE by 20% (0.091 vs. 0.114), MCE by 43% (0.160 vs. 0.279), and NLL by 21% (0.430 vs. 0.547), while simultaneously improving F1 by 3.3% (0.778 vs. 0.753) and Kappa by 4.2% (0.627 vs. 0.602). The unweighted average ensemble (Avg.) is competitive on AUC-PR (0.849 vs. MMB's 0.848, not significantly different) but lags substantially on calibration (ECE 0.133 vs. 0.091) and discriminative metrics. Single-prompt baselines (STD. and BEST) are clearly inferior, with BEST achieving only 0.126 ECE and 0.763 F1. On MJBench-Bias, the unweighted average produces the lowest confidence (0.654–0.683) on the no-preference split, but MMB (0.703–0.705) is the best method that also maintains top-tier HPSv2 performance, making it the most balanced choice for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Prompt Ensembles (BPE) [Tonolini et al., Findings of ACL 2024], which learns global prompt weights via variational inference for text-only black-box LLMs, into the multimodal domain by introducing a mixture structure over image clusters. The key conceptual departure is that BPE assumes all prompts are equally relevant a priori across all inputs, whereas MMB conditions prompt weights on soft image-group assignments derived from CLIP embeddings and spherical k-means clustering. It also situates itself within the broader MLLM-as-a-judge literature (GPT-4V, X-IQE, MLLM-Bench, ViGOR) and prior prompt-ensembling work (Hou et al., Jiang et al., Wightman et al.), but is the first to explicitly address the failure of unimodal ensembling assumptions in vision-language evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibrated MLLM judges enable cost-aware evaluation pipelines where low-confidence judgments can be deferred to human reviewers, making large-scale TTI benchmarking more practical and trustworthy. The finding that SOTA text-only ensembling (BPE) actually degrades discriminative power in multimodal settings highlights a gap that practitioners building TTI evaluation systems are likely to encounter. Limitations include dependence on a single judge model (GPT-4o), two benchmarks, and clustering hyperparameters (K, temperature) that, while shown to be robust, add tuning burden. Open questions include whether the image-cluster inductive bias generalizes to other multimodal tasks (VQA, video) and whether the approach transfers across different MLLM backbones.
