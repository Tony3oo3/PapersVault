## Problem

Existing video reasoning segmentation (VRS) methods struggle in complex real-world scenes with long durations, multiple objects, rapid motion, and heavy occlusions. Prior approaches like VISA suffer from error accumulation in key-frame selection, while VideoLISA uses non-adaptive sampling and a single segmentation token that cannot model multiple interacting instances across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252464/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip of T frames (processed at 224×224 resolution, 8 frames per batch) together with a free-form text query instruction. It produces both a natural-language text response and a binary segmentation mask sequence {m_t} ∈ R^{T×H×W} as mask tracklets. For long videos, a preliminary key-segment extraction step first reduces the input to query-relevant sub-clips before the main segmentation pipeline processes them.

## Method

ViLLa builds on an InternVideo2 visual encoder, QFormer text encoder, and Vicuna-7B LLM, and introduces three core modules. (1) A Key Segment Extractor leverages Grounded-VideoLLM to identify query-relevant temporal spans, adaptively partitioning long videos into shorter semantically dense segments. (2) A Context Synthesizer performs cross-attention between text embeddings and multi-scale visual features to inject frame-specific visual cues into the text, then retains only the top-K highest-response embeddings to keep the LLM input compact. (3) A Hierarchical Temporal Synchronizer employs dual-scale (frame-level and video-level) segmentation tokens processed through a Mask2Former-style transformer decoder, with a momentum-based aggregation (γ=0.03) that gradually modulates video-level tokens with frame-level information to disentangle multi-object interactions across temporal scales. Training is end-to-end with a weighted combination of autoregressive text loss and per-scale BCE+Dice mask losses, using LoRA fine-tuning on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the newly proposed VideoReasonSeg benchmark, ViLLa-InternVideo2-6B achieves 55.4 J&F and 49.9% multiple-choice accuracy overall, outperforming the strongest prior method VISA-InternVideo2-6B (50.5 J&F, 40.5% accuracy) by 4.9 J&F and 9.4 accuracy points. On established referring video segmentation benchmarks, ViLLa reaches 67.5 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 74.3 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, and 49.4 J&F on MeViS, surpassing VISA by 4.5, 3.9, and 4.9 J&F respectively. On ReVOS, ViLLa achieves 57.0 J&F, a 4.6-point improvement over the best prior LLM-based method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLLa outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest LLM-based competitors, ViLLa-InternVideo2-6B beats VISA-InternVideo2-6B by 4.9 J&F (55.4 vs 50.5) and 9.4 MC accuracy (49.9 vs 40.5) on VideoReasonSeg, and by 4.5/3.9/4.9 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS/Ref-DAVIS17/MeViS. VideoLISA-InternVideo2-6B trails by 7.6 J&F (55.4 vs 47.8) on VideoReasonSeg, attributed to its single segmentation token and non-adaptive sampling. Among traditional referring VOS methods, DsHmp [18] is the strongest non-LLM baseline (67.1 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS), but ViLLa still edges ahead by 0.4 J&F there while leading by 9.4 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17 (74.3 vs 64.9) and 3.0 J&F on MeViS (49.4 vs 46.4). Earlier methods like MTTR [5], ReferFormer [64], and LMPM [12] are substantially outperformed (15.6–23.1 J&F vs 55.4 on VideoReasonSeg), reflecting the gap between non-reasoning and reasoning segmentation. On ReVOS, ViLLa leads VISA-InternVideo2-6B by 4.6 J&F (57.0 vs 52.4), confirming consistent gains across reasoning-focused benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLLa extends the video reasoning segmentation (VRS) task introduced by VISA and VideoLISA, which themselves adapted image-level reasoning segmentation (LISA, PixelLM) to the temporal dimension. It departs from VISA's key-frame-selection-plus-tracker pipeline and VideoLISA's single-segmentation-token design by introducing three targeted modules: a Key Segment Extractor (leveraging Grounded-VideoLLM for adaptive temporal grounding), a Context Synthesizer (QFormer-based cross-attention to inject visual cues into text embeddings), and a Hierarchical Temporal Synchronizer (multi-scale frame/video segmentation tokens with momentum-based aggregation, derived from the Mask2Former decoder). The architecture is built on InternVideo2 as the vision backbone and Vicuna-7B as the LLM, fine-tuned with LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VideoReasonSeg benchmark (3k videos, 15k instruction-mask pairs spanning multiple objects, rapid motion, and long-video occlusion) fills a gap in evaluating reasoning segmentation under realistic complexity, and its GPT-4V-aided annotation pipeline is reusable. The hierarchical multi-scale token design offers a practical recipe for combining frame-level and video-level segmentation in LMM-based pipelines. Open questions include scalability beyond 8-frame inputs, the cost of relying on a separate Grounded-VideoLLM for segment extraction, and whether the momentum-based aggregation generalizes to substantially longer or higher-resolution videos.
