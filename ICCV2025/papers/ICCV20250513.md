## Problem

Existing 3D multimodal representation learning methods (Uni3D, ULIP, OpenShape) rely on point clouds as the 3D input, whose sparse and discrete nature limits texture capture and reconstruction fidelity. Although 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offers substantially superior geometric and textural representation, no prior work has built a unified vision-language representation framework directly on 3DGS, leaving a gap between the best 3D representation technology and multimodal 3D understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250513/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3DGS models (each containing ~10–20K gaussian points with position, RGB color at SH degree 0, opacity, scale, and rotation attributes), 36 rendered images per shape at 512×512 resolution, and a single unifying text caption per shape from Cap3D. The 3DGS is first decomposed into local patches via FPS & kNN, then serialized into a token sequence processed by a transformer, producing a single 3DGS embedding that is aligned in the same feature space as frozen EVA-CLIP image and text embeddings. Downstream, this embedding supports retrieval (3D↔text, 3D↔image), zero-shot classification, and few-shot classification.

## Method

CLIP-GS introduces a GS Tokenizer that converts raw 3DGS patches into serialized tokens: opacity and scale are Sigmoid-normalized, rotation is linearized to a 3×3 matrix, and a multi-way spatial ordering strategy (xyz, Hilbert curve, Z-order) reorganizes patches before a refinement block fuses point-cloud-encoder features (for position and color) with 1×3 convolution features (for remaining attributes). The resulting token sequence is processed by transformer layers initialized from Uni3D's point-cloud pre-trained weights rather than 2D image weights, which ablations show yields a +14.6 Top1 advantage over 2D initialization. Training uses a contrastive loss between 3DGS and text embeddings (one-to-one) plus a novel image voting loss that handles the one-to-many 3DGS-to-image correspondence by sampling K=5 rendered views and weighting each view's contrastive contribution by its CLIP text-image similarity score, thereby guiding gradient direction toward the most semantically consistent viewpoints. The entire framework is trained on only ~240K 3DGS triplets generated from Objaverse, leveraging transfer from the pre-trained point-cloud encoder to achieve strong multimodal alignment at a fraction of the data scale used by prior point-cloud methods.

## Results

On multimodal retrieval (Objaverse-GS), CLIP-GS achieves R@1 of 36.8% (Text→3D), 30.0% (3D→Text), 75.6% (Image→3D), and 56.9% (3D→Image), surpassing the best point-cloud baseline Uni3D by +9.0, +6.9, +10.5, and +7.6 points respectively. For zero-shot classification, CLIP-GS reaches 48.5% Top-1 on Objaverse-GS and 86.7% Top-1 on ModelNet-GS, outperforming Uni3D by +0.8% and +0.5%. In few-shot classification on ModelNet-GS (10-shot, 10-way), CLIP-GS achieves 95.4% ± 0.2% accuracy, exceeding the prior best PointRWKV (94.8% ± 2.8%) with substantially lower variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-GS outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated task. In multimodal retrieval, it surpasses the strongest point-cloud multimodal baselines—Uni3D [63], OpenShape [25], and ULIP-2 [49]—by 7–10 R@1 points in all four retrieval directions, with the largest gap in Image→3D (+10.5 R@1 over Uni3D). For zero-shot classification, CLIP-GS edges out Uni3D by +0.8% Top-1 on Objaverse-GS and +0.5% on ModelNet-GS, while also exceeding ULIP [48], OpenShape [25], and both PointCLIP variants [58, 65] by wider margins. In few-shot classification on ModelNet-GS, CLIP-GS (95.4% ± 0.2%) beats the previous best PointRWKV [8] (94.8% ± 2.8%) and all other point-cloud methods including Mamba3D [7], Point-M2AE [57], Point-BERT [54], and DGCNN [43], with a notably tighter standard deviation indicating more stable feature clustering. On Objaverse-GS few-shot (5-shot), CLIP-GS consistently leads Uni3D [63] and OpenShape [25] across 5-, 10-, 20-, and 50-way settings. The uniform superiority across all baselines and tasks underscores the advantage of 3DGS over point clouds as a 3D representation for multimodal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIP-GS extends the 3D multimodal alignment paradigm established by ULIP, OpenShape, and Uni3D, which all align point cloud encoders with CLIP's image-text space. The key departure is replacing point clouds with 3D Gaussian Splatting as the 3D input, motivated by 3DGS's superior texture and reconstruction fidelity. Architecturally, it inherits Uni3D's transformer backbone and point cloud encoder (reused as the GS Tokenizer's internal encoder) but introduces a new GS Tokenizer to serialize 3DGS-specific attributes (opacity, scale, rotation) and an image voting loss to handle the one-to-many 3DGS-to-rendered-image correspondence that point cloud methods sidestep by sampling a single view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant explicit 3D representation across segmentation, generation, and autonomous driving, aligning it with vision-language models is a timely and practically important step. The paper demonstrates that 3DGS-based embeddings outperform point cloud embeddings across retrieval, zero-shot, and few-shot tasks, suggesting a potential shift in the default 3D input for multimodal learning. Limitations include the modest classification gains (+0.5–0.8%), the reliance on a 3DGS generation pipeline that adds per-shape optimization cost, and evaluation on relatively small-scale benchmarks (20K retrieval shapes, 40-class ModelNet). Open questions include whether scaling to Objaverse-XL's 10M+ shapes with 3DGS yields further gains, whether the approach extends to dynamic or scene-level 3DGS, and whether the 3DGS advantage persists when point cloud methods are given equivalent texture information.
