## Problem

Existing universal image generation models rely on language instructions to specify tasks, but the vision-language gap causes task confusion and poor generalization to unseen tasks. Additionally, visual tasks are inherently sparse and isolated across datasets, preventing models from learning shared, transferable representations. No prior framework simultaneously addresses generalizable task specification, dense task distribution, and architectural compatibility with state-of-the-art generative priors.

## Input / Output

The model receives a grid-layout image of size (L×W)×((C+1)×H) where L condition images (2–4, e.g., canny edges, depth maps, reference subjects) occupy the first L−1 columns, C in-context demonstration pairs (0–2) fill the upper rows, and the bottom-right cell is left blank for generation. Each grid cell is fixed at 384×384 pixels, and a text prompt provides layout, task, and content instructions. The output is the generated image cropped from the infilled bottom-right cell, with higher resolutions achievable via post-upscaling.

## Method

VisualCloze's key insight is that the objective of filling a blank grid cell from surrounding visual context is identical to the training objective of a pre-trained image infilling model, eliminating the need for architectural modifications. The authors fine-tune FLUX.1-Fill-dev via LoRA (rank 256) on Graph200K, a graph-structured dataset built from Subjects200K where each image is a central node connected by bidirectional edges to annotations across five meta-tasks (conditional generation, IP preservation, style transfer, editing, restoration), yielding 134 highly overlapping tasks sampled as paths through the graph. During training, 0–2 in-context demonstration pairs are randomly prepended to each query, teaching the model to infer task intent from visual examples rather than relying solely on text. The mask M in the diffusion formulation is a binary matrix that zeros out only the last row-and-column region, so the model learns to generate the target conditioned on all surrounding context images and the text prompt simultaneously.

## Results

On conditional generation and restoration (Table 1), Ours_fill achieves FID 30.60 (canny), RMSE 10.31 (depth), and RMSE 26.53 (deblur) with zero in-context examples, improving to FID 31.15, RMSE 9.68, and RMSE 25.57 respectively with two examples. On subject-driven generation (Table 2), Ours_fill scores DINOv2 80.41, CLIP-I 89.63, and CLIP-T 35.16, outperforming all universal baselines by 5–13 points on DINOv2. On style transfer (Table 3), Ours_fill achieves CLIP text score 0.29 and image score 0.55, surpassing OmniGen by 2% and 3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the specialized ControlNet, Ours_fill substantially outperforms on depth-to-image (RMSE 10.31 vs. 23.70) and deblurring (RMSE 26.53 vs. 37.82), while also achieving higher F1 on canny (0.35 vs. 0.13). Versus the universal OminiControl, Ours_fill wins on depth RMSE (10.31 vs. 21.44) and FID (33.88 vs. 36.23), but OminiControl retains an edge on deblurring RMSE (19.70 vs. 26.53) and FID (26.17 vs. 40.59). Compared to OneDiffusion, Ours_fill improves depth FID by 5.15 (33.88 vs. 39.03) and MUSIQ by 4.36 (64.85 vs. 60.49), with near-identical RMSE (10.31 vs. 10.35). OmniGen is clearly outperformed across all conditions: depth FID drops from 86.08 to 33.88, and CLIP-Score rises from 29.72 to 35.10. On style transfer, Ours_fill beats OmniGen by 2% in text alignment (0.29 vs. 0.27) and 3% in style consistency (0.55 vs. 0.52), but trails the specialized InstantStyle on style consistency (0.55 vs. 0.60) while leading on text alignment (0.29 vs. 0.27).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FLUX.1-Fill-dev, a rectified-flow infilling model, and reframes universal image generation as a visual cloze (infilling) problem over a grid of condition and context images. It departs from prior universal generation models (OmniGen, OneDiffusion, OminiControl) that rely on language instructions or task-specific tokens to disambiguate tasks, instead adopting visual in-context learning—drawing an analogy to few-shot prompting in LLMs—as the primary task specification mechanism. The Graph200K dataset extends the Subjects200K benchmark into a graph-structured multi-task space, addressing the sparsity problem that prior multi-task vision works have identified but not resolved at the data level. The approach also extends earlier visual in-context learning methods (ImageBrush, Painter, etc.) that were limited to single-condition, single-target tasks into a flexible multi-condition, multi-context setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that a pre-trained infilling model's objective is already aligned with universal grid-based generation—requiring no architectural modification—lowers the barrier to building capable universal generators and could be adopted by practitioners working with any strong infilling backbone. The Graph200K dataset and its graph-sampling strategy offer a reusable recipe for densifying sparse visual task distributions, which is a recognized bottleneck for cross-task transfer in vision. Limitations include the low 384×384 resolution, the modest 0–2 in-context examples tested, and the relatively narrow generalization evidence (a single unseen task: side-to-frontal face). Open questions include whether the infilling alignment advantage persists at higher resolutions, whether the graph structure scales beyond the five meta-tasks, and whether more in-context examples yield diminishing returns or continued gains.
