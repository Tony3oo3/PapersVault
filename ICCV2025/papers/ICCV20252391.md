## Problem

Existing proactive defenses against deepfakes are largely limited to GAN-based models, leaving a critical gap as deepfake threats shift toward diffusion model (DM) architectures. Prior DM adversarial attacks target the noising/denoising pipeline when an image serves as a query, but in DM-based deepfakes the source image enters through cross-attention as key-value pairs, rendering those strategies ineffective. The paper addresses this by designing a defense that disrupts the conditioning pathway specific to DM-based face swapping while remaining transferable to GAN-based systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image and produces a protected version with an imperceptible adversarial perturbation bounded by an ℓ∞ clamp (ε). The perturbation is designed to survive JPEG compression at quality factor 75 and various resizing/interpolation modes (BILINEAR, AREA, NEAREST), which are common preprocessing steps in deepfake pipelines. Evaluation is conducted on 100 source-target pairs from CelebA-HQ and VGGFace2-HQ, testing the protected image against six deepfake models spanning both diffusion and GAN architectures.

## Method

FaceShield combines four loss terms optimized via sign-based PGD: (1) a projector loss that maximizes L1 divergence of the CLIP image embedding to corrupt the conditioning signal at its source; (2) an attention-disruption loss that maximizes the variance of cross-attention maps in mid-layers of the denoising UNet, forcing the model to attend to irrelevant regions and preventing faithful identity transfer; (3) an MTCNN loss that exploits multi-scale NEAREST+average-pooling resizing to reduce face-detection probability across interpolation modes; and (4) an ArcFace identity loss that minimizes cosine similarity between original and perturbed identity embeddings. A novel noise-update step applies Sobel-masked Gaussian blur to smooth high-gradient regions for imperceptibility, followed by DCT-domain low-pass filtering to concentrate perturbation energy in low frequencies, preserving effectiveness under JPEG compression.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, FaceShield achieves the highest distortion across all four DM-based deepfake models: L2=0.350 and ISM=0.072 on IP-Adapter (vs. best baseline L2=0.207, ISM=0.235), and L2=0.336, ISM=0.194 on FaceSwap (vs. best baseline L2=0.303, ISM=0.230). On VGGFace2-HQ, the advantage is even more pronounced, with ISM dropping to 0.112 on IP-Adapter (vs. 0.271–0.294 for baselines). Under JPEG Q=75 compression, FaceShield retains strong protection (e.g., ISM=0.112 on IP-Adapter) while baselines degrade further. In terms of imperceptibility, FaceShield produces the least visible noise (LPIPS=0.202, PSNR=32.63, SSIM=0.939 on CelebA-HQ) with a frequency rate of 18.47, indicating noise concentrated in low frequencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines (AdvDM, Mist, PhotoGuard, SDST) are designed to protect images used as *queries* in diffusion-based image editing, where the image enters the denoising UNet directly. In the deepfake setting, however, the source image enters as key-value pairs through cross-attention, rendering these methods largely ineffective: on IP-Adapter (CelebA-HQ), they achieve ISM scores of 0.235–0.273, meaning the deepfake output still closely resembles the source face. FaceShield, by targeting the conditioning pathway (face projector + mid-layer attention variance), reduces ISM to 0.072 on IP-Adapter and 0.194 on FaceSwap, a 3–7× improvement over the best baseline. The gap is most dramatic on IP-Adapter, where FaceShield's L2 distortion (0.350) is roughly 2× that of AdvDM (0.207) and 2.3× that of SDST (0.147). Under JPEG Q=75 compression, FaceShield retains ISM=0.112 on IP-Adapter while baselines were not tested under compression, but the low-frequency concentration (FR=18.47 vs. ~1.7 for baselines) explains the robustness. On GAN-based models (SimSwap, InfoSwap), where no prior attack baselines exist, FaceShield reduces ISM from 0.544 to 0.184 (SimSwap) and from 0.431 to 0.237 (InfoSwap) on CelebA-HQ, confirming cross-architecture transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial-perturbation defense paradigm from GAN-based deepfakes to diffusion-model-based deepfakes, but with a critical conceptual shift: rather than attacking the query path (as prior DM protection methods like AdvDM, Mist, PhotoGuard, and SDST do for image editing), it targets the conditioning path where the source face enters as key-value pairs in cross-attention. It also broadens applicability by jointly attacking shared facial feature extractors (MTCNN, ArcFace) that underpin both GAN- and DM-based pipelines, and introduces a noise-update mechanism combining Sobel-guided Gaussian blur with DCT low-pass filtering to improve imperceptibility and JPEG robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: all four SOTA diffusion-protection methods fail entirely against DM-based deepfakes, revealing that the query-targeting paradigm does not transfer to the conditioning paradigm. By targeting widely-deployed components (MTCNN, ArcFace, IP-Adapter), FaceShield offers a practical, architecture-agnostic shield rather than a model-specific one. However, the authors acknowledge that other purification techniques (beyond JPEG and resizing) may still strip the perturbation, and the evaluation is limited to two face datasets and a handful of deepfake models, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to video deepfakes or newer architectures like autoregressive face generators.
