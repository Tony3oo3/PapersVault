## Problem

Existing NAS performance predictors suffer from distribution shift between small, biased training sets and diverse test sets, causing them to latch onto spurious correlations with redundant architectural features rather than the critical motifs that truly determine performance. No prior work has formulated this issue from a causal perspective to explicitly separate causal from non-causal features and eliminate the confounding backdoor path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural architecture as a directed acyclic graph with D nodes (each a one-hot operation vector of dimension F) and a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{D×D} encoding connections. It produces a scalar predicted accuracy and, as a byproduct, per-node and per-edge importance scores (α_C and α_R) that indicate which operations and connections are critical versus redundant for performance, enabling interpretable visualization of architectural motifs.

## Method

CARL encodes the architecture graph with a GCN to obtain node- and edge-level representations, then applies a substructure extractor (MLP + softmax) to split each representation into a critical part and a redundant part, yielding separate substructure graphs X_C and X_R that are embedded by dedicated GCNs into Z_C and Z_R. To cut the backdoor path between redundant features and performance, CARL generates interventional samples by pairing each architecture's critical representation Z_C with a randomly sampled redundant representation Z_R' from another architecture in the mini-batch. Training uses a three-term loss: a hinge ranking loss on critical representations (L_C), an MSE loss that pushes redundant-feature predictions toward the training-set mean (L_R), and a hinge ranking loss on interventional samples (L_I), weighted by hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂. This forces the predictor to rely on critical features for ranking while rendering predictions invariant to which redundant features are present.

## Results

On the ranking task, CARL achieves the best Kendall's Tau across all six training portions on both NAS-Bench-101 (up to 0.774 at 0.1% training) and NAS-Bench-201 (up to 0.755 at 3% training), outperforming PINAT and NAR-Former by 0.107 and 0.066 respectively at 0.5% training on NAS-Bench-201. On DARTS CIFAR-10, CARL discovers an architecture with 97.67% top-1 accuracy at only 0.25 GPU days, surpassing PINAT (97.58%) and PA&DA (97.66%). On DARTS ImageNet, the searched architecture reaches 76.1% top-1 / 92.8% top-5 accuracy, the best among predictor-based methods. On TransNAS-Bench-101, CARL achieves the best average rank of 12.29 (Micro) and 5.29 (Macro) across seven diverse vision tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARL consistently outperforms all 35 compared baselines across five search spaces. On NAS-Bench-201 ranking, CARL leads PINAT [30] by 0.107 Ktau (0.656 vs. 0.549) and NAR-Former [66] by 0.066 at 0.5% training, with the gap widening at smaller training portions. On DARTS CIFAR-10, CARL (97.67%) edges out PA&DA [31] (97.66%) and PINAT [30] (97.58%) while using fewer GPU days (0.25 vs. 0.4 and 0.3). On DARTS ImageNet, CARL (76.1% top-1) surpasses PRE-NAS [41] (76.0%) and Shapley-NAS [58] (75.7%) at the lowest search cost among predictor-based methods. On NAS-Bench-201 search, CARL matches the global optimum on CIFAR-10 (94.37%) and CIFAR-100 (73.51%) with only 80 queries, beating DCLP [69] and ReNAS [63] on ImageNet-16-120 (46.91% vs. 46.54%). On TransNAS-Bench-101, CARL achieves the best average rank (12.29 Micro, 5.29 Macro), outperforming WeakNAS [56] (20.03, 10.49) and Arch-Graph [15] (22.15, 12.68). On NAS-Bench-NLP, CARL attains the lowest log PPL (4.572) versus NASBOT [18] (4.579) and NPENAS [53] (4.587).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) performance predictors for NAS that learn architecture representations via GNNs, Transformers, or self-supervised pretraining, and (2) causal intervention methods (backdoor adjustment, invariant feature learning) that have been applied to vision and graph classification for generalization. It departs from prior predictors like HOP and AutoBuild, which capture critical features via attention or magnitude ranking, by explicitly modeling the distribution shift between limited training samples and diverse test samples as a causal confounding problem. The substructure extractor and latent-space interventional sample generation are novel mechanisms, but the underlying idea—disentangle causal from spurious features and augment by swapping the spurious component—follows a pattern established in invariant learning literature (e.g., EIIL, IFL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing provides a principled explanation for why performance predictors overfit to redundant features under sampling bias, and the disentanglement-plus-intervention recipe offers a practical remedy that consistently improves ranking and search across five diverse search spaces. The interpretability output (operation importance scores aligned with known motifs) makes the predictor more trustworthy for practitioners. However, the disentanglement is learned end-to-end without a hard guarantee of true causal/redundant separation, and the intervention is approximate (swapping latent representations rather than manipulating actual architecture substructures), leaving open whether more rigorous causal identification could yield further gains. The framework's reliance on a GCN encoder and cell-based graph representation also limits immediate applicability to non-graph or skeleton-based architectures without adaptation.
