## Problem

Prior unbounded scene generation methods assume fixed-height indoor scenes and rely on spatially structured latents (triplanes, dense grids) that degrade or consume excessive memory when scaled to outdoor environments with large height variations (e.g., skyscrapers vs. rural houses). Additionally, no high-quality, unified outdoor scene dataset exists that supports joint training across heterogeneous styles and scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251502/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes previously generated scene chunks as VAE-encoded vector set embeddings (V=16, c=64) and produces a 2×2 grid of new chunks in a raster scan pattern, each chunk being a 50×h_vox×50 voxel occupancy volume (h_vox up to 478). At inference, the predicted chunk height prunes unnecessary occupancy queries, and marching cubes converts the occupancy grid into a mesh. Optionally, SceneTex textures the generated geometry using a manually keyframed camera trajectory (1.2k–1.8k keyframes, 40° FOV).

## Method

A VAE compresses variable-height scene chunks into a uniform vector set of 16 tokens via cross-attention over sampled point clouds, with an auxiliary consistency loss between two independent point cloud samples from the same chunk to prevent posterior collapse. A height-prediction head queries the embedding to estimate chunk height at inference, enabling early termination of occupancy decoding. The diffusion model (DDPM with a 25-layer UNet-style Transformer from Craftsman) is trained for explicit outpainting: it generates four chunks simultaneously in a 2×2 grid, conditioning on up to three previously generated neighbor chunks via four mask/condition configurations that cover all raster-scan scenarios. This eliminates the iterative resampling steps of RePaint-based inpainting, reducing generation time by ~5× for a 21×21 chunk scene. The NuiScene43 dataset (43 Objaverse scenes with cleaned ground geometry and unified scale) enables joint training across heterogeneous styles, allowing the model to blend elements like castles and skyscrapers in a single generated scene.

## Results

For VAE reconstruction, the vector set model achieves IoU 0.989, CD 0.055, and F-Score 0.864, outperforming the best triplane configuration (IoU 0.940, CD 0.064, F-Score 0.831). For diffusion generation quality, the vector set model achieves FPD 0.571 and KPD 0.951 (×10³), substantially better than the triplane baseline (FPD 1.406, KPD 2.589). In terms of speed, the explicit outpainting method generates a 21×21 chunk scene in 215.92 s (embedding) + 87.76 s (decoding), compared to 1022.20 s + 60.28 s for RePaint with 5 resampling steps, yielding roughly a 4.7× speedup in the diffusion stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the triplane baseline (used in BlockFusion [44] and SemCity [26]), the vector set representation wins on every metric: reconstruction IoU improves from 0.940 to 0.989, generation FPD drops from 1.406 to 0.571, and training is ~2.5× faster with half the VRAM (Table 2). Against RePaint [31] for unbounded outpainting, the proposed explicit outpainting model is approximately 4.7× faster in the embedding generation stage (215.92 s vs. 1022.20 s for a 21×21 scene) while producing more coherent inter-chunk connections; RePaint with r=1 resampling steps often collapses into broken chunks, and r=5 improves coherence at the cost of reduced diversity and much longer runtime. Against Trellis [45] (a state-of-the-art image-to-3D object generator), NuiScene produces finer geometric detail on large outdoor scenes because it represents each chunk separately rather than using a single latent for the entire scene, though Trellis benefits from a much larger training corpus. Overall, the paper's method dominates on both quality and efficiency for the unbounded outdoor scene generation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion framework for unbounded 3D scene generation (established by BlockFusion, LT3SD, and SemCity) by replacing spatially structured latents (triplanes, dense feature grids) with vector sets from 3DShape2VecSet, motivated by the need to handle outdoor scenes with extreme height variation that breaks the fixed-cube assumption of prior methods. It further departs from the standard RePaint-based resampling outpainting used in prior work by training an explicit conditional outpainting diffusion model that generates a 2×2 chunk grid in a single forward pass. The overall VAE-plus-diffusion-in-latent-space architecture follows the LDM paradigm, and the UNet-style transformer backbone is borrowed from Craftsman.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: prior unbounded scene generation methods are confined to indoor (3D-Front) or driving (SemanticKITTI, CarlaSC) settings with limited height diversity and geometric quality, while no curated dataset existed for outdoor scenes with varying scales and styles. The NuiScene43 dataset and the demonstrated ability to blend heterogeneous environments (castles, rural houses, skyscrapers) in a single generation open a path toward more diverse world-building. Key open questions include scaling beyond four training scenes, adding semantic or text conditioning for controllable generation, and introducing global context for coherent large-scale planning (e.g., road networks, city layouts) — all acknowledged limitations that currently constrain the method's practical utility.
