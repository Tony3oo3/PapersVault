## Problem

Existing training-free consistent text-to-image methods (StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory) fail to maintain fine-grained background consistency and suffer from a trade-off between foreground identity preservation and action variation when characters undergo large motion changes. Additionally, these methods are built on UNet-based models (SDXL) whose architecture-specific implementations cannot be directly adapted to modern Diffusion Transformers, limiting output quality and scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of text prompts formatted as '[Environment], [Character], [Action]' and internally generates a single identity image first, then produces frame images consistent with it—requiring only one reference image rather than the 2–4 parallel images needed by prior methods. Image tokens operate at 1/16 of the generated resolution within FLUX.1's multimodal transformer, and the foreground-background mask enables users to optionally lock or switch the background independently of the character.

## Method

CharaConsist introduces three key mechanisms on top of FLUX.1's DiT architecture. First, point-tracking attention establishes semantic correspondences between frame and identity images by averaging cosine similarity of attention outputs across transformer layers, then re-encodes the identity image's keys with RoPE at the frame image's positions to mitigate locality bias. Second, adaptive token merge interpolates attention outputs between frame and identity images weighted by per-token matching confidence (S_max), compensating for imperfect correspondences and preserving local geometric structure. Third, a foreground-background mask is extracted by comparing averaged attention weights of image tokens to foreground versus background text tokens, enabling decoupled control over character and scene consistency. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no additional modules or per-subject fine-tuning.

## Results

On the background maintaining task, CharaConsist achieves the best full-image consistency (CLIP-I 0.910) and background consistency (CLIP-I-bg 0.916) among all methods, while also attaining the highest image aesthetics score (IAA 0.831) and image quality score (IQA 0.985). On the background switching task, CharaConsist leads in foreground consistency (CLIP-I-fg 0.881) among training-free methods. The method trails on CLIP-T (0.356 vs. 0.358–0.370) and ID Sim (0.647 vs. 0.664–0.730), which the authors attribute to FLUX.1's domain shift and the training-free nature of the approach, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the training-free baselines StoryDiffusion and ConsiStory, CharaConsist wins decisively on background consistency (CLIP-I-bg 0.916 vs. 0.895 for both) and full-image consistency (CLIP-I 0.910 vs. 0.902/0.903), with the gap widening on the background switching task for foreground consistency (CLIP-I-fg 0.881 vs. 0.873/0.868) and identity similarity (ID Sim 0.587 vs. 0.563/0.502). Against the training-dependent identity-reference methods IP-Adapter and PhotoMaker, CharaConsist achieves substantially higher background consistency (CLIP-I-bg 0.916 vs. 0.881/0.886) and image quality (IAA 0.831 vs. 0.811/0.705), but concedes on facial identity similarity (ID Sim 0.647 vs. 0.664 for IP-Adapter and 0.730 for PhotoMaker), which is the explicit training objective of those methods. CharaConsist also reports the highest image aesthetics (IAA 0.831) and quality (IQA 0.985) scores across all methods. The only metric where CharaConsist trails all baselines is CLIP-T (0.356), which the authors attribute to FLUX.1's inherent domain shift rather than the consistency mechanism itself, as confirmed by their ablation showing FLUX.1's raw CLIP-T is already lower than SDXL-based models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CharaConsist extends the training-free consistent T2I generation line (StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory, 1P1S) by migrating from UNet-based models (SDXL) to the DiT architecture (FLUX.1), which requires fundamentally different attention manipulation since DiTs lack separate self-attention and cross-attention layers. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis of a 'locality bias' in inter-image attention—where models preferentially attend to spatially nearby rather than semantically corresponding tokens—and the introduction of point-tracking attention with RoPE re-encoding to explicitly establish cross-image correspondences, replacing the random token sampling used by prior methods. The adaptive token merge and foreground-background mask extraction further differentiate the approach by enabling decoupled control over character and scene consistency, something prior methods did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiT-based models (FLUX.1, SD3) become the dominant T2I architecture, this work provides the first training-free consistent generation pipeline that operates natively on DiTs, making it immediately applicable to the current generation of high-quality image models without retraining. The foreground-background decoupling and fine-grained detail preservation (clothing, background objects) open the door to practical applications in visual storytelling and video keyframe generation that prior coarse-grained methods could not support. A key open question is how to combine this training-free consistency mechanism with trained identity-reference models (IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker) to simultaneously achieve high facial fidelity and full-scene consistency, which the authors identify as future work.
