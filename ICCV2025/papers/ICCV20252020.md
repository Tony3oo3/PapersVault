## Problem

Existing test-time reasoning techniques (chain-of-thought, process/outcome reward models) have significantly improved text-based LLMs but remain largely unexplored for audio-visual models. Current AVLLMs rely on dominant visual or auditory cues rather than genuine cross-modal reasoning, exhibiting cultural and perceptual biases that lead to incorrect answers. No prior work provides a training-free mechanism to inject structured, step-by-step reasoning into AVLLMs at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a video clip, its accompanying audio track, and a natural-language question as a joint tuple ⟨v, a, q⟩. The multi-agent system internally produces a structured reasoning sequence (steps + justification + final answer), a synthesized multimodal caption, and a scalar evaluation score (1–10) at each iteration. The final output is the target AVLLM's answer generated by conditioning it on the distilled reasoning steps alongside the raw AV inputs, with no parameter updates to the target model.

## Method

AURELIA is a zero-shot, multi-agent actor-critic framework comprising a Reasoning Generator (R), a Summarizer (S), a multimodal Evaluator (E), and a Feedback mechanism (F). R processes the AV input to produce reasoning steps; S synthesizes them into a concise caption; E scores the caption's alignment with the actual video and audio on a 1–10 scale. The Feedback loop iteratively refines R's output to maximize E's score until it exceeds a threshold τ or T iterations are exhausted, selecting the highest-scoring reasoning sequence r*. These optimal steps are then injected into the target AVLLM at inference time as structured context, distilling reasoning knowledge in a training-free manner. The framework is model-agnostic and can mix different LLMs across agent roles (e.g., Gemini for multimodal perception, GPT-4o for textual reasoning), and generalizes across six diverse AV reasoning tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On AVReasonBench (4,500 samples across 6 tasks), closed-source Gemini 1.5 Pro leads with 68.9% mean accuracy on Music-AVQA, 72.5% on AVSD, 82.7 CIDEr on AV-Captioning, and 68.0% on AV-GeoIQ, while the best open-source model VITA (7B) achieves 58.6%, 60.1%, 72.9 CIDEr, and 25.5% respectively. Applying AURELIA's test-time reasoning distillation yields up to 100% relative improvement (e.g., X-InstructBLIP on AV-Meme: 14.0→28.0) and substantial gains on the hardest tasks (AV-GeoIQ, AV-Meme, DM-Match), with VITA improving from 25.5% to 33.8% on AV-GeoIQ and from 41.0% to 50.0% on AV-Meme. On Video-MME, VITA with AURELIA improves overall accuracy from 80.7% to 83.73%, with the largest gains in long-video Knowledge (+4.9 pts) and Artistic Performance (+2.2 pts) categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 18 baselines, Gemini 1.5 Pro consistently outperforms all open-source models across all six AVReasonBench tasks, with the largest gap on AV-GeoIQ (68.0% vs. 25.5% for VITA) and AV-Meme (49.0% vs. 41.0%), reflecting its superior audio comprehension. Reka Core trails Gemini on AV-GeoIQ (42.5% vs. 68.0%) and AV-Meme (19.0% vs. 49.0%), suggesting weaker cultural/geographical reasoning. Among open-source models, VITA (7B) leads on most tasks, benefiting from a strong LLM backbone, while models with robust audio encoders (AVicuna, Video-SALMONN) outperform those with weaker audio processing (PandaGPT, Macaw-LLM) on audio-dependent tasks. AURELIA's reasoning distillation provides consistent gains across all 18 models, with the most dramatic improvements on the hardest tasks: AV-Meme sees up to 100% relative gain (X-InstructBLIP 14.0→28.0, CREMA 14.0→29.0), AV-GeoIQ gains 15–22% relative, and DM-Match gains 18–27% relative. On Video-MME, AURELIA generalizes beyond AVReasonBench, improving VITA's overall score from 80.7% to 83.73%, confirming the framework's task-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-agent actor-critic paradigm from text-based LLM reasoning (chain-of-thought, process reward models) and applies it to the audio-visual domain, where no prior test-time reasoning distillation framework existed. It departs from existing AV benchmarks (AVSD, MusicAVQA, VALOR) by augmenting them with structured step-by-step reasoning and introducing new task formulations (AV-GeoIQ, AV-Compositional) that demand cross-modal integration with world knowledge. The key conceptual departure is that reasoning is generated externally by a multi-agent pipeline and injected at inference time, rather than being learned through fine-tuning or instruction tuning as in prior multimodal reasoning work (LLaVA-CoT, MAmmoTH-VL, Virgo).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVReasonBench fills a notable gap by providing 4,500 AV samples with explicit reasoning chains across six tasks, giving the community a standardized way to measure whether AVLLMs actually reason or rely on perceptual shortcuts. The training-free nature of AURELIA makes it immediately deployable without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot fine-tune large AV models. However, failure cases reveal that the pipeline still struggles with fine-grained audio comprehension and temporal dynamics, and the largest gains (up to 100%) occur on weak baselines with very low absolute scores, while improvements on strong models like Gemini 1.5 Pro are modest (~2-3 points). Open questions include whether the multi-agent overhead scales to longer videos, whether the approach generalizes to streaming/real-time AV settings, and whether the reasoning steps truly improve interpretability or merely act as a prompt engineering trick.
