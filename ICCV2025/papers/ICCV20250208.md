## Problem

Single-point-supervised infrared small target detection (SIRST) remains challenging because the existing LESPS framework suffers from training instability, excessive label evolution, and an inability to fully leverage the performance of embedded detection networks. The core issue is that directly training on all point-labeled samples without considering the model's current capability leads to misidentification of hard samples and degraded final performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes infrared images (randomly cropped into 256×256 patches) with a single point label per target (either a coarse point or centroid point) as the sole supervision signal, and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask. During the EPG pre-start phase, local 20×20 patches centered on each point label are processed for initial pseudo-label generation. The framework is designed as a plug-and-play wrapper around existing SIRST detection networks (e.g., MSDA-Net, UIUNet, DNANet), requiring no architectural modification.

## Method

The PAL framework organizes training into three progressive phases: (1) Model pre-start, where an EPG strategy (Gaussian filtering → Canny edge detection → morphological closing on local patches) generates pseudo-labels and selects only easy samples (target-level recall ≥ 0.8) for initial training; (2) Model enhancement, where a fine dual-update strategy alternates between Coarse Outer Updates (periodically transferring hard samples the model can now recognize into the training pool based on missed-detection and false-detection thresholds) and Fine Inner Updates (refining existing pseudo-labels via adaptive-threshold candidate extraction with a decay factor λ=0.97 to balance annotation expansion and contraction); (3) Model refinement, where remaining hard samples are fully learned and pseudo-labels are further polished. The EEDM loss (edge-enhanced difficulty-mining) guides optimization, and the entire framework is adaptable to any existing SIRST detection network without modification.

## Results

On the comprehensive SIRST3-Test, the PAL framework improves IoU by 9.68%–24.04%, nIoU by 14.53%–31.06%, and Pd by 1.99%–12.03% over the LESPS baseline across eight embedded SIRST networks. The best PAL-equipped network (MSDA-Net) achieves 69.38% IoU and 97.41% Pd, reaching 83.1% of its full-supervision IoU (83.46%) and matching full-supervision Pd. On individual datasets with limited samples, PAL improves IoU by up to 57.28% over LESPS (e.g., DNANet on NUAA-SIRST: 66.57% vs. 9.29%), demonstrating substantially greater stability where LESPS frequently collapses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three traditional model-driven baselines (FKRW, MPCM, NIPPS), PAL-equipped networks achieve 3–5× higher IoU (e.g., MSDA-Net+PAL: 69.38% vs. 14.46–18.82%), confirming that data-driven approaches with progressive learning far surpass hand-crafted filters for this task. The primary comparison is with LESPS [66], the state-of-the-art single-point-supervision framework. PAL wins on every metric across all eight networks and all four datasets: IoU improves by 9.68%–24.04% on SIRST3-Test and by up to 57.28% on individual datasets (DNANet on NUAA-SIRST: 66.57% vs. 9.29%). LESPS exhibits severe instability—on NUAA-SIRST, both DNANet (IoU 9.29%) and UIUNet (IoU 7.11%) essentially fail, while PAL maintains IoU above 62% for both. In terms of Pd, PAL matches or exceeds full-supervision detection probability for MSDA-Net (97.41% vs. 97.41%) and GGL-Net (97.14% vs. 97.74%), whereas LESPS lags by 1–12 percentage points. The Fa values also show PAL is more stable, with several LESPS entries exceeding the 10⁻⁴ invalidity threshold (e.g., MPCM: 123.76, UIUNet on NUAA-SIRST: 550.80) while PAL stays well below it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on the LESPS framework (Ying et al., CVPR 2023), which first demonstrated that existing SIRST detection networks can be trained with single point supervision through iterative pseudo-label evolution. It departs from LESPS by restructuring the training process into a progressive active learning (PAL) framework inspired by automatic curriculum learning, addressing LESPS's instability and excessive label evolution. The EPG strategy repurposes classical image processing (Gaussian filtering, Canny edges, morphological closing) for initial pseudo-label generation, while the FIU mechanism adapts the label evolution idea from LESPS with a decay factor to prevent uncontrolled annotation expansion. The framework is explicitly plug-and-play, wrapping any existing SIRST detection network (MSDA-Net, UIUNet, DNANet, etc.) without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAL framework demonstrates that single point supervision can recover 79–87% of full-supervision IoU across multiple SIRST networks, offering a practical path to drastically reduce the expensive pixel-level annotation burden in infrared target detection. Its plug-and-play nature means existing SIRST architectures can immediately benefit without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the classical image-processing-based EPG strategy generalizes to more complex multi-target or video scenarios, whether the curriculum scheduling (epoch divisions, threshold ramps) can be made fully adaptive rather than hand-tuned, and whether the approach transfers to other weakly supervised detection tasks beyond SIRST.
