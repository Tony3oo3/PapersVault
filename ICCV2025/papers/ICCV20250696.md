## Problem

Existing visual localization methods for autonomous driving either rely on expensive 3D maps, use handcrafted features sensitive to viewpoint and illumination changes, or perform direct pose regression without explicit geometric constraints. Crucially, none explicitly model the two distinct uncertainty sources that degrade localization in urban environments: perceptual uncertainty from occlusions and degraded visual features, and localization uncertainty from non-unimodal pose distributions and degeneracy in specific degrees of freedom.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view or monocular RGB images, a rasterized HD map or OpenStreetMap navigation map (25 cm/pixel sampling), and a noisy 3DoF GNSS pose estimate. For fine-grained localization it operates on a 120 m × 120 m map patch; for large-scale relocalization it uses a 128 m × 128 m patch with perturbations up to 30 m / 30°. The output is a minimal SE(2) pose correction (δx, δy, δφ) applied to the coarse estimate, together with per-DoF Shannon-entropy uncertainty values and a full 3D joint probability distribution over the pose space.

## Method

U-ViLAR is a two-stage differentiable framework. In the PU-Guided Association stage, image features are projected to BEV via a BEVFormer-style view transformation and map features are extracted with a U-Net; cross-attention fuses the two modalities, and a heteroscedastic uncertainty field (trained with road-structure segmentation supervision) is concatenated channel-wise with the visual–map similarity matrix and processed by a 1×1 CNN to yield a PU-aware association distribution, supervised by multivariate-Gaussian soft targets globally and by contrastive learning on local windows. In the LU-Guided Registration stage, a pose decoder regresses independent 3DoF probability distributions from the PU-aware matrix; their Cartesian product forms a 3D joint prior that gates a cost tensor computed by geometrically warping visual BEV features over a discretized solution space centered on the coarse pose, and the final refined pose is obtained via softmax-weighted fusion of the cost tensor with the uncertainty prior, allowing the model to adaptively trust data-driven signals versus the probabilistic prior depending on scene degeneracy.

## Results

On the fine-grained localization task (nuScenes + HD map, multi-view), U-ViLAR achieves 0.040 m lateral MAE, 0.140 m longitudinal MAE, and 0.075° orientation MAE, reducing orientation error by 85.3% over BEV-Locator (0.510°). On the challenging SRoad dataset, U-ViLAR (multi-view) cuts lateral MAE from 0.204 m (ICP baseline) to 0.110 m and longitudinal MAE from 0.614 m to 0.284 m. For large-scale relocalization on nuScenes with OSM (multi-view), U-ViLAR reaches 34.53% position recall@1 m and 60.32% orientation recall@1°, surpassing the strongest baseline MapLocNet-M (20.10% and 58.61%). The model runs at 28 FPS on an NVIDIA V100 and 15 FPS on NVIDIA Orin with TensorRT INT8 quantization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-ViLAR outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against BEV-Locator (multi-view, nuScenes HD map), U-ViLAR reduces lateral MAE from 0.076 m to 0.040 m and orientation MAE from 0.510° to 0.075° (an 85.3% reduction), while also reporting RMSE values that BEV-Locator does not provide. Against OrienterNet (monocular), U-ViLAR consistently wins: on nuScenes fine-grained localization, lateral MAE drops from 0.079 m to 0.069 m; on KITTI relocalization, lateral recall@1 m jumps from 51.26% to 69.12% and orientation recall@1° from 20.41% to 64.92%. Against MapLocNet (multi-view, nuScenes OSM), U-ViLAR improves position recall@1 m from 20.10% to 34.53% and orientation recall@1° from 58.61% to 60.32%. Against U-BEV (multi-view, nuScenes OSM), U-ViLAR more than doubles position recall@1 m (34.53% vs. 16.89%) and additionally reports orientation recall metrics that U-BEV does not provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

U-ViLAR builds on the BEV feature extraction paradigm from BEVFormer and the cross-modal fusion strategies common to recent visual localization work (BEV-Locator, OrienterNet, MapLocNet), but departs from their single-stage or direct-regression designs by explicitly separating the problem into a matching stage (PU-Guided Association) and a search stage (LU-Guided Registration). Where prior methods either regress poses directly without geometric constraints (BEV-Locator) or model pose distributions without explicit image-map correspondences (OrienterNet, MapLocNet), this paper introduces uncertainty-aware probabilistic modeling at both the association and registration levels, drawing on heteroscedastic uncertainty estimation and contrastive learning from the broader deep learning literature. The two-stage coarse-to-fine structure with a 3D joint probability prior over the solution space is the key architectural departure from prior end-to-end localization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment on Baidu's large-scale autonomous driving fleets and support for both HD maps and lightweight OSM navigation maps make this a directly applicable system for urban driving where GNSS is unreliable. The uncertainty outputs (perceptual and localization) provide actionable confidence signals that downstream planning modules can exploit, addressing a gap in prior methods that output point estimates without calibrated error bounds. Open questions remain around performance in extremely observation-limited scenarios (e.g., long tunnels, dense construction zones) and whether the model can generalize across diverse map formats and datasets without retraining, as the authors themselves note as future work.
