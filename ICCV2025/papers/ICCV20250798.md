## Problem

Existing slider-based image generation methods (e.g., ConceptSliders, PromptSlider) train a separate adapter per attribute, causing entanglement where adjusting one slider unintentionally alters other attributes. This makes simultaneous multi-attribute control unreliable, and the sequential application of individual sliders introduces order-dependent artifacts and requires N forward passes for N attributes, creating a scalability bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250798/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

CompSlider takes a text prompt (encoded by T5 into 128 tokens) and N continuous slider values (16 attributes, each normalized to [0,1]) as input, and outputs a 1024-dimensional image condition vector that replaces the CLIP image embedding in a pre-trained eDiff-I-style latent diffusion model. No source image is required at inference; the generated condition vector is fed directly into the frozen foundation model to produce the final image. The model processes all 16 sliders in a single forward pass, enabling simultaneous control with distinct values per attribute.

## Method

CompSlider is a 10-block Diffusion Transformer (DiT, 277M params) that applies the reparameterization trick to directly predict the clean image condition c₀^I rather than noise, operating entirely in the latent space of the conditional prior without backpropagating through the foundation model. Slider values are encoded via sinusoidal positional embeddings concatenated with learnable class embeddings to distinguish attributes. The key training innovation is a disentanglement loss: during training, randomly sampled attribute combinations (v^{S*}) are paired with real ones (v^S), and an MLP classifier is trained to recover the per-attribute difference Δv_i from the two generated conditions, forcing the model to encode each attribute independently regardless of training-data co-occurrence biases. A structure loss (L2 between conditions for small slider differences |Δv_i| ≤ 0.1) regularizes local smoothness and preserves identity. The total loss is L_diff + L_st + L_cls, and the approach requires no paired data of the same subject across attribute intensities.

## Results

On human-related sliders (age, smile, anger, surprise, sadness), CompSlider achieves 81.07% continuity, 90.95% consistency, 59.02% scope, and 14.04% entanglement, outperforming all baselines on every reported metric. The entanglement ratio is nearly halved compared to ConceptSlider (14.04% vs. 27.22%) and Prompt2Prompt (14.04% vs. 28.99%), demonstrating substantially more independent attribute control. In a user A/B study on non-human sliders (vector style, scene complexity), CompSlider receives 54.66% preference versus 34.16% for ConceptSlider.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompSlider outperforms ConceptSlider [15] on all six metrics: continuity (81.07% vs. 73.41%), consistency (90.95% vs. 83.17%), scope (59.02% vs. 54.43%), entanglement (14.04% vs. 27.22%), LPIPS (0.12 vs. 0.16), and CLIP (6.20 vs. 5.76). Against PromptSlider [52], CompSlider wins on continuity (+19.9 pts), consistency (+10.7 pts), scope (+12.8 pts), entanglement (−10.3 pts), and CLIP (+1.41), but PromptSlider achieves a slightly lower LPIPS (0.10 vs. 0.12), indicating marginally better perceptual fidelity at the cost of much weaker controllability. Compared to the editing method Prompt2Prompt [19], CompSlider leads on consistency (+2.48 pts), scope (+9.56 pts), entanglement (−14.95 pts), LPIPS (0.12 vs. 0.19), and CLIP (+2.05), though Prompt2Prompt does not report a continuity score. In the non-human A/B user study, CompSlider is preferred over ConceptSlider 54.66% of the time (vs. 34.16%), with 11.18% ties.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompSlider builds on the eDiff-I framework, which conditions a latent diffusion model on CLIP image embeddings for fine-grained control, and repurposes the DiT architecture to generate those image-condition vectors from slider inputs rather than extracting them from source images. It departs from ConceptSliders, which trains a separate LoRA adapter per attribute and applies them sequentially (causing entanglement), and from PromptSlider, which requires all sliders to share the same value. The key conceptual shift is treating multi-attribute control as a single compositional generation problem in the conditional-prior latent space, with explicit disentanglement and structure losses that have no direct analogue in prior slider-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in controllable generation: users need to adjust multiple attributes simultaneously with independent values, and prior methods either cannot do this or produce entangled, order-dependent results. The random-attribute-sampling training strategy (avoiding costly paired data) and the finding that structure regularization *improves* disentanglement by stabilizing identity are useful insights for anyone designing multi-condition generative models. Limitations include a closed set of 16 predefined attributes, reliance on pre-trained per-attribute classifiers, and evaluation concentrated on human faces; open questions include scalability to open-ended or scene-level attributes and whether the approach transfers to architectures without a CLIP-style image-condition interface.
