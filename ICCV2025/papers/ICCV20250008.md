## Problem

Existing incremental object detection methods rely primarily on knowledge distillation to prevent catastrophic forgetting but overlook the class overlap problem: foreground objects from previously learned classes that appear in current training images remain unlabeled, causing the model to misclassify them as background. This leads to degraded performance on both old and new classes, as distillation-based regularization alone cannot address the missing-annotation issue inherent to the incremental setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels using a Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50) backbone. During incremental training at stage t, the model receives only ground-truth annotations for the current task's classes C_t; objects belonging to previously learned classes ∪_{i=1}^{t-1} C_i in the same images are unannotated. The previous model M_{t-1} is run on each current training image to generate confidence-scored detections for these unannotated old-class objects, which are then processed by the GM-Pseudo module to yield usable pseudo-labels and latent-object masks.

## Method

The framework (GDA-IOD) combines two mechanisms. First, GM-Pseudo models the bimodal confidence-score distribution of M_{t-1}'s predictions on current images using a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model fitted via EM, segmenting predictions into low-confidence (discarded), medium-confidence (treated as latent foreground objects excluded from RPN negative samples), and high-confidence (used as pseudo-labels for supervised training after IoU-based deduplication with ground truth). Second, Gradient Decomposition and Alignment (GDA) decomposes the ROI loss into a pseudo-label component L_ROI^pseudo and the full loss, then at each iteration checks the cosine similarity between the overall gradient g and the pseudo-label gradient g_pseudo; when ⟨g_pseudo, g⟩ < 0, the overall gradient is orthogonally projected onto g_pseudo (scaled by hyperparameter λ=0.6) so that parameter updates preserve previously learned knowledge while still driving learning of new classes.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2007 (single-step, 10+10 setting), GDA-IOD achieves 75.1% mAP on old classes, 74.6% on new classes, and 74.9% overall, surpassing the strongest rehearsal-free baseline BPF (71.7/74.0/72.9) by 3.4% on old and 0.6% on new. On MS COCO 2017 (40+40), GDA-IOD reaches 35.3% AP (55.2% AP50, 38.3% AP75), outperforming BPF (34.4/54.3/37.3) and MMA (33.0/56.6/34.6). In the challenging multi-step 5+5+5+5 setting, GDA-IOD achieves 63.5% mAP across all 20 classes, exceeding the rehearsal-based ABR by 5.1% and BPF by 1.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDA-IOD is rehearsal-free and outperforms all non-rehearsal baselines across every setting. On VOC 10+10, it beats BPF (the strongest rehearsal-free competitor) by 3.4% mAP on old classes (75.1 vs 71.7) and 0.6% on new (74.6 vs 74.0), and by 2.0% overall (74.9 vs 72.9). On COCO 40+40, GDA-IOD achieves 35.3% AP vs BPF's 34.4% and MMA's 33.0%, while on COCO 70+10 it reaches 36.5% AP vs BPF's 36.2%. Against rehearsal-based methods, GDA-IOD surpasses ABR by 2.9% overall mAP on VOC 10+10 (74.9 vs 72.0) and by 5.1% on the multi-step 5+5+5+5 setting (63.5 vs 58.4), despite not storing any past examples. The method also substantially outperforms earlier distillation-based approaches such as Faster ILOD (62.1 on 10+10) and ROSETTA (66.8), confirming that gradient alignment plus GMM-based pseudo-labeling is more effective than feature/prediction distillation alone for preserving old-class knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incremental object detection (IOD) line initiated by ILOD and subsequent distillation-based methods (AFD, MMA, BPF) by replacing knowledge distillation with a pseudo-labeling-plus-gradient-alignment strategy. It builds directly on Faster R-CNN as the base detector and adapts the pseudo-labeling idea from the Continual Detection Transformer [29], but departs from its fixed-threshold selection by introducing a GMM-based adaptive segmentation of confidence scores into low/medium/high regions. The gradient decomposition and alignment mechanism is conceptually related to PCGrad-style orthogonal projection in multi-task learning, but is specifically formulated to preserve old-class knowledge during incremental detection without requiring stored exemplars, distinguishing it from rehearsal-based methods like ABR and GMDP-ABR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in IOD: distillation-based methods ignore the class-overlap problem where unannotated old-class objects in new training images are misclassified as background, and rehearsal-based methods require storing past data (raising privacy and storage concerns). By combining adaptive GMM pseudo-labeling with gradient alignment, GDA-IOD achieves rehearsal-free SOTA on both VOC and COCO, including beating rehearsal-based ABR by over 5 mAP in multi-step settings. Limitations include evaluation only on Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50, reliance on the bimodal confidence assumption (which may not hold for all detector architectures or extreme class imbalances), and the per-step dual-gradient computation adding training overhead. Open questions include whether the GMM-based pseudo-labeling and gradient alignment transfer to single-stage or transformer-based detectors, and whether the approach scales to longer incremental sequences beyond 6 tasks.
