## Problem

Self-supervised 3D head reconstruction from monocular video relies on differentiable mesh rendering for photometric supervision, but mesh rasterization is inherently discontinuous and produces unrealistic renderings, limiting the quality of the learning signal. This paper asks whether replacing mesh rendering with 2D Gaussian Splatting can provide a substantially stronger self-supervised signal for 3DMM geometry prediction without requiring any 3D ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250796/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped RGB face image (resized to 224×224 for the ViT backbone and 448×448 for DINOv2 feature extraction) and produces FLAME 3DMM parameters (shape β, expression ψ, pose θ), a set of 2D Gaussians each bound to a specific mesh triangle, and a rendered head image at 512×512. Because the Gaussians are rigged to the mesh, the output avatar can be re-animated by swapping in new 3DMM parameters, enabling expression and pose transfer from a driver frame during training.

## Method

SHeaP pairs a TokenFace-style ViT that predicts 3DMM parameters and identity features with a novel Gaussians Regressor: a Lightweight GAN cross-attending to DINOv2 features generates a UV feature map, and a graph convolutional network (with adjacency defined by mesh face neighborhoods up to degree 2) maps per-face Gaussian prototypes to 14-dimensional Gaussian attributes. The Gaussians are bound to the FLAME mesh via an anisotropic scaling formulation adapted from GaussianAvatars to 2DGS, allowing them to extend beyond the mesh surface along the normal direction. Training is fully self-supervised: identity is extracted from a source frame, the rigged avatar is re-animated to match a target frame, and photometric losses (L1, VGGFace perceptual, ArcFace identity, expression) plus a geometric coupling loss (L1 on rendered normals and depth between 3DMM and 2DGS) backpropagate through both networks. A key architectural innovation is online densification and pruning of Gaussian prototypes (not the Gaussians themselves), which keeps the total Gaussian count constant while adapting spatial allocation during training.

## Results

On the NoW test set (non-metrical evaluation), SHeaP achieves a median distance of 0.95 mm and mean of 1.18 mm, outperforming all 2D-supervised baselines (best prior: AlbedoGAN at 0.98/1.21 mm) and approaching 3D-supervised methods like TokenFace (0.78/0.95 mm). On the new Nersemble expressive-geometry benchmark, SHeaP achieves 2.18 mm mean / 2.12 mm median distance, beating SMIRK (2.30/2.24 mm), DECA (2.46/2.46 mm), and EMOCA (2.58/2.54 mm). On AffectNet emotion prediction, SHeaP's 3DMM parameters yield the highest valence CCC (0.62), arousal CCC (0.74), and 8-way emotion accuracy (0.70), surpassing both EMOCA and SMIRK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHeaP wins against all 2D-supervised baselines on NoW: it improves median distance by 0.03 mm over AlbedoGAN (0.95 vs 0.98), 0.07 mm over DenseLandmark (1.02), 0.09 mm over FOCUS (1.04), 0.13 mm over CCFace (1.08), 0.14 mm over DECA (1.09), and 0.16 mm over Deep3DFaceRecon (1.11). On the Nersemble expressive benchmark, SHeaP beats SMIRK by 0.12 mm mean (2.18 vs 2.30), DECA by 0.28 mm (2.46), and EMOCA by 0.40 mm (2.58), with the lowest standard deviation (0.40 vs 0.42–0.46). On AffectNet, SHeaP outperforms EMOCA (valence CCC 0.62 vs 0.58; emotion accuracy 0.70 vs 0.68) and SMIRK (valence CCC 0.62 vs 0.56; emotion accuracy 0.70 vs 0.65). SHeaP does not match 3D-supervised methods on NoW—TokenFace (0.78/0.95 mm) and FlowFace (0.87/1.07 mm) remain ahead—but narrows the gap to 0.17 mm median, the smallest among 2D-only approaches. The paper notes that SMIRK uses MICA's shape codes, giving it a 3D-supervision advantage on shape that SHeaP does not have.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHeaP sits at the intersection of two research threads: self-supervised 3DMM predictors (DECA, EMOCA, SMIRK, AlbedoGAN) that rely on differentiable mesh rendering for photometric supervision, and 3DMM-rigged Gaussian avatar methods (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar) that assume 3DMM parameters are already known. The key departure is replacing the mesh renderer with 2D Gaussian Splatting as the photometric supervision signal, while simultaneously predicting both the 3DMM parameters and the Gaussians in a single feedforward pass. The ViT-based 3DMM estimator follows TokenFace's architecture, and the densification/pruning mechanism is adapted from 3DGS, but applied to latent Gaussian prototypes rather than explicit splats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that 2DGS rendering provides a substantially stronger self-supervised signal than differentiable mesh rendering, even before adding auxiliary losses—suggests a general principle for improving photometric supervision in self-supervised 3D reconstruction. However, the method remains scale-free (no 3D supervision), uses a fixed FOV, and the feedforward alignment between Gaussians and mesh still introduces blurring. Open questions include whether multi-view training or offline refinement can close the remaining gap to 3D-supervised methods like TokenFace, and whether the geometric coupling loss generalizes beyond head geometry to other articulated or deformable objects.
