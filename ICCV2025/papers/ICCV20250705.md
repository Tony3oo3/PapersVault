## Problem

Diffusion autoencoders have been proposed for perceptually-oriented image compression but have not achieved state-of-the-art on the competitive ImageNet-1K reconstruction benchmark, which remains dominated by CNN- or GAN-based tokenizers relying on adversarial losses and 2D spatially-aligned latents. The core challenge is that the reconstruction distribution p(x|c) is necessarily multimodal given limited latent information, and naively matching all modes yields poor perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlowMo takes a 256×256 RGB image, patchifies it, and encodes it into a 1-dimensional sequence of binary tokens (via lookup-free quantization) of configurable length (256 or 1024 tokens). Reconstruction requires integrating a rectified flow ODE over n steps (25 at inference) conditioned on the quantized latent, making inference multi-pass. The 1D latent is deliberately not spatially aligned, distinguishing it from the 2D grid latents used by VQGAN-style tokenizers.

## Method

FlowMo is a fully transformer-based diffusion autoencoder (MMDiT architecture, no convolutions) with a two-stage training scheme. Stage 1A (mode-matching pre-training) trains encoder and decoder end-to-end with a rectified flow loss, a perceptual loss on the 1-step denoised prediction, and LFQ entropy/commitment losses on the latent. Stage 1B (mode-seeking post-training) freezes the encoder and co-trains the decoder with a perceptual loss backpropagated through the full n-step ODE sampling chain, selectively dropping reconstruction modes that are not perceptually close to the original image. A shifted sampler (ρ=4) concentrates ODE steps near t=1 to bias sampling toward the mean of p(x|c), and classifier-free guidance is applied in a limited interval. The key insight is that for perceptual reconstruction, seeking a single high-similarity mode outperforms matching the full multimodal distribution.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at 256×256, FlowMo-Lo (0.070 BPP) achieves rFID 0.95, PSNR 22.07, and SSIM 0.649, setting a new state-of-the-art for discrete image tokenization at that compression rate. FlowMo-Hi (0.219 BPP) achieves rFID 0.56, PSNR 24.93, and SSIM 0.785, also state-of-the-art at its BPP. In a dedicated comparison with concurrent work DiTo (continuous latent), FlowMo achieves rFID 0.65 vs. 0.78 and PSNR 26.61 vs. 24.10. For downstream generation with MaskGiT, FlowMo-Lo matches but does not surpass OpenMagViT-V2 on FID (4.30 vs. 3.73), though it improves IS (274 vs. 241) and Precision (0.86 vs. 0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 0.070 BPP, FlowMo-Lo outperforms OpenMagViT-V2 on rFID (0.95 vs. 1.17), PSNR (22.07 vs. 21.63), and SSIM (0.649 vs. 0.640), though it trails slightly on LPIPS (0.113 vs. 0.111). At 0.219 BPP, FlowMo-Hi beats LlamaGen-32 on rFID (0.56 vs. 0.59), PSNR (24.93 vs. 24.44), and SSIM (0.785 vs. 0.768), but loses on LPIPS (0.073 vs. 0.064). FlowMo also substantially outperforms TiTok variants and Cosmos DI at lower BPPs, and surpasses ViT-VQGAN (rFID 0.56 vs. 1.28) at a comparable BPP. Against concurrent work DiTo (continuous latent), FlowMo wins on all four metrics (rFID 0.65 vs. 0.78, PSNR 26.61 vs. 24.10, SSIM 0.791 vs. 0.706, LPIPS 0.054 vs. 0.102). For downstream MaskGiT generation, FlowMo-Lo improves IS and Precision over OpenMagViT-V2 but yields a slightly worse FID (4.30 vs. 3.73) and lower Recall (0.47 vs. 0.51), indicating a complex interplay between tokenizer quality and generative performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowMo sits at the intersection of diffusion autoencoders (Preechakul et al. 2022; Yang & Mandt 2023; Birodkar et al. 2024), the MMDiT/Flux transformer architecture (Esser et al. 2024), and rectified flow (Lipman et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023), but departs from all prior diffusion autoencoder work by introducing a two-stage training scheme—mode-matching pre-training followed by mode-seeking post-training via backpropagation through the sampling chain—inspired by diffusion post-training methods like DRAFT and AlignProp. It also breaks from the dominant VQGAN-style recipe (adversarial losses, CNNs, 2D spatially-aligned latents, distillation from auxiliary tokenizers) by using a fully transformer-based architecture with 1D latent codes and LFQ quantization, while achieving SOTA on the ImageNet-1K reconstruction benchmark that has been dominated by GAN-based tokenizers like OpenMagViT-V2 and LlamaGen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowMo demonstrates that adversarial losses, convolutions, 2D spatially-aligned latents, and distillation from auxiliary tokenizers are not necessary for state-of-the-art perceptual image tokenization, which simplifies the training pipeline and opens the door to more flexible, transformer-native tokenizer designs at scale. The mode-seeking post-training stage is a reusable technique that could transfer to other diffusion autoencoder or generative compression settings. Key open questions include whether the inference-time cost of multi-step ODE sampling can be reduced, whether the modest LPIPS underperformance can be closed, and whether generative models trained on FlowMo latents can eventually match or surpass those trained on traditional tokenizers (currently they match but do not beat).
