## Problem

Existing remote sensing foundation models are either sensor-specific or designed for fixed sensor combinations, requiring architectural modifications and re-training when new sensors are introduced. This limits scalability and generalization across the diverse electromagnetic spectrum captured by optical, SAR, thermal, and RGB sensors. No single unified model can accept arbitrary sensor inputs without sensor-dependent backbones or encoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, SMARTIES consumes aligned multi-sensor image pairs (e.g., Sentinel-2 + Sentinel-1, or fMoW-RGB + fMoW-S2) resized to 224×224, where each image may have 2–13 spectral bands. Each band is independently projected through a wavelength-specific fully-connected layer, then band embeddings are averaged and scaled by C_max=12 to equalize sensors with different band counts. During downstream inference, a single sensor image is tokenized via the relevant projection layers and the ViT encoder produces a [CLS] embedding for classification or per-token features for segmentation; for unseen sensors, projection layers are interpolated by weighted average of the nearest learned layers based on central wavelength distance.

## Method

SMARTIES projects heterogeneous sensor data into a shared spectrum-aware space using 17 wavelength-specific fully-connected projection layers (f₁–f₁₇) that map each S×S band patch to a D-dimensional embedding, enforcing physical consistency across sensors covering similar spectral ranges. A cross-sensor token mixup operation exchanges tokens between paired images from different sensors using a random binary mask (50% ratio) and its mirror, preventing the model from developing bias toward specific spectral combinations. A standard ViT-B/ViT-L encoder-decoder (MAE architecture) is then trained with 75% masking to reconstruct masked patches back to original spectral channels via corresponding reprojection layers, using MSE loss. The entire pretraining uses only 496K paired images over 300 epochs, and the resulting encoder generalizes to unseen sensors at inference by interpolating projection layers for novel wavelength ranges, eliminating the need for sensor-specific backbones or re-architecting.

## Results

On BigEarthNet (10% training data), SMARTIES (ViT-L) achieves 80.5 mAP on SAR-only linear probing, 87.7 mAP on multispectral finetuning, and 86.7 mAP on multi-modal linear probing, outperforming the strongest multi-sensor baseline CROMA by 0.7, 0.1, and 1.5 mAP respectively. On EuroSAT, SMARTIES (ViT-L) reaches 98.9% top-1 accuracy under linear probing and 99.6% under finetuning, surpassing SatMAE (97.7%/99.0%). For unseen-sensor transfer, SMARTIES with projection interpolation achieves 50.2 mIoU on SICKLE crop-type segmentation with a frozen backbone, exceeding fully supervised U-Net (47.7) and DeepLapV3+ (48.5) trained from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMARTIES consistently outperforms all 21 baselines on the primary BigEarthNet benchmark: on SAR-only linear probing it leads by 5.3 mAP over GFM (78.9 vs. 73.6, ViT-B) and by 0.7 mAP over CROMA (80.5 vs. 79.8, ViT-L), while on multi-modal LP it surpasses CROMA by 1.5 mAP (86.7 vs. 85.2, ViT-L). On EuroSAT, SMARTIES (ViT-L) beats SatMAE by 1.2% in LP (98.9 vs. 97.7) and CROMA by 1.3% (98.9 vs. 97.6), and in FT it edges out SatMAE and CROMA (99.6 vs. 99.0/99.2). On RESISC-45, SMARTIES (95.8%) is competitive but trails SatMAE++ (97.5%) and Scale-MAE (95.7%), which are specifically designed for RGB. For unseen-sensor segmentation (Table 5), SMARTIES leads on BurnScars (82.8 vs. 82.4 TerraMind) and SpaceNet7 (62.2 vs. 61.8 DOFA) but is slightly behind DOFA on DynamicEarthNet (38.5 vs. 39.3). On the SICKLE thermal-infrared transfer task, SMARTIES with projection interpolation (50.2 mIoU, frozen backbone) surpasses both U-Net (47.7) and DeepLapV3+ (48.5) trained from scratch, demonstrating that spectrum-aware interpolation enables strong zero-shot sensor generalization without any sensor-specific finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMARTIES builds directly on the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework [17], adopting its masking-encoding-decoding-reconstruction pipeline as the core training objective. It departs from prior RS foundation models in a key architectural way: rather than using sensor-specific backbones (CROMA's dual encoders, SkySense's triple-modal design) or complex multi-modal MAE adaptations (DOFA's hypernetwork, SenPa-MAE's sensor encodings), SMARTIES introduces a spectrum-aware projection space that maps all sensors into a shared embedding via wavelength-indexed linear layers, combined with a cross-sensor token mixup training strategy. This positions the work as a unification of the two existing groups of multi-modal RS FMs—those with fixed sensor architectures and those that blend modalities into large training sets—into a single, sensor-agnostic model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the RS community: a single pretrained model can handle arbitrary sensor combinations at inference without architectural modifications, and the spectrum-aware projection layers make adding new sensors as simple as adding a new linear layer. The demonstrated ability to transfer to truly unseen sensor bands (e.g., thermal infrared on SICKLE) via spectral interpolation—outperforming fully supervised baselines with a frozen backbone—opens a path toward rapid adaptation to new satellite missions. Key limitations include the restriction of interpolation to wavelengths within the pretraining spectral range (no extrapolation), the absence of temporal modeling, and the relatively small pretraining set (496K images) which, while efficient, may limit representation quality for very diverse or rare land-cover types. Future work on temporal extension and broader downstream task coverage is explicitly noted as needed.
