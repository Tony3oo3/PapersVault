## Problem

Existing drone visual geo-localization relies on single-image queries, which are highly vulnerable to occlusions from buildings and trees at low flight altitudes, and suffer from severe viewpoint disparity between oblique drone views and top-down satellite views. Prior 2D geometric transformations (polar, spherical) used to bridge this gap introduce significant spatial distortion and blurring, degrading matching accuracy. The paper addresses both limitations by formulating a video-based geo-localization task that exploits multi-view drone footage and a 3D-aware transformation to produce undistorted Bird's-Eye-View representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a drone-view video (multi-view frames captured in a spiral flight around a target location, subsampled to 2, 5, or 10 FPS) and a satellite-view image as inputs, all resized to 256×256. The drone video is first transformed into a sequence of 512×512 BEV images via 3DGS reconstruction and top-down rendering. At test time, the drone video is treated as a single query or gallery entry by temporally averaging per-frame similarity scores (late fusion), and the final output is a ranked list of candidate locations with match scores from a two-stage retrieval-and-rerank pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is the Video2BEV transformation: camera poses are estimated via structure-from-motion, the scene is reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting, and BEV projections are rendered from a normalized top-down camera pose, preserving fine-grained textures without the distortion inherent to 2D warps. A diffusion-based hard-negative module fine-tunes Stable Diffusion with LoRA (using captions from a visual-LLM) to synthesize semantically similar but fine-grainedly different BEV and satellite images, expanding the negative sample pool beyond in-batch mining. The matching model uses a shared ViT-S encoder trained in two stages: stage 1 optimizes the encoder with instance loss (square-ring partition) and contrastive loss; stage 2 freezes stage-1 weights and trains a two-layer MLP re-ranker with a binary matching loss that explicitly incorporates the synthetic hard negatives. This two-stage design decouples coarse retrieval from fine-grained discrimination, and the BEV representation aligns the drone view with the satellite's top-down perspective, reducing the inter-platform appearance gap.

## Results

On the UniV dataset (45° subset), Video2BEV achieves 96.80% AP for Drone→Satellite and 96.57% AP for Satellite→Drone, matching or slightly exceeding the second-best method Sample4Geo (96.75% and 95.99% AP respectively). On the more challenging 30° subset with increased occlusions, Video2BEV achieves 93.01% AP (D→S) and 93.65% AP (S→D), outperforming the runner-up DWDR by 3.2% and 5.8% AP respectively. In out-of-distribution testing on the unseen SUES-200 dataset, Video2BEV maintains 91.50% AP (D→S) and 92.53% AP (S→D), surpassing the next-best method by over 7% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 45° UniV subset, Video2BEV ties with Sample4Geo on D→S R@1 (both 96.29) but edges it out on AP (96.80 vs 96.75), and clearly wins on S→D (96.57 vs 95.99 AP). The gap widens substantially on the 30° subset where occlusions are more severe: Video2BEV beats the second-best DWDR by 3.2% AP (D→S) and 5.8% AP (S→D), while Sample4Geo degrades sharply to 86.00% AP (D→S) compared to Video2BEV's 93.01%. LPN and FSRA trail significantly across all settings, with LPN scoring only 72.50% AP on 30° D→S. In the OOD evaluation on SUES-200, Video2BEV outperforms Sample4Geo by 7.36% AP (D→S: 91.50 vs 84.14) and 3.73% AP (S→D: 92.53 vs 88.80), demonstrating strong generalization to real-world conditions without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-based drone geo-localization paradigm (LPN, FSRA, DWDR, Sample4Geo) by formulating a video-based task and introducing a 3D-aware transformation via 3D Gaussian Splatting to produce BEV representations, replacing the 2D geometric warps (polar, spherical) used in prior cross-view alignment work. The hard negative synthesis module adapts the diffusion-based generation idea from GeNIe [20] to the geo-localization setting, using LoRA-finetuned Stable Diffusion conditioned on visual-LLM captions to produce semantically consistent but fine-grainedly different negatives. The two-stage training architecture (coarse retrieval + fine-grained re-ranking with matching loss) follows the vision-language pre-training lineage of BLIP and OFA, while the UniV dataset extends University-1652 by adding video modality and a lower 30° elevation angle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The video-based formulation addresses a practical gap: real drones produce video, not single snapshots, and the multi-view nature of circling flights naturally recovers occluded regions that cripple single-image matching. The UniV dataset, while synthetic (Google Earth 3D engine), provides the first video-based drone geo-localization benchmark with two elevation angles, enabling evaluation under more realistic occlusion conditions. A key open question is whether the 3DGS-based BEV transformation generalizes to real-world drone footage with sensor noise, motion blur, and imperfect pose estimation, since UniV is entirely simulated. The strong OOD transfer to SUES-200 (91.50 AP) is encouraging but tested only at 45°; robustness at 30° on real data remains unverified.
