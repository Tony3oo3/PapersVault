## Problem

Single-chip mmWave radars offer compact, weather-robust sensing but suffer from poor angular resolution (~15°), and the field lacks both a large raw radar dataset and a standardized foundational model. Existing learning-based radar methods are trained from scratch on small datasets (≤3.6 hours) using lossy processed representations like CFAR point clouds, leaving the potential of transformer architectures at scale unexplored for radar.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw I/Q radar streams from a TI AWR1843 (3 TX × 4 RX array), which are 4D-FFT'd into range-Doppler-azimuth-elevation cubes of shape (256, 64, 8, 2) and provided as two channels (amplitude and phase angle). These are patched along range and Doppler into 4×2 patches (with azimuth and elevation 'patched out'), yielding 2048 patch tokens. Outputs span multiple spatial bases: 3D polar occupancy (64×128×64 range-azimuth-elevation cells), BEV occupancy (256×1024 range-azimuth), semantic segmentation (640×640 azimuth-elevation, 8 classes), and ego-motion velocity, requiring a change of basis between input and output coordinate systems with vastly different relative resolutions.

## Method

GRT is a direct adaptation of the original Transformer/ViT architecture with a Perceiver I/O-style decoder to handle the change of basis between the 4D radar input space and the various output spaces. The encoder patches the 4D cube along range-Doppler axes, applies a linear projection and sinusoidal positional encoding, and processes tokens through standard transformer layers (2–9 encoder + 2–9 decoder layers, 256–768 dim). The decoder uses a learned output token concatenated to the encoder output, which is tiled into the target decoder shape with a 3D sinusoidal positional encoding and attends back to encoder outputs. The base pre-training task is 3D polar occupancy classification (binary cross-entropy against Lidar ground truth), chosen because it covers all three spatial axes and is fully self-supervised; downstream tasks are obtained by swapping the output head and modifying positional encoding queries. Key empirical findings include that raw complex 4D cubes outperform CFAR or AoA representations by more than 10× equivalent training data, and that GRT exhibits logarithmic scaling of ~20% loss reduction per 10× data increase, with an estimated ~100M samples needed to saturate the model.

## Results

On the Coloradar dataset, fine-tuned GRT achieves a geometric mean Chamfer distance of 0.98 m for 2D BEV occupancy, outperforming the state-of-the-art diffusion model (1.19 m) and RadarHD (1.73 m). On the I/Q-1M dataset, GRT-small achieves a 3D chamfer distance of 4.9 range bins (0.66 m indoors, 1.6 m outdoors, 1.5 m on bike). The model exhibits logarithmic data scaling of approximately 20% loss decrease per 10× increase in training data, and using raw 4D radar cubes instead of CFAR point clouds yields a 31.5% loss reduction, equivalent to more than a 10× increase in training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRT outperforms all three baselines on the primary comparison. On the Coloradar dataset, fine-tuned GRT achieves a geometric mean Chamfer distance of 0.98 m, beating the state-of-the-art diffusion model [70] (1.19 m) by 17% and RadarHD [45] (1.73 m) by 43%; GRT wins on 5 of 6 individual traces, losing only on Aspen (0.61 vs 0.51 m). On the I/Q-1M dataset for 2D BEV occupancy, GRT-small outperforms both the U-Net-based RadarHD and the Swin Transformer-based TFFTRadNet [15] at all training set sizes (10%–100%), demonstrating the scalability advantage of the transformer architecture. Notably, GRT achieves these results after only ~30 minutes of fine-tuning on Coloradar, without any architectural modifications despite the dataset using a different modulation and resolution (128×128) than the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant radar ML paradigm of using CFAR-extracted point clouds as model input, instead feeding raw 4D range-Doppler-azimuth-elevation data cubes directly into a transformer. Architecturally, it adapts the standard Vision Transformer with a Perceiver I/O-style decoder to handle the fundamental mismatch between radar's polar input axes and Cartesian output axes, a problem absent in vision. It extends prior single-chip radar work (RadarHD, TFFTRadNet, diffusion-based methods) that was limited to 2D outputs (BEV occupancy, segmentation) by introducing 3D polar occupancy as a self-supervised base task, and positions the effort explicitly as a foundational-model initiative analogous to how large-scale pre-training transformed vision and NLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The I/Q-1M dataset (1M samples, 29 hours) is 8× larger than the next largest raw radar dataset and the first to enable meaningful scaling-law analysis for radar transformers, revealing ~20% loss reduction per 10× data increase and suggesting ~100M samples are needed for saturation. The finding that raw data outperforms CFAR point clouds by an amount equivalent to 10× more training data directly challenges the field's default assumption that processed representations are the appropriate input. Key limitations include a single radar type (AWR1843), daylight/fair-weather-only collection, and no evaluation across different antenna configurations, leaving open whether these scaling laws and raw-data advantages generalize to other single-chip or cascaded radar hardware.
