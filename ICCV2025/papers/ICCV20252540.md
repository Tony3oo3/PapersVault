## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving systems rely on fixed, sparse trajectory supervision that fails to capture the hierarchical cognitive reasoning human drivers naturally employ—progressing from strategic scene understanding to tactical decisions to precise control. While VLMs have been added as auxiliary semantic modules, they are treated as isolated preprocessing steps rather than being organically embedded across the full decision hierarchy, limiting generalization and interpretability in dynamic traffic scenarios.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view camera images (processed by a BEV scene encoder) and front-view images fed to a VLM with structured prompts that generate free-form driving strategy text and four-category tactical commands (Direction, Emergency, Lane, Speed). The VLM text outputs are parsed via regex into discrete one-hot vectors, then encoded into continuous feature spaces. The final output is a future trajectory (planned over T timesteps at 1–3 s horizons) produced by a two-stage coarse-to-fine decoder, alongside the intermediate strategy text and tactical command features that make the reasoning chain inspectable.

## Method

ReAL-AD introduces a three-tier architecture mirroring human driving cognition. The Strategic Reasoning Injector encodes VLM-generated strategy text through a text encoder and lightweight MLP adapter, then injects it into the ego-query via residual addition, with a cosine-similarity loss aligning strategy semantics to GT trajectory features. The Tactical Reasoning Integrator parses VLM-generated structured commands into one-hot vectors, maps them through learnable embeddings, and splits them into reactive-level (direction + emergency) and regulatory-level (lane + speed) features operating at different decoder layers. The Hierarchical Trajectory Decoder is a two-layer variational autoencoder: the first layer conditions a coarse latent z^c on ego features and reactive commands, while the second layer refines a fine latent z^f conditioned on ego features, z^c, and regulatory commands; both latents are decoded into waypoint sequences via GRU-based temporal modeling. Training uses a composite loss combining baseline planning losses, strategy–trajectory similarity, GT reconstruction, coarse-trajectory regression, and a two-level KL divergence enforcing consistency across the hierarchical latent spaces.

## Results

On nuScenes, ReAL-AD (VAD + MiniCPM-V) achieves an average L2 error of 0.48 m and a collision rate of 0.15%, representing a 33% reduction in L2 error and a 32% decrease in collision rate over the VAD-Base baseline (0.72 m / 0.22%). On Bench2Drive, the same configuration attains 0.84 m average L2 error, 0.11% collision rate, a Driving Score of 41.17, and a Success Rate of 11.36%, outperforming the VAD baseline (0.94 m / 0.20% / 39.42 / 10.00%). The UniAD-based variant achieves 0.77 m L2 and 0.17% collision on nuScenes, and 0.69 m L2 with a 10.00% Success Rate on Bench2Drive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VAD-Base baseline on nuScenes, ReAL-AD (VAD+MiniCPM-V) reduces average L2 error from 0.72 m to 0.48 m (−33%) and collision rate from 0.22% to 0.15% (−32%). Among VLM-augmented competitors, ReAL-AD outperforms VLP-VAD (0.55 m / 0.15%) on L2 while matching its collision rate, beats VLM-AD(VAD) (0.48 m / 0.23%) on collision rate by 0.08 pp, and surpasses Senna (0.59 m / 0.18%) on both metrics. DriveVLM* reports lower L2 (0.31 m) and collision (0.10%), but it uses ego-vehicle status features as input and is explicitly excluded from the fair ranking. On Bench2Drive, ReAL-AD (VAD+MiniCPM-V) improves over VAD on all four metrics (L2 0.84 vs 0.94 m, collision 0.11% vs 0.20%, Driving Score 41.17 vs 39.42, Success Rate 11.36% vs 10.00%), and over UniAD-base on L2 (0.69 vs 0.74 m) and collision (1.40% vs 1.85%). The UniAD-based variant of ReAL-AD also outperforms the UniAD-base baseline on L2 (0.69 vs 0.74 m) and collision (1.40% vs 1.85%) on Bench2Drive, though its Driving Score (39.13) is slightly below VAD's (39.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReAL-AD builds directly on the VAD and UniAD end-to-end planning architectures, extending GenAD's temporal latent trajectory modeling with a dual-stream hierarchical variational decoder. Its key departure from prior VLM-for-driving work (DriveVLM, VLM-AD, VLP, Senna) is treating VLM-generated reasoning not as an auxiliary preprocessing signal but as an organically embedded three-tier cognitive hierarchy (Strategy → Decision → Operation) that conditions each stage of the planning pipeline. This positions the paper at the intersection of structured VLM reasoning and hierarchical trajectory generation, rather than the more common 'VLM as teacher' or 'VLM as feature provider' paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—sparse trajectory supervision in end-to-end driving—by inserting interpretable intermediate reasoning layers, which could make planning failures easier to diagnose and correct. However, the VLM is invoked offline (pre-generated strategy text and commands), raising significant questions about real-time deployment and whether the gains persist when the VLM must operate at inference frequency. Open questions include whether the three-tier decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-agent interactions, whether the hierarchical variational decoder's benefits persist at higher planning horizons, and whether the approach scales to closed-loop settings where compounding errors amplify.
