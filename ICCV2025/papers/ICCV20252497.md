## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot segmentation (CD-FSS) requires generalizing to novel classes in unseen domains with only a few labeled support images, but existing CD-FSS methods are costly to train because they redesign and retrain in-domain FSS models on abundant source-domain base data. Moreover, these retrained models use fixed, manually-selected trainable structures that ignore the distinct characteristics of different target domains (e.g., satellite imagery relying on low-level texture vs. medical images requiring high-level semantics), and they fail to leverage the strong performance of well-trained in-domain FSS models.

## Input / Output

The method takes K-shot support images (each with a ground-truth binary mask) and a single unlabeled query image, both resized to 400×400 pixels, along with a pre-trained in-domain FSS model (e.g., SSP with ResNet-50 backbone). All adaptation occurs at inference time: the support data is used to identify which model layer to train and to construct training pairs, while the query image is segmented using the adapted model. No source-domain data or additional learnable parameters are required at test time, and the method is model-agnostic, applicable to both CNN and transformer-based FSS backbones.

## Method

ISA consists of two modules operating at inference time. The Informative Structure Identification (ISI) module computes a novel structure Fisher score for each convolutional layer—the maximum absolute empirical Fisher information across all parameters in that layer—using only the K support samples, then selects the single layer with the highest score as the trainable structure, freezing all other parameters to prevent overfitting. The Progressive Structure Adaptation (PSA) module then trains this selected layer by hierarchically constructing support-query pairs: it first cyclically designates each support image as a pseudo query (with the remaining K−1 as support), then progressively increases the number of support shots from 1 to K−1, updating parameters sequentially (θ₁* → θ₂* → … → θ_{K−1}*) to gradually close the domain gap. This data-dependent layer selection means different target domains automatically activate different model structures (e.g., low-level layers for texture-heavy satellite imagery, high-level layers for semantic-rich FSS-1000), and the progressive training strategy outperforms direct multi-shot training by up to 2.5 mIoU.

## Results

On the primary CD-FSS benchmark (Table 1), ISA applied to SSP achieves 70.3 mIoU (5-shot) and 60.9 mIoU (1-shot) averaged across four target domains (DeepGlobe, ISIC, Chest X-ray, FSS-1000), improving the SSP baseline by +7.3/+4.1 mIoU without any source domain retraining. In the 5-shot setting, ISA outperforms the strongest CD-FSS baseline DMTNet (66.0) by 4.3 mIoU and DR-Adapter (65.4) by 4.9 mIoU. On the SUIM dataset, ISA reaches 44.1 mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA DR-Adapter (40.3) by 3.8 mIoU. The method also generalizes to transformer-based and SAM-based FSS models, yielding +5.5 to +7.3 mIoU improvements across SSP, PANet, FPTrans, PerSAM, and DR-Adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 5-shot setting, ISA (70.3 mIoU) decisively outperforms all CD-FSS baselines that require source domain retraining: DMTNet (66.0, −4.3), DR-Adapter (65.4, −4.9), APSeg (65.1, −5.2), ABCDFSS (65.0, −5.3), PMNet (64.1, −6.2), and PATNet (62.0, −8.3). In the 1-shot setting, ISA (60.9) ties DR-Adapter (60.9) and marginally beats PMNet (60.8) and ABCDFSS (60.7), while APSeg (61.3) edges ahead by 0.4 mIoU due to its stronger ViT backbone. Against in-domain FSS methods applied without retraining (SSP 63.0, PANet 55.1, HSNet 51.4), ISA yields substantial gains of +7.3, +6.7, and +18.9 mIoU respectively, confirming that the adaptation mechanism is the key contributor. On the SUIM benchmark, ISA (44.1) beats DR-Adapter (40.3) by 3.8 mIoU and MMT (35.9) by 8.2 mIoU. In Table 4, ISA (70.3) also surpasses domain generalization methods (Mixstyle 63.8, DSU 64.2, NP 64.5), TTT methods (TTT 63.1, Tent 63.3), PEFT methods (Adapter 66.4, BitFit 65.9), and SAM-based methods (PerSAM 61.5, Matcher 62.1) by 3.9–8.8 mIoU, demonstrating that data-driven structure selection plus progressive adaptation is superior to fixed-layer or self-supervised adaptation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: test-time training (TTT) for domain adaptation at inference, and the empirical Fisher information metric from continual learning for measuring parameter importance. It departs from the dominant CD-FSS paradigm (PATNet, PMNet, DR-Adapter, DMTNet) that redesigns and retrains models on abundant source-domain base data, instead proposing to adapt a well-trained in-domain FSS model entirely during inference. Unlike prior TTT methods that apply fixed, manually-selected trainable layers uniformly across domains, ISA dynamically identifies which convolutional layer to adapt per target domain via a structure Fisher score, and introduces progressive training over increasing support-shot counts to mitigate overfitting in the few-shot regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can take any existing well-trained FSS model (ResNet-based, Transformer-based, or SAM-based) and equip it with cross-domain capability without the costly source-domain retraining pipeline, making CD-FSS far more accessible. The model-agnostic nature and single-layer, single-iteration adaptation make the method lightweight and easy to deploy. Open questions include how the approach behaves under extreme domain shifts (e.g., synthetic-to-real), whether the single-layer constraint limits ceiling performance, and whether the structure Fisher score generalizes to architectures without clear layer hierarchies (e.g., pure attention models). The paper also notes that foundation models like SAM underperform in non-natural domains, suggesting ISA could serve as a complementary adaptation layer for such models.
