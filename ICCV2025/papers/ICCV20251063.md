## Problem

Existing skeleton-based action recognition methods struggle with fine-grained actions because coarse-grained features dominate the learning process, overwhelming the subtle distinctions needed to separate similar classes. Additionally, the inherent hierarchical parent-child structure among action classes (e.g., 'Reading' and 'Writing' sharing a parent with 'Sit down') is not explicitly exploited, and prior fine-grained methods ignore the interference that coarse-grained features impose on fine-grained feature learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes skeleton sequences as input, represented as joint positions over time with shape N×C×T×V (batch, channels, frames, joints), which are reshaped into spatial features (N×V×CT) and temporal features (N×T×CV) for independent processing. The output is a single action class label produced by a cross-entropy head on the fused coarse- and fine-grained features. The backbone is a GCN-TCN architecture, and the method is evaluated on datasets ranging from 10 to 120 action classes with varying numbers of joints (25 for FSD-10) and frame counts (up to 1,500 for FSD-10).

## Method

HiOD disentangles coarse- and fine-grained features via four independent granularity-aware orthogonal bases (spatial/temporal × coarse/fine), enforced orthogonal through QR decomposition during the forward pass, which project the embedding features into distinct subspaces. A shared orthogonal basis, with MLP-learned projection coefficients, enables cross-granularity interaction by aligning both feature types in a common semantic space. Hierarchical Prototype Contrastive Learning (HiPCL) discovers parent-child class structure via KMeans clustering on fine-grained features, then trains coarse-grained features with soft prototypes (weighted by the cluster assignment matrix) under a weighted contrastive loss, while fine-grained features are trained with hard prototypes (high-confidence samples only) under a standard contrastive loss. The total objective combines cross-entropy on the fused feature, an intermediate supervision loss on the backbone embedding, and both contrastive losses with tunable weights.

## Results

HiOD (6-ensemble) achieves top-1 accuracy of 96.3% on FineGYM, 95.9% on FSD-10, 93.8% (X-Sub) / 97.6% (X-View) on NTU RGB+D, and 90.4% (X-Sub) / 91.9% (X-Set) on NTU RGB+D 120, setting new state-of-the-art on all six benchmarks. The largest gains appear on the fine-grained datasets: +1.7% over the previous best (HD-GCN, 94.6%) on FineGYM and +1.1% over BlockGCN (94.8%) on FSD-10. On the harder confusing-action subset of NTU60 (accuracy <70% under CTR-GCN), HiOD improves by 3.9% over the prior best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiOD outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every reported benchmark. The closest competitors are HD-GCN [14] (94.6% on FineGYM, 94.5% on FSD-10, 93.4% on NTU X-Sub) and BlockGCN [43] (94.8% on FSD-10, 90.3% on NTU120 X-Sub); HiOD surpasses them by 1.7%, 1.1%, 0.4%, and 0.1% respectively. On NTU RGB+D X-View, DS-GCN [36] (97.5%) is the nearest rival, with HiOD edging ahead by 0.1%. Earlier GCN-based methods (ST-GCN, 2s-AGCN, MS-G3D, CTR-GCN, InfoGCN) trail by 5–10+ points, confirming that the granularity disentanglement and hierarchical contrastive learning provide substantial gains over standard graph convolutional backbones. Fine-grained-specific methods (WDCE-Net, MDR-GCN, FR-Head) are also consistently outperformed, with HiOD gaining 1.8–2.4% on FineGYM over MDR-GCN and WDCE-Net.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—GCN-based skeleton action recognition, disentangled representation learning, and hierarchical classification—into a unified framework. It departs from prior fine-grained methods (WDCE-Net, MDR-GCN, FR-Head) that extract local features via attention or decoupled losses by explicitly modeling the interference of coarse-grained features on fine-grained ones through orthogonal basis projection. The use of learnable orthogonal bases (enforced via QR decomposition) as a granularity-aware feature selector, combined with a shared orthogonal basis for cross-granularity interaction, is a novel architectural choice compared to the separate-head or wavelet-based disentanglement strategies common in the literature. The hierarchical prototype contrastive learning with dual soft/hard prototypes extends standard prototype contrastive loss by incorporating a clustering-discovered class hierarchy, going beyond prior hierarchical classification work that requires predefined parent-child relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in fine-grained skeleton action recognition where coarse-grained features dominate training and suppress subtle discriminative signals, a problem particularly acute in domains like gymnastics and figure skating. The KMeans-based hierarchical discovery is a reasonable but somewhat ad hoc solution; its sensitivity to the number of clusters K and its assumption that feature-space proximity reflects semantic hierarchy remain open questions. The method's reliance on a specific GCN-TCN backbone and the modest gains over simpler disentanglement alternatives (channel split, FC heads) suggest the orthogonal basis mechanism, while elegant, may not be the sole driver of performance. Broader applicability to other modalities or to settings where the class hierarchy is genuinely unknown and non-flat remains to be explored.
