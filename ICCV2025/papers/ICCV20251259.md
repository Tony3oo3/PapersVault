## Problem

Existing feature visualization methods (activation maximization, Fourier preconditioning, MACO) produce repetitive patterns, visual artifacts, and irrelevant features that make it difficult for humans to understand what a neuron actually encodes. These problems worsen when scaling to modern architectures like large ResNets and Vision Transformers, and for intermediate neurons where co-activated but irrelevant features (e.g., grass for a beak detector) dominate the visualization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251259/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained network (ResNet50, DenseNet121, ConvNeXt-base, ViT-L-16/32), a target neuron or channel, and a set of reference images (class training images or top-k activating patches for inner neurons), VITAL produces a single RGB image that visualizes the information the target responds to. The reference set is typically a small number of patches (top-k from distinct images), and the optimization runs in roughly 40 seconds for class neurons and 2–3 minutes for inner neurons, with feature distributions precomputed from the references.

## Method

VITAL reframes feature visualization as distribution alignment rather than activation maximization: it optimizes a generated image so that per-channel activation distributions at selected intermediate layers match those of real reference images, using a differentiable sort-matching loss borrowed from style transfer. To eliminate irrelevant co-activations, activations are weighted by LRP relevance scores to the target neuron before distribution matching, so only causally contributing features are preserved. The total loss combines the sort-matching term across a small set of layers (typically first and last block outputs) with auxiliary total-variation and L2 regularization, and a transparency map derived from accumulated gradients highlights the image regions the network attended to during generation. The approach is architecture-agnostic, scaling seamlessly from CNNs to ViTs without requiring generative priors.

## Results

On ImageNet, VITAL achieves near-perfect top-1 classification accuracy (99.9% on ResNet50, ConvNeXt, DenseNet121; 99.8% on ViT-L-16; 89.6% on ViT-L-32), matching DeepInversion and far exceeding MACO (9–66%) and Fourier (17–60%). VITAL also achieves substantially lower FID scores than MACO and Fourier across all architectures (e.g., 58.79 vs. 360.74 on ResNet50; 3.92 vs. 62.55 on ConvNeXt). On CLIP zero-shot prediction, VITAL approaches real-image accuracy (e.g., 66.62% Top-1 on ResNet50 vs. 69.11% for actual ImageNet images), while MACO and Fourier score below 18% and DeepInversion below 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITAL decisively outperforms MACO and Fourier on all three metrics across all five architectures. On top-1 accuracy, VITAL reaches ~99.9% while MACO ranges from 9% (DenseNet121) to 66% (ConvNeXt) and Fourier from 17% to 60%. On FID, VITAL improves over MACO by 6–16× (e.g., 58.79 vs. 360.74 on ResNet50; 147.33 vs. 2318.90 on ViT-L-32). On CLIP zero-shot Top-1, VITAL scores 56–68%, approaching real-image accuracy (65–71%), whereas MACO and Fourier score below 18% and DeepInversion below 30%. Against DeepInversion, VITAL ties on top-1 accuracy (~100%) and is slightly worse on FID for ResNet50 (58.79 vs. 35.76) but comparable on DenseNet121 (0.27 vs. 0.20); however, DeepInversion is not evaluated on ConvNeXt or ViT architectures, and VITAL's CLIP zero-shot scores (58–68%) far exceed DeepInversion's (10–30%), indicating substantially better perceptual realism and generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITAL reframes feature visualization from the long-standing activation-maximization paradigm (Zeiler & Fergus, Mahendran & Vedaldi, through Fourier-based optimization and MACO) into a distribution-alignment problem, borrowing the sort-matching loss from the style-transfer literature (Zhang et al., CVPR 2022) as its core differentiable optimization primitive. It further integrates LRP relevance scores (Lapuschkin et al.) to weight which features should be matched, and adopts the top-k patch selection strategy from CRAFT (Fel et al., CVPR 2023) for constructing reference sets. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire FV lineage that iteratively refined regularization (TV, blurring, Fourier preconditioning, GAN priors) to suppress artifacts, arguing instead that aligning empirical feature distributions with real data naturally discourages both repetitive patterns and off-manifold features without ad-hoc penalties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VITAL provides a practical, architecture-agnostic tool for mechanistic interpretability that scales to ViTs and large CNNs, directly addressing the gap between circuit discovery (where information flows) and feature visualization (what information is encoded). The paper acknowledges that visualizations still fall short of photorealism and that the FV evaluation landscape lacks standardized benchmarks, leaving open questions about whether generative priors (e.g., diffusion models) can be integrated without introducing model-specific artifacts that compromise faithfulness. Future directions include ViT-specific attribution (AttnLRP), CLIP-based multimodal models, and automatic circuit selection, positioning VITAL as a building block for broader interpretability pipelines in safety-critical domains.
