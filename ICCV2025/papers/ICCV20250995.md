## Problem

Existing multi-human image generation methods rely on 2D skeleton-based conditioning that lacks depth cues, producing ambiguous occlusions and anatomical distortions in complex multi-person scenes. Additionally, most personalization approaches focus exclusively on facial identity, failing to preserve or control full-body shape and struggling to disentangle intrinsic body characteristics from clothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250995/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given full-body reference images of 1–5 individuals, target 3D poses (SMPLx parameters), body shape coefficients (SMPLx β estimated via MultiHMR), face embeddings (InsightFace), and a text prompt, the system produces a single personalized multi-human scene image at SDXL resolution. The dual-pathway body shape representation optionally accepts a textual description to refine body composition details (e.g., muscle vs. fat distribution) that SMPLx parameters alone cannot capture, and supports user-defined interpolation/extrapolation between reference body shapes for fine-grained control.

## Method

PersonaCraft replaces 2D skeleton ControlNet with SMPLx-ControlNet (SCNet), which conditions the diffusion U-Net on depth and normal map renderings from SMPLx meshes, providing explicit 3D geometry for occlusion-aware pose conditioning and body shape encoding. For fine-grained occlusions, an Occlusion Boundary Enhancer Network (OccNet) is trained on sparse depth-edge signals restricted to occlusion masks (pixels where a ray intersects >2 SMPLx faces), forcing the model to learn occluded-region synthesis from boundary cues alone. An Occlusion-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (OccCFG) strategy then applies a higher CFG scale (k_occ=5) in occluded regions versus a lower base scale (k_base=3) elsewhere, strengthening 3D pose conditioning where it matters most without over-saturating unoccluded areas. At inference, SCNet residuals, OccNet residuals, and Face Identity ControlNet residuals are injected into the base U-Net with spatially modulated weights (occlusion mask for SCNet/OccNet, face masks for identity), enabling simultaneous full-body multi-human personalization with robust occlusion handling.

## Results

On the COCO-WholeBody test set (1,000 images, 1–5 people), PersonaCraft achieves a total face ID preservation score of 0.317 and body shape preservation of 0.560, substantially outperforming the best baseline (InstantID+OMG at 0.227 and 0.374 respectively). For pose accuracy, PersonaCraft reduces 3D MPJPE to 60.65 mm (vs. 112.6 for InstantID+OMG) and doubles the 2D AP-0.5 to 0.506 (vs. 0.258). In the user study (18,540 responses, 103 participants), PersonaCraft receives 40–44% Top-1 preference across naturalness, face ID, body shape, and text correspondence, roughly 3× the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PersonaCraft dominates all baselines across every metric in the multi-human personalization setting. Compared to InstantID+OMG (the strongest baseline), PersonaCraft improves face ID preservation by 39% (0.317 vs. 0.227), body shape preservation by 50% (0.560 vs. 0.374), and nearly halves the 3D MPJPE (60.65 vs. 112.6 mm). IPAdapter+OMG and IPA-Face+OMG perform even worse on face ID (0.142 and 0.188) and show similar pose errors (~115–116 MPJPE), confirming that 2D-skeleton-based conditioning is fundamentally limited in occlusion-heavy multi-person scenes. In the pose-controlled generation setting (Table 3), PersonaCraft achieves 62.65 MPJPE vs. 102.64 (ControlNet-Flux), 103.62 (HumanSD), 114.93 (ControlNet-SDXL), and 198.14 (T2I Adapter-SDXL), with the largest gap over T2I Adapter-SDXL. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is KID in pose-controlled generation (HumanSD: 0.082 vs. Ours: 0.091), though PersonaCraft still leads on IS (4.113 vs. 3.610) and all pose metrics. In user studies, PersonaCraft achieves 72–75% Top-1 preference in pose-controlled generation and 40–44% in personalization, far exceeding the next-best methods (≤19% and ≤15% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet paradigm for pose-conditioned human generation by replacing 2D skeleton inputs with 3D SMPLx depth and normal map renderings, building on prior 3D-pose works (Champ, HumanLDM) that were limited to single-person scenarios. For personalization, it departs from face-identity-only methods (InstantID, IP-Adapter, OMG) by encoding full-body shape through SMPLx coefficients, and introduces a dual-pathway text refinement to handle cases where SMPLx shape parameters are insufficient. The occlusion-specific components (OccNet, OccCFG) are novel additions that adapt classifier-free guidance spatially, drawing on prior spatially-adaptive guidance work but applying it specifically to human occlusion boundaries derived from 3D mesh geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-person scene generation with accurate occlusion handling and full-body identity preservation is a practically important gap: existing tools either fail on overlapping bodies or only preserve facial identity while ignoring body shape. The SMPLx-based 3D conditioning provides a principled way to inject depth and shape information into diffusion models, and the framework's modularity (separable SCNet, OccNet, Face ControlNet) makes it a useful building block. However, the ablation reveals that the occlusion-specific components (OccNet, OccCFG) contribute only marginal quantitative gains over the base 3D conditioning, suggesting the core benefit comes from 3D geometry rather than the dedicated occlusion machinery. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, handles more extreme body compositions beyond SMPLx's parameterization, and scales to more than 5 people.
