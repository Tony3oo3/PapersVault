## Problem

In heterogeneous federated learning, non-IID data across clients causes local models to overfit their local distributions, leading to slow and unstable convergence. Existing remedies (local regularization, server-side aggregation) reduce gradient inconsistency but do not address the root cause of local overfitting. This paper asks whether the fine-grained visual representations inherent in pre-trained diffusion models can serve as effective steers to provide both semantic diversity and consistent convergence signals during federated training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds 224×224 RGB images with class labels; the local model (ResNet-10 or MobileNetV2) produces a 512-dim embedding that is injected as a conditioning vector into a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet alongside text prompts of the form 'a photo of a [class name]'. The final output is a |C|-dim logit vector for classification. In the self-supervised variant, no labels are required—generic prompts like 'a photo of a similar object' replace class-specific ones, and negative prompts are sampled from Tiny-ImageNet class names.

## Method

FedDifRC introduces two complementary modules that exploit a frozen Stable Diffusion model as a representation source. (1) Text-Driven Diffusion Contrastive Learning (TDCL) feeds the local model's embedding as a condition into the SD UNet at T=0 with the correct-class text prompt to produce a positive diffusion representation, and with mismatched-class prompts to produce negatives; a contrastive loss (Eq. 11) pulls the embedding toward the positive and away from negatives, with a data-dependent normalization factor U. (2) Noise-Driven Diffusion Consistent Restriction (NDCR) adds t-step Gaussian noise to the image, runs it through the SD UNet at timestep t with the class prompt, extracts intermediate feature maps from decoder layers L∈{2,3,4}, applies PCA to reduce dimensions (1280→256, 640→128, 320→128), concatenates them into a fused denoising representation, and imposes an L2 alignment loss (Eq. 12) between the local embedding and this representation. The total local objective is L_TDCL + L_NDCR + L_CE, and the paper proves convergence under non-convex objectives with an explicit bound on the number of rounds R and learning rate η.

## Results

FedDifRC achieves the best accuracy across all evaluated settings and datasets. On CIFAR-10, it reaches 88.27% under NID1_0.2 and 76.45% under the extreme NID2 setting, outperforming the strongest baselines (FedCDA at 86.89% and FedRCL at 75.13%) by 1.4–1.3 points. On CIFAR-100, it attains 63.32% (NID1_0.2) and 53.87% (NID2), and on Tiny-ImageNet it reaches 45.28% (NID1_0.2) and 40.05% (NID2). Under long-tailed CIFAR-10-LT (NID1_0.2), FedDifRC improves over the best baseline by 1.1–1.4 points across imbalance ratios ρ=10 to ρ=200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDifRC consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against the strongest contrastive baselines (MOON, FedRCL), it gains 1.0–1.4 points on CIFAR-10 and 1.1–1.3 points on CIFAR-100. Against the strongest aggregation-based baselines (FedDisco, FedCDA), the margin is 1.1–1.4 points on CIFAR-10 and 1.0–1.5 points on CIFAR-100. On Tiny-ImageNet, FedDifRC leads FedRCL by 1.2–1.3 points across all settings. Under long-tailed CIFAR-10-LT, it surpasses FedCDA (the best baseline) by 1.1–1.4 points as the imbalance ratio increases from ρ=10 to ρ=200. On the domain-shift Office-Caltech benchmark, FedDifRC achieves 60.08% average accuracy, beating FedCDA (57.87%) and FedDisco (57.71%) by over 2 points. In the self-supervised setting (no labels), FedDifRC reaches 31.45% on CIFAR-100 (NID1_0.2), outperforming FedLID (30.43%) and FedEMA (29.41%). Notably, directly aligning raw SD features via L2 loss (FedRCL with †) actually degrades performance compared to FedAvg, confirming that FedDifRC's structured use of conditional and denoising representations is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior diffusion-in-FL work (FedDiff, Phoenix, FedDDA, FedDISC) that uses diffusion models to generate synthetic training data, instead extracting intermediate UNet decoder features from a frozen Stable Diffusion model as direct training signals. It builds on the contrastive-learning-in-FL line (MOON, FedRCL) by replacing hand-crafted positive/negative pairs with text-conditioned diffusion representations, and on the broader trend of repurposing pre-trained model internals (DINO, MAE, CLIP) for downstream tasks. The two complementary modules—text-driven contrastive learning (TDCL) and noise-driven consistency regularization (NDCR)—are novel combinations that leverage both the conditional generative and denoising properties of diffusion models simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedDifRC demonstrates that diffusion model intermediate features carry structured semantic information useful for federated training without requiring the diffusion model to be fine-tuned or to generate new data, offering a computationally lighter alternative to synthetic-data approaches. The self-supervised extension and validation on out-of-distribution domains (medical, remote sensing) suggest the approach captures general visual structure rather than memorized priors. Open questions include the computational overhead of calling a full SD model per sample per round, scalability to larger diffusion models or video, and whether the frozen-SD assumption holds when the local task distribution diverges significantly from LAION-5B.
