## Problem

Point tracking has shown promise for few-shot action recognition, but two fundamental challenges persist: uniform grid-based sampling fails to adapt to object scale (missing small but action-critical objects like a knife), and existing methods treat trajectories merely as feature anchors without explicitly modeling the rich motion dynamics they contain. The paper asks how to both select semantically informative tracking points and explicitly capture intra- and inter-trajectory motion patterns for discriminative action recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250419/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip as input, extracts DINOv2-base patch tokens to obtain appearance features, clusters them into L semantic groups, and samples 256 points (M/L per cluster) from the first frame where each cluster appears. These points are tracked across all T frames using CoTracker to produce M×T×2 trajectories. The output is a single class label per video, produced by a learnable CLS token that attends over the final trajectory-aligned token embeddings, evaluated in N-way K-shot episodes (e.g., 5-way 1-shot) with accuracy averaged over 10,000 episodes.

## Method

Trokens introduces two key components. First, a semantic-aware sampling strategy clusters DINOv2 patch tokens and allocates tracking points proportionally to each cluster, ensuring denser coverage of small action-relevant objects while reducing redundant background trajectories. Second, a Relational Motion Modeling module explicitly encodes motion in two ways: an intra-motion module computes per-timestep Histogram of Oriented Displacements (HoD) with 32 orientation bins to capture each trajectory's own directional dynamics, and an inter-motion module computes pairwise relative displacements between all trajectory pairs at each timestep to capture cross-object interactions. These motion features are projected to a shared dimension and fused with trajectory-aligned appearance tokens via element-wise addition, then processed by a Decoupled Space-Time Transformer (separate temporal and spatial self-attention) with a cross-entropy plus contrastive loss. Only the transformer and motion modules are trained; DINOv2 and CoTracker remain frozen.

## Results

Trokens achieves state-of-the-art few-shot action recognition across six benchmarks. On SSV2 Full, it reaches 61.5% (1-shot) and 76.7% (5-shot) accuracy, outperforming the prior best TATs by 3.8% and 2.1% respectively. On Kinetics, it attains 82.9% (1-shot) and 91.2% (5-shot). On SSV2 Small, UCF-101, HMDB-51, and FineGym, Trokens achieves 53.4%/68.9%, 94.0%/97.9%, 69.8%/82.3%, and 84.4%/89.9% (1-shot/5-shot) respectively, setting new records on all except UCF-101 and HMDB-51 where the recent TEAM method is slightly stronger at 1-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trokens consistently outperforms TATs [39], the most direct prior point-tracking baseline, on SSV2 Full by 3.8–5.3% across 1–5 shots and on SSV2 Small by 3.5–5.3%, confirming that semantic-aware sampling and explicit motion modeling add substantial value over uniform grid sampling. On Kinetics, gains over TATs are more modest (1.0–1.2% at 1–2 shots, near-parity at 3–5 shots), which the authors attribute to Kinetics' strong appearance bias. Against the recent TEAM [42] (CVPR'25), Trokens wins decisively on SSV2 Small (53.4% vs 47.2% at 1-shot; 68.9% vs 63.1% at 5-shot) but trails on UCF-101 (94.0% vs 94.5% at 1-shot) and HMDB-51 (69.8% vs 70.9% at 1-shot; 82.3% vs 85.5% at 5-shot). Compared to earlier strong baselines like MoLo [76] and HYRSM++ [79], Trokens leads by 4.9–6.1% on SSV2 Full 1-shot and by 8.6–10.6% on SSV2 Small 1-shot. On FineGym, Trokens improves over TATs by 2.6% (1-shot) and 2.0% (5-shot), maintaining its lead across all shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Trokens directly extends TATs (Kumar et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced trajectory-aligned space-time tokens using uniform grid sampling and a Decoupled Space-Time Transformer for few-shot action recognition. The paper addresses two specific limitations of TATs: uniform grid sampling fails to adapt to object scale (missing small but action-critical objects), and trajectories are used only as feature anchors without explicit motion encoding. For motion representation, the authors generalize the classical Histogram of Oriented Displacements (HoD, Gowayyed et al., IJCAI 2013) from human skeleton keypoints to arbitrary object trajectories, adding per-timestep computation and learnable projections. The approach also contrasts with implicit motion learning in video transformers and with optical flow methods that are limited to adjacent frames and degrade under occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit, hand-crafted motion descriptors (HoD for intra-trajectory dynamics, pairwise relative displacements for inter-trajectory relationships) provide complementary discriminative signal beyond what transformers learn implicitly, yielding consistent SOTA across six benchmarks. A practically important finding is the efficiency gain: 32 semantic-aware points outperform 256 uniform points (TATs) while using 82% fewer inference FLOPs, suggesting semantic guidance can substantially reduce tracking cost. However, the method remains vulnerable to motion blur and significant camera movement—fundamental limitations of 2D point tracking—and gains on appearance-biased datasets like Kinetics are modest. Future directions include 3D point tracking for robustness and extending the framework beyond few-shot to full classification and broader video understanding tasks.
