## Problem

3D Vision models for novel view synthesis still rely heavily on ground-truth camera pose annotations (typically from COLMAP) during both training and inference, limiting scalability and introducing errors from noisy pose estimates. This paper asks how far a 3D Vision model can be pushed with zero 3D supervision—no camera poses, no scene geometry—while still achieving competitive novel view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250898/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RayZer takes K unposed, uncalibrated RGB images (H×W×3, evaluated at 256×256 with patch size 16) sampled from continuous video frames or unordered image sets. It outputs per-view SE(3) camera poses (relative to a canonical reference view, with 6D rotation + translation), a shared intrinsics matrix (single focal length, centered principal point), a latent set scene representation z ∈ R^{L×d}, and rendered novel-view images. During training, the model's own predicted poses of a held-out image subset I_B are used to render target views for photometric supervision, eliminating any need for ground-truth pose labels.

## Method

RayZer is a pure transformer architecture with a cascaded pose-first design: (1) a camera estimator uses learnable camera tokens and full self-attention over all image tokens to predict SE(3) poses and intrinsics; (2) predicted poses are converted to pixel-aligned Plücker ray maps, which serve as the sole 3D prior connecting camera, pixel, and scene; (3) a scene reconstructor fuses image tokens with Plücker ray tokens from an input subset I_A to predict a latent set scene representation; (4) a rendering decoder takes target Plücker rays and the scene representation to synthesize novel views. Training is fully self-supervised: images are randomly split into I_A (scene prediction) and I_B (photometric supervision), and the model renders I_B using its own predicted poses, optimizing only MSE + perceptual loss. The pose-first paradigm and the explicit SE(3)→Plücker ray conversion act as an information bottleneck that enforces camera-scene disentanglement and enables geometrically meaningful pose interpolation for novel view generation.

## Results

On DL3DV, RayZer achieves 24.36 PSNR / 0.757 SSIM / 0.209 LPIPS (even sample), outperforming the best oracle method LVSM (23.69 / 0.723 / 0.242) by 0.67 dB in PSNR. On RealEstate, RayZer reaches 27.48 PSNR / 0.861 SSIM / 0.146 LPIPS, again surpassing LVSM (27.00 / 0.851 / 0.157). On Objaverse (synthetic, perfect GT poses), RayZer achieves 31.52 PSNR / 0.945 SSIM / 0.052 LPIPS, slightly below LVSM's 32.34 / 0.950 / 0.050 but far above the supervised PF-LRM (25.48 / 0.882 / 0.110). All results use predicted poses for rendering (no GT poses at inference), and RayZer requires zero 3D supervision during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayZer outperforms both oracle methods (GS-LRM and LVSM) on the two real-world datasets: on DL3DV it gains +0.67 dB PSNR over LVSM and +0.87 dB over GS-LRM, and on RealEstate it gains +0.48 dB over LVSM and +3.23 dB over GS-LRM. The authors attribute this advantage to noisy COLMAP pose annotations that cap the performance of oracle methods trained and tested with those poses. On Objaverse, where ground-truth poses come from Blender rendering (perfect annotations), LVSM as a true oracle slightly outperforms RayZer by 0.82 dB PSNR (32.34 vs. 31.52), confirming that the self-supervised gap narrows when pose labels are ideal. Against the supervised pose-free method PF-LRM, RayZer achieves a large margin of +6.04 dB PSNR on Objaverse (31.52 vs. 25.48), demonstrating that self-supervised photometric training yields a far more effective latent pose space than supervised pose regression. Overall, RayZer matches or exceeds methods that require camera pose labels in both training and inference, while needing only 2D image supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayZer builds directly on the latent set scene representation and learned rendering decoder paradigm introduced by SRT and LVSM, but departs fundamentally from their supervised training protocol by eliminating all 3D annotations. Its closest conceptual predecessor is RUST, which also learns from unposed imagery, but RayZer inverts the pipeline (pose-first rather than scene-first), uses explicit SE(3) poses instead of latent pose tokens, and estimates poses for all views rather than only target views. The Plücker ray map serves as the sole 3D inductive prior, bridging the camera estimator and scene reconstructor in a way that prior self-supervised multi-view methods (which rely on warping or epipolar geometry) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that 3D vision models can be trained on massive unlabeled video or image data without the COLMAP bottleneck, which currently limits both scalability and quality of supervised approaches. The finding that COLMAP noise actually caps the performance of 'oracle' methods on real-world datasets (DL3DV, RealEstate) is a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include whether this self-supervised paradigm scales to higher resolutions, larger view counts, and dynamic scenes, and whether the learned pose space generalizes across domains without retraining.
