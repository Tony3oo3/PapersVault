## Problem

Generalizable animatable 3D human reconstruction from a single image remains underexplored because existing approaches either produce static models, rely on scarce synthetic 3D scans for training (limiting in-the-wild generalization), or require slow per-instance optimization with controlled capture conditions. The field lacks both a suitable architecture that unifies 3D human representation with animation capability and a scalable training strategy that avoids dependence on rigged 3D ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251202/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB human image as input and directly predicts a canonical-space 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar in one feed-forward pass (2–7 seconds depending on model size). The output is a set of N_points 3D Gaussians, each parameterized by a residual position offset from SMPL-X surface anchors, anisotropic scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and spherical harmonic appearance coefficients. Animation is achieved by warping the canonical Gaussians to arbitrary poses via Linear Blend Skinning (with diffused voxel skinning for clothing), enabling real-time pose-controlled rendering without per-frame optimization.

## Method

LHM employs a Multimodal Body-Head Transformer (MBHT) that fuses three token streams—geometric tokens from SMPL-X surface points, body image tokens from a frozen Sapiens-1B encoder, and head image tokens from a proposed Head Feature Pyramid Encoding (HFPE) that aggregates multi-scale DINOv2 features (blocks 4, 11, 17, 23) to preserve facial detail lost through spatial downsampling. The MBHT-block, adapted from SD3's MM-transformer, first fuses head 3D tokens with head image tokens, then concatenates with body 3D tokens to jointly attend over body image tokens, enabling part-aware cross-modal reasoning. A head token shrinkage regularization (randomly masking 0–50% of head tokens) prevents the attention mechanism from over-relying on facial features at the expense of body parts. Training is self-supervised on 301K in-the-wild video sequences: the canonical avatar is warped to target poses via LBS, and the model is optimized with photometric losses (RGB, mask, LPIPS) plus canonical-space regularization (as-spherical-as-possible and positional anchoring), eliminating the need for 3D scan supervision entirely.

## Results

On the in-the-wild animatable human reconstruction benchmark (200 video sequences), LHM-1B achieves PSNR 22.003, SSIM 0.930, LPIPS 0.040, and Face Consistency 0.035, outperforming the best baseline AniGS by +3.32 PSNR, +0.059 SSIM, −0.063 LPIPS, and −0.018 FC while running in 6.57 seconds versus 15 minutes. On the static reconstruction benchmark (200 synthetic samples), LHM-0.5B (trained on the same synthetic data for fair comparison) reaches PSNR 25.183 and LPIPS 0.029, surpassing DreamGaussian (PSNR 18.544) by a margin of 6.6 dB. The method also reduces peak memory to 18–24 GB compared to En3D's 32 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LHM outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the static reconstruction baselines (Table 1, synthetic data), LHM-0.5B beats DreamGaussian (the strongest static baseline) by +6.64 PSNR and −0.046 LPIPS, and surpasses PSHuman by +7.63 PSNR; GTA and SIFU trail by even larger margins (≈8 PSNR). Against the animatable baselines (Table 2, in-the-wild data), LHM-1B exceeds AniGS by +3.32 PSNR and −0.063 LPIPS while being roughly 137× faster (6.57 s vs. 15 min), and dominates En3D by +6.77 PSNR with half the memory footprint. Notably, AniGS produces animations that are not faithful to the input image (as noted in Fig. 5), whereas LHM maintains identity consistency without any post-processing for face or hands. The scalability trend (0.5B → 0.7B → 1B) shows consistent gains, with the 1B model achieving the best Face Consistency (0.035) at the cost of slightly higher inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

LHM adapts the multimodal transformer (MM-Transformer) block from SD3 [11] to fuse 3D geometric query tokens with 2D image tokens, repurposing an architecture designed for text-image interaction into a geometry-image fusion mechanism for human reconstruction. The 3D representation follows 3D Gaussian Splatting [24], and the training warping pipeline incorporates diffused voxel skinning from Rec-MV [47] for clothing deformation. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: large reconstruction models (LRMs) that achieve feed-forward static 3D reconstruction, and SMPL-X-based animatable human methods that typically require per-instance optimization or multi-view diffusion. The key departure is training on in-the-wild video with photometric supervision rather than relying on scarce 3D scan ground truth, and introducing a head feature pyramid encoding to address the resolution bottleneck for facial details in a single feed-forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LHM demonstrates that animatable 3D human avatars can be produced in seconds from a single image without per-instance optimization or post-processing for face and hands, a practical speedup of 2–3 orders of magnitude over prior methods (5–15 minutes → 2–7 seconds). The finding that in-the-wild video data substantially outperforms synthetic 3D scans for training (19.75 vs. 21.65 PSNR) challenges the assumption that high-quality 3D ground truth is necessary, opening a path to scaling with readily available video. Limitations remain: the method struggles with loose clothing and large pose changes (causing splits), and training data has biased view distributions that limit coverage of extreme angles. Open questions include whether the feed-forward paradigm can handle occluded or partially visible subjects, and whether the SMPL-X surface-point initialization can be replaced with more flexible geometric anchors for non-standard body shapes.
