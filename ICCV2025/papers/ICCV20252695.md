## Problem

Existing prompt tuning-based OOD detectors (e.g., LoCoOp, SCT) extract OOD proxy features from background regions of a few ID samples, but this yields insufficient feature diversity, causing severe performance degradation on hard OOD benchmarks where they can even underperform zero-shot baselines. The paper identifies that leveraging external auxiliary data (pseudo OOD samples) could provide diverse OOD features, but two challenges remain unsolved: label-free foreground-background decomposition of unlabeled auxiliary data with diverse unknown objects, and avoiding overfitting when regularizing foreground OOD features.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder as input. During training, it consumes a small number of labeled ID images (1 or 16 per class from ImageNet-1K/10/20) alongside unlabeled auxiliary images from ImageNet-Extra 2.0 (500 non-overlapping classes from ImageNet-21K). At inference, it produces an OOD score via GL-MCM (sum of max softmax probabilities from global and local features) and an ID class label via cosine similarity between the global visual feature and learned text prompt embeddings.

## Method

APT is a plug-in module with two components. First, ALK (Adaptive Logit-based KL Divergence) performs label-free foreground-background decomposition by computing the KL divergence between each local region's classification logits and the corresponding global image's logits; regions with KL divergence below a threshold ε (default 0.4) are classified as foreground, exploiting CLIP's superior local-global semantic alignment. Second, PairReg constructs foreground-background pairs by element-wise adding the logits of each foreground region with a randomly sampled background region from another image in the same batch, then applies negative-entropy regularization to both the background regions and these pairs jointly. This pairing enables knowledge transfer from background to foreground representations, preventing overfitting to pseudo foreground objects while still improving hard OOD detection. The total loss is L = L_ID + L_APT, where L_ID is the base method's loss (e.g., SCT or LoCoOp) and L_APT is the auxiliary regularization, making APT a drop-in extension for any existing prompt tuning OOD detector.

## Results

On the hard OOD benchmark ImageNet-1k-OOD (1-shot, ImageNet-1K as ID), SCT+APT achieves FPR95=89.12 and AUROC=65.62, reducing FPR95 by 4.94 points and improving AUROC by 8.92 points over the best prompt-tuning baseline SCT (94.06 / 56.70), and even surpassing the zero-shot GL-MCM baseline (91.35 / 60.32). On standard OOD benchmarks (1-shot average across iNaturalist, SUN, Places365, Textures), SCT+APT achieves FPR95=27.97 and AUROC=93.03, outperforming SCT (31.09 / 92.04). In the 16-shot hard OOD setting, the gap widens further: FPR95 drops to 87.64 and AUROC rises to 65.86 versus SCT's 93.43 / 57.72.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT (plugged into SCT) wins across all nine baselines on every hard OOD benchmark. Against the strongest prompt-tuning baseline SCT, APT reduces FPR95 by 4.94 points (94.06→89.12) and boosts AUROC by 8.92 points (56.70→65.62) on ImageNet-1k-OOD in 1-shot; the gap is even larger in 16-shot (FPR95 93.43→87.64, AUROC 57.72→65.86). Compared to other prompt-tuning methods, APT outperforms LoCoOp by 6.67 FPR95 points and 11.22 AUROC points, IDLike by 5.76 / 10.92, LSN by 7.41 / 12.48, and GalLoP by 5.55 / 9.42 on the 1-shot hard OOD task. Notably, APT is the only prompt-tuning method that consistently beats the zero-shot GL-MCM baseline (FPR95 91.35, AUROC 60.32) on ImageNet-1k-OOD, whereas all other prompt-tuning baselines underperform it. Against outlier-exposure baselines (OE, EnergyOE), APT substantially outperforms both: SCT+OE degrades to FPR95=96.26 / AUROC=55.13 and SCT+EnergyOE to 96.98 / 54.22, confirming that naive OE strategies are ineffective for CLIP-based OOD detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt tuning paradigm for VLM-based OOD detection (LoCoOp, SCT, IDLike) by introducing auxiliary outlier data into the training loop, a concept borrowed from the outlier exposure (OE) tradition in classical OOD detection but never previously applied to CLIP prompt tuning. The key departure is that prior prompt tuning methods extract OOD features exclusively from background regions of few-shot ID images, whereas APT decomposes both foreground and background regions from large-scale unlabeled auxiliary data using a novel label-free KL-divergence criterion (ALK) and a foreground-background pairing regularization (PairReg). The framework is explicitly designed as a plug-in: it wraps around existing ID-data losses (Eq. 2, Eq. 3) without modifying their internal mechanics, positioning APT as an orthogonal enhancement rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, previously underappreciated limitation—lack of OOD feature diversity from few-shot ID data—that causes prompt tuning methods to underperform even zero-shot baselines on hard OOD benchmarks. The plug-in design and the curated ImageNet-Extra 2.0 auxiliary dataset lower the barrier for other prompt tuning methods to benefit from auxiliary data. Limitations include reduced effectiveness on texture-based OOD (where auxiliary natural images provide little signal), dependence on non-overlapping auxiliary classes, and evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-B/16. Open questions include whether the ALK decomposition and PairReg transfer to other VLM backbones (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based models) and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal OOD detection.
