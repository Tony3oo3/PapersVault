## Problem

Existing micro-action research treats recognition as a single-label classification task, ignoring the fact that in real-world scenarios multiple micro-actions temporally co-occur (e.g., concurrent head and hand movements). No prior dataset or method addresses the joint problem of detecting all overlapping micro-actions with their precise temporal boundaries and hierarchical categories, leaving a gap between controlled single-action recognition and realistic multi-action video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251835/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes short video clips of 5–15 seconds at 160×160 resolution and outputs a variable-length set of micro-action proposals, each annotated with a start timestamp, end timestamp, and a two-level category label (one of 7 body-level parts and one of 52 action-level classes). On average each video contains 3.1 overlapping action instances lasting ~4 seconds, and evaluation is subject-independent (no person appears in both training and test sets), making the output a multi-label set prediction rather than a single classification.

## Method

The proposed Dual-path Spatial-Temporal Adapter (DSTA) extends AdaTAD's Temporal-Informative Adapter by adding a parallel spatial path: after a shared down-projection and non-linearity, the feature is processed by both a spatial depth-wise convolution (S-DWConv, 1×1 kernel) capturing subtle inter-frame spatial changes and a temporal depth-wise convolution (T-DWConv) aggregating local temporal context. Two learnable scalars β and γ weight the spatial and temporal branches before concatenation and up-projection, allowing the model to adaptively balance the two pathways. The adapter is inserted into a frozen VideoMAE backbone with ActionFormer as the detection head, and only ~1.8M adapter parameters are trained (vs. 20.9M for full fine-tuning), yielding a 1.91 mAP gain over the temporal-only TIA baseline. The key insight is that micro-actions' discriminative signal lies in minute spatial differences between adjacent frames, which a purely temporal adapter cannot capture.

## Results

On the MMA-52 dataset, the proposed DSTA with VideoMAE-B backbone achieves the best average Detection-mAP of 25.34% (action-level 20.30%, body-level 30.37%), outperforming the AdaTAD baseline (23.08%) by 2.26%. Among feature-based methods, TemporalMaxer achieves the highest AVG mAP of 20.34%, while multi-label detection methods (MS-TCT, PointTAD) perform worst at 5.64% and 8.32% respectively. The gap between the best result (25.34%) and conventional action detection benchmarks highlights the inherent difficulty of detecting subtle, co-occurring micro-actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DSTA consistently outperforms the AdaTAD baseline [40] across both backbone scales: +1.63% AVG mAP on VideoMAE-S (23.43% vs. 21.80%) and +2.26% on VideoMAE-B (25.34% vs. 23.08%), with the gain attributed to the added spatial depth-wise convolution path. Against all feature-based TAD methods, DSTA (VideoMAE-B) leads by 5.00% over TemporalMaxer (20.34%), 5.33% over VideoMamba (20.01%), and 9.30% over TriDet (16.04%). Multi-label detection methods (MS-TCT at 5.64%, PointTAD at 8.32%) suffer the largest performance drop, which the authors attribute to the fundamental gap between conventional actions and subtle micro-actions. End-to-end methods with non-Transformer backbones (TadTR 13.41%, Re2TAL 11.74–12.04%) also trail significantly, suggesting that the VideoMAE backbone combined with adapter-based fine-tuning is critical for this task. Notably, even the best result (25.34%) remains far below conventional action detection performance, underscoring that MMAD is a substantially harder problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the micro-action recognition line (MA-52, iMiGUE, SMG) from single-label classification to a multi-label temporal detection setting, and builds directly on AdaTAD's Temporal-Informative Adapter by adding a parallel spatial depth-wise convolution path. Conceptually, it departs from conventional temporal action detection (ActionFormer, TemporalMaxer, TriDet) by targeting subtle, short-duration, co-occurring body movements rather than large-scale whole-body actions, and from prior micro-action work by requiring precise start/end localization of multiple simultaneous instances rather than clip-level classification. The dual-path spatial-temporal adapter (DSTA) is a minimal architectural extension of the TIA module, motivated by the observation that micro-actions demand both inter-frame spatial discrimination and temporal aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMA-52 dataset is the first public, large-scale benchmark for multi-label micro-action detection, filling a gap left by non-public datasets (iMiGUE, SMG) and recognition-only datasets (MA-52). The strikingly low best mAP of 25.34%—far below conventional TAD benchmarks—reveals that detecting subtle, co-occurring body movements remains an open problem with substantial headroom. Error analysis identifies short-duration and low-coverage instances as the primary failure mode, pointing toward future work on fine-grained temporal modeling and multi-instance disambiguation. The subject-independent evaluation protocol adds rigor but also makes the task harder, ensuring that progress on this benchmark reflects genuine generalization rather than subject memorization.
