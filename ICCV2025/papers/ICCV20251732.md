## Problem

Existing sewing pattern generation methods (e.g., DressCode, SewFormer) can produce visually appealing garments but fail to represent complex design elements such as cubic/circle curves, attachment constraints for collars and waistbands, and stitch-direction reversal. Additionally, these methods ignore human body shapes, making it impossible to generate made-to-measure garments that drape correctly on diverse bodies without clipping or sliding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts three optional modalities: free-form text prompts (tokenized by T5), a 3D body shape (SMPL mesh), and a 2D garment sketch (extracted via PiDiNet edge detection). The output is a full sewing pattern encoded as 29-dimensional per-edge vectors (covering edge geometry, curve type, stitch tags, attachment flags, and 3D panel placement), which can be directly fed into physics-based cloth simulation in standard CG pipelines (Blender, Maya) for draping and animation.

## Method

SewingLDM extends the NeuralTailor vector representation with cubic/circle curve parameters, binary attachment-type flags, and a stitch-reversal flag, then compresses the resulting high-dimensional tensor into a bounded latent space via an autoencoder using finite scalar quantization (tanh + round with n=2). Generation is performed by a DiT-based latent diffusion model trained in two stages: first, a text-conditioned diffusion model is trained with IDDPM loss; second, body-shape and sketch features are extracted by separate embedders, fused through a lightweight transformer, normalized to match the latent's mean and variance, and injected as a residual after the first DiT block. Only the output projection layers of both self- and cross-attention modules are fine-tuned in the second stage, keeping the bulk of the diffusion backbone frozen. This residual-injection design allows the multimodal conditions to act as a distribution-matched perturbation, enabling body-suited and detail-controlled generation without retraining the full model.

## Results

On the GarmentCode dataset, SewingLDM achieves a clothes-to-body distance of 2.20 cm, substantially outperforming DressCode (3.69 cm), Sewformer (5.45 cm), RichDreamer (6.19 cm), and Wonder3D (6.54 cm). In a 30-user study, SewingLDM receives a preference score of 4.60/5, compared to 3.56 for DressCode, 2.10 for Sewformer, and ~1.89 for the 3D mesh methods. All methods run in approximately 3 minutes except Wonder3D (~4 hours). On reconstruction metrics (Table 2), the autoencoder achieves 99.8% panel accuracy and 88.5% edge accuracy, while the full SewingLDM generation model maintains 97.8% panel accuracy and 82.7% edge accuracy with zero simulation failure rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SewingLDM outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the 3D mesh generation methods (RichDreamer and Wonder3D), SewingLDM reduces clothes-to-body distance by roughly 65% (2.20 cm vs. 6.19/6.54 cm) and doubles user preference scores, because mesh methods produce close-surface geometry that clips through bodies during draping. Against the sewing-pattern baselines, SewingLDM improves clothes-to-body distance by 40% over DressCode (2.20 vs. 3.69 cm) and by 60% over Sewformer (2.20 vs. 5.45 cm), primarily due to its body-shape conditioning. In the user study, SewingLDM's 4.60 score exceeds DressCode's 3.56 by 29% and Sewformer's 2.10 by 119%, reflecting superior text alignment and body fit. On reconstruction quality (Table 2), SewingLDM's generation model achieves 82.7% edge accuracy and 84.2% stitch accuracy with 0% failure rate, compared to Sewformer's 44.7% edge accuracy, 2.8% stitch accuracy, and 4.3% failure rate; DressCode could not be evaluated on reconstruction metrics due to exceeding GPU memory limits with its >30k-token representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuralTailor vector representation of sewing patterns by adding cubic/circle edge types, attachment constraints, and stitch-reversal flags to handle complex garments, then compresses the extended representation into a bounded latent space via a finite-scalar-quantization autoencoder. For generation, it adopts the DiT-based latent diffusion architecture and introduces a two-step training strategy: first training a text-conditioned diffusion model, then injecting body-shape and sketch features through a lightweight fusion transformer with minimal fine-tuning of attention output layers. This departs from prior sewing-pattern generators (DressCode, SewFormer) that either ignore body shapes or rely on autoregressive tokenization with prohibitive GPU costs, and from 3D mesh generators (Wonder3D, RichDreamer) that produce close-surface meshes incompatible with CG simulation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sewing patterns are the industry-standard intermediate for garment simulation and animation, so a method that generates body-fitted, complex patterns from intuitive multi-modal inputs (text, sketch, body mesh) directly addresses a practical gap in the digital fashion and CG pipeline. The compact latent space enables centimeter-level precision within fixed GPU memory, making complex-pattern generation feasible on consumer hardware. However, the method still struggles with accessories like zippers and pockets and with very intricate sketches, leaving open the question of how to extend the representation to cover the full breadth of daily garments. Broader impact depends on whether the two-step conditioning strategy generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., motion, fabric properties) without retraining.
