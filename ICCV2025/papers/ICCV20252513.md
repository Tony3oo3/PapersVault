## Problem

Existing RIR estimation methods face a data-efficiency and physical-fidelity tradeoff: learning-based approaches require dense RIR measurements (up to 1,000 samples) and offer no physically grounded guarantees, while physics-based methods like image-source or volume rendering are computationally prohibitive or data-hungry for real-world scenes. No prior work has exploited the strong correlation between visual surface appearance and acoustic reflection properties within a differentiable, physics-based rendering pipeline to achieve accurate RIR prediction from sparse measurements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (13–65 views, 512×512 for HAA), a sparse set of ground-truth RIRs (as few as 3 samples, i.e., 0.1% of the RAF training set), coarse planar room geometry, and source-listener pair locations with source orientation. It outputs a time-domain RIR at arbitrary novel source-listener pairs, decomposed into a learnable source impulse response, a beam-traced reflection response, and a position-dependent residual. RIR segments are 0.32 s at 16 kHz for RAF and 2.0 s at 16 kHz for HAA.

## Method

AV-DAR integrates acoustic beam tracing (cone-shaped volumetric beams sampled via Fibonacci lattice) into an end-to-end differentiable pipeline, replacing the exponentially expensive image-source method and the point-based ray tracing that misses specular paths. A multi-scale reflection response uses Mip-NeRF-style integrated positional encoding to model the elliptical beam-surface contact region, so reflection predictions adapt to beam spread distance and angle. A multi-view vision encoder (DINO-v2 features + two-level cross-attention across views and across k-nearest surface samples) produces a material-aware feature vector that explicitly conditions the frequency-dependent reflection response, linking visual appearance to acoustic behavior. A position-dependent residual component models high-order reflections and late reverberation by treating every surface point as a secondary source integrated via Monte Carlo sampling. The entire pipeline is differentiable, enabling gradient-based optimization of reflection parameters, visual features, and residual MLP jointly from sparse RIR supervision.

## Results

On the Real Acoustic Field (RAF) dataset at 1% training scale, AV-DAR achieves C50 errors of 1.42 dB (Empty) and 1.29 dB (Furnished), T60 errors of 10.7% and 9.61%, and EDT errors of 56.2 ms and 47.4 ms, outperforming all baselines by 16.6%–50.9% at the same data scale. Remarkably, AV-DAR trained on only 0.1% of the data (≈30 RIRs) already matches or exceeds baselines trained on 10× more measurements, e.g., achieving C50 of 1.81 dB and T60 of 16.9% in the Empty scene versus AVR's 2.19 dB and 24.1% at 1% scale. On the Hearing Anything Anywhere (HAA) dataset, AV-DAR achieves the best or near-best performance across all four rooms, with T60 errors as low as 6.3% (Hallway) and 10.8% (Complex Room).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-DAR outperforms all five baselines on the RAF dataset at the same 1% training scale, with the largest gains over the physics-based AVR (e.g., T60 error drops from 24.1% to 10.7% in the Empty scene, a 55.6% relative reduction) and the learning-based NAF++ (T60 from 23.9% to 10.7%, a 55.2% reduction). Against INRAS++ and AV-NeRF, AV-DAR achieves roughly 45–50% lower T60 error and 40–45% lower C50 error. On the HAA dataset, AV-DAR dominates four of the five metric-room combinations, with the sole exception being C50 in the Hallway scene where AV-NeRF (1.03 dB) edges out AV-DAR (1.15 dB), likely because AV-NeRF leverages depth input that is particularly informative in that simple, constrained geometry. Diff-RIR [65], the other differentiable-rendering baseline, is outperformed by AV-DAR on every HAA metric (e.g., T60 of 10.8% vs. 18.5% in the Complex Room), confirming that beam tracing is both more accurate and far more computationally efficient than the image-source method used by Diff-RIR (which required >500 hours of pre-computation for two reflections in one scene).

## Relation to Prior Work

AV-DAR sits at the intersection of differentiable acoustic rendering (DiffRIR's image-source approach, AVR's volume rendering) and geometric acoustics, but departs from both by adopting beam tracing as the differentiable acoustic primitive, which avoids the exponential path enumeration of image-source methods and the expensive 3D volume sampling of NeRF-style rendering. The multi-view visual conditioning via DINO-v2 features and cross-attention draws from the audio-visual room acoustics literature (AV-NeRF, AV-RIR) but is the first to embed such visual material cues directly into a physics-based differentiable reflection model rather than using them as auxiliary regression inputs. The multi-scale reflection response borrows Mip-NeRF's integrated positional encoding to handle the growing beam-surface contact area, a repurposing that is natural but not previously applied to acoustics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× data-efficiency result (0.1% training data matching 1% baselines) and the 16.6–50.9% same-scale improvements are practically significant for real-world deployment where dense RIR measurement is expensive and time-consuming. The interpretability of learned material-specific reflection responses (carpet absorbing high frequencies, metal reflecting them) provides a physically grounded diagnostic that purely data-driven methods cannot offer. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across unseen scenes without per-scene optimization, whether the beam-tracing approximation holds for highly diffuse or irregular geometries, and how the method scales to larger, more complex environments beyond the six tested rooms.
