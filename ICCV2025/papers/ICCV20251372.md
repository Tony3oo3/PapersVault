## Problem

Existing world models for autonomous driving face a critical trade-off: they either capture geometric structure via point cloud forecasting or semantic occupancy via costly manual labels, sacrificing either visual detail or scalable supervision. Moreover, prior approaches—whether used as pretraining, auxiliary heads, or video generators—struggle to retain generalizable, task-agnostic world knowledge that transfers effectively to diverse downstream driving tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251372/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DriveX consumes multi-view camera images (256×704 for DriveX-S, 900×1600 for DriveX-B) and ego-motion actions, with LiDAR point clouds used only as self-supervision signals. It produces grid-structured latent BEV features where each cell maps to a physical world location, predicted at 0.5-second intervals over a 3-second horizon. Semantic supervision labels are auto-generated by foundation models (Grounded SAM, OpenSeeD) into 15 classes, eliminating manual annotation. The spatially-aware BEV output is directly compatible with downstream task heads via the FSA adapter.

## Method

DriveX introduces Omni Scene Modeling (OSM), which unifies three self-supervised objectives—3D point cloud reconstruction via depth estimation, 2D semantic segmentation, and RGB color reconstruction—through a volume rendering pipeline that casts rays from camera and LiDAR viewpoints through a Channel-to-Height transformed BEV feature volume. A decoupled training strategy first trains the world encoder (BEVFormer-style with InternImage-Tiny backbone) for 40 epochs on OSM losses, then trains a separate future decoder for 24 epochs that predicts motion flow fields and warps features to future timestamps via distance-based interpolation, avoiding autoregressive error accumulation. Dynamic-aware ray sampling uses an off-the-shelf tracker (DetZero) to oversample rays within moving-object bounding boxes, prioritizing motion-salient regions. For downstream integration, the Future Spatial Attention (FSA) paradigm uses task-specific queries to aggregate information from predicted future BEV features through coordinate-transformed spatial attention, enabling seamless plug-in enhancement of occupancy prediction, flow estimation, and end-to-end driving models.

## Results

On nuScenes point cloud forecasting, DriveX-B achieves Chamfer Distance of 0.55 m² at 0.5s and 1.10 m² at 3.0s, outperforming the prior SOTA HERMES (0.78 m² at 1.0s, 1.17 m² at 3.0s) by 0.12 m² and 0.09 m² respectively. On FlowOcc3D occupancy flow prediction, DriveX-S with ViewFormer reaches 43.47 mIoU and 0.385 mAVE, improving over the baseline by +0.93 mIoU and reducing motion error by 6.5%. On NAVSIM end-to-end driving, DriveX-S achieves a PDMS of 84.5, surpassing DiffusionDrive-C (83.6) and PARA-Drive (84.0) among camera-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveX-B decisively outperforms all camera-based baselines on point cloud forecasting: it reduces Chamfer Distance by 0.12 m² over HERMES at 1.0s (0.66 vs. 0.78) and by 0.09 m² at 3.0s (1.10 vs. 1.17), while also beating ViDAR by 0.46 m² at 3.0s (1.10 vs. 1.73). On occupancy prediction (FlowOcc3D), DriveX-S integrated with ViewFormer improves mIoU from 42.54 to 43.47 (+0.93) and mAVE from 0.412 to 0.385 (−6.5%), surpassing both the unmodified ViewFormer and the ViDAR-pretrained variant (42.64 mIoU). For end-to-end driving on NAVSIM, DriveX-S (84.5 PDMS) beats DiffusionDrive-C (83.6), PARA-Drive (84.0), TransFuser (83.8), and UniAD (83.4), establishing a new SOTA among camera-only methods. Notably, DriveX's frozen world model retains 83.6 PDMS in end-to-end driving (vs. 83.7 finetuned), a negligible 0.1 gap, whereas ViDAR drops by 6.6 points and DriveWorld by 1.5 points when frozen, demonstrating superior generalizability of the learned representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveX builds on the BEVFormer paradigm for 2D-to-3D view transformation and temporal cross-attention, and on AD-MLP for short-term action prediction, but departs fundamentally from prior world modeling approaches in autonomous driving. Where earlier work either uses point cloud forecasting as a pretraining proxy (ViDAR, 4D-Occ), treats world modeling as an auxiliary head during task-specific training (LAW, SSR), or generates future video sequences (GAIA-1, Vista, DriveDreamer), DriveX learns in a spatially-aware latent BEV space with unified multimodal supervision (Omni Scene Modeling) and introduces a decoupled training strategy that separates representation learning from future decoding. The Future Spatial Attention paradigm further distinguishes the work by providing a lightweight, task-agnostic integration mechanism rather than requiring architectural redesign for each downstream application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DriveX's representations remain nearly lossless when frozen (−0.1 PDMS gap vs. −6.6 for ViDAR) suggests a path toward truly transferable world knowledge that does not require per-task fine-tuning, which could reduce annotation costs and improve robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios. The decoupled training strategy's clear advantage over joint training (0.44 m² Chamfer Distance reduction) offers a practical recipe for training world models more effectively. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons or more complex multi-agent interactions, whether the reliance on foundation models (Grounded SAM, OpenSeeD) for pseudo-labels introduces systematic biases, and how the computational overhead of the full OSM + FSA pipeline compares to simpler baselines in deployment settings.
