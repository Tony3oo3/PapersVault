## Problem

Self-supervised learning for 3D medical images lacks a large publicly accessible pre-training dataset, uses inconsistent architectures and evaluation protocols, and has no standardized benchmark, making it impossible to identify the current state-of-the-art or fairly compare methods. This fragmentation stems from restricted access to large datasets (e.g., UK-BioBank, ABCD) governed by restrictive Data Usage Agreements, and from each SSL paper training on different data, architectures, and downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

Pre-training consumes 114k unlabeled 3D head-and-neck MRI volumes (23 modalities including T1w, T2w, FLAIR, DWI derivatives) at native resolution, with anonymization and anatomy masks to exclude defaced regions from reconstruction loss. Fine-tuning produces either dense segmentation masks (15 datasets spanning brain, abdominal, cardiac, and vascular structures) or binary/multi-class labels (3 classification datasets), using the same pre-trained encoder weights for both task types. The released artifacts are pre-trained checkpoints for both ResEnc-L and Primus-M architectures, integrated into the nnU-Net framework for immediate downstream adaptation.

## Method

The paper introduces the OpenMind dataset (114k 3D brain MRI volumes aggregated from 800 OpenNeuro studies under CC-BY-4.0, including DWI-derived MD/FA/T2 maps) and a standardized benchmark that trains seven SSL methods—MAE, S3D, SimMIM, VoCo, SimCLR, SwinUNETR, VolumeFusion, and ModelsGenesis—on two architectures (ResEnc-L CNN and Primus-M Transformer) for 1000 epochs, then fine-tunes on 15 downstream datasets with five controlled fine-tuning schedules (Default, Frozen, Warm-Up, Valley, Sawtooth). Key findings: reconstruction-based MAE pre-training dominates segmentation (exceeding a 1000-epoch from-scratch nnU-Net in only 150 epochs), while contrastive methods (VoCo, SimCLR) excel at classification, revealing a fundamental global-vs-local feature trade-off. The pre-trained Primus-M Transformer surpasses the ResEnc-L from-scratch baseline on multiple datasets for the first time, and data filtering by Image Quality Score yields modest gains, confirming the dataset is large enough for data-centric research.

## Results

On the 12 segmentation datasets, MAE pre-training with the ResEnc-L CNN achieves 70.91% mean DSC (150-epoch fine-tuning), surpassing both the from-scratch ResEnc-L trained for 1000 epochs (70.47%) and the strong nnU-Net default 1k baseline (68.00%). For the Primus-M Transformer, MAE pre-training reaches 70.42% mean DSC in just 150 epochs, nearly matching the ResEnc-L 1k from-scratch baseline and improving ~3.5 DSC points over its own 1k from-scratch counterpart (67.01%). On classification, the trend inverts: contrastive methods (VoCo 68.18%, SwinUNETR 67.80% mean AP for CNN) outperform MAE (63.57%), which is the weakest pre-training method for global tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAE pre-training is the strongest method for segmentation across both architectures: the MAE-pretrained ResEnc-L (70.91% All) beats the nnU-Net default 1k baseline (68.00%) by ~2.9 DSC points and its own 1k from-scratch counterpart (70.47%) by 0.44 points in only 150 fine-tuning epochs. The MAE-pretrained Primus-M Transformer (70.42%) nearly matches the ResEnc-L 1k from-scratch baseline (70.47%) and exceeds its own 1k from-scratch Transformer (67.01%) by 3.4 points, marking the first time a pre-trained Transformer approaches or exceeds a strong CNN baseline on individual datasets (ATL, COS, ACD). Contrastive methods (VoCo, SwinUNETR, SimCLR) and the hybrid SwinUNETR scheme fail to meaningfully exceed the 150-epoch from-scratch baseline for segmentation, with VoCo and SwinUNETR on Primus-M actually degrading performance by 5–13 DSC points below Scratch 150. For classification, the ranking inverts: VoCo (68.18% mean AP) and SwinUNETR (67.80%) lead for the CNN, while MAE (63.57%) is the worst pre-training method, confirming that reconstruction objectives favor local/dense tasks while contrastive objectives favor global/classification tasks. S3D (sparse autoencoder) and MG (denoising+MAE) are competitive for segmentation (70.36% and 70.30% for CNN) but underperform MAE by ~0.5 DSC points, and SimMIM (69.18% for Transformer) trails MAE by 1.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new SSL method but instead carves new ground by creating the first standardized, large-scale benchmark for 3D medical image self-supervised learning. It repurposes existing SSL paradigms (MAE, S3D, SimMIM, VoCo, SwinUNETR, ModelsGenesis, VolumeFusion, SimCLR) under a unified protocol, addressing the fragmentation caused by prior work relying on restricted datasets (ABCD, PPMI, ADNI), heterogeneous architectures, and inconsistent downstream evaluations. The two architectures evaluated—ResEnc-L from nnU-Net Revisited and the Primus-M Transformer—represent the current state-of-the-art CNN and Transformer baselines in 3D medical segmentation, and the OpenMind dataset is assembled from 800 OpenNeuro studies to overcome the data-access barriers (DUAs, review processes) that have limited prior SSL pre-training to small or proprietary collections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OpenMind dataset (114k volumes, CC-BY-4.0) and the accompanying benchmark provide the field with a reproducible reference point that was previously impossible to establish, enabling fair comparison of SSL methods and rapid adoption of pre-trained checkpoints. The finding that no single pre-training paradigm dominates both segmentation and classification (MAE excels at dense prediction while contrastive methods excel at global classification) highlights a fundamental gap in current 3D SSL objectives and motivates development of methods that learn both local and global representations. Key limitations include the relatively short 1000-epoch pre-training budget, the weaker evidence for data-centric filtering at this scale, and the less reliable classification pipeline, leaving open whether longer pre-training or more sophisticated data curation would shift the observed method rankings.
