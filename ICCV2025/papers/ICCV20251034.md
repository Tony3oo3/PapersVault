## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting methods handle dynamic scenes (4DGS) and view-dependent effects (6DGS) in isolation, with no unified framework that jointly models spatial geometry, temporal dynamics, and angular appearance while maintaining real-time rendering. This gap is critical for scenes where motion and view-dependent appearance are interdependent, such as moving specular highlights or time-varying anisotropic reflections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251034/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (monocular at 800×800 for D-NeRF, 4×4 camera array at 2048×1088 for Technicolor, or physically-based rendered frames at 1000×1000–1600×1600 for 7DGS-PBR) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics. At inference, given a target timestamp t and camera pose (yielding view direction d), it produces a rendered novel-view image at real-time rates (401 FPS without adaptive refinement, ~175 FPS with it) and a 3D Gaussian point cloud. The 7DGS-PBR dataset spans 15–101 timestamps per scene with 10–300 views per timestamp, covering cardiac cycles, volumetric clouds, dust, flame, and translucent deformations.

## Method

7DGS represents each scene element as a 7D Gaussian over position (3D), time (1D), and viewing direction (3D), with a full 7×7 covariance parameterized via Cholesky decomposition to guarantee positive definiteness. The core mechanism is a conditional slicing operation: given observed (t, d), the 7D Gaussian is analytically conditioned to yield a 3D Gaussian whose mean and covariance are shifted/scaled by the cross-covariance blocks, and whose opacity is modulated by exponential decay factors in both temporal and directional deviation. An Adaptive Gaussian Refinement module applies lightweight two-layer MLPs (with multi-frequency positional encoding of time) to predict residual corrections to the spatial mean, temporal mean, directional mean, and covariance, enabling non-rigid deformation modeling before the slicing step. The resulting conditional 3D Gaussians feed directly into the standard 3DGS differentiable rasterizer, and training reuses 3DGS's densification (cloning/splitting guided by spatial-temporal correlation thresholds) with only a raised minimum opacity threshold to account for conditional modulation.

## Results

On the custom 7DGS-PBR dataset (dynamic scenes with complex view-dependent effects), 7DGS achieves an average PSNR of 32.50 dB, outperforming 4DGS (27.79 dB) by +4.71 dB while using only 15.3% of the Gaussian points (98,440 vs. 641,960). On D-NeRF, 7DGS reaches 34.34 dB PSNR (+1.13 dB over 4DGS) with 18.6% of the points, and on Technicolor it achieves 33.58 dB (+0.33 dB) with roughly half the points. The largest single-scene gain is +8.18 dB on heart1. Without adaptive Gaussian refinement, 7DGS renders at 376–401 FPS (vs. ~175 FPS for full 7DGS and ~193 FPS for 4DGS on 7DGS-PBR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

7DGS outperforms 4DGS [38] on all three datasets in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS while using far fewer Gaussian points: +4.71 dB on 7DGS-PBR (32.50 vs. 27.79), +1.13 dB on D-NeRF (34.34 vs. 33.21), and +0.33 dB on Technicolor (33.58 vs. 33.25). On D-NeRF, 7DGS also beats 4DGaussians [35] (33.30 dB), DaReNeRF [19] (31.95 dB), K-Planes [5] (31.61 dB), HexPlane [2] (31.05 dB), and D-NeRF [27] (29.67 dB) in PSNR. On Technicolor, 7DGS edges out Ex4DGS [14] (33.49 dB) and STG [16] (33.23 dB) in PSNR, though STG achieves a lower LPIPS (0.085 vs. 0.101) and Ex4DGS a slightly higher SSIM (0.917 vs. 0.912). 7DGS loses to 4DGaussians [35] on D-NeRF SSIM (0.97 vs. 0.98) and to STG on Technicolor LPIPS. The w/o-AGR ablation confirms the core 7D representation (not just the neural refinement) drives most of the gain, still beating 4DGS by +3.98 dB on 7DGS-PBR while rendering at ~376 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two independent extensions of 3D Gaussian Splatting: 4DGS, which adds a temporal dimension for dynamic scenes, and 6DGS (by the same first author), which adds a directional dimension for view-dependent effects. The key conceptual move is treating space, time, and viewing direction as a single 7D Gaussian and deriving a conditional slicing operation that projects it back to a renderable 3D Gaussian, preserving full compatibility with the 3DGS rasterization and densification pipeline. An additional Adaptive Gaussian Refinement module uses small MLPs to predict residual corrections to Gaussian parameters, addressing the limitation that the base 7D covariance cannot capture non-rigid shape changes over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

7DGS is the first Gaussian Splatting framework to jointly model temporal dynamics and view-dependent appearance in a single representation while maintaining real-time rendering (>400 FPS without AGR), and the new 7DGS-PBR dataset provides a much-needed benchmark for physically-based dynamic scenes (cardiac CT, volumetric clouds, translucent materials). However, the large quality gains (+4.7 dB average on 7DGS-PBR) are partly expected since 4DGS has no angular dimension at all, and on standard benchmarks (D-NeRF, Technicolor) improvements are modest (+1.1 and +0.3 dB). Open questions include whether the 7D representation scales to longer sequences or larger scenes, whether factorized (e.g., 4D × 3D) alternatives could match quality at lower cost, and how the method integrates with keyframe-based motion modeling (Ex4DGS-style) for large displacements.
