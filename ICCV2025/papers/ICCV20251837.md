## Problem

Depth reconstruction of transparent objects from sparse RGB views remains ill-posed due to complex transmission, reflection, and refraction, and existing NeRF/GS-based methods require dense multi-view inputs and lengthy training. A critical but underexplored gap is scene dynamics: when an object is removed, prior methods must rescan the entire scene, making real-time updates impractical. TRAN-D addresses both the sparse-view reconstruction quality and the dynamic scene-update problem simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251837/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes as few as 3–6 sparse RGB images (60° intervals) plus segmentation masks and object-index one-hot vectors produced by a fine-tuned Grounded SAM; for the post-removal state (t=1) it requires only a single bird's-eye-view image for refinement, whereas baselines need 6–9 views. Output is a compact 2D Gaussian representation (~16k–33k Gaussians) from which depth maps are rendered exclusively for the transparent objects (background is excluded), enabling direct per-object depth evaluation against ground truth.

## Method

TRAN-D initializes 2D Gaussians from random points (bypassing SFM, which fails on transparent surfaces) and jointly splats RGB, colorized segmentation masks, and object-index one-hot vectors so that optimization is confined to object regions while suppressing background interference. A novel object-aware 3D loss uses a three-level hierarchical grouping of Gaussians per object: it computes variance of inter-center distances (L_d) and variance of nearest-neighbor distance sums (L_S) to push Gaussians into fully occluded regions, producing denser, more uniform surface coverage without floaters. For scene updates after object removal, the method isolates and deletes the removed object's Gaussians via the one-hot index, renders a depth mesh from the remaining representation, runs a Material-Point-Method simulation in Taichi to predict chain-reaction displacement of neighboring objects, and then re-optimizes the 2D Gaussians against a single post-change image (omitting the object-aware loss already applied at t=0). The full pipeline completes in under one minute at t=0 and ~14 seconds at t=1.

## Results

On the synthetic TRansPose dataset, TRAN-D achieves a MAE of 0.0380 at t=0, reducing error by over 39% compared to the best baseline (TranSplat, 0.0632). At t=1 (post-removal, using only a single image for refinement), TRAN-D reaches a δ<2.5 cm accuracy of 48.46%, more than 1.5× the best baseline (2DGS at 25.05%), while maintaining a competitive MAE of 0.0864. On ClearPose, TRAN-D similarly leads with MAE 0.0461 at t=0 and 0.0910 at t=1. In terms of efficiency, TRAN-D uses only 33.5k Gaussians at t=0 (vs. 850k for InstantSplat) and completes scene update in 13.8 seconds at t=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAN-D outperforms all eight baselines on the TRansPose dataset at t=0, achieving the lowest MAE (0.0380) and highest threshold accuracies across all depth bins. The closest competitor at t=0 is TranSplat (MAE 0.0632), which uses diffusion-based surface features but still lags by ~40% in MAE and 26 points in δ<2.5cm. At t=1 (object removal with a single refinement image), 2DGS achieves a marginally lower MAE (0.0849 vs. 0.0864), but TRAN-D substantially outperforms it on threshold metrics (48.46% vs. 25.05% at δ<2.5cm), demonstrating the benefit of physics simulation for scene updates. Foundation-model-based methods (InstantSplat, FSGS, Feature Splatting) consistently underperform due to their inability to distinguish transparent objects from backgrounds, with MAE values 2–5× higher than TRAN-D. NeRF-based baselines (Dex-NeRF, NFL) perform the worst overall, with Dex-NeRF achieving less than 1% accuracy at δ<2.5cm, highlighting the difficulty of volumetric radiance field approaches for transparent geometry. In terms of efficiency, TRAN-D uses 33.5k Gaussians at t=0 versus 850k for InstantSplat and 297.8k for TranSplat, and completes the t=1 scene update in 13.8 seconds total.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAN-D builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for surface-accurate geometry and extends the object-centric 2DGS framework by jointly splatting segmentation masks and object-index one-hot vectors alongside RGB, isolating transparent objects from background interference. It departs from sparse-view NVS methods (InstantSplat, FSGS, Feature Splatting) that rely on 3D foundation models for initialization—models that generalize poorly to transparent objects—by using random initialization guided by a novel object-aware 3D loss. The physics-simulation-based scene update (MPM in Taichi) is a conceptual departure from prior transparent-object reconstruction work (Dex-NeRF, NFL, TranSplat), which assumes static scenes and requires full rescanning when objects move.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRAN-D addresses a practical gap: transparent object depth reconstruction in dynamic, sparse-view settings where rescanning is infeasible, achieving sub-minute training and 13.8-second scene updates after object removal. However, the method remains heavily dependent on segmentation quality (Grounded SAM fine-tuning), and the authors acknowledge it handles only partial removal or slight movements, leaving complex dynamics and challenging lighting as open problems. The reliance on synthetic benchmarks for quantitative evaluation, with real-world results being qualitative, limits confidence in generalization to uncontrolled environments.
