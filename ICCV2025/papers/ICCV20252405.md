## Problem

Rendering black holes with gravitational lensing requires tracing null geodesics through curved spacetime, traditionally done via iterative numerical integration (Euler or Runge-Kutta) that is extremely computationally expensive, especially for rotating or multiple black holes where analytical simplifications fail. No existing method can efficiently render complex multi-black-hole systems with optically thin accretion disks without spending orders of magnitude more time than desirable for interactive or large-scale visualization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a camera position and viewing direction in a spacetime defined by a superposed Kerr metric (with black hole positions, masses, and spin parameters as fixed scene parameters) and produces a 1920×1080 rendered image showing gravitational lensing, accretion disk emission, and sky-sphere background. During inference, the trained MLPs accept a (position, direction, affine parameter λ) triple and return the ray position in a single forward pass, enabling arbitrary point sampling along the geodesic rather than fixed-step integration. The rendering pipeline decomposes each ray into segments across near-field and far-field regions, samples color points on the accretion disk via coarse-then-fine intersection testing, and composites via volume rendering.

## Method

GravLensX trains separate MLPs (one per black-hole near-field sphere plus one far-field network) to approximate the null geodesic mapping f(p, d, λ) → position, using Fourier feature encoding on both position and direction inputs to capture multi-scale curvature. Training is supervised with data generated by the Euler method (14.4M rays per region), and the loss combines position MSE with a physics-informed velocity MSE computed via automatic differentiation (weighted α=800), ensuring the network respects the geodesic's differential structure. At inference, a three-stage ray-tracing pipeline operates: (1) ray-segment identification that advances λ using region-appropriate step sizes (straight-line distance in far-field, Newtonian-approximated distance in near-field outside region, direct-to-horizon in inside region) to track the ray across region boundaries; (2) color-point sampling that uniformly samples coarse points per segment then refines intersections with the accretion disk; and (3) volume rendering that accumulates transmittance-weighted color. This one-forward-pass geodesic evaluation replaces iterative integration, yielding ~15× speedup over the Euler baseline while remaining compatible with adaptive sampling strategies.

## Results

GravLensX achieves PSNR of 19.47 dB and LPIPS of 0.168 on the 2-black-hole system (with both accretion disk and sky sphere), and PSNR of 20.52 dB / LPIPS of 0.143 on the 3-black-hole system, compared to the Euler-method ground truth. The low LPIPS values indicate perceptually very similar renderings despite the lower PSNR caused by accumulated ray-tracing errors across regions. In terms of efficiency, GravLensX delivers a 15× average speedup over the Euler method when rendering both accretion disk and sky sphere, and a 26× speedup when rendering only the sky sphere at 1920×1080 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the classical Euler-method ray tracer, which serves as the ground truth for both geodesic paths and rendered images. GravLensX does not outperform the Euler method in pixel-level fidelity (PSNR is 19–25 dB, reflecting accumulated positional errors as rays cross multiple near-field and far-field regions), but it matches the Euler method in perceptual quality, with LPIPS values of 0.042–0.168 across configurations. The key advantage is computational: GravLensX reduces rendering time by 15× on average (both components) and 26× (sky sphere only), with the gap widening as the viewpoint moves farther from the black hole center. The paper does not compare against other neural or semi-analytic geodesic solvers (e.g., Gralla & Lupsasca's elliptic-integral approach [10] or James et al.'s affine-transform method [12]), so the efficiency gains are measured solely against the iterative Euler integration baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework of Raissi et al. to the problem of approximating null geodesics in curved spacetime, replacing iterative numerical integration (Euler/Runge-Kutta) with a single forward pass through an MLP with Fourier feature encoding. For the spacetime model, it adopts the superposed Kerr metric of Combi et al. to handle multi-black-hole systems, building on prior rendering work by James et al. (Schwarzschild) and Gralla & Lupsasca (semi-analytic Kerr geodesics). The key departure is the spatial decomposition into near-field and far-field regions, each with its own network, motivated by the vastly different curvature scales, and the two-component loss (position + velocity) that enforces the geodesic ODE as a physics-informed constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× (or 26× for sky-sphere-only) rendering speedup makes multi-black-hole gravitational lensing visualization substantially more tractable, and the single-forward-pass geodesic evaluation is compatible with advanced sampling strategies (hierarchical, proposal networks) that iterative methods cannot easily support. Limitations include the need to retrain per black-hole configuration, the accumulation of error across region boundaries (reflected in modest PSNR), and the restriction to optically thin disks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to time-dependent spacetimes (e.g., merger simulations), whether real-time rendering is achievable with distillation or smaller networks, and whether the near/far-field decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-body or cosmological geometries.
