## Problem

Automatically selecting the best camera viewpoint for instructional how-to videos is impractical to supervise directly: well-edited videos reveal only the chosen views, while the rejected alternatives are lost on the cutting room floor. This paper asks whether human view-switching patterns can be learned from large-scale unlabeled in-the-wild instructional videos and then transferred to multi-view settings with only limited best-view labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250085/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 seconds of past video frames, 32 seconds of past transcribed narrations, past view labels, and the next narration (if it overlaps the 2-second prediction interval). For view selection, it additionally receives the ego and exo frames from the prediction interval itself. Output is a binary ego/exo label per 2-second segment, with the view-switch detector predicting the next view and the view selector choosing between the two simultaneously captured candidate views.

## Method

The core idea is a two-stage bootstrap: first train a view-switch detector on 3,416 hours of unlabeled HowTo100M videos using pseudo-labels generated by combining PySceneDetect scene boundaries with a pre-trained ego/exo classifier, then fine-tune a view selector on only 5,000 labeled Ego-Exo4D samples. The architecture encodes past frames with DINOv2 (augmented with learnable view and temporal embeddings), encodes narrations with Llama 2 (augmented with narration-view and temporal embeddings), and aggregates all features via an 8-layer transformer with a [CLS] token feeding a 2-layer MLP head. The view selector extends the detector by appending DINOv2 features of the candidate ego and exo frames to the transformer input, enabling it to compare both views against the learned switching patterns. Training uses cross-entropy on pseudo-labels for the detector and on the small set of gold best-view labels for the selector, with the key finding that the pretraining on in-the-wild videos provides most of the performance gain over training from scratch.

## Results

On the view selection task (Ego-Exo4D, limited labels), SWITCH-A-VIEW achieves 54.0% accuracy, 57.3% AUC, and 56.0% AP, outperforming the strongest baseline LangView-bigData (53.3% acc, 54.8% AUC) by 2.5% AUC. On the view-switch detection pretext task, the method reaches 63.8% AUC on HowTo100M (10.4% over the best retrieval baseline) and 56.4% AUC zero-shot on Ego-Exo4D. Human annotators achieve 82.3% accuracy as an upper bound, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWITCH-A-VIEW outperforms the SOTA view selector LangView [32] by 2.5% AUC (57.3 vs. 54.8), even though LangView-bigData enjoys access to 98× more training samples. Against proprietary VLMs, the method beats GPT-4o (53.3% AUC) by 4.0% and Gemini 2.5 Pro (51.2% AUC) by 6.1%, demonstrating that task-specific multimodal modeling surpasses general-purpose LLMs for this fine-grained temporal decision. Over the InternVideo2 retrieval baselines (best: 52.3% AUC for frame-based retrieval), the gain is 5.0% AUC, showing that learned cross-modal fusion of past frames, past narrations, and next narration is critical rather than relying on frozen feature similarity. The ablation 'Ours w/o pretraining' (51.6% AUC) confirms that the view-switch detection pretraining on 3,416 hours of HowTo100M is essential, contributing a 5.7% AUC improvement over training from scratch with only 5,000 labeled samples. Simple heuristics (all-ego, random, last-frame, first-person pronoun) all cluster near 50% AUC, underscoring the difficulty of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior automatic cinematography work (which targets classrooms, panoramic recordings, or sports) by focusing specifically on instructional video view selection, and from the authors' own prior LangView [32] by eliminating the need for multi-view training data or view-aligned narrations. The key conceptual move is reframing view selection as a pretext task—view-switch detection in single-view, human-edited in-the-wild videos—where the editing decisions themselves encode human view preferences without explicit labels. Architecturally, it combines standard components (DINOv2 frame encoder, Llama 2 text encoder, transformer aggregator) in a novel multimodal configuration that jointly reasons over past frames, past narrations, next narration, and view history.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that human view-switching patterns in how-to videos are learnable from unlabeled in-the-wild content and transferable to multi-view settings, opening a path toward automating camerawork for instructional content without expensive multi-view annotation. However, absolute performance remains modest (~54% accuracy on view selection), and the authors acknowledge a distribution gap between edited in-the-wild and multi-view videos, clip-level predictions that cause rapid switching, and the challenge of generalizing to continuous (rather than binary ego/exo) view selection. Personalized view selection and integration with new view synthesis are identified as promising future directions.
