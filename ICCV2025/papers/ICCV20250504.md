## Problem

Previous video temporal grounding methods learn continuous features that exhibit weak differentiation between foreground and background moments, making it difficult to separate relevant from irrelevant segments. While vector quantization has been widely explored for image patches, moment-level quantization on videos remains unexplored, and the inherently discrete semantic nature of video moments has been overlooked in favor of purely continuous representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250504/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (T frames) and a natural language query (N words), processing the video through a frozen CLIP spatial encoder followed by a lightweight recurrent temporal encoder. It produces three types of predictions via separate heads on the continuous feature: per-frame classification confidence scores, boundary displacements for start-end timestamps, and clip-wise saliency scores. The moment codebook adds only minimal parameters during training and incurs no extra inference cost, making it a plug-and-play component for both encoder-only and encoder-decoder (DETR) architectures.

## Method

MQVTG introduces a learnable moment codebook (K=1024 codewords) that quantizes video features *after* temporal modeling to respect the cross-clip nature of moments, rather than quantizing isolated clips. Instead of replacing continuous features with discrete codewords (which would lose information due to visual diversity), it treats moment-codeword matching as a soft clustering process: the codebook loss and commitment loss drive the continuous features to learn discriminative structure while the original continuous features are passed to downstream localization heads. The codebook is initialized via k-means clustering on clip-level features extracted from the training set (prior initialization), and a linear joint-projection layer is applied to the codebook to capture temporal semantic correlations between codewords. The overall training objective combines moment retrieval loss (L1 + focal), highlight detection loss (intra-video contrastive), the quantization loss, and an InfoNCE alignment loss for the temporal encoder.

## Results

On QVHighlights-val, MQVTG achieves MR-mAP Avg of 48.81 (vs. 47.59 for R²-Tuning, the strongest prior method) and MR-R1@0.7 of 53.03, while slightly trailing R²-Tuning on MR-R1@0.5 (67.94 vs. 68.71) and HD mAP (40.23 vs. 40.59). On YouTube Highlights, MQVTG attains an average HD score of 78.5, outperforming the best prior method (UVCOM, 76.4) by 2.1 points. On TVSum, MQVTG reaches 88.2 average, surpassing CG-DETR (86.8) by 1.4 points. On Charades-STA, MQVTG achieves mIoU of 51.10 and R1@0.7 of 38.84, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MQVTG outperforms all 15 baselines on MR-mAP Avg (48.81 vs. 47.61 for BAM-DETR, the next best) and MR-R1@0.7 (53.03 vs. 52.90 for RGTR) on QVHighlights-val, but trails R²-Tuning on MR-R1@0.5 (67.94 vs. 68.71) and HD mAP (40.23 vs. 40.59). On Charades-STA, MQVTG leads on R1@0.3 (70.97 vs. 70.91 for R²-Tuning), R1@0.7 (38.84 vs. 37.02), and mIoU (51.10 vs. 50.86), while slightly losing on R1@0.5 (58.84 vs. 59.78). On YouTube Highlights, MQVTG achieves the best average (78.5) by a clear 2.1-point margin over UVCOM (76.4) and R²-Tuning (76.1), with particular strength on the Gym (79.7) and Surfing (84.9) categories. On TVSum, MQVTG leads by 1.4 points over CG-DETR (88.2 vs. 86.8), with notable gains on the DS category (82.9 vs. 75.9). The paper notes that the smaller margins on Charades-STA, TACoS, and Ego4D-NLQ are attributed to low codebook utilization (<10%) and high scene similarity requiring fine-grained discrimination that the limited codebook cannot fully capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the vector quantization paradigm from image representation learning (VQ-VAE, VQGAN) into the video temporal grounding domain, where all prior methods (TR-DETR, QD-DETR, R²-Tuning, UniVTG) learn purely continuous features through complex cross-modal interactions. It builds directly on R²-Tuning's lightweight recurrent temporal encoder and alignment loss as the backbone, and on VQ-VAE's codebook/commitment loss formulation for the quantization supervision. The key conceptual departure is reframing VTG's foreground/background discrimination problem as a clustering problem over a learnable moment codebook, rather than relying on continuous feature separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of moment quantization (minimal added parameters, no inference cost) makes it an immediately adoptable component for existing VTG pipelines, and the consistent gains across six benchmarks and both encoder-only and DETR architectures demonstrate broad applicability. However, codebook utilization remains below 10%, and the authors acknowledge that performance margins shrink on datasets requiring fine-grained discrimination (Charades-STA, TACoS), suggesting the discrete bottleneck limits representational capacity. Open questions include whether higher codebook utilization (e.g., via EMA updates or codebook reset strategies) could unlock further gains, and whether the soft-quantization insight generalizes to other video understanding tasks beyond temporal grounding.
