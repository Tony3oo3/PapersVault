## Problem

Face aging is an inherently ill-posed problem because environmental, genetic, and lifestyle factors produce highly diverse aging outcomes for the same numerical age. Existing methods treat age as a scalar conditioning signal, which is too abstract to capture individual or group-level aging patterns, resulting in generated faces that merely transfer average aging characteristics rather than realistic, personalized transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252518/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image, a set of dozens of Age Prompt images (sampled from FFHQ matching the source's race, gender, and target age group to provide population-level prior), and a text prompt as input, producing a photorealistic aged face image in under 10 seconds without test-time fine-tuning. A hyperparameter α (typically 0.7–0.9) controls the interpolation strength between the source's own age features and the Age Prompt, trading off editability against fidelity to the original face.

## Method

TimeBooth introduces a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, a ViT-based face recognition backbone (TransFace) is extended with two neck networks that extract ID-invariant and age-specific features, supervised by CosFace and MSE losses respectively; a Bidirectional Adversarial Disentanglement (BAD) strategy applies mutual Gradient Reversal Layers so that ID features become age-invariant and age features become ID-invariant. In stage 2, a frozen disentangled encoder feeds an Age Adapter that fuses the two representations via dual cross-attention and a lightweight Q-Former (32 learnable tokens), then injects the fused embedding into a latent diffusion model through image cross-attention layers. Training is stabilized by four strategies: Shuffle (cross-age ID feature swapping), Age Prototype Replacement (K-means cohort prototypes to suppress spurious correlations), Dynamic Training-set Sampling (balancing long-tailed age distributions), and random dropping for classifier-free guidance. At inference, Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) on the unit hypersphere blends the source age feature with the target Age Prompt under guidance α, enabling continuous and controllable aging.

## Results

TimeBooth achieves the best overall Face Quality score (72.20) and dominates the user study across all three subjective metrics (78.6% Face Similarity, 77.1% Age Prompt Similarity, 75.8% Visual Quality), substantially outperforming all baselines. On the Young-to-Old task, it attains the lowest AgeMAE (4.52) and R-AgeMAE (3.53) among all methods, while on Young-to-Child it records the best R-AgeMAE (7.97). Inference is fast (<10 s), matching CUSP and IP2P and far exceeding DreamBooth (10 min) and FADING (3 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the translation-based methods, TimeBooth trades a small KID penalty (7.20 vs. CUSP's 5.30 on Young-to-Old; 9.76 vs. FADING's 4.29 on Young-to-Child) for substantially better age accuracy (AgeMAE 4.52 vs. 6.08/6.96) and far superior user-study scores (78.6% vs. 28.6%/21.4% Face Similarity). Among the personalized-generation baselines, TimeBooth clearly outperforms FaceID on every metric (e.g., KID 7.20 vs. 13.04, Face Quality 72.20 vs. 52.48) and beats DreamBooth on Face Similarity (0.731 vs. 0.674), R-AgeMAE (3.53 vs. 5.76), and Face Quality (72.20 vs. 62.95) while being roughly 60× faster at inference. The combined FaceID+DreamBooth baseline is competitive on Young-to-Child KID (7.73) but still trails TimeBooth on Face Quality (54.36 vs. 72.20) and all three user-study dimensions. Overall, TimeBooth is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier age accuracy, the highest perceptual quality, and sub-10-second inference without test-time fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeBooth builds on the IP-Adapter architecture for image-conditioned diffusion generation and the FaceChain-FACT approach of using ViT-based face recognition backbones, but departs fundamentally from the numerical-age conditioning paradigm that dominates prior face aging work (CUSP, FADING, IP2P). The key conceptual shift is replacing a scalar age label with an image-modality Age Prompt, drawing on the broader insight that richer conditioning signals yield better control in generative models. The bidirectional adversarial disentanglement via mutual GRL layers extends the single-direction domain-adversarial trick from domain adaptation into a symmetric ID/age separation framework, and the Q-Former fusion module adapts the BLIP-2 design for multi-branch feature integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing age as an image prompt rather than a number, TimeBooth addresses the core ill-posedness of face aging and enables group-level and individual-level aging diversity that numerical conditioning cannot express. The plug-and-play, tuning-free inference (<10 s) makes the approach practically deployable in film post-production and forensic pipelines without per-identity fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the Age Prompt paradigm generalizes to other ill-posed attribute transformations (e.g., expression, ethnicity), how the disentangled representation behaves under distribution shift, and whether the K-means cohort prototypes in the APR strategy scale to very large or long-tailed age distributions.
