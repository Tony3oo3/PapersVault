## Problem

Small-scale MLLMs (s-MLLMs) suffer significant performance degradation compared to large-scale counterparts due to inherent capacity limitations, yet existing remedies focus on architectural modifications (e.g., MoE) or data curation rather than training paradigm optimization. Moreover, prior LLM distillation methods transfer knowledge only in the text modality, ignoring the equally critical role of visual representations in multimodal understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by a shared SigLIP-B/14@384px encoder into 2D patches) and a text prompt/question, producing an autoregressive text response. Both teacher and student share the identical frozen visual encoder and a two-layer MLP projector; distillation operates on the LLM's token-level output distributions across three segments: visual tokens, prompt tokens, and response tokens. The architecture follows LLaVA-1.5 with Qwen1.5 or Qwen2.5 LLM backbones at 0.5B–1.8B scale for the student and 3B–7B for the teacher.

## Method

LLaVA-KD introduces two distillation objectives: Multimodal Distillation (MDist), which applies Kullback-Leibler divergence to align both visual-token and response-token output distributions between teacher and student (extending standard LLM distillation beyond text-only), and Relation Distillation (RDist), which computes self-correlation matrices from LLM-generated visual tokens and minimizes their cosine distance to transfer spatial/semantic relationship structure. These are embedded in a three-stage training scheme: Distilled Pre-Training (DPT) applies MDist+RDist during projector-only training to strengthen visual-textual alignment, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) establishes baseline multimodal understanding via standard instruction tuning, and Distilled Fine-Tuning (DFT) re-applies MDist+RDist to refine the student's knowledge. The key insight is that distillation at the pre-training stage improves cross-modal alignment, while distillation at the fine-tuning stage transfers high-level understanding, and both are necessary for optimal performance.

## Results

LLaVA-KD achieves 58.9% Avg10 with Qwen2.5-0.5B and 65.0% Avg10 with Qwen2.5-1.5B, outperforming the TinyLLaVA baselines (56.5% and 64.0% respectively) by 2.4% and 1.0% using the same 1.2M training samples. At the 0.5B scale, LLaVA-KD surpasses LLaVA-MoD (54.1% Avg10) by 4.8% while using only 1.2M samples versus LLaVA-MoD's 5M. At the 1.5B/1.8B scale, LLaVA-KD (Qwen2.5-1.5B) leads all small-scale MLLMs with 65.0% Avg10, exceeding LLaVA-MoD (59.9%) by 5.1% and Imp-2B (58.9%) by 6.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most direct baseline TinyLLaVA (same architecture, conventional PT-SFT training), LLaVA-KD improves Avg10 by 3.2% (Qwen1.5-0.5B: 57.9 vs 54.7) and 2.4% (Qwen2.5-0.5B: 58.9 vs 56.5) at the 0.5B scale, and by 5.3% (Qwen1.5-1.8B: 62.1 vs 56.8) and 1.0% (Qwen2.5-1.5B: 65.0 vs 64.0) at the 1.5–1.8B scale, all with identical 1.2M training data. Against the closest distillation competitor LLaVA-MoD, LLaVA-KD wins by 1.1% Avg7 at 0.5B (55.2 vs 54.1) and by 5.1% Avg10 at 1.8B (62.1 vs 59.9), while using 4× less training data (1.2M vs 5M) and without the MoE architectural modification. LLaVA-KD also outperforms Imp-2B by 1.4% Avg7 (60.3 vs 58.9) and Bunny-2B by 4.0% Avg7 (60.3 vs 56.3) at the 1.8B scale. At the 0.5B scale, LLaVA-KD (Qwen2.5-0.5B) surpasses SPHINX-Tiny (15M samples) by 5.5% Avg7 (56.7 vs 51.2) using only 1.2M samples. The one area where LLaVA-KD trails is MMMU at 0.5B (28.3 vs 33.6 for TinyLLaVA-Qwen2.5-0.5B), suggesting the distillation strategy is less effective on expert-level multi-discipline reasoning at very small model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-1.5 architecture and its standard two-stage PT-SFT training paradigm by inserting knowledge distillation into both the pre-training and fine-tuning phases, creating a three-stage DPT-SFT-DFT recipe. It departs from prior MLLM distillation efforts (LLaVA-MoD, LLaVADI) that rely on architectural modifications like MoE or complex preference-based objectives, instead proposing a training-paradigm-only approach with two new loss components: Multimodal Distillation (jointly aligning visual and response token distributions) and Relation Distillation (matching self-correlation matrices of visual tokens). The method is positioned as complementary to data-centric and architecture-centric approaches for small MLLMs, requiring no structural changes to the student model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, architecture-preserving recipe for closing the performance gap between 0.5B–2B MLLMs and their 4B–7B counterparts, which is directly relevant to on-device deployment. A notable open question raised is the counterintuitive finding that larger teachers (7B) can underperform smaller ones (4B/3B) in distillation, suggesting an unresolved capacity-matching problem. The paper also acknowledges the limitation that teacher and student must share the same LLM family (same vocabulary), leaving heterogeneous distillation as future work. Broader implications include whether the three-stage recipe generalizes beyond the LLaVA-style projector architecture to more complex fusion mechanisms.
