## Problem

Masked autoregressive image generation models have lagged behind diffusion models in both quality and efficiency, largely because discrete tokenizers struggle to maintain reconstruction fidelity at high spatial compression ratios (e.g., 32×). While 1D compact tokenizers achieve aggressive compression, they discard 2D spatial structure and cannot generalize across resolutions, making them impractical for scalable, multi-resolution training. The paper addresses how to build a high-compression 2D tokenizer and a corresponding generation framework that closes this efficiency-quality gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input, encoded by a T5-base text encoder (109M params), and produces a 512×512 (or 256×256) RGB image. The tokenizer compresses each image to a 32× spatial grid (e.g., 256×256 → 8×8 = 64 tokens; 512×512 → 16×16 = 256 tokens), and generation completes in 12 sampling steps. The final image is reconstructed by summing predicted discrete tokens with residual continuous tokens and passing the combined latent through a CNN decoder.

## Method

DC-AR introduces DC-HT, a 2D hybrid tokenizer built on the DC-AE-f32c32 architecture that supports both a discrete path (VQ-quantized tokens) and a continuous path (unquantized latents), trained via a three-stage adaptation strategy: continuous warm-up, discrete learning, and alternate fine-tuning with a frozen encoder/quantizer. The generation framework extends MaskGIT by having a 634M-parameter PixArt-α-style transformer predict discrete tokens through progressive unmasking (cross-entropy loss), while a 37M-parameter diffusion MLP head predicts residual tokens conditioned on the transformer's final hidden states (diffusion loss). Critically, only discrete tokens participate in the transformer's forward pass, preserving MaskGIT's low-step efficiency (~12 steps) while residual tokens add fine-grained detail. A two-stage training pipeline (pre-train at 256×256, fine-tune at 512×512) exploits the tokenizer's cross-resolution generalizability to cut training cost by >1.9×.

## Results

DC-AR achieves a state-of-the-art gFID of 5.49 on MJHQ-30K for 512×512 text-to-image generation, outperforming all compared diffusion and masked autoregressive baselines. On GenEval, DC-AR attains an overall score of 0.69, the highest among all methods tested. In terms of efficiency, DC-AR delivers 0.4 s latency and 10.3 images/s throughput on an A100 GPU, corresponding to 2.0–3.5× lower latency and 1.5–7.9× higher throughput than leading diffusion and masked AR models. The underlying DC-HT tokenizer achieves an rFID of 1.60 on ImageNet 256×256 with only 64 tokens (32× spatial compression), matching 1D compact tokenizers while retaining cross-resolution generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-AR outperforms every compared baseline on both quality and efficiency. Against the strongest diffusion model Sana-0.6B (590M params), DC-AR improves gFID from 5.67 to 5.49 while halving latency (0.4 s vs 0.8 s) and increasing throughput by 1.5× (10.3 vs 6.7 img/s). Compared to SDXL (2.6B params), DC-AR achieves a 1.14-point gFID improvement (5.49 vs 6.63) with 3.5× lower latency and 4.9× higher throughput. Among masked autoregressive models, DC-AR dramatically surpasses Show-o (1.3B) in gFID (5.49 vs 14.59) and throughput (10.3 vs 1.3 img/s), and outperforms TA-TiTok variants by 1.75–2.25 gFID points. On GenEval, DC-AR (0.69) edges out Show-o (0.68) by 0.01 while being 2× smaller, and leads Sana-0.6B (0.64) and SD3-medium (0.62) by 0.05 and 0.07 respectively. Notably, DC-AR is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best gFID, best GenEval score, lowest latency, and highest throughput across all 512×512 comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-AR extends the MaskGIT masked autoregressive paradigm by introducing a hybrid discrete-continuous generation pipeline, building directly on HART's hybrid tokenization concept but replacing its 16× multi-scale design with a single-scale 32× 2D spatial tokenizer. The tokenizer architecture is inherited from DC-AE, which previously achieved 32× compression only for continuous latent diffusion models; the key departure is making this compression level viable for discrete VQ-based AR models through a novel three-stage adaptation training strategy. Unlike 1D compact tokenizers (TiTok, TexTok) that sacrifice spatial correspondence and cross-resolution generalization, DC-HT retains the 2D grid structure, enabling weight reuse across resolutions—a property the paper identifies as critical for cost-effective high-resolution training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 32× spatial compression for discrete AR tokenizers was previously thought infeasible without severe quality degradation, and DC-AR demonstrates that hybrid tokenization plus staged training can close this gap while preserving the 2D spatial structure needed for cross-resolution transfer. The resulting 1.5–7.9× throughput and 2.0–3.5× latency advantages over leading diffusion models (SDXL, Sana) make AR-based text-to-image generation competitive in deployment settings where inference cost is the bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 512×512 to 1K+ resolutions, whether the 12-step discrete unmasking can be further compressed, and whether the hybrid discrete-residual formulation extends naturally to video or 3D generation where token counts grow quadratically.
