## Problem

Representing people in 3D video meetings requires reconstructions that are simultaneously authentic (faithfully matching the current input frame), realistic (plausible novel views), live (real-time on consumer hardware), and stable (temporally consistent). Existing approaches satisfy only subsets of these properties: avatar systems lock appearance to an enrolled model, generative-model-based methods (e.g., EG3D) are constrained by learned priors, and multi-camera rigs demand expensive hardware. No prior method achieves all four properties from a single 2D webcam feed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1080×720 monocular RGB frame and a face bounding box (from an SSD detector), then extracts a face-centered ROI via a homography warp to a virtual symmetric-frustum camera (face covers 1/3 of the FOV angle), providing the warped image plus normalized focal length as network input. The output is a splatter image of two 3D Gaussians per pixel (each with color, opacity, log-scale, z-depth, xyz offset, and Euler-angle rotation), which is rendered via differentiable Gaussian splatting to produce novel-view images up to 40° from the input viewpoint at 28–30 FPS on a consumer GPU.

## Method

A lightweight U-Net (5 resolutions, 2 conv layers each, no self-attention) directly predicts per-pixel Gaussian parameters from the warped ROI image, extending Splatter Image with four key modifications: (1) optimizable channels at each decoding stage to compensate for the shallow network's limited high-frequency capacity; (2) direct bilinear colour sampling of the input image at each Gaussian's reprojection location to preserve texture detail when offsets are large; (3) two Gaussians per pixel (a large-scale layer for occluded regions and a small-scale layer for visible high-frequency detail); and (4) a jitter loss that duplicates each training sample with a perturbed face box and penalizes render differences to enforce temporal stability. Training uses 20,000 synthetic subjects with multi-view supervision (10 views on a 45° spherical cap), a global scale correction via linear least-squares on depth to resolve single-view depth ambiguity, and a composite loss combining RGB distance, VGG perceptual loss, opacity regularization, scale regularization, and the jitter term.

## Results

On the Ava-256 dataset (real multi-camera captures), VoluMe achieves PSNR 19.11, LPIPS 0.1908, DreamSim 0.0715, and ArcFace 0.1216, outperforming the strongest baseline FaceLift (PSNR 16.52, LPIPS 0.2277, DreamSim 0.1065, ArcFace 0.1871) across all metrics. On the synthetic Cafca dataset, it reaches PSNR 20.39 and LPIPS 0.1868, surpassing Splatter Image (PSNR 20.04, LPIPS 0.2244). For input-view reconstruction on FFHQ, VoluMe attains LPIPS 0.0413 and SSIM 0.9456, beating Live 3D Portrait (LPIPS 0.0468, SSIM 0.8981) on LPIPS and SSIM, though trailing on DISTS (0.0649 vs. 0.0407).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoluMe outperforms all seven baselines on the Ava-256 real-data benchmark, with the largest gains over FaceLift (+2.59 PSNR, −0.037 LPIPS, −0.066 ArcFace) and over the direct predecessor Splatter Image (+0.62 PSNR, −0.069 LPIPS, −0.024 ArcFace). On the synthetic Cafca dataset, it leads on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and ArcFace, with only a marginal DreamSim gap versus Splatter Image (0.0286 vs. 0.0276). For input-view reconstruction on FFHQ, VoluMe achieves the best LPIPS (0.0413) and SSIM (0.9456), surpassing Live 3D Portrait (0.0468 / 0.8981) and Splatter Image (0.0876 / 0.9165), but Live 3D Portrait retains a DISTS advantage (0.0407 vs. 0.0649), which the authors attribute to EG3D's learned priors smoothing geometry at the cost of authenticity. The stability (jitter) ablation in Table 4 confirms that the proposed jitter loss reduces temporal flicker from 0.005020 to 0.004967 while also improving PSNR and LPIPS, outperforming Splatter Image's jitter of 0.006353.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Splatter Image [Szymanowicz et al., CVPR 2024], which uses a 2D U-Net to predict a Gaussian splat representation from a single image, by adapting it specifically to human face reconstruction for videoconferencing. Key departures include an ROI homography mapping to correct perspective distortion, two Gaussians per pixel for multi-layer surface representation, a rescaling step during training to resolve depth ambiguity across variable camera distances, and a novel jitter loss for temporal stability. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two prior paradigms: avatar-based methods that require enrolment and fix appearance, and EG3D-based methods (Live 3D Portrait, TriPlaneNet) whose reconstructions are constrained by a generative prior. The 'authenticity' requirement—that the 3D reconstruction must faithfully reproduce the input frame from the original viewpoint—is the distinguishing constraint that rules out both of these families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously satisfy four properties (authentic, realistic, live, stable) for 3D human reconstruction from a single webcam, enabling 3D videoconferencing on commodity hardware via the 'magic window' effect without VR headsets or multi-camera rigs. The practical demonstration at 28 FPS with a standard RGB camera and screen is a meaningful step toward making volumetric communication accessible to everyone. Limitations remain for hands, held objects, extreme angles, and unusual textures (face paint), all attributed to synthetic training data coverage. An open question is whether the purely synthetic training strategy can be extended to full-body or multi-person scenarios, and whether the feed-forward architecture can scale to higher resolutions without sacrificing the real-time constraint.
