## Problem

Existing data selection methods rely on static importance scores or converged surrogate models, overlooking the combined 'group effect' of selected samples and the evolving redundancy structure during training. Moreover, selecting coresets for specific ratios requires complete re-selection procedures, incurring prohibitive re-training costs. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating data selection as a dynamic, ratio-aware optimization problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250790/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled RGB training dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet-1k at 224×224) and a target selection ratio (e.g., 60%–90%). A lightweight target network (e.g., ResNet-18/50) is trained solely to extract evolving feature maps for redundancy estimation and is discarded after selection. The output is a persistent binary selection mask (threshold 0.5 on continuous RL scores) yielding a fixed subset that serves as the surrogate training set for all downstream architectures, with the final ratio constrained within ±1% of the target.

## Method

The paper introduces the ε-sample cover, which quantifies per-sample redundancy via inter-sample feature distances within the same class, and proves (Props. 1–3) that mutually ε-covered samples produce nearly identical model outputs, losses, and gradient updates under SGD. Data selection is then reformulated as a Markov Decision Process solved by a lightweight A2C agent (three linear layers each for actor and critic), where the state encodes evolving feature maps from the target network, the action is a continuous sample-wise score vector, and the reward combines (i) a normalized discrepancy between the current and target selection ratio and (ii) the per-sample ε-coverage degree weighted by the policy. Because the target network's feature space shifts across epochs, the RL agent adaptively re-weights samples to capture training dynamics rather than relying on a one-shot static metric. A transferable variant reuses the pre-trained policy to fine-tune selection from an existing subset in only 15 epochs, achieving >7× speedup with marginal accuracy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-50, RL-Selector consistently achieves the highest accuracy across all selection ratios (60%–90%), with the largest margins at lower ratios (e.g., ~8% over baselines at 60% on Tiny-ImageNet). On ImageNet-1k, the method outperforms all baselines by a large margin and, at the 90% selection ratio, even surpasses models trained on the full dataset. In transfer learning (CIFAR-100→CIFAR-10), the method achieves 91.79% at 60% selection ratio (vs. 90.73% for the best baseline MoSo) and 92.79% at 90% (vs. 92.34% for full data). On challenging benchmarks, ResNet-50 trained on the 90%-selected ImageNet-1k dataset improves over full-data training by +0.10% on ImageNet-A, +0.66% on ImageNet-R, and +0.19% on ImageNet-Hard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL-Selector outperforms all eight baselines across every selection ratio on both CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. At the 60% selection ratio on CIFAR-100 transfer, the method achieves 91.79%, beating the strongest baseline MoSo (90.73%) by 1.06% and Forgetting (88.28%) by 3.51%. At 90% selection, RL-Selector reaches 92.79%, surpassing even the full-data baseline (92.34%) by 0.45%, while the closest competitor MoSo achieves 92.23%. On Tiny-ImageNet with cross-architecture evaluation (ResNet-18→VGG-16), the method leads at 90% selection with 57.64% vs. 57.23% for full data, outperforming Moderate-DS (49.25%) and Self-supervised prototypes (54.71%) by large margins. The advantage is most pronounced at aggressive pruning ratios (60–70%), where static-score methods like EL2N, GraNd, and Forgetting degrade substantially, while RL-Selector's dynamic, redundancy-aware policy maintains robust performance. On ImageNet-1k, the method's lead over baselines widens further due to the larger redundancy in the dataset, and at 90% selection it exceeds full-data training, a result none of the baselines replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of data selection and reinforcement learning, departing from the dominant paradigm of static importance scoring (EL2N, GraNd, Forgetting) and distribution-matching (Moderate-DS, CCS) by formulating selection as a sequential decision problem. The ε-sample cover concept provides a theoretical lens on redundancy that prior methods addressed heuristically, while the use of A2C to optimize sample-wise scores over evolving training dynamics is a novel repurposing of a standard RL algorithm for a data-centric task. Unlike optimization-based coreset methods (Glister, CG-Score) that require full re-training for each selection ratio, the RL formulation naturally handles multiple ratios through a single policy, and the transferable variant (15-epoch fine-tuning from a pre-trained policy) further distinguishes it from prior one-shot selection procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: selected datasets that outperform full-dataset training on challenging benchmarks (ImageNet-A/R/Hard) while reducing compute costs, plus cross-architecture generalization (ResNet-18-selected data transferring to ViT-L and Swin-T) make this a potentially useful tool for practitioners managing large-scale training pipelines. Open questions include whether the RL formulation scales to non-vision modalities or extremely large datasets (e.g., LAION-scale), whether the 1% selection-ratio constraint is fundamental or an artifact of the A2C formulation, and whether the theoretical guarantees (Prop. 1–4, derived under a linearized ReLU assumption) hold for deeper, non-convex architectures in practice. The transferable variant's 7× speedup with minimal accuracy loss suggests a practical path toward amortizing selection costs across multiple target models.
