## Problem

As downstream tasks grow more diverse and complex, LoRA-based instruction tuning for MLLMs suffers from data conflicts that produce knowledge-inconsistent outputs (e.g., misaligned ingredients between recognition and recipe generation). While LoRA-MoE methods mitigate this via expert activation, they require complex routing strategies and incur 1.38–1.59× the inference time of vanilla LoRA, creating a trade-off between conflict resolution and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250915/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed at 336×336 through CLIP ViT-L/14) paired with text questions, and produces free-form text outputs ranging from ingredient lists and recipe narratives to nutrition estimates and general VQA answers. The VCE module additionally draws on intermediate ViT feature maps (layers 2, 8, 14, and 20-to-last) as auxiliary local cues, while Dual-LoRA adapters are injected only into the query and value projection layers of the LLM's transformer blocks, keeping the trainable footprint small enough for single-GPU training.

## Method

Dual-LoRA decouples adaptation into two subspaces: a skill space (matrix S) that stably consolidates holistic domain knowledge, and a task space (matrix T) that applies ReLU-based rank rectification to locally activate task-specific components, yielding the update D(x) = (r/α)·B(Norm(Sx) ⊙ ReLU(Tx)). A theoretical corollary shows this single dual-pathway module can decompose into any set of LoRA experts, matching LoRA-MoE expressiveness without the router or multi-expert inference overhead. The Visual Cue Enhancement (VCE) module complements this by using multi-head deformable attention to aggregate local features from four intermediate ViT layers around each anchor patch, then fusing them residually into the high-level feature map before projection. Training proceeds in two stages: first pre-training VCE and the vision projector, then jointly fine-tuning VCE, the projector, and Dual-LoRA adapters. The full system requires only 1.16× the inference time of vanilla LoRA and 73% of a 4-expert LoRA-MoE, while outperforming both on UniFood, ScienceQA, and Flickr30k.

## Results

On the UniFood multi-task benchmark, Dual-LoRA+VCE achieves the best ingredient recognition (IoU 24.5, F1 35.5) and recipe generation (SacreBLEU 14.7, Rouge-L 42.2) scores, outperforming vanilla LoRA by +1.3 IoU and +2.3 Rouge-L. On ScienceQA, Dual-LoRA+VCA reaches 80.01% single-choice accuracy, a 10-point improvement over vanilla LoRA (70.01%) and 1.6 points over the strongest MoE baseline RoDE (78.39%). On Flickr30k captioning, the full method scores 28.71 SacreBLEU and 67.86 Rouge-L, the best among all compared methods. Inference time is only 1.16× that of vanilla LoRA, versus 1.59× for a 4-expert LoRA-MoE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LoRA (rank 64), Dual-LoRA+VCE wins decisively on ScienceQA (+10.0 pts accuracy) and Flickr30k (+0.82 SacreBLEU, +1.10 Rouge-L), and on UniFood ingredient recognition (+1.3 IoU) and recipe generation (+2.3 Rouge-L), but loses slightly on nutrition estimation average pMAE (49.1 vs 50.2). Compared to the two LoRA-MoE variants (top-2 and softmax, both rank [16,16,16,16]), Dual-LoRA+VCE outperforms on every UniFood and ScienceQA/Flickr30k metric while using the same total parameter budget (rank 64 single adapter vs. four rank-16 experts). Against RoDE (heterogeneous ranks [32,16,8,8]), Dual-LoRA+VCE leads on ingredient recognition (+0.9 IoU), recipe generation (+0.8 Rouge-L), and ScienceQA (+1.62 pts), but RoDE retains a clear advantage on nutrition estimation (51.3 vs 49.1 avg pMAE). On general MLLM benchmarks (Table 5), Dual-LoRA+VCE matches or exceeds LLaVA-1.5-LoRA on LLaVA-Wild (68.1 vs 67.9), POPE (87.2 vs 86.4), and MMVet (32.1 vs 30.2), while trailing slightly on MMBench (65.6 vs 66.1). Inference-time efficiency is a key differentiator: Dual-LoRA+VCE runs at 1.16× vanilla LoRA latency, whereas 4-expert LoRA-MoE requires 1.59×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by decomposing the single low-rank adapter into two coupled subspaces—a stable 'skill space' for holistic knowledge and a rank-rectified 'task space' for localized, instruction-conditioned adaptation—thereby recovering the differentiated local response that LoRA-MoE methods (Wu et al., 2024; Jiao et al./RoDE, 2024) achieve through multiple experts and routing. The key departure from LoRA-MoE is structural: rather than polling multiple experts via a router (sparse, dense, or rectified activation), Dual-LoRA uses a single module with a non-linear gate (ReLU) on the task space to simulate expert-like selectivity, supported by a rank-equivalence argument (Proposition 1 / Corollary 1). The Visual Cue Enhancement module additionally draws on deformable attention (Zhu et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2022) to aggregate multi-level local features, positioning the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and vision feature enrichment for MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance lies in reducing the inference overhead of multi-task MLLM fine-tuning: Dual-LoRA+VCE achieves 1.16× vanilla LoRA latency versus 1.59× for 4-expert LoRA-MoE, while matching or slightly exceeding MoE baselines on food-domain and general benchmarks. However, the performance margins over RoDE are modest (≤1 point on most metrics), and all experiments are confined to LLaVA-1.5-7B with a small set of datasets, leaving open whether the dual-space decomposition generalizes to larger models, more heterogeneous task suites, or non-food domains. A key open question is whether the ReLU-based rank rectification can be replaced by learned, input-dependent gating to better handle tasks of varying complexity without the heterogeneous-expert design that RoDE introduces.
