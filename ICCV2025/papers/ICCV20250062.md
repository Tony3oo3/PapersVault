## Problem

While face retouching detection is well-studied, the inverse problem of recovering the original face from a retouched one (Face Retouching Restoration) remains unaddressed. Existing image restoration and makeup removal methods focus on high-frequency texture recovery, but face retouching introduces structural modifications (enlarged eyes, thinner face shapes, lifted contours) that require restoring low-frequency facial geometry rather than merely denoising textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1024×1024 RGB retouched face image as input and produces the corresponding original face image in a fully blind setting (no reference or template). A router first classifies which of four retouching types (whitening, smoothing, face lifting, eye enlarging) are present, and only the corresponding specialized experts are activated. The final output is a single restored image produced by a UNet-based Combine Module that fuses all activated expert outputs with the original retouched input.

## Method

MoFRR adopts a Mixture of Experts architecture with a ResNet-MAM router, four specialized WaveFRR experts (one per retouching type), and a shared plain-DDIM expert inspired by DeepSeek's shared-expert design. Each WaveFRR applies Discrete Wavelet Transform to split the image into low-frequency (LL) and high-frequency (HH, LH, HL) sub-bands. The low-frequency branch performs conditional DDIM sampling guided by an Iterative Distortion Evaluation Module (IDEM) that uses multi-scale channel attention to produce pixel-wise distortion maps conditioned on a ResNet-50 degree estimator's global prediction. The high-frequency branch refines the high-frequency sub-bands via a one-layer cross-attention (HFCAM) that attends from the restored low-frequency sub-band, followed by IDWT recombination. Training is staged: router and degree estimators are trained first, then WaveFRR branches (with frequency, spatial, and classification losses), and finally the Combine Module on multi-operation images.

## Results

On the RetouchingFFHQ++ dataset (intra-API setting), MoFRR achieves PSNR/SSIM of 33.11/0.949 (Whitening), 38.06/0.943 (Smoothing), 31.26/0.913 (Face Lifting), 38.05/0.958 (Eye Enlarging), and 34.47/0.959 (Multi-operation). The most striking gain is on multi-operation images, where MoFRR surpasses the second-best method (ResDiff) by 5.49 dB in PSNR. In the cross-API generalization test, MoFRR still leads with 31.28 dB PSNR on multi-operation images, outperforming the best baseline by 3.87 dB. Facial feature similarity (cosine similarity via AdaFace/ArcFace) also peaks sharply near 1.0 for MoFRR, confirming identity-preserving restoration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoFRR outperforms all five baselines across every retouching category. The strongest baseline is ResDiff (AAAI'24), which is competitive on single-operation images (e.g., 35.94 dB on Smoothing vs. MoFRR's 38.06 dB) but falls dramatically on multi-operation images (28.98 vs. 34.47 dB, a 5.49 dB gap). Restormer (CVPR'22) is the second-strongest on single operations (37.59 dB on Smoothing) but degrades to 28.19 dB on Multi, showing it cannot handle compound structural changes. PCGAN (CVPR'18), designed for makeup removal, performs worst overall (~22–23 dB), confirming that makeup-removal methods are ill-suited for structural retouching. DR2 (CVPR'23) and Pix2pix (CVPR'17) both produce excessive artifacts or fail to recover facial structure, yielding PSNR values 5–10 dB below MoFRR. The consistent advantage on multi-operation images validates the MoE design: specialized experts handle individual retouching types while the shared expert captures global patterns, a combination no single-architecture baseline can replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoFRR sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based image restoration (DDPM/DDIM), Mixture-of-Experts routing (inspired by DeepSeek's shared-expert design), and wavelet-domain signal processing. It departs from prior makeup-removal work (PairedCycleGAN, PSGAN++, SSAT) by explicitly targeting structural/low-frequency changes (face shape, eye size) rather than surface-level texture, and from general image restoration (DR2, Restormer, ResDiff) by prioritizing semantic fidelity over high-frequency detail recovery. The wavelet decomposition with a DDIM low-frequency branch and a cross-attention high-frequency branch is a novel architectural combination, while the MoE routing over retouching types is a direct application of the expert-isolation paradigm to a vision restoration setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Face Retouching Restoration as a distinct task and releasing RetouchingFFHQ++ (1M+ paired images from four commercial APIs), the paper provides the first dedicated benchmark for a problem with clear forensic and regulatory relevance (identity verification, fraud detection, ad-compliance labeling). The 5.49 dB PSNR gap over the second-best method on multi-operated images strongly validates the divide-and-conquer MoE design, but the approach is limited to four retouching types and requires a trained router plus per-type degree estimators, raising open questions about scalability to arbitrary or novel retouching operations. Whether the wavelet-frequency decomposition generalizes to non-face images or to retouching types involving global color/geometry shifts remains unexplored.
