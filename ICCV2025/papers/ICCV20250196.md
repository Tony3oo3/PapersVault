## Problem

State-of-the-art video Diffusion Transformers (DiT) use 3D full attention that does not explicitly separate temporal and spatial information, making it difficult to decouple foreground motion from background appearance for motion transfer. Previous motion transfer methods relied on separate spatial and temporal self-attention in 3D U-Nets, rendering them incompatible with DiT architectures. Additionally, existing evaluation benchmarks are limited in scale and contain only simple motions, failing to comprehensively assess motion transfer capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250196/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source video (49×480×720) and a text prompt describing the desired foreground and background. It produces a 49×480×720 generated video that preserves the source video's motion pattern while allowing text-driven control over both foreground subject and background scene. Training is performed on a single source video for 500 steps (~1 hour on one A100 GPU), and at inference the shared temporal kernel is removed from the last 65% of DiT blocks to improve edit fidelity.

## Method

DeT injects a shared temporal kernel—a 1D temporal convolution with a down-and-up bottleneck (kernel size 3, mid-dimension 128, GELU activation)—into all DiT blocks, operating on reshaped features along the temporal axis. This kernel acts as a Laplacian smoothing operator that decouples background appearance from foreground motion while simultaneously capturing local temporal dynamics (motivated by the observation that significant 3D attention scores appear only along the diagonal of adjacent frames). A dense point tracking loss further enforces motion consistency by aligning predicted latent features along CoTracker-derived foreground trajectories using masked L2 distance. The method is trained with a combined denoising and tracking loss (λ_TL=0.1) and evaluated on MTBench (100 videos, 500 prompts, three difficulty levels) using a hybrid motion fidelity metric that combines Fréchet distance for global trajectory shape with cosine similarity for local velocity direction.

## Results

On MTBench (100 videos, 500 prompts), DeT with HunyuanVideo achieves the best overall balance: MF=85.9, TC=91.9, EF=31.9. With CogVideoX as the base model, DeT scores MF=85.8, TC=89.6, EF=31.2, outperforming all U-Net-based baselines on motion fidelity while maintaining competitive edit fidelity. On the medium-difficulty subset, DeT reaches MF=92.2, substantially ahead of MotionClone (79.2) and MotionDirector (66.8). Training completes in ~1 hour on a single A100 GPU with 500 optimization steps per source video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeT clearly outperforms all U-Net-based motion transfer methods on motion fidelity: MotionDirector (MF=67.7), SMA (55.1), MotionClone (78.9), and MOFT (52.5) all trail DeT's 85.8–85.9 by 7–33 points, while DeT maintains comparable or better edit fidelity. Among the DiT-adapted baselines, MotionInversion achieves a similar MF (85.0) but suffers severe overfitting with the lowest EF (26.6), indicating it memorizes source appearance rather than transferring motion under text control. DreamBooth (MF=80.4, EF=28.4) and DMT (MF=65.1, EF=30.5) both fail to capture accurate motion, often straightening curved trajectories. MOFT achieves the highest EF (33.0) but the lowest MF (52.5), confirming a strong edit-fidelity-over-motion-fidelity trade-off that DeT resolves. Overall, DeT is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier motion fidelity (>85) and competitive edit fidelity (>31), demonstrating the best trade-off across all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of motion transfer work (MotionDirector, SMA, MotionClone, DMT) that relies on 3D U-Net architectures with explicitly separated spatial and temporal self-attention, where freezing spatial attention during training enables motion-appearance decoupling. The authors identify that state-of-the-art DiT models (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, StepVideo-T2V) use 3D full attention that entangles spatial and temporal dimensions, rendering prior decoupling strategies inapplicable. Their solution—a shared 1D temporal kernel injected into DiT blocks combined with a dense point tracking loss in latent space—repurposes the manifold-smoothing intuition from U-Net temporal attention into a lightweight convolutional module compatible with full-attention transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic recipe for motion transfer on modern DiT video generators, filling a gap left by the architectural shift from U-Net to transformer backbones. MTBench (100 videos, 500 prompts, three difficulty tiers) and the hybrid Fréchet-distance-plus-velocity metric offer a more rigorous evaluation than prior benchmarks that relied on simple straight-line motions. Limitations include the single-video, 500-step fine-tuning protocol, 49-frame/480×720 resolution, and reliance on CoTracker for trajectory annotation; open questions include whether the temporal kernel generalizes to longer sequences, multi-subject scenes, or training-free settings, and whether the 65% layer-dropping heuristic at inference is architecture-specific.
