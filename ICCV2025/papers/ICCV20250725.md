## Problem

Existing video generation models are constrained to narrow, fixed-perspective outputs and cannot produce full 360° panoramic videos, leaving a significant gap in representing the complete visual world. Generating a panoramic video from a single perspective view is heavily under-constrained: the model must extrapolate beyond the observed field of view while maintaining both spatial layout coherence and temporal dynamics of objects across the entire sphere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view perspective video (arbitrary camera motion, FoV between 30°–120°) and produces a full 360° equirectangular panoramic video at 512×1024 resolution with T=25 frames. At inference, camera poses are estimated via MegaSaM SLAM to project the input onto equirectangular maps; the input is encoded as a partially-masked equirectangular video (unmapped regions set to black) that conditions the diffusion process alongside CLIP image features. Long videos are handled iteratively with a context window of S=5 frames.

## Method

Argus builds on Stable Video Diffusion-I2VXL with an EDM-parameterized denoising U-Net, trained via score matching on 242K curated 360°–perspective video pairs from the 3601M dataset. Three key techniques address the panoramic generation challenges: (1) camera motion simulation that synthesizes realistic perspective trajectories (linear drift + oscillatory + noise) from 360° source videos to create diverse training pairs; (2) view-based frame alignment that uses SLAM-estimated relative poses to project all frames onto a shared equirectangular coordinate system, ensuring consistent scene layout (e.g., sky always at top); and (3) blended decoding that decodes both the original and 180°-rotated latents and blends them in pixel space with a distance-based weight to eliminate left-right boundary artifacts. A height-dependent re-weighting function compensates for equirectangular pole distortion, and context-aware training with alternating full-mask and partial-mask conditioning enables iterative long-video generation.

## Results

On the video-to-360° generation task, Argus achieves a PSNR of 21.83 and FVD of 1228.6 on real camera trajectories, substantially outperforming the adapted PanoDiffusion baseline (PSNR 16.44, FVD 2649.0). Argus also improves LPIPS from 0.4138 to 0.2409 and line consistency from 0.6504 to 0.8506, demonstrating superior geometric coherence in extrapolated views. Against video outpainting methods, Argus leads on all VBench metrics (Imaging, Aesthetic, Motion) across 60°, 90°, and 120° FoV settings, with the largest margin at 120° FoV where Imaging quality reaches 0.4895 versus 0.4175 for Be-Your-Outpainter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PanoDiffusion (adapted for video), Argus wins decisively on PSNR (+5.4 dB), LPIPS (−0.17), FVD (−1420), Aesthetic Quality (+0.034), Motion Smoothness (+0.038), and Line Consistency (+0.20), while PanoDiffusion retains a slight edge on Imaging Quality (0.5055 vs. 0.4939), likely due to its per-frame optimization. Against the two video outpainting baselines (Be-Your-Outpainter and Follow-Your-Canvas), Argus outperforms both on all three VBench metrics across every FoV setting; the gap widens with larger FoV, e.g., at 120° Argus scores 0.4895/0.4796/0.9777 (Imaging/Aesthetic/Motion) versus 0.4175/0.3951/0.9628 for Be-Your-Outpainter. The outpainting methods also qualitatively fail to preserve the 360° panoramic property, with generation quality degrading as the distance from the input viewpoint increases, whereas Argus maintains consistent quality across the full panorama.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) latent diffusion architecture and the EDM training framework, repurposing them for the novel task of video-to-360° generation. It departs from prior video outpainting work (Be-Your-Outpainter, Follow-Your-Canvas), which is limited to rectangular frame extensions, and from 360° image panorama methods (PanoDiffusion), which lack temporal coherence. The closest prior work—Imagine360 and VidPanos—handles only pitch changes or panning footage on small datasets, whereas this paper generalizes to arbitrary 3DoF camera motion using a substantially larger 360° video corpus (283K clips vs. 9.6K) and introduces geometry-aware training techniques (camera motion simulation, view-based frame alignment, blended decoding) that are absent from prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Argus is the first model to generate temporally coherent, geometrically consistent 360° panoramic videos from arbitrary perspective inputs, opening practical applications in video stabilization without cropping, free camera viewpoint control in dynamic scenes, and interactive visual question answering. The key open question is whether the geometric and motion-aware techniques transfer to higher resolutions (current output is 512×1024, well below 4K panoramas) and whether the remaining shape inconsistencies and physics artifacts can be resolved at scale. The paper also raises the broader question of whether 360° video corpora can serve as a general-purpose prior for scene understanding tasks beyond generation.
