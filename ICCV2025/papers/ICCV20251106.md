## Problem

Existing traffic simulators model closed-loop motion only for agents already present in the initial scene, so during long-term rollouts (30 s+) the ego vehicle drives into new map regions where no agents exist, causing scenes to become progressively empty and unrealistic. No prior method jointly handles dynamic agent insertion and removal during closed-loop rollout to maintain a realistic scene layout over extended horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

InfGen takes a 1-second driving log (historical states of all visible agents) and a static vector map as input, and produces a 30-second closed-loop rollout structured as a dynamic agent matrix where rows (agents) are added or removed over time. Each output timestep contains predicted 0.5 s motion tokens for all active agents, plus discrete pose tokens (grid position + heading) for newly inserted agents and removal flags for exiting agents. Notably, the model is trained on 9.1 s logs yet produces stable rollouts up to 6× longer than the training horizon.

## Method

InfGen is a unified autoregressive transformer that performs interleaved next-token prediction over a single token sequence combining four modalities: map tokens (fixed-length road vectors), motion tokens (k-disks clustered 0.5 s trajectory segments), pose tokens (discretized ego-relative position grid + heading), and four control tokens that mark mode transitions between motion simulation and scene generation. At each timestep, motion simulation extends the temporal axis of a dynamic agent matrix via a three-layer attention stack (temporal self-attention over the agent's own history, agent-agent cross-attention, map-agent cross-attention), while scene generation extends the spatial axis by using a learnable agent query with a grid-attention layer over an occupancy grid to sample new agent poses autoregressively until a <BEGIN MOTION> control token is emitted. The model is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of next-token prediction losses over all token types on fully tokenized Waymo logs, learning both the two tasks and the automatic switching between them without any task-specific tuning.

## Results

On the 30-second long-term traffic simulation (the paper's primary task), InfGen achieves a composite WOSAC score of 0.6606, outperforming SMART-7M (0.6519) and CatK (0.6584). The most significant gains are in placement-based metrics (overall 0.4542 vs. 0.4324/0.4424) and Agent Count Error (Mean ACE 8.1 vs. 12.0/12.2; ACE Slope 0.15 vs. 0.31/0.32), demonstrating substantially better long-horizon scene stability. On the standard 8-second WOSAC short-term benchmark, InfGen is competitive (Composite 0.7514) but slightly trails CatK (0.7603) and SMART-7M (0.7521).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMART-7M [31], InfGen wins on composite (0.6606 vs. 0.6519), kinematic (0.5966 vs. 0.5839), interactive (0.7619 vs. 0.7542), and all four placement-based sub-metrics, while losing narrowly on map-based (0.8087 vs. 0.8102). Against CatK [41], the pattern is similar: InfGen leads on composite, kinematic, interactive, and placement metrics but trails on map-based (0.8087 vs. 0.8186), which the authors attribute to agents being inserted near road boundaries. On the short-term WOSAC benchmark (Table 1), InfGen (Composite 0.7514) is competitive with SMART-7M (0.7521) and slightly behind CatK (0.7603), while clearly outperforming TrafficBots [40] (0.6976) and GUMP [11] (0.7404). The most striking separation appears in the ACE metric: InfGen's error grows at a slope of 0.15 over 30 s, roughly half the rate of both baselines (~0.31–0.32), confirming that the interleaved scene-generation mechanism prevents the progressive scene emptiness that plagues motion-only rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfGen builds directly on the next-token-prediction formulation for traffic simulation established by SMART [31] and CatK [41], reusing their map and motion tokenizers (k-disks clustering) and position-aware attention from ProSim [25]. The key departure is the introduction of interleaved scene generation alongside motion simulation, inspired by interleaved multimodal token prediction in vision-language models like Chameleon [3]. While prior closed-loop simulators assume a fixed agent set over the prediction horizon, InfGen formulates long-term simulation as a product of motion prediction and dynamic agent insertion/removal, unified under a single autoregressive transformer with mode-control tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies and addresses a fundamental gap in traffic simulation: prior methods produce increasingly empty and unrealistic scenes when rolled out beyond their training horizon, undermining their utility for trip-level evaluation of self-driving systems. The finding that motion-only rollout quality is comparable across methods (Table 3) while scene dynamics diverge sharply suggests the field has been optimizing the wrong bottleneck for long-horizon realism. However, the evaluation is limited to 30-second rollouts on small WOSAC map areas, and the baselines are simply extended short-term models without proper scene-generation mechanisms, so the long-term advantage may be overstated. Open questions include whether the approach scales to true trip-level durations (>5 min), whether interactive RL can replace the purely supervised training, and whether the occupancy-grid-based scene generation generalizes to diverse urban environments.
