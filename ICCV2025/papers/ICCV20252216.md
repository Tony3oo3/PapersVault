## Problem

Existing multimodal LLM benchmarks are restricted to the visual modality and do not assess holistic audio-visual understanding or the reliability of AVLLMs under perturbed inputs. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates AVLLMs along three critical dimensions—adversarial attack, compositional reasoning, and modality-specific dependency—leaving a gap in understanding how these models behave when inputs are inconsistent, events are reordered, or individual modalities are removed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252216/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents AVLLMs with paired video clips and audio tracks alongside multiple-choice text questions (181K test samples across 9 tasks). Adversarial tasks (MCIT, ICIT, MVIT, MAIT) deliberately introduce missing or irrelevant answer choices or swap video/audio content; compositional tasks (COT-Stitch, COT-Swap, CAT) stitch or reorder multi-event sequences; modality tasks (MAT, MVT) remove one modality entirely. For CAVPref training, the method additionally consumes winning/losing response pairs and computes modality-specific similarity scores (CLAP for text, AVSM for audio-visual) to calibrate the loss.

## Method

CAVPref extends standard DPO with two AV-specific conditioning losses (L^V and L^A) that compute reward differences between the winning response under correct versus incorrect/missing video or audio conditioning, forcing the model to learn genuine cross-modal associations rather than relying on text alone. A calibrated β parameter (β = 0.9·ΔS + 0.1) is automatically set from the normalized similarity gap between winning and losing scenarios, using CLAP scores for the response loss and AVSM for the modality losses. A distributionally robust optimization module replaces the average loss with a worst-case risk over distributions ρ-close to the data distribution, implemented as an exponential loss L_R^i = −λ_i·log(E_P[e^{L^i/λ_i}]), which improves tail-category performance without degrading head categories. Binary switching parameters η and γ activate the video or audio conditioning loss only for tasks where the respective modality is perturbed, keeping the formulation task-aware.

## Results

On AVTRUSTBENCH (181K test samples, 9 tasks), the best zero-shot model GPT-4o achieves only 29.78% (Adversarial Attack), 33.53% (Compositional Reasoning), and 50.83% (Modality Dependency) average accuracy, revealing that even SOTA AVLLMs fall far short of human-level trustworthiness. After applying CAVPref, video-SALMONN achieves the largest gain of up to 30.19% (MAIT: 54.77% vs. 24.58% ZS), while VideoLLaMA-2 and Bay-CAT gain up to 29.74% and 25.02% respectively. CAVPref consistently outperforms both SFT and DPO across all 9 tasks, with the most dramatic improvements on tasks involving audio-visual inconsistency (MVIT, MAIT, COT-Swap, MVT, MAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVPref is compared against SFT and standard DPO [53] as model-agnostic training baselines on VideoLLaMA-2, Bay-CAT, and video-SALMONN. CAVPref decisively outperforms DPO on all 9 tasks for all three models, with the largest gaps on tasks involving audio-visual inconsistency: e.g., on MAIT, CAVPref improves video-SALMONN by +30.19% over ZS versus only +10.61% for DPO, and on MVIT by +26.73% versus +11.06%. On linguistic-only inconsistency tasks (MCIT, ICIT, COT-Stitch, CAT), DPO already provides meaningful gains, and CAVPref extends these by 3–6 additional points. The robustness module (comparing CAVPref vs. CAVPref w/o Robustness) adds a further 4–7% on tail categories without degrading head categories. Among the 16 zero-shot AVLLMs evaluated, GPT-4o leads all three dimensions (29.78/33.53/50.83%), followed closely by Reka and Gemini 1.5 Pro, while open-source models like VAST (12.22/25.27/28.42%) and Macaw-LLM (13.74/28.81/34.80%) trail significantly, confirming that even the strongest models remain far from reliable under adversarial and compositional stress.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al.) by introducing AV-specific conditioning losses (L^V, L^A) that force the preference optimization to account for audio-visual consistency, rather than relying solely on text-response preferences. The robustness module draws on distributionally robust optimization (Duchi & Namkoong) to handle tail categories in preference data. Conceptually, the paper departs from the visual-only evaluation paradigm of prior MLLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED-bench) by introducing audio as a first-class modality and by framing evaluation around trustworthiness dimensions (adversarial robustness, compositional reasoning, modality dependency) that no prior benchmark jointly addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVTRUSTBENCH (600K samples, 9 tasks) is likely to become a standard reference for evaluating AVLLM reliability, and the finding that even GPT-4o scores below 35% on adversarial and compositional tasks exposes a significant gap between current capabilities and trustworthy deployment. The CAVPref strategy offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the 30% gains generalize beyond the three tested models, whether the semi-automated annotation pipeline introduces systematic biases, and whether the compositional reasoning deficit reflects a fundamental limitation of current AVLLM architectures rather than a data issue.
