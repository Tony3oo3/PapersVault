## Problem

Previous aerial visual localization over LoD city models (LoD-Loc) relies on high-detail LoD2/LoD3 models for neural wireframe alignment, but the vast majority of available and planned nationwide city models are low-detail LoD1, which lack the structural richness needed for wireframe matching. This limits global scalability of UAV localization to regions with rare high-LoD data, leaving the widely available LoD1 models unusable for pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB query image, a 6-DoF prior pose from onboard inertial sensors (reduced to 4-DoF since pitch and roll are fixed by gravity), and a georeferenced LoD city model (LoD1–LoD3). It outputs a refined 4-DoF camera pose (x, y, z, yaw). During coarse selection, silhouettes are compared at 301×224 resolution; during fine estimation, the mask resolution doubles to 602×448, and the LoD model is projected in real time via OpenSceneGraph at ~630 µs per frame.

## Method

LoD-Loc v2 replaces wireframe alignment with explicit silhouette alignment in a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. First, a SAM2-based U-Net (frozen SAM2 encoder + U-Net decoder + receptive field blocks + adapters, trained with BCE+IoU loss) segments building silhouettes from the query image. Second, a 4D pose cost volume is constructed by uniformly sampling hypotheses in (x, y, z, yaw) around the prior pose; each cell scores the IoU between the projected LoD silhouette and the predicted query silhouette, and the argmax yields the coarse pose. Third, a particle filter with multi-beam hypothesis tracking refines the pose on the SO(3)×T(3) manifold, applying Lie-algebra perturbations only along yaw and translation axes over 20–40 iterations with 52 candidates per iteration. The key insight is that while LoD1 wireframes are too sparse for precise alignment, building silhouettes remain discriminative enough for accurate pose recovery.

## Results

On the newly released UAVD4L-LoDv2 dataset (LoD1), LoD-Loc v2 achieves 93.70%/98.40%/99.50% success rates at (2m,2°)/(3m,3°)/(5m,5°) thresholds for in-trajectory queries, with median translation/rotation errors of 0.72 m / 0.15°. For out-of-trajectory queries it reaches 97.90%/99.80%/100.00% with 0.71 m / 0.14° errors. On high-LoD models (LoD3 UAVD4L-LoD), it improves over LoD-Loc from 84.41% to 98.50% at the (2m,2°) threshold, and on LoD2 Swiss-EPFL from 48.60% to 98.50%. The method also expands the convergence basin, maintaining >90% success at (2m,2°) even with ±200 m prior translation errors, where LoD-Loc drops to 0.12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoD-Loc v2 substantially outperforms the prior LoD-model method LoD-Loc [99] on the LoD1 UAVD4L-LoDv2 dataset: 93.70% vs. 49.56% at (2m,2°) in-trajectory, and 97.90% vs. 54.20% out-of-trajectory, with median translation error reduced from 3.32 m to 0.72 m. Against the texture-model SOTA UAVD4L (RoMA variant), LoD-Loc v2 matches or exceeds performance on in-trajectory queries (93.70% vs. 93.27%) and clearly wins out-of-trajectory (97.90% vs. 95.03%), despite using a far simpler LoD1 reference instead of a full textured mesh. CAD-Loc and MC-Loc, which also operate on LoD models, fail completely on LoD1 (0% or <1% success) due to the large modality gap between textured and non-textured inputs. On high-LoD models, LoD-Loc v2 improves over LoD-Loc by 14.09 points at (2m,2°) on LoD3 (98.50% vs. 84.41%) and by 49.90 points on LoD2 (98.50% vs. 48.60%), confirming that the silhouette-alignment strategy generalizes across detail levels. On Swiss-EPFLv2 (LoD1), LoD-Loc v2 achieves 54.20%/74.60%/92.00% in-place, surpassing LoD-Loc (36.79%/50.56%/69.77%) and UAVD4L-RoMA (45.95%/66.77%/80.73%), though UAVD4L-RoMA retains an advantage on out-of-place queries (98.84% vs. 86.30% at 5m-5°) due to its richer texture reference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LoD-Loc (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which achieved aerial visual localization by aligning neural-predicted wireframes with projected LoD model edges. The key departure is replacing wireframe alignment with explicit silhouette (IoU) alignment, enabling the pipeline to operate on LoD1 models that lack the structural detail required for wireframe matching. The fine pose estimation stage borrows particle filtering on SO(3)×T(3) from prior work (Kwon et al., Choi & Christensen) and adds a multi-beam tracking strategy from MC-Loc, while the segmentation module builds on SAM2 with a U-Net decoder. The paper also positions itself against texture-model-based methods (UAVD4L, CAD-Loc, MC-Loc) and 2D-map-based approaches, arguing that LoD models offer a lightweight, privacy-preserving middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since the vast majority of publicly available and nationally planned 3D city models are LoD1 (as tabulated in the paper for the US, China, Switzerland, Japan, Netherlands, Singapore, and Germany), this work addresses a critical scalability bottleneck: prior LoD-based localization was limited to the small fraction of regions with LoD2/LoD3 data. The released 10.7 km² LoD1 datasets and the demonstrated ability to handle up to 200 m prior translation errors make this a practical step toward GPS-denied UAV localization in arbitrary cities. A stated limitation is degraded performance when building silhouettes dominate the frame, and the method still requires a reasonably accurate sensor prior (pitch/roll assumed correct), leaving open questions about fully autonomous pose recovery without inertial priors and about generalization to highly dense or irregular urban geometries.
