## Problem

While DiT-based video generation has advanced rapidly for text-to-video, it remains unclear how to effectively incorporate image conditions into the DiT architecture, whether a single model can handle both T2V and TI2V simultaneously, and how to generate long videos without prohibitive compute. Additionally, the field lacks a comprehensive, step-by-step recipe showing how architectural choices, training stability techniques, and progressive scaling collectively impact final performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251683/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STIV accepts a text prompt (via T5 + CLIP encoders) and optionally one or more image frames as conditions, which are encoded by a frozen per-frame VAE into latent space. The frame replacement mechanism allows flexible conditioning: zero image frames yields T2V, one initial frame yields TI2V, the last two frames from a prior rollout enable autoregressive long-video extension, and first+last frames enable interpolation. Outputs are generated videos at 256², 512², or 540p (256×480) resolution with 20 base frames, extendable to 92+ frames via rollouts and 200+ frames at 540p.

## Method

STIV integrates image conditions into a PixArt-α-based DiT via frame replacement: the noised first-frame latent is swapped with the un-noised image-condition latent, with the loss masked on that frame, and the image condition is randomly dropped during training to enable multi-task T2V/TI2V learning and resolve motion staleness. The model uses a Flow Matching training objective with CFG Renormalization at inference to prevent velocity overshooting, factorized spatial-temporal attention with 2D/1D RoPE, and cross-attention for text conditioning. Training stability is achieved through QK-Norm, sandwich-norm, and stateless layer normalization on singleton conditions, while efficiency comes from MaskDiT (50% spatial token masking) and the AdaFactor optimizer. A progressive training pipeline (T2I → T2V → STIV) with interpolated RoPE embeddings enables scaling from 600M to 8.7B parameters and from 256² to 540p resolution, and autoregressive rollouts using the last two frames as image conditions extend generation to long videos.

## Results

On VBench T2V at 512² resolution, the best STIV model (M-512 + SFT + TUP) achieves a Total score of 83.1, with Quality 84.2 and Semantic 78.5, surpassing all open-source models (best: LaVie-2 at 81.8) and closed-source models (best: Gen-3 at 82.3). On VBench-I2V at 512², STIV-M-512 achieves a Total score of 90.1 (Quality 82.1, I2V 98.0), outperforming SVD (89.9) and Animate-Anything (89.8). The model also achieves a VBench-Semantic score of 79.5 with UnmaskSFT, exceeding KLING (75.7), PIKA (71.8), and Gen-3 (75.2). For long video generation (92 frames at 256×480), STIV-M-480 scores 81.2 Total on VBench, competitive with CogVideoX-5B (81.9) and Kling (81.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STIV's best T2V model (M-512 + SFT + TUP, Total 83.1) outperforms all 13 open- and closed-source baselines on VBench Total, with the closest competitors being Gen-3 (82.3, +0.8 margin) and KLING (81.9, +1.2 margin). Among open-source models, CogVideoX-5B (81.6) and LaVie-2 (81.8) are the strongest, both trailing STIV by 1.3–1.5 points. On the Semantic sub-metric, STIV's UnmaskSFT variant (79.5) leads all competitors by a wide margin over KLING (75.7) and Gen-3 (75.2), while EMU3 achieves the highest Quality (84.1) but at the cost of very low Semantic (68.4). For the TI2V task on VBench-I2V, STIV-M-512 (Total 90.1) edges out SVD (89.9) and Animate-Anything (89.8) by narrow margins, while substantially outperforming DynamicCrafter-512 (89.1), I2VGen-XL (88.5), and VideoCrafter-I2V (85.1). Notably, STIV achieves a Dynamic Degree of 24.0 versus only 10.2 for a dedicated TI2V model, demonstrating that image condition dropout preserves motion diversity without sacrificing consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

STIV builds on the PixArt-α DiT architecture with cross-attention for text conditioning, adopting Flow Matching (from SiT) as the training objective and MaskDiT for efficient token processing. Its key conceptual departure is the use of simple frame replacement—swapping noised latents with clean image-condition latents—rather than the cross-attention or token-concatenation strategies employed by SD3, CogVideoX, and prior I2V methods like DynamiCrafter or ConsistI2V. The paper positions itself as a unified, transparent 'recipe' that systematically combines stabilization techniques (QK-norm, sandwich-norm), efficiency measures (AdaFactor, MaskDiT), and quality improvements (RoPE, micro conditions, CFG renormalization) into a single scalable pipeline, extending the T2V task to TI2V and autoregressive long-video generation within one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a step-by-step scaling recipe from 600M to 8.7B parameters with comprehensive ablations at each stage, STIV offers the research community a practical blueprint for building competitive video generation models, achieving results on par with closed-source systems like Sora, Kling, and Gen-3. The unified T2V/TI2V/long-video framework via frame replacement and image-condition dropout is notably simple, suggesting that complex conditioning mechanisms may be unnecessary. Open questions remain around error accumulation in autoregressive rollouts beyond 3–5 steps, and whether the approach generalizes to even longer durations or higher resolutions without additional architectural changes.
