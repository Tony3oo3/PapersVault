## Problem

Existing continuous visual primitives face a fundamental trade-off: implicit neural representations suffer from spectral bias that loses information in unlearned frequency bands, while explicit Gaussian primitives are inherently low-pass filters that smooth away high-frequency details like sharp edges and intricate textures. No single primitive has been shown to flexibly capture both low-frequency structure and high-frequency detail across 2D, 5D, and 6D visual signals while maintaining real-time rendering speeds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (Kodak at native resolution, DIV2K downscaled by 2 to 408×1020–1020×1020, Mip-NeRF360/Tanks & Temples/DeepBlending at their standard splits) for static tasks, and temporally-ordered video frames (D-NeRF, NeRF-DS) for dynamic tasks. Outputs are rendered novel views or reconstructed images at real-time frame rates—95–126 FPS for 5D static scenes and 42–84 FPS for 6D dynamic scenes—producing per-pixel RGB values via α-blending over sorted wavelet primitives projected onto the image plane.

## Method

WIPES replaces each Gaussian primitive with a Morlet wavelet primitive defined as a cosine-modulated Gaussian, where the modulation frequency vector f is a learnable per-primitive parameter that shifts the spectral center while the Gaussian covariance controls bandwidth. This single formulation subsumes the standard Gaussian (e = 0) and allows each primitive to simultaneously encode low- and high-frequency content without external frequency priors or handcrafted guidance. A custom differentiable rasterizer projects the 3D wavelet onto the 2D image plane via the same ray-space covariance transform used in 3DGS, then performs α-blending with explicit analytic gradients for all parameters (position, frequency, covariance, opacity, color) to avoid the overhead of automatic differentiation. For dynamic scenes, the deformation MLP additionally outputs a frequency delta δE per primitive per timestep, enabling temporally-adaptive spectral tuning. The entire pipeline drops into existing Gaussian-splatting training loops with only the primitive kernel and gradient computation swapped.

## Results

On 5D static novel view synthesis, WIPES achieves 27.55 PSNR / 0.816 SSIM on Mip-NeRF360, 23.78 PSNR / 0.852 SSIM on Tanks & Temples, and 29.82 PSNR / 0.907 SSIM on DeepBlending, outperforming 3DGS on all three benchmarks while maintaining ~95–126 FPS. On 2D image fitting (Kodak), WIPES-Cholesky reaches 45.87 PSNR, surpassing GSImage-Cholesky (44.06) and WIRE (38.02) by 1.8 and 7.8 dB respectively. On 6D dynamic NVS, WIPES achieves 39.52 PSNR on D-NeRF and 23.95 PSNR on NeRF-DS, beating D-3DGS by 0.36 and 0.26 dB while also rendering faster (84.0 vs 71.7 FPS on D-NeRF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIPES outperforms 3DGS [14] on all three 5D NVS benchmarks in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS (e.g., +0.43 dB on Mip-NeRF360, +0.43 dB on Tanks & Temples, +0.39 dB on DeepBlending), though at a modest speed cost (95.7 vs 118.2 FPS on Mip-NeRF360). Against the INR-based Mip-NeRF360 [3], WIPES trades a tiny 0.14 dB PSNR gap on Mip-NeRF360 for a ~1600× speedup (95.7 vs 0.06 FPS). On 2D image fitting, WIPES beats GSImage [44] by ~1.8 dB PSNR on Kodak but GSImage renders ~25% faster (2249 vs 1779 FPS); both dwarf the INR baseline WIRE [31] by >7 dB. In the frequency-guided comparison (Table 3), WIPES matches or exceeds GES [11] at both densify thresholds without any frequency prior, while using fewer primitives (e.g., 1.401M vs 1.521M on Mip-NeRF360 at 3e-4). For 6D dynamic NVS, WIPES surpasses D-3DGS [38] on both D-NeRF (+0.36 dB, +12.3 FPS) and NeRF-DS (+0.26 dB, +4.1 FPS), and dominates the INR baselines D-NeRF [26] and NeRF-DS [37] by >9 dB in PSNR with orders-of-magnitude faster rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIPES extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline by replacing Gaussian primitives with Morlet wavelet primitives (a Gaussian envelope modulated by a cosine), making the Gaussian a strict special case (zero frequency). For 2D image fitting, it directly modifies GSImage's weighted-summation formulation, and for dynamic scenes it extends D-3DGS's deformation MLP to also predict frequency offsets. Conceptually, the paper departs from both INR-based approaches (which suffer spectral bias and slow MLP decoding) and prior wavelet-in-NeRF methods (which embed wavelets implicitly inside networks), instead treating wavelets as explicit, differentiable rendering primitives with a custom CUDA rasterizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single primitive that adaptively covers both low- and high-frequency content without handcrafted frequency regularization or positional encodings, WIPES simplifies the design space for explicit radiance-field representations and could be dropped into existing Gaussian-splatting pipelines with minimal code changes. The authors acknowledge that the inherited Gaussian training pipeline (fixed hyperparameters, gradient-based densification) remains a bottleneck, and a fully wavelet-native training and densification strategy is left as future work. Whether the modest PSNR gains over 3DGS (≈0.3–0.5 dB in 3D) translate into meaningful perceptual improvements at scale, and whether the slightly lower FPS (95.7 vs 118.2 on Mip-NeRF360) is acceptable for real-time applications, remain open practical questions.
