## Problem

Existing protection perturbation methods for personalized image generation are vulnerable to purification attacks (e.g., JPEG compression, resizing, GridPure) that strip the adversarial noise and restore generation quality. No prior mechanism allows the service provider to detect when a protected image has been purified, leaving a gap between protection and tamper detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512×3 RGB image, a binary authorization message of length L, and a target diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2-1) for protection perturbation generation. The image is divided into 16×16 non-overlapping blocks for BDCT. The output is a single perturbed image in the pixel domain that simultaneously carries both protection and authorization perturbations, plus an extracted authorization message whose bit-error is checked against a threshold to trigger rejection of purified (unauthorized) images.

## Method

ATP operates entirely in the frequency domain via Block Discrete Cosine Transformation (BDCT), where a Bernoulli-sampled binary mask spatially separates protection and authorization perturbations so they do not interfere. The authorization perturbation uses a convolutional autoencoder (inspired by HIDDEN steganography) to embed a binary message into masked frequency coefficients, trained with reconstruction, adversarial, message-consistency, and regularization losses. The protection perturbation is generated by an Improved Frequency Domain PGD (Algorithm 1) that moves the projection and sign operations into the frequency domain, preventing pixel-domain nonlinearities from corrupting the mask-protected coefficients. After BIDCT, both perturbations are uniformly distributed across all pixels, making the authorization message highly sensitive to any purification while remaining invisible to the human eye. This design is plug-and-play: any existing PGD-based protection algorithm (Anti-DB, AdvDM, CAAT, MetaCloak) can be adapted to ATP by replacing its pixel-domain PGD with Algorithm 1.

## Results

Under purification attacks (JPEG, Resize 4×, GridPure), all baseline protection methods suffer unavoidable PSR drops, but when combined with ATP's tamper-proof mechanism, every method achieves a 100% Protection Success Rate by triggering generation rejection. Without purification, ATP-adapted baselines maintain comparable protection quality: e.g., MetaCloak+Ours achieves CLIP-IQAC of −0.3639 and FDFR of 0.7038 on CelebA-HQ, versus −0.3418 and 0.4850 for vanilla MetaCloak. On aesthetic impact, ATP generally improves perturbed-image quality, raising CLIP-IQAC from −0.0139 (MetaCloak) to 0.4923 (MetaCloak+Ours) on CelebA-HQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four protection perturbation baselines (Anti-DB, AdvDM, CAAT, MetaCloak) and one watermarking method (FaceSigns). Under purification attacks, all four baselines show significant PSR degradation (e.g., MetaCloak drops from ~1.0 clean to well below 1.0 under GridPure), whereas ATP-adapted versions of all four achieve 100% PSR by detecting tampering and rejecting the request. Without purification, ATP-adapted methods consistently improve FDFR (e.g., MetaCloak: 0.4850→0.7038 on CelebA-HQ; AdvDM: 0.6100→0.8638 on VGGFace2) and reduce ISM, indicating stronger identity disruption while preserving comparable CLIP-IQAC degradation. On aesthetic impact of the perturbed image itself, ATP generally raises CLIP-IQAC (e.g., MetaCloak: −0.0139→0.4923 on CelebA-HQ), meaning the perturbation is less visually obtrusive. FaceSigns, the only watermarking baseline, exhibits low sensitivity to purification (Bit-error remains low under JPEG/resize), confirming that existing watermarks are incompatible with the tamper-proof design that requires high purification sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the protection perturbation line of work (Anti-DB, AdvDM, CAAT, MetaCloak) by adding a tamper-detection layer rather than improving purification resistance. The authorization perturbation borrows its convolutional autoencoder architecture from the steganography method Hidden [32], while the mask-guided frequency-domain perturbation concept draws on low-frequency adversarial perturbation [10]. The key conceptual departure is shifting the defense paradigm from 'make the perturbation survive purification' to 'detect that purification occurred and reject the request,' which is orthogonal to and composable with any existing protection algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tamper-proof mechanism addresses a well-documented failure mode of all current protection perturbations (performance collapse under even naive purification like JPEG or resizing), and the 100% PSR under purification is a strong practical result for service providers. However, the defense is scoped to the service-provider side: an attacker with local compute can still bypass verification, and the adaptive attack where both the mask and BDCT hyperparameters are known reduces PSR to 0.33. A key open question the authors identify is whether the explicit verification step can be eliminated by designing authorization perturbations whose disruption inherently degrades generation quality, which would close the local-attack gap.
