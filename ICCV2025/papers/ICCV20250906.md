## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs operate at the token level—performing contrastive decoding or penalizing over-trust tokens—without understanding how hallucinated information actually enters the model during processing. This paper reveals that hallucinated knowledge does not propagate from shallow layers but is abruptly injected in deep layers, causing token probabilities to surge and deviate from the input image. The gap is a lack of an information-level mechanism to identify and remove such injected knowledge before it influences the final output.

## Input / Output

SHIFT takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and produces a single hallucination-reduced text response. It operates as a drop-in inference-time intervention on any MLLM (LLaVA-1.5, mPLUG-Owl2, InstructBLIP) and is compatible with any decoding strategy (greedy, beam search, nucleus sampling), requiring only one forward pass with a single backtracking step from the detected mutation layer rather than multiple sampling or rollback iterations.

## Method

SHIFT tracks information flow by computing the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between token probability distributions of adjacent transformer layers, identifying 'mutation layers' where JSD spikes indicate abrupt information injection. For each retained mutation layer (filtered by thresholds ε and δ to exclude minor fluctuations), the method blends the feature encoding vector at that layer with the preceding layer's vector using a convex combination controlled by parameter α, performed on the raw encoding vectors before the affine projection rather than on token probabilities. The smoothed vector is then passed through the remaining transformer blocks to produce the final token prediction. This single-pass, training-free intervention removes hallucinated knowledge at its point of injection, making it approximately 1.5× faster than contrastive decoding baselines and 6× faster than OPERA while achieving superior hallucination reduction across CHAIR, POPE, and MMHal-Bench benchmarks.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (lower is better), SHIFT reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) by up to 12.4% over vanilla LLaVA-1.5 under greedy decoding (49.6→43.8) and by 12.5% under beam search (49.2→36.7). On POPE (higher is better), SHIFT improves accuracy by up to 5.59% (MSCOCO) and 6.0% (GQA adversarial, LLaVA-1.5: 68.40→74.40) over the vanilla model. In GPT-4v-assisted evaluation, SHIFT raises correctness scores by up to 0.84 points (LLaVA-1.5: 5.72→6.56). In terms of efficiency, SHIFT runs in 2519 ms with 15746 MB memory, approximately 1.5× faster than VCD/ICD and 6× faster than OPERA (16657 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHIFT consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model and decoding strategy. Against OPERA (beam search), SHIFT reduces CHAIR_S by 11.4% on LLaVA-1.5 (36.7 vs 45.4) and 11.0% on InstructBLIP (39.0 vs 50.4), while also being ~6× faster (2519 ms vs 16657 ms). Against VCD (nucleus sampling), SHIFT is substantially better—VCD actually *increases* hallucinations on LLaVA-1.5 (CHAIR_S 56.4 vs 48.4 vanilla), whereas SHIFT reduces it to 42.0; on POPE, SHIFT leads VCD by up to 3.8% accuracy. Against ICD, SHIFT improves CHAIR_S by 6.4 points on LLaVA-1.5 (42.0 vs 48.4) and by 20.8 points on InstructBLIP (47.0 vs 67.8), and leads on POPE by up to 4.08% accuracy. The advantage of SHIFT grows with task difficulty: on the adversarial POPE setting, the gap over OPERA widens to 2.6% accuracy, and over ICD to 11.53% on InstructBLIP, indicating that information-flow tuning is particularly effective when hallucination pressure is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant token-level paradigm for hallucination mitigation (contrastive decoding in VCD/ICD, over-trust penalties in OPERA) by shifting the intervention point to the internal information flow between transformer layers. It builds on the authors' own prior work on tracking knowledge evolution in large vision-language models [45] and the broader early-exiting literature, repurposing Jensen-Shannon Divergence as a diagnostic for detecting 'mutation layers' where hallucinated knowledge is abruptly injected. Rather than requiring multiple forward passes or rollback strategies, SHIFT performs a single-pass layer-level smoothing, positioning itself as a complementary, decoding-agnostic alternative to existing training-free methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHIFT offers a practical, training-free hallucination mitigation that is ~6× faster than OPERA and ~1.5× faster than contrastive decoding methods while achieving comparable or better accuracy, making it attractive for deployment in latency-sensitive multimodal pipelines. The diagnostic insight that hallucinated tokens surge in probability at specific deep-layer 'mutation points' rather than propagating gradually from shallow layers provides a new mechanistic lens for understanding MLLM failures. A noted limitation is that when hallucinations originate in very early layers, applying SHIFT can trigger secondary mutations in deeper layers, requiring additional processing. Open questions include whether the mutation-layer framework generalizes to non-LLaVA architectures (e.g., native multimodal models without a separate vision encoder) and whether the JSD-based detection threshold transfers across model scales.
