## Problem

Mamba-based multi-instance learning methods for whole slide image (WSI) classification suffer from two critical limitations: (1) local redundancy, where similar and uninformative patches cause unnecessary computational overhead and dilute discriminative signals, and (2) sparse global representation, where spatially dispersed tumor regions across groups are inadequately aggregated, leading to suboptimal bag-level predictions. Existing masking or selection strategies either sacrifice critical information or incur prohibitive computational costs at WSI scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250611/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted 512-dimensional instance features (ResNet18-ImageNet) from 256×256 patches at 10× or 20× magnification, or combined 512+384-dimensional features (ResNet18 + ViT-S/16-SSL/DINO) for the BRACS dataset. Instances are first partitioned into G groups of M instances each via K-Means clustering on spatial coordinates, then processed through the GMMamba pipeline to produce a single bag-level class label (binary for TCGA datasets, 3-class for BRACS). A tunable mask ratio M_r controls the fraction of low-attention instances pruned within each group.

## Method

GMMamba combines two modules: (1) Intra-group Masking Mamba (IMM), which applies a bidirectional Mamba (BiMamba) block to each spatially-coherent group, then uses an attention-based sparse mask to prune M_r fraction of low-attention instances before a second BiMamba+attention pass, yielding compact group representations; and (2) Cross-group Super-feature Sampling (CSS), which extracts a super-feature via Max-Pooling across all group representations, refines it through cross-attention and multi-head attention to capture dispersed tumor signals, and uses a softmax association matrix Q to re-inject local group details into the global super-feature. A learnable class token aggregated via multi-head attention over the final super-feature group representations feeds an MLP for bag-level prediction. The location-based K-Means grouping (rather than random or feature-based clustering) is key to preserving spatial homogeneity within groups, enabling the masking to target truly redundant instances.

## Results

On TCGA-BRCA, GMMamba achieves 89.1% accuracy, 90.6% AUC, and 93.2% F1, surpassing the strongest baseline ACMIL (86.9% accuracy) by 2.2%. On TCGA-ESCA, GMMamba reaches 94.9% accuracy, 97.0% AUC, and 95.5% F1, outperforming ACMIL by 6.4% in accuracy. On TCGA-Lung, GMMamba attains 87.7% accuracy and 88.0% F1, and on the three-class BRACS dataset it achieves 77.8% accuracy (ResNet18) and 81.9% accuracy (ViT-S/16-SSL), exceeding the best prior methods by 3.9% and 4.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMMamba outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest attention-based method ACMIL [46], GMMamba gains 2.2% accuracy on TCGA-BRCA (89.1% vs. 86.9%) and 6.4% on TCGA-ESCA (94.9% vs. 88.5%), with corresponding F1 improvements of 1.2% and 5.4%. Compared to the Mamba-based MambaMIL [42], GMMamba achieves average accuracy and F1 gains of 7.15% and 8.2% across both BRACS encoders, and 12.8% accuracy on TCGA-ESCA (94.9% vs. 82.1%). The Transformer-based TransMIL [31] and DTFD-AFS [44] trail GMMamba by 42–65 points in accuracy on TCGA-ESCA, while the group-based DTFD-AFS [44] is the closest non-Mamba competitor on ESCA (87.2% vs. 94.9%). On TCGA-Lung, GMMamba (87.7% accuracy) surpasses MHIM-ABMIL [35] (86.7%) and MambaMIL [42] (85.6%) by 1.0% and 2.1% respectively. On the three-class BRACS dataset, GMMamba leads ILRA-MIL [39] by 4.6% accuracy (ResNet18) and ACMIL [46] by 8.0% (ResNet18), confirming consistent gains across binary and multi-class settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMMamba extends the Mamba-based MIL line (MambaMIL, SSMMIL) by introducing a two-level hierarchy: intra-group masking via bidirectional Mamba with attention-based sparse pruning, and cross-group super-feature sampling via a cascade of max-pooling, cross-attention, and multi-head attention. It departs from prior Mamba-MIL methods that process all instances uniformly by employing location-based K-Means clustering to form spatially coherent groups, then adaptively masking low-attention instances within each group. The CSS module is a novel aggregation strategy not seen in prior MIL work, and its demonstrated plug-and-play compatibility with ABMIL, TransMIL, DTFD, SSMMIL, and MambaMIL positions it as a general-purpose enhancement rather than a monolithic architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in WSI classification—computational cost of modeling thousands of instances—while simultaneously improving accuracy, which is valuable for clinical deployment. The CSS module's generalizability (improving five different MIL backbones by 1.9–5.1% on TCGA-ESCA) suggests it could be adopted independently of the full GMMamba pipeline. However, the authors acknowledge that applying a fixed masking ratio across all groups limits performance, and the non-monotonic sensitivity of accuracy to M_r (Table 8) hints that dataset- or group-adaptive masking remains an open problem. The broader question of whether Mamba's linear-complexity advantage translates to meaningful wall-clock savings at WSI scale (vs. optimized Transformer implementations) is not fully quantified.
