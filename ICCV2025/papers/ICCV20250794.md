## Problem

Displaced parallelism accelerates MoE-based diffusion inference by overlapping all-to-all communication with computation, but introduces 2-step staleness—layers compute on activations from two timesteps ago—causing severe quality degradation (FID rising from 5.31 to 8.27). No prior method addresses this staleness issue specifically in expert-parallel diffusion inference, leaving a fundamental tension between communication efficiency and output fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250794/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DICE is an inference-time scheduling framework applied to pre-trained DiT-MoE models (XL: 8 experts × 28 layers; G: 16 experts × 40 layers) for class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation. It takes a class label and random noise as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image, using Rectified Flow with 50 denoising steps (also evaluated at 10 and 20 steps). The framework operates across 8 GPUs in an expert-parallel configuration, modifying only the communication schedule without altering model weights or architecture.

## Method

DICE reduces staleness at three granularities through a training-free, three-fold strategy. (1) Interweaved Parallelism re-schedules the two all-to-all operations (dispatch and combine) so that dispatch completes within the current step (0-step delay) while combine finishes one step later (1-step delay), halving staleness from 2-step to 1-step and buffer size by half, with no added latency. (2) Selective Synchronization exploits the finding that deeper MoE layers are more vulnerable to stale activations (due to high-level semantic processing), synchronizing only the deeper half of layers while keeping shallow layers asynchronous. (3) Conditional Communication uses router scores as a token-importance signal: high-scoring tokens (whose expert-weighted contributions dominate the output, as shown by the gradient ∂‖y‖/∂h ∝ s) are communicated every step, while low-scoring tokens reuse cached activations with a configurable stride, reducing communication volume with minimal quality loss. Together these yield up to 1.26× speedup over synchronous expert parallelism with FID of 6.11 versus 5.31 for the synchronous baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (50-step Rectified Flow, 50K samples), DICE achieves FID 6.11, sFID 10.93, and IS 225.65, substantially outperforming DistriFusion (FID 7.79) and Displaced Expert Parallelism (FID 8.27) while remaining close to the synchronous Expert Parallelism reference (FID 5.31). DICE delivers up to 1.26× speedup over Expert Parallelism at batch size 32 on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs, with the gap narrowing to 1.20× at 10 steps and 1.24× at 20 steps. Interweaved Parallelism alone (a component of DICE) already reduces FID from 8.27 to 6.97, demonstrating that halving staleness from 2-step to 1-step is the dominant quality recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICE is compared against three baselines: synchronous Expert Parallelism (GShard-style, FID 5.31), DistriFusion (displaced sequence parallelism, FID 7.79), and Displaced Expert Parallelism (FID 8.27). DICE wins decisively over both asynchronous baselines on every quality metric: it reduces FID by 1.68 over DistriFusion and 2.16 over Displaced Expert Parallelism, while also improving IS by ~20 points and Precision by 0.01–0.02. Against the synchronous Expert Parallelism reference, DICE trades a modest 0.80 FID increase (5.31 → 6.11) for a 1.20–1.26× speedup, making it the only method that simultaneously accelerates inference and preserves near-synchronous quality. DistriFusion additionally suffers out-of-memory failures at batch size ≥16 on DiT-MoE-XL and cannot run DiT-MoE-G at all, whereas DICE scales to batch size 32. Under fewer steps (10 and 20), DICE's advantage over the displaced baselines widens further (FID 15.13 vs. 27.36/27.61 at 10 steps; 8.60 vs. 13.72/15.27 at 20 steps), confirming that staleness mitigation becomes more critical as the diffusion schedule shortens.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICE builds directly on DistriFusion's displaced parallelism [22], which overlaps communication and computation in diffusion inference by exploiting activation similarity across timesteps, and on standard expert parallelism [18] for distributing MoE weights across devices. The paper departs from this lineage by identifying staleness—the use of outdated activations—as the fundamental quality bottleneck in displaced expert parallelism, and by proposing a three-level (step, layer, token) optimization framework rather than a single scheduling trick. It also positions itself against PipeFusion [41] and AsyncDiff [2], which similarly exploit temporal redundancy, and against MoE-specific systems like FasterMoE [11] and DeepSpeed-MoE [33], noting that none of these address the unique staleness dynamics of iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE-based diffusion models scale beyond 16B parameters, efficient expert-parallel inference becomes a practical necessity, and DICE's staleness-centric framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why naive asynchronous communication degrades quality. The 'free lunch' result—that interweaved parallelism halves staleness with zero additional computation—is a clean scheduling insight likely to be adopted in future distributed diffusion systems. However, the evaluation is confined to ImageNet 256×256, a single model family (DiT-MoE), and 8 GPUs, leaving open whether the layer-sensitivity and token-importance findings generalize to video diffusion, larger expert counts, or heterogeneous hardware topologies.
