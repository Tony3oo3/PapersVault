## Problem

Diffusion-based video depth estimation methods struggle with long videos because they split input into overlapping sliding windows, causing accumulated scale discrepancies across windows that worsen with video length. Additionally, these methods rely solely on 2D diffusion priors that lack awareness of the inherent 3D geometric structure of depth, producing predictions that appear correct in 2D but exhibit significant 3D misalignment (e.g., staggered walls across frames). Existing initialization-based strategies for cross-window consistency prove insufficient, as the injected scale information is lost during the denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video as input and produces a temporally consistent per-frame depth sequence. The video is split into overlapping sliding windows of 90 frames (ScanNet, GMU Kitchen) or 110 frames (Bonn, KITTI), processed sequentially in a streaming fashion. Inference uses 5 Euler denoising steps with guidance applied in the last 2 steps, running on a single A100 GPU. The method is entirely training-free, operating as a plug-in guidance layer on top of the pre-trained DepthCrafter model.

## Method

DepthSync introduces two complementary diffusion guidance mechanisms applied throughout the denoising trajectory. Scale guidance (forward guidance) synchronizes depth scale across windows by decoding the current and previous window latents at each step, computing a least-squares scale-and-shift alignment on the overlapping region, encoding the aligned depth back to latent space, and using an MSE loss to steer the noise prediction. Geometry guidance (backward guidance) enforces intra-window 3D consistency by decoding the predicted depth and optimizing it against multiple constraints: a depth reprojection loss (pairwise projection via PnP with CoTracker3 poses), a tracking loss (3D distance of tracked points), a surface normal loss (aligned with StableNormal predictions), and an edge-aware smoothness loss. The key design choice is forward guidance for scale (operating in latent space for smoothness) versus backward guidance for geometry (operating in decoded image space to avoid backpropagating through the VAE), with the two terms applied sequentially and synergistically at each denoising step.

## Results

On the longest evaluation setting (450 frames, 5 overlapping windows on ScanNet/GMU Kitchen; 590 frames, 5 windows on KITTI/Bonn), DepthSync achieves AbsRel of 0.154 and δ₁ of 0.769 on ScanNet, 0.093 and 0.929 on Bonn, outperforming the strongest baseline DepthCrafter (0.171/0.716 and 0.143/0.801 respectively). The advantage grows with video length: at 150 frames DepthSync leads DepthCrafter by 2.8% in δ₁ on ScanNet, while at 450 frames the gap widens to 5.3%. For geometry consistency, DepthSync achieves the lowest MFC (0.041) and the best pose-estimation metrics (ATE 0.350 m, RPE trans 0.021 m, RPE rot 0.669° at 450 frames), surpassing dedicated pose methods like RelPose++ and PoseDiffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthSync outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and video length tested. Against the closest competitor DepthCrafter (the framework it builds upon), DepthSync improves δ₁ by 5.3% on ScanNet (0.769 vs 0.716) and by 12.8% on Bonn (0.929 vs 0.801) at the longest video lengths, with the gap widening as more sliding windows are required. Against ChronoDepth, the improvement is even larger: 6.9% in δ₁ on ScanNet and 7.6% on Bonn at 590 frames. Single-frame methods (DepthAnythingV2, Marigold) remain competitive on short videos but degrade more sharply on longer sequences due to the absence of temporal modeling. On geometry consistency (MFC), DepthSync achieves 0.041 at 450 frames, matching the best reported value while simultaneously improving depth accuracy. For derived pose quality (Table 2), DepthSync's depth-derived poses beat dedicated pose-estimation methods RelPose++ (ATE 0.350 vs 1.00 m), PoseDiffusion (0.350 vs 1.01 m), and RayDiffusion (0.350 vs 0.942 m) at 450 frames, and also outperform DepthCrafter-derived poses (0.350 vs 0.367 m ATE).

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthSync builds directly on DepthCrafter, a diffusion-based video depth estimator that uses Stable Video Diffusion as a 2D prior, and on the Universal Guidance framework for controlling diffusion sampling. The key conceptual departure is from prior initialization-based consistency strategies (DepthCrafter's diffusion inversion, ChronoDepth's noise-level initialization), which inject scale information only at the start of denoising and rely on cross-attention to propagate it. DepthSync instead applies scale and geometry constraints at every denoising step via forward and backward guidance, treating consistency as a continuous regularization rather than a one-time initialization. The geometry guidance additionally introduces explicit 3D constraints (PnP-based reprojection, tracking, surface normals) that are absent from purely 2D diffusion priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that initialization-based scale injection is fundamentally insufficient for long videos—because scale information is progressively lost during denoising—provides a useful diagnostic for the broader community of sliding-window diffusion video methods. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the guidance means it can be applied to any diffusion-based depth estimator without retraining, making it practically accessible. Limitations include dependence on off-the-shelf tracking (CoTracker3) and normal prediction (StableNormal) networks, the continued need for sliding windows (not truly end-to-end for arbitrarily long videos), and the computational overhead of guidance at each denoising step. Open questions include whether similar guidance strategies can address other consistency failures in diffusion-based video generation tasks beyond depth.
