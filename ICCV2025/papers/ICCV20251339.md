## Problem

MLLMs produce vague region descriptions with inaccurate object localizations because existing instruction-tuning (SFT) only optimizes toward positive samples without penalizing inaccurate negative localizations during inference. Unlike traditional object detection, which relies on positive-negative sample training, current MLLM training lacks direct preference feedback for fine-grained spatial understanding, causing models to be distracted by objects outside the queried region and fail to ground objects accurately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251339/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images with object bounding box annotations (from Objects365) and constructs random multi-object region queries by iteratively expanding from a seed object to include 3–5 nearby objects. The MLLM is prompted with the full image, a cropped region image, and text-based object references to generate grounded region descriptions containing natural-language text interleaved with bounding box coordinates for each mentioned object. The scoring stage uses CLIP embeddings (with a modified local-attention vision encoder) and Grounding DINO box predictions to evaluate each description.

## Method

SPR is a three-step DPO pipeline that requires no human annotation or external MLLMs. First, diverse grounded region descriptions are generated by prompting the MLLM with multiple prompt variants (full image, cropped region, object references) for each random multi-object region. Second, each description is scored by a weighted combination (λ=0.8) of a semantic score (CLIP cosine similarity between the region and description text, averaged with a local-attention full-image similarity) and a localization score (mean IoU between predicted object boxes from Grounding DINO and ground-truth annotations). Third, the highest- and lowest-scored descriptions are paired as preferred/rejected data; the preferred description's bounding boxes are refined by replacing them with matched ground-truth boxes (IoU > 0.5), and the MLLM is fine-tuned via DPO with LoRA. The key insight is that contrasting accurate and inaccurate localizations in preference data mimics the positive-negative sample mechanism in object detection, enabling the model to distinguish precise from imprecise grounding.

## Results

On the Referring Expression Comprehension task (Acc@0.5), SPR consistently improves all three baseline MLLMs: Ferret-7B gains +0.90 on RefCOCO val (87.49→88.39) and +1.46 on RefCOCO testB (82.45→83.91); Ferret-13B gains +0.46 on RefCOCO val (89.48→89.94) and +0.76 on RefCOCO testB (84.36→85.12); CogVLM-Grounding-17B gains +0.19 on RefCOCO val (92.76→92.95). The gains are most pronounced at higher IoU thresholds: at IoU=0.9, Ferret-7B improves by +8.96 (43.25→52.21) and Ferret-13B by +4.06 (49.55→53.61) on average REC. On Phrase Grounding (Recall@1, Flickr30k), Ferret-7B improves from 81.30 to 82.44, and at IoU=0.9 the gain reaches +4.01 (29.98→33.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPR is applied as a plug-in on top of three existing MLLMs (Ferret-7B/13B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, CogVLM-Grounding-17B) and consistently outperforms each of their unmodified versions across all REC and Phrase Grounding splits. Against traditional grounding methods (UNITER, UniTAB, MDETR) and earlier MLLM-based approaches (Shikra, Griffon, LLaVA-Grounding), SPR-enhanced Ferret-13B achieves 89.94/83.29/86.46 on RefCOCO/+/g val, surpassing Griffon-13B (89.4/83.3/85.1) and Shikra-13B (87.83/82.89/82.64). The most striking comparison is at higher IoU thresholds: at IoU=0.9, SPR-enhanced Ferret-7B reaches 52.21 average REC accuracy versus 43.25 for the baseline, a +8.96 gain that no other method in the table reports. On Phrase Grounding, SPR-enhanced Ferret-7B (82.44 Recall@1) approaches the 13B baseline (82.94), effectively closing the model-size gap. The paper also shows that DPO-based SPR outperforms SFT on the same preference data (84.93 vs. 84.35 REC, 82.44 vs. 81.72 Phrase Grounding for Ferret-7B), confirming that the preference-contrastive signal is more effective than positive-only fine-tuning for spatial localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of MLLM research: spatial understanding via instruction fine-tuning (Shikra, Ferret, RegionGPT, Groma, Griffon) and preference optimization for alignment (LLaVA-RLHF, RLHF-V, CLIP-DPO, mDPO). Prior spatial work injects grounding knowledge through SFT on pre-annotated data but provides no negative-sample supervision on the model's actual outputs, while prior preference optimization targets hallucination reduction or image-level alignment rather than fine-grained localization. SPR departs from both by applying DPO specifically to grounded region descriptions, using an automated scoring pipeline (CLIP semantic score + Grounding DINO localization score) to construct preference pairs without human annotation or external MLLMs, and by refining the preferred descriptions' bounding boxes to widen the quality gap between positive and negative samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: MLLMs trained purely with SFT lack the positive-negative contrastive signal that traditional object detection relies on for precise localization, and SPR shows that DPO with automatically constructed spatial preference data can close part of this gap with minimal training overhead (one A100, ~3–5 hours). The finding that gains grow substantially at higher IoU thresholds (up to ~9 points at IoU 0.9) suggests the method specifically improves localization precision rather than mere object recall. Limitations include evaluation on only three MLLMs, preference data drawn from a single source (Objects365), and the authors' own note that SFT and DPO are complementary rather than one replacing the other, leaving open how to jointly optimize both stages. Whether the approach transfers to video, 3D, or denser detection settings remains unexplored.
