## Problem

Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks are either designed for large-scale pretrained models (too complex for compact, developmentally plausible models) or are too simplistic and out-of-domain for infant-inspired training data. Additionally, training exclusively on the SAYCam dataset overlooks the broader, diverse multimodal input streams from which infants naturally learn, limiting the generalizability of resulting models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes baby-egocentric RGB images (sampled from SAYCam video) paired with short child-directed utterances (typically two-word phrases or single category labels). Evaluation tasks require the model to select the correct image from four candidates given a label, match an image to the correct two-word phrase among a positive/negative pair, resolve compositional image-phrase correspondences (Baby Winoground), or generate a full child-directed caption scored by METEOR. Training data consists of 67K filtered SAYCam image-utterance pairs (CLIP similarity > 0.2) augmented with a synthetic dataset of equal size drawn from CC3M, LAION, and SBU.

## Method

BabyVLM introduces a two-step synthetic data pipeline: GPT-4o rewrites general-domain image captions into concise, child-directed utterances (emulating speech used with two-year-olds) while flagging misaligned pairs, and the Hungarian algorithm with CLIP similarity as distance metric selects a visually aligned subset matching the SAYCam domain. The baseline model, BabyLLaVA, pairs a 7M-parameter GPT-2 language model with a 23M-parameter ResNeXt-50 vision encoder via a lightweight MLP connector (with a larger Llama-1.1B + ViT-L variant for capacity ablation), trained from scratch exclusively on the developmentally curated data. Three novel in-domain benchmarks—Visual Two-Word Test (967 pairs testing compositional semantic reasoning), Baby Winoground (365 samples with Stable Diffusion-generated negative images), and SAYCam Caption (294 samples for generative captioning)—are introduced alongside the existing Labeled-S classification task to provide comprehensive, developmentally aligned evaluation.

## Results

On the four in-domain benchmarks, upper-bound models substantially outperform baby models: CLIP-large achieves 0.7100 on Labeled-S, 0.8625 on VTWT, and 0.6740 on Baby Winoground (overall group score), while LLaVA-v1.5-7B reaches 0.7400, 0.7851, and 0.4274 respectively. Among baby models, the contrastive CVCL model leads on Labeled-S (0.6086) and Baby Winoground (0.0932 overall), while BabyLLaVA-GPT2 achieves 0.4195 and 0.0658. On SAYCam Caption (METEOR), all models score below 0.18, with LLaVA-v1.5-7B-ft at 0.1798 and BabyLLaVA-GPT2 at 0.1379, indicating generative captioning remains the hardest task. Baby Winoground reveals a stark asymmetry: baby models score above chance (0.1667) on positive context but near or below chance on negative context (e.g., BabyLLaVA-GPT2: 0.3890 vs. 0.2301).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the upper-bound LLaVA-v1.5-7B, both baby models trail by wide margins: CVCL scores 0.6086 vs. 0.7400 on Labeled-S and 0.0932 vs. 0.4274 on Baby Winoground, while BabyLLaVA-GPT2 lags further at 0.4195 and 0.0658. CLIP-large is an even stronger upper bound, reaching 0.8625 on VTWT and 0.6740 on Baby Winoground, far exceeding any baby model. Among the baby models themselves, the contrastive CVCL consistently outperforms the generative BabyLLaVA on Labeled-S (0.6086 vs. 0.4195) and Baby Winoground (0.0932 vs. 0.0658), though BabyLLaVA slightly edges CVCL on VTWT (0.6252 vs. 0.6494 is actually CVCL winning; BabyLLaVA-GPT2 is 0.6252 vs. CVCL 0.6494, so CVCL wins here too). The larger BabyLLaVA-Llama variant (~50× more parameters) performs similarly or worse than BabyLLaVA-GPT2, suggesting overfitting on the limited developmental data. The key takeaway is that the performance gap between baby and upper-bound models is driven by both model capacity and data alignment, not capacity alone, as evidenced by CVCL's relatively stronger Labeled-S score despite its small size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (vision encoder + MLP connector + LLM) and the SAYCam egocentric infant dataset, extending the CVCL contrastive model of Vong et al. [54] into a generative setting. It departs from prior developmentally-inspired work (Labeled-S, DevBench, ModelVsBaby, MEWL) by simultaneously satisfying three criteria—task diversity, developmental alignment, and in-domain evaluation—that no existing benchmark achieves together. The synthetic data pipeline, which uses GPT-4o to rewrite general-domain captions (CC3M, LAION, SBU) into child-directed utterances and then applies Hungarian matching with CLIP similarity to maintain visual alignment with SAYCam, is a novel data curation strategy not present in prior infant-learning research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BabyVLM provides a principled, reproducible framework for studying data-efficient multimodal learning under realistic developmental constraints, lowering the barrier for independent researchers who cannot afford large-scale pretraining. The finding that a 50× larger model (Llama-1.1B + ViT-L) performs no better than the compact GPT-2 variant highlights a practical ceiling on model capacity when data is severely limited, and the Baby Winoground asymmetry (above-chance from in-distribution context, below-chance from out-of-distribution context) exposes a systematic generalization failure mode in small VLMs. Open questions include whether the child-directed transformation strategy transfers to other specialized domains, whether hybrid generative-contrastive training can close the gap on compositional reasoning, and how to enrich the linguistic variety to improve generative captioning beyond the modest METEOR scores observed.
