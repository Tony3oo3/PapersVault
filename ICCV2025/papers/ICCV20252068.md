## Problem

In vision-language tracking, the initial template patch and language description only align with the target's initial state, yet in complex long-term scenarios the target's appearance and surrounding context change dynamically, making these static cues intractable for sustained guidance. Additionally, existing methods that attempt to distinguish target words from context words via vision-text similarity achieve only ~30% classification accuracy, causing context words to mislead the tracker.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a 128×128 template patch (dynamically updated per STARK strategy), a natural language sentence, and a 256×256 search image at each frame. The text is tokenized and encoded by RoBERTa-Base, while the visual inputs (search image, initial template, and dynamic template) are processed through a one-stream HiViT encoder with a learnable [CLS] token. The output is a single target bounding box predicted by a CNN-based head, with the model operating at 35 FPS (Base) or 30 FPS (Large) on an RTX-3090.

## Method

ATCTrack introduces two complementary target-context guidance modules that produce cues aligned with the current target state. For the visual modality, it constructs a target-context spatial distribution heatmap via search-template feature similarity, embeds it into the [CLS] token through cross-attention with historical memory units (stored in a sliding window of length 4), and injects the resulting memory into search features via parameter-free attention. For the textual modality, a lightweight MLP on RoBERTa features identifies target words (trained with LLM-annotated labels, reaching 96.7% accuracy), and a context words calibration mechanism concatenates the target-word features with visual memory, applies transformer cross-attention to enhance target representation, then uses the enhanced features to modulate the full text adaptively. The final search features integrate both calibrated text (concatenated with the original text as dual-type features) and visual memory cues before being passed to the prediction head, trained end-to-end with focal, L1, GIoU, and binary cross-entropy losses.

## Results

ATCTrack-B achieves 73.7 AUC / 70.1 P on MGIT, 67.5 AUC / 73.6 P on TNL2K, 74.6 AUC / 82.1 P on LaSOT, and 54.6 AUC / 62.8 P on LaSOT_ext. The larger ATCTrack-L variant reaches 74.0 AUC / 76.1 P on MGIT and 68.6 AUC / 75.0 P on TNL2K. The paper reports that ATCTrack-B improves over the previous best results by 6.4%, 4.3%, and 3.5% in precision on MGIT, TNL2K, and LaSOT_ext respectively. Tracking speed is 35 FPS (B) and 30 FPS (L) on an RTX-3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MGIT (the most challenging long-term benchmark), ATCTrack-B surpasses the previous SOTA MemVLT [25] by 4.3 AUC (73.7 vs. 69.4) and 6.4 P (70.1 vs. 63.7), and also outperforms CTVLT [24] by 4.5 AUC. On TNL2K, ATCTrack-L leads all baselines with 68.6 AUC, exceeding SUTrack-L384 [14] (67.9) by 0.7 and ChatTracker-L [67] (65.4) by 3.2. On LaSOT, ATCTrack-L (74.7 AUC / 82.3 P) is marginally below SUTrack-L384 (75.2 AUC / 83.2 P), which the authors attribute to SUTrack's use of larger image resolution (384×384) and additional training data. On LaSOT_ext, ATCTrack-L achieves the best AUC (55.4) and P (64.0), beating SUTrack-L224 [14] by 1.4 AUC and 2.3 P. Overall, ATCTrack sets new SOTA on 3 of 4 benchmarks and is competitive on the fourth, with the largest gains on the complex long-term MGIT benchmark where target-context alignment is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATCTrack extends the vision-language tracking line that began with SNLT and All-in-One, which treat initial template and text prompts as static references, and builds on the one-stream tracking paradigm (STARK, HiViT) and dynamic template updates. It departs most directly from MemVLT (implicit prompt-based modulation of static cues) and QueryNLT/TTC-Track (vision-text similarity for target/context word distinction) by explicitly modeling target-context features aligned with dynamic target states. The key conceptual departure is replacing vision-text similarity-based word classification with a text-only MLP (96.7% accuracy vs. 29.9%), and replacing sparse RoI-based temporal memory with an explicit target-context distribution heatmap embedded in a sliding-window memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental practical limitation in VLT: static initial prompts become misaligned with dynamic target states in long-term sequences, and context words in text descriptions can actively mislead the tracker. The finding that a lightweight text-only classifier outperforms complex multimodal alignment for target word identification by a wide margin (96.7% vs. 29.9%) suggests that prior methods over-engineered this sub-problem. Open questions include whether the LLM-based annotation pipeline generalizes to new domains without re-annotation, whether the temporal memory mechanism scales to very long sequences beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the explicit heatmap construction is necessary or whether learned alternatives could achieve similar alignment.
