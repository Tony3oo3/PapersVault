## Problem

Existing video-based deepfake detectors rely on a single binary classification branch that overfits to the specific manipulation types seen during training, resulting in poor generalization to unseen generation methods. Moreover, as generative AI produces increasingly high-quality deepfakes with subtle spatio-temporal artifacts, vanilla architectures lack the explicit attention mechanisms needed to capture these fine-grained inconsistencies. While vulnerability-driven multi-task learning has proven effective for image-level detection, its extension to the video domain is non-trivial because temporal artifacts are inherently different from spatial ones and require dedicated characterization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips of T consecutive frames (default T=4, tested up to 16) with facial regions cropped via Face-RetinaNet and resized to 224×224, along with 81 facial landmarks per frame extracted using Dlib. It produces three outputs: a binary real/fake label from the classification head, a per-patch temporal vulnerability map (regressed derivatives of the blending boundary over time) from the temporal head, and per-frame spatial vulnerability soft labels (forgery intensity scores) from the spatial head. Training uses only real videos from FF++ with pseudo-fakes generated on-the-fly via the SBV synthesis pipeline, requiring no real fake data.

## Method

FakeSTormer is a multi-task framework built on a revisited TimeSformer backbone where, instead of a single global class token, per-frame spatial tokens and per-frame temporal tokens are appended and interact only with patches along their respective dimension axis via decomposed self-attention, explicitly decoupling spatial and temporal feature learning. The temporal branch h applies two 3D convolution blocks (3×1×1 kernels) on patch embeddings to regress standardized temporal derivatives of the blending boundary, localizing high-change vulnerable patches over time. The spatial branch g uses an MLP on spatial tokens to predict per-frame soft labels encoding forgery intensity. To generate hand-free ground truths and avoid overfitting, the paper introduces Self-Blended Video (SBV), which extends Self-Blended Image with a Consistent Synthesized Parameters module and a Landmark Interpolation module to preserve temporal coherence, coupled with a vulnerability-driven Cutout augmentation that masks the most artifact-prone patches consistently across all frames. The overall loss balances three terms (classification BCE, temporal MSE, spatial BCE) with tunable weights, and the backbone is initialized from pretrained MAE weights.

## Results

On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark (Table 1), FakeSTormer (T=4) achieves 92.4% AUC on CelebDFv2, 98.5% on DeepfakeDetection, 90.0% on DFDCP, 74.6% on DFDC, 74.2% on WildDeepfake, and 96.9% on DiffSwap, all while training exclusively on real FaceForensics++ videos. The most dramatic gains are on DFDCP (90.0% vs. 80.8% for the previous best LFGDIN) and DiffSwap (96.9% vs. 85.7% for LFGDIN). On the data-synthesis comparison (Table 2), FakeSTormer reaches 96.5% on CDF, 94.1% on DFDCP, and 97.7% on DiffSwap, surpassing all prior synthesis-based methods including STC-Pretrain (95.8/89.4/–) and LAA-Net (95.4/86.9/92.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeSTormer outperforms all 29 compared baselines on the majority of test datasets. The largest margins are on DFDCP (90.0% vs. 80.8% for LFGDIN, 76.8% for Swin+TALL, 74.2% for ISTVT) and DiffSwap (96.9% vs. 85.7% for LFGDIN), where the temporal vulnerability branch provides a decisive advantage. On CelebDFv2, FakeSTormer (92.4%) surpasses the previous best LSDA (91.1%) and SBI (90.6%) by 1.3 and 1.8 points respectively. On DeepfakeDetection, FakeSTormer (98.5%) ties with AltFreezing (98.5%) and edges out RECCE (98.2%) and StyleLatentFlows (96.1%). On the large-scale DFDC dataset, FakeSTormer (74.6%) leads FTCN (71.0%) and Xception (70.9%) by 3.6 and 3.7 points. Notably, FakeSTormer achieves these results while training only on real videos (no fake training data), whereas most baselines require both real and fake samples, making the comparison particularly favorable to the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vulnerability-driven multi-task learning paradigm from image-based deepfake detection (FakeFormer [41], LAA-Net [42]) to the video domain, adding a temporal vulnerability regression branch alongside the spatial one. It builds on TimeSformer [2] by introducing per-frame spatial and temporal classification tokens to decouple spatio-temporal feature learning, and on Self-Blended Image (SBI) [48] by extending blending-based data synthesis to video via a Consistent Synthesized Parameters module and a Landmark Interpolation module for temporal coherence. The key departure from prior video deepfake detection methods (FTCN, AltFreezing, ISTVT, etc.) is the rejection of single-branch binary classification in favor of explicit auxiliary supervision on artifact-prone regions, combined with training exclusively on real data augmented with synthesized pseudo-fakes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SBV data synthesis technique is explicitly generic and applicable to any existing video-level deepfake detector, making it a potentially widely adopted tool independent of the specific architecture. The finding that training solely on real data with SBV augmentation matches or exceeds methods trained on real+fake data addresses a practical concern about the availability and diversity of labeled deepfake training data. Open questions include whether the vulnerability concept generalizes beyond face-centric deepfakes to full-body or scene-level manipulations, how the approach scales to longer video sequences beyond 16 frames, and whether the modest gains from auxiliary branches (≈2–3 AUC points over SBV-only) justify the additional architectural complexity in deployment settings.
