## Problem

CLIP's spatial-invariant features and low operating resolution limit its open-vocabulary segmentation performance. While prior work addressed spatial invariance by modifying self-attention, the resolution constraint remains: the widely used Segment-then-Splice paradigm (sliding window → segment each sub-image → splice) exhibits non-monotonic performance degradation at higher source resolutions because each sub-image's receptive field shrinks relative to the full scene, preventing CLIP from classifying objects that span multiple windows.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (shorter side resized to 336–688 px depending on benchmark) and a set of text class names formatted with a standard ImageNet prompt. Internally, the image is split into 336×336 sub-images via a sliding window (stride 224, or 112 for VOC20). The primary output is a pixel-level segmentation mask produced by argmax over cosine similarity between aggregated dense features and text embeddings; a secondary output is a set of visual prompts (attention-modulated pixel values) that can be fed to LVLMs such as LLaVA-1.5-13B to improve multi-modal reasoning.

## Method

Trident introduces a Splice-then-Segment paradigm that inverts the conventional pipeline: instead of segmenting each sub-image independently and splicing masks, it first splices the CLIP+DINO feature maps of all sub-images into a single integral feature map, then applies a global correlation matrix derived from SAM's encoder to aggregate features across the entire image, effectively extending the receptive field beyond individual windows. The correlation matrix is an affinity matrix that combines SAM's last-layer attention weights with a cosine-similarity-based mask (thresholded at the mean similarity) to suppress background attention while preserving object-level semantic correlations. DINO provides spatially covariant, object-level correlation during per-sub-image feature extraction, complementing CLIP's semantic representation. A final SAM refinement step converts the coarse segmentation into point, box, and mask prompts (with a small scaling coefficient α=0.005 on the mask) that are fed to SAM's prompt encoder and mask decoder per class, yielding significantly cleaner boundaries than conventional post-processing such as PAMR or CRF.

## Results

Trident (ViT-B/16) achieves an average mIoU of 45.8% across eight segmentation benchmarks, surpassing the previous training-free SOTA (CASS, 44.4%) and the prior best method ProxyCLIP (42.3%) by 1.4% and 3.5% respectively. With the larger OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 backbone, Trident reaches 48.6% average mIoU, improving over ProxyCLIP-H/14 (44.4%) by 4.2%. Notably, Trident also outperforms the strongest training-based method CLIP-DINOiser (40.3%) by 5.5% average mIoU, while SAM-CLIP retains a narrow edge only on COCO Stuff (31.5% vs. 28.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, Trident (ViT-B/16) outperforms all competitors: it beats CASS (44.4%) by 1.4%, NACLIP (42.5%) by 3.3%, ProxyCLIP (42.3%) by 3.5%, SCLIP (40.1%) by 5.7%, and OVDiff (37.8%) by 8.0% in average mIoU. Against training-based methods, Trident surpasses CLIP-DINOiser (40.3%) by 5.5% and TCL (33.9%) by 11.9%, though SAM-CLIP retains a 3.2% edge on COCO Stuff (31.5% vs. 28.3%) at the cost of extensive multi-dataset training that degrades its open-vocabulary generalization on other benchmarks. With the ViT-H/14 backbone, Trident extends its lead over ProxyCLIP from 3.5% to 4.2% average mIoU, with particularly large gains on Cityscapes (+5.6%) and ADE20K (+2.5%). In the LVLM application (Table 7), Trident's visual prompts improve LLaVA-1.5-13B over both the unmodified model and the API baseline on 4 of 5 benchmarks, with the largest gain on LLaVA-Wild (+6.5 over LLaVA, +4.1 over LLaVA+API).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free open-vocabulary segmentation line initiated by MaskCLIP (which replaced CLIP's QK attention with convolution) and advanced by ProxyCLIP (which substituted DINO-based cosine similarity for that attention). The key conceptual departure is inverting the standard Segment-then-Slice sliding-window pipeline into a Splice-then-Segment paradigm: rather than segmenting each sub-image independently and stitching masks, Trident first splices CLIP and DINO feature maps across all sub-images, then applies a global correlation matrix derived from SAM's encoder to aggregate features before segmentation. This positions Trident as a harmonization of three foundation models (CLIP for semantics, DINO for spatially covariant sub-image correlation, SAM for global receptive-field extension and refinement), rather than a modification of any single model's architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Splice-then-Segment paradigm directly addresses a practical bottleneck—CLIP's fixed low-resolution input—that has constrained all prior training-free segmentation methods, and the non-monotonic resolution-performance relationship (Table 1) provides a clear diagnostic for why existing sliding-window approaches fail at scale. The training-free nature and the SAM refinement strategy make the method immediately deployable without additional training data or compute, and the demonstrated utility for LVLM visual prompt generation broadens its relevance beyond segmentation. Open questions include whether the three-model dependency (CLIP + DINO + SAM) can be reduced, whether the heuristic threshold ε in the affinity matrix generalizes across domains, and whether the per-class SAM refinement scales efficiently to very large category sets like ADE20K's 150 classes.
