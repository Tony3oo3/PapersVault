## Problem

Existing long-term human-scene interaction methods either produce physically implausible motions (kinematics-based) or, when physics-based, are restricted to a narrow set of skills with simplistic planning that ignores emotional and stylistic expression. No prior framework simultaneously achieves physical plausibility, diverse style control, automatic long-term script generation, and scalability to new skills within complex 3D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252094/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text story theme (e.g., 'a boy breaks up with girlfriend, goes back home and gets drunk') and a 3D furnished scene layout from 3DFront as input. It produces two outputs: (1) a structured long-term script consisting of skill tuples (e.g., walk→sit→getup→walk→lie) with per-keyframe captions, target objects, and emotion/style labels, and (2) a physically simulated motion sequence in Isaac Gym where the character executes those keyframes with style-consistent body motions, obstacle avoidance, and correct furniture contacts. The heightmap observation is a 12×12 grid at 0.15 m spacing, and goal conditions are sparse (2D/3D target points or object bounding boxes) rather than dense per-frame references.

## Method

SIMS is a hierarchical framework with three components: (1) a Retrieval-Augmented Script Generation (RASG) planner that builds a short-script database, retrieves top-k scripts per style via CLIP cosine similarity to the user theme, and prompts GPT-4 to concatenate retrieved summaries into a coherent long-term keyframe sequence; (2) seven goal-conditioned RL policies (Walk, Idle, Sit, Lie, Reach, GetUp, Carry) trained with a text-conditioned AMP-style discriminator, where each policy observes proprioception, an egocentric heightmap, a sparse task goal, and a CLIP language embedding for style; and (3) a finite state machine that parses keyframes into skill activations and switches between policies based on root-position overlap thresholds. The language condition is learned via a MotionCLIP module that aligns a motion encoder with the frozen CLIP text encoder, enabling continuous style control without mode collapse. Skills are grouped into three task templates (Loco, HSI, DOI) sharing observation and reward structures, so new skills can be added by training a single policy without retraining the full controller.

## Results

On physical interaction performance (Table 3), SIMS achieves 98.1% success rate on Sit, 87.6% on Lie, 95.2% on Reach, and 92.9% on Carry, with contact errors of 0.028 m (Sit), 0.049 m (Lie), 0.026 m (Reach), and 0.099 m (Carry). On motion diversity (Table 4), SIMS achieves FID of 125.66 (Sit) and 171.24 (Lie) versus UniHSI's 153.84 and 211.22, while APD jumps from ~1.1–1.4 (UniHSI) to ~16.4–16.6 (SIMS), indicating dramatically greater stylistic variation. In user studies, SIMS outperforms UniHSI on all four subjective metrics (Physical Realism 3.4 vs 2.6, Motion Diversity 3.6 vs 2.9, Plot Engagement 3.0 vs 2.4, Emotional Resonance 3.8 vs 3.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniHSI [50], SIMS wins on Sit (98.1% vs 94.3%) and Lie (87.6% vs 81.5%) success rates, but trails on Reach (95.2% vs 97.5%); on diversity, SIMS's APD of 16.55 (Sit) dwarfs UniHSI's 1.14, revealing near-identical motions from UniHSI. Against InterPhys [13], SIMS improves Sit success by 4.4 points (98.1 vs 93.7) and Lie by 7.6 points (87.6 vs 80.0), with contact error on Lie dropping from 0.30 m to 0.049 m, though InterPhys retains a slight edge on Carry success (94.3 vs 92.9) and contact error (0.08 vs 0.099 m). InterScene [26] reports only Sit (97.8%), which SIMS marginally exceeds (98.1%). The five kinematics-based baselines (NSM, SAMP, Humanise, AffordMotion, TeSMo) lack physical plausibility and style diversity entirely, as summarized in Table 1, where SIMS is the only method checking all six capability columns (physical plausibility, automatic planning, style diversity, text-aware, scene-aware, skill scalability). In user studies (Tables 5–6), SIMS leads all physics-based baselines on stylistic diversity across Walk, Sit, Lie, and Carry, while InterPhys edges out SIMS by 0.1 on Sit physical realism (4.1 vs 4.0) but trails on every other skill.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMS extends the physics-based HSI line initiated by InterScene (Pan et al., 3DV 2024) by adding per-skill text embeddings and egocentric heightmaps to its FSM-driven controller, and builds on AMP's adversarial motion prior (Peng et al., TOG 2021) by replacing one-hot or motion-reference conditions with CLIP-based language embeddings for style control. The key conceptual departure is repurposing Retrieval-Augmented Generation from NLP as a high-level motion planner: a short-script database is retrieved via CLIP similarity and then expanded by an LLM into long-term keyframe sequences, replacing the simpler chronological or contact-only planning used by InterScene and UniHSI. This positions SIMS as the first framework to unify automatic long-term planning, style diversity, and physical plausibility in a single pipeline, whereas prior physics-based methods (InterPhys, InterScene, UniHSI) either lack style control or require accurate reference motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a real gap: prior physics-based HSI methods produce physically plausible but stylistically uniform motions, while kinematics-based methods offer diversity at the cost of penetration and foot-skating artifacts. The ViconStyle dataset and the restructured multi-dataset mixture with emotional/style annotations provide a resource the field has lacked for stylized motion research. Open questions include scalability beyond the current 7 skills and single-character indoor setting, whether the RAG-based planner generalizes to multi-agent or outdoor scenarios, and whether the CLIP-based style conditioning can capture finer-grained or compound emotional states. The paper also notes the need for articulated-finger models and more diverse motion capture to push toward truly expressive characters.
