## Problem

Existing synthetic flare and glare datasets rely on non-physical synthesis (e.g., Adobe After Effects), producing oversaturated colors, shape inaccuracies, and unnatural streak edges that fail to capture the diversity of real-world nighttime artifacts. Consequently, deep learning removal methods trained on these datasets leave residual artifacts in complex scenes with large-area glares, diverse flare patterns, and multiple or off-screen-induced streaks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252004/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a 512×512 RGB crop where a random background is composited with a physically rendered flare/glare overlay from the PBFG dataset, and produces a restored image with artifacts removed. A post-hoc histogram-matching module aligns the output's per-channel probability density function with the ground truth to correct color distribution shifts that occur when glare dominates most of the scene.

## Method

FGRNet is built on the Uformer backbone with a four-stage encoder-decoder [1,2,2,2] configuration, where each stage's standard FFN is replaced by a Spatial-Frequency Enhanced Module (SFEM). The SFEM splits the feature map along channels and processes two halves in parallel: a Spatial Reconstruction Unit (SRU) that uses group normalization and sigmoid thresholding to separate spatially informative from redundant features and cross-reconstructs them, and a Frequency-Enhanced Unit (FEU) that applies a 2D real FFT to capture global spectral context, processes the frequency-domain features with 1×1 convolutions (equivalent to point-wise spatial multiplication), and maps back via inverse FFT with a residual connection. The two branches are fused by summation, balancing local detail preservation (SRU) with global artifact suppression (FEU). Training uses a composite loss of L1, VGG perceptual, reconstruction, and negative-SSIM terms, and the PBFG dataset itself is generated via Fraunhofer diffraction integration for glare (starburst, glow, shimmer, streaks) and physically-based ray tracing for flare patterns across 15 optical system configurations.

## Results

On real-world nighttime images, FGRNet+HM trained on the PBFG dataset achieves 30.37 dB PSNR, 0.927 SSIM, and 0.0403 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (Sparse-UFormer at 28.08 dB PSNR) by 2.29 dB in overall PSNR. In glare regions (G-PSNR), the gain reaches 3.14 dB (27.55 vs. 24.41), and in streak regions (S-PSNR) the gain is 2.09 dB (25.71 vs. 23.61). The same FGRNet architecture trained on Flare7K or Flare7K++ also surpasses all baselines, confirming that the PBFG dataset itself provides a significant advantage in physical realism and generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGRNet+HM (trained on PBFG) decisively outperforms all nine baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, Sparse-UFormer [35], trails by 2.29 dB in PSNR, 0.029 in SSIM, and 3.14 dB in G-PSNR. Among frequency-domain methods, FF-Former [39] (27.98 dB PSNR) and Qu et al. [21] (28.02 dB) are competitive but still lag by over 2.3 dB. The most directly comparable baseline, Flare7K++ [6] (27.92 dB), is surpassed by 2.45 dB in PSNR and 3.22 dB in G-PSNR. Notably, even FGRNet without the HM module (28.66 dB) already exceeds all baselines, indicating that the SFEM architecture and PBFG dataset jointly drive the gains, while the HM module adds a further ~1.7 dB in PSNR and ~2.1 dB in G-PSNR for color-distribution consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

FGRNet is built on the Uformer encoder-decoder backbone, replacing its standard FFN with a Spatial-Frequency Enhanced Module that combines a Spatial Reconstruction Unit (local detail) and a Frequency-Enhanced Unit (global context via 2D real FFT). The dataset generation departs from prior phenomenological synthesis (Flare7K, Flare7K++) by employing physically-based Fraunhofer diffraction and Fresnel ray tracing, drawing on Hullin et al.'s real-time lens flare rendering framework. The streak synthesis extends Lee et al.'s curved-edge decomposition with a double-sided arc model, and the overall approach follows the recent trend of incorporating frequency-domain representations into image restoration networks (FF-Former, frequency-compensuted diffusion models).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PBFG dataset addresses a genuine gap: prior synthetic flare/glare datasets relied on non-physical compositing that produced oversaturated colors and unrealistic streak edges, limiting model generalization to real nighttime scenes. By grounding synthesis in diffraction physics and Fresnel reflections, the dataset offers 120 flare and 168 glare templates with comprehensive annotations (flare, light source, streak, glow, shimmer, starburst), providing a more faithful training signal. Open questions include whether the physically-based rendering captures the full diversity of real-world lens imperfections (e.g., manufacturing defects, aging), whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame settings, and whether the HM post-processing module's reliance on ground-truth histograms limits deployment in production pipelines where clean references are unavailable.
