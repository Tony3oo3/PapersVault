## Problem

Current reasoning segmentation methods rely on multimodal LLMs to handle both visual perception and multi-step reasoning simultaneously, which causes them to lose fine-grained spatial and temporal details, require fragile LLM fine-tuning that is costly to maintain as models evolve, and scale poorly to online video processing. This paper addresses the gap between high-level implicit query understanding and low-level pixel-accurate segmentation in dynamic video streams without any model fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames in an online streaming fashion (no full-video access) alongside a single implicit text query (e.g., 'segment objects that moved behind the dining table after the person sat down'). It produces per-frame binary segmentation masks at the original video resolution, with temporal smoothing applied across frames. An intermediate scene-graph digital twin is maintained with a sliding window of w=6 frames by default, adaptively adjusted based on the temporal reasoning demands identified during planning.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages orchestrated by LLM agents. In the planning stage, an LLM planner (gpt-4o-mini) parses the implicit query to select a minimal subset of specialist vision models (SAM-2, DepthAnything-2, OWLv2, DINOv2) and constructs a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of atomic reasoning operations. In the execution stage, the selected models process each incoming frame in parallel to build and update a scene-graph digital twin whose nodes carry visual, spatial (depth/3D position), and temporal (trajectory) attributes, while edges encode pairwise relationships. A base LLM handles semantic reasoning nodes by formatting the scene graph into natural-language context, while a separate LLM-coder (gpt-4o) generates and executes concrete code for spatial/temporal operations (e.g., depth-based 'behind' checks). The entire pipeline is zero-shot and fine-tuning-free, with the 'just-in-time' aspect meaning only query-relevant perception models are activated rather than running all available models on every frame.

## Results

On the proposed JITBench video RS benchmark (200 videos, 895 queries), the method achieves region similarity (J) of 0.839, 0.761, and 0.705 (averaged across L1–L3) for semantic, spatial, and temporal reasoning respectively, and contour accuracy (F) of 0.799, 0.814, and 0.771. The closest competitor, VISA, reaches only J=0.494/0.468/0.269 on the same categories, with the gap most pronounced in temporal reasoning (+0.436 J). On the ReVOS benchmark, the method achieves overall J=0.748 and F=0.773, surpassing VISA (J=0.488, F=0.529) by a wide margin. On the image-based ReasonSeg benchmark, the method attains gIoU of 64.2 (short) and 69.5 (long), outperforming LISA-13B (55.4/63.2) and VISA (45.3/45.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across every reasoning category and difficulty level on both JITBench and ReVOS. Against VISA [29], the strongest video-based competitor, the gap is largest in temporal reasoning (J: 0.705 vs. 0.269, a +0.436 improvement) and spatial reasoning (J: 0.761 vs. 0.468), while the semantic gap is more moderate (J: 0.839 vs. 0.494). Image-based methods adapted frame-by-frame (LISA-7B/13B, GSVA, LLM-Seg, V*) all show severe degradation on spatial and temporal queries, with V* [26] performing worst overall (J ≈ 0.143/0.085/0.066) and LLM-Seg [25] also struggling (J ≈ 0.273/0.223/0.141). On ReVOS [29], the method achieves overall J=0.748 vs. VISA's 0.488 and TrackGPT-13B's 0.432, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond the authors' own benchmark. On the image RS benchmark ReasonSeg [12], the method leads on both short and long queries (gIoU 64.2/69.5 vs. LISA-13B's 55.4/63.2), indicating the digital twin representation benefits even single-frame segmentation. Notably, the method's performance degrades by less than 10% from L1 to L3 across all categories, whereas baselines like LISA-13B drop from J=0.669 to 0.301 in semantic reasoning, highlighting the robustness of the decoupled perception-reasoning design to increasing reasoning chain complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RS paradigm (LISA, GSVA, LLM-Seg, VISA) that fine-tunes multimodal LLMs to jointly perform perception and reasoning, instead decoupling the two via an agent framework where specialist vision models (SAM-2, DepthAnything-2, OWLv2, DINOv2) build a structured scene-graph 'digital twin' and an LLM reasons over that representation. The 'just-in-time' concept repurposes the industrial digital-twin idea—selectively constructing only the scene information a query requires—rather than maintaining a full environment model. It extends VISA's offline video RS to online streaming processing and introduces multi-step reasoning across semantic, spatial, and temporal axes that prior RS methods (which handle at most single-step semantic queries) do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the fine-tuning maintenance burden and catastrophic-forgetting risk that plague production deployment of RS systems, and the new JITBench benchmark (200 videos, 895 queries across three reasoning categories and three difficulty levels) fills a gap in systematic evaluation of multi-step video RS. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer video sequences (the sliding window is only 6 frames by default), whether the LLM API costs (gpt-4o + gpt-4o-mini per query) are acceptable for real-time embodied AI, and how well the digital twin generalizes to object categories and spatial configurations unseen during planning. The large performance margins over baselines, while compelling, are measured primarily on the authors' own benchmark, leaving generalization to other video RS settings partially unverified.
