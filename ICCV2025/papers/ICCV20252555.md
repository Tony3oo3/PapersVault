## Problem

Existing event-based vision architectures treat optical flow estimation (temporal task) and stereo matching (spatial task) as separate problems with specialized pipelines, overlooking the structural correlation between them. This paper asks whether both can be reformulated as a single pixel-wise correspondence matching problem within a shared representation space, enabling a unified model that handles both tasks simultaneously without task-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252555/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes binocular asynchronous event streams, accumulated into event voxels with B=15 temporal bins at 480×640 resolution. For optical flow, two temporal segments (T and T+ΔT) serve as reference and target; for stereo, left and right viewpoint segments play the same role. The output is a dense 2D flow field or 1D disparity map, with the same network architecture and shared parameters producing either depending on which event segments are fed in.

## Method

EMatch reformulates both flow and disparity as pixel-wise correspondence matching: a Temporal Recurrent Network (TRN) splits each event voxel into K chronological groups and processes them through stacked ConvGRU layers to extract task-agnostic temporal features, while a Spatial Contextual Attention (SCA) module with six stacked self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN blocks fills in spatially sparse pixels via global context aggregation. Correspondence is established by computing dot-product similarity between reference and target features, normalizing via softmax into a matching distribution, and taking a weighted average of candidate coordinates to yield the displacement. An iterative refinement head (3 iterations) and a two-level feature pyramid for multi-scale optimization further sharpen the result. Training supports three strategies—single-task, cross-task transfer (initializing one task's shared modules from another), and multi-task unification (alternating flow and disparity supervision)—with the unified variant achieving the best performance on both DSEC and MVSEC.

## Results

On DSEC, EMatch (unified) achieves state-of-the-art optical flow estimation with EPE 0.64, 1PE 7.86%, 2PE 2.83%, and 3PE 1.69%, outperforming the best baseline IDNet (EPE 0.72, 1PE 10.07%) by 11.1% in EPE. For stereo matching on DSEC, EMatch (unified) achieves MAE 0.47 px, 1PE 8.34%, 2PE 2.17%, and RMSE 1.08, surpassing the best baseline TESNet (MAE 0.50, 1PE 9.20%). On MVSEC, EMatch with DSEC pre-training and indoor_flying3 augmentation achieves EPE 0.42 (indoor dt1) and 0.23 (outdoor dt1), and MAE 0.36 px on MVSEC stereo Split 1, confirming consistent superiority across both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMatch (unified) outperforms all 12 baselines on both tasks. For optical flow on DSEC, the closest competitor is IDNet (EPE 0.72, 1PE 10.07%), which EMatch surpasses by 0.08 in EPE and 2.21 points in 1PE; TMA (EPE 0.74) and BlinkFlow (EPE 0.76) trail further behind, while earlier methods like E-RAFT (EPE 0.79) and EV-Flownet (EPE 2.32) are substantially worse. For stereo matching on DSEC, TESNet (MAE 0.50, 1PE 9.20%) is the strongest baseline, with EMatch achieving 0.47 MAE and 8.34% 1PE; DTC-PDS (MAE 0.53, 1PE 9.28%) and Se-cff (MAE 0.52, 1PE 9.58%) are also clearly outperformed. On MVSEC optical flow, EMatch with DSEC pre-training and indoor_flying3 augmentation (EPE 0.42 indoor dt1, 0.23 outdoor dt1) beats TMA (0.45, 0.25) and all other baselines. For MVSEC stereo, EMatch with DSEC pre-training achieves MAE 0.36 px on Split 1, outperforming TESNet (0.46) and DTC (0.46). Notably, EMatch's multi-task unified training yields the best results on both tasks simultaneously without task-specific retraining, demonstrating that cross-task transfer provides consistent gains over single-task training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pixel-wise correspondence matching paradigm from frame-based unification work (Xu et al., TPAMI 2023) to event cameras, reformulating both optical flow and stereo matching as the same matching problem over a shared representation space. For feature extraction, it draws on the iterative refinement architecture of RAFT (Teed & Deng, ECCV 2020) and the global dot-product matching with softmax-normalized correlation volumes from GMFlow (Xu et al., CVPR 2022). The key departure from prior event-based work (E-RAFT, TMA, IDNet for flow; DDES, DTC, Se-cff for stereo) is that those methods are confined to task-specific pipelines, whereas EMatch introduces a Temporal Recurrent Network (ConvGRU-based) and Spatial Contextual Attention to produce task-agnostic dense features suitable for unified matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework offers a practical deployment advantage: a single model handles both flow and disparity without task-specific retraining, and the demonstrated cross-task transfer and multi-task unification consistently improve over single-task baselines on both DSEC and MVSEC. However, the performance gains over strong task-specific SOTA are modest (e.g., EPE 0.64 vs. 0.72; MAE 0.47 vs. 0.50), and evaluation is limited to driving-scenario datasets with only partial indoor coverage on MVSEC. Open questions include whether the shared representation space generalizes to other event-based tasks (depth, tracking, egomotion), whether the unification benefit persists with larger-scale or more diverse training data, and how the framework behaves under extreme sparsity or high-motion conditions where event density varies dramatically.
