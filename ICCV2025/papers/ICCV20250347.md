## Problem

Existing logit-based knowledge distillation methods rely on global softmax, which compresses probability differences among lower-probability classes and introduces redundant information from irrelevant categories when modeling pairwise relationships. This limits the student's ability to capture fine-grained inter-class distinctions, particularly for semantically similar categories that are hardest to separate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) through a pre-trained teacher and a compact student network. Rather than distilling a single global C-dimensional probability vector, LDRLD decomposes the logit outputs into all pairwise combinations of the top-d ranked logits, producing d(d−1)/2 two-class probability distributions as the distillation signal, alongside a local d-class distribution and a remaining (C−d)-class non-target distribution.

## Method

LDRLD recursively extracts the top-d logits (ranked by the student's output) one at a time, masking each extracted logit from subsequent selections, then generates all pairwise combinations of the extracted logits. Each pair is normalized via a local two-class softmax rather than the global C-class softmax, amplifying inter-class probability differences and removing interference from irrelevant categories. An Adaptive Decay Weight (ADW) strategy multiplies each pair's KL loss by a product of Inverse Rank Weighting (larger weight for closer-ranked pairs) and Exponential Rank Decay (faster decay for higher-ranked pairs), directing the student's optimization toward the most confusable category pairs. The total loss combines the task cross-entropy, the weighted local relational loss plus a local logit knowledge integrity term, and a remaining non-target knowledge distillation term, with hyperparameters α and β balancing the three components.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with same-architecture teacher-student pairs, LDRLD achieves top-1 accuracies of 72.20% (ResNet56→ResNet20), 71.98% (ResNet110→ResNet20), 74.16% (ResNet110→ResNet32), 77.20% (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4), 76.35% (WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), 74.98% (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), and 75.06% (VGG13→VGG8), improving over vanilla KD by 1.08%–3.87%. On ImageNet-1K, LDRLD gains +1.22% top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18) and +2.63% top-1 / +1.51% top-5 (ResNet50→MobileNetV1) over KD. On Market-1501 person Re-ID, LDRLD reaches 88.31% Rank@1 and 72.13% mAP, outperforming KD by +3.27% and +6.83% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDRLD outperforms all logit-based baselines (KD, DKD, CTKD, NKD, LCKA, LSKD, SDD, WTTM, TeKAP) across every same-architecture CIFAR-100 setting, with the largest margin of +3.87% over KD on ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4 and +1.28% over the strongest competitor WTTM on the same pair. In different-architecture settings (Table 2), LDRLD again leads all logit-based methods, gaining +2.39% to +3.39% over KD and beating WTTM by +2.09% (ResNet32×4→ShuffleNetV1) and +3.15% (ResNet50→MobileNetV2). On ImageNet-1K, LDRLD achieves 71.88% top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18), slightly below WTTM's 72.19%, but surpasses all other logit-based methods; for the heterogeneous ResNet50→MobileNetV1 pair it reaches 73.12%, exceeding WTTM (73.09%) and TeKAP (72.87%). On Market-1501 Re-ID, LDRLD attains the best Rank@1 (88.31%) and mAP (72.13%), outperforming DKD by +0.81% Rank@1 and +0.30% mAP. Feature-based methods (CRD, ReviewKD, CAT-KD, FCFD) generally achieve higher absolute accuracy on CIFAR-100 but at substantially greater computational cost, whereas LDRLD matches or exceeds them in several settings while operating purely on logits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit-based knowledge distillation lineage initiated by Hinton et al.'s global-softmax KD and the target/non-target decoupling framework of DKD (Zhao et al., 2022). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the global C-class softmax normalization with recursive pairwise (2-class) local softmax, arguing that irrelevant classes introduce redundancy that dilutes inter-class discriminability. The Adaptive Decay Weight strategy (IRW + ERD) further differentiates it from prior decoupling methods (SDD, NKD, ReKD) by introducing rank-aware weighting that prioritizes semantically close category pairs, a mechanism not present in existing logit-decoupling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local pairwise normalization offers a principled way to sharpen inter-class distinctions without the computational overhead of feature-based KD, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. The consistent gains across CNN and ViT-based heterogeneous architectures (up to 8.65% on CIFAR-100) suggest the approach generalizes beyond the CNN-centric KD literature. However, the manual selection of recursion depth d remains a practical limitation, and the method underperforms WTTM on ImageNet-1K same-architecture settings, indicating that the complex 1000-class structure may overwhelm the pairwise mechanism. Future work on adaptive depth selection and scaling to very large class spaces would strengthen the method's applicability.
