## Problem

Existing 3D human pose and shape estimation methods struggle under significant occlusions, yet the datasets used to evaluate them either apply artificial patch-based occlusions (random black patches, clipart overlays) or contain only sparse, infrequent natural occlusions. No large-scale dataset provides realistic, substantial occlusions in diverse scenes for training and benchmarking occlusion-robust HPS methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250967/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 720×720 RGB video frames at 30 fps with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and a human bounding box as input. Outputs include SMPL-X pose and shape parameters, a 10,475-vertex 3D body mesh, 22 2D keypoints, a binary occlusion label per joint, human silhouettes, body-part segmentations, and bounding boxes. Occlusion severity is quantified by the count of visible keypoints: hard (4–9), medium (10–15), and low (16–20) out of 22 total.

## Method

VOccl3D is constructed by rendering synthetic SMPL-X humans into real-world scenes captured via 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from the DL3DV video collection, yielding ~40 background scenes with natural occluders (cars, benches, garlands, trees). Approximately 400 challenging motion sequences from AMASS (filtered by VPoser embedding norm > 40, minimum 180 frames) are combined with ~200 diverse clothing textures from SMPLitex and rendered in Unity at variable FOV (25–50°), producing over 250,000 frames across 400 video sequences. The empirical contribution fine-tunes CLIFF and BEDLAM-CLIFF on VOccl3D (yielding VOccl3D-CLIFF and VOccl3D-B-CLIFF) and fine-tunes YOLO11 on VOccl3D + COCO for occlusion-robust human detection, demonstrating that synthetic occlusion data transfers to real-world benchmarks (3DPW, OCMotion) and that detector quality significantly impacts downstream HPS accuracy under occlusion.

## Results

On the VOccl3D test split, VOccl3D-B-CLIFF achieves the best MPJPE across all three occlusion levels: 136.34 mm (hard), 82.48 mm (medium), and 69.46 mm (low), outperforming the strongest baseline WHAM by 15.81 mm under hard occlusion. On real-world 3DPW with minimal occlusion, VOccl3D-CLIFF achieves 71.10 mm MPJPE, marginally beating BEDLAM-CLIFF (72.0 mm). However, on the occlusion-augmented OcclType2-3DPW, VOccl3D-CLIFF improves to 93.74 mm MPJPE versus 96.80 mm for BEDLAM-CLIFF, demonstrating a more substantial 3.06 mm gain where occlusion is severe. For human detection, fine-tuning YOLO11 on VOccl3D raises mAP50 on 3DPW from 58.99 to 59.89 and mAP75 from 47.14 to 48.26.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOccl3D-B-CLIFF (CLIFF fine-tuned on VOccl3D + BEDLAM) dominates all six baselines on the VOccl3D test split across every occlusion level and metric. Under hard occlusion, it reduces MPJPE by 15.81 mm over WHAM (152.15→136.34), 18.52 mm over BEDLAM-CLIFF (154.86→136.34), and 55.88 mm over the original CLIFF (192.22→136.34). On real-world 3DPW with minimal occlusion, VOccl3D-CLIFF edges out BEDLAM-CLIFF by only 0.9 mm (71.10 vs 72.00 MPJPE), confirming that gains are small when occlusion is absent. The advantage becomes pronounced on OcclType1-3DPW and OcclType2-3DPW (black-patch occlusions on real images), where VOccl3D-CLIFF improves MPJPE by 3.54 mm and 3.06 mm over BEDLAM-CLIFF respectively, and by 2.96 mm and 5.75 mm over CLIFF. STRIDE, a plug-and-play temporal refiner, benefits from VOccl3D-B-CLIFF pseudo-labels (136.43 mm) over BEDLAM-CLIFF pseudo-labels (155.64 mm) under hard occlusion, validating the dataset's utility for downstream video methods. For detection, VOccl3D-YOLO11 improves mAP50 on 3DPW from 58.99 to 59.89, and using its detections in the HPS pipeline reduces MPJPE from 116.52 to 114.85 mm versus the pre-trained YOLO11.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-dataset lineage established by BEDLAM and AGORA, which demonstrated that models trained purely on rendered data can match or exceed real-data-trained counterparts, but departs from them by introducing significant, scene-level occlusions rather than clean studio-like environments. For background scene generation, it replaces conventional 3D asset rendering with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) learned from real-world video, a technique previously used for novel-view synthesis but not for large-scale HPS dataset construction. The fine-tuning experiments build directly on CLIFF and BEDLAM-CLIFF as base architectures, and on YOLO11 as the detection backbone, positioning VOccl3D as a plug-in training resource rather than a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VOccl3D fills a concrete gap: prior occlusion datasets rely on artificial patch overlays or contain only sparse, mild occlusions, leaving methods untested under the heavy, natural occlusions encountered in the wild. The finding that a fine-tuned human detector (VOccl3D-YOLO11) meaningfully recovers HPS accuracy lost to detection failures under occlusion (Table 5) highlights a practical, often-overlooked bottleneck in end-to-end pipelines. Limitations include the synthetic-to-real domain gap (only 40 scenes, 200 textures), the reliance on Unity rendering fidelity, and the fact that real-world evaluation datasets (3DPW, OCMotion) still contain minimal occlusion, so the true benefit under severe natural occlusion remains partially unvalidated. Open questions include whether the 3DGS-based scene pipeline scales to hundreds of diverse environments and whether the occlusion-aware gains transfer to multi-person or dynamic-camera settings.
