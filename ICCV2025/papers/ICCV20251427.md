## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation methods—whether modular VLM-based pipelines or end-to-end VLA models—lack robust spatial affordance understanding of the 'where' and 'how' of object interactions, leading to incomplete or inefficient execution in tasks like wiping a board or stacking objects. Recent point-based and flow-based affordance methods address this but rely on dense spatial representations or embodiment-specific trajectory modeling, making them computationally expensive and difficult to transfer across platforms.

## Input / Output

The model takes a current RGB frame, a previous RGB frame (used to derive motion cues via token subtraction), and a natural-language instruction as input, and outputs a chunk of T=5 normalized 2D waypoints in [0,1]²—the first being the contact point and the remaining four being post-contact trajectory waypoints. At inference, a single forward pass predicts all waypoints simultaneously (4–5 steps total), which are then depth-deprojected to 3D, matched to a GraspNet grasp candidate, and converted into an SE(3) motion trajectory for the robot arm.

## Method

A₀ is a 1B-parameter DiT-based diffusion model (28 layers) that hierarchically decomposes manipulation into high-level affordance prediction and low-level action execution. It encodes images with SigLIP-400M and text with Qwen2.5-7B, injecting both as cross-attention conditions alternately across layers. Two key components differentiate it: Position Offset Attention, which subtracts previous-frame tokens from current-frame tokens to form explicit motion features concatenated with the current visual tokens, and a Spatial Information Aggregation Layer (MLP decoder) that maps latent predictions back to physical coordinates. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pre-training on 1M PixMo contact-point annotations to learn general object localization from text, followed by supervised fine-tuning on ~30k annotated manipulation trajectories (HOI4D, DROID, ManiSkill) with MSE loss on denoised waypoints. Inference uses a fast ODE solver (DPM-Solver) in only 5 denoising steps, enabling single-shot waypoint prediction rather than step-by-step action generation.

## Results

On the Franka Emika platform, A₀-1B achieves an average success rate of 62.50% across four manipulation tasks (Place Object, Open Drawer, Press Button, Wipe Board), outperforming the next-best VLM-based method Molmo (43.75%) by 18.75 percentage points. On the Kinova Gen3 platform, A₀-1B reaches 53.75% average success, surpassing MOKA (45.00%) and ReKep (33.75%). Against end-to-end VLA methods on Kinova, A₀-1B achieves 53.75% versus π₀ (20.00%) and RDT-1B (11.25%), while requiring only 4–5 execution steps compared to 25–50 for VLA baselines. On the trajectory-intensive Wipe Board task, A₀-1B scores 50% on Kinova and 45% on Franka, compared to 10% for π₀ and 0% for RDT-1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2D affordance methods on Kinova, A₀-1B wins on average (53.75% vs MOKA 45.00%, ReKep 33.75%) and dominates on Wipe Board (50% vs 30% and 0%), but loses to MOKA (70%) and ReKep (75%) on Place Object (60%), likely because those methods leverage large vision foundation models (SAM, GPT-4) trained on vast real-world object data. On Franka, A₀-1B substantially outperforms both VLM-based point predictors: Molmo (43.75%) and Magma (16.25%), with the largest gap on Open Drawer (75% vs 40%) and Press Button (70% vs 55%). Against end-to-end VLA methods on Kinova (Table 3), A₀-1B achieves 53.75% average success versus π₀ (20.00%), π₀+FAST (18.75%), and RDT-1B (11.25%), a 33.75-point margin over the second-best. The efficiency advantage is stark: A₀-1B completes tasks in 4–5 steps by predicting a small set of key waypoints in a single inference, whereas VLA methods require 25–50 sequential inference steps. The only clear loss is Place Object on Kinova, where MOKA and ReKep's richer visual priors give them a 10–15 point edge over A₀-1B.

## Relation to Prior Work

A₀ builds directly on the DiT diffusion transformer architecture for its generative backbone and borrows the action execution pipeline (2D-to-3D deprojection, GraspNet-based grasp refinement, SE(3) trajectory generation) from MOKA and RAM. Conceptually, it departs from both modular VLM-based affordance methods (ReKep, MOKA) and end-to-end VLA policies (RDT-1B, π₀) by introducing a hierarchical decomposition: a high-level diffusion model predicts an Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation (object-centric contact point plus post-contact trajectory waypoints), which a separate low-level module converts into executable robot actions. This positions A₀ between dense spatial-representation methods (heatmaps, flow fields) and full action-sequence generation, occupying a middle ground that prior point-based methods (RoboPoint, Track2Act) and flow-based methods (General Flow) did not explicitly target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The embodiment-agnostic design and single-shot inference (4–5 waypoints vs. 25–50 steps for VLA baselines) make A₀ practically attractive for rapid cross-platform deployment, and the 1M-contact-point pre-training pipeline offers a reusable recipe for bootstrapping spatial localization. However, the approach still depends on depth sensing for 2D-to-3D lifting and a VLM prompt for height selection, limiting its applicability in depth-poor or fully dynamic settings. Open questions include whether the hierarchical split generalizes to long-horizon, multi-object, or orientation-sensitive tasks without additional VLM-based task decomposition, and whether the 62.5% average success rate on Franka can be pushed further as the trajectory annotation scale grows.
