## Problem

Existing pose sequence refinement methods either smooth each joint independently in the temporal dimension (SmoothNet) or balance smoothness and precision across views (SynSP), but none explicitly model the spatial graph structure of the human skeleton while simultaneously capturing long-range temporal dynamics. This omission leaves both jitter and pose ambiguity unresolved, particularly in occluded or ambiguous scenarios where joint relationships are critical for disambiguation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a human pose sequence Q = {Q_1, ..., Q_T} in 2D, 3D, or SMPL parameterization (initialized by off-the-shelf estimators such as VIBE, FCN, or SPIN) and outputs a refined sequence in the same representation. Processing operates on sliding windows of 8 or 32 frames with batch size 128, and the model is representation-agnostic, requiring no modification across 2D keypoints, 3D joint coordinates, or SMPL body parameters. For the denoising variant, noisy 3D poses with Gaussian or uniform perturbations serve as input.

## Method

PS-Mamba introduces the ST-GSS block, which couples a Spatial-Temporal Graph network (ST-Graph) with a Spatial-Temporal State Space Model (ST-SSM) in a residual architecture. The ST-Graph uses a tailored ST-GCN with a learnable dynamic graph weight matrix W that adaptively modulates the influence of each joint on its neighbors across frames, capturing spatial dependencies. The ST-SSM replaces the redundant token ordering of standard 2D selective scanning with GST-Scanning (Graph-guided Spatial-Temporal Scanning), which constructs four bidirectional scanning sequences guided by the skeleton graph topology, then processes them through SS2D (2D-Selective-Scan) to model temporal dynamics with linear complexity. The full model stacks four ST-GSS blocks between an encoder and decoder, trained with a combined loss of MPJPE, Procrustes-aligned PA-MPJPE, and joint acceleration error to simultaneously optimize accuracy and smoothness.

## Results

On AIST++/SMPL (WS=8), PS-Mamba achieves 72.54 mm MPJPE, 52.95 mm PA-MPJPE, and 6.88 mm/s² Accel, outperforming SynSP (84.63/59.02/6.08) by 14.21 mm MPJPE (+18.5%), 13.59 mm PA-MPJPE (+22.1%), and 0.42 mm/s² Accel (+9.7%). In the multi-view 3D setting on Human3.6M (WS=8), PS-Mamba reaches 28.63 mm MPJPE and 21.83 mm PA-MPJPE, substantially below SynSP-MV (41.78/33.32). For 3D motion denoising on CMU-Mocap under N(0,400) noise (WS=32), PS-Mamba achieves 17.3 mm MPJPE and 1.5 mm/s² Accel, roughly 50% better than both SynSP and SmoothNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS-Mamba outperforms all learned refinement baselines (SmoothNet, SynSP, MoManifold) across every representation (2D, 3D, SMPL) and both window sizes. Against the strongest prior method SynSP [52], PS-Mamba reduces MPJPE by 14.21 mm on AIST++/SMPL (72.54 vs. 84.63 at WS=8) and by 13.15 mm in the multi-view 3D setting on Human3.6M (28.63 vs. 41.78). Classical filters (1€ Filter, Gaussian1d, Savitzky-Golay) achieve lower Accel in some 2D settings but are consistently worse on MPJPE and PA-MPJPE, confirming that learned spatial-temporal modeling is necessary. In the denoising task (Table 2), PS-Mamba (WS=32) achieves 12.7 mm MPJPE and 1.3 mm/s² Accel on CMU-Mocap under N(0,100), beating SynSP (20.6/3.7) and SmoothNet (35.1/4.3) by large margins. When stacked on top of video-based estimators (VIBE, TCMR, PARE), PS-Mamba consistently yields the lowest MPJPE and Accel, e.g., reducing VIBE's AIST++ MPJPE from 106.9 to 62.79 mm, outperforming VIBE+SynSP (77.00) and VIBE+SmoothNet (97.47).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct research threads: spatial-temporal graph convolutional networks (ST-GCN) for modeling joint relationships, and selective state space models (Mamba) for efficient long-range sequence modeling. Prior pose refinement methods (SmoothNet, SynSP, MoManifold) addressed either temporal smoothness or multi-view precision but neglected the intrinsic spatial graph structure of the skeleton. PS-Mamba departs from these by unifying both: it repurposes ST-GCN's adjacency-based message passing as a spatial encoder and introduces GST-Scanning, a graph-guided bidirectional scanning strategy that feeds structured joint sequences into SS2D (the 2D selective-scan module from VMamba), making the SSM aware of skeletal topology rather than treating joints as an unordered grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pose sequence refinement sits at a practical bottleneck for animation, VR, and virtual avatar pipelines where per-frame estimators produce jittery or ambiguous outputs; a plug-and-play refiner that simultaneously reduces MPJPE and acceleration error across 2D, 3D, and SMPL representations has direct deployment value. The paper's data-driven framing (no physics simulation, no contact labels) keeps the method lightweight and generalizable, but it also means physical plausibility is only implicitly learned, leaving open whether the approach degrades on out-of-distribution motions or very long sequences beyond the 32-frame window tested. A key open question is whether the graph-guided scanning strategy transfers to other structured sequence tasks (e.g., multi-person or hand pose) without re-deriving the adjacency structure.
