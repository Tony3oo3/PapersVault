## Problem

Existing video inverse problem solvers based on image diffusion models either depend on optical flow estimation (which degrades under severe degradations) or operate in pixel space, limiting them to low resolutions. Extending SVI's batch-consistent sampling strategy to latent diffusion models like SDXL produces unsatisfactory reconstruction quality, particularly in temporal consistency (FVD), while full batch processing of high-resolution latents is infeasible on a single GPU due to memory constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 25-frame degraded video sequences at HD resolutions (768×1280 landscape, 1280×768 vertical, or 1024×1024 square) along with a spatio-temporal degradation operator A (e.g., 7-frame temporal averaging combined with ×4 SR, Gaussian deblurring σ=3.0, or 50% random inpainting). It outputs fully reconstructed video frames at the same resolution, supporting multiple aspect ratios simultaneously. The entire pipeline runs on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU in under 6 seconds per frame with ~12.7 GB memory, using SDXL with null-text conditioning and no task-specific training.

## Method

VISION-XL introduces two key strategies for efficient latent-space video inverse problem solving. First, pseudo-batch consistent sampling replaces full batch denoising with sequential per-frame Tweedie denoising, requiring only single-frame memory while preserving batch-wise temporal consistency through shared noise. Second, pseudo-batch inversion initializes the sampling trajectory by VAE-encoding the measurement and applying DDIM inversion to obtain informative latents z_τ, reducing FVD by over 3× compared to random or batch-synchronized noise initialization. At each denoising step, the pipeline decodes the pseudo-batch to pixel space, applies l-step conjugate gradient optimization over the Kyrlov subspace to enforce data consistency with the measurement, applies a scheduled low-pass filter (σ_t = λ√(1−ᾱ_t)) to mitigate VAE error accumulation, re-encodes to latent space, and re-noises to the next timestep. The method is entirely training-free, leveraging the pretrained SDXL prior with null-text conditioning.

## Results

On the spatio-temporal inverse problems (Deblur+, SR+, Inpaint+), VISION-XL achieves FVD of 228.6, 158.5, and 241.1 on DAVIS, and 196.4, 166.2, and 222.4 on Pexels (landscape), substantially outperforming SVI (638.9, 223.4, 208.6 on DAVIS) in FVD for Deblur+ and SR+. On PSNR, the method reaches 28.76 dB (Deblur+), 29.18 dB (SR+), and 28.81 dB (Inpaint+) on DAVIS, and 31.11, 30.82, and 30.13 dB on Pexels (landscape). The method also achieves the best FVD across all four aspect ratios (landscape, vertical, square) on Pexels for every spatio-temporal task, with FVD as low as 138.8 (SR+, square) and 157.9 (Deblur+, square). Inference runs in under 6 seconds per frame at 768×1280 resolution on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU, using 12.7 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVI [13] (pixel-space diffusion with batch-consistent sampling), VISION-XL achieves a 2.8× FVD reduction on Deblur+ (228.6 vs. 638.9) and a 1.4× reduction on SR+ (158.5 vs. 223.4) on DAVIS, with even larger margins on Pexels (4.2× on Deblur+, 2.3× on SR+). However, SVI retains an edge on DAVIS Inpaint+ (FVD 208.6 vs. 241.1; PSNR 29.60 vs. 28.81), likely because pixel-space diffusion preserves fine pixel-level details better for random masking. Against DiffIR2VR [32] (optical-flow-guided latent diffusion), VISION-XL is the only method that handles all spatio-temporal tasks; DiffIR2VR is limited to SR and Inpaint spatial tasks and only reports results on Pexels (vertical/square), where it achieves FVD 125.4 on Inpaint (square) but introduces background artifacts due to optical flow inaccuracies. The classical ADMM-TV baseline is consistently outperformed by 5–8× in FVD across all spatio-temporal tasks, confirming that diffusion-based priors are essential for handling temporal degradations like frame averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SVI (Kwon & Ye, ICLR 2025), which introduced batch-consistent sampling for video inverse problems using pixel-space diffusion models, by migrating the framework into latent space with SDXL. The key conceptual departure is that naive porting of SVI's batch-synchronized noise initialization to latent diffusion models fails (particularly in FVD), necessitating two new mechanisms: pseudo-batch consistent sampling (sequential per-frame denoising to avoid batch memory blowup) and pseudo-batch inversion (DDIM-inverting the measurement to obtain informative initial latents). The broader lineage traces through the DIS family (DPS, DPSG, DDNM) and latent-space inverse solvers (PSLD, TReg, P2L), but the video-specific spatio-temporal formulation with frame averaging is inherited from SVI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to bring a state-of-the-art latent diffusion model (SDXL) to video inverse problem solving, enabling HD-resolution (1280×720+) reconstructions in under 6 seconds per frame on a single 4090 GPU without any task-specific training. The practical impact is significant: it supports multiple aspect ratios and complex spatio-temporal degradations (frame averaging + deblur/SR/inpaint) that prior methods either cannot handle or handle at much lower resolution. Open questions include scaling beyond the 25-frame proof-of-concept, the ad-hoc nature of the scheduled low-pass filter (tied to VAE error accumulation), and whether the VAE encode-decode loop can be further reduced to avoid representation drift over longer sequences.
