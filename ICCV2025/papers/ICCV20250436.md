## Problem

Existing skeleton-based video anomaly detection methods treat each skeleton as a monolithic graph, ignoring the compositional structure of individual keypoints and the causal ordering inherent in temporal sequences. Additionally, their anomaly scores cannot incorporate the uncertainty of the upstream keypoint detector, making them brittle to extraction errors. This paper asks whether a simple autoregressive density model at the keypoint level can serve as a strong, interpretable baseline that addresses both limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250436/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 18-keypoint COCO-format skeleton sequences extracted from video via AlphaPose within YOLOX bounding boxes and tracked across frames with reID; sequences are segmented into 24-frame windows and normalized to zero mean and unit variance. At inference, SeeKer produces a per-frame anomaly score (a confidence-weighted sum of per-keypoint log-conditionals), a binary anomaly label per frame via thresholding, and per-keypoint anomaly contributions that reveal which joints triggered the detection. For multi-person scenes, the per-frame score is the maximum across all tracked skeletons.

## Method

SeeKer factorizes the joint density of a skeleton sequence autoregressively at the keypoint level: each keypoint's location is modeled as a conditional multivariate Gaussian whose mean and covariance are predicted from all preceding keypoints in the current frame and a sliding window of past frames. The prediction network is a causally masked fully connected model (a variant of MADE) that enforces blockwise triangular masks so each keypoint prediction only observes earlier keypoints, and it outputs all N keypoint distributions in a single forward pass. Training is unsupervised maximum-likelihood estimation on normal sequences, reducing to minimizing Mahalanobis distance plus a log-determinant regularization term. At inference, the anomaly score weights each keypoint's negative log-conditional by its detector confidence, integrating pipeline uncertainty into the final decision. Notably, this simple masked FC architecture outperforms a causal transformer decoder, attributed to the low variability and short sequence length of skeleton data.

## Results

On UBnormal, SeeKer achieves 77.9% AUROC (full) and 78.9% AUROC (human-related), surpassing the best previous skeleton-based method STG-NF (71.8%) by over 6 percentage points and the best deep-feature method Ssmtl++ (62.1%) by over 15 pp. On ShanghaiTech, SeeKer reaches 85.5% AUROC and 80.0% AP, ranking second in AUROC but first in AP and RBDC (62.5 vs. 52.1 for STG-NF). On MSAD-HR, SeeKer attains 61.1% AUROC and 60.1% AP, outperforming STG-NF (55.7% AUROC) by 5.4 pp in a setting where all skeleton-based methods only marginally exceed random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UBnormal, SeeKer decisively outperforms the strongest skeleton-based baseline STG-NF [23] by 6.1 pp AUROC, 17.6 pp AP, and 10.3 pp RBDC, while also surpassing the multimodal MULDE [42] (72.8 AUROC) and the best deep-feature method Ssmtl++ [20] (62.1 AUROC) by 5.1 and 15.8 pp respectively. On ShanghaiTech, SeeKer is a close runner-up in AUROC (85.5 vs. 85.9 for STG-NF) but clearly wins on AP (80.0 vs. 77.6) and RBDC (62.5 vs. 52.1), indicating superior spatial localization of anomalies. On the challenging MSAD-HR dataset, where crowded scenes degrade skeleton tracking and all methods hover near random, SeeKer still leads STG-NF [23] by 5.4 pp AUROC and 3.6 pp AP, and MoCoDAD [16] by 7.2 pp AUROC. Notably, SeeKer without post-process Gaussian smoothing (76.8 AUROC on UBnormal) already exceeds STG-NF with smoothing (71.8 AUROC), suggesting the gains are architectural rather than due to heuristic post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeeKer builds directly on the MADE architecture [21] for its autoregressive density estimation, adapting the masked fully-connected design to predict per-keypoint conditional Gaussians over 2D skeleton coordinates. It departs from the dominant skeleton-based anomaly detection paradigm (STG-NF, MoCoDAD, MULDE) that treats each skeleton as a monolithic graph or sequence and models the joint distribution with normalizing flows, diffusion models, or energy-based models. The key conceptual shift is a top-down autoregressive factorization at the keypoint level rather than the skeleton level, combined with principled incorporation of keypoint detector confidence into the anomaly score—something prior graph- or flow-based methods cannot easily accommodate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deliberately simple autoregressive density estimator, framed as an 'overlooked baseline,' outperforms substantially more complex graph- and diffusion-based methods by 5–6 pp AUROC on two benchmarks, suggesting that the field may have over-engineered skeleton anomaly detection. The per-keypoint interpretability (identifying which joints triggered the alarm) and the integration of detector uncertainty offer practical advantages for safety-critical deployments. Limitations include dependence on off-the-shelf skeleton extractors (AlphaPose + YOLOX + reID), applicability only to human-related anomalies, and evaluation on relatively small datasets; open questions include whether the keypoint-level autoregressive formulation transfers to other sequential density estimation tasks or to 3D pose sequences.
