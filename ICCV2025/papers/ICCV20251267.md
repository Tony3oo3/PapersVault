## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene datasets provide at most object-level semantic segmentation, and the few that include articulation annotations (MultiScan, SceneFun3D) lack connectivity graphs, multi-granularity hierarchies, or complete motion specifications needed for simulation-ready scene understanding. This gap limits holistic scene understanding for embodied AI, as models cannot jointly reason about which parts move, how they move, and which parts are interactable. Additionally, reliance on synthetic assets introduces a significant sim-to-real gap that hinders deployment in real-world environments.

## Input / Output

USDNet takes a 3D scene point cloud cropped to a 6×6 m² cuboid as input and jointly produces instance masks for movable and interactable parts, a motion type label (rotation, translation, or background) per part, and continuous motion parameters (3D axis vector and 3D origin vector for rotational parts). The outputs are structured to directly instantiate a USD scene representation, meaning the predicted masks, roles, and motion parameters can be assembled into simulation-ready prims without further processing. Connectivity graphs can additionally be predicted as a separate task using spatial proximity or learned methods.

## Method

USDNet extends the Mask3D backbone (3D sparse convolution + stacked transformer decoder) with a point-wise dense prediction mechanism for articulation parameters. The key insight is that motion parameters are tightly coupled to local geometry, so masked point features of each movable part are passed through MLPs to produce per-point axis and origin predictions, which are then averaged and fused with a query-based axis prediction to combine local and global context. An auxiliary task predicts the spatial vector from each interactable-part point to its part center, accelerating convergence and improving segmentation of small parts (switches, buttons). Training uses a coarse-to-fine strategy for small instances, with a two-phase schedule: 1160 epochs on part segmentation alone, followed by 680 epochs on joint segmentation and motion parameter prediction, optimizing a combined loss of dice/BCE for masks, cross-entropy for motion type, and cos-dissimilarity plus cross-product distance for axis and origin.

## Results

On Articulate3D, USDNet achieves AP50=41.8 and AP25=59.9 for movable part segmentation, and AP50=31.1 for interactable part segmentation. For motion parameter prediction, USDNet reaches AP50+Origin+Axis=25.0, surpassing Mask3D† (19.3) by 5.7 points and SoftGroup† (17.7) by 7.3 points. Cross-dataset evaluation on MultiScan shows a 5.7% improvement from pre-training on Articulate3D, and cross-domain evaluation (URDFormer fine-tuned on Articulate3D) yields a 12.4% gain on MultiScan part detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USDNet is compared against two adapted baselines: SoftGroup† (reusing SoftGroup's instance segmentation network with added origin/axis prediction heads) and Mask3D† (Mask3D backbone with an additional articulation prediction head). On movable part segmentation (Table 2), USDNet outperforms Mask3D† by 2.7 points in AP50 (41.8 vs. 39.1) and 1.0 in AP25 (59.9 vs. 58.9), while SoftGroup† achieves the highest overall AP (22.7 vs. 19.8) due to its IoU-based filtering of false positives but lags on AP50/AP25. On interactable part segmentation (Table 3), USDNet edges out Mask3D† by 0.9 in AP50 (31.1 vs. 30.2), with SoftGroup† performing worst (AP50=14.5). The largest margin appears in motion parameter prediction (Table 4), where USDNet's dense point-wise prediction mechanism yields a 5.7-point gain over Mask3D† and 7.3-point gain over SoftGroup† on the joint AP50+Origin+Axis metric. On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 6), USDNet also leads on both MultiScan (26.0 vs. 23.3) and SceneFun3D (30.5 vs. 22.4) for AP50+Origin+Axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

USDNet extends the Mask3D sparse-convolution + transformer-decoder backbone for 3D instance segmentation and adopts the coarse-to-fine training strategy from SceneFun3D, but departs from both by introducing a dense point-wise prediction branch for articulation parameters (axis and origin) rather than relying solely on query-based aggregation. On the dataset side, Articulate3D builds on ScanNet++ high-fidelity scans but adds annotation layers—part-level segmentation, connectivity graphs, movable/interactable/fixed part roles, and full motion parameters—that no prior real-world dataset (MultiScan, SceneFun3D) provides in combination, and it encodes everything natively in USD format for simulation readiness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Articulate3D is the first large-scale real-world dataset in simulation-ready USD format with the full spectrum of articulation annotations, directly addressing the sim-to-real gap that limits synthetic-only training (e.g., URDFormer's 10% real-world success rate). The demonstrated 12.4% cross-domain improvement from finetuning on Articulate3D and the plug-and-play integration into IsaacSim for PPO-based policy training suggest practical utility for embodied AI and robotic manipulation. Open questions remain around whether scan-derived mesh imperfections (deformation, incomplete geometries) degrade sim-to-real policy transfer, and whether the dense prediction mechanism generalizes beyond indoor scenes.
