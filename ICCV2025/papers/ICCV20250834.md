## Problem

Existing high-resolution strategies for LVLMs rely on limited pre-defined grid partitions, which either lose critical detail when downsampled for gigapixel remote sensing images or incur prohibitive computational costs (over 5 million vision tokens) when grids are expanded without bound. Additionally, benchmarks for evaluating LVLM perception on large RSIs suffer from limited question diversity and constrained image sizes, making it difficult to measure true high-resolution understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250834/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a large RSI (up to 27,328 pixels on the longest side) and a free-form text question, producing a short open-ended text answer. The image is not fed as a single tensor but is dynamically decomposed into a pyramid of non-overlapping tiles at progressively finer ground sample distances; only text-relevant tiles at higher resolutions are encoded by the vision encoder, with a token saving ratio of 25% applied at each level. The LRS-VQA benchmark uses 8 question categories (count, color, category, shape, status, reasoning, rural/urban classification, target background) evaluated via WordNet-based semantic similarity with a 0.8 threshold.

## Method

The approach combines two components: a Region Focus Module (RFM), a lightweight R-layer transformer distilled from selected LLM layers via KL-divergence and MSE losses on text-to-vision attention scores, and a Dynamic Image Pyramid (DIP) that stores tiles at P+1 resolution levels obtained by iterative 2× downsampling. During inference, the model starts from the coarsest DIP level, uses the RFM's last-layer attention to identify the top-α proportion of text-relevant vision tokens, maps their coordinates to select corresponding high-resolution tiles from the next pyramid level, and either prunes tokens or recursively retrieves finer tiles until the last DIP layer or a tile-count threshold is reached. This coarse-to-fine loop means the LLM only processes a small subset of high-resolution tokens rather than the full image, reducing theoretical TFLOPs from 243.37 (anyres-p144) to 36.61 (DIP-4layer) while improving accuracy from 39.65 to 41.89 on MME-RealWorld-RS.

## Results

On the fair comparison (same 484K SFT data), the proposed method with LLaVA-Next-Qwen2 achieves 32.16% average accuracy across MME-RealWorld-RS and three LRS-VQA subsets, outperforming the strongest fixed-grid baseline LLaVA-Next-p9* (31.21%) by ~1.0% and LLaVA-Next-p25* (30.80%) by ~1.4%. With the LLaVA-1.5-Vicuna backbone, the method reaches 31.59% average accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-UHD-v2* (30.42%) and LLaVA-1.5-p4* (29.83%). In the token reduction comparison (Table 3), the method achieves 41.28% accuracy on MME-RealWorld-RS at 75% token pruning, beating anyres-p25 (39.65%) and PDrop (39.85%), while the 4-layer DIP variant reduces vision tokens to the LLM from 21,312 to 2,376 (89% reduction) with only 36.61 TFLOPs vs. 243.37 for the unpruned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the fair same-data comparison, the proposed method consistently outperforms all five fixed-grid high-resolution baselines (LLaVA-1.5*, LLaVA-1.5-p4*, Geochat*, SLiME*, LLaVA-UHD-v2*) by 1.2–4.3% average accuracy, with the largest gap over the base LLaVA-1.5* (31.59% vs. 27.30%). Against the LLaVA-Next variants, the method gains 0.9–1.6% over p4*, p9*, and p25* respectively, showing that dynamic tile selection surpasses even the highest fixed grid (p25 at 1680×1680). In the token reduction comparison (Table 3), the method achieves the best accuracy (41.28%) among all pruning strategies at 75% token drop, beating PruMerge++ (34.86%), VisionZip (33.98%), FastV (37.93%), and PDrop (39.85%), while the 4-layer DIP variant reduces LLM-bound vision tokens by 89% (21,312→2,376) and cuts theoretical FLOPs from 243.37 to 36.61 TFLOPs. On the leaderboard, the method trails Qwen2-VL (34.88%) and LLaVA-OV (33.83%) which use substantially more training data (4.8M) and different architectures, but surpasses IXC-2.5 (31.77%), GPT-4o (27.58%), and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (19.67%). For localization quality (Table 7), the RFM achieves 47.99% recall on LRS-VQA, far exceeding CLIP (20.14%) and RemoteCLIP (30.41%) baselines, though still below the teacher LLM attention (56.14%), confirming effective but imperfect distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-1.5/LLaVA-Next modular LVLM architecture and the anyres grid-tiling strategy from InternVL, but departs from the fixed-grid paradigm by introducing a Dynamic Image Pyramid (DIP) that enables coarse-to-fine, text-guided tile selection. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: high-resolution image processing (which suffers from prohibitive token counts on gigapixel RSIs) and vision token pruning (which typically operates without language guidance). The key architectural novelty is the Region Focus Module (RFM), which distills text-to-vision attention from the LLM's deep layers into a lightweight student, replacing the need for external CLIP/RemoteCLIP similarity maps or multi-step visual chain-of-thought pipelines. The paper also extends the MME-RealWorld-RS benchmark by constructing LRS-VQA with substantially larger images (up to 27,328 px) and more diverse question types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing practical bottleneck: current LVLMs cannot efficiently process the gigapixel-scale RSIs that modern satellite systems produce, and the LRS-VQA benchmark fills a clear evaluation gap for this regime. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both Vicuna-1.5 and Qwen2 backbones) and the ~6.6× theoretical compute reduction (36.61 vs. 243.37 TFLOPs) make the approach broadly applicable. Open questions remain around whether the distillation-based RFM generalizes to unseen RS domains without retraining, whether long-context LLM capabilities could replace the pyramid mechanism entirely, and whether chain-of-thought reasoning can further improve performance on the more complex reasoning and classification question types in LRS-VQA.
