## Problem

Existing adversarial data augmentation methods for Single Domain Generalization generate only localized perturbations, failing to capture the global structure of domain shifts in parameter space. This underexploration limits the model's ability to learn generalizable features across diverse unseen distributions, particularly under large domain shifts where training dynamics are not adapted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250648/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images from a single source domain and outputs class labels for unseen target domains. During training, adversarial transformations τ(x;ω) are applied to source images to simulate domain shifts, and the model is trained with a joint min-max objective that balances prediction loss, feature consistency between original and transformed samples, and L2 weight decay. Evaluation follows the standard leave-one-domain-out protocol on PACS (7 classes), OfficeHome (65 classes), and DomainNet (345 classes).

## Method

LEAwareSGD frames neural network training as a discrete-time dynamical system in parameter space and uses the Lyapunov Exponent (LE) as a feedback signal to guide the model toward the edge of chaos. At each iteration, a small perturbation δθ is propagated through the Hessian-based linearization of the loss, and the LE is computed as the asymptotic growth rate of this perturbation. When ΔLE_t > 0 (the system is approaching the edge of chaos), the learning rate is reduced via η_{t+1} = η_t · exp(−β·ΔLE_t) to allow deeper exploration of generalizable regions; when ΔLE_t ≤ 0, the learning rate is held constant. This LE-guided scheduling is integrated with an adversarial data augmentation objective (min_θ max_ω prediction loss − λ·feature distance + weight decay), where the weight decay term keeps the Hessian approximately positive definite to maintain negative LE and training stability. The result is a training trajectory that explores a broader parameter space than standard SGD or adaptive optimizers, as visualized by t-SNE of parameter trajectories.

## Results

On PACS, LEAwareSGD achieves 69.46% average accuracy, outperforming the second-best method PSDG (67.14%) by 2.32%. On OfficeHome, it reaches 54.38% average accuracy, surpassing AdvST (52.60%) by 1.78%. On DomainNet, it attains 28.15% average accuracy, exceeding AdvST (27.22%) by 0.93%. In the low-data regime (10% source data on PACS), the method achieves 58.73% average accuracy, a 9.47% improvement over AdvST (49.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAwareSGD outperforms all 25 compared baselines across the three SDG benchmarks. On PACS, it beats the closest competitors PSDG (67.14%) and AdvST (67.06%) by 2.32% and 2.40% respectively, with the largest per-domain gains in Art Painting (+1.29% over PSDG) and Photo (+9.56% over PSDG). On OfficeHome, it surpasses AdvST (52.60%) by 1.78% and Mixstyle (50.66%) by 3.72%, winning on all four target domains. On DomainNet, it leads AdvST (27.22%) by 0.93% and SimDE (26.98%) by 1.17%, though it slightly trails SimDE on the Quickdraw domain (6.70% vs. 6.85%). In the optimizer ablation (Table 4), LEAwareSGD (69.46%) outperforms standard SGD (67.06%) by 2.40% and Adam (66.43%) by 3.03%, confirming that the LE-guided learning rate adjustment provides a meaningful advantage over both fixed-rate and adaptive optimizers. The advantage is most pronounced in low-data settings, where the 9.47% gain over AdvST at 10% data ratio on PACS demonstrates that LE-guided exploration is particularly effective when training diversity is limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes concepts from dynamical systems theory—specifically the Lyapunov Exponent and the edge-of-chaos principle—into the optimization loop of adversarial data augmentation for single domain generalization. Rather than building on a single prior method, it sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) adversarial augmentation approaches (ADA, ME-ADA, AdvST) that generate domain-shift-simulating perturbations, and (2) optimizer design for generalization (SAM, GSAM) that targets flat minima. The key departure is using LE as a dynamic feedback signal to modulate the learning rate, framing parameter updates as state transitions in a discrete-time dynamical system rather than as static loss-landscape navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LE-guided learning-rate adjustment offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for balancing exploration and stability during training, and the 9.47% gain in the 10%-data regime on PACS suggests the approach is particularly valuable when data diversity is scarce. However, the theoretical derivation connecting LE to generalization relies on first-order Taylor approximations and loose inequalities, leaving the causal link between operating near the edge of chaos and improved domain generalization somewhat underdeveloped. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer backbones or larger datasets, whether the LE computation (requiring Hessian-vector products) remains tractable at scale, and whether the gains persist when the adversarial augmentation component is replaced by non-adversarial strategies.
