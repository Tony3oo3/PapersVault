## Problem

Transformer-based object trackers achieve strong accuracy but are too heavy for resource-constrained deployment. Existing compression approaches either sacrifice accuracy through lightweight redesign, require complex multi-stage distillation pipelines (e.g., MixFormerV2-S needs ~120 hours of training), or impose rigid structural constraints that force the student to mirror the teacher's architecture. The paper addresses the need for a general, structurally-agnostic compression framework that preserves tracking accuracy while being training-efficient and flexible in student design.

## Input / Output

The compressed tracker takes a template image (128×128) and a search image (256×256) as input and outputs a single bounding box per frame, consistent with standard one-stream tracking. During training, input and output projection layers are inserted before and after the student's transformer layers to align channel dimensions with the teacher model; these projections are removed at inference, so the deployed student operates with its native architecture. The framework is applied to multiple teacher trackers (OSTrack, SUTrack, ODTrack, SeqTrack) with varying numbers of student layers (2–8), demonstrating that the student's depth and width are free design choices.

## Method

CompressTracker introduces three interlocking mechanisms for compressing a teacher tracker into a smaller student. First, stage division partitions the teacher's N_t encoder layers into N contiguous stages matching the student's N layers, establishing a one-to-one correspondence for fine-grained knowledge transfer. Second, replacement training dynamically substitutes randomly selected student stages with their frozen teacher counterparts (Bernoulli sampling with probability p) during each forward pass, so the un-replaced teacher stages provide contextual supervision to the student stages being trained. Third, a progressive replacement schedule gradually increases p from p_init (0.5) to 1.0 over training epochs, creating an easy-to-hard curriculum that eliminates the need for a separate fine-tuning phase. Additional supervision comes from prediction guidance (applying the tracking loss to the teacher's predicted box) and stage-wise feature mimicking (L2 distance between corresponding stage outputs), yielding a total loss L = λ_track·L_track + λ_pred·L_pred + λ_feat·L_feat. After training, all student stages are simply concatenated for inference, and the framework supports arbitrary student transformer architectures regardless of the teacher's structure.

## Results

CompressTracker-SUTrack achieves 72.2% AUC on LaSOT (retaining ~99% of SUTrack's 73.2%) while delivering a 2.42× speedup (134 FPS on GPU, 24 FPS on CPU). Across all four compressed variants (OSTrack, SeqTrack, ODTrack, SUTrack), the framework consistently outperforms every lightweight baseline in both accuracy and inference speed. For example, CompressTracker-OSTrack reaches 66.1% AUC at 228 FPS GPU, surpassing the next-best tracker MixFormerV2-S (60.6% AUC at 325 FPS) by 5.5 AUC points while remaining competitive in speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompressTracker-SUTrack (72.2% AUC, 134 FPS GPU) dominates all baselines on LaSOT, outperforming the closest competitor SUTrack-T (69.6% AUC, 100 FPS) by 2.6 AUC while also being 34% faster on GPU. Against the strongest lightweight tracker MixFormerV2-S (60.6% AUC, 325 FPS), CompressTracker-SUTrack gains 11.6 AUC at the cost of roughly 2.4× lower FPS, and CompressTracker-OSTrack (66.1% AUC, 228 FPS) gains 5.5 AUC while remaining within 1.4× in speed. On TrackingNet, CompressTracker-SUTrack leads with 84.3% AUC versus 82.7% for SUTrack-T and 80.0% for HiT-Base. On UAV123, the gap is similarly clear: 71.0% AUC for CompressTracker-SUTrack versus 69.4% for SUTrack-T and 65.6% for HiT-Base. The only baseline that exceeds CompressTracker variants in raw FPS is MixFormerV2-S (325 FPS), but it trails by 5.5–11.6 AUC depending on the compressed teacher, confirming that CompressTracker achieves a superior accuracy–speed trade-off across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of work on efficient tracking: (1) lightweight architecture design (E.T.Track, HCAT, HiT, FEAR) that sacrifices accuracy for speed, and (2) multi-stage knowledge distillation (MixFormerV2) that imposes structural constraints on the student model and requires complex, time-consuming training pipelines. CompressTracker's key conceptual departure is the stage division strategy combined with replacement training, which treats the teacher model as a sequence of interchangeable functional units rather than an indivisible whole, enabling the student to have an arbitrary transformer architecture. The progressive replacement schedule and the use of a simple L2 feature-mimicking loss (rather than complex distillation objectives) further distinguish it from prior distillation-based compression in both NLP and vision transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practically significant path to deploying high-accuracy transformer trackers on resource-constrained devices, with a 2.42× speedup at ~99% accuracy retention and a single 20-hour training run versus 120+ hours for prior distillation approaches. Its structural agnosticism—supporting any student architecture, any number of stages, and any input resolution—makes it a broadly applicable tool rather than a one-off model. Open questions include whether the stage-division granularity and replacement probability schedule transfer cleanly to non-transformer backbones or to multi-object and video segmentation tasks, and whether the modest accuracy gap (e.g., 72.2 vs. 73.2 AUC on LaSOT) widens on more challenging or longer-duration benchmarks.
