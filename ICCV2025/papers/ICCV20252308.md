## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image compression methods require dozens of denoising steps at the decoder, making them impractical for real-time use, and they typically sacrifice pixel-level reconstruction fidelity because diffusion models do not guarantee consistency with the original image. StableCodec addresses both limitations by enabling a single-step denoising process for extreme compression while preserving fidelity through a dedicated auxiliary decoding branch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252308/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at arbitrary resolution and a fixed general text prompt (used identically in training and inference) as input, and produces a compressed bitstream at ultra-low bitrates (down to 0.005 bpp) along with a reconstructed image. The VAE encoder produces an 8× spatially compressed latent, which is further downsampled to 64× for entropy coding and 256× for the hyperprior before being restored to 8× for the one-step denoising Unet. Inference requires less than 9 GB VRAM with tiling, supporting arbitrary-resolution decoding on a single GTX 1080Ti.

## Method

StableCodec integrates SD-Turbo (a distilled Stable Diffusion 2.1) with a Deep Compression Latent Codec that compresses a noisy latent l_T at 64× spatial resolution using InceptionNeXt analysis/synthesis transforms and a 4-step autoregressive entropy model with quadtree partition, then performs a single-step denoising at timestep T. A Dual-Branch Coding Structure adds a frozen ELIC analysis transform as auxiliary encoder (providing entropy-aware pixel-level semantics) and an auxiliary decoder that routes basic structural information directly from the bitstream, freeing the synthesis transform to focus on high-frequency guidance for the denoising Unet. LoRA adapters (rank 16/32) are applied to the VAE encoder and denoising Unet while the VAE decoder remains unchanged to preserve generative priors. Training uses a two-stage implicit bitrate pruning strategy: a base stage with relaxed bitrate adapts the codec to SD-Turbo, followed by a target stage with higher Lagrange multipliers, MSE+LPIPS+CLIP distortion, and a DINOv2-based GAN loss for perceptual quality.

## Results

StableCodec achieves state-of-the-art FID, KID, and DISTS on CLIC 2020 and DIV2K at ultra-low bitrates as low as 0.005 bpp, outperforming all compared methods by significant margins. At 0.017 bpp, StableCodec produces visually superior reconstructions compared to H.266/VVC (0.036 bpp), ELIC (0.030 bpp), MS-ILLM (0.035 bpp), PerCo (0.033 bpp), and DiffEIC (0.019 bpp). In terms of speed, StableCodec achieves 0.326 s decoding time on Kodak (1 denoising step), comparable to VAE-based ELIC (0.245 s) and GAN-based HiFiC (0.337 s), while diffusion-based competitors require 3.7–33.6 s. Memory consumption stays below 9 GB VRAM with tiling, supporting arbitrary-resolution inference on a single GTX 1080 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableCodec outperforms all 11 baselines on perceptual quality (FID, KID, DISTS) across CLIC 2020 and DIV2K at ultra-low bitrates. Against traditional and neural codecs (H.266/VVC, ELIC, MLIC++), StableCodec achieves substantially lower FID/KID/DISTS at the same or lower bitrate, while ELIC and VVC produce blurry reconstructions. Among GAN-based generative codecs (HiFiC, MS-ILLM, EGIC), StableCodec yields more realistic and consistent details; EGIC and TACO exhibit noticeable artifacts in detailed regions. Against diffusion-based competitors, the advantage is twofold: StableCodec uses only 1 denoising step versus 20 (PerCo), 25 (Text+Sketch), or 50 (DiffEIC), cutting decoding time from 3.7–33.6 s to 0.326 s, while simultaneously achieving better FID and KID. The previous SOTA GLC [26] is outperformed on both FID and KID with well-preserved fidelity. TACO [34] is the sole method that beats StableCodec on LPIPS, but the paper notes it fails to ensure visual quality as reflected in high FID, KID, and DISTS scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableCodec sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) learned image compression with entropy coding (ELIC, MLIC), (2) generative image compression via diffusion models (PerCo, DiffEIC, GLC), and (3) few-step/one-step diffusion distillation (SD-Turbo, progressive distillation). It departs from prior diffusion-based codecs (PerCo, DiffEIC, Text+Sketch) that require 20–50 denoising steps by leveraging SD-Turbo's adversarial distillation to compress a noisy latent in a single step, and from prior transform codecs by operating in the VAE latent space of a pre-trained T2I model rather than a purpose-built autoencoder. The Dual-Branch Coding Structure, which borrows ELIC's analysis transform as a frozen auxiliary encoder and adds a parallel auxiliary decoder for structure apportionment, is a novel architectural combination not seen in prior generative codecs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching the inference speed of mainstream transform codecs (~0.33 s decode) while achieving SOTA FID, KID, and DISTS at bitrates as low as 0.005 bpp, StableCodec makes diffusion-based generative compression practically deployable in real-time pipelines—a regime previously dominated by multi-step methods that were 10–100× slower. The paper's ablations reveal that the auxiliary decoder's structure apportionment contributes the largest single gain (≈55% BD-rate on DISTS), suggesting that separating structural and textural information in the bitstream is a broadly useful principle for extreme-rate coding. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video compression, whether other T2I backbones (SDXL, Flux) yield similar one-step compression benefits, and how the method behaves under distribution shift (e.g., medical or satellite imagery) where the pre-trained generative prior may be less aligned.
