## Problem

Pure-vision-based GUI agents have focused on scaling pre-training data while overlooking contextual modeling challenges. The paper identifies two specific issues: (1) UI screenshots contain dozens to hundreds of loosely-related elements, where unrelated elements actively interfere with comprehension of critical ones, and (2) historical observations introduce 3.4× computational overhead for only a 3.0% performance gain, revealing severe redundancy in how history is modeled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250348/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a current GUI screenshot (RGB), a natural-language task goal, and a history of previous screenshots and actions. At inference, all historical vision tokens are dropped after a shallow LLM layer (k=3), leaving only the text action tokens from history in the sequence, which reduces FLOPs by 27%. The output is a single GUI action (click coordinates, typed text, or scroll) generated autoregressively. Masking of the current screenshot is applied only during training with probability p=0.5 and is omitted at inference to preserve full visual information.

## Method

SimpAgent introduces two training-time mechanisms on top of a Qwen2-VL-2B backbone. First, masking-based element pruning randomly masks a rectangular region (size h,w ~ U(0.5,0.7), center from a uniform distribution) in the current screenshot during training, exploiting the loose-relation property of UI elements so that unrelated elements are pruned with high probability without requiring explicit relation modeling. Second, consistency-guided history compression drops all historical vision tokens after LLM layer k, implicitly forcing the shallow causal-attention layers to compress visual information into adjacent action tokens; to counteract the resulting information loss, a parallel untruncated branch is maintained and the KL divergence between the two branches' action distributions is minimized as an explicit steering signal. The combined training objective sums the standard cross-entropy losses of both branches plus the KL term, requiring no extra parameters and remaining compatible with FlashAttention.

## Results

On AITW, SimpAgent (2B params, no GUI pre-training data) achieves 71.3% step success rate, outperforming the Qwen2-VL baseline (69.0%) by 2.3% and ShowUI (70.0%, 256K pre-training) by 1.3%. On GUI-Odyssey (long-horizon, avg. 15.4 steps), SimpAgent reaches 76.0% step SR, surpassing OdysseyAgent (74.3%) and the Qwen2-VL baseline (74.9%). On AndroidControl (833 apps), SimpAgent achieves 69.1% step SR, exceeding OS-Atlas (67.5%) which was pre-trained on 1.9M GUI samples. The full SimpAgent pipeline reduces inference FLOPs by 27% (from 11.90T to 8.71T) while maintaining or improving performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimpAgent (2B, no pre-training data) outperforms the Qwen2-VL 2B baseline by 2.3% on AITW (71.3 vs 69.0) and 1.1% on GUI-Odyssey (76.0 vs 74.9), and by 0.4–1.1% across Mind2Web splits. Against ShowUI (2B, 256K pre-training), SimpAgent wins on AITW (71.3 vs 70.0) and substantially on Mind2Web Cross-Task (47.1 vs 37.2) and Cross-Domain (43.3 vs 35.2). Against the much larger SeeClick (9.6B, 850K pre-training), SimpAgent leads on AITW by 12.0% (71.3 vs 59.3) and on Mind2Web Cross-Task by 21.6%. On GUI-Odyssey, SimpAgent surpasses OdysseyAgent (9.6B) by 1.7% (76.0 vs 74.3). On AndroidControl, SimpAgent (2B, no pre-training) achieves 69.1% SR, beating OS-Atlas (4B, 1.9M pre-training) at 67.5% and the Qwen2-VL baseline at 68.4%, demonstrating that context-aware simplification can match or exceed large-scale pre-training gains with far fewer resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in pure-vision GUI agents that pursues large-scale GUI grounding pre-training (SeeClick, OS-Atlas, ShowUI) by instead targeting the underexplored problem of contextual modeling within the agent's input. For element context, it repurposes the simple idea of random rectangular masking (common in data augmentation) as a training-time pruning mechanism, avoiding the intractable problem of modeling inter-element relations. For history compression, it builds on the observation from FastV and Victor that shallow LLM layers aggregate visual features into anchor tokens, but replaces their inference-time token selection or trainable summary tokens with a parameter-free direct dropping strategy augmented by a novel KL-divergence-based consistency loss between truncated and full branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27% FLOPs reduction with simultaneous performance gains (2.3% on AITW, 2.3% on GUI-Odyssey) offers a practical path toward deploying GUI agents with lower inference cost, which is critical for real-time interactive applications. The empirical quantification that history observations add 3.4× compute for only 3% gain, and that unrelated elements actively degrade comprehension, provides concrete motivation for the field to move beyond brute-force data scaling. Open questions include whether the masking strategy generalizes to larger models (tested only at 2B), whether the uniform distribution's superiority over the intuitively-motivated inverse Gaussian reflects a fundamental property of GUI layout, and whether the consistency-guided compression extends to longer history horizons beyond 4 steps.
