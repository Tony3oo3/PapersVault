## Problem

CLIP-based models excel at global text-image alignment but lack the fine-grained spatial localization needed for dense prediction tasks like segmentation, while self-supervised backbones like DINOv2 produce highly localized patch features that are not aligned with any textual space. No prior method directly bridges these two embedding spaces without fine-tuning the underlying backbones or relying on multiple visual encoders at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to a shorter side of 448 px, processed via a sliding window with 224 px stride) and a set of free-form textual category labels as input, and produces a full-resolution pixel-level segmentation mask. At inference, the projected text embeddings are compared against DINOv2 patch-level features to yield a similarity map per class, which is optionally shaped by a background-cleaning map derived from attention-weighted averages before thresholding and argmax assignment. An optional PAMR post-refinement stage (10 iterations) sharpens mask boundaries.

## Method

Talk2DINO learns a lightweight nonlinear projection ψ (two affine layers with a tanh activation) that warps CLIP text embeddings into the DINOv2 visual patch embedding space, without fine-tuning either backbone. During training on ~80k COCO Captions image-text pairs, the method exploits DINOv2's last-layer CLS-to-patch self-attention maps: for each of the N attention heads, it computes a weighted-average visual embedding of the dense feature map, selects the head whose embedding has maximum cosine similarity to the projected text, and optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss over the batch. At inference, segmentation is obtained by computing per-patch cosine similarity between the projected text embeddings and DINOv2 features, followed by a background-cleaning step that convexly combines the similarity map with a category-specific attention-weighted foreground map (λ = 5/6) to suppress background regions. The approach requires only the DINOv2 (with registers) visual encoder and the CLIP text encoder, with no additional visual backbone or retrieval database at test time.

## Results

With mask refinement (PAMR), Talk2DINO using DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (registers) achieves an average mIoU of 46.3 across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest competitor FreeDA (CLIP+DINOv2) at 42.2 and ProxyCLIP (CLIP+DINO) at 42.3. On Pascal VOC-20, it reaches 88.5 mIoU, and on COCO Objects (with background) it achieves 45.1 mIoU. The ViT-L/14 variant attains 46.1 average mIoU, with 89.8 on Pascal VOC-20 and 66.1 on Pascal VOC-21. Notably, Talk2DINO uses only a frozen DINOv2 visual backbone (no CLIP visual encoder), yet surpasses methods that combine both CLIP and DINOv2 visual features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Talk2DINO achieves the highest average mIoU (46.3) among all methods with mask refinement, beating the closest competitors FreeDA (CLIP+DINOv2, 42.2) and ProxyCLIP (CLIP+DINO, 42.3) by over 4 points on average. The most direct comparison is with FreeDA using DINOv2 only (39.4 avg), which also bridges DINOv2 and CLIP text but via retrieval-based prototype construction; Talk2DINO's direct learned projection yields a +6.9 mIoU improvement, demonstrating that training a nonlinear warping is more effective than retrieval. On Pascal VOC-20, Talk2DINO leads by 3.3 points over FreeDA (CLIP+DINOv2) and 5.4 over ProxyCLIP (CLIP+DINO). The one benchmark where Talk2DINO does not lead is Cityscapes, where ProxyCLIP (CLIP+DINO) achieves 38.6 vs. Talk2DINO's 38.1, likely due to the driving-scene context favoring CLIP's visual features. Critically, Talk2DINO uses only a frozen DINOv2 backbone (no CLIP visual encoder at inference), while most competitors require both CLIP and DINO/DINOv2 visual features, making it more computationally efficient at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in unsupervised OVS: CLIP-based methods that attempt to fix spatial localization through architectural modifications (SCLIP, NACLIP, ClearCLIP), and DINO-based methods that exploit self-attention for foreground localization but lack language grounding (FreeDA, dino.txt). Talk2DINO departs from both by discarding CLIP's visual encoder entirely and instead learning a lightweight nonlinear projection that maps CLIP's text embeddings directly into DINOv2's patch-level visual space, using DINOv2's self-attention heads as a mechanism for selective spatial alignment during training. Unlike ProxyCLIP or CLIP-DINOiser, which combine both visual backbones at inference time, Talk2DINO requires only DINOv2 as the visual backbone, reducing computational overhead while achieving comparable or superior results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DINOv2's self-supervised visual space is intrinsically compatible with CLIP's text space (a simple linear projection already recovers most of the performance), which opens the door to using any strong self-supervised visual backbone as a spatial encoder paired with a language model's text encoder. The approach is practical: it trains only a small mapping function without fine-tuning either backbone, making it easy to adopt. Limitations include the strong dependence on DINOv2's specific attention properties (ablations show DINO v1 and MAE fail to align), the reliance on registers for clean attention maps, and the fact that the method still requires ~80k image-text pairs for training. An open question is whether this projection-based bridging generalizes to other self-supervised architectures or to tasks beyond segmentation.
