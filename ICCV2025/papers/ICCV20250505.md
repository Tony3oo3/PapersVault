## Problem

Automated evaluation of instruction-guided image editing lacks a reliable, transparent, and scalable solution: traditional pixel-level and CLIP-based metrics ignore edit instructions, open-source VLMs fail to align with human judgment, and proprietary VLMs are opaque and expensive. Critically, no public training dataset exists to fine-tune open-source VLMs as editing scorers, with only small benchmarks using diverse and incompatible evaluation schemes.

## Input / Output

The scorer receives a pair of RGB images (the original input and the edited output) concatenated as multi-image input to the VLM, along with a natural-language edit instruction as text query. It outputs a single continuous numeric score in the range 0–10, decoded from the embedding of a dedicated [SCORE] token through a small MLP head rather than from free-form text generation. This design avoids the hallucination and format-inconsistency issues of prompting VLMs to write scores as text.

## Method

ADIEE automatically constructs a 100K+ sample evaluation dataset without human annotation by exploiting two sources: (1) running nine text-guided editing methods on MagicBrush and assigning scores via CLIP directional similarity (negative CLIP-D → 0, below 0.2 threshold → 0.5, ground-truth → 1) with CLIP-I/DINO-I percentile thresholds to confirm visual failures, and (2) sampling image pairs from multi-turn editing sequences (SEED-Data-Edit) where the score is the fraction of intervening instructions completed (0 for pre-input images, linear interpolation for intermediate edits, 0.5 for over-edited outputs). The scorer is LLaVA-NeXT-8B fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8, 30 epochs) on this dataset, with a special [SCORE] token added to the vocabulary whose final hidden-state embedding is mapped to a scalar score by a multi-layer MLP, following the LISA-style token-decoding paradigm. The trained scorer can additionally serve as a reward model, enabling reward-conditioned fine-tuning of MagicBrush to improve editing quality.

## Results

On AURORA-Bench (point-wise), ADIEE achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.4734 with human ratings, surpassing GPT-4o (0.4038) by 0.0696 (+17.24%) and Gemini-Pro 1.5 (0.1052) by a wide margin. On ImagenHub, ADIEE reaches 0.3450 correlation, trailing only GPT-4o (0.3821) while outperforming all open-source VLMs and Gemini-Pro 1.5 (0.2728). For pair-wise comparison, ADIEE achieves 59.96% accuracy on GenAI-Bench (+4.03% over GPT-4o's 53.54%) and 55.56% on AURORA-Bench (+4.75% over GPT-4o's 50.81%), setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADIEE outperforms all 16 baselines on AURORA-Bench (point-wise), with the closest competitor being GPT-4o (0.4038 vs. 0.4734, a +0.0696 gap). On ImagenHub, GPT-4o (0.3821) narrowly edges out ADIEE (0.3450), making it the only baseline ADIEE does not surpass on that benchmark; all other proprietary and open-source VLMs (Gemini-Pro 1.5 at 0.2728, Qwen2.5-VL at 0.1859, LLaVA-OneVision at 0.0829, etc.) are clearly behind. Among open-source VLMs, the strongest is Qwen2.5-VL (0.2351 on AURORA-Bench), still 0.2383 below ADIEE. For pair-wise comparison, ADIEE leads GPT-4o by 4.03% on GenAI-Bench (59.96% vs. 53.54%) and by 4.75% on AURORA-Bench (55.56% vs. 50.81%), while Gemini-Pro 1.5 performs poorly on AURORA-Bench pair-wise (28.13%). Traditional metrics (CLIP-D, CLIP-T, CLIP-I, DINO-I) all fall well below ADIEE on every benchmark, with CLIP-D being the strongest at 0.3080 on AURORA-Bench—still 0.1654 below ADIEE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLM-as-judge paradigm for image editing evaluation (VIEScore, MEGA-Bench, GenAI-Bench, AURORA-Bench) and the LISA-style score-token-plus-MLP decoder architecture, but departs from prior training-data creation approaches (OmniEdit, RewardEdit20K) that rely on proprietary VLMs like GPT-4o to generate labels. The core conceptual departure is the observation that instruction-guided editing training datasets (MagicBrush, SEED-Data-Edit) implicitly encode evaluation supervision: ground-truth outputs are high-scoring positives while input images serve as canonical failure negatives, enabling fully automated label assignment without human annotation or expensive API calls. The multi-turn sequence scoring scheme (Eq. 4) further extends this by exploiting temporal structure in editing sequences to produce graded intermediate scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100K+ sample dataset and the resulting scorer provide a practical, reproducible path to competitive open-source editing evaluation without dependence on proprietary APIs, which is significant given the cost and opacity barriers of GPT-4o-based pipelines. The dual use as both an evaluation metric and a reward model (boosting MagicBrush by ~9%) demonstrates a useful feedback loop for editing model improvement. However, the scorer still trails GPT-4o on ImagenHub (0.3450 vs 0.3821), the score-assignment heuristics (e.g., blanket 0.5 for over-edits in multi-turn sequences) are somewhat arbitrary, and the approach's generalizability beyond the specific editing models and datasets used for data generation remains to be validated at scale.
