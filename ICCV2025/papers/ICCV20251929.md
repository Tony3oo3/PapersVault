## Problem

Most domain generalization methods pursue a single style-invariant model that discards domain-specific information, potentially losing useful domain-specific statistical properties. This paper asks whether a model with domain-specialized components—each tailored to the unique characteristics of one source domain—can outperform a monolithic invariant model when their predictions are merged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed as 16×16 patches by ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16) from multiple source domains and outputs a single class label. During inference, a learned domain router computes softmax weights over all domain-specific adapters from the MSA output embedding, dynamically routing each image to the adapter best suited to its domain characteristics. The final prediction is a weighted sum of adapter outputs, so no explicit domain label is needed at test time.

## Method

CDA inserts one lightweight adapter per training domain into each ViT block (after the MSA), where each adapter is either a ViT-style bottleneck (down-projection → GELU → up-projection, r=48) or a ResNet-like Conv block (1×1 → 3×3 → 1×1 convolutions, c=8), chosen to match the domain's visual properties (Conv for color/texture-rich domains, ViT for structure-dominated domains). A learnable domain router (a linear gate W applied to the MSA output) produces softmax weights that dynamically assign each image to the most appropriate adapter, and a cross-entropy router loss explicitly trains this gate to identify the correct domain. The overall training objective combines standard ERM classification loss with a weighted router loss (λ ∈ [0.01, 0.1]), and the full backbone is fine-tuned alongside the adapters. Adapter-to-domain assignment is determined either empirically (training single-adapter models and selecting the better performer per domain) or via GPT-4 guidance on a sample image, both yielding comparable results.

## Results

Using a ViT-S/16 backbone (21.7M params), CDA achieves 89.4% on PACS, 81.0% on VLCS, 76.1% on OfficeHome, 51.3% on Terra Incognita, and 50.3% on DomainNet, setting new state-of-the-art on four of five benchmarks. Compared to the best prior ViT-S method (GMoE: 88.1/80.2/74.2/48.5/48.7), CDA gains +1.3%, +0.8%, +1.9%, +2.8%, and +1.6% respectively. The GPT-guided adapter assignment variant (CDA w GPT) achieves nearly identical results (89.3/80.7/75.5/50.8/50.1) at significantly lower setup cost. Scaling to ViT-B/16, CDA reaches 90.1% on PACS and 54.5% on DomainNet with only 20.5–28.7 ms inference latency, versus 160.2 ms for GMoE at the same scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDA outperforms the strongest ResNet-50-based baselines (SWAD 88.1/79.1/70.6/50.0/46.5 and PCL 88.7/–/71.6/52.1/47.7) on PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, and DomainNet, while trailing PCL by 0.8% on Terra Incognita (51.3 vs 52.1). Against the closest same-backbone competitor GMoE (ViT-S), CDA wins on all five datasets with margins of +1.3% (PACS), +0.8% (VLCS), +1.9% (OfficeHome), +2.8% (TerraInc), and +1.6% (DomainNet). The GPT-guided adapter-assignment variant (CDA w GPT) matches the full method within 0.1–0.5% across all benchmarks, confirming that LLM-based domain characterization is a practical alternative to the empirical two-model evaluation. CDA is also shown to be orthogonal to algorithmic DG strategies: combining CDA with SWAD yields 90.3% on PACS (+0.9% over CDA alone) and 51.0% on DomainNet, while CDA + W2D reaches 89.9% and 50.7% respectively. On the CLIP ViT-B/16 setting, CDA achieves 98.6% on PACS and 91.2% on OfficeHome, surpassing DPL (97.3/84.2) and CAR-FT (96.8/85.7), though slightly below SIMPLE+ (99.0) on PACS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant domain generalization paradigm of learning a single style-invariant model by instead constructing domain-specific parameter-efficient adapters (ViT and Conv) within a shared ViT backbone, drawing on the known inductive bias differences between CNNs and transformers. It builds directly on the adapter architecture of Houlsby et al. [18] and the MoE routing mechanism, but diverges sharply from GMoE [27] by customizing each expert's architecture to match domain properties (e.g., texture-rich vs. structure-dominant) rather than using uniform FFN experts. The domain router with an explicit domain-classification loss is a new component not present in standard MoE frameworks, and the use of GPT-4 to guide adapter-to-domain assignment introduces an LLM-in-the-loop design choice absent from prior DG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—from one invariant model to an ensemble of domain-specialized components—offers a complementary axis of improvement that is orthogonal to existing DG regularization and augmentation methods, as demonstrated by the complementarity experiments with SWAD and W2D. The parameter-efficiency advantage over GMoE (roughly 30% fewer parameters and 3× lower inference latency) makes the approach more practical for deployment. However, scalability remains an open question: the number of adapters grows linearly with the number of training domains, and the method still requires domain IDs during training, limiting its applicability to settings with many or unknown domains. Future work on adaptive, ID-free domain representation and more principled adapter-architecture selection would strengthen the approach.
