## Problem

Existing audio-visual acoustic synthesis methods learn sound propagation implicitly from sparse image-audio pairs within neural networks, which fails to capture the physical properties governing sound behavior (room geometry, material properties, emitter/receiver geometry). This leads to significant performance degradation when generalizing to novel sound source positions. The paper asks whether explicitly integrating physics-based sound propagation priors into the audio-visual modeling pipeline can resolve this generalization gap while maintaining realism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images with corresponding camera poses (used to reconstruct a 3D scene mesh via NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting), a mono source sound signal, and the 3D positions of both the sound emitter and the listener. The output is a binaural audio signal (2×n) at the novel listener position, where binaural rendering is achieved by applying a Head-Related Transfer Function conditioned on the listener's head direction. The 3D mesh reconstruction is an intermediate step that converts the visual input into a geometric representation compatible with ray-tracing-based acoustic simulation.

## Method

π-AVAS is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 performs vision-guided audio simulation: a 3D mesh is reconstructed from images (NeRF/Gaussian Splatting → point clouds → Poisson surface reconstruction), and sound propagation between arbitrary source-receiver pairs is modeled by tracing sound rays through the mesh, computing direct and reflected energy via a Neumann series expansion over the mesh surface, then applying HRTF for binaural output. Stage 2 refines the coarsely simulated sound using a conditional flow matching model that learns a vector field transforming the simulated distribution N(x|x_sim, σ²I) to the ground-truth distribution N(x|x_rx, σ²I) along a linear probability path; the vector field is predicted by a network of multi-scale gated convolution blocks (inspired by WaveNet and ViGAS) conditioned on source sound, pose, and timestep via Random Gaussian Fourier Embedding, trained with an STFT-domain L2 loss. A data augmentation strategy shifts training poses along interpolated camera trajectories to alleviate the scarcity of per-scene samples (only 600–1200 at 1 fps).

## Results

On the standard RWAVS benchmark, π-AVAS achieves the best overall MAG of 1.373 and ENV of 0.134, outperforming the previous best (SOAF at 1.411 MAG / 0.142 ENV) by 2.7% and 5.6% respectively. On the novel RWAVS-Gen generalization benchmark, π-AVAS achieves 2.257 MAG and 0.159 ENV, a substantial improvement over the best prior method (NAF at 2.900 MAG / 0.183 ENV), representing a 22% reduction in MAG. On the RAF room impulse response dataset, π-AVAS achieves T60 of 4.8 ms (furnished) and 5.0 ms (empty), beating the prior SOTA AVR (5.0 and 5.5 ms) while training in 5 hours versus AVR's 24 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π-AVAS outperforms all neural rendering baselines (INRAS, NAF, ViGAS, AV-NeRF, AV-GS, SOAF) on both MAG and ENV across all four RWAVS environments, with the largest gains in the Office scene (0.674 MAG vs. SOAF's 0.828) and Outdoors (0.785 vs. SOAF's 0.770, where it is closest). On the RWAVS-Gen generalization benchmark, the advantage is even more pronounced: π-AVAS achieves 2.257 MAG versus NAF's 2.900 and AV-NeRF's 3.168, confirming that physics-based simulation is critical for novel sound sources. On the RAF dataset, π-AVAS surpasses the prior SOTA AVR on T60 (4.8 vs. 5.0 ms furnished), C50 (0.81 vs. 0.95), EDT (16.3 vs. 17.9), and Amplitude (0.24 vs. 0.75), while matching AVR on Phase (1.58) and slightly trailing on Envelope (4.95 vs. 4.52 furnished). Both traditional codec baselines (AAC and Opus) are substantially outperformed on all RAF metrics. The paper further demonstrates that its simulation module can be plugged into existing methods (e.g., improving AV-NeRF's MAG by 0.772 on RWAVS-Gen), showing broad applicability beyond the full pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in audio-visual acoustic synthesis (NAF, INRAS, ViGAS, AV-NeRF, AV-GS, SOAF) where sound propagation is learned implicitly from sparse image-audio pairs within neural fields. Instead, it explicitly reconstructs 3D scene geometry via NeRF or Gaussian Splatting and performs physics-based ray-traced sound propagation, then bridges the realism gap with a conditional flow matching model (building on Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) that learns the residual between simulated and recorded audio. The method also repurposes standard 3D reconstruction tools (Poisson surface reconstruction) and classical acoustics (Neumann series expansion, HRTF) in a pipeline that prior neural-rendering approaches had not combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of implicit neural audio synthesis—poor generalization to novel sound sources due to insufficient geometric information in sparse training images—and offers a practical two-stage recipe (physics simulation + generative refinement) that addresses it. The RWAVS-Gen benchmark isolates generalization to unseen emitters, filling a gap in evaluation that prior benchmarks conflated with in-distribution rendering. Open questions remain around estimating surface material properties from visual cues, modeling low-frequency propagation accurately, and incorporating background noise, all of which limit the current simulation's fidelity. The demonstrated plug-in applicability (Table 4) suggests the simulation module could become a standard preprocessing step for existing neural acoustic synthesis pipelines.
