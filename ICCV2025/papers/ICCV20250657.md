## Problem

Road infrastructure improvements are conventionally reactive—experts analyze causes only after an accident occurs—which is unacceptable for autonomous driving systems that require proactive risk mitigation. No structured dataset exists that links accident-causing road structures to corresponding infrastructure improvement proposals, and state-of-the-art generalist LVLMs lack the domain-specific knowledge to reliably identify such risks from road images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a front-view road structure image (from Mapillary Vistas or BDD100K) paired with a text description of traffic risks generated via a graph-based grounded chain-of-thought (G2CoT) prompt using GPT-4o. It produces a multi-label prediction over 10 predefined infrastructure improvement categories (e.g., road alignment, surface markings, signage) as sigmoid class probabilities, and optionally a visually edited image of the improved road via Instruct Pix2Pix. The text input is not free-form but is constrained to ontology instances drawn from 11 road-structure types and 10 countermeasure types.

## Method

The core contribution is an ontology-driven pipeline: expert knowledge of road traffic systems is formalized as a directed reference graph (11 accident-causing road structures → 10 improvement types), and GPT-4o-generated candidate proposals are filtered via graph matching (intersection of node/edge sets, followed by removal of isolated nodes) to ensure consistency with expert reasoning. The trained model (OD-RASE) combines a vision encoder (Long-CLIP performs best), a text encoder (RoBERTa-Base), and a multi-head cross-attention grounding block that treats image tokens as queries and text tokens as keys/values, feeding a fully connected head for multi-label classification. A separate Instruct Pix2Pix diffusion module takes the original image and a prompt derived from the predicted improvements to render a post-improvement road visualization. Ablations show that ontology-driven filtering raises F1 from 44.26 to 70.26 and accuracy from 0.00 to 42.14, and that the trained model substantially outperforms generalist LVLMs (GPT-4o, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, Phi-3, InternVL2) in zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting (trained on BDD100K, evaluated on Mapillary), the best OD-RASE configuration (Long-CLIP + RoBERTa-Base) achieves an F1-Score of 68.32 and Accuracy of 38.96 on the Mapillary test set. In the reverse direction (trained on Mapillary, evaluated on BDD100K), the same configuration reaches an F1-Score of 76.00 and Accuracy of 44.76. The ontology-driven data filtering ablation (Table 3) shows that filtering improves F1 from 44.26 to 70.26 and Accuracy from 0.00 to 42.14, confirming the critical role of expert-knowledge-based filtering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OD-RASE (Long-CLIP + RoBERTa-Base) decisively outperforms all five generalist LVLM baselines in both zero-shot directions. On the Mapillary test set, OD-RASE achieves F1 of 68.32 versus GPT-4o's 34.27 (a 34-point gap), Phi-3's 27.47, Qwen2-VL's 23.97, LLaVA-1.5's 23.75, and InternVL2's 18.83. The same pattern holds on BDD100K, where OD-RASE reaches F1 of 76.00 while the strongest generalist (GPT-4o) manages only 29.38. GPT-4o is the closest competitor but still trails by over 30 F1 points, and its low precision (24.97 vs. 75.57) indicates it generates many false-positive improvement proposals. The authors conclude that existing generalist models lack sufficient domain-specific knowledge to reliably identify accident-causing road structures and propose appropriate infrastructure improvements, underscoring the necessity of the ontology-driven filtering approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on three distinct research threads: (1) traditional traffic engineering literature on road infrastructure improvements (roundabouts, lane reductions, signage), which the authors formalize into a structured ontology of 11 accident-causing road structures and 10 countermeasures; (2) LVLM-based dataset generation for autonomous driving (e.g., DriveLM, graph-based VQA), from which they adopt GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting but depart by introducing ontology-driven graph matching as a quality-control filter rather than relying on random human sampling; and (3) multi-modal classification architectures (CLIP, Long-CLIP, cross-attention grounding blocks) for the downstream prediction model. The key conceptual departure from prior autonomous-driving safety work is shifting the focus from object-level risk (pedestrians, vehicles) to the road infrastructure itself as the root cause, a dimension the authors argue has been systematically neglected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes autonomous-driving safety from a reactive, post-accident paradigm to a proactive one by identifying at-risk road structures before failures occur, which is a meaningful shift for the field. The ontology-driven filtering mechanism (graph intersection + isolated-node removal) offers a reusable pattern for improving LVLM-generated annotation quality beyond this specific domain. However, significant open questions remain: the analysis is limited to single front-view images without time-series or 3D road geometry, there is no quantitative validation of whether the proposed improvements actually reduce accidents (no traffic simulator is used), and the absolute accuracy figures (38–49%) suggest the system is far from deployment-ready. Scaling to more complex or region-specific road structures and incorporating GIS/gradient data are natural next steps.
