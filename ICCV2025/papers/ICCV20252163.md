## Problem

Multimodal LLMs incur massive computational costs because transformer-based LLMs must process an overwhelming volume of vision tokens (up to 2880 for high-resolution inputs). While prior work compresses tokens before the LLM, the LLM's own transformer layers could selectively skip redundant vision tokens, but integrating Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) into pre-trained MLLMs is non-trivial due to training instability, limited multimodal data, and the fact that token redundancy varies across layers.

## Input / Output

The model accepts high-resolution images (sliced into multiple patches by the vision encoder, yielding up to 2880 vision tokens for LLaVA-NeXT) paired with a text question, and produces a free-form text answer. At each transformer layer, only a fraction R_i% of vision tokens are actually processed by the attention and FFN sub-layers; the remaining tokens pass through unchanged, reducing both FLOPs and KV cache. Text tokens are always fully processed at every layer—MoD is applied exclusively to vision tokens.

## Method

p-MoD upgrades the standard MoD layer with three components. First, TanhNorm normalizes predicted token weights via f(w)=α·tanh(w), ensuring zero-centered, bounded weights that stabilize training when inserting MoD into a pre-trained LLM. Second, STRing applies symmetric token reweighting to both selected and skipped tokens, so the weight predictor receives gradient signal from all tokens and can learn importance scoring from limited multimodal data. Third, and most critically, the Progressive Ratio Decay (PRD) strategy sets a per-layer retention ratio R_l = 0.5·cos(πl/L) + β following a shifted cosine schedule, gradually reducing the fraction of vision tokens processed from shallow to deep layers. This exploits the empirical finding that vision tokens become increasingly redundant in deeper layers, and the shift factor β provides a single knob to trade off efficiency against accuracy.

## Results

p-MoD-LLaVA-NeXT achieves an average of 62.3 across 10 benchmarks (vs. 62.4 for the LLaVA-NeXT baseline) while requiring only 55.6% inference TFLOPs (21.94 vs. 39.46) and 53.7% KV cache storage. p-MoD-LLaVA-v1.5 achieves 49.8 average (vs. 49.6 baseline) with 41.3% fewer TFLOPs. Training cost is reduced to 77.7% of the baseline GPU hours. On the additional 5 benchmarks (Table 2), p-MoD-LLaVA-NeXT matches the baseline exactly at 65.6 average, and p-MoD-LLaVA-v1.5 is within 0.1 (64.4 vs. 64.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA-NeXT baseline, p-MoD matches or marginally exceeds performance on most benchmarks (62.3 vs. 62.4 average) while cutting inference TFLOPs by 44.4% and KV cache by 46.3%; it notably gains +0.2 on ChartQA and +1.1 on OK-VQA. Against LLaVA-v1.5, p-MoD slightly improves the average (49.8 vs. 49.6) with 41.3% fewer TFLOPs. In the head-to-head comparison with other vision-token compression methods at similar compression ratios (~53%), p-MoD substantially outperforms MQT (62.8 vs. 57.6 average), LLaVolta (62.8 vs. 62.0), and FastV (62.8 vs. 61.5), achieving the best score on 10 of 14 individual benchmarks. The key differentiator is that p-MoD performs learnable, layer-wise token selection within the LLM rather than fixed compression before or coarse dropping during the LLM, preserving fine-grained visual information needed for OCR-heavy tasks like DocVQA and ChartQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) mechanism from Raposo et al. [41], originally designed for training LLMs from scratch, to the multimodal setting where MoD modules must be inserted into pre-trained MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT) with limited training data. It departs from prior efficient-MLLM work (MQT, LLaVolta, FastV) that compresses vision tokens either before the LLM or via non-learnable heuristics (average pooling, attention-score-based dropping) in intermediate layers, instead proposing a fully learnable, layer-wise token selection mechanism. The three proposed modifications—TanhNorm, STRing, and Progressive Ratio Decay—address specific challenges absent in the original MoD formulation: numerical stability when integrating into frozen-then-fine-tuned LLMs, gradient starvation of the weight predictor under small multimodal datasets, and the non-uniform redundancy of vision tokens across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~44% reduction in inference TFLOPs and KV cache while matching or slightly exceeding baseline performance across 15 benchmarks makes this a practically important efficiency technique for deploying high-resolution MLLMs, where vision token volume is the dominant cost. The PRD strategy's empirical validation that vision tokens become progressively more redundant in deeper layers provides a principled schedule for compute allocation that could inform future efficient-MLLM designs beyond MoD. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video inputs (where temporal redundancy may interact differently with depth), whether it generalizes to LLM backbones beyond the LLaVA family, and whether the shifted cosine schedule remains optimal at larger model scales or with different token counts.
