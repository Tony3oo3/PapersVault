## Problem

Transformer-based Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) suffer from an 'attention distraction' phenomenon where models disproportionately allocate high attention weights to irrelevant background tokens, neglecting critical object-related features and degrading classification accuracy. This issue arises because spiking attention modules largely inherit the uniform softmax(QK^T)V propagation paradigm from analog Transformers, which lacks a mechanism to differentially regulate contextual relevance. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a biologically inspired lateral inhibition mechanism into the spiking attention pipeline.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either static RGB images (N=3 channels) or neuromorphic event streams (N=2 channels) as input, represented as I ∈ R^{T×N×H×W}, and produces a single class label. Input is processed through a hierarchical 3-stage patchification pipeline (4×4, 2×2, 2×2 patches) that progressively reduces spatial resolution from H/4×W/4 to H/16×W/16 tokens while expanding channel dimensions. Inference uses 4 time steps for static image datasets and 16 time steps for neuromorphic datasets, with the LIF spiking neuron layer converting continuous membrane potentials into binary spike trains at each step.

## Method

SpiLiFormer introduces two lateral inhibition-inspired attention modules into a 3-stage hierarchical spiking Transformer. In shallow stages (1–2), FF-LiDiff attention splits the Query into two channel halves, converts each to spike trains, and sums along the channel dimension to produce separate excitatory (Ae) and inhibitory (Ai) token-importance vectors; their element-wise difference is passed through a spiking neuron layer to yield a binary attention mask applied via Hadamard product to the spiking Key, emulating retinal center-surround inhibition. In the deep stage (3), FB-LiDiff attention performs two forward propagations: the first computes standard spike-driven attention, while the second injects feedback from the final block's output through two learnable prompt vectors (W_p1, W_p2) that generate excitatory and inhibitory feedback attention signals; their difference is linearly transformed and spiking-activated before being added to the Value pathway, simulating long-range cortical lateral inhibition. Training uses surrogate gradients with a combined cross-entropy loss (α=0.5) over both forward passes, and all FB-LiDiff blocks share parameters to minimize model size.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SpiLiFormer (69.10M params, 4 time steps, 384 resolution) achieves 86.66% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA spiking Transformer E-SpikeFormer (173.0M params, 8 time steps, 384 resolution) by 0.46% while using only 39% of the parameters and half the time steps. On CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CIFAR10-DVS, and N-Caltech101, SpiLiFormer achieves 96.63%, 81.63%, 86.7%, and 89.18% top-1 accuracy respectively, setting new SOTA on all four datasets. The model also demonstrates improved adversarial robustness (FGSM/PGD) and more focused attention heatmaps on noisy datasets (CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-1K-C) compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpiLiFormer outperforms all eight baselines on ImageNet-1K. Against the closest SNN competitor QKFormer (64.96M, 4 steps, 384 res), it gains 1.01% top-1 accuracy (86.66% vs. 85.65%) with comparable parameters. Against E-SpikeFormer (173.0M, 8 steps, 384 res), the previous SOTA spiking Transformer, it improves by 0.46% while using only 39% of the parameters and half the time steps. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, SpiLiFormer beats QKFormer by 0.45% and 0.48% respectively with only 0.04% more parameters. On neuromorphic datasets, the gains are larger: +2.70% on CIFAR10-DVS and +1.94% on N-Caltech101 over QKFormer. Compared to ANN baselines, SpiLiFormer surpasses Swin Transformer-B (384 res) by 2.16% while reducing energy consumption by 40% and parameters by 18.67M, and exceeds DeiT-B (384 res) by 3.56%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based SNN lineage (Spikformer, Spikingformer, S-Transformer, QKFormer, E-SpikeFormer) by identifying 'attention distraction'—over-allocation of attention to irrelevant background—as a fundamental limitation inherited from the standard softmax(QK^T)V paradigm. It departs from prior spiking attention designs (SSA, SDSA, S-RWKV, STA) by introducing two novel attention mechanisms, FF-LiDiff and FB-LiDiff, that explicitly separate excitatory and inhibitory pathways, drawing on the neuroscience of retinal and cortical lateral inhibition. While lateral inhibition has appeared in other SNN contexts (LISNN for object recognition, Spiking-LEAF for speech), its integration into a hierarchical Transformer architecture with feedback loops is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The efficiency result is the most practically significant finding: matching or exceeding E-SpikeFormer's accuracy with 39% of the parameters and half the time steps suggests that architectural sophistication (lateral inhibition) can substitute for brute-force scaling in SNNs. The attention distraction diagnosis provides a useful conceptual lens for diagnosing and fixing spiking attention modules more broadly. Open questions include whether the two-forward-pass FB-LiDiff mechanism (which adds 21–23% inference latency on static data) can be made single-pass, whether the approach transfers to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), and whether the mechanism generalizes to other attention variants beyond the Hadamard-product formulation.
