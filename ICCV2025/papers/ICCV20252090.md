## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for VLMs like CLIP suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: modality isolation, where text and vision encoders are tuned independently or coupled only one-way, preventing mutual semantic refinement; and hierarchical semantic decay, where reliance on final-layer features discards transferable low-level representations from intermediate layers, limiting out-of-distribution generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252090/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (split into fixed-size patches with a class token) and a class name text prompt, both augmented with 16 learnable prompt tokens injected at every layer of the ViT-B/16 backbone. It produces a class probability distribution computed via temperature-scaled cosine similarity between the prompted image embedding and prompted text embeddings of all candidate classes. The layer boundary k=6 partitions the 12 transformer layers into two phases with reversed knowledge flow directions.

## Method

HiCroPL establishes bidirectional knowledge flow between text and vision modalities: in layers 1–k, text prompts inject discriminative semantics into visual prompts, while in layers k+1–L, visually enriched prompts reflux to ground text prompts in task-relevant visual concepts. A lightweight Layer-specific Knowledge Proxy (LKP) compresses m prompts per layer into a single proxy token via cross-attention, reducing dimensionality while preserving layer-specific context. The hierarchical knowledge mapper, implemented as a multi-head attention module with layer normalization and feed-forward networks, fuses multi-scale cross-modal knowledge so that deeper prompts retain transferable shallow semantics. Training combines cross-entropy classification loss with a consistency regularization term (λ=12) that aligns prompted embeddings with frozen CLIP embeddings derived from LLM-generated class descriptions, preserving zero-shot capabilities.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task across 11 benchmarks, HiCroPL achieves an average harmonic mean (HM) of 81.75%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CoPrompt (80.47%) by 1.28% HM, 1.89% on base classes, and 0.76% on novel classes. The largest per-dataset gain is on FGVCAircraft (44.82% HM vs. 39.76% for CoPrompt, +5.06%) and DTD (75.17% HM vs. 72.79%, +2.38%). In cross-dataset evaluation (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 other datasets), HiCroPL achieves the best average of 67.68%, with a standout +6.12% gain over CoPrompt on DTD. In few-shot learning (K=1 to 16), HiCroPL yields average gains of 5.40% (1-shot) down to 1.51% (16-shot) over prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCroPL outperforms all 9 baselines on the average HM across 11 datasets. Against the closest competitor CoPrompt (ICLR 2024), it gains +1.89% base, +0.76% novel, and +1.28% HM on average, while also surpassing PromptSRC by +1.63% on base classes. Compared to MaPLe (CVPR 2023), the first method to explore inter-modal coupling, HiCroPL improves by +3.61% base, +2.85% novel, and +3.20% HM, validating the benefit of bidirectional over one-way knowledge flow. On the fine-grained FGVCAircraft dataset, HiCroPL achieves 44.82% HM versus 39.76% for CoPrompt and 40.15% for PromptSRC, a gap of over 5 points. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), HiCroPL attains the best average (67.68%) and leads on 6 of 10 target datasets, with the largest margin (+6.12%) on DTD over CoPrompt. In domain generalization (Table 3), HiCroPL matches or exceeds the best baseline on 3 of 5 ImageNet variants (V2, S, R) while remaining competitive on A, confirming robustness to distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCroPL extends the multi-modal prompt learning paradigm initiated by MaPLe, which first coupled text and vision prompts but only in a one-way (text-to-vision) direction. The paper departs from the broader prompt learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, KgCoOp, PromptSRC, CoPrompt) by introducing bidirectional knowledge flow—text-to-vision in shallow layers and vision-to-text in deeper layers—motivated by the complementary strengths of each modality at different network depths. A key architectural departure is the hierarchical knowledge mapper with a Layer-specific Knowledge Proxy (LKP), which fuses multi-scale cross-modal features rather than relying solely on final-layer representations, directly addressing what the authors term 'hierarchical semantic decay.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of why existing prompt learning methods struggle with generalization (modality isolation + hierarchical semantic decay) and offers a unified mechanism to address both. The consistent 1–2% gains across 11 benchmarks and four evaluation settings (base-to-novel, few-shot, cross-dataset, domain generalization) validate the bidirectional flow design, and the ablation showing T→I|I→T substantially outperforms I→T|T→I gives practical guidance on layer scheduling. However, the method is evaluated only on ViT-B/16 CLIP for image classification, and the domain generalization results are competitive but not clearly superior to CoPrompt, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger backbones, generative VLMs, or non-classification tasks.
