## Problem

Cross-attention maps derived from diffusion models focus on semantic relevance but lack spatial consistency, causing text-guided edits to spill into unintended regions and produce background drift or object identity loss. Naively combining cross- and self-attention to improve localization causes significant information loss, and existing dual-branch editing paradigms still suffer from over-editing artifacts. The paper addresses the need for a training-free mechanism that precisely confines modifications to designated regions while preserving the surrounding image structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source prompt describing the original content, and a target prompt specifying the desired edit (with 'blend words' identifying which terms to modify). It operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net, upsampled to R×R resolution for fine-grained patch-level attention refinement. The output is a single edited image where changes are spatially confined to the target region, with non-editable areas preserved via a learned mask applied at every denoising step.

## Method

LOCATEdit constructs a CASA (Cross And Self-Attention) graph for both source and target branches, where nodes represent patch-level cross-attention values and edges encode symmetrized self-attention relationships between patches. A graph Laplacian regularizer is applied to this graph, yielding a convex optimization with a closed-form solution m* = (Λ + λL)⁻¹Λm₀ that smooths attention values across spatially connected patches while preserving high-confidence regions via a diagonal confidence matrix. A selective pruning operator thresholds the CLIP text-embedding difference to suppress low-magnitude spurious offsets before feeding them through an IP-Adapter for semantic guidance. The refined masks from both branches are combined via element-wise maximum and used to blend target and source latents at each denoising step, ensuring non-target regions remain unchanged throughout the diffusion process.

## Results

On PIE-Bench under the PnP-I/DDIM(50) setting, LOCATEdit achieves the best background preservation across all four metrics: PSNR 29.20, LPIPS 41.60×10⁻³, MSE 26.90×10⁻⁴, and SSIM 86.53×10⁻², while also attaining the highest CLIP Edited similarity (23.02). Under the EF/DPM-Solver++(20) setting, LOCATEdit further achieves the best structure distance (8.71×10⁻³), PSNR (29.16), LPIPS (39.31×10⁻³), MSE (24.01×10⁻⁴), SSIM (86.52×10⁻²), and CLIP Whole similarity (26.07). The method slightly trails P2P (11.64) and ViMAEdit (11.90) on structure distance under PnP-I (13.19), but leads on every other metric in that setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor ViMAEdit [45], LOCATEdit wins on PSNR (29.20 vs 28.75), LPIPS (41.60 vs 43.07), MSE (26.90 vs 28.85), SSIM (86.53 vs 85.95), and CLIP Edited (23.02 vs 22.40), but loses slightly on structure distance (13.19 vs 11.90). Compared to P2P [10], LOCATEdit substantially improves background preservation (PSNR 29.20 vs 27.19, LPIPS 41.60 vs 54.44) and CLIP Edited similarity (23.02 vs 22.13), at the cost of a small structure distance increase (13.19 vs 11.64). P2P-Zero [30] and MasaCtrl [4] are clearly outperformed on all metrics, with P2P-Zero showing particularly poor background preservation (LPIPS 143.87 vs 41.60). Under the EF inverse setting, LOCATEdit dominates all baselines including LEDITS++ [2] and InfEdit [50], achieving the best structure distance (8.71 vs 14.16 for ViMAEdit) and CLIP Whole similarity (26.07 vs 25.51). Overall, LOCATEdit trades a marginal increase in structure distance for significantly better background fidelity and semantic alignment, which the authors argue is the more important trade-off for localized editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOCATEdit builds directly on the dual-branch editing paradigm of ViMAEdit [45] (IP-Adapter guidance, source-to-target cross-attention injection from P2P [10]) and extends it with a graph-based spatial regularization step. The key departure is the CASA graph formulation, which models cross-attention maps as node values and self-attention relationships as edge weights, then applies a graph Laplacian regularizer with a closed-form solution to enforce smooth, spatially coherent attention. This contrasts with prior methods that either naively combine cross- and self-attention (causing information loss) or rely on learned attention regularization losses (DPL, ViMAEdit) without an explicit geometric structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The persistent 'spill' problem in text-guided editing—where modifications leak into non-target regions—remains a practical bottleneck for applications requiring precise, localized edits. LOCATEdit's closed-form Laplacian solution is attractive for deployment since it avoids iterative refinement, and the selective pruning of CLIP embedding differences is a simple but effective noise filter. However, the ablation reveals that individual components (e.g., removing the diagonal weighting matrix) can outperform the full method on structure and background metrics, suggesting the components trade off spatial fidelity against semantic alignment rather than uniformly improving all axes. Evaluation is limited to PIE-Bench (700 images), and the authors themselves flag non-symmetric regularization and more complex editing scenarios as open directions.
