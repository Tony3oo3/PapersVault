## Problem

Existing synthetic image detectors rely on a singular forensic perspective, searching for universal traces that work across all generative techniques. The paper demonstrates empirically that specialized experts trained on specific synthetic methods outperform universal detectors, yet no prior work leverages multiple complementary experts to integrate diverse forensic traces for detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image cropped to 224×224 as input and produces a binary real/fake classification score via a linear head on the aggregated expert features. Internally, the image is processed by N expert adapters (default N=2) on a shared frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, and an Adaptive Weight Generator dynamically combines the N expert trace features into one representation before classification. The model also outputs a continuous fakeness score useful for ranking uncertain samples.

## Method

Forensic-MoE adopts an adapter-backbone architecture where multiple lightweight expert adapters are plugged into a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, each specialized to a different synthetic technique (e.g., ProGAN, SDv1.4). Training follows a two-step paradigm: first, each expert is trained independently on its corresponding synthetic dataset; second, all experts are jointly finetuned with an Expert Knowledge Distillation (EKD) loss that aligns real-image features across experts while encouraging diversity on fake images, plus a BCE classification loss. Each expert uses a multi-stage adapter (4 sub-adapters across ViT stages) with a patch decentralization strategy that inverts class-token attention to re-explore semantically ignored local regions, and applies Discrete Wavelet Transform to capture frequency-domain traces. An Adaptive Weight Generator (AWG) learns input-dependent weights to fuse the N expert outputs into a single discriminative feature for final binary classification.

## Results

Forensic-MoE achieves an overall mean accuracy of 95.14% across 16 test subsets (10 GAN + 7 diffusion), outperforming the best prior method (FatFormer†, 87.28%) by 7.86%. On the GAN-family mean, Forensic-MoE scores 96.59% (1.05% below the best GAN-specialized detector FatFormer† at 97.64%), while on the diffusion-family mean it scores 93.26% (3.53% below the best diffusion-specialized detector DIRE at 96.79%). The key advantage is balance: no single prior method exceeds 87.28% overall because each is limited to a singular forensic perspective, whereas Forensic-MoE's multi-expert design maintains high performance across both families simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Forensic-MoE outperforms all seven baselines on overall mean accuracy by a wide margin. Against the strongest GAN-specialized detector FatFormer† (87.28% overall), Forensic-MoE gains 7.86%, primarily because FatFormer† drops to 73.97% on diffusion images while Forensic-MoE maintains 93.26%. Against the strongest diffusion-specialized detector DIRE (87.00% overall), Forensic-MoE gains 8.14%, as DIRE scores only 79.38% on GAN images. UnivFD (83.79%) and NPR† (83.91%) are mid-tier, with NPR† showing a notable weakness on GAN (89.32%) and diffusion (76.94%). The earlier baselines—CNNSpot (70.80%), GramNet (72.05%), and LGrad (76.03%)—are substantially outperformed by 19–24 percentage points, reflecting their reliance on single-domain artifacts. Notably, extending FatFormer and NPR training with diffusion data (removing the †) does not improve their overall scores and sometimes degrades them (e.g., NPR drops from 83.91% to 76.56%), confirming that a singular detector cannot simultaneously capture diverse forensic traces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture of Experts paradigm from NLP (Shazeer et al., Lepikhin et al.) into the forensic image detection domain, where prior work (UnivFD, FatFormer, NPR, DIRE) has consistently pursued a single universal forensic perspective. It builds on the CLIP/ViT adapter fine-tuning lineage (FatFormer's forgery-aware adapter, CLIPping's transfer strategies) but departs fundamentally by training multiple specialized adapters on distinct synthetic techniques and integrating them into a shared frozen backbone, rather than seeking one detector that handles all generative models. The Expert Knowledge Distillation loss and patch decentralization strategy are novel contributions not present in prior MoE or forensic detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a compelling empirical argument that specialized forensic perspectives outperform universal ones, which challenges the dominant design assumption in synthetic image detection. The 7.86% mean accuracy gain and consistent superiority across both GAN and diffusion families suggest this multi-expert framing is a practical path toward more robust detection as new generative techniques emerge. However, open questions remain: the main experiments use only N=2 experts, generalization to truly unseen techniques (Table 2) still shows meaningful degradation, and the approach's scalability to video, 3D, or rapidly evolving generative paradigms is unexplored. The finding that adding diffusion training data to GAN-tuned detectors (FatFormer†, NPR†) actually degrades performance is a useful cautionary result for the field.
