## Problem

Existing monocular garment reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: implicit NeRF-based approaches produce smooth surfaces that lose high-frequency details like wrinkles and pleats, while explicit template-deformation methods rely on direct vertex displacement that causes jagged artifacts and cannot model dynamically varying topology (e.g., skirt pleats). Neither class handles extremely loose garments with large deformations well, and both struggle to separate geometric from appearance errors during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250694/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence (100 frames) along with pre-computed per-frame SMPL pose/shape parameters (from 4DHumans), normal/depth/part-segmentation maps (from Sapiens), and a base garment mesh in T-pose (from BCNet). It produces per-frame deformed garment meshes in canonical space that are skinned to the corresponding pose, plus a frame-invariant static texture map and per-frame dynamic texture maps. Geometry and appearance are optimized in separate modules to prevent mutual compensation of errors, and the base mesh topology is adaptively refined during training via remeshing.

## Method

NGD parameterizes garment deformation as a Jacobian field decomposed into a frame-invariant static component (global garment shape) and a frame-dependent dynamic component (pose-specific local deformations) predicted by a hash-grid encoder + MLP conditioned on face centers, normals, and PCA-encoded pose. The combined Jacobian field is converted to vertex positions via a Poisson solve, yielding a canonical mesh that is then skinned using SMPL parameters and optimized through NVDiffrast differentiable rendering with diffuse-image supervision (rather than normal maps) to avoid shadow-texture ambiguity. A gradient-based adaptive remeshing strategy selects edges for splitting/flipping based on accumulated rendering-loss gradients, iteratively increasing mesh resolution in high-curvature regions to capture fine details like folds and pockets. An exponentially decaying vertex noise heuristic prevents optimization from trapping in local minima, and appearance is recovered via a separate module learning static and dynamic texture maps with smooth-annealing pose noise for generalization.

## Results

On the 4D-Dress dataset, NGD achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.431×10³ and Normal Consistency of 0.853, outperforming DGarments (CD 0.649×10³, NC 0.833) and SCARF (CD 5.203×10³, NC 0.844) in geometry reconstruction. For novel view synthesis, NGD achieves PSNR up to 47.91 dB, SSIM up to 0.998, and LPIPS as low as 0.008, consistently surpassing SCARF across all four evaluated sequences. The method is trained on a single RTX 4090 GPU, with each 100-frame sequence taking approximately 2.5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGD outperforms all three baselines on both geometry and appearance metrics. Against SCARF [9] (implicit NeRF-based), NGD reduces average Chamfer Distance from 5.203×10³ to 0.431×10³ (a ~12× improvement) while also improving Normal Consistency from 0.844 to 0.853; SCARF's volume-rendering representation inherently produces smooth surfaces that lose high-frequency wrinkle details. Against DGarments [27] (explicit vertex-displacement template), NGD achieves lower CD (0.431 vs. 0.649×10³) and higher NC (0.853 vs. 0.833), with the gap being most pronounced on loose garments (sequence 187: CD 0.923 vs. 1.722×10³) where DGarments' fixed template struggles with large deformations. Against REC-MV [34] (implicit-explicit hybrid), qualitative comparisons (Figure 7) show NGD preserves finer wrinkle and fold details, though no quantitative numbers are reported for REC-MV in the main tables. For novel view synthesis, NGD beats SCARF on every sequence: e.g., on sequence 187, PSNR improves from 25.32 to 25.85 dB, SSIM from 0.918 to 0.948, and LPIPS drops from 0.0828 to 0.0395, reflecting sharper texture reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Neural Jacobian Fields (NJF) [Aigerman et al., 2022], which learns intrinsic mesh mappings via per-face Jacobian fields solved through Poisson equations, and adapts it from the static text-guided deformation setting of TextDeformer [Gao et al., 2023] to a temporally varying, video-supervised garment reconstruction pipeline. It departs from both implicit NeRF-based approaches (SCARF, REC-MV) that produce overly smooth geometry and explicit vertex-displacement template methods (Pergamo, DGarments) that struggle with dynamic topology and produce jagged surfaces, by decomposing the Jacobian field into a frame-invariant global component and a frame-dependent dynamic component predicted by a hash-grid-conditioned MLP. The gradient-based adaptive remeshing strategy, which uses rendering-loss gradients to selectively refine mesh resolution in high-curvature regions, is a novel application of the classical adaptive remeshing operations from [Dunyach et al., 2013] to differentiable-rendering-driven optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a persistent gap in monocular garment reconstruction: the inability of existing approaches to simultaneously capture high-frequency geometric details (wrinkles, pleats) and handle extremely loose garments with dynamic topology changes. The separation of geometry and appearance optimization, combined with the static/dynamic Jacobian decomposition, provides a principled framework that could be adapted to other articulated-object reconstruction tasks. However, the mesh-based representation remains susceptible to self-intersections, and the lack of integrated physics simulation limits physical plausibility; the authors acknowledge that incorporating physics directly into the deformation representation (beyond loss terms) is an important open direction. The reliance on pre-trained priors (4DHumans, Sapiens, BCNet) also means the method's robustness to noisy or missing priors in truly in-the-wild settings remains untested.
