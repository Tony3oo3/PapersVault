## Problem

Existing 3D human mesh recovery methods are trained exclusively on non-amputee data and implicitly assume a standard body structure, causing them to hallucinate intact limbs for individuals with amputations. No prior dataset or framework has been designed to handle this scenario, and collecting real amputee data raises significant ethical, safety, and logistical barriers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251215/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and 2D keypoint heatmaps (from ViTPose at inference, ground-truth at training) as input, concatenated along the channel dimension for the amputation classifier. It outputs SMPL pose parameters θ, shape parameters β, camera translation t, and a 4-element binary amputation indicator (one per limb: left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg). For amputated regions, 2D keypoint supervision is zeroed out while 3D keypoints and SMPL parameters remain active, distinguishing amputation from occlusion in the training signal.

## Method

AJAHR combines a ViT encoder with a Transformer decoder featuring two cross-attention pathways: one from a zero-pose token to image features, and another from BPAC-Net's feature-alignment head. BPAC-Net (ResNet-32 + CBAM) classifies amputation status per limb from the concatenated RGB+keypoint input, and its binary prediction triggers a tokenizer-switching strategy that selects between two frozen VQ-VAE codebooks—one trained on amputee+non-amputee poses, the other on non-amputee only. The A3D synthetic dataset (1M+ images) is generated by setting SMPL pose parameters of amputated parent joints and descendants to zero, compositing diverse BEDLAM textures, and projecting onto inpainted real-world backgrounds. The tokenizer uses 6D continuous rotation representations to avoid numerical instability from zero-pose masking, which is applied only in post-processing based on BPAC-Net's prediction.

## Results

On the amputee-specific A3D dataset, AJAHR achieves the best MVE (73.42 mm), MPJPE (73.19 mm), and PA-MPJPE (49.42 mm), outperforming TokenHMR (76.01/74.70/49.94) and BEDLAM-CLIFF (83.38/88.12/56.45). On the real-world ITW-amputee benchmark, AJAHR attains the lowest MPJPE (129.25 mm) and PA-MPJPE (77.18 mm), though HMR2.0 retains a slight MVE edge (110.33 vs. 116.42). On non-amputee benchmarks, AJAHR matches or exceeds baselines: on EMDB it leads all three metrics (112.83/91.74/58.62), and on 3DPW it achieves the best MPJPE (71.77) and PA-MPJPE (44.94) while TokenHMR holds a small MVE advantage (90.23 vs. 95.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TokenHMR (the closest baseline and the architectural foundation), AJAHR wins on all three metrics on A3D (e.g., PA-MPJPE 49.42 vs. 49.94) and on MPJPE/PA-MPJPE on ITW-amputee (129.25/77.18 vs. 146.12/91.00), but concedes MVE on ITW-amputee (116.42 vs. 136.52 — actually AJAHR wins here too; HMR2.0 is the MVE leader at 110.33). Against BEDLAM-CLIFF, AJAHR dominates across every dataset and metric, with the largest gap on A3D PA-MPJPE (49.42 vs. 56.45, a 12.5% reduction). Against HMR2.0, AJAHR wins on A3D (all metrics) and on EMDB/3DPW MPJPE and PA-MPJPE, but HMR2.0 retains a narrow MVE advantage on ITW-amputee (110.33 vs. 116.42) and 3DPW (90.23 vs. 95.26). On non-amputee benchmarks (EMDB, 3DPW), AJAHR is competitive or best, confirming that the amputation-aware design does not degrade standard HMR performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

AJAHR builds directly on the TokenHMR (CVPR 2024) architecture, adopting its VQ-VAE-based tokenized pose representation and ViT encoder-decoder backbone, while extending it with a body-part amputation classifier (BPAC-Net) and a dual-codebook tokenizer switching strategy. The dataset synthesis pipeline repurposes BEDLAM textures, LaMa inpainting, and Segment Anything for background generation, drawing on the synthetic-data philosophy of BEDLAM itself. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior inclusivity efforts (WheelPose for wheelchair users, Zhou et al. for prosthetic limbs) by targeting limb amputations specifically—a scenario the authors argue has been entirely unaddressed in the HMR literature. The SMPL-based amputation encoding (zeroing pose parameters for amputated subtrees) is a lightweight trick that avoids modifying the body model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework and dataset explicitly designed for amputee mesh recovery, filling a gap the authors document as absent across 704 publicly available human-motion datasets. The A3D dataset (1M synthetic images with full SMPL annotations) and the ITW-amputee benchmark provide the field with the first training and evaluation resources for this setting, enabling future work on inclusive HMR. Key limitations include support only for joint-level amputations aligned with the SMPL kinematic tree, exclusion of prosthetics, and reliance on synthetic data for training. Open questions include generalization to partial amputations (e.g., missing fingers), prosthetic limb modeling, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further closed with real amputee data.
