## Problem

Existing Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) train on images rendered under fixed, simple lighting, which provides insufficient photometric cues for recovering fine-grained surface details. Additionally, glossy or specular surfaces are treated as out-of-distribution inputs, causing significant degradation in reconstruction quality. PRM addresses both limitations by incorporating varying materials and illumination into the training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251982/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a single RGB image is expanded to six fixed views via Zero123++ before being fed into the network. The model outputs an explicit 3D mesh (extracted via FlexiCubes/dual marching cubes) along with a predicted albedo map rather than raw shading color, enabling downstream relighting and material editing. During training, ground-truth supervision includes not only rendered color but also normal, depth, mask, albedo, specular light, and diffuse light maps.

## Method

PRM renders training images online using a real-time split-sum approximation of PBR combined with mesh rasterization, varying metallic/roughness (121 combinations) and environment lighting (679 maps) to create rich photometric cues analogous to photometric stereo. The architecture follows a two-stage design inspired by InstantMesh: the first stage trains a triplane-based model with volume rendering, and the second stage switches to FlexiCubes as the explicit mesh representation, repurposing the color MLP as an albedo MLP. Differentiable PBR is applied to the predicted mesh to render intermediate shading variables (specular and diffuse light maps, normals, depth), which are exclusively tied to surface geometry and provide targeted supervision for fine-grained detail recovery. The total loss combines MSE and LPIPS on color, albedo, and light maps, plus normal, depth, and mask terms, with the light-map supervision playing a role analogous to photometric stereo by constraining normals without albedo interference.

## Results

On GSO, PRM achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.050 and F-Score@0.1 of 0.981 for 3D geometry, and PSNR of 25.125, SSIM of 0.929, and LPIPS of 0.061 for 2D rendering quality. On OmniObject3D, PRM reaches CD 0.053, FS@0.1 0.979, PSNR 25.063, SSIM 0.932, and LPIPS 0.063. These results are evaluated under randomly sampled materials and lighting, making them more challenging than the fixed-lighting settings used by prior methods. PRM outperforms all baselines by a wide margin on every metric across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRM outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both GSO and OmniObject3D. Against the closest competitor InstantMesh, PRM reduces Chamfer Distance by 34% on GSO (0.050 vs. 0.076) and 45% on Omni3D (0.053 vs. 0.096), while boosting PSNR by over 5 dB on both datasets (25.125 vs. 19.988 on GSO; 25.063 vs. 18.608 on Omni3D). TripoSR, CRM, and LGM trail even further: CRM has the worst geometry (CD 0.202 on GSO) and LGM shows the largest gap on Omni3D (CD 0.138 vs. PRM's 0.053). The advantage is most pronounced on 2D rendering metrics, where PRM's PSNR exceeds the next-best method by 5–10 dB, reflecting its ability to accurately predict albedo and handle specular appearances that degrade other methods. Even under the easier fixed-lighting, no-material-change setting (Table 2), PRM maintains its lead over InstantMesh (CD 0.043 vs. 0.048, PSNR 26.377 vs. 23.644 on GSO), confirming the gains are not solely due to the harder evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRM extends the LRM series (Hong et al., InstantMesh) by replacing the standard fixed-lighting, neural-rendering pipeline with a photometric-stereo-inspired approach that renders training images under varying materials (metallic, roughness) and illumination. Architecturally, it inherits InstantMesh's two-stage triplane-to-FlexiCubes design but departs from the LRM family's reliance on volume rendering for predicted views, instead using an explicit mesh with differentiable PBR (split-sum approximation) to produce shading, albedo, and lighting maps for multi-supervision. Conceptually, it bridges the photometric stereo literature (Woodham, Hernandez, Kaya et al.) with feed-forward 3D reconstruction, treating the varying-material/lighting observations as a system of equations to constrain surface normals and albedo jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRM addresses a concrete and widely acknowledged weakness of the LRM series: poor reconstruction of glossy/specular surfaces and insufficient fine-grained geometric detail due to low-frequency training images. The shift from neural rendering to differentiable PBR for predicted views is a practical design choice likely to influence subsequent LRM work, as it enables physically grounded supervision signals (specular/diffuse light maps) that are directly tied to geometry. However, the authors note that reconstruction quality remains sensitive to the quality of the multi-view diffusion inputs (Zero123++), and albedo estimation is still entangled with lighting conditions—open problems for real-world deployment where controlled rendering is unavailable.
