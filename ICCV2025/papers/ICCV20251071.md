## Problem

Existing human motion segmentation methods rely on subspace clustering under the assumption that frame features lie on a Union of Subspaces, yet in practice, video frames capturing complex motions with cluttered backgrounds deviate significantly from this distribution, causing a performance bottleneck. No prior work has exploited the Maximal Coding Rate Reduction principle to learn representations that are provably aligned with a UoS structure while simultaneously performing temporal sequence clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251071/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N pre-extracted frame features (324-dim HoG, 1000-dim VGG-16, or 768-dim CLIP) and produces a doubly stochastic affinity matrix Γ ∈ R^{N×N} via Sinkhorn projection, which is then passed to spectral clustering to yield per-frame motion labels. The network is a lightweight two-layer MLP encoder with separate fully-connected feature and cluster heads; the feature head outputs unit-norm representations Z, while the cluster head outputs unit-norm vectors whose outer product, after Sinkhorn normalization, forms Γ. A sliding window of size s=2 defines the temporal adjacency used in the Laplacian regularizer.

## Method

TR²C jointly optimizes a three-term objective: −ρ(Z,ε) (total coding rate, preventing representation collapse), λ₁ρᶜ(Z,ε|Γ) (class-conditional coding rate over the relaxed doubly stochastic affinity, enforcing UoS structure), and λ₂·tr(ZLZᵀ) (temporal Laplacian regularizer enforcing smoothness along the time axis). The discrete partition Π is replaced by a continuous doubly stochastic matrix Γ, making the problem amenable to gradient-based optimization through a neural network. The encoder, feature head, and cluster head are trained end-to-end via backpropagation for 500 iterations, after which spectral clustering on the final Γ yields segmentation. This is the first application of the MCR² principle to temporal sequence clustering, and the log-det coding rate terms provide a tighter rank approximation than prior subspace clustering objectives.

## Results

TR²C achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five HMS benchmark datasets, reaching 94.12% ACC / 95.91% NMI on Weizmann, 93.54% / 91.83% on UT, and 97.96% / 98.96% on YouTube. It outperforms the strongest baseline GCTSC by 9.11% ACC on Weizmann and 6.54% on UT, and surpasses the best transfer-learning method CDMS by up to 29.98% ACC on YouTube. With CLIP features, TR²C further improves to 96.21% ACC on Weizmann and 99.35% on YouTube, and with DINOv2 features reaches 98.6% ACC on Weizmann.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR²C outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor GCTSC [9], TR²C gains +9.11% ACC on Weizmann (94.12 vs 85.01), +4.86% on Keck, +6.54% on UT, and +2.17% on YouTube, with a near-tie on MAD (83.08 vs 82.97). The transfer-learning methods (TSS, LTS, MTS, CDMS) are substantially outperformed, with CDMS trailing by 29.07% ACC on Weizmann and 29.98% on YouTube, confirming that explicit UoS-aligned representation learning via MCR² is more effective than cross-domain alignment. Conventional subspace clustering methods (LRR, SSC, LSR) and temporal variants (OSC, TSC) lag by 20–40% ACC, attributed to the misalignment between raw HoG features and the UoS assumption. On the TAS comparison (Table 6), TR²C is competitive with state-of-the-art action segmentation methods (e.g., 71.8 MoF on YouTube Instructional vs 71.2 for ASOT), and in the reverse evaluation (Table 7), TR²C with DINOv2 features achieves 98.6% ACC on Weizmann, surpassing GCTSC (90.8%) by 7.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximal Coding Rate Reduction (MCR²) principle from supervised representation learning (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) and its unsupervised image-clustering instantiation MLC (Ding et al., ICCV 2023) to the temporal sequence domain, adding a temporal Laplacian regularizer inherited from Temporal Subspace Clustering (Li et al., ICCV 2015). The key conceptual departure from prior HMS work is that instead of assuming input features already satisfy the Union-of-Subspaces (UoS) assumption or hoping that auto-encoder/graph-based representation learning will implicitly produce UoS-aligned features, TR²C explicitly optimizes representations to conform to a UoS structure via the MCR² objective while simultaneously learning the clustering affinity. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of MCR² to temporal sequence clustering, bridging the gap between the MLC framework for static image clustering and the temporal continuity requirements of video-based motion segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly enforcing UoS alignment through MCR² yields representations that are not only more separable but also significantly more robust to additive noise than competing representation-learning methods (GCTSC), suggesting a principled advantage over heuristic regularizers. The substantial performance gains over both transfer-learning-based and representation-learning-based HMS methods—without requiring additional source-domain data—indicate that the bottleneck in prior HMS work was indeed the misalignment between raw features and the UoS assumption. Open questions include whether the simple sliding-window temporal regularizer can be replaced by learned temporal structures, whether the approach extends to longer or more complex video sequences beyond the current benchmarks, and whether the MCR² framework can be adapted to handle overlapping or hierarchical motion structures that violate the strict UoS assumption.
