## Problem

Previous neural and physics-based bokeh rendering methods are bottlenecked by depth estimation errors, producing visible over-blurring artifacts at depth discontinuities such as hair and fur. While diffusion models can generate photorealistic lens blur from text, their iterative denoising process and standard self-attention mechanism tend to alter input image content and ignore the physical constraints of light propagation, making them unsuitable for structure-preserving lens blur rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251585/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an all-in-focus RGB image, a per-pixel disparity map (estimated by Depth Anything V2 and shared across all compared methods), and a scalar focus disparity value, and produces a single image with physically correct defocus blur in one forward pass. It operates in the SDXL VAE latent space at a fixed timestep T=499, treating the all-in-focus image directly as input without adding any noise, so the output preserves the original image structure while applying the correct blur pattern dictated by the disparity map.

## Method

BokehDiff fine-tunes a pretrained SDXL model via LoRA on the U-Net downsampling layers and the VAE encoder, using a one-step denoising scheme where the all-in-focus image is treated as the 'noisy' input to be transformed into the bokeh image without any added noise. The key architectural innovation is the Physics-Inspired Self-Attention (PISA) module, which replaces standard self-attention with three physics-grounded modifications: (1) energy-conserved normalization that normalizes attention weights along the query dimension so each source pixel's total contribution is preserved, (2) a circle-of-confusion spatial mask that limits each pixel's influence radius based on its disparity offset from the focal plane, and (3) a sampling-based self-occlusion mask that blocks light propagation where intervening geometry is closer to the camera. Training uses a composite loss in pixel space combining MSE, VGG perceptual (LPIPS), multi-scale Sobel edge, and adversarial (ConvNext discriminator) terms, with a synthetic dataset generated by compositing diffusion-model-produced transparent foregrounds over real small-aperture backgrounds and rendering bokeh via ray tracing.

## Results

On the real-world EBB Val294 dataset, BokehDiff achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 24.65, SSIM 0.836, DISTS 0.116, and LPIPS 0.374, outperforming the strongest baseline BokehMe (PSNR 24.01, LPIPS 0.392). On the synthetic BLB Level5 dataset, BokehDiff attains the lowest LPIPS (0.089) despite BokehMe having a slightly higher PSNR (38.80 vs. 36.80), indicating more realistic bokeh patterns. In the user study with 50 photography-experienced participants, BokehDiff leads on all three criteria: accuracy (4.42/5), authenticity (4.37/5), and overall preference (4.56/5), surpassing BokehMe (3.81, 3.93, 4.03) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BokehDiff outperforms all five baselines on the real-world EBB Val294 dataset across every metric, with the largest gains over DeepLens (+1.95 PSNR, −0.045 LPIPS) and MPIB (+1.32 PSNR, −0.029 LPIPS). On the synthetic BLB Level5 dataset, BokehMe achieves a marginally higher PSNR (38.80 vs. 36.80) and SSIM (0.987 vs. 0.981), but BokehDiff's LPIPS is substantially lower (0.089 vs. 0.140), indicating more photorealistic bokeh rendering; the paper attributes BokehMe's PSNR advantage to its strict adherence to the (correct) synthetic defocus map. Multi-layer methods MPIB and Dr. Bokeh degrade severely on BLB Level5 (PSNR 28.16 and 22.65 respectively) due to complex scene layouts that resist layer decomposition. The finetuned Restormer★ baseline, trained from scratch on the same data, performs worst on BLB (PSNR 16.78, LPIPS 0.780), confirming that the diffusion prior and PISA module contribute meaningfully beyond data alone. In the user study, BokehDiff leads all baselines by at least 0.4 points on every criterion, with the largest gap over MPIB (accuracy: 4.42 vs. 1.83) and DeepLens (preference: 4.56 vs. 1.96).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pretrained latent diffusion models (SDXL) for lens blur rendering, a task previously dominated by physics-based multi-plane methods (MPIB, Dr. Bokeh) and hybrid neural-classical renderers (BokehMe, DeepLens). The key conceptual departure is treating the all-in-focus image as a 'noisy' input to a one-step denoising process, drawing on recent one-step diffusion work for super-resolution [51] and cold diffusion [3], while introducing a physics-inspired self-attention (PISA) module that enforces energy conservation, circle-of-confusion spatial limits, and self-occlusion—constraints absent from vanilla attention. The data synthesis pipeline also departs from prior bokeh datasets by generating photorealistic transparent foregrounds with a text-to-image model rather than relying on segmentation or 3D assets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single forward pass of a finetuned diffusion model can produce photorealistic lens blur robust to depth estimation errors, this work opens a path toward practical, real-time bokeh rendering that is no longer bottlenecked by depth accuracy at discontinuities. The PISA module's physics-constrained attention design could inform other rendering or inverse-imaging tasks where global self-attention conflicts with local physical laws. However, the VAE decoder still introduces subtle structural changes, and the approach is tied to the SDXL backbone; whether lighter or less-compressive diffusion backbones (FLUX, SD3) would eliminate this limitation remains open. The scalable data synthesis paradigm is also a transferable contribution for other paired-data-scarce rendering problems.
