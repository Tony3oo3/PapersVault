## Problem

Existing scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods like ACE and DSAC* encode training views directly into the regressor weights, causing them to overfit to the mapping conditions by design. When query images differ in lighting, viewpoint, or scene content, these models fail to generalize. The paper addresses this inherent limitation by decoupling the coordinate regressor from the scene representation, enabling large-scale pre-training with explicit query-view generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250961/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB query image and a pre-computed scene-specific map code (4096 embeddings of 768 dimensions, ~12 MB in full precision) as input. The DINOv2 encoder produces patch embeddings at 14-pixel resolution, which are cross-attended against the map code to yield per-patch 3D scene coordinates and a Laplace uncertainty σ_y. These 2D-3D correspondences are adaptively filtered by uncertainty quantile before PnP+RANSAC recovers the 6-DOF camera pose. Notably, mapping a novel scene requires only posed RGB images and camera intrinsics—no 3D reconstruction or depth data.

## Method

ACE-G replaces ACE's scene-specific MLP with a scene-agnostic coordinate regressor built from N cross-attention-only ViT blocks, where DINOv2 patch embeddings serve as queries and the scene-specific map code embeddings serve as keys and values (permutation-invariant, no positional encoding). A 2-layer MLP head regresses the 3D coordinate and Laplace standard deviation. The key training innovation is alternating between mapping iterations (jointly optimizing network weights and map codes on mapping buffers) and query iterations (freezing map codes and optimizing only the network on buffers from different viewpoints or conditions), explicitly teaching the transformer to bridge the mapping-to-query gap. This pre-training runs over 120,000 mapping-query splits from six datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, MapFree, BlendedMVS, WildRGBD) for 4.4M iterations. For novel scenes, the map code is optimized in ~5 minutes via a 2D reprojection loss (no 3D ground truth needed), and relocalization is a single forward pass followed by uncertainty-quantile prefiltering and PnP+RANSAC.

## Results

On Indoor-6, ACE-G (5 min mapping) achieves 95% correct localization at (5°, 25 cm) with 4.5 cm median translation error, substantially outperforming ACE (70%, 11.0 cm) and DINO-ACE (91%, 5.6 cm), while remaining competitive with GLACE (93%, 3.5 cm). On RIO10, which features long-term scene changes, ACE-G reduces median translation error to 41.1 cm / 13.8° versus 358.4 cm / 58.7° for ACE and 114 cm / 33.4° for GLACE. On the out-of-distribution Cambridge Landmarks dataset, ACE-G (25 min) achieves 25.3 cm / 0.4° median error, still improving over DINO-ACE (43.9 cm / 0.7°) despite no comparable outdoor scenes in pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE-G decisively outperforms ACE across all three datasets: on Indoor-6 it improves correct localization from 70% to 95% and cuts median translation error from 11.0 cm to 4.5 cm; on RIO10 the improvement is even more dramatic (358.4 cm → 41.1 cm median error). Against DINO-ACE (ACE with DINOv2 features), ACE-G gains 4–5 percentage points on Indoor-6 and roughly halves the RIO10 error (83.8 cm → 41.1 cm), confirming that the scene-agnostic transformer regressor adds value beyond simply swapping the encoder. Compared to GLACE, ACE-G is competitive on Indoor-6 (96% vs 93% at 25 min) and far superior on RIO10 (37.0 cm vs 114 cm), but trails on Cambridge Landmarks (25.3 cm vs 13.5 cm), where the smaller mapping–query gap and lack of comparable outdoor pre-training data limit the advantage. MASt3R+Kapture achieves the best absolute accuracy on Indoor-6 (94%) and Cambridge (9.1 cm) but requires 5–10 hours of mapping and ~5 GB storage, and fails entirely on multiple RIO10 scenes (>50% of images unsolved). Reloc3r is the only non-SCR method competitive with ACE-G on RIO10 (47 cm vs 41.1 cm) but requires storing all mapping images and underperforms on Indoor-6 (76% vs 95%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ACE-G directly extends ACE by replacing its scene-specific MLP regression head with a scene-agnostic cross-attention transformer conditioned on a learned scene-specific map code, and by swapping the convolutional encoder for DINOv2. The key conceptual departure from prior SCR methods (DSAC*, ACE, GLACE) is the explicit separation of the coordinate regressor from the map representation, which enables large-scale pre-training on 120K mapping-query splits. The query pre-training procedure—alternating between mapping iterations (jointly optimizing network and map codes) and query iterations (freezing map codes, training only the network on unseen views)—has no direct precedent in SCR, though it is conceptually related to NeuMap's auto-transdecoder framework, which lacks both full scene-agnosticism and any query-view training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE-G addresses the fundamental overfitting limitation of per-scene SCR by design, showing that a pre-trained, scene-agnostic regressor can generalize to unseen lighting and viewpoint conditions while retaining the practical advantages of SCR (minutes-long mapping, ~12 MB map size). The substantial robustness gains on RIO10 (10× reduction in median translation error over ACE) demonstrate that the query pre-training signal is critical for real-world deployment where mapping and query conditions diverge. Limitations include slightly worse fine-grained accuracy than GLACE (attributed to DINOv2's 14-pixel patch size reducing correspondence density) and underperformance on out-of-distribution outdoor scenes (Cambridge Landmarks), leaving open whether the approach scales to very large environments or benefits from higher-resolution encoders.
