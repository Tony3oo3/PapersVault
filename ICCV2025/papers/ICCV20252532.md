## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models generate diverse portraits but offer no intuitive parametric control over shadows, forcing artists into trial-and-error prompt engineering. Existing editing-based relighting methods (SwitchLight, IC-Light, DiFaReli) fail to preserve identity and artistic style across diverse generated portraits, and their training pipelines demand expensive real-world light-stage data or massive compute (e.g., 8 H100 GPUs for a week). The paper asks whether shadow information is already implicitly encoded in well-trained diffusion models and can be accessed with minimal synthetic data and compute.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt for portrait generation plus user-specified shadow controls: a binary mask defining shadow shape and placement, a 3D directional light position for geometrically consistent ray-cast shadows, and a shadow intensity parameter controlled by the number of latent optimization iterations. It operates within Stable Diffusion XL 1.0 at denoising step 40 of 100 (t=0.6), adding roughly 20% inference time. The output is a fully generated portrait image with the desired shadow configuration while preserving the subject's identity and the prompt's artistic style; all optimization remains in the latent feature space and never decodes intermediate noisy RGB images via the VAE.

## Method

Shadow Director's core insight is that a well-trained diffusion model's UNet internal features already encode shadow and identity information that can be read out by small auxiliary networks. Two compact estimators are trained independently on 5.7k synthetic images (CelebA identities relit with GeomConsistentFR): an SD Estimator that predicts 2D shadow and depth maps from UNet internal features (trained with L1 loss), and an ID Estimator that produces identity embeddings invariant to lighting (trained with triplet loss). At inference, shadow control is achieved by pausing denoising at an early step and performing gradient descent on only the UNet's input tensor (the noisy latent feature), minimizing a combined loss of L1 shadow matching against a user-defined target and cosine-similarity identity preservation against a reference embedding. All network parameters stay frozen; the sole optimizable variable is the latent feature map, and the target shadow is either a user-painted binary mask or a depth-based ray-cast shadow from a specified light direction. This design requires only 2 A6000 GPUs and ~8 hours of training, in contrast to baselines needing 10M+ samples or weeks of H100 compute.

## Results

On 800 unseen text-generated portraits, Shadow Director achieves the best scores across all five metrics: CVS (identity preservation) of 0.9460, CLIPIQA+ of 0.4484, NIMA of 5.7735, ARNIQA of 0.5480, and CVTA (lighting-direction text alignment) of 0.2692. In a 40-user preference study, Shadow Director is preferred 45.69% of the time, nearly double the next-best method IC-Light (27.44%). The method requires only 5.7k synthetic training samples and ~8 hours on 2 A6000 GPUs, compared to IC-Light's 10M samples and 140 hours on 8 H100s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Shadow Director outperforms all four baselines on every quantitative metric. On identity preservation (CVS), it leads with 0.9460 versus Hou (0.9386), IC-Light (0.9140), DiFaReLi (0.9049), and SwitchLight (0.8945). On perceptual quality metrics (CLIPIQA+, NIMA, ARNIQA), it surpasses the strongest competitor IC-Light by 0.030, 0.054, and 0.024 respectively, while SwitchLight and DiFaReLi trail by larger margins. In the user study, Shadow Director is preferred at 45.69%, roughly 1.7× IC-Light (27.44%) and 2.2× SwitchLight (20.46%), while DiFaReLi (1.86%) and Hou (2.09%) are rarely selected. Qualitatively, IC-Light and DiFaReLi exhibit noticeable identity shifts and style inconsistencies on cartoon and super-realistic portraits, whereas Shadow Director preserves vibrant colors and facial features while still providing varied shadow control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant editing-based paradigm for portrait relighting (SwitchLight, IC-Light, DiFaReli), which treats shadow manipulation as a post-hoc image editing problem requiring large-scale training data or expensive light-stage captures. Instead, it follows the emerging 'attribute reveal' line of work (Readout Guidance [30], GeoWizard [14], LightIt [26]) that probes visual attributes already implicitly encoded in diffusion model features, but applies this specifically to shadow and identity control during generation. The synthetic training data pipeline builds on GeomConsistentFR [19] for geometrically consistent shadow synthesis, while the identity preservation mechanism draws on triplet-loss contrastive learning from face recognition literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a dramatic reduction in resource requirements: 5.7k synthetic samples and 8 hours on 2 A6000s versus IC-Light's 10M samples and 8 H100s for a week, making shadow control accessible to researchers without large compute budgets. The finding that a small estimation network can extract usable shadow information from UNet internal features validates the broader hypothesis that diffusion models encode rich lighting priors, potentially generalizable to other visual attributes. Open questions include scaling beyond CelebA's straight-on portraits to full-body and complex clothing, handling lighting temperature, and whether the attribute-reveal paradigm extends to video or 3D-consistent relighting.
