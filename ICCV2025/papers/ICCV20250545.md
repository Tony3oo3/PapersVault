## Problem

Multi-modal face anti-spoofing in domain generalization settings suffers from two misalignment issues: (1) intra-domain modality misalignment, where the reliability of each modality (e.g., depth) varies across attack types, making uniform fusion suboptimal, and (2) inter-domain modality misalignment, where introducing additional modalities exacerbates domain shifts and can outweigh fusion benefits. Existing multi-modal FAS methods assume consistent domains and treat modalities equally, while prior multi-modal DG approaches like MMDG rely on Monte Carlo dropout-based uncertainty estimation that introduces randomness and limits performance.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three 224×224×3 images (RGB, depth, infrared) processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone with trainable adapters. In missing-modal protocols, absent modalities are replaced with all-zero tensors (Protocol 2) or learnable substitute tensors (Protocol 3). The final output is a single live/spoof score computed as the mean of per-domain classification hyperplane projections, rather than a single fixed hyperplane.

## Method

DADM builds on a frozen ViT-B/16 with two key components. First, a Mutual Information Mask (MIM) module is inserted at each MHSA layer for every modality pair: it concatenates features, generates sigmoid masks via a lightweight convolution block, reweights features to suppress unreliable regions, and computes an MI maximization loss using averaged aligned features as MI tokens (a special case of the MINE score function that avoids a separate critic network). Second, a dual alignment optimization strategy combines PG-IRM (aligning per-domain classification hyperplanes to a globally optimal one) with a novel angle margin loss that enforces consistency of inter-modality cosine angles across domains. A CDC-Adapter (central difference convolution) captures both intensity and gradient cues, and an MI-guided ReGrad modulates gradient directions based on MI token intensity rather than dropout-based uncertainty.

## Results

On Protocol 1 (fixed-modal, leave-one-out cross-dataset), DADM achieves an average HTER of 13.63% and AUC of 92.96% across four sub-protocols (CPS→W, CPW→S, CSW→P, PSW→C), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art MMDG (19.25% HTER, 87.96% AUC) by 5.62% in HTER and 5.00% in AUC. The largest gain is on PSW→C, where DADM reaches 16.87% HTER / 91.08% AUC versus MMDG's 29.93% / 76.52%. Under Protocol 4 (limited source domains, only 2 of 4 datasets), DADM achieves 12.61% HTER / 93.81% AUC on CW→PS and 20.40% / 89.51% on PS→CW, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DADM outperforms the closest multi-modal DG baseline MMDG [42] by 5.62% in average HTER (13.63% vs. 19.25%) and 5.00% in AUC (92.96% vs. 87.96%) on Protocol 1, with the largest margin on PSW→C (13.06% HTER, 14.56% AUC improvement); the only sub-protocol where DADM is slightly inferior is CSW→P (19.03% vs. 18.95% HTER). Against flexible-modal methods VP-FAS [85] and ViT+AMA [87], DADM improves average HTER by 11.82% and 14.80% respectively, and AUC by 11.88% and 15.51%. Uni-modal DG methods (SSDG, SSAN, SA-FAS, IADG, ViTAF) are adapted to multi-modal input via channel concatenation but remain 10–20% worse in HTER than DADM. On Protocol 4 (limited source domains), DADM achieves 12.61% HTER on CW→PS versus 20.12% for MMDG and 25.20% for SA-FAS, demonstrating that the dual alignment strategy is particularly effective when source data is scarce. On Protocol 2 (missing modalities), DADM attains 21.66% average HTER, outperforming MMDG (24.51%) and the purpose-built flexible-modal VP-FAS (29.74%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MMDG (Lin et al., CVPR 2024), the first multi-modal domain-generalization FAS framework, by replacing its Monte Carlo dropout-based uncertainty estimation with a deterministic mutual information mask (MIM) module that adaptively reweights modality features. It also builds on PG-IRM (Sun et al., CVPR 2023) for the invariant risk minimization objective, but extends the alignment constraint from a single classification hyperplane to a dual alignment that additionally enforces consistency of inter-modality angle margins across domains. The mutual information estimation follows MINE (Belghazi et al., ICML 2018) but simplifies the score function by using averaged MI tokens as a special case, eliminating the need for a separate critic network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-sensor face capture becomes more common in deployment, the gap between multi-modal FAS (which assumes consistent domains) and domain-generalization FAS (which is typically uni-modal) is a practical bottleneck that this paper addresses head-on. The identification of two distinct misalignment failure modes (intra-domain modality reliability and inter-domain angle drift) provides a cleaner diagnostic framework than prior work's single 'domain shift' narrative. Limitations include evaluation on only four datasets with three fixed modalities, and the approach's reliance on PG-IRM's bi-level optimization, which can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the dual alignment generalizes to more than three modalities or to video-based FAS, and whether the MI-based masking transfers to other multi-modal DG tasks beyond face anti-spoofing.
