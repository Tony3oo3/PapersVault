## Problem

Existing diffusion-guided blind face restoration methods assume uniform degradation severity, applying a fixed starting timestep and a single global guidance scale across all pixels. This leads to under- or over-diffusion for differently degraded inputs, kernel mismatch from imperfect degradation estimation, and a fundamental imbalance between identity fidelity and perceptual detail quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250300/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blindly degraded RGB facial image (unknown degradation combining blur, downsampling, noise, and JPEG compression) and produces a restored high-fidelity facial image at the original resolution. The degradation is handled without knowing the true kernel; instead, the method internally maps the input to one or more Gaussian-blurred surrogate observations with estimated standard deviations, which then serve as guidance signals during diffusion sampling. A pre-trained DDPM (shared with DifFace/PGDiff) and a SwinIR restoration network are used as the generative and discriminative backbones respectively.

## Method

DynFaceRestore reframes blind face restoration as Gaussian deblurring via three coupled components. First, Dynamic Blur-Level Mapping (DBLM) uses a SwinIR restoration model plus a learnable estimator network to convert the unknown degraded input into Gaussian-blurred surrogate images with estimated kernel standard deviations, sidestepping the need for exact kernel recovery. Second, a Dynamic Starting Step Lookup Table (DSST) maps each estimated blur level to an optimal diffusion starting timestep (determined offline by finding where diffused HQ and blurred images statistically converge), preventing under- or over-diffusion. Third, a Dynamic Guidance Scaling Adjuster (DGSA), a 3-layer CNN, produces a spatially varying guidance scale map in [0,1] that strengthens fidelity guidance on structural regions (contours) while weakening it on detail-rich regions (hair, wrinkles), trained with wavelet-domain L1 loss and DISTS perceptual loss. Multiple Gaussian-blurred guidance images at three std levels are jointly applied with weighted priority, and the kernel std is iteratively refined during sampling to further reduce kernel mismatch.

## Results

On CelebA-Test (synthetic), DynFaceRestore achieves the best PSNR (24.349 dB), SSIM (0.664), FID (14.780), IDA (0.748), and LMD (3.419), while ranking second in LPIPS (0.332) behind CodeFormer (0.319). On real-world datasets, it attains the best FID on LFW-Test (42.515) and Wider-Test (36.052), though it underperforms on WebPhoto-Test (95.317 FID) due to the small sample size (407 images). The method uses 176.77M parameters and requires 91.82 s inference time on an RTX 3090, comparable to other diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynFaceRestore outperforms all 10 baselines on PSNR, SSIM, FID, IDA, and LMD on CelebA-Test, with the closest competitor being DiffBIR (PSNR 24.127 vs. 24.349) and DifFace (FID 15.032 vs. 14.780). It trails only CodeFormer on LPIPS (0.332 vs. 0.319), indicating CodeFormer retains a slight edge in perceptual similarity despite DynFaceRestore's superior fidelity and identity preservation. Among diffusion-based methods, DynFaceRestore leads all five (DiffBIR, PGDiff, DifFace, DR2, 3Diffusion) on every metric except LPIPS, where 3Diffusion (0.353) and DifFace (0.355) are close. On real-world data, DynFaceRestore achieves the best FID on LFW (42.515 vs. DiffBIR's 43.446) and Wider (36.052 vs. DAEFR's 36.720), but performs worst on WebPhoto (95.317 FID), which the authors attribute to the dataset's small size (407 images) making FID unreliable. GAN-based methods (GPEN, GFPGAN) and codebook-based methods (CodeFormer, DAEFR) are consistently outperformed on fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, IDA) but sometimes match or exceed DynFaceRestore on perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID on WebPhoto).

## Relation to Prior Work

DynFaceRestore builds directly on the Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) framework [4], modifying its fixed degradation operator with a dynamic Gaussian blur kernel and region-wise guidance scaling. It departs from prior diffusion-based BFR methods (DifFace, PGDiff, DiffBIR, 3Diffusion) that assume uniform degradation severity and use a fixed starting timestep and global guidance scale. The key conceptual departure is reframing blind face restoration as a Gaussian deblurring problem via Dynamic Blur-Level Mapping, which converts arbitrary unknown degradation into a parametric Gaussian form, thereby sidestepping the kernel mismatch problem that plagues prior DPS-based approaches. The SwinIR restoration backbone and the pre-trained diffusion model (shared with DifFace and PGDiff) are reused, positioning the contribution as a sampling-strategy improvement rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated limitation in diffusion-guided restoration: the mismatch between fixed sampling parameters and variable real-world degradation. The dynamic starting-step lookup table and region-wise guidance scaling are straightforward but effective mechanisms that practitioners can adopt in other inverse-problem settings. However, the 91.8-second inference time (vs. 6.1 s for DifFace) is a significant practical barrier, and the multiple-guidance hyperparameters (number of guidance images, std offsets) are acknowledged as under-optimized. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-blur assumption generalizes to non-Gaussian real-world degradations (motion blur, sensor noise) and whether the approach extends beyond facial images to general blind restoration.
