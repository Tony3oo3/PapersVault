## Problem

Despite advances in object detection and scene segmentation, AI systems struggle with higher-level visual interpretation tasks that require linking scene elements to prior knowledge and detecting incongruities. Graphical humor is a paradigmatic test case for this capability, yet existing humor datasets lack rigorous controls, limited image diversity, or conflate textual and visual humor. This paper asks whether current vision and vision-language models can genuinely identify the specific visual elements that make an image funny, rather than relying on superficial cues.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes single RGB images (photos, photoshopped photos, cartoons, sketches, AI-generated) or minimally contrastive image pairs where only the humor-inducing element differs (e.g., a phone removed from a surgeon's hand). For vision-only models, a classification head or regression head is appended; for VLMs, tasks are framed as VQA prompts. The comparison task takes two images side-by-side and requires selecting the funnier one, with VLMs additionally producing a brief textual justification. A control set of 650 non-humorous, non-edited images from MSCOCO and Places365 tests whether models learn editing artifacts rather than humor.

## Method

The core contribution is a controlled dataset construction pipeline that generates 1,271 minimally contrastive pairs by surgically removing or replacing the humor-inducing element via Photoshop edits or stable-diffusion inpainting, while applying subtle enhancements to both versions to mask editing artifacts. Human ground truth is collected from 650 participants across three tasks (binary classification, 1–10 funniness rating, pairwise comparison) with rigorous quality controls including repeated-image consistency checks and outlier removal. The evaluation spans vision-only architectures (DINOv2, ViT-Huge/G-14, Swin-V2, ConvNeXt, ResNet152) and vision-language models (LLaVA, BLIP, GPT-4o, Gemini-Flash) in both pretrained/fine-tuned and zero-shot settings. Beyond accuracy, the paper uses attention rollout and layer-wise logit attribution to probe whether models actually attend to the humor-relevant region, revealing that even correct classifications often arise without focusing on the precise incongruous element.

## Results

On the Range Task (RMSE, lower is better), the best-performing model is LLaVA with word-based fine-tuning, achieving 1.68±0.28 on testAllSet and 1.66±0.31 on testOnlyPairs, followed by ViT-G/14 at 1.76±0.08. Zero-shot large multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o: 2.61, Gemini-Flash: 2.06) perform reasonably but trail fine-tuned models. On the Binary Task, pretrained models consistently outperform non-pretrained counterparts, and large VLMs (Gemini-Flash, fine-tuned LLaVA) surpass vision-only models; GPT-4o shows a surprising 10% accuracy gain when evaluated only on original (unmodified) images, suggesting training-data contamination. The Comparison Task is the most challenging, with all models exceeding the 50% chance level but falling well below human performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among vision-only models, ViT-G/14 achieves the best RMSE (1.76±0.08), outperforming ViT-Huge (1.88±0.09), DINOv2 Large (1.98±0.08), Swin2 Large (1.96±0.12), ConvNeXt Large (2.10±0.06), and ResNet152 (2.11±0.09) on the Range Task. Fine-tuned LLaVA variants (1.70–1.68 RMSE) surpass all vision-only baselines, with the word-enhanced fine-tuning providing a small but consistent edge over standard fine-tuning. Zero-shot GPT-4o (2.61) and Gemini-Flash (2.06) trail fine-tuned LLaVA by 0.93 and 0.38 RMSE respectively, though Gemini-Flash outperforms several vision-only models (DINOv2, Swin2, ConvNeXt, ResNet152). On the Binary Task, pretrained models dominate non-pretrained ones, and large VLMs (Gemini-Flash, fine-tuned LLaVA) outperform vision-only models; on the external control set of non-humorous images, ViT-G/14 correctly rejects 81% as 'not funny' versus 62% for DINOv2, confirming models learn beyond editing artifacts. On the Comparison Task, models trained directly on pairwise comparison data outperform larger zero-shot LLMs, indicating that task-specific training remains advantageous over general-purpose reasoning for fine-grained humor judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on computational humor understanding (Chandrasekaran et al. 2016, Hessel et al. 2023) by shifting focus from text-dependent or clipart-based humor to diverse, realistic visual humor (photos, cartoons, sketches, AI-generated images). Its key methodological innovation—minimally contrastive image pairs where only the humor-inducing element is removed—borrows directly from the subtle-activity recognition paradigm of Jacquot et al. (2020), repurposing that control strategy to isolate humor from confounding visual features. Unlike prior humor datasets that rely on caption matching or multi-modal context, HumorDB evaluates purely visual humor through three complementary tasks (binary, range, comparison) with rigorous human psychophysics grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled, multi-task benchmark for visual humor understanding, revealing that even state-of-the-art vision-language models (GPT-4o, Gemini) fall substantially below human performance, particularly on the comparison task and abstract sketches. The contrastive-pair design exposes that models often classify correctly without attending to the humor-inducing region, raising the open question of whether the gap is fundamentally about abstract reasoning (incongruity detection) or simply data coverage. The finding that GPT-4o's performance jumps 10% when modified images are removed suggests potential training-data contamination, an important caveat for interpreting zero-shot LLM results on web-sourced benchmarks. Future work could expand cultural diversity, per-user subjective ratings, and mechanistic interpretability to understand whether humor understanding is emergent or architecturally constrained.
