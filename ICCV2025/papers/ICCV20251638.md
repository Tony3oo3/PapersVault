## Problem

Existing event-based low-light image enhancement methods rely on motion-triggered events that only encode edge information and require scene motion, leaving the high dynamic range and superior low-light responsiveness of event cameras largely unexplored. No prior work has leveraged temporal-mapping events—triggered by transmittance modulation rather than motion—to estimate fine-grained illumination for LLIE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251638/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image (e.g., 640×480) and temporal-mapping events (FPE timestamps from a transmittance-modulation shutter opening) as input, producing an enhanced normal-light RGB image. The temporal-mapping events are captured synchronously with image exposure via a beamsplitter setup, and an illumination manipulation coefficient β allows post-hoc brightness adjustment without re-inference. Real-time operation is achieved at 35.6 FPS on a 640×480 image with only 3.44M parameters.

## Method

RETINEV applies Retinex decomposition (S = R·I) where the illumination component I is estimated from temporal-mapping events via a Time-to-Illumination (T2I) module that converts FPE timestamps to brightness using a DenoiseNet, MLP layers, and Gamma encoding, with a low-light degradation model (LLDM) for realistic synthetic training. The reflectance component R is then decomposed from the low-light image conditioned on the event-derived illumination, and further refined by an Illumination-aided Reflectance Enhancement (IRE) module that uses a transposed cross-modal attention mechanism (Q from R, K/V from I) to capture long-range dependencies at O(c²) spatial complexity instead of O(h²w²). The final enhanced image is reconstructed as Î·R̂. Training is supervised with a combined loss of reconstruction (matching both low-light and normal-light paths to the HDR reference), invariant reflectance consistency, and perceptual loss, with the denoising network pre-trained under the LLDM for 100k iterations before joint fine-tuning.

## Results

On five synthetic datasets, RETINEV achieves 28.60/30.32/32.06/33.65/33.29 dB PSNR on LOL-v1, LOL-v2-real, LOL-v2-syn, SDSD-in, and SDSD-out respectively, with only 3.44M parameters. On the real-world EvLowLight dataset, RETINEV reaches 15.39 dB PSNR* and 0.470 SSIM* while achieving the lowest no-reference PIQE score (9.41) and the best user-study ranking (1.21/8). The method runs at 35.6 FPS on a 640×480 image, substantially faster than all event-based competitors (0.9–4.1 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETINEV outperforms all 13 baselines across every reported metric and dataset. Against the strongest image-only method, Retinexformer [4], RETINEV gains 3.44 dB PSNR on LOL-v1 (28.60 vs 25.16) and 3.88 dB on SDSD-in (33.65 vs 29.77), with a 0.032–0.089 SSIM advantage. Against the best prior event-based method, EvLight [21] (which uses motion events), RETINEV improves by 5.13 dB on SDSD-in and 6.62 dB on SDSD-out, while also reducing parameters from 22.73M to 3.44M and increasing inference speed from 4.1 to 35.6 FPS. The other event-based baselines, ELIE [14] and Liu et al. [25], are substantially weaker (27.46/27.58 dB on SDSD-in) and use 204.95M+ parameters. On the real-world EvLowLight dataset, RETINEV leads EvLight by 0.88 dB PSNR* and 0.035 SSIM*, while achieving a PIQE of 9.41 versus 20.45 for EvLight and a user-study average rank of 1.21 versus 2.31. Notably, EvLight fails to surpass several image-only methods (e.g., SNR-Net, Retinexformer) on PSNR despite using an additional event modality, whereas RETINEV's temporal-mapping events consistently deliver large gains over both image-only and motion-event-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant event-based LLIE paradigm (ELIE, EvLight) that fuses motion-triggered events to enhance edge textures, arguing instead that motion events are fundamentally insufficient for illumination estimation. It builds on the temporal-mapping photography technique (Bao et al., 2024), which converts transmittance-modulation timestamps into brightness values, and repurposes it within a Retinex decomposition framework where the event-derived illumination aids reflectance refinement via cross-modal attention. Unlike image-only Retinex methods (RetinexNet, Retinexformer) that enhance illumination estimated from the degraded RGB image itself, RETINEV sources illumination from the event camera's superior low-light responsiveness, shifting the bottleneck from illumination estimation to reflectance recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that temporal-mapping events carry dense, full-field illumination information that motion events structurally cannot provide, opening a new design axis for event-assisted image enhancement beyond edge/texture fusion. The EvLowLight dataset (60 scenes, 2.5–6 lux, aligned temporal-mapping and motion events at 1216×720) fills a critical gap in real-world evaluation for this new paradigm. However, practical deployment is constrained by the need for a beamsplitter hardware setup with a mechanical shutter, and the real-world evaluation set is small (60 scenes); whether the approach generalizes to other DVS hardware or to video settings remains open. The pixel-wise PSNR/SSIM comparison between event-derived and frame-based outputs is acknowledged as potentially misleading due to differing response functions, leaving perceptual quality as the more meaningful metric.
