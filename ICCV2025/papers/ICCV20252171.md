## Problem

Foundation models for universal instance segmentation (e.g., GLEE) achieve state-of-the-art in-domain and zero-shot performance but incur prohibitive computational costs that preclude deployment on mobile and edge devices. While scaling up model size and data has been extensively explored, the complementary challenge of Pareto-optimal downscaling—reducing model size, training time, and inference FLOPs while preserving open-vocabulary generalization—remains largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (evaluated at 800×800 for high-end GPUs and 384×384 for mobile devices), a text prompt (category list or free-form expression), and optionally a visual prompt for interactive segmentation. It outputs instance-level bounding boxes and segmentation masks, with masks produced by dot-producting refined queries against an upsampled embedding map. The text encoder (CLIP-B) is excluded from all FLOPs and latency measurements, and the model is trained jointly across 15 datasets spanning detection, segmentation, referring expressions, and open-world segmentation in a single unified stage.

## Method

MOBIUS introduces a bottleneck pixel decoder that selects a single feature scale (stride 16) as a representational bottleneck and progressively fuses multi-scale features and text tokens into it via bidirectional cross-attention, intra-scale deformable self-attention, and multi-scale deformable cross-attention, reducing pixel decoder FLOPs by 55% while preserving open-vocabulary performance. Because the bottleneck already condenses multi-scale information, the transformer decoder operates on a single scale, halving decoder FLOPs without performance loss. A language-guided uncertainty calibration loss (a focal loss aligning scaled-cosine classification confidence to IoU-based localization confidence) enables progressive, sigmoidal-threshold query pruning across decoder layers, further reducing transformer decoder FLOPs by an additional 50% at inference. Finally, replacing GLEE's unbounded dot-product classification with scaled cosine similarity and combining it with the calibration loss stabilizes training, eliminating the need for multi-stage curriculum learning and achieving convergence in one-third of GLEE's iterations.

## Results

On the big-model comparison (Table 2), MOBIUS-3 achieves COCO-val APb 57.8 / APm 50.9 and LVIS APb 50.3 at 354G FLOPs, using roughly half the FLOPs of GLEE-Plus (704G) while retaining competitive accuracy. MOBIUS-1 (155G FLOPs) matches GLEE-Lite (239G) on COCO APb (55.0) with 35% fewer FLOPs. On mobile hardware (Table 1), MOBIUS reduces pixel decoder FLOPs by 40.5% and mobile latency by ~70% versus the GLEE-MaskDINO baseline, while outperforming it on zero-shot ODinW (40.3 vs. 37.0 APb). The model runs at 10 FPS on mobile devices and 25 FPS on high-end GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLEE-Plus (704G FLOPs), MOBIUS-3 (354G) achieves 2.6 APb lower on COCO (57.8 vs. 60.4) and 2.4 APb lower on LVIS (50.3 vs. 52.7), but at roughly half the computational cost. Against GLEE-Lite (239G), MOBIUS-1 (155G) matches COCO APb exactly (55.0) and slightly exceeds APm (48.5 vs. 48.4) with 35% fewer FLOPs, though it trails on ODinW zero-shot (40.5 vs. 43.2). Among specialist models, MOBIUS-3's COCO APm of 50.9 matches ViTDet-H (50.9) and approaches MaskDINO-L (54.5), while its LVIS APb of 50.3 is competitive with ViTDet-L (51.2). On the mobile setting (Table 1), MOBIUS outperforms GLEE-RT-DETR on ODinW by 9.6 APb (40.3 vs. 30.7) and on LVIS by 5.1 APb (27.9 vs. 22.8), while reducing FLOPs by 40.5% relative to GLEE-MaskDINO. MOBIUS also surpasses UNINEXT-L (58.1 COCO APb) in zero-shot ODinW (44.0 vs. not reported) and X-Decoder-L in LVIS APm (46.5 vs. 47.1), demonstrating that the bottleneck design preserves open-vocabulary generalization that RT-DETR-based decoders sacrifice.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOBIUS builds directly on GLEE's universal instance segmentation framework and MaskDINO's unified transformer architecture, but deliberately inverts the prevailing 'scale up' paradigm by pursuing Pareto-optimal downscaling. Its core architectural departure is the bottleneck encoder, which replaces MaskDINO's expensive multi-scale transformer pixel decoder with a single-scale deformable-attention design that compresses multi-scale and multi-modal features into one representational bottleneck. The paper also departs from RT-DETR's efficiency strategy (hybrid convolutional fusion), demonstrating that RT-DETR's design sacrifices open-vocabulary generalization, and introduces a language-guided uncertainty calibration loss that replaces the standard focal classification loss to enable inference-time query pruning. Training is unified into a single stage by substituting GLEE's unbounded dot-product classification with scaled cosine similarity plus the calibration loss, eliminating the need for GLEE's multi-stage curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOBIUS is the first generalist instance segmentation model family explicitly designed for edge deployment, achieving real-time inference (10 FPS mobile, 25 FPS GPU) while retaining competitive open-vocabulary performance — a practical gap that prior foundation models (GLEE, GroundingDINO) left unaddressed. The bottleneck encoder and calibrated pruning are modular techniques likely to transfer to other DETR-based architectures seeking efficiency. Open questions include whether the single-scale bottleneck generalizes to video instance segmentation or higher-resolution inputs, whether the calibration loss benefits other multi-modal detection settings, and how far the Pareto frontier can be pushed before open-vocabulary performance degrades. The paper's reliance on 64 H100 GPUs for training also means the training-side efficiency gains, while real, do not yet democratize the full training pipeline.
