## Problem

Adapter-based methods (e.g., ControlNet) for controllable video generation suffer from branch conflicts when multiple independently trained adapters are combined, introduce parameter redundancy per condition, and achieve suboptimal quality compared to full fine-tuning. No prior work has explored whether a unified full-attention mechanism can seamlessly integrate multiple heterogeneous video control conditions (camera, identity, depth) within a single generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251881/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

FullDiT accepts a text prompt (injected via cross-attention), camera parameters as per-frame 3×4 pose matrices (converted to Plücker embeddings, temporal-only), one or more identity images (spatial-only, encoded by a causal 3D VAE), and a depth video (temporal-spatial, same VAE pipeline as the target video). All conditions are patchified into token sequences and concatenated along the sequence dimension before entering the transformer. The model outputs a 384×672, 77-frame video (~5 s at 15 FPS) generated with 50 diffusion steps and classifier-free guidance scale 5.

## Method

FullDiT replaces per-condition adapter branches with a unified sequence: each condition is tokenized (camera via Plücker embedding at patch size 16; identities and depth via VAE encoding followed by patch size 2) and concatenated with the noisy video latent into a single long sequence. The transformer blocks apply 2D self-attention with 2D RoPE for spatial modeling, 3D self-attention with 3D RoPE for joint spatiotemporal interaction across all conditions, cross-attention for text, and feedforward layers, with diffusion timesteps injected via AdaLN-Zero. A progressive training strategy introduces conditions in order of difficulty (text → camera → identities → depth), followed by quality fine-tuning on curated high-quality data. This design eliminates separate adapter parameters, resolves inter-branch conflicts through shared attention, and yields emergent generalization to condition combinations (e.g., camera + identity) never jointly seen during training.

## Results

On FullBench, FullDiT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three single-condition tasks. For camera-to-video, FullDiT reduces RotErr to 1.20 (vs. 1.49 for MotionCtrl, 1.57 for CameraCtrl) and TransErr to 3.31 (vs. 4.41, 3.88), while boosting dynamics to 30.53 (vs. 11.43, 13.72). For identities-to-video, FullDiT improves DINO-I from 39.97 to 46.22 and CLIP-I from 65.63 to 68.59 over ConceptMaster. For depth-to-video, FullDiT cuts MAE to 14.71, nearly halving the error of Ctrl-Adapter (25.63) and ControlVideo (30.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FullDiT outperforms all camera-to-video baselines (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, CamI2V) on every camera control metric, with the largest gains in TransErr (3.31 vs. 4.41 for MotionCtrl) and dynamics (30.53 vs. 11.43). Against ConceptMaster on identities-to-video, FullDiT improves DINO-I by 6.25 points (46.22 vs. 39.97) and CLIP-I by 2.96 points, while also generating significantly more dynamic videos (16.68 vs. 10.14). On depth-to-video, FullDiT nearly halves the MAE compared to both Ctrl-Adapter (14.71 vs. 25.63) and ControlVideo (14.71 vs. 30.10), and surpasses both in dynamics (23.12 vs. 15.47/18.62). The only metric where FullDiT trails is aesthetic score on depth-to-video (5.26 vs. 5.91 for ControlVideo), which the authors attribute to ControlVideo's painting-style output being favored by the LAION-Aesthetic scorer. FullDiT also slightly loses on smoothness for identities (94.95 vs. 95.05), explained by its higher inter-frame motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

FullDiT extends the MMDiT design (Esser et al., ICML 2024), which demonstrated that text and image conditions can be jointly processed via full self-attention rather than cross-attention, to the multi-condition video generation setting. It departs fundamentally from the dominant adapter-based paradigm (ControlNet, CameraCtrl, CamI2V, ConceptMaster) that attaches a separate processing branch per condition, instead tokenizing all conditions—camera (via plücker embedding), identity images, and depth video—into a single concatenated sequence processed by shared 2D/3D self-attention blocks. The progressive multi-stage training strategy, where harder conditions (camera) are introduced before easier ones (depth), is a novel empirical contribution not present in prior single-condition or adapter-based training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FullDiT addresses a practical bottleneck in creative video production: adapter-based methods suffer from branch conflicts, parameter redundancy, and suboptimal quality when multiple conditions are combined, and no prior open-source method handles multi-condition video generation. The emergent ability to combine previously unseen condition pairs (e.g., camera + identity) without explicit joint training data suggests a path toward truly composable video control, though this is demonstrated only qualitatively at 1B scale. Key open questions include how to efficiently adapt the architecture to new modalities (audio, segmentation), whether the full-attention approach scales to larger models and more conditions, and how to integrate text through full self-attention (as in MMDiT) rather than the cross-attention used here due to pre-trained model constraints.
