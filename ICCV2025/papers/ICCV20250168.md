## Problem

Auto-regressive mesh generation faces two critical gaps: existing tokenization schemes produce excessively long sequences that make high-resolution training computationally prohibitive, and no prior method aligns generated meshes with human aesthetic preferences, leaving outputs with geometric defects (holes, missing parts) and suboptimal wireframe topology. The paper addresses both by improving tokenization efficiency and introducing DPO-based preference alignment to 3D mesh generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a point cloud of 16,384 points (sampled from 20k) as conditioning signal, or an RGB image that is first converted to a point cloud via TRELIS. It outputs a structured artist-like triangle mesh with up to 30k faces at a quantization resolution of 512, where the mesh topology is optimized for downstream editing, deformation, and texture mapping rather than merely matching surface geometry.

## Method

DeepMesh is a 500M–1B parameter autoregressive transformer (with cross-attention for point cloud conditioning and an Hourglass architecture for 50% memory savings) trained in two stages. The first stage uses an improved tokenization algorithm that combines local patch traversal, three-level hierarchical block indexing, and offset merging to achieve ~72% sequence compression with a vocabulary of only 4,736 (vs. BPT's 40,960), alongside data curation to filter poor-quality meshes and truncated sliding-window training with face-count-based batch allocation for load balancing. The second stage applies Direct Preference Optimization using ~5,000 preference pairs constructed by first filtering on Chamfer Distance for geometric completeness and then recruiting human raters for aesthetic judgment, aligning the model to produce meshes that are both geometrically faithful and visually appealing.

## Results

On a 100-mesh test set, DeepMesh with DPO achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0884 and Hausdorff Distance of 0.1708, outperforming MeshAnythingv2 (0.1249 / 0.2991) and BPT (0.1425 / 0.2796) in geometric fidelity. In a user study, DeepMesh with DPO is preferred 37% of the time versus 10% for MeshAnythingv2 and 19% for BPT. The tokenization algorithm achieves a 72% compression ratio with a vocabulary size of only 4,736 (vs. 40,960 for BPT) and the fastest training time of 480 s on 80 high-poly meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepMesh outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against MeshAnythingv2 [5], DeepMesh reduces Chamfer Distance by 29% (0.0884 vs. 0.1249) and Hausdorff Distance by 43% (0.1708 vs. 0.2991), while winning the user study 37% to 10%. Against BPT [67], the improvements are 38% in Chamfer Distance (0.0884 vs. 0.1425) and 39% in Hausdorff Distance (0.1708 vs. 0.2796), with a 37%-to-19% user-study margin. EdgeRunner [53] is compared only in the tokenization ablation (Table 2), where DeepMesh's algorithm achieves a comparable compression ratio (0.28 vs. 0.47) but with a much smaller vocabulary (4,736 vs. 512) and faster training (480 s vs. not reported). The DPO post-training stage contributes an additional 12% Chamfer Distance reduction and 3 percentage-point user-study gain over the pre-trained model alone, confirming that preference alignment meaningfully improves both geometry and aesthetics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepMesh extends the auto-regressive mesh generation lineage (MeshGPT → MeshAnything → MeshAnythingv2 → EdgeRunner → BPT) by improving BPT's block-wise indexing to handle high-resolution meshes without the dramatic vocabulary explosion that limits BPT to low resolutions. Its second major contribution is a cross-domain transfer: adapting Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from LLM alignment to 3D auto-regressive mesh generation, a setting where no prior work had applied preference-based post-training. The model architecture itself (Hourglass Transformer with perceiver encoder) follows established designs from MeshTron and Michelangelo, so the novelty is concentrated in the tokenization algorithm and the DPO post-training pipeline rather than in the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that DPO can meaningfully improve 3D mesh generation quality, opening a path toward preference-aligned 3D asset creation that mirrors the LLM post-training paradigm. However, the DPO gains are modest (C.Dist. 0.1001→0.0884, user preference 34%→37%) and rely on only ~5,000 annotated pairs, leaving open whether the approach scales with larger preference datasets or more diverse geometry. The tokenization's 8.6× vocabulary reduction over BPT (4,736 vs 40,960) is a practical efficiency win for high-poly training, but the overall contribution is an incremental step rather than a paradigm shift in 3D generation.
