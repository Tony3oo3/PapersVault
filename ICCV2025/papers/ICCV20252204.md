key_contribution: Proposes AdvRF, a GAN-inspired adversarial reconstruction feedback framework that reformulates fine-grained image retrieval as a visual discrepancy reconstruction task, synergizing category-aware discrepancy localization from a retrieval model with category-agnostic feature learning from a reconstruction model through alternating optimization and knowledge distillation, achieving state-of-the-art Recall@1 on CUB-200-2011 (76.6%), Stanford Cars (94.9%), FGVC Aircraft (88.0%), and Stanford Online Products (84.2%).
limitations: The paper does not include an explicit limitations section. The evaluation is confined to four object-centric benchmarks (birds, cars, aircraft, products) where 'unseen categories' are held-out subcategories within the same object class, leaving open whether the category-agnostic property generalizes to truly novel visual domains. Additionally, the training procedure requires maintaining and alternately updating two full networks (ResNet-50 retrieval + ResNet-34/U-Net reconstruction), and no analysis of training cost, convergence stability of the adversarial loop, or scalability to larger backbones (e.g., ViT) is provided.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing that neither classification-based feedback (66.3%) nor reconstruction-based feedback alone (62.2%) approaches the adversarial combination (76.6%) is a useful demonstration that both components are necessary, but the overall performance gains (~2% over PLEor on CUB, ~1.8% over CaRA on SOP) are solid yet incremental for the field. The conceptual insight that reconstruction encoders naturally produce category-agnostic features is reasonable and not entirely unexpected given the nature of pixel-level reconstruction objectives.

## Problem

Existing fine-grained image retrieval methods couple discrepancy modeling with predefined category supervision, inadvertently embedding category-specific semantics into retrieval representations. This causes visually similar cues across subcategories to appear dissimilar while dissimilar cues within the same subcategory appear similar, critically hindering generalization to unseen categories. The paper asks whether category-aware discrepancy localization from retrieval models can be synergized with category-agnostic feature learning from reconstruction models to decouple discrepancy modeling from categorical supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252204/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 256×256, randomly cropped to 224×224 during training) and produces two outputs: a C-dimensional retrieval embedding obtained via global average pooling over the ResNet-50 feature tensor, and a spatially-resolved discrepancy pattern map (H×W) indicating where visual differences reside. At inference time, only the lightweight retrieval model is deployed (21.1 ms per embedding), as the reconstruction model's category-agnostic representations are distilled into the retrieval model during training, eliminating the need for the heavier reconstruction pipeline at test time.

## Method

AdvRF reformulates FGIR as a visual discrepancy reconstruction task by adversarially coupling a ResNet-50 retrieval model with a ResNet-34/U-Net reconstruction model in a GAN-inspired alternating optimization loop. A lightweight generator (with an exponential moving-average twin) produces a pattern map from retrieval features, which decomposes the reconstruction encoder's category-agnostic image representation into discrepancy and non-discrepancy components via element-wise masking. In the reconstruction feedback phase, the decoder reconstructs the image from each component and the retrieval model is challenged to maximize reconstruction error in discrepancy regions (forcing better localization); in the retrieval feedback phase, the reconstruction model minimizes reconstruction error using residual discrepancies the retrieval model missed. A category-agnostic discrepancy parameterization loss (Frobenius norm between the retrieval embedding and the GAP of the discrepancy component) distills the purified representations into the retrieval model, enabling efficient single-model deployment while preserving category-agnostic visual descriptions.

## Results

AdvRF achieves 76.6% Recall@1 on CUB-200-2011, 94.9% on Stanford Cars 196, 88.0% on FGVC Aircraft, and 84.2% on Stanford Online Products, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. On CUB-200-2011, it surpasses the previous best (PLEor, 74.8%) by 1.8 points at R@1 and by 0.1 points at R@8 (95.0% vs. 94.9%). On SOP, AdvRF reaches 99.1% R@1000, the highest among all compared methods, while also leading at R@1 by 1.8 points over CaRA (82.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvRF outperforms all 16 baselines in Table 3 across every reported metric on all three fine-grained datasets. The closest competitor is PLEor (CVPR 2023), which achieves 74.8% R@1 on CUB-200-2011 versus AdvRF's 76.6% (+1.8), 94.4% on Stanford Cars versus 94.9% (+0.5), and 86.3% on FGVC Aircraft versus 88.0% (+1.7). Among localization-based methods, CaRA (TPAMI 2024) is the strongest, trailing AdvRF by 2.7 points on CUB (73.9% vs. 76.6%) and 3.7 points on Aircraft (84.3% vs. 88.0%). On the coarse-grained SOP benchmark (Table 4), AdvRF leads CaRA by 1.8 points at R@1 (84.2% vs. 82.4%) and by 0.6 points at R@10 (93.7% vs. 92.6%), while also achieving the best R@1000 (99.1%). The advantage is most pronounced on CUB-200-2011, where the gap over the second-best method (FRPT, 74.3%) reaches 2.3 points, suggesting the adversarial reconstruction feedback is particularly effective for highly fine-grained categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in fine-grained image retrieval (FGIR), where both localization-based (CaRA, SCDA) and metric-based (NIA, PLEor, HIST) methods couple visual discrepancy modeling with predefined category supervision, inadvertently embedding category-specific semantics into retrieval representations. Rather than building on a single prior method, AdvRF synthesizes the GAN adversarial training paradigm with reconstruction-based feature learning, repurposing the generator-discriminator dynamic as a mutual refinement loop between a retrieval model (localizing discrepancies) and a reconstruction model (encoding them category-agnostically). The knowledge distillation step for efficient deployment draws on standard distillation practice, but the specific use of a reconstruction model's encoder output as a supervisory signal for the retrieval model's embeddings is a novel combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in FGIR: reliance on seen-category semantics hinders generalization to unseen subcategories, a problem that matters for applications like retrieving novel species or product types. The adversarial feedback mechanism provides a principled way to decouple discrepancy localization from categorical supervision, and the resulting 21.1ms inference time (vs. 36.7ms for the full pipeline) makes the approach practical. Open questions include whether the adversarial training is stable at scale, whether the category-agnostic property holds for truly novel visual domains (not just held-out subcategories of the same object class), and whether the framework extends to multi-modal or video retrieval settings.
