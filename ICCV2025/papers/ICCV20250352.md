## Problem

Detection-free image-to-point cloud registration suffers from two under-addressed issues: (1) channel-level attention mismatches between 2D image features and 3D point cloud features cause misaligned receptive fields and degraded cross-modal matching, and (2) local top-k patch selection produces redundant many-to-one correspondences when scenes contain repetitive structures, undermining registration accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250352/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (h×w×3) and a sparse 3D point cloud (n×3) of the same scene, with no explicit keypoint detection step. Image features are extracted via FPN-equipped ResNet and point cloud features via KPFCNN, both producing 512-dimensional descriptors after positional encoding and transformer enhancement. The output is a rigid transformation [R, t] (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) estimated by PnP-RANSAC from dense pixel-to-point correspondences, evaluated on indoor RGB-D datasets requiring 30–50% scene overlap.

## Method

CA-I2P introduces a Channel Adaptive Adjustment Module (CAA) with two stages: an Intra-Modal Enhancement stage that applies rotation-based cross-dimensional attention (modeling C-H, C-W, and H-W interactions via GO-Pool) for images and channel self-attention for point clouds, fusing enhanced features with originals through learnable weights; and a Cross-Modal Channel Filtering stage that uses instance normalization and a cross-modal covariance matrix to selectively mask channels with high inter-modality variance (optimized via an upper-triangular constraint to prevent modality overfitting). A Global Optimal Selection Module (GOS) then replaces local top-k matching with an entropy-regularized Optimal Transport problem solved by Sinkhorn iterations (~10 steps), enforcing marginal constraints to eliminate many-to-one correspondences globally. Training combines circle loss for coarse and fine matching with a channel-filtering loss L_f, and the full pipeline is supervised end-to-end on paired image-point cloud data.

## Results

On RGB-D Scenes V2, CA-I2P achieves a mean Inlier Ratio of 35.5%, Feature Matching Recall of 93.6%, and Registration Recall of 63.3%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art 2D3D-MATR (32.4%, 90.8%, 56.4%) by 3.1 pp, 2.8 pp, and 6.9 pp respectively. On 7-Scenes, CA-I2P reaches a Registration Recall of 79.5% (vs. 75.8% for 2D3D-MATR) and an Inlier Ratio of 51.6% (vs. 50.1%), with particularly notable gains in the challenging Heads (68.5% vs. 52.1% RR) and Stairs (31.1% vs. 28.4% RR) scenes. The ablation confirms that the full CAA+GOS pipeline (M8) improves RR by 7.5 pp over the base model (M1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA-I2P outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the closest competitor 2D3D-MATR [28], CA-I2P gains 3.1 pp in Inlier Ratio and 6.9 pp in Registration Recall on RGB-D Scenes V2, and 3.7 pp in Registration Recall on 7-Scenes (79.5% vs. 75.8%). The gap over earlier detect-then-match methods is substantially larger: P2-Net [52] trails by 23.1 pp in RR on RGB-D Scenes V2 (38.4% vs. 63.3%), and Predator-2D3D [23] by 33.1 pp (30.2% vs. 63.3%). FCGF-2D3D [9], the oldest baseline, is far behind on all metrics (8.1% IR, 30.4% RR on RGB-D Scenes V2). On 7-Scenes, CA-I2P's advantage over 2D3D-MATR is most pronounced in the Heads scene (68.5% vs. 52.1% RR), where small 3D errors are amplified by close camera proximity, and in Stairs (31.1% vs. 28.4% RR), where repetitive patterns complicate matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detector-free, coarse-to-fine paradigm established by 2D3D-MATR (ICCV 2023), which uses transformers for patch-level matching followed by PnP-RANSAC. The key departure is a two-pronged intervention at the feature level: a Channel Adaptive Adjustment Module (CAA) that applies modality-specific channel attention (rotated pooling for images, self-attention for point clouds) plus covariance-based cross-modal channel filtering, and a Global Optimal Selection Module (GOS) that replaces the standard top-k patch selection with an entropy-regularized Optimal Transport formulation (Sinkhorn iterations), drawing on the OT assignment literature from object detection (OTA, Caron et al.). The channel filtering stage borrows the instance-normalization-plus-covariance-suppression recipe from domain adaptation, adapted here to suppress modality-sensitive feature channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies channel-level attention mismatch as a concrete source of cross-modal registration errors and provides a clean, ablated solution, which is useful for practitioners building I2P pipelines. However, evaluation is confined to two indoor benchmarks (RGB-D Scenes V2, 7-Scenes), and the authors themselves note in Section 3.5 that outdoor scenes with limited overlap would require additional detection steps, leaving generalization to large-scale outdoor registration open. The GOS module increases total inference time (2.84 s vs 1.27 s) despite reducing matching time, raising practical deployment questions. Whether the channel-adaptive filtering generalizes to other cross-modal pairing tasks (e.g., LiDAR-camera, thermal-visible) remains unexplored.
