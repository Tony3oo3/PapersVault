## Problem

Existing group dance generation methods are limited to a fixed number of dancers, requiring architecture-specific retraining to accommodate different group sizes. This paper addresses the gap of generating harmonic, diverse group dance motions for an arbitrary user-specified number of dancers within a single unified framework, while preserving both individual motion diversity and coherent inter-dancer interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 5-second music signal (150 frames of 35-dimensional Librosa features) and a user-specified dancer number h (scalable from 1 to H_max) as input, and produces a 3D group dance sequence S ∈ R^{150×h×151} where each frame encodes 24-joint SMPL pose with 6D rotations, 3D root position, and 4D binary foot contact per dancer. At inference, a mask logits strategy restricts token predictions to the partition corresponding to the requested dancer count, and padding dancers are post-processed away.

## Method

FreeDance is a two-stage supervised framework built on 2D discrete token modeling. Stage one constructs a 2D token map via a VQ-VAE with a person-number-dependent quantization strategy that partitions the codebook into H_max non-overlapping sub-spaces, each encoding motions for a specific dancer count. Stage two employs masked token modeling over the 2D token sequence, where two key modules operate: CRAM computes temporal residuals between consecutive motion tokens to capture spatial deformation, then fuses this with music features via cross-modality matrix multiplication to enforce rhythm alignment while preserving individual diversity; TIM reshapes tokens to flatten the dancer dimension into the temporal axis, computes a self-attention interaction matrix, and injects neighbor-dancer style cues via MaxPool + AdaIN to foster group coordination. The full training objective combines classification loss on masked tokens, reconstruction, velocity, forward-kinematics joint, and foot-contact losses.

## Results

On the combined AIST++ and AIOZ-GDANCE test set, FreeDance achieves the best Beat Align Score (0.2609) and Diversity (3.42) among all compared methods, while maintaining a competitive FID of 1.15 (vs. EDGE's 0.92 and POPDG's 1.24). In user preference studies, FreeDance is preferred over all baselines with win rates ranging from 75.0% (vs. EDGE) to 96.4% (vs. Bailando and POPDG). The method uniquely supports arbitrary dancer counts (1 to H_max) in a single trained model without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeDance outperforms all five baselines on BAS (music-dance alignment) and Diversity, with the largest margin over group-dance methods GCD (0.2316) and CoDancers (0.2460). On FID, the adapted solo methods EDGE (0.92) and POPDG (1.24) slightly edge out FreeDance (1.15), but this comes at the cost of poor group coordination and minimal inter-dancer diversity (Diversity 2.16 and 2.13 vs. 3.42). Among group-dance baselines, GCD has the worst FID (5.07) and BAS (0.2316), while CoDancers is intermediate (FID 3.58, BAS 0.2460) but still trails FreeDance on all three objective metrics. In user studies, FreeDance is preferred over every baseline, with the closest margin being 75.0% vs. EDGE and the widest being 96.4% vs. Bailando and POPDG, confirming that the adapted solo methods produce visually unconvincing group choreography despite their competitive FID scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE discrete token paradigm (van den Oord et al., 2017) from 1D single-dancer motion sequences to a 2D token map that jointly encodes person number and temporal dynamics, enabling a person-number-dependent quantization strategy. It departs from prior group dance methods (GCD, CoDancers, GDanceR, PDVAE) that are locked to a fixed dancer count and require retraining per group size, by formulating free-number generation as a masked token prediction problem over a unified token space. The interaction modeling draws on adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN) from style transfer, repurposed here to inject neighbor-dancer temporal cues into each dancer's motion features, while the residual-based cross-modality alignment (CRAM) is a novel twist on music-motion synchronization that differs from standard cross-attention approaches used in solo dance generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeDance is the first framework to generate group dance with an arbitrary user-specified number of dancers in a single trained model, eliminating the retraining overhead that plagues fixed-size group choreography methods and making the approach directly applicable to AR/VR avatar systems and robotics where group size varies per scene. The 5-second clip limitation is a significant practical constraint, and the paper itself identifies dance extension and transition generation as key future directions. An open question is whether the 2D token map and person-number-dependent quantization strategy generalize cleanly beyond the tested range (1–3 dancers in main experiments, up to 5 in supplementary), and whether the masked token modeling approach scales to the longer, more complex choreographies seen in real performances.
