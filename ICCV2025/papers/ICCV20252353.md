## Problem

Reconstructing articulated objects from RGB images remains challenging because existing methods require 3D supervision or laborious annotations, are prone to local optima in joint optimization, and lack real-time photorealistic rendering. No prior self-supervised, category-agnostic approach combines accurate part-level reconstruction with articulation estimation while preserving the speed and visual fidelity of explicit 3D representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPLART takes two sets of posed RGB images (with known intrinsics and extrinsics in a shared world frame), each capturing the object at one end state of a known articulation type (revolute or prismatic). It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting model with per-Gaussian mobility values enabling part-level segmentation, estimated articulation parameters (axis, pivot, angle for revolute; axis, distance for prismatic), and real-time rendering at arbitrary novel viewpoints and intermediate articulation states. The method assumes a two-part object (one static parent, one mobile child) and does not require depth maps, 3D annotations, or category-specific priors.

## Method

SPLART augments 3DGS with a differentiable mobility parameter m ∈ [0,1] per Gaussian, splitting each Gaussian into static (opacity σ·(1−m)) and mobile (opacity σ·m) components. A three-stage decoupled optimization avoids local minima: Stage 1 independently reconstructs each articulation state via standard 3DGS photometric loss; Stage 2 estimates mobilities using a cross-static formulation that jointly renders the static components from both states plus the target state's mobile component, first with weighted Chamfer distance (geometric) then with photometric supervision; Stage 3 estimates articulation parameters via a cross-mobile formulation that transforms the other state's mobile component into the target state's frame, again combining geometric and photometric losses with multi-restart strategies for robustness. A final mobility correction step uses geometric consistency to fix Gaussians that lost photometric supervision due to occlusion.

## Results

On the PARIS-PMS benchmark, SPLART achieves a 99% articulation success rate with an axis error of 3.44×10⁻² DEG and a rotation error of 4.85×10⁻² DEG, substantially outperforming DTA (13.11×10⁻² DEG axis error) and PARIS (103.12×10⁻² DEG). For novel view synthesis, SPLART reaches a PSNR of 43.70 dB and mIoU of 0.957, compared to PARIS's 32.52 dB and 0.942. On the more challenging SPLART-PMS dataset (with non-binary articulation states), SPLART maintains an 85.5% success rate versus 59.1% for DTA and 27.7% for PARIS, while achieving a PSNR of 37.01 dB and depth MAE of 0.032.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPLART decisively outperforms PARIS [30] across all metrics: on PARIS-PMS, SPLART achieves a 99% success rate versus PARIS's 42%, with axis error reduced from 103.12×10⁻² DEG to 3.44×10⁻² DEG (a ~30× improvement) and PSNR improved from 32.52 to 43.70 dB. Against DTA [60], SPLART achieves substantially lower articulation errors (err_a: 3.44 vs. 13.11×10⁻² DEG; err_r: 4.85 vs. 19.41×10⁻² DEG) while matching its success rate of 99%; however, DTA achieves a slightly lower whole-object Chamfer distance (1.83 vs. 3.61×10⁻³), though DTA requires ground-truth depth input and cannot produce photorealistic renderings. On the harder SPLART-PMS benchmark with non-binary articulation states, SPLART's advantage over DTA widens in success rate (85.5% vs. 59.1%) and prismatic translation error (1.04 vs. 9.25×10⁻³), while DTA retains a marginal edge on revolute rotation error (40.80 vs. 78.31×10⁻² DEG) and pivot error (1.28 vs. 3.93×10⁻³). SPLART also surpasses both baselines on novel view synthesis quality, achieving PSNR of 43.70 dB on PARIS-PMS (vs. 32.52 for PARIS; DTA does not report PSNR) and 37.01 dB on SPLART-PMS (vs. 32.51 for PARIS).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPLART extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing a differentiable per-Gaussian mobility parameter, repurposing the explicit Gaussian representation for part-level articulated object reconstruction. It departs from prior NeRF-based methods (PARIS, DTA) that jointly optimize shape, appearance, and articulation end-to-end with 3D supervision, instead using a decoupled multi-stage strategy with purely geometric and photometric self-supervision from posed RGB images. The cross-static and cross-mobile formulations are novel loss constructions that leverage geometric consistency between two articulation states without requiring depth, part labels, or category priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPLART is the first to bring real-time (100× faster than NeRF-based) photorealistic rendering to articulated object reconstruction while eliminating 3D supervision, making the task accessible to non-expert users with a handheld camera. The key limitation is that it handles only two-part (one static, one mobile) articulations; extending to multi-level or multi-part objects remains open, though the authors note iterative application to incremental state pairs as a potential path. Inherent ambiguities—where multiple articulation models explain the same state transition—also remain unresolved and are acknowledged as a fundamental challenge.
