## Problem

Existing deep watermarking methods for content provenance are constrained to fixed resolutions, lack a reliable mechanism for watermark removal and re-watermarking, and struggle to simultaneously achieve high imperceptibility (PSNR > 40 dB) and robustness against the diverse perturbations encountered on content platforms. TrustMark addresses these gaps by proposing a unified framework that handles arbitrary-resolution watermarking, robust encoding, and high-fidelity watermark removal for the full provenance lifecycle.

## Input / Output

The embedder takes an RGB cover image of arbitrary resolution and a binary watermark vector of length l (tested from 32 to 200 bits), producing a watermarked image at the original resolution. Internally, the network operates at 256×256; the Resolution Scaling algorithm downsamples the input, embeds at 256×256, computes the residual, upsamples only the residual, and adds it back to the original image with a controllable strength factor λ. The extractor (ResNet-50) recovers the l-bit watermark from the perturbed watermarked image, and ReMark takes a watermarked image and outputs a restored image with the watermark signal removed while improving PSNR.

## Method

TrustMark's embedder uses a modified MUNIT backbone (channel normalization removed, depth doubled, internal dimension halved) followed by a post-process module of stacked 1×1 convolutions with SiLU activations and a final tanh, which preserves high-frequency detail better than a single convolutional layer. Training combines a binary cross-entropy recovery loss with a quality loss that includes YUV MSE, LPIPS, a novel Focal Frequency Loss (FFL) operating in the 2D Fourier domain with dynamic spectral weighting, and a GAN loss with gradient penalty; a noise module applies 3 geometric transforms plus 2 random perturbations from a pool of 15 differentiable sources during training. The extractor is a standard ResNet-50 with a final sigmoid FC layer, and training follows a multi-stage schedule that prioritizes watermark recovery before enabling noise simulation and GAN training. ReMark, based on the KBNet restoration backbone, is trained as a denoiser on synthetically watermarked images with pixel, GAN, and watermark-similarity losses to remove the TrustMark signal while enhancing image quality. Resolution Scaling is a post-hoc inference-time algorithm that shifts the arbitrary-resolution burden from the embedder to the extractor, enabling any fixed-resolution watermarking model to operate at arbitrary resolutions with only image interpolation overhead.

## Results

On the three main benchmarks (CLIC, DIV2K, MetFace), TrustMark (α_max=27.5) achieves PSNR of 43.26, 42.39, and 45.34 dB respectively, with bit accuracy of 0.95, 0.95, and 0.96 on noised watermarked images. TrustMark outperforms all baselines on every metric except DIV2K PSNR, where SSL is marginally higher (42.73 vs 42.39 dB). On the RoSteALS benchmark (ImageNet-C corruptions, CLIC dataset), TrustMark (α_max=15) achieves 38.87 dB PSNR and 0.95 bit accuracy, surpassing RoSteALS by +6 dB in PSNR while matching its 0.94 accuracy. For watermark removal, ReMark restores images to 48.48 dB PSNR while reducing bit accuracy to 0.553 (near random), substantially outperforming all adversarial-attack and denoising-based removal baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoSteALS [13], TrustMark wins decisively on PSNR (43.3 vs 30.0 dB on CLIC; 42.4 vs 28.0 on DIV2K) while matching its bit accuracy (0.95 vs 0.94), making RoSteALS the closest robustness competitor but far inferior in imperceptibility. Against RivaGAN [73], TrustMark leads by ~2 dB PSNR and ~16 percentage points in bit accuracy across all three benchmarks. Against SSL [25], SSL edges out TrustMark by 0.34 dB on DIV2K PSNR (42.73 vs 42.39), but TrustMark's bit accuracy is dramatically higher (0.95 vs 0.57 on DIV2K, 0.95 vs 0.60 on CLIC), revealing SSL's fundamental robustness weakness. Against StegaStamp [66] and the classical dwtDctSvd [51], TrustMark dominates on both metrics by wide margins (e.g., 0.95 vs 0.72 accuracy vs StegaStamp; 0.95 vs 0.52 vs dwtDctSvd). For watermark removal (Table 3), ReMark achieves 48.48 dB PSNR while driving bit accuracy to 0.553, outperforming Pinto et al. [57] (40.75 dB), Hönig et al. [36] (39.93 dB, but only partially removes at 0.830 accuracy), I-FGSM [30] (23.48 dB), and Zhao et al. [76] (9.23 dB) on both quality and removal effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrustMark extends the encoder-decoder watermarking paradigm established by HiDDeN, StegaStamp, and RivaGAN by replacing the typical UNet-like embedder with a MUNIT-based style-transfer backbone, adding a 1×1 convolution post-processing layer for high-frequency preservation, and introducing a focal frequency loss (borrowed from image synthesis) to reduce spectral artifacts. The ReMark removal network repurposes KBNet, a state-of-the-art image restoration architecture, for the previously unexplored task of invisible watermark removal and re-watermarking. The Resolution Scaling algorithm is a post-hoc, model-agnostic technique that shifts the arbitrary-resolution burden from the embedder to the extractor, distinguishing it from architecture-specific approaches like RivaGAN's bottleneck-free design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrustMark addresses a practical gap in content provenance workflows: imperceptible, robust watermarking that survives platform perturbations while supporting re-watermarking and arbitrary resolutions, aligning with C2PA standards already adopted by Adobe and other industry partners. Key limitations include degraded accuracy on highly cluttered images, cumulative quality loss over multiple ReMark re-watermarking cycles, and the finding that only residual/skip-connection backbones (ResNet family) converge for the extractor under the multi-noise training regime. Open questions include extension to video, integration with GenAI training-data attribution, and whether the adversarial robustness gains from high-severity noise simulation transfer to stronger attack models beyond I-FGSM.
