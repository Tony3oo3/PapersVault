## Problem

Existing image-to-3D generation methods struggle to reproduce fine-grained geometric details due to three compounding factors: a domain gap between synthetic training renders and real-world input images, inherent ambiguity in RGB signals (lighting, shading, textures), and a scarcity of high-quality 3D training assets with complex surface detail. The paper identifies that normal maps, as a 2.5D representation encoding surface orientation, can serve as an effective intermediate bridge that alleviates the domain gap and provides clearer geometric cues than raw RGB for guiding 3D geometry learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a 3D mesh with rich surface detail as the final output, with a per-pixel normal map as the intermediate representation. The normal estimator operates in a regression (one-step) manner rather than iterative diffusion, producing stable predictions. For 3D generation evaluation, normal maps are rendered from 22 viewpoints around each object to compute both overall normal angle error and sharp-edge-specific error metrics.

## Method

Hi3DGen decomposes image-to-3D generation into two stages connected by normal maps. The first stage, NiRNE, is a dual-stream regression network where a clean stream captures low-frequency structural information and a noise-injected stream (motivated by the frequency-domain SNR analysis of diffusion processes) captures high-frequency sharp details; the two streams are concatenated in a ControlNet-style fashion and trained with a domain-specific schedule (real data first for generalizability, then synthetic data to fine-tune only the noisy stream for sharpness). The second stage, NoRLD, trains a flow-matching latent diffusion model over 3D geometry encoded by a VAE, augmented with an online normal regularization loss that decodes the predicted latent back to explicit 3D geometry and penalizes the L2 difference between rendered and ground-truth normal maps at multiple viewpoints, providing direct geometric supervision during diffusion training rather than as a post-processing step. A 700K-asset DetailVerse dataset, synthesized via a Text→Image (Flux.1-Dev)→3D (Trellis) pipeline with rigorous cleaning, supplies the high-detail training pairs needed for both stages.

## Results

On LUCES-MV, NiRNE achieves a Normal Error (NE) of 21.837° and Sharp Normal Error (SNE) of 26.628°, outperforming the best regression baseline Lotus-d (NE=26.386°, SNE=31.232°) by 4.5° and 4.6° respectively, and the best diffusion baseline Lotus-g (NE=30.062°, SNE=34.189°) by 8.2° and 7.6°. For 3D geometry generation, Hi3DGen is rated highest in a user study by both 50 amateur 3D users and 10 professional 3D artists, surpassing Hunyuan3D-2.0, Dora, Clay, Tripo-2.5, and Trellis in fidelity of overall shape and local detail consistency with input images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NiRNE decisively outperforms all five normal-estimation baselines on both NE and SNE. Against the strongest regression competitor Lotus-d, NiRNE reduces NE by 4.5° (26.386→21.837) and SNE by 4.6° (31.232→26.628), demonstrating that noise-injected dual-stream training recovers sharp details that standard regression misses. Against diffusion-based methods, the gap is even larger: NiRNE beats Lotus-g by 8.2° in NE and 7.6° in SNE, GeoWizard by 9.5°/10.0°, and StableNormal by 9.4°/10.4°, confirming that the noise-injection mechanism captures the high-frequency sensitivity of diffusion without its instability. For 3D generation, Hi3DGen wins the user study against all five competitors (Hunyuan3D-2.0, Dora, Clay, Tripo-2.5, Trellis) from both amateur and professional perspectives, with qualitative comparisons (Fig. 9) showing that Hi3DGen preserves fine geometric details (e.g., roof textures, surface engravings) that are smoothed or hallucinated by the baselines. The ablation in Fig. 6 further confirms that a direct image-to-geometry Trellis baseline produces fake details without the normal bridge, while using less accurate normals from other estimators degrades Hi3DGen's output, validating the necessity of both the normal-bridging strategy and the sharp NiRNE estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of monocular normal estimation and 3D latent diffusion generation, introducing normal maps as an explicit 2.5D intermediate representation to bridge the two. For normal estimation, it departs from the diffusion-vs-regression dichotomy by injecting diffusion-style noise into a regression framework, drawing on ControlNet-style dual-stream concatenation [83] to decouple low- and high-frequency learning. For 3D generation, it builds on Trellis [74]'s structured 3D latent diffusion but adds an online normal-rendering regularization during diffusion training, distinguishing itself from post-processing refinement (CraftsMan) or VAE-stage normal losses (Trellis). The DetailVerse dataset is positioned as a necessary complement to Objaverse, whose assets are predominantly simple in geometric detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The normal-bridging paradigm offers a principled way to inject explicit geometric supervision into latent diffusion training, potentially generalizable to other intermediate representations (depth, curvature) in 3D generation pipelines. The DetailVerse dataset (700K assets with ~40× more sharp edges than Objaverse-XL) addresses a concrete data bottleneck for detail-rich 3D generation. However, the authors acknowledge that generative latent diffusion still produces occasional inconsistent or misaligned details, and reconstruction-level fidelity remains an open challenge. The approach's reliance on a separate normal estimator as a front-end also introduces a potential error-propagation pipeline that could limit end-to-end optimization.
