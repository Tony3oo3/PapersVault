## Problem

Existing Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNs) for pansharpening rely on serial iterative architectures that fail to adequately interact multi-level features across stages and across spatial-spectral modalities. This results in hierarchical abstraction discrepancies between stages, semantic confusion between modalities, and spatial misalignment-induced feature degradation, ultimately limiting reconstruction accuracy and causing noise propagation across iterations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250167/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-resolution panchromatic image (1×H×W) and a low-resolution multispectral image (C×h×w, where H>h and W>w) as input, and produces a high-resolution multispectral image (C×H×W) as output. The LRMS is upsampled via PixelShuffle to match PAN resolution before entering the iterative pipeline. Reconstruction proceeds through N=7 sequential stages, where each stage's decoded output H^(k) serves as the input to the next stage's encoding, forming a recursive multi-level degradation encoding loop.

## Method

UED-Net is a lightweight encoder-decoder DUN with N iterative stages, each comprising three components. The Spatial-Spectral Encoding Module (SSEM), inspired by C-SALSA, recursively encodes spatial and spectral degradations into multi-level feature sequences by combining a global degradation awareness term (cross-attention between the current estimate and the upsampled LRMS/PAN) with a sparse multi-scale prior term (multi-scale residual features), solved via variable splitting with learnable shrinkage and normalization. The Unfolding-Associative Attention Module (UAAM) uses recurrent linear attention (inspired by WKV) with stage-aware positional encodings to capture pixel-level cross-stage correlations, aggregating multi-level causal information from all previous stages into the current decoding. The Progressive Alignment Mechanism (PGAM) employs deformable convolution to estimate inter-modal spatial offsets in a stage-adaptive manner and applies gated per-channel redistribution to balance spatial and spectral contributions. Training is supervised with L1 loss between the final stage output and ground truth, using 16 hidden dimensions split into 4 attention heads.

## Results

On simulated tests, UED-Net achieves the best performance across all three datasets and all five metrics. On GaoFen-2, it reaches PSNR 49.56 dB, SSIM 0.9910, ERGAS 0.4268, SCC 0.5986, and SAM 0.00774. On WorldView-III, it attains PSNR 31.16 dB and SSIM 0.9290. On real GaoFen-2 data (no-reference), UED-Net achieves QNR 0.8667, D_λ 0.0680, and D_s 0.0701, while requiring only 2.51 GFLOPs and 0.168 M parameters—substantially fewer than DISPNet (27.67 GFLOPs) or GPPNN (4.19 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UED-Net outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across all three simulated datasets and the real-data test. Against the SOTA DUN DISPNet (AAAI'24), UED-Net reduces ERGAS by 4.91% on WorldView-II, 4.07% on WorldView-III, and 6.68% on GaoFen-2, while also improving PSNR by 0.47–0.48 dB and SAM by 0.0009–0.0014. Against the SOTA pure DL method SFINet++ (TPAMI'24), UED-Net lowers ERGAS by 12.46% (WV-II), 4.30% (WV-III), and 6.26% (GF-2), and improves PSNR by 1.14, 0.39, and 0.21 dB respectively. Among DUNs, UED-Net surpasses GPPNN (CVPR'21) by 10.77% ERGAS on WV-II and 12.76% on GF-2, and outperforms MDCUN (CVPR'22) by a large margin (e.g., 14.4% ERGAS on WV-II) while using 47× fewer FLOPs (2.51G vs. 118.30G). On real GaoFen-2 data, UED-Net achieves the best QNR (0.8667 vs. 0.8437 for DISPNet and 0.8400 for SFINet++), the lowest D_λ (0.0680), and the lowest D_s (0.0701), confirming its advantage in practical no-reference settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Unfolding Network (DUN) lineage for pansharpening, which itself builds on variational optimization solvers like C-SALSA (Afonso et al., ICASSP 2010) to create interpretable iterative reconstruction architectures. Prior DUNs (GPPNN, MDCUN, MMNet, LGTEUN, NLUNet, DISPNet) have progressively improved cross-stage communication, but the authors argue they still treat stages as a serial pipeline that inadequately handles hierarchical abstraction differences and spatial-spectral modality misalignment. UED-Net departs from this by reframing the DUN as an encoder-decoder system: the SSEM (encoder) recursively extracts multi-level degradation features via C-SALSA variable splitting, while the UAAM (decoder) uses RWKV-inspired recurrent linear attention to aggregate cross-stage features at the pixel level, and a deformable-convolution-based progressive alignment mechanism compensates for inter-modal spatial offsets in a stage-adaptive manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete architectural limitation in DUNs—insufficient cross-stage and cross-modal feature interaction—that has persisted since GPPNN (2021), and does so with a lightweight design (2.51 GFLOPs, 0.17M params) that outperforms both heavier pure-DL methods and prior DUNs across three satellite datasets. The progressive alignment mechanism, which explicitly models spatial misalignment between PAN and MS modalities at each iterative stage, is a practical contribution that could transfer to other multi-modal fusion tasks where registration errors compound over iterations. Open questions include whether the fixed N=7 stages and S=16 hidden dimensions generalize to higher-resolution or hyperspectral settings, and whether the RWKV-style recurrence in UAAM can be further accelerated for very large scenes.
