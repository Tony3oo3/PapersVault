## Problem

Accelerating multimodal contrastive learning via sample selection is hindered by two limitations: coreset methods require an oracle model unavailable in cold-start settings, while online methods fail to adequately handle noisy correspondence. As training progresses, the memorization effect causes clean and noisy samples' similarity scores to overlap, rendering simple loss-threshold separation ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs (images randomly cropped to 224×224, paired with captions) in batches of 1024 per GPU during contrastive pre-training. At each training step, it produces a filtered mini-batch by selecting the top-r fraction of samples ranked by their differential score δ, where r is the selection ratio budget (e.g., 30%, 50%, 70%). The final output is a set of pre-trained image and text encoders (f_θ1, g_θ2) that achieve comparable or superior performance to full-data training with significantly fewer iterations.

## Method

DISSect computes a per-sample differential δ = CLIPScore_hist − CLIPScore_curr, where CLIPScore_hist is obtained either from a fixed warm-up checkpoint or via temporal ensembling (momentum update with parameter β). The key insight is that noisy correspondence samples exhibit a large positive shift in CLIPScore over training (being wrongly memorized), while clean samples show a slight decrease (being partially forgotten), creating a clear separation in δ that absolute CLIPScore values cannot provide due to distributional overlap. During each training step, the model forward-passes the full batch, computes δ for every sample, and retains only the top-k samples with the greatest δ for back-propagation. Theoretically, the paper shows that δ reflects the gradient offset of samples across training stages, as the contrastive loss gradient is inversely proportional to exp(CLIPScore), so the differential captures the decrease in gradient magnitude caused by memorization of noisy data.

## Results

On CC3M pre-training with 50% selection ratio, DISsect-Warmup achieves IR@1=23.58 and TR@1=16.76 on MS-COCO zero-shot retrieval, surpassing full-data training (IR@1=21.42, TR@1=15.38) while using only half the data. On Flickr30K at the same ratio, it reaches IR@1=39.40 and TR@1=32.52, outperforming all baselines including oracle-based CLIPScore (IR@1=39.50, TR@1=30.18) and CLIPLoss (IR@1=39.60, TR@1=29.38). The method achieves approximately 2.85× training acceleration to match full-data performance, and on the noisier YFCC15M dataset, DISsect (R@Sum=167.79) substantially exceeds full-data training (R@Sum=162.43) on MS-COCO retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISsect-Warmup outperforms all online selection baselines by a wide margin: at 50% ratio on MS-COCO, it gains +2.94 IR@1 over SCAN (23.58 vs 20.64), +3.00 over InfoBatch (20.58), and +4.82 over DivBS (18.76). It also surpasses the oracle-model-based coreset methods CLIPScore (22.54) and CLIPLoss (21.86) on MS-COCO IR@1 without requiring any external reference model. On Flickr30K, DISsect-Warmup achieves the best TR@1 (32.52) and TR@10 (69.98), exceeding CLIPScore by +2.34 and CLIPLoss by +3.14 in TR@1, while matching them closely on IR@1 (39.40 vs 39.50/39.60). Against noisy-correspondence-specific methods NCR and GSC (which use dual networks at >2× compute cost), DISsect achieves higher R@Sum on both MS-COCO (148.44 vs 144.72) and Flickr30K (221.10 vs 216.38) with a single network. The method also beats full-data training on MS-COCO at 50% ratio (IR@1: 23.58 vs 21.42), demonstrating that selective training on informative samples can actually improve final quality over indiscriminate full-data training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online sample selection for training acceleration and noisy correspondence handling in multimodal contrastive learning. It builds directly on CLIPScore [15] as the similarity metric whose differential (current vs. historical) forms the selection signal, and on temporal ensembling [24] for adaptively maintaining the historical state without a fixed warm-up epoch. Conceptually, it departs from two established lines: coreset selection methods that rely on an oracle model (CLIPScore with pre-trained CLIP-L, CLIPLoss) and noisy correspondence methods that use dual-network co-teaching (NCR, GSC). The key departure is showing that a single-network differential of the model's own predictions suffices to identify noisy correspondence, eliminating both the oracle dependency and the dual-network overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: DISSect achieves ~2.85× acceleration to match full-data training on CC3M and outperforms full-data training on the noisier YFCC15M, suggesting the method is most valuable precisely where data quality is worst. The finding that standard loss-threshold selection (big-loss, small-loss) underperforms random sampling in multimodal contrastive learning challenges a widely-used heuristic from unimodal settings and motivates the differential-based approach. Open questions include whether the differential mechanism generalizes to non-CLIP contrastive paradigms (e.g., DINO-style, SimCLR-style), whether it scales to billion-pair datasets like LAION-400M, and whether the selection ratio sweet spot (~60%) shifts with model scale or data noise level.
