## Problem

Sign language generation (text-to-sign) remains largely unexplored compared to sign language translation, with most existing methods treating it as a visual content generation task (video, keypoint, or motion) via GANs or diffusion models, thereby neglecting the discrete linguistic structure of sign languages and the benefits of pretrained language models. Additionally, no unified model exists for multilingual SLG, and existing autoregressive approaches using decoupled tokenizers suffer from tripled decoding steps due to flattening all body-part tokens into a single sequence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sentence in the target written language (English for ASL, Chinese for CSL, German for DGS) as input, which is tokenized and concatenated with retrieved word-level motion tokens from external sign dictionaries before being fed to the LM encoder. The output is a 3D sign motion sequence represented as SMPL-X parameters (133 dimensions per frame: 11 upper body joints, 30 hand joints, 10 expression parameters), which can be rendered as a full-body sign avatar. The model operates autoregressively at the token level, generating discrete codes for each body part simultaneously per time step.

## Method

SOKE consists of a decoupled tokenizer (three VQ-VAEs independently quantizing upper body, left hand, and right hand motions into discrete codes) and an autoregressive multilingual generator built on mBART-large. The key architectural innovation is a multi-head decoding strategy where three fully-connected LM heads predict tokens for all body parts simultaneously at each step, with decoder inputs formed as a weighted average of part-wise token embeddings (λ=1/3 for hands), balancing inference efficiency (K steps vs. 3K for sequential) with cross-part information fusion. A retrieval-enhanced mechanism appends motion tokens from word-level sign dictionaries to the text prompt, providing precise sign conditions without directly copying dictionary signs into outputs. The integrated vocabulary combines text tokens, part-wise motion tokens (e.g., <B_1>, <RH_2>), and language identifier tokens (<ASL>, <CSL>, <DGS>) to enable a single unified model across three sign languages.

## Results

SOKE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks using a single unified multilingual model. On How2Sign, it reaches DTW-PA-JPE of 6.82 (body) / 2.35 (hand) and back-translation BLEU-4 of 14.48; on CSL-Daily, 6.24 / 1.71 and 11.30; and on Phoenix-2014T, 4.77 / 1.38 and 11.87. These results substantially outperform the previous best autoregressive baseline S-MotionGPT (e.g., hand DTW-PA-JPE of 4.39 on How2Sign vs. SOKE's 2.35) and the diffusion-based NSA (body DTW-PA-JPE of 7.83 on How2Sign vs. SOKE's 6.82). The multi-head decoding with retrieval further reduces average DTW-PA-JPE by ~20% over the non-retrieval variant while maintaining low inference latency (~1.5 s/video on a single RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOKE outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across all three benchmarks. Against the closest autoregressive competitor S-MotionGPT (reimplemented MotionGPT), SOKE reduces hand DTW-PA-JPE from 4.39→2.35 on How2Sign, 3.78→1.71 on CSL-Daily, and 3.41→1.38 on Phoenix-2014T, while also improving body errors and back-translation BLEU-4 (e.g., 14.48 vs. 11.45 on How2Sign). The diffusion-based NSA [2] achieves a competitive body DTW-PA-JPE of 7.83 on How2Sign but reports no hand-level or multilingual results; SOKE improves body error to 6.82 and adds hand-level and multilingual coverage. T2S-GPT [70], the prior best autoregressive SLG method, is surpassed by SOKE on all three datasets (e.g., hand DTW-PA-JPE 6.39→2.35 on How2Sign). Earlier gloss-based or regression methods (NAR, Prog.Trans., Text2Mesh, Adv.Train.) trail by a wide margin, with DTW-PA-JPE body errors 5–7 cm higher than SOKE. Notably, SOKE is the only method evaluated that handles all three sign languages (ASL, CSL, DGS) in a single model, whereas every baseline is monolingual.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the VQ-VAE tokenizer + autoregressive LM paradigm from general text-to-motion generation (MotionGPT, HumanTomato) to the sign language domain, replacing the monolingual T5 backbone with mBART to enable multilingual generation. It departs from prior autoregressive SLG work (T2S-GPT) by introducing a multi-head decoding strategy that predicts tokens for all body parts simultaneously rather than sequentially flattening them, and from diffusion-based SLG methods (NSA, SignDiff) by explicitly modeling sign language as a discrete linguistic task. The retrieval-enhanced component extends RAG ideas from NLP to sign language, using word-level dictionary signs as auxiliary conditions rather than directly concatenating them into outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified model capable of generating signs across three sign languages (ASL, CSL, DGS) from a single architecture, addressing a notable gap in the literature and demonstrating that cross-lingual sign knowledge transfers effectively. The curated SMPL-X annotations for CSL-Daily and Phoenix-2014T lower the barrier for future multilingual SLG research. Open questions include whether the approach scales to additional sign languages, how co-articulation between adjacent signs is handled at the sentence level, and whether the greedy decoding strategy can be replaced with sampling for more diverse outputs. The reliance on SMPL-X pose representations rather than video generation also limits direct deployment in realistic avatar pipelines.
