## Problem

Existing motion-language models handle only unidirectional tasks (text-to-motion or motion-to-text) and single-person motions, making them unable to understand, generate, or edit interactive two-person motions within multi-turn conversational contexts. Additionally, no large-scale dataset provides multi-turn interactive motion instructions, preventing models from learning the nuances of coordinated social behaviors and complex reasoning over motion sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251044/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of text tokens and discrete motion tokens (RQ-VAE codes from SMPL-X-based two-person motions with global joint positions, velocities, 6D rotations, and ground contact features) within a unified vocabulary. Outputs are autoregressively generated as either text responses, motion token sequences decoded back into continuous two-person motion, or both. The multi-turn format means prior conversation turns (containing both text and motion) serve as context for subsequent generation, enabling tasks like motion editing conditioned on persona instructions or reasoning about past/future events.

## Method

MoLaM integrates an RQ-VAE motion tokenizer (2D convolutions along time, residual quantization into D discrete codes per timestep), a LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone, and a motion decoder into a single autoregressive framework where motion and text share a unified vocabulary. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) motion tokenizer training with reconstruction, codebook, and commitment losses; (2) cross-modal pre-training on paired motion-text data (Inter-X, InterHuman, MotionX) using LoRA adapters for next-token prediction across motion-to-text, text-to-motion, and reaction generation tasks; (3) instruction tuning on the newly introduced Inter-MT² dataset (82.7K multi-turn conversations, 153K motion samples) covering editing, reasoning, and story generation scenarios. Inter-MT² is constructed by using GPT-4o to generate diverse multi-turn instructions from motion captions and InterGEN to synthesize corresponding interactive motions, blending real and generated data. The unified architecture eliminates the error accumulation inherent in two-stage pipelines (e.g., motion-to-text then LLM reasoning) by jointly encoding and reasoning over both modalities in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the traditional motion tasks (Table 4), MoLaM achieves 0.901 top-3 retrieval for motion-to-text, 0.568 top-3 retrieval and 0.059 FID for text-to-motion, and 0.691 MPJPE / 0.019 FID for reaction generation, outperforming all unified baselines and most task-specific methods. On the novel motion reasoning task (Table 2), MoLaM scores 5.252/10 in logical coherence, 4.511/10 in content alignment, and 0.794 MAUVE, surpassing the best two-stage baseline (TM2T+GPT-4o) by over 1.0 points in coherence and alignment. On motion editing (Table 3), MoLaM achieves the lowest FID (0.064) and MPJPE (0.758), with user study confirming significant improvements in content similarity, instruction alignment, and motion quality over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLaM outperforms the TM2T-based two-stage pipeline on both motion reasoning (coherence 5.252 vs. 4.266, alignment 4.511 vs. 3.455) and motion editing (FID 0.064 vs. 0.110, MPJPE 0.758 vs. 0.811), attributed to reduced error accumulation from avoiding the intermediate motion-to-text step. Against MoMask*, MoLaM achieves substantially better reaction generation (MPJPE 0.691 vs. 1.602, FID 0.019 vs. 0.112) but trails slightly on text-to-motion retrieval (0.568 vs. 0.612 top-3). MoLaM dominates the MotionGPT* and MotionGPT*_I unified baselines across every task: M2T retrieval improves from 0.494/0.503 to 0.901, T2M FID drops from 0.123/0.118 to 0.059, and reaction MPJPE falls from 3.444/1.436 to 0.691, indicating the VQ-based tokenizer in MotionGPT struggles with two-person relative joint positions. Compared to InterGEN, MoLaM is slightly behind on T2M retrieval (0.568 vs. 0.645) but achieves a lower FID (0.059 vs. 0.078), and uniquely handles motion-to-text, reaction generation, editing, and reasoning within the same model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion-language model lineage (MotionGPT, MotionChain, MotionPT) from single-person, unidirectional tasks to two-person interactive motions in multi-turn conversational settings. It borrows the RQ-VAE residual quantization framework from image generation (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) and adapts it as a motion tokenizer, while building on LLaMA-3.1-8B as the backbone LLM. The key departure from prior interactive motion work (InterGEN, ReMoS, ReGenNet) is the unified bidirectional architecture that simultaneously accepts and produces both text and motion tokens, rather than treating each task (T2M, reaction generation, editing) as a separate pipeline. The Inter-MT² dataset itself is constructed by combining existing interactive motion corpora (Inter-X, InterHuman) with GPT-4o-generated multi-turn instructions and InterGEN-synthesized motions, bridging the gap between static captioned datasets and conversational instruction formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine gap: no prior dataset or model supported multi-turn interactive motion reasoning, and the Inter-MT² dataset (82K conversations, 153K motion samples) provides a foundation the field lacked. The demonstration that a single LLM-based architecture can handle motion reasoning, editing, T2M, M2T, and reaction generation competitively with task-specific methods suggests a path toward general-purpose motion agents. However, the reliance on synthetically generated data (GPT-4o captions + InterGEN motions) introduces a quality ceiling, and the multi-person generalization (Figure 8) is shown only qualitatively without quantitative validation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger groups, whether real-time inference is feasible, and whether the RQ-VAE tokenizer's information loss fundamentally limits motion fidelity for fine-grained interactions.
