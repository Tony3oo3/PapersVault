## Problem

Existing human interaction synthesis methods face a fundamental tension: kinematic approaches achieve reactivity but lack physical grounding, while physics-based animations enforce plausibility but are agnostic to interaction dynamics and cannot generalize beyond training distributions. No prior framework simultaneously delivers real-time (latency-free) responsiveness, physical feasibility (no foot sliding, penetration, or floating), and safety-critical collision avoidance in dynamic human-avatar, human-humanoid, and human-robot interaction scenarios.

## Input / Output

The system consumes actor motion at 30 fps from an RGB-D camera (pose reconstructed via HybrIK and retargeted to SMPL's 22-joint skeleton) plus an optional text prompt, and produces a 40-frame reactor motion sequence per prediction window. The interaction representation is reactor-centric: the reactor is canonicalized at the root facing forward while the actor is expressed purely in relative coordinates (root transition, rotation, linear velocity, joint positions/velocities, and foot contacts) augmented with a 6×6 binary joint-contact interaction field. The diffusion planner operates at 0.5 Hz while the downstream physics tracker runs at 30 Hz, with the reactor's own generated history fed back as prefix for the next auto-regressive window.

## Method

Human-X employs an auto-regressive DIT-based diffusion planner (8 transformer layers, 512-dim hidden, 8 denoising steps) that jointly denoises both actor and reactor motions conditioned on a 20-frame interaction history and optional text via classifier-free guidance, predicting the clean sample directly rather than the noise. Training uses a composite loss combining the standard DDPM objective with a foot-contact loss (penalizing velocity of grounded feet), an interaction loss (minimizing distance between contacting joints across agents), and a prefix loss (ensuring continuity between the end of history and start of generated motion), alongside a scheduled-sampling strategy that progressively replaces ground-truth history with model predictions. For physical feasibility, a PHC-based actor-aware tracking policy trained with multiplicative actor-critic RL receives both the generated reactor target and the real-time captured actor motion as observations, heavily penalizing trajectory inconsistency to enforce collision-free responses. The frequency mismatch between the 0.5 Hz planner and 30 Hz tracker is mitigated by incorporating both generated and captured actor histories into the policy observation, enabling safety-critical corrections when the actor deviates unexpectedly.

## Results

On the Inter-X dataset (online unconstrained reaction setting), Human-X achieves the best reaction quality with FID of 0.975 (vs. 1.430 for CAMDM, 2.212 for ReGenNet) and the best interaction quality with IV of 0.076 and FID_cd of 1.692. With the physics-based reaction policy (Human-X*), physical plausibility improves dramatically: Penetration drops to 0.026, Skating to 0.007, and Floating to 4.342, all approaching ground-truth levels (0.000, 0.023, 7.956). The system operates at 13.6 ms latency with 8 diffusion timesteps, enabling real-time interaction at 30 Hz.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Human-X outperforms all four baselines on the primary metrics. Against InterFormer (transformer-based), Human-X reduces FID from 6.150 to 0.975 and IV from 0.332 to 0.076, showing the diffusion architecture captures interaction structure far more effectively. Against InterGen and ReGenNet (both diffusion-based but non-auto-regressive), Human-X improves FID by 5.6× and 2.3× respectively, and reduces IV by 3.5× and 2.9×, demonstrating the benefit of joint action-reaction modeling with auto-regressive history. Against CAMDM (the closest competitor, also auto-regressive diffusion), Human-X achieves lower FID (0.975 vs. 1.430), lower IV (0.076 vs. 0.145), and lower FID_cd (1.692 vs. 2.086), with the interaction loss being the key differentiator. The physics-based variant (Human-X*) further dominates on physical plausibility, reducing Skating from 0.127 (CAMDM) to 0.007 and Floating from 9.864 to 4.342, approaching ground-truth levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based human motion generation (HumanML3D, MotionDiffuse), which provides the generative backbone; (2) physics-based character control (PHC, PhysDiff, DeepMimic), which ensures dynamic plausibility; and (3) human-human interaction synthesis (InterGen, ReGenNet, InterFormer, CAMDM), which models actor-reactor dynamics. The key departure is that prior interaction methods are purely kinematic and offline, while prior physics-based methods (e.g., PhysReaction) lack interaction awareness and generalization. Human-X unifies these by coupling an auto-regressive diffusion planner that jointly predicts actions and reactions with an RL-trained actor-aware tracking policy, bridging the gap between interaction-aware reactivity and physically constrained control in a real-time loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously achieve real-time (13.6 ms latency), physics-based, and interaction-aware motion synthesis across human-avatar, human-humanoid, and human-robot settings, with demonstrated deployment in VR and on the Unitree H1 robot. The practical impact for immersive VR/AR and humanoid robotics is significant, as it addresses the safety and responsiveness requirements that purely kinematic methods cannot meet. The authors acknowledge a key limitation: the current formulation synthesizes reactive motions but does not support collaborative task completion with shared goals between humans and machines, which remains an open challenge for true human-robot collaboration.
