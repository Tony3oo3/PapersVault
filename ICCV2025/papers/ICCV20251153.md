## Problem

Multi-modal Large Language Models generate long visual token sequences (often exceeding 2000 tokens for high-resolution or video inputs), creating a quadratic attention cost that hinders real-time deployment. Existing plug-and-play token reduction methods apply uniform progressive merging across all vision encoder layers without quantifying where information loss actually originates, leading to sub-optimal and arbitrary reduction strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251153/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

FOLDER operates on the visual token sequences produced by a pre-trained ViT backbone (e.g., 576 tokens for a 336×336 image in LLaVA1.5, or 2000+ tokens for multi-frame video). It is inserted as a plug-and-play module in the last one or two blocks of the vision encoder (between ATTN and FFN, or at the encoder output), reducing the token count by up to 70% before tokens are projected into the LLM. The reduced sequence preserves positional ordering for LLM compatibility, and the module is task-agnostic, requiring no modification to the LLM or connector.

## Method

Through empirical analysis using SVD-based energy tracking and Earth Mover's Distance between output distributions, the paper identifies that (i) later blocks require far fewer tokens to preserve the same information energy, (ii) early-block reductions propagate and amplify distortion through subsequent layers, and (iii) average merging outperforms direct dropping. Based on these findings, FOLDER performs aggressive token reduction exclusively in the final blocks of the vision encoder. The core algorithm generalizes bipartite matching (as in ToMe) to handle arbitrary reduction ratios beyond 50%: when the target reduction exceeds half the tokens ('reduction overflow'), FOLDER iteratively splits the sequence into two equal partitions, matches each token in partition A to its most similar counterpart in B via a similarity function, retains only the top-r matches, and merges them via averaging. This FOLD operation repeats until the remaining reduction fits within a single pass, at which point tokens are rearranged into their original positional order for LLM compatibility. The module serves dual purposes: as a training-time regularizer that improves performance across benchmarks, and as a training-free inference accelerator achieving 1.7–2.4× speedup with negligible or even positive performance impact.

## Results

On LLaVA1.5-7B, FOLDER at 66% token reduction achieves an average score of 56.7 across 12 benchmarks, matching the original model (56.9) while delivering 1.7× inference speedup. At 75% reduction, combining FOLDER with FastV yields 56.8 average with 1.7× speedup, still above the original. On LLaVA1.5-13B, FOLDER at 66% reduction scores 58.1 average (vs. 58.3 original) with 1.6× speedup, and at 75% reduction with FastV achieves 58.2 average with 1.8× speedup. Training with FOLDER at 66% reduction further boosts performance to 60.4 average (vs. 58.7 original) while accelerating training by 1.5×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOLDER consistently outperforms all vision-encoder-based baselines (Pooling, Turbo, Pyramiddrop) at every reduction ratio. At 66% reduction on LLaVA1.5-7B, FOLDER scores 56.7 average vs. Turbo (53.8), Pooling (53.5), SparseVLM (54.8), and Pyramiddrop (56.0), with the largest gaps on visually dense tasks like OCRVQA (46.1 vs. 34.4 for Turbo) and HallusionBench (39.2 vs. 34.9 for Turbo). Against FastV (which prunes tokens inside the LLM), FOLDER matches or slightly exceeds performance (56.7 vs. 56.5 at 66% on 7B) while being a simpler plug-and-play module that avoids LLM-internal modifications and multi-round conversation issues. At 75% reduction, combining FOLDER with FastV (56.8 on 7B, 58.2 on 13B) surpasses FastV alone (55.8, 56.4) by 1.0 and 1.8 points respectively. In the training setting (Table 3), FOLDER at 66% reduction boosts LLaVA1.5-13B to 60.4 average, outperforming Pyramiddrop (59.8), Pixel-shuffle (57.3), and finetuning-based methods CrossGet (51.8) and Prumerge (53.6) by 0.6 to 8.6 points without requiring additional fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOLDER extends the bipartite-matching token merging paradigm introduced by ToMe [6] and refined by Turbo [31], but departs from their uniform progressive merging across all vision encoder layers. The key conceptual shift, grounded in SVD-based energy analysis and EMD-based propagation studies, is that aggressive token reduction should be confined to the final blocks of the vision backbone, where inter-block error amplification is minimal. Unlike LLM-internal pruning methods (FastV, Pyramiddrop, SparseVLM) that interact with instruction tokens and struggle in multi-turn settings, FOLDER operates entirely within the visual encoder, making it architecture-agnostic and composable with task-oriented attention sparsity techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and dual training/inference applicability make FOLDER a practical drop-in for any MLLM deployment, offering 1.7–2.4× inference speedup and 40% peak-memory reduction at 66–75% token reduction with negligible or even positive performance impact. The finding that token reduction during training acts as a regularizer (improving OCRVQA by 3% and InfoVQA by 0.9%) opens an underexplored direction: structured visual redundancy removal as an implicit data augmentation. Open questions include whether the last-block-only strategy remains optimal for next-generation vision encoders with different depth/width ratios, and how FOLDER interacts with emerging dynamic-resolution or token-adaptive MLLM architectures.
