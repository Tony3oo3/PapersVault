## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models cannot simultaneously achieve precise spatial placement of multiple reference subjects and faithful identity preservation of those subjects in a single generated image. Layout-control methods (e.g., GLIGEN, MIGC) handle spatial constraints but lack reference-based subject synthesis, while subject-driven methods (e.g., IP-Adapter, MS-Diffusion) reconstruct identities but fail to enforce bounding-box layouts. The core technical barrier is 'control collision': when layout control is injected via decoupled cross-attention (DCA), its attention map conflicts with the text-driven attention map, degrading spatial accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global text prompt, N reference subject images (each encoded by CLIP into 256 feature tokens, then resampled to 4 tokens), per-subject bounding box coordinates (Fourier-encoded), and per-subject class text as input, and produces a single 512×512 (or 1024×1024) RGB image in which each subject is placed within its specified box and visually matches its reference. Inference is zero-shot and end-to-end: both the CCA layout branch and the DCA subject branch are active simultaneously in a single forward pass using DDIM (30 steps, CFG 7.5) on a frozen SDXL backbone, requiring no test-time fine-tuning or per-subject optimization.

## Method

MUSE introduces Concatenated Cross-Attention (CCA), which replaces DCA's element-wise addition of two separate attention maps with a single unified attention computed over concatenated key/value projections of both text and layout grounding tokens, thereby expanding layout semantics into the text semantic space and eliminating control collision. The framework is trained via a progressive two-stage strategy: Stage 1 trains only the CCA branch for layout-controllable generation (grounding tokens built from Fourier-encoded bounding boxes fused with CLIP class-text features via MLP), and Stage 2 freezes the layout model and adds a DCA branch for subject synthesis (grounding tokens built by directly adding Fourier-encoded bounding boxes to each of 4 resampled CLIP image tokens per subject). This ordered parameter freezing resolves the optimization conflict between layout and subject objectives that causes severe degradation in joint or reversed training. The final model supports up to 6 subjects per image with per-subject bounding boxes and reference images, achieving 88.4% average layout success rate on MIG Bench and 75.5% SR-0.65 on MS-Bench-Random at 4.1 s inference time.

## Results

On the full LMS task (MS-Bench-Random), MUSE achieves a CLIP-I-local score of 0.779 and a success rate (SR-0.65) of 75.5%, substantially outperforming MS-Diffusion (CLIP-I-local 0.686, SR-0.65 28.9%) and GLIGEN (CLIP-I-local 0.747, SR-0.65 68.2%). On the layout-only MIG Bench, MUSE attains the highest average layout success rate of 88.4% (vs. 86.6% for GLIGEN, 87.6% for InstanceDiffusion, 86.9% for MIGC) while being 3.9–6.5× faster in inference (4.1 s vs. 9.1–26.5 s). Under randomized layouts, MUSE's SR-0.65 degrades only 6.4% from fixed to random, compared to a 41.7% collapse for MS-Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUSE outperforms all four baselines on the primary LMS metrics. Against MS-Diffusion [53], the closest prior LMS method, MUSE gains +0.093 CLIP-I-local and +46.6 points SR-0.65 under randomized layouts (0.779 vs. 0.686; 75.5% vs. 28.9%), indicating MS-Diffusion's DCA-based layout control collapses when positions deviate from training distributions. Against GLIGEN [29], MUSE improves CLIP-I-local by +0.032 and SR-0.65 by +7.3 points on MS-Bench-Random, while also being ~4× faster (4.1 s vs. 15.9 s on MIG Bench). On the layout-only MIG Bench, MUSE leads GLIGEN (88.4% vs. 86.6%), InstanceDiffusion [52] (88.4% vs. 87.6%), and MIGC [64] (88.4% vs. 86.9%) in average success rate, with the gap widening at 5–6 instances (L5: 85.8% vs. 83.4–85.0%; L6: 87.8% vs. 83.2–86.4%). MUSE also maintains the highest text-image alignment (CLIP-T 0.321–0.323) across both fixed and randomized settings, confirming that its CCA mechanism preserves semantic coherence while enforcing spatial constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUSE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: layout-controllable generation (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC) and reference-image subject synthesis (IP-Adapter, MS-Diffusion, SubjectDiffusion). It inherits the grounding-token construction from GLIGEN (Fourier-encoded bounding boxes fused with text/image features via MLP) and the decoupled cross-attention (DCA) injection mechanism from IP-Adapter for subject features. The key departure is the introduction of concatenated cross-attention (CCA) for layout control, which replaces DCA's element-wise addition of separate attention maps with a single unified attention computation over concatenated keys/values, thereby expanding layout semantics into the text semantic space to avoid the 'control collision' the authors diagnose in DCA-based layout methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean diagnosis (control collision) and a minimal architectural fix (concatenation instead of addition in cross-attention) for a practical failure mode in multi-condition diffusion models, which is broadly applicable beyond this specific task. The progressive two-stage training strategy (layout first, then subject) is a useful practical recipe that avoids the optimization conflicts of joint training. Limitations include evaluation only on SDXL at 512 resolution for fair comparison, a maximum of 6 subjects, and reliance on a large-scale LAION-5B training set; open questions include whether CCA generalizes to other control modalities (depth, pose), whether the approach scales to video generation, and whether the two-stage decomposition is necessary or merely sufficient.
