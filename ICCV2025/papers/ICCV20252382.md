## Problem

Training diffusion models from scratch remains prohibitively expensive, requiring thousands of A100 GPU hours even on standard benchmarks. Existing efficiency methods—masking, distillation, and architectural modifications—each impose a trade-off, improving either computational cost or generative quality but not both simultaneously. This paper asks whether a training strategy can reduce per-iteration cost while also accelerating convergence to better generation quality, without modifying the base architecture.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tokenized latent representations (32×32 patches from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE with 8× downsampling) conditioned on class labels (ImageNet-256/512) or text prompts (MS-COCO T2I). Routing is applied exclusively during training: a fixed fraction of tokens is randomly selected per sample to bypass a contiguous block of layers, while inference always uses the standard full pathway with all tokens processed by all layers. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on DiT (transformer), RWKV, and Mamba (state-space) backbones.

## Method

TREAD introduces a training-time token routing mechanism where, at a start layer L_i, a randomly selected subset of tokens (at a fixed selection rate, typically 50%) bypasses layers L_i through L_j and re-enters the computation at end layer L_j, effectively applying an identity mapping over the skipped block for those tokens. The key insight is that residual-based architectures produce highly similar outputs across consecutive layers (verified via cosine similarity), so temporarily skipping layers causes only transient information loss rather than permanent destruction as in masking. The method uses a single standard diffusion reconstruction loss uniformly across all tokens—no auxiliary losses or learnable substitute embeddings are needed. Empirically derived guidelines show that longer routes (early start i≤2, end j≤8) improve both efficiency and effectiveness multiplicatively, and the optimal configuration transfers in an absolute (not relative) manner to larger models. No additional parameters, routing modules, or architectural changes are required, making TREAD orthogonal to and combinable with other techniques like representation distillation (REPA).

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation, DiT-XL/2+TREAD achieves an unguided FID of 3.93 at 680 epochs and a guided FID of 2.09 at 740 epochs (with fine-tuning), compared to the standard DiT-XL/2's 9.62 (unguided) and 2.27 (guided) at 1400 epochs. This corresponds to a 14× convergence speedup at 400K iterations and a 37× speedup relative to the best published DiT result at 7M iterations. TREAD also improves iteration speed to 3.03 it/s versus 1.86 it/s for vanilla DiT, compounding the efficiency gains. On ImageNet-512 and MS-COCO T2I, TREAD reduces FID by over a third (62.88→40.91 and 35.68→20.55 respectively) at 400K iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREAD decisively outperforms the standard DiT-XL/2 on both unguided FID (3.93 vs. 9.62) and guided FID (2.09 vs. 2.27) while requiring roughly half the training epochs. Against MaskDiT, TREAD achieves a lower unguided FID (3.93 vs. 5.69) and guided FID (2.09 vs. 2.28) at a higher iteration speed (3.03 vs. 2.98 it/s), and against SD-DiT it improves unguided FID from 7.21 to 3.93 and guided FID from 3.23 to 2.09. MDT remains the strongest guided baseline (FID 1.79 at 1300 epochs), but TREAD matches it at 740 epochs with interval guidance (FID 1.69) and substantially outperforms it unguided (3.93 vs. 6.23). SiT-XL/2 achieves a marginally better guided FID (2.06 vs. 2.09) but requires 1400 epochs versus TREAD's 740, and TREAD dominates unguided (3.93 vs. 8.61). TREAD is also orthogonal to representation distillation: combining TREAD with REPA on DiT-B/2 yields FID 20.06 at 400K iterations, beating either method alone (21.34 for TREAD, 34.63 for REPA). On non-transformer backbones, TREAD improves RWKV from FID 59.84 to 53.79 and Mamba from 69.39 to 61.17 at 400K iterations, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

TREAD builds on the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the EDM continuous-time diffusion framework (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022), introducing a training-time token routing mechanism that bypasses intermediate layers for a randomly selected subset of tokens. It departs from masking-based approaches (MaskDiT, MDT, SD-DiT) that permanently discard token information, and from Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) which requires a learned router module, by using simple random selection with no additional parameters or architectural changes. The method is positioned as orthogonal to representation distillation (REPA) and other training improvements, enabling compounding gains when combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37× convergence speedup over standard DiT training, achieved without architectural modifications or extra parameters, substantially lowers the barrier to training competitive diffusion models and could democratize access to state-of-the-art generative quality. The architecture-agnostic nature (validated on DiT, SiT, RWKV, and Mamba) and the empirically derived route-placement guidelines make the technique broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the absolute route configuration (i=2, j=B−4) generalizes to very deep models, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether the training-only routing strategy interacts adversely with other emerging training paradigms like flow matching at scale.
