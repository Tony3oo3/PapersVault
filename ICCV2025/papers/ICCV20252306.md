## Problem

Existing unsupervised visible-infrared person re-identification methods focus on enhancing model robustness to color variations during optimization but overlook how color variations degrade the quality of cross-modality pseudo-labels. The paper shows that only 51.67% of matches between visible and channel-augmented clusters overlap, meaning direct visible-to-infrared cluster matching is highly sensitive to color and produces noisy correspondences that misguide training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252306/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled visible and infrared images resized to 288×144 pixels, processed by an AGW encoder with a ResNet50 backbone featuring modality-specific front-end branches and a shared back-end. During matching, visible images are additionally channel-augmented (one RGB channel replicated) to form an augmented set of equal size, and matching operates at the cluster-centroid level rather than per-image. The output is a ranked list of cross-modality identity matches evaluated via Rank-1, mAP, and mINP on SYSU-MM01 (All/Indoor Search) and RegDB (Visible-to-Thermal / Thermal-to-Visible).

## Method

ASM introduces three coupled components. First, the Cross-modality Augmented Matching (CAM) module applies channel augmentation to visible images, computes centroids for visible, augmented, and infrared clusters, and fuses the visible-infrared and augmented-infrared cosine-similarity matrices via a product of sigmoid-scaled terms (controlled by shrinking factors γ_v and γ_a) before performing progressive graph matching, so a visible cluster is matched to an infrared cluster only if both the original and augmented centroids agree. Second, the Soft-labels Momentum Update (SMU) strategy converts each epoch's one-hot inter-modality pseudo-label into a soft-label via exponential moving average (rate α=0.5), absorbing the epoch-to-epoch instability introduced by the stochastic augmentation. Third, two alternating cross-modality contrastive losses are applied: a soft contrastive loss that pulls each image toward its soft-label-weighted infrared (or visible) cluster centroids, and a hard contrastive loss that additionally pulls toward the single hardest image (furthest from centroid) in each cross-modality cluster, jointly promoting modality-invariant learning from both shared and diverse intra-cluster features.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All Search), ASM achieves 65.07% Rank-1, 63.37% mAP, and 51.29% mINP, slightly outperforming the best prior UVI-ReID method SDCL (64.49% Rank-1, 63.24% mAP). On RegDB (Visible to Thermal), ASM reaches 88.23% Rank-1 and 79.69% mAP, a clear margin over SDCL (86.91% Rank-1, 78.92% mAP). On SYSU-MM01 (Indoor Search), ASM attains 71.08% Rank-1 and 76.91% mAP, and on RegDB (Thermal to Visible) it reaches 86.85% Rank-1 and 79.20% mAP. Overall, ASM is the best-performing unsupervised method across all four evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised UVI-ReID methods, ASM outperforms the strongest baseline SDCL (CVPR24) on SYSU-MM01 All Search (65.07 vs 64.49 Rank-1; 63.37 vs 63.24 mAP) and on both RegDB settings (88.23 vs 86.91 Rank-1 V→T; 86.85 vs 85.76 Rank-1 T→V), though it trails SDCL by 0.29% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 Indoor Search. Against PGM (CVPR23), ASM gains roughly 7.8% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 All Search and 18.75% Rank-1 on RegDB V→T, demonstrating the benefit of augmented matching over plain progressive graph matching. Compared to GUR (ICCV23), ASM improves by 1.56% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 All Search and 14.32% Rank-1 on RegDB V→T. Against all UT-ReID baselines (MMT, IICS, ICE, CC, PPLR, RTMem), ASM achieves a 41.37% mAP margin over CC on SYSU-MM01 All Search, confirming the advantage of cross-modality-specific optimization. On the supervised side, ASM surpasses DDAG (ECCV20) and AGW (TPAMI21) across all settings, and its RegDB performance (88.23% Rank-1 V→T) approaches that of SAAI (91.07%) and IDKL (94.72%), though it remains below the top SVI-ReID methods on SYSU-MM01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PGM (Progressive Graph Matching, CVPR 2023), which introduced graph-based cross-modality matching for UVI-ReID, and on the broader line of unsupervised VI-ReID methods (ADCA, GUR, SDCL) that address color variations through data augmentation during the optimization phase. The key conceptual departure is shifting the focus from making the *model* robust to color variations to making the *pseudo-labels* robust: by generating augmented visible clusters via channel augmentation and fusing their similarity with infrared clusters alongside the original visible-infrared similarity, the matching stage itself becomes less sensitive to color. The soft-labels momentum update (SMU) and the dual soft/hard contrastive losses are then designed to stabilize and exploit the noisier but more color-robust pseudo-labels that this augmented matching produces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underexplored failure mode—color variations degrading pseudo-label quality rather than just model features—and provides a targeted remedy. However, the performance gains over the strongest UVI-ReID baseline (SDCL) are modest on SYSU-MM01 (≈0.6 Rank-1, ≈0.1 mAP) and more pronounced on the smaller RegDB dataset, suggesting the benefit is most visible when color/style gaps are larger. Open questions include whether the augmented-matching idea generalizes beyond channel augmentation (e.g., to spatial or semantic augmentations), whether the soft-label momentum schedule is optimal, and whether the approach extends to other cross-modality re-identification settings (e.g., RGB-thermal with larger spectral gaps).
