## Problem

Implicit neural representations (INRs) based on MLPs suffer from spectral bias, learning low-frequency components before high-frequency ones, which limits their ability to capture fine details in images, 3D shapes, and radiance fields. Existing remedies—positional encoding, hand-crafted periodic activations (SIREN, FINER), and wavelet-based activations—still exhibit residual spectral bias and are highly sensitive to initialization and hyperparameter choices, leaving a gap for a flexible, robust mechanism to tune spectral behavior.

## Input / Output

The network consumes continuous coordinate inputs: 2D spatial coordinates for 512×512 RGB images, 3D coordinates for signed distance field (SDF) values of 3D shapes, and 5D inputs (3D position + 2D viewing direction) for NeRF rendering. A key implementation detail is that inputs are mapped through tanh into (−1, 1) before being fed to the Chebyshev polynomial activations, and the Fusion Block employs low-rank linear layers (rank 128 for images, rank 32 for 3D shapes) to balance expressiveness with computational efficiency.

## Method

SL²A-INR introduces a two-block hybrid architecture. The first block (LA Block) is a single linear layer whose per-neuron activation functions are parameterized as Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind of degree K (up to 256) with learnable coefficients, initialized via Xavier uniform and followed by layer normalization; this allows the network to adaptively shape its spectral response without hand-crafted periodicity. The second block (Fusion Block) is a standard ReLU MLP in which every hidden layer's input is modulated by element-wise multiplication with the LA Block's output, effectively injecting high-frequency information into all subsequent layers. This modulation, combined with the learnable polynomial coefficients, enables flexible spectral-bias tuning: increasing K broadens the frequency spectrum the network can capture, while the skip-connection mechanism ensures high-frequency features propagate rather than decay through depth. The approach is trained end-to-end with MSE loss under standard supervised learning, requiring no special initialization protocols beyond Xavier uniform for the polynomial coefficients.

## Results

On 2D image representation (DIV2K, 16 images), SL²A achieves an average PSNR of 36.88 dB and SSIM of 0.933, outperforming the strongest baseline FINER (36.35 dB / 0.924) by 0.53 dB. On 3D occupancy volume reconstruction (Stanford 3D Scanning Repository), SL²A achieves IoU of 99.83–99.89% across all five shapes, substantially exceeding the best baseline (Gauss, 99.68% on Dragon). On novel view synthesis (Blender, 25 training images), SL²A attains the highest PSNR in 6 of 8 scenes, with notable gains on Chair (34.70 vs. 33.90 for FINER) and Materials (28.62 vs. 27.13 for SIREN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SL²A outperforms all five baselines (FINER, Gauss, ReLU+P.E., SIREN, WIRE) on the average image representation metric, achieving 36.88 dB PSNR vs. FINER's 36.35 dB and SIREN's 33.47 dB. On 3D occupancy reconstruction, the gap is most dramatic: SL²A reaches 99.83–99.89% IoU on all five shapes, while the best competing method (Gauss) peaks at 99.68% and SIREN drops to 94.09% on Dragon. On novel view synthesis, SL²A wins 6 of 8 Blender scenes in PSNR, with the largest margin over SIREN on the Materials scene (28.62 vs. 27.13 dB) and over FINER on Chair (34.70 vs. 33.90 dB); it trails FINER slightly on Drums (24.33 vs. 24.90) and Ficus (28.31 vs. 28.70). WIRE is consistently the weakest baseline across all three tasks, confirming that wavelet-based activations struggle with the diverse frequency content present in these signals. The paper also reports that SL²A is more robust to learning-rate and batch-size variations than FINER, Gauss, and SIREN, as shown by a PSNR heatmap over hyperparameter sweeps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) paradigm of learnable activations, but departs from KAN's B-spline parameterization by using Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind in a single initial layer, and from KAN's all-learnable-activation design by pairing that layer with a standard ReLU MLP (the Fusion Block) modulated via skip connections. It positions itself against the broader INR activation-function literature—SIREN's sinusoidal activations, WIRE's wavelet activations, FINER's variable-periodic activations, and positional encoding—arguing that all of these remain sensitive to initialization or retain residual spectral bias, whereas a learnable polynomial activation in the first layer can adaptively tune the spectral spread without hand-crafted periodicity. The NTK eigenvalue analysis connects the method to the harmonic-distortion framework of Ali Mehmeti-Göpel et al., showing that Chebyshev coefficients control the rate of eigenvalue decay and thus the frequency-dependent learning speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single learnable-activation layer plus a conventional ReLU backbone with element-wise modulation yields consistent SOTA or near-SOTA results across image fitting, 3D SDF reconstruction, and NeRF view synthesis, while being notably more robust to learning-rate and batch-size choices than periodic-activation baselines. This reduces the hyperparameter-tuning burden that has been a persistent pain point for SIREN-style INRs. Open questions include whether the approach scales efficiently to very large architectures (the authors acknowledge KAN-inherited cost), whether sparsification or weight sharing can offset the extra parameters, and whether the Chebyshev-degree knob can be made fully adaptive per layer rather than fixed at the first layer.
