## Problem

Existing urban data fusion methods and recent urban MLLMs (GeoChat, Mobility-LLM, CityGPT) each handle only a single modality of urban data, leaving no unified framework for comprehensive multi-modal urban understanding. The two key barriers are the scarcity of high-quality cross-modality alignment data and the potential conflicts among diverse urban tasks that destabilize joint training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252408/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UrbanLLaVA consumes four urban data types simultaneously: structured geospatial data (OpenStreetMap POIs, addresses), trajectory sequences (Foursquare check-ins, OSM traces), satellite imagery (GoogleEarth), and street view imagery (GoogleMap/BaiduMap), all interleaved with natural language instructions. The data is organized hierarchically from location view (single image + geospatial context) through trajectory view (multi-image sequences with navigation instructions) to global view (multi-satellite comparison and cross-view alignment). Outputs are free-form natural language responses covering tasks like address inference, land use classification, navigation guidance, image retrieval, and scene comparison, evaluated with accuracy, top-1, or success-rate metrics depending on the task.

## Method

UrbanLLaVA builds on VILA-1.5-8B and introduces three components. UData generates instruction data across three spatial scales: location view (template-based geospatial QA + MLLM-generated street view captions), trajectory view (text routing, real-world trajectories, and two visual-augmented variants using interleaved image-text and vision-language navigation formats), and global view (satellite captions, multi-satellite comparison with chain-of-thought reasoning, and street-view-to-satellite alignment tasks). UTrain is a three-stage fine-tuning pipeline—task alignment, then knowledge learning, then mixture learning (resampling 1/3 domain data + 1/3 general ShareGPT/UltraChat data)—that explicitly decouples spatial reasoning skill acquisition from urban domain knowledge injection, which the authors show empirically outperforms single-stage or two-stage alternatives. UBench extends CityBench and UrBench into a 12-task evaluation suite covering single-modal and cross-modal urban scenarios across three cities (Beijing, London, New York).

## Results

On the UBench benchmark in Beijing, UrbanLLaVA (VILA1.5-8B base) achieves 0.5682 on GeoQA, 0.2800 on Geo+Traj, 0.8650 on Geo+STV, 0.6663 on Geo+SAT, and 0.7025 on Geo+SS, outperforming all baselines across all five task groups. Compared to its base model VILA1.5-8B, improvements range from +31.47% (GeoQA) to +375.38% (Geo+Traj). Against the best general MLLM baselines, UrbanLLaVA gains range from +3.48% to +132.23% per task. In London and New York, UrbanLLaVA wins 4 out of 5 tasks, with the only losses on GeoQA versus GPT4o (−0.73% and −7.37% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanLLaVA (8B) decisively outperforms its base model VILA1.5-8B on all tasks in all three cities, with the largest gain of +375.38% on Geo+Traj in Beijing (0.2800 vs. 0.0589). Against the strongest open-source baselines, UrbanLLaVA surpasses Qwen2VL-72B by +48.70% on Geo+STV (0.8650 vs. 0.5817) and +61.53% on Geo+SAT (0.6663 vs. 0.3588) in Beijing, and beats InternVL2-26B by +48.70% on Geo+STV. Compared to the proprietary GPT4o, UrbanLLaVA wins on 4 of 5 task groups in Beijing (e.g., +72.63% on Geo+Traj, +132.23% on Geo+SS) but trails slightly on GeoQA in London (−0.73%) and New York (−7.37%), which the authors attribute to lower data quality in those cities and the inherent capability gap between an 8B open model and a commercial API. LLaMA3.2-90B is excluded from Geo+SS comparisons due to its lack of multi-image input support, but UrbanLLaVA still leads it on all four available task groups in every city.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanLLaVA builds directly on VILA as its base architecture and extends the emerging line of urban-specific MLLMs (GeoChat for remote sensing, CityGPT for geospatial data, Mobility-LLM for trajectories) by unifying all four major urban data modalities—structured geospatial data, trajectory data, street view images, and satellite images—within a single model. The multi-stage training pipeline (UTrain) draws on Dong et al.'s findings about data composition effects in LLM fine-tuning, repurposing the insight that decoupling reasoning from knowledge learning improves stability. The evaluation benchmark UBench extends and reorganizes tasks from CityBench and UrBench, adding cross-view and multi-image tasks that neither prior benchmark covered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MLLM to jointly process four urban data types, providing a practical template for building domain-specific multimodal foundation models where data heterogeneity is the core challenge. The three-stage training recipe (task alignment → knowledge → mixture) offers a reusable strategy for any domain where diverse task formats and domain knowledge must be learned simultaneously. Key limitations include evaluation on only three cities with a single 8B base model, and the model still trails GPT-4o on pure geospatial QA in London and New York, suggesting data quality and base model capacity remain bottlenecks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to spatiotemporal series data (a fifth modality mentioned but not yet integrated) and whether the training-order insight generalizes beyond urban tasks.
