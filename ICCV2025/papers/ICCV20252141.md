## Problem

Existing driver intention prediction (DIP) models are black-box systems that predict maneuvers without providing human-understandable justifications, and traditional DIP datasets lack contextual explanations linking actions to their causes. Moreover, standard video architectures encode spatio-temporal information as flattened token representations, making it difficult to extract interpretable, concept-level insights. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing an explainable dataset and a video-aware concept bottleneck architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two synchronized video streams: an in-cabin gaze view (from the Aria eye tracker) and an out-cabin ego-vehicle front view, each 7–15 seconds long. The gaze modality is best represented as a circular crop (radius 350 px) centered on the gaze ground truth rather than an overlay, which adds noise. The output is a 7-class maneuver label (go straight, left/right turn, left/right lane change, slow/stop, U-turn) jointly with 17 multi-label ego-vehicle explanations and 1 single-label gaze explanation, all categorical rather than free-form text.

## Method

VCBM comprises a dual video encoder (I3D, VideoMAE, or MViTv2), a Learnable Token Merging (LTM) module, and a Localised Concept Bottleneck Model (LCBM). LTM performs unsupervised soft clustering over concatenated gaze and front-view tubelet embeddings using a composite distance that combines feature cosine similarity, normalized spatial distance, and normalized temporal distance, reducing N tokens to K learnable cluster centers (K≪N) while preserving spatio-temporal coherence. LCBM then applies a late-averaging bottleneck where each fully connected layer corresponds to one explanation and processes all merged tokens individually before aggregation, rather than collapsing to a single CLS token. Training minimizes a weighted sum of multiclass cross-entropy for the maneuver and multilabel binary cross-entropy for each of the 17 explanations, jointly optimizing prediction and interpretability. The key finding is that transformer backbones (MViTv2) substantially outperform CNNs (I3D) on explanation metrics, confirming that temporal understanding is essential for generating faithful, localized explanations.

## Results

On the DAAD-X dataset, the proposed MViTV2+LTMwB achieves the best explanation performance with 31.22% explanation accuracy, 43.86% explanation F1, 29.17% macro-F1, and 49.11% micro-F1, while maintaining 69.73% action accuracy. The I3D+LTMwB variant achieves the highest action accuracy among bottleneck models at 73.21% with 28.31% explanation accuracy. Transformer-based backbones (MViTv2) consistently outperform the CNN-based I3D on explanation metrics (e.g., 43.86% vs. 39.43% explanation F1), supporting the paper's claim that transformers are better suited for interpretable video tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three backbone architectures (I3D, VideoMAE, MViTv2) both with and without the concept bottleneck layer. Adding LTM to I3D improves explanation accuracy from 25.26% to 28.31% and macro-F1 from 18.53% to 24.10%, at a small cost to action accuracy (74.09% → 73.21%). For the transformer backbone, MViTV2+LTMwB substantially outperforms the plain MViTv2 wB baseline on all explanation metrics (e.g., explanation F1: 43.86% vs. 37.10%; micro-F1: 49.11% vs. 42.10%), while also recovering action accuracy from 63.29% to 69.73%. VideoMAE wB is the weakest bottleneck baseline, achieving only 24.21% explanation accuracy and 38.24% explanation F1, and is not further enhanced with LTM in the main table. Overall, the proposed method wins on explanation metrics across all backlines, with the transformer-based MViTv2 yielding the strongest interpretability, confirming the paper's central finding that transformers are better than CNNs for generating spatio-temporally coherent explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (CBM) from Koh et al. [14], originally designed for image classification, to the video domain by introducing a Learnable Token Merging (LTM) module for spatio-temporal clustering and a Localised Concept Bottleneck Model (LCBM) that preserves fine-grained token-level detail rather than collapsing to a single global representation. It departs from prior explainable driving datasets (BDD-OIA, BDD-X) that offered only single-frame or freeform textual explanations, instead providing structured, multi-label, temporally-grounded annotations derived from both ego-vehicle and eye-gaze perspectives. The video encoder backbones (I3D, VideoMAE, MViTv2) are treated as interchangeable components, positioning VCBM as an architecture-agnostic interpretability layer rather than a new video model from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first concept-bottleneck approach explicitly designed for video, addressing a gap where prior CBMs and concept-relevance methods could not model temporal inputs. The consistent finding that transformer backbones generate better explanations while CNNs predict actions more accurately highlights a fundamental tradeoff that practitioners building interpretable video systems must navigate. Key limitations include the assumption of partial spatial alignment between gaze and front views (GradCAM activations concentrate forward), the small dataset size (1,568 clips), and the long-tail distribution of explanations. Open questions include whether explicit view alignment (e.g., homography) before token merging would improve generalization, and whether the framework scales to longer videos or more granular explanation taxonomies.
