## Problem

Existing image immunization methods add adversarial noise in pixel space to disrupt diffusion-based editing models, but the resulting perturbations are visually noticeable and protection is lost under common purification techniques like JPEG compression. There is no principled way to tune the trade-off between purification-robustness and noise imperceptibility, making prior defenses impractical for real-world use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251006/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image, a JPEG quality setting Q_alg (default 0.95), a perturbation bound ε on quantized DCT coefficients (default 1), and optionally a binary mask for inpainting-specific defense. It outputs an immunized image whose perturbations reside in the quantized DCT coefficient space (integer-valued, 8×8 blocks per YCbCr channel), reducing the parameter count from O(3HW) in pixel space to O(3HW/2) or O(HW) for the Y-channel variant. The immunized image can be saved in any format, not just JPEG.

## Method

DCT-Shield performs adversarial optimization directly on quantized DCT coefficients by embedding the full JPEG encode-decode pipeline into the optimization loop. The image is converted to YCbCr, block-wise DCT-transformed, and quantized; perturbations δ are then added to the quantized coefficients (post-quantization, so gradients flow without being zeroed by the non-differentiable quantization step). The optimization objective minimizes the L2 norm of the VAE encoder's latent representation of the JPEG-decompressed perturbed image, solved via Projected Gradient Descent with an L∞ constraint of ε on δ. Because the perturbations are quantization-aware by construction, images immunized at quality Q_alg remain protected against JPEG purification at any quality level above Q_alg, giving defenders a tunable robustness knob. Three variants are provided: a base version perturbing all YCbCr channels, a mask-based version concentrating noise on inpainting-vulnerable regions, and a Y-channel-only version that maximizes imperceptibility under aggressive compression.

## Results

On the OmniEdit benchmark (150 samples), DCT-Shield achieves the best noise-perception scores among all methods: LPIPS 0.267, FID 35.02, and VIFp 0.776, while maintaining competitive edit-protection (LPIPS 0.684, FID 316.36). Human evaluation confirms this advantage with the highest scores for both imperceptibility (4.22/5) and protection effectiveness (4.35/5). On the inpainting task (56 samples from PPR10K and web portraits), DCT-Shield achieves the strongest protection with LPIPS 0.547 and FID 199.08, outperforming DiffusionGuard (LPIPS 0.518, FID 194.93) and all other baselines, while also scoring highest in human evaluation (4.12/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCT-Shield clearly outperforms all four pixel-space baselines on noise imperceptibility: it achieves LPIPS 0.267 and FID 35.02 versus PhotoGuard (0.284, 57.78), SDS(-) (0.335, 86.36), MIST (0.362, 104.27), and AdvDM (0.353, 148.89), with the gap widening substantially for VIFp (0.776 vs. 0.316–0.543). On edit protection, DCT-Shield is competitive with the strongest baselines PhotoGuard and SDS(-) (LPIPS 0.684 vs. 0.679/0.681; FID 316.36 vs. 336.74/313.74), while decisively surpassing MIST and AdvDM by 15–20 FID points. PhotoGuard edges DCT-Shield slightly on SSIM (0.829 vs. 0.822) and edit-protection FID (336.74 vs. 316.36), but DCT-Shield wins on human evaluation for both noise (4.22 vs. 3.96) and protection (4.35 vs. 4.16). On the inpainting task, DCT-Shield surpasses DiffusionGuard on LPIPS (0.547 vs. 0.518), FID (199.08 vs. 194.93), SSIM (0.531 vs. 0.584), and human evaluation (4.12 vs. 3.96), though DiffusionGuard retains a small advantage on PSNR (16.46 vs. 15.95) and CLIP score (0.664 vs. 0.674). Overall, DCT-Shield achieves the best noise-perception–protection trade-off, particularly at matched perturbation budgets, and uniquely maintains protection after JPEG purification at quality levels above its immunization quality setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of pixel-space adversarial immunization methods (PhotoGuard, MIST, AdvDM, Diff-Protect/SDS, DiffusionGuard) by shifting the optimization domain from raw pixels to quantized DCT coefficients within the JPEG pipeline. It builds on the observation from Diff-Protect [23] that VAE encoders are more vulnerable than U-nets, retaining an encoder-only objective while introducing a fundamentally different perturbation space. The conceptual move is to exploit the fact that JPEG compression already operates in the DCT domain with quantization, making the adversarial perturbation naturally aligned with the most common image purification technique rather than fighting against it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JPEG robustness result addresses a critical practical gap: pixel-space immunizations are routinely defeated by the JPEG compression that occurs whenever images are shared online, making prior defenses largely ineffective in real-world settings. The tunable Q_alg parameter gives defenders a principled knob to balance protection range against visual fidelity, something pixel-space methods cannot offer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, whether adaptive attackers who know the DCT-domain defense can craft targeted countermeasures, and how the method performs against emerging purification techniques beyond JPEG and crop-resize. The paper also notes that the threat model assumes off-the-shelf editing models, leaving the question of fine-tuned or novel editing architectures unaddressed.
