## Problem

Current weakly-supervised video anomaly detection methods suffer from two limitations: (1) a single shared model processes all anomaly categories, causing subtle anomalies (e.g., shoplifting) to be confused with normal events while salient ones (e.g., explosions) are easily detected; and (2) the standard MIL top-k supervision only pushes the most extreme snippets toward abnormal, ignoring the temporal extent and lower-scoring portions of anomalous events, resulting in incomplete anomaly representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes surveillance video clips as input, processed into snippet-level features by an I3D encoder, with only video-level normal/abnormal labels available during training. It outputs a continuous anomaly score in [0, 1] for each temporal snippet, enabling frame-level localization. The TGS loss generates soft pseudo-labels (continuous target scores per snippet) rather than binary top-k targets, allowing the model to learn graded anomaly severity across the full temporal window of an event.

## Method

GS-MoE combines two key innovations. First, Temporal Gaussian Splatting (TGS) replaces the MIL top-k loss: it detects local maxima (peaks) in the model's predicted abnormal scores, determines each peak's width from monotonic score regions, and splats Gaussian kernels centered at those peaks across the temporal axis to produce soft pseudo-labels, so the model is supervised on the full temporal extent of each anomaly rather than only its most extreme snippets. Second, a Mixture-of-Experts architecture assigns a dedicated transformer-based expert (4 self-attention heads + GELU MLP) to each anomaly class, each trained with the TGS loss on its class plus normal videos. A gate model then integrates expert scores with coarse task-aware features from UR-DMU via a bi-directional cross-attention module, a score-refinement projection, and a final transformer+MLP head to produce the unified anomaly prediction, leveraging both class-specific fine-grained cues and general anomaly-aware features.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, GS-MoE achieves 91.58% AUC and 83.86% AUC_A, surpassing the previous best (VadCLIP at 88.02% AUC) by 3.56% and outperforming UR-DMU (70.81% AUC_A) by a remarkable 13.63% on the abnormal-only metric. On XD-Violence, GS-MoE reaches 82.89% AP and 85.74% AP_A, beating UR-DMU (83.94% AP_A) by 1.8% on the anomaly-focused metric. On the MSAD dataset, GS-MoE sets a new state of the art with 87.72% AUC, exceeding the best prior method (TEVAD at 86.82%) by 0.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-MoE decisively outperforms all prior methods on UCF-Crime AUC, beating the closest competitor VadCLIP (88.02%) by 3.56% and UR-DMU (86.97%) by 4.61%. The most striking margin is on UCF-Crime AUC_A, where GS-MoE (83.86%) surpasses UR-DMU (70.81%) by 13.63%, confirming that class-specific expert modeling is critical for detecting subtle anomalies. On XD-Violence, GS-MoE achieves the best AP_A (85.74%), edging out UR-DMU (83.94%) by 1.8%, while its overall AP (82.89%) slightly trails VadCLIP (84.15%) due to the latter's stronger performance on normal videos. On MSAD, GS-MoE (87.72% AUC) surpasses TEVAD (86.82%) and UR-DMU (85.02%), establishing a new benchmark. The cluster-expert variant (7 clusters) still reaches 88.58% AUC on UCF-Crime, outperforming VadCLIP and TPWNG, demonstrating robustness when the number of anomaly classes is not predefined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised video anomaly detection (WSVAD) line of work by reformulating the standard multi-instance learning (MIL) objective introduced by Sultani et al. (CVPR 2018) into a Temporal Gaussian Splatting (TGS) loss that renders pseudo-labels from detected score peaks rather than relying on top-k snippets. Architecturally, it builds on UR-DMU (Zhou et al., AAAI 2023) as a task-aware feature extractor and introduces a Mixture-of-Experts layer where each expert specializes in a single anomaly class, drawing on the broader MoE literature (Eigen et al. 2013; Fedus et al. 2022) but repurposing it for class-specific anomaly modeling rather than token routing. The Gaussian splatting concept is borrowed from 3D scene rendering (Kerbl et al., ACM TOG 2023) and adapted to the 1-D temporal axis of video scores, a domain transfer that is straightforward in principle but non-trivial in its interaction with weak supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.63% AUCA gain over the previous best on UCF-Crime is the most practically significant result, suggesting that class-specific expertise and temporally-aware pseudo-labels address a real bottleneck in detecting subtle anomalies (shoplifting, arson) that shared MIL models miss. The TGS loss is a drop-in replacement for the MIL objective that could benefit other WSVAD methods without architectural changes, as evidenced by the +1.77% AUC gain when applied to the UR-DMU baseline alone. Open questions include how the approach scales when the number of anomaly classes is large or unknown in deployment (the K-Means clustering experiment partially addresses this but still requires a fixed number of clusters), and whether the gate model's bi-directional cross-attention is necessary or whether simpler fusion would suffice. The authors note future work on LLM-based explainability, hinting at a path toward interpretable anomaly reporting.
