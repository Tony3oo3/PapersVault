## Problem

Learning 3D spatial relationships between arbitrary object pairs (e.g., a pizza cutter angled over a pizza) is difficult because real-world 3D data for diverse, unbounded object categories is scarce and existing layout methods are restricted to predefined categories or simple relations like 'on' and 'next to.' This paper addresses the gap by showing that pre-trained 2D diffusion models implicitly encode realistic object-object spatial cues that can be uplifted into 3D training data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt describing the spatial context (e.g., 'A teapot pouring tea into a teacup'), a base object category, and a target object category, all encoded via a pre-trained T5 text encoder. It outputs an OOR sample φ = (R, t, s_target, s_base) where R ∈ SO(3) is the relative rotation, t ∈ R³ the relative translation, and s vectors encode per-axis scales that recover original aspect ratios from a unit-cube normalized canonical space. For multi-object scenes, the output is a set of pairwise OORs arranged via a DAG scene graph, and for applications the model can produce rearranged 3D scenes or human motion sequences.

## Method

The pipeline first synthesizes 3D OOR training data by generating 2D images with FLUX, producing pseudo multi-views via SV3D, reconstructing point clouds with VGGSfM, and registering template meshes to the point cloud using semantic correspondence features (DINO, PCA-reduced) with Procrustes+RANSAC+ICP to extract relative poses and scales. A score-based diffusion model (GenPose backbone) is then trained with a Denoising Score Matching objective to model the conditional OOR distribution p_c^{T→B}, with LLM-based text augmentation expanding training to 475 contexts across 188 object categories. For multi-object scenes, pairwise OORs are sampled simultaneously along a DAG scene graph, with novel collision loss and inconsistency loss terms added to the reverse ODE to prevent overlaps and resolve conflicting pose estimates from multiple paths. The score function also enables optimization-based applications: scene denoising/editing via gradient descent on a log-likelihood objective, and human motion synthesis by maintaining contact constraints across the OOR sequence.

## Results

On pairwise OOR generation (150 scenes, 30 category pairs), the method achieves a VLM score of 75.67 and a user-study preference of 54.02%, substantially outperforming SceneTeller (64.67 / 23.77%) and SMC (49.83 / 22.21%). CLIP and VQA scores are also marginally higher (29.11 and 0.69 respectively). For multi-object OOR (20 scenes, 3–5 objects), the advantage is dramatic: the VLM score reaches 97.50 versus 2.50 for GraphDreamer, and user-study preference is 88.12% versus 11.88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines across two settings. For pairwise OOR, SMC [58] (an LLM-based 3D alignment method) loses on every metric, with the largest gap in VLM score (49.83 vs. 75.67) and user study (22.21% vs. 54.02%); the authors note SMC often misinterprets rotation while handling translation reasonably. SceneTeller [39] (an LLM-based 3D layout method) is closer on CLIP (29.06 vs. 29.11) and VQA (0.68 vs. 0.69) but still trails on VLM score (64.67 vs. 75.67) and user study (23.77% vs. 54.02%), attributed to its inability to capture fine-grained 3D spatial cues without direct 3D data. For multi-object OOR, GraphDreamer [13] (a diffusion-based text-to-3D model) is decisively outperformed: on original renderings the VLM score is 97.50 vs. 2.50 and user-study preference is 88.12% vs. 11.88%, while on ControlNet-guided images the method wins on VQA (0.71 vs. 0.44 with Canny; 0.79 vs. 0.62 with Depth) and ties or slightly trails on CLIP (28.73 vs. 27.09 Canny; 28.47 vs. 28.51 Depth). Overall, the proposed OOR diffusion wins or ties on every reported metric across both pairwise and multi-object settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning spatial relationships from pre-trained 2D diffusion models (CHORUS, ComA) from human-object interactions to object-object spatial relationships, introducing a new formulation called OOR that captures relative poses and scales between unbounded object pairs in canonical space. The OOR diffusion model is architecturally grounded in GenPose's score-based framework for 6D pose estimation, adapted to the spatial parametric space of relative transformations. The 3D data generation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf components—FLUX for image synthesis, SV3D for pseudo-multi-view generation, VGGSfM for reconstruction, and semantic correspondence features for mesh registration—into a coherent uplift pipeline that eliminates the need for manual 3D annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOR formulation addresses a genuine data scarcity problem: 3D spatial relationship data for unbounded object pairs is nearly impossible to collect at scale, and the synthetic pipeline offers a practical workaround. The score-based formulation enables downstream optimization for scene editing and human motion synthesis, broadening the utility beyond mere generation. However, the method depends on template meshes and pseudo-multi-view geometric consistency, and the experimental comparisons are against LLM-based and text-to-3D baselines that were not designed for this specific task, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object scenes or finer-grained shape-dependent relationships.
