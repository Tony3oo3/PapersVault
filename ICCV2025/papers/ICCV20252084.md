## Problem

Existing pseudo-label selection in semi-supervised semantic segmentation relies on fixed confidence thresholds, which fail under network overconfidence where correct and incorrect predictions overlap in high-confidence regions. Moreover, the direct discarding of low-confidence predictions disrupts spatial-semantic continuity, leaving critical contextual regions without supervision and amplifying model cognitive bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (3×H×W) with weak and strong augmentations, plus a small set of pixel-level labeled annotations (N_l ≪ N_u). For each unlabeled image, it produces a per-pixel binary reliability mask M_i, smooth Gaussian loss weights ω_n, and a pseudo-label map ŷ_i^u, all at the original image resolution. The TMP component additionally generates a patch-based perturbation mask R_i (patch size s=32, masking ratio θ=0.7) applied to the input to create a masked variant x̃_i^u for an auxiliary loss.

## Method

CSL constructs a two-dimensional confidence distribution feature space per pixel using maximum confidence p_n(k') and residual dispersion v_n (the negative mean squared deviation of non-maximum class probabilities from their mean), then formulates pseudo-label selection as a trace-maximization convex optimization problem over a selection matrix S. This non-convex combinatorial problem is solved via spectral relaxation (Ky Fan theorem), yielding sample-specific decision boundaries that separate reliable from unreliable predictions more effectively than a fixed threshold. A Gaussian weighting function produces smooth loss weights ω_n for borderline predictions, while reliable predictions retain weight 1. To compensate for the persistent exclusion of low-reliability regions, Trusted Mask Perturbation (TMP) randomly masks subsets of reliable pixels in 32×32 patches, forcing the network to reconstruct semantics from adjacent unreliable regions via contextual dependencies, with the masked-input loss combined at equal weight (λ₁=λ₂=0.5) with the standard weak-to-strong consistency loss.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 (original, 321×321 crop), CSL achieves 76.8%, 79.6%, 80.3%, 80.9%, and 82.3% mIoU under 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, and Full labeled splits, respectively, outperforming the supervised baseline by up to +31.4% in the low-data regime. On Cityscapes, CSL reaches 78.2% mIoU with only 1/16 labels (186 images), surpassing the supervised baseline by +15.1%. On MS COCO, CSL attains 35.1% mIoU with just 1/512 of labeled data, exceeding the strongest baseline LogicDiag by +2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSL consistently outperforms all 22 compared baselines across all three datasets and all labeled-data splits. On PASCAL VOC 2012 (321×321), CSL surpasses UniMatch [69] by +2.4% (1/8) and +1.5% (1/4), and CorrMatch [50] by +1.1% (1/8) and +0.9% (1/4), while matching or exceeding the 513×513-crop methods (RankMatch, AllSpark) despite using a smaller crop. On Cityscapes, CSL leads CorrMatch [50] by +0.9% at 1/16 and +0.7% at 1/2, and outperforms DAW [51] by +1.6% at 1/16. On MS COCO, CSL beats LogicDiag [34] by +2.0% at 1/512 and +0.7% at 1/32, and surpasses UniMatch [69] by +3.2% at 1/512. The gains are most pronounced in the low-label regime (1/16 and 1/512 splits), confirming that CSL's confidence-separable selection is particularly effective when labeled data is scarce and network overconfidence is most detrimental.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSL builds directly on the UniMatch weak-to-strong consistency regularization framework [69] for the training pipeline, but departs fundamentally from the standard confidence-thresholding paradigm for pseudo-label selection. Rather than applying a fixed or adaptive threshold on maximum confidence (as in FixMatch, DAW, or SoftMatch), CSL introduces residual dispersion—a measure of probability variance among non-maximum classes—derived from entropy minimization theory, and formulates the selection problem as a trace-maximization convex optimization solved via spectral relaxation [43]. This positions CSL alongside methods that seek additional reliability metrics beyond confidence (e.g., Monte Carlo dropout, ensembles), but achieves this without the computational overhead of multiple forward passes or auxiliary networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized but underexplored bottleneck in semi-supervised segmentation: the overconfidence-induced overlap between correct and incorrect predictions in high-confidence regions. The convex optimization framing provides a principled, sample-specific decision boundary rather than a global threshold, and the Trusted Mask Perturbation component offers a practical remedy for the supervision gap in low-confidence regions. However, the per-image eigenvalue decomposition adds non-trivial computational cost, and the consistent but moderate gains (1–3% mIoU over SOTA) suggest the approach may be most impactful in low-label regimes where overconfidence is most severe. Open questions include whether the residual dispersion metric generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, instance segmentation) and whether the spectral relaxation approximation remains tight as the number of classes grows.
