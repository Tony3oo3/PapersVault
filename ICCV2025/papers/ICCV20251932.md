## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for large vision-language models applies a single unstructured reward over fixed instructions, which over-emphasizes linguistic nuance while overlooking visual context. The dynamic interplay between what question is asked and what answer is produced—a preference dialogue interaction—is entirely ignored, yet questions with higher visual dependency empirically yield higher-quality answers. This paper addresses the gap by formulating preference optimization over the full question-answer trajectory rather than over a single answer given a fixed prompt.

## Input / Output

The model receives a visual input (single image, multi-image set, or 32 video frames) and self-generates a question via an SFT stage trained with a learning-to-question objective; the question then conditions the answer generation. Preference data is structured as binary trees where the visual input is the root, self-generated questions occupy the first level, and answers are leaf nodes, with GPT-4o performing pairwise trajectory scoring. Unlike standard DPO which conditions on a fixed instruction, SPO's input includes the model's own generated question as part of the optimized trajectory, and the output is a text answer whose quality is jointly evaluated with the question that prompted it.

## Method

SPO introduces a structured trajectory reward r(τ) = λ·r(x,q) + δ·r([x,q],y) that decomposes the preference signal into a question reward and an answer reward, treating questioning and answering as two sequential actions in a depth-2 binary tree. The SPO loss (Eq. 11) sums DPO-style classification losses over all cross-pairs of preferred and dispreferred trajectories within a sub-tree, so the policy is optimized simultaneously on question quality and answer quality rather than on a single leaf-node comparison. Training is two-stage: first, SFT with a learning-to-question objective produces a reference policy capable of self-generating diverse visual questions; second, SPO fine-tunes the policy via the structured reward using LoRA (rank 64) with GPT-4o-labeled preference pairs. The hyperparameters λ and δ control the relative weight of question vs. answer rewards, and setting λ=δ recovers a trajectory-level DPO, while asymmetric settings allow targeted emphasis on either visual questioning or answering.

## Results

On single-image benchmarks (Table 1), SFT-7B+SPO achieves 62.1 on MMStar, 49.2 on MMMU, 1585 on MME, 70.2 on LLaVA-Bench-Wilder, 58.8 on MMVet, 67.3 on RealWorldQA, and 81.0 on MMBench, outperforming SFT-7B+DPO on every metric. At the 0.5B scale, SPO similarly leads DPO across all benchmarks (e.g., 41.5 vs 40.9 on MMStar, 61.3 vs 58.5 on LLaVA-Wilder, 30.0 vs 25.8 on MMVet). On multi-image and video benchmarks (Table 2), SFT-7B+SPO reaches 67.9 on LLaVA-Interleave, 46.7 on MuirBench, 60.5 on EgoSchema, and 65.3 on MLVU, consistently exceeding both DPO and the SFT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPO consistently outperforms DPO (the primary baseline) on all 9 single-image benchmarks at both 0.5B and 7B scales, with the largest gaps on LLaVA-Bench-Wilder (+2.8 at 0.5B, +0.3 at 7B) and MMVet (+4.2 at 0.5B, +0.6 at 7B), confirming that structured trajectory rewards better capture visual-dialogue alignment than a single unstructured reward. At the 7B scale, SPO also surpasses the LLaVA-OV-7B base model on every benchmark (e.g., +2.4 on LLaVA-Wilder, +1.3 on MMVet, +0.2 on MMBench), indicating that preference optimization with SPO recovers and exceeds the performance lost during the resource-constrained SFT stage. On multi-image and video tasks, SPO-7B leads DPO-7B on LLaVA-Interleave (+1.0), MuirBench (+0.5), EgoSchema (+1.0), and MLVU (+0.2), though DPO slightly edges out on NExT-QA (79.8 vs 79.6) and LVid-Bench (58.1 vs 57.8). Against commercial models, SPO-7B remains below GPT-4o and LLaVA-OV-72B on most benchmarks but is competitive with GPT-4V on MMStar (62.1 vs 57.1) and MMBench (81.0 vs 75.0). The ablation (Table 3) further shows that both the question reward r(x,q) and answer reward r([x,q],y) contribute: removing either degrades performance, with the question reward particularly important for open-ended QA (LLaVA-Wilder, LLaVA-Interleave) and the answer reward for fixed-form QA (MME).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPO extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing its single-step, unstructured reward with a trajectory-based structured reward that decomposes preference into question and answer components, modeled as a depth-2 binary tree. The SFT stage builds directly on SQ-LLaVA (Sun et al., 2024), which trains the model to self-generate visual questions, enabling the policy to produce the question-answer pairs that SPO then optimizes over. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard DPO paradigm—where a fixed instruction conditions a single reward on the answer—by treating the question itself as an optimizable action, thereby capturing the dialogue interaction between visual input, questioning, and answering that prior multi-modal DPO methods (e.g., Silkie, LLaVA-Med) ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured trajectory reward offers a principled way to address DPO's known tendency to over-emphasize linguistic nuance at the expense of visual grounding in LVLMs, and the ablation confirming that both question and answer rewards contribute independently is practically useful for practitioners tuning preference pipelines. However, the empirical gains are modest (typically 0.5–2 points over DPO), the experiments are limited to single-turn dialogue and constrained compute (8 GPUs vs. 16 for LLaVA-OV), and the depth-2 tree structure leaves open whether the formulation generalizes to multi-turn or deeper reasoning chains. The finding that SPO is more robust to low-visual-dependency questions than DPO suggests a path toward more reliable open-ended visual chat, but broader validation across architectures and conversation lengths is needed.
