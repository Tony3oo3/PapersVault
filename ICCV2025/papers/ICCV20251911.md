## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs in multimodal models scale quadratically in both computation and memory with input sequence length, making long video understanding prohibitively expensive (e.g., ~100 frames max on a single A100/H100). While linear RNNs like RWKV offer constant-memory inference over arbitrary-length sequences, they overfit to their pretrained context length and lack context-extension mechanisms like RoPE, making them unsuitable for the long visual token sequences produced by video encoders. No prior work has used a fully recurrent LLM backbone in a LLaVA-style architecture for open-ended video understanding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames (sampled at 1 fps or lower, up to 16K frames on a 24 GB GPU) and a text instruction, producing free-form text outputs including QA answers, sparse captions, and dense video descriptions. After the Sorted Visual Token Merge at a retention ratio of 0.1, each frame contributes roughly 60 visual tokens to the LLM, well within the RWKV backbone's 4096-token pretrained context. The sandwich prompting strategy places these merged visual tokens between instruction tokens rather than prepending them, which is critical for instruction following in the recurrent architecture.

## Method

AURORALONG replaces the transformer LLM in a LLaVA-1.5-style pipeline with RWKV-v6-Finch, a linear RNN whose recurrent state has constant size regardless of sequence length, paired with SigLIP (large-patch16-384) as the vision encoder and a two-layer MLP connector. The key technical contribution is Sorted Visual Token Merge (S-ToMe): at each ViT layer, the most similar patch token pairs are merged (reducing token count by a configurable ratio), and the resulting tokens are then sorted in ascending order by the number of original patches they represent, which empirically outperforms random or descending order by leveraging RWKV's data-dependent token shifting to prioritize information-rich large patches. Training follows a three-stage recipe—connector-only pretraining on image-caption pairs, vision-stage fine-tuning with frozen LLM, and end-to-end language-stage training on video QA and captioning data—where the token merge ratio is fixed at 0.1 for all video and multi-image samples to force the model to learn from sparse visual tokens. The model achieves 34× lower GPU memory than transformer baselines at 1,024 frames and 8× faster inference on one-minute videos while matching or exceeding 7B–40B transformer models on MLVU, MVBench, and MovieChat-1K.

## Results

On MLVU (long video QA, ~12-min videos), AURORALONG (2B, 4k pretrained context) achieves 52.7 average, outperforming LLaVA-OneVision (50.3, 0.5B, 132k context), Qwen2-VL (48.7, 2B, 132k), and InternVL2 (48.2, 2B, 200k) while using only 48 frames and ~60 tokens per frame. On MovieChat-1K, it scores 84.0 (Global) and 64.0 (Break), surpassing the next-best ShareGPT4Video (69.0/60.9) by 15 points. On short-video VDC dense captioning, AURORALONG reaches 42.54 average, exceeding Gemini-1.5-Pro (41.73) and all 7–8B open-source baselines. On MVBench, it achieves 53.2 average, competitive with Qwen2-VL (53.5) and InternVL2 (52.9) despite being trained exclusively on public data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURORALONG (2B, 4k context) outperforms all open-weight baselines on MLVU, beating the closest competitors LLaVA-OneVision (50.3) by 2.4 points, Qwen2-VL (48.7) by 4.0, and InternVL2 (48.2) by 4.5, while using 30–50× less pretrained context length. On MovieChat-1K, it leads ShareGPT4Video (69.0) by 15.0 points on Global QA and InternVL-1.5 (57.7) by 26.3 points, a substantial margin given the 2B parameter budget. On MVBench, AURORALONG (53.2) narrowly trails Qwen2-VL (53.5) by 0.3 but edges out InternVL2 (52.9) and LongVA (50.8), confirming competitive multiple-choice performance despite the paper's noted limitation in this area. On short-video VDC dense captioning, AURORALONG (42.54) surpasses Gemini-1.5-Pro (41.73) and all 7–8B open-source models (AuroraCap 38.21, LLaVA-OneVision 37.45, InternVL-2 37.72). The only baseline it does not beat is GPT-4o (54.5 on MLVU), a proprietary model of unknown size, and Qwen2-VL on MVBench by a marginal 0.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inherits the LLaVA-1.5 architecture but replaces the Transformer LLM backbone with RWKV-v6-Finch, a linear RNN that maintains constant hidden-state size regardless of sequence length. It adapts Token Merging (ToMe) from image classification to the video LMM setting and introduces a training-free sorted reordering of merged visual tokens (ascending by patch count) to exploit the unidirectional recurrent nature of RWKV, departing from bidirectional scanning strategies used in prior Mamba/RWKV visual encoders. The sandwich prompting strategy is borrowed from VisualRWKV, and the three-stage training recipe follows AuroraCap. The key conceptual departure is treating the RNN's recurrent state as an implicit positional encoding that benefits from spatially-ordered token input, rather than adding explicit positional embeddings or bidirectional passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 34× GPU memory reduction and 8× inference speedup at 1,024 frames make long video understanding accessible on consumer-grade hardware (24 GB), potentially democratizing a task previously requiring multi-GPU setups. The finding that a 2B model trained exclusively on public data matches or exceeds 7–40B Transformer models on MLVU and MVBench suggests that architectural efficiency can partially substitute for scale in video understanding. Open questions include whether the overfitting behavior at low token-retention ratios (where fewer tokens paradoxically improve captioning) is fundamental to linear RNNs or specific to RWKV-6, and whether the approach scales to even longer videos (hours) without the multiple-choice degradation the authors acknowledge. The limited multiple-choice capability and domain-specific weaknesses suggest the 2B RWKV backbone remains a bottleneck for complex reasoning tasks.
