## Problem

Top-down human pose estimation methods condition on bounding boxes but struggle in multi-body scenes where overlapping people cause merged detections and collapsed poses. Prior work addressed this by conditioning on prior keypoints (e.g., BUCTD) but overlooked instance segmentation masks as a conditioning signal. No existing method enforces mutual consistency among bounding boxes, masks, and poses in a closed iterative loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252087/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and iteratively produces per-instance triplets of bounding boxes, 2D pose keypoints (up to 22 across COCO/AIC/MPII), and binary segmentation masks. During iteration, already-processed instances are masked to opaque black in the image before re-detection, while pose estimation uses semi-transparent masking (α=0.8) to retain contextual information. The loop terminates when the detector finds no new instances in the fully masked image, or after a user-specified number of iterations (typically 2).

## Method

BMP closes a loop among three specialized models adapted for mutual conditioning: (1) RTMDet-L fine-tuned with instance-removal data augmentation so it ignores already-masked instances and detects only remaining ones; (2) MaskPose, a ViTPose-based model that conditions pose estimation on a semi-transparently masked crop (α·I + (1−α)·(I⊙M)) rather than a plain bounding-box crop, with randomly deformed GT masks during training for robustness; and (3) SAM2 prompted with up to 6 pose keypoints selected via a KMeans++-like spread algorithm with confidence thresholding (T_c=0.5), deliberately omitting the detected bounding box to allow SAM to correct detection errors. Each iteration runs detect → pose → segment → mask-out → re-detect, with pose-mask consistency (P-Mc) used to discard SAM masks that are worse than the detector's original. The modular design allows any component to be swapped for a larger alternative, and the same hyperparameters transfer between standard (COCO) and multi-body (OCHuman) evaluation.

## Results

On OCHuman, BMP 2× achieves 49.2 AP for pose estimation, setting a new SOTA that surpasses the previous best (BUCTD 2×, 48.3 AP) by 0.9 AP. MaskPose-b alone improves the ViTPose-B top-down baseline from 42.6 to 45.0 AP on OCHuman through mask conditioning. On COCO, BMP 2× matches the top-down SOTA at 76.5 AP (vs. ViTPose-B 76.4). For detection and segmentation on OCHuman, BMP 2× sets new SOTA at 35.9 bbox AP and 34.0 mask AP, improving over the RTMDet-L detector baseline by 5.9 and 7.5 AP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMP 2× outperforms all 14 baselines on OCHuman pose estimation, with the closest competitor being BUCTD 2× (48.3 AP vs. 49.2 AP, +0.9). Among top-down methods, MaskPose-b (45.0 AP) beats ViTPose-B (42.6 AP) by 2.4 AP and MIPNet (42.5 AP) by 2.5 AP, establishing a new top-down SOTA. On COCO, BMP 2× (76.5 AP) marginally edges out ViTPose-B (76.4) and MIPNet (76.3), while substantially outperforming BUCTD (74.8) and all detector-free methods (DEKR 71.0, HQNet 69.5, CID-w48 68.9). For detection on OCHuman, BMP 2× (35.9 bbox AP) surpasses the strongest detector RTMDet-L (30.0) by 5.9 AP and the large CoDETR SWIN-L (29.6) by 6.3 AP. For instance segmentation, BMP 2× (34.0 mask AP) beats Crowd-SAM (31.4) by 2.6 AP and HQNet (31.1) by 2.9 AP. The foundational model Sapiens 0.3b (41.3 AP) is outperformed by BMP 2× by 7.9 AP on OCHuman while running at roughly half the inference time (1.12 s/img vs. 2.03 s/img combined with RTMDet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the top-down human pose estimation lineage (ViTPose, MIPNet) by introducing mask-conditioned pose estimation (MaskPose), a direction explicitly noted as unexplored in prior work. It also builds on the iterative refinement idea from BUCTD, which conditions pose on prior keypoint estimates, but generalizes the loop to include all three body representations—bounding boxes, masks, and poses—each conditioning the others. The segmentation component repurposes SAM2's point-prompting interface with an automated keypoint selection algorithm, extending the pose-to-mask direction (Pose2Seg, ExPoSeg) by closing the loop back to pose and detection. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against foundational models (Sapiens) that unify human representations at the feature level, arguing instead for explicit mutual constraints between specialized models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BMP loop demonstrates that three moderately sized specialized models (225–369M params) can outperform a larger foundational model (393M params) on both accuracy and speed, challenging the assumption that unified large models are necessary for multi-task human understanding. The modular design means any component can be upgraded independently, and the MaskPose model is a standalone contribution as the first top-down HPE conditioned by instance masks. Key limitations include SAM's sensitivity to inaccurate keypoint prompts (the weakest link in the loop) and the detector's failure to reconnect background instances split by foreground occlusion. Open questions include whether the mutual-conditioning paradigm generalizes beyond humans to other object categories, and whether more efficient prompting or learned visibility predictors could close the gap between automated and human SAM interaction.
