## Problem

Existing video customization methods follow an isolated paradigm where identity and motion are trained independently, ignoring their intrinsic mutual constraints and synergistic interdependencies. This causes identity-motion conflicts throughout the diffusion denoising process, systematically degrading either subject fidelity or motion coherence, and no universal training-step configuration can resolve the degradation across different identities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–10 reference identity images per subject, a motion reference video with challenging dynamic patterns, and a text prompt describing the desired scene. It produces a customized video in which the target subject's appearance is preserved across all frames while faithfully replicating the reference motion trajectory. The backbone is CogVideoX-5B (a DiT-based video diffusion model), and evaluation spans 50 subjects and 21 motion sequences with 50 diverse prompts per case to test editability and scene versatility.

## Method

DualReal introduces adaptive joint training via two complementary units. (1) Dual-aware Adaptation alternates between identity-focused and motion-focused training steps using a binary selector Z; a gradient mask M blocks parameter updates of the non-active adapter to prevent cross-dimension knowledge leakage, while the frozen adapter's forward-pass features serve as a prior that guides the active adapter. (2) StageBlender Controller generates per-block scaling weights ω_i by conditioning an Adaptive LayerNorm on the denoising timestep embedding and pooled text-visual features, then routing them through a downward-propagating MLP into n=7 weight groups that sequentially control assigned DiT depth ranges. This enables shallow blocks to emphasize identity preservation in early denoising steps while deeper blocks progressively prioritize motion coherence, achieving lossless fusion without a fixed inference-time blending ratio.

## Results

On the comprehensive evaluation benchmark (50 identity subjects × 21 motion sequences × 50 prompts), DualReal achieves the best identity fidelity scores: CLIP-I 0.629 and DINO-I 0.551, representing average improvements of 21.7% and 31.8% over the strongest baseline. It also records the top scores on all three temporal motion quality metrics (T.Cons 0.983, Motion Smoothness 0.978, T.Flickering 0.965) and ranks second on text-video consistency (CLIP-T 0.323, tied with LoRA). The Dynamic Degree of 14.96 (deviation +2.94 from the 12.02 benchmark) confirms motion amplitude is preserved without collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualReal outperforms all four baselines on both identity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-I) and all three temporal motion metrics (T.Cons, Motion Smoothness, T.Flickering). Against MotionBooth [53], the closest identity competitor, DualReal gains +0.063 on CLIP-I and +0.092 on DINO-I while also improving every motion metric. Against LoRA [18] and CogVideoX-5B [56], which use decoupled sequential training, DualReal achieves substantially higher identity scores (e.g., DINO-I 0.551 vs. 0.286 and 0.424) and keeps Dynamic Degree far closer to the 12.02 benchmark (14.96 vs. 25.34 and 26.51), indicating those methods over-amplify motion at the cost of identity. Against DreamVideo [52], which blends independently trained adapters at inference, DualReal wins on every metric including CLIP-T (0.323 vs. 0.278), confirming that joint adaptive training avoids the identity-motion conflicts inherent to parameter blending. The only metric where DualReal is not first is CLIP-T, where CogVideoX-5B edges ahead (0.336 vs. 0.323), though the gap is marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the isolated customization paradigm (exemplified by DreamVideo's independent adapter training with parameter blending at inference, and MotionBooth's random-video regularization) by proposing joint identity-motion training within a shared parameter space. It inherits the bottleneck adapter architecture from DreamVideo and operates on the CogVideoX-5B DiT backbone, but introduces two novel mechanisms: gradient-masked alternating optimization to prevent cross-dimension knowledge leakage, and a StageBlender Controller that exploits denoising-stage and DiT-depth structure for adaptive dimension weighting. Conceptually, it reframes the identity-motion conflict as a training-dynamics problem rather than an inference-time blending problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in existing video customization—irreversible identity degradation from motion training steps—and shows that joint training with stage-aware granularity can substantially mitigate it (21.7%/31.8% gains on CLIP-I/DINO-I). The observation that shallow DiT blocks specialize in identity while deep blocks specialize in motion provides a useful architectural insight for future DiT-based personalization. However, evaluation is limited to a single backbone (CogVideoX-5B), 50 subjects, and 21 motion sequences, leaving open questions about scalability to multi-subject scenes, longer videos, and alternative architectures. The Dynamic Degree metric also shows DualReal trails LoRA and CogVideoX-5B baselines, suggesting a trade-off between motion amplitude and identity fidelity that remains unresolved.
