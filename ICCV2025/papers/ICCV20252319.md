## Problem

Existing DWT-based super-resolution methods treat each level of high-frequency wavelet coefficients independently, neglecting the interrelations among multiscale frequency sub-bands. This omission leads to inconsistencies and unnatural artifacts in reconstructed images, as the model cannot learn how fine-grained textures at different scales should cohere. The paper asks whether a transformer operating directly on multi-level wavelet spectra can capture these cross-scale frequency relationships to improve both perceptual quality and fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 16×16 for 8× face SR, or arbitrary resolution for 4× general SR) and produces a high-resolution RGB image. Internally, the noisy HR image at each diffusion step is transformed into a J-level wavelet spectrum (where J = ceil(log₂ N) for upscaling factor N) before denoising, and the final output is reconstructed via inverse DWT. The LR image is embedded as in-context conditioning tokens concatenated with the noised spectrum tokens, rather than being upsampled and concatenated in pixel space as in prior diffusion SR methods.

## Method

DTWSR formulates SR as conditional denoising in the multi-level wavelet spectrum domain using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. A pyramid tokenization scheme assigns smaller patch sizes to the LF sub-band and progressively larger patches to HF sub-bands (p^j = p_min × 2^(J−j)), reducing token count by ~33% while maintaining consistent receptive fields across scales; a 4D positional encoding [level, sub-band, height, width] distinguishes tokens across the spectrum. A dual-decoder architecture separates denoising: LEDec handles smooth LF elementary contents, while HDDec handles all multi-level HF sub-bands plus an LF residual that promotes realignment between LF and HF. Tailored attention masks prevent LR conditioning from leaking into LF denoising in HDDec and block LF tokens from overwhelming HF tokens. Training follows the DDGAN framework with a time-dependent discriminator, combined with L1 reconstruction losses in both pixel and wavelet frequency domains.

## Results

On 8× face SISR (CelebA-HQ, 16²→128²), DTWSR achieves PSNR 24.09, SSIM 0.71, Cons. 0.50, Deg. 53.85, and FID 56.77, attaining the best consistency, identity preservation, and perceptual quality among all compared methods. On 4× general SISR (DIV2K), it reaches PSNR 28.18, SSIM 0.79, Cons. 1.151, and LPIPS 0.097, outperforming all generative baselines on LPIPS and Cons. while remaining competitive with regression-based methods on PSNR/SSIM. On real-world restoration (RealSR), DTWSR leads on MUSIQ (64.04), LIQE (3.79), NIQE (3.55), and PI (3.49), surpassing FlowIE, ResShift, and SinSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 8× face SISR, DTWSR achieves the lowest Cons. (0.50) and Deg. (53.85), substantially outperforming IDM (Cons. 2.14, Deg. 58.07) and WFEN (Cons. 2.13, Deg. 57.96), while matching IDM's PSNR (24.09 vs 24.01) and beating it on FID (56.77 vs 57.07). On 4× general SISR, DTWSR surpasses all generative baselines on LPIPS (0.097 vs 0.096 WGSR, 0.104 DiWa) and Cons. (1.151 vs 1.254 SRDiff, 2.187 WGSR), though regression-based HAT (PSNR 29.83, LPIPS 0.125) and LIIF (PSNR 29.29) retain higher fidelity scores. On large-magnification face SISR (12×/16×), DTWSR maintains Cons. below 1.0 and FID below 61, while IDM degrades significantly (Cons. 8.16–9.57, FID 60.58–63.99). On real-world restoration, DTWSR leads SinSR (MUSIQ 64.04 vs 61.45, NIQE 3.55 vs 5.76, PI 3.49 vs 4.49) and FlowIE (MUSIQ 64.04 vs 56.83, NIQE 3.55 vs 5.68) on most no-reference quality metrics, with only NRQM slightly trailing SinSR (6.70 vs 6.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) for its transformer backbone, in-context conditioning, and AdaLN-Zero mechanism, and on DDGAN (Xiao et al., ICLR 2022) for its time-dependent discriminator training procedure. It departs from prior wavelet-based SR methods (Wavelet-SRNet, WaveFace, WFEN, WaveDM, DiWa) that use DWT with CNN backbones and treat multi-scale sub-bands independently, and from prior diffusion-based SR methods (SR3, SRDiff, ResShift, SinSR) that operate entirely in the pixel domain. The key conceptual departure is formulating SR as denoising in a multi-level wavelet spectra space with a transformer that explicitly models interrelations across frequency sub-bands, combined with a dual-decoder design to handle the distributional mismatch between smooth LF and sparse HF components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in wavelet-based SR: prior methods ignore cross-scale correlations among HF sub-bands, and the ablation (Table 7) confirms that modeling these interrelations yields a meaningful FID improvement (~5 dB). The dual-decoder with tailored attention masks is a practical architectural insight for handling heterogeneous frequency distributions within a single diffusion framework, and the pyramid tokenization offers a ~33% token reduction over uniform patching. Limitations include the fixed Haar wavelet choice, the coupling of wavelet level to upscaling factor (J = ceil(log₂N)), and evaluation limited to standard SR benchmarks; whether the approach extends to other restoration tasks (inpainting, denoising) or benefits from learned wavelets remains open.
