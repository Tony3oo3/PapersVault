## Problem

Gradient projection methods for continual learning (GPM, TRGP, CUBER, SGP) prevent catastrophic forgetting by constraining updates to orthogonal subspaces of prior task feature spaces, but this rigidly freezes shared directions between correlated tasks, blocking forward knowledge transfer. Existing relaxations—coarse task-level trust regions (TRGP), per-task gradient correlation (CUBER), or uniform scaling (SGP)—lack fine-grained adaptability to selectively reuse only the most relevant shared directions. The paper asks whether a regularized, soft-projection framework can simultaneously mitigate forgetting and promote transfer by identifying and exploiting individual overlapping directions rather than entire task subspaces.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a task-incremental supervised setting on RGB images (32×32 for Split CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Split miniImageNet, 28×28 for the 5-Datasets benchmark) with no data augmentation. Each task has its own classification head in a multi-head AlexNet (5-layer) or ResNet-18 architecture, and the model outputs a class label from the task-specific head. Unusually, the method also consumes intermediate layer-wise input activations (b samples per layer) to compute conceptor matrices that encode the geometry of each layer's feature space, which drives both the gradient constraint and the forward-transfer mechanism.

## Method

CODE-CL replaces the hard SVD-based subspace of GPM with conceptor matrices—regularized reconstruction matrices whose singular values lie in (0,1) and act as soft importance weights over feature-space directions. Before learning task t, the method computes a pre-conceptor from current-task inputs and intersects it (via the conceptor AND operation) with the accumulated conceptor C^{t-1} to identify shared directions U*; if the capacity ratio exceeds a threshold ε, the effective weights become W^eff = W + W·U_{:,1:K}·M·U_{:,1:K}^⊤, where M is a learnable K×K matrix that controls how much the model exploits those shared directions. During training, gradients are projected onto the pseudo-orthogonal complement (I − C^{t-1}), preventing interference with prior knowledge while the M matrix is jointly optimized to promote forward transfer along the identified common directions. After each task, the post-training conceptor is merged with the accumulated conceptor via the OR operation (minimal enclosing ellipsoid), progressively building a richer representation of all learned feature subspaces.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-100, CODE-CL achieves 77.21% average accuracy with -1.1% BWT, outperforming the next-best method SGP (75.69%) by 1.52 points and approaching the multitask upper bound of 79.58%. On Split MiniImageNet, CODE-CL reaches 71.16% ACC (exceeding the multitask baseline of 69.46%), and on 5-Datasets it attains 93.51% ACC with only -0.11% BWT. In terms of relative forward transfer (FWT) over the GPM baseline, CODE-CL achieves 5.92% on Split CIFAR-100, 4.17% on Split MiniImageNet, and 1.82% on 5-Datasets, consistently surpassing all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODE-CL outperforms all 12 compared baselines on average accuracy across all three benchmarks. On Split CIFAR-100, it leads SGP (75.69%) by 1.52 points and DFGP (74.59%) by 2.62 points; on Split MiniImageNet it surpasses DFGP (69.92%) by 1.24 points and SGP (68.50%) by 2.66 points; on 5-Datasets it exceeds TRGP (92.81%) by 0.70 points. In terms of backward transfer, CODE-CL's BWT of -1.1% on both Split CIFAR-100 and Split MiniImageNet is comparable to TRGP (-0.1%) and CUBER (+0.1%), while its -0.11% on 5-Datasets matches TRGP's -0.1%. For forward transfer, CODE-CL achieves the highest relative FWT on every benchmark (5.92%, 4.17%, 1.82%), beating the next-best SGP (4.74%, 3.37%, 0.33%) by 1.18, 0.80, and 1.49 points respectively. Older regularization and replay methods (EWC, HAT, A-GEM, ER_Res, OWM) trail by 5–25 points in accuracy, confirming that gradient-projection-based approaches remain the strongest family for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODE-CL repurposes conceptor matrices—originally developed by Jaeger (2014) for controlling recurrent neural networks—as a regularized reconstruction framework that naturally yields pseudo-orthogonal projection operators, replacing the strict SVD-based orthogonality used in GPM. Within the gradient-projection family for continual learning, it departs from TRGP and CUBER (which relax constraints at the coarse task-subspace level) and SGP (which applies uniform scaling) by performing fine-grained, direction-level selection of shared basis vectors between tasks via the conceptor AND operation. The method thus sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads: the conceptor algebra for subspace manipulation and the gradient-projection literature for continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing hard orthogonality with aperture-controlled pseudo-orthogonality and adaptively releasing only the top-K most relevant shared directions, CODE-CL offers a principled knob (α) to trade off stability against plasticity, which prior projection methods handled only heuristically. The consistent ~1–2% accuracy and FWT gains across three benchmarks and two architectures, with memory complexity comparable to GPM/SGP, make it a practical upgrade for practitioners already using gradient-projection CL. Open questions include whether the approach extends to class-incremental or domain-incremental settings, scales to larger backbones (e.g., ViTs), and how the aperture α should be scheduled across long task sequences rather than fixed per dataset.
