## Problem

Visual Language Models lack reliable step-by-step reasoning capabilities in telecommunications, a domain characterized by specialized terminology, circuit diagrams, and waveform plots. Existing telecom datasets are text-only, and the state-of-the-art process annotation method (MATH-SHEPHERD) fails to produce useful labels for telecom problems due to sparse positive samples, leaving no multimodal dataset with step-level correctness scores to train process reward models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251651/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a textual question with a visual image (circuit diagrams, waveform plots, etc.) and produces a sequence of n reasoning steps (averaging ~10 per problem across five telecom sub-domains), each annotated with a correctness score in [0,1], plus a final textual solution. The TC-NAVIGATOR verifier consumes the question, image, and a partial reasoning prefix z_{1...i} to output a per-step score, while the ApPA annotation pipeline takes raw (Q, A, V) triples extracted via Mathpix and generates K reasoning paths per question through MCTS exploration with configurable tree width M and iteration count T.

## Method

The core contribution combines two mechanisms: (1) ApPA, a plan-based MCTS annotation method that first summarizes a high-level 'plan' ψ_i from each gold reasoning step, then uses these plans to guide VLM expansion in the MCTS tree, assigning step-level scores via backpropagation of final-answer correctness and filtering false positives/negatives; and (2) TC-NAVIGATOR, a verifier architecture built on QA-ViT with two reasoning-aware modules—RA1 projects the question and reasoning prefix into compact step-level embeddings for efficient multi-step attention, while RA2 encodes the current step at token level via a pre-trained LLM—both fusing with visual features through QA-ViT's gated self-attention to produce step-aware visual representations. The verifier is trained as either an ORM or PRM via cross-entropy on step scores, and can additionally reinforce generator VLMs through step-by-step PPO, with the key insight that process supervision (scoring each step) substantially outperforms outcome supervision, especially as reasoning length increases.

## Results

On the RMultiplex200K testbed (2000 problems), the best VLM Qwen-2-VL-72B achieves 48.5% accuracy with TC-NAVIGATOR process supervision plus self-consistency (TC+SC), up from 21.3% zero-shot CoT and 42.1% fine-tuning with SC alone. The best LLM, Llama-3.2-90B, reaches 46.1% with TC+SC versus 19.8% zero-shot. Process supervision (TC) consistently outperforms outcome supervision (ORM) by 1.85–4.58% across models, and the gap widens for problems requiring more reasoning steps (e.g., +4.3% in the 12-step CSP category vs. +2.2% in the 8-step SC category).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-NAVIGATOR (process supervision) outperforms the ORM (outcome supervision) baseline by 1.85% on average across all models, with the largest gains for multi-step problems (e.g., Qwen-2-VL-72B: 46.8% TC vs. 44.5% ORM). Against the MATH-SHEPHERD annotation method (TC-SHEPHERD), TC-NAVIGATOR trained on RMultiplex200K yields substantially higher accuracy—Qwen-2-VL-72B drops by over 6% when the verifier is trained on TC-SHEPHERD instead, and Qwen-VL-7B-Chat falls below its zero-shot CoT baseline. Self-consistency (SC, majority voting over 100 solutions) provides only ~2% improvement over CoT prompting without fine-tuning, whereas combining fine-tuning with TC+SC yields 25+ percentage-point gains over zero-shot. VLMs consistently outperform text-only LLMs by 15–20% under identical settings, confirming the importance of visual information for Telecom problems. The ablation in Table 2 shows that removing RA1 or RA2 from TC-NAVIGATOR degrades accuracy by 0.4–1.1%, with RA2 more critical for process supervision (PRM) and RA1 more critical for outcome supervision (ORM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process reward model (PRM) paradigm established in mathematics (PRM800K, MATH-SHEPHERD) to the multimodal Telecom domain, where no prior work has addressed step-level supervision. The annotation mechanism ApPA is a plan-based extension of MATH-SHEPHERD's MCTS framework, adding a plan-generation stage to guide reasoning-step creation, while the TC-NAVIGATOR verifier architecture builds directly on QA-ViT's question-aware visual attention, adapting it for step-level scoring. The key conceptual departure is that existing Telecom datasets are text-only and that the SOTA math annotation method (MATH-SHEPHERD) produces too few positive samples for Telecom, necessitating a domain-specific annotation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RMultiplex200K is the first large-scale multimodal dataset with step-level correctness labels for Telecom, enabling process-supervised training of VLMs in a domain where even GPT-4o achieves only ~22% accuracy. The finding that MATH-SHEPHERD's annotation does not transfer to Telecom underscores that domain-specific annotation strategies are necessary, not optional, for process supervision in specialized fields. Open questions include the interpretability of the verifier's adaptive attention shifts across image regions, whether the ~48% ceiling reflects a fundamental difficulty in Telecom reasoning or a data/method limitation, and whether ApPA's plan-based MCTS generalizes to other expert-heavy domains (e.g., medicine, law) where human annotation is similarly infeasible.
