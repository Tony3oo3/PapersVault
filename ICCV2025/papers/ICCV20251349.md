## Problem

LUT-based super-resolution methods achieve extremely efficient inference but are restricted to fixed scale factors, while arbitrary-scale SR (ASISR) methods based on implicit neural representations incur tera-scale MACs and hundreds of MB of storage, making them impractical for resource-constrained deployment. The recent LUT-based ASISR approach LeRF partially bridges this gap but relies on a single class of steerable interpolation kernels, limiting its ability to capture diverse local textures and scale variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251349/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-resolution RGB image of arbitrary size and a continuous target scale factor (which may be non-integer and anisotropic, e.g., ×1.5×2.0) and produces a corresponding high-resolution super-resolved image. At inference, no neural network is executed; the LR pixel values and scale factor are used purely as indices into precomputed LUTs, with tetrahedral interpolation for non-sampled points, enabling fast CPU-only processing (e.g., 2.73 s for a 1280×720 ×2 output on a 16-core CPU).

## Method

IM-Net learns a pixel-wise interpolation mixing strategy with three components: a weight predictor that outputs K per-pixel weights for K interpolation methods (e.g., nearest, bilinear, bicubic, Lanczos) based on local LR content, a scale modulator that encodes the target scale via sinusoidal functions into a K-dimensional vector to make the weights scale-adaptive, and a refiner that post-processes the weighted sum of interpolated images. Training uses a reconstruction loss plus a novel guidance loss that supervises the weight maps with pseudo-GT weights computed via a temperature softmax over per-interpolation L1 errors against the GT image. After training, all three neural components are converted to LUTs (4D for the weight predictor and refiner, 1D for the scale modulator) using uniform input quantization and MuLUT-style hierarchical indexing with rotation ensemble, yielding IM-LUT that performs ASISR entirely through LUT lookups and basic interpolation at inference.

## Results

On Set5, IM-LUT (NLC) achieves 36.20 dB PSNR for ×2, 32.40 dB for ×3, and 30.19 dB for ×4 SR, outperforming the best LUT-based ASISR method LeRF-G (35.62/31.95/30.05 dB) while using only 360.93 KB of storage versus 1.67 MB. Across all four benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, BSDS100, Urban100) and all tested scale factors (×1.5 to ×4, including non-integer and anisotropic), IM-LUT consistently surpasses LeRF by 0.3–0.6 dB PSNR. Compared to neural network-based ASISR methods (LIIF, MetaSR), IM-LUT trades 1.5–2.5 dB PSNR for a ~28,000× reduction in MACs (225.76M vs. 6.34T) and ~700× reduction in storage (360.93 KB vs. 255.76 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional interpolation methods (Nearest, Bilinear, Bicubic, Lanczos3), IM-LUT (NLC) wins by 1.5–2.5 dB PSNR across all datasets and scales, confirming that learned mixing substantially outperforms any single fixed kernel. Against the closest LUT-based ASISR competitor LeRF (both LeRF-L and LeRF-G variants), IM-LUT achieves higher PSNR at every tested scale factor and on all four datasets (e.g., +0.58 dB on Set5 ×2, +0.58 dB on Urban100 ×2), while also reducing storage from 1.67 MB to 360.93 KB. Against fixed-scale LUT methods (MuLUT, SPF-LUT), IM-LUT scores slightly lower PSNR (e.g., 36.20 vs. 36.65 for MuLUT on Set5 ×2) but supports arbitrary scales with a single model, whereas those methods require separate LUTs per scale. Against neural network-based ASISR methods (LIIF, MetaSR), IM-LUT loses 1.5–2.5 dB PSNR (e.g., 36.20 vs. 38.08 on Set5 ×2) but requires only 225.76M MACs and 360.93 KB storage versus 6.34T MACs and 255.76 MB for LIIF, making it ~28,000× lighter in computation and ~700× smaller in storage. In terms of runtime on CPU, IM-LUT (NLC) completes ×2 SR of a 1280×720 image in 2.73 s, roughly 2× faster than LeRF-G (5.51 s) and ~18× faster than LIIF (48.33 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: LUT-based SR (SR-LUT, MuLUT, SPF-LUT) and arbitrary-scale SR (MetaSR, LIIF, LeRF). It directly builds on LeRF's insight of using LUTs to parameterize interpolation for ASISR, but departs from LeRF's single-kernel steerable resampling by instead learning pixel-wise mixing weights across multiple distinct interpolation functions (nearest, bilinear, bicubic, Lanczos). The LUT transfer mechanism, rotation ensemble, and hierarchical indexing are inherited from SR-LUT and MuLUT, while the sinusoidal scale encoding follows EQSR. The novel architectural contribution is the interpolation mixing network (IM-Net) with its weight predictor, scale modulator, and refiner, plus a temperature-softmax guidance loss that supervises weight maps using pseudo-GT derived from interpolation errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IM-LUT offers a practically compelling solution for resource-constrained ASISR: 360 KB storage and sub-3-second CPU inference versus 255 MB and 48 seconds for LIIF, while outperforming the prior LUT-based ASISR method (LeRF) by ~0.5–0.6 dB PSNR. The acknowledged limitation—that quality is inherently bounded by the interpolation kernels used—leaves open the question of whether richer or learned interpolation bases could close the ~2 dB gap to INR methods without sacrificing the LUT efficiency. For practitioners targeting embedded or CPU-only deployment, this paper provides a clear, well-documented recipe for network-free ASISR with minimal storage, making it a useful reference point for the efficiency-quality tradeoff in super-resolution.
