## Problem

Current VLA models depend on external LLMs or VLMs for high-level planning rather than developing their own implicit reasoning, limiting their robustness and generalizability in multi-task, complex environments. This paper asks whether a robot policy can generate its own task-specific reasoning—structured as a sequential chain of robot affordances—before predicting actions, analogous to how OpenAI's O1 model leverages reasoning chains for complex problem-solving.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (two ZED third-person cameras plus a Realsense D435i wrist camera), a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive state (joint angles encoded as a single token). At inference, it first produces intermediate chain-of-affordance reasoning in two complementary formats—structured text descriptions and pixel-aligned visual overlays (bounding boxes, grasp keypoints, spatial coordinates, and motion trajectories rendered on observation frames)—before the diffusion head outputs a continuous action chunk. A dynamic selection mechanism, driven by proprioception, suppresses redundant affordances (e.g., object and grasp affordances after pickup) to reduce per-timestep computation.

## Method

CoA-VLA builds on DiffusionVLA (Qwen2-VL backbone + diffusion action head) and introduces a four-stage affordance chain—object, grasp, spatial, and movement—each expressed in both textual and visual prompting formats. A visual-textual co-injection module fuses the two modalities: textual affordances are tokenized via the VLM's final embedding plus an MLP, while visual affordance overlays are encoded as patch tokens by a pretrained ViT-Small; the fused tokens pass through two Transformer blocks and are injected into the diffusion denoising process via FiLM conditioning layers. An automated data-generation pipeline (GPT-4o for scene/entity description, Grounding DINOv2 + SAM for bounding boxes, RoboPoint for spatial points, CoTracker for gripper trajectories) produces large-scale affordance annotations, and ChatGPT paraphrases textual affordances to prevent template overfitting. The model is pre-trained on 39K Droid trajectories with synthetic affordance data, then post-trained on 692 trajectories across seven real-world Franka arm tasks.

## Results

On seven real-world Franka robot arm tasks, CoA-VLA achieves an 85.54% average success rate in the in-distribution setting, outperforming DiffusionVLA (76.60%), OpenVLA (54.89%), Octo (44.13%), and Diffusion Policy (42.93%). Under visual generalization (distractors, vibrant backgrounds), CoA-VLA reaches 57.14% versus 44.44% for DiffusionVLA and 22.22% for OpenVLA. On the LIBERO benchmark, CoA-VLA attains a 79.8% average success rate across four task suites, surpassing OpenVLA (76.5%), Octo (75.1%), ScaleDP (72.9%), and Diffusion Policy (72.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoA-VLA outperforms all five baselines on both the real-robot and LIBERO evaluations. Against its direct backbone DiffusionVLA (same architecture, vanilla reasoning), CoA-VLA gains +8.94% in-distribution (85.54% vs 76.60%) and +12.7% under visual generalization (57.14% vs 44.44%) on the Franka arm tasks. The gap widens dramatically against autoregressive VLA baselines: CoA-VLA exceeds OpenVLA by 30.65% in-distribution and 34.92% under visual generalization, despite using a smaller model and less pre-training data. On LIBERO, CoA-VLA leads OpenVLA by 3.3% (79.8% vs 76.5%) and Octo by 4.7%, with the largest margin on LIBERO-Object (+4.7%) and LIBERO-Goal (+6.6%). In qualitative obstacle-avoidance tests, CoA-VLA succeeds in all three scenarios while OpenVLA fails all three and DiffusionVLA succeeds in only one, highlighting the benefit of explicit movement affordance reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' own DiffusionVLA architecture (Qwen2-VL + diffusion action head) and reframes the VLA reasoning problem through the lens of robot affordances, a concept long studied in manipulation (RT-Affordance, RoboPoint, TraceVLA) but previously treated as isolated prediction targets rather than a structured reasoning chain. It departs from prior VLA systems that outsource high-level planning to external LLMs/VLMs by embedding a four-stage affordance chain (object → grasp → spatial → movement) as intermediate outputs within the policy model itself, drawing conceptual inspiration from chain-of-thought prompting in LLMs (OpenAI O1, CoT-VLA, ECoT). The visual-textual co-injection module with FiLM conditioning is a new architectural contribution that unifies pixel-aligned visual cues and natural-language affordance descriptions in a shared embedding space fed to the diffusion policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that structured, task-specific reasoning (rather than generic language generation) can meaningfully improve VLA robustness and generalization, particularly in visually challenging or cluttered settings where prior models degrade sharply. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 6-DoF Franka arm, seven hand-designed tasks, and small trial counts (9–11 per task), leaving open whether the affordance chain generalizes to higher-DoF platforms, longer-horizon tasks, or substantially larger task distributions. An important open question is whether the four-affordance taxonomy is sufficient for more complex manipulation (e.g., deformable objects, multi-contact interactions) or whether the chain must be dynamically restructured per task.
