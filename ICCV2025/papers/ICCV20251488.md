## Problem

Existing unified trajectory modeling methods handle prediction, imputation, and recovery by coupling observed and unobserved behaviors in a shared latent space, which contaminates accurate representations with noise from missing observations and deviates the model from the real-world behavior distribution. No prior framework explicitly uses observation masks as structural boundaries to decouple accurate from uncertain information while simultaneously addressing arbitrary levels of missing data across all three tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251488/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-agent position sequences (x_i ∈ R^{T×2} per agent) paired with binary observation masks (m_i ∈ R^T) indicating which timesteps are visible, and produces 20 candidate completed trajectories per input. The mask format is unified across prediction (future timesteps masked), imputation (interior gaps masked), and recovery (>80% masked), so a single model handles all three tasks without task-specific heads. Evaluation uses minADE₂₀ for accuracy plus four fidelity metrics (out-of-boundary rate, step-size change, path length, and inter-agent path-length difference) computed against ground truth.

## Method

UniMTD decouples observed and unobserved tokens via a Unidirectional Attention (UA) valve: a Unidirectional Masking (UM) layer zeros attention weights from masked positions so clean tokens form an uncontaminated latent space, while a Progressive Removal (PR) mechanism uses a 3-layer LSTM scorer to iteratively unmask the highest-confidence tokens layer-by-layer, projecting them into the preserved distribution. The UA is embedded in a Unidirectional MoE (UMoE) with a shared local expert for common motion knowledge and sparsely activated global experts for agent-specific behaviors, routed by a Complex Adaptive Router that allocates a dynamic number of experts based on the mask ratio. For decoding, a Cached Diffusion (CDiff) model reuses the encoder's deterministic features and coordinates as intermediate states, collapsing a standard 200-step, 6-layer DDPM into a 5-step, single-attention-layer denoising process that reduces inference time from >10 s to 0.18 s while improving generation quality.

## Results

On the three human-motion datasets, UniMTD achieves minADE₂₀ of 1.88 (Basketball-U), 13.65 (Soccer-U), and 0.91 (Football-U), surpassing the previous best UniTraj (4.77 / 94.59 / 3.55) by an averaged 74% and the diffusion-based SSSD (6.18 / 118.71 / 5.08) by 80%. On Vehicle-U, UniMTD records minADE of 13.12 / 10.62 / 15.40 across Easy / Ordinary / Hard subsets, leading all baselines. Across the four fidelity metrics (OOB, Step, Path-L, Path-D), UniMTD obtains averaged advantages of 91%, 66%, 69%, and 58% over data-driven methods on human datasets, and 56%, 33%, 44%, 15% on Vehicle-U. Inference time is 0.18 s for 11 agents, comparable to UniTraj (0.16 s) and far faster than SSSD (>10 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMTD is compared against eight baselines spanning classical (LSTM, Transformer), task-specific (MAT, NAOMI, INAM, GC-VRNN), diffusion-based (SSSD), and unified (UniTraj) methods. Against the closest competitor UniTraj [71], UniMTD reduces minADE₂₀ by 74% on average across the three human datasets (e.g., 1.88 vs. 4.77 on Basketball-U, 0.91 vs. 3.55 on Football-U) and by 56–83% on Vehicle-U subsets. The 200-step diffusion model SSSD [2] is outperformed by 80% on minADE for human data, though SSSD retains a slight edge on the Step metric for Vehicle-U (0.47–0.50 vs. 0.75–0.86), which the authors attribute to SSSD's stronger local velocity modeling in high-dynamic driving scenes. GC-VRNN [73] and INAM [52], designed for imputation/prediction subtasks, are surpassed by UniMTD on all five metrics across every dataset. The simpler baselines (LSTM, Transformer, MAT, NAOMI, Linear Fit) are outperformed by large margins (typically >50% on minADE), confirming that the decoupled unidirectional attention and cached diffusion design yield consistent gains over both traditional sequence models and single-task generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified trajectory modeling paradigm introduced by UniTraj (Xu & Fu, 2025), which first combined prediction, imputation, and recovery under a single BERT-SSM mask format, by reframing the problem from a coupled to a decoupled denoising diffusion setting. The architectural lineage draws on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) for the generative backbone and MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) for the iterative masked-token generation philosophy, but departs sharply from both by introducing a unidirectional attention valve that explicitly blocks information flow from masked to observed tokens, and by replacing the full 200-step diffusion with a 5-step cached diffusion that reuses encoder features as intermediate states. The MoE routing with a complexity-adaptive router is a novel combination of local-global expert division and mask-ratio-dependent expert allocation, not directly inherited from any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle—treating observation masks as hard boundaries between accurate and uncertain latent spaces rather than soft conditions—offers a general design insight applicable to any generative task with partial observations, potentially extending beyond trajectories to video inpainting or sensor fusion. The cached diffusion reducing 200 steps to 5 while improving quality is a practically significant efficiency gain for real-time multi-agent systems. However, all four evaluation datasets are constructed by the authors following prior protocols, limiting external validation; the twin-encoder design and MoE routing add architectural complexity that may be hard to transfer; and the method has not been tested on truly open-world driving or large-scale crowd scenarios where agent counts and scene dynamics differ substantially from sports courts.
