## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods require a predefined vocabulary set at inference time, which limits generalization to unseen scenarios, prevents disambiguation of compositional concepts (e.g., 'bat' as animal vs. sports implement), and restricts semantic expressiveness to fixed labels. This paper reformulates OVSS as a generative text task, eliminating the need for any predefined vocabulary by directly producing category names and descriptions for segmented regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250284/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces, for each semantic region, a segmentation mask (from a plug-in mask extractor such as SAM or a MaskFormer-style module) paired with generated text: a short category name and a longer natural-language description. Because the model consumes binarized masks as attention masks inside CLIP's self-attention layers, it is agnostic to the upstream segmentation model and can be attached to any mask-producing backbone without retraining. At inference, beam search (size 3) yields multiple candidate labels per region to balance accuracy and diversity.

## Method

CRTNet operates in two training stages. First, it adapts CLIP's frozen ViT-B/16 image encoder to extract mask-level proposal features by feeding binarized masks (N=100) as attention masks into the last three transformer layers, where repeated [CLS] tokens aggregate local visual information per mask. Second, it makes these proposal features text-aware: for image-level captions it computes CLIP text–proposal similarity scores and aggregates features via a softmax-weighted sum; for region-level annotations (segmentation or detection) it performs IoU-based bipartite matching to select the correct proposal. A residual two-layer projection module then maps the text-aware features into FlanT5-Base's embedding space, where template-prompted generation ('identify the category: [TAF]' / 'describe the region: [TAF]') produces the final text under standard language-modeling loss. A data engine integrates COCO-Stuff, Visual Genome, and CC3M-595K, using spaCy noun-phrase extraction and CLIP-similarity-based filtering to convert image-level captions into region-level training pairs.

## Results

On the five OVSS benchmarks (VOC, P-59, A-150, P-459, A-847), CRTNet with a trained LM achieves mIoU of 91.4, 49.9, 23.6, 15.1, and 7.9 respectively, without requiring any predefined vocabulary at inference. This yields a 13.7% average mIoU improvement over the best vocabulary-free baselines (e.g., +34.5% over TAG on VOC, +14.1% over FOSSIL on P-59). When trained solely on CC3M caption data, the model still attains 84.7 mIoU on VOC and 7.2 on A-847, outperforming all caption-data-only methods by 6.9% on average. The model also integrates with SAM without retraining, producing accurate category labels and descriptions for arbitrary masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against predefined-vocabulary methods, CRTNet (train LM) outperforms LSeg+ (59.0→91.4 on VOC), ZegFormer (85.6→91.4), SimSeg (88.4→91.4), and DeOP (91.7→91.4, roughly tied) on VOC, and surpasses DeOP on P-459 (9.4→15.1) and A-847 (7.1→7.9), though it trails the strongest methods (SAN, EBSeg, SED) by 2–7 mIoU on VOC and P-59. The critical advantage emerges in the no-vocabulary setting: CRTNet beats TAG by +34.5 mIoU on VOC (56.9→91.4), FOSSIL by +14.1 on P-59 (35.8→49.9), and FOSSIL by +4.8 on A-150 (18.8→23.6), while the LLaVA-based baseline is outperformed by 34.9 mIoU on VOC. When SAN is re-evaluated with a unified vocabulary (simulating real-world deployment), its performance drops substantially (e.g., 94.0→83.6 on VOC), and CRTNet still leads by an average of 5.9 mIoU across VOC, P-59, and P-459. In the caption-data-only comparison (Table 2), CRTNet trained on CC3M-595K achieves 84.7 mIoU on VOC, exceeding the next-best method (OpenSeg at 63.8) by 20.9 points. For semantic expressiveness (Table 10), CRTNet+Llama2 attains the highest Cover score (61.4) and the best Attribute F1 (74.1) among all compared MLLMs including InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, and mPLUG-Owl2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-based OVSS lineage (SimSeg, ZegFormer, DeOP, SAN) and the MaskFormer architecture for mask proposal extraction, but fundamentally departs by replacing the classification/retrieval paradigm with a generative region-to-text formulation. Where prior methods require a predefined vocabulary set at inference to match proposal features against text embeddings, CRTNet instead trains a language model (FlanT5-Base) to directly generate category names and descriptions from text-aware visual features. The text-aware feature adaptation—using CLIP text embeddings to aggregate or select among semantically agnostic proposal features via bipartite matching—is a novel bridge between the frozen CLIP encoder and the generative LM, addressing the challenge that proposal features carry no semantic identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the predefined vocabulary requirement, CRTNet addresses a core practical limitation of all prior OVSS methods: the inability to handle unseen scenarios, compositional concepts, or diverse real-world descriptions without manual vocabulary curation. The seamless integration with SAM as a plug-and-play labeling tool (without retraining) and the demonstrated utility in creating instruction-following datasets for MLLM training suggest broad practical impact beyond benchmark mIoU. Open questions include controlling semantic granularity (e.g., 'golden retriever' vs. 'dog'), resolving SAM's over-segmentation for OVSS, and whether the approach scales to even larger language models or more diverse training data without the current parsing/filtering pipeline.
