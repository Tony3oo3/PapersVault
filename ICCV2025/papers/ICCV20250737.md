## Problem

Diffusion-based dense prediction versatilists (e.g., DDP) assume training and inference data follow an i.i.d. distribution, yet in practice they must generalize to unseen domains within each task. While domain-generalized diffusion methods exist, they are typically designed for a single dense vision task and have not been systematically extended to a multi-task versatilist setting. This paper asks how to make a single diffusion model robustly generalize across unseen domains for multiple dense pixel tasks simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250737/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×1024 for segmentation, 640×480 for depth, or 512×512 for dehazing) and produces a per-pixel prediction map: a 19-class segmentation mask, a continuous-valued depth map, or a dehazed RGB image, depending on the task. A key operational detail is that during inference the model also consumes the DHT high-frequency band of the *target-domain* input image as an additional conditioning signal, so the model is not purely feed-forward on the raw image alone. The encoder (ConvNeXt-L) runs once per image, and its FPN-fused features at 1/4 resolution (256×H/4×W/4) drive the entire diffusion process.

## Method

HarDiff builds on the DDP diffusion framework and introduces a frequency-guided mechanism using the Discrete Hartley Transform (DHT), chosen over FFT because it retains all spectral components in the real domain and permits direct band-wise arithmetic. A band-rejection analysis on ConvNeXt features reveals that the lowest 10% DHT band (V^[90%,100%]) encodes broad structural content (object positions, shapes) while the highest 10% band (V^[0,10%]) encodes fine details (textures, edges). During training, the low-frequency band of the source-domain image features is added to the ground-truth label before the forward noising step, biasing the diffusion to learn task-related structural priors. During inference, the high-frequency band of the *unseen target-domain* image features is injected into each DDIM sampling step, guiding the reverse process to recover target-specific fine details. The decoder is a six-layer deformable-attention Transformer that maps the denoised latent to the final dense prediction, and the whole pipeline is trained end-to-end with a task-specific loss (cross-entropy for segmentation, L1 for depth/dehazing).

## Results

On domain-generalized semantic segmentation (SYNTHIA→C/B/M), HarDiff achieves 57.8% average mIoU, outperforming the second-best method tqdm (55.1%) by 2.7% and the DDP baseline (54.7%) by 3.1%. On the GTA5→C/B/M protocol, it reaches 63.2% average mIoU, surpassing CLOUDS (61.5%) by 1.7%. For depth estimation (NYU-DepthV2→5 targets), HarDiff attains the best δ₁ on all five unseen domains (e.g., 0.901 on Virtual KITTI 2 vs. 0.881 for DME-GT), and for haze removal (RESIDE→4 targets) it achieves the highest PSNR/SSIM on 6 out of 8 test sets (e.g., 39.76/0.99 on SOTS-IN vs. 39.03/0.97 for DiffLI²D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HarDiff consistently outperforms all 40 compared baselines across the three dense vision tasks. In semantic segmentation (Table 1), it beats the strongest VFM-based method tqdm [54] by 2.7% average mIoU (57.8 vs. 55.1) and the strongest diffusion-based method DIDEX [53] by 2.2% (57.8 vs. 55.6); on the GTA5 protocol (Table 2) it surpasses CLOUDS [2] by 1.7% and the DDP baseline [33] by 2.5%. For depth estimation (Table 3), HarDiff achieves the best δ₁ on all five target domains, exceeding DME-GT [88] by 2.0% on Virtual KITTI 2 (0.901 vs. 0.881) and by 1.6% on DIODE-O (0.524 vs. 0.508), while also yielding the lowest RMSE (e.g., 2.406 vs. 3.943 on Virtual KITTI 2). For haze removal (Table 4), it leads on 6 of 8 test sets, e.g., 39.76/0.99 PSNR/SSIM on SOTS-IN (indoor source) vs. 39.03/0.97 for DiffLI²D [82] and 38.90/0.98 for DCMPNet [95]. The ablation (Table 5) confirms both the low-frequency training process (+1.5–1.7% mIoU) and high-frequency sampling process (+0.5–1.2% mIoU) contribute independently, with the full combination yielding the largest gain over the DDP baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

HarDiff builds directly on the DDP diffusion framework for dense visual prediction (Ji et al., ICCV 2023), adopting its label-conditioned diffusion formulation, DDIM sampling, and deformable-attention decoder, while introducing a frequency-guided modification via the Discrete Hartley Transform. It departs from prior frequency-based domain generalization work (which predominantly uses FFT, DCT, or Haar wavelets) by showing that DHT retains all frequency components in the real domain and permits direct band-wise integration, simplifying the pipeline. Conceptually, it extends the line of domain-generalized diffusion methods (PTDiffSeg, DIDEX, CLOUDS, DGInStyle) that are each tailored to a single dense task, positioning itself as a task-agnostic versatilist that plugs the same low-frequency training / high-frequency sampling scheme into segmentation, depth estimation, and dehazing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most diffusion-based dense predictors assume i.i.d. source and target distributions, yet real deployments inevitably encounter domain shift. By demonstrating that a single frequency-decomposition strategy (low-freq structural priors at training, high-freq detail guidance at sampling) consistently improves generalization across three heterogeneous dense tasks and twelve benchmarks, it offers a lightweight, encoder-agnostic module that practitioners can adopt without redesigning the diffusion backbone. Open questions include whether the fixed 10% band thresholds are optimal across all tasks and scales, whether the approach extends to video or 3D dense prediction, and whether the DHT advantage over FFT persists at higher resolutions where complex-number overhead becomes negligible.
