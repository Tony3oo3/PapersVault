## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) compresses pre-trained diffusion models into one-step or few-step generators, but its reliance on reverse KL divergence minimization induces mode collapse by driving low-probability regions to zero. Existing regularizers (ODE-based, GAN-based) merely counterbalance this effect rather than addressing the root cause, and all score distillation methods are constrained by a predefined explicit divergence metric. The paper asks whether an implicit, data-driven discrepancy measure learned adversarially can enable more flexible and fine-grained matching of complex high-dimensional distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and random Gaussian noise as input, producing a generated image (1024×1024 for SDXL/SD3 models) or video (CogVideoX) in as few as 1 NFE (one-step) or 4–8 NFE (multi-step), compared to 50 NFE for the teacher. No visual training data is required—only text prompts from JourneyDB (images) or OpenVid1M/Vript/Open-Sora-Plan (videos). The generator operates in VAE latent space, with the pixel-space discriminator in the pre-training stage decoding latents back to pixel space for additional supervision.

## Method

ADM replaces the DMD loss with a GAN-based adversarial discriminator built from a frozen diffusion model backbone (UNet or DiT) with multiple trainable heads that hierarchically aggregate features. Instead of comparing score estimator endpoints at x_0, the discriminator evaluates predictions at timestep t−Δt obtained by solving the PF-ODE for a small fixed interval, preserving noise-level information. The Hinge GAN loss is theoretically shown to minimize Total Variation Distance, which is symmetric and bounded in [0,1], avoiding the mode-seeking and gradient instability of reverse KL. For one-step distillation, a separate Adversarial Distillation Pre-training (ADP) stage uses hybrid discriminators in both latent space (teacher-initialized) and pixel space (SAM vision encoder) on ODE-pair synthetic data with a cubic generator timestep schedule, providing better support-set overlap before ADM fine-tuning. The unified DMDX pipeline (ADP + ADM) achieves one-step SDXL generation at 50× acceleration with superior fidelity and diversity over DMD2.

## Results

On one-step SDXL-Base distillation (1 NFE), DMDX achieves CLIP 35.26, PickScore 22.27, HPSv2 27.70, and MPS 11.20, surpassing DMD2 (35.22/22.10/27.45/10.69) and even the 50-NFE SDXL-Base teacher (35.03/22.25/27.37/10.70) on three of four metrics. For multi-step distillation, 4-step ADM on SD3-Medium reaches CLIP 34.91, PickScore 22.55, HPSv2 28.45, MPS 11.95, exceeding the 50-NFE teacher (34.76/22.30/27.97/11.37). On video, 8-step ADM on CogVideoX-2b achieves VBench Final 82.07 vs. the 200-NFE teacher's 80.04, a 92% speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMDX outperforms all one-step baselines on SDXL-Base: it beats DMD2 by +0.04 CLIP, +0.18 PickScore, +0.25 HPSv2, and +0.50 MPS, and surpasses the 50-NFE SDXL-Base teacher on PickScore (+0.02), HPSv2 (+0.33), and MPS (+0.49) while trailing by only −0.02 on CLIP. ADD and Lightning are clearly inferior (e.g., ADD scores 10.43 vs. 11.20 MPS). For multi-step distillation on SD3-Medium, 4-step ADM (CLIP 34.91, HPSv2 28.45, MPS 11.95) dominates TSCD (34.02/27.27/10.86), all three PCM variants (≈33.4–33.5 CLIP), Flash (34.40/27.26/10.66), and LADD (34.74/27.49/11.44), and even exceeds the 50-NFE SD3-Medium teacher. On SD3.5-Large, ADM (34.97/22.88/27.73/12.24) beats the teacher (34.97/22.51/27.97/11.58) on PickScore and MPS but trails slightly on HPSv2. For video, 8-step ADM on CogVideoX-2b (VBench Final 82.07) surpasses the 200-NFE teacher (80.04) by +2.03, while on CogVideoX-5b it is marginally below the teacher (80.98 vs. 81.23 Final) but still achieves 96% speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2) line of work by replacing the reverse KL divergence–based score distillation loss with an adversarial discriminator that implicitly learns a data-driven discrepancy measure between real and fake score estimators. The adversarial distillation pre-training stage (ADP) draws from LADD's synthetic-data GAN approach and Rectified Flow's ODE-pair collection strategy, while the hybrid latent+pixel discriminator design is novel. The key conceptual departure from DMD2 is that the GAN loss is not an auxiliary regularizer to counterbalance mode collapse (as in DMD2's GAN term) but the primary distribution-matching objective itself, motivated by the theoretical observation that Hinge GAN converges to Total Variation Distance, which is symmetric and bounded unlike reverse KL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified pipeline (DMDX) that achieves competitive 1-step image generation on SDXL and sets new benchmarks for few-step video synthesis on CogVideoX, with the added benefit of requiring no visual training data. The finding that proper initialization via adversarial pre-training renders TTUR nearly unnecessary challenges a common assumption in the DMD2 community and simplifies training. A notable limitation is that the teacher model may require CFG for accurate score prediction, restricting applicability to guidance-distilled models like FLUX.1-dev. Open questions include whether the adversarial discrepancy measure can be extended to handle guidance-distilled teachers and whether the TVD-based objective generalizes to even more extreme compression (e.g., sub-one-step or autoregressive settings).
