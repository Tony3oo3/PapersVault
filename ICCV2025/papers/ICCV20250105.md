## Problem

Existing sparse 3D lane detectors ignore well-known geometric priors about lane structure (continuity, parallelism) and fail to exploit historic lane observations that could resolve ambiguities under occlusion or poor visibility. Dense BEV-based methods, while incorporating some spatial priors, suffer from error-prone perspective transformations that misalign features with the true road surface. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously within a sparse query-based framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250105/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (720×960) and the ego-pose (homogeneous transformation matrix) for temporal propagation of past queries. It outputs N=40 lane proposals, each parameterized by M=20–30 Catmull-Rom spline control points with per-point x, z, and visibility coordinates (the longitudinal y-component is pre-defined and uniformly spaced to avoid over-parameterization), plus K+1 class probabilities per lane. Because CR splines interpolate their control points, the predicted 3D positions directly lie on the lane curve, unlike B-Spline control points which only approximate it.

## Method

SparseLaneSTP replaces the standard global self-attention in sparse lane transformers with a spatio-temporal attention (STA) module that applies three masked attention types: Same Line Attention (SLA) for intra-lane adjacent-point interactions, Parallel Neighbor Attention (PNA) for inter-lane relations, and Temporal Cross-Attention (TCA) for interactions with ego-motion-propagated queries from a FIFO memory queue of T=3 past frames. The lane representation uses Catmull-Rom splines whose control points lie exactly on the curve, making them compatible with the sparse query design where 3D positions serve as internal states. Training combines standard detection losses (L1 regression, BCE visibility, focal classification) with spatial regularization (parallelism, surface smoothness, curvature suppression) and a novel temporal consistency loss that penalizes deviation from an exponentially moving average of past predictions. A CNN backbone (ResNet-50) feeds a lane instance segmentation branch for query initialization, and deformable cross-attention links queries to image features at re-projected 2D control point locations.

## Results

On OpenLane, SparseLaneSTP achieves a 66.1% F1-Score, surpassing the second-best GroupLane (64.1%) by 2.0 points, while also attaining the lowest geometric errors across all ranges (X-error: 0.203 m near / 0.240 m far; Z-error: 0.066 m near / 0.092 m far). On ONCE-3DLanes, it reaches 82.75% F1-Score with the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.048 m), outperforming LATR (80.59%) and GroupLane (80.73%). On the authors' novel 3D lane dataset with 250 m range labels, SparseLaneSTP scores 68.2% F1-Score and 81.4% Vis-IoU, exceeding the LATR baseline (65.1% / 78.3%) and LaneCPP (63.1% / 77.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseLaneSTP outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every metric across all three benchmarks. On OpenLane, it leads the closest competitor GroupLane [22] by 2.0 F1 points (66.1 vs 64.1) and by a large margin in geometric accuracy (e.g., Z-error near: 0.066 m vs 0.233 m), while also beating the prior sparse-query state-of-the-art LATR [31] by 4.2 F1 points. Among temporal methods, it surpasses PETRv2 [30] (61.2%) and Anchor3DLane-T [16] (54.3%) by 4.9 and 11.8 F1 points respectively, demonstrating that object-centric sparse temporal fusion is more effective than BEV-based or anchor-based temporal strategies for lanes. On ONCE-3DLanes, SparseLaneSTP achieves 82.75% F1 and 0.048 m CD, edging out GroupLane (80.73% / 0.053 m) and LATR (80.59% / 0.052 m). On the authors' own long-range dataset, the 3.1 F1-point gap over the LATR baseline (68.2 vs 65.1) and the 3.1-point Vis-IoU advantage (81.4 vs 78.3) highlight the benefit of temporal propagation for occlusion-aware detection at 150–200 m range, where dense BEV methods like PersFormer (59.2%) degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse query-based 3D lane detection paradigm established by LATR and CurveFormer by integrating two previously separate lines of work: continuous spline-based lane representations (from LaneCPP and 3D-SpLineNet) and object-centric temporal fusion (from Sparse4Dv2 and StreamPETR in 3D object detection). The key conceptual departure is the design of a lane-structure-aware spatio-temporal attention that replaces global self-attention with masked intra-lane, inter-lane, and temporal cross-attention, combined with a Catmull-Rom spline representation specifically chosen so that control points lie on the curve—making them suitable as sparse query positions. The auto-labeling pipeline for the new dataset also departs from the LiDAR-based annotation used in OpenLane and ONCE-3DLanes, instead leveraging temporal accumulation of 2D detections along ego-trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that temporal information, long underutilized in 3D lane detection, provides meaningful robustness gains particularly under occlusion and poor visibility, and that a 2-layer temporal model can outperform a 6-layer non-temporal baseline at higher speed—a practically relevant efficiency finding. The new 511K-image dataset with 250m-range labels and explicit occlusion annotations addresses a real gap in existing benchmarks, which suffer from LiDAR noise in the far range. Open questions include whether the temporal mechanism extends naturally to 3D lane tracking (acknowledged as future work), how it generalizes to multi-camera or surround-view settings, and whether the auto-labeling pipeline's reliance on a 2D detector introduces systematic biases that limit label quality.
