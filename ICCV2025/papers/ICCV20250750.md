## Problem

Extreme image rescaling (16×, 32×) is highly ill-posed, and existing methods either produce overly smooth reconstructions (INN-based) or generate semantically incorrect structures and textures (GAN/VQGAN-based). The core challenge is that the spatially non-uniform degradation introduced by extreme downscaling cannot be addressed by a fixed generative prior strength, leading to a fundamental trade-off between fidelity and perceptual realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250750/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image and jointly produces a low-resolution image at the target size (H/N × W/N, where N ∈ {16, 32}) and a reconstructed high-resolution image. All rescaling operations are performed in the 4-channel latent space of a pre-trained SD 2.1 VAE (which itself applies 8× spatial downsampling), so the effective latent rescaling factor is N/8. At inference, a tiled strategy allows different regions of a large HR image to receive different denoising time-steps.

## Method

TADM decouples the rescaling operation into a Decoupled Feature Rescaling Module (DFRM) that uses CNNs for feature downscaling/upscaling and an invertible neural network for bidirectional mapping between the latent feature domain and the pixel domain, producing both the LR image and rescaled latent features. The rescaled latent features are then enhanced by a single denoising step of a pre-trained SD U-Net (fine-tuned via LoRA), treating the rescaling-induced degradation as an equivalent noise-adding process. The key novelty is a time-step alignment strategy: a lightweight Time Prediction Module (TPM) estimates an appropriate diffusion timestep from the rescaled features, and a hybrid time scheduler (combining the fixed SD scheduler with a learnable zero-initialized convolution) makes the denoising step differentiable with respect to the predicted timestep, enabling end-to-end training that adaptively allocates generative capacity per region.

## Results

On DIV2K at 16× rescaling, TADM achieves the best perceptual metrics: LPIPS 0.2979, DISTS 0.0886, MUSIQ 66.56, and CLIPIQA 0.7189, outperforming the next-best VQIR (LPIPS 0.3174, DISTS 0.1024). At 32×, TADM further extends its lead, improving DISTS by 36.76% over VQIR (0.1684 vs. 0.2663) and achieving CLIPIQA 0.7204. However, flow-based methods IRN and HCFlow retain the highest PSNR (26.04/26.66 vs. TADM's 23.98 at 16× on DIV2K) and SSIM, reflecting their pixel-fidelity advantage. On CLIC2020, TADM similarly dominates perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.2724, DISTS 0.0792 at 16×) while IRN/HCFlow lead in PSNR (28.55/28.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADM decisively outperforms all six baselines on every perceptual metric (LPIPS, DISTS, MUSIQ, CLIPIQA) across both DIV2K and CLIC2020 at both 16× and 32×. Against the closest generative competitor VQIR, TADM improves DISTS by 13.1% at 16× (0.0886 vs. 0.1024) and 36.8% at 32× (0.1684 vs. 0.2663) on DIV2K, and CLIPIQA by 13.2% at 16× (0.7189 vs. 0.6350). Flow-based rescaling methods IRN and HCFlow achieve the highest PSNR and SSIM (e.g., HCFlow 26.66 dB / 0.7176 SSIM at 16× on DIV2K vs. TADM's 23.98 / 0.6736), but their perceptual scores are substantially worse (LPIPS 0.4885 vs. 0.2979), confirming the paper's argument that pixel-fidelity and perceptual quality are in tension for extreme rescaling. SR-based methods (ESRGAN, StableSR, S3Diff) perform poorly overall because they cannot jointly optimize downscaling and upscaling; S3Diff achieves the best no-reference scores among SR methods (MUSIQ 64.37 at 16×) but still trails TADM by a wide margin on reference-based perceptual metrics. Inference efficiency is moderate: TADM takes 0.711 s and 2.598 GB on an RTX 3090, faster than StableSR (135.2 s, 6.38 GB) and S3Diff (2.186 s, 5.025 GB) but slower than IRN/HCFlow (~0.36 s, <0.5 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

TADM carves new ground at the intersection of extreme image rescaling and diffusion-model-based restoration. It departs from prior rescaling methods (IRN, HCFlow) that operate in pixel space by performing rescaling in the latent space of a pretrained SD VAE, and from prior generative-prior rescaling methods (VQIR, GRAIN) that rely on VQGAN or StyleGAN by leveraging the richer natural-image priors of a text-to-image diffusion model. The decoupled feature rescaling module (DFRM) draws on invertible neural networks (FGRN, IRN) but restructures the pipeline to separate feature rescaling from LR image generation, resolving a tension between reconstruction fidelity and visual quality that prior encoder-decoder and INN approaches leave unresolved. The one-step denoising strategy follows the line of work on distilling multi-step diffusion into single-step inference (ResShift, SinSR, OSEDiff), but the adaptive time-step alignment mechanism is a novel contribution not present in prior restoration or rescaling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TADM addresses a practically important gap: extreme rescaling factors (16×, 32×) needed for ultra-HR media storage and transmission, where existing methods produce semantically incorrect structures. The time-step alignment strategy—dynamically allocating generative capacity based on local reconstruction quality—is a transferable idea that could benefit other degradation-aware restoration tasks. However, the method's upper bound is inherently constrained by the VAE's reconstruction quality (Table 4 shows the VAE alone lags well behind 4× rescaling methods), limiting applicability to smaller rescaling factors. Future work on stronger VAEs (SDXL, SD3) and distillation for further efficiency gains are natural next steps, and the tiled inference approach for spatially varying time-steps opens questions about optimal patch-level scheduling strategies.
