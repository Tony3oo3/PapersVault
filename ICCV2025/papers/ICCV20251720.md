## Problem

Synthesizing medically realistic images that preserve anatomical fidelity while accurately modeling diffuse pathologies (e.g., liver cirrhosis) remains unsolved: existing diffusion-based methods rely on natural-image priors, require 25–50 sampling steps, and cannot control pathology severity. No prior work provides fine-grained, graded-severity abdominal MRI pathology synthesis, leaving a critical gap for data augmentation in pathology-aware downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251720/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three conditioning signals: an organ segmentation mask (encoded to latent Z_s by a frozen VAE), a text prompt specifying target organs or cirrhosis severity level ("low", "mild", "severe"; encoded by CLIP or T5), and optionally a reference non-cirrhotic MRI for counterfactual pathology transfer. Outputs are single 2D slices (or volumes) of synthetic CT (BTCV), abdominal MRI (AMOS), or cirrhotic liver T1/T2 MRI (CirrMRI600+), generated in as few as 3–4 diffusion steps rather than the 25–50 steps of vanilla fine-tuning, with inference times dropping from ~10–19 s to ~1.7–3.5 s per image.

## Method

ViCTr is a two-stage framework built on a latent diffusion backbone (UNet or DiT) with a frozen VAE. Stage 1 pretrains on ATLAS-8k CT scans using Elastic Weight Consolidation to lock in anatomical priors, optimizing a composite loss of diffusion, L2 reconstruction, and SSIM terms. Stage 2 fine-tunes with LoRA adapters on a dual-network setup (frozen φ_base + LoRA-augmented φ_adapt) under a consistency loss that aligns intermediate outputs, enabling targeted pathology adaptation without catastrophic forgetting. The key technical novelty is reformulating Tweedie's formula within a rectified-flow (linear-interpolation) trajectory: the ODE dx_t = v_θ(x_t,t)dt + (1−α̅_t)∇_{x_t}log p(x_t)dt adds a score-based bias-correction term to the straight-line flow, and a one-step distillation network T̂(x_0) = x_0 + v(x_0,0) + (1−α̅_0)∇log p(x_0) is trained to predict the final image directly, cutting sampling from dozens of steps to 3–4 while preserving distributional fidelity.

## Results

ViCTr consistently outperforms vanilla fine-tuning across all five diffusion backbones and three datasets. The best configuration (Flux + ViCTr) achieves FID/MFID of 13.28/14.08 on BTCV (CT), 15.55/16.58 on AMOS (MRI), and 19.96/17.01 on CirrMRI600+ (cirrhosis), with the 17.01 MFID representing a 28% reduction over the best vanilla baseline. Inference is reduced from 25–50 diffusion steps (10.7–19.0 s) to just 3–4 steps (1.7–3.5 s) via the Tweedie-corrected one-step distillation. For downstream segmentation, adding 30% ViCTr-synthesized data to nnUNet training improves mDSC by up to +7.9% on CirrMRI600+ (71.02→78.89) and +4.7% on BTCV (80.48→85.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ViCTr against vanilla fine-tuning of five diffusion backbones: Stable Diffusion, SDXL, SD-3, Pixart-alpha, and Flux. ViCTr wins on every backbone–dataset combination, with the largest absolute FID gains observed on CirrMRI600+ (e.g., Flux: 22.46→19.96, a 2.5-point drop; SD-3: 24.49→21.28, a 3.2-point drop). Flux is the strongest backbone overall, achieving the lowest FID/MFID under both vanilla and ViCTr settings, but ViCTr narrows the gap between backbones substantially (e.g., on CirrMRI600+ MFID, the spread across backbones shrinks from 4.55 points in vanilla to 4.45 points in ViCTr). In terms of efficiency, ViCTr reduces diffusion steps by 85–94% (e.g., SD-3 from 50 to 3 steps) and cuts inference time by roughly 70–85% (e.g., SD-3 from 18.98 s to 2.78 s). The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that removing Tweedie's correction degrades FID from 15.55 to 18.78, and replacing the proposed rectified flow with ReFlow, Flow Straight and Fast, or Distribution Matching Distillation yields FIDs of 20.19, 21.37, and 22.33 respectively—substantially worse than ViCTr's 15.55.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViCTr sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022), which replaces curved diffusion trajectories with near-linear paths for efficient sampling, and Tweedie's formula (Robbins, 1992), a classical statistical correction for estimating clean signals from noisy observations. The paper's core methodological contribution is reformulating Tweedie's correction within the rectified flow ODE rather than the standard discrete diffusion schedule, enabling a single-step distillation that reduces inference from ~50 steps to 4. On the training side, it departs from end-to-end fine-tuning by adopting a two-stage paradigm—EWC-regularized pre-training on ATLAS-8k followed by LoRA-based adversarial fine-tuning—that explicitly separates anatomical preservation from pathology-specific adaptation, a design choice not present in prior rectified-flow or diffusion-based medical synthesis methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to synthesize abdominal MRI with graded pathology severity (mild → severe cirrhosis) in a single forward pass addresses a genuine gap: prior diffusion-based medical synthesis methods either generate only normal anatomy or isolated lesions, and none offer continuous severity control for diffuse pathologies. The 28% MFID reduction and +3.8% mDSC gain for nnUNet, validated across five diffusion backbones and three datasets, suggest the framework is robust rather than backbone-specific. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric synthesis (all experiments here are 2D slices), whether the Tweedie correction generalizes beyond Gaussian-like noise assumptions in MRI, and whether the Visual Turing Test (15 samples, 3 radiologists) scales to larger, multi-institutional clinical validation.
