## Problem

Pretraining on event camera data is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale event datasets, the inherent sparsity of event streams that causes overfitting, and the difficulty of jointly integrating spatiotemporal structure, polarity, and sparse activations. Existing event pretraining methods either train large backbones from scratch (risking overfitting on limited data) or apply image-pretrained models without adequately adapting to event-specific characteristics, limiting their versatility across downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes asynchronous event streams and converts them into a Temporal Event Count Map (TECM) of shape 2×T×H×W at 224×224 resolution, where the two channels encode positive and negative polarity and T temporal segments preserve timing information. The STP module transforms the TECM into a Dense Event Stack (DES) of shape T×H×W, which is then consumed by a frozen image-pretrained ViT. Downstream outputs include class labels (classification), per-pixel semantic masks (segmentation via UperNet decoder), and dense optical flow fields (flow estimation via a flow decoder).

## Method

The core contribution is the Spatiotemporal Information Fusion Prompting (STP) module, a lightweight adapter with only 2.2M trainable parameters that bridges event data to frozen image-pretrained ViTs. STP comprises two components: (1) Overlap Patch Embedding, which applies three progressive convolutions with kernel sizes larger than strides (kernels {8,6,6}, strides {4,2,2}) to expand each patch's receptive field from 16×16 to 66×66, filling sparse regions with neighboring context; and (2) a Temporal Transformer with two attention layers operating along the temporal dimension T within each spatial patch, augmented by a Local Temporal Aggregation (LTA) layer that performs weighted aggregation over adjacent time frames (τ ∈ {-1,0,+1}) to strengthen local inter-frame correlations. During pretraining, the ViT backbone is frozen and only the STP and a replaced patch-embedding layer are trained via cross-entropy loss on N-ImageNet, requiring just 100 epochs versus 300 for prior methods. During fine-tuning, both STP and ViT weights are unfrozen and the classification head is swapped for task-specific decoders (UperNet for segmentation, flow decoder for optical flow).

## Results

On N-ImageNet, STP with a frozen ViT-S/16 backbone achieves 68.87% top-1 and 89.65% top-5 accuracy, surpassing the previous best (ECDP, 64.83%) by 4.04% while using only 2.2M trainable parameters and 100 pretraining epochs (vs. 22.1M params and 300 epochs for ECDP). On small-scale datasets, the method reaches 94.74% on N-Caltech101, 98.86% on N-Cars, and 88.76% on CIFAR-10-DVS. For semantic segmentation on DDD17/DSEC (pretrained on E-TartanAir), STP achieves 63.16%/61.77% mIoU, and for optical flow on MVSEC it attains 0.31 AEE on indoor_flying1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STP outperforms all event-camera pretraining baselines on N-ImageNet: ECDP [76] (64.83%), MEM [33] (57.89%), and DMM [28] (not reported on N-ImageNet), with the largest margin over MEM at +10.98%. Against image-pretrained-model approaches, STP surpasses EventBind [82] (63.54%) by 5.33% and EventCLIP [73] (53.20%) by 15.67% on N-ImageNet top-1, while using roughly 1/100 of the trainable parameters (2.2M vs. 304.3M). On small-scale datasets, STP leads ECDP by 4.64% on N-Caltech101 (94.74% vs. 87.66%), 0.93% on N-Cars (98.86% vs. 97.93%), and 10.16% on CIFAR-10-DVS (88.76% vs. 78.00%). For semantic segmentation, STP pretrained on E-TartanAir beats ECDDP [77] by 0.60% mIoU on DDD17 (63.16% vs. 62.56%) and 0.52% on DSEC (61.77% vs. 61.25%). For optical flow on MVSEC, STP achieves the lowest AEE (0.31 vs. 0.36 for ECDDP [77]) and outlier ratio (0.03 vs. 0.04) on indoor_flying1, confirming consistent gains across all three downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior event camera pretraining methods (ECDP, MEM, DMM) that train large backbones (22–86M parameters) from scratch or via self-supervised objectives on limited event data. Instead, it repurposes the prompt-tuning paradigm—originally developed for NLP and adapted to vision tasks like image classification and segmentation—by freezing an ImageNet-pretrained ViT and training only a lightweight Spatiotemporal Information Fusion Prompting (STP) module (2.2M parameters) that adapts event data representations to the frozen model's input space. The STP's Overlap Patch Embedding is a direct modification of ViT's standard patch embedding, while the Temporal Transformer follows ViT's block design with an added Local Temporal Aggregation layer. The TECM input representation extends the standard Event Count Map by preserving temporal segmentation, building on Voxel Grid and Polarity Voxel Grid conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing event camera pretraining from 22–86M trainable parameters and 300–700 epochs to 2.2M parameters and 100 epochs while simultaneously improving accuracy, which is critical given the scarcity of large-scale event datasets. The finding that the frozen-image-model-plus-lightweight-adapter approach outperforms full event-specific pretraining (even before fine-tuning) suggests that image-domain prior knowledge is a more effective regularizer than self-supervised event objectives when data is limited. Open questions include whether this strategy generalizes to truly large event datasets where the data-parameter imbalance disappears, whether the frozen-backbone assumption degrades for event modalities far from RGB (e.g., very high-speed or extreme HDR scenarios), and whether the TECM representation is optimal compared to learned event encodings.
