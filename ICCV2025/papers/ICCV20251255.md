## Problem

Existing RGB-IR UAV object detection datasets like DroneVehicle are constrained by fixed altitudes, limited angles, and narrow weather/illumination ranges, failing to capture real-world imaging complexity. Additionally, prior condition-guided fusion methods rely on single-condition attributes and pretext prediction tasks whose optimization objectives are misaligned with detection, yielding suboptimal multimodal contribution assignment under diverse conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251255/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aligned RGB-IR image pairs at 640×512 resolution. During training, six condition attributes (altitude, angle, time, weather, illumination, scenario) are formatted as text prompts fed to a frozen CLIP text encoder; at inference, these labels are unavailable and the PCD module derives condition guidance purely from visual features. Output consists of oriented bounding boxes with 11 fine-grained vehicle/equipment categories (car, SUV, van, bus, freight car, truck, motorcycle, trailer, excavator, crane, tank truck), evaluated with mAP at IoU 0.5.

## Method

PCDF encodes multi-label imaging conditions as CLIP text prompts via Sample-specific Condition Prompt Learning (SCPL), which uses a hard-gating mask to exclude irrelevant attributes per sample before re-encoding. A three-branch Prompt-guided Condition-decoupling (PCD) network splits visual features into condition-specific and condition-invariant components, with a CMD-based distillation loss aligning the condition-specific branch to CLIP prompt embeddings and an irrelevance loss enforcing orthogonality to the invariant branch. The Condition-aware Dynamic Fusion (CDF) module then maps condition-specific features through a channel-wise normalized soft-gating transformation to produce per-channel RGB/IR weights in [0,1], dynamically reweighting condition-invariant features before concatenation and detection. This design avoids multi-label condition prediction and pretext-task misalignment by directly learning task-specific condition-to-contribution relationships through the detection loss.

## Results

On the ATR-UMOD dataset, PCDF achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 63.3% with IoU=0.5, surpassing the second-best multimodal method YOLOrs (60.0%) by 3.3 percentage points. The best unimodal baselines are YOLOv5s at 50.7% (RGB) and 52.5% (IR), while the best prior multimodal methods include OAFA (57.9%) and CALNet (53.8%). PCDF also achieves the highest per-category mAP on 6 out of 11 object classes, with particularly large gains on Trailer (+5.9% over YOLOrs), Crane (+6.2%), and Excavator (+9.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCDF outperforms all 8 multimodal baselines on overall mAP, with the largest margin over YOLOrs (+3.3%), OAFA (+5.4%), and CALNet (+9.5%). Among unimodal detectors, the best RGB (YOLOv5s, 50.7%) and IR (YOLOv5s, 52.5%) baselines trail PCDF by 12.6 and 10.8 points respectively, confirming the value of condition-aware fusion. Per-category, PCDF leads on Bus, Truck, Tank Truck, Trailer, Crane, and Excavator, while YOLOrs retains a slight edge on Car, SUV, Van, Freight Car, and Motorcycle. The illumination-guided methods IAF R-CNN (41.9%) and TINet (50.4%) underperform PCDF substantially, suggesting that single-attribute condition cues are insufficient compared to multi-condition prompts. Ablation results (Table 4) further show that removing the condition-decoupling module costs 1.2% mAP and replacing the soft-gating CDF with simple concatenation drops performance to 61.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RGB-IR UAV object detection line initiated by DroneVehicle, which was constrained to fixed altitudes, limited angles, and clear-weather conditions. Methodologically, PCDF departs from prior condition-guided fusion approaches (IAF R-CNN, TINet, IGT) that relied on pretext prediction tasks to derive modality reliabilities, instead repurposing CLIP's frozen text encoder to encode multi-label condition attributes as semantic prompts. The sample-specific prompt learning and condition-decoupling mechanism are novel contributions that address two gaps in prior work: the inability of single-attribute condition models to capture multi-dimensional imaging conditions, and the optimization mismatch between pretext tasks and detection objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATR-UMOD fills a critical gap in RGB-IR UAV detection by providing the first dataset with comprehensive condition diversity (80–300m altitude, 0–75° angle, all-day/all-year, 7 weather types, 6 illumination levels, 11 scenarios) and explicit condition annotations, enabling condition-specific performance evaluation that was previously impossible. The consistent 3–6% mAP gains across nearly all condition subsets validate that condition-aware dynamic fusion is a meaningful improvement over rigid fusion strategies. However, the method's reliance on CLIP text prompts adds inference complexity, the 'Foggy' condition reveals residual limitations, and generalization beyond the ATR-UMOD distribution (e.g., to other sensor pairs or detection tasks) remains untested. Open questions include whether the prompt-based condition encoding scales to continuous-valued conditions and whether the decoupling strategy transfers to other multimodal settings.
