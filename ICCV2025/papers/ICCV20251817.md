## Problem

Existing intraoperative 2D/3D registration methods require hours of patient-specific preoperative training (up to 10+ hours) and learn primarily from 2D projections without explicit 3D structural understanding, making them impractical for emergency surgeries where 10–15 minute delays significantly impact patient survival. The paper addresses this by asking whether explicit 3D modeling via sparse Gaussian primitives can reduce training time to minutes while maintaining clinically acceptable accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a preoperative CT volume (512³) and a single intraoperative X-ray image. The CT is first converted into ~50k 3D Gaussian primitives via differentiable DRR rendering, from which 1000 primitives are randomly selected and 200 rays are uniformly sampled per primitive. The X-ray is processed by a 50%-pruned SuperPoint encoder producing K image patches, which are further reduced to K′ via bipartite matching. The output is a 6-DoF camera pose (3×3 rotation + 3D translation) estimated in closed form from the top-50 most relevant rays, with no iterative optimization at inference.

## Method

GaussianReg employs a dual-stream architecture where the 3D stream represents CT anatomy as sparse 3D Gaussian primitives and emits rays from uniformly sampled ellipsoid surfaces toward potential camera positions, parameterized by Canonical Ellipsoid Ray Parameterization (CERP) to eliminate many-to-one ray ambiguity. The 2D stream extracts X-ray features using a pruned SuperPoint encoder, and a bipartite matching-based patch aggregation algorithm reduces patch count by merging the most similar cross-set pairs. A lightweight cross-attention module computes ray–patch similarity, supervised by an L2 loss that matches attention relevance to distance-based scores measuring how closely each ray passes through the ground-truth camera center. At inference, the top-k rays (at most one per primitive) are selected, and the camera position is found by minimizing squared perpendicular distances to all selected rays, while orientation is recovered via SVD on back-projected patch directions, yielding a direct geometric pose estimate without iterative refinement.

## Results

On the DeepFluoro dataset (6 cadaver specimens, 366 X-rays), GaussianReg achieves an average mTRE of 12.18 mm and mPD of 9.91 mm, outperforming all six baselines under a uniform 30-minute training budget. On the Ljubljana dataset (10 neurovascular surgery patients), it reaches 27.62 mm mTRE and 19.12 mm mPD, again the best among all compared methods. Notably, GaussianReg attains 10 mm-level accuracy with only 10 minutes of training, whereas prior methods require several hours to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianReg outperforms all six baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest regression-based method DiffPose [13], it reduces mTRE by 4.97 mm (17.15 → 12.18) and mPD by 2.68 mm (12.59 → 9.91) on DeepFluoro. Against the landmark-based RayEmb [37], the improvement is 6.28 mm in mTRE (~34% reduction). Compared to GDR-Net [42], the best other regression method, GaussianReg gains 4.4 mm in mTRE. On the Ljubljana dataset, the margin over DiffPose widens to 7.97 mm in mTRE (35.59 → 27.62), suggesting stronger generalization to a different anatomical domain (neurovascular vs. pelvic). All comparisons use the same 30-minute training window and 1000 DRR-synthesized X-rays, making the speed-accuracy trade-off directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianReg repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting—originally developed for real-time radiance field rendering—into an explicit 3D structural representation for intraoperative 2D/3D registration, a task previously dominated by either regression-based networks (PoseNet, GDR-Net, DiffPose) that learn pose from 2D projections, or landmark-based methods (FixedLandmark, RayEmb) that detect anatomical features and solve PnP. The key conceptual departure is shifting from 'learn to predict pose from 2D images' to 'use 3D geometry to actively search for pose via ray emission,' which eliminates the need for the 10⁴–10⁶ training samples that prior methods require. The cross-modality attention and bipartite matching aggregation are borrowed from general vision architectures but adapted here to bridge sparse 3D ray features with 2D X-ray patch features under a strict time budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-minute training time (vs. hours for prior methods) directly addresses a critical clinical gap in emergency trauma surgery, where even 10–15 minutes of delay impacts survival. However, the evidence base is limited to two datasets (one cadaver-based pelvic, one clinical neurovascular), and generalizability to other anatomical regions, patient populations, or imaging modalities remains untested. Open questions include whether the ray-based search scales to larger or more complex CT volumes, how the method handles intraoperative tissue deformation, and whether the 10 mm accuracy threshold is sufficient for all surgical sub-specialties. The repurposing of 3DGS as a geometric search tool rather than a rendering tool may inspire similar applications in other registration or localization tasks.
