## Problem

Existing adversarial training (AT) methods are formulated around worst-case robustness (WCR), targeting the single adversarial example that maximizes prediction loss. No dedicated AT method targets probabilistic robustness (PR), which measures the proportion of adversarial examples within a local perturbation ball and is arguably more practical for risk management. This paper bridges the gap by formulating a new min-max optimization specifically for PR-focused adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250439/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels via standard classifiers (ResNet, WRN, ViT, DeiT). During training, the method consumes each input image multiple times: it generates N diverse adversarial perturbations via randomized PGD, then selects one AE for the training step. Evaluation produces both WCR metrics (adversarial accuracy under PGD, C&W, AA attacks) and PR estimates (mean ± std of correct-classification probability under random perturbations within L∞ norm-balls of radii 0.08–0.15).

## Method

AT-PR reformulates the inner maximization of adversarial training to find the 'widest peak' in the loss landscape rather than the 'tallest peak': it seeks an AE x′ = x + δ* that maximizes the radius k of a sub-ball centered on x′ where PR = 0 (all perturbations are adversarial). Algorithm 1 solves this in three steps: (1) apply PGD N times with randomized step sizes, learning rates, and initializations to collect diverse AEs at different local optima; (2) for each candidate AE, run gradient descent toward the nearest decision boundary; (3) select the AE with the largest distance to its boundary, as this corresponds to the center of the widest adversarial region. The selected AE is then used in the standard outer minimization (cross-entropy training), making AT-PR a drop-in replacement for the PGD step in conventional AT. The approach is compatible with efficient AT variants (e.g., Fast-AT) and can be combined with other AT methods like TRADES.

## Results

Across 70 DL models on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and Tiny-ImageNet, AT-PR consistently achieves higher mean PR than all AT-WCR baselines while also reducing the standard deviation of PR across local inputs. For example, on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18, AT-PR improves PR(·,0.15) from 80.47% (PGD) to 87.38%, and on Tiny-ImageNet/ResNet-18 from 82.46% (PGD) to 86.65%. AT-PR also preserves clean accuracy better than PGD (e.g., 53.21% vs. 52.85% on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18; 51.61% vs. 47.43% on Tiny-ImageNet/ResNet-18) while maintaining comparable adversarial accuracy. The generalization error (train-test PR gap) is reduced by up to 9.66% on Tiny-ImageNet compared to PGD's 11.94%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AT-PR outperforms all five baselines on PR across every dataset and norm-ball size tested. Against FGSM (Goodfellow 2015), AT-PR gains 4–25 percentage points in PR(·,0.15) depending on dataset (e.g., 93.21% vs. 67.77% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18). Against PGD (Madry 2018), AT-PR improves PR(·,0.15) by 3.4–4.2 points (e.g., 87.38% vs. 80.47% on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18) while also achieving lower PR standard deviation, indicating more consistent robustness across local inputs. Against the advanced methods TRADES (Zhang 2019), MART (Wang 2019), and ALP/CLP (Kannan 2018), AT-PR consistently achieves the highest PR in Table 3 (e.g., 93.21% vs. 91.22% TRADES, 90.35% MART, 89.76% ALP on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 at γ=0.15). On adversarial accuracy (WCR), AT-PR is slightly below PGD by 1–6 points but surpasses FGSM by a wide margin and is competitive with PGD on several model-dataset combinations (e.g., 45.81% vs. 44.57% PGD on SVHN/ResNet-18). On clean accuracy, AT-PR loses to FGSM but beats PGD by 1–4 points, showing a reduced robustness-accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training (AT) framework from its standard worst-case robustness (WCR) objective to a new probabilistic robustness (PR) objective. The WCR min-max formulation (Madry et al., Goodfellow et al.) has been the dominant paradigm, with variants like TRADES, MART, ALP, and CLP all optimizing the same inner maximization (find the AE with maximum loss). The PR metric itself was introduced by Webb et al. and refined by Tit et al., but no prior work had formulated a dedicated AT optimization targeting PR. The paper's key departure is redefining the inner maximization to find the AE farthest from the decision boundary within a region of zero PR (the 'widest peak' rather than the 'tallest peak'), and developing a three-step numerical algorithm (diverse PGD + gradient-based boundary search + distance selection) to solve it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first AT method explicitly designed for PR, filling a gap where practitioners had no principled training procedure to improve probabilistic robustness without sacrificing worst-case accuracy. The reduced generalization gap (up to ~2–9% lower than PGD across datasets) is practically significant, as it suggests AT-PR may be more deployable in safety-critical settings where train-test consistency matters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale models, how it interacts with certified robustness methods like randomized smoothing, and whether the PR objective transfers to generative and multimodal tasks. The ~2× computational overhead over PGD (mitigated to ~1.5× with Fast-AT) remains a practical barrier for large-scale training.
