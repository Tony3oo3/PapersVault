## Problem

Hierarchical physics-based character control systems that pair a high-level diffusion planner with a low-level RL tracker suffer from a domain gap: kinematic plans violate physical constraints, causing jitter, foot-skating, and the need for per-task controller fine-tuning. This limits generalization to unseen control signals and degrades motion naturalness on long-horizon tasks. The paper asks whether planning and control can be unified into a single model that handles diverse guidance signals without task-specific adaptation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a behavior sequence of length T where each frame is a 398-dimensional token (concatenation of a 32-dim PULSE latent action z_t and the induced next canonicalized state s_{t+1}), along with a CLIP text embedding and optional task-specific guidance losses. At inference, a receding-horizon loop feeds a short history window (h=4 frames) and denoises a future horizon (H=8 for reactive tasks, H=28 for planning), executing only the first T_a=8 decoded joint-position actions before re-planning. The canonicalized state includes root trajectory (position, 6D orientation, linear/angular velocity) and per-joint local features (position, velocity, 6D rotation, angular velocity) for 23 actuated joints.

## Method

UniPhys is a causal transformer decoder trained via behavior cloning under the Diffusion Forcing paradigm, where each frame in a sequence is independently corrupted with a random noise level rather than a uniform one, teaching the model to denoise partially-noisy histories. At inference, a guided denoising loop combines classifier-free guidance (CFG) for text conditioning with Monte-Carlo Guidance (MCG) for task-specific losses (goal distance, velocity matching, SDF-based obstacle repulsion), all applied within a single denoising pass. A stabilization trick sets the noise indicator of past frames to a small positive value (without actually adding noise), preventing the model from treating its own predictions as ground truth and thereby reducing compounding error over long rollouts. The model supports three denoising schedules—full-sequence, autoregressive, and gradual—that trade off robustness against inference speed, and the same trained model handles all four tasks (text-driven control, goal reaching, velocity control, obstacle avoidance) purely through different test-time configurations of horizon, schedule, and guidance loss.

## Results

On text-driven control (Table 2), UniPhys achieves 56.3% correct caption matching with 14.2% wrong, outperforming MaskedMimic (42.9% correct) but trailing CLoSD (61.6% correct). However, UniPhys substantially outperforms both baselines on motion quality: naturalness 3.23 vs. 2.86 (CLoSD) and 2.82 (MaskedMimic), and jerk 1.2 mm/s³ vs. 3.4 and 4.2 respectively. On goal reaching (Table 3), UniPhys achieves 0.95 success rate on far goals (2–6 m) where MaskedMimic fails entirely (0.0), and 0.85 on jogging-style goals vs. 0.64 for CLoSD. For velocity control, UniPhys achieves 0.07 m/s tracking error with 2.0 mm/s³ jerk, compared to MaskedMimic's 0.09 m/s error and 5.0 mm/s³ jerk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLoSD [55], UniPhys trades a small drop in text-to-motion correctness (56.3% vs. 61.6%) for substantially better motion quality: jerk is reduced from 3.4 to 1.2 mm/s³, naturalness improves from 2.86 to 3.23, and foot floating decreases from 20.59 to 16.6 mm. On far goal reaching (2–6 m), UniPhys achieves 0.95 success while CLoSD relies on heuristic intermediate goals that cause discontinuities. Against MaskedMimic [53], UniPhys wins decisively on text correctness (56.3% vs. 42.9%), naturalness (3.23 vs. 2.82), and jerk (1.2 vs. 4.2 mm/s³). The most striking gap is on far goal reaching, where MaskedMimic scores 0.0 (it cannot handle goals beyond ~2 m) while UniPhys reaches 0.95. On velocity control, UniPhys produces much smoother transitions (jerk 2.0 vs. 5.0 mm/s³) at a comparable tracking error (0.07 vs. 0.09 m/s). Overall, UniPhys is the only method that combines competitive semantic fidelity with the smoothest, most physically plausible motions across all four tasks without per-task fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPhys departs from the dominant hierarchical planner-controller paradigm (e.g., CLoSD, MaskedMimic) by collapsing both stages into a single diffusion-based behavior cloning model, eliminating the kinematic-to-physical domain gap that causes jitter and foot-skating in two-stage systems. It builds directly on PULSE's latent action representation for compact action encoding, adopts the Diffusion Forcing training paradigm (per-frame independent noise levels) to handle autoregressive error accumulation, and leverages Monte-Carlo Guidance for task-specific loss-based denoising. The key conceptual departure is treating past predictions as slightly noisy during inference rather than assuming a clean history, which reframes long-horizon behavior cloning as a denoising problem over a noisy context window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying planning and control, UniPhys removes the need for per-task fine-tuning of a low-level RL tracker, offering a single model that handles text-driven control, velocity tracking, goal reaching, and obstacle avoidance through different test-time denoising configurations. The stabilization trick—setting the noise indicator above zero for past frames without adding actual noise—is a simple but critical insight for long-horizon behavior cloning, with ablations showing a ~40× improvement in episode length. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional action spaces (dexterous hands, full-body manipulation), generalizes to terrain and object interaction, and whether the reliance on PULSE's pre-trained latent space limits expressiveness. The released 15.7-hour state-action dataset with BABEL text annotations also provides a useful resource for the physics-based imitation learning community.
