## Problem

Current large vision-language models (LVLMs) trained end-to-end struggle with visually intensive reasoning tasks, particularly geometric math problems, because they fail to accurately extract the precise visual information (angles, spatial relationships, geometric primitives) needed for correct reasoning. The paper identifies that this bottleneck is not a reasoning deficiency but an insufficient visual understanding problem, motivating a decoupled architecture that separates visual interpretation from linguistic reasoning rather than unifying them in a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a geometric diagram image (containing points, lines, angles, and spatial relationships) and a text question as input. In the 'Vision Only' setting, the question is embedded within the image itself, requiring the LVLM to first read the question text before describing the figure. The LVLM outputs a structured natural-language description of all geometric primitives and their relationships, which is then concatenated with the question and fed to the LLM. The LLM produces a step-by-step chain-of-thought derivation ending in a final answer (multiple-choice letter for Geo170k evaluation).

## Method

The proposed decoupled reasoning framework connects a visual interpretation specialist (Qwen2VL-7B) and a linguistic reasoning LLM (Qwen2.5Math-7B) in series, where the LVLM translates geometric images into text descriptions and the LLM performs step-by-step mathematical reasoning over those descriptions. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning of the LVLM on GPT-4o-refined geometric image-description pairs from GeoMM and the LLM on description-to-solution mappings; (2) GRPO-based reinforcement learning on the LVLM where the reward is the correctness of the frozen LLM's final answer, encouraging the LVLM to produce descriptions that are most useful for downstream reasoning; (3) GRPO-based reinforcement learning on the LLM with the LVLM frozen, further refining reasoning over the fixed visual descriptions. A key design choice is skipping training samples where the LLM succeeds or fails on all sampled descriptions, ensuring the reward signal is informative and the LVLM learns to prioritize problem-relevant visual information.

## Results

On the MathVerse testmini benchmark (top-1 accuracy), the decoupled framework (Qwen2VL-7B + Qwen2.5Math-7B) achieves 54.3% on Text Dominant, 46.3% on Vision Intensive, 47.2% on Vision Dominant, and 43.8% on Vision Only. Notably, it surpasses the much larger Qwen2VL-72B + DeepSeek-V3(671B) pipeline on Vision Dominant (47.2% vs. 44.2%) and Vision Only (43.8% vs. 42.9%) while using models that are dozens of times smaller. On general VQA benchmarks, the SFT-only version already outperforms LLaVA-CoT-11B on MMStar (59.1 vs. 57.6), MM-Vet (61.5 vs. 60.3), MathVista (57.1 vs. 54.8), and MathVerse (42.5 vs. 36.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source pipeline (Qwen2VL-72B + DeepSeek-V3 671B), the proposed 7B+7B decoupled method wins on Vision Dominant (47.2% vs. 44.2%) and Vision Only (43.8% vs. 42.9%) but trails on Text Dominant (54.3% vs. 59.1%) and Text Lite (49.0% vs. 53.1%), indicating the advantage is concentrated in visually intensive settings. Compared to the geometry-specific R-CoT-7B, the method gains 3.7 points on Vision Intensive, 8.7 on Vision Dominant, and 15.3 on Vision Only, showing a much more graceful degradation as visual dependency increases. Against the standalone Qwen2VL-7B, the decoupled pipeline improves by 10.0 points on Vision Dominant and 10.0 on Vision Only, confirming that separating interpretation from reasoning is the key factor. The method loses to the closed-source GPT-4o + o1-mini pipeline by a wide margin (e.g., 54.3% vs. 70.6% on Text Dominant), but outperforms GPT-4o alone on Vision Dominant (47.2% vs. 44.3%) and matches it on Vision Only (43.8% vs. 43.7%). Against LLaVA-CoT-11B (the unified end-to-end baseline in Table 1), the decoupled SFT version already leads on all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on MathVerse (42.5% vs. 36.2%). The smaller open-source baselines (G-LLaVA-7B, Math-LLaVA-13B, MultiMath-7B) are outperformed by 20–35 points across all visual-dependency levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end LVLM paradigm (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, LLaVA-CoT) where a connector module fuses visual and textual features for unified reasoning. Instead, it draws on the observation that LVLMs' primary failure mode is imprecise visual extraction rather than weak reasoning, and repurposes the success of RL-based reasoning in LLMs (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1) to jointly optimize a VLM interpreter and an LLM reasoner in series. The approach is conceptually analogous to the GPT-4o + o1-mini pipeline but realized with open-source 7B models and a three-stage outcome-rewarded training process, building on geometric math datasets (GeoMM, Geo170k) established by R-CoT and G-LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: by decoupling visual interpretation from linguistic reasoning, the LLM component can be independently upgraded as stronger models emerge, avoiding costly end-to-end retraining. However, the evaluation is confined to geometric math problems, leaving open whether the decoupled architecture generalizes to visual tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning beyond 2D diagrams (e.g., 3D scenes, charts, or interleaved visual-linguistic inputs). A key limitation is that the VLM-to-LLM text bottleneck may lose information for tasks where visual details are too granular to capture in natural language, and the approach has not been tested on non-geometric visual reasoning benchmarks at scale.
