## Problem

Prevailing neural inertial tracking methods rely almost exclusively on spatio-temporal features extracted via ResNet or LSTM, overlooking the frequency domain of IMU signals where motion-state transitions (gait shifts, posture changes) manifest as distinct spectral patterns. This single-domain focus leads to insufficient inertial representations and inefficient aggregation of heterogeneous features for long-sequence tracking, resulting in suboptimal localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding window of 200 consecutive IMU samples (6 channels: 3-axis acceleration and 3-axis angular velocity at 200 Hz), with each sample rotated from the body frame to the navigation frame using a provided orientation matrix. It regresses a 2D velocity vector per window via an MLP head, trained end-to-end with MSE loss against ground-truth 2D velocities derived from the same sliding mechanism. The orientation source varies by dataset: estimated IMU orientations for RIDI and IMUNet, and a hybrid estimated/ground-truth scheme for RoNIN.

## Method

M²EIT decomposes IMU representation learning into three parallel expert pathways: a 6-layer ResNet spatial decomposition expert (SDE) with 3×1 convolutions for multivariate spatial relationships, a bidirectional Mamba (SSM-based) temporal decomposition expert (TDE) with residual connections for long-range temporal dependencies, and a Haar wavelet frequency decomposition expert (FDE) that applies 1D discrete wavelet transform to disentangle low- and high-frequency bands, each processed by pointwise convolutions. A Multi-Representation Alignment Router (MAR) then bridges the semantic gap: dual cross-domain translation layers (multi-head attention) project temporal and frequency features into the spatial domain, and a soft router (softmax over mean-pooled global expert features) adaptively weights the three aligned representations before a final MLP regresses velocity. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with MSE loss, and the wavelet decomposition level is fixed at 1, halving the temporal resolution per band.

## Results

On the RIDI dataset, M²EIT achieves 1.65 m ATE and 2.00 m RTE, outperforming the best baseline (MobileNet/EfficientNet at 2.12 m ATE) by 22.2%. On RoNIN, M²EIT reaches 3.58 m ATE (seen) and 5.19 m ATE (unseen), improving over RoNIN_ResNet by 22.3% and 12.5% respectively. On IMUNet, M²EIT attains 2.18 m ATE and 2.50 m RTE, surpassing the best baseline (ResMixer at 3.01 m ATE) by 27.6%. Overall, M²EIT achieves 13–50% ATE improvements over all neural network-based baselines across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²EIT outperforms all six baseline families across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest neural baselines (MobileNet, EfficientNet, ResMixer from IMUNet [41]), M²EIT reduces ATE by 22–28% on RIDI, 12–17% on RoNIN (seen), and 28–38% on IMUNet. Compared to the RoNIN_ResNet [11] architecture that M²EIT's spatial expert is built upon, the gains are 29.2% ATE on RIDI, 22.3% on RoNIN (seen), and 28.3% on IMUNet, confirming that the temporal (Bi-Mamba) and frequency (Haar wavelet) experts add substantial value beyond spatial features alone. On the unseen-subject split of RoNIN, M²EIT achieves 5.19 m ATE versus 5.89 m for EfficientNet and 6.34 m for IMUNet, demonstrating stronger generalization to new users. Traditional PDR [30] and RIDI [38] baselines are far behind (8–23 m ATE), highlighting the gap between rule-based and learned approaches. TLIO [19] (without EKF) is competitive on RIDI (2.64 m ATE) but still trails M²EIT by 37.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural inertial tracking (NIT) lineage initiated by RoNIN (ResNet-based spatial features) and IONet/TLIO/CTIN (temporal modeling via LSTM/attention) by introducing a third, previously overlooked domain: frequency. The spatial decomposition expert directly repurposes RoNIN's ResNet181D architecture, while the temporal decomposition expert adapts Mamba's selective state-space mechanism into a bidirectional form for IMU sequences. The overall Mixture-of-Experts framing with a cross-domain alignment router draws from the broader MoE literature (FuseMoE, M3OE) but is the first application of this decomposition-to-fusion paradigm to inertial odometry, where prior work treated spatial and temporal features as the complete representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that frequency-domain decomposition (Haar wavelet) contributes meaningfully on top of spatial and temporal features, the paper challenges the implicit assumption in NIT that spatio-temporal modeling is sufficient for IMU representation learning. The 20%+ ATE improvements across three datasets and the robustness gains under added noise suggest multi-domain modeling is a practical path toward more reliable drift-free tracking. However, all experiments are confined to 200 Hz pedestrian IMU data on three benchmarks, leaving open whether the frequency expert generalizes to higher-frequency sensors, non-pedestrian motion (vehicles, drones), or multi-sensor fusion settings. The authors themselves note future work on multi-sensor tracking under compute constraints, but no such experiments are provided.
