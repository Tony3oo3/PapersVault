## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for segmentation are designed for classification or general pixel-level tasks and fail to handle two challenges unique to tubular structure segmentation (TSS): (1) cross-domain discrepancies in topological structures such as trajectory, curvature, branching patterns, and thickness, and (2) fragile topological continuity caused by shifts in local foreground-background distinguishing features (texture, contrast). No prior TTA framework specifically targets these topological concerns, leading to broken or disconnected tubular predictions in unseen domains.

## Input / Output

TopoTTA takes a single unlabeled target-domain RGB image (resized to 384×384 for most datasets; native resolution for OCTA) and produces a binary tubular structure segmentation mask. The model is pre-trained on a source domain and then adapted online at test time using only that one image, with six total adaptation iterations (three per stage). The method is plug-and-play for any CNN-based TSS backbone (validated on UNet, CS2Net, and DSCNet) and requires no target-domain labels or source-domain access at test time.

## Method

TopoTTA operates in two stages. Stage 1 introduces Topological Meta Difference Convolutions (TopoMDCs), which extend central difference convolution into eight directional variants (orthogonal and diagonal) that capture elongated tubular trajectories; a set of learnable router parameters δ per image patch adaptively weights the combination of TopoMDCs, and only δ is updated via entropy minimization while all pre-trained convolution weights remain frozen. Stage 2 proposes Topology Hard sample Generation (TopoHG), which selects high-confidence foreground regions, identifies adjacent low-confidence background patches, and performs an FFT-based low-frequency swap to synthesize local pseudo-breaks that weaken the model's predictions; a teacher-student consistency loss (cross-entropy with spatially weighted emphasis on pseudo-break regions) then aligns the student's predictions on these hard samples back to the original pseudo-labels, with the teacher updated via EMA. This two-stage design separates topological structure adaptation from continuity refinement, addressing both challenges without altering the pre-trained network's core parameters in Stage 1.

## Results

Across ten cross-domain TSS experiments (four scenarios), TopoTTA achieves an average Dice of 69.44% and clDice of 74.00% with UNet, and 67.68% / 72.70% with CS2Net, outperforming the best baseline (CoTTA) by 3.95% Dice and 4.66% clDice (UNet). The method reduces Betti errors from 51.94 (Source Only) to 43.01 (UNet) and 43.86 (CS2Net). The paper reports an average clDice improvement of 31.81% over Source Only across all ten datasets. Notably, in the DeepGlobe→CNDS setting where all baselines degrade below Source Only, TopoTTA still improves Dice from 81.74 to 89.15 and clDice from 92.22 to 96.35.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoTTA outperforms all seven baselines on both UNet and CS2Net across every metric. Against the strongest baseline CoTTA (teacher-student scheme), TopoTTA gains +3.95% Dice, +4.66% clDice, and reduces Betti errors by 4.29 points (UNet); similar margins hold for CS2Net. Normalization-based methods (TENT, SAR, DomainAdaptor, MedBN) cluster tightly around 63–64% Dice and 66–68% clDice, confirming that BN-statistics updates alone are insufficient for TSS; MedBN performs worst, with Betti errors of 80.05 (UNet) indicating severe topological fragmentation. The medical-segmentation-specific method VPTTA (63.98% Dice, 67.48% clDice) also trails TopoTTA by ~5.5% Dice, suggesting that generic prompt-based adaptation does not capture tubular topology. In the particularly challenging Neub1→Neub2 transfer, TopoTTA achieves 66.88% Dice and 74.95% clDice, surpassing the second-best (CoTTA at 52.21% / 62.78%) by 14.67 and 12.17 points respectively, highlighting its advantage under severe topological distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoTTA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: test-time adaptation (TTA) for domain shift and tubular structure segmentation (TSS). It repurposes Central Difference Convolution (originally for face anti-spoofing) into eight directional TopoMDCs that adaptively reweight existing convolution parameters without adding new ones, and borrows the low-frequency FFT swap from FedDG to synthesize pseudo-breaks for continuity training. The overall two-stage design composes TENT-style entropy minimization (Stage 1) with a CoTTA-style teacher-student consistency loop (Stage 2), but reorients both stages around topology-specific objectives rather than generic confidence or pixel-level consistency. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first TTA framework tailored to TSS, arguing that general-purpose TTA methods fail because they ignore topological structure discrepancies and local foreground-background feature shifts unique to tubular objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first topology-aware TTA pipeline for TSS, the paper addresses a practical gap: clinicians and engineers deploying vessel/road/neuron segmentation models across imaging devices or institutions face continuity-breaking failures that generic TTA cannot fix. The plug-and-play nature (no architectural changes, only router parameters and a hard-sample generation step) makes it immediately adoptable for existing CNN-based TSS pipelines. Limitations include restriction to CNN backbones (no transformer evaluation), reliance on confident pseudo-labels that may be unreliable under severe domain shift, and the fact that TopoHG's frequency-domain swap is tuned for 2D patches—extending to 3D tubular structures (e.g., vascular trees in CT angiography) remains open. Whether the router-based adaptive combination generalizes to topologies far outside the eight canonical directions (e.g., highly branched or coiled structures) is also untested.
