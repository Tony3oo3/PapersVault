## Problem

While the accuracy–adversarial-robustness tradeoff is well characterized for models trained from scratch, it remains largely unknown whether and how this tradeoff persists during fine-tuning of pre-trained models. Specifically, it is unclear how the tradeoff shifts across different parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) strategies and downstream task complexities, and whether adversarial-robustness findings generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes 224×224 RGB images as input and produces class labels, using a ViT-Base backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21k. Fine-tuning is performed on six downstream datasets spanning 10–256 classes (CIFAR-10/100, Caltech-256, CUB-200, Stanford Dogs, DomainNet), and robustness is evaluated continuously at backpropagation-step granularity rather than only at convergence, with PGD (ε=1/255, 15 steps) for adversarial robustness and cross-domain testing on DomainNet for OOD robustness.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary artifacts. First, a theoretical model formalizes how the number of updated parameters k (determined by the fine-tuning strategy) and the downstream task complexity d jointly determine a lower bound on the correlation strength η of non-robust features required to reach a target accuracy, showing that full fine-tuning (k=d) relaxes this bound and increases vulnerability. Second, an empirical evaluation framework decomposes seven fine-tuning strategies (full FT, linear probing, BitFit, LoRA, Adapter, Compacter, IA³) along two axes—information type/location and update mechanism—and continuously tracks adversarial and OOD robustness during fine-tuning using an adaptive sampling schedule (every 50–200 steps early, every 6,000 steps late). Trade-offs are quantified via Pareto frontiers and their area under the curve (AUC), revealing that peripheral updates (BitFit) dominate on simple tasks while attention-layer updates (Compacter) achieve better balances on complex tasks, and that OOD robustness tracks accuracy without exhibiting the adversarial tradeoff.

## Results

The paper reports AUC of Pareto frontiers (higher = better robustness-accuracy balance) across 5 datasets for 7 fine-tuning strategies. BitFit achieves the highest AUC on simpler tasks: 0.21 on CIFAR-10 (75% above the cross-method average) and 0.10 on CIFAR-100 (81.5% above average). Compacter dominates on complex tasks: 0.34 on Caltech-256 (57.5% above average), 0.15 on CUB-200 (34.6% above average), and 0.09 on Stanford Dogs (24% above average). Linear probing and full fine-tuning consistently yield the lowest AUCs, e.g., 0.06 and 0.11 on CIFAR-10, and 0.08 and 0.09 on CUB-200 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a single new method but systematically compares seven fine-tuning strategies (BitFit, Adapter, LoRA, Compacter, IA3, Linear Probing, Full Fine-Tuning) on their adversarial robustness-accuracy trade-off. BitFit (bias-only updates) wins on the two simplest datasets—CIFAR-10 (AUC 0.21 vs. 0.14 for LoRA and 0.11 for Full FT) and CIFAR-100 (0.10 vs. 0.07 and 0.04)—by 75% and 81.5% above the cross-method average, respectively. Compacter (Kronecker-based adapters in attention layers) wins on the three most complex datasets—Caltech-256 (0.34 vs. 0.33 for BitFit and 0.26 for Full FT), CUB-200 (0.15 vs. 0.14 and 0.09), and Stanford Dogs (0.09 vs. 0.08 and 0.05)—by 57.5%, 34.6%, and 24% above average. Linear Probing and Full Fine-Tuning are the weakest baselines across all five datasets, with LP scoring 47.5% below average on CIFAR-100 and 28.2% below on CUB-200. For OOD robustness on DomainNet, the ranking inverts: Full Fine-Tuning achieves the highest peak OOD robustness (73%±2%) while Linear Probing is the lowest (61%±5%), and the specific PEFT mechanism matters far less than the extent of parameter updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robustness-accuracy trade-off literature (Tsipras et al., Ilyas et al., Zhang et al.), which was established for models trained from scratch, into the pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm where upstream and downstream data phenomena interact. It repurposes the PEFT taxonomy (addition-based, reparametrization-based, selection-based) not for efficiency comparison but as a decomposition axis for understanding how *where* and *how* parameters are updated affects adversarial and OOD robustness. The theoretical lower-bound derivation on η (Section 3.1) directly builds on the non-robust feature framework of Tsipras et al. [45], adapting it to the frozen-weights-plus-delta setting of fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, continuous-granularity characterization of how robustness evolves during fine-tuning across seven strategies and six datasets, yielding practical guidance: peripheral updates (BitFit) suit simple tasks while information-intensive layer updates (Compacter) suit complex ones. The finding that OOD robustness tracks accuracy without exhibiting the adversarial-style peak-and-decline trade-off suggests that security and safety robustness are governed by fundamentally different mechanisms, which has implications for how deployment risk is assessed. Limitations include focus on a single ViT-Base architecture, clean (non-adversarially-trained) models, and PGD as the sole attack; whether findings hold for CNN-ViT hybrids, adversarially pre-trained backbones, or black-box threats remains open.
