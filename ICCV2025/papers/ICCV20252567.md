## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pretraining models (Med-VLPMs) rely on deterministic point embeddings that enforce one-to-one mappings, making them ill-suited to the many-to-many relationships pervasive in medical data (e.g., multiple valid radiology descriptions for one CXR, or multiple ECGs/CXRs per patient visit). Additionally, most prior work focuses exclusively on CXR-text alignment, overlooking the broader multimodal integration required in real clinical decision-making.

## Input / Output

PROBMED ingests four modalities—chest X-ray images, 12-lead ECG signals, echocardiogram videos, and free-form clinical text—and encodes each into a D-dimensional Gaussian distribution N(μ, diag(σ²)) in a shared embedding space, rather than a single point vector. Critically, the model does not require all four modalities to be present for a given patient during training (following the ImageBind meta-learning paradigm); instead, it trains on available pairs (CXR-text, ECG-text, ECHO-text, CXR-ECG) and discovers emergent cross-modal alignment (e.g., CXR-to-ECHO) without ever observing those pairs. At inference, the full distribution (not just μ) enables uncertainty-based prompt filtering for zero-shot classification and reparameterized sampling to augment few-shot training sets.

## Method

PROBMED replaces deterministic contrastive embeddings with probabilistic ones: each modality-specific encoder (Swin Transformer for CXR, XResNet-1d-101 for ECG, ECHO-CLIP for echocardiogram, BioBERT for text) is augmented with two parallel heads predicting μ (initialized from the pretrained backbone) and log σ² (randomly initialized), with features aggregated via a Generalized Pooling Operator preceded by Batch Normalization. Cross-modal alignment uses InfoNCE loss with 1−Hellinger distance as the probabilistic similarity, chosen over alternatives (CSD, Bhattacharyya) for its symmetry and boundedness, which stabilizes gradients on noisy medical data. A novel Synthetic Instance Sampling (SIS) loss applies the reparameterization trick to draw multiple samples from each learned distribution and enforces their mutual consistency via cosine-similarity-based InfoNCE, reinforcing meaningful intra-modal variance. A Variational Information Bottleneck (KL-to-standard-normal) loss prevents variance collapse. Training follows an ImageBind-style meta-learning schedule where each gradient step randomly selects one modality pair from the available set, with SIS and VIB losses always active alongside the pair-specific alignment loss.

## Results

On TEXT-to-CXR retrieval, PROBMED achieves R@1=47.9 and R@5=71.4 on MIMIC-CXR with a total recall sum (RSUM) of 196.8, outperforming the best deterministic baseline CXR-CLIP (RSUM=187.1) and the probabilistic PCME++ (RSUM=142.0). On CXR-based zero-shot and few-shot classification across four datasets, PROBMED attains the #1 overall rank in both ZS and FS, with highlights including 86.4% AUROC ZS on COVID Kaggle and 69.5% AUROC ZS on the emergent CheXchoNet task. For ECG-based tasks, PROBMED leads with 64.5% ZS and 87.6% 16S AUROC on PTB-XL, and for ECHO, it reaches 82.6% ZS AUROC on EchoNet-Dynamic. In multimodal CXR+ECG classification for CKD/CHD, PROBMED achieves a total SUM of 458.6%, surpassing the best single-modality and prior multimodal baselines by up to 7.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROBMED outperforms all 10 deterministic Med-VLPM baselines (MedCLIP, CXR-CLIP, BiomedCLIP, CheXzero, MEDBind, BioViL-T, SAT, MedKLIP, ECG-CLIP, EchoCLIP) on overall ZS and FS rank across CXR, ECG, and ECHO tasks, achieving #1 in every modality. The most striking gap is against the closest probabilistic competitor PCME++: on CXR ZS, PROBMED scores 86.4% vs. PCME++'s 48.6% on COVID Kaggle (a 37.8-point margin), and on ECG ZS, PROBMED reaches 64.5% vs. PCME++'s 61.2% on PTB-XL. Among deterministic methods, MEDBind is the strongest CXR baseline (ZS rank 2, FS rank 4), but PROBMED still leads by 7.5 points on CheXchoNet ZS (69.5 vs. 62.0) and 5.5 points on Montgomery 4S (93.1 vs. 89.9). On ECG tasks, PROBMED surpasses MEDBind (the best deterministic ECG model) by 9.2 points on PTB-XL ZS (64.5 vs. 55.3) and 5.8 points on ICBEB 16S (90.1 vs. 87.8). In the multimodal CXR+ECG setting for CKD/CHD, PROBMED's combined embeddings yield a SUM of 458.6%, exceeding MEDBind's prior best multimodal result (435.3%) by 23.3 points, with the largest single-task gain of 7.8% in ZS CKD (78.1 vs. 71.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProbMED extends probabilistic contrastive learning (PCME, PCME++) from two-modal image-text retrieval to a four-modal medical setting (CXR, ECG, ECHO, text), adopting ImageBind's meta-learning strategy for multi-pair training without requiring complete patient records. It departs from PCME++ by replacing the purpose-built closed-form distance (CSD) with the Hellinger distance and introducing a Synthetic Instance Sampling (SIS) loss for within-modality distributional consistency. The architecture reuses pretrained medical encoders (Swin Transformer, XResNet-1d, BioBERT, ECHO-CLIP) with lightweight probabilistic heads, positioning itself as a probabilistic generalization of deterministic Med-VLPMs like CXR-CLIP, BiomedCLIP, and MEDBind.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply probabilistic embeddings to multi-modal medical binding, demonstrating that distributional representations enable practical use cases unavailable to deterministic models—uncertainty-based prompt filtering for zero-shot and distributional sampling for few-shot learning. The emergent alignment of untrained modality pairs (e.g., CXR→ECHO labels) validates the ImageBind-style binding hypothesis in the medical domain. However, the approach is constrained by limited non-text modality pairs (<1000 samples for most), training exclusively on MIMIC, and marginal ECHO improvements, leaving open whether the probabilistic framework scales to larger, more diverse medical corpora or whether the gains over strong deterministic baselines persist at scale.
