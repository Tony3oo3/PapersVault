## Problem

A common assumption in OOD detection is that higher in-distribution (ID) accuracy implies better OOD performance, yet torchvision v2 models achieve ~4% higher ID accuracy than v1 models while suffering a ~12% drop in OOD AUROC. This paper systematically diagnoses torchvision training recipes to identify which specific augmentations cause this degradation and proposes targeted fixes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250388/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods operate on standard RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224, CIFAR-100 at 32×32) and produce a scalar OOD score per sample, thresholded to yield an ID/OOD binary decision. AugDelete consumes a frozen pretrained backbone (CNN or ViT) and only updates the final linear layer using a small fraction (10–30%) of ID training samples, making it a lightweight post-hoc procedure. AugRevise trains the full network from scratch with a revised augmentation recipe and outputs logits compatible with any standard score function (MLS, KNN, NNGuide, etc.).

## Method

The paper first proves (Propositions 4.1–4.2) that label smoothing and mixup reduce the maximal logit of ID samples more than OOD samples during gradient descent, shrinking the ID–OOD logit gap and degrading logit-based scores like MLS. To fix this, AugDelete fine-tunes only the last fully connected layer with standard cross-entropy (no LS/mixup) on cached features, restoring logit separability at minimal cost. AugRevise, for training from scratch, removes label smoothing, retains data-based augmentations (Random Erasing, Trivial Augment), and introduces a virtual separation loss L_vs that enforces the ratio of maximal logits between ID and mixup-generated samples to equal 1/max(λ,1−λ), ensuring ID samples always have higher logits than their virtual OOD counterparts. The final training loss combines the standard cross-entropy, the RegMixup regularizer, and L_vs, followed by an AugDelete pass on the linear layer.

## Results

On the OpenOOD V1.5 benchmark, AugRevise with NNGuide achieves 84.51 AUROC / 49.52 FPR@95 / 82.10% ID accuracy on CIFAR-100, 89.31 AUROC / 37.56 FPR@95 / 87.67% on ImageNet-200, and 87.17 AUROC / 43.64 FPR@95 / 77.70% on ImageNet-1k. The post-hoc AugDelete method recovers OOD performance of torchvision v2 models (e.g., ResNet50-v2 MLS AUROC improves from 72.84 to 83.08 on ImageNet-1k) while preserving ID accuracy. AugRevise outperforms all training-based baselines (MOS, RegMixup, AugMix, LogitNorm) on both AUROC and FPR@95 across all three datasets, while also achieving the highest ID accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugRevise+NNGuide dominates all baselines on every dataset: on CIFAR-100 it beats the strongest baseline (v1+KNN, 81.29 AUROC) by 3.2 AUROC and the best training-based method (RegMixup+MSP, 79.94) by 4.6 AUROC; on ImageNet-200 it surpasses v1+NNGuide (87.83) by 1.5 AUROC and LogitNorm+MSP (87.85) by 1.5 AUROC; on ImageNet-1k it exceeds v1+FSEBO (86.82) by 0.35 AUROC and v1+NNGuide (86.68) by 0.5 AUROC. In FPR@95, AugRevise+NNGuide achieves 37.56 on ImageNet-200, substantially lower than LogitNorm+MSP (40.28) and NPOS+KNN (41.93). On ID accuracy, AugRevise (82.10% CIFAR-100, 87.67% IN-200, 77.70% IN-1k) outperforms all baselines except AugMix+MSP on IN-200 (87.01) and IN-1k (77.63), where the gap is marginal. MOS [17] is notably weaker on ImageNet-200 (75.15 AUROC) and ImageNet-1k (77.80 AUROC), confirming that virtual outlier synthesis without logit-space correction is insufficient. AugDelete (post-hoc) recovers ResNet50-v2 MLS from 72.84 to 83.08 AUROC on ImageNet-1k, matching v1 (83.02) while retaining v2's higher ID accuracy (80.31% vs 76.18%), but it cannot surpass v1 on feature-based scores (KNN, NNGuide) since the impaired features remain untouched.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a diagnostic study of torchvision v2 training recipes, building on the well-established OOD detection literature (post-hoc scores like MLS, EBO, KNN, NNGuide; training-based methods like LogitNorm, MOS, RegMixup). It extends prior observations that label smoothing and mixup harm failure detection [41, 48] and that mixup affects OOD robustness [30], but provides a more comprehensive analysis distinguishing label-based from data-based augmentations and a theoretical account via maximal logit reduction. The virtual separation loss in AugRevise departs from RegMixup's approach of treating mixup loss as an OOD regularizer by explicitly enforcing a logit-ratio constraint between ID and mixed samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since torchvision models underpin most OOD detection benchmarks (OpenOOD v1.5), the finding that standard v2 recipes (label smoothing + mixup) cause a ~12% OOD AUROC drop while improving ID accuracy has immediate practical consequences for the entire OOD detection community. AugDelete offers a nearly free post-hoc fix (retrain only the last layer), but it cannot repair feature-space degradation, leaving feature-based scores (KNN, NNGuide) still impaired relative to v1 models. Open questions include whether the diagnosis generalizes beyond torchvision to other training frameworks (e.g., timm, HuggingFace), whether the virtual separation loss transfers to non-mixup virtual sample generation, and whether deeper architectural changes are needed to fully close the v1-v2 OOD gap.
