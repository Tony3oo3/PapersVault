## Problem

Existing autoregressive 3D generation methods (e.g., MeshGPT, MeshXL) quantize individual mesh faces, producing sequences whose length scales linearly with face count (9× the number of faces). This makes them impractical for high-quality industrial assets with hundreds of thousands of faces. The paper asks whether 3D shapes can be encoded into a fixed-length discrete sequence independent of mesh complexity, enabling GPT-style next-token prediction for 3D generation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single 2D image (encoded by DINO ViT-B16) or a text prompt (encoded by FLAN-T5 XL) as a prefilling conditioning token prepended to the generation sequence. The output is a 3D mesh reconstructed from a neural occupancy field: the autoregressively generated triplane index sequence (fixed length 3×32×32 = 3072 tokens) is mapped back to continuous triplane features, upsampled to 256×256, and queried at 40,960 points (20,480 voxel + 20,480 near-surface) to predict per-point occupancy. The fixed sequence length is the key design constraint—regardless of whether the source mesh has 100 or 500,000 faces, the GPT always generates exactly 3072 tokens.

## Method

TAR3D is a two-stage pipeline: a 3D VQ-VAE that tokenizes shapes, and a 3D GPT that autoregressively generates the token sequence. The VQ-VAE encoder takes 81,920 surface points with normals, injects them via cross-attention into learnable query tokens arranged in a 3×32×32 triplane layout, and a codebook of 16,384 eight-dimensional entries quantizes the resulting triplane features. The decoder uses a plane-information-interaction (PII) design—two self-attention layers where the first processes each plane independently and the second concatenates all three planes along height for cross-plane interaction—followed by upsampling to 256×256 and an MLP occupancy head. The 3D GPT (24-layer decoder-only transformer, 16 heads, 1024 dim, following LlamaGen's GPT-L) predicts the next codebook index autoregressively, conditioned on the prompt embedding. A custom positional encoding called TriPE fuses a 2D RoPE (repeated three times with adjacent placement to encode in-plane spatial position) with a 1D RoPE (repeated h×w times to distinguish which of the three planes a token belongs to), preserving both intra-plane and inter-plane spatial structure in the sequence.

## Results

On the mixed ShapeNet + Objaverse evaluation set for image-to-3D generation, TAR3D achieves a PSNR of 13.626, SSIM of 0.763, CLIP score of 0.868, LPIPS of 0.216, Chamfer Distance of 0.066, and F-Score of 0.303, surpassing all five baselines on every metric. The largest margins are in 3D geometric quality: Chamfer Distance is 0.066 vs. the best baseline Michelangelo at 0.117 (44% reduction), and F-Score is 0.303 vs. 0.226 (34% improvement). In 2D visual quality, LPIPS drops from 0.278 (Michelangelo) to 0.216, and PSNR rises from 11.928 to 13.626.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAR3D outperforms all five quantitative baselines across every 2D and 3D metric. Among multiview-based methods, SyncDreamer, InstantMesh, and LGM all suffer from view-inconsistency artifacts; TAR3D reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.137–0.158 to 0.066 and F-Score from 0.172–0.179 to 0.303. Among 3D-native diffusion methods, Michelangelo is the strongest baseline (PSNR 11.928, Chamfer 0.117, F-Score 0.226), yet TAR3D still leads by 14% in PSNR, 44% in Chamfer Distance, and 34% in F-Score. Shap-E, the weakest baseline, lags TAR3D by 24% in PSNR and 58% in F-Score. Qualitatively, the paper notes that multiview methods produce discontinuous or incorrect geometry, while 3D diffusion methods (Michelangelo, LN3Diff) generate noisy surfaces, whereas TAR3D's autoregressive next-part prediction yields cleaner, more semantically faithful meshes for both image-to-3D and text-to-3D tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAR3D departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for 3D generation (DreamFusion, Michelangelo, LN3Diff) and from prior autoregressive mesh methods (MeshGPT, MeshXL, MeshAnything) that quantize individual mesh faces, resulting in sequence lengths proportional to face count. Instead, it borrows the codebook quantization mechanism from VQ-VAE and the decoder-only next-token prediction architecture from GPT/LlamaGen, applying them to a triplane latent representation that yields a fixed-length sequence regardless of mesh complexity. The TriPE positional encoding is a novel fusion of 2D and 1D RoPE tailored to the triplane structure, and the triplane upsampling follows Direct3D. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of discrete representation learning and autoregressive sequence modeling, transplanting the 'next-token prediction is all you need' philosophy from image generation (LlamaGen, Emu3) into the 3D domain for the first time with triplane quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed-length triplane sequence is a practical advantage over face-based quantization, making autoregressive 3D generation feasible for high-poly assets without the 9× face-count sequence blowup that limits MeshGPT/MeshXL. The substantial quantitative margins over both multiview and 3D-native diffusion baselines suggest the autoregressive route is a viable alternative to diffusion for 3D generation, potentially enabling better multimodal unification with LLMs. However, the evaluation is on a moderate-scale mixed ShapeNet/Objaverse set with a GPT-L model (24 layers), and the authors explicitly flag scaling to Objaverse-XL and more efficient sequence formulations as open problems. Texture generation is handled externally (SyncMVD), and the occupancy-field output requires mesh extraction, leaving the full pipeline less end-to-end than some competitors.
