## Problem

Dual-pixel (DP) depth estimation requires DP-depth paired data, but such datasets are scarce due to hardware constraints. Existing model-based simulators (CoC, L2R, Modeling, DDDNet) rely on ideal thin-lens or phase-symmetric models that ignore lens aberrations and the asymmetric phase splitting of real DP sensors, creating a significant domain gap that degrades depth estimation generalization to real DP images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The simulation pipeline takes an all-in-focus RGB image and a depth map from RGBD datasets (NYU Depth, KITTI) and produces 6-channel simulated DP images (left and right sub-pixel views at F/4, 512×768). Because the DP PSF is spatially varying (shift-variant), each pixel requires its own PSF kernel, making the rendering a per-pixel local convolution rather than a single global filter. The downstream DfDP model consumes the 6-channel DP image and outputs a per-pixel depth map of the same spatial resolution.

## Method

Sdirt computes spatially varying DP PSFs via ray tracing through a known lens system (Canon RF50mm, 4096 rays per object point), modeling the DP pixel's microlens and sub-pixel geometry to capture both aberrations and asymmetric phase splitting. A 5-layer MLP (512 neurons/layer) is trained with L2 loss to predict the left and right PSF kernels (size 21×21) from normalized (x,y) coordinates, replacing expensive per-pixel ray tracing at inference. The rendered DP image is produced by convolving each pixel's predicted PSF with the all-in-focus RGB image. For depth estimation, the paper adapts HANet's cost volume to include reverse disparity (blue arrows in Fig. 4d), since DP images exhibit opposite disparity directions for objects before and after the focus distance, unlike binocular stereo. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end on simulated DP images from NYU Depth/KITTI and evaluated zero-shot on the newly collected DP119 real-world test set.

## Results

On the DP119 test set, the DfDP model trained with Sdirt-simulated images achieves MAE of 0.0845 m and Acc-1 of 98.49% on planar scenes, MAE of 0.1197 m and Acc-1 of 94.74% on box scenes, and MAE of 0.2702 m and Acc-1 of 82.36% on casual scenes. In terms of PSF simulation fidelity, Sdirt achieves 0.915 NCC and 0.133 NSD against real DP PSFs, substantially outperforming all model-based baselines (best competitor: Modeling at 0.707 NCC / 0.423 NSD). For simulated DP image quality, Sdirt attains average PSNR/SSIM of 37.20/0.9845 across 56 planar scenes at varying depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sdirt decisively outperforms all four model-based baselines (CoC, L2R, Modeling, DDDNet) across every scene type and every metric on DP119. On planar scenes, Sdirt reduces MAE by 2.5–3.1× compared to baselines (0.0845 vs. 0.2085–0.2583) and boosts Acc-1 from 56–67% to 98.5%. The gap widens on box scenes, where Sdirt achieves 0.1197 MAE versus 0.3375–0.4177 for baselines (2.8–3.5× improvement). On the most challenging casual scenes with textureless regions, Sdirt still achieves 82.36% Acc-1, nearly 2.5× the second-best CoC (33.18%), while DDDNet drops to just 24.81%. In PSF simulation (Table 1), Sdirt's 0.915 NCC exceeds the best baseline (Modeling, 0.707) by 30% relative, confirming that the ray-traced PSFs are far more faithful to real optics. The consistent dominance across all three scene categories indicates that the domain gap addressed by Sdirt (aberrations + phase splitting) is the primary bottleneck for sim-to-real depth estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant families of DP PSF simulators—calibration-based (Xin et al., Li et al.) and model-based (L2R, Modeling, DDDNet)—by replacing their ideal thin-lens or phase-symmetric aberration models with full geometric ray tracing that captures both lens aberrations and the microlens/sub-pixel phase-splitting geometry. It borrows the ray-tracing infrastructure from differentiable optics work (Wang et al. 2022, Yang et al. 2023/2024) and the MLP-based PSF prediction paradigm from Yang et al. 2023, but applies them to the dual-pixel sensor structure for the first time. The DfDP backbone (HANet-style cost volume) is adapted from stereo matching by adding reverse-disparity bins to handle the bidirectional phase shift inherent to DP imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The domain gap between simulated and real DP data has been a persistent bottleneck for DfDP research; by closing it with physically accurate ray tracing, Sdirt enables training on freely available RGBD datasets while generalizing to a specific real camera without finetuning. The DP119 benchmark is a valuable contribution as the first real DP-depth paired test set with a known, open lens structure and fixed focus, enabling fair evaluation of simulation methods. However, the approach is tightly coupled to a fixed-focus lens with disclosed optical parameters (currently only Canon RF50mm on R6 Mark II), limiting immediate applicability to variable-focus or closed-hardware systems. An open question is whether the ray-traced PSF + MLP pipeline can be extended to variable focus, multi-wavelength (chromatic aberration) scenarios, or non-Canon DP sensors without prohibitive re-calibration.
