## Problem

Learning-based event optical flow estimators (CNN/frame-based) suffer significant accuracy degradation when transferred across domains, while model-based normal flow estimators (plane fitting) are robust but inaccurate. The difficulty of obtaining ground-truth normal flow for event cameras has further impeded learning-based normal flow estimation, leaving a gap for an accurate, transferable, supervised approach that exploits the local nature of normal flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250782/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw asynchronous event stream (t, x, y) in undistorted, normalized camera coordinates (focal length 1), where each event's neighborhood is defined by a normalized Euclidean ball with tunable radii δt, δx, δy and a variable number of neighbors. It outputs a 2D normal flow vector per event, a scalar uncertainty score (circular standard deviation from K rotated ensemble predictions), and—when combined with IMU angular velocity—a 3D unit translation direction for egomotion.

## Method

The core architecture encodes each event's local neighborhood using VecKM, a vectorized kernel-mixture encoder that applies random Fourier features to a sparse adjacency matrix, producing a fixed-dimensional complex vector without explicit grouping or downsampling. A small MLP then maps this encoding to a 2D normal flow prediction. Training uses a novel two-term loss: a radial term that constrains the prediction to lie on the circle with the GT optical flow as its diameter (enforcing the normal-flow orthogonality constraint), and an angular term that prevents the trivial zero-flow solution by encouraging directional alignment with the optical flow. Exploiting the equivariance of motion estimation, the method applies uniform random rotation, random scaling, and random sampling as data augmentation during training, and at inference computes uncertainty by averaging K rotation-equivariant predictions and measuring their circular standard deviation. For egomotion, the depth-positivity constraint is reformulated as a linear SVM classification problem over derotated normal flow magnitudes, yielding a robust translation direction estimate.

## Results

On EVIMO2-imo (the key benchmark with fast independently moving objects), the proposed method trained on the union of all three datasets (M+D+E) achieves an average PEE of 0.396 and %Pos of 97.8%, substantially outperforming all baselines. Even when trained on a single dataset (e.g., EVIMO-imo only, 2.74 min of data), it reaches PEE 0.423 and %Pos 97.9%, still far ahead of the best frame-based baseline (E-RAFT trained on DSEC: PEE 0.705, %Pos 87.9%). On MVSEC, where scenes are low-resolution and static, TCM achieves slightly lower PEE (0.516 vs. 0.995 for Ours-M), but the gap reverses dramatically on EVIMO2. For egomotion estimation on EVIMO2-sfm, the SVM-based solver reduces RMS translation error by ~35% compared to the depth-positivity baseline (e.g., 0.136 vs. 0.217 m/s on sfm 03_04_0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed point-based method decisively outperforms all frame-based baselines on EVIMO2, where independently moving objects stress-test the estimators: it achieves PEE 0.396–0.423 vs. 0.705 (E-RAFT-D), 1.105 (TCM-D), 1.383 (TCM-M), 1.470 (E-RAFT-M), and 1.800 (MultiCM). Against the closest point-based competitor, PointNet (trained on EVIMO-imo), the method reduces PEE from 0.933 to 0.423 (a 55% improvement) and raises %Pos from 95.0% to 97.9%, benefiting from VecKM's ability to use all neighboring events without downsampling. On MVSEC, the advantage narrows: TCM-D achieves PEE 0.516 vs. 0.995 for Ours-M, because MVSEC's sparse, static scenes favor CNNs' large receptive fields for smoothing. However, the paper argues this is a degenerate case and that the method's strength is cross-domain transferability, which frame-based methods lack. For egomotion estimation, the SVM solver beats the depth-positivity baseline [7] on 8 out of 10 EVIMO2-sfm sequences (e.g., 0.136 vs. 0.217 m/s RMS on sfm 03_04_0), with the two losses attributed to sequences where the depth-positivity constraint happens to align better with ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes VecKM, a local point cloud geometry encoder originally designed for 3D point clouds, as the backbone for per-event normal flow estimation from raw event streams — the first learning-based point-based method applied to this low-level event vision task. It departs from the dominant frame-based CNN paradigm (E-RAFT, TCM) by operating purely on local event neighborhoods in normalized camera coordinates, and from model-based normal flow estimators (plane fitting, PCA) by introducing supervised training with a novel two-term radial-angular loss that uses optical flow as supervision. For the downstream egomotion solver, it builds on the depth-positivity-constraint formulation of Barranco et al. but reformulates the optimization as a linear SVM problem for improved stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain transferability remains a critical weakness of learning-based event flow estimators, and this paper demonstrates that a purely local, point-based approach with rotation augmentation and normalized coordinates can match or exceed global CNN-based methods across nine transfer settings — a practical recipe for deploying event flow estimators across heterogeneous camera hardware. The uncertainty quantification via ensemble inference provides a principled filtering mechanism for downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the supervised requirement (GT optical flow) can be relaxed to self-supervision, whether the approach scales to higher-resolution event cameras, and whether global context can be carefully reintroduced for tasks beyond per-event flow.
