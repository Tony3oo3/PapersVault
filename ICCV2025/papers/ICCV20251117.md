## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for VLMs are fragmented across isolated datasets, limited to single visual modalities (images or videos), and systematically underrepresent key cognitive factors such as view-taking (spatial orientation) and dynamic scene reasoning. No unified benchmark exists that covers the full spectrum of spatial intelligence as decomposed in cognitive science literature, making it impossible to holistically assess a VLM's visuospatial capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251117/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation item consists of a visual stimulus (a single natural or synthetic image, a pair of ego/exo images, or a short video clip) paired with a multiple-choice text question containing 2–6 answer options. The model outputs a single selected option, which is scored using a Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA) metric that normalizes for the varying number of choices per question, with GPT-4o serving as an automated judge for open-ended reformulations. The benchmark spans 8,068 QA pairs drawn from 31 source datasets, with 4,260 image-based and 3,808 video-based items.

## Method

SITE is constructed via a two-path curation strategy. The bottom-up path surveys 30 existing VQA datasets, filters 223,083 candidate samples through GPT-4o into six coarse spatial categories (Counting, Relationship, Localization, 3D Info, Multi-View, Movement), and applies stratified sampling to balance category representation. The top-down path applies three cognitive-science classification systems—figural/vista/environmental scales, spatial visualization vs. orientation, and intrinsic/extrinsic × static/dynamic—to identify coverage gaps, revealing a critical absence of view-taking and dynamic tasks. To fill these gaps, two novel task types are designed on Ego-Exo4D: Ego-Exo View Association (matching egocentric and exocentric views of the same scene) and Shuffled Frames Reordering (predicting temporal order of key motion events across viewpoints). All tasks are standardized into a unified multiple-choice VQA format, and evaluation uses the CAA metric with GPT-4o-based automated scoring to ensure consistent, reproducible assessment across 9 state-of-the-art VLMs.

## Results

On the full SITE benchmark (8,068 QA pairs), the best-performing VLM GPT-4o achieves 37.8% Chance-Adjusted Accuracy (CAA) overall, falling ~30 points below the human upper bound of 67.5%. Among open-source models, InternVL-2.5-8B (32.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (31.4%) lead, while Gemini-1.5-Pro scores 32.5%. Multi-view reasoning is the weakest category for all VLMs (all below 22% CAA vs. 87.5% human), and 3D understanding scores remain below 27% across all models. On the novel view-association task, humans achieve 100% accuracy while GPT-4o reaches only 35.7% (ego→exo), and on frames reordering GPT-4o scores below chance (−2.01% CAA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads overall (37.8% CAA) and dominates on localization (56.0%), 3D understanding (26.9%), multi-view reasoning (22.0%), and spatial relationship reasoning (54.6%), but it is outperformed by Qwen2.5-VL-7B on counting (52.6% vs. 44.6%) and by InternVL-2.5-8B on movement prediction (28.7% vs. 18.4%). Among open-source models, InternVL-2.5-8B (32.8%) edges out Qwen2.5-VL-7B (31.4%) overall, while Qwen2.5-VL-7B is the best open-source model on localization (44.1%) and counting (52.6%). Gemini-1.5-Pro (32.5%) is competitive with the top open-source models but trails GPT-4o by 5.3 points overall. On the novel view-association task, GPT-4o achieves 35.7% (ego→exo) and 20.7% (exo→ego), far below the 100% human ceiling, while on frames reordering GPT-4o drops below chance (−2.01%/−5.16%), suggesting it cannot map temporal events across viewpoints. The paper further shows a 0.902 Pearson correlation between SITE CAA scores and robot manipulation success on LIBERO-Spatial, with Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieving 38% success rate while smaller models fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying 30 existing spatial-reasoning datasets (VQA, GQA, CLEVR, VSR, CV-Bench, 3DSR-Bench, VSI-Bench, etc.) under a single standardized multiple-choice VQA format, while simultaneously grounding the benchmark's structure in three cognitive-science classification systems (figural/vista/environmental scales, visualization vs. orientation, intrinsic/extrinsic × static/dynamic). Prior benchmarks each cover only a narrow slice—CV-Bench lacks viewpoint transformation, 3DSR-Bench is single-image only, VSI-Bench is confined to indoor video—so SITE departs from them by explicitly filling the view-taking and dynamic-scene gaps with two novel task types (ego-exo view association and shuffled frame reordering) built on Ego-Exo4D. The bottom-up dataset survey combined with top-down cognitive-science taxonomy is itself a methodological departure from prior benchmarking efforts that typically adopt a single organizing principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SITE provides the first comprehensive, multi-modal (image + video, single + multi-view) evaluation of VLM spatial intelligence, and its results reveal that even GPT-4o lags humans by ~32% overall and by over 70% on multi-view reasoning, with all models scoring below 10% CAA on that category. The demonstrated 0.902 Pearson correlation between SITE scores and robot-manipulation success on LIBERO-Spatial (though based on only four models) suggests that spatial-intelligence benchmarks are a meaningful proxy for embodied-AI readiness, motivating targeted improvements in view-taking and dynamic spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether the view-taking gap will close with scale or requires explicit multi-view training data, and whether the correlation generalizes beyond the small toy experiment to broader embodied tasks.
