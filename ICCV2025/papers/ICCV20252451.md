## Problem

Prior knowledge distillation research focuses on improving the transfer mechanism (loss functions, feature matching, relation modeling) but largely overlooks how the selection and weighting of training data affects distillation efficiency. This paper asks: which samples carry the most useful knowledge for a student model, and how can we exploit this to train faster without sacrificing accuracy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252451/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) and produces class labels, but its intervention is at the data level rather than the model level. It consumes two per-sample KL divergence signals computed during training — teacher-student KL (KL_TS) for subsampling and teacher-ground-truth KL (KL_TG) for loss weighting — and outputs a weighted, subsampled training set that replaces the full dataset in each epoch.

## Method

KDAS combines two complementary techniques. First, quantity-based subsampling selects the top-K% samples with the highest teacher-student KL divergence every τ epochs, with K following a curriculum that decreases from 60% to 40% over training (more samples early when the information gap is large, fewer later). Second, quality-calibrated loss weighting assigns each sample a multiplicative weight w(x) based on its teacher-ground-truth KL divergence: samples with KL_TG below the 20th percentile (teacher too confident, little dark knowledge) or above the 80th percentile (teacher potentially wrong) receive linearly decreasing weights bounded below by γ=0.5. The final loss is L_KDAS = (1−α)L_H + α·w(x)·L_S, applied only over the subsampled set. This plug-and-play strategy is applied on top of vanilla KD, DKD, and LogitSTD, yielding up to 36% training-time reduction with equal or improved accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, KDAS applied to vanilla KD achieves up to +2.14% top-1 accuracy improvement (e.g., WRN-40-2→ResNet8x4: 73.97%→76.11%) while reducing training time by ~28% on average across nine teacher-student pairs. On ImageNet, KDAS with vanilla KD yields +0.22–0.36% accuracy gains with 35–36% training time reduction. When combined with advanced methods (DKD, LogitSTD), KDAS provides smaller but consistent accuracy improvements (+0.07–1.18%) with ~10% training time savings. On vision transformer students (DeiT-Ti, T2T-ViT7, PiT-Ti, PVT-Ti), KDAS+KD achieves +1.40–4.56% accuracy over vanilla KD with ~25% faster training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla KD [9], KDAS wins on every tested configuration: accuracy improves by +0.05 to +2.14% while training time drops by 27–36%, with the largest gains on cross-architecture pairs (e.g., WRN-40-2→ResNet8x4: +2.14%). Against DKD [33], KDAS provides smaller but consistent gains (+0.00 to +0.75% accuracy) with ~9–13% training time reduction; the one tie is R56→R20 where both achieve 71.97%. Against LogitSTD [24], KDAS improves accuracy by +0.08 to +1.18% (largest on R32x4→WRN-40-2: 77.92→79.10) with ~9–12% time savings. In the vision transformer experiments (Table 4), KDAS+KD substantially outperforms AT [32] (e.g., DeiT-Ti: 77.81% vs. 73.51%) but falls short of the more advanced LG [14] and AutoKD [15] methods (e.g., DeiT-Ti: 77.81% vs. 78.15% and 78.58%), indicating that KDAS is most impactful as a plug-in accelerator for standard KD rather than a replacement for specialized distillation objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard KD framework of Hinton et al. [9] but shifts focus from how to better transfer knowledge (logit-based, feature-based, relation-based methods) to how to better select which data to use during transfer. It draws a conceptual parallel to hard example mining [22] but deliberately departs by using only the teacher-student KL divergence (soft target) rather than total training loss, and inverts the typical curriculum learning progression by starting with more samples and decreasing over time. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play complement to existing KD losses (DKD, LogitSTD) rather than a replacement, positioning data selection as an orthogonal axis of improvement that prior KD research has largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: a simple adaptive sampling strategy that reduces training time by 28–36% while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, applicable across CNN and transformer architectures and composable with any existing KD loss. This is particularly relevant as the field pushes toward deploying smaller models on resource-constrained devices, where distillation training cost becomes a non-trivial bottleneck. Open questions include whether the KL-based quantity/quality metrics generalize beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation), whether the fixed percentile thresholds (20th/80th) for quality penalization are truly universal, and whether the periodic teacher forward pass for KL_TS computation remains affordable when the teacher is orders of magnitude larger than the student.
