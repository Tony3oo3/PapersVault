## Problem

Current MLLMs lack the ability to autonomously control their own visual perception processes; they depend on manually designed pipelines for region cropping, feature enhancement, or multi-encoder integration. This means the model cannot decide on its own when to re-encode a specific image region or when to supplement its vision features with an additional encoder, limiting its adaptability across diverse visual tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251271/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (at 224 or 512 max resolution) and a text query as input. During autoregressive generation, the MLLM may emit special Visual Perception Tokens before producing its final text answer: a Region Selection Token encodes a grid-cell coordinate pair (on an 8×8 grid) specifying a crop region, while a Vision Re-Encoding Token carries a free-form hidden state that conditions a cross-attention projector over additional vision features. The triggered perception process appends new image embeddings to the LLM context, after which the model generates the natural-language response.

## Method

The paper introduces two new token types into the MLLM vocabulary. The Region Selection Token is a 6-token sequence (start, x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max, end) using dedicated cell-index tokens on a k×k grid (k=8); upon emission, the image is cropped to that cell region and re-encoded by the original vision encoder, with the resulting embeddings appended to the LLM input. The Vision Re-Encoding Token is a 3-token sequence whose middle <Re-Encoding_Control> token has no supervised loss; its hidden state serves as keys and values in a cross-attention projector that processes features from an additional encoder (DINOv2, SAM, or the original CLIP branch) as queries, enabling fine-grained, learned control over which supplementary features reach the LLM. Training uses 829k samples across four task categories; Region Selection Tokens are learned via standard next-token prediction, while the Re-Encoding Control token is trained with a mask-modeling strategy where 50% of samples restrict answer tokens to attend only to the VPT tokens and their successors, forcing the hidden state to encode task-relevant control information. The base model is Qwen2-VL (2B fully fine-tuned, 7B via LoRA), and the approach yields a 30.9% average improvement for the 2B model, outperforming the 7B baseline by 20.0%.

## Results

On the average score across all benchmarks (GPT-4o evaluated, 0–1 scale), the 2B model with Visual Perception Tokens (DINO, Free Choice) at 512 resolution achieves 0.749, improving over the 2B baseline (0.572) by 30.9% and outperforming the 7B baseline (0.624) by 20.0%. The largest gains appear on Fine-Grained VQA (CUB Birds: 0.911 vs. 0.697 for 7B) and Spatial Reasoning (VSR: 0.733 vs. 0.641 for 7B). On Text/OCR tasks, DocVQA improves from 0.301 (2B) to 0.576 (2B+VPT), and hallucination (POPE) rises from 0.847 to 0.950.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline family is Qwen2-VL (2B and 7B) without Visual Perception Tokens. The 2B+VPT model (DINO, Free Choice, 512 res) achieves an average of 0.749, surpassing the 7B baseline (0.624) by 0.125 absolute (20.0% relative) and the 2B baseline (0.572) by 0.177 (30.9% relative). The advantage is most pronounced on Fine-Grained VQA (CUB Birds: 0.911 vs. 0.697, +32.7% over 7B) and Spatial Reasoning (VSR: 0.733 vs. 0.641, +14.4% over 7B), while on General VQA (LLaVA Instruction Tuning) the 2B+VPT model (0.802) slightly trails the 7B baseline (0.780) only marginally. On the zero-shot hallucination benchmark POPE, 2B+VPT (0.950) substantially outperforms both 2B (0.847) and 7B (0.866). When the 7B model is also augmented with VPT (CLIP re-encoding), it reaches 0.773 average, confirming that the token mechanism scales with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'crop and re-input' mechanism from Visual CoT [26], which used natural-language bounding boxes to trigger region cropping, by replacing them with specialized Region Selection Tokens that operate in a non-linguistic token space, avoiding the coordinate-alignment difficulties MLLMs face with pixel-level boxes. It also departs from prior visual prompting and perception-enhancement work [35, 36, 46] that relies on externally designed pipelines or auxiliary VLMs by making the perception control signal an autoregressive output of the MLLM itself. The Vision Re-Encoding Token with its cross-attention projector conditioned on the token's hidden states, trained via a mask-modeling strategy, is a novel architectural element not present in prior MLLM perception-enhancement approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 2B model with Visual Perception Tokens outperforms a 7B model without them (0.749 vs 0.624 average) suggests that iterative, self-controlled perception can be a more effective path to improving MLLM visual understanding than simply scaling parameters, which has practical implications for deployment on resource-constrained settings. The 'Free Choice' mechanism, where the model autonomously decides when and which token to emit, points toward a broader paradigm of MLLMs as active perception agents rather than passive encoders. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Qwen2-VL to other architectures, whether it extends to video or 3D inputs, and whether the GPT-4o-based evaluation fully captures the quality gains. The reliance on an additional frozen vision encoder (DINO/SAM) also raises questions about the computational overhead in real-time settings.
