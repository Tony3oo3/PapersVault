## Problem

Existing label noise learning methods (sample selection, label correction, sample re-weighting) typically depend on prior knowledge such as noise rates, predefined thresholds, or clean validation subsets to sustain performance, limiting their practicality in real-world scenarios where such information is unavailable. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a prior-knowledge-free framework that eliminates reliance on these external priors while maintaining robustness across diverse noise settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of RGB images paired with noisy one-hot labels (no knowledge of the noise rate or pattern is required) and outputs a robust classifier that predicts clean class labels. For synthetic benchmarks (CIFAR100N, CIFAR80N), a 7-layer CNN backbone is used; for real-world fine-grained datasets (Web-Aircraft, Web-Bird, Web-Car, Food101N), a ResNet50 pre-trained on ImageNet-1K serves as the backbone. The training pipeline has two phases: a warm-up phase using standard cross-entropy on the raw noisy labels, followed by a robust training phase where the noisy labels are never directly used for optimization.

## Method

CA2C trains two twin models with identical architectures under distinct learning paradigms: the P-model performs partial label learning (PLL) using Top-K candidate labels, while the N-model performs negative learning (NL) using the complementary (non-candidate) labels, thereby avoiding direct exposure to noisy labels. An asymmetric co-training mechanism then enables cross-guidance label generation, where each model's predictions generate supervision for the other (P-model predictions produce complementary labels for N-model; N-model predictions produce candidate labels for P-model), facilitating knowledge exchange and mitigating error accumulation. A confidence-based re-weighting strategy maintains a memory bank tracking the frequency of candidate labels per sample across epochs, computing a confidence coefficient that re-weights a joint loss combining hard and soft disambiguation terms to improve the P-model's tolerance to disambiguation failures. The entire framework requires no noise rate, threshold, or clean validation set, distinguishing it from prior co-training approaches that differentiate twin models only by data volume or random initialization.

## Results

On synthetic noisy datasets, CA2C achieves an average test accuracy of 58.57% across CIFAR100N and CIFAR80N under Sym-20%, Sym-80%, and Asym-40% noise settings, outperforming the strongest baselines ACT (56.63%) and SED (55.91%) by 1.94% and 2.66% respectively. On the challenging Asym-40% setting, CA2C reaches 65.59% on CIFAR100N and 65.71% on CIFAR80N, surpassing ACT by 2.11% and 1.31%. On real-world noisy datasets, CA2C achieves 86.43% average accuracy on Web-Aircraft/Bird/Car (vs. 85.83% for SED) and 86.83% on Food101N (vs. 86.24% for VRI), all without requiring any prior knowledge such as noise rates or clean validation subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA2C outperforms all 21 compared baselines in average accuracy across the six synthetic noise settings (58.57% vs. 56.63% for ACT and 55.91% for SED). The margin is most pronounced on CIFAR100N Asym-40%, where CA2C (65.59%) beats ACT (63.48%) by 2.11% and SED (58.29%) by 7.30%, indicating superior robustness under asymmetric noise. On CIFAR80N Sym-80%, CA2C (40.47%) trails SED (42.57%) by 2.10%, the only setting where a competitor edges ahead, though CA2C still leads in overall average. On real-world Web datasets, CA2C achieves 86.43% average, surpassing SED (85.83%) and ACT (85.58%) by 0.60% and 0.85% respectively. On Food101N, CA2C (86.83%) exceeds VRI (86.24%) and CoDis (86.13%) by 0.59% and 0.70%. Notably, CA2C achieves these gains without requiring noise rates, predefined thresholds, or clean validation subsets, unlike most baselines (e.g., DivideMix, L2RW, L2B) that depend on such priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

CA2C builds directly on two prior works from the same group: NPN (AAAI 2024), which first decomposed the noisy-label problem into partial label learning and negative learning within a single model, and ACT (ACM MM 2024), which introduced asymmetric co-training by varying training data volume between twin models. The key conceptual departure is that CA2C assigns PLL and NL to *separate* models with identical architectures rather than combining them in one network, and differentiates the twin models by learning paradigm rather than data quantity. This paradigm-deconstruction strategy, combined with cross-guidance label generation between the models, positions CA2C as a synthesis that addresses the error-accumulation limitation of NPN and the limited model-diversity limitation of ACT simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prior-knowledge-free design is practically significant: real-world noise rates and clean validation subsets are typically unavailable, yet most competing LNL methods (DivideMix, L2RW, Co-teaching) require at least one such prior. The clean separation of PLL and NL into independent models offers a reusable architectural principle for other robust-learning settings. However, the method is validated only on image classification with modest backbones (7-layer CNN, ResNet50), and the fixed K=2 candidate set may not generalize to very high-dimensional label spaces. Open questions include whether the paradigm-deconstruction idea transfers to tasks like segmentation or detection, and whether the twin-model overhead is justified at scale.
