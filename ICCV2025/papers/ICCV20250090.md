## Problem

Previous task-driven image restoration (TDIR) methods handle individual degradation types separately and struggle when images suffer from multiple complex degradations simultaneously, leaving minimal clues for restoration. While diffusion priors like StableDiffusion can generate visually plausible results, their standard form—generating from pure noise with ~50 denoising steps—fails to restore task-relevant details, producing redundant content that dilutes crucial information needed for downstream vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250090/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images degraded by complex mixtures (downsampling at scale 1–16, Gaussian blur σ∈[0,8], noise σ∈[0,10], JPEG quality 50–100) and produces a restored image that feeds into a jointly trained task network. The final output is the task prediction (class label, pixel-wise segmentation mask, or detection boxes) computed as H(EDTR(I_LQ)), where the restoration and task network are co-optimized. The restored image itself is also a valid output, with wavelet color correction ensuring low-frequency fidelity to the pre-restored input.

## Method

EDTR restructures the diffusion process for TDIR in three ways: (1) pre-restoration and partial diffusion—LQ images are first restored by a pixel-error-based SwinIR network, then encoded and corrupted with only mild noise (t_p=200 out of T=1000) before denoising begins, directly preserving LQ clues rather than generating from pure noise; (2) short-step denoising (1–4 steps instead of ~50) to prevent redundant detail generation that dilutes task-relevant information; (3) tailored training losses—high-level feature (HLF) loss measuring feature distance between restored and HQ images in two task networks, feature matching (FM) loss aligning the task network's features with an HQ-pretrained reference, and standard task loss, all applied in an alternating schedule (EDTR updated by HLF, task network updated by task+FM loss). The architecture uses a frozen StableDiffusion-2.1 backbone with trainable ControlNet and VAE decoder, plus the SwinIR pre-restoration module, and applies wavelet color correction at decode time for faithful reconstruction.

## Results

Under Mixture-A degradation (8× downsampling + JPEG), EDTR-4 step achieves 74.1% top-1 accuracy on CUB200, 65.3% mIoU on PASCAL VOC2012 segmentation, and 33.4% mAP on detection, outperforming the previous SOTA TDIR method SR4IR by +2.6%, +9.1%, and +11.1% respectively. Under the more severe Mixture-B degradation (variable downsampling + blur + noise + JPEG), EDTR-4 step reaches 68.4% accuracy, 62.9% mIoU, and 31.9% mAP, improving over SR4IR by +5.0%, +11.9%, and +9.3%. The detection improvement is particularly striking, with EDTR-4 step achieving ~50% relative mAP gain over SR4IR in the Mixture-A setting (33.4% vs. 22.3%). EDTR also achieves the highest Q-Align perceptual quality scores across all three tasks, confirming visually pleasing restoration alongside task performance gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDTR consistently outperforms all four baselines across every task and degradation setting. Against the strongest TDIR baseline SR4IR [20], EDTR-4 step gains +2.6% classification accuracy and +9.1% mIoU under Mixture-A, and +5.0% accuracy and +11.9% mIoU under Mixture-B, with the largest relative gain in detection (~50% mAP improvement in Mixture-A: 33.4% vs. 22.3%). The pixel-error-based SwinIR [27] serves as a strong restoration baseline but lags significantly in task performance (e.g., 21.0% mAP vs. 33.4% for EDTR-4 step in Mixture-A detection), confirming that pixel-fidelity alone is insufficient for TDIR. The earlier TDIR methods TDSR [14] and RSRSSN [67] are substantially weaker, with TDSR achieving only 16.2% mAP in Mixture-A detection and RSRSSN reaching 22.3%, both far below EDTR. Notably, EDTR also surpasses all baselines on the Q-Align perceptual quality metric, indicating that the diffusion prior enables both better task-relevant detail restoration and more visually appealing outputs simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based image restoration (DiffBIR, StableSR) and task-driven image restoration (TDIR) methods like SR4IR and TDSR. It departs from conventional SD-based IR by rejecting the standard pipeline of generating from pure noise with ~50 denoising steps, instead proposing partial diffusion (starting from a pre-restored LQ image with mild noise) and short-step denoising (1–4 steps). It also extends prior TDIR work, which handles single degradation types, to a more realistic multi-degradation setting, and integrates the diffusion prior directly within task-driven training rather than treating it as a separate stage (as in UniRestore).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fewer denoising steps (1–4) outperform the conventional 50 steps for TDIR challenges the standard assumption that more diffusion steps yield better downstream task performance, offering a practical and computationally efficient recipe for adapting diffusion priors to task-oriented restoration. The method's generalization to unseen datasets and real-world images suggests broad applicability, though the reliance on a frozen SD backbone plus ControlNet plus SwinIR makes the pipeline computationally heavy. Open questions include whether the short-step insight transfers to video restoration or other generative priors, and whether the approach scales to tasks requiring even finer-grained spatial details.
