## Problem

Existing universal image restoration methods are trained on small-scale synthetic datasets with limited, isolated degradation types, creating a severe domain gap that caps their generalization in real-world scenarios. Publicly available real-world paired datasets are too small and lack coupled degradations to support the training of robust foundation models for image restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250912/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image (LQ) as input, which is cropped into 1024×1024 patches for processing, and produces a restored high-quality RGB image (HQ). A degradation type indicator is used to route the generalist's intermediate output to the appropriate specialist model from an expert pool (weather, illumination, or text repair experts). The diffusion process runs with only 4 timesteps at inference, and the final output is stitched back from the restored patches.

## Method

FoundIR is an ensemble framework combining a diffusion-based generalist model and multiple degradation-aware specialist models. The generalist model employs a residual diffusion process that transfers residual components between LQ and HQ images in a Markov chain, with an added condition constraint β_t on the degraded input to prevent collapse into degradation-specific features, thereby learning a degradation-agnostic common representation space. Training uses an incremental learning strategy that first learns on isolated degradation data (class D_i) and then jointly trains on both isolated and coupled degradation data (class D_c), mitigating catastrophic forgetting at million-scale. The specialist models (weather, illumination, text repair experts) refine the generalist's output for specific challenging scenarios, with the generalist's restoration result guiding the specialist's adaptation to reduce training cost.

## Results

On the proposed FoundIR benchmark (20 degradation types), FoundIR achieves the best average PSNR across all isolated and coupled degradations, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffIR by 4.14 dB on the L+N (low-light + noise) category. On public real-world benchmarks, FoundIR leads on all four: 4KRD (26.60 PSNR), RealRain-1K (31.34 PSNR), HazeRD (20.73 PSNR), and UHD-LL (16.23 PSNR). On unknown real-world tasks (TOLED/POLED under-display camera restoration), FoundIR achieves 29.67 and 17.04 PSNR respectively, surpassing DiffUIR (29.55 / 15.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundIR outperforms all 16 compared baselines across the 20 degradation types on the proposed benchmark. The closest competitor is DiffIR [54] (24.68 avg PSNR), which FoundIR surpasses by 4.14 dB specifically on the L+N coupled task; DiffUIR [62] (23.27 avg) and Real-ESRGAN [52] (24.26 avg) trail further behind. On public benchmarks, FoundIR leads by a wide margin: +3.18 dB over DiffUIR on RealRain-1K (31.34 vs 28.16), +5.31 dB on HazeRD (20.73 vs 15.42), and +1.22 dB on 4KRD (26.60 vs 25.38). On the unknown-task benchmarks TOLED and POLED, FoundIR edges out DiffUIR by 0.12 dB and 1.42 dB respectively, confirming generalization to unseen degradation types. Notably, retraining existing universal methods (AirNet, PromptIR, DiffIR, DiffUIR) on the proposed dataset yields consistent gains (e.g., DiffUIR improves by 6.06 dB on RealRain-1K), validating the dataset's contribution independently of the model architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundIR builds directly on the residual diffusion framework of RDDM [31], extending its forward process with condition constraints β_t to prevent collapse onto a single degradation mode, and adopts the reverse process and loss from DiffUIR [62]. The ensemble architecture (generalist + specialist expert pool) is inspired by RestoreAgent [7] and LLM ensemble paradigms, but repurposed for image restoration with a key departure: the generalist learns degradation-agnostic common representations rather than degradation-specific features, which prior universal methods (AirNet, PromptIR, DiffIR) all require the network to extract from inputs. The incremental learning strategy (isolated → coupled degradation classes) departs from the standard practice of mixing all degradation types in each batch, explicitly addressing catastrophic forgetting at million-scale data volumes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The million-scale paired dataset with 20 degradation types (7 isolated, 13 coupled) is the paper's most impactful contribution, as it removes the data bottleneck that has limited universal image restoration to small synthetic benchmarks and enables genuine foundation-model-scale training. The data scaling experiments (10%→100%) provide concrete evidence that real-world data volume continues to yield gains, a finding that should guide future dataset construction in the field. Open questions include whether the specialist models can be made fully adaptive to truly unknown degradations (the current expert pool is hand-curated), whether the mechatronic shooting system's specific degradation combinations generalize to all real-world coupling patterns, and whether the approach extends to video restoration or higher resolutions without prohibitive compute costs.
