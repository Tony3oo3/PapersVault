## Problem

Existing part-aware 3D reconstruction methods either rely on 3D supervision, produce coarse geometric primitives that lose fine detail, or yield poor rendering quality when decomposing scenes into meaningful parts. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both high-fidelity appearance reconstruction and geometrically accurate part decomposition in a fully self-supervised manner from multi-view images alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images (49–64 views for DTU, 50 rendered views for ShapeNet) along with per-pixel foreground masks, requiring background-free scenes. It produces a two-level output: a block-level decomposition where each part is a parametric superquadric mesh with 2D Gaussians attached to its surface for texture, and a point-level refinement where Gaussians are freed from the superquadric constraint to capture irregular geometry. The number of parts is not fixed a priori but adapts during training via an add/remove mechanism.

## Method

PartGS introduces a hybrid representation where each scene is decomposed into blocks, each consisting of a superquadric (parameterized by shape exponents ε₁, ε₂ and scales s₁, s₂, s₃) with 2D Gaussians uniformly sampled on its triangular faces. Crucially, Gaussian rotation and scale are derived from the superquadric face geometry via Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization rather than optimized independently, creating a coupled parameter space that is more compact than standard Gaussian splatting. Training is self-supervised through a rendering loss (L1 + D-SSIM) augmented with four novel regularizers—coverage (ray-based occupancy matching), overlap (Monte Carlo soft-occupancy penalty), parsimony (opacity L2 norm), and opacity entropy (cross-entropy with mask labels)—that prevent local minima in superquadric shape optimization. A two-stage strategy first optimizes the coupled block-level representation, then decouples Gaussians for point-level refinement with an additional enter-loss that penalizes Gaussians penetrating adjacent blocks. The block count adapts dynamically: DBSCAN clusters uncovered points to spawn new blocks, while blocks whose opacity falls below a threshold are pruned.

## Results

On DTU, PartGS (point-level) achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.98 and PSNR of 35.04 dB, outperforming all part-aware baselines (DBW: CD 4.73, PSNR 16.44; PartNeRF: CD 9.59, PSNR 17.97) while approaching the non-part-aware 2DGS (CD 0.81, PSNR 34.07). On ShapeNet, point-level PartGS reaches a mean CD of 1.25 and PSNR of 38.39 dB across four categories, surpassing DBW (CD 4.81, PSNR 24.73) by a wide margin. Training time is approximately 40 minutes per scene, roughly 10× faster than PartNeRF (~8 h) and 3× faster than DBW (~2 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest part-aware baseline DBW [36], PartGS (point-level) reduces Chamfer distance from 4.73 to 0.98 (79% improvement) and boosts PSNR from 16.44 to 35.04 dB (+18.6 dB) on DTU, while training 3× faster. PartNeRF [47] is dominated on every metric: PartGS achieves 9.8× lower CD, +17 dB PSNR, and 12× shorter training time. Compared to the non-part-aware 2DGS [21], PartGS (point-level) trades a small CD increase (0.98 vs 0.81) for superior PSNR (35.04 vs 34.07) and LPIPS (0.015 vs 0.019), at the cost of 4× longer training (40 min vs 10 min). Against Neuralangelo [30], PartGS is slightly worse in CD (0.98 vs 0.61) but achieves higher PSNR (35.04 vs 33.84) and is over 15× faster. EMS [32] and MBF [41] require 3D ground-truth point clouds as input and cannot render images, making them non-competitive in the image-based setting; when combined with NeuS for image input, their CD (4.97 and 2.85) still lags PartGS (point-level) at 0.98.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartGS sits at the intersection of two research threads: primitive-based shape decomposition (EMS, MonteBoxFinder, PartNeRF, DBW) and mesh-aligned Gaussian splatting (SuGaR, GaMeS, 2DGS). It departs from prior shape-decomposition methods by replacing 3D supervision with self-supervised differentiable rendering, and from prior mesh-based GS methods by jointly optimizing superquadric geometry and Gaussian appearance through shared pose parameters rather than first reconstructing a mesh and then attaching Gaussians. The key conceptual departure from DBW (the closest prior work) is the coupling of 2D Gaussian parameters to superquadric surfaces, which yields a more compact representation while enabling photorealistic texture via spherical harmonics—something pure superquadric methods cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartGS demonstrates that part-aware 3D decomposition and high-fidelity rendering are not mutually exclusive, closing a gap where prior methods forced a trade-off between structural interpretability and visual quality. The 4× speedup over PartNeRF and 3× over DBW makes part-aware reconstruction practically viable for interactive applications like part-level editing and compositional 3D content generation. A key limitation is the reliance on background-free inputs (via SAM segmentation), and the authors note that extending to background modeling and larger, more complex scenes remains open. The finding that the full model intentionally sacrifices peak Chamfer distance for decomposition rationality highlights an unresolved tension between geometric accuracy and structural meaningfulness in part-aware reconstruction.
