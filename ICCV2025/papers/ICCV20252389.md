## Problem

Deep learning dehazing methods trained on synthetic data suffer a significant domain gap when applied to real-world hazy images, while physics-based methods relying on the Atmospheric Scattering Model (ASM) cannot capture the spatially varying, non-homogeneous haze patterns encountered in practice. Additionally, paired real-world hazy-clean datasets are scarce, and existing diffusion-based restoration approaches require many iterative sampling steps, making them computationally expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hazy RGB image and a spatially varying transmission map (initially estimated via Dark Channel Prior from the hazy image itself) as joint inputs to a U-Net backbone. It produces a dehazed RGB image and a refined transmission map in a single forward pass (N=1 Euler step). The adaptive step size (1−T)/N means pixels with thicker haze (lower T) undergo proportionally more transformation than lightly hazed regions, preserving fine details in clearer areas.

## Method

HazeFlow reformulates the ASM as the ODE dI_T = (J−A)dT, turning dehazing into a velocity-field estimation problem solved via a Rectified Flow framework. The architecture combines an NCSN++-based U-Net with a dedicated T-Net that embeds the spatially varying transmission map at multiple scales and jointly predicts both the velocity and a refined transmission map (T-Refinement) to correct DCP errors. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: Pretrain on MCBM-generated non-homogeneous synthetic pairs, Reflow on real hazy images with pseudo-clean counterparts (relaxing the uniform atmospheric light assumption to a spatially varying one), and Distillation with real-world degradation augmentation for robustness. Non-homogeneous haze is synthesized via Markov Chain Brownian Motion, which spatially varies the scattering coefficient β to model realistic particle distribution, addressing the limitation of prior methods that assume a single scalar β per image.

## Results

On the unpaired RTTS dataset, HazeFlow achieves a FADE score of 0.583 and BRISQUE of 5.01, outperforming the second-best method (CORUN) by 0.241 in FADE and 6.95 in BRISQUE. On the paired Dense-HAZE dataset, HazeFlow attains the best PSNR (17.14 dB), SSIM (0.6158), and LPIPS (0.4278) across all metrics, surpassing the next-best method Dehamer by 0.52 dB in PSNR and 0.226 in LPIPS. On NH-HAZE, HazeFlow achieves the best LPIPS (0.2606) and SSIM (0.6847) while ranking second in PSNR (20.06 vs. 20.66 for Dehamer). Inference requires only a single step (0.21 s), compared to 100 steps for DACLIP (38.09 s) and 20 steps for Wang et al. (2.50 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the unpaired RTTS benchmark, HazeFlow dominates on haze density (FADE 0.583 vs. 0.824 for CORUN) and naturalness (BRISQUE 5.01 vs. 11.96), while CORUN retains a marginal edge in NIMA (5.34 vs. 5.30). On Fattal's dataset, HazeFlow leads in FADE (0.264 vs. 0.338), BRISQUE (13.36 vs. 14.82), and PAQ2PIQ (76.44 vs. 76.12), though DAD slightly edges it in NIMA (5.46 vs. 5.40). On the paired Dense-HAZE dataset, HazeFlow outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over Dehamer (PSNR 17.14 vs. 16.62, LPIPS 0.4278 vs. 0.6543) and DehazeDDPM (PSNR 17.14 vs. 15.45, LPIPS 0.4278 vs. 0.5808). On NH-HAZE, HazeFlow achieves the best LPIPS (0.2606) and SSIM (0.6847) but trails Dehamer in PSNR (20.06 vs. 20.66), indicating a slight trade-off between pixel-level fidelity and perceptual quality. Against diffusion-based methods (DACLIP, DiffUIR, Refusion, Wang et al.), HazeFlow matches or exceeds their quality while using only 1 inference step versus 3–100 steps, yielding a 15×–180× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

HazeFlow directly builds on Rectified Flow (RF) [35], adapting its Reflow and Distillation mechanisms to the dehazing domain, but introduces a key conceptual departure: reformulating the Atmospheric Scattering Model as an ODE (dI_T = (J−A)dT) so that the velocity field is physically grounded in transmission rather than a generic time variable. This positions the work at the intersection of physics-based dehazing (DCP, color-lines) and modern flow-based generative modeling, while the MCBM-based non-homogeneous haze generation extends prior augmentation strategies (RIDCP, PANet) by modeling spatially varying density through stochastic particle motion rather than uniform β sampling. The three-stage pipeline (Pretrain → Reflow → Distillation) mirrors RF's training recipe but is specialized to handle the absence of paired real-world data and the spatially varying transmission map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-step inference (0.21 s) achieving SOTA on real-world benchmarks while outperforming 100-step diffusion methods by large margins makes this practically relevant for latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and aerial surveillance. The MCBM haze generator is training-free and broadly applicable (validated across NAFNet, DehazeFormer, FocalNet), offering a drop-in improvement for any dehazing pipeline. Open questions include whether the DCP-based initial transmission map remains a bottleneck under extreme or highly non-uniform haze, whether the pseudo-clean generation in the Reflow stage accumulates errors over iterations, and whether the ODE formulation generalizes to other atmospheric degradations (rain, snow, fog with different scattering properties). The paired-dataset evaluation is limited to 5 test pairs per benchmark, leaving generalization claims on paired data somewhat under-supported.
