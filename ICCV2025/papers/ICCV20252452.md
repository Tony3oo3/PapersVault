## Problem

Pool-based prompt learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt) have proven effective in traditional incremental learning, but their application to Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FSCIL) has remained unexplored. This paper is the first to study this setting and reveals an unanticipated performance degradation in novel sessions, which it traces to a token-dimension saturation problem: with scarce training samples, multiple prompts compete for limited task-relevant information, causing attention drift to task-irrelevant background regions and overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252452/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces a class label as output. Unusually, the spatial prompts are generated from the input image itself rather than being fixed learnable tokens: local prompts are produced by a pool of small CNN generators applied to the image, and global prompts are derived from the image's Discrete Fourier Transform spectrum partitioned into concentric frequency rings. The final enhanced image is fed into a frozen ViT backbone with VPT-Deep, so the spatial prompts modulate the input representation before tokenization rather than being appended as additional tokens.

## Method

LGSP-Prompt shifts pool-based prompt learning from the token dimension to the spatial dimension to avoid saturation. It comprises two complementary components: (1) Local Spatial Prompting, which maintains a pool of CNN-based prompt generators producing position-sensitive spatial maps, dynamically selects the most relevant prompts via cosine similarity to the CLS token, and aggregates them with attention-weighted element-wise multiplication; (2) Global Spatial Prompting, which transforms the input into the frequency domain via DFT, partitions the centered spectrum into K concentric rings, and applies differentiable sigmoid-based ring masks weighted by learnable parameters to adaptively emphasize different frequency bands. The two spatial prompts are integrated with the original image through learnable scaling factors (α_l, α_g) and residual connections before being processed by a VPT-Deep ViT backbone. This design ensures that prompt diversity operates in the spatial domain where information capacity is not bounded by the number of training samples, fundamentally circumventing the token-dimension competition that degrades novel-session performance.

## Results

On CUB-200 (10-way-30-shot base, 10×10-way-5-shot novel), LGSP-Prompt achieves 81.39% average accuracy across all 11 sessions, outperforming the best prompt-pool baseline DualPrompt (78.53%) by 2.86 points and the strongest VPT-based method PriViLege (77.50%) by 3.89 points. On FGVCAircraft, LGSP-Prompt reaches 25.82% average accuracy versus PriViLege's 21.16% (+4.66 points). On iNF200, it achieves 57.62% average accuracy, surpassing PriViLege (55.53%) by 2.09 points. The base-session accuracy (S0) of 85.72% on CUB-200 is also the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGSP-Prompt outperforms all 16 baselines on CUB-200, with the largest margins over traditional FSCIL methods: iCaRL (63.83%, +17.56 pts), FOSTER (69.78%, +11.61 pts), and CEC (76.13%, +5.26 pts). Among the most competitive recent methods, it surpasses DualPrompt (78.53%, +2.86 pts), TEEN (78.11%, +3.28 pts), and PriViLege (77.50%, +3.89 pts). Notably, the prompt-pool baselines L2P (76.21%) and CODA-Prompt (74.63%) underperform even the non-pool VPT-based PriViLege, confirming the token-dimension saturation phenomenon the paper identifies. On FGVCAircraft, LGSP-Prompt leads PriViLege by 4.66 points (25.82% vs. 21.16%) and Approximation by 9.20 points. On iNF200, the advantage over PriViLege narrows to 2.09 points (57.62% vs. 55.53%), while the gap over Yourself (36.97%) remains substantial at 20.65 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) as the base adaptation mechanism and on the pool-based prompt learning paradigm introduced by L2P and DualPrompt, which maintain dynamic prompt pools for continual learning. However, it departs from this lineage by identifying that the token-dimension prompt pool — the core mechanism in L2P/DualPrompt — actually degrades novel-session performance in FSCIL due to what the authors term 'token-dimension saturation.' Rather than refining the token-dimension pool, the paper shifts the pool to the spatial domain, combining local convolutional spatial prompts with global frequency-domain (DFT) prompts, drawing on spatial prompting ideas from instance-level visual prompting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The token-dimension saturation diagnosis provides a useful explanatory framework for why prompt pool methods transfer poorly from full-data continual learning to few-shot settings, and could inform prompt design in other data-scarce regimes. The spatial-domain alternative is practical and achieves consistent SOTA across three fine-grained FSCIL benchmarks. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain component (GSP) contributes meaningfully beyond a well-designed spatial filter, whether the approach generalizes to coarser-grained or non-fine-grained FSCIL settings, and whether the saturation phenomenon extends to other parameter-efficient adaptation methods beyond prompt pools.
