## Problem

Diffusion models require many sequential denoising steps, making inference prohibitively slow—generating a single 5-second 720P video can take over two hours on a single GPU. Existing training-free acceleration methods either ignore statistical structure between adjacent steps or depend on attention/feature similarity that is tied to specific network architectures, limiting their generality. This paper seeks a network-structure-agnostic, training-free mechanism to skip redundant denoising steps while preserving output quality.

## Input / Output

The method operates on any pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5/v2/v3.5, CogVideoX 2B, Animated-Diff) without modifying weights, taking a text prompt and initial noise as input and producing a generated image or video. It is scheduler-agnostic, working with DDIM, DPM-Solver++, and EDM samplers, and is compatible with float16 inference, INT8 quantization, and classifier-free guidance. The acceleration is realized by reducing the number of full network forward passes (e.g., 50→38 steps for SDv2, 4→3 steps for distilled video models) while keeping the same output resolution and format.

## Method

The key observation is Local Transition Coherence: during a mid-range interval of the denoising trajectory, the transition operators Δx_{t+1,t} = x_t − x_{t+1} of consecutive steps are nearly collinear (small inter-step angle θ). Within this identified acceleration interval, LTC-Accel replaces the expensive network evaluation at step t with a cheap linear extrapolation x_t* = x_{t+1} + w_g·γ·Δx_{t+2,t+1}, where γ is the ratio of denoising-progress increments (based on √SNR) and w_g is a scalar weight computed by minimizing the L2 discrepancy between the true and approximated transitions. A two-step algorithm first computes w_g per step using the approximated trajectory, then performs a local search over the entire interval to minimize cumulative error; an optional PSNR-guided bias refinement further tightens fidelity. Because the method only exploits the output-space relationship between adjacent steps, it imposes no architectural constraints and is orthogonal to caching (DeepCache), schedule optimization (Align Your Steps), quantization, and distillation, enabling multiplicative speedups when combined.

## Results

LTC-Accel achieves a 1.67× speedup on Stable Diffusion v2 (DDIM, 50→38 steps) with ImageReward improving from 0.4039 to 0.4096, and a 1.58× speedup on Animated-Diff (30→19 steps) with Textual Faithfulness dropping only from 0.3662 to 0.3465. When combined with DeepCache, the total speedup reaches 2.34× on SDv2, and when combined with the distilled Animated-Diff-Lightning model, a 10× overall speedup is achieved (4→3 steps), enabling real-time video generation above 16 FPS. On CogVideoX 2B, LTC-Accel delivers 1.54× speedup (40→26 steps) with Frame Consistency remaining nearly unchanged (0.9514→0.9511).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepCache [26], LTC-Accel matches or slightly exceeds ImageReward at fewer steps (e.g., 0.4096 vs. 0.4039 at the 50-step setting) and achieves an additional 1.41× speedup on top of DeepCache's own acceleration, reaching 2.34× total. Against Align Your Steps [37], LTC-Accel with 20 steps surpasses 30 Ays steps in ImageReward (0.2131 vs. 0.1901) while being 1.50× faster, though at lower step counts (8 vs. 10) there is a small quality gap (0.1309 vs. 0.1332). Against AnimateDiff [4], LTC-Accel achieves 1.43–1.58× speedup with Textual Faithfulness dropping by only ~0.02 and Frame Consistency remaining essentially unchanged (e.g., 0.9676→0.9681 at 30 steps). Against CogVideoX 2B [53], the speedup is 1.43–1.58× with minimal quality degradation (Text drops ≤0.02, Smooth drops ≤0.005). Against the distilled AnimateDiff-Lightning [20], LTC-Accel provides an additional 1.33–1.60× speedup (4→3 or 8→5 steps) while maintaining Textual Faithfulness within 0.01–0.07 of the original, demonstrating orthogonality to distillation-based acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying Local Transition Coherence — a statistical correlation between the *outputs* of adjacent denoising steps (the transition operators Δx) — as a basis for acceleration, rather than relying on internal feature similarity (as in DeepCache) or attention patterns that are tied to specific architectures. It departs from the solver-optimization line (DDIM, DPM-Solver++) and schedule-optimization line (Align Your Steps) by operating at a higher level: approximating entire steps via neighboring-step trajectories. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to both training-based (distillation) and training-free (caching, quantization) accelerators, making it composable rather than competitive with prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic nature of LTC-Accel is its strongest practical contribution: a single acceleration strategy applicable across Stable Diffusion variants, CogVideoX, and Animated-Diff without per-model redesign, which is a meaningful gap left by caching methods. The demonstrated composability (2.34× with DeepCache, 10× with distillation for real-time video) suggests it could become a default layer in diffusion inference pipelines. However, the standalone speedup (1.5–1.7×) is moderate, the method weakens below ~3 steps, and it still requires per-model tuning of the acceleration interval [a,b] and cycle r, leaving open whether a universal or auto-detected interval could eliminate this overhead.
