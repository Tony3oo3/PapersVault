## Problem

While diffusion and flow models have achieved remarkable success in generative tasks, their potential as feature extractors for discriminative tasks like image classification remains underexplored. Existing approaches that repurpose generative models for classification (e.g., DDAE, generative classifiers) rely on intricate, model-dependent designs that select specific internal layers or combine representations in ad hoc ways, limiting generalizability and leaving significant performance gaps compared to state-of-the-art methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet) and produces class labels via a lightweight two-layer MLP classifier. Uniquely, the feature representation is not a single fixed vector but a continuous trajectory: any point x_t along the reverse ODE path (t ∈ [0,1]) can serve as the input to the classifier, enabling flexible selection of feature hierarchy depth. The pretrained generative model itself (U-Net or Transformer) is the backbone, with no access to intermediate activations required.

## Method

PRG follows a two-stage pretrain-then-finetune paradigm. In stage one, a reversible continuous-time generative model (one of three variants: PRG-GVP using a Generalized VP-SDE with triangular coefficients, PRG-ICFM using interpolant conditional flow matching, or PRG-OTCFM using optimal transport conditional flow matching) is pretrained unsupervised via a flow-matching objective. In stage two, the generative process is reversed: the ODE is integrated backward from data x_0 toward the terminal state x_1, and intermediate features x_t are fed to a classifier trained jointly with the flow model via neural ODE backpropagation. The key theoretical insight is that this reverse process maximizes the mutual information between the input and its representation, as score/flow matching losses provide a lower bound on the reconstruction likelihood. The approach is model-agnostic (works with U-Net, Transformer, or any architecture), requires no access to internal layer activations, and allows features to be extracted at any point along the trajectory rather than at a fixed layer.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PRG-OTCFM-S achieves 97.65% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best generative method DDAE (97.2%) and discriminative baselines WideResNet-28-10 (96.3%) and ResNeXt-29 (96.4%). On Tiny-ImageNet, PRG-OTCFM-S reaches 71.33%, outperforming DDAE (69.4%) and WideResNet-28-10 (69.3%). On ImageNet at 64×64 resolution, PRG-OTCFM-XL achieves 78.13% top-1 accuracy, beating DMSZC-DiT-XL2 (77.5%) and iGPT-L (72.6%) among generative methods, though trailing supervised Swin-B (83.5%). For OOD robustness on CIFAR-10-C, PRG-OTCFM-S attains 92.26% accuracy under all corruptions (only 5.39% drop from clean), outperforming PDE+ (89.11%) and SBGC (76.24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRG consistently outperforms all generative baselines across all three main datasets: on CIFAR-10, PRG-OTCFM-S (97.65%) beats DDAE (97.2%) by 0.45 points and HybViT (96.0%) by 1.65 points; on Tiny-ImageNet, it surpasses DDAE (69.4%) by 1.93 points; on ImageNet-64, it exceeds DMSZC-DiT-XL2 (77.5%) by 0.63 points and iGPT-L (72.6%) by 5.53 points. Against discriminative baselines, PRG-OTCFM-S on CIFAR-10 (97.65%) exceeds WideResNet-28-10 (96.3%) and ResNeXt-29 (96.4%), while on ImageNet-64 it slightly trails ResNet-152 (77.8%) and is well below Swin-B (83.5%). For OOD robustness on CIFAR-10-C, PRG-OTCFM-S (92.26% all-corruption, 93.10% noise) outperforms PDE+ (89.11%/85.59%), SBGC (76.24%/75.38%), AugMix (89.09%/88.71%), and AutoAug (85.37%/76.47%), with the smallest clean-to-corrupted drop (5.39%) among all methods. In transfer learning (ImageNet→CIFAR-10/Tiny-ImageNet), PRG-SiT-XL2 (98.72%/78.33%) beats DDAE-DiT-XL2 (98.4%/77.8%) and MAE ViT-B/16 (96.5%/76.5%), but trails iGPT-L (99.0%) on CIFAR-10 and SimCLR Res-50-4x (98.6%) on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRG extends the denoising autoencoder lineage (Vincent et al.) by replacing the discrete denoising objective with a continuous-time flow/diffusion process, then inverts the pretrained generator to extract features rather than selecting specific internal layers as DDAE does. The theoretical grounding draws on the Probability Flow ODE (Song et al.) and flow matching (Lipman et al., Tong et al.), while the fine-tuning mechanism relies on Neural ODEs (Chen et al.) for gradient computation through the reversed trajectory. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior generative-classifier approaches (SBGC, HybViT, DMSZC) that model p(x|y) or require intricate layer-selection heuristics, instead proposing a model-agnostic 'run the generator backward' recipe that treats the entire reverse trajectory as a continuous feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic reversal framework offers a practical path to repurpose any community-pretrained diffusion or flow model (demonstrated with SiT-XL) as a representation extractor without architectural modifications, which could accelerate the adoption of generative pretraining for discriminative tasks. However, the method is evaluated only at 64×64 resolution and still trails strong supervised transformers (Swin-B at 83.5% vs. PRG's 78.1% on ImageNet), leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions with VAEs or transformer backbones. The OOD robustness results (92.3% on CIFAR-10-C) suggest the reversed features carry genuine semantic structure, but whether this advantage persists at scale and under adversarial attacks remains untested.
