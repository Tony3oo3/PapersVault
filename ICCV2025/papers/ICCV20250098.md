## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture generation methods predominantly produce rhythmic beat gestures while neglecting semantically meaningful representational gestures (iconic, metaphoric, deictic). Prior semantic-aware approaches either model only global discourse-level context or only local salient-word semantics, failing to unify both levels within a single framework and to explicitly exploit the relevance of semantic annotations in guiding generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities per 34-frame clip: raw speech audio (encoded by a frozen HuBERT model), a tokenized text transcript (embedded via pre-trained FastText), and a speaker identity embedding. It outputs synchronized 3D hand (38 joints) and body (9 joints) gesture sequences in Rot6D joint-rotation representation. For extended generation, an overlap-and-combine strategy stitches consecutive clips by copying the last 4 frames of each clip into the first 4 frames of the next, ensuring smooth temporal continuity.

## Method

SemGes is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains separate VQ-VAEs for hand and body motion, learning discrete codebooks and a compositional motion latent space via reconstruction of positions, velocities, and accelerations plus a commitment loss. Stage 2 fuses the three input modalities through a cross-modal Transformer encoder (self-attention over concatenated audio+identity features, cross-attention with text semantics as key-value), producing a multimodal latent code that is vector-quantized against the Stage 1 codebooks and decoded into gestures. Three losses drive training: a semantic coherence loss (cosine similarity between text embeddings and frozen motion encoder outputs for both hands and body), a semantic relevance loss (a piecewise quadratic/linear penalty weighted by an annotation relevance factor λ that emphasizes frames with semantic gesture annotations), and a multimodal quantization consistency loss aligning the fused latent code with ground-truth quantized motion codes.

## Results

On the BEAT dataset, SemGes achieves the best FGD of 4.467 (vs. 6.623 for the next-best DiffSheg), the highest Diversity of 305.706 (vs. 257.674), and the top SRGR of 0.256 (vs. 0.250). On TED-Expressive, SemGes attains the best FGD of 7.263 and a substantially higher Diversity of 302.772 (vs. 156.236 for CaMN). The method trades off Beat Consistency (BC = 0.453 on BEAT vs. 0.922 for DiffSheg), which the authors attribute to the focus on semantic alignment over strict rhythmic synchronization; on beat-dominant subsets, BC rises to 0.689.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemGes outperforms all four baselines on FGD and Diversity across both datasets, with the largest margin on Diversity (e.g., 305.7 vs. 257.7 on BEAT, 302.8 vs. 156.2 on TED-Expressive). On BEAT, SemGes also achieves the highest SRGR (0.256), narrowly beating DiffSheg (0.250) and clearly surpassing CaMN (0.231) and LivelySpeaker (0.229). The sole metric where SemGes underperforms is Beat Consistency: on BEAT it scores 0.453 versus 0.922 (DiffSheg), 0.891 (LivelySpeaker), and 0.876 (DiffGesture); on TED-Expressive it scores 0.671 versus 0.712 (DiffSheg). The authors argue this trade-off is expected because the semantic coherence and relevance losses prioritize meaning alignment over rhythmic beat matching, and they show that on beat-dominant gesture subsets the BC rises to 0.689. Among baselines, DiffSheg is the strongest competitor on FGD and BC, while CaMN leads on SRGR among the non-SemGes methods on BEAT; LivelySpeaker and DiffGesture are consistently outperformed on FGD by a wide margin (e.g., 13.378 and 9.632 vs. 4.467 on BEAT).

## Relation to Prior Work

SemGes builds most directly on CAMN [27], which introduced semantic relevance annotations for co-speech gesture generation, by adding a global semantic coherence module (cosine-similarity alignment between text and VQ-VAE motion latents) that CAMN lacks. The two-stage VQ-VAE motion prior follows the compositional latent-space paradigm established by EMAGE [28] and Semantic Gesticulator [57], while the cross-modal Transformer encoder with HuBERT audio features and FastText word embeddings extends the multi-modal conditioning of LivelySpeaker [58] and DiffSHEG [10]. The key conceptual departure is unifying discourse-level semantic coherence and fine-grained representational gesture relevance (iconic, metaphoric, deictic) within a single training objective, rather than treating them as separate concerns as prior methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in co-speech gesture generation: most methods produce convincing but semantically shallow beat gestures, while representational gestures that visually encode meaning remain underexplored. The demonstrated trade-off between semantic alignment (high FGD, SRGR, Diversity) and rhythmic beat consistency (lower BC) highlights an open tension in the field—whether both can be optimized simultaneously or whether task-specific weighting is needed. Limitations include the reliance on gesture-type annotations (unavailable for TED Expressive), the 34-frame clip length constraining discourse-level modelling, and the 4-frame overlap strategy for long sequences being a simple heuristic rather than a learned continuity mechanism. Future work could explore whether the semantic coherence loss generalizes to full-body/holistic generation and whether the VQ-VAE codebook can be made more interpretable in terms of gesture types.
