## Problem

Diffusion-based category-level 6D pose estimation (e.g., GenPose) suffers from slow training convergence because the encoder is learned end-to-end with the denoising network across multiple noise levels, and requires an additional energy-based network to filter outlier pose samples during inference. No prior work has explored combining direct pose regression supervision with diffusion score-matching to address both issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252641/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented depth image, backprojects it into a 1024×3 point cloud, and outputs a single 6D pose (a 6D rotation representation mapped to SO(3) via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, plus a 3D translation vector). Unlike GenPose which samples K=50 pose candidates and aggregates them via mean-pooling after filtering with a separate ranking network, this method produces one guided pose per inference pass, halving the parameter count (2.2M vs. 4.4M) and eliminating the aggregation step entirely.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training strategy: first, the PointNet++ encoder is pre-trained with a direct pose regression head (geodesic distance loss for rotation, L2 for translation), then the encoder, regression head, and denoising diffusion head are jointly optimized with total loss L_φ + L_θ, providing complementary supervision that accelerates convergence by ~1.5×. At inference, a time-dependent score scaling guidance w_t = w_min + (w_max − w_min)·exp(−5t) multiplies the predicted score at each denoising step, applying minimal guidance at high-noise steps to preserve the multimodal distribution of symmetric objects while strongly guiding samples toward high-density regions at low-noise steps. This simple exponential schedule replaces the need for an additional energy-based ranking network, enabling reliable single-pose inference that matches or exceeds multi-pose baselines.

## Results

On REAL275, the proposed method (G+J) with a single pose sample (K=1) achieves 52.6 mAP at 5°2cm and 73.4 mAP at 10°2cm, surpassing GenPose with 50 samples (52.1/72.4) while using only 2.2M parameters. On CAMERA25, the same configuration reaches 80.4/85.1, outperforming GenPose K=50 (79.9/84.6). On the challenging HouseCat6D dataset, the method achieves 8.9/26.2 with K=1, beating GenPose K=50 (8.5/25.3) and matching RGB-D+prior methods like SecondPose (11.0/25.3) using only depth input. On ROPE, score scaling guidance integrated into GenPose++ yields VUS@5°2cm of 10.9 and VUS@10°2cm of 20.8 with a single sample, exceeding GenPose++ K=50 (10.0/19.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline GenPose [69], the proposed method with score scaling guidance (G) and joint learning (J) achieves higher accuracy with a single sample (K=1) than GenPose requires 50 samples plus an energy-based ranker to match: 52.6 vs. 52.1 at 5°2cm and 73.4 vs. 72.4 at 10°2cm on REAL275, while halving inference time (8.23 vs. 6.20 FPS) and parameter count (2.2M vs. 4.4M). On CAMERA25, Ours(G+J) K=1 (80.4/85.1) edges out GenPose K=50 (79.9/84.6). On HouseCat6D, Ours(G) K=1 (8.9/26.2) surpasses GenPose K=50 (8.5/25.3) and approaches SecondPose (11.0/25.3) which uses RGB-D plus DINOv2 semantic priors. On ROPE, integrating score scaling into GenPose++ [70] yields VUS@5°2cm of 10.9 vs. 10.0 for GenPose++ K=50, confirming the guidance generalizes to multi-modal RGB-D settings. Among depth-only methods without shape priors, the proposed method outperforms HS-Pose [73] (46.5/68.6) by 6.1/4.8 points on REAL275, and GPV-Pose [11] (32.0/50.0) by a wide margin. The method also achieves a 1.5× training speedup (7.5 days vs. 11 days) over GenPose's from-scratch training, reaching the baseline's best 10°5cm performance in just 0.5 days.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GenPose, which first adapted denoising diffusion for category-level 6D pose estimation, by addressing two of its key limitations: slow end-to-end training convergence and the need for a separate energy-based ranking network to filter outlier pose samples. The joint learning strategy (pre-training the encoder with direct pose regression, then co-optimizing with the diffusion head) draws on multi-task learning principles, while the time-dependent score scaling guidance departs from the classifier-free guidance paradigm by directly scaling the predicted score magnitude rather than extrapolating between conditional and unconditional predictions. The work also positions itself against the broader regression-based category-level pose estimation literature (NOCS, DualPoseNet, VI-Net, AG-Pose) and the emerging generative alternatives (DiffusionNOCS, GenPose++), arguing that the generative formulation is superior for handling multi-modal pose distributions but has been held back by training and inference inefficiencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the ranking network halves model parameters (4.4M → 2.2M) and enables single-pose inference that matches or exceeds 50-pose + filtering baselines, making diffusion-based pose estimation substantially more deployable in real-time tracking scenarios (36 FPS vs. 20 FPS). The score scaling technique is simple enough to be adopted as a plug-in for other diffusion-based pose estimation methods, as demonstrated by its integration into GenPose++ on the ROPE benchmark. Open questions include whether the exponential scaling schedule is optimal or dataset-dependent, whether the approach extends to other generative pose representations (e.g., keypoint diffusion), and whether the joint learning benefit persists when the encoder is replaced by a stronger pre-trained backbone. The paper does not explore failure modes under severe occlusion or category shift beyond the tested benchmarks.
