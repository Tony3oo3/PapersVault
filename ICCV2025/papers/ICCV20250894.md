## Problem

Classical structure-from-motion solvers (5-point, 8-point) assume synchronized point correspondences from pairs of instantaneous views, a premise that breaks down for rolling shutter cameras and event cameras that sample asynchronously. No existing geometric solver natively recovers 3D structure and camera motion from fully asynchronous, timestamped point feature tracks across arbitrary sensing modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver consumes timestamped 2D point tracks (x_ij, t_ij) from any mixture of global shutter, rolling shutter, or event cameras, along with IMU angular rate ω and camera intrinsics K. It produces 3D point locations P_i and a scale-less camera linear velocity v in a single linear solve. Notably, no temporal synchronization is required across tracks, and the minimal case needs only three observations of a single point; rolling shutter timestamps are corrected per-row, while event camera timestamps are used as-is at microsecond resolution.

## Method

Under a constant-velocity motion model with known rotation, the paper derives a linear point incidence relation [f'_ij]_× P_i − t'_ij [f'_ij]_× v = 0 that links each 2D observation at arbitrary time t_ij to the 3D point and camera velocity. Stacking all constraints yields a large sparse linear system A x = 0, which is solved efficiently via a Schur complement reduction: the block-diagonal structure of the point-point submatrix enables O(M) inversion, collapsing the problem to a 3×3 system B v = 0 solved by SVD. Points are then recovered by back-substitution, and the two-fold sign ambiguity is resolved by enforcing positive depth. The entire pipeline is wrapped in a RANSAC loop that samples M tracks with n observations each, scores hypotheses by mean angular reprojection error, and refines on inliers—requiring no iterative nonlinear optimization.

## Results

The solver is evaluated on angular velocity error (θ_err, in degrees) across three sensing modalities. On global shutter images (Event Camera Dataset), the method achieves mean errors of 15.1–17.1° (median 7.2–11.6°), reducing to 9.8–13.3° when restricted to sequences with >90% inlier ratio. On rolling shutter images (TUM), mean errors are 24.7–27.5° (19.3–22.6° with inlier filtering). On event camera data (VECtor and Event Camera Dataset), mean errors range from 12.6–19.3°, and combining event and global shutter tracks (E+GS) further reduces errors to as low as 7.0° mean / 5.9° median on the boxes_translation sequence. The minimal-case solver runtime is 63 µs on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is eventail (Gao et al., CVPR 2024), a linear N-point solver for line-based motion estimation from events. The proposed point-based solver outperforms eventail on every sequence across all three modalities. On global shutter images, mean velocity error drops from 21.9–34.8° (eventail) to 15.1–17.1° (ours), a 6–18° absolute improvement. On rolling shutter images, the gap widens to 18–21° (43.8–45.5° vs. 24.7–27.5°), largely because eventail struggles to find sufficient 3D lines in those scenes while point tracks are more readily available. On event data, the improvement is 10–22° in mean error. A key additional finding is that fusing event and global shutter tracks (E+GS) on the DAVIS camera further reduces error below either modality alone (e.g., 7.0° vs. 13.0° on shapes_translation), demonstrating the solver's ability to exploit complementary asynchronous sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line-feature-based asynchronous motion solvers of Gao et al. [10, 11] from straight-line observations to general 3D point tracks, replacing the line incidence relation with a point incidence relation that is a direct generalization of the classical epipolar constraint. It also subsumes the rolling shutter pose estimation literature (Saurer et al. [40], Kukelova et al.) by treating row-wise timestamp offsets as a special case of arbitrary per-observation timestamps. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the unified linear formulation that simultaneously handles global shutter, rolling shutter, and event camera tracks within a single N-point system, rather than requiring modality-specific pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver provides the first closed-form, single-stage linear method for structure and motion from fully asynchronous point tracks, removing the need to aggregate events into pseudo-frames or restrict to line features. Its practical impact is broad: any system combining collocated sensors (e.g., DAVIS cameras producing both events and frames) can now fuse their tracks in one geometric solve. Key limitations include the hard dependence on externally provided angular rate (IMU), and the finding that higher-order derivative estimation (acceleration) is too noise-sensitive for real data, leaving the full 6-DoF dynamics problem open.
