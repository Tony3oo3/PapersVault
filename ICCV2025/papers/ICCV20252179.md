## Problem

All existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on 2D vision-language models or textual annotations during training or inference, with no model capable of learning semantics end-to-end from 3D data alone. This gap is compounded by the absence of a large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting dataset for indoor scenes, leaving generalizable open-vocabulary understanding in 3DGS entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes the full parameter set of a 3DGS scene (center, scale, color, opacity — 59 dimensions per Gaussian, averaging 1.42M Gaussians per scene) and produces a d-dimensional language feature vector per Gaussian in a single forward pass. At inference, per-Gaussian features are compared against text embeddings for open-vocabulary queries, and zero-shot segmentation is obtained by majority voting over 25 nearest Gaussians at each evaluation point. The approach requires no 2D image input or per-scene optimization at test time, achieving 0.24 min/scene versus 107 min for prior methods.

## Method

SceneSplat adapts a Point Transformer V3 encoder-decoder to map raw 3DGS parameters to CLIP-aligned per-Gaussian language features, trained with a combination of cosine similarity, L2, and a class-level bidirectional contrastive loss (applied with warm-starting to stabilize early training). Language supervision is collected by running SAMv2 segmentation and SigLIP2 feature extraction on 2D training views, then lifting 2D feature maps to 3D via Occam's LGS in an optimization-free manner. For label-free pretraining, the GaussSSL scheme combines Masked Gaussian Modeling (reconstructing randomly masked Gaussian parameters), DINO-style self-distillation with iBOT masked-feature alignment, and optional autoencoder-compressed Language-Gaussian Alignment, enabling training on the full 7,916-scene SceneSplat-7K dataset without per-scene annotations.

## Results

On the zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation benchmarks, SceneSplat trained on ScanNet alone achieves 18.9 f-mIoU on ScanNet200 (200 classes) and 10.8 f-mIoU on Matterport3D (160 classes), surpassing the strongest prior method Mosaic3D by 5.9% and 2.2% f-mIoU respectively. When extended to three training sources (ScanNet, ScanNet++, Matterport3D), SceneSplat reaches 21.4 f-mIoU on ScanNet200, 13.8 on Matterport3D, and 28.4 on ScanNet++ (100 classes), outperforming Mosaic3D (which uses 8.3× more training scenes) by 5.7%, 0.7%, and 10.4% f-mIoU respectively. In the supervised setting (Table 4), SceneSplat with self-supervised pretraining achieves 77.2 mIoU on ScanNet20 and 35.9 mIoU on ScanNet200, exceeding the Point Transformer V3 baseline by +0.8% and +0.9% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneSplat decisively outperforms all prior open-vocabulary 3D methods. Against Mosaic3D [21] (the strongest concurrent work), SceneSplat trained on ScanNet alone gains +5.9% f-mIoU on ScanNet200 (18.9 vs. 13.0) and +2.2% on Matterport3D (10.8 vs. 8.6), while using the same single training source. With three sources, SceneSplat (2.92× scenes) beats Mosaic3D (24.3× scenes) by +5.7% on ScanNet200, +0.7% on Matterport3D, and +10.4% on ScanNet++, demonstrating that 3DGS-native feature learning is far more data-efficient than 2D-to-3D distillation. Against earlier baselines, the margins are even larger: RegionPLC [54] scores 9.2 f-mIoU on ScanNet200, OV3D [15] scores 8.7, OpenScene [34] scores 6.4, and PLA [8] scores only 1.8, all substantially below SceneSplat's 18.9. In the supervised segmentation setting (Table 4), SceneSplat with self-supervised pretraining edges out Point Transformer V3 [51] by +0.8% mIoU on ScanNet20 (77.2 vs. 76.4) and +0.9% on ScanNet200 (35.9 vs. 35.0), while matching PTv3 on ScanNet++ (42.4 vs. 42.6). The runtime comparison (Table 9) shows SceneSplat processes a scene in 0.24 min versus 107 min for Occam's LGS [4], a 445.8× speedup, since no per-scene 2D feature extraction or optimization is needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding, where methods like OpenScene, PLA, RegionPLC, and Mosaic3D all require 2D vision-language models (CLIP, SAM) during training or inference to compensate for limited 3D semantic data. SceneSplat instead builds a feed-forward encoder on the Point Transformer V3 backbone that consumes raw 3DGS parameters (center, scale, color, opacity) directly, eliminating the 2D fusion step at inference. For self-supervised pretraining, it adapts the DINOv2 self-distillation framework and Masked Autoencoder-style reconstruction to the Gaussian primitive domain, combining them with a novel Masked Gaussian Modeling objective. The label collection pipeline repurposes Occam's LGS for optimization-free 2D-to-3D feature lifting, but only as a one-time offline annotation step rather than a runtime dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 445× speedup over per-scene language-embedded 3DGS methods (Occam's LGS) makes open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding practical for real-time or large-scale deployment, and the SceneSplat-7K dataset (7,916 scenes, 11.27B Gaussians) provides the first standardized benchmark for 3DGS-based indoor scene reasoning. However, the self-supervised pretraining component yields only marginal gains (+0.1–0.5% mIoU) and even degrades performance on ScanNet++, suggesting the label-free learning signal from unlabeled 3DGS scenes is not yet a strong enough regularizer. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the 3DGS-specific inductive bias (opacity, scale) transfers to other representations, and whether the modest self-supervised gains can be amplified with better augmentation strategies or larger unlabeled pools.
