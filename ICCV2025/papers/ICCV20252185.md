## Problem

Existing VideoLLMs are limited to holistic scene comprehension with text-only outputs, unable to perform fine-grained object-centric reasoning or generate segmentation masks. No prior framework supports arbitrary visual prompts (beyond masks) at any single timestamp in a video while simultaneously producing both textual answers and grounded segmentation outputs for the referred objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video (T frames), a text question, and an optional visual prompt (one of 8 types: mask, mask contour, rectangle, ellipse, triangle, scribble, arrow, or point) specified as an RGBA overlay at a single user-chosen timestamp. The output is a free-form textual answer, optionally accompanied by per-frame segmentation masks (T×H×W) triggered by a special <SEG> token in the LLM's generated sequence, enabling multi-round interactive object-centric dialogue.

## Method

RGA3 builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the visual-language backbone and incorporates a SAM2 (Hiera-L) decoder for grounding. The key architectural contribution is the Spatial-Temporal Overlay Module (STOM), which propagates a single-frame visual prompt to all video frames via CoTracker3 bidirectional point tracking around the prompt centroid, then alpha-blends the propagated prompts directly onto frames—avoiding the feature instability of mask pooling for partial-overlap prompts like arrows or scribbles. During inference, the LLM generates text tokens; when a <SEG> token appears, its corresponding last-layer hidden state is projected into SAM2's prompt encoder space and combined with dense Hiera-L features to produce segmentation masks. Training uses a multi-task objective (autoregressive cross-entropy for text plus BCE and DICE losses for masks) with LoRA fine-tuning on the LLM and frozen visual encoders, co-training on QA and segmentation datasets with random visual prompt augmentation.

## Results

On the newly introduced VideoInfer benchmark (open-ended referring VideoQA with 8 visual prompt types), RGA3-7B achieves 46.7% GPT-4o accuracy and 2.60 score, outperforming the strongest generalist (GPT-4o detail:high at 45.5/2.50) and the best specialist (VideoRefer-7B at 40.4/2.29). On VideoRefer-Bench (multiple-choice), RGA3-7B reaches 74.0% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o with Set-of-Mask (71.3%) and VideoRefer-7B (71.9%). For video reasoning object segmentation on ReVOS, RGA3-7B achieves 58.0 J&F (28.6 robustness), exceeding VideoLISA-3.8B (48.4 J&F, 20.1 R) and VISA-7B (46.9 J&F, 15.5 R) by roughly 10 points in J&F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGA3-7B wins across all 16 baselines on every benchmark tested. On VideoInfer, it beats the closest generalist GPT-4o (detail:high) by 1.2 points in GPT-4o accuracy (46.7 vs 45.5) and the closest specialist VideoRefer-7B by 6.3 points (46.7 vs 40.4). On VideoRefer-Bench, RGA3 surpasses GPT-4o with Set-of-Mask prompting (74.0 vs 71.3 avg) and VideoRefer-7B with region features (74.0 vs 71.9). On ViP-Bench (image-level), RGA3-7B slightly edges out ViP-LLaVA-13B (49.4 vs 48.2 manual accuracy) despite being a 7B model not specifically designed for image tasks. For video segmentation on ReVOS, RGA3-7B leads VideoLISA-3.8B by 9.6 J&F (58.0 vs 48.4) and VISA-7B by 11.1 J&F (58.0 vs 46.9), with a particularly large gap in robustness score (28.6 vs 20.1 and 15.5 respectively). On Ref-DAVIS-17, RGA3-7B achieves 72.8 J&F, outperforming VideoLISA-3.8B (67.7) and VISA-7B (69.4) by 5.1 and 3.4 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—object-centric referring QA (VideoRefer, ViP-LLaVA, Osprey) and language-instructed video object segmentation (VISA, VideoLISA, LISA)—into a single model that accepts arbitrary visual prompts and produces both textual answers and segmentation masks. It builds directly on SAM2 for the grounding decoder and Qwen2.5-VL as the visual-language backbone, but departs from prior referring QA methods that rely on mask pooling or region-feature extraction by introducing STOM, which propagates visual prompts across time via point tracking and alpha blending rather than requiring pre-segmented object masks. The VideoInfer dataset extends the object-centric video QA task from auto-generated, perception-heavy questions (as in VideoRefer-700K) to manually curated, reasoning-focused open-ended questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By supporting arbitrary visual prompts (points, scribbles, arrows, boxes) at any timestamp and generating segmentation masks alongside text, RGA3 enables multi-round, interactive object-centric video understanding that was previously fragmented across separate systems. The VideoInfer benchmark, with its emphasis on temporal reasoning and indirect inference, addresses a gap where existing datasets over-represent simple perception questions. Open questions include whether the tracking-based prompt propagation (CoTracker3) scales reliably to very long or fast-motion videos, and whether the unified LLM+SAM2 architecture can be made more efficient—the SAM2 decoder currently dominates inference latency at ~4.5s/sample.
