## Problem

Existing LVLM acceleration methods fall into two disconnected paradigms: parameter-dependent approaches that require costly retraining after pruning, and token-dependent strategies that rely on rule-based selection (e.g., attention ranking) which cannot consistently identify the most relevant tokens. Moreover, prior work treats parameters and inputs as independent compression targets, ignoring their demonstrated correlation. This paper addresses the gap by showing that deep layers can be skipped without retraining, that noise perturbation improves token selection, and that a joint optimization model is necessary to integrate both pruning dimensions.

## Input / Output

The meta-router receives the full visual token sequence (e.g., 576 tokens from CLIP-ViT), the text instruction embeddings, and a set of 16 learnable layer embeddings as concatenated input, and outputs ranked scores for both token retention and layer retention. The LVLM backbone remains entirely frozen; only the ~40M-parameter meta-router is trained. At inference, the user specifies how many tokens (M) and layers (K) to discard, and the meta-router selects which specific ones to remove, with the pruned model then producing the standard autoregressive text response.

## Method

PAR introduces a lightweight transformer-based meta-router that jointly decides which visual tokens to prune and which deep layers to skip, operating on the concatenated sequence of visual tokens, text instruction, and learnable layer embeddings. The router is trained via DPO in a fully self-supervised manner: for each unlabeled image-question pair, multiple pruning actions are generated by perturbing attention-based token rankings with Gaussian noise and randomly selecting layers, then classified as positive or negative based on KL-divergence between the pruned model's output and the full model's output. A key design choice is adding Gaussian noise (N~(0,10)) to rule-based attention scores before token selection, which empirically expands the search space and captures better token combinations than deterministic ranking alone. The framework is flexible—users can activate only the token branch, only the layer branch, or both—making it a plug-and-play module applicable to already-deployed LVLMs without any weight updates to the backbone.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, PAR (T=176, L=28) achieves 77.7 on AOKVQA, 70.0 on SQA, 1300 on MME, 76.1 on POPE, 62.2 on MMBench, and 62.7 on SEED-Bench at 56.0% TFLOPs, outperforming FastV by 3.3% at a comparable compression ratio. At a more aggressive setting (T=128, L=24, 45.9% TFLOPs), PAR still scores 76.4/69.6/1286/73.6/60.2/59.4, exceeding FastV by 7.8% lower TFLOPs while maintaining competitive accuracy. On Qwen-VL-Chat-9B, PAR (T=102, L=30) achieves 74.7/66.8/1464/82.5/57.3/55.1/69.3/61.7 at 73.3% TFLOPs, surpassing FastV (72.2/65.9/1405/81.4/53.5/49.1/64.8/60.1 at 72.0%) across all eight benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAR consistently outperforms all five baselines across the eight benchmarks. Against FastV [7] (the strongest token-pruning baseline), PAR (T=176, L=28) gains +10.5 on POPE (76.1 vs 65.6), +6.4 on SEED (62.7 vs 56.3), and +2.1 on MMBench (62.2 vs 60.1) at a slightly lower TFLOPs (56.0% vs 53.7%). Against LLaVolta [6] at the same 53.7% TFLOPs, PAR (T=144, L=24) leads by +5.8 on POPE (75.9 vs 70.1) and +4.5 on SEED (60.2 vs 55.7). Against ShortGPT [31] (layer-only skipping at 75% TFLOPs), PAR (T=256, L=28) at 62.4% TFLOPs surpasses it by +10.9 on POPE (80.6 vs 69.7) and +12.1 on MMBench (62.6 vs 50.4). Against RoE-LLaVA [47] and γ-MoD [29], which require retraining or architectural redesign, PAR matches or exceeds their reported scores (e.g., 70.4 vs 68.7 on SQA vs RoE-LLaVA; 1351 vs 1342 on MME vs γ-MoD) without any weight updates. On Qwen-VL-Chat-9B, PAR (T=102, L=30) beats FastV by +1.1 on POPE, +1.6 on SEED, and +3.8 on MMBench at a comparable 73.3% vs 72.0% TFLOPs, confirming cross-model generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAR extends the layer-redundancy findings from ShortGPT (NLP) into the multimodal domain and departs from prior token-pruning methods (FastV, LLaVolta, LLaVA-PruMerge) that rely on fixed attention-score rules, instead learning a lightweight meta-router via DPO in a self-supervised manner. It also repurposes DPO—originally designed for language-model alignment—as a training signal for compression decisions, and introduces Gaussian-noise perturbation to expand the token-selection search space beyond what rule-based ranking can capture. Conceptually, the key departure from prior work is the joint modeling of layer and token pruning: previous efforts treat parameter and input compression as independent research lines, whereas PAR's meta-router adaptively coordinates both, motivated by the empirical observation that token choices shift layer-wise redundancy rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: PAR can be attached to already-deployed LVLMs without retraining the backbone (only ~10 minutes on a single 3090 GPU), making it a genuinely plug-and-play acceleration module. The empirical finding that deep layers in LVLMs are highly redundant and that token-layer interactions are non-trivial provides useful guidance for future compression research. Limitations include evaluation on only two backbones (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen-VL-9B), a small 5k-sample training set, and the ~40M-parameter meta-router overhead; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to much larger models (70B+), video inputs, or settings where token dropping is unacceptable (e.g., medical imaging).
