## Problem

Existing automated Wikipedia-style article generation systems (e.g., Storm, Co-Storm) are limited to text-only output, ignoring the visual content that makes real Wikipedia articles informative and engaging. Additionally, generated articles often lack breadth, depth, and factual reliability, and existing benchmarks focus on well-covered topics, leaving underexplored subjects untested.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250610/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a topic in one of three modalities: a text string, a single image, or a text-image pair. For image inputs, Google Vision Search extracts metadata and NER keywords to seed the outline. The output is a structured Wikipedia-style article with sectioned prose, relevant images placed at LLM-proposed positions with generated captions, and verifiable web references. The article is refined iteratively through multiple self-reflection passes before finalization.

## Method

WikiAutoGen is a zero-shot multi-agent pipeline built on GPT-4o/GPT-4o-mini/GPT-o3-mini via DSPy, organized into four modules: (1) an Outline Proposal module that structures the topic using LLM reasoning plus external search (Google Vision Search + NER for images); (2) a Textual Article Writing module where a persona generator spawns n role-specific agents that collaboratively explore the web under an 'asker' agent's guidance, then a writing agent drafts the article; (3) a Multi-Perspective Self-Reflection module that evaluates content from four viewpoints (supervisor, writer, reader, editor) across seven criteria (reliability, engagement, informativeness, coherence, readability, consistency, helpfulness), providing iterative feedback at both the discussion and drafting stages; and (4) a Multimodal Article Writing module that proposes image placements, retrieves candidates from multiple sources, selects the best image via CLIP semantic ranking followed by a multimodal LLM judgment, and polishes the integrated article for cross-modal coherence. The key differentiator is the combination of true multimodal retrieval (text + image) with the multi-role self-reflection loop, which iteratively refines both textual and visual content without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the WikiSeek benchmark, WikiAutoGen achieves average textual quality scores of 78.73 (text input), 77.49 (image input), and 78.82 (image-text input), outperforming the best baselines by 8.8, 29.1, and 15.1 points respectively. For image quality, WikiAutoGen scores 68.99, 68.78, and 70.98 across the three modalities, exceeding the next-best method by 11.7, 15.3, and 12.1 points. Against commercial deep-research systems, WikiAutoGen reaches an average of 89.01 (vs. OpenAI's 93.06) while generating articles in ~8 minutes, over 3.75× faster than OpenAI's ~30 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WikiAutoGen outperforms all four baselines across every input modality and every evaluation dimension. Against Co-Storm (the strongest text-only baseline), WikiAutoGen gains +8.8 points on text input (78.73 vs. 69.96), +29.1 points on image input (77.49 vs. 44.39), and +15.1 points on image-text input (78.82 vs. 63.77). Storm and OmniThink perform comparably to each other but are consistently 10–20 points below WikiAutoGen on text input and 30+ points below on image input, where their lack of native multimodal retrieval is most exposed. oRAG (Self-RAG) is the weakest baseline on image input (48.38 avg) but remains competitive on text input (60.71), still trailing WikiAutoGen by ~18 points. On image quality metrics (Table 4), WikiAutoGen leads by 11–15 points over the next-best method across all modalities, with the largest gap in helpfulness (77.63 vs. 56.13 on image input).

## Relation to Prior Work

WikiAutoGen extends the multi-agent Wikipedia-style article generation line initiated by Storm and Co-Storm, which rely exclusively on textual retrieval and LLM-driven discourse. Its key conceptual departure is the integration of a visual modality (image retrieval via CLIP, Google Vision Search, and NER-based keyword extraction) into both the input (image or image-text topics) and the output (embedded figures with captions), a capability absent from all prior expository-writing systems. The multi-perspective self-reflection module builds on the Navigator-Reasoner heuristic from Mirror [43] but augments it with live multi-web search, enabling grounded, externally verifiable feedback rather than closed-loop LLM discussion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of WikiSeek—a benchmark targeting underexplored Wikipedia topics with fewer than 500 characters of existing coverage—addresses a real gap: prior benchmarks (Surfer100, FreshWiki) focus on well-documented, easy topics where retrieval is straightforward. The practical finding that a multi-agent pipeline using GPT-4o/o3-mini can approach OpenAI's commercial deep-research quality at roughly 3.75× lower latency suggests a viable path toward scalable, cost-efficient knowledge synthesis. Open questions include whether the LLM-as-judge evaluation (GPT-4o) inflates scores relative to human perception, whether the multimodal advantage persists for highly technical or rapidly evolving topics, and how the system scales beyond the 300-topic benchmark.
