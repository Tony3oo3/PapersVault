## Problem

Object-level Vision-Language Models (OVLMs) like GLIP and GroundingDINO struggle with rare or novel categories underrepresented in their pre-training data, and text prompts alone lack sufficient granularity for fine-grained distinction. Existing approaches to incorporate visual exemplars—whether through full fine-tuning or adding trainable cross-attention modules (e.g., MQ-Det)—disrupt the OVLM's pre-trained object-text alignment, degrading generalization. The paper asks whether visual exemplar semantics can be introduced without altering the OVLM's architecture or weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image, K support images (each with a target bounding box used for background-blur prompt engineering), and a text prompt of class names. Support images are processed by the frozen OVLM visual encoder to extract multi-scale features, which are projected into the text feature space as a single textualized visual token per support image. These tokens are concatenated with BERT-processed text prompt tokens and fed into the unmodified OVLM, which outputs bounding boxes with class labels for the query image.

## Method

VisTex-OVLM introduces visual textualization: a lightweight Multi-Scale Textualizing Block (MSTB) applies parameter-shared 3×3 stride-2 convolutions to progressively downsample multi-scale visual features from the frozen OVLM encoder, followed by an MLP that maps the concatenated features to the text feature dimension, producing one textualized visual token per support image. A non-parametric Multi-Stage Fusion (MSF) strategy (max pooling) consolidates tokens from all L stages of the visual encoder into a single token per exemplar. The resulting textualized tokens are simply concatenated with BERT text prompt tokens and input directly into the unmodified OVLM, preserving its object-text alignment entirely. Only the MSTB is trainable (≈63M parameters for GLIP-L), trained end-to-end on the base set using the OVLM's original pre-training loss; at inference, it directly textualizes novel-class support images without any fine-tuning on those classes.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC (AP50, mean over 3 splits), VisTex-GLIP achieves 71.8, surpassing the previous SOTA MTL-FSOD (71.2) and non-VLM methods like SNIDA-MFD (60.7) by over 11 points. On MSCOCO (mAP, 30-shot), VisTex-GLIP reaches 53.6, outperforming MQ-Det (48.1) and GLIP-FF (49.4) by 5.5 and 4.2 points respectively. In open-set scenarios on LVIS MiniVal, VisTex-GLIP attains 50.7 AP without any training on the target dataset, exceeding GLIP-FF (49.5) and OWL-ViTv2 (47.2). VisTex-DINO also improves over GroundingDINO-FF on both PASCAL VOC (52.6 vs 49.0) and MSCOCO 30-shot (55.4 vs 53.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisTex-GLIP achieves the highest mean AP50 on PASCAL VOC (71.8), narrowly beating MTL-FSOD (71.2) and substantially outperforming SNIDA-MFD (60.7), MQ-Det (57.5), and GLIP-FF (54.2) by 11.1, 14.3, and 17.6 points respectively. On MSCOCO 30-shot mAP, VisTex-GLIP (53.6) surpasses MQ-Det (48.1) by 5.5 points and GLIP-FF (49.4) by 4.2 points, while VisTex-DINO (55.4) edges out GroundingDINO-FF (53.0) by 2.4 points. In open-set LVIS MiniVal evaluation, VisTex-GLIP (50.7 AP) exceeds GLIP-FF (49.5) and OWL-ViTv2 (47.2), though VisTex-DINO (42.8) trails OWL-ViTv2 (47.2) on this particular benchmark. Across the 16 unseen ODinW35 and medical datasets, VisTex-GLIP and VisTex-DINO consistently outperform or match all baselines, with VisTex-GLIP achieving 77.5 AP on subset A (vs. GLIP-FF 75.8) and VisTex-DINO reaching 70.2 (vs. OWL-ViTv2 65.4). The key advantage over MQ-Det is that VisTex-OVLM directly incorporates visual information rather than merely re-weighting text tokens, yielding 14+ point gains on PASCAL VOC while preserving the OVLM's pre-trained object-text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on object-level VLMs (OVLMs) such as GLIP and GroundingDINO, which establish strong object-text alignment through multi-stage cross-attention architectures. It departs from MQ-Det, the prior OVLM-based image prompting method, which inserts trainable cross-attention modules into the text encoder to modulate text tokens with visual exemplar information; VisTex-OVLM instead projects visual exemplars into the text feature space via lightweight multi-scale textualizing blocks, leaving the OVLM entirely unmodified. The image prompt engineering (background blur around bounding boxes) follows CLIPSeg's approach for segmentation, adapted here for detection. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both fine-tuning-based transfer (which disrupts alignment) and prompt-tuning methods (which add learnable tokens), arguing that a frozen OVLM with textualized visual tokens is the cleanest way to incorporate few-shot visual information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: only the lightweight MSTB requires training (on base classes), while the OVLM remains frozen, enabling deployment on resource-constrained devices for open-vocabulary detection with image prompts. The empirical demonstration that any structural or weight modification to an OVLM degrades its source-domain object-text alignment (Fig. 1) provides a useful diagnostic for the community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to OVLMs without multi-stage encoders, how it scales to very large numbers of support images, and whether the non-parametric max-pooling fusion is optimal compared to learned fusion. The marginal PASCAL VOC improvement over MTL-FSOD (71.8 vs. 71.2) suggests the method's advantage is most pronounced in genuinely open-set or domain-shifted scenarios rather than standard few-shot benchmarks.
