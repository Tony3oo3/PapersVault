## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D semantic occupancy methods rely on processing large-scale voxel features, incurring high computational costs while degrading fine-grained details. They also struggle to accurately capture occluded targets and distant objects, and the inherent heterogeneity between camera and LiDAR features leads to inconsistencies that degrade prediction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250112/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-camera surround-view images cropped to 256×704 and 10 LiDAR sweeps voxelized into a sparse 3D grid. Both modalities are projected into a unified BEV space before fusion. The output is a 3D semantic occupancy map over 17 categories, produced by rearranging fused BEV features from the channel dimension to the height dimension (Channel-to-Height), yielding per-voxel semantic labels within a 102.4 m × 102.4 m × 8 m evaluation volume at 0.2 m resolution (Occ3D-nuScenes) or 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m at 0.4 m resolution (nuScenes-Occupancy).

## Method

RIOcc encodes LiDAR and camera data into a shared BEV representation, then refines each branch independently: the LiDAR branch applies Dual-branch Pooling (DBP) combining Channel-wise and Grid-wise attention to adaptively highlight important semantic channels and geometric regions, while the camera branch uses a Wavelet Encoder (DWT/IWT decomposition) to extract edge and structural details and a lightweight 2D U-Net Semantic Encoder for high-level semantic features. Cross-modal fusion is performed by a Deformable Dual-Attention (DDA) module that first applies Gaussian-encoded distance weighting to LiDAR features to compensate for sparsity at long range, aggregates local regions into representative tokens, and then performs bidirectional deformable cross-attention between LiDAR and camera tokens to dynamically select complementary information. The fused multi-scale BEV features are passed through an occupancy head and a Channel-to-Height transformation to produce the final 3D voxel-level semantic prediction. Training uses a composite loss combining cross-entropy, Lovasz-Softmax, scene-wise and class-wise affinity losses, depth supervision, and an auxiliary semantic loss on the U-Net output.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes dataset, RIOcc achieves a state-of-the-art 54.21 mIoU using only a ResNet-50 backbone at 256×704 resolution, outperforming the previous best method RadOcc (49.38 mIoU) by 4.83 mIoU. On the nuScenes-Occupancy validation set, RIOcc attains 35.4 IoU and 25.9 mIoU, surpassing OccMamba (25.1 mIoU) by 0.8 mIoU and EFFOcc (22.9 mIoU) by 3.0 mIoU. Notably, RIOcc achieves these results with a significantly lighter image backbone (R50 vs. R101) and lower input resolution (256×704 vs. 900×1600) than most competing multi-modal methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIOcc outperforms all 24 compared baselines on both evaluation datasets. On Occ3D-nuScenes, it leads the closest multi-modal competitor RadOcc [54] (Swin-B, 512×1408) by 4.83 mIoU (54.21 vs. 49.38) and OccFusion [56] (R101, 900×1600) by 5.47 mIoU, while using a substantially lighter R50 backbone at 256×704 resolution. Among camera-only methods, it surpasses the best (GEOcc* at 43.64 mIoU) by over 10 mIoU, highlighting the benefit of LiDAR-camera fusion. On nuScenes-Occupancy, RIOcc achieves 25.9 mIoU, edging out OccMamba [20] (25.1) by 0.8 mIoU and EFFOcc [38] (22.9) by 3.0 mIoU. The margin over single-modal LiDAR methods (e.g., PointOcc at 23.9 mIoU) is 2.0 mIoU, and over the original OpenOccupancy multi-modal baseline M-CONet [47] (20.1 mIoU) it is 5.8 mIoU. Per-class gains are most pronounced on small or distant objects such as traffic cone (+12.15 over RadOcc) and bicycle (+11.97 over RadOcc), consistent with the paper's claim that DDA improves occluded and distant target detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIOcc builds directly on the BEVFusion [25] multi-modal BEV extraction pipeline and the FlashOcc [53] Channel-to-Height mechanism for converting 2D BEV features into 3D voxel predictions. It departs from the voxel-centric paradigm of OpenOccupancy [47] and subsequent multi-modal occupancy methods (OccFusion, Co-Occ, OccMamba) by performing all feature refinement and cross-modal interaction in the BEV plane rather than in 3D space, reducing computational cost. The Deformable Dual-Attention (DDA) module extends deformable attention ideas from object detection into the occupancy setting, while the Wavelet Encoder and Dual-branch Pooling represent novel feature-refinement strategies not seen in prior occupancy work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RIOcc demonstrates that a relatively lightweight configuration (256×704 input, ResNet-50 backbone, 4×3090 GPUs) can outperform heavier multi-modal occupancy methods by a substantial margin, suggesting that architectural design in the BEV domain matters more than raw input resolution or backbone capacity for this task. The DDA module offers a principled mechanism for cross-modal complementarity that could transfer to other BEV-based perception tasks. Open questions include whether the efficiency advantage persists at higher resolutions or on more challenging datasets, whether the method generalizes beyond the nuScenes sensor suite, and whether the multi-scale fusion strategy scales to real-time deployment constraints in production autonomous driving systems.
