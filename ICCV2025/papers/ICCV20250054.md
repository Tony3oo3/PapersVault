## Problem

Existing driving world models excel at predicting future scene evolution (e.g., point cloud forecasting) but cannot interpret or reason about the driving environment, while driving VLMs understand scenes yet lack predictive capability. This paper addresses the gap by asking how world knowledge and future scene generation can be seamlessly integrated into a single unified world model for autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250054/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes six surrounding camera images (nuScenes), a text instruction or question, and ego-motion conditions (planned future positions and heading for each prediction frame) as input. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a textual response (scene description or VQA answer) via next-token prediction, and future point clouds at 1s, 2s, and 3s horizons rendered from SDF-based volume rendering along LiDAR-configured rays. The BEV representation is downsampled to a 50×50 spatial grid (2500 tokens) to fit within the LLM's context window, and the model relies only on the current frame rather than a 3-second history.

## Method

HERMES encodes multi-view images into a compressed BEV representation via a CLIP encoder and BEVFormer v2, then feeds the flattened BEV tokens into a 1.8B-parameter InternVL2 LLM alongside text tokens. The key architectural innovation is a set of world queries, initialized by max-pooling the raw BEV features and augmented with ego-motion and frame embeddings, which are appended to the LLM input sequence; through causal attention, these queries absorb world knowledge from the understanding context. A current-to-future link module (3 cross-attention blocks) then uses the LLM-encoded BEV as query and the world-knowledge-enhanced queries as key/value to generate future BEV features for each prediction frame. A shared BEV-to-Point Render upsamples these features, adds a height dimension, applies 3D convolutions, and performs differentiable SDF-based volume rendering to produce point clouds. Training jointly optimizes next-token prediction loss for understanding and L1 depth loss for generation (weighted 1:10), with current-frame point cloud prediction serving as an auxiliary regularizer.

## Results

On the (OmniDrive-)nuScenes validation set, HERMES achieves a 3s Chamfer Distance of 1.17, reducing generation error by 32.4% compared to ViDAR (1.73) while using only the current frame (vs. 3s history). For scene understanding, HERMES attains CIDEr 0.741, METEOR 0.384, and ROUGE 0.327, outperforming OmniDrive (CIDEr 0.686) by 8.0% and matching the separated unification baseline (CIDEr 0.745) while achieving substantially better generation (3s Chamfer 1.17 vs. 1.37). HERMES also outperforms GPT-4o (CIDEr 0.244) and LLaVA-OV (CIDEr 0.284) by a wide margin on understanding metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generation-only baselines, HERMES substantially outperforms both 4D-Occ (3s Chamfer 2.11) and ViDAR (3s Chamfer 1.73), achieving 1.17 at 3s using only the current frame rather than a 3-second history. Against understanding-only baselines, HERMES surpasses OmniDrive (CIDEr 0.686) by 8.0% and OmniDrive-BEV (CIDEr 0.595) by 24.5%, while also exceeding GPT-4o (CIDEr 0.244) and LLaVA-OV (CIDEr 0.284) by large margins. Compared to the separated unification baseline (which shares BEV features but uses independent LLM and generator modules), HERMES achieves nearly identical understanding scores (CIDEr 0.741 vs. 0.745, METEOR 0.384 vs. 0.384) while improving 3s generation by 14.6% (Chamfer 1.17 vs. 1.37), demonstrating that the unified world-query mechanism enables effective cross-task knowledge transfer that the separated architecture cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of driving research: (1) Driving World Models focused on future scene generation (e.g., ViDAR, 4D-Occ) and (2) LLM-based driving understanding models (e.g., OmniDrive, DriveLM). Architecturally, it builds on BEVFormer v2 for multi-view-to-BEV tokenization and InternVL2 as the LLM backbone, but its key departure is the 'world queries' mechanism—learnable queries initialized from BEV features that, through the LLM's causal attention, absorb world knowledge from text tokens and then condition a current-to-future cross-attention link for generation. This contrasts with the straightforward 'separated unification' baseline where understanding and generation share BEV features but are handled by independent modules, which the paper shows underperforms due to missed cross-task interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HERMES demonstrates that a single compact model (1.8B LLM) can simultaneously perform 3D scene understanding (VQA, captioning) and future point-cloud generation at competitive or superior levels to task-specific specialists, suggesting that unified driving world models are feasible without prohibitive compute. However, the scope remains limited: generation is restricted to point clouds (no future images), perception tasks (detection, tracking) are unexplored, and the understanding gains over OmniDrive are modest (~8% CIDEr). Open questions include whether the world-queries paradigm scales to longer horizons, richer generation modalities, and closed-loop planning, and whether the current-to-future cross-attention link can be replaced by more efficient interaction mechanisms as generation length increases.
