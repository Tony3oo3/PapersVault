## Problem

Existing invisible image watermarking methods suffer from fragility under common transformations (resizing, cropping, compression, noise) and are particularly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations and regeneration-based attacks (e.g., diffusion model re-synthesis). The fundamental trade-off between watermark imperceptibility and robustness remains unresolved, with prior frequency-domain approaches easily disrupted by standard image manipulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250475/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB cover image (evaluated at 256×256, 512×512, and 1024×1024 resolutions) and a binary message of configurable length (48–256 bits), producing a visually indistinguishable watermarked image. The decoder takes the (potentially attacked) watermarked image and recovers the binary message using a learnable threshold. Embedding is localized via a radial mask of radius r=100 centered in the high-frequency sub-band, with a strength factor s=20 controlling embedding intensity and a set of 32 convolutional layers refining spectral features.

## Method

SpecGuard embeds watermark bits in the high-frequency S_HH sub-band obtained via wavelet projection, then applies an FFT-based spectral projection (using symmetric extension to yield real-valued coefficients) to transform the sub-band into the spectral domain. A radial mask restricts embedding to a central region, and the binary message is injected with a strength factor s through additive modulation, followed by convolutional layers with LeakyReLU to harmonize the embedded signal. The decoder mirrors this pipeline in reverse and introduces a learnable threshold θ, justified by Parseval's theorem (which guarantees spectral-spatial energy equivalence), that adapts during training to distinguish high-energy '1' bits from low-energy '0' bits even under distortion. Training uses a composite MSE loss balancing encoder fidelity (λ_enc=0.7) and decoder recovery accuracy (λ_dec=1.0), with the threshold θ updated via gradient descent to optimize bit recovery under diverse attack conditions.

## Results

SpecGuard achieves the highest PSNR of 42.89 dB and SSIM of 0.99 at 128-bit message length, outperforming all 10 SOTA baselines (best competitor: EditGuard at 41.30 dB PSNR). It also attains the lowest FID of 17.0 and highest Bit Recovery Accuracy (BRA) of 0.99 at 128 bits, while maintaining PSNR ≥ 40.86 dB even at 256 bits. Under robustness evaluation (Table 3), SpecGuard achieves Avg P of 0.998 for brightness/contrast/JPEG attacks and 0.998 for crop, with Q@0.95P values of 'inf' (no attack degrades performance below threshold) for most distortion types, substantially exceeding Tree-Ring, Stable Signature, and StegaStamp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecGuard outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, FID, BRA) across all three bit lengths (64, 128, 256). The closest competitor is EditGuard (CVPR'24), which trails SpecGuard by 1.29 dB PSNR (41.30 vs 42.89) and 0.02 SSIM at 128 bits, while MuST (AAAI'24) lags by 1.99 dB PSNR and drops sharply in BRA at 256 bits (0.90 vs 0.98). Pre-processing methods (Tree-Ring, Stable Signature, Yang et al., SleeperMark) are uniformly weaker, with PSNR values 9–13 dB lower than SpecGuard. On robustness (Table 3), SpecGuard achieves 'inf' Q@0.95P for brightness, contrast, noise, JPEG, and all adversarial attacks, meaning no tested perturbation degrades detection below 95% accuracy, whereas Tree-Ring and StegaStamp show finite or negative-infinite values on several of these. For regeneration attacks (Rinse-2xDiff, Regen-VAE), SpecGuard maintains Avg P of 0.993–0.995, far exceeding Stable Signature (0.001) and Tree-Ring (0.510–0.832).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecGuard carves new ground by combining wavelet-based multi-resolution decomposition with FFT-approximated spectral projection inside hidden convolutional layers, a pipeline not previously integrated in a single watermarking architecture. It departs from prior frequency-domain methods (e.g., DCT-based or direct DFT embedding) by targeting high-frequency sub-bands (S_HH) after wavelet decomposition, and from CNN-only approaches (HiDDeN, StegaStamp) by grounding the embedding in explicit spectral mathematics rather than purely learned spatial features. The adaptation of Parseval's theorem as a learnable decoding threshold is a novel repurposing of a classical signal-processing identity for end-to-end watermark extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpecGuard addresses the long-standing imperceptibility–robustness trade-off in invisible watermarking and provides practical validation on social-media compression and Photoshop neural filters, which are underrepresented in prior evaluations. Its demonstrated resilience against diffusion-based regeneration (Rinse, RegenVAE) and adversarial embedding attacks is timely given the proliferation of AI image manipulation tools. Open questions include whether the spectral-domain approach generalizes to video or 3D content, how it performs under multi-watermark or collaborative ownership scenarios, and whether the fixed radial-mask and strength-factor design can be made adaptive per-image without additional overhead.
