## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based talking head methods suffer from insufficient 3D geometry and poor detail reconstruction when trained from monocular video alone, while generative-prior approaches face a fundamental mismatch: the latent space is entangled with identity and global expression, yet audio primarily drives local lip motion. This entanglement leads to suboptimal audio-visual synchronization and limits multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is trained on a single monocular video (~6,500 frames at 25 FPS, cropped to 512×512) with per-frame audio features, camera parameters, and AU45 eye-blink signals. At inference, it takes an arbitrary audio clip, an eye-blink signal, and a target camera pose to produce a 3D Gaussian Splatting head representation that is rendered in real time (~71 FPS) via a tile-based rasterizer, yielding photorealistic talking head frames from any viewpoint.

## Method

DGTalker reformulates audio-driven talking head synthesis as disentangled latent space navigation within a Gaussian generative prior (GGHead). The latent code is decomposed into a frozen canonical vector w_can (encoding identity with neutral expression), an expression vector, and a lip vector, each expressed as a linear combination of learnable orthogonal blendshape bases (k_e and k_l vectors respectively). A dual-encoder architecture regresses expression coefficients from audio plus eye-blink signal and lip coefficients from audio alone, which are then linearly interpolated with w_can to form the final latent code fed to a personalized generator. To enforce disentanglement during training, a Masked Cross-View Supervision strategy randomly pairs the lip code from one frame with the expression code from another, renders the resulting non-existent 3DGS head from two viewpoints, and applies region-specific pixel and perceptual losses (upper-face mask from one source, lower-face mask from the other), applied with probability p=50% to avoid distributional shift.

## Results

In the self-reconstruction setting (Table 1), DGTalker achieves the best image quality across all metrics: PSNR 28.94, LPIPS 0.065, SSIM 0.863, and FID 15.15, outperforming the next-best HFA-GP* (PSNR 27.42, FID 31.22) by a wide margin. On motion quality, DGTalker attains the lowest AUE (1.209) and competitive Sync-C (6.295) and Sync-E (8.936), though TalkingGaussian edges ahead on LMD (3.944 vs. 3.997) and Sync-C (6.629 vs. 6.295). In the novel-view setting (Table 2), DGTalker dominates all five metrics (FID 80.01, IDSIM 0.436, AUE 2.525, Sync-E 9.565, Sync-C 5.255), and in the generalized audio-lip sync setting (Table 3) it achieves the highest Sync-C on both unseen audio clips (3.947 and 4.069).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGTalker clearly outperforms all four baselines on image quality: it leads PSNR by 1.5 dB over HFA-GP* (28.94 vs. 27.42), 2.7 dB over GaussianTalker, and 4.9 dB over ER-NeRF, while achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.065) and FID (15.15). Against TalkingGaussian, the closest 3DGS competitor, DGTalker wins on PSNR (+2.7), LPIPS (−0.029), SSIM (+0.016), FID (−6.6), and AUE (−0.045), but concedes LMD (3.997 vs. 3.944), Sync-E (8.936 vs. 8.460), Sync-C (6.295 vs. 6.629), and FPS (71 vs. 94). HFA-GP*, despite sharing the GGHead backbone, suffers from poor audio-lip synchronization (Sync-C 2.469 vs. 6.295) due to its failure to disentangle the latent space, and also shows the worst AUE (1.842). In the novel-view and generalized audio settings, DGTalker achieves the best scores on every reported metric, confirming that the advantage is not limited to the canonical viewpoint. Overall, DGTalker trades a modest ~23 FPS reduction versus TalkingGaussian for substantially better visual fidelity and expression accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGTalker sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based talking head synthesis (GaussianTalker, TalkingGaussian) and 3D-aware GAN generative priors (HFA-GP, Talk3D, GGhead). It departs from prior generative-prior methods by rejecting the full-latent-space navigation paradigm (HFA-GP) and the external motion-compensation network (Talk3D), instead reformulating the problem as disentangled latent space navigation with separate lip and expression blendshape bases. The masked cross-view supervision strategy is a novel training mechanism not seen in prior talking head or GAN editing literature, borrowing the spirit of region-specific losses from ER-NeRF but applying it across synthetic cross-view compositions to enforce disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental mismatch in audio-driven 3D head synthesis: audio correlates with local lip motion while generative latent spaces encode global, entangled identity and expression. The disentangled framework with well-defined semantic components (canonical, expression, lip) offers a practical path toward controllable, multi-view-consistent, real-time talking head generation that prior 3DGS methods could not achieve without generative priors. Limitations include per-identity training (no multi-identity generalization), reliance on a fixed GGhead backbone, and evaluation on only 8 short clips. Open questions include whether the disentangled navigation extends to multi-identity or emotion-conditioned settings, and whether the MCS strategy generalizes beyond the binary upper/lower face split.
