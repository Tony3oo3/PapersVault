## Problem

Existing factuality benchmarks such as SimpleQA and Chinese SimpleQA evaluate only text-modality LLMs, leaving the factuality of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) unmeasured. MLLMs are particularly susceptible to hallucinations introduced by visual inputs, yet no comprehensive, bilingual benchmark exists to assess whether they can produce factually grounded short answers from image-question pairs. SimpleVQA fills this gap as the first multimodal factuality evaluation benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251740/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a real, unaltered RGB image (no embedded text revealing the answer) with a concise natural-language question in English or Chinese, requiring a single, time-stable short factual answer. Evaluation is performed via an LLM-as-a-judge that classifies each response as Correct (CO), Not Attempted (NA), or Incorrect (IN), from which Correct-Given-Attempted (CGA) and a harmonic-mean F-score are derived. Questions are constrained to be answerable with knowledge available before September 2024 and must have been answered incorrectly by at least one of four reference MLLMs to ensure difficulty.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed through a five-step pipeline: (1) collecting seed Q&A pairs from existing VQA datasets and search engines, (2) using GPT-4o to rephrase and align items into short-answer factual format, (3) LLM-based quality verification followed by dual-annotator human review, (4) difficulty screening that discards any question all four reference MLLMs answer correctly, and (5) extracting atomic facts (the minimal visual-recognition sub-question) for each item. The key analytical contribution is the Complex Fact Question (CFQ) experiment: by decomposing each VQA into an atomic visual-recognition question and then re-asking the original question with the atomic fact explicitly provided, the framework disentangles failures due to poor visual understanding from failures due to insufficient internalized world knowledge. The final dataset contains 2,025 bilingual samples spanning 9 task categories (e.g., Object Identification, Location & Building, Text Processing) and 9 domain categories (e.g., Natural Science, Chinese History & Culture, Engineering & Technology), enabling fine-grained diagnosis of MLLM factuality across both visual comprehension and knowledge retrieval dimensions.

## Results

On SimpleVQA (2,025 bilingual VQA samples), Gemini-2.0-flash achieves the highest overall F-score of 54.4, followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet (50.9) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.8). GPT-4o scores 49.1 F-score with 47.2% correct (CO) and 45.0% incorrect (IN). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct leads at 50.8 F-score, outperforming several closed-source models, while the weakest model (Janus-pro-7B) achieves only 33.0 F-score. Text-only LLMs without image input drop dramatically, with DeepSeek-R1 reaching only 10.08% CO and GPT-4o just 5.23% CO, confirming the benchmark's reliance on visual information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.0-flash leads all 18 evaluated MLLMs with a 54.4 F-score, outperforming GPT-4o (49.1) by 5.3 points and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (50.9) by 3.5 points. In the cross-lingual breakdown, Gemini-2.0-flash dominates Chinese (56.3 F-score) while Claude-3.5-Sonnet edges ahead in English (52.8 vs. 52.5), and ERNIE-VL shows a notable Chinese advantage (54.6) over its English performance (42.7). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (50.8 F-score) surpasses GPT-4o-mini (37.3), Doubao-vision-pro-32k (28.3), and Qwen-Max (27.5), and is competitive with GPT-4o (49.1) and ERNIE-VL (48.8). The CFQ ablation reveals that providing atomic facts explicitly improves accuracy by 4.5–14.6 points (e.g., InternVL2.5-78B-MPO goes from 55.36% to 69.95%), indicating that visual understanding failures are a larger bottleneck than knowledge gaps. Text-only LLMs without images achieve only 2–10% CO, confirming that the benchmark genuinely requires multimodal input.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimpleVQA extends the text-only factuality evaluation paradigm established by SimpleQA and Chinese SimpleQA into the multimodal setting, adding image-conditioned factual questions with static, verifiable answers. It departs from existing multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MMMU, MME, MM-Vet) that assess general vision-language understanding or reasoning, by specifically targeting factual knowledge boundaries with short-answer format and LLM-as-a-Judge scoring. The atomic fact decomposition (CFQ) methodology is a novel analytical contribution that separates visual comprehension failures from knowledge internalization failures, a diagnostic not present in prior VQA or factuality benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SimpleVQA provides the first bilingual, multi-task, multi-scenario benchmark specifically designed to measure factual accuracy in MLLMs, filling a gap between text-only factuality benchmarks and general multimodal evaluation suites. The CFQ decomposition offers a practical diagnostic tool for identifying whether model failures stem from visual understanding or knowledge gaps, which is critical for targeted model improvement. Open questions include whether the 2,025-sample scale is sufficient for stable cross-model comparisons, how to improve visual-knowledge alignment in MLLMs, and whether the static-answer constraint adequately captures real-world factuality challenges. The finding that even top models (Gemini-2.0-flash at ~54% F-score) struggle significantly, combined with documented overconfidence in incorrect answers, underscores that multimodal factuality remains a major unsolved problem.
