## Problem

Existing methods that learn manipulation from action-unlabeled videos rely on object-centric flow prediction, which fundamentally fails on deformable objects, occluded scenes, and non-object-displacement tasks (e.g., rotating a switch or pressing a button). The paper asks whether shifting the prediction target from objects to the robot embodiment itself can overcome these three limitations while still requiring no low-level action labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes action-unlabeled RGB videos (5 per task, fixed camera), a language instruction, and a standard URDF file specifying joint kinematics; depth maps from an RGB-D camera are used at inference for 3D reconstruction. It outputs a pixel-wise trajectory of N_p=400 randomly sampled embodiment points over a T=8-step horizon, an auxiliary goal image, and optimized end-effector poses (position + gripper state) ready for robot control. All point coordinates are normalized for scale invariance, and images are resized to 128×128 for the diffusion backbone.

## Method

EC-Flow replaces object-centric flow with embodiment-centric flow: a conditional diffusion model (DDIM) predicts future uv-coordinates and visibility of sampled robot-arm points, conditioned on the initial frame (ResNet-50), language instruction (CLIP text encoder), and starting point positions. A jointly trained goal-image prediction branch shares diffusion timesteps with the flow branch, forcing the predicted motion to produce physically plausible object states aligned with the language instruction. For action computation, a URDF-aware pipeline filters tracked points by visibility and displacement, assigns them to joints via 2D bounding boxes derived from URDF geometry, lifts them to 3D using depth, and then optimizes the end-effector pose by minimizing reprojection error through inverse kinematics—requiring only the robot's standard URDF file and no action labels at any stage.

## Results

On the Meta-World simulation benchmark (9 tasks, 5 videos per task), EC-Flow achieves 72.0% average success rate, surpassing the best object-centric flow baseline Track2Act (55.6%) by 16.4% and AVDC (48.9%) by 23.1%. In real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 arm (7 tasks, 5 videos per task), EC-Flow reaches 54/70 success versus Track2Act's 21/70 and Behavior Cloning's 31/70. The paper reports 62%, 45%, and 80% success-rate improvements over prior object-centric flow methods on occluded object handling, deformable object manipulation, and non-object-displacement tasks, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EC-Flow decisively outperforms both object-centric flow baselines: Track2Act [1] (55.6% avg) and AVDC [16] (48.9% avg) on Meta-World, with the largest gaps on btn-top-press (1.00 vs 0.40) and hammer-strike (0.88 vs 0.24), tasks heavily affected by occlusion. Against imitation-learning baselines, EC-Flow also wins: Diffusion Policy [6] (29.8%), BC-R3M [22] (36.0%), BC-Scratch (20.4%), and UniPi [7] (9.3%) all trail significantly, confirming that action-unlabeled video learning with embodiment-centric flow surpasses action-labeled approaches even with only 5 demonstrations per task. In real-world evaluation, EC-Flow (54/70) beats Track2Act (21/70) by 157% and Behavior Cloning (31/70) by 74%, with Track2Act scoring 0/10 on all four deformable and non-displacement tasks where EC-Flow achieves 3/10, 6/10, 9/10, and 7/10 respectively. The one task where EC-Flow does not lead is shelf-place (0.04), where AVDC achieves 0.20, though all methods perform poorly on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the object-centric flow paradigm (Track2Act, AVDC, General Flow) that infers robot actions from inter-frame object transformations, instead predicting flow on the robot's own embodiment. Conceptually, it repurposes the diffusion-based flow prediction framework (DDPM/DDIM) and off-the-shelf point tracking (CoTracker) but redirects the prediction target from scene objects to the robot arm's joints, leveraging URDF kinematics to decompose global flow into joint-specific actions. The goal-image prediction auxiliary task draws on video generation ideas (UniPi, Dreamitate) but uses them as a grounding constraint rather than a planning mechanism, creating a tighter coupling between motion prediction and semantic task alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for low-level action labels entirely, EC-Flow addresses a major practical bottleneck in robotic manipulation data collection, requiring only 5 unlabeled video demonstrations per task and a standard URDF file for deployment. The unified handling of deformable objects, occlusions, and non-displacement tasks (pressing, rotating) under a single framework is practically significant, as these are common real-world scenarios where prior methods fail outright. Open questions include scalability to multi-camera or dynamic scenes, sensitivity to depth sensor quality (noted in the fold-clothes task), and whether the approach generalizes to dexterous hands or multi-embodiment settings beyond single-arm manipulation.
