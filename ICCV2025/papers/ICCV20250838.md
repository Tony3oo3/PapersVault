## Problem

Existing anomaly detection benchmarks (MVTec-AD, VisA) are saturated at 99.9% AUROC and target controlled manufacturing with fixed poses and few object categories, leaving the far harder retail logistics setting unaddressed. In retail logistics, millions of diverse items exhibit significant pose and appearance variability, defective samples are extremely rare per item, and leading anomaly detection methods fail to generalize. This paper asks how to build generalizable visual defect detection under limited instances per item, scarce defective samples, and heavy intra-class variation.

## Input / Output

Each query is a 2048×2048 top-down RGB image of a retail item in a logistics tray, paired with 1–3 unlabeled reference images of the same item and a U-Net-generated segmentation mask (also released as square item-crops with 10% padding). The primary evaluation task is binary classification (no defect vs. any defect), while the dataset additionally provides fine-grained annotations for defect severity (no/minor/major) and one or more defect types from seven categories (penetration, deformation, actuation, deconstruction, spillage, superficial, missing unit). The 238,421 images span 48,376 unique items, split by item identifier into 85/5/10 train/val/test to prevent cross-split leakage.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the Kaputt dataset (238,421 images, 29,316 defective instances, 48,376 items) and a four-scenario benchmark evaluating zero-shot VLMs (CLIP, Claude, Pixtral), few-shot anomaly detectors (PatchCore, WinCLIP), supervised classifiers (ResNet50, ViT-S, AutoGluonMM), and combined approaches. The key empirical finding is that no unsupervised or anomaly detection method exceeds 56.96% AUROC, while supervised ViT-S reaches 94.27% AUROC only with abundant defective training data, collapsing to 57.7% AP under a realistic 1% defective rate. A notable negative result shows that naively incorporating reference images via feature averaging degrades performance (96%→87% AP on training set), indicating current methods cannot exploit references under pose and packaging variability. The dataset is 40× larger than MVTec-AD with 48,376 item categories versus 15, making it a substantially harder benchmark that exposes the inability of existing methods to leverage normal samples under heavy intra-class variation.

## Results

On the Kaputt test set (10,067 samples), the best supervised baseline (ViT-S fine-tuned on DINOv2) achieves 90.67% AP_any and 94.27% AUROC, while the best anomaly-detection method with training (PatchCore50-ft) reaches only 60.14% AUROC. No unsupervised or zero-shot method surpasses 56.96% AUROC (Claude-icl), confirming that existing AD approaches fail under the heavy pose and appearance variation of retail logistics. When defective training samples are reduced to a 1% rate, even the supervised ViT-S drops from 90.67% to 57.7% AP_any.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 15 baselines across four scenarios on the Kaputt benchmark. Supervised methods dominate: ViT-S (94.27% AUROC) outperforms ResNet50 (88.36%) by 5.91 points and AutoGluonMM (92.47%) by 1.80 points on AUROC. Among unsupervised/AD methods, PatchCore50-ft (60.14% AUROC) is the best, but it still trails the supervised ViT-S by 34.13 AUROC points. Zero-shot VLMs (Claude-icl at 56.96%, CLIP at 56.05%) and few-shot AD methods (PatchCore50 at 54.69%, WinCLIP-few at 52.41%) all cluster near chance level (~50%), with no method exceeding 57% AUROC without training data. Notably, adding reference images naively degrades performance: AutoGluonMM-ref (84.29% AUROC) underperforms AutoGluonMM without references (92.47%), and PatchCore50-ft (60.14%) only marginally improves over PatchCore50 (54.69%), suggesting current methods cannot effectively exploit reference images under pose and appearance variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the visual anomaly detection task from controlled manufacturing settings (MVTec-AD, VisA) to the far more open-ended domain of retail logistics, where millions of distinct items appear in random poses with limited reference availability. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes the existing anomaly detection and VLM evaluation framework (PatchCore, WinCLIP, CLIP, Claude, Pixtral) as a stress test, introducing the reference-image paradigm (1–3 unlabeled normal images per item) as a practical constraint absent from prior benchmarks. The dataset's scale (238K images, 48K items) and the explicit inclusion of pose/viewpoint variance as a first-class challenge mark a deliberate departure from the fixed-pose, few-category assumption underlying all prior industrial AD benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~43-point AUROC gap between manufacturing AD (99.9% on MVTec) and retail logistics AD (56.96% on Kaputt) quantifies how far current methods are from handling real-world item and pose variability, and the supervised-to-unsupervised drop (94.27% → 57.7% with only 1% defective training rate) highlights the data-scarcity bottleneck that motivates the field. The negative result that naively averaging reference-image embeddings degrades supervised performance (92.47% → 84.29% AUROC) is a useful caution for future work combining training data with references. Open questions the paper raises—generalizing AD under heavy intra-class variation, effectively leveraging limited and potentially defective references, and producing explainable defect reasoning—define a concrete research agenda for the next several years.
