## Problem

Duet dance generation requires modeling complex lead-follower dynamics, spatial partner relationships, and musical synchronization, yet existing duet datasets (DD100, InterDance) are limited in scale and lack textual annotations. No prior dataset integrates all three modalities—3D motion, music, and fine-grained text—for duet dance, making it impossible to study text-controlled partner interactions or reactive follower synthesis in a dance context. This paper addresses this gap by introducing MDD, the first large-scale multimodal duet dance benchmark spanning 15 genres with over 10K structured text descriptions.

## Input / Output

For Text-to-Duet, the model consumes a natural language description (averaging 41 words covering spatial relationships, body movements, and rhythm) and music features (54-dim MFCC via Librosa or 4800-dim Jukebox encoder) to produce synchronized SMPL-X motions for both leader and follower. For Text-to-Dance Accompaniment, the leader's SMPL-X motion sequence is additionally provided as input, and the model generates only the follower's motion. All motions are parameterized in SMPL-X format (pose θ ∈ R^{N×55×3}, shape β ∈ R^{N×10}, translation t ∈ R^{N×3}) captured at 120 fps from 53-marker OptiTrack recordings.

## Method

The dataset was captured using an OptiTrack system with 16 infrared cameras and 53 retro-reflective markers at 120 fps, with 30 professional dancers performing across 15 genres (Ballroom, Latin, Social). Annotations were collected via a web-based tool where dancers segmented their own motions into ≤10-second clips and described them using a structured vocabulary spanning spatial relationships, body movements, and rhythm; GPT-4o refined grammar while a second annotator verified accuracy. For baselines, MDM (diffusion) and InterGen (transformer) were adapted for Text-to-Duet by concatenating or cross-attending music features into the generation pipeline, while Duolando (GPT-style autoregressive) was extended with CLIP-based text encodings via cross-modal attention for Text-to-Dance Accompaniment. All models were trained for 3000 epochs with AdamW (batch size 64) and evaluated under text-only, music-only, and multimodal conditioning configurations.

## Results

For the Text-to-Duet task, InterGen with Jukebox music features achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.138) and Beat Echo Degree (0.454), while InterGen (text-only) attains the lowest FID (0.405) among all baselines, substantially outperforming MDM (best FID 1.420). For Text-to-Dance Accompaniment, the multimodal DuoLando (both text + music) achieves the best FID (0.698), R-Precision Top-1 (0.078), and BED (0.395), outperforming its text-only (FID 1.538) and music-only (FID 0.721) variants. Across both tasks, music-only conditioning generally yields worse text-alignment metrics (MM Dist, R-Precision) than text-inclusive configurations, confirming the complementary value of both modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper adapts three existing methods as baselines: MDM (diffusion-based motion generation), InterGen (diffusion-based multi-human interaction generation), and Duolando (GPT-style follower generation). For Text-to-Duet, InterGen consistently dominates MDM across all metrics: the best InterGen variant (text-only) achieves FID 0.405 versus MDM's best of 1.420, and R-Precision Top-1 of 0.113 versus 0.082. Adding Jukebox music features to InterGen further improves R-Precision Top-1 to 0.138 and BED to 0.454, suggesting richer music representations help synchronization. For Text-to-Dance Accompaniment, DuoLando with both text and music (FID 0.698, BED 0.395) outperforms its text-only variant (FID 1.538) by more than 2× in FID, and also beats the music-only variant (FID 0.721) on text-alignment metrics (MM Dist 2.113 vs. 2.633). Notably, MDM exhibits an anomalously high BAS score (0.231) likely due to jittery motions that the metric rewards, while Duolando reports ground-truth BAS (0.170) lower than its own generated motions, highlighting metric interpretation caveats the authors flag.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDD carves new ground by being the first dataset to simultaneously integrate text, music, and 3D motion for duet dance generation, whereas prior duet datasets (DD100/Duolando, InterDance) pair only motion with music, and interactive motion datasets (InterGen, Inter-X) pair motion with text but lack music and dance-specific vocabulary. The paper repurposes existing architectures—InterGen (diffusion-based two-person generation), MDM (diffusion for single-person motion), and Duolando (GPT-style follower synthesis)—as adapted baselines rather than building on a single methodological lineage. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of text-conditioned motion generation, music-driven dance synthesis, and reactive two-person interaction, unifying these threads under a shared duet-dance formulation with fine-grained choreographic annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MDD fills a clear gap: no prior dataset offered 10+ hours of professional duet motion with both music alignment and dance-specific text annotations across 15 genres, making it a necessary resource for anyone studying multi-person dance generation. However, the adapted baselines reveal that current architectures still produce motions far from ground-truth quality (FID ~0.4–2.2 vs. 0.065 for GT), and the finding that text-only InterGen sometimes outperforms the text+music variant suggests that simply concatenating modalities into existing pipelines is insufficient. Open questions include whether purpose-built architectures can exploit the rich spatial-relationship and rhythmic annotations, whether the GPT-4o-refined text truly improves controllability beyond surface fluency, and how well models generalize across the wide BPM and genre range the dataset covers.
