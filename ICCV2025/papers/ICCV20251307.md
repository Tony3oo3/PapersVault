## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs (prompting, SFT, RLHF fine-tuning) offer no fine-grained inference-time controllability: users cannot dynamically trade off object precision against recall, nor adjust the amount of test-time compute spent on grounding. While reward-guided decoding provides such controllability for text-only LLMs, no equivalent technique exists for MLLMs, where reward models must jointly process visual and textual information to evaluate cross-modal grounding quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a short text instruction (e.g., 'Describe this image in detail') and produces a free-form multi-sentence caption. A key design choice is that reward evaluation occurs at sentence boundaries (delimited by '.') rather than at arbitrary token positions, avoiding scoring artifacts from incomplete words. The two reward models are substantially smaller than the base MLLM (PaliGemma 3B for hallucination scoring; OWLv2 + Sentence-BERT + NLTK for recall estimation), so each evaluation requires only a single forward pass, keeping the per-step overhead low relative to generation.

## Method

MRGD builds two complementary multimodal reward models: (1) r_hal, a PaliGemma-based classifier trained with a Bradley-Terry preference loss plus MSE anchoring terms on ~30k preference pairs from LLaVA-RLHF, RLHF-V, POVID, and Sugar-Crepe (fine-tuned with LoRA for one epoch), and (2) r_rec, a non-parametric estimator that composes OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection, Sentence-BERT word embeddings, and NLTK POS tagging to compute a thresholded all-to-all semantic similarity between detected and captioned objects. At decoding time, the MLLM autoregressively generates k candidate sentence completions at each step; each is scored by the weighted linear combination s = w·r_hal + (1−w)·r_rec, and the highest-scoring completion is appended to the context before the next round of sampling. The user controls the precision–recall trade-off by setting w ∈ [0,1] and the compute–grounding trade-off by varying the number of candidates k and the evaluation period T (number of sentences between reward evaluations), with T=∞ reducing to standard best-of-k rejection sampling.

## Results

On COCO (CHAIR benchmark), MRGD with w=1.0 reduces instance-level hallucination rate (C_i) from 15.05% (greedy) to 4.53%, a ~70% reduction, while sentence-level hallucination (C_s) drops from 48.94% to 18.19%. On AMBER, CHAIR drops from 7.6 to 3.4 and Hal. from 31.8 to 15.9. Object recall is moderately affected: Rec. decreases from 81.30% to 76.04% at w=1.0, but combining both reward models at w=0.5 recovers recall to 78.63% with only a 0.8% increase in C_i. The method generalizes to Llama-3.2-Vision (C_i: 5.82→4.38) and SmolVLM-2 (C_i: 6.06→4.32) without retraining the reward models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRGD with w=1.0 achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all compared methods: C_i=4.53 on COCO, beating the best fine-tuning baseline CSR (7.3) by 38%, the best guided decoding baseline CGD (9.48) by 52%, and greedy decoding (15.05) by 70%. On AMBER, MRGD (CHAIR=3.4) outperforms CGD (5.1) by ~33% and mDPO (4.4) by ~23%. Notably, VCD [21] and LLaVA-RLHF [42] actually exhibit *higher* hallucination rates than greedy decoding on these captioning benchmarks (15.76 and 16.09 vs. 15.05), suggesting their gains on discriminative VQA benchmarks do not transfer to generative captioning. The only method approaching MRGD's hallucination rate is POVID (C_i=5.4), but it does not report recall or AMBER results. When both reward models are combined (w=0.5), MRGD achieves a favorable precision-recall balance (C_i=5.34, Rec.=78.63, Cov.=60.8) that no single baseline matches, demonstrating the unique controllability advantage of the reward-guided approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-guided decoding paradigm established for text-only LLMs (ARGUS, FUDGE, value-augmented sampling, best-of-k) to the multimodal setting, where reward models must jointly process visual and textual inputs. It departs from prior MLLM hallucination mitigation work (SFT, RLHF/DPO fine-tuning, prompting, and CLIP-guided decoding) by offering fine-grained, on-the-fly inference-time controllability rather than fixed model behavior. The key architectural departure is training a dedicated multimodal reward model (PaliGemma backbone with LoRA) on preference data, rather than repurposing an off-the-shelf vision-language model as in CGD, and combining it with a compositional recall reward built from OWLv2, Sentence-BERT, and NLTK.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRGD provides the first practical framework for navigating the inherent precision-recall trade-off in MLLM captioning at inference time, with the reward models transferable to new MLLMs without retraining. The finding that RLHF-fine-tuned and contrastive-decoding methods can perform worse than greedy on generative captioning benchmarks (despite strong VQA results) challenges the assumed correlation between discriminative and generative hallucination metrics. Open questions include extending beyond object hallucinations to attributes, counts, and spatial relationships, handling semantically incomplete outputs, and whether gradient-based optimization could replace the search-based approach for greater efficiency.
