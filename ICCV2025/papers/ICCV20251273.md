## Problem

Existing training-free acceleration methods for diffusion models (uniform pruning, feature caching, step reduction) treat all sampling timesteps identically, ignoring the well-documented slow-fast-slow learning speed variation inherent in diffusion pretraining. This uniform treatment causes aggressive pruning during the critical middle denoising phase where the model rapidly captures coarse-grained features, leading to severe quality degradation especially at high sparsity levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251273/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained diffusion models (DiT-XL/2 at 256×256 with class labels, or SDXL-base-1.0 at 1024×1024 with text prompts) and produces a set of stage-specific pruned sub-networks that collectively replace the original dense model during inference. Calibration data consists of 1024 images (ImageNet-1K or MS COCO 2017) resized, encoded to latents, and noised to timesteps within each target stage to match the desired SNR; for CFG models, null-label pairs are included. The final output is a generated image produced by sequentially applying the appropriate pruned sub-network at each timestep.

## Method

MosaicDiff is a training-free, three-phase structural pruning framework (Divide, Prune, Conquer) that aligns pruning intensity with the pretraining learning-speed trajectory. In the Divide phase, a closed-form score function score(t) = E[Grad(t)] + λ·ln(SNR(t)) is computed from the noise schedule α̅_t, and a threshold M·max(score) partitions the reverse process into three stages; the middle fast-learning stage receives conservative sparsity (~0.04) while the early and late slow-learning stages receive aggressive sparsity (~0.6 and ~0.1). In the Prune phase, each stage's sub-network is pruned via second-order structural pruning that extends Optimal Brain Surgeon: SNR-aware calibration data is fed through the model to compute per-layer Hessian matrices, from which saliency scores identify which attention heads and MLP neurons to remove, with a weight-update step compensating remaining parameters. In the Conquer phase, the three pruned sub-networks are stitched together so that inference at each timestep uses the sub-network matched to that stage's SNR range, yielding up to 0.5 sparsity on DiT-XL/2 and 0.3 on SDXL with minimal FID degradation.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (675M params, 256×256, ImageNet-1K), MosaicDiff at 0.33 sparsity achieves FID 2.24 with 1.32× speedup at 50 DDIM steps, outperforming the unpruned baseline (FID 2.26) while reducing MACs from 5.72T to 3.92T. At 20 steps, MosaicDiff reaches FID 3.20 (vs. 3.48 for vanilla), and at 10 steps achieves FID 12.28 (vs. 12.38 vanilla) with 1.33× speedup. On SDXL (2.6B params, 1024×1024, MS COCO 2017), MosaicDiff at 20% sparsity achieves FID 23.79 and CLIP 0.32, closely matching the unpruned model (FID 24.90) while Diff-Pruning degrades to FID 404.87 at the same sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MosaicDiff overwhelmingly outperforms Diff-Pruning [Fang et al., NeurIPS 2023] at every sampling step: at 50 steps, FID drops from 180.76 to 2.24, and at 10 steps from 270.26 to 12.28, indicating that uniform structural pruning across all timesteps is catastrophically harmful. Against Learning-to-Cache [Ma et al., 2024], MosaicDiff achieves lower FID at all step counts (2.24 vs. 2.27 at 50 steps; 3.20 vs. 3.46 at 20 steps; 12.28 vs. 12.79 at 10 steps) while also delivering higher IS and Precision, and requiring less training overhead (0.5 GPU-hours vs. 16.3 hours). Compared to DiP-GO [Zhu et al., 2024], MosaicDiff at 0.25 sparsity and 100 steps achieves FID 2.67 with 8.52T MACs, beating both the vanilla model (FID 3.17, 11.86T) and DiP-GO at 0.6 sparsity (FID 3.01, 11.86T). On SDXL, MosaicDiff at 20% sparsity (FID 23.79, CLIP 0.32, SSIM 0.64) substantially outperforms EcoDiff [Zhang et al., 2024] at the same sparsity (FID 34.41, CLIP 0.31, SSIM 0.50) and at 24% sparsity (FID 61.00). Against DeepCache [Ma et al., CVPR 2024], MosaicDiff at 20 steps achieves FID 24.17 with 57.63T MACs, matching DeepCache-N=5 (FID 24.43, 37.26T) in quality while using fewer steps, and at 10 steps reaches FID 24.32 with only 28.82T MACs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MosaicDiff extends the line of structural pruning for diffusion models (Diff-Pruning, EcoDiff) by introducing trajectory-aware, stage-specific sparsity rather than the uniform pruning applied across all timesteps in prior work. It repurposes the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) second-order pruning framework with SNR-aware calibration data, and draws on noise-schedule analysis (Choi et al., Nichol & Dhariwal) to derive a closed-form importance score that maps pretraining learning-speed dynamics (slow-fast-slow) onto inference-time pruning budgets. The key conceptual departure is treating the reverse denoising trajectory as a mirror of pretraining phases, a connection explicitly absent from prior training-free acceleration methods like DeepCache or Learning-to-Cache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, fine-tuning-free nature of MosaicDiff (0.5 GPU-hours for DiT-XL/2 vs. 16+ hours for L2C) makes it practically attractive for practitioners who cannot afford retraining. The stage-specific sparsity insight—that the fast-learning middle phase is most sensitive to pruning while early and late phases tolerate aggressive sparsity—provides a principled, noise-schedule-derived rationale that could inform other acceleration strategies beyond pruning. Limitations include the memory overhead of storing multiple pruned subnetworks and the current restriction to two pruned structures (attention heads, MLP intermediate dims); whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, 3D generation, or architectures with more heterogeneous layer types remains open.
