## Problem

Neural image compression networks rely on quantization surrogates (e.g., additive uniform noise) to enable end-to-end training, which introduces train-test mismatch and high gradient estimation error that hinders both optimization efficiency and efficacy. While structural regularization has been widely applied to variational autoencoders, no regularization method tailored to the minimum-latent-entropy constraint of compression networks had been explored. This paper identifies that minimizing latent entropy is information-theoretically equivalent to maximizing conditional source entropy, and leverages this equivalence as a plug-and-play regularizer.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 256×256 RGB image patches and produces a compressed bitstream (via entropy coding of quantized latents) and a reconstructed image. The regularizer introduces an additional source entropy model q_θ(X|X̂) whose architecture exactly mirrors the latent entropy model of the compression network; this model is trained only during optimization and imposes zero inference overhead. Training uses 256×256 patches from Flickr20k at four Lagrangian bit-rate points (λ ∈ {0.0018, 0.0035, 0.0067, 0.0130}).

## Method

The key insight is an information-theoretic identity: for a known source, H(U) = H(X) − H(X|X̂) (direct coding) or H(U) = H(X) − (H(X|X̂) − H(U|X̂)) (transform coding), so minimizing latent entropy is equivalent to maximizing conditional source entropy. The proposed regularizer adds −α·E[log q_θ(X|X̂)] to the standard rate-distortion loss, where q_θ(X|X̂) is a neural source entropy model parameterized identically to the latent entropy model. Training follows a GAN-style alternating strategy: at each step, the compression network is updated with the combined rate-distortion-plus-regularizer objective, then frozen while q_θ is updated via maximum likelihood. The paper shows that aligning the source and latent entropy model architectures is critical—using a stronger or weaker source model degrades performance—and that the regularization factor α must be tuned (too large amplifies the H(U|X̂) gap, too small renders the regularizer negligible).

## Results

The proposed information-theoretic regularizer consistently reduces BD-Rate across all five tested compression architectures. On natural images (Kodak, Tecnick, CLIC 2024), average BD-Rate improvements range from −0.88% (hyperprior) to −3.03% (MLIC++), with the largest single-dataset gain being −4.01% on Tecnick for MLIC++. On four out-of-domain datasets (pixel-style, screen-content, game, pathology), the regularized models again outperform their unregularized counterparts, with gains up to −3.44% (MLIC++ on game) and −3.08% (MLIC++ on pixel-style). The regularizer imposes no inference overhead but increases training time by 8.1%–39.3% depending on the model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are ablation-style: each of the five compression architectures (hyperprior, autoregressive, attention, ELIC, MLIC++) is trained identically with and without the proposed conditional source entropy regularizer, and BD-Rate is measured on the same test sets. The regularized version wins on every model–dataset combination reported. The gains are largest for the most complex architectures: MLIC++ achieves −3.03% average BD-Rate on natural images and up to −4.01% on Tecnick, while ELIC reaches −1.56% average and −2.00% on Tecnick. For the simpler classic models (hyperprior, autoregressive, attention), improvements are more modest (−0.88% to −1.06% average) but still consistent. Notably, the regularization effect is most pronounced in early training for ELIC (over 2.5% BD-Rate saving at 0.25×10⁶ steps) and stabilizes for the other models after ~0.5×10⁶ steps. On out-of-domain data, the regularizer's advantage is sometimes amplified (e.g., attention model on pathology: −2.38% vs. −0.92% in-domain), supporting the claim that the regularizer improves generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the end-to-end neural image compression framework of Ballé et al. (2016), which established the rate-distortion training objective with quantization surrogates (AUN, STE). Rather than improving the surrogate or the entropy model architecture, the paper introduces a fundamentally different angle: an information-theoretic structural regularizer derived from the equivalence between minimizing latent entropy H(U) and maximizing conditional source entropy H(X|X̂). This departs from the VAE regularization literature (adversarial, relational, consistency regularizers), which the authors explicitly note is inapplicable to compression due to the minimum-latent-entropy constraint, and from the quantization-surrogate optimization literature, which addresses gradient estimation error from a different direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The regularizer is plug-and-play, interpretable, and imposes zero inference overhead, making it an attractive drop-in addition to any neural compression pipeline. The consistent BD-Rate gains across five architecturally distinct models (hyperprior through MLIC++) and the larger out-of-domain improvements (e.g., −2.38% on pathology with attention vs. −0.92% in-domain) suggest the regularizer genuinely improves representation quality rather than merely fitting training data. Open questions include whether the H(U|X̂) correction term can be practically modeled to tighten the bound, whether the strict alignment requirement between source and latent entropy models can be relaxed, and whether the approach extends to video or other modalities. The moderate training overhead (8–39%) and the sensitivity to the regularization factor α remain practical considerations.
