## Problem

Existing extreme image compression methods built on generative tokenizers (e.g., VQGAN) rely on a single codebook to cluster dataset-level semantics, which fails to capture diverse individual-object details at very low bitrates. This results in noticeable fidelity degradation—especially for objects with precise geometric features—because the limited codebook capacity cannot simultaneously serve common semantic learning and diverse detail representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, embeds it into 16×16 patch tokens, and produces two separate bitstreams: a semantic stream of VQ codebook indices (fixed-length coded) and a detail stream of scalar-quantized features (arithmetic coded with a quadtree entropy model). Decoding fuses both streams through a latent adaptor and a pretrained VQ-GAN pixel generator to reconstruct the image. Evaluation covers full-resolution Kodak and CLIC2020, 768×768 center-cropped CLIC2020, and 512×512 MS-COCO 30K, targeting bitrates as low as 0.01 bpp.

## Method

DLF decomposes the image latent into two parallel branches: a semantic branch that uses a 1-D ViT tokenizer (16×16 windows with 32 learned 1-D tokens per window) to cluster dataset-level semantics into compact VQ tokens, and a detail branch that uses Swin-Transformer and ConvNeXT blocks to encode individual-level details via scalar quantization with spatially adaptive quantization steps and a quadtree entropy model. A cross-branch Interactive Transform (IT) module—stacked multi-head self-attention layers that jointly process intermediate features from both branches—eliminates redundancy between the two bitstreams and extends the semantic branch's receptive field beyond individual windows. Training follows a two-stage progressive strategy: first, latent-domain rate-distortion alignment using the pretrained VQGAN encoder as a supervision signal (with the 1-D tokenizer frozen for initialization), then end-to-end pixel-domain fine-tuning with generative loss. The detail branch learns to adaptively allocate more bits to perceptually critical regions (e.g., object outlines) while spending minimal bits on common content already well-represented in the semantic codebook.

## Results

On the CLIC2020 dataset, DLF achieves BD-Rate savings of 27.93% (LPIPS) and 53.55% (DISTS) relative to the MS-ILLM anchor, and on Kodak it saves 67.82% on DISTS (though it is 43.05% worse on LPIPS). On MS-COCO 30K, DLF outperforms all non-diffusion-based methods in FID while remaining competitive with the diffusion-based PerCo. On cropped CLIC2020 (768×768), DLF surpasses PerCo in FID by a large margin and achieves the best rate-distortion trade-off for both FID and DISTS. DLF also delivers a 2.6× higher compression ratio than HybridFlow while decoding ~10× faster than diffusion-based codecs (0.252 s vs. 2.443 s for PerCo).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MS-ILLM anchor, DLF achieves substantial bitrate savings on CLIC2020 (−27.93% LPIPS, −53.55% DISTS) and Kodak DISTS (−67.82%), though it is 43.05% worse on Kodak LPIPS, indicating a trade-off on that specific small dataset. DLF clearly outperforms the tokenizer-based GLC on all four metric-dataset combinations (e.g., −27.93% vs. −3.59% on CLIC2020 LPIPS). Compared to the dual-branch HybridFlow, DLF achieves a 2.6× higher compression ratio, attributed to its cross-branch interaction eliminating redundancy that HybridFlow's independent encoders cannot. On fidelity metrics, DLF beats both diffusion-based PerCo and DiffEIC (e.g., −4.02% vs. +101.74% on Kodak LPIPS for PerCo), while on generation realism (FID) PerCo remains competitive on MS-COCO 30K but DLF surpasses it on cropped CLIC2020. In terms of speed, DLF decodes in 0.252 s versus 2.443 s (PerCo) and 4.093 s (DiffEIC), making it roughly 10–16× faster while delivering better fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DLF extends the tokenizer-based extreme image compression line (GLC, Mao et al., Xue et al.) by decomposing the VQGAN latent into a semantic branch (using the 1-D tokenizer for compact VQ encoding) and a detail branch (using scalar quantization with an entropy model), rather than compressing the latent in a single branch. It departs from HybridFlow, the closest dual-branch competitor, by introducing a cross-branch Interactive Transform module that jointly processes both branches to eliminate redundancy, whereas HybridFlow uses two independent encoders. The pixel generator is inherited from VQGAN, and the two-stage progressive training (latent alignment then pixel fine-tuning) follows GLC and the 1-D tokenizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLF demonstrates that a well-designed dual-branch architecture with cross-branch interaction can match or exceed diffusion-based codecs in fidelity while decoding ~10× faster (0.25s vs 2.4s for PerCo), making it a practical alternative for extreme low-bitrate compression where diffusion models are too slow. The ablation showing >60% bitrate loss without the interactive design reveals that naive parallel dual-branch coding is fundamentally wasteful, a finding that should inform future multi-stream codec designs. The main limitation is that encoding speed still does not meet real-time requirements, and the method has not yet been extended to video compression despite the authors' prior work in that direction.
