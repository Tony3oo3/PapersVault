## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting relighting methods compress per-image illumination into a single latent vector, enabling only global appearance adjustments while failing to independently manipulate distinct outdoor shading effects such as direct sunlight, sky radiance, and indirect lighting. This limitation prevents realistic relighting of unconstrained outdoor scenes where diverse lighting conditions and dynamic shadow effects are essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251821/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained outdoor photo collection captured under varying temporal and weather conditions (no controlled lighting or multi-view rig required) and produces a relightable 3D Gaussian scene. At inference, it outputs relit novel-view images, individually decomposed shading components (sun, sky, indirect), a sun visibility map, and a reflectance map—all synthesized in a single rasterization pass at real-time speed (32 FPS on an RTX 3090).

## Method

GaRe decomposes global illumination into three physically interpretable shading components (sun, sky, indirect) plus reflectance, each encoded as per-Gaussian features decoded by lightweight MLPs with per-image embeddings. Sun visibility is extracted via binary clustering on residuals between globally illuminated images and ambient-only rendered counterparts, avoiding explicit 3D geometry. A region-based supervision framework with a structural consistency loss (SCL) enforces that each shading component shares the same spatial structure as the reflectance map, resolving under-constrained regions. For shadows, point-based ray tracing queries Gaussian visibility along arbitrary light directions, with a two-step filter (sky + front) to handle floating and transient Gaussians; results are baked into per-Gaussian visibility features via Fibonacci-sampled hemisphere directions, enabling real-time shadow rendering without runtime ray tracing.

## Results

On four outdoor scenes (Staats Theater, Brandenburg Gate, Trevi Fountain, Sacre Coeur), GaRe achieves the best PSNR on three of four scenes (22.84, 28.16, 23.84 dB) and the best SSIM on all four (0.774, 0.938, 0.806, 0.875). It also attains the lowest LPIPS on Staats Theater (0.243) and Brandenburg Gate (0.129), while GS-W leads on LPIPS for Trevi Fountain (0.156) and Sacre Coeur (0.130). Training takes 4.6 hours with a rendering speed of 32 FPS, positioned between the faster WildGaussians (108 FPS) and the slower SWAG (13 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaRe outperforms all NeRF-based baselines (NeRF-W, NeRF-OSR, Ha-NeRF) by a wide margin on every scene and metric, with PSNR gains of 1.8–9.4 dB over the strongest NeRF method. Among 3DGS-based methods, GaRe surpasses the vanilla 3DGS baseline by 9.4 dB PSNR on Staats Theater and 8.8 dB on Brandenburg Gate. Against the closest competitors, GaRe beats WildGaussians by 0.26 dB PSNR on Staats Theater and 0.39 dB on Brandenburg Gate, and beats GS-W by 1.15 dB on Staats Theater and 0.20 dB on Brandenburg Gate. However, GS-W achieves lower LPIPS on Trevi Fountain (0.156 vs. 0.201) and Sacre Coeur (0.130 vs. 0.163), and higher PSNR on Sacre Coeur (23.24 vs. 22.70), indicating GaRe's advantage is most pronounced on scenes with strong direct-sunlight effects. In terms of efficiency, GaRe trains in 4.6 hours (faster than WildGaussians at 7.4 h and GS-W at 6.4 h) but renders at 32 FPS, slower than WildGaussians (108 FPS) and GS-W (43 FPS) yet faster than SWAG (13 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

GaRe builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time scene representation and on intrinsic image decomposition principles to separate reflectance from illumination. It departs from prior unconstrained-collection methods (WildGaussians, GS-W, SWAG) that compress per-image global illumination into a single latent vector, instead decomposing it into physically interpretable components (sunlight, sky radiance, indirect lighting) that can be independently manipulated. The residual-based visibility extraction and structural consistency loss are novel contributions that address limitations of region-based supervision, while the ray-tracing-based shadow baking extends prior Gaussian relighting work (GS3, Relightable 3D Gaussian) to handle arbitrary light directions in a single rasterization pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a gap between unconstrained outdoor NVS (which handles appearance but not lighting) and controlled relighting (which handles lighting but requires constrained datasets), enabling diverse shading manipulation and dynamic shadow generation from casual photo collections in real time. The physically interpretable decomposition opens the door to fine-grained lighting control (e.g., changing sun angle, sky conditions, or indirect illumination independently) that single-embedding methods cannot support. Limitations include the Lambertian surface assumption, restriction to outdoor scenes, and a 32 FPS rendering speed that, while real-time, trails faster baselines like WildGaussians (108 FPS). Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to indoor scenes with complex specular materials and whether the per-scene training requirement can be relaxed toward feed-forward or zero-shot relighting.
