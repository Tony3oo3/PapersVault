## Problem

Existing domain-specific CLIP models (e.g., BioCLIP, ArborCLIP) tune on large biological datasets but ignore the fine-grained nature of such data, relying on a single [cls] token that cannot capture subtle inter-species differences. Additionally, this domain-specific tuning causes severe catastrophic forgetting, dropping general-domain performance (e.g., ImageNet-1K from 67% to 18.6% for BioCLIP) and limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251988/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed by ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 into M×768 token feature matrices) and text prompts (processed by a causal autoregressive transformer into N×768 token sequences). Rather than reducing each modality to a single [cls] embedding, DALIP operates on the full token feature sequences to compute distributional statistics. At inference, the model produces image-text similarity scores for zero-shot classification across 15+ downstream benchmarks spanning biological, general, remote sensing, and medical domains.

## Method

DALIP replaces the standard [cls]-token InfoNCE objective with a distribution alignment loss that matches the similarity between feature distributions of image-text pairs, approximated by first- and second-order statistics via the characteristic function's Taylor expansion. For second-order statistics, a Multi-head Brownian Distance Covariance (MBDC) module splits token features into h heads, computes Brownian Distance Covariance within each head to capture nonlinear inter-token correlations (avoiding the d²-dimensional outer product), then applies layer normalization and a two-layer FFN for cross-head information interaction, yielding a compact d̃-dimensional representation (d̃ ≪ d²). The final loss is a weighted sum of InfoNCE terms on first-order statistics (standard CLIP matching) and second-order statistics (MBDC representations), trained end-to-end with standard backpropagation. To mitigate catastrophic forgetting, the authors construct PlantMix-13M (10M plant + 3M general images) using a generalization-guided data mixing law that predicts optimal domain ratios from downstream accuracy curves.

## Results

On the primary comparison (Table 3), DALIP with ViT-B/16 tuned on PlantMix-13M achieves 61.6% general-domain mean and 51.3% plant-domain mean zero-shot accuracy (overall 56.4%), outperforming OpenCLIP on the same data (54.9%/50.2%) and dramatically surpassing BioCLIP (8.7%/44.7%) and ArborCLIP-O (4.8%/42.2%) which suffer severe catastrophic forgetting. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, DALIP reaches 68.2%/52.8% (overall 60.5%), and with SigLIP-B/16 it achieves 67.9%/60.7% (overall 64.3%). On the biological-only benchmark (Table 2), DALIP tuned on TOL-10M averages 46.3% across ten tasks, beating BioCLIP by 7.6% and ArborCLIP-O (trained on 4× more data) by 11.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BioCLIP (TOL-10M), DALIP wins on plant-domain mean by 6.6% (51.3% vs 44.7%) while gaining a massive 52.9% on general-domain mean (61.6% vs 8.7%), directly addressing catastrophic forgetting. Against ArborCLIP-O (ARBOR-40M, 4× more training data), DALIP achieves 9.1% higher plant mean and 56.8% higher general mean. Against OpenCLIP tuned on the same PlantMix-13M data, DALIP gains 6.7% on general mean and 1.1% on plant mean. Against FLAIR (fine-grained CLIP variant, same data), DALIP leads by 18.3% on general mean and 4.5% on plant mean. Against SigLIP and ProLIP (same SigLIP-B/16 backbone), DALIP-SigLIP outperforms SigLIP by 1.0% general and 1.3% plant mean, and beats ProLIP by 4.9% general and 6.3% plant mean. On the remote sensing and medical imaging generalization tests (Table 4), DALIP also surpasses GeoRSCLIP by 1.0–3.2% and MGCA by 0.5–1.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DALIP extends the CLIP training paradigm by replacing the single [cls] token alignment with a distribution-level matching objective, approximated via first- and second-order statistics of token features. It departs from domain-specific CLIP works (BioCLIP, ArborCLIP) that simply fine-tune on large domain datasets without modeling the fine-grained nature of the data, and from distribution-matching variants (ProLIP, EAU) that use learnable vectors rather than exact second-order statistics. The MBDC module repurposes Brownian Distance Covariance from statistics into an efficient multi-head second-order feature encoder, and the PlantMix-13M dataset introduces a data-mixing-law-guided construction that balances domain-specific and general-domain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-documented failure mode: domain-specific CLIP models suffer severe catastrophic forgetting (BioCLIP drops from 67% to 18.6% on ImageNet), which limits their use as backbones in multimodal LLMs. The data-mixing-law approach provides a principled recipe for balancing domain and general performance, though the gap to the original OpenCLIP on general tasks (49.2% vs 67.0%) remains substantial. Open questions include whether the distribution-alignment objective and MBDC generalize beyond biological/fine-grained data to other domains, and whether regularization or replay strategies can further close the general-domain gap without sacrificing domain-specific gains.
