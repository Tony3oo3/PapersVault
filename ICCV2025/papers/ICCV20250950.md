## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) lack quadratic relation-aware knowledge, so directly mapping category-level image-text correspondences to open-vocabulary scene graph generation fails: VLMs either cannot generalize to unseen relations or overfit to the base training set's relation distribution. Existing OV-SGG methods address this by adding large-scale pre-training data or carefully crafted instruction prompts, but none directly optimize the VLM's internal structure to construct generalizable relation knowledge from a limited base set.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and retains the top-36 detected object bounding boxes for pairwise relation recognition. At inference, it autoregressively generates free-form relation names via a language model conditioned on visual relation features, then scores each generated relation against ground-truth category names using BERT text-embedding cosine similarity to produce the final relation logit for ranking. Outputs include object bounding boxes, subject-object relation triplets, and open-vocabulary relation labels that may fall outside the training vocabulary.

## Method

VL-IRM builds on a frozen GLIP backbone (Swin Transformer + BERT) and introduces two interacting modules. First, a Generative Relation Recognition module links the relation predictor to a FlanT5-base encoder-decoder language model: visual relation features (concatenating visual and spatial pair encodings) serve as the encoder input, and the decoder autoregressively generates relation names, with learnable context prompt tokens prepended to each relation's text embedding to bridge the text-to-visual modality gap. Second, a Hierarchical Relation Extension module operates on two axes: (a) space-wise, the trained relation model produces pseudo labels for unannotated object pairs (filtered by confidence > 0.3) to combat label sparsity; (b) semantic-wise, a dynamic debiasing strategy uses NWGM approximation to estimate per-relation-class bias from batch-level logit statistics, then adjusts both the classification logits and the generation loss weight to demand larger margins for over-represented base relations. Training combines binary cross-entropy on debiased logits, weighted language-modeling loss, and GIoU+L1 box regression, with the visual backbone and text encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the Visual Genome SGDet task (50% novel relations), VL-IRM achieves R@100 = 19.7 and mR@100 = 10.6 (Novel+base), with Novel R@100 = 14.3 and Novel mR@100 = 7.5, outperforming PGSG (mR@100 = 8.3, Novel mR@100 = 5.2) and VS3 (mR@100 = 5.7, Novel mR@100 = 0.0). On the PredCls task, the method reaches R@100 = 37.5 and mR@100 = 14.5, surpassing SVRP (R@100 = 35.9, mR@100 = 10.8) and PGSG (R@100 = 33.9, mR@100 = 13.9). On OpenImage V6 SGDet, VL-IRM attains R@100 = 59.4 and mR@100 = 24.6, substantially exceeding PGSG (R@100 = 43.3, mR@100 = 23.0). When combined with OVSGTR under the 30% novel setting, the method achieves R@100 = 25.0 and Novel R@100 = 17.7, a 4.07-point gain over OVSGTR alone on Novel R@100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGSG [21], the closest OV-SGG competitor, VL-IRM consistently wins on mR metrics: mR@100 improves from 8.3→10.6 (VG SGDet 50%), 13.9→14.5 (VG PredCls), and 23.0→24.6 (OIV6 SGDet), while Novel mR@100 jumps from 5.2→7.5 and 8.9→10.1 respectively. VS3 [43] and SGTR [20] both score 0.0 on all Novel metrics, confirming their inability to generalize to unseen relations, whereas VL-IRM achieves Novel R@100 of 14.3 and 18.3 on VG. On the PredCls task, VL-IRM outperforms SVRP [8] by 1.6 points on R@100 (37.5 vs 35.9) and 3.7 points on mR@100 (14.5 vs 10.8), and beats CaCao [40] by 1.9 points on mR@100 (14.5 vs 12.6). In the fully open-vocabulary OvD+R-SGG setting (Table 4), OVSGTR+Ours surpasses OVSGTR [5] by 3.07% on Novel R@50 (12.27 vs 9.20) and 4.07% on Novel R@100 (15.26 vs 11.19), while legacy methods (IMP [38], VCTREE [31], TDE [32]) score near zero on Novel relations. Notably, VL-IRM achieves these gains without additional pre-training data or instruction prompts, relying solely on the base VG training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the frozen VLM paradigm established by GLIP, adapting it for open-vocabulary scene graph generation without the additional pre-training data (COCO, Flickr30k, SBU) or instruction prompt engineering that OVSGTR and PGSG rely on. It departs from prior OV-SGG methods by introducing a generative relation recognition module (FlanT5-based) that produces relation names autoregressively from visual features, rather than matching against a fixed set of text embeddings. The semantic-wise extension adapts the Logit Adjustment framework with a NWGM-based dynamic estimation, and the spatial extension uses the model's own pseudo-labels rather than external grounding models, drawing on prompt learning for context token generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete structural gap in VLMs for SGG—the absence of quadratic relation-aware knowledge—and shows that this gap causes complete failure on novel relations (0.0 recall) when using standard text-embedding matching. The generative approach is practically appealing because it allows the model to output relation labels outside the training vocabulary without requiring new pre-training data, and the modular design (demonstrated by the OVSGTR+Ours combination) suggests it can be plugged into existing pipelines. Limitations include evaluation only on VG and OIV6, a frozen backbone that constrains capacity, and the added inference cost of autoregressive generation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs or more complex multi-relation scenes, and whether the pseudo-labeling strategy remains reliable as the base vocabulary grows.
