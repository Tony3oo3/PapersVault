## Problem

Existing black-box adversarial attacks on vision-language pre-training (VLP) models suffer from poor transferability due to three limitations: data augmentation strategies that disrupt global semantic and geometric structure, restricted exploration of the adversarial distribution space, and reliance solely on the final adversarial image to guide text perturbation. These issues cause optimization to deviate from the optimal attack path and lead to modality-specific overfitting, severely limiting cross-model attack success rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image and its paired text caption as input, with image perturbations bounded by an L∞ norm of ε_v = 8/255 and text perturbations limited to a single word substitution (ε_t = 1) from a 10-candidate synonym set. The NURBS local deformation operates on a 30×30 control point grid with per-point displacements sampled from U(-10, 10) pixels, while global scaling factors are drawn from U[1.1, 1.8]. The output is a jointly perturbed image-text pair that maximizes the surrogate model's loss and transfers to black-box targets including CLIP, ALBEF, TCL, and large MLLMs like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.

## Method

GLEAM integrates three synergistic modules within an MI-FGSM optimization loop. First, a NURBS-based Local Feature Enhancement (LFE) module generates diverse local deformations via rational B-spline surface displacement (degree-3 basis, 30×30 control mesh), preserving global semantic coherence while introducing local geometric variation. Second, a Global Distribution Expansion (GDE) module applies adaptive random scaling and context-aware padding to broaden the adversarial distribution space. The per-iteration gradient is averaged over N independently sampled local transformations and K global transformations, then accumulated with momentum. Third, a Cross-Modal Feature Alignment (CMFA) module departs from prior work by using the entire trajectory of intermediate adversarial images {v_0^adv, ..., v_T^adv} to compute word replacement scores in a modified PWWS algorithm, rather than relying only on the final adversarial image, thereby reducing text overfitting and improving cross-modal transferability.

## Results

On cross-model transfer (the paper's primary setting), GLEAM achieves 86.66% TR and 86.53% IR R@1 ASR when transferring from ALBEF to CLIP_ViT on Flickr30K, outperforming the strongest baseline SA-AET (55.58% TR, 63.89% IR) by over 30 percentage points. On MLLMs, GLEAM reaches 66% ASR on BLIP-2, 57% on Qwen2-VL, 56% on Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and 39% on GPT-4o, compared to 12–15% for SA-AET and 2–10% for other baselines. In white-box settings, GLEAM achieves >99% ASR across all source-target pairs, matching or exceeding SA-AET and DRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLEAM consistently outperforms all five baselines in cross-model transfer settings. Against SA-AET (the strongest prior method), GLEAM gains 30+ percentage points on ALBEF→CLIP_ViT transfer (86.66% vs 55.58% TR) and ~10 points on CLIP_ViT→CLIP_CNN transfer (88.35% vs 83.98% TR). DRA and SGA are substantially weaker, with DRA reaching only 46–65% ASR in cross-architecture transfers versus GLEAM's 79–91%. Sep-Attack and Co-Attack, which lack the multi-scale augmentation strategies, perform worst in transfer (typically <50% ASR cross-model). On MLLMs (Table 3), the gap widens dramatically: GLEAM achieves 33–66% ASR versus 2–15% for all baselines, with the largest margin against GPT-4o (39% vs 6% for SA-AET). TMM is only reported for ALBEF and TCL sources and shows moderate performance (38–50% cross-model), consistently below GLEAM by 20–40 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLEAM builds on MI-FGSM as its gradient optimization backbone and adapts PWWS for text perturbation, but its core novelty lies in replacing the multi-scale scaling and noise augmentation used by SGA and SA-AET with NURBS-based local deformations borrowed from geometric modeling in CAD. This departs from the prior line of work (Co-Attack, SGA, DRA, SA-AET, TMM) that either disrupts global semantic structure during augmentation or restricts sampling to adversarial sub-triangles. The cross-modal feature alignment module further extends PWWS by leveraging the full trajectory of intermediate adversarial images rather than only the final one, addressing a limitation the authors identify across all prior VLP attack methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that transferable adversarial examples achieve 33–66% attack success rates against frontier MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Qwen2-VL) raises urgent robustness concerns for deployed vision-language systems, while the finding that current defenses (NRP, DiffPure, JPEG) reduce but do not eliminate vulnerability underscores that no adequate mitigation exists yet. A key open question is whether the NURBS-based geometric deformation strategy generalizes beyond 2D images to video or 3D modalities, and whether the CLIP-proxy evaluation protocol used for MLLMs faithfully captures real-world misalignment. The work also highlights a tension between perturbation imperceptibility and attack effectiveness that remains unresolved for practical deployment.
