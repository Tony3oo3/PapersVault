## Problem

Existing multi-label zero-shot learning methods that tune CLIP prompts with class names fail to handle novel classes across different semantic granularity levels, because (1) class names alone carry sparse semantic detail—especially for coarse or abstract categories—and (2) CLIP's contrastive loss causes hierarchical class names (e.g., 'dog' vs. 'animal') to compete when matching the same visual features. This paper is the first to explicitly target zero-shot multi-label classification across varying semantic granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes only LLM-generated text descriptions (covering visual attributes, hierarchical relationships, and co-occurrence scenes) for each class—no annotated images are required. At inference, an RGB image is split into K spatial snippets, each resized to the original resolution and passed through the frozen CLIP ViT encoder; the final output is a per-class logit score combining global (class-token) and local (patch-token) predictions aggregated across all snippets, from which Top-K multi-label predictions are extracted.

## Method

The framework reconstructs class names as learnable token vectors in CLIP's shared image-text embedding space by minimizing an MSE loss between the encoded reconstructed prompt and the encoded LLM-generated text descriptions, plus a pair-based cosine-similarity loss that penalizes inter-class similarity to enhance discriminability. For local feature alignment, the method adopts ClearCLIP's modifications to the final ViT block (removing the residual connection, using query-query self-attention, and discarding the FFN) so that patch tokens can be directly matched against class embeddings. At inference, a multi-snippet semantic aggregation (MSSA) module crops the image into K snippets, computes per-snippet global and local scores, concatenates local semantic maps spatially, fuses them with the full-image local map via max-pooling and averaging, and combines the aggregated global and local logits to produce the final per-class prediction—improving complementarity between coarse-grained (global) and fine-grained (local) recognition.

## Results

On NUS-WIDE ZSL, RCNn achieves F1@Top-3 of 48.4, F1@Top-5 of 45.3, and mAP of 53.3, surpassing the previous best (CoMC, F1@3=41.2) by 7.2 points. On Open Images ZSL, RCNn reaches F1@Top-10 of 93.3, F1@Top-20 of 98.0, and mAP of 70.2, outperforming MKT (F1@10=86.8, mAP=68.1) by 6.5 and 2.1 points respectively. In the GZSL setting, RCNn remains competitive without any image training: on NUS-WIDE it achieves mAP 17.9 (vs. MKT 18.3), and on MS-COCO it reaches F1@Top-3 of 60.7, trailing only DualCoOp (62.9) which trains on seen-class images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the ZSL task, RCNn (trained solely on LLM-generated text) outperforms all 12 baselines across all three datasets. On NUS-WIDE it beats the closest language-driven competitor CoMC by 7.2 F1@3 points (48.4 vs. 41.2) and the best vision-driven method DualCoOp by 7.1 points; on Open Images it surpasses MKT by 6.5 F1@10 and 2.1 mAP points. In the GZSL task, where vision-driven methods benefit from training on seen-class images, RCNn narrows the gap considerably: on NUS-WIDE its mAP (17.9) is within 0.4 of MKT (18.3) and exceeds DualCoOp (12.0) by 5.9 points, while on MS-COCO it trails only DualCoOp (60.7 vs. 62.9 F1@3). On Open Images GZSL, however, RCNn (F1@10=26.8, mAP=2.4) is outperformed by MKT (F1@10=43.6, mAP=3.7) and CLIP-FT (F1@10=43.3, mAP=3.7), which leverage 7,186 seen classes with ≥100 images each. The training-free TagCLIP, which also treats all classes as novel, is outperformed by RCNn by over 10 F1 points on MS-COCO GZSL and by 13–25 points on NUS-WIDE ZSL F1@3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based multi-label zero-shot learning line (DualCoOp, MKT, CLIP-FT) by replacing image-data-driven prompt tuning with a purely text-driven class name reconstruction pipeline. It builds on ClearCLIP's local-token alignment for multi-label scoring and on the broader trend of using LLMs to generate auxiliary text for vision tasks (e.g., CoMC, TaI-DPT), but departs from those by structuring LLM queries around visual attributes, hierarchical relationships, and co-occurrence scenes specifically to address semantic granularity. The pair-based discriminability loss and multi-snippet semantic aggregation at inference are novel components not present in prior ML-ZSL or prompt-learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that zero-shot multi-label classification can be trained entirely on LLM-generated text without any annotated images, which is practically significant for large-scale datasets (NUS-WIDE, Open Images) where annotation costs are prohibitive. The explicit treatment of semantic granularity—ensuring that predictions are consistent across hierarchical levels (e.g., 'dog' and 'animal')—addresses a real weakness in prior prompt-tuning methods that the authors identify but few have tackled. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of classes without LLM description quality degrading, whether the multi-snippet inference cost is acceptable in deployment, and whether the GZSL gap versus image-trained methods (still present on Open Images) can be closed with richer text supervision.
