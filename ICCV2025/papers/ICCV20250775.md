## Problem

Existing multi-view photometric stereo methods rely on stage-by-stage pipelines that first estimate per-view photometric stereo cues (e.g., normal maps) and then fuse them into 3D geometry, causing error accumulation and cross-view inconsistency. These methods also typically require light calibration and view-aligned capture, limiting their practical flexibility. The paper asks whether geometry, reflectance, and lighting can be jointly recovered in a single end-to-end optimization directly from raw multi-view one-light-at-a-time images, without any intermediate PS cues or light calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view OLAT images (one directional light active per image, with the number of viewpoints per light allowed to differ), corresponding foreground masks, and camera extrinsics as input; no light calibration or normal maps are required. It produces a neural SDF (extracted as a mesh via marching cubes), a spatially varying BRDF represented as per-point latent codes plus a BRDF MLP, and per-light camera-space directions with relative RGB intensities. A notable practical feature is support for view-unaligned OLAT capture, where viewpoints need not be aligned across different lighting conditions, enabling capture with a handheld camera-light setup.

## Method

The forward rendering pipeline has three components: a spatial MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding that predicts both the signed distance and a BRDF latent code for each scene point; a BRDF MLP that predicts reflectance values conditioned on the latent code and an angular encoding of the normal-view-light directions (dot products with the halfway vector plus a high-order term); and a shadow MLP that refines binary SDF-transmittance shadow factors to account for inter-reflection leakage. Geometry is rendered via shadow-aware volume rendering with SDF-derived opacity, and the entire system is optimized in a single stage using a weighted L1 color loss (inspired by RawNeRF but with L1 rather than L2), a mask loss, and an Eikonal regularizer. The angular encoding provides a rotation-invariant representation so that surface points sharing the same BRDF map to similar latent codes regardless of world-space orientation, and the weighted L1 loss handles the high dynamic range of directional-light images. This end-to-end design eliminates the need for per-view PS estimation, light calibration, or view alignment, and the paper shows it outperforms normal-guided stage-by-stage baselines in shape, normal, and lighting accuracy while generalizing to challenging reflectance types.

## Results

On DiLiGenT-MV (5 objects, 20 views), the method achieves the best L2 Chamfer distance and normal MAE on three of five objects (BUDDHA, COW, READING) across all lighting conditions from 1 to 96 lights per view. For light direction estimation, the method achieves a mean angular error of 1.16°–2.51° with only 4 lights, compared to 2.74°–5.89° for PS-NeRF, and remains robust even with a single light source (1.16°–2.49° MAE). The ablation confirms that the combination of weighted L1 loss and angular encoding yields the best overall quality (0.16 mm CD, 4.86° normal MAE, 2.67° light MAE, 32.40 dB test PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPIR [13] (end-to-end, point-based rendering), the proposed method achieves lower Chamfer distance and normal MAE on BUDDHA, COW, and READING, while DPIR produces noisy zero-level sets and disk-aliasing artifacts; on test-view rendering, DPIR reports slightly higher PSNR (due to L2 loss) but the proposed method is perceptually more accurate with more realistic shadows. Against SuperNormal [8] (stage-by-stage, normal-map-guided), the proposed method wins on geometry for three of five objects and avoids the 'crack' artifacts caused by cross-view normal inconsistency, though SuperNormal retains an edge on BEAR and POT2 under dense (96-light) conditions. Against PS-NeRF [49] (semi-end-to-end, normal-initialized), the proposed method achieves consistently lower light-direction MAE across all objects and lighting counts—for example, at 4 lights the gap is 2.8° on COW (1.99° vs 4.79°) and 4.7° on READING (1.16° vs 5.89°)—and uniquely remains stable even with a single light source, where PS-NeRF has no corresponding entry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant stage-by-stage MVPS pipeline (light calibration → per-view PS cues → multi-view fusion → reflectance recovery, as in SuperNormal [8] and PS-NeRF [49]) by performing single-stage end-to-end optimization from raw pixels, a design shared only with DPIR [13]. It builds on NeuS-style SDF volume rendering [46] and multi-resolution hash encoding [37] for geometry, and on the neural latent-driven BRDF concept from NeRFactor [54], but trains the BRDF MLP from scratch rather than relying on a pre-trained reflectance model. The angular encoding of normal-view-light directions draws on the rotation-invariant parameterization of Rusinkiewicz [43], and the weighted L1 loss adapts the RawNeRF [35] idea to the OLAT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for light calibration and intermediate normal maps, the method substantially lowers the barrier to MVPS capture—view-unaligned OLAT images can be collected with a handheld camera-light setup, opening the door to portable 3D scanning. Key limitations remain: inter-reflections are not modeled (causing failures in concave reflective regions), and high-frequency color details and sharp specular lobes are not faithfully reproduced. Open questions include whether the heuristic angular encoding admits a principled theoretical justification, whether the approach extends to point or area light sources, and whether the end-to-end advantage persists at larger scene scales or with more complex materials.
