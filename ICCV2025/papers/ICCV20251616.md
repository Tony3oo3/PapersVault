## Problem

Generalizable depth completion requires large-scale datasets with metric depth annotations to cover diverse real-world distributions, but collecting such labels is labor-intensive and time-consuming. Existing generalizable methods (e.g., SPNet, OMNI-DC) rely heavily on these expensive annotations, leaving a gap in how to expand training data coverage with minimal additional labeling effort while preserving the geometric diversity needed for cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251616/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a sparse depth map as input, where the sparse depth can originate from uniform pixel sampling (0.1%–10% of valid pixels), vehicle LiDAR with 4–64 beam lines, or a visual-inertial odometry system with 150–1500 feature points. It outputs a dense metric depth map in millimeters, compatible with the SPNet architecture at 320×320 resolution. The method introduces no additional inference cost over SPNet, retaining its 126.6 img/s throughput on a single 3090 GPU.

## Method

PacGDC exploits 2D-to-3D projection ambiguity by decomposing it into orthogonal shape and position components, then synthesizing diverse pseudo depth labels that vary in scene scale while maintaining shape consistency with image semantics and position consistency with sparse depth inputs. Concretely, multiple monocular depth foundation models (DepthAnything, DepthPro) serve as scale manipulators, producing pseudo dense labels whose local and global scales diverge from ground truth. Random interpolation between ground-truth and foundation-model predictions, followed by random relocation, further fills geometric coverage gaps. Sparse depth maps are sub-sampled directly from each pseudo dense label (using uniform and LiDAR/SFM patterns), and 390K unlabeled SA1B images extend the visual input pool. The resulting pseudo triplets are used to train the SPNet framework, shifting the learning objective from fitting dataset priors to geometric alignment across diverse scales.

## Results

On the primary zero-shot benchmark (Table 2), PacGDC achieves the best mean RMSE of 1966 mm and mean MAE of 731 mm across six unseen datasets (ETH3D, Ibims, NYUv2, DIODE, Sintel, KITTI) with uniformly sampled sparse depths at 10%/1%/0.1%. On sensor-captured sparse depths (Table 3), it achieves mean RMSE 1147 mm and MAE 342 mm on VOID (VIO) and KITTI (LiDAR), outperforming the next-best OMNI-DC (1191/358). In the few-shot setting on KITTI with 64-line LiDAR, PacGDC reaches RMSE 830 mm and MAE 220 mm with only 1000 training samples, surpassing full-shot supervised baselines trained on 86K samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PacGDC outperforms all generalizable depth completion baselines across every dataset in the zero-shot setting. Against the closest competitor SPNet (its base architecture), PacGDC reduces mean RMSE from 2271 to 1966 mm (−13.4%) and mean MAE from 791 to 731 mm (−7.6%), with the largest gains on Sintel (RMSE 3312→2961) and KITTI (RMSE 3124→2673). Against G2-MonoDepth, the improvement is even more pronounced (mean RMSE 2387→1966, −17.6%). OMNI-DC is competitive on ETH3D (929 vs. 907 RMSE) and Ibims (165 vs. 160), but PacGDC dominates on Sintel (7733→2961 RMSE) and DIODE (4848→4721). Fully supervised methods (CFormer, NLSPN, LRRU, ImprovingDC) retrained on the same data are far behind, with mean RMSE ranging from 6408 to 9284, confirming that generalizable frameworks are essential for zero-shot transfer. In the few-shot setting (Table 4), PacGDC with just 1 sample achieves RMSE 1078 mm, beating LRRU (2138) and ImprovingDC (1358) by large margins, and with 1000 samples reaches RMSE 830 mm, outperforming full-shot supervised baselines like S2D and TWISE shown in Figure 6.

## Relation to Prior Work

PacGDC builds directly on SPNet as its base architecture, inheriting its scale-propagation mechanism and efficient inference, but shifts the contribution from network design to training data synthesis. Conceptually, it repurposes the well-known scale ambiguity in monocular depth estimation—typically treated as a limitation of foundation models like DepthAnything and DepthPro—into a data augmentation strategy, where 'wrong-scale' predictions become useful pseudo-labels. This departs from prior generalizable depth completion work (G2-MonoDepth, OMNI-DC) that focused on architectural or loss-level changes, and from standard pseudo-labeling in semi-supervised learning that aims to improve label quality rather than deliberately diversify geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-efficiency angle is practically significant: deploying depth completion in new environments typically requires expensive metric depth annotation, and PacGDC shows that a small number of real labels plus synthesized pseudo-triplets can approach or exceed full-shot supervised performance. The decomposition of projection ambiguity into shape and position provides a clean theoretical justification for why foundation model predictions (which preserve shape but distort scale) are well-suited as scale manipulators. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other dense prediction tasks, how sensitive performance is to the choice and number of foundation models, and whether the gains saturate as more unlabeled data is added.
