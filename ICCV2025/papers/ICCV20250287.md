## Problem

Conventional visual grounding assumes third-person images and explicit object-name queries, but real-world egocentric AI assistants must infer which object a user needs from implicit intention sentences (e.g., 'I need a little boost to reach the sink') in first-person views with occlusions and dynamic viewpoints. No existing dataset or training method connects egocentric visual data to intention-based queries, and current MLLMs either misidentify explicitly mentioned context objects as targets or hallucinate objects not present in the scene.

## Input / Output

The model receives an egocentric RGB image (sourced from Ego4D, with inherent motion blur, small objects, and perspective distortion) paired with a free-form human intention sentence that does not name the target object. The output is a bounding box (x1, y1, x2, y2) localizing the intended object; because intentions are subjective, multiple alternative ground-truth boxes are permitted per sample, and evaluation takes the maximum IoU across all valid boxes. Under RoG, the model additionally produces an intermediate object-category prediction before the final box.

## Method

Reason-to-Ground (RoG) instruction tuning decomposes visual intention grounding into two chained stages within a single MLLM: a <reason> token prompts the model to infer the target object category from the implicit intention sentence, and a subsequent <ref> token with that explicit category prompts bounding-box regression. This disentanglement prevents the model from naively associating explicitly mentioned but unintended objects (e.g., 'phone' in 'gather my phone and belongings') with the correct target. Training uses LoRA supervised fine-tuning on a hybrid mixture of RefCOCO/+/g (explicit queries), GPT-4-generated RefCOCOIntention/+/g (intention queries on exocentric images), and EgoIntention (egocentric intention queries), preserving conventional grounding while adding intention reasoning. The approach is model-agnostic and was applied to MiniGPTv2, Qwen-VL, CogVLM-grounding, and Groma, with MiniGPTv2 showing the largest gains (47.3% overall P@0.5 vs. 35.1% for the GPT-4 + GroundingDINO pipeline) due to its stronger adaptability to new task-specific tokens.

## Results

On EgoIntention, RoG SFT applied to MiniGPT-v2 achieves 47.3% overall Precision@0.5, surpassing the best off-the-shelf two-stage pipeline (R-D GPT-4 + GroundingDINO, 35.1%) by 12.2 points and the zero-shot MiniGPT-v2 (19.9%) by 27.4 points. RoG SFT also maintains or slightly improves standard referring expression comprehension: MiniGPT-v2 scores 87.8% on RefCOCO val (vs. 87.4% zero-shot) and 85.2% on RefCOCOg test (vs. 84.1% zero-shot). On Qwen-VL, RoG SFT reaches 33.6% overall P@0.5 on EgoIntention while preserving RefCOCO performance (89.3% val vs. 89.3% zero-shot). The hallucination error rate drops from 30.1% (zero-shot) to 12.0% (RoG SFT) on the context split, and from 58.2% to 24.3% on the uncommon split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoG SFT (MiniGPT-v2) outperforms the strongest off-the-shelf hybrid baseline, R-D GPT-4 + GroundingDINO (35.1% overall P@0.5), by 12.2 points, demonstrating that jointly modeling reasoning and grounding within a single MLLM is more effective than chaining separate models. The D-R variant (GroundingDINO first, then GPT-4) performs substantially worse at 17.8%, confirming that narrowing the search space via reasoning before detection is critical. Grounding-specialist MLLMs (CogVLM-grounding at 5.4%, Groma at 7.2%) fail dramatically on EgoIntention because they lack the intention-reasoning capability needed to infer which object satisfies an implicit need. Among generalist MLLMs, RoG SFT improves MiniGPT-v2 from 19.9% to 47.3% and Qwen-VL from 21.7% to 33.6% overall P@0.5, with MiniGPT-v2 benefiting more due to its stronger adaptability to new task-specific tokens. Crucially, RoG SFT does not sacrifice conventional grounding: it slightly improves RefCOCO/+/g scores over zero-shot (e.g., 87.8 vs. 87.4 on RefCOCO val), whereas Naive SFT causes a small degradation (86.6 vs. 87.4), validating the disentangled reasoning-then-grounding design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual grounding lineage from explicit object descriptions (RefCOCO family) and verb/phrase-level affordance queries (ADE-Aff, PAD, COCO-Tasks) to a new formulation: implicit human intention sentences in egocentric views. It departs from the closest prior datasets—RIO (template language, no ego-view) and IntentionVG (fixed viewpoint, single intention per object)—by combining first-person Ego4D imagery, free-form multi-intention annotations, and supplementary bounding boxes for alternative valid objects. On the method side, RoG builds on the MLLM instruction-tuning paradigm (MiniGPTv2, Qwen-VL) and LoRA fine-tuning, but introduces a two-stage <reason>→<ref> decomposition that explicitly separates intention inference from object localization, contrasting with prior approaches that feed the intention sentence directly to a grounding token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoIntention provides the first large-scale benchmark for a task that is central to wearable AI assistants but has been absent from the visual grounding literature, and it clearly demonstrates that both specialist grounding MLLMs and generalist MLLMs fail at implicit intention reasoning in first-person views. The RoG instruction-tuning recipe is model-agnostic and preserves (or slightly improves) conventional referring-expression performance while boosting intention grounding, making it a practical drop-in for existing MLLM pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video-based egocentric settings, to multi-object or sequential intentions, and whether the 7B-scale models can close the remaining gap to GPT-4-level reasoning without substantially larger architectures.
