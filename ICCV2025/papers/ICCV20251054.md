## Problem

Reference-based sketch generation requires precise transfer of stroke attributes (line thickness, curvature, texture sparsity) to semantically relevant content regions while preserving structural integrity. Training-based methods fail to generalize to unseen stroke styles, and diffusion-based stylization techniques suffer from content leakage in cross-attention layers that distorts key edges. No existing approach simultaneously achieves expressive stroke control, foreground-aware composition, and content-style equilibrium without dataset-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB content image and an RGB reference sketch image of arbitrary resolution as input. It extracts object prompts from the content image via BLIP, computes contours using TEED, and obtains inverted latents for all three images (content, reference, contour) via DDPM inversion. The output is a stylized sketch (grayscale or color) that preserves the content's semantic structure while adopting the reference's stroke style, texture sparsity, and high-level semantic abstraction, with optional foreground/background separation.

## Method

Stroke2Sketch is a training-free framework built on Stable Diffusion that transfers stroke attributes by dynamically aligning self-attention patterns between content and reference features. The core mechanism, Cross-image Stroke Attention (CSA), swaps and blends key-value vectors from the reference sketch into the content's self-attention layers during denoising (K_ske = K_ref + α·K_cnt), transplanting stroke characteristics without entangling style and geometry. A Directive Attention Module clusters self-attention maps via KMeans to isolate foreground regions and restricts stroke transfer to semantically salient areas, mimicking how artists prioritize focal elements. A Semantic Preservation Module injects contour features as soft positional queries (Q_{i+1} = γ·Q_cont + (1−γ)·Q_ske) during early denoising steps, combined with CLIP-based text guidance, to maintain structural coherence without rigid ControlNet-style constraints. Finally, Stroke Details Propagation Enhancement applies adaptive contrast operations and a multi-pass CFG scheme (stroke guidance + text guidance + self-attention) to refine fine details and suppress noise.

## Results

On the Stroke2Sketch-dataset, Stroke2Sketch achieves the best ArtFID (32.455) and FID (22.435) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest training-free competitor InstantStyle (ArtFID 32.532, FID 23.940) and the best training-based method Semi-Ref2Sketch (ArtFID 33.242, FID 24.359). On LPIPS, the method scores 0.5315, essentially tied with Semi-Ref2Sketch (0.5306) and clearly ahead of all other baselines. In a user study with 2,000 votes per criterion, Stroke2Sketch received the highest preference across content extraction, stroke stylization, and overall quality, with the paper reporting 87% user preference and 92% accuracy in line correspondence tests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stroke2Sketch outperforms all training-based baselines on ArtFID and FID: Ref2Sketch is the weakest (ArtFID 45.292, FID 34.650), Informative-drawing is mid-pack (ArtFID 34.214, FID 25.035), and Semi-Ref2Sketch is the strongest training-based method but still trails on ArtFID (33.242 vs. 32.455) and FID (24.359 vs. 22.435), though it edges out Stroke2Sketch by 0.001 on LPIPS. Among training-free methods, InstantStyle is the closest competitor (ArtFID 32.532, FID 23.940), with Stroke2Sketch winning by a narrow margin on ArtFID and a more comfortable 1.5-point gap on FID; IP-Adapter, InstantStyle+, and StyleID all lag further behind (ArtFID 33.457–37.656). CSGO and RB-Modulation appear only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 8), where the paper reports they preserve content but fail to match reference stroke styles. In direct qualitative comparisons, CLIPascene requires manual post-processing in Adobe Illustrator to achieve consistent stroke styling, while CIA transfers appearance rather than stroke-specific attributes, making both less suitable for the sketch generation task. Overall, Stroke2Sketch wins or ties on every quantitative metric and is preferred in the user study across all three evaluation criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of reference-based sketch extraction (Ref2Sketch, Semi-Ref2Sketch) and training-free style transfer in diffusion models (IP-Adapter, InstantStyle, Cross-Image Attention). It repurposes the key-value swapping paradigm from Cross-Image Attention [1] specifically for stroke attribute transfer, combined with DDPM inversion [16] for latent initialization and SDEdit-style refinement [27] for detail propagation. The key departure from prior work is the explicit separation of stroke attributes (thickness, curvature, texture sparsity) from semantic content via cross-image stroke attention, foreground-focused attention via KMeans clustering of self-attention maps, and contour-injected query blending—none of which require any parameter training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of Stroke2Sketch removes the need for large paired sketch datasets, which have been a persistent bottleneck for reference-based sketch methods. The foreground-prioritization mechanism (DAM) addresses a compositional concern—artists emphasize focal elements while simplifying backgrounds—that prior uniform-stylization methods ignore. However, the quantitative margins over the closest baseline (InstantStyle: ArtFID 32.455 vs. 32.532) are narrow, and the authors acknowledge failure cases with overly simplistic or complex reference sketches. An open question they raise is whether semantic information can be fully decoupled from stroke attributes to improve adaptability across even more diverse artistic styles.
