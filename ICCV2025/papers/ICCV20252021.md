## Problem

Despite the rapid progress of diffusion models, a rigorous theoretical understanding of the Lipschitz continuity and second momentum properties of the diffusion process has been lacking. Existing analyses either assume these smoothness properties without deriving concrete formulations or rely on restrictive assumptions (e.g., identity covariance matrices). This paper bridges the gap by providing explicit, tight bounds on these properties for the analytically tractable yet universally approximating case of Gaussian mixture target distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a k-mixture of Gaussians as the target data distribution p₀ and the standard normal N(0,I) as the prior at time T, with the forward process governed by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck SDE. The key outputs are closed-form Lipschitz constant and second momentum bounds for the score function ∇log pₜ(x) that are independent of the number of mixture components k, along with concrete total variation and KL divergence error guarantees for DDPM, DPOM, and DPUM solvers as functions of discretization steps N, dimension d, and score estimation error ε₀. The Lipschitz bound depends inversely exponentially on dimension d through the determinant terms, making it particularly informative for high-dimensional settings.

## Method

The core insight is that if the target distribution is a k-mixture of Gaussians, the density at every time step t of the diffusion process remains a k-mixture of Gaussians (Proposition 3.2), since the forward process xₜ = aₜx₀ + bₜz is a linear combination of a Gaussian mixture and a standard Gaussian. Building on this structural preservation, the paper derives a Lipschitz constant bound L = O(1/poly(σ_min(pₜ))) for the score function that is independent of k (Lemma 3.5), and a second momentum bound m₂² = O(1) also independent of k (Lemma 3.6). These bounds are then plugged into existing error analysis frameworks for SDE solvers (Euler-Maruyama, Exponential Integrator) and ODE solvers (DPOM, DPUM) to yield concrete TV and KL divergence guarantees with explicit step complexity: e.g., Õ(d/ε₀²) steps for DDPM TV error and Õ(L²√d/ε) steps for DPUM TV error. A small-scale empirical study with 3-mode 2D Gaussian mixtures confirms the theoretical prediction that larger covariance (smaller Lipschitz constant) leads to better log-likelihood and Mahalanobis distance.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: it proves that for a k-mixture of Gaussians target distribution, the Lipschitz constant of the score function is O(1/poly(σ_min(p_t))) and the second momentum is O(1), both independent of the number of mixture components k. Table 1 summarizes the resulting error guarantees: DDPM (SDE) achieves TV error Õ(ε₀²) with Õ(d/ε₀²) steps, while DPUM (ODE) achieves the same with only Õ(√d/ε₀) steps. Preliminary experiments on a 3-mode 2D Gaussian dataset confirm that larger covariance (smaller Lipschitz constant) leads to better log-likelihood (from -9901 at L=3.69×10⁷ to -18.75 at L=0.033) and lower Mahalanobis distance (from 19799 to 20.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-to-method empirical comparison; the `compares_to` set is empty. Instead, the paper positions itself against prior theoretical work that assumed Lipschitz continuity and bounded second momentum as black-box conditions (e.g., [10, 13, 14, 21, 35]) without deriving concrete formulations. The key advance is providing the first closed-form Lipschitz bound (Lemma 3.5) and second momentum bound (Lemma 3.6) that are explicitly independent of the number of Gaussian mixture components k, even as k→∞. Compared to [16], which requires bounded condition numbers and bounded mean/covariance balls, this paper's analysis does not require identity covariance assumptions. Compared to [73], which only identifies the existence of Lipschitz singularities, this paper derives explicit quantitative bounds in terms of mixture parameters. The DPUM solver (Thm 5.8) improves upon DPOM (Thm 5.7) by reducing iteration complexity from Õ(L³d/ε²) to Õ(L²√d/ε), a gain attributable to the underdamped Langevin corrector step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of diffusion model convergence (Chen et al. 2022, 2023; Chen, Lee, Lu 2023) by filling a specific gap: prior work assumes Lipschitz continuity and second momentum of the score function as abstract conditions without deriving their concrete values. By restricting the data distribution to a k-mixture of Gaussians (a universal approximator for smooth densities), the authors show the entire diffusion process remains a k-mixture of Gaussians and derive closed-form Lipschitz and second momentum bounds that are independent of k. This departs from concurrent GMM-under-diffusion work (Gatmiry et al. 2024, Shah et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2024), which focuses on sample complexity under additional structural assumptions (identity covariances, bounded condition numbers), whereas this paper targets the smoothness parameters themselves without such restrictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The k-independence of the Lipschitz bound is practically significant: real data distributions (images, video) may require millions of Gaussian components, and prior theoretical analyses either assumed smoothness abstractly or imposed restrictive structural conditions on the mixture. These concrete bounds directly instantiate the step-complexity guarantees for DDPM, DPOM, and DPUM solvers, giving practitioners a clearer picture of how data geometry (via σ_min, det_min, β, γ) controls sampling cost. Limitations include the requirement that σ_min(p_t) remain constant over time, the bounded-region condition (Condition 3.4), and very preliminary 2D experiments with only 3 modes. Open questions include whether the bounds are tight, whether k-independence extends to non-Gaussian mixture families, and how the exponential-in-dimension dependence manifests in high-dimensional practical settings.
