## Problem

The densification operation in 3D Gaussian Splatting relies on a binary threshold to choose between split (for over-reconstruction) and clone (for under-reconstruction), creating an irreconcilable trade-off: a high threshold preserves structural coverage but produces blurry rendering, while a low threshold sharpens details but leaves geometry missing. This fixed-threshold mechanism also leads to redundant populations of similarly-scaled Gaussians in textureless regions, inflating memory without improving quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images (following the standard train/test splits of 3D-GS) and produces a 3D scene represented as a collection of anisotropic Gaussians with position, covariance, spherical-harmonic color, and opacity. During training, supervision is applied through an L=3-layer image pyramid, so early stages optimize against downsampled targets (e.g., 1/4 and 1/2 resolution) before full-resolution refinement, decoupling coarse structure learning from fine detail recovery.

## Method

ResGS replaces the split/clone dichotomy with a single adaptive operation called residual split: for any Gaussian flagged for densification, a downscaled replica (scale divided by λ_s=1.6) is spawned with the same rotation, SH coefficients, and opacity, while the original's opacity is reduced by a factor β=0.3 to compensate for overlap. This simultaneously expands spatial coverage (addressing under-reconstruction) and injects finer detail (addressing over-reconstruction) without a threshold-based decision. The full pipeline adds two progressive-training mechanisms: (1) an image-pyramid supervision schedule that gradually increases target resolution across L stages, and (2) a varying gradient-threshold selection scheme that tracks each Gaussian's 'fineness level' l_i and progressively lowers the densification threshold for coarser Gaussians as training advances, ensuring that refinement is deferred to later iterations rather than applied prematurely to the initial coarse representation.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, ResGS (AbsGS variant) achieves SOTA across all metrics: PSNR 28.00, SSIM 0.833, and LPIPS 0.174, outperforming the closest competitor Mini-Splatting-D (PSNR 27.51, SSIM 0.831, LPIPS 0.176) and the original 3D-GS (PSNR 27.21, SSIM 0.815, LPIPS 0.214). On Tanks&Temples, ResGS attains the best SSIM (0.867) and LPIPS (0.132), though Octree-GS edges it on PSNR (24.52 vs. 24.38). On Deep Blending, ResGS achieves the best LPIPS (0.227) with competitive PSNR (29.91) and SSIM (0.902), while its small variant (Ours-Small) uses only 289 MB of memory yet still reaches PSNR 30.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResGS (AbsGS) achieves SOTA on Mip-NeRF360 across all three metrics, beating the strongest 3D-GS variant Mini-Splatting-D by +0.49 PSNR and −0.002 LPIPS, and the original 3D-GS by +0.79 PSNR and −0.040 LPIPS. On Tanks&Temples, ResGS wins on SSIM (0.867 vs. 0.866 for Octree-GS) and LPIPS (0.132 vs. 0.140 for Mini-Splatting-D), but trails Octree-GS on PSNR by 0.14 dB (24.38 vs. 24.52). On Deep Blending, ResGS secures the best LPIPS (0.227) but is narrowly behind Mini-Splatting-D on SSIM (0.902 vs. 0.906) and FreGS on PSNR (29.91 vs. 29.93). The small variant (Ours-Small) achieves PSNR 27.94 on Mip-NeRF360 with only 342 MB memory, outperforming full-size 3D-GS (27.21 at 734 MB) while using less than half the storage. Table 3 further shows that simply replacing split/clone with residual split in 3D-GS, AbsGS, Pixel-GS, and Mini-Splatting-D consistently improves PSNR by 0.1–0.3 dB and reduces memory, confirming the densification operation's broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D-GS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its representation and rendering backbone, and incorporates AbsGS's absolute-value gradient computation and periodic opacity reduction as training components. Its key conceptual departure is replacing the binary split/clone densification decision—which relies on a fixed scale threshold—with a single adaptive operation (residual split) that simultaneously expands coverage and injects detail. The coarse-to-fine training pipeline draws on progressive training ideas from Pyramid NeRF, BungeeNeRF, and FreGS, but applies them specifically to the Gaussian densification schedule via an image pyramid and a level-aware gradient threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The residual split operation is a simple, drop-in replacement for split/clone that consistently improves PSNR and reduces memory across four different 3D-GS variants (3D-GS, AbsGS, Pixel-GS, Mini-Splatting), suggesting it could become a standard densification primitive in the 3D-GS ecosystem. However, the paper acknowledges that it lacks explicit regularization for weakly textured regions, which limits its performance on Deep Blending; future work could combine residual split with surface-aware or texture-aware regularization. The broader open question is whether the coarse-to-fine schedule and level-based selection generalize to dynamic or 4D Gaussian splatting settings where the scene representation changes over time.
