## Problem

Self-attention in diffusion models imposes quadratic computational complexity that becomes prohibitive at high resolutions (e.g., 11,010 T FLOPs for 16K images in SD1.5). Despite this cost, the paper's systematic layer-wise analysis reveals that self-attention in pre-trained diffusion models predominantly exhibits localized patterns resembling convolutional inductive biases, with global interactions being smooth and low-intensity. The central question is whether explicit global self-attention can be replaced by a structured convolutional approach without sacrificing generative fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252092/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates an RGB image at resolutions ranging from 512×512 up to 16K, operating on VAE-compressed latent representations within existing pre-trained diffusion models (SD1.5, SDXL, PixArt). Only the self-attention modules are replaced; cross-attention for text conditioning, the VAE encoder/decoder, and all other network parameters remain frozen. The model is trained at 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL, PixArt) but generalizes to higher resolutions, where it notably outperforms self-attention baselines that produce fragmented artifacts.

## Method

ΔConvFusion replaces each self-attention block with a ΔConvBlock consisting of two branches: (1) a Pyramid Convolution with n stages of alternating downsampling (average pooling) and upsampling (bilinear interpolation) around a scaled simple gate with depth-wise convolutions, capturing high-frequency distance-dependent local interactions; and (2) a global average pooling branch followed by a 1×1 convolution, approximating the low-frequency spatially invariant attention bias. Training uses two-level knowledge distillation: a feature-level L2 loss aligning each ΔConvBlock output with the corresponding frozen self-attention output, and an output-level ε-prediction loss with Min-SNR weighting to align final denoising predictions. Only ΔConvBlock parameters are updated (batch size 16 on one A100), yielding 6929× FLOPs reduction at 16K resolution and 3.4× speedup over LinFusion at 1024×1024 while matching or exceeding baseline generation quality on DINOv2 Score, FDD, and CLIP Score.

## Results

ΔConvFusion achieves the highest DINOv2 Score (DS) across all tested base models: 44.72 on SD1.5-512, 45.14 on SDXL-1024, and 42.56 on PixArt-512, surpassing both self-attention baselines and LinFusion. On the Fréchet DINOv2 Distance (FDD) metric, ΔConvFusion maintains comparable fidelity to self-attention counterparts (e.g., 143.72 vs. 147.59 on SDXL-1024). In terms of efficiency, the method reduces FLOPs by up to 6929× at 16K resolution and achieves a 3.4× inference speedup over LinFusion at 1024×1024 on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the self-attention baseline, ΔConvFusion consistently wins on DS (image quality/diversity) across all four model-resolution combinations, with the largest gain on SDXL-1024 (+2.49 DS). On FDD (realism), ΔConvFusion is competitive or better for SDXL and PixArt-1024 but slightly worse for SD1.5-512 (200.15 vs. 210.86) and PixArt-512 (181.06 vs. 173.88). Against LinFusion, ΔConvFusion achieves higher DS on both SD1.5 (44.72 vs. 44.23) and SDXL (45.14 vs. 43.24), and lower FDD on SDXL (143.72 vs. 148.61), though it trails on FDD for SD1.5 (200.15 vs. 181.78). Against DiTFastAttn on PixArt, ΔConvFusion wins on DS for both resolutions (42.56 vs. 41.19 at 512; 42.95 vs. 42.16 at 1024) and on FDD at 1024 (181.75 vs. 184.31), but loses on FDD at 512 (181.06 vs. 171.25). Overall, ΔConvFusion trades a small FDD penalty in some settings for clear gains in perceptual quality (DS) and text-image alignment (CLIP), while delivering 3.4–3.37× speedups over LinFusion and up to 6929× FLOPs reduction over self-attention at 16K resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the prevailing efficiency paradigm for diffusion models. Prior work (LinFusion, DiTFastAttn, Mamba-2-based approaches) operates under the assumption that global spatial interactions are necessary and focuses on making them cheaper (structured state-space models, windowed attention, attention compression). In contrast, this paper argues through systematic layer-wise analysis that self-attention in trained diffusion models is predominantly local, and that a structured convolutional replacement (Pyramid Convolution Blocks) can match or exceed performance without any global interaction mechanism. The method borrows the scaled simple gate from Chen et al. [4] for non-linear spatial interactions and uses Min-SNR loss weighting [11] for stable distillation training, but the core architectural insight—that attention's global component is a smooth, low-intensity bias approximable by average pooling—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-attention in diffusion models is overwhelmingly local challenges a core architectural assumption in the field and opens the door to linear-complexity diffusion models that scale to ultra-high resolutions (16K) without the quadratic memory bottleneck. The 6929× FLOPs reduction at 16K and 3.4× speedup over LinFusion at 1024×1024 are practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether this locality property holds for video diffusion, whether cross-attention (text-image interaction) exhibits similar structure, and whether the distillation approach generalizes to models trained from scratch rather than fine-tuned from pre-trained attention-based checkpoints. The slightly degraded FDD on PixArt suggests the replacement is not lossless in all regimes.
