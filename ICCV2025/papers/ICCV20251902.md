## Problem

Selecting the best model from a pool of pre-trained candidates traditionally requires annotating a large validation set, and existing active model selection methods remain label-inefficient because they treat candidate models independently (ignoring consensus and disagreement) and treat categories independently (ignoring correlations in category-specific errors). CODA addresses both limitations by modeling inter-classifier agreement and per-category error structure within a single probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251902/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes C-dimensional prediction vectors (soft scores or one-hot labels) from |H| candidate models over a static pool of |D| unlabeled test points, and iteratively queries a human expert for one ground-truth label per time step. At each step it outputs the argmax of P_Best, a probability distribution over which model is best, along with regret and cumulative regret metrics for evaluation. The setup is pool-based (all unlabeled data available upfront) and agnostic to whether predictions are soft or hard, operating on both RGB image and text classification tasks.

## Method

CODA adapts the Bayesian Dawid-Skene model so that each classifier is represented by a C×C confusion matrix with per-row Dirichlet priors, capturing per-category performance characteristics. Priors are initialized from a consensus label (argmax of summed model predictions) to encode inter-model agreement, then updated via partial Dirichlet-categorical conjugate updates (learning rate η=0.01) as ground-truth labels arrive. P_Best is computed by integrating over mixtures of per-row Beta marginals weighted by estimated class prevalence, yielding the probability that each model's accuracy draw exceeds all others. The acquisition function selects the next point to label by maximizing expected information gain, defined as the reduction in Shannon entropy of P_Best after a virtual update for each hypothetical class label. This unified treatment of model consensus, category-level error correlation, and Bayesian belief updating is what distinguishes CODA from prior methods that either use importance-sampling estimators or simple per-model posterior updates.

## Results

CODA achieves state-of-the-art cumulative regret at step 100 on 18 out of 26 benchmark tasks, with dramatic improvements in the best cases: 2.4 regret on painting→real (vs. 179.3 for the next-best method, a ~90× reduction), 11.9 on sketch→real, and 4.8 on QQP. Across all tasks, CODA reduces regret by over 80% compared to the next-best method on 5 datasets, by over 50% on 11 datasets, and by over 25% on 15 datasets. The method's worst failures are on CoLA (2226.7 regret) and CivilComments (318.6), where data imbalance combined with highly biased models undermines the consensus-based priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODA outperforms all five baselines on 18 of 26 tasks. Against the strongest prior method, ModelSelector [45], CODA achieves substantially lower regret on most tasks (e.g., 2.4 vs. 293.4 on painting→real; 4.8 vs. 186.8 on QQP; 23.5 vs. 148.5 on MNLI), but loses on real→clipart (231.7 vs. 153.2) and sketch→clipart (432.4 vs. 38.1). Uncertainty sampling [13] is the most competitive challenger, winning on PACS (6.9 vs. 57.9), CivilComments (13.3 vs. 318.6), and CoLA (169.5 vs. 2226.7), where CODA's consensus priors are undermined by extreme class imbalance and model bias. Active Testing [31] and VMA [40] are consistently outperformed by CODA across nearly all tasks, with CODA achieving 5–20× lower regret in the DomainNet126 transfer settings. Random sampling serves as a weak baseline that CODA beats on 22 of 26 tasks, though it narrowly wins on Camelyon17 (175.2 vs. 288.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

CODA adapts the Dawid-Skene model of annotator agreement (Dawid & Skene 1979) and its Bayesian instantiation (Passonneau & Carpenter 2014) from the crowdsourcing literature to the setting of machine learning model selection, representing each classifier by a per-category confusion matrix with Dirichlet priors. It departs from prior active model selection methods (ModelSelector, VMA, Active Testing) in two key ways: (1) it models interdependencies between classifiers through consensus and disagreement rather than treating each model independently, and (2) it captures category-level error correlations rather than aggregating to a single scalar per model. The pool-based formulation also contrasts with the online/streaming setting that dominated earlier active model selection work (Karimi et al. 2021, Kassraie et al. 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 1.9M pre-trained models available on HuggingFace and growing application-specific model zoos, the practical cost of selecting the right model for a new dataset is a real bottleneck; CODA's 70% reduction in required labels and its 26-task benchmark suite (the largest empirical study of active model selection to date) provide both a practical tool and a shared evaluation standard for the field. The method's failure on data-imbalanced tasks with highly biased models (CivilComments, CoLA) highlights an open problem in handling class imbalance and use-case-specific metrics within the active selection loop. Broader open questions include extending beyond accuracy-based loss, incorporating human domain knowledge into priors, and jointly allocating annotation effort across active learning, active testing, and active model selection.
