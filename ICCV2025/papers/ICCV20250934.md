## Problem

Memory-based continual learners remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks, and directly combining adversarial training with continual learning reveals two underexplored challenges: adversarial samples amplify negative gradients toward past classes, accelerating catastrophic forgetting, and the limited replay buffer causes gradient obfuscation that degrades adversarial robustness relative to joint training. This paper addresses both challenges without requiring additional unlabeled data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) arriving as a streaming sequence of T classification tasks, with a small episodic memory buffer (200–5,120 samples) storing past-task data. At inference, the model outputs a class label over all C classes seen so far, operating in both class-incremental (no task ID) and task-incremental (task ID available) settings. Adversarial perturbations are generated via PGD with ε=8/255 and step size 2/255 during training.

## Method

The proposed framework consists of two plug-in modules. First, Anti-Forgettable Logit Calibration (AFLC) applies a task-dependent logit offset v_i = −log(n_i/Σn_j) − α_i (α=3.5) that suppresses past-class logits and boosts current-class logits, with a Further Prior adjustment that averages past/current offsets and applies them to future classes to balance gradient flow; this reduces the negative gradient magnitude from adversarial samples toward previously learned classes. Second, Robustness-Aware Experience Replay (RAER) computes a robustness difficulty factor k for each sample (the number of successful PGD-10 attack steps, k∈[0,10]) during the existing adversarial example generation pass, and stores only samples with k < ρ (ρ=5) in memory, thereby excluding boundary-overfitting outliers and preserving distribution-representative data to mitigate gradient obfuscation. The overall loss is standard cross-entropy on calibrated logits for both current-task adversarial samples and memory-buffer adversarial samples, and the framework integrates with any memory-based CL method (ER, DER, X-DER) and any adversarial training variant (Vanilla AT, TRADES).

## Results

On Split-CIFAR10 (buffer 200, class-incremental), ER+TRADES+Ours achieves 43.34% clean FAA and 19.85% adversarial FAA (PGD-20), reducing clean forgetting by 43.85% and adversarial forgetting by 34.17% compared to ER+TRADES alone. On Split-CIFAR100 (buffer 2000, class-incremental), the method reaches 28.73% clean FAA and 12.75% adversarial FAA, with forgetting reductions of 46.33% (clean) and 27.52% (adversarial). The paper reports improvements in 93% of evaluation metrics across all settings, with the largest gains in class-incremental scenarios where adversarial training most severely accelerates forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ER+TRADES (the strongest adversarial baseline in task-incremental settings), the proposed method achieves the largest gains: +20.92% clean FAA, −43.85% clean forgetting, +4.13% adversarial FAA, and −34.17% adversarial forgetting on Split-CIFAR10 (buffer 200, class-incremental). Against ER+AT, improvements are more modest (+7.50% clean FAA, −9.40% clean forgetting) but still consistent across both incremental settings. ER+SCORE achieves the highest clean FAA (48.65%) but collapses on adversarial robustness (2.40% PGD-20 FAA), whereas the proposed method maintains 18.40–19.85% adversarial FAA while also improving clean metrics. In Table 3, the method outperforms GSS+AT (+9.34% clean FAA, −13.06% forgetting) and ASER+AT (+5.61% clean FAA, −6.05% forgetting) on all metrics, and improves X-DER+AT by +9.21% clean FAA and −9.28% adversarial forgetting, confirming that AFLC generalizes beyond ER's logit masking. The one notable trade-off is that ER+TRADES+Ours still lags ER+AT on clean FAA in class-incremental settings (43.34% vs. 35.68% for ER+AT+Ours), reflecting the inherent clean-robust accuracy trade-off of TRADES.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of memory-based continual learning (building on ER, DER, DER++, and X-DER) and adversarial training (building on Vanilla AT and TRADES), identifying the first systematic analysis of why their direct combination fails without additional unlabeled data. AFLC generalizes X-DER's logit masking into a continuous, task-adaptive calibration framework, while RAER introduces a robustness-difficulty-based data selection criterion distinct from prior gradient-based or Shapley-value-based replay strategies. Unlike Chen et al. [12], which achieved robust continual learning by querying large amounts of unlabeled data, this work explicitly targets the constrained setting where only a small episodic memory is available, making the identified challenges (accelerated forgetting and shattered gradients) specific to the memory-based regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic framework for why adversarial training and continual learning conflict, and offers a lightweight, plug-and-play solution (AFLC + RAER) that integrates with existing CL and AT pipelines without architectural changes or extra data. However, absolute performance remains far below joint-training upper bounds (e.g., ~43% vs. ~79% clean FAA on Split-CIFAR10 with buffer 200), and the method has only been validated on small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) with modest buffer sizes. Open questions include whether the logit calibration and robustness-difficulty selection generalize to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), longer task sequences, and more aggressive attack budgets, and whether the clean-robust accuracy trade-off can be further narrowed as buffer sizes grow.
