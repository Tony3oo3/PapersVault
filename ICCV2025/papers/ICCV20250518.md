## Problem

Temporal video grounding (TVG) depends on labor-intensive manual annotations for both language queries and temporal boundaries, with datasets like ActivityNet Captions requiring over 5,200 annotation hours. Existing unsupervised approaches still rely on manually cleaned, unscalable video collections, and direct pretraining on MLLM-generated pseudo-annotations from wild videos introduces systematic errors including idle videos, mismatched query-video pairs, and imprecise temporal boundaries.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language query and an untrimmed video as input, where the video is uniformly sampled into n_v2f frames and encoded via a frozen CLIP visual encoder alongside the CLIP text encoder for the query. The output is a set of U temporal boundary predictions (start, end timestamp pairs) each accompanied by a confidence score, from which the highest-confidence prediction is selected. During pretraining, the supervision signal is a pseudo boundary progressively refined by the ReCorrect algorithm rather than a fixed ground-truth annotation.

## Method

The paper introduces the Vid-Group dataset (52.7K wild videos, 200K pseudo-annotations generated by GPT-4o) and the ReCorrect algorithm for self-correcting pretraining. ReCorrect operates in two phases: (1) semantics-guided refinement computes CLIP-based frame-level semantic similarities and a moment contrastive score γ to discard the bottom R% of samples and iteratively shrink or expand temporal boundaries based on local contrastive comparisons; (2) memory-consensus correction maintains a per-sample memory bank that accumulates model predictions across epochs, computes consensus scores via pairwise IoU among stored instances, and selects the highest-consensus boundary as the pseudo ground truth for a weighted loss that blends the consensus-corrected target with the initially refined boundary. The TVG backbone is SimBase with frozen CLIP features, and the entire pipeline requires no manual annotations, enabling zero-shot and unsupervised adaptation to downstream benchmarks.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting, ReCorrect achieves R@0.5 of 51.15 and mIoU of 45.63 on Charades-STA, and R@0.5 of 33.35 and mIoU of 35.96 on ActivityNet Captions, outperforming all prior zero-shot methods. In the unsupervised setting (pretraining on Vid-Group + finetuning on unlabeled target videos), ReCorrect reaches R@0.5 of 54.42 (mIoU 48.66) on Charades-STA and R@0.5 of 37.83 (mIoU 39.74) on ActivityNet Captions, achieving approximately 85% of the fully-supervised SimBase upper bound in mIoU (86.7% and 84.4% respectively). The Vid-Group dataset contains 52.7K videos and 200.3K pseudo annotations with zero manual annotation cost, compared to 5.2K hours for ActivityNet Captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-shot setting (Table 2), ReCorrect outperforms all prior zero-shot methods: it surpasses the previous SOTA TFVTG [48] by 4.2 points in R@0.7 (28.54 vs. 24.32) and 1.1 points in mIoU (45.63 vs. 44.51) on Charades-STA, and by 1.8 points in R@0.7 (15.15 vs. 13.39) and 1.9 points in mIoU (35.96 vs. 34.10) on ActivityNet Captions. It also beats Moment-GPT [39] by 7 points in R@0.7 and over 5 points in mIoU on both datasets. In the unsupervised setting (Table 3), ReCorrect (pretraining + finetuning) achieves 70.96 R@0.3 on Charades-STA, surpassing SPL [47] (60.73) by over 10 points and Kim et al. [13] (52.95) by nearly 18 points. The GPT-4o Pretraining baseline (61.77 R@0.3 zero-shot) shows that the ReCorrect correction mechanism adds approximately 5 points over direct pretraining on raw pseudo labels. Ablation studies confirm that each component (cleaning, boundary adjustment, memory consensus) contributes incrementally, with the full pipeline yielding the best results in both zero-shot and unsupervised settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SimBase as the TVG model architecture and departs from prior TVG pretraining work (LocVTP, ProTeGe, BSPT) that focuses on pretraining feature extraction backbones; here, the TVG model itself is pretrained with a fixed CLIP backbone. It also diverges from unsupervised TVG methods that rely on manually cleaned datasets (Charades-STA, ActivityNet Captions) by instead using wild videos with MLLM-generated pseudo labels, and from zero-shot TVG methods (Luo et al., TFVTG, VTG-GPT) that apply static, training-free boundary refinement. The ReCorrect algorithm introduces a dynamic, iterative self-correction loop—semantics-guided refinement followed by memory-consensus correction—that is distinct from the one-shot similarity-based boundary adjustments used in prior zero-shot and unsupervised approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Vid-Group dataset (52.7K videos, 200K annotations, zero manual cost) is a practical resource that could reduce the annotation bottleneck limiting TVG research and deployment. Achieving ~85% of fully-supervised SOTA without any manual labels on the target dataset is a meaningful step toward annotation-free video grounding, though the remaining 15% gap (especially at R@0.7) suggests MLLM pseudo-label quality remains a ceiling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer or more diverse video domains, whether the memory-consensus mechanism scales to larger model families, and whether the 40% cleaning ratio is optimal or dataset-dependent.
