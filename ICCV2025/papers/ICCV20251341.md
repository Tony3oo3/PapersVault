## Problem

Leveraging diverse motion planning datasets (human-human and human-robot interaction) for robust target-domain planning is hindered by domain imbalance and catastrophic forgetting when naively combining datasets, and by high inference cost when using ensemble models. Existing model merging techniques from vision/NLP fail in motion planning because they ignore the hierarchical feature structure (agent encoders, interaction encoder, decoder) and the multi-metric, multi-objective nature of planning evaluation.

## Input / Output

The planner takes ego-centric x,y trajectories sampled at 2.5 FPS: 8 timesteps of past ego and surrounding agent positions plus 12 timesteps of predicted surrounding agent trajectories, and outputs a 12-timestep future ego trajectory. All source datasets (ETH-UCY, CrowdNav, THOR, SIT) are preprocessed into this uniform format. Beyond the trajectory input, the method additionally consumes a pool of parameter checkpoints extracted from models fine-tuned on individual source domains, which are merged to produce the final planning model without requiring source data at inference time.

## Method

IMMP operates in two stages. First, Interaction-Conserving Pre-Merging trains a separate model per source domain and builds a checkpoint pool by selecting (a) the best-performing checkpoint for each individual evaluation metric (ADE, FDE, Collision Rate, Miss Rate) and (b) intermediate checkpoints at fixed intervals to capture generalized, non-overfit features. Second, Interaction Transfer with Merging partitions the planning model parameters into four functionally distinct groups—ego encoder (θ_ego), surrounding-agent encoder (θ_surr), interaction encoder (θ_inter), and decoder/other (θ_else)—computes task vectors (τ_i = θ_i − θ_0) per group, and learns module-specific linear weights w_{i,θ} to combine them. This interaction-level granularity preserves the hierarchical feature structure while allowing each component to adapt independently to the target domain, and the merged model can optionally be fine-tuned on the target dataset for further gains.

## Results

On the SIT dataset with GameTheoretic, IMMP achieves ADE 0.3380, Collision Rate 5.12×10⁻⁵, FDE 0.7626, and Miss Rate 0.6446, outperforming Target Only (ADE 0.4343) and Domain Adaptation (ADE 0.4388) while matching the 1× inference cost of single-model baselines. With fine-tuning (IMMP+Finetune), performance further improves to ADE 0.3157 and Miss Rate 0.5934, surpassing all baselines including Ensemble-WTA (ADE 0.3695 at 7× cost). On THOR, IMMP reaches ADE 0.1165 and Miss Rate 0.1330, and IMMP+Finetune achieves ADE 0.0975 and Miss Rate 0.0912, beating Target Only (ADE 0.1003, Miss Rate 0.0929). The same pattern holds across DTPP and DIPP backbones, with IMMP consistently outperforming all merging baselines (Averaging, Task Arithmetic, Ties Merging) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMMP consistently outperforms all seven baselines across both target datasets (SIT, THOR) and all three planning backbones (GameTheoretic, DTPP, DIPP). Against Domain Generalization [17], IMMP reduces ADE by 59% on SIT/GameTheoretic (0.3380 vs 0.8338) and 69% on THOR (0.1165 vs 0.3804), confirming that naively combining source datasets causes severe domain imbalance. Against Domain Adaptation [16], IMMP achieves lower ADE (0.3380 vs 0.4388 on SIT) and substantially lower Collision Rate (5.12×10⁻⁵ vs 1.26×10⁻³), while also improving Miss Rate (0.6446 vs 0.7201). Against Ensemble-WTA [2], IMMP matches or beats ADE (0.3380 vs 0.3695 on SIT) at 1× inference cost versus 7×, and dramatically outperforms on THOR (ADE 0.1165 vs 0.2112). Among merging baselines, Task Arithmetic [22] is the closest competitor (ADE 0.4132 on SIT/GameTheoretic) but still trails IMMP by 18% on ADE and 12% on Miss Rate; Averaging [10,68] and Ties Merging [72] perform substantially worse, with Ties Merging actually degrading below Domain Generalization (ADE 1.1876 vs 0.8338 on SIT), indicating that its trimming step discards critical motion-planning features. Notably, IMMP+Finetune surpasses even Target Only on THOR (ADE 0.0975 vs 0.1003, Miss Rate 0.0912 vs 0.0929), demonstrating that the merged checkpoint provides a superior initialization for target-domain fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts model merging techniques—originally developed for vision and NLP tasks (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, Model Soups)—to the motion planning domain, where they had not previously been explored. It departs from conventional domain generalization and adaptation approaches (e.g., UniTraj, which pools multiple trajectory datasets) by operating entirely on parameter checkpoints rather than raw data, thereby sidestepping domain imbalance and catastrophic forgetting. The key methodological departure is the introduction of interaction-level merging granularity, which partitions planning model parameters into ego-encoder, surrounding-agent-encoder, interaction-encoder, and decoder modules before merging, rather than applying uniform model-level or parameter-level merging. The two-step process of interaction-conserving pre-merging (building a metric-specific checkpoint pool) followed by weighted task-vector merging is novel to this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMMP offers a practical, architecture-agnostic framework for robot planners to benefit from diverse motion datasets without requiring joint training or access to source data at inference time, which is valuable given the data scarcity in HRI settings. The finding that domain generalization (pooling source datasets) can underperform target-only training highlights a non-trivial domain gap problem that practitioners may not anticipate. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small datasets (SIT, THOR) and three specific planning backbones; open questions include whether the interaction-level partitioning generalizes to end-to-end driving models with more complex architectures, how the method scales to dozens of source domains, and whether the pre-merging phase's computational cost (training multiple models per source domain) is justified in practice.
