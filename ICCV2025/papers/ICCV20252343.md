## Problem

Existing medical image de-identification methods either indiscriminately degrade diagnostic details (blurring, pixelation, noise injection) or fail to preserve task-generalizable semantics at high privacy levels. No prior approach simultaneously preserves rich, task-agnostic medical semantics and supports a continuous range of adjustable privacy levels, leaving a critical gap between privacy protection and clinical utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252343/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (chest X-ray or fundus photograph) resized to 256×256 and produces a de-identified image of the same resolution. Privacy level is controlled by a threshold T defined as the r-th quantile of an identity-similarity map, with r selectable from {0.95, 0.7, 0.4, 0.2} to span a wide range of protection strengths. The output image is task-agnostic and can be directly fed into downstream classification, segmentation, or captioning models without any further adaptation.

## Method

DCM-DeID decouples identity removal from semantic preservation into three stages. First, ID-Blocking uses a ViT-based ReID network to compute a per-patch cosine-similarity map against the global identity embedding, then binarizes it at threshold T to mask identity-related regions. Second, ID-Free Medical Semantics Extraction leverages pretrained Medical Foundation Models (MGCA for X-ray, RetFound-ViT for fundus) to obtain rich features, which are then encoded into a 32-channel discrete code space under a Minimum Description Length (rate-distortion) objective with a GMM entropy model; a Gumbel-Softmax binary mask partitions codes into identity-associated and semantics-associated subsets, discarding the former. Third, a dual-conditioned latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion UNet with reduced channels) fuses the ID-masked image and the ID-free semantic feature via bi-directional cross-attention to synthesize the final de-identified image. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of feature reconstruction, identity-code rate-distortion, and diffusion objectives.

## Results

At the strictest privacy setting (CMC-R1=10%), DCM-DeID achieves AUROC of 72.86% on MIMIC-X, 73.66% on Chest-Xray, and 86.12% on CheXpert for X-ray classification, substantially outperforming all baselines. For X-ray captioning, it reaches a BLEU score of 0.1750 (vs. 0.1453 for the second-best Feat-Noise), and for X-ray segmentation it attains a Dice score of 27.98% (vs. 22.23% for ID-Adv). On fundus images, DCM-DeID achieves 65.23% AUROC on EyePACS and 54.66% Dice on REFUGE2 at 10% attack SR, again leading all competitors. At the relaxed 20% attack SR, performance further improves to 74.35% AUROC (MIMIC-X) and 62.04% Dice (REFUGE2), while the original-image upper bounds are 82.13% AUROC and 90.08% Dice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCM-DeID consistently outperforms all five baselines across every task and dataset at both 10% and 20% attack SR. Against the strongest competitor Feat-Noise, it gains +7.02 AUROC on MIMIC-X and +0.0297 BLEU on X-ray captioning at 10% SR. The adversarial methods ID-Adv and Privacy-Net, which jointly optimize identity removal and medical preservation, perform poorly on X-ray classification (59.36% and 57.09% AUROC on MIMIC-X at 10% SR, below even the simple Pixel-Blur baseline of 64.15%), confirming that directly optimizing conflicting objectives is suboptimal. MAE, which shares the same diffusion backbone as DCM-DeID, is competitive at high attack rates (76.12% AUROC at 40% SR) but trails by over 13% AUROC at 10% SR, demonstrating that the divide-and-conquer semantic compensation strategy—not just the generative model—drives the gains. On fundus segmentation (REFUGE2), DCM-DeID leads ID-Adv by 13.43% Dice at 10% SR, and on EyePACS classification it surpasses ID-Adv by ~9% AUROC, confirming cross-modality generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCM-DeID departs from the dominant paradigm in medical image de-identification, where methods like ID-Adv and Privacy-Net jointly optimize identity removal and medical preservation in a single adversarial loop. Instead, it adopts a divide-and-conquer strategy: identity is first blocked via a ReID-guided similarity map, then medical semantics are independently recovered using pretrained Medical Foundation Models (MGCA, RetFound-ViT) and a diffusion-based re-synthesis stage. The feature decoupling mechanism is a direct repurposing of the Minimum Description Length / rate-distortion framework from neural image compression (Balle et al., Minnen et al.), transplanted into the medical identity-semantic separation problem with a GMM entropy model and Gumbel-Softmax mask. The diffusion re-synthesis stage builds on Stable Diffusion's latent-space UNet architecture, adapted with a dual-conditioned cross-attention fusion of the ID-masked image and the decoupled semantic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark for adjustable medical image de-identification, covering seven datasets across two modalities and three downstream tasks (classification, captioning, segmentation), filling a gap where prior methods were evaluated inconsistently or on single tasks. The finding that ~44–55% of MFM features encode identity information is practically important: it means that simply using medical foundation model features as conditioning signals silently leaks patient identity, a risk that prior label-conditioned generative approaches did not address. Key limitations include restriction to 2D single-slice images (no 3D MRI/CT volumes), reliance on a multi-step diffusion model (~540 ms inference), and evaluation on only two modalities. Open questions include whether the MDL-based decoupling generalizes to volumetric data, whether single-step distillation can close the speed gap, and whether the framework extends to modalities where identity and diagnostic signals are more deeply entangled (e.g., brain MRI).
