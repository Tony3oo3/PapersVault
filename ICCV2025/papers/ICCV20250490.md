## Problem

Reconstructing 4D dynamic scenes from casually captured monocular video is ill-posed because each timestamp is observed from only one viewpoint. Existing geometric priors (depth, optical flow) do not linearly correlate with RGB rendering quality and cannot fill occluded regions, while generative video diffusion models either require posed training data that is infeasible to collect for dynamic scenes or are limited to static scenes. The paper addresses this gap by unifying geometric warping with generative inpainting in a single pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured monocular video (no pose annotations required) and produces a motion-field-based 3D Gaussian Splatting 4D scene from which arbitrary novel views can be rendered in real time. At inference, COLMAP provides camera intrinsics and poses, and Depth Anything V2 supplies per-frame depth maps that are aligned to metric scale via RANSAC against the COLMAP sparse point cloud. The inpainting diffusion model itself is trained on unposed web videos from OpenVid-1M, where occlusion masks are synthesized from CoTracker 2D point trajectories rather than requiring any camera geometry.

## Method

The core reformulation treats view augmentation as video inpainting: observed frames are depth-warped to novel viewpoints, producing masked videos with occlusion holes that a fine-tuned video diffusion model (based on Videoofusion/ModelScope) completes. The diffusion model is modified in two ways: the masked latent is concatenated with the binary mask channel, and an anchor video (the original unwarped monocular clip) is encoded and concatenated as additional spatiotemporal context, enabling the model to leverage full-frame observations for coherent filling. Training data is generated from unposed web videos by using CoTracker to identify pixels in later frames not associated with any tracked point from frame 1, naturally simulating warping occlusions. To mitigate depth-estimation errors that cause severe artifacts at large warping angles, the method employs an iterative view augmentation strategy that progressively expands viewpoints over N iterations, selecting frames with minimal warping angles at each step and updating a data buffer with newly synthesized views and their aligned depths. A robust IV RGB loss, which backpropagates only through the minimum-error pixel within a 3×3 neighborhood, further reduces sensitivity to residual misalignments between rendered and augmented supervision.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset, Vivid4D achieves mPSNR 15.20, mSSIM 0.5004, and mLPIPS 0.4930 (overall scene), outperforming all five baselines across every metric. On the HyperNeRF dataset, it reaches mPSNR 19.45, mSSIM 0.5446, and mLPIPS 0.4449, again leading all competitors. The gains are most pronounced in the dynamic foreground (e.g., mPSNR 18.85 vs. 18.53 for ViewCrafter on HyperNeRF) and in the overall scene, where the method's ability to fill previously invisible regions provides the largest advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vivid4D outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. On the iPhone dataset, it leads the closest competitor CoCoCo by +0.21 mPSNR and −0.035 mLPIPS (overall), while on HyperNeRF it beats CoCoCo by +0.45 mPSNR and ViewCrafter by +0.019 mSSIM. The advantage is largest in the dynamic foreground: on HyperNeRF, Vivid4D scores 18.85 mPSNR vs. 18.53 for ViewCrafter and 18.51 for StereoCrafter. The two general-purpose 4D reconstruction baselines (4D GS and Shape of Motion) trail the most, with 4D GS showing visible black holes and Shape of Motion exhibiting white unobserved regions that Vivid4D fills via its inpainting-based view augmentation. Notably, the video-inpainting baselines (CoCoCo, StereoCrafter, ViewCrafter) are integrated into the same warping pipeline for a fair comparison, isolating the benefit of Vivid4D's anchor-conditioned inpainting model and iterative strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vivid4D sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: geometric-prior-based monocular 4D reconstruction (Shape of Motion, 4D GS, which use depth/flow/tracking for supervision) and generative-prior-based novel view synthesis (ViewCrafter, StereoCrafter, GS-DiT, which use diffusion models to inpaint warped views). The key departure is reformulating view augmentation as an anchor-conditioned video inpainting task trained on unposed web videos via CoTracker-synthesized occlusion masks, rather than requiring posed training data or random masks. The iterative view expansion strategy and IV robust loss are novel design choices that address the well-known problem of depth estimation errors compounding under large-angle warping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pipeline for improving monocular 4D reconstruction without requiring multi-view captures or posed training data, which is a significant practical advantage for casual video applications. The finding that direct depth supervision yields only marginal gains while depth-guided warping plus inpainting yields substantially larger improvements validates the core thesis that geometric priors do not linearly translate to RGB quality. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, handling of complex non-rigid deformations, and whether the approach can extrapolate beyond observed regions rather than merely interpolating between augmented viewpoints.
