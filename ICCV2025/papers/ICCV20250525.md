## Problem

Vision-based 3D occupancy prediction methods rely exclusively on street-view imagery, making them inherently limited by occlusions from static and dynamic objects and by sparse sampling in distant regions due to perspective projection. While satellite-street cross-view perception has been explored for 2D localization and static map construction, no prior work has integrated satellite imagery into 3D occupancy prediction in a real-time, automatic manner—previous approaches required manual keypoint alignment and neglected dynamic objects and 3D geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250525/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six-camera street-view RGB images (256×704 or 512×1408 depending on variant) and a 400×400 satellite image patch cropped from Google Earth using the ego-vehicle's GPS & IMU pose (covering an 80m×80m area aligned to driving direction). The primary output is a 200×200×19 3D semantic occupancy grid with 19 classes, supplemented by auxiliary outputs: a satellite-view semantic segmentation map, a satellite-view height map, and a BEV dynamic-region attention map used for fusion gating. The satellite imagery is historical (captured before the street-view observation), introducing an unavoidable temporal gap that the method must handle.

## Method

SA-Occ builds on FlashOcc's efficient 2D prediction head and introduces three key modules. (1) A satellite BEV branch uses a U-shaped ResNet-18 extractor with ASPP, augmented by 3D-Proj Guidance that projects 3D occupancy labels into the satellite view to provide static-only semantic and height supervision (bridging the 2D-to-3D gap), plus a soft gated convolution to filter high-frequency noise. (2) A Uniform Sampling Alignment (Uni-SA) module in the street branch performs backward projection of predefined uniform 3D points onto image planes, complementing LSS's depth-based forward projection to match the uniform sampling density of satellite BEV features. (3) A Dynamic-Decoupling Fusion module estimates a dynamic-region attention map from street BEV features (supervised by 2D binary maps projected from 3D annotations), uses it as a spatial gate to suppress satellite features in dynamic regions (resolving temporal asynchrony), and applies dynamic-encoding spatial attention to enhance dynamic-static interaction. The total loss combines cross-entropy for occupancy, satellite semantics, height, depth, and a CE+Dice term for dynamic region prediction.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, SA-Occ(V1) achieves 39.05% mIoU with a single-frame input (ResNet-50, 256×704), a +6.97% absolute improvement over the FlashOcc(M1) baseline (32.08%) while adding only 6.93 ms latency. Scaling to dual-frame (V2: 40.65%), 8-frame (V3: 41.69%), and Swin-B dual-frame (V5*: 44.64%) configurations, SA-Occ consistently outperforms all prior methods in each group. The single-frame model surpasses dual-frame baselines by +1.21% to +3.04% mIoU and remains competitive with 8- and 16-frame methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-Occ dominates all single-frame baselines: it outperforms FlashOcc(M1) by +6.97% mIoU, BEVDetOcc(1f) by +7.41%, CTF-Occ by +10.52%, TPVFormer by +11.22%, BEVFormer by +12.17%, and MonoScene by +32.99%. Against dual-frame ResNet-50 methods, SA-Occ(V2) beats FlashOcc(M2) by +2.81% and BEVDetOcc(2f) by +4.55%. In the multi-frame group, SA-Occ(V3) with only 8 frames surpasses FBOcc(16f) by +2.58%, FastOcc(16f) by +2.48%, and BEVDetOcc(8f, larger resolution) by +2.43%. In the heaviest Swin-B dual-frame group, SA-Occ(V5) exceeds FlashOcc(M3) by +0.77% and BEVDetOcc by +2.27%, with the extended-training variant (V5*) reaching 44.64% mIoU. Notably, SA-Occ's single-frame model already outperforms all dual-frame ResNet-50 baselines, and its 8-frame variant matches or exceeds 16-frame methods, demonstrating that satellite-view integration provides a more effective accuracy boost than simply increasing temporal context.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-Occ builds directly on FlashOcc's efficient 2D prediction head architecture and extends it with a satellite BEV branch, while adapting DualBEV's backward-projection concept into a Uniform Sampling Alignment module that replaces probabilistic correspondences with uniform 3D point sampling. It departs from prior satellite-street cross-view work (SatForHDMap, OpenSatMap) in two key ways: replacing manual keypoint alignment with automatic GPS/IMU-based cropping from Google Earth, and shifting from static 2D map construction to dynamic 3D occupancy prediction. The Dynamic-Decoupling Fusion module is novel in addressing the temporal asynchrony problem specific to pre-existing satellite imagery, a challenge absent from real-time multi-sensor fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practically relevant direction for autonomous driving by demonstrating that readily available historical satellite imagery, aligned via onboard GPS/IMU, can substantially complement street-view perception in occluded and long-range regions without requiring new hardware. The single-frame model outperforming multi-frame baselines suggests satellite information may reduce the need for temporal stacking, potentially lowering latency in deployment. Key open questions include: generalization beyond Occ3D-nuScenes, handling of large temporal gaps between satellite capture and driving, satellite imagery availability and resolution in diverse geographic regions, and whether the approach extends to other cross-view perception tasks beyond driving. The reliance on Google Earth also raises practical concerns about coverage, licensing, and freshness of satellite data.
