## Problem

Self-attention and window attention in restoration transformers break translation equivariance—a fundamental inductive bias inherited from CNNs—causing slower convergence and worse generalization. Existing remedies face a dilemma: full self-attention offers global receptive fields at O(N²) cost, while sliding window attention is linear but rigidly constrained to a fixed local window. The paper asks how to recover translation equivariance in restoration transformers without sacrificing either global context or computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250056/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TEAFormer accepts a low-quality or degraded RGB image (e.g., bicubically downsampled for ×2/×4 SR, defocus-blurred, Gaussian-noised, or multi-degradation-corrupted) and outputs a restored high-quality RGB image at the target resolution. For super-resolution the input resolution is scaled by the factor (×2 or ×4); for deblurring, denoising, and all-in-one tasks the spatial dimensions are preserved. Training uses 64×64 patches (SR) or 128×128 patches (all-in-one) with L1 loss, and the network employs a residual-in-residual structure of Translation Equivariance Groups, each containing multiple Translation Equivariance Blocks.

## Method

TEAFormer introduces Translation Equivariance Adaptive (TEA) attention, which combines two components in parallel: (1) Adaptive Sliding Key-Value Self Attention (ASkvSA), which uses a 2D depth-wise convolution on K and V to generate per-pixel adaptive indices that shuffle key-value pairs within a fixed sliding window, and (2) Downsampled Self Attention (DSA), which applies average pooling to K and V to provide coarse global context at reduced token count. The two branches are fused with learnable scalar weights. The design is grounded in two theorems: slide indexing (Theorem 3.2) guarantees TE for any operator reading from a fixed sliding window, and component stacking (Theorem 3.3) ensures that parallel or serial compositions of TE operators remain TE. With hyperparameters w=15, k=3, N_d=16, TEA achieves O(N) complexity comparable to window attention while maintaining a global receptive field, yielding faster NTK convergence, better SRGA generalization, and SOTA PSNR/SSIM across SR, deblurring, denoising, and all-in-one restoration benchmarks.

## Results

On 4× image super-resolution, TEAFormer achieves 28.67 dB PSNR / 0.8489 SSIM on Urban100, surpassing the previous best transformer IPG (28.13 dB) by 0.54 dB and HAT (27.97 dB) by 0.70 dB with comparable parameters (~22M). On the 5D all-in-one restoration benchmark, TEAFormer reaches 31.22 dB average PSNR, exceeding the prior SOTA InstructIR by 1.67 dB across all five degradation types (dehazing, deraining, denoising, deblurring, low-light). For defocus deblurring on DPDD, TEAFormer-S achieves 26.45 dB combined PSNR (0.27 dB over GRL-S), and TEAFormer-D reaches 27.23 dB (0.19 dB over GRL-D). On Gaussian denoising at noise level 50 (color Urban100), TEAFormer gains 0.13 dB over GRL-B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEAFormer outperforms all 29 compared baselines across every evaluated task. In 4× SR, it leads IPG (CVPR 2024) by 0.54 dB on Urban100 with 2× faster inference (386.7 ms vs. 646.7 ms), and beats HAT (CVPR 2023) by 0.70 dB with similar parameter counts (~22M vs. 20.8M). On the 5D all-in-one benchmark, TEAFormer surpasses InstructIR (ECCV 2024) by a substantial 1.67 dB average PSNR (31.22 vs. 29.55), with the largest gap in dehazing (+4.47 dB) and deraining (+2.60 dB), while also outperforming architecture-focused baselines like NAFNet (27.76 dB) and Restormer (27.60 dB) by over 3 dB. For defocus deblurring, TEAFormer-S gains 0.44 dB over GRL-S on indoor scenes, and TEAFormer-D leads GRL-D by 0.33 dB on indoor PSNR. In Gaussian denoising at σ=50, TEAFormer edges out GRL-B by 0.13 dB on color Urban100 and 0.12 dB on grayscale Urban100. On mixed degradation (low-light+haze+rain), TEAFormer exceeds DiffUIR-L (CVPR 2024) by 0.42 dB PSNR with fewer parameters (15.4M vs. 36.3M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SwinIR architecture (window attention for restoration) and Restormer (efficient transformer design), but departs fundamentally from both by identifying that standard self-attention and window attention break translation equivariance (TE), a property that CNNs provided for free through their sliding convolution paradigm. The paper formalizes TE with two theorems (slide indexing and component stacking) and proposes TEA, which combines adaptive sliding key-value indexing (extending SkvSA with learned spatial offsets) and downsampled self-attention to achieve a global receptive field at linear complexity while preserving TE. This positions the work at the intersection of inductive-bias-aware transformer design (following ViTAE, AACNet) and efficient restoration architecture (following SwinIR, HAT, IPG), but uniquely frames the SA-vs-WA tradeoff as a TE-preservation problem rather than purely a computational one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that restoring translation equivariance in restoration transformers yields a dramatic ~47× improvement in NTK convergence condition number and measurable generalization gains (SRGA), suggesting that the slow convergence and poor generalization of restoration transformers may be partly attributable to the loss of this inductive bias rather than solely to architectural choices. The adaptive sliding mechanism resolves the long-standing dilemma between global receptive field (self-attention) and linear complexity (window attention), offering a practical design principle for future restoration architectures. Open questions include whether the average pooling in DSA (which only approximately satisfies TE) can be replaced with strictly equivariant alternatives, and whether other geometric equivariances (rotation, scale) can be similarly incorporated into restoration transformers as the authors hint in their conclusion.
