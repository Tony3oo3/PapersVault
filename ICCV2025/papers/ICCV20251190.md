## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods produce high-fidelity digital designs that often cannot be physically realized: they may use non-standard components, contain floating or disconnected parts, or collapse under gravity. No prior approach generates brick assembly structures directly from freeform text while simultaneously guaranteeing physical stability and brick-by-brick buildability with standard interlocking toy bricks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251190/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a freeform text prompt (e.g., 'a small castle with a tower') and outputs an ordered sequence of bricks, each specified as a 1-unit-tall axis-aligned cuboid with dimensions h×w and position (x,y,z) in a 20×20×20 grid, arranged in raster-scan order from bottom to top. The output is guaranteed to be collision-free, composed of bricks from a standard inventory, and physically stable on a baseplate. Optionally, the structure can be augmented with UV textures or per-brick uniform colors via a separate text-driven texturing step.

## Method

BRICKGPT repurposes LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct as an autoregressive next-brick predictor by fine-tuning it on StableText2Brick (47k+ stable structures from 28k+ ShapeNetCore objects with GPT-4o-generated captions) using a custom plain-text brick format that encodes dimensions and coordinates, reducing token count versus LDraw. During inference, two mechanisms enforce physical realizability: (1) brick-by-brick rejection sampling that resamples any generated brick violating format, inventory, or collision constraints, and (2) physics-aware rollback that, after the full structure is generated, solves a nonlinear force-equilibrium program (via Gurobi) to compute per-brick stability scores and, if any brick is unstable, truncates the sequence to just before the first unstable brick and regenerates from that point. This deferred stability check avoids over-constraining the model's exploration space during construction, where intermediate partial structures may be temporarily unstable but become stable upon completion.

## Results

On 250 held-out prompts from StableText2Brick, BRICKGPT achieves 100% structural validity and 98.8% physical stability, with a mean per-brick stability score of 0.996 and minimum of 0.915. It also attains the highest DINOv2 image similarity (0.880) and competitive CLIP text similarity (0.324) among all methods. The median generation time is 40.8 seconds per structure, with a median of 2 physics-aware rollbacks required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRICKGPT decisively outperforms all four 3D-generation baselines (LLaMA-Mesh, LGM, XCube, Hunyuan3D-2) on physical stability: 98.8% stable vs. 50.8%, 25.2%, 75.2%, and 75.2% respectively, and even surpasses the strongest augmented baseline (Hunyuan3D-2 + stability analysis at 88.4%) by over 10 points. On validity, BRICKGPT matches LGM, XCube, and Hunyuan3D-2 at 100% while exceeding LLaMA-Mesh (94.8%). The minimum stability score (0.915) is substantially higher than all baselines, indicating that even the weakest brick in a BRICKGPT structure is far more secure than in competing methods. On text-image alignment, BRICKGPT ties Hunyuan3D-2 on CLIP (0.324) and leads all methods on DINOv2 similarity (0.880 vs. 0.868 for Hunyuan3D-2), confirming that the physics constraints do not sacrifice prompt fidelity. Pre-trained LLaMA in zero-shot and 5-shot in-context settings produce almost entirely invalid and unstable outputs (0% and 2.4% valid), underscoring the necessity of fine-tuning on StableText2Brick.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm of LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct for next-brick prediction, drawing a direct analogy between language modeling and sequential physical assembly. It builds on the StableLego stability analysis framework for scoring per-brick structural integrity, but departs from prior brick-assembly work (which requires a given 3D shape as input) by conditioning directly on freeform text. The approach also contrasts with text-to-3D mesh generators (LLaMA-Mesh, LGM, XCube, Hunyuan3D-2), which produce geometrically valid but physically unrealizable outputs, by enforcing physics-aware constraints (rejection sampling and rollback) during autoregressive inference rather than post-hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The StableText2Brick dataset (47k+ stable structures with captions) and the physics-aware rollback mechanism provide a reusable foundation for physically constrained generative design beyond bricks. The 98.8% stability rate with only 2 median rollbacks demonstrates that lightweight physics checks at inference time can effectively enforce complex structural constraints without retraining. Limitations include the 20×20×20 grid restriction, axis-aligned cuboid bricks only, and evaluation on 250 prompts from the authors' own dataset; open questions include scaling to larger structures, diverse brick geometries, and whether the rollback strategy generalizes to other assembly domains (e.g., mechanical parts, architectural elements).
