## Problem

Existing diffusion-based relighting methods operate on single views, producing inconsistent relighting across viewpoints that limits their utility for 3D relighting. Inverse rendering alternatives are slow and constrained to simple material models. No prior learned framework jointly leverages multi-view consistency cues and inferred material properties to produce high-quality, view-consistent relighting in a feed-forward manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary number of posed RGB images of an object under unknown illumination, a target HDR environment map, and camera poses. Material maps (albedo, roughness, metallicness) are inferred from the input images via StableMaterialMV and concatenated with Plücker ray maps and encoded source images as channel-wise conditioning. The output is a set of multi-view consistent relit images at the target illumination; for 3D relighting, novel views are first rendered via 3D Gaussian Splatting and then inserted into the denoising set. Training proceeds at 256×256 then upscales to 512×512, and the distributed inference scheme scales to hundreds of views.

## Method

LightSwitch finetunes a Stable Diffusion 2.1 UNet in two stages: first for single-view material-guided relighting (conditioning on encoded source image, material maps, and Plücker rays via channel concatenation, with a lighting cross-attention module attending to encoded environment maps and directional embeddings), then for multi-view relighting by inserting multi-view self-attention layers so all latent pixels attend across views. The target environment map is split into a normalized and a tonemapped version to capture both strong and soft lighting, and classifier-free guidance (10% lighting dropout, scale 3) is used. At inference, a distributed denoising scheme shuffles mini-batches of latents across DDPM iterations so each latent eventually attends to all others, approximating full multi-view attention while scaling to arbitrary view counts and enabling parallel computation. For novel view relighting, a 3D Gaussian Splat is optimized on training views, the query view is rendered and inserted into the denoising set, yielding consistent relighting in ~2 minutes on 8 GPUs.

## Results

On 2D relighting (BlenderVault synthetic), LightSwitch achieves 26.01 PSNR / 0.888 SSIM / 0.216 LPIPS (image-level rescaling), outperforming DiLightNet (23.84 PSNR) and Neural Gaffer (24.34 PSNR). On 3D novel-view relighting over the Objects with Lighting dataset, LightSwitch reaches 25.43 PSNR / 0.84 SSIM / 0.297 LPIPS, surpassing all learned inverse-rendering baselines except the physics-based Mitsuba+NeuS (26.24 PSNR). On NeRF-Synthetic, LightSwitch matches or exceeds per-object baselines (e.g., 26.65 PSNR on Chair vs. 26.58 for MaterialFusion) while completing relighting in ~2 minutes versus 15–480 minutes for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In 2D relighting, LightSwitch outperforms both single-view diffusion baselines—DiLightNet (23.84 PSNR) and Neural Gaffer (24.34 PSNR)—by 1.7–2.2 PSNR, with the largest gap appearing in scene-level consistency (quality drop of 0.15 PSNR vs. 0.49 for DiLightNet). On the Objects with Lighting 3D benchmark, LightSwitch (25.43 PSNR) surpasses all learned inverse-rendering methods including InvRender (23.45), NeRD (21.71), NeRFactor (20.62), NVDiffrec (22.60), and MaterialFusion (20.75), trailing only the non-learned Mitsuba+NeuS pipeline (26.24 PSNR) which requires explicit geometry and PBR rendering. On NeRF-Synthetic, LightSwitch wins on 4 of 5 objects in PSNR (Chair, Hotdog, Lego, Mic) and achieves the best LPIPS on 4 of 5, while running 75× faster than MaterialFusion (~2 min vs. ~240 min) and 240× faster than TensoIR (~2 min vs. ~480 min). The closest competitor, R3DGS, is 7.5× slower and lags by 2–3 PSNR on most objects. Overall, LightSwitch matches or beats every learned baseline in relighting quality while reducing inference time by one to two orders of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightSwitch extends the direct-relighting diffusion line (Neural Gaffer, DiLightNet) from single-view to multi-view by incorporating multi-view self-attention (as in MVDream/ImageDream) and conditioning on inferred material maps from StableMaterialMV, a material diffusion prior the authors previously developed. It departs from inverse rendering methods (MaterialFusion, NVDiffrec, TensoIR) by replacing slow optimization over a differentiable renderer with a feed-forward denoising process, while retaining the key insight that material properties (albedo, roughness, metallicness) should inform relighting. For novel-view relighting, it borrows 3D Gaussian Splatting purely as a rendering tool to synthesize query views before feeding them into the relighting diffusion, rather than as the relighting representation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching or exceeding state-of-the-art inverse rendering quality (e.g., Mitsuba+NeuS) in ~2 minutes versus hours of optimization makes high-fidelity relighting accessible for interactive and production pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that predictions are not guaranteed to be physically plausible and that the fixed latent space of the base diffusion model limits encoding of sharp fine details like specular reflections. A key open question is how to more tightly couple learned relighting with physics-based rendering to ensure physical consistency without sacrificing the speed and generative quality of the diffusion approach.
