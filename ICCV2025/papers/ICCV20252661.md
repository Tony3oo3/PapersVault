## Problem

Structure-from-motion pipelines assume scene rigidity and fail on videos containing dynamic objects, yet existing motion segmentation methods either rely on synthetic training data with significant domain gap, ignore 3D geometric constraints, or produce only sparse correspondence-level masks rather than dense object-level segmentation. There is no zero-shot method that robustly combines epipolar geometry with semantic priors to produce coherent, temporally stable motion masks for in-the-wild video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of RGB frames and produces per-pixel binary motion masks identifying all dynamic objects relative to a fixed world frame. Optical flow is computed between adjacent frames with RAFT, and SAMv2 image-encoder features (last layer) serve as the semantic input to a lightweight MLP (1 hidden layer, 8 neurons) trained per sequence. Initial masks are at the resolution of SAMv2 feature maps and are subsequently upscaled to full resolution via SAMv2's video segmentation refinement, which alternates between odd and even frames for temporal consistency.

## Method

RoMo is a zero-shot iterative method that couples epipolar geometry with pre-trained semantic features. First, RANSAC-estimated fundamental matrices (via Sampson distance scoring of RAFT flow correspondences) yield sparse, noisy per-pixel labels of likely-static and likely-dynamic regions. Second, a shallow MLP is trained on SAMv2 encoder features using these labels as weak supervision, with a Geman-McClure robust kernel to tolerate thresholding noise and a Lipschitz regularizer for smoothness. Third, the predicted masks are used to reject bad correspondences, re-estimate fundamental matrices, and fine-tune the classifier over two iterations, which reduces flickering when large dynamic objects dominate frames. Finally, coarse masks are passed to SAMv2's video segmentation module for high-resolution, temporally consistent output. The key distinction from prior work is the joint use of 3D geometric constraints (epipolar geometry) and 2D semantic priors (SAMv2 features) without any task-specific or synthetic supervision.

## Results

On the FBMS59 motion segmentation benchmark, RoMo achieves 75.5 IoU, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline (STM, 59.2) by 16.3 points and the best synthetically supervised baseline (OCLR-flo, 70.0) by 5.5 points. On the new Casual Motion dataset, RoMo reaches 79.3 IoU vs. 66.4 for OCLR-adap and 54.2 for EM. For dynamic SfM on the Casual Motion real-world dataset, RoMo+COLMAP achieves ATE 2.79 cm and RPE-R 1.60°, substantially outperforming MonST3R (ATE 8.00, RPE-R 2.25°) and LEAP-VO (ATE 13.12, RPE-R 3.86°). On MPI Sintel, RoMo+TheiaSFM achieves RPE-R of 0.217°, more than 2× better than the next-best method (ParticleSfM, 0.535°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMo outperforms all unsupervised motion segmentation baselines by a wide margin: over Motion Grouping (53.1→75.5 IoU on FBMS59), EM (57.8→75.5), and STM (59.2→75.5). It also surpasses the synthetically supervised OCLR-flo (70.0) and OCLR-adap (69.8) on FBMS59, and by an even larger gap on Casual Motion (79.3 vs. 66.4 for OCLR-adap), suggesting that geometric cues generalize better than synthetic supervision to in-the-wild scenes. For dynamic SfM on the real-world Casual Motion dataset, RoMo+COLMAP (ATE 2.79, RPE-R 1.60°) beats MonST3R (ATE 8.00, RPE-R 2.25°) by ~3× in ATE, LEAP-VO (ATE 13.12, RPE-R 3.86°) by ~5×, and ParticleSfM (ATE 5.46 on its 5-scene subset, RPE-R 2.00°) by ~2×. On MPI Sintel, RoMo+TheiaSFM achieves the best RPE-R (0.217°) and RPE-T (0.026 m), outperforming LEAP-VO (RPE-R 1.25°), ParticleSfM (RPE-R 0.535°), MonST3R (RPE-R 0.732°), and CasualSAM (RPE-R 0.615°). Notably, COLMAP without masks fails on 1 of 8 Casual Motion scenes, while COLMAP+OCLR-adap masks fails on 5 of 8, underscoring that mask quality is critical for downstream SfM robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMo carves new ground by repurposing three well-studied components—optical flow (RAFT), epipolar geometry (fundamental matrix + Sampson distance), and foundation-model features (SAMv2)—into a simple iterative loop bridged by an 8-neuron MLP. It departs from unsupervised motion segmentation methods (Motion Grouping, STM, EM) that rely solely on flow decomposition without 3D geometric constraints, and from supervised methods (OCLR, SIMO) that depend on synthetic training data with a significant domain gap to real scenes. Unlike dynamic SfM pipelines (CasualSAM, ParticleSfM, LEAP-VO, MonST3R) that either produce sparse masks or are monolithic frameworks, RoMo outputs dense pixel-level motion masks as a standalone plug-in that can be paired with any SfM backend such as COLMAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: standard SfM pipelines like COLMAP fail on casually captured video with dynamic objects, and prior robust-SfM methods either require heavy learned components or produce only sparse correspondence-level masks. RoMo's zero-shot approach outperforming synthetically supervised baselines suggests that geometric cues combined with foundation-model features can substitute for task-specific training data in motion segmentation. The new Casual Motion dataset (8 real scenes with ground-truth camera trajectories and motion masks) fills a notable gap in dynamic SfM evaluation. Open questions include performance in low-parallax or fully dynamic scenes where epipolar constraints degenerate, and whether the method's reliance on SAMv2 features limits its applicability to domains where that model's objectness prior is weak.
