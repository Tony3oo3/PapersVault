## Problem

Unified 3D point cloud segmentation (semantic, instance, and panoptic) remains challenging due to the sparse, unstructured nature of point clouds and the scarcity of annotated data, making fine-grained class and instance differentiation difficult in complex scenes. Existing unified methods such as OneFormer3D rely solely on point cloud features without leveraging external multimodal knowledge, limiting their ability to capture rich semantic and visual cues needed to distinguish closely related object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252339/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (N×6 with xyz coordinates and RGB values), voxelized into M superpoints, along with offline-precomputed CLIP text embeddings (K descriptions per class, generated by LLaMA 3.1) and CLIP image embeddings (L reference images per class, sourced from the internet). It simultaneously outputs per-point semantic class scores (M×C), instance masks (N×S), and panoptic masks, all produced by a single unified decoder. Notably, the multimodal queries are generated entirely offline in a closed-set paradigm, so inference requires no paired images, captioning models, or real-time vision-language modules.

## Method

VDG-Uni3DSeg builds on the OneFormer3D transformer decoder and introduces two types of multimodal reference queries: LLM-generated class descriptions (via an in-context LLM-DesGen pipeline) and internet-sourced reference images, both encoded into query vectors by CLIP and projected into the 3D feature space. A sparse 3D U-Net backbone extracts point features, which are refined by a Spatial Enhancement Module that applies sparse attention over randomly sampled point subsets to capture scene-wide spatial dependencies at low cost. The mask decoder uses B cross-attention fusion layers to integrate 3D point information into the multimodal queries, with residual connections preserving original semantic and visual information. A novel Semantic-Visual Contrastive (SVC) Loss aligns spatially enhanced point features with both description and image embeddings of the correct class while repelling embeddings of other classes, promoting multimodal feature discriminability. Final semantic predictions are obtained by max-pooling over K description scores and L image scores per class, then summing the two refined predictions for an ensemble that leverages both high-level semantics and fine-grained visual details.

## Results

On S3DIS Area-5, VDG-Uni3DSeg achieves 74.1 mAP50 and 60.1 mAP for instance segmentation, 71.5 mIoU for semantic segmentation, and 66.3 PQ (68.0 PQ_th) for panoptic segmentation, surpassing OneFormer3D by +2.1 mAP50, +1.7 mIoU, and a remarkable +9.6 PQ_th. On ScanNet validation, the method reaches 78.5 mAP50, 76.2 mIoU, and 71.5 PQ, improving over OneFormer3D by +1.3 mAP50 and +0.4 mIoU. On the fine-grained ScanNet200 benchmark, VDG-Uni3DSeg attains 45.1 mAP25, 29.7 mIoU, and 31.3 PQ, maintaining competitive performance across all three unified tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline OneFormer3D [27], VDG-Uni3DSeg consistently wins on S3DIS across all metrics, with the largest gain being +9.6 PQ_th (68.0 vs 58.4) on Area-5, indicating substantially better instance-level panoptic quality. On ScanNet validation, the method edges out OneFormer3D by +1.3 mAP50 (78.5 vs 77.2) and +0.4 mIoU (76.2 vs 75.8), though it slightly trails by −0.5 on PQ_th (70.0 vs 70.5). On the ScanNet hidden test set, VDG-Uni3DSeg loses 1.6 points on mAP25 (88.0 vs 89.6) to OneFormer3D but recovers on mAP50 (+0.3) and mAP (+1.0). Compared to task-specific instance segmentation methods like Mask3D [42] (71.9 mAP50) and PBNet [61] (66.4 mAP50), the unified framework achieves 74.1 mAP50 on S3DIS, demonstrating that multimodal guidance benefits even instance-level detection. On ScanNet200, VDG-Uni3DSeg ties OneFormer3D on mAP50 (40.0) and slightly underperforms on PQ_st (77.9 vs 78.6), but leads on mAP25 (+0.9), mAP (+0.5), and mIoU (+0.7), showing robustness to fine-grained class distinctions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends OneFormer3D (CVPR 2024), which unified semantic, instance, and panoptic 3D segmentation in a single transformer decoder, by injecting multimodal reference knowledge as additional queries. The key departure from prior unified 3D segmentation is the use of LLM-generated class descriptions and internet-sourced reference images, encoded via CLIP, as offline semantic and visual anchors—contrasting with open-vocabulary methods that require real-time 2D-3D alignment. The Spatial Enhancement Module draws on sparse attention mechanisms from long-sequence transformer literature, and the overall architecture follows the OneFormer/OneFormer3D lineage of task-conditioned queries with a shared mask decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The offline multimodal reference paradigm offers a practical path to enriching 3D segmentation without the computational overhead of real-time vision-language fusion or the annotation burden of large-scale 3D labeling. However, the method remains closed-set and depends on the quality and availability of internet images per class, which may limit generalization to rare or novel object categories. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary or outdoor large-scale scenes, and whether the modest per-metric gains (typically 1–2 points over OneFormer3D) justify the added pipeline complexity of LLM prompting and image retrieval in production settings.
