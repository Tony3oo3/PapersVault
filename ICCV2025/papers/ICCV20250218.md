## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods for amodal completion treat the entire occluder as a single inpainting region, which in human-object interaction (HOI) scenarios is significantly larger than the actual occluded area of the object, leading to overextended or implausible completions. No prior work specifically addresses amodal completion in HOI by exploiting the physical constraints inherent to these interactions, such as human body topology and contact points, to precisely delineate where occluded parts are most likely to reside.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a segmented RGB image of the occluded subject, binary masks for the human, object, contact points, and SMPL body parts, plus a free-text prompt describing the object category. In the in-the-wild variant, all masks and the prompt are generated automatically via SAM (segmentation), HMR (SMPL parameters projected to 2D contact points), and a VLM (interaction description), eliminating the need for ground-truth annotations. The output is a full-resolution amodally completed image that can subsequently drive 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, Triplane single-view 3D mesh recovery, or joint human-object novel-pose/view synthesis.

## Method

The method introduces a contact-aware convex hull operation that combines a dilated human-object boundary mask with a binary contact map to compute a convex hull, whose intersection with the occluder mask yields a primary region M_p (high occlusion probability) and a secondary region M_s (lower probability). Building on this, a multi-regional inpainting strategy extends the pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2 inpainting model without any additional training: the denoising process is split at timestep T' = ⌊T·r⌋, first establishing coarse structure within M_p over steps T→T', then progressively refining details across M_p ∪ M_s over steps T'→0, with the strength parameter r controlling when secondary-region inpainting begins. This differentiated denoising prioritizes the most occluded area while preserving the integrity of less-affected regions, and the entire pipeline operates on a single image with T=50 DDIM steps.

## Results

On BEHAVE, the proposed method achieves 77.64% mIoU and 26.91 CLIP score for object amodal completion, while on InterCap it reaches 72.34% mIoU and 26.97 CLIP score. The mIoU gains are substantial: 6.6 points over the next-best method (Xu et al., 71.03%) on BEHAVE and 3.1 points over pix2gestalt (69.23%) on InterCap. In user preference studies, the method is preferred over all baselines, with win-rates of 94.0% vs. Naive outpainting, 92.4% vs. LaMa, 88.0% vs. Inst-Inpaint, 68.0% vs. pix2gestalt, and 65.8% vs. Xu et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the highest mIoU across both datasets, outperforming the closest competitor Xu et al. [37] by 6.6 points on BEHAVE (77.64% vs. 71.03%) and 3.1 points on InterCap (72.34% vs. 69.23%). Against pix2gestalt [22], the gains are even larger: 8.1 points on BEHAVE mIoU and 4.0 points on InterCap mIoU, while also surpassing it on CLIP (26.91 vs. 23.45 on BEHAVE). The method beats LaMa [31] and Inst-Inpaint [40] on both CLIP and mIoU, with mIoU improvements of 17.2 and 13.9 points respectively on BEHAVE. The sole metric where the method trails is CLIP score against Naive outpainting [25] (26.91 vs. 27.34 on BEHAVE; 26.97 vs. 27.55 on InterCap), which the authors attribute to Naive outpainting generating content across the entire canvas, inherently favoring broader visual alignment with the text query. Despite this CLIP gap, the method is strongly preferred by users (94.0% win-rate vs. Naive outpainting), confirming that the mIoU advantage translates to perceptually superior completions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based amodal completion (pix2gestalt, Xu et al.) by introducing a contact-aware, multi-regional inpainting strategy that splits the occluder into primary and secondary regions via convex hull operations on contact points and human-object boundaries. Unlike prior single-mask inpainting approaches, it applies differentiated denoising strengths (via the SD-inpaint strength parameter) to each region, establishing coarse structure in the high-priority region before refining the lower-priority one. The method is entirely training-free, composing pre-trained components (Stable Diffusion, SAM, HMR, VLM) into a pipeline that also handles in-the-wild data without ground-truth annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to explicitly frame amodal completion around HOI-specific cues (contact points, human topology), the paper provides a practical, annotation-free pipeline that bridges 2D completion to downstream 3D reconstruction (Gaussian Splatting, Triplane) and novel-view/pose synthesis. Key limitations include single-image processing without temporal consistency, restriction to single human-object pairs, and dependence on the diffusion model's training distribution for unseen objects. Open questions include whether the multi-regional strategy generalizes to multi-subject scenes, whether temporal coherence can be added for video, and whether the convex-hull region identification holds for highly non-convex occlusions.
