## Problem

Existing video-language benchmarks primarily focus on generic action recognition, instructional videos, or long-form YouTube content, and do not thoroughly assess MLLMs' ability to comprehend complex temporal dependencies with rapid scene transitions and dense multimodal cues. Advertisement videos, which compress rich narratives into short durations with high visual complexity, remain underexplored in MLLM evaluation. Additionally, no quantitative measure exists to compare video complexity across benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds advertisement videos (average 79.6 seconds, 200 total) into MLLMs alongside four-option multiple-choice questions spanning three task types: visual finding (425), visual summary (312), and visual reasoning (363). Models consume videos at varying frame counts (16–128 frames for open-source models, 1 fps for Gemini-1.5 Pro), and an optional Whisper-generated audio transcript can be appended as text. Output is a single selected answer letter, scored as accuracy per task type and overall, with a text-only GPT-4o baseline (21.27%) confirming that visual input is indispensable.

## Method

The paper introduces VideoAds, a 200-video, 1100-question benchmark built from YouTube advertisement videos filtered for visual complexity and narrative density, with human-annotated VQA pairs covering visual finding, summarization, and multi-step reasoning. A novel quantitative video complexity metric is defined using DINO-v2 feature embeddings: complexity duration (D_cpx) computes the sum over frames of (1 − weighted cosine similarity to neighboring frames within a window of size d), and video complexity (V_cpx) normalizes this by total frame count to yield a per-second change density. The benchmark is evaluated across 10+ open-source and closed-source MLLMs at multiple frame counts, with ablations on audio transcript augmentation and guided chain-of-thought prompting (explicit intermediate sub-questions rather than generic 'think step-by-step'). Key findings include that Qwen2.5-VL-72B (73.35%) outperforms GPT-4o (66.82%) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (69.66%), while Gemini-2.5 Pro leads at 80.04% and human experts reach 94.27%, exposing a significant temporal-reasoning gap.

## Results

On VideoAds, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest model accuracy of 80.04% overall, followed by Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 73.35% (128 frames), Gemini 1.5 Pro at 69.66%, and GPT-4o at 66.82% (128 frames). Human experts achieve 94.27% accuracy, revealing a substantial gap of ~14 points over the best model. A text-only GPT-4o baseline scores just 21.27% (near the 25% random-guess floor), confirming that visual reasoning is indispensable. Across all models, performance degrades sharply from Visual Finding (75–84%) to Visual Reasoning (62–77%), indicating that complex narrative inference remains the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL-72B (73.35%) outperforms both GPT-4o (66.82%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (69.66%) overall, a notable reversal of the typical open-source vs. proprietary gap in video understanding. However, GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro still lead on Visual Finding (75.06% and 75.29% respectively), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B dominates on Visual Summary (73.72% vs. 63.14%/67.31%) and Visual Reasoning (70.80% vs. 62.26%/66.39%). Gemini 2.5 Pro extends its lead across all three sub-tasks (84.47/78.53/77.13), confirming that purpose-built temporal modeling in the latest proprietary models still sets the ceiling. Among open-source 7B models, MiniCPM-V2-6 (57.17%) and LLaVA-OneVision (55.08%) are competitive, while LongVA (42.33%) and InternVideo2-chat (40.09%) lag significantly. The ~14-point gap between the best model (80.04%) and human performance (94.27%) is largest on Visual Reasoning (77.13% vs. 95.04%), underscoring that multi-step narrative inference over fast-paced ad content remains unsolved for current MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark specifically targeting advertisement videos for MLLM evaluation, a domain previously underexplored in video understanding. It builds conceptually on existing video QA benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, TempCompass, EgoSchema) but departs from them by focusing on high-complexity, fast-paced content with rapid scene transitions and dense multimodal narratives. The quantitative video complexity metric, based on DINO-v2 feature variance over time, extends qualitative complexity discussions in prior work (e.g., TempCompass) into a structured, comparable measure. The VQA task design (visual finding, summary, reasoning) follows established video QA paradigms but is instantiated on a fundamentally different content type that demands multi-step temporal reasoning over disjointed scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial gap between human performance (94.27%) and the best MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 80.04%), underscoring that current models still struggle with the dense temporal reasoning that advertisement videos demand. The finding that open-source Qwen2.5-VL-72B outperforms GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro on this benchmark challenges the assumption that proprietary models uniformly lead in video understanding, while the consistent benefit from audio transcripts highlights underexplored cross-modal reasoning. The video complexity metric provides a reusable tool for comparing benchmarks along a temporal-dynamics axis. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond the 200-video scale, whether higher FPS sampling alone can close the human-model gap, and how to train MLLMs to handle the multi-hop narrative reasoning that remains the weakest link.
