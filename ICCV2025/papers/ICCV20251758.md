## Problem

Existing pseudo word methods for Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval either compress the entire image into a single pseudo word (causing semantic omission of co-occurring objects) or decompose it via vanilla attention into multiple pseudo words (introducing background noise and ambiguous activations). Both approaches ignore the fact that CIR editing targets are naturally hierarchical—spanning object parts, whole objects, and foreground/background—leading to semantic entanglement that prevents adaptive modification of specific objects at the appropriate granularity.

## Input / Output

At training, the method consumes image-caption pairs from CC3M (no triplet annotations needed); at inference, it takes a reference image and a modification text. The reference image is parsed into a three-level hierarchy (global feature, n₁=32 mid-level objects, n₂=8 fine-grained parts) via a two-stage grouping module, while the modification text is parsed by Stanford CoreNLP into noun subjects (k_s=3) and phrases (k_p=5). The output is a set of hierarchical pseudo words (one global token plus object-aware tokens at two levels) mapped into the CLIP/BLIP word embedding space, which are concatenated with the modified text to form a composed query for ranking candidate target images.

## Method

HIT decouples pseudo word learning into two stages. First, a multi-stage grouping module (inspired by GroupViT) uses learnable group tokens and Gumbel-Softmax differentiable assignment to progressively merge patch features into a three-level hierarchy of visual objects, with a linear classifier providing confidence scores per object. Second, a text-adaptive filtering strategy employs bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) to optimally align the parsed visual objects with noun/phrase embeddings extracted from the paired caption, screening out only the objects relevant to the editing intent. The filtered objects are then mapped via a shared network into hierarchical pseudo words (global, object-level, phrase-level), trained with a CLIP-style contrastive loss plus an auxiliary parsing loss. At inference, the modified text drives filtering with special logic for noun-present vs. noun-absent cases, and the three-level pseudo words are combined with the modified text to compute multi-level similarities against candidate images.

## Results

On Fashion-IQ (BLIP backbone), HIT achieves 34.5 R@10 and 55.8 R@50 average, surpassing the strongest ZS-CIR baseline ISA (29.6/49.5) by approximately 5% and 6%. On CIRR (BLIP backbone), HIT reaches 36.9 R@1, 67.7 R@5, 79.1 R@10, and 94.7 R@50, outperforming ISA by roughly 6% in average recall. On CIRCO (BLIP backbone), HIT achieves 22.6 mAP@50, exceeding ISA (14.0) by over 7% across all mAP@K metrics. Overall, HIT outperforms prior state-of-the-art ZS-CIR methods by 5%–8% in average recall across three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIT outperforms all 11 compared baselines across all three benchmarks (Fashion-IQ, CIRR, CIRCO) and both backbones (CLIP, BLIP). Against the strongest ZS-CIR competitor ISA [9] (multiple pseudo words via asymmetric attention), HIT gains ~5% R@10 and ~6% R@50 on Fashion-IQ (BLIP), ~6% average recall on CIRR (BLIP), and over 7% mAP@K on CIRCO (BLIP). Single-pseudo-word methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE, Context-I2W, KEDs, LinCIR) are consistently outperformed by 3–5% on average recall, confirming the benefit of hierarchical multi-level pseudo words. Slerp+TAT [14], which fine-tunes encoders, achieves competitive results on CLIP but is surpassed by HIT when a stronger BLIP backbone is used. Compared to supervised methods (Table 8), HIT-BLIP (36.9 R@1 on CIRR) approaches SSN (43.9 R@1) without requiring any triplet annotations, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo word learning paradigm established by Pic2Word and SEARLE, which map a reference image into the word embedding space to reduce ZS-CIR to text-to-image retrieval. It departs from prior single-pseudo-word methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE, Context-I2W, KEDs) and multi-pseudo-word methods using vanilla attention (ISA) by introducing a hierarchical decomposition inspired by GroupViT's learnable group tokens, combined with a text-adaptive bipartite matching filter. The key conceptual departure is the explicit argument that CIR editing objects are naturally hierarchical (parts → objects → foreground/background), and that prior methods suffer from semantic omission or redundancy because they collapse all levels into flat representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical pseudo word framework provides a more interpretable and structured alternative to attention-based pseudo word generation, with consistent 5–8% gains across three benchmarks and two backbones. A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is that fine-grained object decomposition (e.g., distinguishing 'fur' from 'grass') remains imprecise without explicit segmentation supervision, suggesting a ceiling on the leaf-level hierarchy. Open questions include whether the fixed three-level hierarchy generalizes to scenes with more complex part-object relationships, and whether the reliance on Stanford CoreNLP for noun/phrase extraction introduces brittleness on non-English or highly informal modification texts.
