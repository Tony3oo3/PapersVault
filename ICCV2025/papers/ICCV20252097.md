## Problem

Prior training-free methods improve CLIP's spatial localization by modifying intermediate attention (e.g., self-self or proxy attention), but this coherence is not reliably propagated to final segmentation outputs due to subsequent operations like projections and FFNs. Moreover, intermediate attention maps lack direct interaction with text-derived class representations, leaving a semantic gap between internal spatial arrangements and the model's actual patch-level predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (processed at 224×224 patch resolution) and a set of class names formatted as text prompts ('A photo of a [CLASS]'), producing a dense per-patch segmentation mask via argmax over class logits. It operates as a training-free plug-in on the last attention block of ViT backbones (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14), requiring no parameter updates or post-processing, and adds only 3–5% inference overhead over the base model.

## Method

FSA introduces a feedback loop that feeds output-based patch semantics back into the intermediate attention. First, attention isolation subtracts logits from a parallel uniform-attention branch to isolate the net contribution of the initial attention map. Pairwise patch similarity is then computed via KL divergence on these isolated logits, converted to a [0,1] similarity score. A confidence-based pruning step sorts each row, selects patches until cumulative confidence reaches threshold p (0.45), and applies adaptive exponential scaling (λ=2) to amplify selected patches while suppressing the rest. The resulting sparse feedback attention is integrated via three adaptation strategies (pre-multiplying, post-multiplying, or replacing the initial attention before V projection), which are ensembled by averaging their combined attention matrices before multiplying V. The entire pipeline is training-free and orthogonal to the choice of attention type (Q-K, self-self, or Proxy) and backbone.

## Results

The paper reports mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) averaged across 8 open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks. The best configuration (ProxyCLIP + FSA on ViT-H/14) achieves 45.8 mIoU average, improving over the ProxyCLIP baseline by +1.4. The largest absolute gains come from integrating FSA into MaskCLIP: +7.9 mIoU (ViT-B/16), +18.7 mIoU (ViT-L/14), and +14.1 mIoU (ViT-H/14). FSA consistently improves all four integrated methods across all three backbones, with gains ranging from +0.3 to +18.7 mIoU depending on the base method and backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSA is a plug-in module that is integrated into four existing training-free methods (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, ProxyCLIP) and consistently improves all of them across all three backbones (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14) on all eight benchmarks. The largest gains are observed when FSA is added to MaskCLIP, which relies on value embeddings without explicit attention modification: +7.9 mIoU (ViT-B/16), +18.7 mIoU (ViT-L/14), and +14.1 mIoU (ViT-H/14). SCLIP benefits moderately (+2.4 to +6.0 mIoU), while ClearCLIP shows smaller but consistent improvements (+0.7 to +6.0 mIoU). ProxyCLIP, which already leverages external VFM spatial features for strong localization, sees the smallest but still positive gains (+0.7 to +1.4 mIoU), confirming that FSA is most impactful when the base method has weaker spatial coherence. No comparison shows a degradation, and the method adds only 3–5% inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free paradigm for CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation, building directly on ProxyCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, and MaskCLIP, which each modify intermediate attention or other forward-pass components to improve spatial coherence. The key conceptual departure is introducing a feedback loop: rather than only refining intermediate attention in the forward direction, FSA computes patch-level semantic similarity from the model's own output logits (which integrate both visual and textual cues) and feeds this back to modulate the initial attention maps. The attention isolation mechanism (subtracting uniform-attention logits) and confidence-based sparse pruning are novel components that make this feedback signal cleaner and more targeted, positioning FSA as an orthogonal plug-in rather than a replacement for existing attention modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in nature of FSA—adding only 3–5% overhead while consistently improving four SoTA methods across three backbones and multiple attention types—makes it a practical drop-in for anyone working with CLIP-based segmentation. The paper's core observation that intermediate attention coherence is not reliably propagated to final outputs due to subsequent projections and FFN layers is well-motivated and empirically validated, though the magnitude of gains on strong baselines (ProxyCLIP) is modest (+0.6 to +1.4 mIoU). Open questions include whether the feedback mechanism could be applied at multiple transformer layers rather than only the last, whether iterative adaptation (which the paper reports as unhelpful) could be made effective with different scheduling, and whether the approach generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation.
