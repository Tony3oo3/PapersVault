## Problem

Existing learning-based non-prehensile manipulation methods rely heavily on multi-view cameras and precise pose tracking for state estimation, and they fail to generalize across varying physical conditions such as object mass, table friction, and slipperiness. This paper addresses both limitations by developing a policy that operates from a single-view point cloud without external tracking while explicitly adapting to diverse dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single-view partial point cloud (from a side-mounted RealSense D435), robot joint positions/velocities, end-effector pose, and a visual goal representation constructed by transforming the initial point cloud to the target 6D pose (rather than requiring an explicit task state). The output is a two-part action: an end-effector subgoal residual ΔT_ee ∈ SE(3) executed via inverse kinematics, plus joint-space impedance parameters (positional gains and damping factors in R^7 each) for variable-impedance force control. Simultaneously, the model predicts the next task state as a 3D translation and 9D rotation representation, providing an additional supervisory signal during training.

## Method

DyWA employs a teacher-student distillation pipeline where a PPO-trained RL teacher with privileged full point cloud and physics parameters is distilled into a student via DAgger (500K iterations). The student is a World Action Model that jointly predicts the robot action and the next task state, creating a synergistic learning signal that improves action convergence beyond imitation loss alone. A Dynamics Adaptation Module (inspired by RMA) encodes L past observation-action pairs through a 1D CNN into a compact adaptation embedding, supervised by the teacher's full geometry and physics embeddings to recover information missing from the single-view partial observation. This embedding is decoded into a dynamics vector that conditions the world action model via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), applying learned per-feature scaling and shifting in early layers to adapt behavior to varying mass, friction, and scale. The low-level action is executed through a variable impedance controller, and domain randomization over object mass, scale, friction, and restitution enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

On the most challenging evaluation track (unknown state, single-view observation), DyWA achieves 82.2% success rate on seen objects and 75.0% on unseen objects, outperforming the strongest baseline CORN(PN++) by 31.5 and 25.6 percentage points respectively. In the known-state, three-view setting, DyWA reaches 87.9% (seen) and 85.0% (unseen), matching or slightly exceeding CORN(PN++) (87.3/84.3). In real-world experiments, DyWA achieves a 68% average success rate across 10 diverse objects (including slippery and non-uniform mass objects), compared to 36% for CORN with external pose tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyWA consistently outperforms both baselines across all three evaluation tracks. Against HACMan [56], the gap is extreme: in the single-view unknown-state track, DyWA achieves 82.2% vs. HACMan's 1.5% (seen) and 75.0% vs. 2.9% (unseen), a gap of over 80 percentage points, largely because HACMan's primitive-based approach requires strict per-point correspondence and cannot handle partial observability. Against CORN [8], DyWA improves by 53.2 points (82.2 vs. 29.0) on seen objects and 45.2 points (75.0 vs. 29.8) on unseen objects in the single-view track. Even the enhanced CORN(PN++) variant, which replaces CORN's shallow MLP encoder with the same PointNet++ backbone as DyWA, still trails by 31.5 points (82.2 vs. 50.7) on seen objects, confirming that the performance gain comes from the dynamics adaptation and world modeling rather than the visual encoder alone. In real-world experiments, DyWA (68% average) more than doubles CORN-with-tracking (36%), and uniquely succeeds on slippery objects and half-filled bottles where CORN fails entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CORN's simulation environment, reward design, and variable impedance control architecture, while drawing on RMA's rapid motor adaptation concept for the dynamics adaptation module. It departs from both CORN and HACMan by eliminating the reliance on multi-view cameras and external pose tracking, instead using a single-view partial point cloud with a learned dynamics embedding from historical trajectories. The key architectural departure is the 'world action model' formulation—jointly predicting actions and next states during distillation—which extends standard DAgger imitation with an additional world-modeling supervisory signal, and uses FiLM conditioning (borrowed from vision-language integration) to inject the dynamics embedding into the policy network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing hardware requirements to a single RGB-D camera and no pose tracker while maintaining 68% real-world success across diverse objects, friction levels, and non-uniform mass distributions, this work makes non-prehensile manipulation more deployable in unstructured settings. The unified simulation benchmark (varying camera views and pose availability) addresses a gap in the field and provides a standardized evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the point-cloud-only input can be extended to handle symmetric, transparent, or specular objects (acknowledged limitations), and whether the world-model-plus-dynamics-adaptation synergy generalizes beyond tabletop rearrangement to more complex multi-object or long-horizon tasks.
