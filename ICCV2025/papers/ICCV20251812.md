## Problem

SD-based Real-ISR methods irreversibly lose fine-scale structures (small text, textures) because the VAE's 8× spatial compression discards high-frequency details that the diffusion process cannot recover. Simply replacing it with a 4× VAE introduces two new challenges: aligning the new latent space with the pre-trained UNet without retraining, and managing the doubled computational cost of higher-resolution latent features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251812/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-resolution image (generated via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and a text prompt extracted by DAPE and a CLIP text encoder. It produces a 512×512 high-resolution image in a single diffusion step (t=1). Internally, the VAE-D4 encoder maps the input to 128×128×4 latent features (versus 64×64×4 for the standard VAE-D8), which are processed by the denoising UNet before being decoded back to full resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage Transfer VAE Training (TVT) strategy that converts the 8×-downsampled VAE into a compact 4× variant while preserving latent-space compatibility with the pre-trained SD UNet. Stage 1 trains a VAE-D4 decoder conditioned on VAE-D8 encoder outputs (using L1, LPIPS, and GAN losses) so it can reconstruct high-quality images from the original latent distribution; Stage 2 trains the VAE-D4 encoder with the decoder fixed (using L1 and LPIPS losses) to generate features matching that distribution. To handle the doubled latent resolution efficiently, a compute-efficient UNet replicates the first and last layers of the pre-trained UNet to process 128×128 features via stride-2 convolutions and nearest-neighbor upsampling, while the original UNet continues processing 64×64 features; only the replicated layers are fully fine-tuned and the rest use LoRA, reducing FLOPs by 51% compared to naively running the full UNet at 128×128 resolution.

## Results

On the Real-ISR task, TVT (1-step) achieves the best PSNR across all three test sets: 25.81 dB on RealSR, 28.27 dB on DRealSR, and 24.23 dB on DIV2K-val, outperforming the closest one-step competitor OSEDiff by 0.66, 0.35, and 0.51 dB respectively. TVT also leads on SSIM (0.7596/0.7899/0.6292) and FSIM, and achieves the best or second-best scores on most no-reference metrics (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, Q-Align, TOPIQ, HyperIQA). On the STISR task (RealCE), TVT attains the best SSIM (0.7030), LPIPS (0.1644), character recognition accuracy (ACC 0.3096), and NED (0.6621), while trailing OSEDiff slightly on PSNR (19.69 vs. 19.89). TVT also achieves the lowest FLOPs (1.97T) among all compared SD-based methods, 13% fewer than OSEDiff (2.27T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVT consistently outperforms all multi-step diffusion baselines (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, PASD) on reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FSIM) across all three Real-ISR datasets, with the largest PSNR gains over StableSR (+1.11 dB on RealSR, +0.20 dB on DRealSR, +0.95 dB on DIV2K-val). Among one-step methods, TVT beats OSEDiff by 0.66 dB PSNR and 0.0255 SSIM on RealSR, and by 0.35 dB PSNR on DRealSR, while also achieving lower FLOPs (1.97T vs. 2.27T). TVT also surpasses S3Diff by a wider margin (1.43 dB PSNR on RealSR), confirming that the VAE-D4 adaptation is more effective than degradation-guided LoRA alone. On the STISR benchmark (RealCE), TVT achieves the best SSIM, LPIPS, ACC, and NED, surpassing OSEDiff in text recognition accuracy (0.3096 vs. 0.2998) and NED (0.6621 vs. 0.6400), though OSEDiff retains a slight PSNR edge (19.89 vs. 19.69). TVT also outperforms non-diffusion text-prior methods (TBSRN, TATT, TSRN) and the diffusion-based DCDM and DiffTSR on all STISR metrics, validating the benefit of the higher-resolution latent space for fine text structure recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and the one-step diffusion paradigm for Real-ISR established by OSEDiff (Wu et al., 2024), which uses LoRA adapters and VSD loss to compress multi-step denoising into a single forward pass. However, it departs from all prior SD-based Real-ISR methods (StableSR, PASD, SeeSR, OSEDiff, S3Diff) by identifying the 8× VAE compression as the fundamental bottleneck for fine-structure preservation, rather than the diffusion process itself. The key conceptual departure is intervening at the VAE level—transferring from 8× to 4× downsampling via a two-stage training strategy—while keeping the pre-trained UNet largely intact, rather than modifying the UNet or adding control signals as prior methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a well-known but previously unaddressed limitation of the entire SD-based Real-ISR pipeline: the irreversible loss of fine-scale details (small text, textures) due to aggressive VAE spatial compression. The practical impact is significant because the TVT strategy requires no UNet retraining and actually reduces FLOPs (1.97T) below the strongest one-step competitor OSEDiff (2.27T), making it a drop-in improvement for existing SD-based restoration pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even lower compression ratios (e.g., 2×), how it generalizes to video super-resolution, and whether the compute-efficient UNet design can be further optimized for deployment on resource-constrained devices, which the authors acknowledge remains challenging.
