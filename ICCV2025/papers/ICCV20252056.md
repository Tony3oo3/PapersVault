## Problem

Existing visual foundation models (CLIP, SAM, DINO) are trained with image-level supervision and suffer fundamental perceptual errors on fine-grained tasks involving small, dense texts in documents. No foundation model operates at BPE token granularity to align individual visual text regions with their corresponding language subwords, leaving a gap for document understanding, text segmentation, and text retrieval tasks that require precise spatial-semantic correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TokenFD takes a single RGB image (448×448 or 1024×1024) and produces token-level visual embeddings where each embedding corresponds to a BPE subword's pixel mask via mean-pooling; the language side uses a simple token embedding layer rather than a full text encoder. TokenVL extends this with multi-scale adaptive cropping, dividing the input into up to six non-overlapping 448×448 sub-images plus a global resized image, then compresses each 4×4 patch window into a single token via a learnable abstractor before feeding the flattened sequence into an LLM. Outputs range from segmentation masks and retrieval similarity maps (frozen VFM) to free-form text answers for VQA (full MLLM).

## Method

TokenFD pre-trains a ViT encoder (patch size 14) with a 2-layer deconvolution to 4× resolution and a linear projection to match language embedding dimension, then aligns token-level visual features (mean-pooled within each BPE token mask) with token embeddings using three losses: L1 distance, cosine similarity, and a SigLIP-style sigmoid loss with learnable temperature and bias. This 'image-as-text' alignment is trained on the self-constructed TokenIT dataset (20M images, 1.8B token-mask pairs) where each BPE token has a one-to-one pixel-level mask. TokenVL builds on the frozen TokenFD by adding a token abstractor (a learnable <text> embedding that adaptively selects the most informative feature within each 4×4 window) and performs two-stage LLM training: first, LLM-guided Token Alignment that simultaneously runs VQA text parsing and explicitly aligns each BPE token's language embedding with its spatial image region using the token masks; second, supervised instruction tuning on diverse document VQA datasets with the alignment branch removed to avoid inference overhead.

## Results

TokenVL-8B achieves the highest OCRBench score of 860 among 8B-class models, outperforming InternVL2.5-8B (822) by +38 and TextHawk2-7B (784) by +76. On document VQA benchmarks, TokenVL-8B reaches 94.2 on DocVQA, 86.6 on ChartQA, and 79.9 on TextVQA, while TokenVL-2B achieves 89.9, 81.1, and 76.4 respectively. As a frozen VFM, TokenFD attains 48.77% average on text segmentation (linear probe), 63.62% mAP on bilingual text retrieval, and 72.73% ANLS on VQA, substantially surpassing CLIP-L, SAM-H, and InternViT2.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenVL-8B outperforms InternVL2.5-8B on 9 of 10 benchmarks, with the largest margins on KLC (+17.0), SROIE (+10.2), WTQ (+8.7), and TabFact (+10.4), while narrowly losing on InfoVQA (76.5 vs. 77.6). Against the strong 7B expert model TextHawk2, TokenVL-8B leads on DocVQA (+4.6), ChartQA (+5.2), TextVQA (+4.8), WTQ (+15.2), and TabFact (+7.1). TokenVL-8B also surpasses closed-source GPT-4o on DocVQA (+1.4), ChartQA (+0.9), WTQ (+14.8), and TabFact (+4.1), and dominates Claude-3.5 Sonnet and Gemini-1.5-Pro on ChartQA (+34.8 and +51.9) and TabFact (+31.7 and +14.0). At the 2B scale, TokenVL-2B beats MiniMonkey-2B and InternVL2.5-2B on every reported benchmark, with particularly large gains on SROIE (+12.3 over InternVL2.5-2B) and KLC (+22.7). As a frozen VFM, TokenFD achieves 63.62% average mAP on bilingual text retrieval versus 13.29% for InternViT2.5 and 3.62% for CLIP-L, and 48.77% average on text segmentation (linear probe) versus 42.21% for InternViT2.5 and 34.51% for SAM-H.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the image-level contrastive alignment paradigm of CLIP/SigLIP and the pixel-level segmentation paradigm of SAM by introducing token-level (BPE subword) visual-language alignment as a new granularity for VFM pretraining. It directly adapts SigLIP's sigmoid loss formulation to the token level and replaces CLIP's complex text encoder with a simple learnable token embedding layer, arguing that the 'image-as-text' semantic alignment is sufficient without a full language model. For the MLLM stage (TokenVL), it builds on the multi-scale adaptive cropping strategy from UReader/Mini-Monkey and the two-stage training paradigm (pre-training + SFT) from InternVL, but adds an explicit LLM-guided token alignment loss that uses BPE token masks to spatially anchor language tokens to image regions within the LLM's hidden layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TokenIT dataset (20M images, 1.8B token-mask pairs) is a significant open resource that could benefit the broader text-image community beyond this paper's specific models. The token-level VFM concept offers a principled alternative to the current trend of stacking multiple VFMs (e.g., CLIP + SAM) in MLLMs, potentially reducing redundancy and computational cost. However, the ablation reveals that the LLM-level token alignment contributes only ~0.6% average gain on final VQA benchmarks, suggesting the primary benefit comes from the VFM-level pretraining rather than the explicit spatial alignment in the LLM. Open questions include whether this token-level approach generalizes to non-text visual content and whether the 4× feature upscaling via deconvolution is the optimal resolution strategy.
