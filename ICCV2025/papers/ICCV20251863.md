## Problem

Current 3D generation pipelines rely on separate models for color, depth, and surface normals, producing inconsistent geometry predictions, high memory/latency costs, and an inability to solve inverse problems (e.g., 3D inpainting) that require sampling from the joint appearance-geometry distribution given partial observations. No prior method learns a single generative prior over color, depth, and normals simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251863/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Orchid operates at 512×512 resolution and supports three modes: text-conditioned generation (text prompt → color + depth + normals), color-conditioned estimation (RGB image → depth + normals, with text dropped), and joint inpainting (partial color-depth-normal observations → completed maps). Depth is preprocessed as normalized inverse depth (1/d shifted to start at zero and scaled by its median deviation), and normals are unit vectors in [-1,1]³. The joint latent space is 8 channels (3 RGB + 1 depth + 3 normals), and outputs can be directly fed into 3D Gaussian Splatting for mesh-free 3D reconstruction.

## Method

Orchid comprises a joint VAE and a text-conditioned latent diffusion model. The VAE extends a color-pretrained encoder/decoder by adding four channels (depth + 3 normals) to produce a shared 8-channel latent, trained with per-modality reconstruction losses, adversarial/perceptual color losses, KL regularization, and a distillation loss that anchors the joint latents to the original color VAE's distribution to preserve pretrained diffusion priors. The LDM is a transformer UNet (Stable Diffusion-style) that denoises the joint latents using v-prediction with classifier-free guidance; for color-conditioned mode, a frozen color-only VAE latent is appended as an additional condition channel. Training combines ~2.5M paired color-depth-normal images (Omnidata, Virtual KITTI, Hypersim, DIODE) with ~100M pseudo-labeled images distilled from teacher depth and normal models, enabling zero-shot generalization. The key architectural insight is that encoding all three modalities in a single latent space enforces mutual consistency between depth and normals and allows direct sampling from the joint distribution for inverse problems like 3D inpainting.

## Results

On zero-shot monocular surface normal estimation, Orchid achieves the best Mean angular error across all four held-out datasets: 15.2° on NYUv2, 14.2° on ScanNet, 16.3° on iBims-1, and 31.7° on Sintel, outperforming the strongest baseline DSINE by 1.2°–3.2° on each. For zero-shot depth estimation, Orchid is competitive with the SOTA depth-only model DepthAnything v2 (e.g., 5.7 vs 4.5 AbsRel on NYUv2; 7.7 vs 7.4 on KITTI) while jointly predicting normals. Orchid also produces significantly more consistent depth-normal pairs, with a mean inconsistency of 0.040 on NYUv2 and 0.082 on KITTI, versus 0.059/0.144 for the best multi-model combination (DepthAnything v2 + DSINE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Orchid outperforms all joint depth-normal baselines on both tasks: versus GeoWizard [13], it improves depth AbsRel from 5.6→5.7 (NYUv2) and 14.4→7.7 (KITTI) and normal Mean from 18.9→15.2 (NYUv2) and 17.4→14.2 (ScanNet). Against the diffusion-based Lotus-G [18], Orchid wins on normals across all four datasets (e.g., 15.2 vs 16.9 Mean on NYUv2) and is comparable on depth. Versus Marigold [23], Orchid is clearly superior on both depth (5.7 vs 6.1 AbsRel on NYUv2) and normals (15.2 vs 20.9 Mean on NYUv2). On depth, Orchid is slightly behind the depth-only SOTA DepthAnything v2 [61] (5.7 vs 4.5 AbsRel on NYUv2; 7.7 vs 7.4 on KITTI) but matches or exceeds it on ETH3D (7.3 vs 6.8) and is competitive overall while simultaneously producing normals. For normals, Orchid beats the specialized DSINE [2] by 1.2° on NYUv2, 2.0° on ScanNet, 0.8° on iBims-1, and 3.2° on Sintel, and substantially outperforms the feed-forward Omnidata v2 [9] baseline. In the depth-normal consistency metric, Orchid (0.040/0.082) is 32–75% more consistent than the best multi-model combination DA+DSINE (0.059/0.144), and far ahead of GeoWizard (0.122/0.324). For 3D inpainting, Orchid qualitatively outperforms the multi-step baseline that chains Stable Diffusion inpainting with Marigold-dc [52] depth completion and Lotus-G [18] normal prediction, producing geometrically consistent results without discontinuous edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

Orchid extends the Stable Diffusion / latent diffusion model paradigm by replacing the color-only VAE with a joint color-depth-normal VAE and training a single LDM to denoise all three modalities simultaneously. It departs from the prevailing multi-step pipeline (generate color → predict depth → predict normals) and from prior joint-geometry models like GeoWizard that handle depth and normals but not color, or Marigold that handles one geometry modality at a time. The inpainting capability builds directly on RePaint's denoising-based approach, and the diffusion machinery (DDPM schedule, v-prediction, classifier-free guidance, zero-terminal-SNR) is inherited from the standard LDM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By learning a single joint appearance-geometry prior, Orchid eliminates the inconsistency and inefficiency of multi-model pipelines and enables inverse problems (e.g., 3D inpainting) that are intractable when appearance and geometry are sampled independently. The significantly improved depth-normal consistency (Table 4) and the fact that a joint model outperforms specialized normal predictors (Table 3) suggest that shared latent structure is genuinely informative for geometry. Open questions include multi-view consistency for 3D reconstruction, scaling beyond 512×512, and whether the joint prior transfers to video or dynamic scenes. The reliance on approximate camera intrinsics for 3DGS reconstruction and the absence of metric depth limit immediate deployment in robotics or AR.
