## Problem

Vision-Language Models consistently fail at physical reasoning tasks—stability assessment, collision prediction, and causal explanation—despite strong scene-description capabilities, because their training data lacks annotations of physical relationships. Repeatedly fine-tuning large or closed-source VLMs for each physical reasoning regime is expensive and impractical, creating a need for a modular, scalable mechanism to inject physical knowledge without modifying foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251108/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PCB receives an RGB image or a video (8 frames sampled for video inputs) and produces a question-agnostic physical scene description in one of two formats: Human-like Narration (natural-language sentences describing object properties, spatial relations, and events) or Structured Physics (frame-by-frame tagged observations with location, velocity, and energy values). This textual context is then concatenated with the original visual input and the user's text question, and fed to a foundation model (e.g., GPT-4o) which produces the final physically grounded answer. The PCB output is independent of the specific question, enabling one PCB to serve multiple downstream queries on the same scene.

## Method

Physics Context Builders are small VLMs (PaliGemma-3B) fine-tuned via LoRA on simulation-generated data to translate visual inputs into rich physical descriptions, effectively decoupling perception from reasoning. Training minimizes auto-regressive negative log-likelihood of the target descriptions conditioned on the input frames, jointly optimizing vision and language components. At inference, the PCB's generated context is prepended to the user query and passed to a larger foundation model for in-context physical reasoning, requiring no modification to the foundation model. A multi-agent triage layer (inspired by OpenAI Swarm) routes incoming queries to the appropriate specialized PCB based on the question-image pair. Simulation is used exclusively during training to produce precise physical annotations, eliminating the need for expensive simulators at inference while enabling successful Sim2Real transfer from synthetic to real-world scenes.

## Results

On CLEVRER, PCB-augmented GPT-4o (Human Narration) improves average accuracy by 11.1% over zero-shot, reaching 75.6% on descriptive questions (vs. 62.7%) and 41.6% on explanatory questions (vs. 30.7%). On Falling Tower, PCB-augmented GPT-4o-mini achieves the largest relative gain of 13.8% average accuracy, with tower stability jumping from 53.1% to 84.7% (+31.6%). Sim2Real transfer is effective: GPT-4o-PCB reaches 70.0% real-world tower stability (vs. 55.0% zero-shot), and the fine-tuned PaliGemma-3B PCB maintains 65.0% real-world tower stability. Human Narration consistently outperforms Structured Physics descriptions across all models and question types on CLEVRER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PCB-augmented models against three zero-shot CoT baselines: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, and Gemini 1.5 Pro. PCBs consistently outperform all three baselines across every question type on both CLEVRER and Falling Tower. The largest gains appear on explanatory and stability questions where zero-shot models are weakest: GPT-4o-mini's explanatory accuracy on CLEVRER jumps from 9.3% to 26.8% (+17.5 pp), and its tower stability on Falling Tower rises from 53.1% to 84.7% (+31.6 pp). Gemini 1.5 Pro benefits most on explanatory CLEVRER questions (+19.9 pp with HN), while GPT-4o shows the most consistent absolute gains across all categories. PaliGemma-3B-mix (the smallest baseline) is the weakest zero-shot performer (e.g., 38.9% descriptive on CLEVRER) and is used primarily as the base model for fine-tuning PCBs rather than as a direct comparison target. Notably, PCBs provide the largest relative improvements for smaller models (GPT-4o-mini: +11.8% avg on CLEVRER) compared to larger ones (GPT-4o: +11.1%), suggesting PCBs partially compensate for weaker visual perception in smaller VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the observation from Nagar et al. [31] that LLMs with textual scene descriptions outperform VLMs with visual embeddings for physical reasoning, by automating the generation of those descriptions through specialized fine-tuned VLMs (PCBs). It departs from simulation-in-the-loop approaches [29, 42, 43] that require expensive physics engines at inference time, instead using simulation solely to generate training annotations for smaller perception modules. The multi-agent PCB routing draws on OpenAI's Swarm architecture [7], while the overall modular philosophy aligns with chain-of-thought and multimodal CoT ideas [41, 47], but repurposes them as trainable, task-specific perception components rather than prompting strategies. Unlike PhysBench [10], which proposes augmenting VLMs with generic vision foundation models (SAM, Depth Anything), PCBs train dedicated simulators-informed VLMs that produce physics-specific textual context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCBs offer a practical, low-cost path to improving physical reasoning in closed-source foundation models (GPT-4o, Gemini) without any architectural modification, which is significant given the growing ecosystem of proprietary VLMs. The successful Sim2Real transfer from simulation-trained PCBs to real-world scenes is encouraging for deployment, though the current scope is limited to rigid-body dynamics and static stability. Key open questions include how to generate physical descriptions from unannotated real-world video, how to compose multiple PCBs for scenes involving multiple physical phenomena simultaneously, and why counterfactual and predictive reasoning see only modest gains (1.7–9.5%) despite large improvements on descriptive and explanatory tasks—suggesting that richer, possibly counterfactual-aware context descriptions are needed.
