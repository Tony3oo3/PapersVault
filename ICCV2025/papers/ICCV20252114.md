## Problem

Existing composed video retrieval (CoVR) benchmarks such as WebVid-CoVR rely on short, generic modification texts (average 4.6 words) that fail to capture subtle visual and temporal differences between video pairs, limiting fine-grained retrieval. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a dataset with dense, context-aware modification texts and a model architecture that better leverages such detailed descriptions for precise video retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: a reference video (represented by its single middle frame for efficiency), a dense video description averaging 81 words that captures both spatial and temporal content, and a dense modification text averaging 31 words that encodes specific changes relative to the reference. The output is a fused multimodal embedding compared via cosine similarity against target video embeddings in a database, with the top-k most similar videos returned as retrieval results. The modification text is deliberately structured to require contextual interpretation with the input video rather than serving as a standalone target description.

## Method

The architecture comprises three components: a ViT-L vision encoder processing the middle frame, a BLIP text encoder for the video description, and a BLIP-2 grounding text encoder that fuses all three modalities. Unlike prior pairwise fusion that processes (q,t), (d,t), and (q,d) separately, the proposed unified fusion first combines visual and description embeddings via a learnable weighted mean (α=0.36), then grounds this combined representation with the modification text through cross-attention, self-attention, and FFN layers within a single grounding encoder. This preserves fine-grained modification details that pairwise fusion dilutes. Training uses a symmetric contrastive loss (temperature τ=0.07, λ=1) with frozen vision and text encoders and a trainable grounding encoder, over 5 epochs with batch size 1024. The unified approach is also 3× faster than pairwise fusion since it avoids redundant processing of input components.

## Results

On the Dense-WebVid-CoVR test set, the proposed unified fusion model with Cross-Attention (CA) text fusion achieves 71.26% Recall@1, 89.12% Recall@5, 94.56% Recall@10, and 98.88% Recall@50 in the Visual+Text setting trained on Dense-WebVid-CoVR. This represents a 3.4% absolute gain over the previous state-of-the-art (Thawakar et al., 67.86% R@1) and an 8.1% gain over CoVR-BLIP (63.12% R@1). In the zero-training setting with CA fusion, the method still achieves 48.08% R@1, outperforming Thawakar et al. (39.20%) by 8.9%. On Ego-CVR (zero-shot), the method reaches 14.6% global R@1 and 44.8% local R@1, surpassing TFR-CVR (14.1% / 44.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Thawakar et al. [29], which uses a pairwise fusion scheme (processing q-t, d-t, and q-d separately). The proposed unified fusion consistently outperforms it: +3.4% R@1 when trained on Dense-WebVid-CoVR (71.26 vs 67.86) and +8.9% R@1 in the zero-training setting (48.08 vs 39.20), attributed to avoiding dilution of dense modification text details. Against CoVR-BLIP [30], the gap is even larger (+8.1% R@1 trained, +12.5% R@1 untrained). On Ego-CVR (zero-shot), the method edges out TFR-CVR [12] by 0.5% global R@1 (14.6 vs 14.1) and 0.6% local R@1 (44.8 vs 44.2), while substantially outperforming CIReVL [16] (2.0% global R@1). On CIRR (zero-shot), the method achieves 44.08% R@1, beating Thawakar et al. [29] (40.12%) and CoVR-BLIP [30] (38.48%), and on FashionIQ zero-shot it reaches 26.12% R@10 (Dress), surpassing Thawakar et al. (24.57%) and CoVR-BLIP (21.95%). The method wins all head-to-head comparisons across all four datasets and both trained/zero-shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composed video retrieval (CoVR) task introduced by Ventura et al. (WebVid-CoVR, AAAI 2024) by replacing its short, generic modification texts (avg. 4.6 words) with dense, context-rich descriptions (avg. 31.2 words) generated via Gemini-Pro and GPT-4o. Architecturally, it departs from the pairwise fusion scheme of Thawakar et al. (CVPR 2024), which processes each modality pair separately, in favor of a unified fusion strategy that integrates query video, description, and modification text within a single grounding text encoder using cross-attention. The broader CoIR literature (CIRR, FashionIQ) informs the evaluation protocol, but the core contribution is a denser video-domain benchmark paired with a simpler yet more effective fusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Dense-WebVid-CoVR dataset (1.6M triplets, 7× denser modification text, fully verified test set) provides the CoVR community with a substantially more challenging and realistic benchmark that better reflects real-world video editing and media production queries. The finding that text-only retrieval achieves 50% R@1 on this dataset raises an important open question about how much of the task is genuinely multimodal versus reducible to text-to-video retrieval, a concern the authors partially address through query structuring strategies. Remaining open questions include scaling to multilingual settings, handling very long videos, and whether the 2–3% annotation noise in the training set could be further reduced without prohibitive manual cost.
