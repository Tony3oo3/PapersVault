## Problem

Inverse rendering and 3D reconstruction in urban scenes are severely challenged by multi-illumination conditions (varying solar position, weather, season) and complex indirect light/shadow effects from dense building layouts, yet these effects on intrinsic decomposition consistency and reconstruction accuracy remain unexplored due to the absence of a suitable dataset. Existing urban datasets like MatrixCity control illumination only via light direction and intensity, while multi-illumination datasets focus on isolated objects or small gardens, leaving the urban-scale interaction between diverse sky conditions and indirect lighting unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes SceneCity Blender city assets (450+ object categories, 80 material properties), 300+ HDRI sky maps spanning dawn to night, and camera trajectories generated via uniform circular/grid sampling plus adaptive street-level and aerial views, calibrated with COLMAP. Rendering is performed at 1024×768 with up to 512 samples in Blender Cycles, producing per-pixel G-Buffer channels that are decomposed into albedo, shading (direct + indirect diffuse), glossy/transmission/emission residual, depth, normals, material attributes (roughness, metallic, specular), and semantic labels. The final dataset contains over 50K images (30K for intrinsic tasks, 20K for reconstruction) across five hierarchical block scales, with each camera view rendered under two randomly selected HDRI maps at four random rotations and ambient intensities.

## Method

LightCity is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) city-scale asset collection and block-level annotation using the SceneCity Blender add-on, (2) camera trajectory generation combining uniform sampling with adaptive street/aerial viewpoints and COLMAP-based pose filtering, and (3) PBR rendering via Blender Cycles where the G-Buffer is used to formally decompose the rendering equation into albedo A(x)=g_D(x), shading S(x)=g_D_direct(x)+g_D_indirect(x), and residual R(x) capturing glossy, transmission, background, and emission. The key differentiator is the use of 300+ HDRI sky maps with adjustable rotation and ambient intensity to model view-dependent sky textures and realistic indirect illumination, rather than the simple direction/intensity control used in prior synthetic datasets. The paper then benchmarks three tasks—single-view intrinsic decomposition (DMP, DPF, CDID, PIE-Net, IntrinsicAny), multi-view inverse rendering (NeRF-OSR, GS-IR), and multi-illumination urban reconstruction (NeRF-W, NeRF-OSR, Wild-gaussian, Gaussian-wild, NexusSplats)—revealing that current methods fail to maintain intrinsic consistency across illumination changes, struggle to disentangle indirect light in urban inverse rendering, and exhibit floating artifacts under multi-illumination in 3DGS-based reconstruction.

## Results

On the LightCity intrinsic decomposition benchmark, DMP mixed-finetuned with LightCity (H+L) achieves the best albedo si-PSNR of 22.80 and shading si-PSNR of 26.58, substantially outperforming DPF (18.94/21.09), CDID (15.80/20.25), and PIE-Net (15.49/16.15). For multi-illumination urban reconstruction, NeRF-W leads on simpler blocks (F2: 26.06 PSNR, F3: 29.07 PSNR) while Gaussian-wild excels on complex blocks (E1: 27.78, E2: 27.94 PSNR). A critical finding is that all methods suffer significant PSNR drops (3–6 dB) when evaluated on single-illumination test sets versus multi-illumination test sets, revealing poor generalization to unseen lighting conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For intrinsic decomposition on LightCity, DMP with LightCity mixed training (H+L) dominates, achieving 22.80 albedo si-PSNR vs. 18.94 for DPF (H+L), 16.82 for IntrinsicAny, and 15.49 for PIE-Net; the 6+ dB gap over non-diffusion methods highlights the benefit of diffusion priors combined with multi-illumination outdoor data. On the EDEN out-of-domain test, DPF (H+L) leads in albedo (13.11 si-PSNR) while DMP (H+L) leads in shading (11.54 si-PSNR), confirming that LightCity's diverse lighting transfers well. For urban reconstruction, NeRF-W outperforms all 3DGS-based methods (Gaussian-wild, Wild-gaussian, NexusSplats) on simpler blocks F2/F3 by 0.3–3.9 dB PSNR, but Gaussian-wild surpasses NeRF-W on complex blocks E1/E2 (27.78 vs. 26.61 and 27.94 vs. 28.71 PSNR), suggesting 3DGS better handles dense geometry at the cost of sensitivity to lighting inconsistencies. NeRF-OSR consistently underperforms both NeRF-W and 3DGS methods, with the worst single-illumination PSNR on E2 (13.53), indicating its image-based disentanglement struggles with shadow-albedo separation. In inverse rendering (Table 4), GS-IR achieves higher SSIM (0.630–0.700) than NeRF-OSR (0.583–0.641) for albedo estimation, but both methods entangle illumination with base color, confirming that current inverse rendering remains unsolved for urban-scale scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of synthetic urban scene generation and multi-illumination benchmarking. While prior urban datasets like MatrixCity control illumination only via light direction and intensity, and object-level inverse rendering datasets (OpenIllum, Stanford-ORB) or small-scale garden datasets (EDEN) capture richer lighting, none combine urban-scale spatial complexity with diverse HDRI-driven sky illumination and explicit indirect light/shadow decomposition. LightCity repurposes the SceneCity Blender add-on and Blender Cycles PBR pipeline to fill this gap, unifying three previously fragmented evaluation settings (intrinsic decomposition, multi-view inverse rendering, and novel view synthesis) under a single multi-illumination urban benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a critical gap: no prior resource captures the combined challenge of multi-illumination and complex indirect lighting at urban scale, which is essential for autonomous driving and digital twin applications. The benchmarking reveals that current intrinsic decomposition models lack illumination invariance, inverse rendering methods struggle to disentangle view-dependent indirect light from albedo, and 3DGS-based reconstruction methods produce floating artifacts under multi-illumination training. Open questions include whether illumination-invariant representations can be learned at urban scale, how to properly separate indirect light contributions in PBR-based inverse rendering, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap limits transferability of findings to real urban imagery.
