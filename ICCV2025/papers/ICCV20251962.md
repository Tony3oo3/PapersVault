## Problem

Existing plug-and-play methods that use latent diffusion models as priors must repeatedly decode latents into pixel space to compute the likelihood score, then re-encode back, incurring high computational cost and introducing artifacts from differentiating through the autoencoder decoder on out-of-distribution latents. This paper asks whether the entire plug-and-play restoration loop can be performed in the latent space alone, eliminating the autoencoder from the iterative sampling process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251962/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 512×512 RGB measurement y (Gaussian blur, ×4/×8 bicubic downsampling, 256-pixel box inpainting, or JPEG q=10, each with additive Gaussian noise σ_y=0.01 or 0.03), a pretrained LDM (SD-v1.5 or RealisticVision-v5.1), and a per-degradation trained latent operator H_θ. The encoder is applied exactly once to y at the start, and the decoder exactly once to the final latent at the end; all intermediate diffusion steps operate purely on latents. The output is a single restored 512×512 image.

## Method

SILO introduces a 'latent plug-and-play' paradigm by learning a latent degradation operator H_θ that emulates the pixel-space degradation A entirely in the latent domain, trained with the supervised loss E[‖H_θ(E(x)) − E(y)‖] over clean/degraded image pairs. During inference, the method follows the DPS sampling algorithm in latent space: at each reverse-diffusion step it computes the Tweedie denoised latent ẑ₀ᵗ, then updates the latent by subtracting a gradient step proportional to ∇_{z_t}‖E(y) − H_θ(ẑ₀ᵗ)‖², which serves as a Lipschitz-bounded proxy for the pixel-space data-fidelity term. Because gradients flow only through the denoiser and H_θ—never through the autoencoder—the method avoids the noisy Jacobian and out-of-distribution decoder issues that plague prior latent P&P methods, yielding 2.6–10× faster inference and improved perceptual quality (e.g., KID 5.23 vs. 12.94 on ×8 SR).

## Results

On FFHQ, SILO achieves a KID of 5.23 versus 12.94 for the best prior method on ×8 super-resolution, while also improving LPIPS distortion across all tested degradations (Gaussian blur, ×4/×8 SR, inpainting, JPEG). SILO is 2.6–10× faster in inference compared to prior latent plug-and-play methods, and remains 1.7–7× faster even when including the one-time training cost of the latent degradation operator. Similar gains in perceptual quality (KID, FID) and distortion (LPIPS) are observed on COCO natural images for inpainting and ×4 SR tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SILO is compared against four prior latent plug-and-play methods: LDPS and ReSample (Song et al., ICLR 2023) and GML-DPS and PSLD (Rout et al., 2023). SILO outperforms all four baselines on both perceptual quality (KID, FID) and distortion (LPIPS) across every degradation tested on FFHQ and COCO. The most dramatic gap appears in ×8 super-resolution, where SILO's KID of 5.23 nearly halves the 12.94 achieved by the best competing method. In terms of runtime, SILO is 2.6× faster than the naive LDPS baseline and up to 10× faster than ReSample, which requires solving an optimization sub-problem at each diffusion step. PSLD, which relies on an orthogonal projection assuming linear degradations, cannot handle non-linear degradations such as JPEG compression, whereas SILO handles all tested degradations uniformly. Even after amortizing the training time of the latent operator H_θ, SILO retains a 1.7× speedup over LDPS and 7× over ReSample.

## Relation to Prior Work

SILO adapts the DPS plug-and-play framework (Chung et al., ICLR 2023) from pixel space to the latent space of LDMs (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), but departs fundamentally from all prior latent plug-and-play methods (LDPS, GML-DPS, PSLD, ReSample, STSL) that repeatedly decode latents to pixel space to apply degradations and re-encode. Rather than patching the pixel-latent transition with projection steps, optimization sub-problems, or text regularization, SILO introduces a 'latent plug-and-play' paradigm in which a short-trained operator H_θ emulates the degradation entirely in latent space, eliminating the autoencoder from the restoration loop. This reframes the core difficulty: instead of making the pixel-latent round-trip work, it removes the round-trip altogether.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the autoencoder from the iterative restoration loop, SILO achieves a 2.6–10× inference speedup and 1.7–7× total speedup (including operator training) while simultaneously improving perceptual quality (e.g., KID 5.23 vs. 12.94 for ×8 SR), addressing two long-standing pain points in latent diffusion inverse solvers. The main limitation is that the method relies on the VAE encoder handling degraded measurements gracefully, which holds for common degradations (blur, downsampling, inpainting, JPEG) but may fail for more exotic measurements like phase retrieval. Open directions include using alternative encoders for w, designing H_θ as a parametric family conditioned on degradation parameters for blind restoration, and extending the latent plug-and-play paradigm beyond the specific VAE latent space.
