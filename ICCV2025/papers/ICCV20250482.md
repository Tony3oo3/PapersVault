## Problem

Prototype learning methods for few-shot segmentation produce conservative predictions (low false positives but high false negatives), while affinity learning methods produce aggressive predictions (low false negatives but high false positives due to foreground-background mismatches). No prior work has explicitly exploited this complementary conservatism-aggressiveness trade-off to improve segmentation accuracy on unseen classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250482/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a k-shot support set (k RGB images with binary pixel-wise masks) and a single query RGB image, all from the same unseen object category, and outputs a binary segmentation mask for the query image. The support mask is consumed directly by the PFE and ASC modules to compute class weights and identify certain FG-BG mismatches, while the prototype predictor's soft predictions (values in [0,1]) are injected at every attention block of the affinity learner. The framework is demonstrated under 1-shot and 5-shot episodic settings on PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i.

## Method

PAHNet pairs a frozen pre-trained prototype predictor (SSP) with a trainable affinity learner (SCCAN or HDMNet), inserting two modules into each attention block. The Prototype-guided Feature Enhancement (PFE) module, placed before self-attention, constructs an adaptive prototype by fusing masked-average-pooled features from both the conservative prototype predictions and the aggressive affinity predictions, then concatenates this prototype with support and query features via 1×1 convolutions to strengthen FG discriminability. The Attention Score Calibration (ASC) module, placed within cross-attention, builds a re-weighting matrix from the prototype predictor's soft predictions and the support mask to down-weight potential FG-BG correlations, and additionally applies a hard mask (setting scores to −∞) for pixel pairs where the prototype predictor is highly confident of a mismatch. This dual mechanism simultaneously enhances legitimate FG-FG matching while suppressing the aggressive over-activation of background regions that plagues pure affinity learners.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i, HDMNet+PAHNet achieves 71.5% mean mIoU (1-shot) and 76.1% mean mIoU (5-shot), with FB-IoU of 82.4% and 85.7% respectively. On COCO-20^i, the same configuration reaches 52.3% mean mIoU (1-shot) and 59.2% mean mIoU (5-shot), with FB-IoU of 75.2% and 78.1%. These are the best results reported on both benchmarks across all compared methods in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAHNet (as HDMNet+PAHNet) outperforms all 10 compared baselines on both PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The closest competitor is HMNet [40] (NeurIPS'24), which PAHNet surpasses by 1.1% mIoU on PASCAL-5^i 1-shot (71.5 vs. 70.4) and by 2.0% on 5-shot (76.1 vs. 74.1); on COCO-20^i the margins are smaller (0.2% and 0.3% respectively). AENet [41] (ECCV'24) is the second-closest baseline, beaten by 1.2% (PASCAL 1-shot) and 1.9% (PASCAL 5-shot), and by 1.0% and 2.1% on COCO. ABCBNet [50] (CVPR'24) trails by 0.9% on PASCAL 1-shot and by 4.1% on COCO 5-shot. The two affinity-learning baselines that PAHNet is directly integrated with—SCCAN [39] and HDMNet [26]—see the largest absolute gains from adding PAHNet: +4.8%/+5.0% mIoU for SCCAN and +2.1%/+4.3% for HDMNet on PASCAL-5^i (1-shot/5-shot), confirming that the hybrid framework's benefit scales with the aggressiveness of the underlying affinity learner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established few-shot segmentation paradigms—prototype learning (e.g., SSP) and affinity learning (e.g., SCCAN, HDMNet)—and identifies a systematic, complementary error pattern between them: prototype methods are conservative (high FN, low FP) while affinity methods are aggressive (high FP, low FN). Rather than proposing a new paradigm, PAHNet repurposes a frozen prototype predictor to calibrate an affinity learner through two lightweight modules (PFE and ASC) inserted into each attention block. This departs from prior hybrid or ensemble approaches by operating at the feature and attention-score level within a single forward pass, and by explicitly framing the design goal as balancing conservatism against aggressiveness rather than simply combining predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conservative/aggressive diagnostic provides a useful lens for understanding why different FSS architectures fail in characteristic ways, and the modular PFE+ASC design can be plugged into any attention-based affinity learner without architectural changes. However, the approach depends on a pre-trained prototype predictor (SSP) whose quality and conservatism are not independently tuned, and the threshold parameters (γ_fg, γ_bg) are fixed heuristically. Open questions include whether the balance principle generalizes to cross-domain or zero-shot segmentation, whether the specific choice of prototype predictor matters beyond its conservatism, and whether the FG-BG mismatch suppression transfers to denser or multi-class settings.
