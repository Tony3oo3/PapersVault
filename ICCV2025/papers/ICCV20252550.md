## Problem

Existing multimodal medical image fusion methods assume high-quality, pixel-aligned source images, yet real clinical data frequently suffers from degradation (motion artifacts, metal artifacts, noise) and spatial misalignment. Conventional staged pipelines that chain separate restoration, registration, and fusion models lack inter-task collaboration, leading to suboptimal downstream performance and high computational overhead. No prior work addresses all three challenges—degradation, misalignment, and fusion—within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D image pair at 160×160×160 resolution: a degraded, spatially misaligned source image (e.g., motion-corrupted MRI-T2, metal-artifact CT, or noisy low-dose PET) and a high-quality reference image from a different modality. It produces a single fused 3D volume that is simultaneously restored, aligned, and fused, while internally predicting a dense deformation field and a degradation-type classification. The framework is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining cross-entropy (degradation classification), contrastive (modality alignment), L2 (deformation field), and fusion losses (SSIM, intensity, gradient, CrCb).

## Method

UniFuse integrates four modules in a single forward pass. First, the Degradation-Aware Prompt Learning (DAPL) module encodes both input images through a shared CNN encoder and two sequential Mamba blocks that process features in forward, reverse, and bidirectional orders, yielding three hidden states that capture multi-directional degradation information; these states drive a classification head and generate a shared degradation prompt via a Hadamard product with a learned prompt set. Second, the Omni Unified Feature Representation (OUFR) module feeds the patchified features of both modalities together with the three hidden states into Spatial Mamba blocks, using a contrastive loss to suppress modality-specific feature divergence. Third, the Feature Alignment (FA) module predicts a deformation field through stacked RegBLKs conditioned on the degradation prompt. Fourth, the Universal Feature Restoration & Fusion (UFR&F) module is a U-shaped network whose skip connections embed an Adaptive LoRA Synergistic Network (ALSN): a base network plus multiple low-rank LoRA branches, where each branch's contribution is weighted by the degradation classification probability, enabling task-adaptive restoration and fusion without substantially increasing parameter count (68% reduction vs. a multi-expert alternative).

## Results

On BraTS2020 (MRI-T1 + motion-degraded MRI-T2), UniFuse achieves Q_psnr=23.07 dB, Q_cc=0.908, Q_ssim=1.529, and Q_vif=1.916, outperforming the best multi-stage baseline (M+RMRFus: 17.47 dB, 0.884, 0.612, 1.016) by 5.6 dB in PSNR and 2.5× in SSIM. On SynthRAD2023 (MRI + metal-artifact CT), it reaches Q_psnr=20.35 dB and Q_ssim=1.241, and on FDG-PET/CT (CT + noisy PET), Q_psnr=23.01 dB and Q_cc=0.871. Critically, UniFuse requires only 395 G FLOPs versus 13,061–190,071 G for the multi-stage baselines, a 33–481× reduction in computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All ten multi-stage baselines (combining a restoration model, CorrMLP registration, and a fusion network) are outperformed by UniFuse on every metric across all three datasets. The strongest fusion backbones (RMRFus, ALMFnet) paired with the best restoration (MC-CDic or AMIR) still trail UniFuse by 4–6 dB in PSNR and by 0.3–1.0 in SSIM on BraTS2020; the gap widens on SynthRAD2023 where metal artifacts degrade baselines most (e.g., A+BSAFus drops to Q_cc=0.693 vs. UniFuse's 0.812). The All-in-One restoration method AMIR consistently underperforms task-specific restorers (MC-CDic, OSCNet, CDAG) in the multi-stage pipelines, confirming that decoupled restoration introduces error propagation. The most dramatic advantage is computational: UniFuse's single-stage design requires 395 G FLOPs, compared to 31,920–190,071 G for the staged pipelines, because it eliminates the separate registration and restoration passes entirely. No baseline matches or exceeds UniFuse on any reported metric in any dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant staged paradigm in medical image fusion—where restoration, registration, and fusion are handled by independently trained models—by unifying all three into a single end-to-end framework. Conceptually, it repurposes prompt learning (from NLP and restoration literature) to encode degradation type information that simultaneously guides both cross-modal alignment and feature restoration, and adapts LoRA (originally for parameter-efficient fine-tuning) as a multi-branch mechanism for degradation-specific feature processing within a U-shaped fusion network. The use of Spatial Mamba for cross-modal feature representation draws on the broader state-space model trend in vision, but applies it specifically to mitigate modality differences in the alignment stage rather than for general feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real clinical imaging pipelines routinely produce degraded and misaligned multi-modal pairs, yet virtually all existing fusion methods assume clean, registered inputs. By demonstrating that a single model can handle motion artifacts, metal artifacts, and PET noise alongside spatial misalignment—with dramatically lower FLOPs (395G vs. 13K–190K G for multi-stage pipelines)—the paper offers a deployable alternative to chaining separate restoration, registration, and fusion models. Open questions include generalization to unseen degradation types, validation on larger or more diverse clinical datasets, and whether the unified optimization truly outperforms a well-tuned staged pipeline with shared representations. The paper's own future work acknowledges the need for improved adaptability and efficiency for wider clinical use.
