## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting enables real-time novel view synthesis but produces severe artifacts (holes, oversized Gaussians, spiky surfaces, floaters) when rendering from viewpoints that deviate from the training trajectory, preventing free scene exploration. Existing diffusion-based extrapolation methods assume known evaluation viewpoints and lack multi-view consistency, while depth-regularized 3DGS variants cannot fill genuinely missing information. The paper tackles how to reconstruct a complete, explorable scene within a bounded region from only partially covering input views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251891/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of RGB images with known camera poses that only partially cover the target scene, and produces a fully optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting scene renderable in real-time from any viewpoint within a bounding box derived from the input views. The bounding box constrains reconstruction to avoid unconstrained content generation beyond observed data. The video diffusion model additionally consumes a scene text description and the nearest training image as cross-attention conditioning when generating pseudo-observations.

## Method

ExploreGS operates in three stages: (1) initial 3DGS optimization plus a rasterization-based occupancy grid that classifies voxels as occupied or free using top-3 transmittance visibility scores; (2) information-gain-driven virtual camera trajectory sampling via best-first search over 14 motion primitives (6 translations, 4 rotations, 4 orbit rotations), where information gain is measured by a shared 2D map tracking which Gaussians have been observed from which of D=32 discretized viewing directions, followed by a DynamiCrafter-based video diffusion model that enhances degraded renderings into pseudo-ground-truth images using next-frame prediction conditioned on the nearest training view and scene text; (3) confidence-aware fine-tuning that introduces G-IOU (intersection-over-union of visible Gaussian sets between a virtual view and its nearest training view) as image-level confidence and pixel-aligned LPIPS as pixel-level confidence, weighting the virtual-view loss to protect well-reconstructed regions from diffusion model inaccuracies.

## Results

On Wild-Explore, ExploreGS achieves 16.96 PSNR, 0.534 SSIM, and 0.413 LPIPS, outperforming all 3DGS-based baselines on PSNR and SSIM while matching the best LPIPS (tied with 3DGS+Depth at 0.413). On Curated Nerfbusters, it reaches 16.29 PSNR, 0.479 SSIM, and 0.434 LPIPS, again leading on all three metrics. The upper bound (3DGS trained on both train and test splits) reaches 30.37 PSNR on Wild-Explore and 29.34 on Curated Nerfbusters, indicating substantial headroom but confirming that ExploreGS closes the gap more effectively than any baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExploreGS outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and SSIM across both datasets. On Wild-Explore, it gains +1.14 dB PSNR over the closest competitor ViewExtrapolator (16.96 vs. 16.02) and +1.15 dB over 3DGS (16.96 vs. 15.81); on Curated Nerfbusters, the margin over ViewExtrapolator widens to +1.31 dB (16.29 vs. 14.98). For LPIPS, ExploreGS ties 3DGS+Depth on Wild-Explore (0.413) and edges it out on Curated Nerfbusters (0.434 vs. 0.436), while DNGaussians and ViewExtrapolator show notably worse perceptual quality (0.450 and 0.442 on Wild-Explore, respectively). The depth-regularized variants (3DGS+Depth, DNGaussians) fail to close the gap because they cannot synthesize missing content, and ViewExtrapolator's heuristic single-camera sampling limits its viewpoint coverage. Overall, ExploreGS is the only method that simultaneously improves geometric fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) and perceptual quality (LPIPS) over all baselines on both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExploreGS sits at the intersection of two active research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time novel view synthesis and diffusion-prior-based enhancement of degraded renderings. It builds directly on 3DGS-Enhancer [21], which fine-tunes a video diffusion model to clean up 3DGS renderings, but departs from it in two key ways: (1) it replaces the frame-interpolation setup with next-frame prediction conditioned on scene text and the nearest training view, and (2) it introduces an information-gain-driven virtual camera sampling strategy rather than assuming evaluation viewpoints are known, as in ViewExtrapolator [19] and ExtraNeRF [33]. The sampling strategy draws on active-learning and next-best-view literature (FisherRF [9], AutoNeRF [22]) but adapts it to the 3DGS representation via a Gaussian-visibility coverage map, and the confidence-aware fine-tuning replaces distance-based weighting with a G-IOU metric that accounts for rotational viewpoint changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: 3DGS-based reconstructions degrade severely outside training trajectories, limiting real-world use in AR/VR where users expect free exploration. The Wild-Explore benchmark and curated Nerfbusters split provide a more realistic evaluation protocol than existing datasets, which mostly test interpolation. However, the quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (ViewExtrapolator) are modest (~1 dB PSNR), and the method inherits the quality ceiling of the underlying video diffusion model (DynamiCrafter). Open questions include whether stronger diffusion backbones would close the remaining gap, whether the bounding-box constraint can be relaxed for large-scale scenes, and whether the compute cost of generating and fine-tuning on virtual views scales acceptably for production use.
