## Problem

Existing image captioning models generate 'average' captions and lack fine-grained control over language patterns such as length, descriptiveness, and vocabulary specificity. Prior controllable captioning works rely on discrete cluster indices that only allow abrupt jumps between states, are restricted to a single property (length) or a single dataset, and cannot represent intermediate states between clusters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251470/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and three continuous conditioning scalars in [0,1] (length, descriptiveness, uniqueness) as input, producing a single free-form image caption. At inference, users can either specify the scalars directly or provide an example sentence from which the Condition Calculator derives the corresponding values. The underlying architecture is LLaVA (CLIP ViT-L vision encoder + LLaMA-2 7B decoder), so standard image resolutions apply.

## Method

CaptionSmiths introduces two modules: a Condition Calculator that automatically quantifies each caption's length (token count), descriptiveness (ratio of adjectives/nouns excluding non-descriptive words), and vocabulary uniqueness (mean inverse word frequency across the dataset) into normalized [0,1] scalars, with an optional decorrelation step via linear regression; and a Condition Encoding that maps each scalar to a token embedding by linearly interpolating between two learnable endpoint vectors representing extreme states (e.g., very short vs. very long). This continuous interpolation—equivalent to a single linear layer in the token parameter space—replaces discrete cluster indices, adding only 2×d parameters per property while enabling slider-like smooth control. The conditioning embeddings are prepended to the caption in the LLaVA autoregressive training loop, so the model learns to modulate its output language pattern based on the interpolated condition vectors across a mixed 1.3M-pair dataset.

## Results

On the mixed-dataset evaluation (Table 1), CaptionSmiths (7.1B) achieves CIDEr of 104.8 on MSCOCO, 37.4 on LN COCO, and 29.7 on Docci, substantially outperforming the unconditioned Vanilla baseline (13.2, 21.7, 7.5) and the dataset-prompted Concap (95.9, 23.5, 8.3). In self-retrieval on COCO (Table 2), CaptionSmiths reaches R@1 = 36.9 and ClipScore = 78.9, surpassing even ground-truth captions (R@1 = 32.6, ClipScore = 76.9). On the single-dataset COCO analysis (Table 3), CaptionSmiths attains CIDEr 112.6 and BLEU@4 12.5, outperforming the length-controllable baseline LC (CIDEr 103.6, BLEU@4 11.8) while reducing length mismatch from 13.8 to 1.6 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unconditioned Vanilla model trained on the same mixed data, CaptionSmiths improves CIDEr by 8× on MSCOCO (104.8 vs 13.2), 1.7× on LN COCO (37.4 vs 21.7), and 4× on Docci (29.7 vs 7.5), confirming that target-aware conditioning is essential for cross-dataset generalization. Compared to Concap (dataset-specific prompts), CaptionSmiths wins decisively on LN COCO (CIDEr 37.4 vs 23.5) and Docci (29.7 vs 8.3) while roughly matching on MSCOCO (104.8 vs 95.9), showing that continuous property conditioning generalizes better than discrete dataset labels. Against specialized 21.3B models fine-tuned per dataset, the single 7.1B CaptionSmiths is competitive on MSCOCO (CIDEr 104.8 vs 98.3) and substantially better on Docci (29.7 vs 9.1), demonstrating that one model can replace three. Open-source VLMs (LLaVA-1.5, Blip-3, Qwen2-VL) with concise/detailed prompting lag far behind, with CIDEr below 85 on MSCOCO and below 6 on LN COCO/Docci. In the controlled COCO-only comparison (Table 3), CaptionSmiths outperforms the length-controllable baseline LC on all four metrics (CIDEr 112.6 vs 103.6, BLEU@4 12.5 vs 11.8), and reduces length mismatch from 13.8 to 1.6 tokens, validating the advantage of continuous interpolation over discrete cluster conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L + LLaMA-2 7B) and repurposes the linear interpolation principle from model soup [41] into the token embedding space for conditioning. It departs from prior controllable captioning work (Deng et al.'s length-controllable captioning, Concap's dataset-aware prompting, Chen et al.'s sentence-embedding clustering) by replacing discrete cluster indices with continuous scalar values and extending control from a single property (length) to three simultaneously (length, descriptiveness, uniqueness). The key conceptual shift is from 'which bucket does this caption fall into' to 'where on a slider between two extremes does this caption sit,' enabling smooth intermediate states that discrete methods cannot represent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical, parameter-efficient (only 2d new parameters per property) way to deploy a single captioning model across diverse applications without retraining, which is valuable for accessibility tools and content generation pipelines. The finding that a two-endpoint continuous interpolation outperforms a 100-cluster discrete approach on both control precision and lexical alignment challenges the intuition that finer discretization is always better. Open questions include whether the three chosen properties are the most useful axes of control, how the method handles hallucination in long captions (acknowledged in the appendix), and whether the approach generalizes to additional properties like structural coherence or image-content alignment.
