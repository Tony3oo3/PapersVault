## Problem

Existing listener behavior generation methods predict low-dimensional motion codes (3DMM parameters, VQ-VAE tokens) and then render photorealistic faces, inevitably losing fine-grained detail and expressive richness. They also restrict listener responses to limited reactive motions in short temporal windows, failing to capture the nuanced, well-timed, and diverse nonverbal feedback that characterizes natural human listening. The paper asks whether an end-to-end video diffusion model can directly synthesize high-fidelity, controllable listener portraits in pixel space while maintaining temporal coherence with the speaker.

## Input / Output

The model consumes speaker audio (Wav2Vec2-base intermediate features from all 12 layers, resampled to 12 FPS), speaker facial motion (EMOCA 3DMM coefficients at 12 FPS), a single listener reference frame (encoded into diffusion latent space via SliceVAE), and an optional free-form text prompt (encoded via T5 and BERT, truncated at 512 tokens). Output is a photorealistic listener head video at 256×256 resolution, generated in 4-second clips of 144 frames. For longer sequences, DiTaiListener-Edit refines 6-frame overlap regions between consecutive 49-frame segments to produce seamless transitions, enabling unbounded-length listener videos.

## Method

DiTaiListener builds on a pre-trained DiT video diffusion backbone (EasyAnimateV5.1-12b) and introduces the Causal Temporal Multimodal Adapter (CTM-Adapter), which augments each MM-DiT Full Attention block with two Temporal Causal Attention (TCA) modules that process speech and motion features separately using causal masks so that listener frames at time t attend only to speaker cues at times ≤ t. The three attention branches—speech-guided, text-video (MM-DiT), and motion-guided—are combined via learned weighted summation (α, β, γ), enabling the model to adaptively time reactions (e.g., immediate smile mimicry vs. delayed head nods). For long-form generation, DiTaiListener-Edit is a separate video-to-video diffusion model that takes the last K′ frames of one segment and the first K′ frames of the next, along with the listener identity frame, and reconstructs temporally smooth bridging frames. Text control is achieved by training on Gemini 1.5-generated affective behavior descriptions, allowing users to specify listener emotions or dialog context at inference time.

## Results

On the RealTalk test set, DiTaiListener achieves an FID of 7.99, a 73.8% improvement over the strongest baseline DIM (30.75), and an FVD of 45.42 versus DIM's 204.49. On ViCo, the method reaches FID 10.14 and FVD 53.54, again substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline DIM: FID 49.75, FVD 288.27). In the 3DMM (EMOCA) parameter space on RealTalk, DiTaiListener achieves FD-Exp of 14.28 and FD-Pose of 0.02, competitive with or better than methods explicitly trained in that space. User studies with 50 participants confirm DiTaiListener is the clear preference (64.03% overall) over all baselines combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTaiListener decisively outperforms all four pixel-space baselines on both RealTalk and ViCo. Against DIM (the strongest prior method), DiTaiListener reduces FID by 73.8% on RealTalk (7.99 vs. 30.75) and 79.5% on ViCo (10.14 vs. 49.75), with FVD dropping from 204.49 to 45.42 on RealTalk. L2L and RLHG are further behind, with FIDs of 56.43 and 30.55 respectively on RealTalk, reflecting their reliance on VQ-VAE motion codes and PIRenderer-based rendering that limits photorealism. ELP (evaluated only on ViCo) achieves FID 67.12, the worst among pixel-space baselines. INFP, a diffusion-based method evaluated only in 3DMM space on ViCo, reports FD-Exp of 18.63, which DiTaiListener improves upon (17.49) despite not being trained in that parameter space. In user studies, DiTaiListener receives 72.17% preference for feedback correctness, 65.64% for diversity, and 54.29% for smoothness, far exceeding the next-best method (DIM at 16.11% smoothness).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTaiListener departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in listener behavior generation (predict motion codes in 3DMM/VQ-VAE space, then render via PIRenderer) by directly synthesizing photorealistic listener video in pixel space through a diffusion transformer. Architecturally, it builds on EasyAnimate's MM-DiT backbone, adopts the causal masking strategy from L2L for temporally grounded speaker-to-listener alignment, and leverages the Decoupled Multimodal Attention mechanism from Esser et al. to inject speech and motion features. The novel CTM-Adapter extends IP-Adapter-style spatial conditioning into a temporal causal attention setting, and DiTaiListener-Edit introduces a segment-smoothing approach to long video generation that avoids the error accumulation of teacher forcing and the blurring of prompt traveling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end diffusion framework for listener response generation, eliminating the information bottleneck of intermediate motion representations and enabling text-controlled, diverse listener behaviors that were infeasible with discrete motion codes. The dramatic FID gap over two-stage baselines (10.14 vs. ~50 on RealTalk) validates that pixel-space diffusion can capture nuanced, data-driven listener behaviors (natural blinks, subtle expressions) that motion-code pipelines inherently lose. Key limitations remain: 256×256 resolution at 12 FPS, inference efficiency for real-time use, and reliance on Gemini-generated text annotations for training text control. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-body or multi-person listener scenarios and how to achieve real-time inference for interactive applications.
