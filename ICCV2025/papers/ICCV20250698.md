## Problem

Existing self-supervised video alignment methods (e.g., VAVA) rely on Kantorovich optimal transport with an optimality prior, which struggles with order variations, background/redundant frames, and repeated actions. Meanwhile, although both video alignment and action segmentation require fine-grained temporal understanding, their interaction in a multi-task learning setup has never been explored, and prior work trains them as entirely separate models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For video alignment, the method takes a pair of video clips (N and M frames) and produces a soft frame-to-frame assignment matrix T* ∈ R^{N×M}, where the correspondence for frame x_i is given by argmax_j T*_ij. For action segmentation, a single video clip is mapped to per-frame action labels via K learnable centroids. Frame embeddings are extracted by a shared ResNet-50 encoder (alignment) or MLP encoder (segmentation), and a virtual frame is appended to each video to explicitly absorb background/redundant content before computing losses.

## Method

The core contribution is a fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) optimal transport formulation with a structural prior for video alignment (VAOT): the KOT term captures visual similarity between frame embeddings, while the GW term with distance matrices C^x, C^y (encoding temporal proximity within a radius r) enforces temporal consistency on the transport map, handling order variations and repeated actions in a single global alignment. The solution is obtained efficiently via projected mirror descent with entropy regularization, converging in fewer than 25 iterations at O(NM) per iteration on GPUs. For the joint model (VASOT), the VAOT module is combined with the ASOT action segmentation module under a unified optimal transport framework sharing a single encoder, trained with a weighted multi-task loss L_joint = w_align·L_xy + w_seg·(L_xa + L_ya). A key empirical finding is that video alignment substantially boosts action segmentation performance, while the reverse effect is negligible, justifying the asymmetric benefit of the multi-task setup.

## Results

On video alignment, VAOT achieves 94.63% Acc@1.0 and 91.63% Progress on Pouring, 86.92% Acc@1.0 and 84.31% Progress on Penn Action, and 38.64% Acc@1.0 on IKEA ASM, outperforming the closest competitor VAVA by 2.2, 2.4, and 9.5 points respectively. On action segmentation, the joint VASOT model achieves 70.9/75.1/49.3 MoF/F1/mIoU on Desktop Assembly and 60.6/57.4/34.5 on 50 Salads (Eval), surpassing the single-task ASOT baseline by up to 7.1 points in F1 and 4.4 points in mIoU. The multi-task VASOT model matches VAOT on video alignment while improving action segmentation, confirming that video alignment boosts segmentation but not vice versa.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On video alignment, VAOT outperforms all six baselines (SAL, TCN, TCC, LAV, VAVA, GTCC) across all three datasets. The largest margin is on IKEA ASM where VAOT achieves 38.64% Acc@1.0 versus VAVA's 29.10% (+9.5 pts) and LAV's 25.54% (+13.1 pts), demonstrating the advantage of the fused Gromov-Wasserstein formulation over both dynamic time warping and Kantorovich optimal transport. On Pouring, VAOT leads VAVA by 2.2 pts in Acc@1.0 and 8.0 pts in Progress; GTCC reports competitive τ (88.10 vs 88.28) but lacks AP metrics. On action segmentation, VASOT consistently beats all eight baselines (CTE, VTE, UDE, ASAL, TOT, UFSA, ASOT, HVQ) on every dataset and metric. The most notable gains are over ASOT (the single-task baseline): +7.1 F1 and +4.4 mIoU on 50 Salads (Eval), and +7.1 F1 and +3.4 mIoU on Desktop Assembly, confirming that the joint video alignment signal substantially improves segmentation. VASOT also surpasses HVQ by 3.1 MoF and 0.7 F1 on Breakfast, and UFSA(T) by 5.5 MoF and 6.8 F1 on 50 Salads (Eval).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) optimal transport formulation with structural priors from ASOT (action segmentation) to the video alignment problem, replacing VAVA's Kantorovich-based approach with a formulation that handles order variations, background frames, and repeated actions in a single global alignment framework. It then unifies the resulting VAOT module with ASOT into a joint multi-task model (VASOT), which is the first to combine video alignment and action segmentation under a single optimal transport objective. The virtual-frame mechanism for background handling and the temporal prior R are inherited from VAVA, while the entropy-regularized projected mirror descent solver follows the standard FGW literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical benefit of training and storing a single model for both video alignment and action segmentation reduces deployment cost, and the asymmetric finding—that video alignment substantially boosts action segmentation while the reverse effect is negligible—provides useful guidance for multi-task design in temporal video understanding. However, the video alignment gains over VAVA, while consistent, are moderate (e.g., ~4.6 points on IKEA ASM), and the action segmentation improvements over the ASOT single-task baseline are small on some datasets. Open questions include whether the unified framework scales to longer videos or more complex multi-phase activities, and whether the fixed equal weighting (w_align = w_seg = 1) is optimal across all settings.
