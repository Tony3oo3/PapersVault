## Problem

Applying 3D Gaussian Splatting to large-scale aerial urban scenes suffers from excessive memory consumption, slow training, prolonged partitioning (e.g., CityGS requires ~2 hours), and significant rendering quality degradation. Existing block-based methods train redundant regions around block boundaries to avoid artifacts, wasting computational resources, and lack an efficient Level-of-Detail representation that adapts scene complexity to viewing distance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a COLMAP sparse point cloud, aerial RGB images (downsampled 4×, 1.6k resolution for MatrixCity), and camera poses as input. The scene is uniformly partitioned into blocks, and for each block, visibility masks are generated by reprojecting sparse 3D points into image space to determine which views contribute to that block's training (threshold τ_p=800 visible points). The output is a hierarchical octree-structured set of neural Gaussians per block, merged into a global scene that supports real-time LOD rendering via a multi-MLP rasterization pipeline.

## Method

HUG combines three key innovations: (1) a visibility-based partitioning strategy that assigns views to blocks by counting reprojected sparse points and generates per-block visibility masks, completing in ~1 minute versus ~2 hours for CityGS; (2) a hierarchical neural Gaussian representation built on an octree of anchors (each anchor generates multiple Gaussians via a small MLP), where anchor selection per view is distance-based with predicted LOD levels; and (3) a hierarchical weighted image supervision loss that renders the full scene plus per-level sub-images with geometrically decreasing L1/SSIM weights (λ/2^{K+1-k}, γ/2^k) and an opacity mask for higher levels, ensuring all anchor levels remain actively optimized. Additional mechanisms include a dynamic gradient threshold that decays over training (τ_g·η^{⌊i/M⌋}), a dynamic anchor level transition that promotes anchors with persistently high gradients to finer LOD levels, visibility-based anchor pruning, and a greedy vote-based refiltering strategy for seamless multi-block scene fusion.

## Results

HUG achieves state-of-the-art results on all five benchmarks (MatrixCity, Residence, Rubble, Building, Sci-Art), attaining the best SSIM and LPIPS on every scene. On MatrixCity, HUG reaches 28.02 PSNR / 0.883 SSIM / 0.142 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest baseline CityGS (27.46 / 0.865 / 0.204) by 0.56 dB in PSNR. The only exception is PSNR on Sci-Art, where Mega-NeRF scores 25.60 vs. HUG's 21.83, which the authors attribute to the large number of distant views in that scene. HUG also outperforms Octree-GS by over 1.5 dB PSNR on MatrixCity, demonstrating the benefit of the block-based reconstruction pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUG outperforms all six baselines on SSIM and LPIPS across every scene where comparisons are available. Against CityGS [16], the closest competitor, HUG gains 0.56 dB PSNR on MatrixCity (28.02 vs. 27.46), 0.65 dB on Rubble (26.42 vs. 25.77), and 0.80 dB on Building (22.35 vs. 21.55), while matching CityGS on Residence SSIM (both 0.813) but still leading in PSNR and LPIPS. Against Octree-GS [25], HUG exceeds it by 1.61 dB PSNR on MatrixCity (28.02 vs. 26.41), though Octree-GS reports results only on that single scene. Against Hier-GS [11], HUG leads by 1.35 dB PSNR on MatrixCity and 4.14 dB on Rubble, with consistent SSIM/LPIPS advantages everywhere. Against VastGS [14] and 3DGS [10], HUG wins on all metrics in all four real-world scenes. The sole loss is PSNR on Sci-Art, where Mega-NeRF [32] achieves 25.60 versus HUG's 21.83, which the authors attribute to the scene's many distant views; HUG still leads Mega-NeRF on SSIM (0.846 vs. 0.770) and LPIPS (0.204 vs. 0.390) for that scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUG sits at the intersection of two recent 3DGS sub-lines: the anchor-based neural Gaussian representation from Scaffold-GS and the octree-structured LOD hierarchy from Octree-GS. It departs from the dominant block-based reconstruction paradigm (CityGS, VastGS) that trains redundant boundary regions to avoid seam artifacts, replacing that overhead with a visibility-mask-driven partitioning that focuses training strictly on in-block content. The paper is the first, to the authors' knowledge, to combine block-trained neural Gaussians with an integrated LOD pipeline end-to-end for aerial urban scenes, rather than deferring LOD construction to a post-training compression step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1-minute partitioning (vs. ~2 hours for CityGS) and the elimination of redundant boundary training make large-scale aerial reconstruction substantially more practical, and the hierarchical weighted supervision offers a reusable recipe for stabilizing multi-level anchor optimization. However, the method still depends on COLMAP for sparse geometry, cannot handle dynamic objects, and lacks a unified aerial-ground framework—open problems the authors explicitly flag. For practitioners building city-scale rendering pipelines, the visibility-based partitioning and multi-MLP rendering strategy are immediately adoptable components.
