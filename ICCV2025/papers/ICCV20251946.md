## Problem

Existing IP protection methods for diffusion models either embed watermarks into the generation pipeline (degrading image quality) or rely on fine-tuned parameters that are easily removed by fine-tuning or pruning attacks. There is no zero-distortion, white-box verification method that can robustly trace stolen or post-processed diffusion models without altering the original model's generative performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a diffusion model's UNet upblock features at selected timesteps, combined with image and text prompt inputs, and processes them through a plug-in DKe module (a ResNet-like classifier) to produce classification logits. From these logits, it generates Boundary Representations of Knowledge (BRoKs) as sets of multimodal (image, text) sample pairs positioned near class-pair decision boundaries. Verification of a suspect model involves plugging it into the pre-trained DKe and computing a matching accuracy score (0–100%) between the suspect's top-2 logit predictions and the target model's BRoK class pairs.

## Method

PlugMark introduces a plug-in Diffusion Knowledge Extractor (DKe) that connects to a frozen diffusion model's UNet upblock features and maps them to a classification result via a ResNet-like head, treating the extracted knowledge as a classifier whose decision boundaries uniquely fingerprint the model. A variance-based timestep selection mechanism identifies the k most stable timesteps (lowest feature variance across repeated forward passes) and fuses their features with inverse-variance weighting to ensure robustness against fine-tuning and pruning. For boundary representation, the paper proposes a novel gradient-based adversarial generation method using a combined similarity loss (constraining logit gap between two classes) and max-margin loss (separating them from all other classes) to produce multimodal (image, text) sample pairs near the decision boundary. Only the DKe is trained while the diffusion pipeline remains entirely frozen, guaranteeing zero distortion to generative quality. Verification is performed by plugging a suspect model into the pre-trained DKe and checking whether its top-2 logits match the BRoK class pairs, yielding a matching accuracy that distinguishes post-processed versions from unrelated models.

## Results

PlugMark achieves a FID of 24.25, identical to the unwatermarked model, confirming zero distortion to generative quality. On CIFAR-10, it maintains a matching accuracy of 99.18% after 100 steps of DreamBooth fine-tuning, 98.83% after 1000 steps, and 98.00% under 10% weight pruning, with TPR at FPR=10⁻⁶ remaining ≥0.995 across all post-processing conditions. In contrast, all three baseline watermarking methods (Stable Signature, AquaLoRA, WatermarkDM) drop to TPR ≤0.035 after just 1000 fine-tuning steps and ≤0.002 under 10% pruning. Negative (unrelated) models yield only ~10% matching accuracy, confirming uniqueness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three diffusion-native watermarking baselines, PlugMark dominates on both fidelity and robustness. Stable Signature [14] and AquaLoRA [13] both degrade FID (24.77 and 24.88 vs. 24.25) and collapse in TPR to 0.020 and 0.035 respectively after 1000 fine-tuning steps, while PlugMark retains TPR of 0.995. WatermarkDM [68] shows the largest FID degradation (25.92) and its TPR drops to 0.229 at 1000 steps and 0.000 under 10% pruning, whereas PlugMark stays at 0.995 and 1.000. For the adversarial-sample generation comparison (Table 3), PlugMark's gradient-based BRoK method achieves TPR of 1.000/1.000/0.991 at origin/100/2000 steps, substantially outperforming FGSM [17] (0.833/0.513/0.001), CW [3] (0.767/0.439/0.003), and IPGuard [2] (0.920/0.662/0.005), confirming that the proposed boundary-sample strategy is critical for fine-tuning robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlugMark extends the zero-watermarking paradigm from IPGuard [2], which fingerprints standard DNN classifiers via adversarial boundary samples, to the diffusion model setting by introducing a plug-in knowledge extractor (DKe) that maps UNet denoising features to a classification task. It departs fundamentally from the dominant line of diffusion-native watermarking (Stable Signature, AquaLoRA, WatermarkDM), which embeds watermarks into the generation pipeline and thus degrades image quality and is vulnerable to fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion model's intrinsic multi-timestep knowledge—rather than an externally injected signal—as the fingerprint, represented through multimodal (image, text) boundary sample pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-distortion property (FID unchanged) and robustness to 200k-step fine-tuning and 30% weight pruning address two critical practical gaps in open-source model IP protection that existing methods cannot simultaneously satisfy. However, the experimental scope is limited to CIFAR-10/100 and MNIST with SD-v2, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale datasets, larger architectures (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models), or more sophisticated adversarial removal strategies. The per-model DKe training requirement and the need for white-box UNet access also constrain immediate deployment in model-marketplace scenarios where only partial access may be available.
