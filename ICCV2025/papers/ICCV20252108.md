## Problem

Text-guided segmentation methods for images (RES, ReasonSeg) and videos (R-VOS, ReasonVOS) are developed in isolation, overlooking shared task structures and preventing collaborative multi-task optimization. Existing MLLM-based approaches either fail to capture temporal coherence in video or rely on cumbersome multi-specialist pipelines (e.g., VISA requires a separate frozen Video-LLM and video tracker), impeding end-to-end training and widespread deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an image or key frame (H×W×3), an optional reference video sequence of T_r frames (default T_r=4), and a natural-language instruction. It outputs N mask proposals (each H×W) with associated confidence scores; during inference the highest-scoring mask is selected per instruction, while training uses Hungarian matching. For image-only tasks, the input image itself doubles as the OVP reference, unifying the image and video code paths within a single architecture.

## Method

InstructSeg builds on a 3B MLLM (Mipha-3B with Phi-2-2.7B LLM, fine-tuned via LoRA) paired with a frozen Swin-B visual encoder and a Mask2Former-style decoder. Two key modules differentiate it: (1) an Object-aware Video Perceiver (OVP) that uses N₁=3 perceiver layers with shared learnable queries to perform cross-attention over projected frame features concatenated with text tokens, compressing temporal and object-specific information from reference frames into fixed-length tokens fed to the LLM; (2) a Vision-guided Multi-granularity Text Fusion (VMTF) module that first adaptively average-pools detailed text embeddings into a global embedding, then runs N₂=3 cross-attention + FFN layers fusing both global and detailed text embeddings with fine-grained visual features, avoiding the information loss of simple token averaging. The LLM jointly decodes four token streams (visual, text, compressed reference, and mask tokens) in a single forward pass, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a unified loss (autoregressive text CE + mask classification CE + per-pixel BCE + DICE) across seven datasets simultaneously.

## Results

On referring expression segmentation, InstructSeg achieves 85.8 cIoU on RefCOCO val, 80.1 cIoU on RefCOCO+ val, and 79.3 cIoU on RefCOCOg val, surpassing the previous best (PSALM) by +2.2, +7.2, and +5.5 respectively. On reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg), it reaches 61.9 gIoU and 65.2 cIoU, exceeding LISA-7B by +9.0 gIoU. On the challenging ReVOS video reasoning benchmark, the 3B-parameter InstructSeg achieves 54.5 J&F overall, outperforming the 13B VISA model by +7.0 J&F. On Ref-YouTube-VOS, it attains 67.5 J&F (+4.5 over VISA-13B), and on Ref-DAVIS17 it reaches 71.1 J&F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructSeg (3B) outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every IVS benchmark. On image-level RES, it surpasses the strongest specialist (PolyFormer, 74.8 RefCOCO val) by +11.0 cIoU and the strongest MLLM-based method (PSALM, 83.6) by +2.2 cIoU; on the harder RefCOCO+ split the margin widens to +7.2 over PSALM. On ReasonSeg, InstructSeg leads LISA-7B (52.9 gIoU) by +9.0 and SEEM (25.5 gIoU) by +36.4, confirming that multi-granularity text fusion is critical for reasoning-based segmentation. On video tasks, the 3B InstructSeg beats the 13B VISA model on ReVOS overall J&F (54.5 vs. 47.5, +7.0) and on Ref-YouTube-VOS J&F (67.5 vs. 63.0, +4.5), while also exceeding TrackGPT-13B (59.5 J&F) by +8.0. On multi-modal QA benchmarks (Table 5), InstructSeg with a 2.7B LLM backbone matches or exceeds larger 7B/13B MLLMs (e.g., 68.6 MMB vs. LLaVA-1.5's 64.3; 69.2 SQA vs. Qwen-VL-Chat's 68.2), demonstrating that the segmentation training does not degrade general vision-language reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructSeg builds on the Mipha-3B lightweight MLLM and Mask2Former decoder, but its core contribution is an end-to-end unification of four previously separate text-guided segmentation tasks (RES, ReasonSeg, R-VOS, ReasonVOS) under a single 'Instructed Visual Segmentation' framework. It departs from prior MLLM-based segmentation methods (LISA, PixelLM, PSALM) that handle only static images, and from VISA, which assembles multiple frozen specialists (Video-LLM + tracker) in a non-end-to-end pipeline. The two novel modules—Object-aware Video Perceiver for temporal/object condensation and Vision-guided Multi-granularity Text Fusion for integrating global and detailed text—represent the architectural departure from both image-only and multi-specialist video approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single 3B-parameter end-to-end model can match or exceed 7B/13B multi-specialist pipelines across seven IVS benchmarks, the paper offers a practical, deployable alternative to complex segmentation stacks. The zero-shot generalization observed (image-trained model performing competitively on video tasks, and vice versa) suggests the unified training recipe captures transferable representations. Open questions include whether the perceiver-based temporal modeling scales to very long videos, whether the approach extends to other pixel-level tasks (e.g., panoptic or instance segmentation), and whether the multi-granularity text fusion generalizes to even longer or multi-turn instructions.
