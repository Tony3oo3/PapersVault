## Problem

Previous text-guided video prediction methods adapt text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) to the TVP task, but they suffer from poor frame consistency and temporal stability because they lack video dynamics priors and are trained on small domain-specific datasets. Pretrained Image2Video diffusion models like Stable Video Diffusion possess strong video dynamics priors from large-scale pretraining but lack fine-grained textual control for instruction-following. The paper addresses how to transfer such a pretrained video generation model to domain-specific TVP tasks while injecting fine-grained multi-modal text conditioning at low training cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes the first K frames of a video (1 reference frame for Bridge/Epic100, 2 for SSv2) at 256×256 resolution along with a free-form text instruction describing the action to perform. The initial frames are VAE-encoded and concatenated with noisy latents along the channel dimension as conditioning. The output is the remaining N−K frames (12 total frames for SSv2/Epic100, 16 for Bridge Data), reconstructed via the VAE decoder. At inference, classifier-free guidance with separate text (s_T=12) and visual (s_V=1.5) guidance scales controls the balance between instruction adherence and frame fidelity.

## Method

AID builds on Stable Video Diffusion's frozen 3D U-Net and introduces three key components. First, a multimodal LLM (LLaVA) predicts multi-state temporal descriptions from the initial frame and instruction, providing frame-level action states that a single text prompt cannot capture. Second, a Dual Query Transformer (DQFormer) with two branches integrates conditions: the upper branch cross-attends the CLIP text instruction embedding with the CLIP visual embedding to produce a multi-modal embedding (ME), while the lower branch uses learnable queries to decompose both the instruction and MLLM-predicted state embeddings into per-frame decomposed embeddings (DE); the concatenated MCondition is injected into the UNet via cross-attention. Third, three parameter-efficient adapters (spatial, short-term temporal with 3D depthwise conv, and long-term temporal with self-attention) are inserted alongside the UNet's attention blocks, with only these adapters, the DQFormer, and cross-attention layers trained while all original SVD weights remain frozen.

## Results

AID achieves FVD of 50.23 on SSv2, 21.57 on Bridge Data, and 52.78 on Epic100, with corresponding KVD values of 0.02, 0.04, and 0.05. These represent 55.5%, 91.2%, and 80.6% FVD improvements over the previous state-of-the-art Seer (112.9, 246.3, and 271.4 FVD respectively). The Bridge Data result of 21.57 FVD is particularly notable, indicating generated videos are very close to ground-truth quality. All experiments use 256×256 resolution with text conditioning, matching the evaluation protocol of Seer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AID outperforms all eight quantitative baselines across all three datasets on both FVD and KVD. The most direct comparison is with Seer [19], the previous text-conditioned SoTA at 256×256, where AID reduces FVD by 55.5% on SSv2 (50.23 vs. 112.9), 91.2% on Bridge (21.57 vs. 246.3), and 80.6% on Epic100 (52.78 vs. 271.4). Among other text-conditioned methods at the same resolution, VideoFusion [40] (FVD 163.2/501.2/349.9) and Tune-A-Video [65] (FVD 291.4/515.7/365.0) are substantially outperformed, with AID achieving roughly 3–24× lower FVD. Non-text baselines such as MCVD [61], PVDM [74], SimVP [14], and TATS [15] are far behind, confirming the importance of text conditioning. The commercial models Gen-2 [11] and Pika [31] are compared only qualitatively in Figure 5; both fail to follow the action instruction on SSv2 and produce unstable or semantically incorrect videos on Bridge, whereas AID maintains temporal consistency and instruction adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of TVP work (Seer, Tune-A-Video, VideoFusion) that adapts image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion to video prediction, instead transferring a pretrained video diffusion model (Stable Video Diffusion) to domain-specific prediction tasks. The DQFormer architecture draws on the Q-Former design from BLIP-2 but introduces a dual-branch structure that separately handles global text-visual alignment and frame-level temporal decomposition. The use of an MLLM (LLaVA) to predict intermediate video states as conditioning signals is a novel repurposing of multimodal language models for video generation prompting, extending prior work that used LLMs only for prompt expansion or layout planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video dynamics priors learned during large-scale pretraining are a critical missing ingredient in prior TVP methods, and that parameter-efficient adapters (freezing the 3D U-Net) can transfer these priors to narrow domains like robotic manipulation or egocentric cooking with minimal training cost. This opens a practical pathway for deploying high-quality video prediction in resource-constrained, domain-specific settings without retraining from scratch. Limitations include evaluation at only 256×256 resolution on short clips (12–16 frames), and the approach's dependence on a specific pretrained base model (SVD), leaving open whether the strategy generalizes to other video diffusion architectures or scales to longer, higher-resolution sequences.
