## Problem

While powerful data-driven parametric models like SMPL exist for humans and animals, no equally expressive model exists for plants. Existing plant representations are either hand-crafted procedural models that are difficult to fit from sensor observations, or leaf-only parametric models that fail to capture whole-plant topology, articulation, and non-rigid deformation. This gap limits the ability to reconstruct, animate, and simulate crop plants from real-world data.

## Input / Output

For training, the pipeline consumes multi-view RGB videos (captured with GoPro in the field) processed through a 4DGS→2DGS Gaussian splatting pipeline to extract 3D meshes, plus 2D leaf scans for shape basis learning. For inference, it accepts either a single RGB image (segmented via SAM + Mask-RCNN, lifted to 3D with a depth estimator) or a 3D point cloud (segmented via PointTransformer-V3). The output is a complete parametric plant: a tree-structured topology graph, per-node articulation (quaternion + 1D translation + scale), shape PCA coefficients, and deformation PCA coefficients, which together generate a triangulated mesh with explicit leaf/stem part boundaries suitable for biophysical simulation.

## Method

Demeter decomposes plant geometry into four parametric components: a variable tree topology Γ, per-node articulation θ (forward kinematics with quaternion rotations and path-length translations), shape β (Catmull-Rom spline control points on a 2D grid per leaf, compressed via PCA from 2D scans), and deformation γ (joint angles on a 2D skeletal structure of main and sub-veins, also PCA-compressed from 3D data). The shape and deformation bases are learned separately: shape from thousands of 2D leaf scans mapped via Laplace-equation UV correspondence, and deformation from 3D point clouds through a multi-stage joint optimization (coarse pose → forward kinematic fitting → full energy minimization with Chamfer distance) that disentangles articulation, shape, and deformation. For reconstruction without ground-truth annotations, a PointTransformer-V3 network performs instance segmentation on the point cloud, a minimum spanning tree infers topology, and the same energy minimization recovers all parameters. This species-agnostic pipeline was demonstrated on soybean, maize, pepper, rose, and tobacco.

## Results

On the Demeter-Soybean dataset, Demeter achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.0016 (normalized), outperforming NKSR (0.0030) and SimpProc (0.0376), while using only 3.375 KB of storage versus NKSR's 5785.6 KB. On Maize, Demeter achieves CD 0.0071, beating SimpProc (0.0557) but trailing NKSR (0.0023), though still at a fraction of NKSR's storage (1.77 KB vs. 3686.4 KB). For single-image reconstruction, Demeter attains an IoU alignment score of 0.328, surpassing One-2-3-45++ (0.296) and Meshy (0.206).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Demeter outperforms SimpProc (CropCraft) by a wide margin on Chamfer distance for both soybean (0.0016 vs. 0.0376, ~23× lower) and maize (0.0071 vs. 0.0557, ~8× lower), while remaining compact at 3.4 KB and 1.8 KB respectively. Against NKSR, Demeter achieves lower CD on soybean (0.0016 vs. 0.0030) but higher CD on maize (0.0071 vs. 0.0023); however, Demeter is roughly 1700× more compact on soybean (3.4 KB vs. 5785.6 KB) and 2100× on maize, and produces a smooth, biophysically functional mesh whereas NKSR yields thick, unsmooth leaves with poor connectivity. For single-image reconstruction, Demeter (IoU 0.328) beats One-2-3-45++ (IoU 0.296) and Meshy (IoU 0.206), producing a complete and input-faithful plant shape where baselines either miss details or misalign with the input. Overall, Demeter is the only method that simultaneously achieves smooth geometry, shape/deformation disentanglement, and learnability, making it uniquely suited for downstream biophysical simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Demeter transplants the SMPL-style learned parametric shape paradigm (PCA-based linear models for shape and deformation) from humans and animals to plants, but introduces a critical departure: variable topology as a first-class parameter, since unlike humans or quadrupeds, individual plants of the same species can have different branching structures. It contrasts with the dominant procedural/grammar-based plant modeling tradition (L-systems, CropCraft), which is difficult to invert from observations, and with general-purpose 3D reconstruction (NeRF, multi-view stereo, NKSR) that struggles with thin, occluded plant structures without shape priors. The model also explicitly disentangles shape from non-rigid deformation via a skeletal joint structure, addressing a gap in prior leaf-only parametric models that failed to model whole plants or separate these factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-driven parametric model that captures whole-plant morphology (topology, articulation, shape, deformation) from real-world field data, enabling downstream biophysical simulations (e.g., photosynthesis via Helios) that were previously infeasible with procedural or mesh-based representations. The accompanying 600-plant soybean dataset with full topology and instance annotations fills a gap in agricultural 3D benchmarks. Key limitations include the assumption of 2D leaves and uniform-thickness stems (excluding cacti, banyan trees, algae), the omission of flowers and fruits, and evaluation primarily on soybean and a few other species—generalization to the full diversity of plant morphology remains open.
