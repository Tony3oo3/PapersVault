## Problem

FlashAttention eliminates the need to materialize attention maps in HBM, which breaks existing training-free token compression methods that rely on those maps to score token importance. This incompatibility prevents practitioners from stacking the two most impactful efficiency techniques—fused attention kernels and token pruning—on pretrained vision models without retraining or architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied at inference time to pretrained models (UMT, DeiT, ResNet, ViM) without any retraining. For video tasks, inputs are 12-frame clips at 224×224 resolution paired with text queries or questions; for image classification, standard ImageNet images. The output format is identical to the base model (retrieval rankings, QA answers, or class labels), but with progressively fewer tokens processed in deeper layers, yielding up to 5.5× throughput gains while preserving or improving accuracy.

## Method

Representation shift computes the L2 distance between each token's embedding before and after a layer operation (specifically the MLP sub-layer) as a training-free, model-agnostic importance score: tokens with small shifts are deemed redundant and pruned. The key insight is that the MLP operates per-token independently, producing a more discriminative shift signal than the attention layer, which diffuses information across tokens. Because the score is derived purely from residual-stream activations, it requires no attention map and is therefore compatible with FlashAttention, CNNs (where it measures shift across convolutional stages), and state space models. Ablations confirm L2 distance outperforms L1 and cosine metrics, and pruning at the MLP layer outperforms pruning based on attention or the full block shift.

## Results

On video-text retrieval with UMT-L, representation shift + FlashAttention achieves a 5.5× throughput speedup (66 vid/s vs. 12 vid/s baseline) while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., 62.9% R@1 on ActivityNet vs. 53.2% for attention-based pruning, a +9.7% gain). On video QA, the method yields ~4× speedup on UMT-B (128 vs. 32 vid/s) and ~3.8× on UMT-L (46 vs. 12 vid/s) with minimal accuracy drop. On ImageNet-1K with DeiT-S, representation shift + FlashAttention reaches 77.8% top-1 accuracy at 5948 img/s, outperforming attention-based pruning (72.1% at 4844 img/s) by +5.7% accuracy and 1.2× throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against attention-based token pruning (EViT [33] / BAT [39] scoring), representation shift consistently wins on both accuracy and throughput: on DeiT-S it achieves 77.8% vs. 72.1% top-1 (+5.7%) at 5948 vs. 4844 img/s, and on UMT-L video retrieval it gains up to +9.7% R@1 (ActivityNet) while running 2.9× faster (66 vs. 23 vid/s). Compared to vid-TLDR [13], the paper replaces its attention-based importance metric with representation shift and adds FlashAttention, boosting throughput from ×1.36 to ×4.10 on UMT-B (131.4 vs. 43.6 vid/s) while preserving R@1 (e.g., 50.8% on MSRVTT). On the SSM architecture ViM-T, representation shift achieves 75.5% accuracy at 1754 img/s, edging out ToP-ViM [74] (75.1% at 1758 img/s) by +0.4% accuracy at comparable speed. Overall, the method dominates on speed in every comparison and matches or exceeds accuracy, with the largest accuracy gains appearing on UMT-L where attention-based pruning degrades most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously incompatible lines of work: token compression for Vision Transformers (EViT, BAT, DynamicViT, vid-TLDR) and fused attention kernels like FlashAttention. Prior token pruning methods rely on attention maps to score token importance, but FlashAttention deliberately avoids materializing those maps to reduce HBM I/O, making the two techniques mutually exclusive. The authors repurpose a simple observation—that the L2 distance between a token's embedding before and after a layer operation reflects its informativeness—as a training-free, model-agnostic importance metric that requires no attention scores, thereby unifying the two acceleration strategies. The approach also extends the token-compression paradigm to architectures (CNNs, SSMs) that prior attention-based methods could not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: combining FlashAttention with representation-shift pruning yields up to 5.5× throughput gains on video-text retrieval, a combination that was previously impossible. The model-agnostic nature of the metric (applicable to Transformers, CNNs, and SSMs without retraining) makes it a broadly useful tool for efficient inference. Limitations include the need for brief fine-tuning when applying pruning to CNNs (due to spatial resolution changes), and the metric remains heuristic with no theoretical guarantee of optimality. Open questions include whether representation shift can guide token merging rather than just pruning, whether it transfers to generative or autoregressive vision models, and whether the MLP-layer/L2-norm choice generalizes to all architectures without per-model tuning.
