## Problem

Automated 3D brain landmark detection requires large annotated datasets, struggles to generalize across MRI contrasts (T1w vs. T2w, different field strengths), and existing self-supervised keypoint-discovery methods produce landmarks that lack the subject-consistent anatomical protocols needed for clinical use. CABLD addresses all three gaps by requiring only a single annotated reference atlas, enforcing inter-subject landmark consistency, and using synthetic contrast augmentation to eliminate the need for real multi-contrast training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled 3D brain MRI scan (resampled to 193×229×193 voxels at 1×1×1 mm³) and one reference template (ICBM152) with 32 expert-annotated AFIDs landmarks as the sole supervision signal. It outputs 3D coordinates for those 32 landmarks in the subject space along with a thin-plate-spline deformation field mapping the subject to template space. Notably, the model is trained exclusively on T1w scans yet generalizes to T2w and 7T data without any contrast-specific training examples.

## Method

A 9-block 3D CNN (32→512→32 filters with instance normalization and max pooling) produces one feature map per landmark, from which a center-of-mass layer regresses 3D coordinates. Training is self-supervised and multi-task: a registration loss (MSE between the TPS-warped subject and template) is combined with a two-term consistency loss that penalizes both cross-subject landmark disagreement (L_consistency_1) and subject-to-template deviation (L_consistency_2), all computed over M=2 randomly sampled scans per batch. The TPS deformation field is parameterized by the landmark pairs with a randomly sampled regularization parameter λ to jointly learn affine and nonlinear warps. For contrast invariance, five 1×1×1 random-convolution layers with LeakyReLU are applied as data augmentation, simulating diverse MRI contrasts without real multi-contrast data. An adaptive mixed loss schedules the registration-to-consistency weighting via a sigmoid of training progress (curriculum-style), letting the model first learn spatial alignment before refining anatomically consistent landmark placement.

## Results

CABLD achieves the lowest mean radial error (MRE) across all three T1w test datasets: 3.27±2.24 mm on HCP T1w, 3.89±2.69 mm on OASIS, and 5.11±3.19 mm on SNSX, outperforming the best baseline (ANTs with MI) by 0.38–0.95 mm. Success detection rates (SDR) at 3 mm reach 54.48% (HCP), 39.24% (OASIS), and 29.63% (SNSX), and at 6 mm reach 93.69%, 87.15%, and 71.01% respectively. Notably, CABLD was trained only on T1w scans yet generalizes to T2w MRI, achieving 3.99±2.25 mm MRE and 86.43% SDR at 6 mm on HCP T2w, surpassing all baselines at the 6 mm and 9 mm thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CABLD outperforms all seven baseline families with statistical significance (p<0.05) on MRE across all three T1w datasets. Against the strongest traditional method, ANTs with MI, CABLD reduces MRE by 0.38 mm (HCP), 0.26 mm (OASIS), and 0.95 mm (SNSX), while also achieving substantially higher SDR at 3 mm (e.g., 54.48% vs. 45.52% on HCP). Among deep learning baselines, CABLD surpasses MultiGradICON (4.10 mm) and uniGradICON (4.12 mm) on HCP T1w by ~0.85 mm MRE, and outperforms KeyMorph (best config: 4.67 mm) and BrainMorph (4.11 mm) by similar margins. Notably, CABLD also beats the fully supervised 3D CNN (4.65 mm on HCP) by 1.38 mm MRE, demonstrating that its self-supervised consistency-based training with a single reference atlas annotation is more effective than supervised learning on the same architecture. On the unseen T2w contrast, CABLD (3.99 mm) matches ANTs-MI (3.91 mm) in MRE while achieving the highest SDR at 6 mm (86.43%) and 9 mm (98.99%), whereas uniGradICON degrades severely (13.44 mm MRE) and 3D SIFT fails entirely (54.90 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

CABLD departs from prior self-supervised landmark discovery methods (KeyMorph, BrainMorph, Chao et al.) that produce registration-driven keypoints lacking clinical protocol consistency, by injecting expert anatomical knowledge through a single population-averaged atlas (ICBM152) and enforcing inter-subject and subject-template consistency losses. It also extends the 2D random convolution (RC) technique of Xu et al. to 3D volumetric medical imaging for the first time, using it as a contrast augmentation strategy to achieve modality-agnostic detection without requiring multi-contrast training data. The adaptive mixed loss, inspired by curriculum learning, addresses the multi-task interference between registration and landmark detection that prior multi-task frameworks have handled less explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the annotation requirement to a single atlas and demonstrating strong generalization to unseen contrasts (T2w) and field strengths (7T), CABLD addresses two major practical barriers in clinical landmark detection: data scarcity and scanner/protocol variability. The demonstrated downstream utility for Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease diagnosis (F1 scores of 81.2% and 93.5%) suggests the detected landmarks carry genuine clinical signal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to other anatomies or modalities, the impact of increasing the consistency batch size M beyond 2, and whether the TPS-based deformation fields can be replaced by learned deformations for higher-fidelity registration without sacrificing the consistency guarantees.
