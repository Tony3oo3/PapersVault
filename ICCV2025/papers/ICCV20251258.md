## Problem

Existing deep learning-based point cloud registration methods require manual tuning of voxel size and search radius for each new environment, and their learned keypoint detectors and raw-coordinate inputs cause severe out-of-domain failures. The paper identifies these three factors—environment-specific parameters, brittle learned detectors, and unnormalized coordinates—as the key barriers to zero-shot generalization, and notes the absence of a comprehensive benchmark spanning diverse indoor/outdoor scenes, sensor types, and geographic regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapping unordered 3D point clouds (source P and target Q) of arbitrary scale and density, with no user-specified voxel size, search radius, or scale alignment required. It outputs the relative rotation matrix R ∈ SO(3) and translation vector t ∈ R³, estimated via RANSAC from consensus-maximized inlier correspondences. The pipeline is fully self-adaptive: voxel size is derived from PCA-based sphericity of the input clouds, and multi-scale search radii are determined by matching local point density to target thresholds.

## Method

BUFFER-X replaces learned keypoint detectors with farthest point sampling (FPS) at three independent scales (local, middle, global), and uses Mini-SpinNet with patch-wise scale normalization (dividing coordinates by the per-scale radius to bound them to [-1,1]) and PCA-defined reference axes to eliminate dataset-specific inductive biases. A geometric bootstrapping step adaptively determines the voxel size via sphericity (λ₃/λ₁ from PCA eigenvalues) and per-scale search radii via density-aware radius estimation, removing all manual parameter tuning. Correspondences are established through intra-scale nearest-neighbor matching on vector features, followed by pairwise 3D transformation estimation using cylindrical feature cross-correlation processed by a 3DCCN, and finally cross-scale consensus maximization to select globally consistent inliers. Training is a simplified two-stage process: contrastive learning for Mini-SpinNet descriptor discriminability, then Huber-loss training of the 3DCCN for yaw offset prediction, with inter-patch radius augmentation to expose the network to diverse point distribution patterns.

## Results

BUFFER-X achieves an average rank of 1.55 across 11 diverse datasets (indoor and outdoor) without any prior information or manual parameter tuning, compared to 3.82 for BUFFER with oracle tuning. On outdoor LiDAR datasets (KITTI, WOD, KAIST, ETH, Oxford), BUFFER-X achieves 99.67–100% success rate zero-shot, while all other deep learning baselines drop to near 0% without tuning. On the in-domain KITTI benchmark (Table 2), BUFFER-X achieves 7.74 cm RTE, 0.27° RRE, and 99.82% success rate, competitive with state-of-the-art methods like PARENet (4.90 cm RTE) and Predator (5.60 cm RTE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking result is that all deep learning baselines (FCGF, Predator, GeoTransformer, BUFFER, PARENet) fail catastrophically on outdoor LiDAR datasets without oracle tuning—achieving 0–7% success rate on KITTI—while BUFFER-X maintains 99.82% zero-shot. Even with oracle tuning, BUFFER-X outperforms GeoTransformer (99.82 vs. 92.43 on KITTI) and PARENet (99.82 vs. 84.86 on KITTI), and matches or slightly exceeds BUFFER+oracle (99.82 vs. 99.46 on KITTI, 100.00 vs. 100.00 on WOD). On indoor datasets, BUFFER-X leads on 3DMatch (95.58%) and ScanNet++F (99.90%) but trails PARENet on 3DLoMatch (74.18% vs. 80.50%), which the authors attribute to the consensus maximization step favoring high-overlap scenarios. Among conventional methods, FPFH+FGR and FPFH+TEASER++ achieve strong outdoor performance (98–100%) but degrade significantly on indoor scenes (52–62% on 3DMatch), whereas BUFFER-X maintains >93% across all indoor datasets. On the in-domain KITTI benchmark (Table 2), BUFFER-X (7.74 cm RTE, 99.82% success) is competitive with PARENet (4.90 cm) and Predator (5.60 cm) but slightly behind in translation accuracy, confirming that the zero-shot generalization gains do not come at the cost of in-domain precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

BUFFER-X is a direct extension of BUFFER (Ao et al., CVPR 2023), which itself builds on SpinNet's (Ao et al., CVPR 2021) patch-wise scale normalization and cylindrical convolutional network for descriptor generation. The key departures are threefold: replacing the learned keypoint detector with farthest point sampling, introducing a PCA-based geometric bootstrapping mechanism to adaptively determine voxel size and multi-scale search radii at test time, and formulating cross-scale correspondence selection as a consensus maximization problem. The paper also positions itself against the broader family of deep learning-based registration methods (FCGF, Predator, GeoTransformer, PARENet) by identifying that their reliance on fixed hyperparameters and raw coordinates is the primary barrier to zero-shot generalization, rather than insufficient model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for per-dataset manual parameter tuning (voxel size, search radius), which is a significant usability barrier for deploying registration in new environments. The 11-dataset benchmark spanning indoor/outdoor, multiple sensor modalities, and three continents provides the field with a much more rigorous generalization evaluation than prior single-dataset or two-dataset comparisons. A notable limitation is the degraded performance on 3DLoMatch (10–30% overlap), where the consensus maximization objective of selecting the largest inlier set can miss the true global optimum in partial-overlap scenarios. Open questions include whether the consensus formulation can be adapted for low-overlap cases and how to further reduce inference latency for real-time deployment.
