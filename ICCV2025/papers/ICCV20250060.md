## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods for MLLMs predominantly target hallucination factors, overlooking the intrinsic quality dimensions essential for general multi-modal comprehension. This confines improvements to hallucination mitigation and limits discriminative power when differentiating responses. The paper asks: what factors within preference pairs critically determine alignment towards enhanced general comprehension capabilities?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250060/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a seed dataset of (image, instruction, reference response) triples drawn from 15 open-source VQA, text understanding, and open-instruction datasets. During preference construction, images are additionally perturbed (noise, blur, resize) for contrastive sampling, and the verifier model evaluates each candidate response against four criteria: logical entailment to the reference, detail completeness, contradiction absence, and instruction adherence. The final output is 89k preference pairs (win/loss response pairs) used for DPO training, with response length increasing across progressive rounds as harder samples are recovered.

## Method

IPA constructs preference pairs through a three-stage automated pipeline: (1) Sampling generates diverse candidates via normal and contrastive (perturbed-image) decoding from multiple generator models; (2) Revision uses a reviser model to produce diagnostic feedback and refine responses, expanding task-relevant depth while preserving natural output characteristics; (3) Verification decouples alignment signal extraction from formatting variability by checking whether each response logically entails the reference, contains critical details, avoids contradictions, and adheres to the instruction. A progressive three-round collection pipeline then maximizes hard-sample utilization: Round 1 builds initial preferences, Round 2 applies DPO self-improvement to both generator and reviser before reprocessing failed samples, and Round 3 augments the reviser with reference-guided feedback to recover the remaining difficult cases. The key design insight is grounding preference assessment in instruction fulfillment efficacy rather than hallucination patterns, yielding a clear decision boundary that generalizes across diverse instruction types.

## Results

On Qwen2VL-7B, IPA achieves the best scores on 8 out of 9 benchmarks: HallusionBench (54.3), POPE (87.2), MMMU (54.6), MMStar (61.7), MMVet (64.2), MME Perception (1687.4), LLaVABench (84.0), and OCRBench (87.3). The only benchmark where IPA does not lead is MMHal-Bench (3.7 vs. MMPR's 3.9). Compared to the unaligned Qwen2VL-7B baseline, IPA yields gains of +4.3 on HallusionBench, +1.0 on POPE, +0.8 on MMMU, +1.0 on MMStar, +1.1 on MMVet, +11.1 on MME Perception, +7.4 on LLaVABench, and +1.1 on OCRBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPA outperforms all five compared preference alignment methods on 8 of 9 benchmarks. Against the closest competitor MMPR, IPA leads on HallusionBench (54.3 vs. 53.4), POPE (87.2 vs. 86.4), MMMU (54.6 vs. 54.3), MMStar (61.7 vs. 58.5), MMVet (64.2 vs. 61.7), MME-P (1687.4 vs. 1680.5), LLaVABench (84.0 vs. 83.5), and OCRBench (87.3 vs. 86.3), but trails on MMHal-Bench (3.7 vs. 3.9). Hallucination-focused methods (RLHF-V, RLAIF-V, Topic-Overwrite) show minimal or negative gains on general VQA benchmarks—for example, RLAIF-V drops MMVet from 63.1 to 58.2—while IPA maintains consistent improvements across all dimensions. VLFeedback achieves the second-best HallusionBench score (52.2) but sacrifices POPE accuracy (84.7 vs. 87.2 for IPA), illustrating the accuracy-consistency tradeoff the paper highlights. On the weaker LLaVA-1.5-7B model (Table 4), IPA-sub still yields balanced gains over all baselines, confirming the method's generalizability across model capacity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant hallucination-oriented preference alignment paradigm (RLHF-V, RLAIF-V, MMPR, Topic-Overwrite) by re-grounding preference construction in a broader criterion: instruction fulfillment efficacy rather than hallucination detection. It repurposes the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm as a verification mechanism (checking logical entailment, detail completeness, contradiction absence, and instruction adherence against a reference) and borrows contrastive decoding from VCD to diversify flawed response patterns. The progressive multi-round pipeline with self-improvement of generator and reviser models via DPO is a novel orchestration of existing components, but no single prior method is directly extended; rather, the paper synthesizes sampling, revision, and verification into a unified scalable framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preference alignment grounded in general instruction fulfillment can yield consistent (if modest) improvements across hallucination, VQA, and text understanding simultaneously, suggesting a more holistic alignment signal than hallucination-specific criteria. However, absolute gains are typically 1–2 points, and the method is validated only on 7B-scale models, leaving open whether the instruction-oriented verification paradigm scales to tens-of-billions-of-parameters models where the base model's own verification quality may differ substantially. The finding that naive reference-guided sampling degrades performance (inducing imitation rather than genuine improvement) is a useful practical caution for future preference data pipelines.
