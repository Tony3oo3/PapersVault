## Problem

While UNet-based diffusion models have mature plug-and-play control ecosystems (ControlNet, IP-Adapter), DiT-based conditional generation suffers from three critical gaps: quadratic attention cost when concatenating condition tokens, unstable multi-condition coordination under single-condition training, and parameter conflicts that break compatibility with community-customized models. No existing DiT framework simultaneously achieves efficient inference, flexible multi-condition control, and true plug-and-play integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt plus one or more condition images (spatial maps such as Canny edges, depth, or OpenPose skeletons, and/or subject/face reference photos). During training, all condition images are downsampled to a fixed 512×512 resolution before VAE encoding, yet the model generates outputs at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios (e.g., 1024×1024, 1024×768) via position-aware interpolation. Inference uses 25 flow-matching sampling steps on a single A100 GPU, producing a single controlled image per forward pass.

## Method

EasyControl extends FLUX.1-dev with a parallel Condition Branch where LoRA low-rank projections are applied exclusively to condition tokens while text and noise branch weights remain frozen, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with custom LoRA models and zero-shot multi-condition generalization. A Position-Aware Training Paradigm downscales conditions to 512×512 and uses Position-Aware Interpolation (scaling position encodings by the resize ratio) for spatial conditions and a fixed PE offset for subject conditions, preserving spatial alignment at arbitrary output resolutions. Causal Conditional Attention masks condition-to-denoising queries during training, decoupling condition computation from the denoising timestep; Causal Mutual Attention further blocks cross-condition queries during multi-condition inference to prevent interference. This causal structure enables a KV Cache that precomputes and stores all condition key-value pairs at the first timestep and reuses them across all 25 denoising steps, reducing inference latency by up to 75% compared to the unoptimized baseline.

## Results

On a single A100 GPU generating 1024×1024 images with 25 denoising steps, EasyControl (Full) achieves 16.3 s inference time for single-condition and 18.3 s for double-condition generation, using only 15 M and 30 M additional parameters respectively. This represents a 58% speedup over its own ablated version without PATP and KV Cache (38.9 s single, 72.4 s double), while matching or beating ControlNet (16.5 s, 3 B params) and OminiControl (31.6 s, 15 M params) in single-condition latency. In multi-condition settings, EasyControl is 75% faster than its ablated baseline and 75% faster than Multi-ControlNet (20.3 s, 6 B params) while using an order-of-magnitude fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet, EasyControl matches single-condition latency (16.3 s vs 16.5 s) while using 15 M parameters versus ControlNet's 3 B, and qualitatively preserves text rendering and avoids color inconsistencies that ControlNet exhibits under Canny and Depth control. Against OminiControl, EasyControl is nearly 2× faster (16.3 s vs 31.6 s) with the same 15 M parameter budget, and better maintains identity consistency in subject control where OminiControl introduces artifacts. In multi-condition settings, EasyControl (18.3 s, 30 M) is competitive with ControlNet+IP-Adapter (16.8 s, 4 B) in speed but uses ~133× fewer parameters, and outperforms Multi-ControlNet (20.3 s, 6 B) in both speed and the ability to satisfy both conditions simultaneously. Qualitatively, Uni-ControlNet fails to generate coherent images under Depth control and struggles with text alignment in multi-condition scenarios, while ControlNet+Redux and Uni-ControlNet fail to preserve both identity consistency and spatial controllability. Compared to ID customization methods (UniPortrait, PhotoMaker, InstantID) integrated with ControlNet, EasyControl achieves superior identity consistency and controllability in face+OpenPose multi-condition generation (detailed in supplementary Sec. F).

## Relation to Prior Work

EasyControl extends the DiT-based conditional generation line (OminiControl, In-context LoRA, Uni-ControlNet) by replacing the dominant token-concatenation paradigm with an isolated Condition Branch that applies LoRA exclusively to condition tokens, leaving text and noise branches untouched. It inherits the architectural foundation from FLUX.1 dev and the low-rank adaptation principle from LoRA, but departs from prior DiT control methods by introducing Causal Attention with a KV Cache mechanism—a technique previously confined to autoregressive LLM inference—and a Position-Aware Training Paradigm that decouples control-signal resolution from output resolution. Compared to the mature UNet ecosystem (ControlNet, IP-Adapter), the paper explicitly positions itself as a migration bridge, addressing the efficiency, multi-condition, and plug-and-play gaps that have hindered DiT adoption for controllable generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, lightweight (15–30 M parameters) drop-in solution for the growing DiT ecosystem, where prior control methods either required full-model fine-tuning or incurred prohibitive token-length costs. The zero-shot multi-condition generalization—training each condition independently yet combining them at inference via Causal Mutual Attention—is a useful property for practitioners who lack paired multi-condition training data. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully beyond two conditions, whether the KV Cache strategy transfers to video DiTs with temporal attention, and whether the 512×512 control-signal resolution cap limits fidelity for fine-grained spatial tasks at very high output resolutions.
