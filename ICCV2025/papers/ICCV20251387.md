## Problem

Interactive text-to-video retrieval methods have emerged to refine ambiguous user queries through clarifying questions, but existing approaches rely on heuristic or ad-hoc question generation without explicitly quantifying the multiple uncertainties that degrade retrieval—text ambiguity, text-video mapping uncertainty, and frame-level visual noise. This lack of principled uncertainty modeling limits how effectively interactive systems can target and reduce the specific sources of retrieval failure.

## Input / Output

The system takes an initial (potentially vague) text query and a preprocessed video database where each video has been offline-sampled by TQFS into K high-quality, semantically diverse frames and captioned by VideoLLaVA. At each interaction round, the system produces a clarifying question (selected from three uncertainty-driven levels) and a refined query, ultimately outputting ranked video candidates. User responses are either real or simulated via a VideoQA module (temperature 0.7), while internal generation modules (captioning, question generation, query refinement) run at temperature 0.1. The visual encoder processes 8 frames at 224×224 with 768-dim embeddings, and the full pipeline is capped at 10 interaction rounds.

## Method

UMIVR introduces three training-free uncertainty metrics that jointly drive adaptive question generation: a Text Ambiguity Score (TAS) computed via semantic entropy over clustered caption similarities, a Mapping Uncertainty Score (MUS) derived from Jensen-Shannon divergence between the normalized similarity distribution and an ideal one-hot distribution, and a Temporal Quality-based Frame Sampler (TQFS) that selects the highest-quality frame per temporal bin followed by K-means-based semantic deduplication. Based on threshold comparisons (α for TAS, β for MUS), the system generates open-ended questions for high text ambiguity, targeted distinguishing questions for high mapping uncertainty, or enrichment questions for low uncertainty. All components—retrieval, captioning, question generation, response simulation, and query refinement—are unified within a single VideoLLaVA-7B model (LanguageBind encoder + LLM via align-before-projection), eliminating the multi-model ensemble overhead of prior interactive TVR systems. The iterative loop progressively reduces TAS and MUS across rounds, with TQFS serving as a plug-in enhancement transferable to other TVR models.

## Results

On MSR-VTT-1k, UMIVR achieves Hit@1 of 68.9% after just 3 interaction rounds, surpassing the SOTA non-interactive method HunYuan (62.9%) by 6.0 points, and reaches 78.9% Hit@1 after 8 rounds. On the AVSD dataset, UMIVR attains R@1 of 49.9% and BRI of 1.02 after 6 rounds, outperforming the best interactive baseline (ivrHeuristic: R@1 43.4%, BRI 1.23). Across MSVD and ActivityNet, UMIVR consistently leads on all metrics (R@1, R@10, Hit@1, Hit@10, BRI) at every interaction round, with Hit@1 reaching 79.3% on MSVD and 50.1% on ActivityNet after 5 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMIVR outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest non-interactive SOTA (HunYuan, R@1 62.9%), UMIVR surpasses it in Hit@1 after only 3 rounds (68.9 vs. 62.9) and in R@1 after 6 rounds (65.9 vs. 62.9). Among interactive baselines on AVSD, UMIVR leads ivrHeuristic (the best interactive competitor) by 6.4 points in R@1 (49.9 vs. 43.4) and 9.8 points in Hit@1 (63.3 vs. 53.5) at round 6, while also achieving a substantially lower BRI (1.02 vs. 1.23). The gap over ivrAuto is even larger (R@1: 49.9 vs. 37.3; Hit@1: 63.3 vs. 45.1), and ivrAutoWoAug shows minimal improvement over ivrAuto, confirming that heuristic augmentation alone is insufficient. On MSVD and ActivityNet, UMIVR maintains consistent leads of 2–10 points over ivrHeuristic across all rounds and metrics. In the text-to-image generalization experiment on VisDial, UMIVR outperforms ChatIR, PlugIR, and ivrHeuristic on both R@10 and Hit@10, reaching 85.0% R@10 and 91.8% Hit@10 after 10 rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VideoLLaVA as a unified multimodal backbone and on the semantic entropy framework (Kuhn et al., ICLR 2023) for measuring linguistic uncertainty, repurposing both for interactive video retrieval. It departs from prior interactive TVR methods (IVR, VIRED, D2V) that rely on heuristic or template-based question generation by introducing principled, training-free uncertainty metrics—semantic entropy for text ambiguity, Jensen-Shannon divergence for mapping uncertainty, and a temporal quality-based frame sampler for visual noise. Unlike earlier uncertainty-aware TVR work (TAM, UATVR, PAU) that addresses only a single uncertainty type, UMIVR unifies all three within one interactive pipeline, and unlike multi-model ensemble approaches, it consolidates retrieval, captioning, and QA into a single VideoLLaVA model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit, training-free quantification of three distinct uncertainty sources provides a more rigorous and interpretable foundation for interactive retrieval than the ad-hoc question strategies used previously, and the TQFS module offers a practical plug-in that improves existing TVR models without retraining. However, the evaluation relies on simulated user responses via a VideoQA module rather than real human interaction, and the threshold sensitivity (α, β) suggests the method may require per-dataset calibration. Open questions include whether the uncertainty-guided question strategy generalizes to open-domain video corpora with no pre-computed captions, and whether the gains persist when the backbone model is weaker or the video database is substantially larger.
