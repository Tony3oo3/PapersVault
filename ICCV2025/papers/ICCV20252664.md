## Problem

Existing SAM-based visual reference segmentation methods (e.g., VRP-SAM) generate deterministic prompts that tend to lie near the boundaries of the target prompt region, where small perturbations cause large mask changes due to high loss-landscape curvature. Because SAM can produce identical masks from many different prompts, this boundary-proximity creates instability that undermines zero-shot generalization to unseen object categories, yet no prior method explicitly regularizes prompt embeddings toward flatter, more central regions of the loss landscape.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image with a visual annotation (points, scribbles, boxes, or masks) indicating the object of interest, along with a target image to be segmented. The reference annotation is converted into enhanced image features via a ResNet-50 encoder, and the target image is processed by a frozen SAM image encoder. The output is a single binary segmentation mask for the target image, produced by prompting the frozen SAM mask decoder with 50 mean prompt embeddings (each a 256-dim vector) predicted by the variational prompt encoder.

## Method

ProSAM replaces the deterministic prompt generator of VRP-SAM with a variational prompt encoder that predicts a multivariate Student's t-distribution over prompt embeddings, parameterized by a mean μ_z and diagonal covariance σ_z². During training, K=10 prompts are sampled via the reparameterization trick (using Gaussian ε and chi-squared δ random variables) and the expected BCE + Dice loss over these samples is minimized, which implicitly penalizes loss-landscape curvature and pushes μ_z toward the center of the target prompt region. The heavy-tailed t-distribution (degrees of freedom ν=5) is critical: it increases the probability of sampling outlier prompts when μ_z is near the boundary, producing larger gradients that enforce a stronger 4th-order curvature penalty compared to a Gaussian. At inference, only the predicted mean prompt is used to prompt the frozen SAM decoder, yielding the same computational cost as non-probabilistic baselines while producing masks that are significantly more robust to latent-space perturbations.

## Results

On COCO-20i, ProSAM with mask annotation achieves a mean mIOU of 54.63, outperforming VRP-SAM (53.28) and Matcher (52.7), and approaching SegGPT (56.1) which is trained in-domain. On PASCAL-5i, ProSAM reaches 72.04 mean mIOU with mask annotation, surpassing VRP-SAM (71.14). Under domain shift from COCO-20i to PASCAL-5i, ProSAM achieves 77.65 mean mIOU, the best among all compared methods. ProSAM uses only 1.73M learnable parameters, far fewer than DCAMA (47.7M) or HDMNet (4.2M), while still achieving the highest mIOU on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSAM consistently outperforms VRP-SAM [32], the prior SOTA, across all four annotation types (point, scribble, box, mask) on both COCO-20i and PASCAL-5i, with the largest gains on mask annotation (+1.35 mIOU on COCO-20i, +0.90 on PASCAL-5i). Against Matcher [20], which uses a heavier DINOv2 ViT-L backbone, ProSAM achieves superior mIOU (54.63 vs. 52.7 on COCO-20i) with a lighter ResNet-50. ProSAM substantially outperforms PerSAM [45] (54.63 vs. 23.0) and Painter [35] (54.63 vs. 33.1) on COCO-20i, though it remains slightly below SegGPT [36] (54.63 vs. 56.1), which benefits from in-domain training. In the few-shot segmentation comparison (Table 2), ProSAM achieves the best mIOU on both datasets (54.63 / 72.04) with only 1.73M learnable parameters, beating DCAMA [30] (50.9 / 69.3, 47.7M params) and HDMNet [27] (50.0 / 69.4, 4.2M params). Under domain shift from COCO-20i to PASCAL-5i (Table 3), ProSAM leads all baselines at 77.65 mean mIOU, ahead of VRP-SAM (76.44) and DGPNet [11] (70.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProSAM builds directly on VRP-SAM (Sun et al., CVPR 2024), the current SOTA training-based method for SAM-based visual reference segmentation, adopting its architecture and training protocol while replacing the deterministic prompt generator with a variational prompt encoder that outputs a multivariate t-distribution over prompt embeddings. The variational inference framework, including the reparameterization trick and heavy-tailed t-distribution, is borrowed from the VAE and t-VAE literature (Kingma 2013; Kim et al., ICLR 2024) but repurposed here to regularize prompt generation rather than data generation. The key conceptual departure from prior SAM-based methods is the explicit identification of prompt boundary instability as a robustness failure mode, which existing approaches including VRP-SAM, Matcher, and PerSAM do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing prompt robustness as a first-class concern and showing that variational training implicitly penalizes loss-landscape curvature, the paper provides a principled and minimally invasive fix (two extra linear layers plus Monte Carlo sampling) that consistently improves zero-shot generalization on novel classes. The approach is lightweight (1.73M learnable parameters) and compatible with any image encoder, making it easy to adopt in existing SAM-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the t-distribution formulation is optimal compared to other heavy-tailed alternatives, whether the benefit persists in multi-object or video segmentation settings, and whether the same variational principle can be extended to other prompt types (text, box) beyond visual references.
