## Problem

Existing monocular 3D clothed human reconstruction methods (e.g., PIFu, SIFU) assume occlusion-free inputs and produce fragmented, multiview-inconsistent reconstructions when the subject is partially occluded in in-the-wild images. Moreover, most approaches rely on labor-intensive SMPL annotations or 3D scan supervision for training, limiting their practical deployability. The paper addresses both gaps simultaneously: achieving occlusion-resilient, multiview-consistent reconstruction without any geometric prior annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single occluded RGB image (H×W×3) of a clothed human as input and is also compatible with stereo or multiview inputs. The diffusion stage produces four occlusion-free views at fixed camera rotations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°), which are then combined with the original occluded image to predict a set of 3D Gaussians (n_g × 14 parameters: 3D mean, 3D scaling, 4D rotation quaternion, opacity, and 3D color). The final output is N occlusion-free novel view renderings from arbitrary camera angles, with no SMPL or 3D supervision required at training or inference time.

## Method

CHROME is a two-stage end-to-end pipeline. The first stage (F_D) is a latent diffusion model initialized from Zero123++ and fine-tuned with a ControlNet branch that conditions on 2D target poses (projected from a 3D pose estimated by Humans in 4D), generating four pose-consistent, occlusion-free views from the occluded input; the VAE encoder extracts features from visible regions to anchor appearance. The second stage (F_R) is an asymmetric UNet (following LGM) that concatenates the occluded input with the four synthesized views, encodes camera poses as 9-channel ray-embedding feature maps, and predicts per-pixel 3D Gaussians whose parameters are rendered via differentiable 3DGS. Training is supervised purely with 2D photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) and silhouette shape loss on rendered views, eliminating the need for 3D scan or SMPL supervision. The key insight is that the diffusion stage bridges the 2D-3D information gap by hallucinating occluded regions in a pose-controlled, cross-view-consistent manner before the reconstruction stage integrates all views into a cohesive Gaussian field.

## Results

On the in-domain occluded THuman2.0 dataset, CHROME achieves 20.54 dB PSNR, 0.9098 SSIM, and 0.0893 LPIPS for novel view texture reconstruction, outperforming the best baseline PIFu (17.11 dB PSNR) by approximately 3.4 dB. For geometric reconstruction, CHROME attains 1.712 cm Chamfer Distance, 1.347 cm P2S error, and 0.697 Normal Consistency, surpassing all baselines by substantial margins (e.g., 1.03 cm lower CD than PIFu). In zero-shot settings on CustomHumans, CHROME maintains a ~3.8 dB PSNR advantage (18.54 vs. 14.77 for PIFu), and on the naturally occluded AHP dataset it achieves 17.77 dB PSNR versus 14.88 for PIFu.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHROME decisively outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. On in-domain THuman2.0, it gains ~3.4 dB PSNR over PIFu (the strongest baseline at 17.11 dB) and ~4.6 dB over SiTH (15.98 dB), while also reducing Chamfer Distance from 2.744 cm (PIFu) to 1.712 cm. In zero-shot CustomHumans evaluation, the PSNR gap widens to ~3.8 dB over PIFu (18.54 vs. 14.77), and geometric CD drops from 5.751 cm (PIFu) to 4.142 cm. On the naturally occluded AHP dataset, CHROME leads PIFu by ~2.9 dB PSNR (17.77 vs. 14.88) and reduces LPIPS from 0.1620 to 0.1296. Notably, all baselines require ground-truth SMPL or 3D scan supervision during training, whereas CHROME operates without any such geometric prior, yet still achieves superior performance in both in-domain and zero-shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHROME sits at the intersection of two research threads: multiview diffusion models (initialized from Zero123++ and conditioned via ControlNet) and 3D Gaussian Splatting-based reconstruction (following the asymmetric UNet design of LGM). It departs from the dominant line of clothed human reconstruction (PIFu, SIFU, GTA, SiTH) by eliminating the reliance on SMPL pose/shape annotations and 3D scan-level supervision, instead learning geometric priors implicitly through photometric losses in 2D projected space. The two-stage decomposition—first synthesizing occlusion-free multiview images with a pose-controlled diffusion model, then predicting a 3D Gaussian field conditioned on both the occluded input and synthesized views—is a novel architectural combination not present in prior single-image human reconstruction or single-image-to-3D generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for labor-intensive SMPL annotations and 3D supervision, CHROME lowers the practical barrier to deploying single-image 3D human reconstruction in real-world, in-the-wild settings where occlusions are the norm rather than the exception. The zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (CustomHumans, AHP) and the demonstrated flexibility with stereo inputs suggest the pipeline could serve as a building block for downstream applications like virtual try-on, digital avatars, and AR. Open questions include the computational cost of the diffusion stage at inference, whether the four-view synthesis is sufficient for complex poses or clothing, and how the method scales to multi-person scenes with inter-person occlusions (briefly mentioned via MultiHuman/OCHuman but not deeply evaluated).
