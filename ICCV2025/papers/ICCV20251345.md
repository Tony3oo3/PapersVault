## Problem

While VLMs can classify single partially occluded objects, no existing benchmark tests whether they can form spatial world models to infer multiple fully occluded objects by recognizing and extrapolating visual patterns. This paper asks: can VLMs amodally complete a pattern of objects to count those hidden behind an occluder, a task humans perform with near-perfect accuracy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251345/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image showing objects in a regular pattern (grid, circle, triangle, pyramid) with a black rectangular box occluding a subset of objects, plus a text instruction naming the target object and explicitly requesting a count that includes objects behind the occlusion. The model generates a free-form response, from which a Llama 3.1 8B-based extractor pulls a single integer; failed or incoherent extractions are assigned the maximum sMAPE penalty of 100%. Both occluded and unoccluded versions of every image are evaluated to isolate the effect of occlusion.

## Method

CAPTURE comprises two complementary splits: CAPTURE^real (924 images filtered from FSC-147 for identifiable regular patterns, with manually overlaid occluding boxes across 92 object types) and CAPTURE^synthetic (1,250 generated images with controlled variables—object count 5–15, dots or squares, rectangle/circle/pyramid arrangements, five page positions, five colors). Six VLMs (GPT-4o, InternVL2, Molmo, Qwen2-VL, MiniCPM-o 2.6, Kimi-VL-A3B) are evaluated with per-model, per-split prompt tuning and sMAPE as the primary metric. To disentangle counting failures from world-modeling failures, the authors run oracle experiments that supply visible or all object coordinates as text (reducing the task to text-based counting) and a FLUX.1-Fill inpainting pipeline that fills the occluded region with a diffusion-generated approximation, allowing them to attribute error to either visual counting or spatial inference.

## Results

On CAPTURE, the best VLM (GPT-4o) achieves 13.34% sMAPE on CAPTURE-real (unoccluded) and 14.75% on the occluded split, while the average across six VLMs is 21.95% and 27.59% respectively. On CAPTURE-synthetic, GPT-4o reaches 5.90% (unoccluded) and 9.71% (occluded), with the six-VLM average at 11.89% and 15.64%. Humans complete the occluded task with only 3.79% sMAPE on CAPTURE-real and 0.92% on CAPTURE-synthetic, roughly 7× and 14× better than the average VLM. The object-detection baseline COUNTGD achieves 3.15% sMAPE unoccluded and 10.34% occluded on CAPTURE-real, outperforming all VLMs in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o is the strongest VLM on both splits, achieving 14.75% sMAPE on CAPTURE-real (occluded) and 9.71% on CAPTURE-synthetic (occluded), but still trails the COUNTGD object-detection baseline (10.34% on real occluded) and is far below human performance (3.79% real, 0.92% synthetic). Molmo, despite explicit 'point-and-count' training, shows a large 32.49% error on CAPTURE-real occluded, the worst among the four main VLMs. Qwen2-VL suffers the largest occlusion penalty on the real split (+10.37%), while GPT-4o's penalty is smallest (+1.41%), suggesting stronger world-modeling. On CAPTURE-synthetic, Molmo's error nearly doubles from 8.40% to 17.73% with occlusion (+9.33%), the largest synthetic penalty. The hybrid COUNTGD+VLM system reduces VLM error substantially but still underperforms COUNTGD alone, confirming that VLMs remain subpar even at counting only the occluded objects. Overall, no VLM approaches human-level amodal counting, with the average four-VLM error being 7× (real) and 14× (synthetic) that of humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three previously separate threads—amodal completion (pixel-level, single-object), spatial-reasoning VQA (SpartQA, GSR-Bench), and dense counting (FSC-147, COUNTGD)—into a single VLM-appropriate task: counting patterned objects whose pattern continues behind an occluder. Prior amodal completion work targets dense prediction (segmentation, inpainting) and is ill-suited to text-output VLMs, while existing counting benchmarks assume all objects are visible. The closest prior effort, CountNet3D, addresses occluded counting but is restricted to 3D LiDAR input and retail shelves, making CAPTURE's 2D, pattern-based, multi-object formulation genuinely unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and measurable gap: even GPT-4o incurs ~15% sMAPE on occluded real images while humans achieve under 4%, and the oracle experiments cleanly decompose the failure into a visual-counting component and a world-modeling component. This decomposition is actionable—practitioners can target either the vision encoder's counting fidelity or the LLM's ability to extrapolate patterns from partial evidence. Open questions include whether scaling or better world-model pretraining closes the gap, whether diffusion-based inpainting can serve as a reliable external world model (the paper shows it only marginally helps), and whether the regular-pattern constraint limits generalization to truly irregular occluded scenes.
