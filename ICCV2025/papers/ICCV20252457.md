## Problem

Existing deep HDR reconstruction methods follow either a two-stage 'Alignment-Fusion' paradigm that loses information during pre-alignment, or a 'Fusion-Only' paradigm that lacks explicit alignment and produces ghosting artifacts. Both paradigms are empirically designed without a principled mathematical foundation, limiting their reliability and interpretability. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating HDR reconstruction as a MAP estimation problem and unfolding the resulting iterative optimization into an end-to-end trainable network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252457/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three multi-exposure LDR images (under-exposed, normal-exposed, over-exposed) along with their gamma-corrected counterparts, yielding 6-channel inputs per image that are projected into a shared feature space via shallow 3×3 convolutions. The normal-exposed image serves as the spatial reference, and the output is a single reconstructed HDR image produced by a Sigmoid-activated convolution on the final feature map with a residual connection to the reference feature. Training uses 128×128 patches with a combined L1 and VGG-19 perceptual loss computed in the μ-law tone-mapped domain (μ=5000) rather than the raw HDR domain.

## Method

AFUNet formulates multi-exposure HDR reconstruction as a MAP problem with two spatial correspondence prior terms that explicitly model alignment between non-reference LDRs and the reference, then applies Half-Quadratic Splitting to decouple the fusion subproblem into proximal updates and a closed-form data-consistency step. The resulting alternating alignment-fusion iteration is unfolded into T=4 stacked Alignment Fusion Modules (AFMs), each containing: a Spatial Alignment Module (window-based cross-attention transformer, acting as a learned gradient descent operator), a Spatial Fusion Module (Transformer-based), two Channel Fusion Modules (Restormer-style channel attention transformers serving as proximal operators), and a Data Consistency Module (implementing the closed-form least-squares update with learned degradation matrices D₁, D₃, D₂ᵀ, and B⁻¹ via independent MLPs). All stages share the same architecture but have independent parameters, and the entire network is trained end-to-end with the Adam optimizer over 400 epochs. The key design choice is performing alignment before fusion within each iteration (the 'AF' paradigm), which the ablation shows outperforms the reverse 'FA' ordering by 0.19 dB in PSNR-μ.

## Results

On Kalantari's dataset (the most comprehensive evaluation with 15 competing methods), AFUNet achieves 44.91 dB PSNR-μ, 42.59 dB PSNR-l, 0.9923 SSIM-μ, 0.9906 SSIM-l, and 66.75 HDR-VDP2, ranking first on PSNR-μ, SSIM-μ, SSIM-l, and HDR-VDP2. On Hu's dataset, AFUNet reaches 48.83 dB PSNR-μ and 52.13 dB PSNR-l, and on Tel's dataset it achieves 43.31 dB PSNR-μ and 47.83 dB PSNR-l, both leading all compared methods. The model uses 4 iterative stages by default, with a 3-stage variant already surpassing prior SOTA in PSNR-μ and SSIM-μ on Kalantari's dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFUNet outperforms the strongest Transformer-based baselines CA-ViT and SCTNet by 0.59 dB and 0.48 dB in PSNR-μ, respectively, on Kalantari's dataset, and leads on all four PSNR/SSIM metrics plus HDR-VDP2. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor LFDiff, AFUNet gains +0.15 dB PSNR-μ and +0.21 HDR-VDP2 while matching PSNR-l and SSIM-l exactly. Notably, SAFNet achieves a higher PSNR-l (43.18 vs. 42.59 dB) on Kalantari's dataset, indicating AFUNet's advantage is concentrated in the tone-mapped domain and perceptual quality rather than linear-domain fidelity. On Hu's dataset, AFUNet leads LFDiff by +0.09 dB PSNR-μ and +0.09 dB PSNR-l, and on Tel's dataset it surpasses SCTNet by +0.76 dB PSNR-μ and +0.32 dB PSNR-l. Across all three benchmark datasets, AFUNet consistently ranks first or second on every metric, with the only loss being PSNR-l on Kalantari's dataset where SAFNet's selective alignment strategy yields a 0.59 dB advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFUNet departs from both dominant HDR reconstruction paradigms—the sequential 'Alignment-Fusion' pipeline and the implicit 'Fusion-Only' approach—by formulating the problem as a MAP estimation with spatial correspondence priors and solving it via cross-iterative (alternating) alignment and fusion. The iterative scheme is grounded in Half-Quadratic Splitting (Geman & Yang, 1995) to decouple data fidelity from regularization, while the learned modules draw on Swin Transformer's window-based cross-attention for alignment and Restormer-style channel attention for fusion. Unlike the prior deep-unfolding HDR work of Mai et al. (2022), which models HDR as low-rank tensor completion, AFUNet avoids that restrictive assumption and instead learns the degradation operators and proximal mappings directly, making it more general for real-world multi-exposure scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mathematical foundation (MAP + HQS + unfolding) for what has largely been empirical architecture design in deep HDR reconstruction, which could guide future work toward more interpretable and theoretically grounded models. However, the quantitative gains over strong baselines (LFDiff, SAFNet) are modest—typically 0.1–0.3 dB in PSNR—raising the question of whether the added theoretical structure yields meaningful practical advantages beyond marginal SOTA improvements. Open questions include whether the cross-iterative formulation scales to more than three exposures, whether the learned degradation operators generalize to unseen camera systems, and whether the interpretability benefit of unfolding translates into better robustness under distribution shift.
