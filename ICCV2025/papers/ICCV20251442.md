## Problem

Existing camera-trajectory-guided image-to-video generation methods rely on relative-scale camera parameters, causing scale inconsistencies when applied to real-world scenes where metric depth is critical. Additionally, users lack an intuitive way to specify precise camera trajectories for arbitrary images without prior knowledge of scene depth or scale, making these methods difficult to use in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image, a text prompt, and a user-drawn camera trajectory in an interactively rendered 3D point cloud (constructed from monocular metric depth via Depth Anything V2). At inference, the user drags within the 3D scene to design trajectories and receives real-time preview video rendering before committing to the costly diffusion generation. Outputs include 16-frame camera-controlled videos at 256×256 or 512×320 resolution, as well as loop videos and generative frame interpolations via different input concatenation modes.

## Method

RealCam-I2V integrates monocular metric depth estimation as a preprocessing step to build a metric-scale 3D point cloud from the reference image, which serves as a unified spatial reference for both training and inference. During training, COLMAP-reconstructed camera extrinsics (relative scale) are aligned to the metric depth point cloud via a per-frame scale factor, converting all camera trajectories to absolute metric scale before conditioning the diffusion model. At inference, users interactively draw camera trajectories in the 3D scene with preview rendering, and a proposed scene-constrained noise shaping mechanism overlays preview-video pixels onto the model-predicted clean latent at high-noise timesteps (t > 0.9) to constrain initial layout and camera dynamics while preserving generative capacity in lower noise stages. The entire approach is implemented as a plugin on DynamiCrafter, freezing the base model and depth predictor while training only the camera-conditioning and noise-shaping parameters.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, RealCam-I2V achieves the best camera control across all metrics: RotErr 0.3884, TransErr (metric) 2.2317, CamMC (metric) 2.3609, and FVD (StyleGAN) 45.460. Compared to the prior SOTA CamI2V, RealCam-I2V delivers over 30% improvement on metric-scale translation and camera-pose metrics (TransErr 3.29→2.23, CamMC 3.42→2.36) and over 13% improvement on FVD (62.4→53.7 VideoGPT, 53.4→45.5 StyleGAN). On Vbench-I2V, the full model scores 85.71 total with a Camera Motion sub-score of 93.32, versus 22.67 for the unconditioned DynamiCrafter base.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealCam-I2V outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor CamI2V (which uses Plücker embeddings and epipolar attention), the gains are modest on relative-scale metrics (RotErr 0.412→0.388, TransErr-rel 1.341→1.294) but dramatic on metric-scale metrics (TransErr-metric 3.293→2.232, a 32% reduction; CamMC-metric 3.423→2.361, a 31% reduction), confirming that metric-scale alignment is the primary source of improvement. Against CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl, which rely on relative-scale camera conditioning, RealCam-I2V reduces metric TransErr by 59% and 67% respectively, and FVD (StyleGAN) by 23% and 25%. The unconditioned DynamiCrafter base shows the largest gap (FVD 92.2→45.5), illustrating the combined benefit of camera conditioning plus metric alignment plus noise shaping. On Vbench-I2V, the Camera Motion sub-score jumps from 22.67 (DynamiCrafter) to 93.32 (RealCam-I2V), while Dynamic Degree is preserved (35.77 vs. 34.15), showing that camera control does not sacrifice video dynamism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DynamiCrafter as its base image-to-video diffusion model and departs from the line of camera-trajectory-guided methods (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, CamCo, CamI2V) that condition on relative-scale camera extrinsics. The key conceptual departure is introducing monocular metric depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) as a preprocessing step to construct a metric-scale 3D point cloud, then aligning COLMAP SfM reconstructions to this metric scale during training. The scene-constrained noise shaping mechanism draws on SDEdit and DDIM inversion ideas but applies them to camera-trajectory preview frames in a 3D scene, rather than to image editing. The interactive 3D scene interface for trajectory drawing is a novel usability contribution not present in prior trajectory-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental practical gap: existing camera-controlled I2V methods are unusable for arbitrary real-world images because users lack depth and scene-scale information to specify trajectories. The finding that a simple metric-scale alignment preprocessing step yields >30% improvements on metric-scale camera metrics across multiple prior methods (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, CamI2V) suggests the field's reliance on relative-scale training was a significant hidden limitation. However, the work is constrained by training exclusively on static RealEstate10K scenes, and the authors acknowledge no suitable dynamic-scene dataset with metric camera annotations currently exists, leaving the generalization to dynamic content as an open question.
