## Problem

Conventional text-to-image generation treats text as a conditioning signal that guides denoising from Gaussian noise, introducing complex conditioning mechanisms and noise scheduling. While flow matching can in principle learn direct mappings between arbitrary distributions, prior cross-modal attempts like CrossFlow still operate on 2D latent representations, adding computational overhead that undermines efficiency. The paper asks whether text and image modalities can be directly transformed into each other within a unified, compact latent space without any conditioning machinery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250686/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, a text prompt is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into 77 tokens of 768 dimensions, then projected into a 77×16 latent space and flowed into image tokens of the same shape, which are decoded by a 1D image VAE decoder into a 256×256 RGB image. For image-to-text generation, a 256×256 RGB image is encoded into 77×16 image tokens, flowed into text tokens, and decoded by a text decoder into CLIP tokenizer indices (i.e., a caption). The unified 77×16 latent shape is 3.3× smaller than the 32×32×4 shape used by standard 2D flow matching at 256px resolution, and only 20 sampling steps are needed at inference.

## Method

FlowTok encodes both modalities into a shared compact 1D latent space of shape 77×16: images via an enhanced TA-TiTok tokenizer (ViT-B encoder with RoPE and SwiGLU FFN, 77 latent tokens of 16 dimensions), and text via a frozen CLIP encoder followed by a 6-block Transformer projector with KL-divergence regularization to model text tokens as a Gaussian distribution. A CLIP-style contrastive alignment loss (with a learnable temperature) is applied between the projected text tokens and an MLP-mapped version of the original CLIP embeddings to preserve semantic information during dimensionality reduction. The generative model is a stack of DiT blocks performing vanilla flow matching directly between text tokens (source) and image tokens (target), using only self-attention—no cross-attention, no concatenation, and no noise scheduling. Training combines the flow matching velocity loss with the KL and alignment losses (γ₁=1e-4, γ₂=1), and the same formulation is simply reversed for image-to-text generation by swapping source and target distributions.

## Results

On COCO FID-30K, FlowTok-H (1.1B) achieves 9.67, roughly on par with CrossFlow (9.63) and SD1.5 (9.62), while FlowTok-XL (698M) reaches 10.06. On MJHQ-30K FID, FlowTok-H achieves 7.15, surpassing SDXL (8.76), PixArt-α (9.85), and Show-o (14.99) despite using only publicly available data. In terms of efficiency, FlowTok-H trains in 26.1 8-A100 days (vs. 1041.6 for SD2.1) and samples at 18.2 images/s (vs. 1.1 for CrossFlow and 1.0 for Show-o), a 20× speedup over CrossFlow. The compact 1D latent (77×16) is 3.3× smaller than standard 2D latents (32×32×4), enabling 8K batch size on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CrossFlow (the closest paradigm using text as source distribution), FlowTok-H matches COCO FID-30K (9.67 vs. 9.63) while being 20× faster at inference (18.2 vs. 1.1 img/s) and training 3× cheaper (26.1 vs. 78.8 8-A100 days); FlowTok-XL further reduces training to 20.4 days. On MJHQ-30K, FlowTok-H (7.15) substantially outperforms SDXL (8.76), PixArt-α (9.85), and Show-o (14.99), despite using only open-source data, while FlowTok-XL (7.68) already beats all of them. On COCO FID-30K, FlowTok-H (9.67) is slightly behind PixArt-α (7.32) and Show-o (9.24), but these models use proprietary data and larger parameter counts. For image-to-text generation on COCO, FlowTok-XL outperforms CrossFlow on BLEU-4 (37.1 vs. 36.4) and CIDEr (117.0 vs. 116.2), and is competitive with SCD-Net (37.3 B@4, 118.0 CIDEr) while operating under a simpler direct-flow formulation. Overall, FlowTok trades a small COCO FID gap against proprietary-data models for dramatically lower training cost, faster inference, and superior aesthetic quality on MJHQ-30K.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowTok departs from the dominant text-to-image paradigm where text serves as a conditioning signal (via cross-attention, concatenation, or embeddings) guiding denoising from Gaussian noise, instead treating text tokens as the source distribution for flow matching directly into image tokens. It builds on TA-TiTok's 1D image tokenization (enhanced with RoPE and SwiGLU) and the DiT architecture, while contrasting with CrossFlow, which performs cross-modal flow matching but in a 2D latent space requiring a text VAE. The key conceptual shift is unifying both modalities into a compact 77×16 1D token space, eliminating the need for noise scheduling and complex conditioning mechanisms entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.3× latent compression and resulting 20× inference speedup over CrossFlow (and 10× over Show-o) make text-to-image research dramatically more accessible, with FlowTok-H trainable on just 8 A100 GPUs versus 32–64 for comparable models. The symmetric formulation naturally extends to image-to-text generation without architectural changes, suggesting a general template for cross-modal flow. Open questions include whether the fixed 77-token CLIP constraint limits expressiveness at higher resolutions (the paper only evaluates at 256px), whether the 1D bottleneck degrades fine-grained spatial detail, and how the approach scales to additional modalities beyond text and image.
