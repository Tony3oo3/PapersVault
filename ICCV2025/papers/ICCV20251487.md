## Problem

Existing diffusion distillation methods face a persistent trade-off for few-step generation: distribution matching (e.g., DMD, SiD) is designed for one-step output and lacks flexibility to extract extra information with more sampling steps, while trajectory matching (e.g., progressive distillation, LCM) operates at the instance level and yields suboptimal image quality due to the difficulty of matching full ODE trajectories with limited model capacity. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-quality few-step generation, flexible step adjustment, and low training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251487/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an initial Gaussian noise sample as input, producing a generated image (512×512 for SD-v1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL and PixArt-α) or video (CogVideoX-2B) in as few as 1–4 NFEs. Training is entirely image-free, requiring only text prompts from JourneyDB; no paired image data is needed. The resulting generator supports flexible deterministic sampling at any step count M ≤ K by conditioning on the desired number of steps, without retraining.

## Method

TDM unifies trajectory distillation and distribution matching by aligning the student's ODE trajectory with the teacher's at the distribution level via a marginal reverse KL divergence computed at each intermediate timestep, rather than enforcing point-to-point instance matching. A separate fake score network approximates the student's score (trained with importance-sampled denoising), making the objective image-free and avoiding costly teacher ODE solving. A sampling-steps-aware objective conditions both student and fake score on the target step count K, decoupling learning targets across steps to enable flexible deterministic sampling. A Pseudo-Huber surrogate loss (inspired by consistency models) normalizes the gradient for more stable optimization. The full pipeline trains a K-step generator in as few as 500 iterations (2 A800 hours for PixArt-α), outperforming the multi-step teacher on human preference at 1024 resolution.

## Results

On SDXL (4-step), TDM achieves 34.88 HPS, 6.28 AeS, and 36.08 CLIP Score, surpassing SDXL-Lightning (32.71 HPS), Hyper-SD (34.14 HPS), and DMD2 (31.46 HPS) while requiring only 2 A800 days of training (vs. 160 A100 days for DMD2). On PixArt-α (4-step, 1024px), TDM reaches 33.21 HPS and 6.42 AeS, outperforming its 25-step teacher (32.21 HPS) with just 500 training iterations and 2 A800 hours (~0.01% of teacher training cost). For text-to-video, TDM distilled from CogVideoX-2B improves VBench total score from 80.91 to 81.65 using only 4 NFE. On SD-v1.5, TDM-unify-SFT achieves 31.31 HPS at 4 steps and 28.90 HPS at 1 step, both state-of-the-art among distillation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDM outperforms all 11 compared baselines on their respective backbones. On SDXL (4-step), TDM (34.88 HPS) beats SDXL-Lightning by +2.17 HPS, Hyper-SD by +0.74 HPS, and DMD2 by +3.42 HPS, while also exceeding the 25-step teacher (33.19 HPS). On PixArt-α (4-step), TDM (33.21 HPS) surpasses LCM-1024 by +2.66 HPS and YOSO-512 by +2.61 HPS, and notably outperforms its own 25-step teacher (32.21 HPS). On SD-v1.5 (4-step), TDM-unify-SFT (31.31 HPS) leads DMD2 (29.49) by +1.82 and Hyper-SD (30.24) by +1.07; at 1-step, TDM-unify-SFT (28.90) exceeds Hyper-SD (28.01) and InstaFlow (22.98) by large margins. Training cost is dramatically lower: TDM requires 2 A800 days for SDXL vs. 160 A100 days for DMD2 and 32 A100 days for LCM, and only 2 A800 hours for PixArt-α vs. 14.5 A100 days for LCM. For text-to-video, TDM (4 NFE) improves VBench total score from 80.91 (CogVideoX-2B) to 81.65, outperforming its teacher with 10× fewer NFEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDM unifies two previously separate paradigms in diffusion distillation: distribution matching via score distillation (DMD, Diff-Instruct, SiD), which is designed for one-step generation and lacks multi-step flexibility, and trajectory distillation (Progressive Distillation, LCM, PeRFlow), which performs instance-level ODE trajectory matching but suffers from numerical errors and high model capacity requirements. The key conceptual departure is aligning student and teacher trajectories at the *distribution level* rather than the instance level, using an image-free reverse KL objective derived from score distillation (Diff-Instruct, VSD) while retaining the multi-step structure of trajectory distillation. The surrogate training objective with Pseudo-Huber metric is borrowed from Consistency Models (iCT), and the sampling-steps-aware conditioning extends the fixed-step deterministic sampling of prior methods like SDXL-Lightning and PeRFlow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in training cost (2 A800 hours for PixArt-α vs. 160 A100 days for DMD2 on SDXL) makes high-quality few-step distillation accessible to small research groups, potentially democratizing diffusion acceleration research. The finding that a 4-step student can outperform its multi-step teacher (attributed to avoiding PF-ODE numerical errors) challenges the assumption that more sampling steps always yield better quality. Open questions include whether the image-free approach's performance ceiling (bounded by the teacher) can be broken without expensive GAN-based data injection, and whether the distribution-level trajectory matching generalizes to even more complex generation tasks beyond text-to-image and short video.
