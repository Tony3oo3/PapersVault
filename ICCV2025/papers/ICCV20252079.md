## Problem

Transferring pretrained representations from vision models to diffusion-based generative models is hindered by input mismatch (clean vs. noisy images), architecture mismatch (pixel vs. VAE latent space), and divergent loss functions. Prior acceleration methods like REPA rely on external pretrained backbones (e.g., DINOv2), adding substantial GPU memory and training overhead. The paper asks whether a single self-supervised pretraining paradigm can simultaneously improve both image generation and recognition without any external model dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252079/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, RGB images (224×224) are encoded by a frozen SD-VAE into 28×28×4 latent features, then further divided into 14×14×h patch tokens via a 2×2 PatchConv layer; 75% of patches are randomly masked and reconstructed by an asymmetric decoder. For downstream generation, the pretrained ViT encoder and PatchConv initialize DiT or SiT models that produce 256×256 ImageNet samples; for understanding, the same weights initialize a ViT classifier (ImageNet-1k) or segmentation backbone (ADE20K). The VAE normalization (mean=0.5, std=0.5) is used instead of standard ImageNet normalization to maintain distribution consistency between pretraining and fine-tuning.

## Method

USP performs masked latent modeling: a frozen VAE encodes the input image, a 2×2 PatchConv converts the latent into patch tokens, a standard ViT encoder processes unmasked patches, and an 8-block asymmetric decoder reconstructs masked patches using per-patch-normalized MSE loss in latent space. The key architectural insight is that operating in VAE latent space naturally aligns the pretraining input distribution with that of latent diffusion models, eliminating the clean-vs-noisy mismatch. For generation initialization, the AdaLN-Zero layers are modified to reintroduce trainable scale (γ) and bias (β) parameters so that pretrained backbone weights transfer seamlessly into DiT/SiT, and positional embeddings are bicubically interpolated from 224×224 to 256×256. After pretraining, the decoder is discarded and only the ViT encoder plus PatchConv are retained, meaning downstream tasks incur zero additional training cost or memory overhead. This yields 11.7× and 46.6× convergence speedups on DiT-XL/2 and SiT-XL/2 respectively, while maintaining competitive linear-probe and fine-tuning accuracy on ImageNet and ADE20K segmentation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 generation, USP-initialized DiT-XL/2 achieves FID 9.73 at 400K steps (vs. 19.94 for the vanilla DiT baseline) and FID 8.93 at 1.2M steps (vs. 9.62 at 7M steps), yielding an 11.7× convergence speedup. On the SiT-XL/2 flow-matching framework, USP achieves FID 7.38 at 400K steps versus 16.97 for the vanilla SiT baseline (46.6× speedup). For image understanding, ViT-Base-USP attains 66.9% linear-probe accuracy on ImageNet (vs. 65.1% for MAE) and 48.6% mIoU on ADE20K segmentation (vs. 48.1% for MAE at 1600 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USP consistently outperforms all external-model-based acceleration methods. Against REPA (DINOv2-B), USP achieves lower FID at every model size (e.g., 9.73 vs. 12.3 on DiT-XL/2, 7.38 vs. 7.9 on SiT-XL/2) without requiring a frozen teacher backbone or extra GPU memory. Against ReaLS (DINOv2-L), USP wins by 8.2 FID on DiT-B/2 (27.1 vs. 35.27) and by 6.9 FID on SiT-B/2 (22.10 vs. 27.53). Against EQ-VAE, USP leads by 7.0 FID on DiT-XL/2 (9.73 vs. 14.5) and by 4.0 FID on SiT-B/2 (22.10 vs. 31.2). Against the vanilla DiT and SiT baselines, USP reduces FID by roughly 50% at 400K steps and matches the 7M-step DiT-XL/2 FID (9.62) in only 1.2M steps. On image understanding, USP matches or exceeds MAE on linear-probe accuracy (66.9% vs. 65.1% Base; 74.5% vs. 73.7% Large) and segmentation mIoU (48.6% vs. 48.1%), while outperforming SimMIM (56.7% LP) and SemMAE (65.0% LP) on linear probing. Notably, USP is orthogonal to REPA and VAVAE: combining USP+REPA yields FID 6.26 and USP+VAVAE yields FID 4.2 (with CFG), both surpassing either method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

USP extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE) by shifting the reconstruction objective from pixel space into the frozen VAE latent space, thereby decoupling pretraining from the noisy-input requirement of diffusion models. It departs from representation-alignment methods like REPA, which require an external pretrained backbone (e.g., DINOv2) as a teacher during diffusion training, by instead performing a single self-supervised pretraining pass whose weights directly initialize the diffusion model. The approach also builds on the observation that diffusion models learn discriminative features (DDAE, REPA) but reframes this: rather than aligning to an external representation during generation training, USP pretrains the representation itself in a task-agnostic latent space and transfers it via weight initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.7× and 46.6× convergence accelerations on DiT-XL/2 and SiT-XL/2, respectively, represent a substantial practical efficiency gain for training diffusion transformers, and the method adds zero extra training cost or GPU memory overhead at the downstream stage. Its demonstrated orthogonality to REPA and VAVAE (combining yields further gains) positions it as a composable building block rather than a competing paradigm. Limitations include evaluation only at 256×256 on ImageNet, reliance on a frozen VAE whose reconstruction quality caps understanding performance, and 'comparable' rather than superior recognition accuracy versus MAE. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video generation, or higher resolutions, and whether the VAE choice can be co-optimized with the pretraining objective.
