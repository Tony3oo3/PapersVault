## Problem

Instruction-guided image editing with diffusion models is highly sensitive to the initial noise seed, producing inconsistent results ranging from severe background distortion to no meaningful edit. Users must manually trial multiple seeds with full inference, which scales linearly in cost and lacks an established framework for early-stage filtering. Existing seed selection methods for text-to-image generation rely on external verifiers (e.g., CLIPScore, aesthetic models) that require full inference, making them impractical for efficient candidate elimination in image-to-image editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a text edit instruction, and a set of N candidate random seeds as input, and outputs a single best edited image (the selected seed fully denoised to completion) along with a per-seed Background Inconsistency Score ranking. All computation occurs in the VAE latent space: the source image is encoded once, and candidate latents are denoised only up to a stopping timestep t_stop (typically 60–70 out of 100) before selection. The method is model-agnostic, supporting both DDIM-based diffusion models and rectified flow models with corresponding Tweedie's formula or velocity-field-based latent estimation.

## Method

ELECT (Early-timestep Latent Evaluation for Candidate selecTion) is a zero-shot, training-free framework that selects the optimal seed by computing a Background Inconsistency Score (BIS) at an early denoising timestep rather than after full inference. The BIS is defined as the Hadamard product of a soft background mask—derived from the squared mean edit relevance map aggregated across all candidate seeds and timesteps in [80,100]—and the absolute difference between the Tweedie-estimated denoised latent and the source image latent, effectively measuring unwanted background changes in a self-supervised, relative manner. By stopping denoising at t_stop and evaluating only the latent estimate, ELECT reduces NFE by 41% on average (up to 61%) while matching or surpassing full-inference Best-of-N selection. The framework further extends to joint seed-and-prompt selection by incorporating an MLLM (GPT-4o) that generates alternative instruction prompts when seed selection alone yields failures, improving VIEScore by +0.56 on average for out-of-distribution instructions.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, ELECT (N=11 seeds, t_stop=60, 500 NFE) consistently outperforms both single-seed Vanilla (100 NFE) and Best-of-N by S^BIS (500 NFE) across all five editing models. For example, on IP2P, ELECT reduces background MSE from 248.5 (Vanilla) to 127.5 (ELECT) vs. 146.2 (Best-of-N), while improving VIEScore min(BC,IF) from 3.43 to 3.67. On the more challenging MagicBrush test set, ELECT achieves MSE 75.9 vs. 139.2 (Vanilla) and 80.4 (Best-of-N) for MagicBrush, with VIEScore min improving from 3.99 to 4.22. The paper reports an average 41% reduction in NFE (up to 61%) compared to full-inference Best-of-N for comparable performance, and a +0.56 average VIEScore gain when extending to joint seed+prompt selection via MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELECT is evaluated against five instruction-guided editing models (IP2P, MagicBrush, InsDiff, MGIE, UltraEdit), each compared in three settings: single-seed Vanilla, full-inference Best-of-N by S^BIS (500 NFE), and ELECT (500 NFE, early stopping at t_stop=60). ELECT wins on all background-consistency metrics (MSE, LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) and VIEScore min(BC,IF) across every model. The largest gains appear on IP2P (MSE reduced by 48.6% vs. Vanilla, 12.8% vs. Best-of-N) and InsDiff (MSE reduced by 51.5% vs. Vanilla, 13.6% vs. Best-of-N), where single-seed outputs suffer severe background distortion. On UltraEdit (Rectified Flow), ELECT's advantage over Best-of-N is smaller (MSE 63.8 vs. 67.0) but still consistent, with the largest LPIPS improvement (92.3 vs. 96.3). On MagicBrush, ELECT achieves the best MSE and VIEScore but a marginally lower PSNR than Best-of-N (26.13 vs. 26.25), indicating near-parity on this harder dataset. The key efficiency claim is that ELECT matches or exceeds Best-of-N performance while using fewer effective denoising steps per candidate (60 vs. 100), enabling evaluation of more seeds (N=11) within the same NFE budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELECT extends the Best-of-N inference-time scaling paradigm—well established for LLMs and recently adapted to T2I diffusion models—into the instruction-guided I2I editing domain, where no prior framework existed for early-stage seed filtering. It builds directly on WYS's edit relevance map [31] as a proxy for foreground/background segmentation, modifying the formulation to extract latents from the denoising trajectory rather than injecting explicit noise. The key conceptual departure from prior seed-selection methods (rejection sampling, noise resampling, seed optimization) is the shift from absolute quality metrics requiring external verifiers to a self-supervised, relative background-consistency criterion (BIS) computed at early timesteps via Tweedie's formula, eliminating the need for full inference or auxiliary scoring models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 41–61% NFE reduction addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: users of instruction-guided editing tools currently resort to manual trial-and-error across seeds, and prior automated approaches require expensive external verifiers or full inference for every candidate. ELECT's model-agnostic, zero-shot design (validated across five editing architectures including both DDIM and rectified-flow models) makes it immediately deployable as a plug-in layer. Open questions include whether the relative BIS scoring generalizes to multi-edit or video editing scenarios, whether the acknowledged over-optimization-for-preservation failure mode becomes more prevalent on harder distributions, and whether the MLLM-based prompt selection component can be made more principled than its current heuristic 0/0.5/1 scoring.
