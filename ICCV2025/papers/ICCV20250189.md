## Problem

Existing text-to-motion datasets (HumanML3D, KIT-ML) provide only coarse captions that omit specific body part movements and their timing, making it impossible to answer questions like when a hand should be raised or how long a wave should last. Prior attempts to enrich captions via LLMs (e.g., Action-GPT) generate plausible but misaligned descriptions because the LLM has no access to the actual motion sequence. This paper addresses both gaps by constructing a large-scale dataset with strictly motion-aligned, fine-grained spatial and temporal annotations.

## Input / Output

For dataset construction, the pipeline takes SMPL pose parameters of motion sequences (pre-processed to 20 FPS, max 10 s, re-targeted to a standard skeleton) and produces two annotation types: BPM snippet descriptions (avg. 18 words per 0.5 s snippet) and BPM paragraphs (avg. 247 words per full sequence). For the generation benchmark, models receive concatenated embeddings of a coarse HumanML3D caption and the detailed BPM text (with <Motionless> tokens for empty snippets and <SEP> tokens preserving temporal order), and output a 263-dimensional 3D motion sequence. The fixed 0.5 s snippet duration is chosen to minimize start-end pose similarity while respecting PoseFix's maximum pose-pair time difference.

## Method

The dataset construction pipeline has three stages: (1) segment each motion into fixed 0.5 s snippets, (2) apply PoseFix's correctional text generation model to each snippet's start-end pose pair to produce a body-part-movement description, and (3) use Gemini to organize all snippet descriptions into a coherent, diverse paragraph. For benchmarking, three classical text-to-motion models (MDM, T2M-GPT, MoMask) are adapted by replacing CLIP with T5-Base to handle long detailed text, applying mean pooling over the encoder output, and training on concatenated coarse+fine text embeddings. The zero-shot editing pipeline leverages the same construction pipeline at inference: a coarse caption generates an initial motion, the pipeline extracts its BPM descriptions, the user edits them, and the adapted model regenerates the motion from the modified text—enabling spatial and temporal edits without retraining.

## Results

On the FineMotion benchmark, (T&DT)-MoMask(BPMP) achieves the best overall performance with FID of 0.055 and R-Precision Top-3 of 0.813, outperforming all baselines including FineMoGen (FID 0.151, R-Top3 0.784) and T2M-GPT (FID 0.123, R-Top3 0.781). The most striking improvement is for MDM, where adding fine-grained text boosts Top-3 retrieval accuracy from 0.606 to 0.759 (+15.3%) and FID from 3.137 to 0.488. (T&DT)2M-GPT(BPMSD) achieves FID 0.091 and R-Top3 0.789, while (T&DT)-MoMask(BPMSD) reaches FID 0.088 and R-Top3 0.811. All three adapted variants consistently outperform their coarse-text counterparts trained on HumanML3D alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

(T&DT)-MoMask dominates all baselines on both FID and R-Precision, achieving FID 0.055 and R-Top3 0.813, surpassing FineMoGen (FID 0.151, R-Top3 0.784), Fg-T2M (FID 0.243, R-Top3 0.783), and T2M-GPT (FID 0.123, R-Top3 0.781). The largest relative gain is observed for MDM, where fine-grained text improves Top-3 retrieval by +15.3% (0.606→0.759) and reduces FID from 3.137 to 0.488, indicating that the diffusion-based model benefits most from temporal alignment cues. (T&DT)2M-GPT is competitive with FineMoGen and Fg-T2M on R-Precision while achieving a lower FID (0.091 vs. 0.151/0.243), though it trades some multi-modality (1.594 vs. 2.696/1.614). All three FineMotion variants sacrifice some motion diversity (MModality) compared to their coarse-text counterparts, which the authors attribute to the constraining effect of fine-grained descriptions. In the zero-shot editing user study, the proposed pipeline outperforms both T2M-GPT and FLAME on meeting editing requirements and naturalness, with a particular advantage in temporal editing (adjusting motion length).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-motion generation line by addressing the granularity gap in existing motion-language datasets. It builds directly on HumanML3D for motion sequences and on PoseFix for generating correctional body-part-movement text between pose pairs, while leveraging Gemini to organize snippet descriptions into coherent paragraphs. It departs from prior text-enhancement approaches (Action-GPT, SemanticBoost, MotionScript) that relied on LLMs to hallucinate descriptions without motion alignment, and from FineMoGen/HuMMan-MoGen which required extensive manual annotation of temporal boundaries, by proposing a fully automated, scalable pipeline that produces temporally precise and motion-aligned annotations at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FineMotion provides the first large-scale (442k snippet + 95k paragraph descriptions) dataset with strictly aligned spatial and temporal body-part annotations, enabling fine-grained motion generation and zero-shot editing that were previously infeasible with coarse captions. The demonstrated +15.3% Top-3 improvement for MDM and the successful zero-shot editing pipeline validate the dataset's practical utility. However, the fine-grained constraints reduce motion diversity (lower MModality), and the re-generation-based editing may introduce unintended changes outside the edited region. Open questions include whether the 0.5s snippet granularity generalizes to faster or more complex motions, and whether the pipeline extends beyond the HumanML3D/AMASS motion distribution.
