## Problem

Adversarial Training (AT) creates an inherent accuracy-robustness trade-off, commonly attributed to insufficient learning of hard adversarial samples. This paper reveals a counterintuitive finding: failure cases are actually learned *better* (with smaller perception change) than success cases under the conventional AT objective, meaning the real culprit is over-sufficient perception consistency that forces the model to ignore perturbation information needed to position the decision boundary appropriately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250351/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels via softmax over C classes. During training, each benign sample x is paired with a PGD-generated adversarial sample x′ and an interpolation point x̃ = x + α·(x′−x); the model's logits at all three points are used to compute the Robust Perception regularization, making the training loop require three forward passes per sample in addition to the standard adversarial forward pass.

## Method

RPAT introduces a new AT objective called Robust Perception, which requires that the model's perception (logits) change proportionally to the perturbation magnitude along the path from benign to adversarial sample, rather than remaining nearly constant as conventional AT encourages. Concretely, it appends an MSE (or KL/JS/Cosine) regularization term to the standard cross-entropy AT loss that penalizes the mismatch between the normalized perception change from x to the interpolation point x̃ and from x̃ to x′, enforcing a smooth linear transition. Theoretically, this objective limits second-order nonlinear effects (via Hessian) and bounds the Jacobian variation (via Lipschitz constant), both of which smooth the decision boundary. The method is architecture-agnostic and plugs into any existing AT framework (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, Consistency-AT, ReBAT) by simply adding the regularization term, with a single hyperparameter λ controlling its weight and α (default 0.5) setting the interpolation point.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreActResNet-18 under ℓ∞ norm, RPAT++ achieves 82.63% clean accuracy and 51.00% Auto-Attack robustness (Mean 66.82, NRR 63.07), surpassing the previous SOTA ReBAT (82.09/50.72/66.41/62.70). Under ℓ2 norm, RPAT++ reaches 89.06% clean and 71.26% AA robustness (Mean 80.16, NRR 79.17), the best in the comparison. On CIFAR-100 ℓ∞, RPAT++ attains 56.84% clean and 27.68% AA (Mean 42.26, NRR 37.23), again outperforming all 12 SOTA baselines. With WideResNet-34-10 on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞, RPAT+++ (with CutMix) achieves 87.57% clean and 55.79% AA (Mean 71.68, NRR 68.16), setting a new SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPAT++ (built upon ReBAT) outperforms all 12 SOTA baselines on every reported metric for both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under ℓ∞ norm. On CIFAR-10 ℓ∞, it gains +0.54% clean accuracy and +0.28% AA robustness over ReBAT (the strongest prior method), while also improving Mean by +0.41 and NRR by +0.37. On CIFAR-100 ℓ∞, the gains over ReBAT are +0.71% clean, +0.08% AA, +0.40 Mean, and +0.23 NRR. Under ℓ2 norm, RPAT++ achieves the best scores in the table (89.06/71.26 on CIFAR-10; 65.63/42.85 on CIFAR-100), with a particularly large NRR advantage of +2.47 over the next best (KD+SWA) on CIFAR-10. With WideResNet-34-10 on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞, RPAT++ beats ReBAT by +1.51% clean and +0.19% AA, and the CutMix-augmented RPAT+++ further extends the lead to 87.57/55.79 (Mean 71.68, NRR 68.16), surpassing ReBAT* (86.66/55.64). The method also consistently improves upon the four standard AT baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, Consistency-AT) when the Robust Perception term is appended, with Consistency-AT+RPAT showing the largest gains (e.g., +0.70% clean and +1.26% AA on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard adversarial training (AT) min-max formulation (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART) and the SOTA ReBAT method, appending a new Robust Perception regularization term to their objectives. Its key conceptual departure is from the dominant narrative in the accuracy-robustness trade-off literature (GAIRAT, MAIL, MMA, TE, SOVR, etc.), which attributes the trade-off to *insufficient* learning of hard adversarial samples; here the authors show the opposite—that failure cases are already *over-learned* in perception consistency, and the real bottleneck is decision-boundary placement. The theoretical grounding connects to the local linearity hypothesis (Goodfellow et al.) and Lipschitz regularization literature, positioning Robust Perception as a principled bridge between these two perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing the trade-off as an over-learning problem rather than an under-learning one, this work redirects the design space for future AT methods: instead of reweighting or adaptively perturbing hard samples, one should preserve gradient signal along the perturbation direction to guide boundary placement. The method is lightweight (a single interpolation-based MSE/KL term) and plugs into any existing AT pipeline, making it broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the ideal Robust Perception objective (exact proportionality for all α ∈ [0,1]) can be better approximated, whether the insight generalizes to non-image modalities or larger-scale models, and whether the over-learning phenomenon persists under stronger attacks or different threat models.
