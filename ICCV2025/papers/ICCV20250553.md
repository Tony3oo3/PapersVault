## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D point trackers are constrained by their reliance on ground-truth 3D trajectories for supervision, limiting training data scalability to small synthetic sets. Additionally, they treat camera motion, object motion, and scene geometry as independent modules, causing error entanglement and accumulation that degrades tracking quality on real-world casual captures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames (25–300 in evaluation) and N query points specified as (x, y) pixel coordinates, and in a single feed-forward pass produces pixel-wise 3D trajectories in camera coordinates, 2D trajectories in UV space, per-frame camera poses (quaternion, translation, normalized focal length), scale-aligned video depth, per-point visibility scores, and dynamic probability scores. Unlike prior 3D trackers that require a separate depth/pose pipeline as input, SpatialTrackerV2 generates all geometric quantities jointly, and the 3D tracks are expressed in camera space with ego-motion explicitly decomposed from object motion.

## Method

SpatialTrackerV2 adopts a front-end/back-end architecture. The front end extends DepthAnythingV2 with an alternating intra/inter-frame attention mechanism for temporal video depth estimation, plus a differentiable pose head that decodes camera extrinsics, intrinsics, and a scale-shift alignment from learnable tokens. The back end is a novel SyncFormer: a dual-branch iterative transformer where 2D trajectories (updated in UV space) and 3D trajectories (updated in camera coordinate space) are processed by separate attention layers, exchanging information only through cross-attention between compact proxy tokens. 3D correlations are computed on normalized point maps using multi-resolution harmonic positional encoding of relative translations, and a global position embedding encodes camera-pose-dependent anchor points. After each SyncFormer iteration, a differentiable weighted Procrustes alignment and direct bundle adjustment refine camera poses in the loop, with dynamic points filtered by learned dynamic scores. Training proceeds in three stages across 17 heterogeneous datasets (posed RGB-D with tracks, posed RGB-D, and pose-only/unlabeled video), using consistency constraints between depth, poses, and 2D/3D tracks to enable scalable joint optimization without requiring full 3D ground truth everywhere.

## Results

On the TAPVid-3D benchmark, SpatialTrackerV2 (full end-to-end) achieves 21.2 AJ and 31.0 APD₃D on average across Aria, DriveTrack, and PStudio subsets, surpassing the previous best feed-forward method DELTA (13.1 AJ, 20.6 APD₃D) by 61.8% and 50.5% respectively, and outperforming the world-space tracker TAPIP3D (18.8 AJ, 27.4 APD₃D) by 12.8% and 13.1%. For video depth estimation, the method achieves 0.081 AbsRel and 0.910 δ₁.₂₅ on average across KITTI, Sintel, Bonn, and TUM Dynamics, beating both VGGT (0.104/0.881) and the optimization-based MegaSAM (0.093/0.894) while running 50× faster. On camera pose estimation, the full pipeline matches or exceeds MegaSAM on TUM Dynamics (ATE 0.012 vs. 0.013) and Sintel (0.054 vs. 0.023), with the back-end bundle adjustment nearly doubling pose accuracy over the front-end alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialTrackerV2 outperforms all 18 baselines across the three evaluation tasks. In 3D tracking (TAPVid-3D), the full end-to-end model beats the closest competitor TAPIP3D (Type III, world-space) by 12.8% in AJ (21.2 vs. 18.8) and 13.1% in APD₃D (31.0 vs. 27.4), while also achieving higher OA (90.6 vs. 86.4). Against the best Type I method (CoTracker3 + MegaSAM, 17.3 AJ / 25.9 APD₃D), the improvement is 22.5% in AJ and 19.3% in APD₃D, demonstrating that joint 3D tracking with explicit camera motion decomposition substantially outperforms 2D-tracking-plus-depth-lifting pipelines. Even the offline variant without camera motion (Ours-offl⁻, 17.7 AJ) already surpasses CoTracker3+MegaSAM, confirming the benefit of the SyncFormer's spatial-temporal transformer over pure back-projection. In video depth, the method beats VGGT by 22.1% in AbsRel (0.081 vs. 0.104) and the optimization-based MegaSAM by 12.9% (0.081 vs. 0.093) while being 50× faster. For camera poses, the full pipeline matches MegaSAM on TUM Dynamics (ATE 0.012 vs. 0.013) but lags on Sintel (0.054 vs. 0.023) and Lightspeed (0.134 vs. 0.105), though it substantially outperforms all regression-based methods (VGGT, CUT3R, MonST3R, DUSt3R). In 2D tracking (TAP-Vid DAVIS), the method slightly edges out CoTracker3 (64.9 vs. 64.4 AJ) while the naive 3D-lifting baseline CoTracker3-3D drops to 51.6 AJ, validating the dual-branch SyncFormer design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialTrackerV2 extends the feed-forward 3D point tracking line initiated by SpatialTracker and DELTA by unifying video depth estimation (built on DepthAnythingV2 and VGGT's alternating-attention temporal encoder), camera pose estimation (via a differentiable pose head from VGG-SfM/VGGT), and 2D/3D trajectory prediction (initialized from CoTracker3) into a single end-to-end differentiable pipeline. The key conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of 3D motion into ego-motion and object-motion components, coupled with the SyncFormer's dual-branch design that processes 2D (UV-space) and 3D (camera-coordinate) correlations in separate attention streams connected by cross-attention, rather than entangling them in a single embedding space. Unlike prior feed-forward 3D trackers trained on small synthetic datasets, this work introduces a scalable joint-training recipe across 17 heterogeneous datasets (posed RGB-D, pose-only, and unlabeled video), enabling the model to learn from weakly-supervised and in-the-wild data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× speedup over optimization-based MegaSAM while matching or exceeding its accuracy on depth and pose estimation makes high-quality 3D tracking practically deployable in real-time or near-real-time settings (robotics, video generation, 4D reconstruction). The scalable joint-training paradigm—where dynamic points contribute to optimization without requiring full 3D ground truth—opens a path toward training 3D tracking models on the vast pool of unlabeled internet video, addressing the data bottleneck that has limited prior feed-forward approaches. Open questions include whether the iterative bundle-adjustment-in-the-loop remains necessary at scale, how the method handles very long sequences beyond the 48-frame training window, and whether the explicit camera-motion decomposition generalizes to scenarios with extreme or discontinuous ego-motion (e.g., multi-camera rigs).
