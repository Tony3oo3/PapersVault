## Problem

Existing point cloud MAE methods either require separate pre-training per domain (wasting compute and underutilizing available data) or naively mix cross-domain data during pre-training, which causes out-of-domain information to be misinterpreted as noise and degrades downstream performance. The paper asks how to design a single pre-training procedure that effectively leverages point clouds from multiple domains (object, face, scene) without letting domain mismatch harm any individual task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds uniformly sampled to 4,096 points, tokenized via FPS+KNN into patches and randomly masked at a fixed ratio. During pre-training the output is the reconstructed masked patches (supervised by chamfer distance) plus a domain feature vector from the DFG; during fine-tuning the encoder, DFG, and task head jointly produce task-specific outputs (class labels, part labels, detection boxes) conditioned on both a class feature and a domain feature extracted via cross-attention from the encoder's output.

## Method

DAP-MAE introduces two key modules on top of a standard Transformer-based MAE. The Heterogeneous Domain Adapter (HDA) consists of three parallel two-layer MLPs (one per domain); in pre-training it operates in adaptation mode, routing each point cloud to its domain-specific MLP so the encoder learns domain-aware representations, while in fine-tuning it switches to fusion mode, freezing the three MLPs and linearly combining their outputs with learned coefficients (predicted by two small MLPs) that weight the target domain's output as primary and the other two as auxiliary. The Domain Feature Generator (DFG) uses cross-attention between a class token and a domain token (selected by the input's domain) against the encoder features, trained with a contrastive loss during pre-training to produce separable domain features; during fine-tuning both the class feature and domain feature are concatenated with the point cloud feature and fed into the downstream task head, providing domain-conditioned guidance for adaptation.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN object classification, DAP-MAE achieves 95.18% (OBJ_BG), 93.45% (OBJ_ONLY), and 90.25% (PB_T50_RS), matching or surpassing all single-modal and most cross-modal baselines. On facial expression recognition, it reaches 89.83% on BU-3DFE and 88.45% on Bosphorus, outperforming the best 3D-only method DrFER by 0.66% and 1.68% respectively. For 3D object detection on ScanNetV2, DAP-MAE attains 43.2% AP50 and 64.0% AP25, surpassing 3DETR by 5.3% and 1.9%. In few-shot learning on ModelNet40, it achieves 97.5% (5-way 10-shot) and 98.9% (5-way 20-shot), the best among all single-modal and cross-modal methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAP-MAE outperforms the single-modal baseline ReCon-SMC by 1.03% on ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG (95.18% vs. 94.15%) and by 0.86% over its own cross-domain version (94.32%), demonstrating that simply mixing cross-domain data is insufficient without the proposed HDA and DFG. On facial expression recognition, DAP-MAE beats the strongest 3D-only method DrFER [20] by 0.66% (BU-3DFE) and 1.68% (Bosphorus), and surpasses all 3D baselines including CM-CNN [28] and FFNet-M [40]. For 3D object detection, DAP-MAE exceeds the cross-modal method ACT [7] by 1.1% AP50 and 0.2% AP25, and outperforms the detection-specific PiMAE [3] by 3.8% AP50 and 1.4% AP25. In part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, DAP-MAE achieves the best single-modal mIoU_c (84.9%) and mIoU_I (86.3%), narrowly trailing only the cross-modal ReCon-full [35] on mIoU_I (86.4%). In few-shot learning, DAP-MAE leads all single-modal methods on 5-way settings (97.5% 10-shot, 98.9% 20-shot) but is slightly behind Point-FEMAE [57] on 10-way 10-shot (93.3% vs. 94.0%). Notably, cross-domain pre-training without DAP-MAE's architecture actually degrades performance for Point-MAE (90.02%→92.77% on OBJ_BG but 88.89%→87.34% on BU3DFE) and ReCon-SMC (42.7%→42.5% AP50 on detection), confirming that naive domain mixing introduces interference noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAP-MAE extends the Point-MAE paradigm (FPS+KNN tokenization, patch masking, chamfer-distance reconstruction) and adopts ReCon's dual-branch transformer as its architectural backbone, but departs from both by introducing cross-domain pre-training. Prior MAE-based point cloud methods (Point-MAE, PiMAE, 3DFaceMAE) are pre-trained and fine-tuned within a single domain, while cross-modal approaches (ACT, I2P-MAE, TAP) require additional image or text modalities. DAP-MAE's key architectural departure is the Heterogeneous Domain Adapter, which switches between domain-specific processing (adaptation mode) during pre-training and learned linear fusion (fusion mode) during fine-tuning, paired with a Domain Feature Generator trained via contrastive loss to extract task-relevant domain signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single cross-domain pre-training replaces multiple domain-specific pre-training runs, reducing computational cost while matching or exceeding single-domain baselines across five tasks. The paper also surfaces an important negative result—naively mixing cross-domain data for pre-training degrades face and scene task performance—highlighting that domain heterogeneity is not merely a data-scaling issue but a representation-alignment problem. The main limitation acknowledged is the inability to incorporate new domains without full retraining, leaving continual domain expansion and cross-modal integration as open directions.
