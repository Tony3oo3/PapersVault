## Problem

Current Video Large Language Models (ViLLMs) excel at general video understanding but cannot perform identity-aware comprehension of specific individuals, such as recognizing that 'Wilson is receiving chemotherapy' or 'Tom is discussing with Sarah.' Existing personalization approaches (e.g., Yo-LLaVA, MyVLM) are limited to static images and cannot model the dynamic, temporally evolving visual cues present in videos, leaving a critical gap for applications in smart healthcare and smart home environments.

## Input / Output

During training, the model learns from a single reference video per subject (1080×1920, 8 evenly sampled frames) augmented into 81 videos with ~1,455 QA pairs. At inference, it receives a query video and a text question referencing the learned subject via special tokens (e.g., <Li>, <Sh>), producing a natural-language answer across four categories: existence verification, appearance description, action recognition, and location identification. The LLM backbone is Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 fine-tuned with LoRA, and the visual encoder uses a Q-Former architecture.

## Method

PVChat introduces a ReLU Routing Mixture-of-Heads (ReMoH) attention mechanism that replaces MoH's non-differentiable Top-k head selection with a ReLU-driven router, dividing heads into always-active shared heads and conditionally-activated routed heads for adaptive, fully differentiable expert selection. Two novel losses stabilize training: Smooth Proximity Regularization (SPR) uses exponential distance scaling to progressively enforce target sparsity, while Head Activation Enhancement (HAE) counteracts over-sparsification by penalizing excessive inactivity. A systematic data augmentation pipeline synthesizes identity-preserving positives via ConsisID and PhotoMaker+LivePortrait, retrieves hard negatives via CLIP from Laion-Face-5B and CelebV-HQ, and generates QA pairs with InternVideo2 and ChatGPT-4o. Training follows a two-stage progressive strategy: Stage 1 pre-trains on single-frame images for static identity attributes (existence, appearance), then Stage 2 fine-tunes on video QA for dynamic reasoning (action, location), with selective unfreezing of later visual encoder blocks for cross-frame integration.

## Results

PVChat achieves 0.901 Accuracy, 0.562 BLEU, 0.952 BERTScore, 4.940 Entity Specificity, and 4.201 Descriptive Completeness across four question categories (existence, appearance, action, location) averaged over medical, TV series, anime, and real-world datasets. This represents a substantial margin over both baselines: Accuracy improves by +0.431 over InternVideo2 (0.342) and +0.431 over VideoLLaMA2 (0.470), while BLEU improves by over 6× compared to both baselines (0.046 and 0.082 respectively). The Entity Specificity score of 4.940 indicates that PVChat responses consistently contain subject-specific personalized information, whereas baselines score around 3.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVChat outperforms both baselines on all five evaluation metrics. Against InternVideo2 (which supports multi-round conversation, allowing the reference video to be shown in round 1 and the query video in round 2), PVChat gains +0.559 in Accuracy (0.901 vs. 0.342), +0.516 in BLEU (0.562 vs. 0.046), and +1.90 in Entity Specificity (4.940 vs. 3.041). Against VideoLLaMA2 (which does not support multi-round input, so a text description of the reference video was used as a proxy), PVChat gains +0.431 in Accuracy (0.901 vs. 0.470) and +0.90 in Descriptive Completeness (4.201 vs. 3.301). The paper notes that both baselines consistently fail on negative samples, incorrectly asserting the target subject is present in the video, while PVChat correctly identifies absence. The qualitative examples in Figure 6 further illustrate that baselines produce generic or evasive responses (e.g., 'I cannot see the subject'), whereas PVChat provides specific, identity-aware descriptions of the subject's actions and condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVChat extends the personalized image understanding line (Yo-LLaVA, MyVLM) from static images to video, introducing the first ViLLM that learns identity-specific features from a single reference video. Architecturally, it builds directly on Mixture-of-Heads (MoH) by replacing the non-differentiable Top-k head selection with a ReLU-driven routing mechanism inspired by ReMoE, adding only two MLP parameters. The data augmentation pipeline repurposes existing identity-preserving generation tools (ConsisID, PhotoMaker, LivePortrait) and face retrieval (CLIP on Laion-Face-5B) to synthesize training data, while the two-stage image-to-video training strategy follows the progressive learning paradigm common in multimodal pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practical pathway toward identity-aware video understanding in healthcare monitoring and smart-home settings, where recognizing specific individuals from a single reference clip is essential. However, the evaluation is limited to 25 characters across 6 scenarios on a single GPU, and the baselines (InternVideo2, VideoLLaMA2) are general-purpose video models not designed for personalization, making the large accuracy gap partly a task-mismatch artifact. Open questions include scalability to dozens or hundreds of subjects, robustness to domain shift in real deployment, privacy implications of storing identity-specific model parameters, and whether the ReMoH mechanism generalizes beyond the Mistral-7B backbone.
