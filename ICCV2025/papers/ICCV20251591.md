## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models shift from next-token to next-scale prediction, but each scale step must process the entire token map, causing attention complexity to scale as O(n⁴) with image resolution n×n. This super-linear growth prevents existing VAR models from scaling to higher resolutions such as 2K, even with FlashAttention enabled. The paper identifies that the last two large-scale steps account for 60% of total runtime yet are robust to token pruning, since low-frequency tokens have already converged by that stage.

## Input / Output

FastVAR takes a text prompt (encoded via an LLM into a start token) and produces a high-resolution RGB image, operating as a training-free post-hoc acceleration layer on top of pre-trained VAR backbones (HART at 0.7B and Infinity at 2.0B parameters). At 1024×1024 it achieves 0.95s latency and 11.9–14.7 GB GPU memory, and extends zero-shot to 1344×1344 (≈2K) generation in 1.3s with 15 GB memory on a single RTX 3090. The method is resolution-agnostic within the scale schedule of the underlying VAR model and does not require retraining or access to attention maps.

## Method

FastVAR applies a training-free 'cached token pruning' strategy to the last N scale steps (the Texture Filling Stage) while keeping the earlier Structure Construction Stage intact. Pivotal Token Selection (PTS) scores each token by the L2 norm of its high-frequency residual (input minus spatial global-average-pool), then retains only the Top-K tokens for the forward pass, reducing both compute and KV-cache size. Cached Token Restoration (CTR) recovers the pruned slots by bilinearly interpolating the per-layer output cached at the (K−N)-th scale step to the current resolution and scattering those values back into the pruned positions, exploiting the observed diagonal cross-scale attention sparsity. A progressive pruning-ratio schedule assigns larger ratios to later (larger) scale steps, and the entire pipeline is orthogonal to FlashAttention, yielding a combined 2.7× speedup with <1% GenEval drop.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark at 1024×1024 resolution, Infinity+FastVAR achieves 2.7× speedup (0.95s vs 2.61s) with a negligible GenEval drop from 0.73 to 0.72, while HART+FastVAR achieves 1.5× speedup (0.63s vs 0.95s) with identical GenEval score of 0.51. On MJHQ30K, HART+FastVAR reduces FID from 30.61 to 28.19 on the 'people' category while maintaining 1.5× acceleration. FastVAR also reduces GPU memory by 22.2% (from 18.9GB to 14.7GB) and enables zero-shot 2K (1344×1344) generation in 1.3s with only 15GB memory on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion baselines, Infinity+FastVAR (0.72 GenEval, 0.95s) substantially outperforms SDXL (0.55, 4.3s), PixArt-Sigma (0.55, 2.7s), and SD3-medium (0.62, 4.4s) in both quality and speed. Against AR baselines, it achieves 39.7× speedup over LlamaGen (0.32 GenEval, 37.7s) and 53× over Show-o (0.68, 50.3s) while matching or exceeding their GenEval scores. Against its own unpruned VAR baselines, FastVAR delivers 2.7× speedup on Infinity with only 0.01 GenEval drop (0.73→0.72) and 1.5× on HART with zero GenEval loss. Compared to ToMe token merging, FastVAR achieves 1.51× speedup with FID 28.19, whereas ToMe at 1.36× speedup degrades FID to 35.22, confirming that frequency-based pivotal token selection is more effective than similarity-based merging for VAR. Combined with FlashAttention, FastVAR further reduces latency from 2.61s to 0.95s and memory from 16.1GB to 11.9GB (20.4% reduction), demonstrating orthogonal compatibility with existing attention optimizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastVAR is a post-training acceleration method for Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, building directly on the next-scale prediction paradigm introduced by VAR [50] and its production-scale instantiations HART [48] and Infinity [22]. It departs from diffusion-specific acceleration techniques (DeepCache, ToMe) and LLM token-pruning methods (H2O, LazyLLM) by exploiting the unique structure of next-scale generation: the observation that large-scale steps are simultaneously the latency bottleneck and the most pruning-robust. Unlike attention-map-based pruning, FastVAR's frequency-based Pivotal Token Selection operates in the spatial domain via a DC-component approximation, making it compatible with FlashAttention and training-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FastVAR removes the primary barrier to deploying VAR models at high resolution on consumer hardware, enabling 2K image generation in 1.5s with 15GB memory on a single RTX 3090 — a regime where even FlashAttention-accelerated baselines run out of memory. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means it can be retrofitted onto existing VAR checkpoints without retraining. Open questions include whether the frequency-based heuristic generalizes to non-VAR autoregressive architectures, whether the progressive pruning schedule can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and whether the 100% pruning ratio (skipping entire scale steps) remains stable as model capacity or resolution increases further.
