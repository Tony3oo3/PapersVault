## Problem

Existing cascade cost volume methods for omnidirectional stereo matching assume a unimodal depth distribution, constraining the sampling range to a single matchable object and producing oversmoothed depth artifacts at object boundaries. Additionally, multi-view volume fusion in OSM introduces false matches from occlusions in peripheral regions with large azimuth angles and creates discontinuities at seam regions, degrading cost volume reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four fisheye RGB images (768×800) captured by cameras with 220° FOV oriented front, right, back, and left, and produces a panoramic inverse depth map at 360×640 resolution covering φ ∈ [−π/4, π/4] and θ ∈ [−π, π]. Depth is parameterized on an inverse depth scale with N=192 uniformly sampled spheres, and the final depth index is predicted via softargmax over 8 hypotheses in the last cascade stage.

## Method

MDP-Omni adopts a three-stage cascade cost volume architecture (48→32→8 hypotheses) with two key contributions. First, Depth Prior-based Sampling (DPS) uses the multimodal probability distribution from the previous stage as a parameter-free depth prior: it identifies the min and max indices where probability exceeds 0.01, then uniformly samples the next stage's hypotheses within that range, naturally expanding the range for multimodal (boundary) cases and contracting it for unimodal (planar) cases. Second, the Azimuth-based Multi-view Volume Fusion (AMVF) module assigns azimuth-dependent weights to each camera's spherical feature volume using a sigmoid-like function (k=3), suppressing unreliable peripheral regions where occlusion-induced false matches occur, then fuses opposite-facing volumes via weighted element-wise multiplication to produce a single 360° cost volume. 3D sinusoidal positional embeddings are added to the cost volume to encode omnidirectional spatial context, and the network is trained with KL divergence loss on multimodal GT distributions plus Smooth L1 regression, pre-trained on OmniThings and fine-tuned on OmniHouse and Sunny.

## Results

On OmniThings (pre-trained), MDP-Omni achieves MAE 1.20 and RMS 3.79, reducing MAE by 12.4% and RMS by 7.8% over OmniMVS+₃₂. After fine-tuning on OmniHouse and Sunny, Ours-ft achieves MAE 1.96 on OmniThings (30.2% improvement over RomniStereo₃₂-ft), MAE 0.33 on Sunny (8.3% improvement), and RMS 1.53 on Cloudy (11.0% improvement). The method also requires only 2,460 MB GPU memory and 0.25 s inference time, compared to 6,282 MB / 0.62 s for OmniMVS+₃₂.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDP-Omni outperforms all five baselines on OmniThings and Sunny in both pre-trained and fine-tuned settings. In the fine-tuned setting, Ours-ft reduces MAE by 30.2% over RomniStereo₃₂-ft on OmniThings (1.96 vs. 2.81) and by 8.3% on Sunny (0.33 vs. 0.36), while also achieving the lowest >1 error (4.51 vs. 5.19) on Sunny. On the weather-variant datasets, Ours-ft leads RomniStereo₃₂-ft by 11.0% RMS on Cloudy (1.53 vs. 1.72) and 9.3% RMS on Sunset (1.46 vs. 1.61). The one notable exception is OmniHouse (fine-tuned), where S-OmniMVS-ft achieves the best MAE (0.42) and RMS (1.06), slightly ahead of Ours-ft (0.44 / 1.15); the authors attribute this to OmniHouse's predominantly flat regions (ceilings, floors) where the narrower DPS sampling range is less advantageous. Against the non-learning baseline Sphere-Stereo, MDP-Omni reduces MAE by over 87% on OmniThings (1.20 vs. 9.14), confirming the substantial benefit of learned cost regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascade cost volume paradigm (Gu et al., CVPR 2020) to omnidirectional stereo matching, building on the spherical sweeping framework (Im et al., ECCV 2016) and the OmniMVS+ interleaved volume fusion (Won et al., TPAMI 2021). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the unimodal, parameter-dependent sampling range adjustment at each cascade stage with a parameter-free multimodal depth prior (DPS), extending the nonparametric depth distribution model of Yang et al. (CVPR 2022) from standard MVS to the 360° setting. The azimuth-based multi-view volume fusion (AMVF) module further departs from prior OSM fusion strategies by introducing geometrically motivated weighting to suppress occlusion-induced false matches in peripheral regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free design of DPS is practically attractive: it avoids the additional learnable parameters that prior adaptive sampling methods require, while still capturing multimodal depth distributions at object boundaries. The significant memory reduction (2,460 MB vs. 6,282 MB for OmniMVS+) and 60% speedup make the approach more deployable for real-time omnidirectional perception. However, the acknowledged weakness on flat, low-variation scenes (OmniHouse) suggests that the narrower sampling range, while beneficial for boundaries, can amplify initial prediction errors in homogeneous regions. Open questions include whether the DPS strategy transfers to standard MVS or monocular depth estimation, and whether the azimuth weighting generalizes to non-uniform camera rigs.
