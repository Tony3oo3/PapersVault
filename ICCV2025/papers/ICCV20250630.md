## Problem

Interferometric Hyperspectral Imaging (IHI) reconstruction is hampered by two interrelated gaps: the absence of large-scale training datasets due to the high cost and hardware dependency of real interferogram acquisition, and the inability of generic deep learning restoration networks to eliminate IHI-specific degradation artifacts (notably stripe patterns from the scanning mechanism) without physical priors. Existing model-driven reconstruction pipelines are constrained in both performance and efficiency, while learning-based methods remain largely unexplored for this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded interferogram in the optical path difference (OPD) domain with dimensions W×L×H (2048×256×120 for LASIS) and produces a reconstructed hyperspectral image in the wavenumber domain with dimensions W×H×Λ (2048×120×70 channels, 450–900 nm). Training is performed on 256×256×70 patches synthesized from HSIs via the calibrated degradation model, while evaluation uses full-resolution scenes of 256×2048×70, including a cross-domain Houston remote sensing scene and real LASIS calibration interferograms.

## Method

The paper contributes a two-part pipeline: (1) a simplified yet physically grounded degradation model decomposed into optical (response coefficient A, background β, non-uniformity M) and electronic (Poisson shot noise, dark current, stripe-correlated readout noise) stages, with a calibration-based parameter estimation procedure that enables realistic IHI data synthesis from arbitrary HSIs; and (2) IHRUT, a deep unfolding transformer that embeds the FT-based degradation model into iterative data-fidelity updates. The key architectural innovation is the Stripe Pattern Enhancement (SPE) attention, which applies H-direction average pooling to produce per-stripe spectral attention maps, enabling independent modeling of within-stripe correlations that conventional channel attention cannot capture. The data module fuses a projection branch (residual y−Fx_k) and a momentum branch (x_k−x_{k−1}) through two successive SPE weightings, while the prior module uses a lightweight cross-stage spatial-spectral transformer with window-based multi-head self-attention, requiring only ~50% of the parameters and FLOPs of comparable U-shaped unfolding transformers.

## Results

On the 6-scene HSOD-BIT synthetic benchmark, IHRUT-5 achieves 44.25 dB PSNR / 0.988 SSIM, outperforming the strongest unfolding baseline MAUN-7 (43.33 dB) by 0.92 dB and the best E2E method SCUNet (35.18 dB) by over 9 dB. On the real LASIS Calibration dataset, IHRUT-5 reaches 39.46 dB PSNR / 0.992 SSIM, a 1.31 dB gain over MAUN-7 (38.15 dB) and a 7.12 dB gain over SCUNet (32.35 dB). IHRUT-5 also maintains the lowest complexity among top performers at 3.20M parameters and 106.36 GFLOPs, compared to MAUN-7's 4.78M / 165.92 and RDLUF-3's 9.29M / 155.72.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IHRUT-5 outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest unfolding competitor MAUN-7, IHRUT-5 gains +0.92 dB on HSOD-BIT, is essentially tied on Houston (+0.01 dB), and leads by +1.31 dB on the real Calibration set, while using 33% fewer parameters and 36% fewer GFLOPs. Compared to RDLUF-3, the advantage is larger: +1.55 dB (HSOD-BIT), +1.27 dB (Houston), and +1.85 dB (Calibration). All E2E methods (DRUNet, Restormer, SCUNet, SST, SERT) trail IHRUT-5 by 7–16 dB, with SCUNet and SERT being the closest E2E baselines at ~35 dB on HSOD-BIT. The traditional LASIS pipeline and the simple inverse-FT (F′) baseline are far behind, confirming that model-guided deep unfolding is essential for IHI reconstruction. Notably, GAP-Net performs worst among unfolding methods (19.94 dB), suggesting that the GAP projection strategy is poorly suited to the IHI degradation structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for hyperspectral reconstruction (GAP-Net, RDLUF, PADUT, MAUN) to the interferometric hyperspectral imaging (IHI) setting by reformulating the linear imaging matrix F to encode the full FT-based degradation chain (modulation, gain, background, noise). The momentum branch draws on classical momentum gradient descent (Beck & Teboulle) and its deep-learning adaptations, while the calibration-based parameter estimation pipeline is inspired by camera noise calibration work (Wei, Zhang) but applied to the IHI-specific stripe-pattern noise structure. The key conceptual departure is the Stripe Pattern Enhancement (SPE) attention mechanism, which explicitly models the per-stripe spectral correlations inherent to scanning interferometers—a characteristic that standard channel attention or convolution cannot capture—and the physics-grounded degradation model that enables realistic data synthesis where no large-scale IHI training set exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IHI is the dominant hyperspectral imaging modality aboard large-scale Earth-observation satellites, so improving reconstruction quality directly impacts downstream remote sensing applications. The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks simultaneously: the absence of large IHI training datasets (solved via a calibrated physics-based synthesis pipeline) and the inadequacy of generic denoising networks for IHI-specific stripe artifacts (solved via model-guided unfolding with SPE). The lightweight 5-stage IHRUT (3.2M parameters) outperforming heavier unfolding baselines suggests that targeted physical priors can substitute for model capacity. Open questions include generalization to other IHI instrument types beyond LASIS, whether the stripe-pattern assumption holds for spatially or spatio-temporally modulated spectrometers, and scalability to full-frame satellite scenes rather than 256×256 patches.
