## Problem

Both text-to-motion (T2M) and motion-to-text (M2T) tasks depend heavily on expensive paired motion-language data, and existing approaches either treat them as independent problems or use M2T merely as a fixed auxiliary component for T2M. The strong reciprocity between the two tasks—where each can serve as an evaluator for the other—remains largely unexplored, limiting the ability to leverage abundant unpaired, unidirectional data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250804/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text descriptions (natural language sentences) and 3D human motion sequences (SMPL-X body keypoints at 20 FPS) as inputs, and produces quantized discrete motion token sequences (via VQ-VAE codebook indices) and natural language descriptions as outputs. Crucially, DRL can consume unpaired, unimodal data—either text-only or motion-only samples—without requiring aligned pairs, with motions drawn from Motion-X (12K clips) and texts from 13,581 refined descriptions. The motion tokenizer (VQ-VAE) is pretrained and frozen, so only the autoregressive Transformer networks are trained during DRL.

## Method

DRL establishes a symmetric closed-loop where T2M model f_θ and M2T model g_φ act as mutual evaluators: starting from either modality, a sample is forwarded through the primal model to the dual modality, then reversed through the dual model back to the original, and the cycle-consistency discrepancy provides the training signal. For motion, the discrepancy is a differentiable cross-entropy loss on VQ token indices, enabling standard backpropagation; for text, it is a non-differentiable CIDEr + BertScore reward optimized via policy gradient (SCST with PPO). Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 jointly optimizes both models on labeled pairs plus unlabeled motions using supervised and cycle-consistency losses, while Stage 2 uses only unlabeled texts with RL to further refine both models. The architecture pairs a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer with CLIP text encoding and autoregressive Transformer decoders for both tasks, and the entire DRL pipeline adds no extra parameters at inference compared to training each model independently.

## Results

Under the standard Ori. evaluation protocol on HumanML3D, DRL reduces FID by 0.012 for TM2T*-T2M (0.092→0.080) and 0.036 for MotionGPT* (0.363→0.327), while boosting MModality from 1.882→2.095 and 2.280→2.425 respectively. Under the proposed Upd. protocol, gains are amplified: FID drops by 0.024 for TM2T*-T2M (0.063→0.039) and 0.041 for MotionGPT* (0.231→0.190). For motion understanding, DRL yields a +7.43 CIDEr improvement for TM2T*-M2T (43.47→50.90) and +7.58 for MotionGPT* (5.68→13.26) under Ori., with consistent R-Precision gains under Upd. (e.g., 0.199→0.210 for TM2T*-M2T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRL consistently improves both re-trained baselines over their non-DRL counterparts across all protocols. Under Ori., TM2T*-T2M+DRL achieves FID 0.080, outperforming T2M-GPT (0.116) and AttT2M (0.112), while MotionGPT*+DRL (FID 0.327) narrows the gap to MoMask (0.045) and BAMM (0.055) but remains behind them. Under the Upd. protocol, TM2T*-T2M+DRL (FID 0.039) surpasses both MoMask (0.045) and BAMM (0.055) in generation fidelity, and MotionGPT*+DRL (FID 0.190) improves substantially over its baseline (0.231). For motion understanding, TM2T*-M2T+DRL achieves CIDEr 50.90, exceeding the original TM2T (16.80) and MotionGPT (29.20) reported in their respective papers, while MotionGPT*+DRL (CIDEr 13.26) more than doubles its non-DRL counterpart (5.68). The paper notes that SOTA methods (TM2T, MotionGPT, AvatarGPT) were evaluated with different NLG metric implementations, so direct CIDEr comparisons carry some caveat.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRL adapts the dual learning paradigm from neural machine translation (He et al., NeurIPS 2016) to the cross-modal motion-language domain, where T2M and M2T serve as reciprocal evaluators in a closed-loop bootstrapping cycle. Architecturally, it builds on the VQ-VAE + autoregressive Transformer pipeline established by T2M-GPT (CVPR 2023) and the reciprocal motion-text formulation of TM2T (ECCV 2022), while departing from unified LLM-based frameworks like MotionGPT and AvatarGPT that treat M2T as a byproduct rather than a co-equal task. The paper also introduces a generalized evaluation protocol (Upd.) that replaces dataset-specific contrastive encoders with a fixed DistilBERT text encoder and an expanded motion encoder, addressing a known limitation of the standard HumanML3D evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ability to leverage unpaired unimodal data (12K motions, 13.5K texts from Motion-X) as a substitute for expensive paired annotations is practically significant for a field where motion capture and human labeling remain costly bottlenecks. The architecture-agnostic design means DRL can be layered onto future T2M/M2T models without modification, and the generalized evaluation protocol offers a more reliable benchmark for the community. Open questions include whether the bootstrapping loop converges stably at larger model scales, how many DRL rounds are needed before diminishing returns set in, and whether the approach extends to other cross-modal pairs (e.g., audio-motion, video-motion).
