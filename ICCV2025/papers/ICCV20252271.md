## Problem

All standard human keypoint detection metrics (OKS, PCK, PDJ) require human-annotated ground truth, a process that is labor-intensive, costly, and limits scalability to real-world or in-the-wild settings where annotations are unavailable or incomplete. This paper asks whether vision-language models can serve as an annotation-free, perception-aligned alternative for evaluating keypoint detection quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252271/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented person crop (obtained from an upstream object detector), the predicted keypoint coordinates from a detection algorithm, and the keypoint name as a text query. In stage 2, a blue dot is rendered at each predicted coordinate to form a visual prompt image. The output is a per-person scalar KPAScore computed as a visibility-gated weighted average of per-keypoint accuracy scores, where keypoints with visibility ≤ 0.5 are excluded entirely.

## Method

KPAScore uses a two-stage VLM question-answering pipeline. In stage 1, LLaVA-V1.6-34B is asked whether each keypoint is present in the image; the probability of a 'Yes' response serves as the visibility score and acts as a binary gate (threshold 0.5). In stage 2, a blue dot is overlaid at the predicted keypoint coordinate as a visual prompt, and a LoRA-fine-tuned GLM-4V-9B (trained on 10k localization samples) is queried whether the dot corresponds to the named keypoint; the 'Yes' probability is the accuracy score. The final KPAScore is the visibility-weighted mean of √(k_i · v_i) across visible keypoints, where the square root prevents the product of two sub-unity probabilities from collapsing. The key design insight is replacing numerical coordinate queries—which VLMs answer at near-chance level (~50%)—with visual dot prompts, which improve accuracy by ~5.4% on average and, after fine-tuning, reach 80–84% accuracy.

## Results

On COCO, KPAScore exhibits a strong positive Pearson correlation with OKS across seven keypoint detection methods, ranging from 0.69 to 0.73 (average 0.71). The proposed KPAPose ensemble, guided by KPAScore, achieves a KPAScore of 62.65 and OKS of 73.63, surpassing both ViTPose-H* (60.98 / 72.95) and RTMPose-l (59.05 / 72.47). On KPUBench, the two-stage VLM framework (LLaVA-V1.6-34B + GLM-4V-9B-finetuned) reaches 84.53% accuracy and 78.10% probabilistic expectation for precision localization, up from 62.57% and 58.17% for the un-fine-tuned GLM-4V-9B. In human-perception alignment tests on complex action scenes, KPAPose improves KPAScore from 63.09 to 89.03 and human ratings from 6.55 to 7.65 on the hardest actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KPAScore is validated against the traditional OKS metric (from the COCO paper [17]) as the primary ground-truth-based baseline. The two metrics show a strong positive Pearson correlation (0.69–0.73, mean 0.71) across all seven detection methods, confirming that KPAScore tracks detection quality in a manner consistent with OKS. However, KPAScore is notably more sensitive to large keypoint displacements: at a 50% offset, KPAScore drops to near zero while OKS remains above 50, indicating KPAScore better reflects perceptual degradation. For the ensemble comparison, KPAPose (selecting highest-KPAScore keypoints across methods) outperforms ViTPose-H* [27] by +1.67 KPAScore and +0.68 OKS, and outperforms RTMPose-l [12] by +3.60 KPAScore and +1.16 OKS, demonstrating that KPAScore can serve as an effective annotation-free fusion signal. The paper also notes that KPAScore and OKS diverge in edge cases: OKS penalizes slight deviations even when perceptually acceptable, while KPAScore can assign lower scores to anatomically implausible predictions that happen to be numerically close to ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the VQA-score paradigm (Lin et al. [18])—originally designed for image-text alignment evaluation—into a keypoint detection metric, replacing ground-truth distance calculations with VLM-based question answering. It draws on visual prompting techniques (blue-dot overlays) from the segmentation and localization literature to compensate for VLMs' weak spatial reasoning, and introduces a two-stage decomposition (presence detection followed by precision localization) that has no direct analogue in prior keypoint evaluation. The KPUBench benchmark is positioned as a diagnostic tool for VLMs' keypoint comprehension, filling a gap where no prior benchmark systematically measured this capability across model families and scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: COCO requires 5.1M keypoint annotations and MPII over 400k, making annotation-free evaluation genuinely valuable for wild-scene deployment and rapid prototyping. However, the Pearson correlation with OKS is only 0.71, and the second-stage VLM requires 10k fine-tuning samples (itself derived from annotated data), which partially undermines the 'annotation-free' framing. Open questions include whether KPAScore generalizes to 3D keypoints, non-human articulations, or datasets with different keypoint conventions, and whether the metric's perceptual bias (tolerating minor deviations, penalizing anatomically implausible ones) is a feature or a confound when comparing algorithms.
