## Problem

Reconstructing sharp, high-speed video from low-frame-rate (30 FPS) RGB cameras is ill-conditioned due to motion blur and non-linear motion, while existing event-camera-based methods suffer from event saturation, missed edges parallel to motion, and timestamp distortion under rapid scene changes. No prior approach simultaneously achieves blur-free, color-faithful, high-frame-rate reconstruction without the prohibitive bandwidth of dedicated high-speed cameras.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252619/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two blurry 10-bit RGB keyframes (30 FPS) and N high-speed (≥757 FPS) spatio-temporal difference frames from the Tianmouc complementary vision sensor: spatial difference (SD, 2 channels capturing ±45° gradients) and temporal difference (TD, 1 channel). Per-frame conditioning is a 10-channel tensor concatenating RGB₀, cumulative TD up to frame n, SD at n, remaining TD after n, and RGB₁. The output is N sharp, color-rich RGB frames at the sensor's native 320×640 resolution, reconstructed in pixel space rather than latent space to preserve fine detail.

## Method

CBRDM is a two-stage cascaded pixel-space diffusion model: a coarse stage reconstructs all N frames simultaneously at 48×96 using a Bi-directional Recurrent Denoising UNet, followed by a super-resolution stage that refines each frame individually at 320×640 with a spatial self-attention UNet conditioned on the upsampled coarse output and full-resolution conditions. The key architectural contribution is the Bi-directional Recurrent Block (BRB), where each downsampling UNet block receives hidden states from both a forward and a backward recurrent pass, granting temporal awareness across all intermediate frames without the parameter overhead of temporal attention or the fixed-length constraint of temporal convolution. Training uses DDIM with 1000 denoising steps and a standard ε-prediction objective; inference uses 200 steps (coarse) and 50 steps (super-resolution). Training data is generated via a DMD-based vision chip characterization pipeline that projects high-speed RGB video onto the sensor to produce authentic CVS responses, enabling the model to generalize to real-world captures without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, CBRDM achieves 27.65 PSNR, 0.8986 SSIM, and 0.1769 LPIPS, outperforming the best RGB-only baseline (AMT at 21.74 PSNR) by approximately 6 dB in PSNR. On SportsSlomo, the method reaches 25.43 PSNR, 0.8252 SSIM, and 0.1647 LPIPS, surpassing AMT (22.56 PSNR) by ~3 dB. The 'Only Interpolation' ablation (†) shows that even when given clean RGB inputs, baselines remain far behind, confirming that the advantage stems from the complementary spatio-temporal difference data rather than deblurring alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBRDM decisively outperforms all three RGB-based baselines (XVFI, AMT, LDMVFI) on both GoPro and SportsSlomo across every metric, with PSNR gains of 5.9–7.9 dB over the strongest competitor (AMT). The diffusion-based LDMVFI, despite sharing the generative paradigm, lags behind by 7 dB on GoPro, indicating that the complementary SD/TD conditioning is the primary source of the advantage rather than the diffusion framework alone. In real-world comparisons against event-camera methods (CBMNet, TimeLensXL, REFID) using a DAVIS 346 sensor, CBRDM produces sharper, more color-faithful reconstructions, while the event-based methods suffer from event-rate saturation under rapid scene changes. The publicly available CVS UNet baseline [46] reconstructs structure reasonably but exhibits noticeable color loss and blurring, which CBRDM's bi-directional recurrent temporal mechanism and two-stage cascaded design effectively eliminate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video frame interpolation and deblurring literature by introducing a fundamentally different input modality: the Tianmouc complementary vision sensor (CVS), which provides 30 FPS RGB frames alongside high-speed (≥757 FPS) spatio-temporal difference (SD/TD) streams, replacing the event-camera data used by prior methods like CBMNet, TimeLens-XL, and REFID. Methodologically, it builds on the diffusion-model-for-low-level-vision line (LDMVFI, deblurring diffusion) but departs from standard UNet diffusion by proposing a Bi-directional Recurrent Denoising UNet Block for temporal awareness and a two-stage cascaded architecture (coarse low-resolution reconstruction followed by super-resolution) to manage computational cost. The DMD-based sensor characterization pipeline for generating training data is a notable departure from the typical simulator-plus-beam-splitter approach used in event-camera research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first complete algorithmic framework for the Tianmouc CVS (a Nature 2024 sensor), demonstrating that a low-bandwidth dual-pathway sensor can replace expensive high-speed cameras for continuous recording while enabling high-fidelity reconstruction. The DMD-based data conversion method is a transferable technique for training algorithms on novel sensors without massive real-world data collection. Key limitations include the tight coupling to the specific Tianmouc hardware, the computational cost of the two-stage diffusion pipeline (8× RTX 4090 for training), and the fact that the large quantitative gap over RGB-only baselines is partly attributable to the additional SD/TD information rather than purely algorithmic superiority. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer temporal sequences, whether real-time inference is feasible, and how well it generalizes to other complementary sensor configurations.
