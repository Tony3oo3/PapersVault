## Problem

Fusing unbiased monocular priors from vision foundation models into stereo matching is limited by three issues: the natural gap between affine-invariant relative monocular depth and absolute binocular disparity, over-confidence in iterative disparity updates that causes local optima when monocular features are implicitly fused, and noisy early disparity predictions that misguide direct depth fusion. The paper identifies and addresses these bottlenecks to enable effective exploitation of VFM monocular priors for domain-generalized stereo matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a left-right stereo image pair as input. The left image is additionally fed into a frozen DepthAnything v2 (resized to 512 longest side) to extract monocular depth and intermediate features, which are then bilinearly resized to H/4 × W/4 to interact with the stereo pipeline. The output is a dense disparity map at H/4 × W/4 resolution, produced through iterative refinement followed by a global registration-based fusion step.

## Method

The architecture is built on RAFT-Stereo's iterative structure and adds three key components. First, a binary local ordering map (computed via LBP-like fixed-weight convolutions with sigmoid) converts both monocular depth and binocular disparity into relative ordering relationships, unifying their representations and providing noise-robust local guidance. Second, an iterative local fusion module uses a two-stream design: one stream computes ordering maps from both depth sources to produce a Beta-distributed guidance signal that re-weights the GRU-predicted disparity update (with a gradual release schedule t/T to handle early-iteration noise), while the other stream predicts the update from cost volume and monocular context features. Third, a global fusion module formulates the alignment between optimized disparity and monocular depth as a pixel-wise linear regression (estimating scale a and shift b via convolutions), then fuses the registered monocular depth with the disparity using a confidence map derived from cost volume, GRU hidden state, and the final iteration's local guidance.

## Results

On Middlebury (H), the method achieves EPE 1.15 and Bad 2.0 of 8.39% across all regions, reducing error by over 50% compared to RAFT-Stereo (EPE 1.92, Bad 2.0 12.6%) and outperforming the best no-extra-data method Mocha-Stereo (EPE 2.66, Bad 2.0 10.18%). On ETH3D, it reaches EPE 0.25 and Bad 1.0 of 1.88%, surpassing all baselines including NerfStereo (EPE 0.29, Bad 1.0 2.94%). On the Booster dataset, the method achieves a 10-point improvement in Bad 2.0 for transparent regions (59.83% vs. 66.44% for Mocha-Stereo) and EPE 2.26 overall, nearly halving the error of prior methods. On DrivingStereo, it achieves the best EPE in all four weather conditions (Sunny 0.93, Cloudy 0.92, Rainy 1.29, Foggy 0.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all no-extra-data baselines across every dataset. On Middlebury (H), it achieves EPE 1.15 vs. 1.92 for RAFT-Stereo, 2.66 for Mocha-Stereo, and 2.63 for IGEV — a near-doubling of accuracy. On ETH3D, it reaches EPE 0.25 and Bad 1.0 of 1.88%, beating Mocha-Stereo (0.28 / 3.47%) and IGEV (0.33 / 4.0%) by a wide margin. Against methods that use additional data or augmentation, the method matches or exceeds NerfStereo on Middlebury (1.15 vs. 1.42 EPE) and ETH3D (0.25 vs. 0.29 EPE) without any extra stereo data, while HVT-RAFT is slightly better on KITTI 2012 (0.87 vs. 0.87 EPE, tied) but worse on Middlebury (1.37 vs. 1.15 EPE). On Booster, the method achieves EPE 2.26 overall and 7.93 on transparent regions, improving Bad 2.0 by over 10 points in transparent areas compared to Mocha-Stereo (59.83% vs. 66.44%) and NerfStereo (59.83% vs. 62.67%). On DrivingStereo, it achieves the best EPE in all four weather conditions, with the largest gap in rainy conditions (1.29 vs. 1.35 for Mocha-Stereo and 1.46 for NerfStereo).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RAFT-Stereo iterative refinement architecture but departs fundamentally from prior domain-generalized stereo matching work (FC-PSMNet, ITSA-PSMNet, Graft-PSMNet, Selective-RAFT) that focused on learning domain-invariant features or augmenting training data. Instead, it leverages unbiased monocular priors from a frozen vision foundation model (DepthAnything v2), following the recent direction of Monster and FoundationStereo, but identifies and addresses three specific failure modes in that fusion: the affine-invariant-to-absolute depth gap, over-confidence in iterative disparity updates causing local optima, and noisy early iterations corrupting direct depth fusion. The key conceptual departure is the binary local ordering map (LBP-like) that converts depth into a relative ordering representation, unifying monocular and binocular depth spaces, combined with a pixel-wise linear registration formulation for the final fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, large-scale monocular depth model can be effectively integrated into a stereo pipeline with minimal overhead (0.08s added), achieving near-doubling of performance on ill-posed regions without any additional stereo data or augmentation — a practical recipe for practitioners who need robust depth in the wild. The finding that naively injecting monocular context features into RAFT-Stereo actually degrades performance (due to over-confidence in iterative updates) is a useful cautionary result for the community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific VFM used, how it behaves at higher resolutions or with different stereo backbones, and whether the binary ordering representation could benefit other depth fusion tasks (e.g., multi-view stereo, LiDAR-camera fusion).
