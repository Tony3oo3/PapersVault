## Problem

General-purpose LMMs underperform in industrial anomaly detection for two reasons: they lack the visual acuity to focus on subtle defect regions against complex backgrounds, and they treat defects as isolated visual phenomena without understanding the manufacturing process deviations that cause them. Existing LMM-based IAD methods (e.g., AnomalyGPT, Myriad) address neither gap adequately, relying on coarse image tokens or simulated anomaly data that fail to capture fine-grained attributes and causal defect reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB industrial product image, a product-specific manufacturing process description (text), and optionally a normal reference image (1-shot setting). During inference, a vision expert (MuSc, AnomalyCLIP, or April-GAN) produces an anomaly map; pixels scoring above 0.9 define suspicious regions that are cropped into up to four fixed-size high-resolution patches. The output is a binary normal/anomalous decision accompanied by a chain-of-thought explanation that references specific manufacturing steps and visual attributes (color, shape, layout, material) to justify the classification.

## Method

Triad integrates two key modifications into LLaVA-based architectures (LLaVA-1.6 and LLaVA-OneVision-ov). First, the Expert-guided Region-of-Interest (EG-RoI) tokenizer replaces or augments the AnyRes module by cropping only expert-identified suspicious regions as high-resolution auxiliary patches (rather than uniformly tiling the image), reducing redundancy while preserving fine-grained defect detail; patch tokens are average-pooled, flattened, and concatenated with base-image tokens before projection into the language model's embedding space. Second, a manufacturing-driven reasoning paradigm is introduced via the CoT-M data organization strategy, which synthesizes chain-of-thought trajectories linking visual anomalies to specific manufacturing steps through three complementary modes: GPT-generated CoT from attribute captions plus MFG text, checklist-style templates for uncaptioned images, and text-only synthetic attribute augmentation. Training uses supervised fine-tuning (AdamW, cross-entropy loss) over all LMM parameters on the InstructIAD dataset (2,098 manually annotated samples across 40 product classes) combined with CoT-M-generated data, with 12K pairs sampled from the original LLaVA fine-tuning sets to preserve general capabilities.

## Results

In zero-shot anomaly detection, Triad-ov-7B achieves 91.2% accuracy on MVTec-AD and 80.2% on WFDD, surpassing the strongest general-purpose LMM (LLaVA-OneVision-ov-7B at 91.0%/79.8%) and the 72B Qwen2-VL (87.1%/81.1%). When augmented with manufacturing process descriptions, Triad-ov-7B further improves to 92.6% on MVTec-AD and 81.1% on WFDD, while most general LMMs degrade (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B drops 23.3%). In the 1-shot setting, Triad-ov-7B reaches 94.1% on MVTec-AD with manufacturing processes, outperforming Qwen2-VL-72B (90.4%) by 3.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Triad-ov-7B outperforms all general-purpose LMMs in zero-shot MVTec-AD accuracy: it beats LLaVA-OneVision-ov-7B (91.0%) by 0.2%, Qwen2-VL-72B (87.1%) by 4.1%, and GPT-4o (82.2%) by 9.0%. The most striking advantage appears when manufacturing process information is added: Triad-ov-7B improves to 92.6%, while Qwen2-VL-72B drops to 79.5% (−7.6%) and LLaVA-OneVision-ov-7B drops to 80.8% (−10.2%), yielding a 13.1-point gap over Qwen2-VL-72B. Against domain-specific IAD methods, Triad-ov-7B surpasses Myriad (79.3%) by 11.9 points in zero-shot and AnomalyGPT (86.1% in 1-shot) by 7.0 points in the 1-shot setting. In the 1-shot MVTec-AD evaluation, Triad-ov-7B with MFG (94.1%) exceeds Qwen2-VL-72B (90.4%) by 3.7% and Myriad (85.4%) by 8.7%. Notably, Triad is the only method that consistently benefits from manufacturing process descriptions across both 0-shot and 1-shot settings, whereas nearly all general-purpose LMMs suffer significant degradation (up to 30.3% for Qwen2-VL-2B).

## Relation to Prior Work

Triad builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA-OneVision architecture, replacing the uniform AnyRes patching strategy with an expert-guided region-of-interest tokenizer (EG-RoI) that leverages anomaly maps from dedicated IAD vision experts (MuSc, AnomalyCLIP, April-GAN) to supply high-resolution suspicious regions. It positions itself against prior LMM-based IAD methods—AnomalyGPT and Myriad—arguing that their reliance on coarse image tokens, simulated anomaly data, or limited vision-language alignment prevents fine-grained attribute recognition and manufacturing-aware reasoning. The manufacturing-driven paradigm (CoT-M + InstructIAD) is a conceptual departure from all prior IAD work, which treats defects as isolated visual phenomena rather than causal outcomes of specific production steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that manufacturing process descriptions, when properly integrated through instruction-tuning and chain-of-thought reasoning, can improve anomaly detection accuracy in a way that general-purpose LMMs cannot exploit on their own—most off-the-shelf models actually degrade significantly (up to 30%) when given manufacturing context, while Triad improves. This highlights a critical gap: domain-specific causal knowledge requires targeted training to be useful, not just prompt engineering. Open questions include whether the approach scales to products without readily available manufacturing documentation, how sensitive performance is to the quality of vision expert region proposals in production settings, and whether the manufacturing-driven paradigm generalizes beyond the 21 products and 3 benchmarks evaluated.
