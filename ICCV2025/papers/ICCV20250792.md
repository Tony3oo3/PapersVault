## Problem

Data-free post-training quantization (DFQ) for vision Mamba models (VMMs) was entirely unexplored. Two fundamental obstacles exist: (1) VMM's recurrent S6 state transitions lack explicit self-attention, causing implicit attention to fail at distinguishing foreground from background and producing semantically weak synthetic calibration data, and (2) VMM activations exhibit dynamic outlier channel variations across time-steps, rendering the static calibration strategies used for ViTs ineffective.

## Input / Output

The framework requires no real calibration data; it starts from a batch of 128 random Gaussian noise images and randomly generated task-specific targets (class probabilities, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise maps). After the generation and quantization stages, the output is a low-precision (W4A4 or W4A8) VMM that processes standard RGB images to produce task-specific outputs—class labels, bounding boxes with masks, segmentation maps, or generated images—identical in format to the full-precision model.

## Method

OuroMamba is a two-stage DFQ framework. Stage 1 (OuroMamba-Gen) constructs a patched hidden state h_p(t) by aggregating spatially neighboring states in the 2D latent space, weighted by the discretization tensor Δ(t) which naturally emphasizes informative regions; this yields an enhanced implicit attention matrix α_p that effectively separates foreground from background. Patch-level contrastive learning (InfoNCE) is then applied to α_p to iteratively refine the Gaussian noise into semantically rich synthetic images. Stage 2 (OuroMamba-Quant) performs mixed-precision quantization with per-time-step dynamic outlier detection: a magnitude threshold θ identifies outlier channels online, which are quantized at 8-bit per-channel while inliers share a 4-bit group scale, and an adaptive outlier list with periodic refresh (every 10 time-steps) prevents stale channel accumulation. Efficient CUTLASS-based hybrid GEMM kernels fuse INT4/INT8 tensor-core computation with dequantization, achieving up to 2.36× latency speedup over FP16.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, OuroMamba achieves near-lossless W4A8 quantization with ≤1.8% top-1 accuracy drop across all five VMM/hybrid models (e.g., 79.81% on Vim-S vs. 81.60% FP baseline). At the more challenging W4A4 setting, OuroMamba yields an average accuracy improvement of 7.84% over PTQ4VM and 19.40% over QMamba, while using only 128 synthetic calibration samples versus 256–1024 real samples. On COCO detection, OuroMamba achieves up to 21.1 higher box AP and 18.1 higher mask AP; on ADE20K segmentation, up to 6.6 higher mIoU; and on Zigma image generation (W4A4), FID of 39.2 on FacesHQ vs. 89.6 (PTQ4VM) and 90.3 (QMamba).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OuroMamba outperforms all four baselines across every model and quantization setting. The two ViT-specific PTQ methods, BRECQ and DopQ-ViT, suffer catastrophic degradation on VMMs—up to 72% top-1 accuracy loss at W4A4 (e.g., BRECQ drops to 11.62% on LVMamba-S)—because they cannot handle VMMs' dynamic inter-time-step outlier variations. Among VMM-specific baselines, PTQ4VM achieves acceptable W4A8 results (e.g., 74.37% on Vim-S) but degrades sharply at W4A4 (69.60%), partly because its SmoothQuant-style outlier migration into weights complicates weight quantization. QMamba, which uses static temporal grouped quantization, performs comparably to PTQ4VM at W4A8 but collapses at W4A4 (33.64% on Vim-S, 59.35% on VMamba-B) due to its inability to adapt to shifting outlier channel positions. OuroMamba, using only 128 synthetic samples (vs. 256 for PTQ4VM and 1024 for QMamba), achieves 79.81% (W4A8) and 75.93% (W4A4) on Vim-S, surpassing the best baseline by 5.44 and 6.33 points respectively, and extends this advantage to detection (up to +21.1 box AP), segmentation (+6.6 mIoU), and diffusion-based generation (FID 39.2 vs. 89.6 on FacesHQ at W4A4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-free quantization (DFQ) paradigm established for ViTs (PSAQ-ViT, CLAMP-ViT) to vision Mamba models, which lack the explicit self-attention mechanism those methods rely on. It builds on the implicit attention reformulation of the S6 layer (Ali et al., Han et al.) but identifies that this implicit attention is insufficient for synthetic data generation due to scanning-direction bias and state forgetfulness. The patched neighborhood interaction in latent state space is a novel adaptation that bridges the gap between ViT contrastive learning and VMM's recurrent structure, while the dynamic per-time-step outlier detection departs from QMamba's static temporal grouping and PTQ4VM's weight-shift strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VMMs gain traction as sub-quadratic alternatives to ViTs, the inability to quantize them without access to training data is a significant deployment barrier in privacy-sensitive settings; OuroMamba addresses this with a practical two-stage pipeline requiring only 128 synthetic samples. The dynamic outlier detection mechanism is a reusable insight for any SSM-based quantization, and the 2.36× kernel speedup makes the approach deployable. However, the assumption that inlier dynamic ranges remain stable across time-steps may not hold for future SSM architectures, and the method has not been validated on non-selective or multi-scale SSM variants beyond the tested models.
