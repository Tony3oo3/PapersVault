## Problem

Web-crawled image-text pairs used to pre-train CLIP inevitably contain noisy correspondence: false positives (mismatched pairs treated as matched) and false negatives (semantically similar unpaired samples forced apart). Existing robust pre-training methods require training VLMs from scratch with refined alignment or external model guidance, making them impractical for billion-scale datasets. This paper asks whether the harmful effects of noisy correspondence can be directly erased from an already-pretrained CLIP via a lightweight fine-tuning procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252485/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NCU takes a pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32) and fine-tunes it on the same or a subset of the original web-crawled dataset (CC3M, CC12M, or YFCC15M-R) with a batch size of 2,048. Within each batch, the forget set is dynamically identified by ranking pairs on a symmetric similarity confidence score ω_i, selecting the lowest P% as false positives. The output is a refined CLIP whose image and text encoders produce embeddings with improved positive-negative separation, evaluated via zero-shot classification, cross-modal retrieval (R@K), and linear probing.

## Method

NCU is a two-stage fine-tuning framework. First, it learns hardest negative semantics by introducing m shared learnable prompt vectors that, when concatenated with token features of each caption, produce a negative embedding t_i^Neg; these prompts are trained with three losses—a margin-bounded similarity separation (L_sep), a relation-structure preservation term (L_rel) that enforces ⟨t_i, t_j^Neg⟩ ≈ ⟨t_j, t_i^Neg⟩, and a sigmoid-based multi-positive matching loss (L_itm) that pulls t_i^Neg toward unpaired images while pushing it away from its own image. Second, it formalizes the twin-goals unlearning (forgetting false positives by zeroing transport mass between v_i and t_i, and forgetting false negatives by using t_i^Neg as a distance upper bound) as a single masked optimal transport problem over an extended (N×(N+1)) cost matrix, solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp. The resulting transport plan is blended with an identity matrix and used as a soft alignment target, with the model fine-tuned by minimizing KL divergence between the target and the model's row/column-wise softmax similarity distributions, plus a retained separation term for stability.

## Results

On zero-shot ImageNet1K classification, NCU achieves 20.0% top-1 accuracy (ViT-B/16, CC3M) and 43.4% (ViT-B/16, CC12M), outperforming CLIP by 4.0% and 2.8% respectively. Across 16 downstream classification datasets, NCU gains 2.5%–4.0% average top-1 accuracy over CLIP. On cross-modal retrieval, NCU improves average recall by 7.7% on Flickr30K and 4.8% on MSCOCO (CC3M, ViT-B/32) over vanilla CLIP, demonstrating that unlearning noisy correspondence yields a more discriminative embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCU is compared against three baselines: vanilla CLIP, Gradient Ascent (GA), and SoftCLIP. NCU consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset-architecture combination in Table 4, achieving the highest ImageNet1K zero-shot top-1 accuracy in all four settings. Against SoftCLIP (the strongest robust pre-training baseline), NCU gains 1.1%–2.3% without requiring external guidance (e.g., ROI features), and notably surpasses SoftCLIP on CC12M+ViT-B/32 (36.7 vs. 34.4) where SoftCLIP's self-similarity modeling fails to exploit false-positive supervision. Gradient Ascent, while a naive unlearning strategy, still yields modest gains (0.3%–1.3%) over CLIP, confirming that some unlearning signal is beneficial, but NCU's explicit hardest-negative guidance provides substantially larger improvements (2.8%–4.0%). On retrieval tasks, NCU improves average recall by up to 7.7% over CLIP on Flickr30K, a margin not reported for the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the machine unlearning paradigm (Bourtoule et al., 2021) for a novel application: erasing noisy correspondence knowledge from an already-trained CLIP rather than retraining from scratch with refined alignment. It departs from the dominant line of robust VLM pre-training (SoftCLIP, PyramidCLIP, FFF) that requires expensive from-scratch training with external guidance, instead treating the pre-trained model as a starting point to be selectively 'corrected.' The technical machinery draws on optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) for soft alignment and prompt learning for negative semantics, but the key conceptual move is framing robustness as a forgetting problem solvable via a unified OT objective with mask-based constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is significant: practitioners with a pre-trained CLIP can improve robustness via lightweight fine-tuning (10 epochs, 16 GPUs) without access to the full training pipeline or external teacher models, and the finding that even <20% of the original data suffices for meaningful gains makes this applicable to proprietary or partially accessible datasets. However, the work is limited to CLIP at million-scale (CC3M/CC12M/YFCC15M-R), and open questions remain about whether the unlearning approach generalizes to larger VLMs (BLIP-2, VisionLLM, InternVL) or billion-scale pre-training where noise patterns may differ. The paper also leaves open whether the OT formulation is truly necessary versus simpler re-weighting schemes at scale.
