## Problem

Existing language-guided 6-DoF grasping methods rely on explicit instructions that name target objects directly, and they underexploit LLMs' knowledge of objects' physical properties (material, surface, shape) that are critical for selecting safe and effective grasp poses. Additionally, no public benchmark exists for evaluating multi-target grasp detection under implicit, contextually-derived verbal commands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251936/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored 3D point cloud (N points with 3D coordinates and RGB values) and a flexible language instruction that does not explicitly name target objects or specify their count (e.g., 'I want to watch TV' rather than 'grab the remote'). The point cloud is projected into four complementary RGB-D views for processing. The output consists of multiple 6-DoF grasp poses (rotation ∈ SO(3), translation ∈ ℝ³, gripper width ∈ ℝ⁺) each paired with a confidence score, along with structured CoT reasoning tokens covering target identification, physical property descriptors, and selected grasp actions.

## Method

GraspCoT is a unified multimodal LLM architecture (initialized from LLaVA-3D) that jointly processes 3D-aware visual tokens and CoT-derived textual tokens within a single transformer. The visual branch projects the point cloud into four complementary views, encodes RGB patches via CLIP, fuses depth-derived 3D positional embeddings, back-projects into geometrically grounded 3D patches, and voxel-pools them into visual tokens. The textual branch implements a three-stage fill-in-the-blank QA pipeline: (1) target parsing with supervised category slots, (2) physical property analysis across material/surface/shape dimensions using open-world descriptor libraries with unsupervised reasoning tokens, and (3) grasp action selection from a predefined verb library. All LLM output tokens pass through a self-attention layer feeding parallel regression (Hungarian-matched L1 loss) and classification (focal loss) heads for pose and confidence prediction, trained jointly with cross-entropy QA loss. The IntentGrasp benchmark (1M scenes, ~3M objects) is constructed by prompting Llama3-70B to generate 3–5 implicit instructions per scene from the Grasp-Anything-6D dataset.

## Results

On the IntentGrasp benchmark under flexible language instructions, GraspCoT achieves a coverage rate (CR) of 0.9715 at θ=0.4, 0.8797 at θ=0.3, and 0.5587 at the strictest threshold θ=0.2, along with an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) of 0.2520 and an EMD-Weighted Collision-Free Rate (EW-CFR) of 0.4229. The strictest-threshold CR@0.2 of 0.5587 represents the headline result, outperforming the strongest baseline (LGrasp6D) by 0.2238 in absolute terms. The EMD of 0.2520 reflects a 14.1% relative reduction over LGrasp6D, indicating superior distributional alignment between predicted and ground-truth grasp poses in SE(3) space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraspCoT is compared against two recent language-guided 6-DoF grasp detection baselines: 3DAPNet (Nguyen et al., ICRA 2024) and LGrasp6D (Nguyen et al., ECCV 2024), both adapted to accept flexible instructions in place of their original explicit commands. GraspCoT decisively outperforms both on every metric. Against 3DAPNet, the CR@0.2 gap is 0.2683 (a 92% relative improvement), EMD drops by 17.8%, and EW-CFR rises by 20.1%. Against LGrasp6D, the CR@0.2 gap is 0.2238 (a 67% relative improvement), EMD drops by 14.1%, and EW-CFR rises by 9.9%. The progressive widening of the CR gap from θ=0.4 to θ=0.2 (0.0466 → 0.1220 → 0.2238 vs. LGrasp6D) indicates that GraspCoT's advantage is most pronounced under high-precision requirements, suggesting the CoT reasoning mechanism contributes geometrically finer pose predictions rather than merely broader coverage. The w/o CoT ablation further confirms that the CoT pipeline itself accounts for a 25.4% relative gain in CR@0.2 over the same architecture without reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraspCoT extends the language-guided 6-DoF grasping line (3DAPNet, LGrasp6D, OVGNet) by replacing their decoupled text-encoder + pose-regression pipelines with a unified 3D MLLM architecture (building on LLaVA-3D and 3D-LLM-style visual token construction). It departs from recent LLM-for-grasping work (GraspGPT, ThinkGrasp, RT-Grasp, DeliGrasp) that treats LLMs primarily as semantic parsers or intent inferrers, instead leveraging their commonsense physics knowledge through a structured Chain-of-Thought QA pipeline that reasons about material, surface, and shape properties before selecting grasp actions. The multi-target, flexible-instruction setting also generalizes beyond the single-object, explicit-command assumption prevalent in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IntentGrasp (1M scenes, ~3M objects) is the first public benchmark for multi-target grasp detection under implicit, context-dependent verbal commands, filling a clear gap that limits evaluation of real-world human-robot interaction. The finding that unsupervised physical-property descriptors resolved by an LLM yield a ~22% CR@0.2 gain over no-reasoning suggests LLM world knowledge about materials is more directly transferable to geometric pose prediction than previously assumed. Open questions remain: performance on slender objects (chopsticks) still degrades, dynamic/moving targets are unaddressed, and real-world success rates (~54%) indicate substantial room for improvement before deployment in unstructured environments.
