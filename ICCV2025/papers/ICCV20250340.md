## Problem

Existing floorplan localization methods rely exclusively on depth-based structural cues, which produce highly ambiguous predictions in environments with repetitive or symmetric layouts where room corners are indistinguishable. Although floorplans inherently encode rich semantic information (windows, doors, room types), prior work ignores these cues, leaving a significant gap in localization accuracy for challenging indoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250340/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image with a limited field of view (~80°) that may have non-zero pitch and roll, along with a 2D floorplan map represented as a discrete semantic label matrix (categories: wall, window, door, empty space) at 0.1 m spatial resolution and 10° angular granularity. It outputs a 2D camera location (x, y) and orientation angle θ, derived by maximizing a structural-semantic probability volume over the discretized pose space. Optionally, if room-type polygons are available in the floorplan, the predicted room label is used to mask the probability volume before extraction.

## Method

The core contribution is a semantic ray prediction network built on a pretrained ResNet50 backbone that, via cross-attention with l=40 learnable ray tokens and an optional CLS token, predicts a discrete semantic category per ray and an optional room type from a single image. A key design choice is a majority-voting interpolation scheme (window of 3) to downsample semantic rays to 7 for the coarse stage, since linear interpolation is invalid for discrete class labels. The resulting semantic probability volume is fused with the F3Loc depth probability volume via a weighted sum (w_s=0.4, w_d=0.6) to form a structural-semantic probability volume. A coarse-to-fine strategy then extracts Top-5 candidate poses from the low-resolution volume and refines them by comparing full-resolution (40-ray) ground-truth and predicted rays, with the highest-similarity candidate selected as the final pose.

## Results

On S3D, the full method (Ours_r) achieves 57.49% recall at 1m/30° orientation tolerance, compared to 21.3% for F3Loc and 8.7% for LASER — a 2.7× and 6.6× improvement respectively. On ZInD, Ours_r reaches 31.86% R@1m30° versus 11.46% (F3Loc) and 13.64% (LASER). At the fine-grained 0.1m threshold on S3D, recall jumps from 1.5% (F3Loc) to 5.70% (Ours_r). Adding room-type predictions yields an additional 9.2% gain on S3D and 8.7% on ZInD over the semantic-only variant (Ours_s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: F3Loc (depth-ray-based floorplan localization) and LASER (latent-space rendering for 2D visual localization). On S3D, Ours_r outperforms F3Loc by 36.2 percentage points on R@1m30° (57.49% vs. 21.3%) and by 48.8 points on R@0.5m (45.53% vs. 14.6%), while surpassing LASER by 48.8 points on R@1m30° (57.49% vs. 8.7%). On ZInD, the gains are similarly substantial: Ours_r beats F3Loc by 20.4 points on R@1m30° (31.86% vs. 11.46%) and LASER by 18.2 points (31.86% vs. 13.64%). The paper notes that F3Loc actually underperforms LASER on ZInD (11.46% vs. 13.64% R@1m30°), suggesting F3Loc's depth-only approach is particularly fragile on the unfurnished ZInD homes, whereas the semantic cues in the proposed method provide a more robust signal. The Oracle upper bound (using ground-truth rays) reaches 94.57% on S3D and 65.13% on ZInD, indicating substantial remaining headroom from ray prediction accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends F3Loc, which performs floorplan localization by matching predicted depth rays against floorplan geometry, by introducing a parallel semantic ray prediction network that classifies each ray into discrete categories (wall, window, door). The two probability volumes are fused via a weighted combination, and a coarse-to-fine refinement strategy (low-resolution search followed by high-resolution Top-K evaluation) is added to recover precision lost during interpolation. The semantic prediction network itself borrows architectural ideas from vision transformers (learnable tokens, cross-attention) atop a ResNet50 backbone, while the majority-voting interpolation for discrete labels is a novel design choice absent from prior depth-ray pipelines. Compared to LASER, which embeds semantic point features into a shared latent space, this work models semantics as explicit per-ray class predictions, enabling direct geometric comparison with floorplan geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3× recall improvement over F3Loc demonstrates that semantic cues are not merely complementary but critical for resolving ambiguities in repetitive or symmetric indoor layouts, a long-standing weakness of purely geometric floorplan localization. The coarse-to-fine strategy offers a practical accuracy–efficiency tradeoff (Top-K refinement), and the optional room-label masking shows that even coarse metadata can substantially narrow the search space. However, absolute recall remains modest (≈58% at 1 m on S3D, ≈38% on ZInD), and the ORACLE gap reveals that ray prediction quality is the dominant bottleneck; scaling to more complex multi-floor environments, incorporating additional modalities (text, 3D geometry), and reducing dependence on clean floorplan semantics are open directions.
