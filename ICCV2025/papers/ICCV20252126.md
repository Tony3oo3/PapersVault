## Problem

Existing image-conditioned generation methods (depth- and canny-conditioned) cannot accurately constrain the content of multiple instances or regions simultaneously; even state-of-the-art models like FLUX and 3DIS suffer from attribute leakage where visual properties bleed across different instances. This limits user control in multi-instance scenes, where the number of controlled elements directly degrades generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252126/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a depth map or canny edge map as structural guidance, a global text prompt, per-instance text descriptions, and bounding boxes or masks for instance localization. Bounding boxes are further refined into precise instance masks via SAM-2 segmentation. The output is a single RGB image in which each specified instance renders with its correct visual attributes while the overall scene maintains visual coherence; generation uses 20 sampling steps with CFG scale 10.0 (depth) or 30 (canny) on FLUX.1-Canny/Depth.

## Method

DreamRenderer is a training-free, plug-and-play controller on FLUX that introduces two mechanisms into the Joint Attention layers. First, Hard Text Attribute Binding creates replicated 'Bridge Image Tokens' for each instance that participate in attention but are excluded from the final output; each instance's text tokens attend exclusively to their own bridge tokens, simulating single-instance generation so that T5 text embeddings (which lack intrinsic visual information) bind the correct visual attributes without cross-instance confusion. Second, a layer-wise analysis of FLUX's 57 Joint Attention layers reveals that input and output layers encode global scene information while middle layers govern per-instance attribute rendering; accordingly, Hard Image Attribute Binding (restricting each instance's image tokens to attend only to themselves and their text) is applied solely in the middle layers, while Soft Image Attribute Binding (allowing attention to all image tokens) is used in the remaining layers to preserve overall visual harmony.

## Results

On the COCO-POS benchmark (depth-guided), DreamRenderer achieves 62.50% Image Success Ratio (SR), 94.51% Instance Success Ratio (ISR), and 84.36% MIoU, outperforming 3DIS (53.88% SR) and FLUX (44.83% SR). Under the more challenging canny-guided setting, it still leads with 23.28% SR vs. 18.07% (3DIS) and 16.38% (FLUX). On COCO-MIG, DreamRenderer as a plug-and-play re-renderer boosts the Image Success Ratio of GLIGEN by 26.8% (4.4%→31.2%), InstanceDiffusion by 19.9% (14.5%→34.4%), MIGC by 8.3% (28.4%→36.7%), and 3DIS by 7.4% (29.7%→37.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamRenderer is evaluated as a plug-and-play controller on top of four layout-to-image baselines (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC, 3DIS) and also compared directly against FLUX and 3DIS on COCO-POS. Against GLIGEN, the improvement is the most dramatic: Image SR jumps from 4.4% to 31.2% (+26.8%), and ISR nearly doubles from 29.7% to 67.8%. Against InstanceDiffusion, Image SR improves from 14.5% to 34.4% (+19.9%). Against the two strongest baselines, MIGC and 3DIS, the gains are more modest but consistent: Image SR rises by 8.3% and 7.4% respectively, with ISR gains of 4.3 and 9.0 percentage points. On COCO-POS (depth-guided), DreamRenderer beats 3DIS by 8.62% in SR (62.50% vs. 53.88%) and FLUX by 17.67%, while also improving MIoU and AP. The gains grow with the number of controlled instances: over 3DIS, the ISR advantage widens from +2.5% at 2 instances to +10.5% at 6 instances, confirming that the Bridge Image Token mechanism scales well with scene complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamRenderer builds directly on the FLUX rectified-flow transformer architecture, extending the line of image-conditioned generation (depth/canny) that began with ControlNet-style approaches. It departs from prior multi-instance generation methods (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC, 3DIS) by introducing a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism rather than requiring model retraining or architectural modification. The key conceptual departure is the Bridge Image Token strategy, which avoids the quality degradation of full token isolation by using replicated tokens that guide text-attribute binding without contributing to the final output, and the empirical finding that only FLUX's middle layers require hard instance binding while input/output layers must remain globally connected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature means DreamRenderer can immediately enhance any FLUX-based pipeline without retraining, making it practically accessible for animation, game development, and visual restoration workflows. The layer-wise analysis of FLUX (identifying which layers encode global vs. instance-level information) provides transferable insight for the broader DiT community. However, the method is currently tied to the FLUX architecture and requires depth/canny maps plus bounding boxes or masks as inputs, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the bridge-token and selective-binding ideas generalize to other transformer-based generators (e.g., SD3, video models) and whether the approach scales to very large numbers of instances or more complex compositional scenes.
