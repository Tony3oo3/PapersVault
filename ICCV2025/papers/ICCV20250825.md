## Problem

Diffusion-based dense prediction (DDP) models, such as Marigold and Lotus, degrade significantly when tested on domains far from their training distribution, yet no training-free domain adaptation method has been designed specifically for the DDP framework. The paper identifies that exposure bias in diffusion sampling manifests as domain shift in the noise prediction statistics, and that this bias can be corrected by aligning noise statistics across domains without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely during the diffusion sampling process on a frozen pre-trained DDP model, requiring no weight updates. For source-available DA, it additionally needs a small set of source-domain images to pre-compute reference noise statistics at each timestep. For source-free DA, it performs B multiple initial-noise samplings of the same target image to estimate confidence regions. The conditional input is task-specific (a single RGB image for depth/segmentation, a pair of RGB images for optical flow, or a low-resolution image for super-resolution), and the output is the corresponding dense prediction (depth map, flow field, HR image, or segmentation mask) at the model's native resolution.

## Method

The core mechanism is Domain Noise Alignment (DNA), which computes the per-timestep ratio ΔN(t) of L2 norms between target-domain and source-domain noise predictions, then derives a scaling coefficient λ_t via a cumulative error schedule (Eq. 2–3) that avoids over-correction across steps. For source-available DA, source-domain noise statistics are pre-computed directly; for source-free DA, the paper estimates them by sampling B initial noises, computing per-pixel variance along the batch dimension, and using the low-variance (high-confidence) regions as a proxy for source-domain statistics. Three refinements improve the source-free variant: (1) online noise alignment uses the approximate x̂₀ at each step to update the confidence mask without pre-computation, (2) noise consistency scaling removes a pairwise-distance-based consistency factor from λ_t to avoid over-weighting regions that are merely noise-consistent, and (3) a linear threshold schedule progressively expands the high-confidence region from early to late denoising steps, inspired by progressive pseudo-labeling in UDA.

## Results

On depth estimation (the primary task), FreeDNA applied to Marigold reduces AbsRel from 33.7 to 26.3 on NuScenes-night and from 23.8 to 17.0 on RobotCar-night under source-available DA, while under source-free DA it achieves AbsRel 28.8 and 18.2 respectively. For optical flow, DDVM+ours cuts EPE from 7.41 to 4.93 on FCDN (source-available) and from 7.41 to 6.33 (source-free). On blind super-resolution, DiffBIR+ours improves PSNR from 24.05 to 25.19 on DRealSR, and on semantic segmentation, Ji et al.+ours raises mIOU from 53.7 to 57.2 on ACDC. All improvements are achieved without any training or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeDNA consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across four tasks. In depth estimation, it surpasses the strongest trained DA methods (S2R-DepthNet, DESC) by 19–21 AbsRel points on NuScenes and 15–16 points on RobotCar under source-available DA, and beats the source-free baseline Ada-depth by 19.7 and 16.7 AbsRel points respectively. For optical flow, DDVM+FreeDNA (EPE 4.93) beats all three trained DA baselines (TST 8.18, UNDAF 7.47, UCDA-Flow 6.34) on FCDN, and under source-free DA it outperforms TTA-MV (9.42) by 3.09 EPE. In blind super-resolution, FreeDNA adds ~1.1 dB PSNR to both DiffBIR and StableSR on DRealSR. In semantic segmentation, Ji et al.+FreeDNA (mIOU 57.2) exceeds DAFormer (55.4) and DACS (40.1) on ACDC under source-available DA, and beats CMA (50.1) by 5.1 mIOU under source-free DA. Notably, all gains are training-free, whereas most baselines require supervised or adversarial training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the noise scaling technique developed to address exposure bias in diffusion models (Ning et al. [54], [55]) for the distinct problem of domain adaptation in diffusion-based dense prediction (DDP). The key conceptual bridge is the observation that domain bias in DDP manifests in noise prediction statistics in a manner analogous to exposure bias, validated through Fourier amplitude analysis showing that domain differences are primarily encoded in the amplitude component. Unlike prior diffusion-for-DA work (DATUM [6], DIDEX [53]) that uses diffusion models as generators to augment source domains, this paper directly modifies the DDP model's sampling process, positioning itself as the first training-free DA method tailored specifically to the DDP framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of DNA makes it immediately deployable on any pre-trained DDP model without retraining, which is practically significant given the high cost of retraining large diffusion models. The source-free variant, which estimates source-domain statistics from high-confidence regions via multi-noise sampling, addresses a realistic deployment scenario where source data is unavailable. Limitations include the global (non-local) nature of the adjustment, which can produce errors in specific regions, and reduced effectiveness on complex multi-factor domain shifts (e.g., nighttime + non-uniform fog). Open questions include whether local or spatially-varying noise adjustments could further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to other diffusion architectures beyond the tested DDP models.
