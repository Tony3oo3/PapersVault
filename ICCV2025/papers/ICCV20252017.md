## Problem

Existing text editing methods struggle with two coupled challenges: accurate stroke-level rendering of complex characters (especially Chinese) and controllable style preservation or transfer. Glyph-based approaches lack character-level textual guidance, while style disentanglement methods rely on synthetic font datasets that fail to capture the diversity of real-world text appearances such as gradients, outlines, and 3D effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image at 1024 resolution, a binary mask delineating the editable text region, a target text prompt, and optionally a reference style image (either the original text for style preservation or an external exemplar for style injection). A standard-font glyph rendering is generated on-the-fly within the edit bounding box and concatenated in latent space as an additional conditioning channel. The output is a full-resolution edited image where only the masked region is modified, with the surrounding context preserved.

## Method

TextMaster builds on SDXL inpainting with a Glyph-Style Dual-Control architecture. For glyph accuracy, it injects a standard-font glyph image aligned to the edit bounding box in latent space, encodes each character independently via a token-separated ChatGLM encoder (with per-character position and mask tokens to prevent cross-semantic interference), and applies a perceptual loss computed on PPOCR OCR features within the edit region. For layout, it selects the top-3 cross-attention layers with strongest positional response and applies a CIOU loss between binarized attention maps and ground-truth character bounding boxes. For style control, a Style-Adapter decouples style from glyph content by subtracting DINOv2 features of the rendered glyph from features of the segmented text, injecting the residual style vector via IP-Adapter cross-attention restricted to the mask region; an In-Context Reference mechanism additionally places a reference character at the image bottom so self-attention can learn fine-grained color and typographic details. Training proceeds in two stages: five epochs on AnyWord-3M with the full loss (denoising + perceptual + CIOU), followed by style-poster fine-tuning with the Style-Adapter and In-Context Reference modules.

## Results

On the AnyText-Benchmark, TextMaster achieves 92.57% Chinese accuracy and 93.58% English accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best method AnyText2 (61.33% / 62.35%) and TextCtrl (80.96% English only). In terms of style fidelity, TextMaster attains FID of 11.93 (Chinese) and 10.35 (English), and LPIPS of 0.0247 / 0.0238, surpassing all baselines including TextCtrl (FID 6.01, LPIPS 0.0234 English-only). On the TextMaster-Benchmark, TextMaster reaches 91.80% accuracy for both Chinese and English with FID below 10, confirming consistent superiority across diverse editing scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextMaster outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across all three benchmarks. The closest competitor, TextCtrl, achieves strong style metrics (FID 6.01, LPIPS 0.0234 on English) but trails TextMaster by 12.6 points in English accuracy (80.96% vs. 93.58%) and 13.0 points in NED; the authors note TextCtrl tends to retain original text rather than performing true content editing. AnyText2, the strongest multilingual baseline, lags TextMaster by over 30 points in Chinese accuracy (61.33% vs. 92.57%) and 31 points in English accuracy (62.35% vs. 93.58%). General-purpose T2I models (SDXL, FLUX) and text-rendering methods (TextDiffuser2) are far behind, with English accuracy below 55% and FID values 2–4× higher. On the TextMaster-Benchmark, TextMaster extends its lead, achieving 91.80% accuracy for both languages with FID under 10, while AnyText2 drops to 57.84% Chinese accuracy and TextCtrl to 82.23% English accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextMaster builds directly on the SDXL inpainting pipeline and repurposes IP-Adapter's cross-attention mechanism as the injection point for a DINOv2-based style feature, extending it from identity preservation to font-style transfer. It departs from prior glyph-control methods (GlyphDraw, GlyphControl, AnyText) by replacing per-character aligned glyph images with field-aligned standard-font glyphs and a token-separated ChatGLM encoder, and from prior style-disentanglement approaches (DiffSTE, AnyText2) that rely on synthetic font datasets by instead subtracting DINOv2 content features from segmented style features to obtain a content-free style vector. The CIOU loss applied to selected cross-attention maps for layout learning and the in-context reference mechanism (placing a reference character in the image and leveraging self-attention) are novel combinations not seen in prior text-editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework delivers a large and consistent accuracy gap over prior methods (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.61 ACC on AnyText-Benchmark Chinese), making high-fidelity multilingual text editing practically viable for production pipelines. The finding that style injection is not merely a cosmetic concern but is necessary for accurate glyph reconstruction challenges the common assumption that content and style can be optimized independently. Open questions include whether the multi-stage training recipe (base → in-context → style-adapter → joint fine-tuning) generalizes to low-resource languages, whether the 1024-pixel resolution constraint limits applicability to small-text scenarios, and whether the DINOv2 feature-subtraction approach scales to highly complex artistic styles (3D, metallic, multi-layer effects) beyond the crawled poster dataset.
