## Problem

Existing text-to-image and text-to-3D methods cannot generate Vector Displacement Maps (VDMs), which are non-natural 2D images where each pixel stores a 3D displacement vector, making them unsuitable for producing reusable sculpting brushes compatible with artists' workflows. Furthermore, standard Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) suffers from semantic coupling when generating sub-object structures, where the full-object semantics in the diffusion model's training data cause unwanted parts (e.g., a tortoise's head when generating only a shell) to appear in the output.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the desired brush structure, an optional binary region mask to restrict where mesh deformation occurs, and an optional user-specified 512×512 three-channel VDM (each channel encoding X, Y, or Z displacement in [0,1]) to initialize the base mesh volume and direction. The output is a 512×512 three-channel VDM brush directly loadable in Blender or ZBrush, where each pixel's three channels encode the 3D displacement vector applied to the target mesh surface during sculpting. Three initialization modes are supported: zero-valued (flat plane), spike-pattern (for protruding structures), and user-specified VDM (for custom volume/direction control).

## Method

Text2VDM reframes VDM generation as diffusion-guided mesh deformation rather than direct image synthesis: a dense planar mesh is constructed from the initial VDM, and its vertices are iteratively deformed by optimizing a Semantic Enhancement SDS loss computed on differentially rendered normal maps. To maintain mesh quality during large deformations, the optimization uses Sobolev preconditioning via the mesh Laplacian (v* = (I + λL)v with λ=15), which solves a sparse linear system each step to diffuse gradients and prevent triangle flips. The key novelty is the Semantic Enhancement SDS loss, which mitigates semantic coupling by applying weighted blending to prompt tokens through Compel: each part-related word's embedding is computed as e_w = e_0 + s·(e − e_0) with s = 1.1², producing a semantically focused text embedding that directs the diffusion model toward the sub-object distribution without the noisy gradients of negative-prompt suppression (CSD) or the temporal instability of Attend-and-Excite.

## Results

On 40 distinctive text prompts, Text2VDM achieves the highest Geometry CLIP Score of 0.2556, outperforming Paint-it (0.2375), Text2Mesh (0.2497), and TextDeformer (0.2477). In a user study with 32 participants, Text2VDM is preferred for geometry quality (75.3%) and text consistency (67.6%), far exceeding the next-best method (Text2Mesh at 18.3% and 27.3%). The only metric where a baseline wins is mesh self-intersection: TextDeformer achieves 0.04% versus Text2VDM's 0.77%, though the paper attributes TextDeformer's low self-intersection to over-smoothed results that lose described features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text2VDM outperforms all three direct-generation baselines on the primary metric (Geometry CLIP Score): Paint-it (0.2375), Text2Mesh (0.2497), and TextDeformer (0.2477) all score below the proposed 0.2556. Paint-it also suffers the worst mesh quality with 19.42% self-intersection, while Text2Mesh shows 7.18%; TextDeformer achieves the lowest self-intersection (0.04%) but at the cost of over-smoothed geometry that loses prompt-described features. In the user study, Text2VDM dominates with 75.3% preference for geometry quality and 67.6% for text consistency, compared to Text2Mesh's 18.3%/27.3% and TextDeformer's 3.3%/3.4%. In the ablation on semantic coupling (Figures 7–8), CSD with negative prompts fails to decouple coupled semantics and produces unstable optimization, while Attend-and-Excite's latent-code-based enhancement is incompatible with SDS due to temporal randomness across camera poses and noise levels, making Text2VDM's Compel-based weighted blending the most effective approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion) as the core optimization signal and Sobolev preconditioned optimization (Nicolet et al.) for maintaining mesh quality during large deformations, but reframes the problem from full-object 3D generation to sub-object VDM brush generation. It departs from prior semantic control strategies—negative-prompt-based CSD and cross-attention-based Attend-and-Excite—by showing both are ineffective for decoupling sub-object semantics, and instead proposes a simpler weighted-blending approach via Compel to enhance part-related tokens. The mesh deformation formulation also distinguishes it from Text2Mesh (normal-direction displacement) and TextDeformer (Jacobian parameterization), both of which struggle with baking results into reusable VDMs due to limited deformation range or global drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Text2VDM is the first framework to generate reusable, software-compatible sculpting brushes from text, bridging a gap between generative 3D research and practical artist workflows in Blender and ZBrush. The identification of semantic coupling as a fundamental limitation of SDS for sub-object generation is a useful diagnostic that may inform future text-to-3D methods beyond brush generation. Open questions include multiview consistency (a known SDS artifact), reliance on T2I model accuracy (e.g., wrong finger counts), and whether the approach extends to more complex modular 3D assembly beyond single-brush generation.
