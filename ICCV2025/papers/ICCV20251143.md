## Problem

Current 4D radar-camera fusion methods rely on one-stage BEV pipelines that suffer from feature misalignment: monocular depth ambiguity causes position drift when projecting image features to BEV, and the sparse, noisy radar points cannot provide sufficient 3D grounding for the image. The paper argues that the potential of 4D radar and the fusion mechanism itself remain largely unexplored, with no prior work employing a two-stage cross-view fusion strategy for this modality pair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse 4D radar point cloud (0.1k–2k points per frame, each carrying xyz, Doppler velocity, and RCS, voxelized at 0.05 m × 0.05 m × 0.1 m) and a single front-view camera image processed by a frozen Swin-Tiny backbone. It outputs refined 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores over a detection range of up to 51.2 m (VoD) or 70.4 m (TJ4DRadSet). A distinctive aspect is the intermediate proposal stage: Stage 1 produces coarse 3D proposals via an RPN on a fused BEV map, which Stage 2 then refines using per-proposal multi-view feature aggregation.

## Method

CVFusion is a two-stage cross-view fusion network. In Stage 1, radar voxel features (from a SECOND backbone) and camera BEV features (from depth-based view transformation) are fused by a Radar-Guided Iterative (RGIter) BEV module that generates radar occupancy weight maps to iteratively re-weight and downsample camera BEV features across three scales before concatenation, producing a fused BEV map fed to an RPN for proposal generation. In Stage 2, each proposal is refined by two parallel branches: Point-Guided Fusion (PGF) projects radar points within the proposal onto the image plane and queries image features via Cross-Modality Deformable Attention (CMDA), while Grid-Guided Fusion (GGF) encodes uniform grid cells by position and sequentially fuses front-view and BEV features through two CMDA blocks, handling proposals with few or no radar points. The two branch outputs are combined via a transformer self-attention layer before a refine head predicts final box offsets and confidence. The key insight is that radar-guided iterative fusion corrects depth-induced misalignment at the BEV level, while the multi-view (point, FV, BEV) cross-modality aggregation at the instance level compensates for radar sparsity and enriches each proposal with complementary spatial and semantic cues.

## Results

On the VoD validation set, CVFusion achieves 65.41% mAP in the entire annotated area and 82.42% mAP in the driving corridor area, outperforming the previous best 4D radar-camera method LXL by 9.10% and 9.49% mAP respectively. On the TJ4DRadSet test set, CVFusion reaches 40.00% 3D mAP and 44.07% BEV mAP, improving over LXL by 3.68% and 2.87% respectively. Notably, CVFusion surpasses the LiDAR-input PointPillars baseline on most VoD metrics (e.g., 65.41% vs. 61.16% mAP), marking the first radar-camera fusion model to match a classical LiDAR detector. The full two-stage pipeline adds 5.7% mAP over the Stage-1-only variant (59.70% → 65.41%), confirming the value of cross-view proposal refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVFusion outperforms all 14 compared baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest 4D radar-camera competitors, it beats LXL [33] by 9.10% mAP (65.41 vs. 56.31) on VoD and 3.68% 3D mAP (40.00 vs. 36.32) on TJ4DRadSet, and surpasses HyDRa [31] by 4.51% mAP on VoD. Compared to 3D-radar-camera methods (FUTR3D [2], BEVFusion [18], RCFusion [39], RCBEVDet [14]), CVFusion gains 5–16% mAP, demonstrating the benefit of 4D radar's elevation channel combined with cross-view fusion. Against the two-stage LiDAR-camera LoGoNet [13], CVFusion improves mAP by 6.66% (65.41 vs. 58.75) on VoD despite using only radar instead of LiDAR. Most strikingly, CVFusion exceeds the LiDAR-input PointPillars [12] on VoD (65.41% vs. 61.16% mAP), the first radar-camera model to match a classical LiDAR detector. On TJ4DRadSet, CVFusion also dominates radar-only methods (SMURF [15]: 40.00 vs. 32.99 3D mAP; RPFA-Net [34]: 40.00 vs. 29.91) and the radar-camera RCFusion [39] (40.00 vs. 33.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

CVFusion departs from the dominant one-stage BEV fusion paradigm used by prior 4D radar-camera methods (RCFusion, LXL, RCBEVDet), which the authors argue suffers from feature misalignment due to monocular depth ambiguity. It adapts the two-stage RPN + RoI-pooling + refine architecture from PV-RCNN [26] to the radar-camera setting, and borrows the Grid Position Encoder from LoGoNet [13] for its GGF branch. The Cross-Modality Deformable Attention (CMDA) block directly extends the deformable attention mechanism from Deformable DeTR [41] to cross-modal point-to-image querying. The key conceptual departure is treating the fusion problem as a multi-view (point, front-view, BEV) aggregation task within each proposal rather than a single BEV-space concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 4D radar + single camera system can match a LiDAR-input PointPillars network on most metrics, which is a notable milestone for sensor cost reduction in autonomous driving. However, the LiDAR comparison uses a relatively simple baseline (PointPillars), and the method's 5.4 FPS inference speed limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the two-stage cross-view fusion generalizes to multi-camera setups, whether the radar-guided iterative mechanism scales to larger detection ranges, and whether the approach maintains its advantage under the adverse weather conditions that motivate 4D radar in the first place.
