## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised affordance grounding methods rely on one-way exocentric-to-egocentric knowledge transfer, where activation maps from cluttered interaction images are aligned to egocentric object images. This unidirectional pipeline fails in complex scenarios: exocentric activations are vague and include background or human body parts, and occluded interactions (e.g., 'lie on', 'ride') make appearance-based cross-domain transfer unreliable. The paper identifies that the object-centered, clean nature of egocentric images is underutilized and could be fed back to sharpen exocentric activation, but no prior work exploits this bidirectional loop.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired exocentric human-object interaction images and egocentric object images (both resized to 224×224; for video, 8 equally-spaced frames) along with image-level affordance category labels, with no pixel-level annotations. DINO ViT extracts patch features (H×W×C) from both views. The output is a per-pixel affordance localization map P ∈ R^{H×W×N} giving the probability that each pixel belongs to each of N affordance categories, produced by a dedicated pixel decoder, alongside shared activation maps G ∈ R^{H×W×N} for both exocentric and egocentric views.

## Method

LoopTrans establishes a closed-loop knowledge transfer with three stages. First, a Shared CAM (SCAM) module with unified parameters processes both exocentric and egocentric DINO features simultaneously, jointly trained with a classification loss that maximizes the product of sigmoid scores across views, enabling cross-view activation consistency without explicit feature alignment. Second, DINO feature clustering partitions the egocentric image into K semantic parts; the part with highest IoU to the egocentric activation map becomes a pseudo-mask, and a pixel decoder is trained with combined dice and MSE losses to produce precise localization. Third, a denoising distillation mechanism feeds the egocentric localization back into SCAM via parallel noise-absorbing heads: a contrastive loss aligns exocentric affordance features with clean egocentric localization features while pushing noise features (captured by the additional heads) away, effectively suppressing background and human-body interference in exocentric activation. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of classification, denoising distillation, pixel, and correlation losses.

## Results

On AGD20K-Seen, LoopTrans achieves KLD 1.088, SIM 0.445, and NSS 1.322, outperforming the previous best WSMA (KLD 1.176, SIM 0.416, NSS 1.247) by an average of 6.7% across all three metrics. On AGD20K-Unseen, it reaches KLD 1.247, SIM 0.403, NSS 1.315, and on HICO-IFF it attains KLD 1.399, SIM 0.379, NSS 1.226. On video benchmarks, LoopTrans achieves KLD 1.130 / SIM 0.431 / AUC-J 0.827 on EPIC and KLD 1.429 / SIM 0.358 / AUC-J 0.804 on OPRA, surpassing the best weakly-supervised baselines by 4.6% and 6.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoopTrans outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every metric on all three image benchmarks. Against the strongest prior method WSMA [45] (which uses CLIP text prompts for knowledge transfer), LoopTrans improves KLD by 7.5% on AGD20K-Seen (1.088 vs 1.176), 6.6% on AGD20K-Unseen (1.247 vs 1.335), and 4.5% on HICO-IFF (1.399 vs 1.465), while also leading on SIM and NSS in every setting. Compared to LOCATE [24], which refines transfer via part-level feature selection, LoopTrans gains 11.3% on average across KLD/SIM/NSS on AGD20K-Seen, demonstrating that the closed-loop shared CAM handles occlusion and complex scenes more effectively than one-way feature alignment. On video benchmarks, LoopTrans beats HAG-Net [27] by 4.6% on EPIC (KLD 1.130 vs 1.209) and Hotspot [33] by 6.1% on OPRA (KLD 1.429 vs 1.537), and in the image-to-video generalization setting it surpasses WSMA by ~7.5% without requiring any temporal module. The method also handles fully occluded interactions (e.g., 'lie on', 'ride') where prior one-way methods fail to localize affordance regions correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised affordance grounding line initiated by Cross-view-AG (CVPR 2022) and refined by LOCATE (CVPR 2023) and WSMA (AAAI 2024), all of which employ a one-way exocentric-to-egocentric feature alignment pipeline built on CAM. The key departure is the introduction of a closed-loop transfer mechanism: a shared CAM with unified parameters across exo/ego views, DINO-based part clustering for pixel-level pseudo-masks, and a denoising distillation loss that reverse-propagates egocentric localization back into exocentric activation. Conceptually, this repurposes the standard CAM architecture (Zhou et al., CVPR 2016) from a one-shot localization tool into a bidirectional knowledge-extraction engine, and borrows the contrastive distillation paradigm from knowledge distillation literature to suppress background noise in exocentric views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop formulation offers a principled answer to a persistent weakness in affordance grounding: when interaction regions are fully occluded by the human body (e.g., 'lie on', 'ride'), one-way exocentric-to-egocentric appearance transfer breaks down, and this paper demonstrates that egocentric object-centric cues can sustain activation even in those cases. For embodied AI and robot manipulation, reliable affordance localization under occlusion is a practical bottleneck, and the denoising distillation mechanism provides a reusable recipe for cross-domain knowledge transfer beyond affordance grounding. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D affordance grounding or multi-object scenes, whether the DINO clustering step remains effective at higher resolutions, and how the method performs when exocentric and egocentric views come from substantially different camera geometries or object categories unseen during training.
