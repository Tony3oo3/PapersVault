## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods predominantly assume homogeneous architectures between teacher and student, leaving cross-architecture distillation (e.g., CNN-to-ViT, ViT-to-MLP) largely unexplored. The two prior generic approaches either fail on heterogeneous pairs (FitNet drops to 24% on CIFAR-100) or sacrifice spatial information by projecting into logits space (OFA-KD), limiting downstream task performance. The paper identifies two root causes: view mismatch from differing architectural receptive fields, and teacher unawareness where the frozen teacher cannot adapt to the student's learning trajectory.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K, variable resolution for COCO) and produces class labels for classification or bounding boxes for detection. Unusually, the method operates on intermediate features extracted at four stages from both a pre-trained teacher and a trainable student of potentially different architectures (CNN, ViT, or MLP). During training, the teacher model is modified in-place by lightweight prompt-tuning blocks that consume the student's previous-iteration features as feedback, while at inference only the unmodified student model is deployed with zero additional overhead.

## Method

PAT introduces two modules to enable feature-level distillation across heterogeneous architectures. The Region-Aware Attention (RAA) module patchifies student features from all four stages into N_q/4 patches each, projects them to a shared dimension, concatenates them, and passes them through a self-attention layer so the student learns to reblend spatial information across patches and stages into a perspective matching the teacher's; the output is then split back into four stages for standard hierarchical feature matching. The Adaptive Feedback Prompt (AFP) module inserts a fusion block (FB) and prompt block (PB) before each teacher stage, where the FB concatenates the previous-iteration feature difference (student minus teacher) with the current teacher feature, and the PB generates distillation-friendly teacher features via prompt tuning with minimal parameters. A KL-divergence regularization loss between the original and AFP-modified teacher predictions prevents the teacher from collapsing to the student's features. The total objective combines cross-entropy, logits KL-divergence, hierarchical context loss (HCL) for feature matching, and the regularization term, with the additional parameters existing only during training.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, PAT achieves an average improvement of 8.17% over the best baseline across 12 heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, with peak gains of 16.94% (ConvNeXt-T → ResMLP-S12, 83.50% vs. 66.56% from-scratch) and 11.59% (ConvNeXt-T → DeiT-T, 79.59% vs. 68.00%). On ImageNet, PAT reaches 72.22% top-1 accuracy (Mixer-B/16 → MobileNetV2), a 3.35% gain over the best baseline. On COCO object detection, PAT achieves 35.62 mAP (Swin-T → ResNet18) and 32.97 mAP (Swin-T → MobileNetV2), improving over the student-only baseline by 2.36% and 3.50% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAT outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of 12 CIFAR-100 teacher-student pairs, with an average gain of 8.17% over the best per-pair baseline. Against the prior SOTA OFA-KD (logits-based), PAT wins on 10 of 12 pairs, with the largest margin being +2.28% on ConvNeXt-T → DeiT-T (79.59 vs. 75.76) and +2.42% on ConvNeXt-T → Swin-P (80.74 vs. 78.32); OFA retains the edge only on the two MobileNetV2 pairs where its logits-space projection happens to align well. Against feature-based baselines, PAT's advantage is most dramatic: FitNet collapses to 24.06% on ConvNeXt-T → Swin-P and 45.47% on ConvNeXt-T → ResMLP-S12 due to severe view mismatch, while PAT achieves 80.74% and 83.50% respectively. CC, RKD, and CRD (which learn relational/contrastive signals rather than direct feature imitation) show negative average improvements on CIFAR-100 (−0.33%, −1.40%, −0.02%), confirming that direct feature matching—once view mismatch and teacher unawareness are addressed—dominates. On ImageNet, PAT leads all baselines on 3 of 4 pairs (e.g., 72.22% vs. 72.12% OFA on Mixer-B/16 → MobileNetV2; 77.59% vs. 77.31% OFA on Swin-T → ResMLP-S12). On COCO detection, PAT surpasses FitNet by 0.39 mAP (ResNet18) and 0.49 mAP (MobileNetV2), and outperforms all logits-based methods (KD, DKD, OFA) by 2.25–3.50 mAP, validating that spatially rich feature distillation is critical for downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAT extends the heterogeneous knowledge distillation line initiated by OFA-KD, which projected student features into a logits space to bypass architectural differences but discarded spatial information. Where OFA and prior feature-based methods (FitNet, CC, RKD, CRD) either ignore or inadequately handle the perspective gap between architectures, PAT introduces a region-aware attention module to reblend student features into a teacher-compatible view, and an adaptive feedback prompt module (building on prompt tuning from Pro-tuning and CAKD) to make the teacher dynamically responsive to student learning. The feature-matching loss leverages the Hierarchical Context Loss from ReviewKD, and the overall teacher-student paradigm follows the standard KD formulation of Hinton et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAT demonstrates that feature-level distillation can be made to work across truly heterogeneous architectures (CNN↔ViT↔MLP) while preserving spatial information critical for downstream tasks like object detection, where logits-only methods like OFA underperform. However, the practical cost is substantial: PAT requires 14.48M extra parameters and roughly 3× the training epochs of standard KD, and the gains over OFA are most pronounced when feature-based baselines are weakest (e.g., FitNet at 24% on one pair), suggesting the comparison may overstate the advantage. Open questions include whether the four-stage assumption generalizes to deeper or non-hierarchical architectures, whether the approach extends to other modalities (video, audio), and whether the training overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the perspective-alignment benefit.
