## Problem

Diffusion-based audio-driven talking portrait methods suffer from slow iterative sampling (tens of minutes for a few seconds of video) and temporal inconsistency when lifted from image to video generation. Additionally, they heavily rely on auxiliary facial priors (bounding boxes, 2D landmarks, 3D meshes) that impose strong spatial bias and restrict the diversity and fidelity of head movements. FLOAT addresses both issues by replacing pixel-space diffusion with flow matching in a learned orthogonal motion latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 source portrait image, a driving audio signal (resampled to 16 kHz and encoded via Wav2Vec 2.0), and a 7-class speech emotion label (angry, disgust, fear, happy, neutral, sad, surprise) from a pre-trained speech emotion predictor. It outputs a 512×512 talking portrait video at 25 FPS, generated in windows of 50 frames (~2.4 s) with 10 preceding frames carried over for smooth inter-window transitions. The source image is decomposed into an identity latent and a motion latent, and only the motion latent is generated by the flow matching model before being recombined with the identity latent for decoding.

## Method

FLOAT operates in two phases: first, a motion latent autoencoder is pre-trained to encode images into a 512-dim latent space with explicit identity-motion decomposition, where the motion component is expressed as a linear combination of M=20 learned orthonormal basis vectors (inspired by LIA); second, an OT-based flow matching model with a DiT-style transformer vector field predictor samples motion latents conditioned on audio, emotion, and source motion. The key architectural choice is frame-wise AdaLN conditioning (each frame modulated by its own audio/emotion/pose condition) followed by masked self-attention over 2T=4 neighboring frames, decoupling per-frame conditioning from temporal modeling. Training combines the OT flow matching loss with a velocity loss on one-frame differences to enforce temporal consistency, and inference uses an incremental classifier-free vector field with separate guidance scales for audio (γ_a=2) and emotion (γ_e=1), requiring only ~10 NFEs. The orthonormal basis structure further enables closed-form test-time pose editing (λ-control) by projecting generated motion latents onto the basis and modifying individual coefficients.

## Results

On HDTF, FLOAT achieves the best FID (21.10), FVD (162.05), LSE-D (7.29), and LSE-C (8.22) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best diffusion-based method Hallo (FID 25.36, FVD 197.20) by a clear margin. On RAVDESS, FLOAT similarly leads with FID 31.68, FVD 166.36, LSE-D 6.99, and LSE-C 5.73, substantially ahead of EDTalk (FID 75.02) and EchoMimic (FVD 320.22). FLOAT requires only ~10 NFEs for sampling versus 50+ for diffusion-based baselines, achieving the highest inference FPS on a single V100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOAT outperforms all five baselines on FID, FVD, LSE-D, and LSE-C across both HDTF and RAVDESS, with the largest margins over non-diffusion methods SadTalker (FID 71.95 vs. 21.10) and EDTalk (FID 50.08 vs. 21.10). Among diffusion-based methods, FLOAT beats Hallo by 4.26 FID and 35.15 FVD on HDTF, and EchoMimic by 12.45 FID and 134.71 FVD on HDTF. The closest competitor is Hallo, which edges out FLOAT on CSIM (0.869 vs. 0.843 on HDTF) and E-FID (1.039 vs. 1.229 on HDTF), suggesting slightly better identity preservation and expression diversity. On RAVDESS, FLOAT dominates more comprehensively, winning on FID, FVD, E-FID, P-FID, LSE-D, and LSE-C, with only CSIM slightly behind Hallo (0.810 vs. 0.860). Overall, FLOAT achieves the best or near-best result on 6 out of 7 metrics on both datasets while requiring only ~10 NFEs versus 50+ for diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOAT positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) flow matching as a faster alternative to diffusion models for generative modeling, (2) learned motion latent spaces for talking-head animation (inspired by LIA's orthonormal basis decomposition and VASA-1's motion latent sampling), and (3) DiT-style transformer architectures for conditional generation. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based talking portrait pipeline (EMO, Hallo, EchoMimic) by replacing iterative denoising with OT-based flow matching in a compact motion latent space, and from VASA-1 by introducing a computable orthonormal basis that enables closed-form test-time motion editing (λ-control). The frame-wise AdaLN conditioning mechanism is a deliberate architectural departure from DiT's single-time-step modulation, designed to decouple per-frame driving conditions from temporal attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLOAT addresses two practical pain points in audio-driven talking portrait generation: the high sampling cost of diffusion models (reducing from ~50 NFEs to ~10) and the lack of post-hoc controllability over generated motion. The orthogonal motion latent space with a closed-form basis is a genuinely useful design choice that enables interpretable, parameter-level editing of head pose without retraining or external driving signals. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer video sequences beyond the 2.4-second window, whether the 512-dim latent with 20 basis directions is sufficient for more diverse motions, and whether the speech-driven emotion module generalizes to multilingual or non-English speech. The paper does not explore real-time inference or deployment constraints, which remain important for the avatar and video-conferencing applications it targets.
