## Problem

MLLMs for chart understanding rely heavily on OCR-extracted text, causing numerical hallucinations when chart annotations are sparse. Existing chain-of-thought methods improve logical reasoning but fail to enhance the model's fundamental visual perception, as MLLMs cannot localize key chart positions to verify their reasoning steps against the actual visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chart image (processed at dynamic resolution via Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL) and a text question as input, and produces a final numerical answer along with step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning where grounding steps are accompanied by bounding box coordinates (normalized to 0–999). Training data is constructed from chart-code quadruples where data-point text labels are deliberately removed to force visual reading; the pipeline re-renders charts with special character markers inserted at key positions, then extracts BBox coordinates via OCR on the re-rendered images. Four instruction formats are used: standard VQA, localization (predict BBox), reasoning with edited chart (BBox redrawn on input), and reasoning steps (text-only continuation).

## Method

PointCoT augments standard CoT by inserting a reflective grounding interaction: at each grounding step, the model outputs a bounding box identifying the relevant chart region, and the chart is re-rendered with that annotation to verify the reasoning step's visual focus. An automated four-stage pipeline (step decomposition via LLM, code editing to insert position markers, code re-rendering, and OCR-based position extraction) constructs 19.2K chart samples with paired CoT and BBox annotations, expanded to 62.3K instruction pairs. Training is two-stage: first, chart knowledge alignment on diverse instruction data (MMC-Instruct, ChartGemma, ChartQA, ChartBench); second, chart-specific annealing tuning on the PointCoT data. The approach is built on Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL, whose native grounding capabilities (point/BBox output) are critical for the reflection mechanism to function effectively.

## Results

ChartPoint_Q2.5 achieves 87.74% relaxed accuracy@0.05 (avg) on ChartQA and 65.95% on ChartBench, surpassing its Qwen2.5-VL baseline by +1.38% and +5.04% respectively. ChartPoint_Q2 reaches 85.28% on ChartQA and 62.61% on ChartBench, improving over Qwen2-VL by +2.12% and +3.71%. The gains are most pronounced on ChartBench's extra-type charts (Area, Box, Radar, Scatter, Node, Combin.), where ChartPoint_Q2.5 scores 65.03% versus 57.26% for Qwen2.5-VL (+7.77%), confirming that grounding reflection benefits sparse-annotation scenarios most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChartPoint_Q2.5 outperforms all 19 compared baselines on both ChartQA and ChartBench. Against the strongest general MLLM baseline Qwen2.5-VL, it gains +1.38% on ChartQA (87.74 vs 86.36) and a substantial +5.04% on ChartBench (65.95 vs 60.91). Among specialist chart models, the advantage is even larger: ChartPoint_Q2.5 surpasses ChartMoE+PoT by +3.10% on ChartQA and +14.28% on ChartBench (65.95 vs 51.67), and beats TinyChart+PoT by +7.26% and +39.24% respectively. The improvement is most dramatic on ChartBench's extra-type charts (Radar, Scatter, Node, Combin.), where ChartPoint_Q2.5 scores 65.03% versus 55.58% for ChartMoE and 22.56% for TinyChart, indicating that grounding reflection generalizes well to uncommon chart types. On ChartQA's human-annotated subset (the harder split), ChartPoint_Q2.5 reaches 81.36% vs 78.96% for Qwen2.5-VL, confirming gains in sparse-text-annotation scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm for chart understanding (ChartThinker, DOMINO, LaMenDa) by introducing a reflective visual grounding mechanism: the model generates bounding boxes at each reasoning step and interacts with re-rendered charts to verify its spatial focus. It departs from prior instruction-tuning approaches (ChartLlama, ChartGemma, ChartMoE) that scale data volume or resolution, arguing instead that the fundamental gap is weak visual grounding rather than insufficient training data. The method builds directly on Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL for their native grounding capabilities, and leverages the ChartAlign quadruple dataset (table, JSON, code, chart) as the substrate for an automated annotation pipeline that pairs CoT steps with code-based re-rendering and OCR-based position extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode—MLLMs produce plausible reasoning chains but cannot localize the chart regions those steps reference—and shows that explicit grounding supervision during SFT closes this gap, particularly on sparse-annotation benchmarks like ChartBench where OCR shortcuts are unavailable. A key practical limitation is that PointCoT is highly dependent on the base model's pre-existing grounding ability: it yields negligible gains on Qwen-VL or ChartMoE but 1–5% improvements on Qwen2-VL/2.5-VL, suggesting the technique is a complement to, not a replacement for, strong visual grounding. Open questions include whether the code-editing + re-rendering pipeline generalizes to non-programmatic charts (hand-drawn, scanned documents) and whether the bounding-box reflection can be extended to multi-chart or temporal reasoning settings.
