## Problem

Multimodal Knowledge Graphs (MMKGs) promise to bridge the modality isolation of textual KGs and reduce LLM hallucinations, but their construction is impeded by two obstacles: the lack of large-scale fine-grained entity-image annotations needed to train extraction models, and the tendency of visual relation detectors to produce superficial spatial links or hallucinated connections that corrupt graph integrity. Existing MMKG construction paradigms remain resource-intensive, relying on manually annotated captions and predefined relation taxonomies.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images (from CrisisMMD social media posts or ScienceQA diagrams) and optionally external textual knowledge, producing a structured multimodal knowledge graph represented as entity-relation-entity triplets with embedded image storage references. The resulting MMKG is remarkably compact—489 MB for the full ScienceQA corpus versus 739 MB for Visual Genome's scene graph component alone—while retaining direct entity-to-image linkage. At inference time, the KG is queried via LightRAG's hybrid retrieval to augment LLM prompts for downstream classification or QA tasks.

## Method

VaLiK employs an expansion-reduction paradigm with three stages. First, a Chain-of-Experts cascade of pretrained VLMs (BLIP-2 → LLaVA → Qwen2-VL) iteratively generates increasingly detailed textual descriptions from each image, where each expert conditions on the original visual features and the preceding expert's output via cross-attention, eliminating the need for annotated captions. Second, a sliding-window cross-modal similarity verification mechanism scores each token window against the image using a frozen CLIP encoder, discarding windows below an empirical threshold τ to prune hallucinated or redundant content. Third, a powerful LLM (DeepSeek-R1-70B) performs symbolic structuring of the purified descriptions into MMKG triplets following the LightRAG framework, with image storage locations embedded in the graph. The entire process is annotation-free, zero-shot, and modular—new VLMs or LLMs can be swapped in without architectural changes.

## Results

On CrisisMMD, VaLiK (Text-Image, Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 68.90% / 50.02% / 50.69% accuracy on Tasks 1/2/2-Merged, surpassing the Qwen2.5-72B baseline (67.95% / 50.51% / 50.29%) and LightRAG Text-only (67.49% / 45.11% / 45.94%) by 4.90% average. On ScienceQA, Qwen2.5-72B augmented with VaLiK (Text-Image) reaches 84.77% average accuracy, exceeding CoT(GPT-4) (83.99%) and the unenhanced Qwen2.5-72B (78.37%) by 6.40%. The image-only VaLiK KG requires only 489 MB storage versus 739 MB for Visual Genome's scene graph component alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScienceQA, VaLiK (Text-Image, Qwen2.5-72B) achieves 84.77% average, outperforming CoT(GPT-4) (83.99%), GPT-4 (82.69%), DDCoT (80.15%), and Chameleon (79.93%), while the 7B variant (83.16%) surpasses the unenhanced Qwen2.5-7B (74.72%) by 8.44% and the Visual Genome-augmented baseline (75.08%) by 8.08%. On CrisisMMD, VaLiK (Text-Image) with Qwen2.5-7B reaches 68.90%/50.02%/50.69% across three tasks, beating LightRAG Text-only (67.49%/45.11%/45.94%) by 4.90% average and matching or exceeding Qwen2.5-72B (67.95%/50.51%/50.29%). Among multimodal VLMs on CrisisMMD, VaLiK (Qwen2-VL-72B-I, Text-Image) scores 68.89%/49.78%/49.31%, surpassing GPT-4o (68.20%/47.58%/49.55%), LLaVA-34B (56.44%/25.15%/25.07%), BLIP-2 (61.29%/40.86%/40.72%), and CLIP (43.36%/17.88%/20.79%). The Mmkg baseline (73.47%) underperforms even the unenhanced Qwen2.5-7B (74.72%) on ScienceQA, indicating that pre-constructed MMKGs with manual annotations are less effective than VaLiK's annotation-free approach. Ablation results confirm that the CoE-based VLM cascade contributes +3.05% (CrisisMMD) and +4.63% (ScienceQA) average gains, while the similarity verification module adds a further +1.41% to +2.54% on CrisisMMD tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VaLiK builds on the LightRAG framework for KG-based retrieval-augmented generation and adapts the Chain-of-Experts cascade principle to a vision-language setting, chaining multiple pretrained VLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA, Qwen2-VL) to iteratively enrich image descriptions. It departs from prior MMKG construction methods (MSPT, Scene-MMKG, TIVA-KG) that depend on extensively annotated entity-image corpora or predefined relation taxonomies, replacing those with an annotation-free pipeline that uses cross-modal similarity verification (via CLIP) to filter hallucinated content from VLM-generated captions. The approach also contrasts with knowledge-augmented multimodal learning methods (MR-MKG, GraphAdapter) that assume a preconstructed MMKG is already available, instead providing an end-to-end construction pipeline from raw images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual image captions and predefined relation labels, VaLiK lowers the barrier to building task-specific MMKGs, making multimodal knowledge augmentation more accessible for domain adaptation without expensive annotation campaigns. The storage efficiency (489 MB vs. 739 MB for Visual Genome's scene graph alone) and the ability to enable text-only LLMs to perform multimodal reasoning are practically significant for resource-constrained deployments. However, the cross-modal similarity verification module shows inconsistent effects across datasets (helpful on CrisisMMD, slightly harmful on ScienceQA image-only natural science), raising open questions about how to calibrate pruning thresholds for diverse visual complexity. Generalization beyond the two tested benchmarks and to more complex, multi-entity visual scenes remains unvalidated.
