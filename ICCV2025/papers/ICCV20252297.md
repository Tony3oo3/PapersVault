## Problem

Automating text-guided image inpainting faces two unsolved challenges: generating prompts that are semantically coherent with the image context (rather than simple object labels), and automatically assessing whether a generated inpainting is realistically convincing to human observers. Existing inpainting detection datasets are also limited in scale, source diversity, and model coverage, leaving a gap for benchmarking forensic methods against modern generative pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes an RGB image (up to 2048p), object masks (from dataset annotations or OneFormer panoptic segmentation), and image captions (from dataset labels or BLIP-2), and produces inpainted images, LLM-generated context-aware text prompts, and binary deceptiveness labels. Inpainting is performed by five pipelines (HD-Painter, BrushNet, PowerPaint, ControlNet, Inpaint-Anything/Remove-Anything) spanning eight models, yielding both spliced (unmasked region preserved) and fully regenerated images. One-sixth of images undergo a second inpainting round with different masks to simulate multi-edit scenarios.

## Method

SAGI is a model-agnostic pipeline with two components. SAOR approximates the human prompt-selection distribution P(p,o|S(I)) by feeding object labels and image captions to an LLM (ChatGPT 3.5 or Claude Sonnet 3.5), which selects an object and generates a contextually rich prompt that maintains semantic consistency with surrounding content. UGDA assesses realism at test time using a VLM (GPT-4o) via a two-stage protocol: first a standalone realism check, then a comparative assessment where the VLM ranks the original and inpainted images in two reversed orders; if the VLM's response varies between orders (indicating aleatoric uncertainty) or consistently favors the inpainted image, the sample is labeled deceiving. This uncertainty-based criterion exploits the observation that realistic inpaintings sit closer to the VLM's learned distribution of semantically coherent images, making the VLM less confident in distinguishing them from originals. The pipeline is applied across multiple inpainting models to construct SAGI-D (95,839 inpainted images from 77,900 originals across MSCOCO, RAISE, and OpenImages), which serves as a benchmark for retraining forensic detectors.

## Results

Retraining four state-of-the-art inpainting detection models on SAGI-D yields an average in-domain IoU improvement of +37.4% and out-of-domain IoU improvement of +26.1% over their original pretrained versions. TruFor shows the largest gains (+52.5% in-domain, +46.4% out-of-domain IoU), while CAT-Net achieves the highest absolute in-domain IoU of 69.2%. In the human study, UGDA-filtered (deceiving) inpaintings reduce human detection accuracy from 73.7% to 35.2% (a 38.5-point gap), far exceeding the 0.4–12.9-point separations achieved by standard quality/aesthetic metrics. On the AUC comparison (Table 6), SAGI-D-trained models average 93.1 detection AUC and 88.9 localization AUC, outperforming TGIF-trained models (73.6 / 71.0) and original models (67.7 / 64.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four forensic baselines (TruFor, CAT-Net, PSCC-Net, MMFusion) are substantially outperformed by their SAGI-D-retrained counterparts across every evaluation split. TruFor benefits the most, jumping from 12.2% to 64.7% in-domain IoU (+52.5%) and reaching 72.4% IoU on fully-regenerated (FR) images where the original model scored only 19.6%. CAT-Net achieves the highest in-domain IoU (69.2%) but shows the weakest out-of-domain transfer (+2.1%), while PSCC-Net and MMFusion show more balanced cross-domain gains. Compared to training on the TGIF dataset, SAGI-D training yields consistently higher AUCs: the average detection AUC rises from 74.3 (TGIF-trained) to 78.3 (SAGI-D-trained), and localization AUC from 71.0 to 88.9, with SAGI-D-trained TruFor exceeding 99% detection AUC on both test sets. The paper also positions SAGI-D against the CocoGlide and TGIF datasets in Table 4, showing it surpasses both in scale (95,839 vs. 74,976 vs. 512 images), source diversity (3 datasets vs. 1), model diversity (8 inpainting models vs. 3 vs. 1), and maximum resolution (2048p vs. 1024p vs. 256p).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes existing LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude) and VLMs (GPT-4o) into a unified inpainting pipeline, departing from prior work that relied on simple object labels or handcrafted prompts for text-guided inpainting. It extends the inpainting detection dataset lineage (CocoGlide, TGIF) by introducing two novel components: semantic alignment via LLM-generated context-aware prompts (SAOR) and an uncertainty-guided realism filter based on VLM input-reordering (UGDA). The forensic detection evaluation builds on established architectures (PSCC-Net, CAT-Net, TruFor, MMFusion) but positions SAGI-D as a substantially more diverse and higher-quality training resource than prior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGI-D (95k+ inpainted images across 3 source datasets, 8 inpainting models, up to 2048p) fills a critical gap as the largest and most diverse AI-inpainting detection benchmark, and retraining forensic models on it yields 37.4% in-domain and 26.1% out-of-domain IoU improvements. A key open question is the dependence on foundation model capabilities: both SAOR and UGDA inherit biases and failure modes of the underlying LLM/VLM, as shown by the paper's own failure cases. The finding that standard quality and aesthetic metrics (NIMA, QAlign, CLIP-based) are largely uncorrelated with human-perceived inpainting realism challenges the field's reliance on these proxies and suggests uncertainty-aware VLM assessment may be a more principled path forward for realism evaluation beyond inpainting.
