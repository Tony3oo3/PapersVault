## Problem

Video diffusion models achieve impressive realism but demand high-end GPU resources, making them impractical for on-device deployment. Prior acceleration work focused on reducing sampling steps but did not address the per-step memory and compute cost that prevents mobile execution. This paper presents the first image-to-video diffusion model optimized specifically for mobile hardware.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single conditioning RGB image, which is encoded into a VAE latent (duplicated 14 times and concatenated with a noise latent of shape 14×64×32) and a CLIP image embedding for cross-attention, along with FPS and motion-bucket micro-conditioning scalars. It produces a 14-frame video clip at 512×256 pixels in a single UNet forward pass, completing generation in 1.7 seconds on a Xiaomi 14 Pro (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3). The motion bucket is computed as the area under the normalized cumulative sum of singular values of a spatiotemporally reshaped grayscale frame stack.

## Method

Starting from Stable Video Diffusion's spatio-temporal UNet, the paper applies a cascade of optimizations: (1) temporal multiscaling inserts a 2× temporal downsample after the first UNet block and a corresponding upsample before the last, reducing FLOPs by 34%; (2) cross-attention is simplified by removing the no-op QK^T similarity computation since the context is a single CLIP token, which is critical for avoiding OOM on mobile; (3) channel funneling inserts low-rank funnel matrices between consecutive affine layers (with coupled singular initialization via truncated SVD) to reduce inner dimensionality at inference without changing output feature-map size; (4) learned temporal block pruning assigns trainable importance scores to each temporal residual block, samples a fixed budget of blocks per training step using Brewer's sampling with a straight-through estimator, and at inference retains only the top-n blocks (up to 70% pruned); and (5) adversarial finetuning following SF-V collapses the 50-step denoising loop into a single forward pass using a frozen UNet-encoder discriminator with spatial and temporal projection heads. The full pipeline is trained in two stages: 100k diffusion-loss iterations on 4 A100s, followed by 10k adversarial-finetuning steps on 2 GPUs.

## Results

MobileVD generates 14 latent frames at 512×256 px in 1.7 seconds on a Xiaomi 14 Pro (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3) with a single UNet forward pass (1 NFE), achieving FVD of 171 on UCF-101 at 7 FPS. This requires only 4.34 TFLOPs per step (45 ms GPU latency), roughly half the compute of the finetuned SVD baseline (8.60 TFLOPs, 82 ms) and SF-V (8.24 TFLOPs, 76 ms). At the higher 1024×576 resolution, MobileVD-HD achieves FVD 184 with 1 NFE and 23.63 TFLOPs, compared to SVD's FVD 149 at 50 NFEs and 45.43 TFLOPs. On VBench-I2V, MobileVD matches SVD's dynamic degree (95.77 vs 95.69) at motion bucket 40 while maintaining comparable subject and background consistency scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the finetuned SVD baseline at 512×256, MobileVD achieves a slightly better FVD (171 vs. 196) while halving the per-step compute (4.34 vs. 8.60 TFLOPs) and reducing NFE from 50 to 1, and is the only method that runs on the phone (1780 ms vs. OOM). Compared to SF-V (the closest one-step competitor), MobileVD matches FVD within 3 units (171 vs. 168) but cuts FLOPs in half (4.34 vs. 8.24 TFLOPs) and phone latency by 51% (1780 vs. 3630 ms). At 1024×576, MobileVD-HD (FVD 184, 1 NFE) dramatically outperforms UFOGen (FVD 1917) and LADD (FVD 1894) by over 10×, and beats AnimateLCM (FVD 281, 8 NFE) by 97 FVD units while using half the FLOPs (23.63 vs. 45.43 TFLOPs). MobileVD-HD is within 3 FVD of SF-V at high resolution (184 vs. 181) but with roughly half the compute. The only baseline MobileVD does not beat on FVD is the original 50-step SVD at 1024×576 (149 vs. 184), though that model requires 50× more forward passes and cannot run on-device.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its base architecture and adopts the adversarial finetuning framework from SF-V for single-step generation, but departs from prior acceleration work (AnimateLCM, LADD, UFOGen) that focused exclusively on reducing sampling steps without addressing the memory and per-step compute bottlenecks that prevent mobile deployment. The two novel architectural contributions—channel funneling (a mergeable width-reduction scheme distinct from low-rank decomposition or channel pruning) and learned temporal block pruning via straight-through estimator gates—have no direct precedent in the video diffusion literature. The paper also positions itself against concurrent work SnapGen-V by finetuning a publicly released SVD checkpoint rather than training from scratch, making the approach accessible to low-compute practitioners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first image-to-video diffusion model deployed on a consumer smartphone (1.7 s for 14 frames at 512×256 on a Xiaomi 14 Pro), this work demonstrates that on-device video generation is practically achievable, with implications for privacy-preserving content creation and offline use cases. Key limitations remain: output is capped at 14 frames at 512×256, the model inherits SVD's lack of text conditioning, and the UNet architecture was chosen partly because DiT-based alternatives have quadratic memory costs that are even harder to fit on mobile NPUs. Open questions include whether more efficient autoencoders can push resolution and duration higher at the same latent cost, and whether the channel-funneling and temporal-pruning techniques transfer to DiT-based video models as the field shifts toward that architecture.
