## Problem

Instruction-based image editing datasets are typically generated by automated pipelines (LLM + diffusion model) that produce edited images misaligned with their editing instructions, creating noisy supervision signals. Prior work attempted to compensate by adding large VLMs to the model architecture or introducing recognition pre-training tasks, but these approaches add substantial computational overhead without resolving the fundamental data-quality issue. SuperEdit asks whether improving supervision signal quality alone—without architectural changes or extra pre-training—can close the performance gap.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single original RGB image and one free-form text editing instruction (≤77 CLIP tokens) and outputs the edited image via a 30-step DDIM sampler. During training, the model additionally consumes a rectified instruction and a contrastive (wrong) instruction per sample, both encoded by the same CLIP text encoder, to produce two noise predictions that are compared via triplet loss. The base architecture is the 1.1B-parameter InstructPix2Pix U-Net with channel-concatenated original-image conditioning, and the rectification/contrastive construction is performed offline by GPT-4o.

## Method

SuperEdit introduces two data-level interventions on top of the standard InstructPix2Pix training loop. First, it rectifies editing instructions by prompting GPT-4o with original–edited image pairs and a unified guideline derived from diffusion generation priors (early timesteps govern global layout, mid timesteps govern local object attributes, late timesteps govern fine details, and style changes span all stages), producing instructions that accurately describe the actual visual differences. Second, it constructs contrastive supervision by having GPT-4o generate a single-attribute perturbation (quantity, spatial position, or object type) of the rectified instruction to form a negative instruction; during training, the model predicts noise for both the positive and negative instructions, and a triplet loss (with margin m) penalizes the model if the negative-instruction noise is closer to the true noise than the positive-instruction noise. The total loss is the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE on the positive instruction plus a weighted triplet term, and no additional modules, pre-training tasks, or VLMs are introduced into the editing model itself.

## Results

On the Real-Edit benchmark (GPT-4o evaluated), SuperEdit achieves 69.7% Overall Accuracy and 3.91 Overall Score, outperforming the previous SOTA SmartEdit (58.3%, 3.59) by 11.4 percentage points in Overall Acc and 0.32 in Overall Score. This is accomplished with only 40K training samples (30× less than SmartEdit's 1.2M) and a 1.1B parameter model (13× smaller than SmartEdit's 14.1B), without any additional VLM modules or pre-training tasks. In human evaluation, SuperEdit scores 3.47 Overall versus SmartEdit's 2.93 (+10.8%), with particularly large gains in Preserving (+16.0%) and Quality (+14.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperEdit outperforms all nine baselines on every metric of the Real-Edit benchmark. Against the primary SOTA SmartEdit [24] (which adds a 13B LLaVA VLM to the InstructPix2Pix framework), SuperEdit gains 11.4% in Overall Acc (69.7% vs. 58.3%) and 0.32 in Overall Score (3.91 vs. 3.59), while using 30× less training data and 13× fewer parameters. MultiReward [16] ties SmartEdit on Overall Acc (58.3%) but trails SuperEdit by 11.4 points; it also has a notably weaker Preserving score (3.43 vs. 4.14). Among methods without extra VLM modules, MagicBrush [58] is the closest competitor on Preserving (70% vs. 77%) but falls far behind on Following (51% vs. 67%) and Overall (57.0% vs. 69.7%). Methods that rely on additional pre-training (InstructDiffusion [14], MGIE [12], KOSMOS-G [33]) all score below 52% Overall Acc, with KOSMOS-G particularly weak on Preserving (9%). In human evaluation, SuperEdit leads SmartEdit by 10.8% Overall (3.47 vs. 2.93), with the largest gap in Preserving (+16.0%), confirming that rectified and contrastive supervision improves both instruction following and preservation of unedited regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InstructPix2Pix diffusion editing framework but departs sharply from the dominant trend of addressing noisy supervision by augmenting the model architecture with large VLMs (SmartEdit, MGIE, KOSMOS-G) or adding recognition pre-training tasks (InstructDiffusion, Emu-Edit). Instead, it takes a data-centric approach: using GPT-4o guided by timestep-specific diffusion generation priors to rectify editing instructions so they better match the actual image pairs, and introducing contrastive triplet loss to sharpen the model's sensitivity to subtle instruction differences. The diffusion prior insight—that early, mid, and late sampling stages handle global layout, local attributes, and details respectively—is borrowed from T2I analysis literature but repurposed here as a structured prompt for VLM-based instruction rectification, a novel application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a 1.1B InstructPix2Pix model trained on only 40K rectified samples outperforms 14.1B VLM-augmented models trained on 1.2M samples, suggesting that supervision quality can substitute for architectural complexity and data scale in instruction-based editing. This offers a cheaper, simpler recipe for practitioners who lack the compute for large VLMs or extensive pre-training. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-dependent rectification pipeline generalizes to multi-step or compositional edits, whether the contrastive triplet loss transfers to other diffusion editing paradigms (e.g., flow-matching or autoregressive), and whether further data scaling beyond 40K continues to yield gains or eventually saturates.
