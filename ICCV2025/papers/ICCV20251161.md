## Problem

No published VQA work accounts for ambiguity about where the content described in a question is located in the image, a gap that can lead to incorrect answers with serious consequences for users such as blind individuals. Without a benchmark, it is impossible to measure how well models recognize or resolve this 'focus ambiguity,' where a question's referential language (e.g., 'this,' 'the screen') maps to multiple plausible image regions.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image paired with a natural-language visual question and produces two outputs: a binary ambiguity label (ambiguous/unambiguous) and instance segmentation masks covering all plausible image regions the question's focus phrase could refer to. Questions span vague demonstratives ('this', 'it'), specific categories ('bus'), and detailed descriptions ('person in blue next to the car'), and the segmentations ground the question's focus rather than the answer, which can differ (e.g., 'What is above the mirror?' grounds the mirror, not the objects above it). The dataset contains 5,500 examples with 12,880 instance segmentations, split into 70/70/5,360 for zero/few-shot evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is the VQ-FocusAmbiguity dataset, constructed by extending four sources (PACO, MSRA-B, and AnswerTherapy's VQAv2 and VizWiz subsets) with human-annotated question-focus segmentations and binary ambiguity labels, yielding a nearly even 2,437/3,063 ambiguous/unambiguous split. For benchmarking, the paper defines two novel tasks: (1) ambiguity recognition, where four foundation models (GPT-4o, InternVL2-76B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Molmo-7B) are evaluated under five prompting strategies (zero-shot, zero-shot CoT, zero-shot enhanced CoT, few-shot, few-shot enhanced CoT) to output a one-word classification; and (2) focus localization, where three approaches (GLaMM, GPT-4o+GLaMM, Molmo+SAM) generate segmentation masks for all plausible focus regions, scored by mAP, union IoU, and max IoU. A key analytical finding is that question groundings differ from answer groundings in 79% of ambiguous and 36% of unambiguous examples, establishing question focus as an independent reasoning step. All models perform poorly overall, with chain-of-thought prompting yielding the largest accuracy gains (e.g., +18.4 pp for Molmo) and Molmo+SAM achieving the best localization mAP (24.3) due to its bias toward pointing at multiple regions.

## Results

For question ambiguity recognition (binary classification), GPT-4o achieves the best accuracy of 69.6% and F1 of 69.8% under zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting, while the 7B models Qwen2.5-VL and Molmo reach 65.5% and 56.9% accuracy respectively with the same prompting strategy. The much larger InternVL2 (76B) lags behind at only 56.7% accuracy, likely due to lacking region-level counting pretraining. For focus region localization (instance segmentation), Molmo+SAM achieves the best mAP of 24.3, followed by GPT-4o+GLaMM at 14.24 and standalone GLaMM at 13.24, with all models performing poorly overall, confirming the dataset's challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other models on ambiguity recognition, reaching 69.6% accuracy (ZS-CoT) versus 65.5% for Qwen2.5-VL, 56.9% for Molmo, and 56.7% for InternVL2. Notably, chain-of-thought prompting dramatically boosts the 7B models: Molmo improves from 38.5% (ZS) to 56.9% (ZS-CoT), a +18.4 pp gain, and Qwen2.5-VL improves from 57.2% to 65.5%, allowing these small models to match or surpass the 76B InternVL2. For localization, Molmo+SAM leads in mAP (24.3) due to its tendency to point to multiple regions, but has the lowest union IoU (36.4) because SAM fails to segment whole objects from single-point prompts; GLaMM alone achieves the highest union IoU (41.9) but consistently outputs only a single mask, limiting its mAP to 13.24. GPT-4o+GLaMM achieves the best max IoU (47.83 under FS prompting) but its generated descriptions poorly correlate with the number of ground-truth regions, capping its mAP at 14.24. Overall, no model exceeds 70% accuracy on recognition or 25 mAP on localization, confirming that focus ambiguity remains an open challenge for current vision-language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by treating the question itself as a source of ambiguity, distinct from the answer-level ambiguity studied in prior VQA work (e.g., subjectivity, granularity differences). While Visual7W and AnswerTherapy locate where answers reside in images, and referring expression comprehension handles specific linguistic expressions, no prior dataset or benchmark addresses the case where vague question language (e.g., 'this', 'it') could refer to multiple plausible image regions. The dataset is constructed by extending existing segmentation corpora (PACO, MSRA-B) and VQA datasets (VizWiz-VQA, VQAv2 via AnswerTherapy), repurposing their ground-truth segmentations to create question-focus annotations rather than answer annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 79% of ambiguous questions have question groundings that differ from answer groundings challenges the implicit assumption in answer-grounding literature that locating the answer suffices to resolve what the question is about. All benchmarked models perform poorly (best accuracy ~70% for ambiguity recognition, best mAP ~24% for focus localization), establishing a clear gap for future research. The practical stakes are high for VQA services used by blind and visually impaired users, where misidentifying the question's focus could lead to harmful or useless answers. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on this dataset (or similar) can close the gap, and whether the findings generalize beyond the 5,500-example scale and the specific ambiguity types coded here.
