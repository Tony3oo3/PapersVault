## Problem

State-of-the-art egocentric perception models process all available sensory modalities simultaneously with heavy clip-based architectures, incurring prohibitive computational costs for real-time AR/VR deployment. Prior work has explored model distillation or adaptive modality selection in isolation, but distillation is static and cannot adapt to varying task demands, while adaptive selection typically relies on expensive models that limit efficiency gains. No unified framework jointly learns lightweight distillation and context-aware modality selection to balance accuracy and compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel audio, sparsely sampled video frames, and behavioral signals (head pose, eye gaze, motion trajectory) from wearable devices. A key design constraint is that for action recognition, the policy enforces a strict one-frame-per-action selection guided by audio previewing, rather than sampling multiple frames as in prior distillation approaches. For active speaker localization, the number of audio channels processed is dynamically chosen per segment rather than fixed at four. Outputs are task-specific: verb/noun/action class labels, spatio-temporal speaker locations, or predicted future gaze/orientation/trajectory at 300/500/700 ms horizons.

## Method

EGOADAPT unifies two modules trained jointly: a Cross-Modal Feature Distillation (CFD) module that transfers knowledge from a heavy video teacher (e.g., TIM) into a lightweight student fusing sparse frame, audio, and behavioral features via L1 feature matching, KL-divergence distillation, and ground-truth classification losses; and a Task-Aware Multisensory Policy Learning (TeMPLe) module built on an LSTM that outputs binary per-modality decisions via Gumbel-Softmax sampling, making the discrete selection differentiable for end-to-end backpropagation. The policy's action space is task-adaptive: for ASL and behavior anticipation it selects which modalities and audio channels to activate, while for action recognition it uses a multi-head attention + recurrent CNN handshaking module on audio to detect salient temporal events, then picks one representative frame per event. A three-stage training curriculum first pre-trains the distillation module, then trains the policy with a combined loss that includes per-modality cost penalties (λ_k) and incorrect-prediction penalties (γ), and finally jointly fine-tunes both modules. This joint training yields a ~22% improvement in ASL over using either distillation or policy learning alone, achieving up to 89% GMACs reduction and 9.6× energy reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy.

## Results

On EPIC-Kitchens (action recognition), EGOADAPT achieves 76.65% verb, 66.83% noun, and 56.74% action top-1 accuracy at only 7.14 GMACs, outperforming the strongest efficiency-focused baseline Ego-only (73.33/59.48/52.59 at 507.39 GMACs) by 3.35–7.84% while using ~71× fewer GMACs. On EasyCom (active speaker localization), EGOADAPT reaches 89.74 mAP with 0.070 GMACs, 0.39M parameters, and 0.003 J energy—nearly matching the teacher MUST (89.88 mAP) while reducing GMACs by ~9×, parameters by 82%, and energy by ~10×. On Aria Everyday Activities (behavior anticipation), EGOADAPT achieves the lowest MAE across gaze, head orientation, and trajectory at all three time horizons (e.g., 8.53° gaze at T300ms) with 0.003 J energy, reducing relative MAE by 18.08% over the SOTA GLC baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGOADAPT outperforms all 17 baselines across the three tasks on the joint accuracy–efficiency frontier. On EPIC-Kitchens, it beats the strongest efficiency-oriented method Ego-only [66] by 3.35% (verb), 7.84% (noun), and 4.98% (action) while using ~71× fewer GMACs (7.14 vs. 507.39); it slightly trails the teacher TIM AV [5] (77.19/67.22/57.57) by <1% per metric but at 3.7× lower compute. On EasyCom ASL, EGOADAPT (89.74 mAP) nearly matches the teacher MUST [78] (89.88 mAP) at 9.2× fewer GMACs (0.070 vs. 0.642) and 82% fewer parameters (0.39M vs. 2.17M), and decisively outperforms all other baselines—e.g., LocoNet [70] (71.83 mAP), AdaMML [49] (76.90 mAP), and VS-VIO [76] (72.31 mAP)—by 17–18 mAP points. On AEA behavior anticipation, EGOADAPT achieves the lowest MAE in every cell of the 3×3 behavior–horizon grid, reducing relative MAE by 18.08% over GLC [38] and 13.5% over MuSTAVB [78], while consuming 0.003 J energy versus 0.029–1.371 J for all other methods. The only baseline that marginally exceeds EGOADAPT on raw accuracy is TIM AV [5] on AR (by <1% per metric) and MUST [78] on ASL (by 0.14 mAP), both at substantially higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGOADAPT unifies two previously separate research threads: cross-modal knowledge distillation (extending Listen to Look [17] and EgoDistill [59] to a broader sensory suite including multi-channel audio and behavioral data) and adaptive modality selection via policy learning (building on AdaMML [49]'s LSTM-based Gumbel-Softmax framework). The key conceptual departure is the joint, differentiable training of both modules through a three-stage curriculum, rather than treating distillation as a static compression step and policy selection as a separate routing mechanism. The paper also extends the action recognition setting by enforcing a strict one-frame-per-event policy guided by audio previewing, going beyond the multi-frame sampling used in prior distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For AR/VR and wearable-device deployment, the demonstrated 89% GMACs reduction, 82% parameter reduction, and 9.6× energy savings while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy make this a practically relevant efficiency framework. The on-device 4-bit quantization results (77–79% mAP at ~7–10 mW) suggest near-term deployability on edge hardware. Open questions include whether the joint training strategy generalizes to tasks with richer action spaces (spatial resolution selection, network quantization as policy actions) and whether the three-stage curriculum is necessary or whether end-to-end training with proper regularization could achieve similar results. The paper's scope is limited to three specific egocentric tasks, and generalization to longer-horizon or spatially complex tasks remains untested.
