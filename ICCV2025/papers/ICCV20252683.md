## Problem

Precise control over multiple continuous visual attributes (e.g., nose width, eye openness) in pretrained T2I diffusion models remains difficult, especially for indescribable attributes that rarely appear in text. Existing approaches either require paired synthetic data, depend on pretrained GAN latent spaces, or train separate models per attribute, limiting scalability and real-world applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a vector of continuous numeric attribute values (e.g., 20 facial attributes on a 0-1 scale, or car size/pose/body type) as conditioning, and produces a single generated image reflecting all specified attributes simultaneously. Training uses unpaired real images with per-image attribute labels extracted via dlib landmarks, RetinaFace, and DeepFace; no positive-negative pairs or synthetic data are required. The adapter scales to an arbitrary number of attributes with negligible additional memory, and a scalar lambda at inference time controls the strength of attribute conditioning relative to text.

## Method

Att-Adapter is a plug-and-play module inserted into a frozen diffusion U-Net via a decoupled cross-attention layer that adds an attribute-specific attention term (weighted by lambda) alongside the standard text cross-attention. The attribute condition is mapped through a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) that learns a one-to-many mapping from the attribute vector to a latent representation, with a variational encoder q(Z|V,C), a conditional prior p(Z|C), and a decoder p(V'|Z). The CVAE's KL-divergence term regularizes the adapter against identity overfitting; without it, fixing attribute values causes the model to converge to a single face identity, while the denoising loss preserves attribute accuracy. Training jointly optimizes the CVAE ELBO (with alpha=0.0001 on the KL term) and the standard diffusion denoising objective, using a pretrained image encoder (CLIP or face-recognition model) to extract the image feature V. At sampling time, the conditional prior p(Z|C) is sampled via the reparameterization trick, yielding diverse outputs for the same attribute values.

## Results

On the FFHQ latent control benchmark, Att-Adapter achieves a Control Range (CR) of 67.87 (single-attribute) and 90.39 (multi-attribute), outperforming the strongest baseline S-I-GANs (49.45 and 68.4) by 37% and 32% respectively. Disentanglement (DIS) scores are 29.8% (single) and 32.3% (multi), again the best among all methods. On the absolute control setting (FFHQ), Att-Adapter achieves the lowest facial composition L1 error (15.57 vs. LoRA's 17.48) and race cross-entropy (0.36 vs. 0.48), while on EVOX it reaches 95% size accuracy versus LoRA's 67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Att-Adapter wins decisively against all six baselines on both CR and DIS in the latent control setting. Against S-I-GANs (the strongest GAN-based baseline), it gains +18.4 CR (single) and +22.0 CR (multi), attributed to training on real images rather than synthetic GAN-generated pairs. Against W+ Adapter and ConceptSlider, the margin is even larger (e.g., 67.87 vs. 26.59/26.73 CR single), partly because those methods rely on pretrained StyleGAN latent spaces that cap their expressivity. In the absolute control setting on FFHQ, Att-Adapter beats LoRA on facial composition L1 (15.57 vs. 17.48) and race CE (0.36 vs. 0.48), while ITI-GEN (both naive and CDM variants) fails to apply finetuned attributes at all (L1 of 52–70). On EVOX, Att-Adapter matches LoRA on discrete attributes (pose 98% vs. 99%, body 76% vs. 75%) but substantially outperforms it on the continuous size attribute (95% vs. 67%), confirming the advantage of continuous numeric conditioning over LoRA's discretized tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

Att-Adapter builds on the decoupled cross-attention mechanism from IP-Adapter [41] as its core injection point for conditioning, and repurposes the Conditional Variational Autoencoder [35, 44] as a regularizer to prevent the adapter from collapsing to a single identity. It departs from the dominant line of continuous-attribute control methods (W+ Adapter, PreciseControl, ConceptSlider) that all depend on a pretrained StyleGAN latent space or paired synthetic data, and from LoRA-based approaches that require discretizing continuous values. The paper's key conceptual move is framing multi-attribute control as a one-to-many mapping problem solvable by CVAE, rather than a one-to-one regression that overfits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that precise, continuous, multi-attribute control in a new domain is achievable with unpaired real data and a single plug-and-play adapter, removing the need for expensive paired data generation or domain-specific GAN training. This opens the door to practical domain-adaptation pipelines (e.g., automotive design, product visualization) where attribute labels are naturally unpaired. Limitations include reliance on external attribute extractors (dlib, DeepFace) that may not exist for arbitrary domains, evaluation on only two datasets (faces, cars), and the open question of how the method scales to dozens or hundreds of correlated attributes. The finding that multi-attribute joint training improves disentanglement over single-attribute training is a useful practical insight for adapter design.
