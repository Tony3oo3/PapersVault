## Problem

Perpetual 3D scene generation via iterative outpainting suffers from semantic drift, where accumulated deviations cause the scene to gradually transform into an entirely different environment (e.g., desert becoming a lake). Existing methods either exhibit this drift prominently (WonderJourney) or suppress it by restricting camera motion, resulting in monotonous, content-poor scenes (SceneScape, InfiniteNature-Zero). The paper tackles the open challenge of generating long-range 3D view sequences that are both semantically consistent with the initial scene and visually diverse in content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as the initial scene view, a user-specified camera trajectory for the view sequence, and optional text prompts describing new or modified concepts during refinement. It produces a long-range sequence of 3D scene views along with an accumulated 3D point cloud representation. The initial image is processed by SAM for concept segmentation and monocular depth estimation for unprojection; the SceneConceptGraph is constructed from the first view and dynamically updated at each generation step, with test-time training taking approximately 25 seconds per refinement on a single A6000 GPU.

## Method

ScenePainter introduces a two-stage framework built around a hierarchical SceneConceptGraph with three concept levels (global style, category regions, individual objects) and three relation types connecting them. In the construction stage, each concept-relation pair is encoded as a dedicated textual embedding via a two-phase customization (Textual Inversion for text handles, then DreamBooth for full model weights) trained with a combined loss of masked reconstruction, scene-prior preservation, and cross-attention alignment. In the refinement stage, the customized text-to-image model is converted to an outpainter via Blended Latent Diffusion, and during generation the graph is dynamically updated with test-time training (50 UNet steps) to add, modify, or mute concepts while the outpainter's scene-specific prior is re-aligned in parallel. The key distinction from prior work is treating concept-relation pairs—not individual objects—as the unit of textual conditioning, enabling the outpainter to maintain spatial layout, style, and semantic coherence across arbitrarily long view sequences.

## Results

On the Single Image Customization task, ScenePainter achieves the best scene fidelity scores of 0.931 (DINO) and 0.952 (CLIP-I), outperforming the strongest baseline IP-Adapter (0.899 / 0.922) by 3.2 and 3.0 points respectively. On the 3D Views Generation task, a user study shows ScenePainter is preferred over WonderJourney on all three criteria (89.3% quality, 83.4% diversity, 92.6% consistency) and over SceneScape (92.1% quality, 97.6% diversity, 93.8% consistency), demonstrating a strong balance between visual diversity and semantic consistency that neither baseline achieves alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScenePainter outperforms all four single-image customization baselines on both DINO and CLIP-I fidelity metrics, with the largest margin over InstantBooth (+14.6 DINO, +12.0 CLIP-I) and the smallest over IP-Adapter (+3.2 DINO, +3.0 CLIP-I). For 3D view sequence generation, ScenePainter is strongly preferred over WonderJourney on consistency (92.6%) and quality (89.3%), while also winning on diversity (83.4%), indicating that the semantic drift issue in WonderJourney degrades all three criteria. Against SceneScape, ScenePainter wins overwhelmingly on diversity (97.6%) and quality (92.1%), confirming that SceneScape's conservative camera motion produces monotonous scenes. The combined baseline BAS+WJ (Break-A-Scene + WonderJourney) still suffers from monotonous layouts, shifted style, and geometric disorder due to the absence of multi-level concept-relation customization and test-time refinement, further validating the necessity of the full SceneConceptGraph framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScenePainter extends the 'navigate-and-imagine' paradigm for perpetual 3D scene generation (WonderJourney, SceneScape, InfiniteNature-Zero) by addressing the semantic drift problem that these methods suffer from due to accumulated outpainting deviations. It repurposes the two-phase Textual Inversion + DreamBooth customization strategy from Break-A-Scene, but elevates it from object-level to scene-level by introducing the SceneConceptGraph—a hierarchical graph encoding multi-level concepts (environment, category regions, individual objects) and their spatial/semantic relations. The outpainting model is initialized via Blended Latent Diffusion with the scene-specific prior learned during construction, and the graph is dynamically refined at test time to allow controlled scene evolution, a mechanism absent in prior 3D generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic drift problem is a fundamental bottleneck for long-range 3D scene generation and long video synthesis; ScenePainter's structured concept-relation representation offers a principled way to maintain scene identity over many iterative outpainting steps while still permitting user-guided diversity. However, the evaluation is limited to 30 scenes and a small user study rather than large-scale quantitative 3D metrics, and the method depends on SAM segmentation quality and a single A6000 GPU, raising questions about scalability to very long sequences or complex multi-room indoor scenes. An open question is whether the test-time graph refinement can handle rapid scene transitions (e.g., moving from a forest to a city) without requiring full re-construction of the SceneConceptGraph.
