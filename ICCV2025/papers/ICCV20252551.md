## Problem

In Data-Free Knowledge Distillation, the generator must produce synthetic images that approximate the original training distribution without ever seeing it. Existing methods rely on manually induced priors (BatchNorm statistics, variance/norm constraints) that are structurally independent of the teacher and fail to generalize to larger, more complex datasets where the distribution space is too broad for hand-crafted rules to cover.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252551/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained teacher (ResNet, WideResNet, or VGG variants), a student architecture, and uniformly sampled pseudo labels encoded via Label-Text Embedding (LTE) as the generator input. Unlike prior DFKD methods that feed full-resolution Gaussian noise, CPNet uses lower-resolution label embeddings, which contributes to its faster generation speed. The output is a trained student model; the intermediate synthetic images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) are semantically meaningful but not photorealistic, serving as feature-level training signals rather than visual reconstructions.

## Method

CPNet couples the generator with the teacher by explicitly constructing G = T⁻¹: the teacher is split into L sequential blocks separated by BatchNorm layers, and each block is assigned a sub-generator that mimics its inverse transformation, connected end-to-end in a flow-based manner. Each sub-generator's output is normalized to match the corresponding BatchNorm running statistics, embedding the teacher's intermediate-layer knowledge directly into the generation process and forming an autoencoder over label information. For diversity, a novel feature-level adversarial loss converts ground-truth labels into penultimate-layer feature constraints via inverse Softmax and the Moore-Penrose inverse of the classification head, then penalizes cosine similarity between the resulting vector and the head weights to push generated features away from the current student's predictions. Training proceeds in two phases: a warmup that optimizes the generator with classification, BatchNorm, and alignment losses, followed by alternating generator/student updates that add the adversarial term and periodic generator re-initialization to prevent overfitting.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (300 epochs), CPNet achieves top-1 accuracy of 95.26% (R34→R18), 94.09% (W40-2→W16-2), 92.0% (W40-2→W16-1), 94.22% (W40-2→W40-1), and 92.18% (V11→R18). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 77.68% (R34→R18), 71.9% (W40-2→W16-2), 63.23% (W40-2→W16-1), 71.98% (W40-2→W40-1), and 71.8% (V11→R18). On TinyImageNet (R34→R18), CPNet attains 64.91%. In terms of efficiency, CPNet-100 completes training in 1.39 GPU-hours on CIFAR-10 and 1.78 hours on CIFAR-100, yielding a 7.48× average speedup over DeepInversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPNet-300 outperforms the strongest prior method NAYER-300 on 10 out of 11 teacher-student configurations, with the largest margin of +1.0% on CIFAR-100 (W40-2→W16-1: 63.23 vs 62.23) and +0.74% on TinyImageNet (64.91 vs 64.17); it trails NAYER only on CIFAR-10 V11→R18 (92.18 vs 92.37). Against DDA, CPNet wins on 9 of 11 settings, with particularly large gains on CIFAR-100 W40-2→W16-1 (+4.27%) and W40-2→W40-1 (+2.67%), but loses on CIFAR-10 R34→R18 (95.26 vs 95.64) and V11→R18 (92.18 vs 93.02). Compared to Spaceship, CPNet dominates on CIFAR-100 (gains of +0.27 to +5.17%) and TinyImageNet (+0.87%), while Spaceship edges ahead on CIFAR-10 R34→R18 (95.39 vs 95.26) and V11→R18 (92.27 vs 92.18). Against older baselines (DeepInversion, DAFL, ZSKT, CMI, FastDFKD, MAD), CPNet achieves consistent and substantial improvements across all reported settings. Notably, CPNet even surpasses the accuracy of models trained on the original dataset for WideResNet16-2, WideResNet16-1, and WideResNet40-1 on CIFAR-10, and ResNet18 on CIFAR-100. In terms of training efficiency, CPNet-100 is 7.48× faster than DeepInversion and 1.2× faster than the next-best method (SSD-KD), completing CIFAR-10 training in just 1.39 GPU-hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DFKD paradigm where the generator and teacher model are structurally independent (as in DAFL, ZSKT, CMI, MAD, Spaceship, DDA, SSD-KD, and NAYER). It builds directly on DeepInversion's BatchNorm statistics constraint and NAYER's Label-Text Embedding input, but re-architects the generator as an explicit inverse transformation of the teacher (G = T⁻¹), forming a coupled autoencoder that eliminates the need to model the intractable P(x̂|z) term. The feature-level adversarial loss, which converts ground-truth labels into penultimate-layer feature constraints via inverse Softmax and Moore-Penrose inverse of the classification head, extends prior logit-level adversarial training to a more geometrically grounded formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The coupled autoencoder design offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc prior constraints (variance matching, L2-norm regularization) that have limited DFKD scalability to larger datasets, and the 1.2×–7.48× speedup makes the approach more practical for iterative deployment. However, all experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet with small backbones; whether the inverse-transformation approximation remains tractable and accurate on ImageNet-scale data with deeper, more complex architectures remains an open question. The reliance on uniform label distribution and fixed teacher parameters also constrains applicability to imbalanced or multi-task settings.
