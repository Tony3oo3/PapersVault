## Problem

Existing real-world video super-resolution methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches produce over-smoothed results, while image diffusion models adapted for video lack temporal consistency because they are trained on static images. Although text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models offer strong spatio-temporal priors, directly applying them to real-world VSR introduces two unresolved challenges—artifacts from complex, unknown degradations that global attention mechanisms handle poorly, and fidelity loss caused by the strong generative capacity of large T2V models.

## Input / Output

STAR takes a low-resolution video with unknown real-world degradations (noise, blur, compression) and a text prompt describing the video content as input, and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution video. The LR video is encoded into latent space via a VAE and fed through ControlNet as a spatial conditioning signal, while the text prompt is encoded and injected via cross-attention. Training uses 720×1280 clips of 32 frames with 15 diffusion steps at inference.

## Method

STAR builds on I2VGen-XL (a U-Net-based T2V model) enhanced with two key components. First, Local Information Enhancement (LIE) inserts a CBAM-based local attention block before each global attention layer in both spatial and temporal blocks, enabling the model to first address local degradation artifacts and then aggregate global features—this joint global-local learning significantly improves high-frequency detail restoration. Second, a Dynamic Frequency (DF) Loss applies DFT to the decoded prediction at each diffusion step, separating low- and high-frequency components, and uses a time-dependent weight c(t)=(t/t_max)^α to prioritize low-frequency (structural) fidelity in early steps and high-frequency (texture/edge) fidelity in later steps, decoupling the fidelity objective to reduce learning difficulty. The overall training objective combines a v-prediction loss with the DF loss weighted by b(t)=1−t/t_max, and the model is initialized from VEnhancer weights with only the ControlNet and inserted LIE modules trained on ~200K OpenVid-1M pairs.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks, STAR achieves the best SSIM (0.7164 on UDM10, 0.8371 on OpenVid30), LPIPS (0.1885 on UDM10, 0.1011 on OpenVid30), DOVER (0.5422 on UDM10, 0.7393 on OpenVid30), and E*warp (1.82 on OpenVid30, 7.30 on REDS30), while attaining second-best PSNR (23.91 on UDM10, 25.30 on OpenVid30) behind RealViformer. On the real-world VideoLQ dataset (no ground truth), STAR achieves the best DOVER (0.5431) and second-best E*warp (6.38). On AIGC35, STAR leads all three non-reference metrics: ILNIQE (26.20), DOVER (0.7014), and E*warp (4.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all nine baselines on DOVER (video quality) across every dataset, with the largest margins over StableSR (0.5422 vs. 0.3490 on UDM10) and DBVSR (0.5422 vs. 0.0959). On perceptual quality (LPIPS), STAR leads on UDM10 (0.1885) and OpenVid30 (0.1011), beating the next-best MGLDVSR (0.2195, 0.2074) and RealViformer (0.2325, 0.1881). RealViformer remains the strongest PSNR competitor (24.00 vs. 23.91 on UDM10; 26.21 vs. 25.30 on OpenVid30), but STAR surpasses it on SSIM, LPIPS, DOVER, and E*warp on all synthetic sets. VEnhancer achieves the lowest E*warp on UDM10 (1.44) but suffers severe fidelity loss (PSNR 17.25), while STAR balances both (PSNR 23.91, E*warp 2.68). On the real-world VideoLQ set, STAR leads DOVER (0.5431) and trails only RealViformer on E*warp (6.38 vs. 5.10); on AIGC35, STAR wins all three non-reference metrics, including ILNIQE (26.20 vs. MGLDVSR's 26.25) and E*warp (4.09 vs. RealViformer's 4.14). GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN, DBVSR, RealBasicVSR) are consistently outperformed on perceptual and quality metrics, confirming the advantage of T2V diffusion priors over adversarial training for detail richness.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR extends the line of diffusion-based video super-resolution by replacing image-diffusion backbones (StableSR, Upscale-A-Video, MGLDVSR) with full text-to-video diffusion models (I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX), which inherently encode temporal priors learned from large-scale video data. It builds directly on VEnhancer's initialization and ControlNet conditioning, but departs from prior T2V-based VSR work (VEnhancer, LaVie-SR) by targeting real-world degradations rather than AI-generated video artifacts. The two key departures are architectural (Local Information Enhancement to compensate for the limited local receptive fields of global-attention T2V models) and optimization-based (Dynamic Frequency Loss to decouple low- and high-frequency fidelity across diffusion steps), neither of which has a direct precedent in the VSR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that powerful T2V diffusion priors are a viable and superior backbone for real-world VSR, with the scaling experiment (CogVideoX-5B over I2VGen-XL) suggesting further gains as video generation models grow. The DF loss offers a general, training-time-only mechanism for fidelity control that avoids the architectural complexity of prior approaches like StableSR's CFW or DiffBIR's two-stage pipeline. Open questions include whether the method can operate without text prompts (currently required by the T2V backbone), how it behaves at higher upscale factors or longer video sequences, and whether the frequency-scheduling principle transfers to other generative restoration tasks beyond VSR.
