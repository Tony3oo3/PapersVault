## Problem

Existing Dynamic Scene Graph Generation methods rely on two-stage pipelines that independently optimize entity localization and predicate prediction, preventing end-to-end learning and incurring high computational costs from redundant candidate boxes. More fundamentally, these methods overlook visual relationship dependencies between entities and predicates and among predicate subclasses (Attention, Spatial, Contact), which are essential for deeper contextual understanding of video scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of video frames as input and produces a set of scene graph triplets <subject, predicate, object> with bounding boxes in a single forward pass, using DETR-style set prediction with Hungarian matching rather than a two-stage detect-then-predict pipeline. The entity decoder outputs paired subject-object bounding boxes and object categories, while the multi-branch predicate decoder outputs triplet-level predictions across three predicate subclasses, which are then aggregated into final predicate scores by taking the maximum across associated triplet scores. Evaluation is conducted under both 'No Constraint' (multiple predicates per entity pair) and 'With Constraint' (one predicate per pair) settings on the Action Genome dataset.

## Method

ARN extends DETR to DSGG with a cascaded Entity Decoder and multi-branch Predicate Decoder, where each branch corresponds to one predicate subclass (Attention, Spatial, Contact). CLIP text embeddings from hand-crafted triplet prompts (e.g., 'The person is [attention predicate] at the [object]') initialize classifier weights, extending predicate prediction to triplet prediction and injecting domain-agnostic semantic priors. The Predicate Association Parsing (PAP) module uses a conditional weight mapping mechanism to organize complementary predicate subclass cues and fuse fine-grained paired entity features into the current branch representation. A Hierarchical Attention (HA) mechanism then performs a two-level quadruplet cross-attention (Q_cur, Q_comp, K, V) that first associates complementary predicate cues with spatio-temporal context, then propagates those features back to the current predicate query, enabling associative reasoning across predicate subclasses while maintaining stable end-to-end training.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset (SGDet task, No Constraint setting), ARN achieves R@10=37.6, R@20=46.8, R@50=54.1, and mR@10=29.7, outperforming the best two-stage method OED (R@10=35.3, R@20=44.4, R@50=51.8) by 2.3–2.4 points across R@K metrics. Under the With Constraint setting, ARN reaches R@10=35.1 and R@20=41.8, surpassing OED by 1.6 and 0.9 points respectively, though it trails OED slightly on R@50 (47.2 vs. 48.9). ARN also achieves 162 FPS with 54.06M parameters, outpacing OED (121 FPS, 54.54M) and using far fewer parameters than two-stage methods like STTran (151M) and TEMPURA (248.8M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARN outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the No Constraint SGDet setting, with the largest margins over two-stage methods: +7.8 R@10 over TEMPURA, +5.5 over DSG-DETR, and +4.2 over the end-to-end OED*. Against the strongest two-stage baseline OED, ARN gains 2.3 points on R@10, 2.4 on R@20, and 2.3 on R@50 (No Constraint), and 1.6/0.9 points on R@10/R@20 under With Constraint. The only metric where ARN trails a baseline is With Constraint R@50 (47.2 vs. OED's 48.9), which the authors attribute to semantically similar triplet predictions occupying top ranks. In terms of efficiency, ARN (54.06M params, 162 FPS) is comparable in size to OED (54.54M, 121 FPS) but substantially lighter than STTran (151M) and TEMPURA (248.8M), while running 34% faster at inference. On the PredCls task, ARN also leads with R@10=83.7 (No Constraint), edging out OED (83.3) and TD² (81.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR set-prediction framework from object detection to Dynamic Scene Graph Generation, departing from the dominant two-stage pipeline (separate entity localization and predicate prediction) used by most prior DSGG methods such as STTran, TEMPURA, and TRACE. The closest prior work is OED, which also pursues end-to-end DSGG but relies on independently optimized spatial context that degrades during joint training. ARN differentiates itself by introducing CLIP-based triplet cue modeling (extending predicate prediction to full triplet prediction) and two novel modules—Predicate Association Parsing and Hierarchical Attention—that explicitly model visual relation dependencies between entities and predicate subclasses, a dimension prior methods largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying entity detection and triplet prediction in a single end-to-end architecture, ARN eliminates the redundant candidate generation and NMS overhead of two-stage pipelines while achieving state-of-the-art results on Action Genome. The CLIP-driven triplet formulation and the conditional weight mapping mechanism offer a reusable recipe for injecting cross-modal semantic priors into structured prediction tasks. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Action Genome/Charades), and the slight R@50 regression under the With Constraint setting suggests the triplet-level scoring may produce semantically redundant predictions that crowd out diverse relationships. Open questions include whether the multi-branch predicate decoder generalizes to datasets with more heterogeneous predicate taxonomies and whether the approach transfers to longer video sequences.
