## Problem

Existing monocular 3D object detection methods, including unified cross-dataset models like Cube R-CNN and Uni-MODE, operate under a closed-set assumption where training and testing share identical scenes and object categories. Real-world deployment requires detecting novel objects in unseen environments, but 3D data lacks the rich visual-language pairs needed for open-set classification, and monocular depth estimation remains challenging across diverse scenes. This paper addresses both obstacles by introducing the first end-to-end open-set monocular 3D object detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (resized to a canonical 800×1333 resolution with center padding) and free-form language prompts describing objects of interest. It outputs 3D oriented bounding boxes in camera coordinates, parameterized as [x, y, z, w, l, h, R] where R uses a 6D rotation parameterization rather than yaw-only, along with open-set class labels determined by similarity between object queries and text embeddings. The model also produces an auxiliary per-pixel metric depth map as a byproduct of the depth estimation head.

## Method

3D-MOOD lifts open-set 2D detection (built on G-DINO's early visual-language fusion) into 3D via a differentiable 3D bounding box head that predicts projected center offset, scaled logarithmic metric depth, scaled logarithmic dimensions, and 6D rotation from 2D object queries. A geometry-aware 3D query generation module conditions each 2D query with camera intrinsics (via cross-attention on camera embeddings) and depth features (via cross-attention on the auxiliary depth head's output), injecting geometric priors that improve generalization across scenes. A canonical image space aligns image resolution and camera intrinsics across all training datasets, resolving the ambiguity between image, intrinsic, and metric depth that plagues multi-dataset training. An auxiliary metric depth head, conditioned on camera embeddings following UniDepth, provides dense geometric supervision beyond the sparse 3D box annotations. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with combined 2D detection loss, 3D box regression loss, and scale-invariant log depth loss, enabling joint optimization of the open-set 2D detector and 3D lifting parameters.

## Results

On the proposed open-set benchmark, 3D-MOOD (Swin-B) achieves ODS of 23.8 on Argoverse 2 and 31.5 on ScanNet, substantially outperforming Cube R-CNN (8.9 and 19.5) and OVM3D-Det (8.8 and 16.3). Critically, 3D-MOOD detects novel classes with ODS(N) of 14.8 (AV2) and 15.7 (ScanNet), whereas all closed-set baselines score 0.0 on novel categories. On the closed-set Omni3D benchmark, 3D-MOOD (Swin-B) sets a new SOTA with AP_3D^omni of 30.0, surpassing Uni-MODE (28.2) and Cube R-CNN (23.3). In cross-domain zero-shot transfer within Omni3D indoor datasets, 3D-MOOD consistently outperforms both Cube R-CNN and Uni-MODE across all three train/test splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cube R-CNN [4], 3D-MOOD (Swin-B) wins decisively on the open-set benchmark: ODS improves from 8.9 to 23.8 on Argoverse 2 (+14.9) and from 19.5 to 31.5 on ScanNet (+12.0), with the gap driven entirely by novel-class detection (ODS(N) 14.8 vs 0.0). On the closed-set Omni3D test, 3D-MOOD also surpasses Cube R-CNN by 6.7 AP (30.0 vs 23.3). Against OVM3D-Det [18], which uses a non-end-to-end pipeline with pseudo-GT generation, 3D-MOOD achieves +15.0 ODS on AV2 and +15.2 on ScanNet, demonstrating the advantage of joint 2D-3D training over decoupled pseudo-labeling. On the closed-set Omni3D benchmark, 3D-MOOD (Swin-B) outperforms Uni-MODE [23] by 1.8 AP (30.0 vs 28.2) and all earlier single-dataset methods (ImVoxelNet 9.4, SMOKE 9.6, FCOS3D 9.8, PGD 11.2) by a wide margin. In cross-domain zero-shot transfer (Table 2), 3D-MOOD beats both Cube R-CNN and Uni-MODE in all three indoor train/test configurations, e.g. 25.6 vs 15.2 vs 14.7 AP on Hypersim when trained on Hypersim and tested zero-shot on the other two datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on G-DINO's open-set 2D detection framework and UniDepth's camera-conditioned depth estimation, but departs from the closed-set 3D detection paradigm established by Cube R-CNN and Uni-MODE on Omni3D. Rather than attempting open-set 3D classification directly (which suffers from scarce 3D-visual-language pairs), it lifts open-set 2D detections into 3D via a differentiable bounding box head, enabling end-to-end joint training. This contrasts with OVM3D-Det's non-e2e pipeline that generates pseudo 3D GT from 2D foundation models, which the paper shows is inferior due to the lack of 3D supervision during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first proper open-set monocular 3D detection benchmark (Omni3D → Argoverse 2/ScanNet) and demonstrating that e2e 2D-to-3D lifting outperforms pipeline-based approaches even when the latter has access to better depth models, this work validates a practical path toward deployable 3D perception in unseen environments. The canonical image space and geometry-aware query generation are broadly applicable techniques for cross-dataset 3D training. Open questions remain around whether the approach scales to truly arbitrary object categories without any 3D training signal, and whether the reliance on a strong 2D open-set detector (G-DINO) becomes a bottleneck in highly novel scenes.
