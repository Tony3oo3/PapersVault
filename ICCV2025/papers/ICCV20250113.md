## Problem

While it is known that certain random Gaussian noises ('golden noises') produce higher-quality and more semantically faithful images in text-to-image diffusion models, no machine learning framework exists to systematically predict such noises. Existing noise optimization methods are task-specific, introduce significant inference delays, require pipeline modifications, and demand subject tokens per prompt, limiting their practical adoption and generalization across models.

## Input / Output

NPNet takes a random Gaussian noise in the latent space of a T2I diffusion model (e.g., 4×64×64 for SDXL) together with the text prompt embedding produced by the model's frozen text encoder, and outputs a perturbed 'golden noise' of the same shape. The output is a drop-in replacement for the initial noise in any standard diffusion sampling pipeline, requiring no architectural changes to the base model. The module adds only ~0.4 s of inference latency and ~500 MB of memory per image.

## Method

The core idea is to learn a small perturbation (a 'noise prompt') that transforms random noise into golden noise, conditioned on the text prompt. Training data is collected via re-denoise sampling: applying DDIM-Inversion(DDIM(x_t)) with a CFG scale inconsistency (ω_l > ω_w) injects semantic information into the noise, and pairs are filtered by HPSv2 to retain only quality-improving examples (100k pairs from Pick-a-Pic). The NPNet architecture has two branches: (1) a singular value predictor that performs SVD on the source noise, applies multi-head self-attention to predict new singular values while reusing the original singular vectors (justified by Davis-Kahan similarity), and reconstructs the target noise; (2) a residual predictor using a ViT backbone with AdaGroupNorm to inject the text embedding and predict the residual between source and target noise. The two branches are combined with trainable scaling parameters α and β, and the network is trained with MSE loss against the collected golden noise targets.

## Results

On SDXL with Pick-a-Pic, NPNet achieves ImageReward 65.01 (vs. 58.01 Standard, 63.27 Inversion), HPSv2 28.68, PickScore 21.86, and CLIPScore 0.8408, outperforming both baselines across all six metrics. On DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo with HPD, NPNet reaches HPSv2 34.08 (vs. 30.96 Standard) and ImageReward 135.98 (vs. 129.89 Standard), with a 52.49% MPS winning rate. The fine-tuned NPNet on Hunyuan-DiT (using only 600 noise pairs) also surpasses Standard and Inversion on all metrics, e.g., ImageReward 108.29 vs. 99.22 on HPD. NPNet adds only ~3% inference time and ~500 MB memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Standard (random noise) baseline, NPNet consistently wins on all six metrics across all three datasets and both models; the largest gains are in ImageReward (e.g., +7.0 on SDXL/Pick-a-Pic, +6.1 on DreamShaper/HPD) and HPSv2 (e.g., +3.12 on DreamShaper/HPD). Against the Inversion baseline (DDIM-Inversion as a noise-modification reference), NPNet also wins on nearly all metrics, with the most notable advantage in MPS winning rate (e.g., 52.14% vs. 51.41% on SDXL/Pick-a-Pic; 52.49% vs. 46.94% on DreamShaper/HPD). In the orthogonal experiments (Table 5), NPNet is combined with DPO [40] and AYS [43]: DPO+NPNet improves ImageReward from 68.82 to 75.06 and HPSv2 from 28.42 to 28.70 over DPO alone, while AYS+NPNet lifts ImageReward from 50.74 to 59.50 and AES from 6.0310 to 6.1239 over AYS alone, confirming that NPNet is complementary to existing alignment methods rather than competing with them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing body of work identifying that certain initial noises ('golden noises') produce higher-quality diffusion outputs [2, 5, 11, 38], but departs fundamentally from prior approaches that optimize noise per-prompt at inference time (e.g., Attend-and-Excite, InitNO) by reframing the problem as a learnable, prompt-conditioned transformation. The data collection pipeline leverages DDIM-Inversion combined with classifier-free guidance scale inconsistency to inject semantic information into noise, extending the DDIM/CFG machinery [14, 48] in a way not previously exploited for noise construction. Architecturally, the singular-value-prediction component is grounded in the Davis-Kahan theorem, providing a principled justification for why a small perturbation in singular values (rather than singular vectors) suffices to transform source noise into target noise—a structural observation not previously leveraged in diffusion noise optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NPNet's plug-and-play design (3% extra inference time, ~500 MB memory) and demonstrated cross-model generalization (SDXL → DreamShaper → Hunyuan-DiT with only 600 fine-tuning samples) make it a practical drop-in improvement for production T2I pipelines, addressing a key limitation of prior noise-optimization methods that require pipeline modifications and per-prompt optimization. Open questions include whether the noise-prompt concept extends to video, 3D, or audio diffusion, and whether the SVD-based architecture generalizes to latent spaces with different dimensional structures. A limitation is that the approach still requires collecting model-specific noise pairs (even if only 600 for fine-tuning), and the 'golden noise' target is defined by a human-preference proxy (HPSv2) rather than a ground-truth quality signal.
