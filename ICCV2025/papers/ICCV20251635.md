## Problem

Existing neural 3D geometry representations face fundamental limitations: implicit functions (SDFs, occupancy fields) cannot accurately model thin structures or non-watertight/open surfaces, while point clouds and meshes are fixed discretizations that lose information about the underlying continuous geometry. No prior representation simultaneously handles arbitrary genus, connectivity, and boundary conditions while remaining network-friendly and supporting infinite-resolution sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D Gaussian noise samples (drawn from N(0,1)) as input and produces 3D surface points lying on the target geometry M ⊂ R³ as output. The mapping is learned per-object, with each object treated as a single training sample; the output can be sampled at arbitrary resolution (e.g., 1M+ points) to approximate the surface at any desired density. For textured geometry, the input extends to 6D (3D position + 3D color), and for dynamic objects, a temporal dimension is added to the denoiser input.

## Method

GEOMDIST models a 3D surface as a probability distribution Φ_M over surface points, learned by a diffusion model that maps Gaussian noise to this distribution via a learned ODE trajectory. The forward sampling (E) integrates the ODE dx = (x − D_θ(x,t))/t · dt from noise to surface, while the inverse sampling (D) reverses the trajectory to map surface points back to noise space, enabling correspondence and compression. The network uses magnitude-preserving layers (adapted from Karras et al. 2024) with 6 blocks and 512 channels (~5.5M parameters), where inputs and outputs are standardized to zero mean and unit variance. Training resamples 2^25 surface points each epoch to simulate an infinite training set, optimizing the denoiser under the EDM framework with noise-augmented targets. This per-point training paradigm—treating each spatial point as an individual sample rather than the whole object—distinguishes the approach from prior 3D diffusion works that generate shapes from a single latent code.

## Results

On the Loong shape, the proposed GEOMDIST network achieves a Chamfer distance of 2.140 (×10³), outperforming the Hashing Grid baseline (4.133) by ~48% and the MLP baseline (2.647) by ~19%. Across four diverse shapes (Wukong, lamp, lion, Parthenon), using a Gaussian initial distribution consistently yields lower Chamfer distance than a uniform distribution (e.g., 2.715 vs. 2.845 on Wukong; 3.246 vs. 3.734 on lion). The method requires only 5.53M parameters (6 blocks, C=512) and ~2.5 minutes per epoch on 4 A100 GPUs, while producing uniformly distributed surface samples that closely approximate the target geometry at 1M-point resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GEOMDIST network outperforms both the Hashing Grid (InstantNGP) baseline (4.133 vs. 2.140 Chamfer ×10³) and the DeepSDF-style MLP baseline (2.647 vs. 2.140) on the Loong shape, with the Hashing Grid gap being particularly large (~48% improvement). Against vector field-based methods (Neural UDF [8], Neural Vector Fields [47], Cap-UDF [58]), GEOMDIST produces more uniformly distributed samples with lower Chamfer distance, as these methods fail to adequately cover the target surface even at 1M samples. Compared to SDF-based representations (InstantNGP), GEOMDIST can model open, non-watertight, and thin-structure geometry that SDFs fundamentally cannot represent, while using significantly fewer network parameters. The Gaussian initial distribution slightly outperforms the uniform distribution across all four tested shapes (e.g., 3.246 vs. 3.734 on lion), confirming the choice of Gaussian noise as the source distribution. Overall, GEOMDIST wins on all direct numerical comparisons, with the largest margins against the Hashing Grid and vector field baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the EDM diffusion framework (Karras et al., 2022) and its magnitude-preserving layer design (Karras et al., 2024) from shape *generation* to shape *representation*, treating each spatial point as an individual training sample rather than each object. It departs from coordinate-based neural representations (SDFs, unsigned distance fields, vector fields) that map coordinates to scalar/vector values, instead learning a trajectory field that maps Gaussian noise to surface points via an ODE. Unlike prior diffusion-based 3D works that generate novel shapes from a dataset, GEOMDIST encodes a single shape as a probability distribution, and unlike PointFlow or similar normalizing-flow approaches evaluated only on toy datasets, it demonstrates high-fidelity detail on complex real-world geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEOMDIST addresses a persistent gap in neural 3D representations: the inability of SDFs and related implicit methods to handle non-watertight surfaces, thin structures, and arbitrary genus without architectural workarounds. The distribution formulation naturally supports infinite-resolution sampling, texture encoding, and dynamic (4D) extension, opening avenues for compression, rendering, and geometry processing on distributions. Open questions the authors highlight include defining neural geometry operators on distributions, incorporating regularizers (area/volume preservation) into the learned mapping, and developing joint sampling-and-meshing algorithms. A practical limitation is the training cost—resampling 2^25 surface points per epoch over 1000+ epochs—and the non-bijective nature of the noise-to-surface mapping, which constrains certain inverse applications.
