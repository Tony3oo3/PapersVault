## Problem

Existing video stylization methods face a fundamental tradeoff: generator-based (diffusion) approaches produce visually striking results but suffer from temporal flickering, blur in dynamic scenes, and GPU memory that scales linearly with video length, while non-generative methods either exhibit residual flickering under optical flow constraints or sacrifice artistic expressiveness through relaxed style losses. No prior method simultaneously achieves strong artistic stylization, strict temporal consistency, and practical memory efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content video (sequence of RGB frames) and a single style reference image, producing a temporally consistent stylized video. All experiments use 768×432 resolution (downsampled for fair comparison with generator-based methods that overflow GPU memory at HD). The pipeline is entirely test-time optimization with no pre-trained model; per-frame optimization uses VGG-19 features for content preservation and Nearest Neighbor Feature Matching (NNFM) for style, with optional global color matching as a preprocessing step on the content frame.

## Method

FlowStyler decomposes stylization into two complementary per-pixel transformation fields: a stylization velocity field that drives geometric deformation via Lagrangian particle advection (treating each pixel as a particle with adaptive splatting for mass conservation), and an orthogonality-regularized 3×3 color transfer matrix field that performs bounded spectral adaptation while preserving illumination consistency. Temporal consistency is enforced by propagating both fields (and the Adam optimizer's momentum/variance states) between consecutive frames via masked semi-Lagrangian advection guided by optical flow, which prevents vibration artifacts from per-frame optimizer reinitialization. An occlusion-aware temporal lookup strategy reconstructs field parameters in newly occluded regions by backward-tracing optical flow through a window of historical frames with adaptive normalization by valid visibility masks, eliminating motion trailing artifacts. The entire framework is non-generative and requires only ~3.6 GB GPU memory regardless of video length.

## Results

On 60 DAVIS 2017/2019 video-style pairs (768×432), FlowStyler achieves the lowest optical-flow warping errors (2.5×10⁻³ at 1-frame, 14.2×10⁻³ at 10-frame) and the highest style-fidelity scores (DINO 0.72, CLIP 0.67) among all compared methods. GPU memory remains stable at 3.6 GB across both 50- and 100-frame sequences, compared to 40–64 GB for generator-based baselines. In a 2AFC user study with 328 participants (~2000 votes per baseline), FlowStyler is preferred 91% of the time over AnyV2V, 79% over TokenFlow, 75% over CAP-VSTNet, and 74% over UniST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowStyler outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against generator-based methods, it reduces 1-frame warping error by 10× (vs AnyV2V) and 4.4× (vs TokenFlow), while improving DINO style fidelity by 18% and 22% respectively; TokenFlow matches FlowStyler on CLIP (0.65 vs 0.67) but lags on DINO and suffers visible temporal flickering. Against non-generative baselines, FlowStyler achieves 24% higher DINO and 29% higher CLIP over CAP-VSTNet (which uses relaxed style losses that degrade artistic detail), and 22%/31% improvements over UniST, while also reducing 1-frame warping error by 1.6× and 4.2×. CAP-VSTNet uses the least GPU memory (0.44 GB) but at the cost of the lowest style fidelity; FlowStyler's 3.6 GB footprint is still 17× lower than TokenFlow and 17.2× lower than AnyV2V, enabling home-GPU deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowStyler extends the Transport-based Neural Style Transfer (TNST) framework—originally designed for volumetric fluid stylization—into the video domain by replacing physically simulated advection with optical-flow-guided field propagation. It departs from prior non-generative video stylization methods (e.g., CAP-VSTNet, UniST) that rely on relaxed style losses or optical-flow warping of pixel values, instead propagating explicit geometric and color transformation fields to avoid the misalignment problem inherent in flow-based pixel warping. The momentum-preserving optimization (propagating Adam optimizer states across frames) and occlusion-aware temporal lookup are novel augmentations not present in prior TNST or video stylization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6 GB stable memory footprint (vs. 60+ GB for diffusion-based competitors) makes artistic video stylization feasible on consumer hardware, addressing a practical barrier that has limited real-world adoption of generator-based methods. However, the method remains style-image-driven with VGG-based perceptual losses, limiting it to low-level texture and stroke transfer rather than semantic or text-guided stylization. Open questions include whether the field-propagation paradigm scales to longer videos with complex camera motion, and whether the authors' planned SDS integration can resolve the gradient instability they observed in initial text-driven attempts without sacrificing the temporal consistency that motivates the non-generative design.
