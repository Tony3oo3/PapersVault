## Problem

Existing RIR prediction methods either ignore material properties entirely or rely on fixed semantic-to-material mappings, making it impossible for users to explore how a scene would sound under different material configurations. No prior work supports dynamically reassigning materials to objects and surfaces at inference time to generate a corresponding target RIR, limiting applications in AR/VR, architectural design, and creative audio production.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251445/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB image (90° FoV), a 0.5-second binaural echo response at 16 kHz (converted to a 2×256×256 STFT magnitude spectrogram), a semantic segmentation mask, and a user-defined target material mask as input. The material mask is specified by clicking on objects in the semantic segmentation and assigning one of 12 material classes; unselected regions retain their original material. The output is a target binaural RIR spectrogram (2×256×256) predicted as a weighted combination of the source RIR plus a learned residual, rather than a direct spectrogram regression.

## Method

M-CAPA is a three-component encoder-decoder: a multimodal scene encoder (four-layer convolutional UNet blocks for RGB, semantic mask, and source RIR spectrogram, concatenated into a joint embedding), a target material encoder (convolutional encoder for the material mask), and a conditional RIR generator that fuses both embeddings via a convolutional fusion layer and decodes through four deconvolution layers with skip connections from the acoustic encoder. The key architectural insight is that the generator predicts both a weighting mask W_T (which parts of the source RIR to emphasize or dampen) and a material residual B_T (new reverberation energy to introduce in previously inactive frequency-time bins), combined as Â_T = W_T ⊙ A_S + B_T. This dual-output formulation is necessary because new materials can create reverberation patterns absent from the source RIR, which pure masking approaches cannot capture. Training is end-to-end supervised with a composite loss of L2, L1, and an energy-decay alignment term (λ₁=λ₂=0.5, λ₃=5×10⁻³) on the Acoustic Wonderland Dataset (~1.68M data points from 84 Matterport3D scenes rendered in SoundSpaces 2.0).

## Results

On the Acoustic Wonderland Dataset, M-CAPA (audio-visual input) achieves the best performance across all three test splits. On the most challenging unseen-materials split (D_uu), it reaches L1=5.27×10⁻², STFT=3.87×10⁻², RTE=91.44 ms, and CTE=8.44 dB, outperforming the strongest audio-visual baseline AV-RIR (L1=7.59×10⁻², STFT=7.17×10⁻², RTE=99.10 ms, CTE=11.35 dB). Even the vision-only variant of M-CAPA (L1=6.06×10⁻², STFT=5.76×10⁻², RTE=92.80 ms, CTE=9.05 dB on D_uu) surpasses all competing methods, including those with audio-visual inputs. Performance degrades modestly from seen profiles (D_us) to unseen profiles (D_uu) and unseen pairings (D_uk), confirming the increasing difficulty of generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-CAPA outperforms all baselines across every metric and test split. Against the strongest audio-visual competitor AV-RIR [35], M-CAPA (audio-visual) reduces L1 error from 7.31 to 5.10 (×10⁻²) and STFT from 6.65 to 3.61 on D_us, with similar margins on D_uu and D_uk. In the vision-only group, M-CAPA dramatically outperforms Image2Reverb [41] (L1: 5.92 vs 14.68) and FAST-RIR++ [26,33] (L1: 5.92 vs 16.73, STFT: 5.49 vs 25.06) on D_us, with the gap widening on D_uu where FAST-RIR++ degrades to STFT=28.39 while M-CAPA stays at 5.76. Notably, even the vision-only M-CAPA variant beats the audio-visual AV-RIR on all four metrics, suggesting that the material-conditioned generation mechanism is the key differentiator rather than the input modality. The retrieval-based baselines (Material Agnostic/Aware) and the simple Direct Mapping baseline all underperform M-CAPA, confirming that explicit material-conditioned generation is necessary rather than memorizing or retrieving training RIRs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of RIR prediction and material-aware acoustics. Prior RIR prediction methods either require full 3D geometry (ray tracing, FDTD), infer materials implicitly from RGB images, or rely on fixed semantic-to-material mappings (e.g., all walls are brick). The M-CAPA model departs from all of these by accepting a user-defined material segmentation mask as an explicit inference-time condition, enabling arbitrary material reassignment without 3D meshes. Architecturally, it repurposes standard UNet encoder blocks and STFT spectrogram representations, with the key methodological novelty being the joint prediction of a weighting mask W_T and a residual B_T (rather than masking alone) to capture both attenuation and novel reverberation introduced by new materials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The material-controlled RIR generation task opens practical avenues for AR/VR, architectural design, and creative audio where users need to preview acoustic changes before physically altering a space. The Acoustic Wonderland Dataset (~1.68M data points across 84 real-world scenes and 2,673 material profiles) provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating material-aware RIR methods under controlled generalization splits. Key limitations remain: the model cannot handle entirely unseen material classes at inference, struggles with irregularly shaped objects, and the real-world validation is limited to a small 5-user study on 2 scenes. Open questions include language-based material queries, robustness to acoustic noise, and whether the residual formulation generalizes to more complex multi-source or outdoor scenarios.
