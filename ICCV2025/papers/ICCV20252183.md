## Problem

Pointmap-based 3D reconstruction methods like DUSt3R achieve strong results on static scenes but produce substantial inaccuracies on dynamic scenes because moving objects violate the multiview geometric constraints that global alignment relies on. The paper addresses how to extend the existing 3D pointmap formulation to recover full 4D representations (dynamic geometry, camera poses, and 3D point trajectories) from monocular video without retraining the underlying 3D model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252183/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames as input and produces a jointly optimized set of outputs: per-frame dense 3D pointmaps in world coordinates, camera poses and intrinsics, depth maps, motion masks separating dynamic from static regions, and both 2D and lifted 3D point tracking trajectories. The 3D model operates on a sparse scene graph using a strided sliding window over image pairs to reduce computational cost, and all outputs are refined through a shared optimization rather than being predicted independently.

## Method

C4D builds on pretrained DUSt3R-based pointmap weights and introduces two temporal correspondences—short-term optical flow and long-term point tracking via a novel Dynamic-aware Point Tracker (DynPT) that additionally predicts whether each tracked point is moving in world coordinates. DynPT's mobility predictions, combined with optical flow, enable correspondence-guided motion mask estimation: a fundamental matrix is fitted via LMedS using only static-point correspondences, and epipolar (Sampson) error on all flow correspondences reveals dynamic regions. The 4D reconstruction is then refined through a correspondence-aided optimization that augments the standard global alignment loss with three objectives: Camera Movement Alignment (matching ego-motion to optical flow in static regions), Camera Trajectory Smoothness (penalizing abrupt pose changes), and Point Trajectory Smoothness (smoothing lifted 3D trajectories via adaptive 1D convolution and propagating displacements to all points). This design avoids retraining the 3D backbone while leveraging temporal structure to separate and correctly handle dynamic content.

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 3), C4D-M (using MonST3R weights) achieves ATE 0.103, RPE trans 0.040, and RPE rot 0.705 on Sintel; ATE 0.071, RPE trans 0.019, RPE rot 0.897 on TUM-dynamics; and ATE 0.061, RPE trans 0.017, RPE rot 0.538 on ScanNet, outperforming MonST3R on all three datasets. For video depth (Table 4), C4D-M achieves Abs Rel 0.327 and δ<1.25 60.7% on Sintel, Abs Rel 0.061 and δ<1.25 96.5% on Bonn, and Abs Rel 0.089 and δ<1.25 90.6% on KITTI under scale-and-shift alignment, and the best scale-only depth accuracy across all three datasets. For point tracking (Table 5), DynPT achieves D-ACC of 87.9–94.1% on Kubric dynamic benchmarks and competitive TAP-Vid scores (AJ 61.6, OA 87.4 on DAVIS) while uniquely predicting point mobility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C4D-M outperforms the concurrent MonST3R on all three camera pose benchmarks: on Sintel it reduces ATE from 0.109 to 0.103 and RPE rot from 0.737 to 0.705; on TUM-dynamics it dramatically reduces RPE trans from 0.223 to 0.019 and RPE rot from 1.037 to 0.897; on ScanNet it improves ATE from 0.068 to 0.061. Against CasualSAM, C4D-M wins on Sintel (ATE 0.103 vs 0.141) and ScanNet (ATE 0.061 vs 0.158) but CasualSAM has a slight edge on TUM-dynamics RPE trans (0.010 vs 0.019). Compared to specialized visual odometry methods, C4D-M is competitive with LEAP-VO on Sintel ATE (0.103 vs 0.089) without requiring ground-truth intrinsics, and substantially outperforms DROID-SLAM and DPVO on RPE rot (0.705 vs 1.912/1.975). For video depth under scale-only alignment, C4D-M achieves the best Abs Rel on all three datasets (0.338 Sintel, 0.063 Bonn, 0.091 KITTI), surpassing MonST3R (0.345, 0.065, 0.159) and DepthCrafter (0.692, 0.217, 0.141). For point tracking, DynPT is within 0.2–0.9 points of CoTracker on TAP-Vid DAVIS (AJ 61.6 vs 61.8, OA 87.4 vs 88.3) while uniquely providing mobility predictions that no prior TAP method offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

C4D extends the DUSt3R/MASt3R pointmap-based 3D reconstruction paradigm to dynamic scenes by incorporating two types of temporal correspondences (short-term optical flow and long-term point tracking) that the original global-alignment formulation lacks. Its DynPT tracker is architecturally derived from CoTracker but adds a mobility prediction head and a 3D-aware ViT encoder from DUSt3R, distinguishing it from prior TAP methods that cannot separate camera-induced from object-induced motion. Compared to the concurrent MonST3R, which fine-tunes DUSt3R on dynamic data, C4D retains pretrained 3D weights and instead introduces correspondence-guided optimization objectives (CMA, CTS, PTS) plus a static-point fundamental-matrix motion mask, making it a plug-in upgrade rather than a retraining effort.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, modular path to 4D reconstruction: any DUSt3R-family checkpoint can be upgraded to handle dynamic scenes without retraining, and the dual-correspondence motion mask provides a clean geometric mechanism (epipolar error from static points only) that is interpretable and robust. Limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf optical flow quality, DynPT trained solely on synthetic Kubric data (raising generalization questions for real-world mobility prediction), and per-video optimization that may not scale to very long sequences. Open questions include whether the correspondence-aided objectives can be learned end-to-end, how the approach handles highly non-rigid deformations, and whether the static/dynamic binary split suffices for scenes with partially moving structures.
