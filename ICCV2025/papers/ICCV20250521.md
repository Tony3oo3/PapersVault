## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D object detection frameworks rely on scene flow to identify moving objects, which limits them to at most 3 semantic classes, cannot detect stationary objects (e.g., parked cars, barriers), and degrades significantly at low frame rates (2 Hz). No prior method has established an annotation-free baseline for detecting all 10 nuScenes classes without requiring high-frequency temporal information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-frame LiDAR point clouds and RGB images from 6 camera angles (no scene flow or multi-frame alignment required). The 2D teacher models (FC-CLIP for panoptic, SAN for semantic segmentation) process the images to produce pixel-level masks, which are projected onto the point cloud via known sensor extrinsics. The 3D detector (VoxelNeXt) outputs 7-DoF bounding boxes (center, size, orientation) with class labels across 10 categories, evaluated with nuScenes mAP and NDS metrics.

## Method

AnnofreeOD generates pseudo boxes entirely from single-frame 2D knowledge: 2D open-vocabulary segmentation masks are transferred to 3D via sensor calibration, denoised at the instance level with DBSCAN, and converted to boxes through clustering after ground removal. Orientation is estimated by matching DINOv2 patch features between the 2D segmentation and 24 pre-rendered orientation diagrams (sampled at 15° intervals), selecting the angle with the most aligned match vectors. To handle residual pseudo-label noise, the paper introduces Noise-Resistant Regression (NRR): each box face is scored for reliability via Proximity-Density (PDscore), unreliable faces are represented as 3D probability intervals bounded by class-specific commonsense priors, and during training Dynamic Gaussian Box Augmentation (DGBA) samples augmented boxes from these intervals. The standard L1 regression loss is replaced with a Negative Log-Likelihood loss over a Gaussian distribution, transforming deterministic box regression into probability optimization that tolerates noisy supervision.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, AnnofreeOD achieves 45.5% AP and 35.8% NDS for class-agnostic detection, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (UNION) by +6.0% AP and +4.1% NDS without using scene flow. For 3-class detection, it reaches 34.4% mAP and 36.6% NDS, improving over UNION by +9.3% mAP and +12.2% NDS. On the 10-class task (test set), AnnofreeOD establishes the first annotation-free baseline at 22.5% mAP and 26.7% NDS, achieving approximately 40% of fully supervised VoxelNeXt performance (64.5% mAP, 70.0% NDS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnnofreeOD outperforms all annotation-free baselines across every evaluation setting. In class-agnostic detection, it beats UNION (39.5% AP, 31.7% NDS) by +6.0% AP and +4.1% NDS, and substantially surpasses scene-flow-only methods OYSTER (9.1% AP) and LISO (10.9% AP) by over 35 AP points. In 3-class detection, it improves over UNION by +9.3% mAP (34.4 vs. 25.1) and +12.2% NDS (36.6 vs. 24.4), and is the only method to achieve non-zero AP for the Cyclist class (7.9%). Against pure clustering (HDBSCAN), gains are even larger: +31.7% AP in class-agnostic and +29.5% mAP in 3-class settings. Notably, AnnofreeOD does not use scene flow, yet achieves the best translation error (ATE 0.328) and scale error (ASE 0.362) among all annotation-free methods, though it lags on orientation (AOE 1.441) and velocity (AVE 1.956) due to the absence of motion cues. Compared to supervised baselines, AnnofreeOD exceeds the 1%-labeled model in both class-agnostic and 3-class settings, approaching the 10%-labeled performance without any human annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant scene-flow-based unsupervised 3D detection paradigm (MODEST, OYSTER, LISO, CPD) by repurposing 2D-to-3D knowledge distillation — a technique previously applied to annotation-free 3D segmentation (CLIP2Scene, SEAL, SLidR) — for the first time to generate pseudo bounding boxes for all 10 nuScenes classes. It also builds on the partial 2D-assisted detection attempts of LiSe and UNION, which still relied on scene flow, by fully eliminating temporal dependencies in favor of single-frame 2D open-vocabulary segmentation (FC-CLIP, SAN) as the teacher signal. The Noise-Resistant Regression (NRR) with Box Augmentation is a novel training paradigm that reframes box regression as Gaussian probability optimization, distinguishing it from the iterative self-training loops used by prior scene-flow methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnnofreeOD establishes the first 10-class annotation-free 3D detection baseline, demonstrating that ~40% of fully supervised performance is achievable with zero human labels and no scene flow — a practical milestone for scenarios where annotation is prohibitively expensive. The conceptual finding that scene flow is not indispensable challenges a core assumption in the unsupervised detection literature, suggesting broader applicability to low-frame-rate, indoor, or non-continuous 3D scenes. Key limitations remain: performance on rare classes (trailer, construction vehicle) is near zero, orientation and velocity errors (AOE, AVE) are substantially worse than scene-flow methods, and the 40% gap to supervised detection leaves significant room for improvement. Open questions include whether integrating scene flow as a complementary signal (rather than a dependency) can close the gap, and whether the NRR/BA framework generalizes to multi-task or end-to-end driving pipelines.
