## Problem

Existing layered image editing methods decompose scenes into object-centric foreground and background layers while ignoring spatial geometry, making them incapable of depth-aware operations such as placing an object at a user-specified depth or compositing two scenes at a chosen depth boundary. Additionally, naive layer compositing in image space produces unrealistic 'cut-paste' artifacts that require separate harmonization models for photorealistic blending.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For scene composition, the method takes two RGB images, their corresponding depth maps (from off-the-shelf predictors like Depth Anything or ZoeDepth), and a scalar depth value d that partitions each scene into foreground (depth < d) and background (depth ≥ d) layers. For object insertion, it additionally requires an object image and a 2D bounding box specifying where the object should appear. The output is a single photorealistic composite image where the depth ordering is preserved and lighting/color are naturally harmonized without any post-hoc processing step.

## Method

The framework has two components: Depth-Guided Layer Decomposition (DeGLaD) creates binary foreground/background masks by thresholding the depth map at user-specified depth d, and Feature-Guided Layer Compositing (FeatGLaC) performs progressive layer blending in the diffusion latent space. Rather than alpha-compositing latents at a single timestep (which trades off identity preservation against realistic blending), FeatGLaC initializes the composite latent from the background and at each denoising step extracts U-Net decoder features from both source latents and the composite, then applies a guidance energy that forces the composite's foreground features to match the source foreground and its background features to match the source background. The composite latent is updated via K gradient steps on this energy at each timestep, allowing the diffusion model to naturally handle lighting and color harmonization while preserving layer structure. The approach is model-agnostic and zero-shot: for scene composition it uses a depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion v2 model with guidance over timesteps 0–38 (K=5), and for object insertion it lifts the Anydoor inpainting model with guidance over timesteps 30–50 (K=3).

## Results

On the Depth Edit Benchmark for depth-aware object insertion, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all four metrics: DINO-sim 0.545 (vs. 0.507 for Anydoor), KID 4.8×10², Δdepth 2.989×10², and CLIP-sim 84.86. For depth-aware scene composition, the method achieves LPIPS 0.263 and FID 123.32, outperforming PAIR-Diffusion (LPIPS 0.45, FID 140.54) and DeGLaD+SDEdit (LPIPS 0.395, FID 106.24) in structural preservation, though DeGLaD+SDEdit reports a lower FID at the cost of altering scene structure. User studies with 40 participants confirm consistent superiority in realism, identity preservation, and depth consistency over all baselines for both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For object insertion, the method outperforms all three baselines on every metric. IP-Adapter (CLIP-sim 27.81) and PbE (CLIP-sim 60.12) struggle significantly with amodal bounding boxes, producing poor object identity and depth placement (Δdepth 9.366 and 6.733 respectively). Anydoor is the closest competitor (CLIP-sim 83.23, Δdepth 3.176) but generates visible border artifacts and slightly worse identity (DINO-sim 0.507 vs. 0.545). For scene composition, the method beats PAIR-Diffusion on both LPIPS (0.263 vs. 0.45) and FID (123.32 vs. 140.54), and beats DeGLaD+SDEdit on LPIPS (0.263 vs. 0.395) though DeGLaD+SDEdit achieves a lower FID (106.24) by altering scene structure. DeGLaD Image achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.036) but suffers from cut-paste artifacts and higher FID (132.6), which the paper attributes to the lack of generative blending. The user study confirms these quantitative findings, with the proposed method preferred over all baselines in realism and depth consistency for both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-guidance paradigm of Dhariwal & Nichol [20] from class-conditional steering to a new use case: progressively fusing spatially-masked layers during denoising. It departs from existing object-centric layering methods (which decompose images into per-object transparent layers) by introducing depth-threshold-based decomposition, and from post-hoc harmonization pipelines by performing compositing entirely within the diffusion latent space via feature-level guidance. The pipeline composes null-text inversion [69] for image-to-latent mapping, Anydoor [19] for object-level inpainting, and depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion [1] for structural preservation, all unified under a single training-free guidance framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes two previously undefined editing tasks (depth-aware scene composition and depth-aware object insertion) along with a dedicated benchmark, giving the community a concrete evaluation target for 3D-aware 2D editing. The zero-shot, training-free nature of the approach makes it immediately deployable, and the feature-guided compositing mechanism is model-agnostic and potentially reusable in other layered-editing settings. Key limitations include inherited diffusion-model biases, per-step optimization overhead, and dependence on the quality of the underlying inpainting model for object identity; open questions include whether the approach extends to video, handles multi-object depth ordering more gracefully, and how sensitive it is to depth-estimation errors in complex scenes.
