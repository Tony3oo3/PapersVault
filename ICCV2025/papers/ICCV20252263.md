## Problem

Existing deep incomplete multi-view clustering (DIMVC) methods suffer from two critical limitations: (1) tight coupling between the network architecture and missing-data handling modules makes them method-specific and hard to transfer across different multi-view interaction paradigms, and (2) they implicitly assume IID missing patterns, ignoring the distribution shifts that arise in real-world scenarios where missing data is structured and non-random (e.g., sensor failures introducing biased noise).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view data (M views, N samples) where a subset of samples have one or more views missing, and a corresponding complete-data subset used for pre-training. It produces a final feature matrix H of shape N×M that integrates original complete features, distribution-aligned incomplete features, and imputed features for all missing view slots, from which k-means yields cluster assignments for every sample. The framework is modular: the complete-data training objective (mutual information, contrastive, or KL-divergence loss) is swappable, and the same pipeline can be attached to any existing DMVC encoder as a drop-in interface.

## Method

BRIDGE decomposes DIMVC into two decoupled stages. Stage 1 trains view-specific autoencoders on complete data with a pluggable multi-view interaction loss (MI, contrastive, or KL-divergence), establishing a unified representation backbone. Stage 2 performs domain adversarial transfer learning: complete features are treated as the source domain and incomplete features as the target domain, with a Gradient Reversal Layer and domain discriminator forcing the feature extractor to produce domain-invariant representations, while a k-means-supervised classifier preserves cluster structure. For imputation, BRIDGE combines inter-view prediction (a learned cross-view mapping trained on complete features) with intra-view label-based averaging (aggregating same-pseudo-label complete features), weighted by a coefficient λ, yielding robust imputed features even under high missing rates. Final clustering is obtained by k-means on the assembled N×M feature matrix.

## Results

BRIDGE+CL (the contrastive-learning variant) achieves the best clustering accuracy across nearly all dataset and missing-rate settings. On the two-view datasets (Table 1), it reaches 97.4% ACC on BDGP (M=0.3), 83.5% on BBCSport (M=0.3), 99.0% on Hdigit (M=0.3), and 98.6% on MNIST-USPS (M=0.3). On the multi-view datasets (Table 2), it achieves 96.6% on CIFAR-10, 98.6% on CIFAR-100, 50.7% on Reuters, and 59.2% on NUS-WIDE (all at M=0.3). The proposed FCR metric (Final-to-Complete Accuracy Ratio) shows BRIDGE+DD attains the best average of 98.66%, indicating the strongest incomplete-data processing capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRIDGE+CL outperforms all 10 baselines on 11 of 12 columns in Table 1 (two-view) and all 12 columns in Table 2 (multi-view). The strongest competitor is DSIMVC, which is closest on BDGP (96.1 vs. 97.4 at M=0.3) and MNIST-USPS (98.1 vs. 98.6 at M=0.3), but falls far behind on BBCSport (61.6 vs. 83.5, a 22-point gap) and NUS-WIDE (47.9 vs. 59.2 at M=0.5). On CIFAR-100, BRIDGE+CL achieves 98.6% ACC at M=0.3, surpassing the next-best DIMVC (93.9%) by nearly 5 points. Mutual-information-based baselines (COMPLETER, DCP, APADC) perform poorly on BBCSport (33–45%) and Hdigit (43–87%), while BRIDGE+CL maintains 83.5% and 99.0% respectively. Under Non-IID missing patterns (Figure 3), BRIDGE+CL also consistently outperforms all baselines on NUS-WIDE, confirming that the domain-adversarial alignment module provides robustness beyond what existing methods offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRIDGE sits at the intersection of deep multi-view clustering (DMVC) and incomplete multi-view clustering (DIMVC), drawing on domain-adversarial training (Ganin et al., JMLR 2016) for distribution alignment and cross-view prediction (DCP, Lin et al., TPAMI 2022) for missing-data imputation. Its key departure from prior DIMVC methods is the modular decoupling of the complete-data training stage (where any standard DMVC objective—mutual information, contrastive, or KL-divergence—can be plugged in) from the incomplete-data transfer stage, breaking the tight coupling that has characterized methods like APADC and COMPLETER. The paper also introduces Non-IID missing patterns as a formal consideration, repurposing the gradient reversal layer and domain discriminator from the domain adaptation literature to handle distribution shifts between complete and incomplete data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Non-IID missing patterns formulation addresses a practically important gap: real-world sensor failures and environmental perturbations produce structured, non-random missingness that violates the IID assumption underlying most existing IMVC methods, and the paper demonstrates measurable performance degeneration under such conditions. The unified framework reduces redundant engineering across the DMVC and DIMVC communities by providing a single interface, and the FCR metric offers a more diagnostic evaluation of incomplete-data handling than raw accuracy alone. However, the Non-IID experiments are concentrated on NUSWIDE with two specific perturbation types, leaving open how well the domain-adversarial approach generalizes to more complex or multi-view-correlated missingness patterns, and the reliance on k-means pseudo-labels for the classifier introduces a potential circularity that could limit performance on datasets with non-convex cluster structures.
