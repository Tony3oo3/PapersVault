## Problem

Pixel-prediction approaches like VideoMAE provide stable training signals but learn slowly and may not capture high-level semantics, while latent-prediction approaches like V-JEPA achieve better semantic representations but require careful architectural tricks (EMA teachers, stop-gradients) to avoid representation collapse. The paper identifies that ViT layers in MAE models converge in order of depth—shallow layers early, deep layers late—and asks whether this convergence order can be exploited to progressively transition from pixel to latent prediction in a single, stable training procedure.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of T frames at H×W resolution (224×224 in experiments), splits it into 2×16×16 spatiotemporal patches, and randomly masks 95% of patches. During training, the unmasked patches are encoded by a ViT backbone while decoding latents (initialized with 3D RoPE positional embeddings) are appended to reconstruct either pixels or intermediate layer activations. For evaluation, the full unmasked video is passed through the frozen network and patch embeddings from the 95%-depth layer are fed to attention-based readout heads for action classification or depth estimation.

## Method

LayerLock progressively freezes ViT layers according to a fixed schedule (e.g., freeze 1 layer every 10K steps after an initial pixel-prediction phase) and simultaneously switches the prediction target from raw pixels to the activations of the most recently frozen layer. At each stage, a new patch-wise linear decoder projects the processed latent tokens to match the target layer's feature dimension, and training uses a simple L2 loss with a mini learning-rate warmup at each target switch. The key insight is that frozen layers provide stable, stop-gradient-like targets that prevent the representation collapse observed when latent losses are added to MAE without freezing (SSv2 accuracy drops from 50.1% to 3.7%). The approach is applied to both pixel-prediction (4DS/VideoMAE) and latent-prediction (V-JEPA) baselines, and is complemented by a novel 3D rotary positional embedding that partitions feature dimensions across time, height, and width axes.

## Results

LayerLock improves action classification accuracy by 3.0 points on SSv2 (66.1% vs. 63.1%) and 4.2 points on Kinetics700-2020 (56.3% vs. 52.1%) for the 4DS-G MAE model, while maintaining or slightly improving depth estimation (ScanNet relative error 1.00 vs. 1.02). At the larger 4DS-e scale, gains are 2.5 points on SSv2 (67.1% vs. 64.6%) and 3.5 points on K700-2020 (57.9% vs. 54.4%). Applied to the latent-prediction V-JEPA-L model, LayerLock yields a 4.9-point SSv2 improvement (57.0% vs. 52.1%) and a 1.0-point K700 gain. Progressive freezing also reduces total training FLOPs by 9% and peak memory by 16% at the 250M-example scale, with FLOP savings reaching 19% at 1B examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerLock is compared against two baselines: the 4DS video MAE model (pixel prediction) and V-JEPA (latent prediction with EMA teacher). Against 4DS-G, LayerLock wins on all three tasks: +3.0 SSv2 accuracy, +4.2 K700-2020 accuracy, and a marginal ScanNet depth improvement (1.00 vs. 1.02). Against 4DS-e, LayerLock again wins on both action classification tasks (+2.5 SSv2, +3.5 K700) but shows a small depth regression (0.98 vs. 0.94 relative error). Against V-JEPA-L, LayerLock achieves a substantial +4.9 SSv2 gain and +1.0 K700 improvement, while also improving ScanNet depth (1.51 vs. 1.57). In all cases, LayerLock adds only a negligible parameter overhead (e.g., 1,848M → 1,868M for 4DS-G) while delivering consistent gains across both semantic and low-level dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerLock sits at the intersection of two major lines of self-supervised video representation learning: pixel-prediction approaches (VideoMAE, 4DS) that are stable but data-hungry, and latent-prediction approaches (V-JEPA, BYOL) that are data-efficient but prone to representation collapse. The progressive freezing mechanism draws a loose conceptual parallel to Freezeout [6] for supervised training acceleration, but here it is repurposed to generate stable latent targets and to dynamically shift the prediction objective from pixels to deeper features. The authors also introduce 3D rotary positional embeddings as an orthogonal architectural improvement, building on the 1D RoPE formulation [43].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for EMA teacher networks or asymmetric architectures to avoid collapse, LayerLock offers a simpler and more scalable recipe for training very large video models (up to 4B parameters) while simultaneously reducing FLOPs (up to 19%) and peak memory (16%). The practical efficiency gains open the door to longer videos, higher resolutions, and deeper backbones within fixed compute budgets. Open questions include whether the freezing schedule heuristics transfer across architectures and dataset scales, how the method behaves with even longer temporal contexts, and whether the patch-subselection trade-off (5% vs. 100% patches for latent loss) can be further optimized.
