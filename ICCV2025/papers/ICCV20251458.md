## Problem

Pseudo-labeling is central to semi-supervised and cross-domain semantic segmentation, but its efficacy is hampered by intrinsic label noise, and existing noise-mitigation strategies (consistency regularization, augmentation, denoising) become unreliable at higher noise rates typical of low-annotation settings. Meanwhile, diffusion-based synthetic data generation approaches either lack task specificity (text-to-image SD) or fail to match target domain distributions (layout-to-image SD), leaving a gap in how to effectively exploit pseudo-label knowledge for generating useful training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled set, a large unlabeled target set, and derives two intermediate representations: reliable pixel-level pseudo-labels (high-confidence regions masked by an uncertainty map) converted to one-hot format for attention manipulation, and structured pseudo-labels (refined via unsupervised segmentation) used as spatial layout conditions during sampling. Text prompts are automatically constructed from category names present in the reliable pseudo-labels. The output is a set of diverse synthetic RGB images paired with corresponding semantic masks, which pass through connected-component filtering and inverse-frequency class resampling before being treated as additional labeled data for segmentation model retraining.

## Method

Pseudo-SD fine-tunes Stable Diffusion on target data by proposing Partial Attention Manipulation (PAM): during the denoising U-Net's forward pass, the text-image cross-attention map is replaced at spatial positions corresponding to reliable pseudo-labels with one-hot class indicators, while uncertain regions retain their original attention, allowing reliable semantics to act as seeds that propagate text-based meaning to surrounding areas. For sampling, the method uses Structured Pseudo-Labels—complete pseudo-labels whose spatial structure is enhanced by unsupervised semantic segmentation (e.g., STEGO or U2Seg) to fix poor connectivity and reduce generative hallucinations—as full attention conditions for layout-controlled generation. A connected-component removal strategy then filters synthesized regions that a reference segmentation model cannot recognize, and a hard-adapted class resampling scheme oversamples minority categories to address class imbalance. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, injecting synthetic data into existing semi-supervised or domain-adaptation training loops.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012 (ResNet-101), Pseudo-SD achieves 82.7% mIoU with 1,464 labels and 78.9% with only 92 labels, outperforming the SOTA UniMatch by +1.7% and +2.5% respectively. On Cityscapes, improvements range from +1.1% (1,488 labels) to +4.7% (46 labels) over the best baseline. On COCO (Xception-65), Pseudo-SD reaches 51.9% mIoU with 3,697 labels and 39.9% with 232 labels, surpassing AllSpark by +1.0% and +5.8% respectively. For cross-domain segmentation, Pseudo-SD achieves 77.0% mIoU on GTA5→Cityscapes UDA (+1.1% over MIC) and 62.9% on GTA5→Cityscapes SFUDA (+5.2% over Cross-match).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pseudo-SD consistently outperforms all semi-supervised baselines across four benchmarks. On Pascal VOC, it surpasses UniMatch [57] (the strongest SSSS baseline) by +2.5% mIoU at 92 labels and +1.7% at 1,464 labels, and beats AllSpark [43] and CorrMatch [41] by similar margins. On COCO, the gap widens dramatically: Pseudo-SD exceeds AllSpark [43] by +5.8% (232 labels) and +1.0% (3,697 labels), and outperforms LogicDiag [21] by +6.8% at the lowest label budget. For cross-domain tasks, Pseudo-SD beats MIC [12] by +1.1% on GTA5→Cityscapes UDA and HRDA [6] by +2.3% on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes UDA; in the more challenging source-free setting, it surpasses Cross-match [60] by +5.2% (G2C) and +4.6% (S2C). Against diffusion-based synthesis methods (DatasetDM [50], Dataset Diffusion [30], FreeMask [58], ControlNet [65]), Pseudo-SD achieves substantially larger gains, confirming that pseudo-label-guided fine-tuning is critical for task-specific generation. When plugged into existing methods (Table 4), Pseudo-SD adds +4.7 to +9.7 mIoU to ST++ [56], +4.4 to +8.7 to UniMatch [57], and +4.4 to +5.2 to DAFormer [14] and HRDA [6] in domain adaptation, demonstrating its model-agnostic complementarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pseudo-SD builds on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its generative backbone but departs fundamentally from both pseudo-labeling methods (UniMatch, CPS, CCT) that train directly on noisy pseudo-labels, and from prior diffusion-based synthesis approaches (DatasetDM, FreeMask, ControlNet) that either lack task specificity or require mask-image pairs. The key conceptual shift is repurposing pseudo-labels not as training targets or layout conditions for a separate model, but as fine-tuning signals for SD itself via partial attention manipulation, with structured pseudo-labels then serving as layout control during sampling. This positions the work at the intersection of semi-supervised/cross-domain segmentation and diffusion model adaptation, filling a gap where prior diffusion-based methods failed to generate target-domain-consistent data under label scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, model-agnostic plug-and-play module that consistently improves existing SSSS and CDSS methods (Table 4), with particularly large gains in source-free UDA settings (+5.3%) where no real target labels exist. The core insight—that noisy pseudo-labels, while unreliable as direct supervision, contain sufficient category-level and partial spatial information to fine-tune a generative model—reframes how the community might exploit pseudo-label knowledge beyond noise regularization. Open questions include the compute cost of SD fine-tuning at scale, whether the approach extends to tasks without clean category-level semantics (e.g., panoptic or instance segmentation), and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the initial pseudo-labels at very low label budgets.
