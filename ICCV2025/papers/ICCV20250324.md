## Problem

Existing sample selection strategies for noisy label learning rely on loss or prediction confidence to identify clean samples, which causes the selected set to be dominated by easy examples and excludes harder but informative instances. This bias limits the model's expressive power and generalization, particularly under high noise rates where distinguishing genuinely noisy samples from hard-to-learn ones becomes critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250324/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or variable-resolution prostate MRI slices for PROMISE12) paired with potentially noisy labels, along with a small clean meta-dataset (2% of training data) used for the bi-level meta-learning optimization. For classification tasks it outputs a softmax probability vector over classes; for the medical segmentation task it outputs a pixel-wise binary mask. The CNN backbone (PreAct ResNet18 or ResNet50) extracts features that feed both a classification head and a projection head for contrastive learning.

## Method

DCD computes sample difficulty as the Euclidean distance between a sample's feature vector and a dynamically updated class center, where the center is a weighted average of class features with weights λ_i = α_i + β_i learned via a bi-level meta-learning objective that minimizes validation loss. The meta-learning framework jointly optimizes per-sample weights for the noisy label (α_i) and pseudo-label (β_i) without constraining their sum to one, providing greater flexibility than prior bootstrapping approaches. DCD values are converted into importance weights Γ_i via a softmax over the exponential of DCD, which up-weights hard samples (far from the class center) and down-weights easy ones in the cross-entropy loss. This weighted loss is integrated into a semi-supervised training pipeline (building on UNICON) that combines supervised loss on a selected clean subset with contrastive, unlabeled-data, and regularization losses, enabling the model to focus on a sparse set of informative hard instances rather than being dominated by trivially classified examples.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 90% symmetric noise, DCD achieves 65.3% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline L2B by 4.6 percentage points. On Tiny-ImageNet with 50% symmetric noise, DCD reaches 68.4% accuracy (+4.1% over DISC). On the real-world Clothing1M dataset, DCD attains 78.34% accuracy (+0.84% over L2B), and on WebVision it achieves 83.29% Top-1 / 94.51% Top-5 (+1.89% / +1.51% over LSL). For medical image segmentation on PROMISE12, DCD achieves 82.81% Dice score (+1.98% over L2B) with improved HD (6.52 vs 6.68) and ASD (1.78 vs 2.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCD outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every dataset and noise setting. The most significant gains are over L2B [64] (the strongest prior method): +4.6% on CIFAR-100 at 90% symmetric noise, +4.1% on Tiny-ImageNet at 50% symmetric noise, +0.84% on Clothing1M, +1.89% Top-1 on WebVision, and +1.98% Dice on PROMISE12. Against UNION [19], DCD improves by up to 20.5% on CIFAR-100 at 90% symmetric noise (65.3% vs 44.8%) and 3.1% on CIFAR-100 at 40% asymmetric noise. DISC [27], the second-strongest baseline on Tiny-ImageNet, is surpassed by 1.8–4.1% depending on noise level. On WebVision, DCD exceeds LSL [20] by 1.89% Top-1 and 1.51% Top-5, and on ILSVRC12 it leads by 1.10% Top-1. For medical segmentation, DCD beats L2B [64] by 1.98% Dice and also outperforms UNet++ [65], NL re-weighting [32], and CRF [33] on all three metrics (Dice, HD, ASD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised noisy-label learning pipeline established by UNICON [19] and MixMatch [4], adopting their sample-selection and contrastive-loss framework as the training backbone. It departs from the standard small-loss selection paradigm by introducing Dynamic Center Distance (DCD), a feature-space metric that quantifies sample difficulty independently of classification loss, and couples it with a meta-learning framework (inspired by the weighted pseudo-label formulation of Reed et al. [38]) to compute adaptive per-sample weights and dynamically updated class centers. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods conflate 'hard' with 'noisy' via shared indicators like high loss or low confidence, whereas DCD explicitly separates these by measuring geometric distance to a meta-learned class centroid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized blind spot in noisy-label learning: the systematic exclusion of hard-but-informative samples from the clean training set, which limits model expressiveness. By providing a loss-independent difficulty signal and demonstrating consistent gains across six datasets spanning natural images, real-world web images, and medical MRI segmentation, the work offers a practical drop-in refinement for existing SSL-based noisy-label pipelines. Open questions include whether the 2% clean validation set requirement generalizes to truly label-scarce regimes, whether the meta-learning overhead is justified at very large scale, and whether DCD's geometric intuition transfers to non-CNN backbones (e.g., vision transformers) where feature-space geometry differs substantially.
