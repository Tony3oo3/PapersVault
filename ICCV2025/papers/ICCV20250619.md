## Problem

Existing neural scene representations cannot adapt to discrete scene changes (objects appearing or disappearing, lighting shifts) without either full retraining or catastrophic forgetting of prior scene knowledge. Dynamic 3D Gaussian methods model only continuous motion, and prior continual-learning approaches for NeRF are slow and lack explicit geometric change detection. No method combines 3D Gaussian Splatting with continual learning to handle arbitrary, long-term scene evolution while retaining renderable history.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250619/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian field, a set of multi-view RGB images with camera poses at a new time step, and SAM instance segmentation masks for layout-invariant region detection. It produces an updated 3D Gaussian field with a 4D hash-grid appearance model and a visibility pool that tracks per-Gaussian active/inactive states across all time steps, enabling real-time novel-view rendering at any past or current timestamp and explicit visualization of added (green) and removed (red) geometric elements. Time steps are normalized to [0,1] per scene, and generative replay uses only stored camera extrinsics rather than raw images.

## Method

GaussianUpdate introduces a three-stage progressive update that decouples appearance and geometry to avoid optimization ambiguity. Stage 1 optimizes a 4D hash grid + tiny MLP (global appearance model) only within layout-invariant regions identified by SAM-based IoU masking, learning lighting changes without interference from geometric edits. Stage 2 adds new Gaussians initialized from COLMAP sparse points for emerging objects and learns per-Gaussian removal factors (steep sigmoid) to prune disappeared objects, with DBSCAN filtering sparse outliers before bounding-box pruning. Stage 3 jointly refines the appearance model and added Gaussians, then applies importance pruning (max α·T across views < 0.05) to control memory. A visibility pool records which Gaussians are active at each time, and generative replay re-renders past views from saved camera poses to prevent catastrophic forgetting without storing historical images.

## Results

On the WAT dataset (10 real-world scenes), GaussianUpdate achieves an average PSNR of 26.55 dB and SSIM of 0.844, outperforming CLNeRF (25.87 dB / 0.834) and 4DGS (25.96 dB / 0.829) in the ablation setting. On the Synthetic NeRF dataset (8 scenes), it reaches 35.70 dB PSNR on Lego and 37.59 dB on Hotdog, consistently leading both baselines. Rendering speed is 34 FPS on WAT, compared to 0.75 FPS for CLNeRF, enabling real-time novel view synthesis across different time steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLNeRF [2], GaussianUpdate wins on 9 of 10 WAT scenes for PSNR (losing only Kitchen by 0.38 dB) and on all 10 scenes for SSIM, with the largest PSNR gap on Community (+1.00 dB) and Car (+1.08 dB). On Synthetic NeRF, it leads CLNeRF on all 8 scenes for both metrics, with gains up to +2.17 dB (Ship) and +0.97 dB (Mic). Against 4DGS [31], GaussianUpdate outperforms on all 10 WAT scenes for both PSNR and SSIM, with the largest advantage on Living Room (+1.69 dB PSNR) and Car (+0.68 dB). On Synthetic NeRF, it again leads 4DGS across all 8 scenes, with the largest margin on Lego (+2.47 dB). A key practical advantage is rendering speed: 34 FPS versus 0.75 FPS for CLNeRF, making real-time multi-time visualization feasible. Compared to the upper-bound model using memory replay (storing all past GT images), GaussianUpdate with generative replay achieves nearly identical quality (within ~0.2 dB) while requiring no stored images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting with continual learning, adapting the paradigm established by CLNeRF (which applies continual learning to implicit NeRF representations) to the explicit 3DGS setting. It departs from dynamic 3DGS methods (4DGS, Deformable 3DGS) that model continuous deformation fields, instead targeting discrete scene changes (object appearance/disappearance) through a multi-stage strategy that decouples global appearance updates from geometric layout changes. The generative replay mechanism, borrowed from the continual learning literature (Shin et al.), is repurposed here to exploit 3DGS's efficient rendering: past views are re-synthesized from the current Gaussian field rather than stored as images, and a visibility pool tracks which Gaussians are active at each timestep.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving real-time (34 FPS) continual scene updates with the ability to visualize geometric changes over time, something implicit NeRF-based methods cannot do due to their lack of explicit geometry. The visibility pool and explicit Gaussian management enable applications like AR/VR where scene changes must be tracked and rendered interactively. However, the method inherits 3DGS limitations (poor handling of reflections/mirrors) and depends on external tools (SAM for segmentation, COLMAP for sparse points), adding pipeline complexity. Open questions include whether the multi-stage strategy generalizes to more complex or partially overlapping scene changes, and whether the approach scales to very long temporal sequences without accumulated drift.
