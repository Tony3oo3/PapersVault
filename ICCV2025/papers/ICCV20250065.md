## Problem

Existing tool-use VQA datasets rely on synthetic or over-simplified visual scenarios, single-step queries with explicit tool hints, or costly human annotation, creating a significant gap with real-world user interactions that involve complex visual contexts and implicit multi-step reasoning. This limits both the evaluation and fine-tuning of Large Foundation Models on realistic tool-augmented tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a real-world image (sourced from photography, e-commerce products, school exam charts, and other practical scenarios) with a text query that implicitly embeds multi-step tool-use reasoning (average 2.78 steps) and a toolset of 10 multimodal tools spanning perception, operation, logic, and creativity domains. The output is a concise answer (average 2.69 tokens, either text or image) accompanied by the full tool-use trajectory as a sequence of (tool_name, arguments) pairs. Queries average 15.74 tokens, deliberately avoiding explicit tool-name hints to mirror natural user phrasing.

## Method

ToolEngine is an automated data synthesis pipeline with three core components: (1) Real-World Example Construction, where human-annotated examples encode prior knowledge about typical tool-use scenarios; (2) Image-guided DFS on a tool graph, where ChatGPT-4o acts as a controller that selects the next tool and generates its arguments at each step, conditioned on the input image, the current trajectory, and retrieved examples; (3) LCS-based Example Matching, which dynamically retrieves the top-k most relevant examples at each DFS step by computing the Longest Common Subsequence between the current partial trajectory and each stored example trajectory, enabling adaptive multi-step reasoning rather than relying on a fixed example set. The resulting 23K samples (90.8% human-verified accuracy) are used to fine-tune LLaVA-7B via cross-entropy loss over multi-turn dialogues in the AgentLego framework, where each turn corresponds to one tool call or the final answer.

## Results

On the ToolVQA test set, the fine-tuned LLaVA-7B achieves 18.8% end-to-end accuracy in VLM+tool mode, surpassing GPT-3.5-Turbo (18.37%) and dramatically outperforming the untrained LLaVA-7B baseline (1.17%). In step-by-step mode, the tuned model reaches 86.62% instruction accuracy and 61.61% tool selection accuracy, compared to 73.24% and 30.46% for GPT-3.5-Turbo. On five out-of-distribution benchmarks, the tuned model outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo on four of five (TextVQA: 47 vs. 36.3; TallyQA: 64.3 vs. 61; InfoSeek: 13.8 vs. 11.3; GTA: 33.29 vs. 23.62), while trailing on TEMPLAMA (21.43 vs. 33.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LLaVA-v1.5-7B [Liu et al., 2023], the untrained vision-language model before fine-tuning on ToolVQA. Fine-tuning on ToolVQA yields a 16.4× improvement in end-to-end accuracy (18.8% vs. 1.17% in VLM+tool mode) and a 17.4× improvement in tool selection accuracy (61.61% vs. 9.43%), demonstrating that the dataset effectively transfers tool-use capability to a 7B model. Against the large closed-source GPT-3.5-Turbo, the tuned 7B model matches or exceeds performance on the in-domain ToolVQA test set (18.8% vs. 18.37%) and wins on four of five OOD benchmarks (TextVQA, TallyQA, InfoSeek, GTA), though it lags on TEMPLAMA (21.43% vs. 33.67%), likely due to GPT-3.5's broader pretraining exposure. The paper also notes that GPT-4o remains substantially stronger (34.96–37.62% end-to-end), indicating that even the best open-source fine-tuned model has not yet closed the gap to frontier closed-source systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tool-augmented VQA line of work (InfoSeek, VPD, M&M's, GTA, MM-Traj) by addressing two gaps the authors identify in prior datasets: reliance on synthetic/simplified visual contexts and explicit single-step tool hints. Its methodological contribution is the ToolEngine pipeline, which repurposes image-guided DFS (previously used in text-only tool-use data generation like ToolLLM) with a novel LCS-based dynamic example-matching mechanism to produce multi-step, real-world tool-use trajectories at scale. The fine-tuning experiments build on the LLaVA instruction-tuning paradigm within the AgentLego framework, positioning the work at the intersection of VLM fine-tuning and tool-agent research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ToolVQA provides the first large-scale (23K) benchmark combining real-world visual contexts with implicit multi-step tool reasoning, filling a gap between synthetic benchmarks and the costly human-annotated GAIA. The finding that VLM+tool underperforms LLM+tool for several models (including GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL) challenges the assumption that visual modules naturally enhance tool-use reasoning, pointing to an architectural bottleneck. However, absolute performance remains low (best ~38% for GPT-4o), and the persistent weakness in argument prediction and answer summarization suggests that dynamic integration of noisy tool outputs remains an open problem that fine-tuning alone does not resolve.
