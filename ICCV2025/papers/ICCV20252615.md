## Problem

Existing guidance sampling methods for diffusion models (CFG, PAG, SEG, AG) all require additional neural function evaluations, heuristic layer identification, or extra training, and are fundamentally incompatible with guidance-distilled models that have already internalized the guidance signal. The paper asks whether a universal, training-free boosting method can be designed that adds no extra NFEs, requires no layer search, and works seamlessly with both standard and guidance-distilled diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (U-Net or Transformer backbones such as SDXL, Flux, SANA, SDXL-Turbo, DMD2) as input, and produces a generated RGB image at the model's native resolution. Crucially, PLADIS operates purely as an inference-time modification: it replaces the softmax operator in every cross-attention layer with an extrapolation between softmax and α-Entmax, requiring no retraining, no additional forward passes, and no per-layer hyperparameter tuning. The only user-facing hyperparameters are the sparsity level α (default 1.5) and the extrapolation scale λ (default 2.0).

## Method

PLADIS modifies the cross-attention computation during inference by extrapolating between dense (softmax) and sparse (α-Entmax) attention: At_Ours = At + λ(At_α − At), where λ > 1 amplifies the sparse component. The key insight is that sparse cross-attention provides noise robustness analogous to Sparse Hopfield Networks (SHN), and since diffusion denoising operates on inherently noisy latents, this robustness translates directly into better text-image alignment and generation quality. The paper proves a retrieval error bound (Theorem 2) and a polynomial noise-robustness guarantee (Corollary 2.1) for 1 < α ≤ 2, grounding the empirical gains in theory. Unlike self-attention (where sparsity collapses to diagonal and breaks generation), cross-attention sparsity enforces stricter matching between image patches and text tokens. Because the modification is implicit within a single forward pass and touches only cross-attention, PLADIS is compatible with CFG, PAG, SEG, and guidance-distilled models without any architectural changes.

## Results

On MS-COCO (30K samples, 25 steps), PLADIS reduces FID by 4.2–4.9 and improves CLIPScore by 0.7–1.1 across all guidance configurations (CFG, PAG, SEG). With CFG, PLADIS achieves FID 19.01 (vs. 23.39 baseline), CLIPScore 26.61 (vs. 25.91), and ImageReward 0.622 (vs. 0.425). On guidance-distilled models (4-step sampling), PLADIS consistently improves CLIPScore by 0.16–0.84 and ImageReward by 0.01–0.11 across SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning, DMD2, and Hyper-SDXL. On GenEval, PLADIS raises the overall score from 0.671 to 0.713 (Flux-Schnell) and from 0.666 to 0.691 (Flux-dev).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLADIS is applied additively on top of each guidance baseline and consistently wins on all metrics. Against CFG, PLADIS reduces FID by ~4.4 (23.39→19.01) and lifts ImageReward by 0.20 (0.425→0.622) without any extra NFEs. Against PAG, the gains are larger: FID drops by ~4.2–4.9 and ImageReward improves by 0.25–0.31, making PAG+PLADIS the strongest configuration (FID 20.11, ImageReward 0.726 with CFG). Against SEG, PLADIS yields the largest FID reduction (~4.8–4.9) and CLIPScore gain (~1.0–1.1). On guidance-distilled models (4-step), PLADIS improves all four baselines—SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning, DMD2, and Hyper-SDXL—on CLIPScore and ImageReward, with the largest gains on SDXL-Lightning (+0.84 CLIPScore, +0.11 ImageReward on DrawBench) and the smallest on DMD2 (+0.16–0.39 CLIPScore, +0.01–0.09 ImageReward), confirming that PLADIS is most beneficial when the base model has more room for text-alignment improvement. No baseline is beaten by PLADIS in any configuration tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLADIS sits at the intersection of two research threads: α-Entmax sparse attention (Peters et al., 2019), which has been widely used in NLP but largely unexplored in vision, and Sparse Hopfield Networks (Hu et al., 2024), which provide a retrieval-error and noise-robustness framework for sparse attention dynamics. It departs sharply from the dominant line of guidance sampling methods (CFG, PAG, SEG, AG) that either require extra NFEs to compute conditional/unconditional or strong/weak model differences, or rely on heuristic layer selection. By targeting cross-attention exclusively and extrapolating between dense and sparse attention within a single forward pass, PLADIS avoids both the extra-inference bottleneck and the layer-search hyperparameter, making it the first guidance-compatible method that also works with step-distilled models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-line cross-attention replacement with no training, no extra NFEs, and no layer-specific tuning that improves FID by ~4–5 and CLIPScore by ~1 across SDXL, Flux, SANA, and guidance-distilled models (4-step and 1-step). The SHN-based retrieval error bound (Theorem 2) and its noise-robustness corollary provide the first theoretical justification for why sparsity helps in diffusion cross-attention, grounding an otherwise empirical observation. Open questions include whether the optimal α and λ are architecture- or prompt-dependent, whether the approach extends to video diffusion or autoregressive multimodal generation, and why self-attention sparsification fails (diagonal concentration) while cross-attention sparsification succeeds—a mechanistic understanding that remains incomplete.
