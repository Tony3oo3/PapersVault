## Problem

Event cameras produce events only when moving intensity patterns cross a contrast threshold, meaning motion and appearance are inherently entangled in the data. Yet prior work treats optical flow and image intensity estimation as separate tasks, ignoring this coupling. This paper addresses the absence of a unified, unsupervised framework that jointly recovers both quantities from a single event stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single event voxel grid (15 time bins) at the native event-camera resolution (e.g., 640×480 for DSEC, 970×625 for BS-ERGB) and simultaneously outputs a dense optical flow field (2 channels) and a logarithmic-scale intensity image (1 channel). During training, two consecutive event samples are fed to the network to compute a temporal-consistency loss; at inference, only one voxel grid is needed. The raw flow output is downsampled by 16× average pooling and bilinearly upsampled back to enforce smoothness before evaluation.

## Method

A single U-Net (15 input channels, 3 output channels) is trained unsupervised on DSEC with a composite loss that couples flow and intensity. The key new component is an event-based photometric error (PhE) derived from the event generation model: each event and its predecessor are warped to a reference time using the predicted flow, and the loss penalizes the discrepancy between the predicted intensity increment (via bilinear sampling of the predicted intensity at the two warped locations) and the measured polarity×contrast. This PhE is combined with a contrast-maximization (CMax) loss on the image of warped events (which constrains flow via edge sharpness), total-variation regularization on both flow and intensity, and a temporal-consistency term that warps the predicted intensity from frame i to i+1 using the predicted inter-frame flow and compares it against the independently predicted intensity at i+1. The complementary nature of PhE (appearance-focused) and CMax (motion-focused) yields a well-behaved joint objective without the event-collapse pathology of CMax alone.

## Results

On the DSEC optical flow benchmark, the method achieves EPE 1.15, AE 4.89°, and %Out 5.81% in the unsupervised/model-based category, representing 20% and 25% reductions in EPE and AE over the best prior USL method (MotionPriorCM: EPE 3.20, AE 8.53°). For image intensity on the HDR dataset (no-reference metrics), it achieves BRISQUE 25.03, NIQE 3.78, and MANIQA 0.40, outperforming the only other USL method (BTEB: BRISQUE 51.47, NIQE 6.24, MANIQA 0.18) by roughly 50% and surpassing most supervised methods. Inference time is 15.11 ms at 640×480 while simultaneously predicting both flow and intensity, compared to 10.95 ms for E2VID which predicts intensity only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest USL optical flow baseline MotionPriorCM [16], the method reduces EPE from 3.20 to 1.15 (−64%) and AE from 8.53° to 4.89° (−43%), while also cutting %Out from 15.21% to 5.81%. It outperforms all other MB/USL methods (RTEF, MultiCM, BTEB, EV-FlowNet, VSA-SM, Paredes et al.) across every reported metric on DSEC. In the supervised category, E-RAFT [13] still leads on EPE (0.79 vs. 1.15) and %Out (2.68% vs. 5.81%), but the method beats E-RAFT on AE (4.89° vs. 10.56°, a 39% reduction). For image intensity, the method is competitive with BTEB [28] on BS-ERGB (MSE 0.10 vs. 0.09; SSIM 0.31 vs. 0.36) but substantially outperforms it on the HDR dataset (BRISQUE 25.03 vs. 51.47; NIQE 3.78 vs. 6.24; MANIQA 0.40 vs. 0.18), and achieves the best MANIQA score among all methods including supervised ones (ET-Net: 0.32, E2VID: 0.30). On inference time, the method (15.11 ms) is slower than FireNet (4.94 ms) and E2VID (10.95 ms) for intensity-only prediction, but faster than SPADE-E2VID (36.07 ms) and ET-Net (173.56 ms), while simultaneously outputting both flow and intensity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the contrast maximization (CMax) framework [7] for event-based flow estimation and the photometric consistency idea from BTEB [28] for intensity reconstruction, but departs from both by deriving a unified event-based photometric error that is a function of *both* optical flow and image intensity simultaneously. Prior joint-estimation works (IVM, SOFIE) were model-based and limited in scope, while BTEB used two separate networks in a sequential pipeline. The key conceptual departure is treating flow and intensity as inherently coupled quantities in event data and exploiting that coupling through a single U-Net with a combined CMax + PhE + temporal-consistency loss, rather than solving them as independent problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a single lightweight U-Net can produce both flow and intensity with state-of-the-art unsupervised accuracy and minimal inference time, this work makes event-based perception more practical for real-time robotics and autonomous driving where running two separate networks is prohibitive. The derived PhE loss is a reusable theoretical tool that can be adapted to other event-based estimation problems. Open questions remain around event-free regions (where regularization fills in potentially incorrect values), handling flickering-light outliers, and whether combining events with frame data or adding recurrent connections could further close the gap to supervised methods in challenging scenarios.
