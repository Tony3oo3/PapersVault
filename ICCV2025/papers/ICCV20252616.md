## Problem

Predicting both the capture time and geo-location of an image from visual cues alone remains unsolved: prior time-prediction methods require GPS metadata as input, while state-of-the-art geo-localization models ignore temporal information entirely. Additionally, the absence of standardized benchmarks and evaluation protocols for time-of-capture prediction has hindered fair comparison across studies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image with no auxiliary metadata (no GPS, no EXIF timestamp) and produces both a GPS coordinate and a time-of-capture estimate (normalized month-hour pair) by retrieving the highest-similarity gallery entry in a shared embedding space. The unified space additionally supports compositional retrieval (querying by joint time+location to retrieve matching images) and text-to-location/time retrieval via the pretrained CLIP text encoder, where text prompts like 'warm summer evening in Oslo' yield spatial and temporal distributions without explicit time tokens.

## Method

GT-Loc employs a triple-encoder architecture (image, location, time) that aligns all three modalities in a shared high-dimensional embedding space. The image encoder is a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 projected through a trainable MLP; the location encoder follows GeoCLIP's pipeline (Equal Earth Projection → multi-scale Random Fourier Features → MLP); and the time encoder mirrors the location encoder but operates on a novel normalized cyclic month-hour pair representation. The key innovation is the Temporal Metric Learning (TML) loss for image-time alignment: instead of hard positive/negative pairs (which fail because temporally adjacent images look nearly identical), TML assigns soft targets proportional to the toroidal distance between each pair's month-hour coordinates, then minimizes cross-entropy between the softmax-normalized cosine similarity vector and the softmax-normalized toroidal distance vector. Image-location alignment uses a standard CLIP-style contrastive loss with a dynamic queue, and the two losses are summed for joint optimization.

## Results

On the SkyFinder zero-shot time prediction benchmark, GT-Loc achieves a Time Prediction Score (TPS) of 77.00, with a month error of 1.40 and hour error of 2.72, outperforming the best prior method (Salem et al. with CLIP backbone, TPS 75.02) by ~2 TPS points. On geo-localization, GT-Loc reaches 14.41% accuracy at 1 km on Im2GPS3k and 0.88% on GWS15k, slightly surpassing GeoCLIP (14.11% and 0.6%) and PIGEON (11.3% and 0.7%). The joint time-location training also improves time prediction over the time-only variant TimeLoc (TPS 75.49), confirming the benefit of the shared embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GT-Loc outperforms all three replicated time-prediction baselines (Zhai et al., Padilha et al., Salem et al.) by a wide margin, even when those baselines are retrained with a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone; the closest competitor, Salem et al. w/ CLIP, trails by 1.98 TPS points (75.02 vs. 77.00). Against standard classification/regression heads on frozen backbones (CLIP, DINOv2, OpenCLIP), GT-Loc's retrieval-based approach consistently wins, with the best head-based baseline (CLIP + regression) reaching only 74.33 TPS. On geo-localization, GT-Loc edges out GeoCLIP by 0.30 points on Im2GPS3k (14.41 vs. 14.11) and 0.28 points on GWS15k (0.88 vs. 0.6), and surpasses PIGEON by 3.11 and 0.18 points respectively. GT-Loc also substantially outperforms the LLaVA-based Img2Loc (8.0% on Im2GPS3k) while using at least 15× fewer parameters. Notably, the time-only ablation TimeLoc (75.49 TPS) is 1.51 TPS below the full GT-Loc, demonstrating that joint location training enriches temporal representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

GT-Loc directly builds on GeoCLIP's image-location alignment pipeline (Equal Earth Projection + multi-scale Random Fourier Features + contrastive loss) and extends it with a third modality encoder for time. It departs from prior time-prediction methods (Zhai et al., Padilha et al., Salem et al.), which all require GPS metadata as input, by formulating time prediction as a retrieval problem in a shared embedding space with no auxiliary inputs. The key conceptual departure is the Temporal Metric Learning loss, which replaces hard positive/negative contrastive assignments with soft targets derived from toroidal distance, motivated by the observation that temporal neighbors in visual appearance are nearly indistinguishable—unlike spatial neighbors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly predict time-of-capture and geo-location from a single image without any metadata, and the unified embedding space enables compositional (time+location→image) and text-based retrieval that prior single-task methods cannot support. The finding that joint optimization outperforms time-only baselines even when those baselines receive ground-truth GPS as input suggests that location and time cues are genuinely complementary in the visual signal. Open questions include how the gallery-based retrieval resolution scales, whether the approach generalizes to indoor or low-sunlight scenes, and how to incorporate the year dimension, which the paper explicitly excludes.
