## Problem

Prior online mistake detection methods predominantly target procedural errors (incorrect action ordering or missed steps) but fail to capture execution errors such as motor inaccuracies or improper equipment use, limiting their applicability to real-world task monitoring. Additionally, existing approaches only flag that a mistake occurred without explaining why, depriving users of actionable guidance for correction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous egocentric RGB video stream processed in sliding segments up to the current timestep t (features extracted at 2 fps), optionally augmented with per-frame hand pose representations (coordinate dimensions × timesteps × joints × hands) predicted by Mediapipe. It outputs per-timestep binary mistake logits and, when the sigmoid of a logit exceeds a threshold τ, triggers a gated projection of the Video Q-Former features into the Llama2-7B embedding space to generate a free-text explanation of the detected error.

## Method

MistSense encodes RGB frames with a frozen ViT-L and extracts temporal relations via a BLIP-2-style Video Q-Former (BERT-based cross-attention with learnable queries), while a separate TCN-based hand pose encoder feeds a structurally identical Hand Q-Former; the two query-conditioned feature sets are summed before a classification head. An ablation shows that using separate Q-Formers per modality outperforms a shared Q-Former by 2.5% F1, and adding hand pose to RGB yields a further 3.5% gain over RGB alone. For explanation generation, the Video Q-Former features are gated by the mistake logit, linearly projected into the LLM space, and combined with a prompt to condition Llama2-7B. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) weighted binary cross-entropy for detection with the video encoder and Q-Former frozen, (2) cross-entropy on annotated explanations with only the projection layer unfrozen, and (3) LoRA fine-tuning of the LLM while freezing the projection layer.

## Results

On Epic-tent-O, MistSense with RGB+hand pose achieves an overall F1-Score of 63.3% and a mistake F1 of 38.3%, outperforming the best prior method (DO, 46.5% F1) by 16.8 points in overall F1. On Holo Assist, MistSense (RGB+hand pose) reaches 55.7% F1-Score, surpassing the GazeCompl baseline (51.0%) by 3.8 points without requiring an eye-gaze modality. For error explanation generation, MistSense achieves the highest CIDEr score on both Holo Assist (0.76 vs. 0.68 for VideoLLaMA) and Epic-tent (3.09 vs. 2.61 for Video-ChatGPT), indicating superior semantic alignment of generated explanations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Epic-tent-O, MistSense (RGB only) outperforms DO [30] by 13.3 points in overall F1 (59.8 vs. 46.5) and by 6.1 points in mistake F1 (29.8 vs. 23.7); adding hand pose further lifts the overall F1 to 63.3 and mistake F1 to 38.3. All other baselines (Count-Based, PREGO, MSG2) trail by 14–34 points in overall F1. On Holo Assist, MistSense (RGB) achieves 54.8% F1, beating GazeCompl [25] (51.0%, which uses an additional eye-gaze modality) by 3.8 points and TSFormer [4] (35.1%) by 19.7 points. For error explanation, MistSense matches or exceeds Video-ChatGPT [24] and VideoLLaMA [40] on CIDEr across both datasets (0.76 vs. 0.61/0.68 on Holo Assist; 3.09 vs. 2.61/1.78 on Epic-tent), while Video-ChatGPT edges out MistSense slightly on BLEU for Holo Assist (0.55 vs. 0.53). The hand pose modality consistently contributes 2.5–3.5 points of F1 improvement over RGB-only across both datasets, confirming its value for execution-error detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

MistSense builds directly on the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture [20] and its video extension (Video Q-Former) from VideoLLaMA [40], repurposing the vision-language pipeline from captioning to mistake detection and explanation. It departs from prior online mistake detection methods (PREGO [9], DO [30], MSG2 [14]) that treat errors primarily as temporal-ordering violations by jointly modeling spatial execution correctness through a TCN-based hand pose encoder [32] and a separate Hand Q-Former. The LLM explanation component adapts the Q-Former-to-LLM projection paradigm from BLIP-2/VideoLLaMA but redirects it from generating scene descriptions to producing targeted error rationales, a use case not explored in prior vision-language work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify procedural and execution error detection with automatic textual explanations in an online egocentric setting, making it directly applicable to AR/VR assistants and industrial training scenarios where users need not just an alert but guidance on *why* an action was wrong. Key limitations include difficulty with very short (1.5–2 s) mistake spikes, reliance on Mediapipe hand pose predictions that may degrade in occluded views, and the limited scale of the Epic-tent dataset. Open questions include whether additional modalities (gaze, depth) would further close the gap on execution errors, and whether the explanation quality generalizes beyond the relatively templated error descriptions in current benchmarks.
