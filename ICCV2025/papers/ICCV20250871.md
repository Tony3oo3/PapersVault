## Problem

Existing LiDAR SLAM pipelines rely on point clouds, surfels, meshes, or implicit neural representations, each introducing trade-offs between accuracy, memory, and runtime that limit online deployment. While 2D Gaussian Splatting has proven effective for visual SLAM, no pipeline has explored Gaussian primitives as the sole scene representation for a pure LiDAR odometry and mapping system, leaving open whether this representation can match SOTA registration and mapping quality at lower computational cost.

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw 360° LiDAR point clouds (e.g., Ouster OS0-128, Hesai QT64) and projects them onto a spherical frame to produce range maps that drive both optimization and registration. It outputs per-frame ego-motion estimates (SE(3) poses), a local 2D Gaussian scene representation maintained via keyframing, and optionally a 3D mesh extracted by sampling rendered range/normal maps and running Poisson reconstruction. The local model is reset when visibility conditions degrade, keeping the active primitive count bounded for real-time operation.

## Method

Splat-LOAM represents the scene exclusively with 2D Gaussian primitives (centroid, two tangential vectors, scaling, opacity) and renders them onto a spherical image via a custom differentiable tile-based rasterizer that uses exact ray-splat intersections rather than the local affine approximation, explicitly handling the azimuthal coordinate singularity through a shift-and-propagate bounding-box strategy. Mapping initializes Gaussians by back-projecting weighted-sampled range-map points with normals directed toward the sensor, then refines them with a composite loss combining L1 range error, normal-alignment regularization, opacity-coverage correlation, and a thresholded scaling penalty that permits anisotropic growth up to a cap before penalizing. Tracking registers each new keyframe against the local Gaussian model using a joint geometric (PCA-based kd-tree point-to-plane) and photometric (spherical range-map) residual, optimized with a second-order Gauss-Newton update in the Lie algebra. A keyframing strategy with geometric-distribution sampling over recent keyframes and no opacity reset prevents catastrophic forgetting while keeping the active primitive count and per-iteration cost stable.

## Results

On the Newer College dataset, Splat-LOAM achieves the best Chamfer-L1 (5.37 cm on quad-easy) and F-score (96.74%) among all methods, outperforming N³-Mapping (8.04 cm, 94.54%) and OpenVDB (7.92 cm, 88.85%). On Oxford Spires, Splat-LOAM is competitive with N³-Mapping, achieving slightly higher F-scores (94.41% vs 93.47% on keble-02) at comparable C-l1 (7.43 vs 7.01 cm). On the synthetic Mai-City dataset, N³-Mapping retains a small edge (C-l1 2.64 vs 3.64 cm; F-score 99.06% vs 97.27%), while Splat-LOAM substantially outperforms VoxBlox and PIN-SLAM across all sequences. The paper also reports that Splat-LOAM matches SOTA odometry (RPE) performance while using minimal GPU memory (~1–2 GB active) and maintaining stable mapping FPS between 200k–300k primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Splat-LOAM clearly outperforms the classic volumetric baselines (OpenVDB and VoxBlox) on every sequence, with the largest margins on VoxBlox (e.g., C-l1 5.37 vs 16.50 cm on NC quad-easy; F-score 96.74% vs 64.63%). Against the neural-implicit PIN-SLAM, Splat-LOAM wins on all seven sequences in both C-l1 and F-score, with the gap most pronounced on Oxford Spires (e.g., 7.43 vs 11.83 cm on keble-02). The closest competitor is N³-Mapping: on Oxford Spires the two methods are nearly tied (C-l1 within 0.4 cm, F-score within 1%), while on Mai-City N³-Mapping retains a modest advantage (C-l1 2.64 vs 3.64 cm; F-score 99.06% vs 97.27%). Notably, N³-Mapping fails entirely on the NC math-easy sequence, whereas Splat-LOAM completes it with C-l1 9.15 cm and F-score 90.02%. For odometry (RPE), Splat-LOAM's joint geometric + photometric tracker matches or exceeds MAD-ICP and PIN-SLAM, confirming that the Gaussian representation supports competitive tracking without additional sensor modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts 2D Gaussian Splatting (Huang et al., 2024) and its exact ray-splat intersection rasterizer to the LiDAR domain by replacing pinhole projection with spherical projection, a non-affine mapping that requires reformulating the ray parametrization to cover the full 360° azimuthal space. For geometric registration, it borrows the PCA-based kd-tree segmentation from MAD-ICP (Ferrari et al., 2024), while the tile-based rasterization paradigm traces back to 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023). The key departure from prior Gaussian Splatting SLAM work (Gaussian-SLAM, Splat-SLAM, LoopSplat) is the elimination of all camera/photometric input: here, LiDAR range measurements alone drive both the mapping loss and the tracking residual, making this the first pure-LiDAR Gaussian Splatting odometry and mapping pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a lightweight, explicit 2D Gaussian representation can match SOTA LiDAR mapping accuracy (e.g., N³-Mapping, PIN-SLAM) at a fraction of the GPU memory and runtime, this work opens a practical path toward real-time dense LiDAR SLAM on embedded hardware. The spherical rasterizer and the combined geometric-photometric tracker are reusable components for future LiDAR-centric perception systems. However, the pipeline currently lacks loop closure and rolling-shutter compensation, and the 'on par' (not superior) mapping results suggest the representation's full potential—particularly for large-scale or dynamic scenes—remains unexplored.
