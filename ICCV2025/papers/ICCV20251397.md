## Problem

Feed-forward 4D reconstruction from monocular video is severely limited by the scarcity of 3D-annotated dynamic scene data, and existing methods like DUSt3R/MonST3R require expensive per-video optimization or large supervised datasets. Video diffusion models encode rich implicit priors about camera motion, perspective, and object dynamics from large-scale pretraining, but these priors remain locked in pixel space and are not directly actionable for geometric reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of N frames (no camera parameters provided) and produces, per frame, a viewpoint-invariant point map (H×W×3, coordinates in the first frame's reference), a disparity map (H×W×1), and a ray map (H×W×6, Plücker coordinates). At inference, the video is processed in overlapping 16-frame clips with stride 4, and a global multi-modal alignment optimization fuses all modalities across clips to recover a coherent 4D reconstruction including camera extrinsics and intrinsics. All quantities are recovered up to a global scale (and disparity up to a shift), as is inherent to monocular input.

## Method

Geo4D repurposes the DynamiCrafter video diffusion model by fine-tuning its denoising U-Net to generate three complementary geometric modalities in latent space, with a fine-tuned VAE decoder (trained with a heteroscedastic uncertainty loss) for point maps. Video conditioning uses a hybrid mechanism: CLIP-encoded global frame features injected via cross-attention in each U-Net block, plus local VAE spatial features concatenated channel-wise to the noised latents. The key architectural insight is that the three modalities—point maps (limited dynamic range), disparity maps (zero at infinity), and ray maps (defined for all pixels regardless of geometry)—are redundant in principle but complementary in practice, serving as additional supervisory signals during training. At inference, a group-wise multi-modal alignment optimization (Eqs. 4–10) jointly recovers global camera parameters, disparity, and point maps by minimizing a weighted combination of point-map alignment, disparity alignment, ray-map camera alignment, and trajectory smoothing losses across all sliding-window clips.

## Results

On video depth estimation, Geo4D achieves Abs Rel of 0.205 (δ<1.25: 73.5%) on Sintel, 0.059 (97.2%) on Bonn, and 0.086 (93.7%) on KITTI, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. The most notable gains are over DepthCrafter (the strongest video-depth-only baseline), with 24.0% lower Abs Rel on Sintel and 17.3% lower on KITTI, despite Geo4D solving the more general 4D reconstruction problem. For camera pose estimation, Geo4D achieves the best relative rotation error (RPE-R: 0.547° on Sintel, 0.635° on TUM-dynamics) while remaining competitive on translation metrics (ATE: 0.185 on Sintel vs. 0.108 for MonST3R).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo4D decisively outperforms all eight baselines on video depth estimation across Sintel, Bonn, and KITTI. Against the strongest video-depth-only method DepthCrafter [22], Geo4D reduces Abs Rel from 0.270 to 0.205 on Sintel (24% improvement) and from 0.104 to 0.086 on KITTI (17.3%), while also improving δ<1.25 from 69.7% to 73.5% on Sintel. Against MonST3R [113], the dynamic extension of DUSt3R, Geo4D achieves substantially lower Abs Rel (0.205 vs. 0.335 on Sintel, 0.086 vs. 0.104 on KITTI) and higher δ<1.25 (73.5% vs. 58.5% on Sintel). Single-frame methods (Marigold, DepthAnything-V2) are far behind, confirming the importance of temporal context. For camera pose, Geo4D wins on rotation (RPE-R: 0.547° vs. 0.732° for MonST3R on Sintel; 0.635° vs. 1.217° on TUM-dynamics) but trails MonST3R on translation (ATE: 0.185 vs. 0.108 on Sintel; RPE-T: 0.063 vs. 0.042), indicating that the generative approach excels at orientation but is slightly less precise on absolute camera position.

## Relation to Prior Work

Geo4D repurposes the DynamiCrafter video diffusion model as a backbone for 4D geometric reconstruction, extending the line of work that adapts generative models for geometric tasks (Marigold for single-frame depth, DepthCrafter for video depth). It adopts DUSt3R's viewpoint-invariant point map representation and extends MonST3R's dynamic scene formulation, but departs from both by using a pre-trained video generator's motion and geometry priors instead of image-matching pretraining or 3D-annotated supervision. The key methodological departure is the joint prediction of three complementary modalities (point, disparity, and ray maps) fused via a group-wise multi-modal alignment optimization, building on Ray Diffusion's camera-from-rays formulation and MonST3R's trajectory smoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant practical contribution is demonstrating that a video diffusion model trained exclusively on synthetic 4D data can generalize zero-shot to real-world dynamic videos, eliminating the need for expensive 3D annotations in dynamic scenes. This opens a path toward video foundation models that natively encode 4D geometry, potentially enabling applications like text-to-3D-animation or world-model-based planning. However, the authors acknowledge that the point map encoder-decoder remains a bottleneck for reconstruction quality, and the approach still requires a multi-step alignment optimization at inference time, limiting its speed relative to purely discriminative methods. An open question is whether the multi-modal fusion strategy generalizes to even longer videos or more extreme domain gaps.
