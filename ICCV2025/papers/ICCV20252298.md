## Problem

Diffusion-based adversarial purification (e.g., DiffPure) often produces purified images that deviate more from the original than the adversarial perturbation itself, losing critical early-layer features and causing misclassification. The score function in off-the-shelf diffusion models is trained to predict means under isotropic covariance, limiting its ability to restore subtle data covariates, and retraining is incompatible with test-time defense. The paper asks how to improve purification fidelity without retraining the diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a standard RGB image (adversarial or clean) at its native resolution (e.g., 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k). It first applies a one-shot SR upsample (×2) followed by a downsample back to the original resolution, then feeds the result into a diffusion purification pipeline (VP-SDE or iterative One-Shot Denoising). The output is a purified image at the original resolution, which is passed to a standard classifier for a class label. The SR step adds only ~0.01 s of latency versus ~1.08 s for the diffusion step, making the pipeline effectively plug-and-play with off-the-shelf models.

## Method

PuriFlow inserts a super-resolution step (upsample then downsample) before the forward diffusion process, exploiting the finding that SR, while weaker than diffusion at removing adversarial noise globally, is superior at restoring features captured by early CNN layers. The key insight is synergistic: SR corrects feature distortions that diffusion alone cannot recover, positioning the perturbed data closer to the data manifold so the trained score function performs a more accurate orthonormal projection during reverse diffusion. A geometric analysis (Proposition 1) formalizes this by showing that SR followed by downsampling projects off-manifold adversarial data within a small δ of the tangent space at the true data point. The pipeline is entirely plug-and-play, combining off-the-shelf SR models (ESRGAN, MDSR, EDSR) with off-the-shelf diffusion models (VP-SDE, iOSD/ScoreOpt) without any retraining, and the ×2 upsample ratio is shown to offer the best efficiency–performance trade-off.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-28-10 under BPDA+EOT (ε=8/255), PuriFlow with iterative One-Shot Denoising (SR+iOSD) achieves 94.14% standard and 91.01% robust accuracy, outperforming the best prior purification method ScoreOpt (93.75%/88.08%) by 2.93 points in robust accuracy. With VP-SDE diffusion (SR+SDEs), PuriFlow reaches 90.06%/83.00%, surpassing DiffPure (89.02%/81.40%) and contrastive-guided diffusion (92.61%/81.94%). On ImageNet-1k under AutoAttack (ε=4/255), PuriFlow achieves 71.68%/52.92% on ResNet-50 and 75.69%/53.12% on DeiT-S, exceeding all adversarial training baselines by 10–15 points in robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuriFlow consistently outperforms its direct diffusion-based counterparts: SR+iOSD beats ScoreOpt [60] by 2.93 points in robust accuracy (91.01% vs. 88.08%) while also improving standard accuracy by 0.39 points, and SR+SDEs beats DiffPure [38] by 1.60 points in robust accuracy (83.00% vs. 81.40%). Against contrastive-guided diffusion (Bai et al. [5]), PuriFlow([60]) gains 9.07 points in robust accuracy (91.01% vs. 81.94%), though it trails by 1.53 points in standard accuracy. On ImageNet-1k under AutoAttack, PuriFlow([38]) surpasses the strongest adversarial training method (Salman et al. [42], 37.89% robust) by 15.03 points on ResNet-50 and (Bai et al. [6], 35.50%) by 17.62 points on DeiT-S. Under the targeted DiffAttack, PuriFlow improves robust accuracy by 14.16% (CIFAR-10) and 15.62% (ImageNet-1k) over diffusion-only purification. In the preprocessor-blind setting at a very short diffusion time (t′=10), PuriFlow gains 11.05% (ResNet-50) and 15.26% (DeiT-S) in robust accuracy over DiffPure while maintaining comparable standard accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based adversarial purification (DiffPure [38], ScoreOpt [60]) by inserting a super-resolution upsampling-downsampling step before the forward diffusion process. While prior work explored SR as a standalone defense [7, 36] or diffusion as a standalone defense [38, 60], PuriFlow identifies a specific complementary division of labor: SR restores early-layer CNN features that diffusion's score function cannot recover, while diffusion handles broader noise removal. The theoretical grounding draws on the manifold-constraint perspective of Chung et al. [12], formalizing why off-manifold adversarial examples benefit from SR's projection toward the data tangent space before the score function is applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (off-the-shelf SR + off-the-shelf diffusion, ~0.01s SR overhead) makes this immediately deployable without retraining, and the consistent 5–15% robust accuracy gains across multiple attack types and architectures (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-1k, CNNs and ViTs) are practically meaningful. However, the approach still requires both an SR model and a diffusion model at inference time, and the standard accuracy occasionally drops slightly relative to diffusion alone. Open questions include whether the SR-diffusion synergy generalizes to other generative backbones (e.g., flow matching, autoregressive models) and whether the manifold-based analysis extends beyond the idealized SR assumption in Proposition 1.
