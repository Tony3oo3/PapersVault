## Problem

Despite widespread observation that generative models leave distinct traces on their outputs, no formal framework exists to define, represent, and analyze these fingerprints in a principled way. The first such definition (ManiFPT) assumes a Euclidean data manifold, an assumption violated by real-world data like images that follow non-Euclidean geometry, limiting its applicability and generalizability to unseen datasets and model types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252057/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single generated image x_G and a real image dataset X_R (used once to learn the latent Riemannian manifold via a VAE). The artifact is computed in the VAE's latent space as a difference vector between the encoded generated sample and its Riemannian center-of-mass projection onto the learned manifold, not in pixel space. This artifact vector is then fed to a fine-tuned ResNet-50 classifier that outputs a source-model label among 27 architectures spanning GAN, VAE, Flow, and Score-based families, evaluated at 64×64 and 256×256 resolutions.

## Method

The core contribution is a Riemannian-geometric definition of GM artifacts and fingerprints that generalizes the Euclidean ManiFPT definition to non-Euclidean manifolds. A VAE with separate mean and variance estimators is trained on real data, and the Euclidean metric on the data space is pulled back to the latent manifold via the decoder's Jacobians (g = J_μᵀJ_μ + J_σᵀJ_σ), yielding a learned Riemannian metric. For each generated sample, its k-nearest neighbors are found on the manifold using geodesic distances (not Euclidean), and the projection is estimated as the Riemannian Center of Mass of those kNNs via iterative gradient descent on the manifold, ensuring the projection lies on the manifold itself. The artifact is the difference vector between the generated sample and this RCM projection, and the fingerprint is the collection of all such artifacts over the model's support. A ResNet-50 classifier trained on these artifact features performs multi-class model attribution, outperforming all Euclidean-based and frequency-based baselines across four datasets and two modalities.

## Results

On model attribution (multi-class classification of source generative model), R-GMfpts_RCM achieves 78.17% accuracy on GM-CIFAR10, 74.7% on GM-CelebA, 65.8% on GM-CHQ, and 67.1% on GM-FFHQ, outperforming all 12 baselines on every dataset. The Fréchet Distance Ratio (FDR), measuring fingerprint separability, reaches 60.1 (CIFAR-10), 125.9 (CelebA), 74.5 (CHQ), and 60.8 (FFHQ). The Euclidean variant R-GMfpts (without geodesic RCM) also beats all baselines, confirming that the Riemannian geometric structure of the learned manifold is the key improvement over prior Euclidean definitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed R-GMfpts_RCM outperforms all 12 baselines on all four datasets. The closest competitor is ManiFPT (the prior Euclidean manifold-based method [60]): R-GMfpts_RCM improves accuracy by 8.0 points on CIFAR-10 (78.17 vs. 70.19 for ManiFPT_FREQ), 1.9 points on CelebA (74.7 vs. 72.8), 11.0 points on CHQ (65.8 vs. 54.8 for ManiFPT_FREQ), and 3.3 points on FFHQ (67.1 vs. 63.8). Frequency-based methods (Durall [15], Dzanic [16], Corvi [12]) and color-based methods (McCloskey [47], Nataraj [48]) trail by 10–35 accuracy points, confirming that handcrafted spectral or color features are insufficient for fine-grained model attribution. Supervised CNN classifiers (Wang [65], Yu [69], Marra [45,46]) achieve mid-range performance (51–64% accuracy) but are consistently outperformed by the Riemannian artifact features. Notably, the Euclidean variant R-GMfpts (using L2 kNN instead of geodesic RCM) already beats ManiFPT on CIFAR-10 (72.01 vs. 70.19) and CelebA (73.6 vs. 72.8), while the full Riemannian RCM variant adds a further 6.2-point gain on CIFAR-10, demonstrating that the geodesic structure of the learned manifold is the critical differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ManiFPT [60], the first formal definition of generative model fingerprints, by replacing its Euclidean manifold assumption with a Riemannian geometric framework. The Riemannian metric is learned via a pullback construction from a VAE decoder, drawing on the information-geometry literature of Arvanitidis et al. [3, 4] and Hauberg [23], while the projection step uses a gradient-based Riemannian center of mass algorithm from Afsari et al. [2]. The key conceptual departure is that artifacts are now measured as deviations along geodesics on a learned curved manifold rather than as L2 distances in a flat embedding, and the projection is a kNN-based RCM rather than a single nearest neighbor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative models proliferate across industries and AI regulation tightens, reliable model attribution is becoming a practical necessity for IP protection, content verification, and detecting model collapse from regurgitative training. The Riemannian framework provides a principled, geometry-aware definition that generalizes across datasets, model families, and modalities more effectively than prior Euclidean approaches, as evidenced by strong cross-dataset transfer (including semantically dissimilar domains like CIFAR-10 ↔ CelebA). Open questions include whether the VAE-based manifold learning scales to video or text modalities, whether the pullback metric captures the true data geometry or merely the VAE's inductive bias, and how fingerprints behave under adversarial perturbations designed to erase them.
