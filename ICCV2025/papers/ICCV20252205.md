## Problem

Multi-concept personalization of T2I diffusion models suffers from concept mixing, where learned subjects interfere with each other in generated images. Existing adapter-based methods require merging LoRA weights at inference, causing cross-concept interference, while text-embedding-based methods disrupt the pre-trained model's token-attention binding capacity. No prior approach simultaneously avoids adapter merging, preserves attention binding, and disentangles entangled attention during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing multiple personalized concept tokens (e.g., 'a {dog} sitting next to a {cat}, with a background of {forest}') and a database of pre-trained LoRA adapters, one per concept. At inference, each adapter is selectively attached to its corresponding token via a one-hot mask, and the latent is optimized for N=10 denoising steps using 24×24 cross-attention maps extracted from all attention layers and heads. The output is a single RGB image in which all specified concepts appear as distinct, non-mixed subjects, evaluated at standard Stable Diffusion 2.1 resolution.

## Method

ConceptSplit comprises two components. Token-wise Value Adaptation (ToVA) trains LoRA adapters exclusively on the value projection of cross-attention while freezing the key, preserving the pre-trained model's token-attention binding capacity; at inference, each adapter modifies only its designated token's value vector via a one-hot selection, eliminating the need for weight merging. Latent Optimization for Disentangled Attention (LODA) operates in two stages during denoising: Stage 1 optimizes the input latent to maximize the harmonic mean of pairwise KL divergences between concept tokens' attention distributions (using a negative-ReLU loss with threshold τ), and Stage 2 (Attention Fixing Guidance) locks the disentangled attention by amplifying each token's highlighted region (p=3) while suppressing other tokens' regions (m=−1e8) through a percentile-based binary mask applied to the U-Net's attention forwarding. The key insight is that modifying only values (not keys) preserves attention binding, and optimizing the latent rather than the attention map directly achieves disentanglement in a self-guided manner.

## Results

On the 2-object scenario (without background), ConceptSplit achieves D-IA of 0.759 and GenEval of 0.902, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (EDLoRA: D-IA 0.566, GE 0.342; DreamBooth: GE 0.624). In the 3-object scenario (with background), ConceptSplit reaches D-IA 0.557 and GE 0.384, while all baselines drop below D-IA 0.40 and GE 0.13. For single-object tasks, ConceptSplit remains competitive (D-IA 0.809 w/o background, 0.590 w/ background) and achieves the highest TA (0.286) in the with-background setting, confirming robustness without sacrificing single-concept fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptSplit decisively outperforms all five baselines on D-IA and GenEval in multi-object settings. Against EDLoRA (the strongest adapter-based baseline), ConceptSplit gains +0.193 D-IA and +0.560 GE on 2-object w/o background, and +0.164 D-IA and +0.380 GE on 3-object w/ background. Against Cones2 (the strongest text-embedding-based baseline), the GE gap is even more dramatic: 0.902 vs 0.582 (2 obj, w/o bg) and 0.384 vs 0.080 (3 obj, w/ bg). Custom Diffusion and DreamBooth collapse to near-zero GE (≤0.03) in 3-object scenarios, confirming severe concept mixing. On single-object tasks, ConceptSplit is competitive but not uniformly best: EDLoRA leads on GE (0.998 vs 0.972 w/ bg) and Custom Diffusion leads on C-IA (0.873 vs 0.838 w/o bg), while ConceptSplit wins on D-IA (0.809 vs 0.800) and TA (0.286 w/ bg). The paper's key claim is that the large GE and D-IA margins in multi-object scenarios demonstrate effective concept disentanglement that no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptSplit sits at the intersection of adapter-based multi-concept personalization (Custom Diffusion, EDLoRA/Mix-of-Show) and attention-control techniques (Prompt-to-Prompt, Attend-and-Excite), but departs from both in a specific way. Unlike adapter methods that merge LoRA weights at inference (causing concept interference), ToVA applies adapters token-wise to the value projection only, eliminating the need for merging. Unlike text-embedding methods (Textual Inversion, Cones2) that modify embeddings feeding into the key projection and thereby disrupt the pretrained attention binding, ConceptSplit freezes the key and trains only the value. The LODA inference stage extends Attend-and-Excite's latent-optimization idea from boosting attention intensity to actively diverging attention distributions via KL-divergence minimization, followed by a novel Attention Fixing Guidance step to prevent re-entanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that modifying the key projection in cross-attention degrades token-attention binding (evidenced by sustained high attention entropy) while value-only modification preserves it provides a useful design principle for future personalization methods. The substantial GE improvements (e.g., 0.902 vs. 0.342 for two objects) demonstrate that compositional correctness, not just similarity scores, can be meaningfully improved. However, performance on three-object scenarios remains weak (GE ≈ 0.34–0.38), and the authors acknowledge that generalization is bounded by the underlying pretrained diffusion model. Open questions include whether the value-only training principle transfers to transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., SD3, Flux) and whether the two-stage LODA can be made more scalable to larger concept counts.
