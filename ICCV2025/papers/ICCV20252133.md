## Problem

Existing CLIP-based continual learning methods treat CLIP as a feature extractor and overlook its defining cross-modal property—the modality gap, the inherent separation between image and text feature cones. Cross-entropy optimization during sequential task training progressively expands this gap, eroding the pre-trained vision-language knowledge that provides stability against forgetting, while direct alignment losses that shrink the gap cause overspecialization on limited downstream data. The paper asks how to handle the modality gap in continual learning to simultaneously preserve pre-trained knowledge (stability) and retain capacity for new data (plasticity).

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed by CLIP ViT-B/16) and class-name text prompts (processed by the CLIP text encoder) to produce a single class label per image. At inference, the prediction is a weighted sum of two scores: the standard CLIP text-based cosine similarity and a softmax over a learned visual-space cosine classifier, combined via a fixed hyperparameter β (default 4). No replay buffer or task identifier is required at test time; the model must classify over all previously seen classes simultaneously.

## Method

MG-CLIP introduces a two-stage strategy built around the modality gap. First, Modality Gap Preservation (MGP) monitors the mean negative cosine similarity between image and text features during LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8 on key/value attention); training for each task is halted when the relative deviation from the original CLIP's negative similarity exceeds a threshold α=10%, preventing cross-entropy from expanding the gap and disrupting pre-trained knowledge. Second, Modality Gap Compensation (MGC) addresses the fact that the preserved gap restricts text classifiers to a lower-rank subspace of the image feature space: an auxiliary cosine classifier is trained in the visual feature space for new-class prototypes, operating without text features so the gap imposes no constraint. At inference, the two classifier outputs are fused additively. The entire approach requires no replay data, no architectural expansion, and only 0.54M additional trainable parameters.

## Results

MG-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across all five benchmarks without any replay data. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 87.00% Avg and 80.57% Last accuracy, surpassing the best replay-based method CLAP (86.13/78.21) by 1.53% in Last accuracy. On the challenging VTAB benchmark, where zero-shot CLIP only achieves 31.5% Last accuracy, MG-CLIP reaches 94.67% Avg and 91.53% Last, outperforming the best replay-based CLAP by 1.86% in Last accuracy. On ImageNet-100, it slightly trails replay-based RAPF (80.23) and CLAP (79.16) in Last accuracy but outperforms all non-replay methods by at least 1.72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG-CLIP (non-replay) outperforms all 11 baselines on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-1K, and VTAB in Last accuracy. On CIFAR-100, it beats the strongest replay-based CLAP (78.21) by 2.36% and the best non-replay MagMax (79.00) by 1.57%. On ImageNet-R, it surpasses CLAP (79.98) by 2.69% and MagMax (80.85) by 1.82%. On VTAB, the gap is most dramatic: MG-CLIP achieves 91.53% Last vs. CLAP's 89.67% and MOE4CL's 61.70%, a 29.83% margin over the next-best non-replay method. On ImageNet-100, MG-CLIP (78.38) is the only non-replay method within 2% of the best replay-based RAPF (80.23), while the non-replay CLAP* drops to 72.00%, confirming that CLAP's gains largely depend on replay. The zero-shot evaluation (Table 2) further shows MG-CLIP preserves pre-trained knowledge better than replay-based methods, which suffer severe degradation (e.g., PROOF drops to 9.75% on Food101 vs. MG-CLIP's 85.70%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based continual learning paradigm (PROOF, CLAP, RAPF, MOE4CL, MagMax), which treats CLIP primarily as a feature extractor and focuses on cross-modal interaction or feature fusion, by instead centering on the modality gap as a structural property to be actively managed. It also inverts the prior modality-gap literature (Liang et al. 2022, Fahim et al. 2024), which views the gap as a defect to be minimized for better alignment, by arguing that in continual learning the gap encodes pre-trained knowledge and should be preserved. The technical machinery—LoRA fine-tuning, cosine classifiers, and adaptive epoch scheduling—is standard; the novelty lies in the diagnostic use of negative-pair cosine similarity as a stopping criterion and the complementary visual-space classifier that operates outside the text-feature subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a simple, replay-free recipe (adaptive early stopping + a small visual classifier) that achieves state-of-the-art class-incremental accuracy across five benchmarks while also preserving zero-shot generalization, a dimension most prior CLIP-CL methods neglect. The zero-shot evaluation protocol (Table 2) is a useful addition to the field, revealing that replay-based methods can actually degrade out-of-domain generalization more than non-replay approaches. Limitations include reliance on a single fine-tuning strategy (LoRA on attention K/V), a fixed hyperparameter β for classifier fusion, and no exploration of how the modality-gap perspective interacts with other regularization or distillation techniques—open questions the authors explicitly flag for future work.
