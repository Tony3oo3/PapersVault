## Problem

Current language-guided visual navigation models produce only actions without explicit perception or planning, making their decision-making opaque and limiting long-term navigation efficiency. Additionally, agents frequently revisit the same locations during extended exploration, accumulating redundant historical observations that degrade performance. RoboTron-Nav addresses both gaps by unifying perception, planning, and prediction through multitask collaboration and by adaptively filtering redundant historical observations in 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251039/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes the current RGB frame, a window of W=60 historically sampled frames (filtered by spatial adjacency threshold ε=0.1 and cosine similarity threshold τ=0.95), camera intrinsics/extrinsics, the agent's 3D position, and a natural language instruction. During training it outputs both a discrete navigation action (via an action head) and a free-form natural language answer (via an LLM head) for EQA queries; at inference only the navigation action is produced. Historical features are not fed raw but are max-pooled, position-encoded with sinusoidal x/y projections, and passed through a fully connected layer before entering the LLM.

## Method

RoboTron-Nav fuses a CLIP-pretrained ViT (2D patch features) and a UVFormer module (3D unified-view spatial tokens) into a visual encoder, whose outputs are concatenated with position-enhanced historical features and language tokens before being decoded by an LLM. The key architectural contribution is the Adaptive 3D-aware History Sampling algorithm, which iterates over past observations in reverse chronological order and discards any frame that is both spatially within ε of an already-selected frame and semantically above τ cosine similarity, thereby converting dense, disorganized trajectories into sparse, organized context. A second contribution is the multitask collaboration strategy: GPT-4o generates EQA pairs from navigation trajectories that explicitly model hierarchical reasoning (scene description → path planning → commonsense reasoning), and joint training on these EQA pairs alongside navigation data forces the shared LLM to internalize perception and planning skills that transfer to improved action prediction. Position-enhanced historical features encode the agent's x/y trajectory via axis-separated sinusoidal projections added to each historical token, preventing redundant re-exploration of previously visited locations.

## Results

On the CHORES-S ObjectNav benchmark (200 test trajectories across 200 houses), RoboTron-Nav achieves a success rate (SR) of 81.1%, setting a new state-of-the-art with a 9% absolute improvement over the previous best method (RING, 72.1%). It also visits the highest percentage of rooms (67.8%), indicating thorough exploration. However, its episode-length weighted success (SEL) of 43.9% trails RING (53.0%) and SPOC (46.2%), suggesting that while RoboTron-Nav finds targets more often, its paths are somewhat less efficient in length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTron-Nav outperforms all five baselines on success rate, with the largest margins over PIRLNav (81.1% vs. 20.0%) and JSRL (81.1% vs. 21.0%), which rely on RL fine-tuning with sparse rewards. Against the strongest prior method RING (72.1% SR), RoboTron-Nav gains 9 percentage points in SR and visits 67.8% of rooms versus RING's unreported figure. On path efficiency (SEL), RoboTron-Nav (43.9%) is surpassed by RING (53.0%) and SPOC (46.2%), indicating that the multitask EQA training and history sampling improve target-finding reliability at a modest cost in path length. SPOC* (60.0% SR) shows that scaling expert trajectories helps but still falls 21 points short of RoboTron-Nav. EmbSigLIP (36.5% SR) and the RL-based methods confirm that simple CLIP-embedding or sparse-reward approaches struggle substantially in this long-horizon, multi-room setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTron-Nav builds directly on RoboUniView's UVFormer module for 3D spatial encoding and extends the VLM-based navigation paradigm (EmbSigLIP, SPOC, RING) by introducing a multitask collaboration strategy that jointly trains on navigation and embodied question answering. Unlike prior ObjectNav methods that map sensory inputs directly to actions, this paper explicitly models the hierarchical reasoning process (scene perception → path planning → action prediction) through GPT-4o-generated EQA pairs, departing from the trajectory-mimicking approach of SPOC and the generalist-policy approach of RING. The adaptive 3D-aware history sampling strategy is a novel contribution that addresses observation redundancy in long-term navigation, a problem acknowledged but not systematically solved by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9% absolute SR improvement over RING on CHORES-S is meaningful, and the multitask collaboration technique (using EQA to sharpen navigation) is a practical recipe likely to be adopted by other embodied navigation systems. However, the evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (CHORES-S), the EQA training data is entirely GPT-4o-generated (raising questions about quality and bias), and the SEL metric is actually lower than RING's, suggesting the agent explores more broadly at the cost of path efficiency. Open questions include whether the EQA benefit generalizes to other environments and object categories, whether the approach transfers to real-world deployment, and whether the 3D-aware sampling strategy scales to much longer trajectories or more complex scenes.
