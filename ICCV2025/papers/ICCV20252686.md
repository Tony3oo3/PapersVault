## Problem

Learning-based color constancy methods are trained on specific camera raw color spaces and fail to generalize to unseen cameras without retraining or fine-tuning. Prior cross-camera approaches like C5 require additional unlabeled images from the test camera at inference time, making performance dependent on those images' characteristics. No prior work leverages the pre-calibrated color correction matrices (CCMs) already embedded in camera ISPs to enable zero-shot cross-camera adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single raw image (converted to a 64×64 uv-histogram) and two 3×3 pre-calibrated CCMs (for low and high CCT, typically 2500K and 6500K) extracted from the camera's ISP firmware or DNG file. No additional images from the test camera are required. The output is a single illuminant RGB color vector, estimated as a weighted sum over the uv-histogram probability map and inverted from log-chroma coordinates.

## Method

CCMNet builds on the CCC hypernetwork framework, replacing C5's additional-image requirement with a Camera Fingerprint Embedding (CFE). The CFE is generated by mapping 51 predefined illuminants along the Planckian locus (2500K–7500K) from CIE XYZ into the test camera's raw RGB space via linear interpolation of the two CCMs, converting the resulting trajectory into a 64×64 uv-histogram, and encoding it with a lightweight CNN (4 conv layers + 2-layer MLP) into an 8-dimensional vector. This CFE is concatenated with the query image's uv-histograms and fed to a hypernetwork that generates camera-adaptive CCC filters and bias. A key training-time innovation is imaginary camera augmentation: two training cameras' spectral sensitivities and CCMs are linearly interpolated with a random ratio α to synthesize novel raw images and corresponding CCMs, expanding the diversity of camera color spaces seen during training without requiring additional physical cameras.

## Results

CCMNet achieves the lowest mean angular error across all three benchmark datasets: 2.23° on Gehler-Shi, 1.68° on Cube+, and 2.32° on NUS-8, outperforming the strongest prior method C5 (2.36°, 1.87°, 2.68° respectively). Under the cross-sensor (CS) validation protocol on NUS-8, CCMNet further improves to 1.71° mean angular error, beating C5(m=9, CS) at 1.77°. The model is also notably compact at 1.05 MB (excluding the CFE encoder), roughly half the size of C5(m=9) at 2.09 MB, making it well-suited for ISP integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCMNet outperforms all 14 baselines on mean angular error across every dataset. Against the closest competitor C5 [6], CCMNet improves mean error by 0.13° on Gehler-Shi (2.23 vs. 2.36), 0.19° on Cube+ (1.68 vs. 1.87), and 0.36° on NUS-8 (2.32 vs. 2.68), while also halving model size (1.05 MB vs. 2.09 MB for C5 m=9). Compared to SIIE [3], CCMNet reduces mean error by 0.54° on Gehler-Shi and 0.46° on Cube+, and is roughly 10× smaller (1.05 MB vs. 10.3 MB). Against FFCC [13], CCMNet achieves 0.72° lower mean error on Gehler-Shi and 1.01° lower on NUS-8. In the cross-sensor protocol, CCMNet (1.71°) edges out C5(m=9, CS) (1.77°) and substantially outperforms SIIE(CS) (2.05°) and DMCC(CS) (2.80°). Statistical methods (Gray-world, Gray-Edge, Shades-of-Gray, etc.) are consistently 1–3° worse in mean error, confirming the advantage of learned approaches for cross-camera generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCMNet extends the CCC/FFCC uv-histogram framework (Barron 2015, 2017) and the C5 hypernetwork (Afifi et al. 2021) that dynamically generates filters and biases from additional test-camera images. The key departure is replacing C5's reliance on 6–8 unlabeled images from the target camera with a static Camera Fingerprint Embedding (CFE) derived from two pre-calibrated CCMs already present in ISP firmware, eliminating the need for any additional test-time data. The imaginary camera augmentation further departs from prior raw-to-raw transfer approaches by exploiting CCM linearity to synthesize virtual sensor responses, expanding training diversity beyond the limited set of physical cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By leveraging CCMs that are already calibrated and embedded in every consumer camera ISP, CCMNet offers a practical path to true zero-shot cross-camera white balance without per-camera retraining or additional image capture—addressing a major deployment bottleneck in manufacturing. The 1.05 MB footprint (roughly half of C5) makes ISP integration feasible. A noted limitation is that some smartphones ship only a single fixed raw-to-sRGB matrix rather than proper CIE XYZ CCMs, which would require an additional conversion step. Open questions include whether the approach extends to nonlinear color correction, multi-illuminant scenes, or cameras with more complex spectral sensitivities beyond what a 3×3 CCM can capture.
