## Problem

Feedforward 3D scene-level generation remains largely unexplored because no existing VAE can encode the large number of unstructured 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives in a scene into a compact, structured latent space. Scene-level 3DGS representations are unbounded, exhibit severe scale inconsistency across scenes due to SfM initialization, and contain noise artifacts from insufficient multi-view observations, making unified latent representation learning extremely challenging. The paper demonstrates that all existing 3D VAEs (PointNet, L3DG, PointTransformer) fail to converge on even a few hundred scenes and show zero generalization to unseen inputs.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a fixed-size set of N=40K 3D Gaussian primitives per scene (after semantic-aware filtering and normalization to a bounded sphere), where each primitive carries position, rotation quaternion, opacity, scaling, RGB color, and spherical-harmonic coefficients, all augmented with Fourier positional encodings (L_B=51) and nearest-voxel anchor coordinates from a V=40 resolution grid. The output is a 64×64×4 latent embedding (matching Stable Diffusion's latent size) that can be decoded back into full 3DGS parameters in continuous 3D space. For downstream generation, the latent is conditioned on BLIP-generated text captions or RGB images via a diffusion UNet, producing a denoised latent that is decoded into a novel 3DGS scene.

## Method

Can3Tok is a transformer-based VAE whose encoder compresses N input Gaussians into M=256 canonical tokens via a single cross-attention layer, where the learnable query is initialized with positions and descriptors of a regular voxel grid in canonical space, injecting a structured geometry prior that guides representation learning on irregular data. The compressed tokens pass through 8 self-attention blocks before being projected into mean and log-variance for the reparameterization trick. The decoder uses 16 self-attention blocks followed by linear layers with non-linear activations to map the latent back into continuous 3D space (no predefined output query, unlike Perceiver IO). A critical data-processing pipeline addresses scale inconsistency by mean-shifting all Gaussians to the origin and rescaling positions and per-Gaussian scaling factors into a bounded sphere, and improves quality via LangSam-guided semantic segmentation with K-NN propagation to select the cleanest 40K primitives. Training minimizes L2 reconstruction loss across all 3DGS feature channels plus a KL divergence term (λ=1e-6), with SO(3) rotation augmentation for invariance.

## Results

On the DL3DV-10K test set, Can3Tok achieves an L2 error of 30.1 and a failure rate of 2.5%, dramatically outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, PointTransformer, yields an L2 error of 230.7 (7.7× higher) and a 70% failure rate, while L3DG and PointNet VAE both fail completely (100% failure rate) with L2 errors of 1200.4 and 1823.0 respectively. The failure rate is defined as the percentage of test scenes whose reconstruction L2 error exceeds 1000.0, indicating complete loss of scene structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Can3Tok decisively outperforms all three baselines on both metrics. Against L3DG (a 3D-convolutional VAE for object-level 3DGS), Can3Tok reduces L2 error from 1200.4 to 30.1 (40× improvement) and eliminates the 100% failure rate, dropping it to 2.5%. Against PointNet VAE, the improvement is even more stark: L2 error drops from 1823.0 to 30.1 (60× improvement) with failure rate going from 100% to 2.5%. The strongest baseline, PointTransformer, still trails Can3Tok by 7.7× in L2 error (230.7 vs. 30.1) and has a 70% failure rate versus Can3Tok's 2.5%. The paper notes that PointNet and L3DG cannot even converge on more than 500 training scenes, while PointTransformer converges but produces stretched and distorted reconstructions on all test samples. This suggests that existing 3D VAE architectures are fundamentally incompatible with the unstructured, scale-inconsistent nature of scene-level 3DGS data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Can3Tok adapts the Perceiver IO cross-attention mechanism to compress unstructured 3DGS primitives into a fixed set of canonical tokens, but departs from it by removing the pre-defined output query in the decoder to support continuous 3D reconstruction rather than discrete prediction. It positions itself against a lineage of object-level 3D VAEs (PointNet, L3DG, PointTransformer) that the authors demonstrate are fundamentally incompatible with scene-level 3DGS due to unbounded scale and unstructured geometry. The broader framing follows the Stable Diffusion paradigm of learning a shared latent space to enable conditional generation, but extends it from 2D images to scene-level 3D Gaussian representations—a gap the authors identify as the key bottleneck preventing feedforward 3D scene generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a unified, low-dimensional latent space can be learned over thousands of scene-level 3DGS representations, removing the per-scene optimization bottleneck that has limited prior scene generation work (WonderJourney, LucidDreamer). The finding that scale normalization is as critical as model architecture—without it, even Can3Tok fails completely—provides a practical recipe for anyone attempting large-scale 3DGS generative modeling. Limitations include a 2.5% failure rate tied to low-quality 3DGS reconstructions, restriction to the 3DGS representation, and evaluation on a single dataset (DL3DV-10K); open questions include whether the latent space supports truly unbounded or multi-scene generation and whether the preliminary text/image-to-3DGS demos can be scaled to competitive quality.
