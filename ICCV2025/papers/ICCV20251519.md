## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting entangles geometry, material, and illumination during reconstruction, preventing independent editing of scene components. Existing GS-based inverse rendering methods fail under non-Lambertian conditions because they (1) cannot simultaneously model global ambient lighting and spatially localized high-frequency effects, (2) bake cast shadows into albedo estimates, and (3) lack sufficient priors to resolve the ill-posed light-material-geometry decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a scene along with its axis-aligned bounding box (default range [-3,3]³) to initialize a 3D grid of SGMs. Outputs are stored as G-Buffer maps (albedo, roughness, normals, per-splat shadow visibility vectors) plus explicit lighting parameters: per-SGM positions, emission directions, sharpness, amplitude, and RGB chromaticity weights, alongside a learnable HDR environment map. Relighting at inference is achieved purely by modifying light parameters and applying standard shadow maps, without retraining.

## Method

GS-ID formulates incident radiance as a sum of SGM-based local lighting and environment-map ambient lighting, rendered through a Cook-Torrance BRDF with a per-splat visibility modulation. Each SGM comprises 16 spherical Gaussians parameterized by direction, sharpness, amplitude, and RGB weights; SGMs are initialized on a 3×3×3 grid and adaptively aggregated and pruned (energy-aware thresholding) during optimization to capture irregular highlight regions. A deshadowing module assigns each splat a learnable unit vector encoding dominant shadow direction, alpha-blended into a screen-space shadow field that modulates per-light visibility via a sigmoid. To resolve decomposition ambiguity, pretrained RGB↔X diffusion priors supply pseudo-GT normals (stabilizing geometry) and albedo/roughness maps (guiding material estimation) as additional loss terms. The full pipeline uses deferred shading with custom CUDA kernels for efficient G-Buffer-aware rendering, training in two phases: 20k iterations for geometry with normal priors, then 10k iterations for joint light-material optimization.

## Results

On the TensoIR Synthetic dataset, GS-ID achieves the best performance across nearly all metrics: 39.13 dB PSNR for novel view synthesis (vs. 35.09 for TensoIR), 33.49 dB PSNR for albedo estimation, 0.007 roughness MSE, and 4.602° normal MAE. For relighting, GS-ID reaches 28.69 dB PSNR, outperforming the next-best GS-based method (GS-IR at 24.37 dB) by over 4 dB. Training completes in 40 minutes, an 87% reduction compared to TensoIR's 3.9 hours, while maintaining real-time 60 fps rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-ID outperforms all six baselines on albedo estimation (33.49 dB PSNR vs. 30.29 for GS-IR, 29.47 for RelightGS, 29.28 for TensoIR) and novel view synthesis (39.13 dB PSNR vs. 37.57 for RelightGS, 35.33 for GS-IR, 35.09 for TensoIR). The largest margin is in relighting, where GS-ID achieves 28.69 dB PSNR versus 24.37 for GS-IR (+4.32 dB) and 24.41 for RelightGS (+4.28 dB), demonstrating the benefit of the adaptive SGM lighting model. The only metric where GS-ID does not lead is normal MAE: TensoIR's spatially continuous MLP branch achieves 4.100° versus GS-ID's 4.602°, though GS-ID still beats all other methods. Among GS-based baselines, GS-ID dominates across every metric including roughness MSE (0.007 vs. 0.027 for GS-IR) and training time (40 min vs. 41 min for RelightGS). On the ADT dataset, GS-ID further extends its lead with 29.13 dB albedo PSNR (vs. 27.22 for RelightGS) and 40.41 dB NVS PSNR, confirming consistent gains on real-scanned objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-ID extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting inverse-rendering line (GS-IR, GSshader, RelightGS) by replacing their global environment-map-only lighting with a hybrid model that combines a learnable environment map for ambient light with spatially-varying spherical Gaussian mixtures (SGMs) for near-field, localized illumination — a repurposing of the SGM formulation from Wang et al. (2009) originally designed for dynamic reflectance. It departs from prior shadow-handling approaches (visibility MLPs in MII, ray tracing in RelightGS) by introducing per-splat learnable unit vectors that are alpha-blended into a screen-space shadow field, and it borrows the deferred-shading / G-Buffer paradigm from game engines (Unity, Unreal) to make per-primitive PBR evaluation tractable at 3DGS scale. The diffusion-model priors from RGB↔X [34] are used in a novel dual role: normal supervision for geometry reconstruction and albedo/roughness supervision for material disambiguation, going beyond the single-purpose normal priors in DN-Splatter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GS-ID makes real-time, user-controllable relighting of 3DGS scenes practical by decoupling geometry, material, and lighting into independently editable components, which is a prerequisite for downstream applications like scene composition and virtual production. The 4× training speedup from deferred shading and the 87% reduction in training time versus TensoIR lower the barrier to adopting inverse rendering on 3DGS. Open questions remain around the anisotropic-vs-isotropic representation mismatch the authors acknowledge, the reliance on a single diffusion model (RGB↔X) for both geometry and material priors, and whether the SGM count and grid resolution scale gracefully to larger, unbounded scenes beyond the object-centric and small-scene benchmarks used here.
