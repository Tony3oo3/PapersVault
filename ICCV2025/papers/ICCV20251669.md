## Problem

Existing FL poisoning defenses rely on predefined fixed metrics (e.g., cosine or Euclidean distance) to flag malicious models, making them vulnerable to adaptive adversaries who tailor attacks to evade those specific criteria. Additionally, the natural diversity of non-i.i.d. client datasets makes it difficult to distinguish genuinely unusual models from poisoned ones using rigid thresholds. No prior defense dynamically learns detection criteria directly from model representations while simultaneously handling both adaptive attackers and complex non-i.i.d. distributions.

## Input / Output

Sibai takes the flattened parameter vectors of the global model and a candidate local model as input, processing them through a layer-wise PCA pipeline (slicing into chunks of up to 20,000 parameters, retaining 200 principal components per slice) augmented with engineered cosine and Euclidean distances to the global model. The output is a per-client binary decision: a sigmoid score above 0.5 flags the local model as malicious and excludes it from FedAVG aggregation. Unlike offline backdoor detection methods requiring model inversion or trigger enumeration, Sibai operates directly on raw parameter vectors, enabling inspection of all clients in a federation without time-consuming per-model preprocessing.

## Method

Sibai employs a Siamese network meta-classifier consisting of twin 1D CNNs with shared weights that map the PCA-reduced global and local model representations into fixed-length embedding vectors, trained with triplet loss to maximize separation between benign and malicious model pairs. A fully connected decision network with sigmoid output classifies the L2 distance between the two embeddings, replacing a fixed threshold with a learned boundary. The system is trained offline on a shadow model dataset of 5,720 benign and 4,145 malicious models spanning five attack types and multiple non-i.i.d. degrees (i.i.d., 1-class non-i.i.d. at q=0.3/0.6/0.9, and inter-client non-i.i.d.), enabling few-shot generalization to unseen federations. The key distinction from prior defenses is that Sibai dynamically learns a diverse set of complex model characteristics rather than relying on a small set of handcrafted metrics, making it impractical for attackers to adapt without access to the Siamese network's internal representations.

## Results

Sibai achieves perfect F1 (1.0000) across all five poisoning attacks in the baseline i.i.d. scenario (PDR=0.5) and maintains F1 ≥ 0.8750 in the low-PDR i.i.d. scenario. In the most challenging inter-client non-i.i.d. setting, Sibai's worst F1 is 0.8750 (untargeted attack), still surpassing every baseline. Against adaptive adversaries using Euclidean-distance adaptation (α=0.3), Sibai retains F1 ≥ 0.9411 with backdoor accuracy (BA) below 6% for all attacks except edge-case (18.87%, unchanged from undefended). Across all 12 evaluation scenarios, Sibai prevents effective backdoor establishment (BA < 19%) while keeping main-task accuracy degradation (MA diff.) within ±7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sibai outperforms all six baselines in the majority of scenario–attack combinations. MESAS, the strongest baseline, collapses to F1 = 0.0000 on Pixel in the low-PDR i.i.d. scenario (①) and on Random in the 0.9 non-i.i.d. scenario (④), while Sibai maintains 1.0000 and 0.8235 respectively. Krum and M-Krum are consistently weak, capping at F1 ≈ 0.64 and 0.78 across all scenarios. HDBSCAN is highly inconsistent—achieving 1.0000 for Pixel/Blend/Edge in the adaptive scenario (⑪) but 0.0000 for Random in the same setting—whereas Sibai achieves 1.0000 for Random and Semantic there. FLTrust shows moderate performance (F1 0.57–0.89) but allows high BA (up to 98–100%) in non-i.i.d. scenarios, failing to prevent effective backdoors. In terms of backdoor prevention (Table 7), Sibai and MESAS are the only methods that keep BA at 0% in the 0.9 non-i.i.d. and inter-client non-i.i.d. scenarios, while Krum, FoolsGold, and Trimmed Mean/Median all permit BA of 100% for Pixel and Blend attacks in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sibai repurposes the Siamese network architecture from few-shot image recognition (Koch et al., 2015) into a meta-classifier for comparing DNN model parameters in federated learning, a setting where prior defenses (Krum, MESAS, FLTrust, FoolsGold, HDBSCAN) rely on fixed, hand-crafted metrics like cosine or Euclidean distance. It draws conceptual inspiration from offline meta-classifier backdoor detectors (MNTD, One-pixel Signature, Universal Litmus Patterns, DeepInspect) but adapts them for online FL inference by operating directly on PCA-reduced flattened model weights rather than requiring per-model trigger inversion or adversarial perturbation testing. The key departure from the FL defense literature is replacing rigid metric thresholds with a learned, dynamic decision boundary, positioning Sibai as the first meta-classifier-based detection-and-filtering defense for FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sibai addresses two persistent weaknesses in FL poisoning defenses—vulnerability to adaptive adversaries and poor performance under non-i.i.d. data—by learning discriminative criteria from a diverse shadow model set rather than relying on fixed metrics that attackers can target. The practical limitation is the requirement for a substantial offline shadow model dataset (520 simulated federations, ~10K models) and consistent local model architectures, which constrains deployment in heterogeneous federations. Evaluation is limited to SqueezeNet/CIFAR-10 and a small CNN/MNIST with 20-client federations, leaving open questions about scalability to larger, more diverse federations and whether the shadow model requirement can be reduced. The authors suggest future work combining Sibai's detection with targeted influence reduction for a layered defense.
