## Problem

Existing 3D scene modeling methods from single images either require expensive real-world annotations or train on monotonous synthetic data that fails to generalize to unseen objects and domains, and they typically ignore architectural elements or produce surface-only meshes incompatible with graphics pipelines. The paper asks whether holistic 3D scene modeling—including both architecture and cluttered object arrangements—can be achieved from a single real-world image using foundation models without any end-to-end training or human annotations.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image (synthetic, real-world, or text-generated via Flux-1) and produces a compositional 3D scene consisting of retrieved CAD objects with full 9-DoF poses (position, rotation, scale), planar architecture surfaces, and a scene graph encoding support hierarchies. Multiple plausible scene arrangement hypotheses are generated per input, each using different semantically-similar retrieved shapes. The output is a compact, editable scene representation rather than a dense mesh, making it directly usable in simulation and graphics pipelines.

## Method

Diorama decomposes the problem into two modular components. The open-world perception stage combines OWLv2 and SAM for open-vocabulary object detection and segmentation, Metric3DV2 for metric depth and normals, a novel PlainRecon pipeline (object inpainting → depth estimation → normal-based clustering → plane fitting) for planar architecture, and GPT-4o for generating a support-hierarchy scene graph. The CAD-based modeling stage performs hierarchical shape retrieval via DuoDuoCLIP (text query for category grouping, then image query for appearance re-ranking), zero-shot 9-DoF pose estimation using DINOv2 patch features to establish 2D-3D correspondences solved with Umeyama+RANSAC plus a GigaPose scale network, and a four-stage differentiable layout optimization (orientation alignment, placement on support surfaces, volume penetration avoidance, and collision refinement via SAT) that refines all object poses to enforce physical plausibility.

## Results

On the Stanford Scene Database (SSDB), Diorama achieves a scene-aware alignment accuracy (Acc) of 0.08 versus 0.04 for ACDC, with a Chamfer Distance of 9.5 vs. 14.0 for shape retrieval, and 85.1% user preference in a pairwise study. The zero-shot 9-DoF pose estimation module reaches 0.37 Acc and 0.47 rAcc, outperforming GigaPose (0.27 Acc, 0.36 rAcc) and ZSP (0.25 Acc, 0.36 rAcc). For planar architecture reconstruction, PlainRecon with Metric3DV2 depth achieves 58.6 IoU and 0.447 CDb, surpassing ACDC (46.8 IoU, 0.563 CDb) and RaC (45.2 IoU, 0.908 CDb). The system is 12× cheaper ($0.12 vs. $1.44 API cost) and 6× faster (3.7 vs. 23.2 min) than ACDC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ACDC [11], the only other modular zero-shot system, Diorama wins on every metric: scene-aware alignment accuracy doubles (0.08 vs. 0.04), Chamfer Distance improves by 32% (9.5 vs. 14.0), and user preference is 85.1% vs. 14.9%, while being 12× cheaper and 6× faster. In the pose estimation comparison (Table 4), Diorama's zero-shot 9-DoF method outperforms GigaPose [53] on overall alignment (0.37 vs. 0.27 Acc) and collision (6.42 vs. 7.91), and beats ZSP [23] on all alignment sub-metrics (rAcc 0.47 vs. 0.36, sAcc 0.70 vs. 0.59). For 3D shape retrieval (Table 3), the hierarchical DuoDuoCLIP strategy (DD-H) achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance across all object categories and both in-distribution (SSDB) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, e.g., 5.5 vs. 8.5 (CLIP-H) and 8.6 (OS-H) on Household-SS. In architecture reconstruction (Table 2), PlainRecon with Metric3DV2 depth outperforms both RaC [63] (58.6 vs. 45.2 IoU) and ACDC (58.6 vs. 46.8 IoU) on IoU, PE, EE, and CDb, though RaC achieves a slightly lower RMSE (1.23 vs. 1.37). On ScanNet, Diorama handles out-of-distribution objects (laptop, backpack) that both ROCA [24] and DiffCAD [21] cannot process due to their limited training categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diorama extends the CAD-retrieval-based scene modeling lineage (IM2CAD, Mask2CAD, ROCA, DiffCAD) by replacing every trained module with zero-shot foundation-model components: OWLv2+SAM for detection/segmentation, GPT-4o for scene graph generation, Metric3DV2 for depth/normals, DuoDuoCLIP for 3D shape retrieval, and DINOv2-based correspondence for 9-DoF pose. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it is entirely training-free, whereas all prior CAD-alignment methods require end-to-end or weakly-supervised training on annotated data, and (2) it explicitly models planar architecture (via PlainRecon) alongside objects, which most prior CAD-retrieval systems ignore. The differentiable scene layout optimization draws on DeepPanoContext-style relation-based refinement but adds a stage-wise, semantic-aware formulation with support-volume handling for nested objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fully zero-shot, modular pipeline of off-the-shelf foundation models can produce holistic, compositional 3D indoor scenes (architecture + objects + spatial relations) from a single RGB image without any task-specific training, opening a path to scalable scene creation for robotics, AR/VR, and simulation without manual 3D design. Key limitations remain: retrieval-based modeling sacrifices exact geometry and texture, RANSAC-based pose estimation is sensitive to occlusion and noisy depth, and the system lacks part-level articulation. Open questions include whether retrieved CAD models can be deformed and textured to close the appearance gap, whether learned affordance models can replace the current scene-graph-based support reasoning, and whether the pipeline can be made real-time for interactive applications.
