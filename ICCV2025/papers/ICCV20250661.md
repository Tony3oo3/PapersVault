## Problem

Visual grounding tasks (RIS, REC) rely on a single, often brief textual description to localize a target object, yet the same target can be described in many ways capturing different attributes (color, position, texture). This mismatch between rich visual details and sparse textual cues causes the model to misidentify similar objects when the given description is ambiguous or incomplete.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 480×480 RGB image and a single free-form text description as input, and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask for RIS or a tight-fitting bounding box (derived from the mask) for REC without a separate detection decoder. Internally, the single text is expanded into N=2 latent expressions of varying token lengths (k^i = {4, 6}), and the final mask is computed by averaging sigmoid similarity maps between the upsampled visual feature map and the class tokens of both the original text and all latent expressions. For GRES, a single empty token is added for binary no-target classification.

## Method

Built on a pre-trained BEiT-3 ViT-B encoder with shared multi-head self-attention jointly processing visual patches, text tokens, and N latent expressions across 12 layers, the framework introduces three components: (1) a latent attribute initializer that randomly drops input text tokens and applies length-transform layers to generate diverse initial expressions; (2) a subject distributor that replaces the subject token in every latent expression with a shared visual subject token at each layer to enforce consistent subject reference, combined with a visual concept injector that retrieves N_c=100 orthogonal concept tokens from target-related patches (selected by mean-similarity thresholding) and injects them into attribute tokens via a slot-attention-style normalized attention to bind distinct visual concepts to different attribute tokens; and (3) a positive-margin contrastive loss that aligns latent expression class tokens with the original text class token while capping the positive similarity at min(1, γ + s_i) to preserve representational diversity. The segmentation loss combines BCE and Dice terms on the averaged probability map, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with AdamW (batch 64, 4× A6000 GPUs).

## Results

On the combined RefCOCO(+/g) training setting for RIS, Latent-VG achieves 81.01 mIoU on RefCOCO val, 76.92 on RefCOCO+ val, and 76.10 on RefCOCOg val(U), outperforming the same-backbone One-Ref (79.83 / 74.68 / 74.06) by 1.2–2.2 mIoU. On the GRES benchmark (gRefCOCO), it reaches 72.45 mIoU and 70.42 N-acc on val, surpassing the best prior method HDC (68.23 mIoU, 63.38 N-acc) by over 4 mIoU. For REC in the combined setting, Latent-VG attains 94.64% on RefCOCO testA and 80.59% on RefCOCO+ testB, exceeding One-Ref (94.31% / 79.47%) without any task-specific detection decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against One-Ref [51], the closest competitor sharing the same BEiT3-B backbone, Latent-VG wins on every RIS split in the combined setting (e.g., +1.2 mIoU on RefCOCO val, +2.2 on RefCOCO+ val, +2.0 on RefCOCOg val(U)) and on REC testA/testB (94.64% vs 94.31%, 80.59% vs 79.47%), though it trails by 0.14% on RefCOCO val REC (91.75 vs 91.89). Versus dual-encoder methods like EEVG [4] and ReMamber [52], the gains are larger (2.5–7 mIoU on RefCOCO+), indicating the latent expression mechanism is particularly effective for the harder, more descriptive RefCOCO+ split. On GRES, Latent-VG outperforms all six compared methods (ReLA, EEVG, MABP, HDC, LISA, GSVA-7B) by 4.2 mIoU over the best (HDC) on gRefCOCO val, with only a minimal empty-token modification for no-target cases. For REC, Latent-VG surpasses LLM-based methods (Ferret-7B, LION-12B, GroundingDINO) that use substantially larger models and more training data, achieving 94.64% on RefCOCO testA with a 768-dim ViT-B backbone versus their 7B–12B parameter counts. The method also beats SAM-based approaches (Prompt-RIS, Chen et al.) by 2–5 mIoU on RefCOCO+ testB, where distinguishing similar objects is most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BEiT-3's shared self-attention architecture for joint vision-language encoding, extending it with a latent expression generation mechanism that departs from the single-textual-input paradigm dominant in prior RIS and REC work. Conceptually, it draws on latent-space text augmentation (SimCSE, E-Mix) but introduces a structured constraint—shared subject with distinct attributes—via the subject distributor and visual concept injector modules, which have no direct precedent in the visual grounding literature. The positive-margin contrastive loss modifies standard InfoNCE/Triplet objectives by allowing a controlled margin of divergence among positive samples, a design choice motivated by the need to preserve diversity rather than enforce strict alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental semantic gap in visual grounding: sparse textual descriptions cannot capture the full visual richness of a target, leading to misidentification among similar objects. By generating multiple complementary latent expressions, the framework effectively enriches the textual signal without requiring additional input, and does so with minimal overhead (~12M params, 3 GFLOPs). The notable GRES result—outperforming task-specific methods with only an empty-token addition—suggests the latent expression mechanism provides robustness beyond its original design scope. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video grounding or multi-object referring, and whether the benefits persist with larger backbones or more complex scenes where the number of confusable objects grows.
