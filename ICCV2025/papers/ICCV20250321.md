## Problem

Video-to-video moment retrieval (Vid2VidMR) requires localizing a semantically matching moment in a target video given a query video, but existing methods struggle with semantic frame-level alignment and modeling complex temporal dependencies between the two videos. Text-based queries often fail to capture nuanced actions (e.g., a bicycle kick), motivating video queries, yet prior video-query approaches lack explicit mechanisms to enforce temporal correspondence between query and target frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250321/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Both the target and query videos are uniformly sampled at 2-second intervals into M and N frames respectively, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder and projected to 1024-dimensional embeddings via a two-layer MLP. The model outputs per-frame foreground/background probabilities (via sigmoid) and left/right boundary offsets at every temporal position, from which a single moment interval is extracted using 1-D non-maximum suppression (threshold 0.7) applied to the highest-scoring foreground position.

## Method

MATR is a transformer encoder-decoder architecture that conditions target video representations on query features through explicit dual-stage sequence alignment using differentiable soft-DTW. Pre-fusion alignment is computed on raw CLIP embeddings before the encoder, while post-fusion alignment operates on the encoder's fused target-query representations; the post-fusion alignment matrix selects a contiguous sub-sequence of target features that are fed into a decoder with 10 learnable queries and cross-attention for fine-grained refinement. The final query-aligned representation concatenates encoder and decoder outputs, which are passed through convolutional prediction heads for foreground classification (BCE loss) and boundary offset regression (smooth L1 + generalized IoU loss). A self-supervised pre-training strategy randomly samples clips from unlabeled videos and trains the model to localize them, with augmentations (frame reversal, Gaussian noise, speed changes) to improve generalization.

## Results

On ActivityNet-VRL, MATR (finetuned with video query) achieves 56.8 mIoU and 57.1 R@1, outperforming the best fully-supervised Vid2VidMR method (FFI+SRM) by 8.1% in mIoU and the best recall method (GDP, 44.0 R@1) by 13.1%. On the newly introduced SportsMoments dataset, MATR reaches 59.2 mIoU and 56.5 R@1, surpassing the strongest baseline (UniVTG finetuned+V) by 15.6% in mIoU and 14.7% in R@1. Even in zero-shot mode, MATR achieves 32.6 mIoU and 30.1 R@1 on ActivityNet-VRL, already competitive with several fully-supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fully-supervised Vid2VidMR methods, MATR (finetuned+V) achieves 56.8 mIoU and 57.1 R@1 on ActivityNet-VRL, surpassing the best mIoU method (FFI+SRM, ~48.7) by 8.1% and the best R@1 method (GDP, 44.0) by 13.1%. Among VLM baselines, the strongest is TimeChat (26.4 mIoU, 23.8 R@1), which MATR outperforms by over 30 points in mIoU, demonstrating that task-specific architectures substantially outperform general-purpose video-language models for moment retrieval. Among Text-VMR methods adapted for video queries, UniVTG (finetuned+V) is the strongest baseline at 49.1 mIoU and 50.7 R@1 on ActivityNet-VRL; MATR exceeds it by 7.7% mIoU and 6.4% R@1, with an even larger gap on SportsMoments (15.6% mIoU, 14.7% R@1). For image-based VMR, the best method UniVTG reaches only 38.8 mIoU and 42.0 R@1, confirming that temporal dynamics in video queries are critical. On SportsMoments, MATR's 59.2 mIoU and 56.5 R@1 represent a 14.4% and 14.7% absolute gain over the strongest implemented baselines, validating the dual-stage alignment and self-supervised pre-training design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATR positions itself within the video-to-video moment retrieval (Vid2VidMR) task formally introduced by Feng et al. (ECCV 2018), but departs from prior video-query methods (GDP, SRL, FFI+SRM) by introducing an explicit dual-stage soft-DTW alignment within a transformer encoder-decoder framework, rather than relying on feature fusion or hierarchical learning alone. It also repurposes text-VMR architectures (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, UniVTG, CG-DETR) as baselines by swapping their text encoders for CLIP vision encoders, bridging the text-query and video-query literatures. The self-supervised pre-training objective—localizing randomly sampled clips within the same video—is a novel initialization strategy not seen in prior Vid2VidMR work, drawing inspiration from self-supervised video representation learning but applied specifically to temporal localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: users often cannot articulate complex visual moments (e.g., a bicycle kick) in text, making video-as-query a more natural interface. The introduction of SportsMoments, a fine-grained sports dataset with ~770K query-target pairs from full match footage, fills a notable void since existing sports datasets (Sports-1M) target coarse classification rather than moment-level retrieval. Open questions include whether the dual-stage alignment generalizes to longer videos or more diverse domains, whether multimodal queries (text + video) could further improve retrieval, and whether the self-supervised pre-training strategy transfers effectively to other temporal grounding tasks. The paper's reliance on CLIP ViT-B/32 as a frozen backbone also raises questions about whether task-specific video encoders could yield further gains.
