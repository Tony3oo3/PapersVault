## Problem

Realistically inserting a person from a reference image into a background scene requires simultaneously solving affordance-aware placement (where and how the person should pose) and identity-preserving personalization (maintaining facial features, clothing, and body shape). Prior methods treat these as separate sub-problems and rely on task-specific training or per-scene test-time optimization, leaving no unified training-free solution that handles both challenges jointly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252627/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference person image (ideally full-body, resized so the figure is roughly consistent in scale), a background scene image, and two text prompts: one describing the scene with the human inserted (e.g., 'a person running on the curved road') and one describing the subject's appearance. BLIP-2 auto-generates these prompts, and the subject description replaces the generic 'a person' in the scene prompt to strengthen identity transfer. The output is a single composite RGB image at SDXL resolution, generated in under one minute on a single V100 GPU with 32 GB memory using a DDIM sampler with 50 steps.

## Method

Teleportraits is a three-step training-free pipeline built on SDXL. First, ReNoise-Inversion (two fixed-point iterations) recovers initial latent noise for both the scene and reference images, enabling the model to start generation from a trajectory that reconstructs the input. Second, classifier-free guidance (weight 7.5) applied to the scene's inverted latent steers the model to generate a human at a semantically plausible location and pose per the text prompt, while latent blending with SAM-derived foreground/background masks during later denoising steps (t ∈ [10, 20]) preserves background fidelity. Third, mask-guided self-attention extracts key and value patches from the reference image's forward diffusion pass, masks them with a subject segmentation, and concatenates them with the generation's keys and values in the Up-2, Up-3, and Up-4 self-attention layers (applied only to the conditional CFG branch), transferring fine-grained visual identity without any per-subject optimization.

## Results

On the Text2Place benchmark (25 celebrities × 26 scenes = 650 images), Teleportraits achieves the best text-prompt alignment (CLIP-T = 0.287), the best background preservation (LPIPS = 0.025), and the highest VLM scores for text alignment (4.93/10) and background fidelity (6.29/10). Person insertion success rate is 97.4%, and identity preservation scores are CLIP-I = 0.596 and DINO = 0.244. The method generates each image in under 1 minute on a single V100 GPU, compared to up to 1 hour for Text2Place's per-subject optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Teleportraits outperforms Text2Place on every metric: higher CLIP-T (0.287 vs. 0.267), higher person insertion rate (97.4% vs. 84.2%), substantially lower LPIPS (0.025 vs. 0.094), and better VLM scores across subject identity, text alignment, and background preservation. When Teleportraits' predicted mask is fed into Text2Place's inpainting pipeline (Mask+Text2Place), the insertion success rate jumps to 91.8%, confirming that Teleportraits' affordance-aware placement is the bottleneck in Text2Place. Against AnyDoor, Teleportraits wins decisively on text alignment (VLM-T: 4.93 vs. 1.82) and background preservation (VLM-BG: 6.29 vs. 5.58), but AnyDoor scores higher on raw identity similarity (CLIP-I: 0.681 vs. 0.596; DINO: 0.447 vs. 0.244) because it essentially copy-pastes the reference person without adjusting pose or angle, inflating pixel-level similarity at the cost of semantic plausibility. In the human study (51 raters, 36 samples), Teleportraits ranks first on subject identity, text alignment, and scene consistency, confirming that the automated identity gap with AnyDoor does not translate to perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Teleportraits unifies two previously separate sub-problems—affordance-aware human placement and subject personalization—into a single training-free pipeline built on SDXL. For placement, it departs from Text2Place's Score Distillation Sampling optimization and Kulal et al.'s trained inpainting by showing that classifier-free guidance on an inverted scene latent already produces semantically correct human positions, requiring no per-scene tuning. For personalization, it adapts the extended self-attention mechanism from Consistory [45] but restricts it to the conditional CFG branch only and adds a mask-guided gating via SAM, eliminating Consistory's need for a separate DIFT feature-injection step. The inversion step follows ReNoise-Inversion [13], and background fidelity is maintained through latent blending from Blended Latent Diffusion [2].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that large-scale diffusion models already encode scene-affordance knowledge sufficient for plausible human placement, the paper suggests a broader principle: many 'editing' tasks may be solvable training-free by steering existing model representations rather than fine-tuning. The practical speedup (<1 min vs. up to 1 hour for Text2Place) makes personalized human insertion viable for interactive applications like e-commerce and avatar creation. Limitations include evaluation on only 25 celebrities × 26 scenes, reliance on BLIP-2 for prompt generation and SAM for segmentation (both potential failure points), and no exploration of multi-person insertion or video. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-human subjects, more complex multi-agent interactions, and whether the mask-guided self-attention can be extended to preserve fine-grained attributes (e.g., specific accessories) without additional conditioning.
