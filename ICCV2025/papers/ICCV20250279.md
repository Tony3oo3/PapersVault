## Problem

Most remote sensing object detection methods are closed-set, optimizing for fixed categories on specific datasets and failing to generalize across diverse RS scenarios. Existing open-vocabulary approaches for RS images are further limited by small-scale training data and do not address the unique demands of RS interpretation, including oriented bounding box regression, small-object detection, and the simultaneous need for high precision and real-time inference speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250279/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RS images (clipped to 800×800 or 1024×1024 patches depending on dataset) along with either text prompts (10–15 GPT-4-generated descriptions per category) or image prompts (224×224 crops from enlarged ground-truth boxes, selected via a DINOv2-based classifier for top-100 confidence per category). During inference, 3–7 prompt embeddings are randomly sampled per category, and the model simultaneously outputs both oriented and horizontal bounding boxes with category assignments, achieving 20.8 FPS on a single 2080Ti GPU.

## Method

OpenRSD builds on the RTMDet-L backbone and introduces a prompt construction module that encodes text via SkyCLIP and images via DINOv2 into a shared 256-dimensional embedding space. Two parallel detection heads operate on these embeddings: an alignment head that performs contrastive classification via normalized inner product (fast, scalable to large vocabularies) and a fusion head that applies three layers of bidirectional cross-attention with learnable class embeddings to enable deeper prompt–feature interaction (higher precision). A multi-stage training pipeline first pretrains the detection modules on labeled and unlabeled RS data (using SAM proposals and DINOv2 cluster centroids as pseudo-prompts), then fine-tunes on ten heterogeneous datasets with mixed prompts, and finally performs self-training where the fine-tuned model generates pseudo-labels filtered by SkyCLIP cosine similarity (>0.24) to expand coverage across 200 categories in the 474k-image ORSD+ dataset.

## Results

On oriented bounding box (OBB) detection across seven datasets, OpenRSD with text prompts achieves 69.3 average AP50, outperforming the best closed-set baseline MTP (66.8) and the RTMDet-R-L backbone (66.2) while running at 20.8 FPS. For horizontal bounding box (HBB) detection, OpenRSD (text prompt) reaches 71.4 average AP50, surpassing Grounding DINO-T (69.3) by 2.1% and YOLO-World-L (62.7) by 8.7%, while being 4× faster than Grounding DINO-T (20.8 vs. 5.4 FPS). On the fine-grained HRSC2016 ship detection benchmark, OpenRSD achieves 88.1 AP50, a 9.0-point gain over Oriented R-CNN with Swin-T (79.1). In cross-dataset generalization (Table 3), OpenRSD scores 78.1 average AP50 on four held-out datasets, exceeding RTMDet-R-L (75.9) by 2.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For OBB detection, OpenRSD (69.3 avg AP50) outperforms all closed-set baselines: MTP (66.8, ViT-B backbone), RTMDet-R-L (66.2), and Oriented R-CNN with Swin-T (65.3), while also beating the open-vocabulary CastDet* (61.9) and PKINet* (50.6) that share the same ORSD+ training data. The largest single-dataset gain is on HRSC2016 (88.1 vs. 79.1 for Oriented R-CNN Swin-T), indicating strong fine-grained ship detection. For HBB detection, OpenRSD (71.4) surpasses Grounding DINO-T (69.3) by 2.1% average AP50 while running 4× faster (20.8 vs. 5.4 FPS), and beats YOLO-World-L (62.7) by 8.7% average AP50 at comparable speed. In cross-dataset generalization on four held-out benchmarks (VEDAI, CORS, DOSR, SODA-A), OpenRSD (78.1) leads RTMDet-R-L (75.9) and S²ANet (73.2), with a notable 5.2-point advantage on the small-object SODA-A dataset. The only dataset where OpenRSD trails a baseline is FAIR1M-2.0 OBB, where RTMDet-R-L (47.6) slightly edges OpenRSD text prompt (46.1), though the gap narrows with the fusion head.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenRSD builds directly on the RTMDet real-time detection framework, extending it from closed-set to open-prompt detection by integrating prompt-based classification. Conceptually, it unifies two previously separate architectural paradigms in open-vocabulary detection—alignment-based heads (à la DetCLIP, OWL-ViT) and cross-modal fusion heads (à la GLIP, Grounding DINO)—into a single dual-head design, rather than committing to one approach. It departs from prior RS-specific OVD work (CastDet, OVA-DETR, LAE-DINO) by simultaneously addressing oriented bounding boxes, multi-modal prompts (text and image), and real-time inference, and by scaling training data to 470k images across 200 categories via a multi-stage pipeline that prior RS OVD methods did not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-prompt RS detector that supports both oriented and horizontal boxes at real-time speed (20.8 FPS), making it practically deployable for large-scale RS image analysis where Grounding DINO (5.4 FPS) is infeasible. The ORSD+ dataset (474k images, 200 categories) and the multi-stage training recipe provide a reusable foundation for the RS community. However, the approach remains bounded by the 200-category vocabulary and depends on SkyCLIP/DINOv2 for prompt extraction, leaving truly open-world detection (arbitrary novel categories) unresolved. Open questions include whether the self-training loop can be iterated to expand the vocabulary, and whether the dual-head design generalizes to other sensor modalities (SAR, multispectral).
