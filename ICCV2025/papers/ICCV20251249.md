## Problem

Existing COLMAP-free 3D Gaussian Splatting methods, particularly CF-3DGS, rely on RGB-space photometric regularization to estimate relative camera poses between adjacent frames. This approach degrades significantly under complex camera trajectories with drastic rotation and translation, where limited overlap between views causes the joint optimization of poses and Gaussians to converge to local minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251249/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of unposed RGB images along with the camera intrinsic matrix K. For each frame, it additionally extracts DINO semantic features (from the 9th transformer layer) and a DPT mono-depth map used to initialize the 3D Gaussian point cloud. The output is a fully optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting scene with estimated camera poses for all frames, from which novel views can be rendered in real time. Training follows an incremental strategy: Gaussians are first fit to a single frame, then relative poses are estimated frame-by-frame using the co-regularization losses.

## Method

PCR-GS introduces two complementary pose co-regularization mechanisms on top of the CF-3DGS incremental pipeline. First, feature reprojection regularization leverages DINO's viewpoint-robust semantic features: 2D pixels from frame i are back-projected into 3D using the rendered depth map, transformed by the relative pose T_i into frame i+1's coordinate system, and re-projected to 2D; the L2 discrepancy between the reprojected DINO features and the target frame's DINO features is minimized to regularize the relative pose. Sparse correspondences found via the Best Buddies algorithm on DINO features provide a robust initialization for T_i. Second, wavelet-based frequency regularization decomposes both the rendered and ground-truth images into LL, LH, HL, and HH frequency bands, exploiting the fact that small rotational errors manifest as high-frequency structural shifts that RGB-space loss is insensitive to. An annealing schedule progressively shifts the frequency loss weight from low-frequency (LL) to high-frequency components over iterations 100–200 to avoid noise. The total objective combines RGB photometric loss (weight 0.6), feature reprojection loss (0.2), and wavelet frequency loss (0.2).

## Results

On Tanks&Temples (8 scenes, 4 fps sampling for complex trajectories), PCR-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 23.68 dB, SSIM of 0.73, and LPIPS of 0.23, outperforming the closest baseline CF-3DGS (19.79 / 0.60 / 0.33) by ~4 dB in PSNR. For pose estimation, PCR-GS reduces mean RPE_t to 0.109 and ATE to 0.008, roughly halving CF-3DGS's RPE_t (0.211) and ATE (0.013), while NeRF-based baselines (BARF, NeRFmm, Nope-NeRF) are 30–75× worse in RPE_t. On the Free-Dataset (3 large-scale scenes), PCR-GS improves mean PSNR from 15.00 (CF-3DGS) to 17.78 and reduces mean RPE_r from 4.673 to 1.748.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCR-GS is compared against four COLMAP-free baselines: CF-3DGS (the closest 3D-GS baseline), Nope-NeRF, BARF, and NeRFmm (all NeRF-based). PCR-GS wins on every scene and every metric across both datasets. Against CF-3DGS, the gains are most pronounced on scenes with large camera motion (e.g., Family: +7.4 dB PSNR; Ballroom: +2.7 dB PSNR), while on the smoother Church scene the margin is smaller (+0.2 dB), confirming the method's advantage scales with trajectory complexity. Against the NeRF-based baselines, PCR-GS leads by 1.7–9.6 dB in PSNR and by 30–75× in RPE_t, indicating that the 3D-GS representation combined with pose co-regularization is substantially more robust than implicit NeRF approaches under unposed, high-motion settings. On the Free-Dataset (large-scale, no central object), PCR-GS improves PSNR by 2.78 dB over CF-3DGS and reduces RPE_r by 63%, further validating generalization beyond the Tanks&Temples benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCR-GS extends the CF-3DGS pipeline (incremental single-frame 3D-GS initialization with relative pose estimation between adjacent frames) by replacing its sole reliance on RGB photometric loss with two complementary regularizers: DINO-based feature reprojection for semantic alignment and wavelet-based frequency-space loss for rotation refinement. It also draws on the broader pose-free neural fields literature (NeRFmm, BARF, Nope-NeRF) but is the first to apply such co-regularization strategies specifically within the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework. The conceptual departure from CF-3DGS is the argument that RGB-space regularization is insufficient under drastic inter-frame camera motion, motivating the use of view-robust semantic features and multi-scale frequency decomposition as more stable pose constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D-GS deployment: COLMAP's computational cost and failure on sparse or repetitive textures, while specifically targeting the underexplored regime of complex camera trajectories where prior pose-free methods degrade. The ablation showing that DINO feature reprojection alone yields a ~5 dB PSNR jump over the CF-3DGS baseline suggests that semantic feature alignment is a substantially underutilized signal for pose estimation in this setting. Limitations include dependence on pre-trained DINO and DPT models, testing on only 11 scenes, and the somewhat self-serving choice to re-sample Tanks&Temples at 4 fps (vs. CF-3DGS's 20 fps) to create harder trajectories, which inflates the apparent gap over the baseline. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, robustness to scene types where DINO features may be less discriminative, and whether the wavelet annealing schedule generalizes beyond the tested iteration ranges.
