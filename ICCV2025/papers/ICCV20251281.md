## Problem

Existing text-to-image models rely on natural language prompts to express occlusion (e.g., 'a dog behind a cat'), which is imprecise and fails in multi-object scenes, while layout-to-image methods control object positions but do not explicitly encode occlusion relationships. No prior work provides a training-free mechanism to precisely control which objects occlude which in generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251281/cropped_page0_idx11.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an occlusion graph (a directed graph specifying which objects occlude which), per-object bounding boxes (user-specified or auto-parsed by an LLM such as DeepSeek R1), and independent per-object text prompts, plus a single scalar semantic opacity α ∈ [0,1) per object. It outputs a standard RGB image from a pre-trained diffusion model (SDXL or FLUX) in which the occlusion ordering is physically enforced. Bounding boxes serve as rough positional hints rather than precise layout constraints, and the background can be specified with a blank prompt.

## Method

LaRender replaces every cross-attention layer in a pre-trained diffusion model with a Latent Rendering layer that adapts NeRF's discretized volume rendering integral to the latent feature space. Given an occlusion graph, objects are topologically sorted back-to-front; each cross-attention layer produces per-object latent features by attending to individual object prompts, which are then stacked as layers under a virtual orthographic camera. Transmittance maps are estimated by element-wise multiplying bounding box masks with normalized cross-attention maps of subject tokens (extracted via dependency parsing), and the Latent Rendering formula accumulates transmittance across layers to fuse latent features according to physical visibility rules. A density scheduling function σ_i(t) = D_i·T/(T+1−t) starts in an 'opaque mode' during early denoising steps (preventing concept destruction) and decays to the target semantic density D_i by the final step. The entire mechanism introduces zero learnable parameters, making it fully training-free and compatible with any pre-trained diffusion backbone.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++ (3D Spatial), LaRender achieves the best UniDet score of 0.416, outperforming the strongest baseline FLUX (0.401) and SDXL (0.357). In user studies, LaRender dominates with AUR of 4.94±0.11 and HPSR of 0.767±0.070 on T2I-CompBench++, and AUR of 4.81±0.29 and HPSR of 0.767±0.094 on RealOcc, substantially exceeding all baselines. CLIP score remains competitive at 30.98 (vs. SDXL's 31.12), and inference time is nearly identical to the base model (7.46s vs. 7.44s for SDXL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRender outperforms all four baselines on every occlusion-related metric across both datasets. Against text-to-image baselines, LaRender surpasses FLUX (UniDet 0.416 vs. 0.401) and SDXL (0.416 vs. 0.357) on the UniDet metric, and leads by a wide margin in user studies (HPSR 0.767 vs. 0.293 for FLUX and 0.322 for SDXL on T2I-CompBench++). Against layout-to-image baselines, LaRender beats MIGC (UniDet 0.416 vs. 0.373; HPSR 0.767 vs. 0.448) and 3DIS (0.416 vs. 0.337; 0.767 vs. 0.311), confirming that bounding-box-based layout control alone cannot capture occlusion ordering. The only metric where LaRender trails is CLIP score (30.98 vs. SDXL's 31.12), a minor 0.14-point drop attributed to receiving per-object prompts rather than a single full prompt. Inference time is essentially unchanged from the base SDXL model (7.46s vs. 7.44s), while FLUX and 3DIS require 122s and 104s respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRender draws a direct conceptual bridge between volumetric rendering (Drebin et al., 1988) and its NeRF-style discretization (Mildenhall et al., 2021), transplanting the transmittance-accumulation formula into the latent space of a pre-trained diffusion model by replacing cross-attention layers. This departs from text-to-image methods that rely on imprecise prompt-based occlusion cues, layout-to-image methods (MIGC, 3DIS) that control bounding-box positions but not occlusion order, and 3D-aware generation (NeRF/GAN hybrids) that require training and are domain-limited. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first work to formulate occlusion control as a distinct generative task, separate from spatial layout or scene-graph relationship generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, drop-in replacement of cross-attention with a physics-grounded rendering step makes precise occlusion control accessible without paired occlusion data or fine-tuning, which is practically important for advertising, concept design, and complex scene composition. The 'free lunch' of semantic opacity control—enabling effects like fog density, rain concentration, light intensity, and lens strength—suggests the latent rendering framework may generalize beyond occlusion to other physically-motivated controls. Open questions include layer-specific behavior of the rendering mechanism (acknowledged but deferred), robustness to unreasonable layouts, and whether the concept-mixing failure mode can be mitigated; the small evaluation datasets (70 RealOcc examples, 300 T2I-CompBench++ prompts) also leave scalability to larger, more diverse scenes untested.
