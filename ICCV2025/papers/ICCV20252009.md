## Problem

State-of-the-art infrared and visible image fusion methods (Transformer-based, diffusion-based) achieve strong quality but fail to meet real-time requirements even on high-end GPUs, while existing lightweight methods only reach quasi-real-time in limited scenarios. The core gap is the absence of a fusion approach that simultaneously delivers competitive quality and true real-time speed on low-power edge and mobile devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252009/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a grayscale infrared image (H×W) and a 3-channel visible image (3×H×W) as input and produces a single-channel fused image. During training, source images are cropped to 96×96 patches; at inference the MM-LUT operates per-pixel via a 4-dimensional lookup indexed by (visible intensity, infrared intensity, visible gradient, scene context), with cubic spline interpolation between discrete lattice points. The deployed model is a compact LUT table plus a small 5-block CNN encoder, replacing the full teacher network.

## Method

LUT-Fuse decomposes fusion into a 4D learnable lookup table (MM-LUT) whose axes are low-order approximation encodings (zeroth-order infrared intensity, zeroth-order visible intensity, first-order visible gradient) and a high-level joint contextual scene encoding produced by a 5-block 3×3-convolution CNN. Rather than using conventional non-learnable quantization to populate the LUT, the paper introduces an efficient distillation strategy: a pre-trained teacher fusion network (MM-Net, trained with Text-IF-style losses) guides optimization of all LUT parameters via gradient descent, with a composite loss combining L1 intensity distillation, SSIM distillation, L2 smoothness regularization, and monotonicity regularization. This transforms the LUT from a fixed quantization artifact into a continuously optimized, generalizable mapping. At inference, fusion reduces to computing the four lookup indices per pixel and performing trilinear interpolation in the 4D lattice, yielding sub-5 ms processing on a desktop GPU and ~18–30 ms on a Jetson Orin NX—roughly one-tenth the time of the fastest lightweight baselines.

## Results

On MFNet, FMB, and LLVIP, LUT-Fuse achieves the best MI (2.560, 2.999, 2.446) and EN (6.394, 6.662, 7.545) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive on CC, SSIM, and Q^{AB/F}. In terms of speed, LUT-Fuse processes a 640×480 frame in 4.70 ms on an RTX 4060 Ti and 18.23 ms on a Jetson Orin NX, making it the only method to achieve real-time (>30 FPS) performance across all datasets and both platforms. This represents roughly a 5× speedup over the next-fastest lightweight method (Tar-DAL: 21.83 ms on MFNet) and over 100× speedup over diffusion-based methods like Diff-IF (1723 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUT-Fuse achieves the highest MI and EN across all three datasets, outperforming the strongest baselines EMMA (MI 2.540 on MFNet) and CDDFuse (MI 2.710 on FMB) by 0.02 and 0.29 respectively. On Q^{AB/F}, Diff-IF (0.688) and Text-IF (0.683) lead on MFNet, while CDDFuse (0.657) and Text-IF (0.651) lead on FMB; LUT-Fuse is competitive but trails by 0.05–0.06 on these gradient-based metrics. The most striking advantage is speed: LUT-Fuse runs in 4.70 ms (MFNet) vs. Tar-DAL's 21.83 ms, SDNet's 35.41 ms, and Diff-IF's 1723 ms on the same RTX 4060 Ti, and is the only method achieving real-time on the Jetson Orin NX (18.23 ms at 480P vs. Tar-DAL's 114.21 ms). On downstream tasks, LUT-Fuse achieves the best mAP@0.5 (0.941) and mAP@0.5:0.95 (0.614) in object detection on LLVIP, surpassing Text-IF (0.938 / 0.597) and U2Fusion (0.943 / 0.609), while in semantic segmentation it ranks second with a marginal gap to Text-IF. The teacher network LUT-Fuse(MM-Net) retains slightly higher Q^{AB/F} (0.706 vs. 0.628 on MFNet), confirming that the distillation to LUT preserves most fusion quality at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the lookup-table paradigm—previously applied to image enhancement (Zeng et al., 2020) and multi-exposure fusion (MEFLUT, 2023)—for the first time in multi-modal infrared-visible image fusion. It departs from the standard quantization-based LUT construction by introducing a distillation strategy that transfers fusion knowledge from a powerful teacher network (MM-Net, based on Text-IF) into a learnable 4D LUT parameterized by low-order approximation encoding (Taylor-inspired zeroth/first-order terms) and a high-level contextual scene encoder. Conceptually, this transforms LUT population from a fixed quantization problem into a differentiable optimization problem, sidestepping the ground-truth absence that plagues fusion tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LUT-Fuse is the only method achieving real-time (and super-real-time) fusion on both a mid-range GPU (RTX 4060 Ti) and a low-power edge device (Jetson Orin NX), where all existing methods—including those explicitly designed for real-time operation—fail. This opens the door to deploying high-quality multi-modal fusion in resource-constrained settings such as autonomous vehicles and mobile inspection systems. Open questions include whether the 4D LUT structure generalizes to other multi-modal fusion tasks (e.g., SAR-optical, medical), how the fixed bin resolution scales to very high-resolution inputs, and whether the performance gap between LUT-Fuse and its MM-Net teacher (notable in SSIM and Q^AB/F on LLVIP) can be further closed without sacrificing the speed advantage.
