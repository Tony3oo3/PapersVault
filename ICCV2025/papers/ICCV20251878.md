## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving models rely on a single expert trajectory as their sole imitation target, leading to three key limitations: single-mode learning that fails to capture diverse planning solutions, over-reliance on ego-vehicle status (status leakage), and absence of planning-oriented feature modeling that would enable motion-guided instance interactions. This restricts the robustness of the overall decision-making process, particularly in closed-loop settings where perceptually decoupled planning models still outperform end-to-end approaches.

## Input / Output

The student model consumes panoramic camera images on nuScenes (camera-only, no LiDAR) and three cropped forward camera images (1024×256) plus rasterized BEV LiDAR on NAVSIM, while the teacher model operates on structured scene annotations (agent states with position, orientation, velocity, dimensions, and binary masks; lane point sets). The output is a set of N_E multi-mode planning trajectories (each T timesteps × 2D), per-mode motion classification scores, and ego-status vectors, with the planning vocabulary derived from trajectory clustering by vectorization and end-points.

## Method

DistillDrive trains a structured-scene teacher model (agent encoder + scene encoder + planning decoder with IL head and Q-learning/IRL-based state optimization) and distills its multi-mode planning knowledge into an end-to-end student built on SparseDrive's sparse temporal perception. A generative module maps both expert trajectories and ego/agent instance features into Gaussian distributions in a shared latent space, enabling distribution-wise interaction via GRU-MLP fusion supervised by KL divergence with Hungarian matching for agent alignment. Multi-stage distillation aligns encoder features (L2), decoder features (L2 through an MLP adapter ensuring isomorphism between hetero-source models), and multi-mode classification probabilities (KL divergence). The RL component uses Q-learning to map predicted ego states to discrete motion decisions (left/right/straight) with a learnable weighted exponential reward, explicitly decoupling state-space understanding from trajectory regression to mitigate ego-status leakage.

## Results

On the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark, DistillDrive (Student) achieves 86.2 PDMS, 81.0 EP, 98.1 NC, and 94.6 DAC, outperforming the Transfuser baseline (83.5 PDMS, 78.6 EP) by 2.7 PDMS and 2.4 EP points. On nuScenes open-loop planning, the student model reduces average collision rate to 0.06% (vs. 0.13% for SparseDrive, a 50% reduction) and average L2 error to 0.57 m (vs. 0.63 m, a 10% reduction). The teacher model, using ground-truth annotations, reaches 86.5 PDMS on NAVSIM and 0.53 m L2 / 0.05% collision on nuScenes, confirming the distillation gap is small.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM closed-loop, DistillDrive (Student) beats Transfuser by 2.7 PDMS (86.2 vs. 83.5) and 2.4 EP (81.0 vs. 78.6), and surpasses Hydra-MDP by 3.2 PDMS (86.2 vs. 83.0) and 3.4 EP (81.0 vs. 77.6). It also outperforms UniAD (83.4 PDMS) and VADV2 (80.9 PDMS) by 2.8 and 5.3 PDMS respectively. On nuScenes open-loop, DistillDrive (Student) achieves 0.57 m average L2 and 0.06% average collision, improving over SparseDrive (0.63 m, 0.13%) by 10% in L2 and 50% in collision rate, and over VAD (0.72 m, 0.21%) by 21% in L2 and 71% in collision. Notably, the student model even exceeds the teacher on NC (98.1 vs. 97.5) and TTC (93.6 vs. 92.8) on NAVSIM, suggesting multi-mode distillation enables breakthroughs beyond the teacher's own performance. The student matches the teacher's EP exactly (81.0) while using only camera+LiDAR inputs rather than ground-truth annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DistillDrive builds directly on SparseDrive's sparse temporal representation for the student's perceptual backbone and VADV2's planning vocabulary (clustering trajectories into multi-mode instances) for the planning decoder. It departs from prior end-to-end planning methods (UniAD, VAD, SparseDrive, DiffusionDrive) that supervise with a single expert trajectory by introducing a structured-scene teacher model that provides diversified multi-mode planning instances as distillation targets. The knowledge distillation framing extends PlanKD and Hydra-MDP, which distill from rule-based or simulation-based teachers, by using an isomorphic hetero-source teacher trained on ground-truth scene annotations and combining it with Q-learning/IRL-based state optimization and a Gaussian-distribution generative model for motion-guided latent-space interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized weakness in end-to-end driving—over-reliance on ego status and single-mode imitation—by showing that multi-mode distillation from a structured teacher can substantially reduce collision rates (50% on nuScenes open-loop) and improve closed-loop NAVSIM scores. Notably, the student model exceeds the teacher on NC and TTC in NAVSIM, suggesting distillation can induce generalization beyond the teacher's capabilities. However, the nuScenes evaluation is open-loop, the teacher requires ground-truth annotations (limiting real-world deployability), and the generative model's autoregressive variant hurt performance, leaving open questions about scaling this distillation paradigm to reactive, closed-loop simulation and integrating world models as the authors themselves note.
