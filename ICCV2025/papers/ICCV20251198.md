## Problem

Existing object-level scene reconstruction methods rely on passive data acquisition and static decomposition, which is inherently ambiguous (e.g., whether to separate a drawer from its cabinet) and fails to capture the true dynamic nature of environments. The paper argues that decomposition granularity is highly context-dependent and should be progressive, guided by the intentional human-object interactions that are the dominant force shaping real-world scene dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a streaming egocentric RGB-D video where the user proactively interacts with objects, processing each incoming frame for camera and 6-DoF object tracking, performing joint optimization every 10 frames, and storing keyframes every 30 frames. It outputs a decomposed 3D Gaussian scene map consisting of a background component and independently tracked object instances, each parameterized by RGB color, center position, isotropic variance, and opacity, from which color, depth, silhouette, and instance-ID images can be rendered in real time.

## Method

The core system is an online dynamic SLAM pipeline with four modules: (1) prompted segmentation that detects depth inconsistencies between the rendered map and observed depth to generate SAM2 prompts, with a flexible-length memory bank to retain complete object observations; (2) camera and object pose estimation that treats both as locally static problems by masking out currently interacting objects; (3) mask refinement that uses 3D rendering of tracked objects to correct 2D segmentation errors via additional positive/negative prompts; and (4) progressive decomposed reconstruction that decouples newly interacted objects from the background Gaussian map by projecting Gaussians onto multi-view masks. The key insight is that hand-object interactions define a stable compositional granularity (the independently moving part), which reduces the joint optimization of camera pose, object pose, and Gaussian parameters to a tractable bundle-adjustment-like problem, achieving photorealistic rendering in ~4k iterations versus 30k for offline baselines.

## Results

On the HOI4D dataset, the proposed method achieves the best camera localization accuracy (ATE = 0.076 m) and rendering quality (PSNR = 29.12 dB for static, 27.58 dB for dynamic) among all compared SLAM methods. On the more challenging MHOI dataset with 3–8 interacted objects per sequence, the method reduces ATE to 0.093 m, substantially outperforming NeuDySLAM (0.189 m) and static-assumption methods Co-SLAM (0.221 m) and SplaTaM (0.293 m). For novel view synthesis, the method matches EgoGaussian on static parts (SSIM 0.96, PSNR 29.12) while requiring only ~4k optimization iterations versus 30k for all baselines. The rendered instance masks also outperform raw SAM2 predictions, with mIoU improving from 0.318 to 0.835 on the structurally complex scissors sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuDySLAM (the strongest dynamic SLAM baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on both ATE (0.076 vs 0.094 m on HOI4D; 0.093 vs 0.189 m on MHOI) and static PSNR (29.12 vs 25.15 dB), with the gap widening on MHOI because NeuDySLAM masks out all previously-moved objects, discarding too many features. Against static-assumption methods Co-SLAM and SplaTaM, the advantage is even larger (ATE reduced by 56% and 51% respectively on HOI4D), and these methods do not reconstruct dynamic objects at all. For novel view synthesis, the method outperforms 4DGS (PSNR 29.12 vs 25.33 static; 27.58 vs 25.34 dynamic) and Def-3DGS (29.12 vs 25.85 static; 27.58 vs 25.71 dynamic) by 3–4 dB. Compared to EgoGaussian, the method is slightly lower on dynamic PSNR (27.58 vs 30.33 dB) and LPIPS (0.10 vs 0.09), but achieves comparable static quality (PSNR 29.12 vs 30.99) while using only ~4k iterations versus 30k, and performs its own camera tracking rather than relying on ground-truth poses. For segmentation, the rendered masks surpass SAM2 by a large margin on the scissors sequence (mIoU 0.835 vs 0.318) and by smaller margins on other sequences (e.g., 0.925 vs 0.913 on Seq 1), demonstrating the value of the 3D-consistent refinement pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of object-decomposed radiance fields (Panoptic Lifting, Gaussian Grouping), dynamic SLAM (NeuDySLAM, Co-SLAM), and agent-in-the-loop scene understanding (iLabel, Total-decom, Roboexp). It departs from static decomposition methods by making granularity interaction-defined rather than model-defined, and from dynamic SLAM by not merely masking moving objects but actively decomposing and reconstructing them as independent instances. The closest concurrent work, EgoGaussian, also leverages hand-object interaction in egocentric video with 3D Gaussian Splatting, but operates offline; this paper extends the paradigm to an online, streaming setting with progressive decomposition and joint camera/object pose tracking within a unified SLAM loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction-guided decomposition elegantly resolves the fundamental ambiguity in object-level reconstruction (e.g., drawer vs. cabinet) by letting the user's physical action define what counts as an independently moving unit, which is directly useful for downstream robotic manipulation. The new MHOI dataset with 3–8 interacted objects per sequence is a practical contribution for evaluating multi-object decomposition. However, the method assumes approximately rigid body motion and intentional single-agent interaction, leaving open questions about scalability to many simultaneous objects, non-rigid deformations, and multi-agent or autonomous (non-human) interaction settings.
