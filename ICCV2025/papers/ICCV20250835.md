## Problem

Streaming video dialogue requires a model to proactively decide when to respond and generate real-time answers as video frames arrive, but existing per-step LLM invocation methods incur O(n³) transformer computation that grows with the number of past frames, capping throughput at ~10–15 FPS on an A100. This makes it impossible to match the linear O(n) rate of incoming frames, let alone the 24–100+ FPS needed for smooth video or gaming applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250835/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous stream of video frames (up to 100 FPS) and a single user query (text) issued at an arbitrary time step. Rather than producing one response per query, the model emits a sequence of proactive text responses interleaved with silence tokens across the entire stream, each triggered only when a query-relevant event is detected. The perception stage compresses each frame into a single token (not a patch grid), and the Cognition Gate outputs a binary </response> or </silence> decision per frame before any LLM generation occurs.

## Method

STREAMMIND decouples perception from cognition via an event-gated LLM invocation paradigm. The Event-Preserving Feature Extractor (EPFE), built on the Selective State Space Model (Mamba), processes each frame's CLIP spatial features through a constant-cost recurrence (h_{t+1} = Ah_t + Bx_t, y_t = Ch_t) to produce one perception token and update its hidden state, eliminating the quadratic token accumulation of transformer-based connectors. A Cognition Gate, constructed via Shallow Layer Transfer (initializing from the first 4 LLM layers and fine-tuning autoregressively on </response>/</silence> labels), decides per frame whether to open the gate and invoke the full LLM. Training is two-stage: first, EPFE and LLM are jointly trained for spatiotemporal–linguistic alignment; second, the Cognition Gate is trained with a class-balanced cross-entropy loss to handle the ~310:1 silence-to-response ratio. This architecture achieves 100 FPS on a single A100 while maintaining SOTA accuracy on Ego4D, SoccerNet, COIN, and Ego4D LTA benchmarks.

## Results

On Ego4D streaming dialogue, STREAMMIND achieves 43.34% TriggerAcc and 39.73% TimVal, substantially outperforming VideoLLM-Online (32.34%/29.66%) and VideoLLM-MoD (32.36%/29.65%). On SoccerNet, the gap widens to 52.18% TriggerAcc and 47.36% TimVal versus ~31%/28% for both baselines. In terms of language quality, STREAMMIND reaches 77.3% correctness and 60.2% fluency on Ego4D (vs. ~48%/45% for baselines) and 89.2% correctness on SoccerNet (vs. ~53%). On offline COIN benchmarks, it achieves SOTA step recognition (63.7%), task recognition (93.2%), and procedure forecasting with goal (54.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STREAMMIND decisively outperforms both VideoLLM-Online [8] and VideoLLM-MoD [60] on all streaming metrics: TriggerAcc improves by ~11 points on Ego4D and ~21 points on SoccerNet, while correctness jumps from ~48% to 77.3% (Ego4D) and ~53% to 89.2% (SoccerNet). Against LION-FS [31] (compared only on Ego4D in Table 2), STREAMMIND achieves lower TimeDiff (1.89 vs. 2.15), higher fluency (60.2% vs. 46.1%), lower PPL (2.02 vs. 2.09), and substantially higher correctness (77.3% vs. 52.4%). On the Ego4D LTA offline benchmark, STREAMMIND ties VideoLLM-Online-8B-v1+ and VideoLLM-MoD on Verb ED (0.689) but slightly trails on Noun (0.655 vs. 0.671/0.676) and Action (0.881 vs. 0.884), while still beating CLIP [46] (0.739/0.769/0.941) by a wide margin. In the feature-extractor ablation (Table 8), EPFE surpasses both Q-Former [28] (TriggerAcc 26.65%) and STC from VideoLLaMA 2 [12] (27.54%) by a large margin (43.34%), confirming the state-space design's advantage for streaming perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the per-step LLM invocation paradigm established by VideoLLM-Online and VideoLLM-MoD, where the full LLM is invoked at every frame incurring cubic computational cost. Instead, it introduces an event-gated paradigm that decouples perception from cognition via a Cognition Gate, drawing on the Selective State Space Model (Mamba) to build a constant-cost Event-Preserving Feature Extractor (EPFE) that replaces prior projection modules like Q-Former and STC. The Shallow Layer Transfer method—initializing the gate from the first few LLM layers—repurposes pretrained transformer depth for a lightweight binary decision task, a design choice not explored in prior streaming video LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 100 FPS on a single A100 (vs. 10–15 FPS for prior streaming video LLMs) removes a key bottleneck for real-time human-AI interaction applications such as AI companions, game AI copilots, and interactive media. The perception-cognition decoupling paradigm is conceptually clean and could generalize to other always-on multimodal settings. However, evaluation is limited to two streaming datasets (Ego4D, SoccerNet) with only two reimplemented streaming baselines, the binary response/silence gate may struggle with nuanced multi-turn dialogue, and the 2 fps training rate leaves open whether the architecture scales to higher temporal resolutions or richer interaction patterns.
