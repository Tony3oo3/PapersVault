## Problem

Robotic manipulation suffers from a persistent gap between high-level task semantics and low-level geometric primitives: existing methods either depend on costly manual annotation of keypoints and axes (limiting scalability and generalizability) or use automated detection without verification, causing error propagation from undetected or misaligned primitives. No prior framework simultaneously automates primitive extraction, semantically anchors them to functional affordances, and self-corrects detection failures in a unified closed-loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 3D object meshes (5,231 objects from RoboCasa and Objaverse, filtered for manipulability) rendered as 8 multi-view RGB images per object (4 horizontal orthographic + 4 oblique projections), along with natural-language task descriptions. It produces geometric primitives formalized as (E, S, F) triplets—geometric entity, structural description, and functional role—annotated in both 2D image space and 3D mesh space, yielding a 41,848-image annotated dataset. A secondary output is Qwen2.5VL-PA, a LoRA-fine-tuned VLM trained on the derived Robocasa-PA benchmark of 8,343 single-choice visual questions spanning three reasoning types.

## Method

PASG operates in three stages: (1) Semantic SAM performs multi-granularity segmentation on multi-view images, followed by geometric-topological keypoint extraction (mask centroids, approxPolyDP corner detection, PCA-derived boundary intersections) and two-stage filtering (DBSCAN for local redundancy, farthest-point sampling for global distinctiveness); (2) GPT-4o performs task-driven semantic primitive identification—decomposing inferred manipulation tasks into subgoals and mapping each to required point/axis primitives—then aligns these semantic primitives to detected geometric ones via multi-candidate matching with confidence scores; (3) a dynamic self-refine loop (Algorithm 1) detects low-confidence (<0.5) or unmatched primitives and triggers hierarchical re-segmentation at progressively finer Semantic SAM granularity levels (up to 5 iterations), forming a segment-align-detect-resample closed loop that achieves 98% matching success. For downstream reasoning, Qwen2.5-VL is fine-tuned via LoRA on the Robocasa-PA benchmark, achieving 77.8% overall accuracy (+33.9% absolute over the base model) with stable in/out-of-distribution performance within ±2%.

## Results

On the Robocasa-PA spatial-semantic reasoning benchmark, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5VL-PA achieves 77.79% overall accuracy on the in-distribution test set and 79.69% on the out-of-distribution test set, surpassing the strongest general-purpose VLM baseline (GPT-4O at 43.19%/41.79%) by over 34 percentage points. On the RoboTwin manipulation platform, PASG-generated annotations yield a 77.83% average task success rate across six tasks, competitive with human annotations (84.67%) and outperforming them on Block Hammer Beat (82.0% vs. 79.0%) and Empty Cup Place (76.0% vs. 73.0%). A data-efficiency study shows that fine-tuning on just 5% of the training data (279 samples) already yields ~10% absolute accuracy gain, with the in/out-of-distribution gap remaining within ±2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5VL-PA dominates all seven baselines on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits of Robocasa-PA. Against the strongest general-purpose VLM, GPT-4O (43.19% in-dist., 41.79% OOD), the fine-tuned model gains +34.6 and +37.9 percentage points respectively. GPT-4V (39.04%/39.00%) and GPT-4O-mini (32.26%/34.96%) trail GPT-4O, with GPT-4O-mini showing a notable weakness on Task 2 (30.26% in-dist.). Among open-source models, Qwen-2.5VL without fine-tuning (43.91%/43.33%) is the closest baseline, indicating that the LoRA fine-tuning on Robocasa-PA data contributes the bulk of the improvement rather than the base architecture alone. Spatially-oriented models perform poorly: SpaceMantis (29.15%/21.70%) collapses on Task 2 (10.54% in-dist.), and RoboPoint (14.40%/15.32%) scores 0% on Task 2, suggesting that existing spatial-affordance models lack the compositional primitive-reasoning capability that PASG's benchmark targets. On the RoboTwin manipulation tasks, PASG's automated annotations achieve 77.83% average success versus 84.67% for human annotations, with PASG actually exceeding human labels on two of six tasks (Block Hammer Beat, Empty Cup Place), demonstrating that automated primitive extraction can match or surpass manual curation in practical manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASG extends the line of work on geometric primitive extraction for manipulation (ReKep, CoPa, OmniManip, FUNCTO) by replacing manual or task-level annotation with an object-level, fully automated pipeline built on Semantic SAM's multi-granularity segmentation and GPT-4o's spatial reasoning. It departs from SoFar's predefined directional priors and Robotwin's human-annotated object-level primitives by introducing a closed-loop self-refinement mechanism that iteratively resamples segmentation when VLM confidence drops below threshold. The semantic anchoring step, which couples each geometric primitive with a functional triplet (E, S, F), generalizes prior work that treated primitives as purely geometric entities, and the fine-tuned Qwen2.5VL-PA model builds directly on the Qwen2.5-VL architecture via LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the annotation bottleneck that has limited scalability of manipulation datasets: PASG generates semantically grounded primitives at scale (5,231 objects, 41,848 images) with 91.6% semantic identification accuracy, and the resulting Robocasa-PA benchmark (8,343 questions) is the first to evaluate functional primitive understanding in manipulation contexts. However, the pipeline still depends on GPT-4o as the semantic annotator, and the 75.8% strict alignment accuracy reveals that complex spatial semantics remain a challenge. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to real-world (non-simulated) objects with texture variation and partial occlusion, and whether the semantic anchoring quality degrades for objects outside the 150+ everyday categories tested.
