## Problem

Temporal Action Detection on long-form videos is bottlenecked by memory: prior adapter-based methods like AdaTAD (TIA) unified spatial and temporal processing in a single module, preventing simultaneous high-resolution use of both dimensions, and relied on 1D convolutions that struggle with long-range temporal dependencies. This paper tackles both limitations by decoupling the two processing paths and replacing convolutions with transformer encoders, enabling ~120× more input frames than prior approaches under the same hardware budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251026/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed RGB video (up to 6144 frames at 224×224 spatial resolution) split into sequential temporal chunks; the frozen backbone processes one chunk at a time while adapters attend to all chunks simultaneously. Output is a set of action proposals, each with predicted start time, end time, action category, and confidence score, evaluated via mAP at multiple IoU thresholds. The chunking mechanism is what allows the backbone to see global context without exceeding per-chunk memory limits.

## Method

AdaTAD++ introduces TIA++ adapters that separate spatial and temporal processing into independently trainable sub-modules: the spatial path uses a 2D pooling CNN followed by spatial pooling attention to compress spatial dimensions, while the temporal path replaces 1D depthwise convolutions with two lightweight transformer encoders operating on the spatially-reduced tokens to capture long-range dependencies. A two-phase training protocol first optimizes spatial adapters at high spatial / low temporal resolution, then temporal adapters at low spatial / high temporal resolution, so that inference can combine both at full resolution without the memory cost of joint training. An alternate arrangement (TIA§++) further reduces memory by detaching adapters from the backbone's gradient flow and concatenating spatial and temporal outputs before projection. The approach trains fewer than 6.7M parameters on top of a frozen VideoMAEv2-G or SlowFast-R50 backbone, achieving state-of-the-art mAP on ActivityNet-1.3, THUMOS14, and EPIC-Kitchens 100.

## Results

On THUMOS14, AdaTAD++ with VideoMAEv2-G backbone achieves 76.3 mAP at 768×224² input and 78.6 mAP at 1536×224², surpassing the previous SoTA AdaTAD (75.4 and 76.9 mAP respectively). With separable spatial-temporal training at 1536×224², the method reaches 79.5 mAP on THUMOS14 and 43.55 mAP on ActivityNet-1.3. On EPIC-Kitchens 100, AdaTAD++ achieves 32.0 mAP (verb) and 30.8 mAP (noun), improving over AdaTAD by +2.7 and +1.5 mAP respectively. The method processes up to 6144 frames with high spatial resolution using only 38.5 GB memory (A100-80G), compared to 40.6 GB for LoSA and 29 GB for TALLFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaTAD++ consistently outperforms all compared baselines across both main benchmarks. On THUMOS14, it surpasses LoSA (71.0 mAP) by +5.3 mAP, InternVideo (71.5) by +4.8 mAP, TriDet (69.3) by +7.0 mAP, and ActionFormer (66.8) by +9.5 mAP, while using less memory than LoSA (38.5 vs 40.6 GB) and TALLFormer (38.5 vs 29 GB at 768 frames). Against its direct predecessor AdaTAD, the gains are +0.9 mAP at 768×224² and +1.7 mAP at 1536×224², with the separable training variant adding a further +2.1 mAP over AdaTAD's separable training (79.5 vs 77.4). On EPIC-Kitchens 100, AdaTAD++ beats AdaTAD by +2.7 mAP (verb) and +1.5 mAP (noun), and outperforms ActionFormer (VideoMAE-L) by +3.5 and +3.5 mAP respectively. The alternate arrangement AdaTAD§++ achieves 78.0 mAP at 1536×224² with 50.9 GB memory, improving over AdaTAD's alternate arrangement (75.8 mAP, 43.6 GB) by +2.2 mAP while using comparable memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends AdaTAD (Liu et al., CVPR 2024), which introduced Temporal Informative Adapters (TIA) for parameter-efficient temporal action detection on frozen video backbones. The key departures are threefold: decoupling spatial and temporal adapter components into independently trainable modules, replacing the 1D depthwise convolutions in the temporal branch with transformer encoders (enabled by the memory savings from decoupling), and introducing a two-phase training protocol that alternates high-spatial/low-temporal and low-spatial/high-temporal resolution. Within the broader PETL landscape (LoRA, AdaptFormer, ConvAdapter), the paper positions adapter-based refinement as a middle ground between full fine-tuning and query-based transformer architectures, borrowing the self-attention mechanism from the latter while retaining the frozen-backbone efficiency of the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling ~120× more input frames (6144 at high spatial resolution) under the same GPU budget as prior methods, which is critical for long-form video understanding where temporal context spans hundreds of seconds. The two-phase training strategy offers a general recipe for memory-constrained fine-tuning that could transfer beyond TAD to other video tasks. Open questions include whether the two-phase protocol is truly optimal versus other scheduling strategies, how the approach behaves with even larger backbones (beyond VideoMAEv2-G), and whether the transformer-in-adapter design generalizes to tasks requiring denser spatial reasoning. The paper's focus on third-person and first-person cooking datasets leaves open how well the method transfers to other domains with different temporal structures.
