## Problem

While LLMs have been extensively benchmarked for creative capabilities, MLLMs lack a dedicated evaluation for their creative intelligence, which the Triarchic Theory of Intelligence identifies as a distinct and underexplored dimension. Existing MLLM benchmarks focus on analytical (perception, reasoning) and contextual (agent-based) intelligence, leaving visual-creative intelligence largely unassessed, and the few partial-creation benchmarks that exist use coarse evaluation criteria and lack the rich contextual framing needed for real-world creative tasks.

## Input / Output

Each test case provides one or more images (up to 9 per question; 1,001 images total spanning 25+ categories) alongside a structured text query containing four components: Role (identity to assume), Background (prior knowledge not visible in the image), Instruction (operations to perform), and Requirement (constraints). The majority of queries exceed 500 tokens, making them substantially longer than those in comparable benchmarks. The model produces an open-ended creative text response, which is then scored against instance-specific criteria rather than a single ground-truth answer.

## Method

Creation-MMBench comprises 765 test cases across 51 fine-grained tasks in four categories (Literary Writing, Common Functional Writing, Professional Functional Writing, Creative Multimodal Understanding), each with instance-specific evaluation criteria split into General Subjective Criteria and Visual Factuality Criteria. Evaluation employs GPT-4o as a judge in two complementary modes: Unitary Scoring assigns a 1–10 Visual Factuality Score per question, while Pairwise Comparison against a GPT-4o-1120 reference yields a Reward score normalized to [−100, +100]. A Dual Evaluation protocol (swapping response positions and averaging) mitigates position bias in the judge. Additionally, a text-only variant (Creation-MMBench-TO) replaces images with GPT-4o-generated descriptions, enabling controlled study of how visual instruction tuning affects the base LLM's creative abilities.

## Results

On Creation-MMBench (765 cases, 51 tasks), GPT-4o-1120 serves as the pairwise baseline (Reward = 0.00, VFS = 8.72). Gemini-2.0-Pro achieves the highest Reward of +4.48 with a VFS of 8.53, while the best open-source model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, scores Reward −5.82 and VFS 8.33. Performance degrades sharply for smaller open-source models: LLaVA-OneVision-7B drops to Reward −43.49 and VITA-1.5 to −53.31. Professional Functional Writing is the weakest category across all models (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Pro scores only +3.33 Reward there), while Common Functional Writing is the strongest (Gemini-2.0-Pro: +12.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, the primary comparison is across 20 MLLMs rather than against a single prior method. Proprietary models (Gemini-2.0-Pro, GPT-4o) consistently outperform open-source models: the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Reward −5.82) still trails the GPT-4o baseline by ~6 points, while the weakest open-source models (VITA-1.5, LLaVA-OneVision-7B) fall 43–53 points behind. A key secondary finding (Table 3) shows that visual instruction tuning degrades creativity: for example, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (text-only LLM) achieves Reward +0.82 on Creation-MMBench-TO, but its VLM counterpart Qwen2.5-VL-72B drops to −5.82 with image input, and further to −4.05 when the VLM processes text descriptions. The paper also positions Creation-MMBench against prior partial-creation benchmarks (MLLM-Bench with 20 creative questions, AlignMMBench with 353), noting that Creation-MMBench is the first dedicated creativity benchmark with instance-level criteria, specific role assignments, multi-image support, and visual factuality checks—features absent in both prior benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM creativity evaluation literature (LiveIdeaBench, RPGBench, Torrance-based tests) into the multimodal setting, positioning MLLM creative intelligence as the underdeveloped third pillar of the Triarchic Theory of Intelligence alongside analytical and contextual intelligence. It departs from existing partial-creation MLLM benchmarks (MLLM-Bench, AlignMMBench, VisIT-Bench) by being the first dedicated, comprehensive creative-intelligence benchmark for MLLMs, introducing instance-specific evaluation criteria, role-based queries, and a visual factuality score that prior benchmarks lack. The MLLM-as-a-judge evaluation framework builds on the widely adopted LLM-as-judge paradigm but adds dual-evaluation to mitigate position bias and a separate unitary scoring track for visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap in MLLM evaluation: prior benchmarks assess analytical or contextual intelligence well, but creative intelligence has been largely unmeasured for multimodal models. The finding that visual instruction tuning degrades the base LLM's creative abilities (observed across multiple open-source models) raises an important open question about whether multimodal adaptation inherently trades off language-level creativity, and whether this can be mitigated through training data or architecture. The observed disconnect between strong objective benchmark scores (OC Score) and weak creative performance in open-source models suggests that current evaluation suites systematically overestimate MLLM readiness for open-ended, context-aware generation tasks.
