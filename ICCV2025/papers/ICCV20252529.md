## Problem

Recovering 3D object pose and shape from a single image remains ill-posed due to depth ambiguities, occlusions, and vast intra- and inter-class shape variance. Existing feed-forward or diffusion-based methods trained on synthetic data struggle to generalize to in-the-wild images with occlusions and uncommon poses, lack an explicit feedback loop for refining noisy estimates, and largely ignore pixel alignment by treating pose as secondary to geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252529/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a category name (e.g., 'chair') as input, along with auxiliary signals estimated from the image: a segmentation mask (from rembg or dataset annotations), and normal and depth maps (from OmniData). It outputs a 3D object shape as a morphable SDF latent code (decoded to a mesh via FlexiCubes) and a 6-DoF pose (rotation, translation, scale). The entire pipeline is zero-shot at inference time—no per-image retraining is needed—and converges in roughly 3 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

SDFit fits a DIT morphable SDF to an image via iterative render-and-compare optimization, jointly refining a 256-dim shape latent code and SE(3) pose to minimize a composite energy over mask IoU, normal MSE, depth SSI-MAE, contour distance transform, and a geometric regularization term. Shape initialization is cast as a retrieval problem: OpenShape embeds both the input image and all DIT training shapes into a shared latent space, and the closest shape's latent code seeds the optimization. Pose initialization decorates the initial shape with hybrid ControlNet+DINOv2 features (following Diff3F's multi-view decoration), matches them to image features to establish 2D-3D correspondences, and solves for pose via RANSAC+PnP. The regularization term uses DIT's warper to map both the running and initial shapes into a canonical template space, computing per-vertex MSE in world space—this geometrically constrains the hypothesis without locking topology, allowing e.g. missing armrests to grow during fitting.

## Results

On Pix3D (Chair + Sofa), SDFit-feat@Sinit achieves a Chamfer Distance of 3.53, F@1 of 0.25, F@2 of 0.46, and CLIP similarity of 30.53, matching or exceeding all feed-forward and diffusion baselines on most metrics. For image alignment (joint pose + shape), SDFit-feat@Sinit reaches a Mean 2D IoU of 85.4% on Pascal3D+ and Pix3D, outperforming ZeroShape+RnC (78.1%) and OpenShape+RnC (66.8%). Under 40% occlusion on Pix3D, SDFit maintains a mean CD of 3.74 while ZeroShape degrades to 6.40, and on COMIC (hand-object grasps) SDFit achieves 3.25 vs. ZeroShape's 6.53.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZeroShape [28], SDFit is competitive on CD (3.53 vs. 3.44) but clearly wins on F@1 (0.25 vs. 0.23) and CLIP similarity (30.53 vs. 29.33), indicating more class-consistent shapes; under 40% occlusion the gap widens dramatically (CD 3.74 vs. 6.40 on Pix3D, 3.25 vs. 6.53 on COMIC). Against TripoSR [62], SDFit trades a small CD disadvantage (3.53 vs. 3.41) for substantially better F@1 (0.25 vs. 0.13) and F@2 (0.46 vs. 0.32), reflecting more accurate surface reconstruction. Against SDFusion [12], SDFit wins on every metric (CD 3.53 vs. 3.95, F@1 0.25 vs. 0.16, F@2 0.46 vs. 0.38, CLIP 30.53 vs. 29.87). For image alignment, SDFit-feat@Sinit achieves 85.4% Mean IoU, beating ZeroShape+RnC (78.1%) by 7.3 points and OpenShape+RnC (66.8%) by 18.6 points, demonstrating that jointly refining shape and pose via the mSDF yields significantly better pixel alignment than fixing shape and optimizing pose alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDFit draws a direct analogy from human pose estimation (SMPL/SMPLify) to object pose and shape estimation, but identifies three key differences that prevent trivial transfer: greater shape variance, variable topology, and the absence of reliable 2D-3D joint correspondences. It builds on DIT for the morphable SDF representation, OpenShape for retrieval-based shape initialization, and Diff3F for decorating textureless shapes with foundation-model features. The key departure from prior work is the joint optimization of both shape (via mSDF latent code) and pose in a render-and-compare loop, whereas existing methods either fix the shape (ROCA, Pavllo et al.) or perform feed-forward regression without a feedback loop (ZeroShape, TripoSR). The use of DINOv2 and ControlNet features to establish 2D-3D correspondences for morphable shapes (as opposed to fixed meshes) is a novel application of Diff3F to a long-standing problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDFit uniquely treats pose and shape as jointly optimizable first-class citizens, and its occlusion robustness (stable performance under 40%+ occlusion while feed-forward baselines degrade sharply) addresses a critical gap for in-the-wild deployment. The mSDF shape prior constrains the search to the manifold of valid shapes while still permitting topology changes, offering a principled middle ground between rigid templates and unconstrained generation. Open questions include scalability beyond the ShapeNet-trained DIT to open-world categories, convergence reliability of the optimization for highly ambiguous poses, and whether the foundation-model-based correspondence step can be made more robust for textureless or highly symmetric objects. The 60× speedup for shape decoration (reducing Diff3F views from 100 to 8) without accuracy loss is a practical finding that lowers the barrier to adoption.
