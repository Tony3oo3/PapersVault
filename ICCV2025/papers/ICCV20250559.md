## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods designed for full-precision models degrade significantly when applied to quantized neural networks due to two quantization-specific issues: random label assignment injects noise that low-bit parameter spaces amplify, and gradient norm imbalance between forgotten and retained data becomes more pronounced under discrete optimization. No prior work addresses unlearning directly within the quantized model training loop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained quantized model (e.g., ResNet-18 with 4-bit weights and activations, or MobileNetV2 with 2-bit weights), the full training dataset, and a designated forgotten subset (either an entire class or random samples at 10–50% of the data). Unlearning is performed via quantization-aware training with fake quantization nodes (LSQ+ by default), and the output is a quantized model whose parameters have been adjusted to remove the influence of the forgotten data while preserving performance on retained data and generalization on test data.

## Method

Q-MUL introduces two mechanisms operating in sequence. First, Similar Labels (SL) replaces the conventional random-label strategy: for each forgotten sample, the model's current predicted probability distribution is queried, and the label whose predicted probability is closest to the true label's probability (excluding the true label itself) is assigned, keeping gradient update directions aligned and minimizing noise injection. Second, Adaptive Gradient Reweighting (AGR) computes the expected gradient norms of the loss on the forgotten and retained subsets before each epoch, then assigns inverse-proportional weights (α_f = G_r/(G_f+G_r), α_r = G_f/(G_f+G_r)) to a weighted cross-entropy loss so that both subsets contribute equally to parameter updates. The full pipeline is executed under QAT with the straight-through estimator, and the weights are recomputed every epoch to track the evolving gradient landscape.

## Results

On 4-bit quantized ResNet-18 under random data forgetting (10% forgotten), Q-MUL achieves an Average Gap (AG) of 1.30% on CIFAR-10 and 3.11% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the previous best method SalUn (1.31% and 6.05% respectively). On CIFAR-100 at 10% forgetting, Q-MUL attains FA=75.71%, RA=97.89%, TA=67.27%, and MIA=52.11%, with gaps of only 0.95%, 2.09%, 5.16%, and 4.25% from the Retrain golden standard. At higher forgetting ratios (30% and 50%), Q-MUL maintains the lowest AG of 3.90% and 7.06% on CIFAR-100, respectively, while all other baselines degrade substantially (e.g., IU reaches AG >65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-MUL achieves the lowest Average Gap (AG) against Retrain across all six baselines on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. On CIFAR-100 (10% forgotten), Q-MUL (AG=3.11%) nearly halves the gap of the closest competitor SalUn (AG=6.05%), primarily by reducing the FA gap from 7.46% to 0.95% and the MIA gap from 10.42% to 4.25%. On CIFAR-10 (10% forgotten), Q-MUL (AG=1.30%) marginally edges out SalUn (AG=1.31%), with both substantially outperforming RL (1.92%) and ℓ₁-sparse (3.45%). The influence-function-based IU method catastrophically fails on quantized models, producing near-random accuracy on CIFAR-100 (FA=3.69%, AG=69.51%), while FT and GA show large MIA gaps (39–47%) indicating poor forgetting. At higher forgetting ratios (30%, 50%), Q-MUL's advantage over SalUn widens on CIFAR-100 (AG 3.90% vs 5.02% at 30%; 7.06% vs 7.33% at 50%), confirming robustness as the unlearning burden increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the approximate machine unlearning line (Random Labels, SalUn, Influence Unlearning, Gradient Ascent, l1-sparse) to the quantized-model setting, where prior methods assume continuous parameter spaces and unrestricted gradient dynamics. The key conceptual departure is identifying two quantization-specific failure modes—noise amplification from random label assignment and amplified gradient-norm imbalance between forgotten and retained data—that are invisible in full-precision models. The unlearning training is framed as a form of Quantization-Aware Training, connecting the MU literature to the QAT literature (LSQ+, PACT, DSQ) in a way no prior MU work has done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: edge-deployed quantized models that undergo periodic fine-tuning on user data must comply with GDPR-style deletion requests, and no prior method addressed this intersection. However, the evaluation is limited to image classification on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet) and only 2–4 bit quantization; whether the gradient-imbalance effect persists at higher bit-widths (8-bit) or in NLP/vision-language settings remains open. The Similar Labels mechanism also depends on the model's current output distribution, raising questions about its behavior when the model is poorly calibrated or when the forgotten class is the only class the model confidently predicts.
