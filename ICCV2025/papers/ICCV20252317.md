## Problem

Existing token merging methods for diffusion models select destination tokens using ad hoc heuristics such as spatial proximity or random sampling, which can compress or discard critical visual details in highly informative regions. This leads to suboptimal resource allocation where crucial textures and structures are lost while less important regions retain unnecessary detail, degrading generation quality especially at high merging ratios.

## Input / Output

The method operates on latent tokens within diffusion model layers (U-Net or transformer), taking text prompts for image/video generation or a single reference image for multi-view generation. Outputs are 768×768 images (Stable Diffusion), six 320×320 novel views (Zero123++), or 16-frame 512×512 videos (AnimateDiff), or 512×512 images from PixArt-α. The importance signal is derived from classifier-free guidance scores already computed during inference, requiring no additional forward pass or architectural modification.

## Method

The key insight is that classifier-free guidance (CFG) scores serve as a free, per-token importance indicator: tokens with high CFG magnitude strongly influence the generation toward satisfying the conditioning prompt. Rather than selecting top-k tokens as destination anchors (which causes redundancy and leaves unimportant tokens as independent), the method constructs a dynamic pool of important tokens of size P = (1−r)·(1+p), where r is the merging ratio and p is a hyperparameter. Destination tokens are randomly sampled from this pool to ensure diversity, while independent tokens are chosen as the most dissimilar tokens within the pool, guaranteeing all post-merging computation operates on important regions. Bipartite soft matching via cosine similarity then assigns remaining source tokens to their nearest destination, and merging is performed by averaging grouped tokens, reducing the token count to N·(1−r) for the subsequent attention computation.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 2 (768×768, MSCOCO 2014 validation, 30K samples), the proposed importance-based token merging achieves FID 17.75 and CLIP 31.76 at a 75% merging ratio, outperforming ToMeSD (FID 20.89) and ToFu (FID 19.29). At a 50% merging ratio, the method reaches FID 13.42 vs. 13.50 (ToMeSD) and 15.81 (ToFu), while CLIP scores remain near-identical (~31.8) across all methods. On the diffusion transformer PixArt-α, the gap widens dramatically: at r=0.7, FID drops to 50.76 vs. 95.46 for ToMeSD. Inference latency and GPU memory are essentially unchanged relative to ToMeSD (e.g., 5.8 s vs. 5.7 s at r=0.7 on an A5000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMeSD [3] (the primary baseline using spatially random destination tokens), the proposed method wins on FID at every merging ratio ≥ 0.5, with the advantage growing sharply at aggressive compression: at r=0.75, FID improves from 20.89 to 17.75 (≈15% relative reduction), and on PixArt-α the FID gap reaches 44.7 points (95.46 → 50.76) at r=0.7. Against ToFu [36], which combines token pruning and merging, the method achieves lower FID at all ratios (e.g., 16.22 vs. 18.94 at r=0.70 on SD2). Against ATC [21] (agglomerative token clustering), only qualitative comparison is provided because ATC is CPU-bound and non-batched, taking minutes per image versus ~5 s for the other methods; visually, the proposed method produces finer details and more coherent structures. CLIP scores remain statistically indistinguishable across all methods (within 0.05), indicating that the FID gains reflect improved sample quality rather than a shift in semantic alignment. On multi-view (Zero123++) and video (AnimateDiff) tasks, the method similarly outperforms ToMeSD, e.g., PSNR 14.58 vs. 13.12 and LPIPS 0.283 vs. 0.349 at r=0.75 for multi-view, and VBench total 79.12 vs. 78.59 at r=0.75 for video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token merging paradigm for diffusion models introduced by ToMeSD (Bolya & Hoffman, 2023), which used bipartite soft matching with randomly selected destination tokens. The key departure is replacing this random spatial selection with importance-based selection, where destination and independent tokens are drawn from a pool of high-importance tokens identified via classifier-free guidance (CFG) scores. The work also positions itself against ToFu (Kim et al., 2024), which combines pruning and merging but retains the same suboptimal destination selection, and ATC (Haurum et al., 2025), which uses hierarchical clustering but is computationally prohibitive for generative tasks. Conceptually, the paper bridges the CFG literature (which connects guidance to log-likelihood gradients) with the token reduction literature, repurposing an already-computed quantity as a free importance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free, adds zero computational overhead (CFG is already computed during inference), and generalizes across U-Net and transformer architectures as well as image, multi-view, and video generation tasks, making it a broadly applicable drop-in acceleration technique. The practical impact is significant: at aggressive merging ratios (r=0.75), it closes much of the quality gap between full and reduced inference, enabling near-real-time generation with substantially better fidelity than prior token merging. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other generative paradigms (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based models), whether the importance signal degrades in early diffusion timesteps where CFG is less structured, and whether combining it with other acceleration techniques (caching, quantization) yields compounding benefits without quality loss.
