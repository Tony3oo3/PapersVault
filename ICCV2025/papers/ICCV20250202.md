## Problem

Existing VLLMs face a spatio-temporal token imbalance when processing videos: uniform frame sampling neglects temporal dynamics, while reducing per-frame visual tokens sacrifices spatial detail. Both strategies risk exceeding the LLM context window on long videos or losing task-relevant information, and neither adapts to the specific question being asked.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video of L frames (spanning 10 seconds to 1 hour) and a text prompt as input, and produces a free-form textual response (caption or QA answer). A pre-set token budget θ constrains the total number of visual tokens fed to the LLM, and the framework is designed to integrate with existing VLLM backbones (LLaMA-VID, VideoLLaMA2) or operate standalone with Qwen2 as the language model.

## Method

B-VLLM introduces a three-stage token budgeting pipeline. First, a text-conditioned Q-Former operates on [CLS] tokens from all frames, using Gumbel-Softmax (temperature τ) to discretely select L* task-relevant frames without requiring full per-frame visual tokens. Second, a temporal frame token merging step detects and averages duplicated selections via cosine similarity (threshold γ) to recover distinct frames. Third, a spatial Q-Former samples R fine-grained tokens per selected frame conditioned on the text prompt, followed by an optional progressive Bipartite Merging loop that iteratively halves the token set until the budget θ is met. The final visual tokens are projected into LLM space via a learnable MLP and concatenated with the text token sequence.

## Results

B-VLLM achieves state-of-the-art performance across video benchmarks: 50.8 on MVBench, 52.1 on Perception Test, 22.6 on VNBench, 51.9 on EgoSchema, and 60.8/51.8/47.9 on VideoMME (short/medium/long). When integrated with LLaMA-VID, it yields a +10.5% gain on VideoMME-Short and +8.1% on VideoMME-Long; with VideoLLaMA2, it adds +3.8% on VideoMME-Long. On image benchmarks, B-VLLM (using only 32 tokens per image) surpasses LLaVA-1.5 (576 tokens) by 7.1% on MMBench, 7.7% on ScienceQA-Image, and 1.0% on SEED-Bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-VLLM outperforms all video baselines on every reported metric. Against VideoChat2 (16 uniform frames), B-VLLM leads by 0.3 on MVBench but by a large margin on long-video settings: +14.7 on VideoMME-Long (47.9 vs 33.2) and +10.2 on VNBench (22.6 vs 12.4). Against LLaMA-VID (2 tokens/frame, 1fps), B-VLLM gains +11.8 on MVBench (50.8 vs 39.0), +20.7 on VideoMME-Short (60.8 vs 34.2), and +20.8 on VideoMME-Long (47.9 vs 27.1), confirming that adaptive frame selection resolves the context-window bottleneck. Against VideoLLaMA2 (8 uniform frames), B-VLLM improves MVBench by +5.3, EgoSchema by +9.7, and VideoMME-Long by +10.2, while slightly underperforming on VideoMME-Short (60.8 vs 48.9 is actually a gain; the -1.7 is for the integrated variant). On image benchmarks, B-VLLM with only 32 tokens per image beats LLaVA-1.5 (576 tokens) on MMBench (71.9 vs 64.7), ScienceQA (74.5 vs 66.8), and SEED (67.1 vs 66.1), but trails on VizWiz (45.1 vs 50.0), VQAv2 (76.6 vs 78.5), and TextVQA (54.8 vs 58.2), where fine-grained OCR and spatial reasoning demand more tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

B-VLLM positions itself against two dominant strategies in video VLLMs: extreme per-frame token compression (LLaMA-VID's 2 tokens/frame) and uniform frame sampling (VideoLLaMA2's fixed 8 frames). It repurposes the Q-Former from BLIP-2 as a text-conditioned frame selector operating on [CLS] tokens—a component most prior VLLMs discard—and introduces Gumbel-Softmax-based discrete frame selection combined with iterative Bipartite Merging for granular token-budget control. The token-merging lineage (ToMe, SD-TOME, VID-TOME, TOME-LLaVA) is extended here with a progressive, threshold-driven merging loop that prior methods lacked in flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—context-window overflow on long videos—with a modular framework that can be grafted onto existing VLLMs (LLaMA-VID, VideoLLaMA2) without retraining the backbone LLM, making it immediately adoptable. The empirical finding that 8–16 well-selected frames with sufficient spatial tokens nearly match 32 frames suggests substantial temporal redundancy in current video understanding tasks, which could inform more aggressive compression in future systems. Open questions include whether [CLS]-token-based selection generalizes to fine-grained spatial queries (the authors acknowledge this limitation) and whether the approach scales to hour-long videos without further architectural changes.
