## Problem

Learning-based LiDAR localization methods, particularly Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR), suffer catastrophic performance collapse when the query point cloud is rotated relative to training data (e.g., driving in reverse or making turns), with recall dropping below 1% beyond 30° yaw rotation. Existing methods and benchmarks largely overlook this rotation sensitivity, and common datasets lack trajectories with significant directional changes, leaving a gap in evaluating real-world robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR point cloud (64-beam spinning sensor, 64×1024 resolution) as input, randomly downsampled to 4,096 points for the canonicalization stage. It produces two outputs: a canonicalized point cloud (reoriented to a consistent reference frame defined by the training-set trajectory orientation) and a 6-DoF pose estimated via RANSAC on regressed world-coordinate correspondences. The canonicalized point cloud serves as an intermediate representation that is fed into the downstream SCR network, making the pipeline modular and integrable with existing localization backbones.

## Method

RALoc introduces a Point Cloud Canonicalization (PCC) module built on Vector Neurons (VNN) that exploits SO(3)-equivariance (φ(Rx) = Rφ(x)) to regress a rotation matrix aligning arbitrary input orientations to a canonical frame, followed by Schmidt orthogonalization to enforce valid rotation. The key architectural contribution is the Equivariant Key Feature Aggregation (EKFA) module, which computes rotation-invariant attention via Frobenius inner products between Q and K features, selects top-k key points to filter scene noise (dynamic objects, occlusions), and aggregates values through masked matrix multiplication. The canonicalized point cloud is then passed to SGLoc for Scene Coordinate Regression, where RANSAC estimates the final 6-DoF pose. Training uses a combined loss of MSE on the canonicalization rotation matrix (driving it toward identity) and L1 on SCR correspondences, requiring only 0.4M additional parameters over SGLoc with 26 ms inference time.

## Results

On the BiLiLo dataset (the paper's introduced benchmark with real reverse trajectories), RALoc achieves a mean position/orientation error of 4.00 m / 6.57° without any rotation augmentation, while baselines without augmentation collapse to 162–196 m position error on the same trajectories. On the Oxford Radar RobotCar dataset with large Yaw rotation (±180°), RALoc maintains 3.24 m / 3.91° error, whereas SGLoc and LiSA degrade to 340 m / 91° and 329 m / 78° respectively. On Oxford without rotation, RALoc achieves 3.24 m / 3.91°, slightly behind LiSA (2.74 m / 1.32°) and SGLoc (3.14 m / 1.88°) in orientation but competitive in position. Inference time is 26 ms with only 0.4 M additional parameters over SGLoc.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RALoc is compared against three baselines: SGLoc (SCR), LiSA (SCR with semantics), and DiffLoc (APR with diffusion). On the BiLiLo dataset without rotation augmentation, RALoc (4.00 m / 6.57°) dramatically outperforms all baselines, which suffer catastrophic failures on reverse trajectories (SGLoc: 190.80 m, LiSA: 162.26 m, DiffLoc: 196.14 m). Even with SO(3) rotation augmentation, RALoc still beats SGLoc (4.00 m vs. 6.96 m) and LiSA (4.00 m vs. 14.55 m) on BiLiLo. On the Oxford dataset without rotation, RALoc is slightly behind LiSA (3.24 m / 3.91° vs. 2.74 m / 1.32°) and SGLoc (3.14 m / 1.88°) in orientation error, but competitive in position. However, under large Yaw rotation on Oxford (±180°), RALoc maintains 3.24 m / 3.91° while SGLoc and LiSA degrade to 340 m / 91° and 329 m / 78° respectively, and DiffLoc to 136 m / 43°. The trade-off is clear: RALoc sacrifices ~2° orientation accuracy on standard (no-rotation) Oxford evaluation in exchange for robustness that prevents complete localization failure under rotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RALoc builds directly on SGLoc as its Scene Coordinate Regression backbone and on Vector Neurons (VNN) as the equivariant network foundation, while adopting the Frobenius inner product attention mechanism from VN-Transformer for its EKFA module. The key conceptual departure is that prior SCR methods (SGLoc, LiSA) and APR methods (DiffLoc) treat the input point cloud orientation as fixed, implicitly assuming the query aligns with the training distribution; RALoc is the first to insert an SO(3)-equivariant canonicalization stage before SCR, explicitly perceiving and correcting rotation at the feature level. The paper also positions itself against the data augmentation strategy for rotation robustness, arguing that equivariance provides a stronger inductive bias without the capacity and performance trade-offs of augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practically critical vulnerability: even state-of-the-art LiDAR localization methods suffer catastrophic failure (recall <1% at 30° yaw) when the vehicle direction deviates from the training distribution, a situation that arises routinely in real driving (lane changes, turns, oncoming traffic). The BiLiLo dataset with genuine reverse trajectories fills a notable gap in evaluation, since existing benchmarks (Oxford, NCLT, Boreas) lack opposing-direction data and prior rotation studies relied on synthetic rotation of point clouds. Open questions include whether the equivariant canonicalization generalizes to indoor or multi-modal settings, whether the slight orientation accuracy trade-off (RALoc vs. SGLoc without rotation) is acceptable in safety-critical deployment, and how the approach scales to larger, more diverse urban environments beyond the campus-scale BiLiLo collection.
