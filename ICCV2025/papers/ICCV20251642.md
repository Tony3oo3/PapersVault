## Problem

Existing AV2AV translation systems (e.g., AV2AV) rely on simple speaker embeddings in the vocoder and generate audio and visual components separately, failing to preserve speaker-specific paralinguistic characteristics such as voice timbre, emotional expression, and facial dynamics during cross-lingual translation. No prior method employs advanced conditional generative techniques with dual-modality guidance to jointly maintain these paralinguistic elements in a zero-shot setting.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a source audio-visual video, from which discrete AV speech units are extracted via m-AVHuBERT, an x-vector speaker embedding is computed from the source audio, and frame-level facial emotion embeddings are extracted via EmoFAN. It produces a translated mel-spectrogram that is converted to a raw audio waveform by HiFi-GAN and a lip-synced face video by Wav2Lip. A Duration Length Regulator interpolates the generated audio to match the original source video length, a constraint critical for real-world dubbing but absent in prior AV2AV work.

## Method

MAVFlow uses Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) as its core generative mechanism for mel-spectrogram synthesis, learning a conditional vector field that transports noise to target mel-spectrograms guided by multimodal paralinguistic features. The key architectural contribution is dual guidance: a global x-vector speaker embedding (averaged over multiple utterances during training, utterance-specific at inference) is concatenated to every frame's latent feature, while frame-level EmoFAN facial emotion embeddings are applied individually per timestep, capturing dynamic emotional shifts. These two embeddings—compressed to 80 dimensions each—are jointly fed into a Transformer-based CFM decoder alongside the translated discrete AV units and a masked mel-spectrogram, enabling the model to integrate global speaker identity with local emotional dynamics. The model is initialized from a LibriTTS-pretrained CFM checkpoint and fine-tuned on MuAViC, with the U2U translation module and unit extractor inherited from AV2AV to isolate the contribution of the CFM renderer.

## Results

On the MuAViC dataset, MAVFlow achieves the highest zero-shot speaker similarity (SS) across all four X-En language pairs: 0.49 (Es-En), 0.51 (Fr-En), 0.53 (It-En), and 0.48 (Pt-En), outperforming the direct AV2AV baseline by an average of 36% relative improvement. MAVFlow also achieves the lowest MCD-DTW (8.76–9.60) and MCD-DTW-SL (10.97–12.47) scores, confirming superior temporal alignment and duration consistency. In subjective evaluation, MAVFlow scores 3.49 (Similarity) and 4.01 (Naturalness) on MOS, surpassing AV2AV (3.33 / 3.58) and all cascaded systems. On visual quality (LRS3), MAVFlow achieves LSE-C of 8.30 and FID of 5.69, improving over AV2AV by +0.87 LSE-C and −0.61 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAVFlow consistently outperforms the direct textless baseline AV2AV [13] on all speaker similarity metrics across all four language pairs, with SS gains of +0.14 to +0.20 (e.g., 0.51 vs. 0.31 on Fr-En) and DTW-SL reductions of up to 3.32 (11.43 vs. 14.75 on It-En). Against the 4-stage cascaded system (AVSR+NMT+TTS+TFG), MAVFlow achieves higher SS (e.g., 0.51 vs. 0.34 on Fr-En) and substantially lower DTW-SL (10.97 vs. 17.00), while the 2-stage textless system (A2A+TFG) [29] performs worst on SS (0.02–0.11), confirming that removing speaker guidance entirely is detrimental. On translation quality (ASR-BLEU), MAVFlow slightly edges out AV2AV (e.g., 31.33 vs. 31.27 on Fr-En) but remains below the 4-stage cascaded system (28.70–30.55), indicating a small trade-off in semantic fidelity for paralinguistic preservation. In subjective MOS evaluation, MAVFlow surpasses AV2AV by +0.16 on Similarity and +0.43 on Naturalness, while cascaded systems lag significantly (2.81–2.89 Similarity). On visual quality, MAVFlow improves LSE-C by +0.87 and reduces FID by −0.61 over AV2AV, and on CREMA-D emotion evaluation, it achieves 36.46% audio Emo-Acc vs. 33.66% for AV2AV and 72.68% visual Emo-Acc vs. 67.20%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAVFlow builds directly on the AV2AV framework [13], which introduced discrete m-AVHuBERT units and U2U translation to bypass intermediate text in audio-visual speech translation. The key departure is replacing AV2AV's simple d-vector-conditioned vocoder with an OT-CFM-based mel-spectrogram generator that accepts dual multimodal guidance: x-vector speaker embeddings from audio and EmoFAN facial emotion embeddings from visual input. This positions the work at the intersection of conditional flow matching for TTS (Matcha-TTS [44]) and multimodal speaker-conditioned generation, extending the CFM paradigm from single-modality text conditioning to joint audio-visual paralinguistic conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multilingual dubbing and video translation: maintaining speaker identity and emotional expression when the same speaker must be rendered in a language they did not originally speak. The finding that higher-quality mel-spectrograms (from CFM with dual guidance) cascade into better face generation via Wav2Lip (LSE +0.87, FID −0.61) suggests that investing in audio synthesis quality has downstream visual benefits, a cross-modal coupling that prior separated-pipeline approaches overlooked. However, face generation remains a separate Wav2Lip module rather than being jointly learned, zero-shot emotion accuracy is still moderate (~36%), and the approach depends on pretrained components (m-AVHuBERT, EmoFAN, Wav2Lip) that limit end-to-end optimization. Open questions include whether fully joint audio-visual generation (rather than sequential mel→face) would further improve consistency, and whether the dual-guidance strategy generalizes beyond the five languages tested.
