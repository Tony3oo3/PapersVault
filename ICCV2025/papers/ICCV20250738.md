## Problem

Previous event-based deblurring methods fuse event and image features in a single step, overlooking that events contain two inherently conflicting information types: sharp edge details captured over short exposure periods and motion trajectories accumulated over longer periods. This one-step fusion biases the model toward capturing either edge or motion, resulting in coarse edges and blurred textures. The paper identifies this edge-motion conflict as unexplored in the event community and proposes to disentangle the two cues before integrating them with RGB features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250738/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB image and a corresponding event stream represented as SCER (Symmetric Cumulative Event Representation) with 2N=16 frames accumulated symmetrically around a midpoint. The event stream is internally split: channels closest to the midpoint (E_{N-1}, E_N) serve as edge priors, while channels at both extremes (E_0, E_{2N-1}) serve as motion priors. The output is a single deblurred image at the same resolution as the input, trained on 256×256 crops with a batch size of 16.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stage U-Net (built on EFNet) that separates event features into edge and motion cues before fusing them with image features. The Edge-aware Sharpening module concatenates short-exposure event features with image features as a query, then performs cross-attention with the full multi-scale event features as key and value, selectively enhancing edge information. The Motion-driven Scale-adaptive Deblurring module extracts motion cues via cross-attention using extreme SCER frames as queries, then feeds these cues into a convolution to compute spatially-varying offsets for deformable convolutions, enabling the receptive field to adapt to different blur kernel scales. The second stage applies an EMGC (Event Mask Gated Connection) module that uses event-triggered masks to route decoder features toward severely blurred regions. Training uses a weighted sum of PSNR losses from both stages, with Adam optimizer and cosine annealing over 400k iterations.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, the proposed single-frame method achieves 36.70 dB PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, matching the multi-frame FEVD method and outperforming the best single-frame event-based method SpkNet by +0.58 dB. On real-world datasets, the method attains 38.53 dB on REBlur, 32.05 dB on EVRB (surpassing the multi-frame CMTA by +0.67 dB), and 32.14 dB on REVD. The approach uses only a single blurred image and its corresponding event stream, yet rivals or exceeds methods requiring 2–7 frames for inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-frame event-based baseline SpkNet (36.12 dB), the proposed method gains +0.58 dB on GoPro, and over EFNet (35.46 dB) it improves by +1.24 dB. In the multi-frame category, the single-frame result (36.70 dB) ties FEVD (36.70 dB), beats CMTA-5 (36.55 dB) by +0.15 dB, and trails CMTA-7 (36.78 dB) by only −0.08 dB. On the real-world EVRB dataset, the method surpasses the multi-frame CMTA by +0.67 dB (32.05 vs. 31.38) and the single-frame EFNet by +1.07 dB. On REBlur it leads EFNet by +0.41 dB (38.53 vs. 38.12). The one notable loss is on REVD, where the multi-frame FEVD (32.99 dB) still outperforms the proposed single-frame method (32.14 dB) by 0.85 dB, suggesting that in some real-world scenarios additional temporal context remains beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EFNet's two-stage U-Net architecture, SCER event representation, and EMGC module, but departs from the one-step event-image fusion paradigm used by EFNet, REFID, and UEVD. The key conceptual departure is the observation that event features contain two inherently conflicting signals—sharp edges from short-exposure accumulation and motion trajectories from long-exposure accumulation—and that indiscriminate fusion biases the model toward one at the expense of the other. Additionally, while prior event-based video deblurring methods (FEVD, CMTA) use events for frame-to-frame alignment, this paper repurposes motion cues to guide deformable convolutions for single-frame deblurring, shifting the role of events from temporal alignment to spatial receptive-field adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single-frame event-based method can match or slightly surpass multi-frame video deblurring approaches (e.g., CMTA-7) suggests that the temporal information in events can be exploited more efficiently than previously assumed, potentially reducing the need for multi-frame inference in practical deployments. The edge/motion disentangling principle may generalize to other event-based tasks where heterogeneous temporal information is fused with images. However, the performance margins are modest (≤0.58 dB), the method still depends on high-quality event streams (limiting real-world applicability where event cameras are noisy or unavailable), and it remains unclear whether the deformable-convolution-based blur kernel estimation generalizes to highly non-uniform or multi-object blur patterns beyond the tested datasets.
