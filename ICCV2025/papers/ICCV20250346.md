## Problem

Existing physics-based motion imitation methods can restore physical plausibility for daily motions (walking, running) estimated from monocular video, but fail on high-difficulty motions like backflips, gymnastics, and martial arts. This failure stems from two compounding issues: video motion capture algorithms produce flawed/noisy reference motions on extreme poses, and the complex dynamics of high-difficulty movements exceed the tracking capacity of current single-policy controllers, which also suffer catastrophic forgetting across diverse skills.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a video clip and the corresponding 3D motion sequence (SMPL joint positions and 6D rotations) estimated by any upstream video motion capture algorithm, along with per-frame human segmentation masks (from SAM) and 2D keypoints extracted from the video. It outputs a physically plausible 3D human motion in the same SMPL format, designed as a plug-and-play post-processing module that can be appended to any existing capture pipeline without modifying the front-end. The method also applies to text-to-motion generation outputs, restoring physical realism while preserving generation quality metrics.

## Method

The approach combines two modules: (1) a Mask-conditioned Motion Correction Module (MCM), a diffusion-based in-betweening model that detects flawed motion segments via OKS/mask-matching scores and regenerates them conditioned on ViT-extracted mask features and surrounding motion context, fine-tuned with a physics-informed loss from successful PTM simulations; and (2) a Physics-based Motion Transfer Module (PTM) that uses a pre-train-and-adapt strategy where a motion prediction prior (MultiTransMotion) is trained on large-scale data and then frozen during test-time RL adaptation, while a PPO controller with AMP style reward is updated on the specific test motion using residual actions (p_t^d = p_t^r + a_t). The adaptation employs a relative reconstruction reward that ignores absolute root position, a joint-distance-based early termination condition to handle noisy references, and a mask-guided reward combining CLIP semantic similarity and IoU between the projected humanoid mesh and the video segmentation mask to maintain video consistency.

## Results

On the in-the-wild high-difficulty dataset (Table 2), GVHMR+Ours achieves OKS 0.865, MPS 0.718, SP 0.089, GP 0.256, Float 12.762, and FS 0.651, dramatically outperforming GVHMR alone (GP 9.999, Float 137.969) and GVHMR+PhysPT (GP 6.616, Float 54.032). On EMDB, ground penetration drops from 82.266 (GVHMR) to 0.248 (GVHMR+Ours), and floating from 510.298 to 3.625. In motion imitation (Table 7), PTM achieves 96.87% success rate versus 72.74% for PHC+ and 45.63% for UHC on the merged AIST++/kungfu set. For text-to-motion (Table 6), the plug-and-play module reduces GP from 69.726 to 0.241 (T2M-GPT) and from 20.672 to 0.233 (MoMask) while preserving FID and diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRAM [72] and GVHMR [56] (video motion capture baselines), the proposed method preserves or slightly improves camera-coordinate accuracy (e.g., MPJPE 91.78 vs 91.81 for TRAM on AIST++) while reducing ground penetration by 1–2 orders of magnitude (GP 20.56→0.50 for TRAM, 82.27→0.25 for GVHMR on EMDB) and floating by up to 140× (510.30→3.63 on EMDB). Against PhysPT [83] (physics-aware transformer), the paper's method is decisively superior: PhysPT severely degrades world-coordinate metrics (WA-MJE jumps from 109.10 to 781.13 for GVHMR on EMDB) and cannot handle flawed motions, whereas the proposed method maintains or improves WA-MJE (91.15) while fixing all physical artifacts. Against PHC+ [40] (perpetual humanoid control), PTM achieves 96.87% success rate in motion imitation versus 72.74% for PHC+ (Table 7), and in the in-the-wild ablation (Table 5), PHC+ with test-time adaptation still fails in 58% of cases versus 13% for the full method. Against UHC [37], PTM more than doubles the success rate (96.87% vs 45.63%) and reduces MPJPE from 116.24 to 65.51. For text-to-motion, the plug-and-play module reduces GP from 69.73 to 0.24 (T2M-GPT [81]) and from 20.67 to 0.23 (MoMask [15]) with negligible FID change, confirming generalizability beyond video capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based motion imitation line (PHC+, UHC, DeepMimic/AMP) by introducing a pre-train-and-adapt paradigm with a frozen motion prediction prior (MultiTransMotion) to handle noisy, high-difficulty reference motions that break existing single-policy controllers. It departs from prior physics-informed video capture methods (PhysPT, SimPoE, PhysCap) that rely on simplified dynamics or per-motion parameter tuning, instead using a two-stage pipeline: a mask-conditioned diffusion model (MCM) to repair kinematic flaws, followed by RL-based test-time adaptation (PTM) for physical restoration. The use of segmentation masks (via SAM) as a stable 2D guidance signal—rather than the more common 2D keypoints—is a deliberate design choice motivated by the instability of keypoint detection under motion blur and extreme poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and the 206-video in-the-wild high-difficulty benchmark (rhythmic gymnastics, martial arts, yoga) provide the community with both a practical tool and a challenging evaluation set that existing methods fail on. The demonstrated ability to reduce ground penetration from 82 to 0.24 (EMDB) and achieve 87% simulation success where PHC+ manages only 21% highlights a significant gap in current motion imitation for complex dynamics. Key limitations include single-person-only handling, 2–3 day A100 pre-training cost, and 2,000–4,000 adaptation steps for high-difficulty motions, which limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the pre-train/adapt strategy generalizes to multi-person interactions and whether the frozen-prior mechanism can be made more sample-efficient.
