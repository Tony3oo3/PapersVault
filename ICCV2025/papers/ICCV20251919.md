## Problem

Vehicle Re-ID suffers from large intraclass appearance discrepancy caused by multi-view variations, yet existing datasets only cover constrained view ranges and no task-specific pre-training paradigm has been developed for this domain. Mainstream vehicle Re-ID methods remain limited by generic ImageNet pre-training, which has a significant domain gap with vehicle imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251919/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 RGB vehicle images paired with multi-view semantic segmentation masks that partition each image into a background region and four view regions (front, rear, side, top). The pre-trained model outputs a single view-invariant feature vector from a mean encoder (parameter-averaged from two homogeneous MAE encoders), which is then fine-tuned with cross-entropy and triplet losses for identity matching. The supporting DiffVERI dataset provides 1.7M images at 496×485 resolution with diverse synthesized views generated via DreamBooth fine-tuning on VERI-Wild.

## Method

VehicleMAE employs two homogeneous masked autoencoders (CLIP-B/16 backbone) trained jointly via two modules. The VMIM module applies an asymmetric mask sampling strategy that generates non-overlapping visible patch maps for the two MAEs, forcing each to learn complementary reconstruction knowledge; reconstruction targets are restricted to the identity-related vehicle body region only (not the full image), covering both RGB pixels and multi-view semantic labels. The PPMD module establishes a cross-guided teacher-student relationship: within the current epoch, the two MAEs act as mutual students distilling decoder features via KL divergence, while in subsequent epochs the previous-epoch models serve as cross-teachers (φ₁′ guides φ₂ and vice versa) distilling encoder intermediate features. After 300 epochs of pre-training on DiffVERI, all decoders are discarded and the final backbone is the parameter average of the two encoders, yielding a single 57.3M-parameter model for downstream Re-ID.

## Results

On VeRi-776 (supervised), VehicleMAE achieves 87.6 mAP and 97.4% Rank-1, outperforming the best prior method (CLIP-ReID at 83.3 mAP / 97.4% Rank-1) by 4.3 mAP. On VehicleID (supervised), it reaches 85.9% Rank-1 and 94.9% Rank-5, surpassing VehicleNet (79.5% / 92.0%) by 6.4 Rank-1 points. In the unsupervised setting, VehicleMAE attains 42.5 mAP and 87.6% Rank-1 on VeRi-776, and 73.3% Rank-1 / 85.6% Rank-5 on VehicleID, consistently leading all compared methods including MAE, CL-MAE, and CMAE re-implemented on DiffVERI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the supervised setting, VehicleMAE (87.6 mAP / 97.4% Rank-1 on VeRi-776) outperforms the strongest ResNet-based method HCI-Net (83.8 / 96.6) by 3.8 mAP and the CLIP-based CLIP-ReID (83.3 / 97.4) by 4.3 mAP; on VehicleID it leads VehicleNet (79.5% Rank-1) by 6.4 points and TANet (78.2%) by 7.7 points. In the unsupervised setting, VehicleMAE (42.5 mAP / 87.6% Rank-1 on VeRi-776) edges out STDA (42.3 / 87.4) and NNNI (42.3 / 86.3) by 0.2 mAP and 1.3 Rank-1 points respectively, and surpasses AdaMG (41.0 / 86.2) by 1.5 mAP. Among MAE-based baselines re-implemented on DiffVERI, VehicleMAE improves over CMAE (41.6 / 84.7) by 0.9 mAP and 2.9 Rank-1, over CL-MAE (41.0 / 84.0) by 1.5 mAP and 3.6 Rank-1, and over vanilla MAE (37.6 / 81.2) by 4.9 mAP and 6.4 Rank-1. On VehicleID (unsupervised), VehicleMAE (73.3% Rank-1 / 85.6% Rank-5) beats CMAE (71.1 / 84.9) by 2.2 Rank-1 and CL-MAE (70.8 / 82.6) by 2.5 Rank-1. The paper does not report any comparison where VehicleMAE underperforms a baseline on the reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE encoder-decoder architecture (He et al., CVPR 2022) to vehicle Re-ID by introducing two task-specific modifications: an asymmetric mask sampling strategy that forces two homogeneous MAEs to learn complementary knowledge, and identity-related region reconstruction that limits the reconstruction target to the vehicle body rather than the full image. It also draws on the self-supervised pre-training paradigm established for person Re-ID (Fu et al., LUPerson) but is the first to apply large-scale masked image modeling specifically to vehicle Re-ID. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes DreamBooth (a subject-driven text-to-image diffusion model) and SAM with LoRA adapters for multi-view semantic annotation, combining generative AI tools in a way not previously applied to Re-ID benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffVERI (1.7M images with multi-view semantic annotations) fills a clear gap: prior vehicle Re-ID datasets are limited to constrained view ranges from real surveillance footage, making it difficult to learn view-invariant representations. The practical impact is that task-specific pre-training on a diverse-view benchmark can substantially narrow the domain gap between generic ImageNet pre-training and vehicle Re-ID, as demonstrated by consistent gains over both ResNet- and ViT-based baselines. However, the synthetic data generated by DreamBooth may not fully capture real-world sensor artifacts, lighting variations, and occlusion patterns, and the evaluation is limited to two standard benchmarks with a single backbone (CLIP-B/16). Open questions include whether the mutual distillation mechanism generalizes to other Re-ID domains (person, object) and whether the diffusion-generated views truly transfer to real multi-view surveillance scenarios.
