## Problem

Inverse adversarial training methods (e.g., UIAT) generate high-confidence examples to guide adversarial logit alignment, but this paper reveals that the resulting high-confidence outputs are driven by biased feature activation: the model's attention systematically shifts from discriminative foreground objects to irrelevant background regions. This spurious correlation bias causes overfitting to non-causal contextual cues, degrading both adversarial robustness and generalization performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250788/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and internally generates both forward adversarial examples (PGD, ε=8/255) and inverse adversarial examples (inverse PGD, ε=4/255). A Grad-CAM attention map is computed on the natural image to partition the inverse adversarial example into foreground and background pixel regions via a threshold ω. The network outputs standard class logits, but the training signal additionally produces debiased high-confidence logits (full inverse-adversarial logits minus background-only logits) used as a regularization target.

## Method

DHAT augments standard adversarial training with two complementary regularizers. First, Debiased High-Confidence Logit Regularization (DHLR) uses a Grad-CAM attention map to mask out background pixels from the inverse adversarial example, computes the logits of this background-only input, and subtracts them from the full inverse-adversarial logits to obtain debiased high-confidence targets; adversarial example logits are then aligned to these debiased targets via KL divergence. Second, Foreground Logit Orthogonal Enhancement (FLOE) minimizes the p-norm of the residual after projecting the high-confidence logits onto the background-feature logits in affine space, directly reducing the correlation between predictions and background cues. The total loss is L_AT + λ₁·L_DHLR + λ₂·L_FLOE, and DHAT can be plugged into existing AT baselines (AWP, CFA) by replacing their L_AT term.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WRN28-10, ε=8/255), DHAT achieves 60.49% PGD-10 robust accuracy and 53.10% AutoAttack accuracy, with a robust generalization gap of only 3.51%—the lowest among all compared methods. On CIFAR-100, DHAT reaches 35.82% PGD-10 and 30.17% AA, and on ImageNet-1K (ε=4/255) it attains 46.83% PGD-10 and 41.70% AA. When combined with CFA (DHAT-CFA), performance further improves to 62.67% PGD-10 on CIFAR-10 and 48.27% PGD-10 on ImageNet-1K, setting new state-of-the-art robustness across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHAT outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and attack metric. On CIFAR-10, DHAT surpasses the closest competitor UIAT by 1.83% under PGD-10 (60.49% vs. 58.66%) and by 0.93% under AutoAttack (53.10% vs. 52.17%), while reducing the robust generalization gap from 7.92% to 3.51%. Against MART, the margin is larger: 4.24% on PGD-10 and 2.43% on AA, with the robust gap halved (3.51% vs. 9.52%). DHAT also beats AWP (57.80% PGD-10), FSR (56.69%), CFA (57.87%), and SGLR (57.53%) by 2.69–3.80% on PGD-10 on CIFAR-10. The most striking advantage is in generalization: DHAT's robust gap of 3.51% on CIFAR-10 is roughly half that of UIAT and less than half that of MART or SGLR, confirming that the debiasing mechanism effectively eliminates the spurious correlation overfitting that plagues inverse-adversarial-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the inverse adversarial training framework of UIAT (Dong et al., CVPR 2023), which itself builds on the unadversarial example concept of Salman et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and the logit-alignment paradigm of MART (Wang et al., ICLR 2020). The key departure is a diagnostic finding: UIAT's high-confidence logits are contaminated by biased background feature activation, so the paper replaces the raw high-confidence target with a debiased version (DHLR) and adds an orthogonality constraint (FLOE) to decouple logits from background features. Conceptually, it repositions the logit-alignment objective from 'align with whatever the model is confident about' to 'align with what the model is confident about *after removing spurious background contributions*.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of spurious correlation bias as a hidden failure mode of inverse adversarial training is a useful diagnostic for the adversarial-robustness community, since UIAT and related methods are increasingly adopted. The robust generalization gap reduction (e.g., 3.51% vs. 9.38% on CIFAR-10) suggests the method addresses a real overfitting mechanism rather than merely squeezing out a small accuracy bump. Open questions include whether the Grad-CAM-based background separation generalizes to transformer-based backbones, whether the threshold ω can be learned rather than hand-tuned per dataset, and whether the debiasing principle transfers to other alignment-based defenses beyond logit-level regularization.
