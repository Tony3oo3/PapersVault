## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video enhancement methods rely solely on the knowledge embedded in pretrained video diffusion models, which limits their ability to generate fine-grained local details such as texture. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a high-quality image reference as an additional knowledge source to bridge the fine-grained detail gap between low-quality and high-quality videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252065/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality video as input, which is first up-sampled to the target resolution via bicubic interpolation and encoded into latent space by a 2D-VAE. A key frame (default: first frame) is extracted to synthesize a semantics-aligned HQ image reference. The output is a high-quality video at the target resolution, with the noise level (n) adjustable at inference to trade off between fidelity to the input and generation of novel details.

## Method

GenVE pursues two complementary alignments: (1) global semantic alignment, where an SDXL-based image diffuser up-scales a key frame using a weighted summation of LQ frame features during denoising (with a cosine-scheduled coefficient γ_t that decays from 0.5 to 0), producing a semantics-aligned HQ image reference; and (2) local texture alignment, where a ControlNet-derived video controller integrated into the I2VGen-XL 3D-UNet employs a Local-Aware Cross-Attention (LCA) module that treats video feature patches as queries and image reference patches as keys/values, augmented with a local attention bias matrix and random patch reshuffling within a neighborhood of size d to enforce spatially-local texture transfer. Three conditioning augmentation strategies—noise augmentation (forward diffusion with random steps), temporal augmentation (optical-flow warping of the image reference via RAFT), and random masking of reference latents—are applied during training to improve robustness and temporal coherence.

## Results

GenVE achieves the best scores across all three metrics (MUSIQ, DOVER, CLIP-IQA) on all three datasets. On YouHQ40, GenVE reaches 69.93 MUSIQ, 83.50 DOVER, and 0.596 CLIP-IQA, outperforming the strongest baseline VEnhancer (68.99 / 77.08 / 0.594). On the real-world VideoLQ benchmark, GenVE scores 68.23 MUSIQ and 74.30 DOVER, and on the AI-synthesized AIGC-Vid dataset it attains 64.56 MUSIQ, 77.02 DOVER, and 0.658 CLIP-IQA, again leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenVE outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is VEnhancer, which also uses a diffusion-based video enhancement paradigm with ControlNet-style conditioning; GenVE surpasses it by +0.94 MUSIQ and +6.42 DOVER on YouHQ40, +2.04 MUSIQ and +2.83 DOVER on VideoLQ, and +0.26 MUSIQ and +0.56 DOVER on AIGC-Vid. LaVie-SR, a cascaded latent diffusion model, achieves competitive DOVER scores (79.64 on YouHQ40) but lags GenVE by 3.86 points on that metric and by a wider margin on MUSIQ. Upscale-A-Video, which freezes spatial layers of an image diffusion model and trains only temporal propagation, is consistently outperformed by GenVE on all metrics, with the largest gap on DOVER (e.g., 83.50 vs. 74.06 on YouHQ40). DATSR, a reference-based image super-resolution method, performs poorly on video metrics (e.g., 30.04 DOVER on YouHQ40), confirming that image-only SR does not transfer well to the video enhancement setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenVE extends the diffusion-based video enhancement paradigm established by LaVie, Upscale-A-Video, and VEnhancer, which all treat enhancement as conditional video diffusion guided by the LQ video itself. Its key departure is borrowing the reference-based super-resolution idea (from TTSR, Landmark, CrossNet) and transplanting it into the video diffusion setting: a semantics-aligned HQ image reference is first synthesized via an image diffuser, then a local-aware cross-attention (LCA) module transfers patch-level texture from that reference into the 3D-UNet video controller. The conditioning augmentation strategies (noise, temporal warping via RAFT optical flow, and random masking) are novel combinations not seen in prior video enhancement work, drawing from data augmentation practices in image SR and robustness training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation of current diffusion-based video enhancers—their inability to generate convincing fine-grained local textures—by introducing an image reference as an explicit texture source, which is a practical and interpretable solution. The ablation showing that masking conditioning contributes the single largest DOVER gain (~2 points) suggests that forcing the model to search broadly across the reference, rather than relying on a single aligned patch, is more important than the attention mechanism itself, an insight that could inform future reference-based generation. Open questions include computational cost (two sequential diffusion processes plus optical flow estimation), scalability to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the approach extends to other degradation types beyond the synthetic multi-tier model used in training.
