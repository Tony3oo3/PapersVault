## Problem

Existing ControlNet-based models apply global conditions that affect the entire image, making it impossible to independently control individual elements or resolve conflicts when multiple conditions describe different parts of a scene. This leads to condition misunderstandings, artifacts in overlapping regions, and an inability to specify element ordering or occlusion relationships, severely limiting multi-condition image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250812/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts per-element content conditions (canny/HED edge maps, depth, normal, segmentation, color, or text) and per-element layout conditions (dot, box, or mask maps) at 1024×1024 resolution, along with a user-specified element ordering for occlusion resolution. Unlike standard ControlNet which requires a single merged global condition image, DC-ControlNet consumes conditions independently for each element, and the output is a single composited image where each element respects its own content and layout while honoring the specified inter-element order.

## Method

DC-ControlNet decouples global control into a two-level hierarchy: an Intra-Element Controller and an Inter-Element Controller. The Intra-Element Controller encodes multiple content types via pluggable encoders (ControlNet encoder for edges, ControlNetPlus for geometry, ConvEncoder for color, BERT for text), then injects them into a layout embedding (dot/box/mask) through ResBlock + Transformer blocks with cross-attention using RoPE and a fixed positional offset Δ to handle content-layout misalignment; Cross-Normalization preserves the original feature distribution to prevent convergence issues. The Inter-Element Controller assigns order embeddings to define layer relationships, then applies a Spatial Reweighing Transformer (2D RoPE, sigmoid-gated weights) and a Layer Reweighing Transformer (1D order embedding, softmax-gated weights) to resolve occlusion and spatial conflicts between elements. Training is two-stage on the newly introduced DMC-120k dataset (120k multi-element samples generated via SDXL/FLUX with GroundingDino-based decoupling), using a foreground-reweighted MSE loss plus an L1 transform loss, based on SDXL with AdamW at lr=1e-4 for 50k steps on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the DMC-120k test set, DC-ControlNet achieves 90.12% object-existence accuracy (Acc_exist) and 89.52% occlusion accuracy (Acc_occ), substantially outperforming all baselines. The FID score of 23.46 is the lowest among all methods, and CLIP Score (0.3042) and CLIP Aesthetic Score (6.1820) are also the highest. The most striking gap is in occlusion accuracy, where DC-ControlNet (~89.5%) roughly doubles the performance of the next-best baselines (HiCo at 45.76%, UniControlNet at 45.70%), reflecting its ability to correctly handle element ordering and occlusion relationships.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-ControlNet wins on every metric against all eight baselines. The largest margin is in occlusion accuracy (Acc_occ): DC-ControlNet reaches 89.52% while the strongest baselines (HiCo 45.76%, UniControlNet 45.70%, T2I-Adapter 45.45%) cluster around 45%, a gap of roughly 44 percentage points. In object-existence accuracy, DC-ControlNet (90.12%) leads the closest competitor MultiControlNet (81.80%) by 8.3 points, while UniControl (43.03%) and ControlNet++ (46.12%) trail by over 40 points. On FID, DC-ControlNet (23.46) is 15.7 points better than MultiControlNet (39.15) and dramatically ahead of ControlNet++ (111.63). Among layout-to-image methods (LayoutDiffusion, InstanceDiff, HiCo), DC-ControlNet improves FID by 27–40 points and Acc_occ by ~44 points, confirming that decoupled element-level control is far more effective than bounding-box-only layout conditioning. The CLIP-based quality metrics (Score and Aesthetic) show smaller but consistent gains, with DC-ControlNet leading by 0.012 in CLIP Score and 0.18 in CLIP Aes over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-ControlNet extends the ControlNet paradigm by decomposing its monolithic global conditioning into a two-level hierarchy: an Intra-Element Controller that fuses content (edge, depth, color, text) with layout (dot/box/mask) per element, and an Inter-Element Controller that resolves occlusion and ordering across elements. It borrows Cross-Normalization from ControlNeXt to stabilize feature injection and foreground reweighing from GeoDiffusion for the loss, but departs fundamentally from both ControlNet's single-global-condition design and layout-to-image methods (LayoutDiffusion, HiCo) that restrict spatial control to bounding boxes. The key conceptual shift is treating multi-condition generation as a structured, element-wise problem with explicit layer ordering rather than an ad-hoc feature-merging problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in creative workflows: users cannot independently specify content and layout for individual objects while controlling their relative depth ordering, a gap that both ControlNet and layout-to-image methods leave open. The DMC-120k dataset and its element-decoupled annotation protocol provide a reusable benchmark for this setting. However, evaluation is confined to the authors' own dataset, the VLM-based accuracy metrics introduce subjectivity, and the method is tied to the SDXL/ControlNet ecosystem; open questions include scalability to many elements, extension to video, and whether the hierarchical decoupling generalizes to architectures beyond U-Net-based diffusion models.
