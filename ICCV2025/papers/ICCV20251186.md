## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation amplify or suppress logits without directly detecting which tokens are hallucinated, often introducing new hallucinations rather than reducing them. The lack of precise, adaptive control over visual uncertainty means these methods cannot selectively enhance accurate tokens while suppressing spurious ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query as input, and additionally processes a filtered version of the image (e.g., sharpen, Sobel, Gaussian) to generate hallucination-amplified logits at decoding time. The output is a generated text response, with the candidate token set constrained to those whose probability exceeds β (0.1) times the maximum token probability. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates purely at inference time on any LVLM architecture.

## Method

FuzzyCD operates in two stages. First, in fuzzy rule inference, the log-probability of the top-1 token from both the original and filtered-image logits is fuzzified into 'low,' 'medium,' and 'high' confidence categories using Gaussian membership functions parameterized by the dataset mean and standard deviation. A Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy model with nine rules (all 3×3 combinations) computes rule weights as products of membership degrees, and each rule applies a corresponding amplification coefficient to the filtered-image logits; notably, Rule 3 (original low, filtered high) applies a negative coefficient to reduce rather than amplify, preventing suppression of correct tokens. Second, in contrastive decoding, the amplified logits are combined via a weighted average across all filters, the logit with the highest Jensen-Shannon divergence from the original distribution is identified, and the final logits are computed as (1+α)·original − α·amplified, from which the next token is sampled.

## Results

On the POPE/MSCOCO benchmark, FuzzyCD achieves 88.97% accuracy with LLaVA-1.5 (Random setting), a 6.07%p gain over the default model (82.90%) and 1.24%p over the strongest baseline VCD (87.73%). For InstructBLIP, FuzzyCD reaches 86.66% accuracy (Random), outperforming the default by 6.03%p. On the MME benchmark, FuzzyCD attains the highest total score of 641.66, surpassing VDD (620) and VCD (604.66), with particularly large gains in object existence (+19.33%p) and object counting (+56.66%p) over default decoding. On ROPE, FuzzyCD improves multi-object accuracy by 7.16%p (in-wild) and 14.56%p (homogeneous) over the base LLaVA-1.5 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FuzzyCD outperforms all four baselines (VCD, ICD, VDD, OPERA) on every POPE/MSCOCO setting for both LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP. On LLaVA-1.5 Random, FuzzyCD leads VCD by 1.24%p in accuracy and 1.24%p in F1; on InstructBLIP Popular, it surpasses the next-best ICD by 2.27%p in accuracy. On ROPE, FuzzyCD achieves the highest multi-object accuracy in all three settings (21.12% in-wild, 46.44% homogeneous, 7.45% heterogeneous), beating VDD (the strongest competitor) by 3.32%p and 5.67%p in the in-wild and homogeneous settings respectively. On MME, FuzzyCD attains the best total score (641.66) and leads in object existence (195) and counting (163.33), though it trails VDD slightly on color accuracy (160 vs. 165). The model-scale ablation confirms FuzzyCD's advantage persists across architectures from 7B to 72B, with the largest gains (5.12–5.41%p) on 7B models and smaller but positive gains (0.72–4.46%p) on larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding lineage for hallucination mitigation (VCD, ICD, VDD, OPERA), which amplifies hallucination logits via distorted or filtered visual inputs and subtracts them from original logits. The key conceptual departure is introducing a detection-first mechanism: rather than blindly amplifying and subtracting, FuzzyCD uses log-probability as a confidence score and applies Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy inference to dynamically weight how much amplification each token receives based on whether hallucination is actually likely. This repurposes the T-S fuzzy model from control theory as a soft gating mechanism over contrastive decoding, addressing the prior methods' inability to distinguish genuine hallucination logits from correct tokens that happen to appear in filtered-image outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy, FuzzyCD offers a practical path to reducing object hallucination without the computational and annotation costs of fine-tuning or RLHF approaches. The consistent gains across POPE, ROPE, and MME on both LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP, plus the model-scale ablation showing benefits even at 72B, support broad applicability. However, the fuzzy rule structure (3 categories × 9 rules) is somewhat ad hoc and hand-designed, and the paper acknowledges limited evaluation on attribute-level tasks and general VQA. Open questions include whether the T-S rule architecture is optimal versus learned alternatives, and whether the approach transfers to video or multi-turn dialogue settings where hallucination dynamics differ.
