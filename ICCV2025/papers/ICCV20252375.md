## Problem

Pose-free surface reconstruction from sparse unposed images remains challenging because traditional SfM-based pose estimation fails without dense visual overlap, and existing joint learning approaches require additional geometric priors such as depth maps, sufficient view overlap, or extra camera intrinsic information. The paper addresses the gap of simultaneously achieving multi-view consistent camera pose estimation and geometrically consistent surface reconstruction from as few as 2–6 unposed images, without relying on dense feature matching or external geometric supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of 2–6 unposed RGB images as input and jointly produces per-image camera poses (extrinsic and intrinsic parameters recovered by solving the intersection of neural bundle rays) and a 3D surface mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a triplane-based SDF. Camera poses are parameterized as sets of 7-dimensional neural bundle rays (Plücker coordinates plus a depth scalar) rather than standard 6DoF vectors, and the SDF is a triplane representation with a Transformer-based image encoder and MLP decoder, queried at ~16,000 points per training iteration.

## Method

GCRayDiffusion formulates camera pose estimation as a diffusion process over neural bundle rays, where each ray carries an additional depth attribute that enables tracing its intersection point with the object surface, creating a differentiable bridge between pose and geometry. The key architectural innovation is a geometric consistent denoiser that conditions the noise prediction on the triplane-based SDF values evaluated at the ray end points, injecting global 3D geometric consistency into the bundle adjustment and yielding multi-view consistent poses. During joint training, the SDF learning is regularized by the denoising trajectory: intermediate noisy sampling points are supervised with ground-truth SDF values, while the final denoised points are forced to lie on the zero level-set of the SDF (F_θ(R_T^d) → 0), effectively coupling pose refinement with surface learning. The system is trained in two stages—coarse SDF pre-training followed by fine-tuning of both the diffusion denoiser and the triplane SDF—with a classifier-free guidance scale of 3 applied to the SDF conditioning for optimal pose accuracy.

## Results

On the Objaverse dataset (4 input images), GCRayDiffusion achieves 92.32% rotation accuracy (RotAcc@15°) and 62.62% translation accuracy (CenAcc@0.1), outperforming the closest competitor DUSt3R (92.26% / 62.03%) and significantly surpassing RayDiffusion (87.20% / 41.28%) and FORGE (88.36% / 41.33%). For surface reconstruction, GCRayDiffusion achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.125), Hausdorff Distance (0.323), Normal Consistency (0.997), and F-score (0.99) on Objaverse, with consistent improvements on GSO (HD 0.302 vs. DUSt3R's 0.366) and CO3D (CD 0.169, NC 0.977, F-score 0.953). The method maintains its advantage across 2–6 input images, with the largest margins over SfM-based baselines (COLMAP, RelPose++) exceeding 60 percentage points in rotation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCRayDiffusion outperforms all seven baselines on camera pose estimation across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor DUSt3R, the gains are modest but consistent: +0.06% RotAcc and +0.59% CenAcc on Objaverse (4 images), and +1.0% to +1.4% CenAcc on GSO and CO3D. The advantage over diffusion-based pose estimators is more pronounced: RayDiffusion trails by 5.1% RotAcc and 21.3% CenAcc on Objaverse, while PoseDiffusion lags by 17.1% RotAcc and 24.4% CenAcc. SfM-based methods (COLMAP, RelPose++) are far behind, with COLMAP achieving only 28.74% RotAcc versus 92.32% for GCRayDiffusion. FORGE shows a notable weakness in translation accuracy (41.33% vs. 62.62% on Objaverse), and its performance degrades sharply as image count increases beyond 3. On surface reconstruction, GCRayDiffusion achieves the best CD and NC on all three datasets, with the largest HD improvement over DUSt3R on GSO (0.302 vs. 0.366, a 17% reduction); however, DUSt3R retains a slight edge on GSO F-score (0.961 vs. 0.958) and CO3D HD (0.387 vs. 0.401). PF-LRM, evaluated only on CO3D, is substantially weaker (F-score 0.650 vs. 0.953), confirming that joint pose-shape prediction without explicit geometric regularization is less effective for sparse views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ray-based camera parametrization introduced by RayDiffusion (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024) by augmenting each Plücker-coordinate ray with a depth attribute, enabling explicit on-surface point tracing. It departs from prior diffusion-based pose estimators (PoseDiffusion, RayDiffusion) by conditioning the denoiser on a triplane-based SDF of the entire scene, creating a geometrically consistent bundle adjustment. The bidirectional coupling—where SDF values guide ray denoising and ray sampling points regularize SDF learning—distinguishes it from joint learning approaches like SC-NeuS, FORGE, and DUSt3R, which optimize poses and surfaces through photometric or depth cues without an explicit diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that coupling a diffusion-based bundle adjustment with triplane SDF learning yields consistently better sparse-view (2–6 images) pose estimation and surface reconstruction than prior SOTA methods like DUSt3R, with the ablation showing that SDF conditioning is critical especially for translation accuracy (62.6 → 37.5 without it). A key limitation is that the model is pre-trained on single-object data (Objaverse) and does not generalize to scene-level reconstruction, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The narrow guidance-scale sweet spot (scale=3 optimal, rapid degradation at 5+) and the 75-hour single-GPU training cost are practical considerations for adoption. Open questions include whether the bidirectional SDF–diffusion coupling can be made more efficient and extended to unbounded multi-object scenes.
