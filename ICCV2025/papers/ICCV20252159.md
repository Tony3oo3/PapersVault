## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing methods require supervised training on triplets of input images, ground-truth edited images, and edit instructions. These triplets are either synthesized by existing editing tools (introducing attribute-entangled, scene-entangled, and global-change biases) or human-annotated (costly and limited in scale), constraining both the diversity of achievable edits and the scalability of training. No prior approach enables training on large-scale real-image datasets without pre-existing edit pairs.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a natural-language edit instruction and outputs a single edited image, operating within the Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent space. During training, each sample additionally requires a reverse instruction, an input caption, and an edited caption (all generated by LLMs such as GEMINI or GEMMA2), but the ground-truth edited image is never used. Training is performed in a single denoising step per iteration, with noise added to the input image rather than to a target edited image, which biases the model toward preserving original structure.

## Method

UIP2P introduces the Edit Reversibility Constraint (ERC), which pairs every forward edit with its corresponding reverse edit within a single training step and enforces consistency across three spaces. First, a CLIP direction loss aligns the vector of change in image embedding space with the vector of change in text embedding space, ensuring the edit follows the instruction semantically. Second, an attention map alignment loss (L2 distance across U-Net down/up layers) forces the model to focus on the same spatial regions during both forward and reverse edits, improving localization. Third, a reconstruction loss (pixel-wise L2 plus CLIP cosine similarity) ensures the reverse edit faithfully recovers the original image. The total ERC loss is a weighted sum of these terms plus a CLIP similarity loss between the edited image and its caption, all computed on a single-step noise prediction. Reverse instructions and edited captions are generated by fine-tuned LLMs (GEMMA2), enabling training on arbitrary image-caption datasets like CC3M and CC12M without any edit pairs.

## Results

On the MagicBrush test set (single-turn), UIP2P trained on CC12M achieves L1=0.0619, L2=0.0174, CLIP-I=0.9318, DINO=0.9039, and CLIP-T=0.2964, substantially outperforming InstructPix2Pix (L1=0.1122, CLIP-I=0.8524) and HIVE (L1=0.1092, CLIP-I=0.8519). In the multi-turn setting, UIP2P (CC12M) reaches L1=0.0976 and DINO=0.8235, again leading all baselines. In the user study, UIP2P attains the highest Elo score of 1681 (edit success) and 1659 (localization accuracy), with a 22–23% win rate across 18,000 votes from 50 participants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UIP2P (CC12M) outperforms InstructPix2Pix by a wide margin on all five MagicBrush metrics in both single-turn (e.g., L1 0.0619 vs. 0.1122, DINO 0.9039 vs. 0.7428) and multi-turn settings (L1 0.0976 vs. 0.1584). Against HIVE, which leverages human feedback for reward-based fine-tuning, UIP2P still leads on every metric (single-turn CLIP-I 0.9318 vs. 0.8519; multi-turn DINO 0.8235 vs. 0.6463). In the user study, UIP2P achieves the highest Elo score (1681) for edit success, surpassing MGIE (1584), SmartEdit (1543), MagicBrush (1482), HIVE (1465), and IP2P (1395); for localization accuracy, UIP2P (1659) also leads, though MagicBrush (1601) is the closest competitor. Notably, UIP2P is trained without any ground-truth edited images, whereas IP2P and HIVE rely on supervised triplets or human-annotated rewards, making the performance gap particularly significant. The scalability trend is clear: moving from IP2P (~200K synthetic pairs) to CC12M (~12M real image-caption pairs) yields consistent gains across all metrics, confirming that ERC benefits from large-scale real-image data.

## Relation to Prior Work

UIP2P builds directly on InstructPix2Pix (IP2P), retaining its Stable Diffusion U-Net architecture and classifier-free guidance conditioning, but replaces the supervised triplet objective with an Edit Reversibility Constraint (ERC) that enforces forward-reverse edit consistency in image, text, and attention spaces. Conceptually, ERC extends cycle-consistency ideas from domain-pair translation (CycleGAN, CycleDiffusion, CycleNet) to arbitrary instruction-based edits within a single training step, and repurposes CLIP's semantic alignment (previously used for per-edit optimization in StyleCLIP/DiffusionCLIP) as a differentiable training loss. The use of LLMs (GEMINI, GEMMA2) to auto-generate reverse instructions is a practical enabler that distinguishes UIP2P from prior cycle-consistency methods, which required predefined domain pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for ground-truth edited images, UIP2P unlocks training on large-scale real-image datasets (CC3M, CC12M) that were previously inaccessible to instruction-based editing, potentially reducing the field's dependence on biased synthetic triplets from Prompt-to-Prompt or DALL-E 3. The single-step training paradigm also offers a meaningful efficiency gain over multi-pass refinement. Open questions remain around photorealism (CLIP alignment may sacrifice pixel-level fidelity), the quality ceiling of LLM-generated reverse instructions, and whether ERC generalizes to more complex multi-object or structural edits beyond the tested scope. The paper acknowledges that perceptual or adversarial losses could address the visual quality gap in challenging edits.
