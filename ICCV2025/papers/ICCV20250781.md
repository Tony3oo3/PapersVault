## Problem

Structured pruning of trained vision models reduces inference cost and memory but typically demands hundreds of epochs of retraining to recover lost accuracy, while dynamic methods like token merging avoid retraining yet leave the memory footprint unchanged. No existing approach simultaneously addresses training cost, storage, and inference speed with only minimal fine-tuning after structural modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained network (DeiT, Swin-Transformer, or ConvNeXt in Tiny/Small/Base sizes) and a calibration set from ImageNet-1k to gather per-neuron activation statistics via Welford's algorithm. It produces a structurally pruned network with reduced MLP hidden dimensions, where both the input and output dimensions of each MLP are preserved to maintain dimensional compatibility with the surrounding architecture. The pruned model then performs standard ImageNet-1k classification on RGB images, optionally followed by 10 epochs of knowledge-distillation fine-tuning from the unmodified base model.

## Method

Variance-Based Pruning (VBP) is a one-shot structured pruning method applied exclusively to MLP hidden layers. It first computes per-neuron activation variance using Welford's algorithm over a calibration set, then globally ranks all hidden neurons across the network and prunes the lowest-variance fraction. The key mechanism is Mean-Shift Compensation: because the pruned neuron's activation is approximated by its mean, the constant contribution W₂·Δ_μ is algebraically folded into the output bias b₂, allowing both the corresponding row of W₁ and column of W₂ to be physically removed. This exploits the linearity of the second linear layer and the fact that weights are fixed in a trained network, making variance the provably optimal criterion (it minimizes expected reconstruction error under mean replacement). The result is a genuinely smaller network requiring only ~10 epochs of fine-tuning to recover 99% of original accuracy, compared to 300+ epochs for prior structured pruning methods.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, VBP applied to DeiT-Base at 55% MLP pruning retains 70.48% of original accuracy immediately after pruning and recovers to 80.67% (98.74% of the 81.73% baseline) after just 10 epochs of fine-tuning, while reducing MACs by 34.93% and parameters by 36.01% for a 1.44× speed-up. At a more conservative 20% pruning rate, DeiT-Base retains 98.98% accuracy with no fine-tuning needed, achieving a 1.11× speed-up with 13% size reduction. Across Swin-Base (55%) and ConvNeXt-Base (50%), VBP similarly reaches 98.70% and 97.6% of original accuracy with 34–42% MACs reduction and up to 1.49× speed-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two standard one-shot pruning criteria adapted for structured pruning, VBP dominates: on DeiT-Base at 50% pruning, VBP retains 66.40% accuracy immediately after pruning versus 53.24% for SNIP and 0.37% for Magnitude, and reaches 80.99% final accuracy after 10 epochs of fine-tuning versus 80.40% (SNIP) and 78.88% (Magnitude). Compared to the SoTA structured ViT pruner NViT (CVPR'23), VBP with 1 epoch of pruning achieves 72.37% retention and 82.32% final accuracy, outperforming NViT at the same 1-epoch budget (69.10% / 81.92%) and even exceeding NViT's 50-epoch pruning result (81.13% / 82.18%) while using fewer parameters (56.18M vs. 56.37M). In the hybrid ToMe+VBP setting, combining VBP (50% MLP pruning) with ToMe at lower token-reduction rates (14, 12, 10) yields 80.09–80.45% final accuracy at 1.70–2.05× speed-up with 58.24M parameters, whereas ToMe alone at comparable speed-ups (2.15×, 1.84×, 1.75×) drops to 75.74–79.97% accuracy while retaining the full 86.57M parameter count. Overall, VBP wins on every comparison axis: higher accuracy retention, higher final accuracy, fewer parameters, and comparable or better speed-ups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-based neuron importance criterion from Molchanov et al. [18] and Weinstein et al. [23], which applied activation-statistics-driven pruning during or before training, to the post-training setting where network weights are fixed. The key conceptual departure is the Mean-Shift Compensation step, which borrows the information-preservation philosophy of dynamic pruning (e.g., ToMe [3]) and folds it into a structured pruning operation by redistributing pruned neurons' mean activations into the next layer's bias. This positions VBP between the two major pruning paradigms: it achieves the memory and compute savings of structured pruning (unlike ToMe) while retaining the accuracy preservation of dynamic pruning (unlike NViT [27], which requires 300 epochs of fine-tuning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VBP addresses the practical bottleneck of deploying trained ViTs and similar architectures on resource-constrained hardware by reducing both model size and inference cost with only 10 epochs of fine-tuning, compared to hundreds of epochs for prior structured pruning methods. The demonstrated orthogonality to token-merging methods (ToMe) opens a path to hybrid compression achieving 2× speed-ups with additional parameter savings. Limitations include the restriction to MLP layers only, the significant accuracy retention drop for convolution-heavy architectures like ConvNeXt (as low as 20% for Tiny), and the fact that the method is validated exclusively on ImageNet-1k classification. Open questions include generalization to NLP transformers, detection/segmentation tasks, and whether the variance criterion remains optimal under different training regimes or data distributions.
