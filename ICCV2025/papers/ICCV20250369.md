## Problem

Abstracting a 3D shape into a sparse set of curves that faithfully represent both its geometry and texture from all viewing angles remains unsolved. Existing approaches such as occluding contours are inherently 2D and view-dependent, producing flickering artifacts and capturing only low-level geometric boundaries while ignoring texture and high-level visual concepts. No prior method produces a view-consistent 3D stroke representation that simultaneously encodes shape structure and semantic texture features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (potentially textured, with arbitrary topology and quality) and optional user-selected 3D keypoints on the surface; when keypoints are absent, they are auto-detected via Backto3D feature back-projection with KMeans clustering. The output is a sparse set of 3D cubic Bezier curves (each defined by four 3D control points), where the curve count directly controls abstraction level (20 geometry + 20 texture curves in experiments). Curves are rendered through perspective projection of control points followed by differentiable rasterization with DiffVG, and their adherence to the surface (enforced by SDF loss) makes them usable as deformation handles.

## Method

WIR3D optimizes Bezier curve parameters via a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 abstracts coarse geometry using RN101 CLIP intermediate activations against Freestyle renders, while Stage 2 freezes those curves and adds new ones optimized for texture using RN50x64 CLIP against surface renders. The key novelty is a localized keypoint loss that spatially weights CLIP layer-3 and layer-4 feature map differences with Gaussian falloff maps constructed from projected 3D keypoints, directing optimization toward user-specified or auto-detected salient regions. A neural SDF loss (fitted MLP on the shape's signed distance field) anchors curves to the surface, and an NDC view-regularization loss prevents curves from escaping the render frame. The two-stage split is motivated by the empirical finding that RN101 is more sensitive to geometric structure while RN50x64 captures higher-level visual concepts, and the localized weighting enables interactive detail refinement by adding keypoints and re-optimizing in ~1 minute.

## Results

WIR3D achieves the best performance across all four metrics in the main comparison: LPIPS 0.227 (lower is better), CLIPimg 0.909 (higher is better), User Rank 0.88 (88% of 96 users prefer WIR3D over 3Doodle), and Coverage 0.008 (1-direction Chamfer distance, lower is better). The Coverage metric shows a >2× reduction relative to 3Doodle (0.020) and >4× reduction relative to NEF (0.056). In a separate deformation perceptual study (N=42), WIR3D-based curve handles are preferred over ARAP 80% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIR3D outperforms 3Doodle [7] on all four metrics: LPIPS (0.227 vs. 0.246), CLIPimg (0.909 vs. 0.900), User Rank (0.88 vs. 0.12, i.e., 88% of 96 users prefer WIR3D), and Coverage (0.008 vs. 0.020, a 2.5× improvement). The authors note that the CLIPimg gap is small and that CLIP is known to be insensitive to large visual differences, which motivates the user study as the more reliable signal. Against NEF [69], WIR3D achieves a >4× reduction in Coverage (0.008 vs. 0.056) and substantially better LPIPS (0.227 vs. 0.313); NEF frequently fails to fit reasonable point clouds, producing meaningless curves. In the deformation application, WIR3D curve-based handles are preferred over ARAP [53] 80% of the time (N=42), with the key advantage being that curves wrap semantically meaningful regions (e.g., deforming only a cow's horn while leaving its head intact). Qualitatively, 3Doodle struggles with untextured shapes (missing geometry, flattened forms) and is consistently insensitive to facial structures, whereas WIR3D's localized keypoint loss ensures these features are captured.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIR3D extends the 3D stroke abstraction framework of 3Doodle by adding a two-stage optimization that separates geometry (supervised by CLIP RN101) from texture (supervised by CLIP RN50x64), and introduces a localized keypoint loss that spatially weights CLIP intermediate activations to direct optimization toward user-specified features. The semantic loss structure is adapted from CLIPasso, and automatic keypoint detection borrows the back-projection strategy from Backto3D. The key conceptual departure from prior curve-based abstraction work (3Doodle, NEF, wire art reconstruction) is the explicit treatment of texture and high-level visual concepts as first-class abstraction targets, rather than limiting to geometric boundaries or occluding contours.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The localized keypoint loss provides a practical mechanism for user-interactive control over 3D abstraction detail, and the SDF-anchored curves double as intuitive deformation handles (80% user preference over ARAP), opening a path toward curve-based 3D editing workflows. Limitations include dependence on keypoint quality for fine detail, expensive preprocessing (neural SDF fitting, Freestyle rendering, keypoint detection can take ~2 hours for complex meshes), and the fixed curve count per stage. Open questions include whether the two-stage geometry-then-texture decomposition is strictly necessary versus joint optimization, and how the method scales to very high curve counts or highly textured shapes.
