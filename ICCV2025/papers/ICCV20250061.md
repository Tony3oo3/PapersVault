## Problem

Existing systems generate singing vocals and human motions in isolation—text-to-vocal, text-to-motion, or audio-to-motion—despite strong evidence that sound and motion are psychologically coupled. Cascaded pipelines that chain a vocal generator with a motion generator accumulate errors across stages and increase computational cost. No prior work jointly generates coherent singing vocals and 3D whole-body motions (face, body, hands) from textual lyrics in a single unified model.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw rap lyrics text as input and simultaneously produces a singing vocal waveform and 3D whole-body motion in SMPL-X parameters (jaw poses, 63-dim body poses, 90-dim hand poses, 100-dim facial expression, and global translation). The vocal output is decoded from discrete HuBERT semantic tokens, F0 pitch tokens, and a singer embedding via a HiFi-GAN vocoder, while motion is decoded from three separate VQ-VAE codebooks. Temporal alignment between the two outputs is enforced by interleaving tokens along a shared time axis, with vocal tokens generated before motion tokens so that lip movements can condition on the preceding vocal content.

## Method

The framework unifies three modalities into a shared discrete token space: a T5 tokenizer for lyrics, a Vocal2unit model (HuBERT semantic encoder + YAAPT F0 VQ-VAE encoder + global singer encoder) for audio, and three compositional VQ-VAEs (one each for face, body, and hand) for motion. A decoder-only transformer then performs autoregressive next-token prediction over the interleaved audio and motion token sequence, conditioned on T5-encoded text features, trained with standard cross-entropy loss. Within each modality, tokens are interleaved temporally (e.g., face-body-hand per timestep for motion; HuBERT-pitch per timestep for audio), and the two modality sequences are concatenated with modality-specific start tokens. The key design choice is using separate VQ-VAEs per body part rather than a single joint codebook, which the ablation shows preserves facial expression detail that would otherwise be dominated by body dynamics. The entire system is trained end-to-end on the newly curated RapVerse dataset (108.44 h vocals + 26.8 h motion-annotated performances), with tokenizers pre-trained separately before joint transformer training.

## Results

On the RapVerse test set, the proposed method achieves the best FID (17.58) and LVD (7.23) among all motion generation baselines, while also attaining the highest motion diversity (DIV 14.08). For vocal generation, the unified model reaches a MOS of 3.64±0.15, slightly below the specialized DiffSinger (3.72±0.12) but well above FastSpeech2 (3.41±0.18). The method also outperforms the cascaded DiffSinger+TalkShow pipeline on every motion metric (e.g., FID 17.58 vs. 23.42, LVD 7.23 vs. 9.38), demonstrating that joint generation avoids error accumulation across stages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against text-to-motion baselines T2M-GPT and MLD, the proposed method substantially outperforms on both FID (17.58 vs. 23.45 and 26.34) and diversity (14.08 vs. 11.75 and 12.15), though these baselines do not report face or synchrony metrics. Among audio-to-motion methods, Ours achieves the lowest FID (17.58 vs. 17.75 for MotionCraft, 18.23 for TalkShow) and the best LVD (7.23 vs. 8.45–9.54), indicating more realistic facial expressions; however, EMAGE edges out slightly on beat constancy (0.488 vs. 0.485) and vertex MSE (1.96 vs. 2.03). The unified model clearly surpasses the cascaded DiffSinger+TalkShow pipeline across all five motion metrics, confirming that joint generation avoids the error propagation inherent in sequential pipelines. For vocal quality, the method (MOS 3.64) trails the dedicated singing-voice synthesizer DiffSinger (3.72) by a small margin but exceeds the general TTS system FastSpeech2 (3.41), showing that the added burden of simultaneous motion generation costs only ~0.08 MOS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—text-to-vocal synthesis (DiffSinger, FastSpeech2), text-to-motion generation (T2M-GPT, MLD), and audio-to-motion generation (TalkShow, EMAGE, MotionCraft)—into a single autoregressive transformer that jointly produces singing vocals and SMPL-X whole-body motion from lyrics. The vocal tokenizer directly adapts the Polyak et al. (2021) speech-resynthesis-from-discrete-representations framework (HuBERT semantic encoder + VQ-VAE pitch encoder + singer encoder + HiFi-GAN vocoder), while the motion tokenizer follows the compositional VQ-VAE paradigm popularized by MotionGPT and MLD. The key conceptual departure is replacing cascaded pipelines (text→vocal→motion) with a single decoder-only transformer operating over interleaved multi-modality tokens, so that vocal and motion tokens are generated in a shared autoregressive context rather than sequentially through separate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RapVerse dataset (108 h of English rap vocals + 26.8 h of synchronized SMPL-X motion) fills a clear gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides lyrics, singing vocals, and whole-body motion, and existing large vocal corpora are predominantly Chinese while motion corpora lack audio. The finding that a straightforward autoregressive transformer over VQ tokens can rival specialized single-modality systems (e.g., MOS 3.64 vs. DiffSinger 3.72; FID 17.58 vs. MotionCraft 17.75) suggests that unified multi-modality token prediction is a viable and computationally cheaper alternative to cascaded pipelines. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes beyond rap/English to other genres and languages, whether the 26.8 h motion subset is sufficient for diverse whole-body dynamics, and how the framework scales to longer sequences or more complex musical structures.
