## Problem

Concept erasure techniques for text-to-image models are evaluated in controlled settings but lack a standardized framework to stress-test their robustness across diverse concept relationships. This paper identifies a critical gap: erasure causes 'concept entanglement,' where removing a target concept unintentionally suppresses visually similar, binomially paired, or semantically related non-target concepts, producing distortions and quality degradation that current evaluations miss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250072/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a text prompt and a Stable Diffusion model (v1.4) as input, producing generated images (10 per prompt with different random seeds) scored by three complementary metrics: CLIP zero-shot classification (efficacy, generality, sensitivity), RAHF quality scores (artifact and aesthetic with spatial heatmaps), and Gecko VQA alignment (Gemini 1.5-based question-answering over 6,246 text-image pairs). Concepts are organized into four relationship dimensions—visual similarity, artistic style, subset-superset, and binomial—each with 8–15 curated concepts and 10+ prompts varying in complexity and length.

## Method

EraseBench is constructed by leveraging LLMs and the ImageNet hierarchical taxonomy to generate clusters of related concepts, verified by human annotators for renderability, with prompts generated via Gemini Flash across varying styles and complexities. Five state-of-the-art erasure techniques (ESD, UCE, Receler, MACE, AdvUnlearn) are applied to each primary concept and evaluated on non-target concepts within the same dimension using CLIP, RAHF, and Gecko metrics. The paper further investigates mitigation strategies—progressively adding entangled non-target concepts to retain sets, introducing anchor concepts, and comparing intra-type versus inter-type multi-concept erasure—revealing a fundamental trade-off where preserving non-target concepts increases the risk of target concept leakage.

## Results

Across all four EraseBench dimensions, every erasure technique reduces sensitivity (non-target concept preservation) relative to the original Stable Diffusion model (85.0% visual similarity), confirming systematic over-erasure. UCE best preserves non-target concepts (82.7% sensitivity on visual similarity) but has the weakest erasure efficacy (41.8%), while Receler achieves the strongest erasure (8.1% efficacy) at the cost of dropping sensitivity to 65.4%. Gecko VQA alignment scores for non-erased concepts drop significantly (α=0.01, Wilcoxon signed-rank test): UCE −3.4, MACE −9.8, AdvUnlearn −9.0 points versus the original model. RAHF artifact scores decline from ~87 (original) to 66–75 post-erasure, indicating measurable quality degradation on non-target concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five state-of-the-art concept erasure techniques on EraseBench rather than proposing a new method. UCE [11] consistently achieves the highest sensitivity (non-target preservation) across all four dimensions—82.7% on visual similarity, 87.5% on subset-superset, and 86.1% on binomial—making it the least destructive to related concepts, but it has the weakest erasure efficacy (41.8% on visual similarity vs. 8.1% for Receler). Receler [18] delivers the strongest erasure (lowest efficacy scores: 8.1% visual, 8.7% artist, 4.2% subset-superset) and the best generality, yet its sensitivity drops sharply (22.8% on artist style), indicating severe over-erasure of related concepts. MACE [30] and AdvUnlearn [54] occupy a middle ground with competitive efficacy (15.6% and 8.7% on visual similarity) but sensitivity scores (66.4% and 64.3%) well below the original model's 85.0%. ESD [10] is the weakest overall, showing moderate efficacy (24.5%) but the lowest harmonic mean on visual similarity (61.70) and artist style (47.74). On explicit/safety-critical concepts (Table 2), UCE leads on binomial (76.13 HM) while Receler leads on visual similarity (68.47 HM), confirming that no single technique dominates across all relationship types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation of concept erasure for T2I diffusion models by shifting focus from whether the target concept is successfully removed to the collateral damage inflicted on related non-target concepts. Prior evaluations (e.g., UnlearnCanvas [55], Pham et al. [35]) assessed erasure efficacy and recovery vulnerability but did not systematically stress-test the impact across structured relationship dimensions (visual similarity, artistic style, subset-superset, binomial). EraseBench repurposes the existing erasure methods (ESD, UCE, Receler, MACE, AdvUnlearn) as subjects of a new multidimensional evaluation protocol, introducing a 'sensitivity' metric and combining CLIP, RAHF, and Gecko scores to capture both alignment and quality degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all five SOTA erasure methods consistently degrade non-target concepts across every relationship dimension raises serious concerns about deploying sanitized models in safety-critical or commercial settings. The retain-set and anchor-set experiments reveal a fundamental trade-off: preserving related concepts increases the risk of target-concept leakage, suggesting that current erasure paradigms may need architectural rethinking rather than hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4 to newer architectures (e.g., rectified flow transformers), whether automated concept selection can scale the benchmark, and whether multi-concept erasure strategies (intra-type vs. inter-type) can close the gap between erasure efficacy and non-target preservation.
