## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods are limited by insufficient semantic-spatial knowledge extraction, and while foundation models like CLIP (global semantics) and DINO (local spatial details) offer complementary strengths, naive feature fusion produces suboptimal depth representations due to inherent granularity mismatches between the two. No prior work has systematically integrated these foundation models into self-supervised MDE pipelines with a mechanism to harmonize their multi-grained features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (640×192 on KITTI) and produces a dense per-pixel depth map. During the coarse training stage, it additionally consumes lane markings from TuSimple to select 7 ordinal-ordered patches along the road for contrastive supervision. The hybrid encoder extracts 4-scale features from ResNet blocks (layers 2, 5, 8, 11) of both CLIP and DINOv2, and the final depth is upsampled via DPT. The module is designed as a plug-and-play replacement for the DepthNet branch in existing self-supervised MDE pipelines (Monodepth2, ManyDepth, Mono-VIFI), requiring only 256 learnable depth tokens and a single A800 GPU for 10 epochs.

## Method

Hybrid-depth introduces a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. In the coarse stage, multi-grained features from CLIP and DINO are concatenated after spatial interpolation, and 7 patches are selected along lane markings with guaranteed ordinal depth ordering; two contrastive losses are then applied—an intramodal loss enforcing that closer patches are more self-similar than farther ones, and a language-guided loss aligning patch features with depth-rank text prompts (e.g., 'very distant' to 'very close')—while keeping the text encoder frozen. In the fine stage, handcrafted depth tokens are replaced by 256 learnable tokens, and an ALIGN operation (inner product between hybrid features and depth text embeddings) acts as a granularity calibrator that balances CLIP's semantic richness against DINO's spatial precision. The calibrated features are combined with auxiliary camera pose from PoseNet and upsampled via DPT, trained with standard photometric (L1 + SSIM) and edge-aware smoothness losses. This design allows the module to drop into any existing self-supervised MDE pipeline as an enhanced depth encoder without modifying the reconstruction or pose estimation branches.

## Results

On the KITTI Eigen split, Monodepth2 + Hybrid-depth achieves the best results across all metrics: Abs Rel 0.093, Sq Rel 0.596, RMSE 4.113, RMSElog 0.167, and δ<1.25 0.910, outperforming the previous best (RPrDepth: Abs Rel 0.097, Sq Rel 0.658, RMSE 4.279) by a clear margin. ManyDepth + Hybrid-depth and Mono-VIFI + Hybrid-depth also surpass all prior self-supervised methods on every metric. On the nuScenes BEV perception task, integrating Hybrid-depth improves BEVDet's mAP from 0.283 to 0.325 and FB-BEV's mAP from 0.312 to 0.348.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monodepth2 + Hybrid-depth outperforms all 15 prior self-supervised MDE baselines on every KITTI metric, with the largest gains over Monodepth2 (Abs Rel 0.115→0.093, a 19% relative reduction) and RPrDepth (Abs Rel 0.097→0.093, RMSE 4.279→4.113). The closest competitor, SQLDepth (Abs Rel 0.094, RMSE 4.320), is surpassed by 0.001 in Abs Rel and 0.207 in RMSE. ManyDepth + Hybrid-depth and Mono-VIFI + Hybrid-depth similarly dominate their respective baselines and all other methods. Against CLIP-based depth estimation methods (Table 2), Monodepth2 + Hybrid-depth achieves Abs Rel 0.093 versus 0.303 (Auty et al.), 0.384 (Hu et al.), and 0.473 (DepthCLIP), representing a 3–5× improvement. On nuScenes BEV perception, Hybrid-depth improves BEVDet mAP by 4.2 points (0.283→0.325) and FB-BEV mAP by 3.6 points (0.312→0.348), confirming transferability to downstream 3D tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised monocular depth estimation (building on Monodepth2, ManyDepth, and Mono-VIFI) and foundation-model-based representation learning (CLIP, DINOv2). It departs from prior CLIP-for-depth work (DepthCLIP, Auty et al., Hu et al.) that treated CLIP as a zero/few-shot depth encoder, instead proposing a structured coarse-to-fine scheme where CLIP and DINO are jointly trained with depth-aware contrastive losses and language prompts as a 'granularity calibrator.' The plug-and-play design—replacing only the DepthNet branch while retaining the PoseNet and photometric loss of existing pipelines—distinguishes it from methods that require architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play depth encoder that consistently improves three distinct self-supervised MDE frameworks by a large margin (e.g., Monodepth2 Abs Rel drops from 0.115 to 0.093) offers a practical path for practitioners to upgrade existing pipelines without retraining from scratch. The transfer to BEV perception (BEVDet, FB-BEV on nuScenes) suggests the learned depth features carry broader 3D geometric value. However, the coarse stage relies on lane markings (driving-specific), depth evaluation is limited to KITTI, and the method's generalizability to non-driving or indoor scenes remains untested—open questions for broader adoption.
