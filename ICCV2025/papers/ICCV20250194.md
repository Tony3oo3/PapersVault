## Problem

Coral reef semantic segmentation is fundamentally harder than object segmentation because corals grow in highly stochastic, amorphous shapes with no predictable structural prior, yet existing approaches (superpixel-based, data-driven, and promptable foundation models) fail to exploit the intrinsic self-repeated and asymmetric properties of corals. Promptable models like SAM produce under-inclusive and over-inclusive masks for corals, and data-driven methods require expensive dense annotations and cannot generalize to unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB coral reef image, a small set of sparse point annotations (5–100 points with semantic labels), and frozen features extracted from any pretrained foundation model (DINO, DINOv2, SAM, SAM 2, or CoralSCOP) at various spatial resolutions (e.g., 37×37 to 64×64). It produces a dense per-pixel semantic segmentation mask via KNN (K=1) label propagation in the rectified feature space. Notably, the Rec(·) module is trained once using dense masks auto-generated by SAM 2 (no human labels), and at inference time only the sparse points and frozen FM features are required—no retraining or fine-tuning of the backbone is needed.

## Method

CoralSRT introduces a lightweight feature rectification module Rec(·) (a DVT-style transformer without positional embedding) trained in a self-supervised manner: given dense semantic-agnostic masks from SAM 2, it constructs a target feature F′ by replacing each pixel's feature with the segment's median (or mean) value, then minimizes the L2 distance between F′ and Rec(F). The key insight is that forcing features within a segment to converge toward its centrality reduces the high stochasticity of coral features, strengthening within-segment affinity and making KNN-based label propagation far more effective. At inference, rectified features are clustered with the sparse point labels to produce dense masks. The approach is task- and model-agnostic, requires no human annotations or domain-specific data for training Rec(·), and can even be optimized on general images (COCO-Stuff) to achieve meaningful gains on coral reefs.

## Results

On the constructed multi-site testing set (1,109 images from 10 countries), CoralSRT+FeatUp(DINOv2) achieves the best sparse-to-dense conversion, reaching 65.78 mIoU / 74.76 mPA with 100 sparse points, outperforming the strongest baseline DINOv2-Reg (61.13 mIoU / 70.00 mPA) by +4.6 mIoU. With only 5 points, CoralSRT(CoralSCOP) attains 31.93 mIoU / 35.19 mPA, surpassing the best prompt-based model CoralSCOP (18.14 mIoU) by +13.8 mIoU. In the zero-shot setting on Mosaics UCSD, CoralSRT(DINOv2) reaches 44.66 mIoU / 53.03 mPA at 50 points, beating the best specialist algorithm HIL (42.52 mIoU) without any domain-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoralSRT consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every sparse-point budget (5–100 points) on the multi-site testing set. Against prompt-based models (SAM, SAM 2, CoralSCOP), CoralSRT's feature-space label propagation yields +13.8 mIoU over CoralSCOP at 5 points and +5.6 mIoU at 100 points, confirming that promptable segmentation suffers from under-/over-inclusive masks for amorphous corals. Among feature-based baselines, CoralSRT(DINOv2) improves over vanilla DINOv2 by +2.95 mIoU at 5 points and +3.17 mIoU at 100 points, while CoralSRT+FeatUp(DINOv2) further surpasses FeatUp alone by +2.05 mIoU at 100 points. CoralSRT also improves SAM and SAM 2 features by +6.1 and +2.9 mIoU respectively at 5 points, demonstrating that the rectification module is model-agnostic. In the zero-shot Mosaics UCSD experiment, CoralSRT(DINOv2) beats the best specialist algorithm HIL [42] by +2.14 mIoU at 50 points without any training on that dataset, and outperforms FeatUp(DINOv2) by +0.93 mIoU under the same setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoralSRT departs from the dominant paradigm of using promptable foundation models (SAM, SAM 2, CoralSCOP) directly for coral reef segmentation, arguing that their under-inclusive and over-inclusive mask outputs are fundamentally ill-suited to corals' amorphous, self-repeated growth patterns. Instead, it repurposes frozen FM features (DINO, DINOv2, SAM series) as a feature space for KNN-based label propagation, and introduces a lightweight self-supervised rectification module (architecturally derived from DVT) that forces within-segment features toward their centrality. This positions the work at the intersection of self-supervised feature learning and sparse-to-dense conversion, bridging the gap between large-scale pre-training and instance-specific segmentation without any human annotation or FM retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, task- and model-agnostic recipe for coral reef researchers to convert existing sparse point annotations into dense semantic masks without collecting new data, retraining models, or requiring domain expertise in model design. The introduction of CoralWorld (2.64M unlabeled images) and the first multi-country test set with dense annotations are valuable community resources. However, the method cannot produce separated per-coral-instance masks (a limitation versus SAM/CoralSCOP), and its applicability to other amorphous 'stuff' domains (cells, seagrass, biofoulings) remains to be validated. An open question is whether the centrality-based rectification generalizes to domains where within-segment heterogeneity is genuinely high rather than merely stochastic.
