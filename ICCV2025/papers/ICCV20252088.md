## Problem

Chinese calligraphy, with its intricate scripts, arbitrary layouts, and historical glyph variations, remains computationally intractable: conventional OCR tools falter on stylistic nuances, and existing VLMs lack calligraphy-specific training data, leading to severe hallucinations and shallow reasoning. No prior system simultaneously achieves precise full-page recognition, cultural knowledge grounding, and multilingual interpretation of calligraphic artworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a high-resolution RGB calligraphy page image and a natural-language instruction (in Chinese or English). The image is first decomposed into individual character patches via YOLOv10 detection and OrderFormer reading-order recovery; each patch is cropped, padded, and resized to a fixed h×w resolution before being encoded by the ViT. Outputs span multiple granularities: full-page transcribed text, multiple-choice classifications (style, layout, authorship), English bilingual rephrasing scored by sentence-transformer cosine similarity, and an intent-analysis score (0–10) generated by an LLM judge. Regional (cropped) inputs are also supported for hallucination-detection evaluation.

## Method

CalliReader plugs three modules into InternVL2-8B (InternViT-300M-448px + InternLM2.5-7b-chat), adding only 0.88 B parameters (~10 % of the base VLM). (1) Character-wise Slicing uses a fine-tuned YOLOv10 detector and a 4-layer OrderFormer encoder to extract ordered character patches, reducing page-level recognition to per-character tasks while preserving layout semantics. (2) CalliAlign, a 0.57 B perceiver-resampler with 3 learnable queries, compresses 256 ViT visual tokens per character into 3 pseudo-text embeddings (98.8 % compression) that are trained via L2 loss to match layer-normalized text embeddings from a 742 K single-character dataset, enabling efficient scaling without modifying the vision backbone. (3) Embedding Instruction Tuning (e-IT) concatenates tokenized user queries with CalliAlign pseudo-text embeddings (and optional global page features) into a unified embedding sequence, then applies LoRA fine-tuning to the LLM on 7,357 page-level samples augmented with 6,000 synthetic single-character sequences from the ChinesePoetry corpus—bypassing the need for large-scale page-level annotations and reducing hallucination by anchoring the LLM to aligned visual cues rather than raw pixels.

## Results

On CalliBench full-page recognition, CalliReader achieves F1 scores of 0.907 (Easy), 0.813 (Medium), and 0.609 (Hard), with NED of 0.121, 0.232, and 0.516 respectively. On the Hard (cursive) tier, CalliReader leads the best fine-tuned competitor (InternVL2-8B+ft, F1=0.511) by 9.8% in F1 and reduces NED by 6.3%. In contextual VQA, CalliReader reaches 95.06% accuracy on author identification, 77.81% on layout, and 61.14% STSim on bilingual interpretation, outperforming all baselines including GPT-4o-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalliReader decisively outperforms all OCR baselines (EasyOCR, Ali-OCR, PP-OCRv4, YOLOv10) by 10–85% F1 across tiers, confirming that conventional OCR tools cannot handle calligraphic stylistic variation. Among off-the-shelf VLMs, GPT-4o (F1=0.400 Easy) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (F1=0.534 Easy) trail CalliReader by 50+ points, while the strongest open-source VLM InternVL2-8B (F1=0.617 Easy) is surpassed by 29 points. Against fine-tuned VLMs, CalliReader leads InternVL2-8B+ft by 1.1% F1 on Easy but by a substantial 9.8% on the Hard cursive tier (0.609 vs. 0.511), with a 6.3% NED reduction. In contextual VQA (Table 3), CalliReader achieves 95.06% author accuracy vs. 83.92% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B+ft and 48.66% for GPT-4o-mini, while fine-tuned baselines show severe degradation in bilingual interpretation (28.34% STSim for Qwen2.5-VL-7B+ft vs. 61.14% for CalliReader). On reasoning-model comparisons (Table 8), CalliReader (75.33/77.81/95.06% on style/layout/author) surpasses QvQ-72B (66.13/71.71/80.82%) and DS-Align (34.17/33.87/44.27%), demonstrating that precise recognition is a prerequisite for reliable cultural reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CalliReader builds on InternVL2-8B as its base VLM and repurposes the perceiver resampler architecture from Flamingo as the backbone of CalliAlign, a character-level visual-to-text alignment module. It departs from standard VLM slicing strategies (which use adaptive aspect-ratio cropping) by introducing character-wise slicing with a dedicated YOLOv10 detector and OrderFormer for reading order, motivated by pilot experiments showing that single-character slices maximize recognition fidelity. The embedding instruction-tuning (e-IT) paradigm is a novel departure from conventional LoRA fine-tuning, unifying synthetic and authentic data in a shared embedding space to combat the data scarcity inherent in page-level calligraphy annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CalliBench is the first benchmark to jointly evaluate full-page recognition, hallucination resistance, and culturally-grounded multimodal reasoning (style, layout, authorship, bilingual interpretation, intent) for Chinese calligraphy, filling a gap where prior OCR and VQA benchmarks offered only fragmented or shallow evaluation. The finding that strong reasoning models (QvQ-72B, DeepSeek-Align) fail at contextual VQA without accurate character recognition challenges the assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning can compensate for perceptual errors, positioning precise recognition as a hard prerequisite for knowledge-intensive visual tasks. Limitations remain for cursive scripts and complex layouts, and the e-IT approach—while effective here—has not been validated on other low-resource visual-text domains, leaving open whether the embedding-space augmentation strategy generalizes beyond calligraphy.
