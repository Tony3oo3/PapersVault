## Problem

Existing federated unlearning methods attempt to identify and modify specific model parameters associated with unlearning data, but complex parameter interactions in deep networks make it difficult to isolate such parameters, leading to either degraded remaining-data utility or incomplete erasure. This paper identifies that in the retrained model's representation space, unlearning data does not distribute randomly but clusters within semantically related remaining classes (e.g., 76% of 'automobile' maps to 'truck'), suggesting a transformation-based approach rather than parameter modification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a well-trained global model (ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10/FMNIST/EuroSAT, ResNet-50 for CIFAR-100), a set of unlearning classes C_F, and local datasets across K=20 clients under IID or Non-IID (Dirichlet δ=0.5) distributions. Unlearning samples are relabeled to target transformation classes identified via the original model's output probabilities, and the process iterates over R-1 federated rounds with SGD. The output is a global unlearned model θ^R that achieves 0% accuracy and 0% MIA-ASR on unlearning data while preserving remaining-data accuracy within ~0.11% of full retraining.

## Method

FUCRT consists of two components. First, Transformation Class Identification (TCI) leverages the original model to find semantically related target classes: for each unlearning class, it sums prediction probabilities of correctly classified samples across remaining classes, thresholds to form local transformation sets, and aggregates them globally via maximum consensus; each unlearning sample is then relabeled to its highest-probability target class. Second, Targeted Transformation Learning (TTL) fine-tunes on the relabeled data using a composite loss: standard cross-entropy, a cross-class contrastive fusion loss that explicitly pulls unlearning representations toward their target class representations (beyond standard same-class attraction), and an auxiliary cross-client fusion loss that anchors local representations to globally aggregated class prototypes to ensure consistency under data heterogeneity. This transformation-based approach lets the model adaptively adjust parameters rather than surgically modifying them, achieving 100% erasure with 19–96% time reduction versus retraining.

## Results

FUCRT achieves 0% accuracy on unlearning data and 0% MIA ASR across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT), matching the Retrain baseline's erasure guarantee. On remaining-data accuracy (utility), FUCRT scores 90.06%, 75.25%, 90.82%, and 94.92% under IID and 89.82%, 74.88%, 90.95%, and 87.38% under Non-IID, outperforming the best SOTA baseline (FedOSD) by an average of 2.96% (IID) and 3.78% (Non-IID). FUCRT completes unlearning in 68 s (IID) and 82 s (Non-IID), reducing time by 19.13%–96.38% compared to other methods, versus 1925 s for full retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUCRT matches Retrain on erasure (0% U-set accuracy, 0% ASR) while being ~28× faster (68 s vs. 1925 s IID), and its remaining-data accuracy is within 0.11% on average of Retrain, sometimes exceeding it. Against Fine-tune, FUCRT achieves perfect erasure (0% vs. up to 52.04% U-set accuracy and 0% vs. up to 95.70% ASR) at comparable or better utility. FUCRT dominates Gradient-ascent by a wide margin on utility (e.g., 90.06% vs. 10.94% on CIFAR-10 IID), as Gradient-ascent destroys remaining-data performance. Over the closest SOTA competitors—FUDP, FUMD, VeriFi, FedAU, and FedOSD—FUCRT gains 2.96% (IID) to 3.78% (Non-IID) average R-set accuracy while also achieving 0% ASR, whereas FedAU retains 69–99% ASR and FedOSD shows non-zero ASR (up to 34.36%) under Non-IID. FUCRT also has the lowest unlearning time (68 s IID, 82 s Non-IID) among all methods that achieve effective erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in federated unlearning that attempts to identify and modify specific model parameters associated with unlearning data (e.g., channel pruning in FUDP, gradient ascent, auxiliary classifiers in FedAU, orthogonal descent in FedOSD). Instead, FUCRT reframes unlearning as a representation transformation problem: rather than removing information, it relabels unlearning samples to semantically related remaining classes and fine-tunes with a cross-class contrastive fusion loss. The cross-class fusion mechanism draws on supervised contrastive learning, and the auxiliary cross-client fusion via global prototypes addresses data heterogeneity in a way that prior FU methods (which typically operate on individual parameters or layers) do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUCRT provides a practical path to Retrain-level erasure (0% accuracy and 0% ASR on unlearning data) while outperforming all effective baselines in utility preservation and reducing unlearning time by up to 96.38%, making frequent unlearning requests more feasible in real FL deployments. The empirical observation that unlearning data clusters in semantically related categories (e.g., 76% of 'automobile' maps to 'truck') offers a useful inductive bias that could inform other unlearning or model-editing tasks. Limitations include evaluation only on four small-scale image datasets with ResNet backbones, class-level unlearning only, and the reliance on the original model's prediction probabilities for transformation class identification, which may be less reliable for poorly trained or adversarially robust models. Open questions include scalability to larger architectures (ViTs, diffusion models), sample-level unlearning, and whether the transformation approach generalizes to non-visual modalities.
