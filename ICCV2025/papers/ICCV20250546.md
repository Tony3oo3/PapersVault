## Problem

Existing style attribution methods for text-to-image diffusion models require training specialized external models (e.g., fine-tuned CLIP or DINO backbones) or collecting custom datasets, making them resource-intensive and impractical for real-time copyright enforcement. No prior approach can perform style retrieval using only the diffusion model's own internal features without any retraining or additional modules.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (center-cropped to 512×512), encodes it into the Stable Diffusion v2.1 latent space, adds Gaussian noise to a chosen timestep (default t=25), and passes the noised latent through the UNet denoising network with a null text embedding. The output is a compact style feature vector consisting of channel-wise means and variances (2C values) extracted from a single up-sampling block (default idx=1), and pairwise style similarity is computed as the 2-Wasserstein distance between the resulting diagonal-covariance Gaussians. No text prompt, training data, or external feature extractor is required at inference time.

## Method

IntroStyle reframes the diffusion model's UNet denoising network as an autoencoder whose encoder-side features carry stylistic information. Given an image, it is encoded to a latent, noised to timestep t, and forwarded through the UNet with a null text embedding; intermediate features at up-sampling block idx are then reduced to channel-wise mean and variance statistics, inspired by AdaIN's use of first- and second-order moments for style control in StyleGAN. The resulting 2C-dimensional vector parameterizes a diagonal-covariance multivariate Gaussian, and style similarity between two images is measured by the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance between these Gaussians. The entire pipeline is training-free: it requires no fine-tuning, no external feature extractor, and no contrastive or supervised objective, relying solely on the pre-trained diffusion model's internal representations. Ablations show the method is robust to the choice of up-block index (0–3) and similarity metric (L2, Gram, JSD, W2), with t=25 and idx=1 yielding the best performance.

## Results

On WikiArt, IntroStyle achieves mAP@1 of 0.887 and mAP@10 of 0.852, outperforming the previous best method CSD (0.646 and 0.538) by approximately 24 and 31 points respectively. On DomainNet, IntroStyle reaches mAP@1 of 0.954 and mAP@10 of 0.949, surpassing CSD (0.833 and 0.811) by roughly 12–14 points. On the proposed ArtSplit dataset, IntroStyle scores 0.823 on Style-Eval@10 (vs. 0.682 for the best baseline DINO) while simultaneously achieving the lowest Semantic-Eval@10 of 0.114, confirming its ability to isolate style from semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntroStyle decisively outperforms all nine baselines across both WikiArt and DomainNet. The closest competitor, CSD ViT-L [38] (the prior state-of-the-art trained with contrastive objectives), is beaten by ~24 points on WikiArt mAP@1 (0.887 vs. 0.646) and ~12 points on DomainNet mAP@1 (0.954 vs. 0.833). General-purpose representation models (CLIP, DINO, MoCo, SSCD) perform substantially worse on style retrieval, with CLIP ViT-L being the strongest among them at 0.595 mAP@1 on WikiArt—still 29 points below IntroStyle. The GDA variants [43], which fine-tune a linear head on a pre-trained backbone, cluster between 0.44–0.52 mAP@1 on WikiArt, well behind both CSD and IntroStyle. On the ArtSplit dataset (Table 2), IntroStyle achieves 0.823 Style-Eval@10 versus 0.682 for DINO and 0.636 for CSD, while also attaining the lowest Semantic-Eval@10 (0.114 vs. 0.143 for GDA DINO), demonstrating superior style-semantic disentanglement. Notably, IntroStyle is training-free and requires no external feature extractor, whereas all baselines either require dedicated training (CSD, GDA) or rely on large pre-trained models (CLIP at 427M params, DINO at 21M) that are still outperformed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the AdaIN-inspired channel-wise statistics (mean and variance) from the style transfer literature and applies them to intermediate features of a pre-trained diffusion UNet, rather than to a separate encoder. It departs from prior style attribution methods (CSD, GDA) that require training external contrastive models or fine-tuning CLIP/DINO backbones, and from unlearning-based attribution that requires per-target model retraining. The conceptual bridge to diffusion features for downstream tasks (DDAE, correspondence, segmentation) motivates the hypothesis that the denoising network already encodes style information, but the specific combination of channel-wise Gaussian statistics with 2-Wasserstein distance as a training-free style metric is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature makes style attribution immediately deployable alongside any existing diffusion model without additional training data or compute, which is practically important for IP protection and style-based rejection sampling. The 20+ point margin over the best trained baseline (CSD) on WikiArt is unusually large for a retrieval task, suggesting prior methods were fundamentally misaligned with style. Key limitations include the 808M parameter requirement (the full SD UNet), evaluation on only Stable Diffusion v2.1, and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to other architectures (e.g., DiT-based models). The ArtSplit dataset addresses a genuine gap in fine-grained style evaluation but is synthetic, raising questions about transfer to real-world style distributions.
