## Problem

Existing unified MLLMs for visual generation rely on single-granularity conditioning from the language model to a diffusion decoder, creating a fundamental trade-off: coarse features enable diverse text-to-image generation but lack the fine detail needed for precise editing and inpainting, while fine features provide controllability at the cost of diversity. No prior framework simultaneously handles both extremes within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252037/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Images are encoded by a frozen CLIP-Large (224×224) into a 16×16 feature grid, then downsampled via successive 2×2 average pooling to produce five granularity levels (256, 64, 16, 4, 1 tokens). The MLLM consumes each image as 341 flattened feature tokens (1+4+16+64+256) interleaved with text tokens, a 33% overhead over standard 256-token encoders. Outputs are decoded by granularity-specific SDXL diffusion decoders into full-resolution images, or as text responses for understanding tasks.

## Method

PUMA extracts multi-scale visual features from CLIP via hierarchical average pooling (f₀–f₄) and trains a set of SDXL-based diffusion decoders, each conditioned on one granularity level through modified cross-attention. A LLaMA-3 8B autoregressive MLLM then progressively generates these features from coarsest to finest level, trained with a combined cross-entropy (text) and L2 regression (image features) loss. A lightweight two-layer Q-Former router, trained on 10K samples with perceptual-loss-derived labels, automatically selects the optimal granularity per instruction—favoring coarse levels for diversity-oriented tasks and fine levels for precision-oriented ones. Training follows a staged pipeline: fine-tune decoders on frozen-encoder features, then train the MLLM, then optimize the router on a small dataset.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, PUMA achieves an overall score of 0.61, matching the best unified model (Janus) and approaching the specialized SD3 (0.62), while significantly outperforming other unified models in counting (0.60 vs. Janus 0.30, SEED-X 0.26) and two-object composition (0.73 vs. Janus 0.68). On MSCOCO 30K, PUMA attains the best CLIP-T score (0.321) and competitive diversity (LPIPS_d 0.603), while on fine-grained image reconstruction (ImageNet), the f0-scale decoder achieves PSNR 18.16 and LPIPS 0.221, substantially surpassing Emu2 (PSNR 15.72) and SEED-X (PSNR 10.86). For image editing on Emu-Edit, PUMA achieves the best CLIP-T (0.262) while maintaining strong source preservation (CLIP-I 0.839, second only to EMU-Edit's 0.859).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified competitor Janus, PUMA ties on overall GenEval score (0.61) but substantially outperforms it in counting (0.60 vs. 0.30) and two-object composition (0.73 vs. 0.68), while Janus leads in spatial position (0.46 vs. 0.30) and attribute binding (0.42 vs. 0.22). Compared to SEED-X, PUMA improves GenEval overall by 0.12 (0.61 vs. 0.49) and counting by 0.34 (0.60 vs. 0.26), and achieves dramatically better fine-grained reconstruction (PSNR 18.16 vs. 10.86, LPIPS 0.221 vs. 0.515). Against Emu2, PUMA wins on GenEval overall (0.61 vs. 0.54), generation diversity (LPIPS_d 0.603 vs. 0.329), and fine-grained decoding (PSNR 18.16 vs. 15.72), though Emu2 retains a slight edge on CLIP-I for MSCOCO (0.686 vs. 0.711 — actually PUMA wins here too at 0.711). On image editing, PUMA achieves the best CLIP-T (0.262) surpassing EMU-Edit (0.231) and OmniGen (0.233), but trails EMU-Edit on source preservation (CLIP-I 0.839 vs. 0.859, DINO 0.780 vs. 0.819). For conditional generation, PUMA's canny-to-image SSIM (0.313) matches ControlNet (0.312) and far exceeds T2I-Adapter (0.231). On understanding benchmarks, PUMA (8B) outperforms SEED-X (17B) on GQA (60.1 vs. 47.9) and MME (1472.7 vs. 1457.0) despite using fewer parameters, though it trails SEED-X on MMBench (67.1 vs. 75.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

PUMA extends the MLLM-plus-diffusion-decoder paradigm established by SEED-X, Emu2, and DreamLLM, where an autoregressive language model generates continuous visual features that condition a diffusion-based image decoder. Its key departure is the introduction of multi-granular feature extraction (via successive 2D average pooling on CLIP features) and a lightweight Q-Former-based router that adaptively selects the appropriate granularity per task, addressing the diversity-controllability trade-off that single-scale methods like SEED-X and Emu2 cannot resolve within one framework. Unlike SEED-X, which handles fine-grained control only through conditional image input limited to editing, PUMA unifies all granularity levels as both inputs and outputs of the MLLM, enabling a single model to span diverse T2I generation through precise inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-granular formulation provides a principled and practical solution to a fundamental tension in unified MLLMs: coarse conditioning enables creative diversity while fine conditioning enables precise manipulation, and prior architectures forced a single compromise. The automatic granularity router removes the need for task-specific model variants, making the framework more deployable. Limitations include the 224×224 input resolution, 8B parameter scale, and the router being trained on only 10K samples, raising questions about robustness to novel task instructions. Open questions include whether this approach extends to video generation, 3D content, or higher-resolution settings, and whether the progressive coarse-to-fine generation order is truly optimal versus parallel multi-scale prediction.
