## Problem

Vector quantization (VQ) has achieved extreme low-bit compression for CNNs and Transformers, but directly applying existing VQ methods to Visual Mamba networks (ViMs) yields unsatisfactory accuracy due to weight outliers in Mamba-based blocks, excessive GPU memory and calibration overhead, and truncation errors from soft-to-hard assignment conversion. No post-training VQ framework had been designed specifically for ViMs prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViM model (Vim-T/S/B or VMamba-T) and a small calibration set of 100 images per category (batch size 128) to produce quantized weight codebooks and index assignments at 3-bit, 2-bit, or 1-bit precision. Only the linear projection layers within ViM blocks are quantized, while activations remain in full precision. At inference, the quantized model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, variable resolution for detection/segmentation) and produces task-specific outputs; a custom CUDA kernel stores codebooks in constant GPU memory and decodes assignments in parallel to achieve near-full-precision inference speed.

## Method

ViM-VQ introduces two components for efficient post-training VQ of ViMs. First, a fast convex combination optimization algorithm identifies top-n nearest candidate codewords per sub-vector, forms differentiable convex combinations via softmax on learnable scores, and simultaneously updates both the combinations (ratios) and the convex hulls (candidate codewords) via gradient descent on a reconstruction loss; an adaptive replacement strategy swaps out candidates whose ratios fall below a threshold for new nearest codewords to the current quantized sub-vector, dynamically shifting the search region without perturbing network outputs. Second, an incremental vector quantization strategy progressively confirms codewords with soft assignment ratios exceeding 0.99 as hard assignments at each calibration iteration, allowing remaining soft assignments to compensate for the truncation error introduced at each conversion step. The calibration objective combines a task loss, a block-wise knowledge distillation loss aligning Mamba block outputs between full-precision and quantized models, and a selectively applied regularization term that encourages rapid convergence of the convex combinations.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K image classification, ViM-VQ achieves 74.79% / 72.17% / 69.93% top-1 accuracy at 3-bit / 2-bit / 1-bit for Vim-T (FP: 76.07%), 80.10% / 78.66% / 72.02% for Vim-S (FP: 80.48%), and 80.34% / 79.46% / 75.58% for Vim-B (FP: 81.88%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation (VMamba-T), ViM-VQ reaches 44.4% mIoU at 2-bit and 41.1% at 1-bit, and on MSCOCO object detection it achieves 46.0% box AP at 2-bit and 40.9% at 1-bit. All uniform quantization baselines (GPTQ, MambaQuant, PTQ4VM) collapse to near-zero accuracy at 2-bit, while ViM-VQ retains over 70% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three UQ baselines (GPTQ, MambaQuant, PTQ4VM) fail catastrophically at 2-bit, dropping to 0.16–3.62% top-1 accuracy on Vim-T/S/B, confirming that uniform quantization is infeasible for ViMs at extreme low-bit. Basic K-Means VQ (VQ†) substantially outperforms UQ but still lags ViM-VQ by 1.1–3.9 points at 2-bit (e.g., 71.25% vs. 72.17% on Vim-T). PQF improves over basic VQ but remains 10+ points behind ViM-VQ at 2-bit and collapses at 1-bit (4.20% vs. 69.93% on Vim-T). The two strongest VQ competitors—DKM (global weighted average) and VQ4DiT (local weighted average)—are within 0.5–1.1 points of ViM-VQ at 2-bit, but ViM-VQ consistently leads across all three model scales and all bit-widths. Notably, ViM-VQ also eliminates the calibration-to-inference accuracy gap that affects DKM and VQ4DiT (e.g., DKM drops from 71.98% calibration to 71.25% inference on Vim-T, while ViM-VQ maintains 72.17% in both phases), thanks to its incremental quantization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViM-VQ extends the vector quantization (VQ) lineage from CNNs and Transformers (PQF, DKM, JLCM, VQ4DiT) to Visual Mamba networks, an architecture family where VQ had not been explored. It directly builds on the authors' prior VQ4DiT work (local weighted-average VQ for diffusion transformers) but departs from it by introducing adaptive convex-hull replacement to escape suboptimal candidate sets and an incremental soft-to-hard assignment strategy to eliminate truncation error. On the ViM-specific side, the paper contrasts with recent uniform quantization approaches (MambaQuant, PTQ4VM) that target the same outlier problem but operate in the UQ paradigm, arguing that VQ's codebook flexibility is essential at extreme low-bit where UQ collapses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first post-training VQ framework for ViMs, filling a clear gap as Mamba-based vision models grow in adoption and edge-deployment pressure increases. The practical CUDA kernel that nearly matches full-precision inference speed makes the compression immediately deployable rather than purely academic. Limitations include evaluation on only two ViM architectures (Vim, VMamba), weight-only quantization (activations remain FP), and a single-GPU experimental setup; whether the convex-combination and incremental strategies transfer to other SSM variants (e.g., Mamba-ND, LocalMamba) or to joint weight-activation quantization remains open.
