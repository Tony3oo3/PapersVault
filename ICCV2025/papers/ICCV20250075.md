## Problem

Vision Transformers remain underexplored in face recognition, where CNNs still dominate, because CNN-inspired single-step training paradigms fail to unlock ViT's potential—leading to convergence instability and suboptimal performance at scale. The paper identifies that the interplay between high-dimensional ViT feature distributions and the lack of local inductive biases causes unstable cluster formation and slow margin convergence when standard margin-based losses are applied directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standardized 112×112 RGB face crop (generated via RetinaFace detection, augmented with stochastic horizontal flipping and normalization) and produces a unit-norm face embedding vector in a d-dimensional space. The ViT backbone (S/B/L variants) omits the [CLS] token and instead concatenates all patch features from the final layer, which are then projected through a two-layer MLP (512-d each with intermediate BatchNorm) to yield the final embedding used for cosine-similarity-based verification.

## Method

LVFace introduces Progressive Cluster Optimization (PCO), a three-stage training strategy that decomposes ViT face recognition learning into: (1) Feature Alignment using negative class sub-sampling (NCS at ratio 0.1) with CosFace loss to mitigate hard-negative noise during early convergence; (2) Centroid Stabilization, which maintains an adaptive exponential-moving-average feature expectation per class and adds a regularization term penalizing the cosine distance between samples and their class prototype, preventing hard positives from destabilizing cluster centers; (3) Boundary Refinement, which disables NCS to expose the model to all negative classes and tighten intra-class boundaries. Stage transitions are governed by a Cosine Stage Scheduler (CSS) that monitors the mean-square cosine similarity between batch features and their class centroids, advancing to the next stage only when this score exceeds empirically set thresholds (0.2 and 0.35), ensuring representations achieve statistically significant discriminative improvement before the optimization target shifts.

## Results

Trained on WebFace42M with a ViT-L backbone, LVFace achieves 98.49% on the MFR-Ongoing MR-All metric, 97.25% TAR@FAR=1e-5 and 98.06% TAR@FAR=1e-4 on IJB-C, and 96.74% TAR@FAR=1e-4 on IJB-B. On the Glint360K-trained variant (ViT-L), LVFace reaches 97.02% on IJB-C (1e-5) and 96.51% on IJB-B (1e-4). LVFace secured 1st place on the MFR-Ongoing academic leaderboard as of March 2025.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVFace (ViT-L, WebFace42M) outperforms all four baselines on the MFR-Ongoing MR-All metric (98.49%), beating TopoFR (98.13%), UniTSFace (98.16%), UniFace (97.92%), and Partial FC (97.85%). It also leads on IJB-C at both FAR thresholds (97.25% at 1e-5, 98.06% at 1e-4), surpassing the closest competitor Partial FC (97.23%, 98.00%) and TopoFR (97.10%, 98.01%). The only sub-task where LVFace trails is Mask (93.56% vs. TopoFR's 93.96%) and East Asian (91.02% vs. TopoFR's 92.85%), though it compensates with large margins on Children (+0.74%), African (+0.82%), and Caucasian (+0.55%). On the Glint360K-trained comparison (Table 2), LVFace-L (97.02% IJB-C 1e-5) exceeds TransFace-L (96.29%), AdaFace R200 (95.96%), and ArcFace R200 (95.71%) by 0.7–1.3 points, with consistent gains across ViT-S, ViT-B, and ViT-L scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVFace builds on three distinct threads: the CosFace margin-based loss (Wang et al., 2018) as its base objective, the negative class sub-sampling strategy from Partial FC (An et al., 2022) for efficient large-scale training, and the feature-expectation prototype mechanism from EPL (Fan et al., 2024) for centroid stabilization. The key departure is in training paradigm: rather than applying a single margin loss end-to-end as prior ViT-based FR methods (FaceTransformer, TransFace) do, LVFace decomposes optimization into three sequential stages governed by a cosine stage scheduler, drawing conceptual inspiration from the multi-stage training pipelines of LLMs and VLMs. This positions the paper as an argument that the bottleneck for ViTs in face recognition is not architectural but optimization-strategic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LVFace demonstrates that ViTs can match or exceed strong CNN baselines in face recognition when trained with a purpose-built progressive schedule, removing a key objection to adopting Transformers in biometric pipelines and opening the door to unified vision-language face models. The practical impact is reinforced by 1st place on the MFR-Ongoing leaderboard and consistent gains across IJB-B/C at multiple backbone scales. Open questions include whether the fixed stage-transition thresholds (δ₁=0.2, δ₂=0.35) generalize across dataset scales and domains, whether the three-stage decomposition is optimal or whether finer-grained scheduling could yield further gains, and whether the approach transfers to other embedding-based recognition tasks (e.g., speaker, gait).
