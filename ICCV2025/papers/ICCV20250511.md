## Problem

Cost-volume-based LF disparity methods are constrained by a predefined disparity range and high memory overhead, limiting their ability to handle large-parallax scenes. While self-attention (parallax attention) offers a promising alternative, LFs introduce two unresolved challenges: different views have different baselines so their attention maps encode disparity at different scales, and occlusions in some views make matching ambiguous. The paper addresses how to unify these scale-varying attention maps and selectively aggregate only occlusion-free matching information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4D light field L(u,v,x,y) and processes it at 3×3 or 5×5 angular resolutions (central view I_(0,0) as reference), with spatial patches of 100×100 during training. It outputs the disparity map of the central view D_c ∈ R^{H×W} regressed as a weighted sum over disparity candidates, along with per-view binary occlusion masks O_(u,0) and O_(0,v) that label which pixels are occluded relative to the central view. The disparity range is set to [-20, 20] for SLFD and [-4, 4] for HCI.

## Method

ECAAN computes horizontal and vertical EPI parallax attention maps via Q·K^T on EPI features, then aggregates them through two key blocks. The Epipolar Consistent Scale Unification (ECSU) block re-samples each view's attention map to a common disparity range, then up-samples using a deconvolution layer with magnification factor f = N/|v| (where N is the LCM of {1,…,V/2}), placing original similarity scores at their correct positions in a unified scale space without interpolation. The Consistent Occlusion-Free Aggregation (COFA) block detects occlusions by thresholding attention maps with a guided filter, then feeds the up-sampled attention maps through a transformer to compute inter-view consistency weights, which are combined with occlusion masks (mapped to −∞) via softmax to produce occlusion-aware fusion weights. The final disparity is regressed from the fused attention map, and training is fully unsupervised using an improved photometric loss masked by extended occlusion labels, a PAM loss on multi-scale attention maps, and a smoothness loss.

## Results

On the large-disparity SLFD dataset, ECAAN₅ (5×5 views) achieves MSE×100 of 0.087 (Lion), 0.101 (Rooster clock), 0.408 (Toy bricks), and 0.211 (Electro devices), representing a 68% average MSE reduction over the second-best unsupervised method (UnMVFM). ECAAN₃ (3×3 views) also outperforms all baselines, with MSE×100 of 0.173, 0.177, 0.332, and 0.290 on the same scenes. On the small-disparity HCI benchmark (7×7 views), ECAAN achieves an average MSE of 4.16 and Bp(0.07) of 10.24, competitive with other unsupervised methods (UnLFDisp: 4.44/7.71; UnGAN: 4.18/8.70) but trailing supervised methods (OACCNet: 1.69/3.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the large-disparity SLFD dataset, ECAAN₅ decisively outperforms all 13 baselines across every scene and metric. Against the strongest unsupervised competitor UnMVFM [31], ECAAN₅ reduces MSE by 68% on average (e.g., 0.087 vs 0.360 on Lion, 0.101 vs 0.261 on Rooster clock) and Bp(0.3) by up to 87% (1.14 vs 8.77 on Lion). ECAAN₅ also surpasses the best supervised method LFAttNet [24] (MSE 0.372 on Lion) by a factor of 4.3×, despite being trained without ground-truth disparity. On the small-disparity HCI benchmark, ECAAN is competitive among unsupervised methods (avg MSE 4.16 vs UnLFDisp 4.44, UnGAN 4.18) but trails supervised approaches like OACCNet (1.69) and AttMLFNet (1.78), which benefit from GT supervision unavailable in the unsupervised setting. The gap to supervised methods on HCI narrows on scenes with complex occlusions (e.g., Backgammon: ECAAN 10.18 vs OACCNet 3.94), suggesting the advantage of supervision is most pronounced in simpler geometric scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the parallax attention mechanism from PAM (Wang et al., TPAMI 2022), which was designed for stereo correspondence between two views, to the multi-view light field setting where correspondences exist along epipolar lines in multiple directions simultaneously. It departs from the dominant cost-volume paradigm (EPINet, LFAttNet, UnMVFM, etc.) that constrains disparity search to a predefined range, instead regressing disparity from attention-weighted candidates. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that parallax attention maps from views at different baselines encode disparity at different positional scales, requiring an epipolar-consistent re-sampling strategy (deconvolution with view-dependent magnification) rather than naive resizing, and that occlusion patterns differ per view, necessitating occlusion-aware aggregation weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 68% MSE reduction on large-disparity SLFD scenes demonstrates that attention-based correspondence can overcome the fundamental range limitation of cost-volume methods, opening a path toward handling extreme parallax without prohibitive memory costs. However, the method does not surpass supervised approaches on small-disparity HCI scenes, and real-world generalization remains imperfect, suggesting the unsupervised photometric signal is still insufficient for fine-grained accuracy. Open questions include whether the epipolar-consistent aggregation strategy scales to very large angular resolutions (beyond 5×5), whether the deconvolution up-sampling can be replaced by learned resampling, and whether the approach transfers to other multi-view geometry tasks beyond disparity.
