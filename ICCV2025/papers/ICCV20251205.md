## Problem

Autoregressive next-token prediction is the dominant pre-training paradigm for language models, but its effectiveness for learning general visual representations from unlabeled video has not been systematically studied. The paper asks whether a causal transformer trained purely on next-visual-token prediction, with minimal inductive biases, can produce representations competitive with purpose-built self-supervised vision methods across diverse downstream tasks, and how such models scale with compute relative to language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Videos and images are independently tokenized per frame using dVAE (8k vocabulary) into 16×16 discrete tokens at 128×128 resolution, arranged in raster-scan order to form sequences of 4096 tokens (16 frames or 16 images per sequence). The model outputs the next-token prediction during pre-training; for downstream evaluation, intermediate layer features are aggregated via learned attention pooling into a single vector per sample, which is then probed with a linear head for classification, tracking, or control tasks.

## Method

Toto is a causal transformer following the LLaMA architecture (pre-norm RMSNorm, SwiGLU activations, RoPE positional embeddings) trained on next-visual-token prediction over 1T+ tokens from ImageNet, Kinetics-600, Ego4D, and HowTo100m. The key architectural choice is using dVAE discrete tokens rather than raw pixels or continuous patch embeddings, enabling a unified format for both images and videos. The paper systematically ablates tokenizer choice (dVAE vs. VQGAN vs. patch-normalized), backbone architecture (LLaMA vs. GPT-2 vs. Mamba), and probing strategy, finding that attention pooling outperforms average pooling by ~8% due to the skewed receptive field of causal attention, and that middle layers (≈50% depth) yield the best representations. A low-resolution pre-training followed by one-epoch high-resolution fine-tuning with RoPE base-value adjustment outperforms full-resolution pre-training at lower compute cost. Scaling analysis with μP shows a power-law loss-compute relationship (L(C) = 7.32·C^{-0.0378}), indicating video autoregressive models scale but at a slower rate than language models.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, Toto-1b (1.1B params) achieves 75.3% top-1 accuracy via attention probing, outperforming iGPT-XL (72.0%, 6.8B params) by 3.3 points with 6× fewer parameters. On Kinetics-400 action recognition, Toto-1b reaches 74.4% top-1, competitive with generative baselines like VideoMAE (79.8%) and Hiera (77.0%). On Ego4D short-term action anticipation, Toto-large achieves 2.70 overall mAP, the best among all compared methods. On CATER object permanence, Toto-large outperforms purpose-built models (V3D, TFC-V3D) by 7.6 and 8.3 points at 16 frames respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among autoregressive generative models, Toto-1b (75.3%) clearly outperforms iGPT-XL (72.0%) by 3.3 points on ImageNet while using only 1.1B vs. 6.8B parameters, demonstrating the benefit of token-based (vs. pixel-based) autoregressive modeling. Against discriminative self-supervised methods, Toto-1b trails DINO (80.1%), MAE (80.9%), and DINOv2 (86.4%), confirming the known gap between generative and discriminative pre-training on recognition. On video action recognition (K400), Toto-1b (74.4%) is competitive with generative baselines (VideoMAE 79.8%, MVD 79.4%) but lags discriminative models (DINOv2 84.4%, OpenCLIP 83.3%). On Ego4D action anticipation, Toto-large (2.70 mAP) beats all compared methods including MAE-ST (2.60), VideoMAE (2.55), and StillFast (2.48). On DAVIS tracking, Toto-1b (46.1 J&F) surpasses MAE-base (31.5) but falls short of DINO-base (54.3); however, Toto-large at 512 resolution (62.4) exceeds DINO-base. On CATER object permanence, Toto-large (62.8%/72.9% at 16/32 frames) outperforms V3D (55.2%/69.7%) and TFC-V3D (54.6%/70.2%), suggesting autoregressive pre-training captures temporal reasoning well.

## Relation to Prior Work

Toto applies the LLaMA-style autoregressive transformer (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE) to video by tokenizing frames with dVAE and performing next-token prediction, extending the iGPT paradigm from images to video sequences. It departs from discriminative self-supervised approaches (MAE, DINO, VideoMAE) by using a purely generative objective, and from prior autoregressive vision work (iGPT, AIM) by operating on discrete visual tokens rather than raw pixels and by jointly training on images and videos in a unified sequence format. The paper is explicitly framed as an empirical study, systematically ablation-testing tokenizer choices, architecture variants (LLaMA vs. GPT-2 vs. Mamba), probing strategies, and resolution schedules rather than proposing a single novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive empirical evidence that LLaMA-style autoregressive pre-training on video tokens yields competitive representations across a wide range of downstream tasks (recognition, tracking, robotics, object permanence), validating the 'next token prediction' recipe for video. The scaling-law finding—that video models follow a power law with a slower exponent (−0.0378) than language models (−0.048)—suggests that video's inherent temporal redundancy limits how efficiently additional compute translates into loss reduction, pointing to better tokenizers and data curation as key levers for future progress. Practical takeaways (attention pooling over average pooling, middle-layer probing, lower-resolution pre-training followed by RoPE-based fine-tuning) are immediately actionable. Open questions include whether the slower scaling is fundamental to video or an artifact of the current dVAE tokenizer, and whether the approach can close the gap to discriminative methods like DINOv2 at scale.
