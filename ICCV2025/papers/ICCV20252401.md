## Problem

Prior OOD-aware prompt tuning methods like CoOp-OOD disentangle invariant and spurious features only in the image modality, leaving textual features unaddressed. This creates ambiguous cross-modal mappings where spurious text embeddings can inadvertently align with invariant image features, weakening robustness on novel classes and under distribution shift. The paper asks whether explicitly disentangling both modalities and enforcing consistent cross-modal alignment (invariant-to-invariant, spurious-to-spurious) can resolve this ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252401/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and a text prompt (the class name) processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone, using 16 few-shot samples per class for prompt tuning. Both the vision and text encoder outputs (512-dim embeddings) are projected into separate 512-dim invariant and spurious subspaces. The final output is a single class label predicted from the similarity between invariant image and invariant text features, with spurious features explicitly regularized to be uninformative for classification.

## Method

DiMPLe decomposes both image and text features into invariant and spurious subspaces via separate linear projections, then enforces conditional independence between these subspaces within each modality using an HSIC-based mutual information penalty. It introduces deep multi-modal prompting where learnable prompt tokens are injected into both vision and language transformer branches up to layer J, with vision prompts conditioned on language prompts through a per-layer linear projection to ensure cross-modal consistency. The training objective combines three terms: a cross-entropy loss aligning invariant image-text features for classification, a KL-divergence uniformity loss pushing spurious features toward a uniform distribution (preventing shortcut learning), and the conditional mutual information penalty separating invariant from spurious components. This joint disentanglement across both modalities, rather than image-only as in CoOp-OOD, ensures that invariant text features align exclusively with invariant image features while spurious counterparts are kept isolated, yielding consistent feature associations during inference.

## Results

Averaged over 11 datasets, DiMPLe achieves 76.09% base accuracy, 73.35% novel accuracy, and 74.70% harmonic mean (HM), yielding absolute gains of +15.27 (base) and +44.31 (novel) over CoOp-OOD. On domain generalization (ImageNet → 4 variants), DiMPLe attains 69.73% on the source ImageNet and remains competitive with CLIP on all OOD targets (e.g., 61.2% on ImageNetV2 vs. CLIP's 60.83%), while CoOp-OOD drops to 34.53% on ImageNetV2. On the CelebA group-fairness benchmark, DiMPLe improves worst-group accuracy from 31.11% (CoOp-OOD) to 70.0% without explicit group information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMPLe substantially outperforms CoOp-OOD across all metrics: +15.27 on base accuracy (76.09 vs. 60.82), +44.31 on novel accuracy (73.35 vs. 29.04), and +35.39 on HM (74.70 vs. 39.31), confirming that multi-modal disentanglement is critical for OOD generalization. Against CLIP, DiMPLe gains +6.75 on base accuracy and +3.0 on HM but trails by 0.87 on novel accuracy, indicating a slight trade-off in zero-shot novel-class transfer. DiMPLe also beats the enhanced CoOp-OOD† (which adds conditioned vision prompts) by +13.37 on base, +7.75 on novel, and +10.57 on HM, showing that the disentanglement objective itself—not just the prompting architecture—drives the improvement. However, DiMPLe underperforms MaPLe (82.28/75.14/78.55) and ProDA (81.56/72.30/76.65) on base accuracy and HM, suggesting that meta-learning-based prompt decomposition still holds an edge on in-distribution performance, though DiMPLe narrows the gap on novel classes (73.35 vs. 75.14 for MaPLe).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMPLe extends CoOp-OOD's image-only feature disentanglement to the text modality, addressing what the authors identify as a critical gap: without disentangling text features, spurious textual elements can inadvertently align with invariant visual features, undermining OOD robustness. It adopts MaPLe's deep multi-modal prompting architecture (learnable tokens in both vision and language branches) and conditions vision prompts on language prompts via linear projection, creating a unified cross-modal prompt space. The method departs from CoOp-OOD by introducing a three-component loss (conditional mutual information minimization via HSIC, KL-divergence spurious regularization, and contrastive invariant alignment) that operates symmetrically across both modalities rather than treating text as a fixed alignment target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that invariant image features can misalign with spurious text prompts, and that this ambiguity propagates under distribution shift—provides a principled justification for cross-modal disentanglement that prior prompt-tuning work overlooked. However, DiMPLe still trails MaPLe and ProDA on average harmonic mean (74.70 vs 78.55 and 76.65), suggesting the disentanglement objective may be too restrictive or that the linear projection decomposition is insufficient for capturing complex spurious structure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger CLIP variants, whether the HSIC-based mutual information estimator remains effective at higher dimensions, and whether the fixed loss weighting (α=1.0, β=2.0) generalizes across architectures and dataset regimes.
