## Problem

Existing Gaussian splatting methods for surface reconstruction achieve fast rendering but require dense input views and per-scene optimization, preventing generalization to new scenes. Conversely, generalizable neural implicit methods (e.g., VolRecon, UFORecon) can handle sparse views but rely on per-pixel volume rendering along camera rays, making them 100×+ slower. The paper bridges this gap by combining the efficiency of rasterization-based Gaussian rendering with cross-view geometric reasoning to enable fast, generalizable surface reconstruction from only 3 sparse views.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 calibrated RGB images at 600×800 resolution with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input, and produces pixel-aligned 2D Gaussian surfel primitives (center, scale, opacity, rotation, color per pixel) that are rendered to per-view depth and normal maps via differentiable splatting. These depth maps are fused through TSDF to yield the final 3D surface mesh. During training, 4 source views plus 1 target view at 512×640 are used with a 3-stage cascade sampling 48, 24, and 8 depth hypotheses per stage.

## Method

GSRecon builds a cascaded cost volume per view (following TransMVSNet) to estimate MVS depth, then introduces a geometry-aware cross-view enhancement (GCE) module that back-projects depth maps to 3D, aggregates features via a sparse voxel U-Net, decodes 2D Gaussian parameters, renders depth maps in milliseconds, and injects the rendered depth as a residual volume into the next stage's cost volume for adaptive correction. For final Gaussian prediction, a hybrid cross-view feature aggregation (HCFA) module combines the voxel branch with a point branch that uses view-based neighbor search (projecting points onto images and searching structured t×t neighborhoods) to reduce K-NN complexity from O((NHW)²) to O(NHW). The two branches' features are concatenated and passed through Gaussian heads to predict fine-grained 2D surfel parameters. Training uses a composite loss of L1 rendering loss (color + depth), cross-entropy MVS depth loss, and a normal alignment loss, optimized for 16 epochs on 2× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On the DTU Favorable set (15 scenes), GSRecon achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.95 mm, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art UFORecon (0.99 mm) and even surpassing per-scene optimization methods like NeuSurf (0.99 mm) and SparseCraft (1.04 mm). On the more challenging Unfavorable set, GSRecon reports a 16.7% average improvement over UFORecon. In terms of efficiency, GSRecon completes reconstruction in 0.8 s, which is approximately 120× faster than UFORecon (102 s) and 799× faster than per-scene 2D GS (639 s), while using 8.4 GB of GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSRecon outperforms all 10 baselines on the DTU Favorable set in mean Chamfer Distance. The closest competitor is UFORecon [34] (0.99 vs. 0.95 mm), the previous state-of-the-art generalizable method; GSRecon also beats per-scene methods NeuSurf [20] (0.99 mm) and SparseCraft [54] (1.04 mm) despite being feedforward. Among generalizable neural implicit methods, GSRecon leads SuRF [38] (1.05 mm), C2F2NeuS [47] (1.11 mm), ReTR [27] (1.17 mm), GenS [37] (1.34 mm), VolRecon [39] (1.38 mm), and SparseNeuS [31] (1.64 mm) by 0.10–0.69 mm. On the Unfavorable set (large viewpoint variation), GSRecon achieves a 16.7% average improvement over UFORecon, and on depth map accuracy it surpasses UFORecon by 13.24 points in <1 mm accuracy (78.63% vs. 65.37%) on the Favorable set. In computational cost, GSRecon (0.8 s) is ~120× faster than UFORecon (102 s), ~101× faster than ReTR (81 s), and ~799× faster than per-scene 2D GS [19] (639 s), at the cost of slightly higher GPU memory (8.4 GB vs. 7.1 GB for UFORecon). On outdoor generalization (BlendedMVS, Tanks and Temples), GSRecon produces more complete and accurate surfaces than all compared methods without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSRecon bridges two previously separate lines of work: generalizable neural implicit surface reconstruction (SparseNeuS, GenS, VolRecon, UFORecon), which relies on slow per-ray volume rendering, and 2D Gaussian Splatting (2D GS, SuGaR), which is fast but requires per-scene optimization and dense views. The paper adopts the MVS cost-volume construction from TransMVSNet and the 2D Gaussian surfel representation from 2D GS, but departs from both by introducing a cross-view enhancement loop that uses Gaussian-rendered depth as a geometric cue to refine the MVS cost volume at subsequent cascade stages, and by replacing expensive 3D K-NN neighborhood search with a structured 2D view-based search for fine-grained feature aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 120× speedup (0.8 s vs. ~100 s) over the prior state-of-the-art UFORecon makes feedforward surface reconstruction from just 3 views practically viable in interactive or near-real-time settings, a regime previously dominated by per-scene optimization methods. The ablation revealing that the point branch degrades performance on unfavorable sets while the voxel branch helps is a useful diagnostic: under large viewpoint variation, contextual coherence matters more than fine-grained detail. Open questions include whether the three-stage cascade and its associated memory overhead scale to larger outdoor scenes, and whether the method's reliance on MVS-style depth hypotheses limits its applicability to scenes with very large depth ranges or unbounded environments.
