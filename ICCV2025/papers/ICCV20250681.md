## Problem

Existing monocular semantic scene completion methods treat the task as a single-stage problem, failing to distinguish between visible (visual frontier) and invisible (occluded/out-of-view) regions. This leads to feature entanglement, where learned features for visible and invisible voxels are conflated, and geometric inconsistency, where the reconstructed visible and invisible regions are misaligned or structurally conflicting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250681/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, from which a depth map is estimated via MobileStereoNet to identify the visual frontier. The output is a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid at 256×256×32 resolution (0.2 m voxel size over a 51.2 m × 51.2 m × 6.4 m scene), with per-voxel class labels across 19–20 semantic categories depending on the dataset. The depth map is consumed internally for frontier identification and unsigned distance computation rather than being a direct input modality.

## Method

VisHall3D decomposes monocular SSC into two stages: (1) VisFrontierNet reconstructs visible voxels by encoding the visual frontier geometry via a truncated unsigned distance function (replacing hard binary lifting), fusing it with 2D image features through RoI pooling, and refining with 3D deformable attention over a depth-probability-lifted feature volume; (2) OcclusionMAE, a denoising Masked Autoencoder, hallucinates the full occupancy grid by adding geometric noise (sampling from neighboring voxels in horizontal and depth directions) to the visible-voxel predictions, injecting the noise level via adaptive batch normalization, and decoding with a 3D U-Net conditioned on the refined frontier features. The key design choice is that noise perturbs only geometry (voxel positions) while preserving semantics, preventing the network from generating non-existent classes. This decoupling allows each stage to specialize: VisFrontierNet focuses on accurate frontier tracing, while OcclusionMAE handles the generative completion of occluded and out-of-view regions.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, VisHall3D achieves an IoU of 46.50% and mIoU of 17.46%, surpassing the previous best monocular single-frame method CGFormer (44.41% IoU, 16.63% mIoU) by 2.09% IoU and 0.83% mIoU. On SSCBench-KITTI-360, VisHall3D reaches 49.12% IoU and 20.95% mIoU, outperforming CGFormer by 1.05% IoU and 0.90% mIoU. Notably, VisHall3D also exceeds the best stereo method (StereoScene, 43.34% IoU) and the best temporal method (HTCL, 44.23% IoU) on SemanticKITTI, while maintaining comparable parameter count (127.8M vs. 122.4M for CGFormer) and faster inference (0.34s vs. 0.41s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisHall3D outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both datasets. On SemanticKITTI, it beats the strongest monocular single-frame competitor CGFormer [43] by 2.09% IoU and 0.83% mIoU, and also surpasses the best temporal method HTCL [15] (44.23% IoU, 17.09% mIoU) and the best stereo method StereoScene [14] (43.34% IoU, 15.36% mIoU). On SSCBench-KITTI-360, VisHall3D leads CGFormer by 1.05% IoU and 0.90% mIoU, and notably exceeds early LiDAR-based methods SSCNet [34] (mIoU 16.95%) and LMSCNet [32] (mIoU 13.65%) in mIoU, demonstrating that monocular approaches can now rival multi-sensor pipelines. Per-class gains are most pronounced on small or occluded objects: truck IoU improves from 4.30% (CGFormer) to 7.50%, and bicycle from 3.70% to 2.90% (a slight loss), while person improves from 1.70% to 2.30%. The method also achieves the best fence (19.50% vs. 18.70%) and vegetation (27.30% vs. 24.50%) scores, indicating stronger geometric consistency in complex regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisHall3D extends the monocular semantic scene completion lineage (MonoScene → VoxFormer → Symphonies → CGFormer) by introducing an explicit two-stage decomposition: a visibility-aware frontier reconstruction stage (VisFrontierNet) followed by a denoising-MAE-based hallucination stage (OcclusionMAE). VisFrontierNet departs from the hard-assignment lifting used in Symphonies and VoxFormer by replacing binary pixel-to-voxel correspondence with a truncated unsigned distance function, while OcclusionMAE repurposes the denoising masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) for 3D occupancy generation with geometric-only noise injection. The paper's core conceptual departure from prior work is treating visible and invisible regions as fundamentally different sub-problems rather than a single unified prediction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage decomposition offers a principled way to address feature entanglement and geometric inconsistency that have limited prior monocular SSC methods, and the unsigned distance encoding is a practical improvement over hard lifting that practitioners can adopt independently. However, the approach still depends on an external depth estimator (MobileStereoNet), and the mIoU gains over the strongest baseline (CGFormer) are modest (~0.8–0.9%), leaving open whether the decomposition's benefits persist under weaker depth priors or in more complex indoor/urban scenes. A key open question is whether the noise-injection strategy in OcclusionMAE can be made adaptive per-scene rather than relying on fixed horizontal/depth ranges.
