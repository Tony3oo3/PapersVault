## Problem

Supervised retinal lesion segmentation methods require large annotated datasets, explicit fine-tuning on multi-vendor scans, and still struggle to detect small lesions such as microaneurysms. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a fully unsupervised framework that segments and classifies retinal lesions without ground-truth labels, extensive training, or vendor-specific adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB fundus image as input, which is first decomposed into approximately 100 superpixels via SLIC; each superpixel patch is then resized to 224×224×3 for feature extraction. The output is a two-level result: a binary segmentation mask distinguishing lesion from healthy superpixels (evaluated at pixel level), followed by per-lesion-superpixel class labels drawn from a four-class vocabulary (hard exudates, soft exudates, microaneurysms, haemorrhages) assigned via zero-shot vision-language matching.

## Method

The core contribution is a Neural Graph Featurization (NGF) scheme that constructs an undirected graph over SLIC superpixels, then traverses it along a Hamiltonian path to iteratively propagate and refine node and edge embeddings using six pre-trained feature extractors (PVT for initial node encoding, plus smaller networks for edge, upstream, downstream, and node-update operations). The Hamiltonian path imposes a directed upstream/downstream structure on each node's neighborhood, allowing the iterative updates to capture both appearance and geometric context simultaneously. Convergence is monitored via a normalized XOR metric on the adjacency matrix (K=5 window, σ=1% threshold), after which a normalized-cut graph partition separates the converged latent embeddings into lesion and non-lesion clusters. Finally, a zero-shot vision-language classifier (PVT visual encoder + BERT text encoder with cosine similarity over prompt templates) assigns each lesion superpixel to its specific pathology class without any task-specific training.

## Results

The proposed unsupervised method achieves F1-scores of 0.89 (ODIR), 0.93 (BIOMISA), 0.92 (IDRiD), and 0.92 (FIVES), consistently outperforming all supervised baselines by 2–9 percentage points in F1 across the four datasets. IoU scores range from 0.80 to 0.86, with the largest gains observed on IDRiD (+0.08 over DMSF) and FIVES (+0.07 over SegDrOWS). In the few-shot variant (20 examples per class), F1 improves further to 0.94, 0.96, and 0.95 on ODIR, BIOMISA, and IDRiD respectively, while the zero-shot variant already surpasses all supervised SOTA methods without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed unsupervised method outperforms all four supervised baselines on every dataset and every metric. On ODIR, it beats DMSF [10] by +0.05 F1 (0.89 vs 0.84) and MSAG [34] by +0.07 F1 (0.89 vs 0.82). On BIOMISA, the margin over the strongest baseline DMSF is +0.02 F1 (0.93 vs 0.91), while DRUNET [2] trails by +0.05 F1. On IDRiD, gains are larger: +0.04 F1 over DMSF and +0.07 F1 over DRUNET. On FIVES, the advantage is most pronounced, with +0.04 F1 over SegDrOWS [21] and +0.09 F1 over MSAG. Notably, the proposed method requires no ground-truth labels or task-specific training, whereas all baselines are fully supervised, making the performance gap particularly striking given the absence of any labeled training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes several established components—SLIC superpixelization, Hamiltonian path traversal, normalized-cut graph partitioning, and vision-language models (PVT + BERT)—into a novel unsupervised pipeline for retinal lesion segmentation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant supervised deep learning paradigm (RTNet, DMSF, MSAG, DRUNET) by eliminating task-specific training entirely, and from prior graph-based fundus methods (Liu et al., Lei et al., Salam et al.) by using an iterative message-passing scheme over a Hamiltonian-ordered complete graph rather than learned GNN architectures. The zero-shot VLM classification stage for lesion subtyping (hard/soft exudates, microaneurysms, haemorrhages) extends the use of prompt-based vision-language models from general recognition to fine-grained medical pathology labeling without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the dependency on large annotated datasets and vendor-specific fine-tuning, which are major barriers to deploying retinal screening tools across diverse clinical settings with heterogeneous scanners. The zero-shot variant already outperforming supervised SOTA methods suggests that pre-trained visual and linguistic knowledge, combined with structured graph reasoning, may be sufficient for lesion discrimination without task-specific supervision. Open questions include whether the Hamiltonian-path-based traversal is truly necessary versus simpler graph propagation, how the method scales to larger or more diverse lesion taxonomies, and whether the multi-model pipeline (six pre-trained feature extractors plus PVT and BERT) can be simplified without sacrificing the reported gains. The authors themselves note future work in broader medical and non-medical domains for fine-grained small-object segmentation.
