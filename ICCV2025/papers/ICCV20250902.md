## Problem

Photorealistic style transfer requires strict structural preservation while transferring color characteristics, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: generation-based approaches sacrifice content integrity and real-time efficiency, while global 3D LUTs preserve structure yet apply identical transformations to same-color pixels across different semantic regions (e.g., sky vs. sea). No prior method combines LUT-level efficiency with the spatially adaptive, context-aware color adjustments that professional color grading demands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a style reference image in RGB space and a content image in LOG color space (e.g., ACES), producing a stylized RGB output. The 4D LUT is parameterized as 3×2×17×17 (RGB channels × 2 context bins × 17×17 color discretization), and the context map is a single-channel [0,1]^{H×W} tensor at content resolution. LUT generation (0.21 s) is decoupled from per-pixel application (0.01 s), enabling the same style LUT to be reused across multiple content frames at over 16 FPS on 4K video.

## Method

SA-LUT consists of two components: (1) a Style-guided 4D LUT Generator that extracts multi-scale VGG features from the style image, predicts softmax weights over N=64 learnable 4D basis LUTs, and fuses them into a style-specific LUT via a weighted sum plus identity residual; and (2) a Context Generator that computes cross-attention between lightweight CNN-encoded content and style features to produce a spatially varying context map. The final transformation applies quadrilinear interpolation over the 4D LUT using the concatenated [context, RGB] as the 4-channel lookup coordinate, blending between two 3D LUT slices per pixel to achieve region-specific color grading. Training uses a dual-stream strategy—synthetic LUT-supervised pairs for direct pixel loss and real photographs with a patch-similarity discriminator for adversarial style learning—combined with LPIPS, total-variation, monotonicity, and adversarial losses.

## Results

On the PST50 benchmark, SA-LUT achieves the best scores across all four quality metrics: LPIPS 0.12, PSNR 25.29 dB, SSIM 0.92, and H-Corr 0.51. Compared to the closest LUT-based baseline NLUT, SA-LUT reduces LPIPS by 66.7% (0.36→0.12), improves PSNR by 4.7 dB, and boosts SSIM by 15%. Inference is also dramatically faster for the style-encoding stage: 0.21 s for 4D LUT generation versus 16.11 s for NLUT (≈75× speedup), with only 0.01 s per-frame application cost, enabling real-time 4K video stylization at >16 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-LUT outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric (LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, H-Corr). Against the nearest LUT-based competitor NLUT [4], the gains are most dramatic: LPIPS drops from 0.36 to 0.12 (−66.7%), PSNR rises from 20.59 to 25.29 dB, and SSIM improves from 0.80 to 0.92, while LUT generation is ~75× faster (0.21 s vs 16.11 s). Versus the strongest non-LUT baseline Neural Preset [15], SA-LUT still leads on all four metrics (LPIPS 0.12 vs 0.19, PSNR 25.29 vs 23.03, SSIM 0.92 vs 0.89, H-Corr 0.51 vs 0.44) and adds explicit spatial adaptivity. Global-preset methods (Deep Preset [11], ModFlow [16]) are faster in raw inference but trail substantially in perceptual quality and style fidelity. In the user study (133 participants, 20 image pairs), SA-LUT was preferred 48.79% of the time versus 33.60% for Neural Preset and 17.61% for NLUT, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived realism and style similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-LUT extends the 4D LUT concept introduced by Liu et al. [20] for image enhancement by making the LUT style-conditioned (predicted from VGG-extracted style features via learned basis LUTs) and deriving the context dimension from content-style cross-attention rather than from a single image. It also builds on the LUT-based style transfer line initiated by NLUT [4], which demonstrated that neural 3D LUTs can handle video PST, but departs from NLUT's spatially invariant mapping by introducing a context-aware fourth dimension. The method positions itself against both feed-forward PST networks (WCT, WCT², DPST) that sacrifice efficiency and structural fidelity, and global preset methods (Deep Preset, Neural Preset) that lack spatial adaptivity, occupying a middle ground that preserves LUT hardware efficiency while enabling region-specific color grading.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of PST50, the first benchmark with professional ground-truth stylized images for photorealistic style transfer, addresses a long-standing evaluation gap that has hindered objective comparison in the field. The practical significance is amplified by real-time 4K video processing at 16 FPS, making the method directly applicable to professional color grading workflows where immediate visual feedback is essential. Limitations include the single-channel context map (which may be insufficient for scenes requiring more than two distinct transformation regimes), the reliance on LOG color space input (limiting applicability to professional pipelines), and the relatively small benchmark size (50 pairs). Open questions include whether the 2-bin context dimension can be scaled to handle more complex multi-region stylization and whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond color transfer, such as spatially adaptive tone mapping or local exposure correction.
