## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for accelerating diffusion models follow a 'cache-then-reuse' paradigm that directly copies features from previous timesteps. As the timestep gap increases, feature similarity decreases exponentially, causing severe quality degradation and capping achievable acceleration at roughly 3–4×. This paper identifies that the fundamental limitation is treating feature caching as a static reuse problem rather than a temporal prediction problem.

## Input / Output

TaylorSeer is a training-free, inference-time acceleration applied to pre-trained diffusion transformers (FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo, DiT-XL/2). It takes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts for image/video generation or class labels for ImageNet—and produces the same outputs (1000×1000 images, 256×256 images, or video clips). The method requires (O+1) fully-activated timesteps per caching window of size N to predict all intermediate features, where O is the Taylor expansion order; no additional parameters or search are introduced.

## Method

TaylorSeer replaces direct feature reuse with Taylor-series-based forecasting. It observes that features in diffusion models follow stable, smooth trajectories across timesteps, and that their finite differences (derivatives) are themselves stable. At each fully-activated timestep, the method caches the feature value and its first O forward finite differences (computed recursively from O+1 consecutive full activations). Intermediate features are then predicted via an m-th order Taylor expansion: F_pred(x_{t-k}) = F(x_t) + Σ_{i=1}^{O} [Δ^i F(x_t) / (i! · N^i)] · (−k)^i. Higher orders (O≥2) capture nonlinear feature trajectories, enabling near-lossless acceleration at 4.5–5.5× where all prior caching methods fail, with an error bound that scales as O(|k|^{O+1}) for smooth feature functions.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 256×256), TaylorSeer (N=5, O=3) achieves FID-50k of 2.65 at 4.53× speedup, compared to 6.06 (DuCa), 6.55 (ToCa), and 6.58 (FORA) at similar acceleration ratios. On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), TaylorSeer (N=6, O=2) attains an ImageReward of 1.0039 at 4.99× FLOPs speedup, exceeding the full 50-step baseline (0.9898), while FORA drops to 0.7761 and ToCa to 0.9086. On HunyuanVideo (VBench), TaylorSeer (N=5, O=1) scores 79.93% at 5.00× speedup, outperforming FORA (78.83%), ToCa (78.86%), and DuCa (78.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaylorSeer decisively outperforms all six caching baselines (Δ-DiT, FORA, TeaCache, ToCa, DuCa, AdaCache) across all three benchmarks, with the gap widening dramatically at higher acceleration ratios. On DiT-XL/2 at ~4.5× speedup, TaylorSeer achieves FID 2.65 versus 6.06 (DuCa), 6.55 (ToCa), and 6.58 (FORA) — a 3.4+ FID improvement over the previous best. On FLUX at 4.99× FLOPs speedup, TaylorSeer (ImageReward 1.0039) exceeds even the unaccelerated 50-step baseline (0.9898), while FORA collapses to 0.7761 and ToCa to 0.9086. On HunyuanVideo at 5.00× speedup, TaylorSeer (VBench 79.93%) leads FORA (78.83%), ToCa (78.86%), and DuCa (78.72%) by 1–1.2 percentage points. At lower acceleration ratios (~2.8×), TaylorSeer already matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines (e.g., FID 2.34 vs. DuCa's 2.88 on DiT), confirming that the Taylor-forecasting approach is beneficial even before the high-acceleration regime where competitors fail. TeaCache and AdaCache are only competitive at moderate speedups (≤3×) and degrade sharply beyond that, consistent with the 'cache-then-reuse' limitation the paper identifies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established 'cache-then-reuse' paradigm in diffusion model acceleration (DeepCache, FORA, TeaCache, ToCa, DuCa, Δ-DiT), which stores features at one timestep and directly copies them to subsequent timesteps. The key conceptual shift is reframing feature caching as a sequential prediction problem: rather than reusing stale features, TaylorSeer uses finite-difference approximations of feature derivatives and Taylor series expansion to forecast what features should be at future timesteps. This repurposes a classical numerical analysis tool (Taylor polynomial extrapolation) for a domain where it had not previously been applied, and the observation that both features and their derivatives follow stable, smooth trajectories across timesteps (visualized via PCA) provides the empirical justification for the approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TaylorSeer enables approximately 5× training-free acceleration on FLUX and HunyuanVideo and 4.5× on DiT-XL/2, a regime where all prior caching methods suffer severe quality degradation, making near-real-time diffusion inference practically achievable without retraining. The method's simplicity (a few lines of finite-difference arithmetic per layer) and architecture-agnosticism suggest broad adoptability across future diffusion models. Open questions include whether the smoothness assumption holds for all model families (e.g., very early or very late timesteps where feature dynamics may be less regular), how the approach interacts with other acceleration techniques like distillation or step reduction, and whether even higher-order expansions or adaptive order selection could push acceleration beyond 5× without quality loss.
