## Problem

While diffusion-based posterior sampling has become the standard for solving inverse problems, the shift toward flow-matching models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3.0, FLUX) has left a gap: existing flow-based inverse solvers like FlowChef and PnP-Flow apply heuristic guidance to the ODE trajectory without a principled posterior sampling derivation. This paper asks how to rigorously extend diffusion posterior sampling to the flow framework, preserving both data consistency and generative quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded measurement y = Ax₀ + n (Gaussian noise, σₙ = 0.03) from a known linear forward operator A (average pooling, bicubic downsampling, Gaussian blur, or motion blur at scale 12 or kernel 61), a text prompt for conditioning, and a frozen pre-trained latent flow model (Stable Diffusion 3.0-medium). It operates at 768×768 resolution in the latent space and outputs a single reconstructed clean image, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning of the backbone. Text prompts can be manually specified or automatically extracted from the measurement via DAPE.

## Method

FlowDPS derives a flow-version of Tweedie's formula that decomposes the flow ODE velocity into a clean-image estimate x̂₀|t and a noise estimate x̂₁|t, then applies the likelihood gradient ∇log p(y|x̂₀|t) to the clean component and stochastic noise ε to the noise component. This yields an adaptive guidance coefficient βₜ that monotonically decreases to zero, naturally prioritizing data consistency in early denoising steps (where global structure is set) and reverting to the unconstrained generative trajectory in later steps (where fine details are refined). In the latent setting, the gradient is computed through the decoder with multiple conjugate gradient steps, and an interpolation parameter γ = σₜ blends the initial and data-consistency-updated estimates to prevent trajectory deviation. The resulting sampler takes the same algebraic form as generalized DDIM, making it directly compatible with existing latent flow architectures like Diffusion Transformers.

## Results

On AFHQ (768×768), FlowDPS achieves 25.88–26.07 PSNR and 15.71–23.00 FID across four linear inverse problems (SR×12 avg-pool, SR×12 bicubic, Gaussian deblur, motion deblur), outperforming the closest flow-based competitor FlowChef by ~1–2 dB PSNR and 5–15 FID points. On FFHQ, FlowDPS reaches 26.23–27.00 PSNR and 33.75–41.31 FID, and on DIV2K it achieves 20.46–21.10 PSNR and 56.19–72.13 FID. LDM-based solvers (ReSample, LatentDAPS, PSLD) degrade substantially when applied to the flow-based Stable Diffusion 3.0 backbone, with PSLD dropping below 16 PSNR on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDPS outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and task. The closest competitor is FlowChef (the only other flow-based solver), which FlowDPS beats by approximately 1–2 dB PSNR and 5–15 FID points (e.g., 25.88 vs. 23.93 PSNR and 16.85 vs. 21.14 FID on AFHQ SR×12 avg-pool). Among LDM-based solvers, ReSample is the strongest but still trails FlowDPS by ~1 dB PSNR and ~20 FID points on AFHQ, while LatentDAPS and PSLD perform markedly worse—PSLD in particular collapses to 13–16 PSNR and FID values exceeding 100–250 on several tasks. The paper attributes the poor transfer of LDM-based solvers to the fundamentally different sampling geometry of flow ODEs versus diffusion SDEs, motivating the need for a purpose-built flow posterior sampler. On the more challenging DIV2K dataset (natural scenes with multiple objects), FlowDPS still leads by 2–3 dB PSNR over FlowChef and 5–10 dB over LDM-based methods, confirming robustness to higher scene complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework to the flow matching setting by deriving a flow-version of Tweedie's formula and decomposing the flow ODE into clean-image and noise-estimation components, mirroring the DDIM structure in diffusion models. It builds directly on flow matching theory (Lipman et al.) for the generative backbone and on DPS/DDS (Chung et al.) for the posterior sampling and multi-step data-consistency update strategy. The key departure from prior flow-based inverse solvers (FlowChef, PnP-Flow) is that FlowDPS applies the likelihood gradient to the decomposed clean-image component with an adaptive time-varying coefficient, rather than to the full intermediate state, and incorporates stochastic noise into the noise component to preserve generative quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As flow-based models (Stable Diffusion 3.0, FLUX) become the dominant generative paradigm, this paper provides the first rigorous posterior sampling framework tailored to them, filling a gap that LDM-based solvers leave open. A practically important finding is that existing latent diffusion inverse solvers (PSLD, ReSample, LatentDAPS) degrade substantially when applied to flow backbones, confirming that the diffusion-to-flow transition is non-trivial for inverse problems. Limitations include evaluation only on linear inverse problems at 768×768 resolution, dependence on a near-perfect autoencoder for the latent-space gradient approximation, and the need for text prompts in the latent setting. Open questions include extension to nonlinear/ill-posed problems, video restoration, and whether the decomposition-based guidance generalizes to other flow parameterizations beyond affine coupling.
