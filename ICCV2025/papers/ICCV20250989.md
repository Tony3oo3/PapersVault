## Problem

Existing few-shot image generation methods are dominated by GAN-based approaches that operate in Euclidean feature space, limiting their ability to capture hierarchical semantic structure essential for generalizing from few examples. These methods suffer from suboptimal image quality under data scarcity, reduced diversity from 1-to-1 latent-to-image mappings, and reliance on human-annotated class labels. No prior work integrates hyperbolic hierarchical representation with diffusion models to achieve high-quality, controllable, and label-free few-shot generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 reference image from an unseen category and produces multiple diverse images of the same category. The reference image is compressed via CLIP's class token into a 1024-dim semantic vector (content bottleneck), which is further projected into a 512-dim hyperbolic code on the Poincaré disk. Generation is controlled by two knobs: the stochastic subcode z_T (encoding strength from 0 to 1) and the hyperbolic radius r_D, which governs whether edits affect identity-relevant or identity-irrelevant attributes. Pseudo-labels for training are obtained via CLIP zero-shot classification rather than human annotations.

## Method

HypDAE is a two-stage diffusion autoencoder. Stage I trains a lightweight MLP on top of a frozen CLIP image encoder (class token only, with strong augmentations to prevent memorization) to produce a semantic condition c for a pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet, which simultaneously acts as a stochastic encoder via DDIM backward sampling to extract z_T. Stage II trains a hyperbolic encoder-decoder (5-layer Transformer encoder → exponential map → hyperbolic FFN → logarithmic map → 30-layer Transformer decoder) that projects c into the Poincaré disk, trained with a hyperbolic NLL classification loss (pushing fine-grained embeddings to the disk boundary and abstract ones toward the center) plus L2/cosine reconstruction loss. At generation time, diverse images are produced by either sampling new z_T or shifting the hyperbolic code along geodesics at a fixed radius r_D, where smaller radii yield more abstract, diverse outputs and larger radii preserve identity-relevant features. Pseudo-labeling via CLIP zero-shot classification eliminates the need for human-annotated labels, and the approach outperforms GAN-based baselines on FID and LPIPS across four datasets.

## Results

In the 1-shot setting, HypDAE (Pseudo) achieves the best FID on Flowers (24.43), Animal Faces (13.14), and NABirds (7.57), and the best LPIPS across all four datasets (0.7630, 0.7431, 0.5560, 0.7966). On VGGFaces, LSO attains a slightly lower FID (4.15 vs. 5.96), but the paper attributes this to the dataset's low 64×64 resolution. The Euclidean ablation (HypDAE-Euc) confirms the hyperbolic space contributes meaningfully, e.g., reducing Animal Faces FID from 20.72 to 13.14. A user study (Table 4) further shows HypDAE scoring highest on Quality (3.45), Fidelity (3.58), and Diversity (3.86) out of 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HypDAE (Pseudo) achieves the lowest FID among all 1-shot methods on Flowers (24.43 vs. SAGE 43.52, HAE 50.10, LSO 35.87), Animal Faces (13.14 vs. SAGE 27.43, HAE 26.33), and NABirds (7.57 vs. SAGE 19.45, HAE 21.85), representing roughly 2× FID improvements over the strongest GAN-based competitors. On VGGFaces, LSO reports a lower FID (4.15 vs. 5.96), but the paper notes this is confounded by the dataset's 64×64 resolution and differing numbers of unseen categories across methods. HypDAE also attains the highest LPIPS (diversity) on every dataset, e.g., 0.7630 on Flowers versus 0.4739 for HAE and 0.4392 for SAGE, indicating substantially greater intra-class variation. The 3-shot GAN baselines (DAWSON, F2GAN, WaveGAN, F2DGAN) are uniformly outperformed by large margins on both FID and LPIPS. In the user study (Table 4), HypDAE leads all three subjective metrics—Quality (3.45 vs. 2.67 HAE), Fidelity (3.58 vs. 2.68 SAGE), and Diversity (3.86 vs. 2.53 HAE)—confirming the quantitative gains are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

HypDAE sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: the diffusion autoencoder framework of DiffAE [44], which decomposes image generation into a semantic subcode and a stochastic subcode, and the hyperbolic attribute editing approach of HAE [35], which encodes hierarchical image semantics in the Poincaré disk but relies on GANs for generation. The key departure is integrating hyperbolic hierarchical representations into a diffusion-based pipeline for the first time, replacing GAN decoders with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and eliminating the 1-to-1 mapping constraint that limited HAE's diversity. The paper also distinguishes itself from diffusion-based personalization methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) by operating at the category level without test-time fine-tuning or prompt engineering, and from prior GAN-based few-shot generators by leveraging foundation-model priors for quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling hyperbolic geometry with diffusion models, HypDAE provides an interpretable, radius-based control knob for semantic diversity that was unavailable in prior GAN-based few-shot generators, and it demonstrates that human-annotated labels are not necessary—CLIP pseudo-labels (with accuracy as low as ~39% on NABirds) can match or exceed real labels. This opens the door to label-free hierarchical generation at scale. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic hierarchy generalizes to more complex or multi-level taxonomies, how the method behaves when CLIP pseudo-labels are systematically biased, and whether the two-stage training pipeline can be unified end-to-end without the data requirements that motivated the separation.
