## Problem

Existing composite degradation restoration methods rely on fixed embeddings that cannot distinguish different intensity combinations of the same degradation types (e.g., 'heavy haze + light rain' vs. 'light haze + heavy rain' collapse into one category). Moreover, degradation intensity is governed by multiple physical factors (depth, scattering coefficient, illumination, streak density, etc.), making a single fixed embedding insufficient for comprehensive scene characterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250092/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single composite degraded RGB image as input and produces a restored clear image of the same resolution. Text descriptions of degradation types are used only to initialize degradation-level prototypes via a pre-trained GloVe encoder; no text is required during training or inference. The proposed VCD dataset provides 100 training scenes (11,100 degraded images) and 20 test scenes (2,220 degraded images), each clear background paired with 111 degraded variants at varying intensities and combinations.

## Method

DPL maintains two levels of learnable prototypes: degradation-level prototypes (one C-dim vector per degradation type, initialized from GloVe text embeddings) and factor-level prototypes (K=8 per degradation plus a shared public set, initialized as degradation-level prototype plus Gaussian noise). Image features from a transformer-based U-Net encoder are matched to both prototype sets via entropy-regularized optimal transport solved with Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations (~1 ms on GPU), producing dual-level scene embeddings. These embeddings are injected into the decoder through Dual Scene Embedding Transformer Blocks (DSETB), where a Dual-level Scene Cross Attention (DSCA) module uses the summed scene embeddings as queries against image-feature keys/values. A Prototype Scatter Learning Loss (L_psl), formulated as an LDA-inspired ratio-trace objective, maximizes between-degradation and within-degradation-nearest scatter while minimizing within-degradation-farthest scatter to prevent prototype collapse and encourage diverse factor representations. Factor-level prototypes are updated by gradient descent, while degradation-level prototypes follow a momentum update (β=0.99) from the mean of their corresponding factor-level prototypes.

## Results

DPL achieves the best performance across all four evaluation datasets. On the proposed VCD dataset (variable-intensity composite degradations), DPL reaches 25.27 dB PSNR and 0.802 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline OneRestore† (23.99 dB / 0.781) by 1.28 dB. On CDD-11, DPL scores 29.55 dB / 0.884 SSIM; on BID-task.II, 32.22 dB / 0.936 SSIM; and on the real-world IVIPC DQA set, it attains the lowest NIQE (3.786) and BRISQUE (25.01) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPL outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is OneRestore† (which uses scene-description text as extra input), and DPL still leads by 0.83 dB PSNR on CDD-11, 1.28 dB on VCD, and 1.16 dB on BID-task.II. Among one-to-many methods, WGWSNet is the strongest (23.89 dB on VCD), yet DPL exceeds it by 1.38 dB, showing that explicit dual-level prototype matching is more effective than learning weather-general/specific features. One-to-one methods such as Restormer (22.90 dB on VCD) and OKNet (21.62 dB) lag substantially, confirming that single-degradation models struggle with composite inputs. On the real-world IVIPC DQA set, DPL achieves the lowest NIQE (3.786) and BRISQUE (25.01), beating the next-best OneRestore (3.861 / 28.29) by a clear margin, indicating superior perceptual quality on unseen real rain scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composite degraded image restoration line initiated by BIDeN (Han et al., ECCV 2022) and OneRestore (Guo et al., 2024), which introduced the one-to-composite paradigm. It departs from OneRestore's fixed text-embedding approach by replacing static degradation descriptors with dynamic dual-level prototypes (degradation-type and factor-level) matched via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport, drawing on prototype learning from instance segmentation (Zhou et al.) and cross-scale snow removal (Chen et al.). The prototype optimization strategy also diverges from the prevailing contrastive/pairwise paradigm, instead adopting an LDA-inspired scatter-based objective that holistically shapes the prototype distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VCD dataset fills a genuine gap: prior composite degradation benchmarks (CDD-11, BID-task.II) use fixed intensities, making it impossible to evaluate whether a model truly adapts to varying degradation severity. The finding that the scatter learning loss alone contributes +2.19 dB PSNR—more than all other components combined—suggests that prototype distribution quality is the dominant bottleneck in prototype-based restoration, a point the field has underappreciated. Open questions include whether the dual-level prototype framework generalizes beyond the four weather degradations studied, whether the approach scales to open-ended or unseen degradation types, and whether the synthetic VCD protocol transfers to truly uncontrolled real-world composites.
