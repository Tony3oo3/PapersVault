## Problem

Event cameras suffer severe performance degradation under rain, as rapid rain streaks generate dense noise that overwhelms valid scene information. Existing event deraining methods either convert asynchronous event streams into frame-like representations (sacrificing microsecond temporal resolution and sparsity) or rely on point-based architectures with quadratic attention complexity that cannot scale to high event rates (>10M events/s). No prior work has achieved a point-based event deraining framework that simultaneously preserves native temporal precision, removes rain effectively, and runs in real time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams where each event is a 4-tuple (x, y, t, p) of spatial coordinates, timestamp, and polarity, grouped into fixed 0.1-second temporal windows with 5 windows per forward pass. Events are transformed into a 4D pseudo-point cloud (x, y, z, T_n) where z is the normalized intra-window timestamp and T_n is the global window index, then serialized into SSM-compatible sequences via z-order and Hilbert curves. The output is a per-event binary label (rain or background) produced by a linear layer followed by softmax, making deraining a classification task rather than a reconstruction problem.

## Method

PRE-Mamba is a U-Net-style encoder-decoder whose core building block is the Multi-Scale State Space Model (MS3M), which runs parallel intra-window (appearance) and inter-window (motion) branches inspired by the Motion-aware SSM, fuses them via cross-product attention, and applies a multi-spatial-scale convolutional pathway (kernels 1, 3, 5) to capture diverse rain streak widths. A Spatio-Temporal Decoupling and Fusion (STDF) module precedes each block, extracting spatial features via depthwise 1D convolutions and temporal features via window-index embeddings, then modulating spatial features with temporal representations through Hadamard products to prioritize the event camera's temporal discrimination. Training uses a joint loss combining binary cross-entropy with a frequency-domain regularization term (L_fft) that aligns predicted and ground-truth label sequences in the FFT domain, enforcing physically consistent global rain patterns (low-frequency for dense rain, high-frequency for sparse rain) without the overhead of embedding FFT layers in the network. The entire model has only 0.26M parameters and 6.23G FLOPs, processing 100K events in ~0.1 s on a single GPU.

## Results

On the EventRain-27K synthetic dataset, PRE-Mamba achieves an average SR/NR/DA of 0.95/0.91/0.93 across six rainfall intensities (5–150 mm/h), outperforming all baselines at every intensity level. At light rain (5 mm/h), it reaches SR=0.994, NR=0.914, DA=0.954; at heavy rain (150 mm/h), it maintains SR=0.908, NR=0.895, DA=0.902. The model is also highly efficient: 0.26M parameters, 6.23G FLOPs, and 0.0987 s inference for 100K events (scaling to 0.398 s for 1M events), achieving a 204.5× speedup over EDnCNN and 25.3× over EDformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRE-Mamba outperforms all eight baselines on SR, NR, and DA across every rainfall intensity tested. The strongest learning-based baseline, EDnCNN, reaches DA=0.937 at 5 mm/h but degrades to DA=0.886 at 150 mm/h, while PRE-Mamba maintains DA=0.902 at the same intensity—a 1.6-point gap that widens at heavier rain. EDformer achieves the highest SR at 5 mm/h (0.981) but suffers a sharp NR drop (0.818→0.834) as rain intensifies, whereas PRE-Mamba's NR stays above 0.895 throughout. AEDNet shows the most dramatic degradation under heavy rain (NR drops from 0.850 to 0.547), confirming that denoising-focused architectures generalize poorly to structured rain patterns. All five filter-based methods (TS, DWF, KNoise, YNoise, RED) remain far below learning-based approaches, with DA values between 0.50 and 0.62, as they cannot distinguish rain streaks from valid scene motion. In terms of efficiency, PRE-Mamba (0.26M params, 6.23G FLOPs, 0.099 s/100K events) is 204.5× faster than EDnCNN (614K params, 234G FLOPs, 20.2 s) and 25.3× faster than EDformer (49.8K params, 8.4G FLOPs, 2.5 s), while DistillNet, though fast (0.20 s), requires 18.96M parameters and voxelized processing incompatible with point-based evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant frame-based paradigm in event camera deraining (DistillNet, EGVD, RainVID&SS), which converts asynchronous event streams into voxelized or grayscale representations at the cost of temporal resolution and sparsity. It also extends the point-based event processing line (PointNet, GNN, SNN) that has been applied to denoising and classification but never to deraining, by adapting the selective State Space Model (Mamba) — originally designed for causal sequential language modeling — to the asynchronous, sparse, microsecond-resolution nature of raw event data. The Multi-Scale State Space Model draws directly from MSSM (a motion-aware SSM for LiDAR moving-object segmentation), repurposing its dual-branch appearance/motion decomposition for rain-background separation, while the frequency-domain regularization borrows the spectral-consistency idea from image deraining (FreqMamba, FourierMamba) but reformulates it as a lightweight loss term rather than an in-network FFT layer to avoid prohibitive compute on million-event sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRE-Mamba establishes the first point-based event deraining benchmark (EventRain-27K) and demonstrates that a 0.26M-parameter SSM can match or exceed 45M-parameter CNN/Transformer baselines at 200×+ speed, making real-time deraining feasible on resource-constrained platforms (drones, autonomous vehicles) where event cameras are most valuable. The generalization to snow without architectural changes suggests the learned spatiotemporal rain dynamics transfer to other precipitation patterns, though the paper does not quantify this. Open questions include whether the 5-window / 0.1 s temporal context is sufficient for very heavy or oblique rain, how the method scales to higher-resolution sensors (>1280×720), and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further closed without the self-recorded artificial rain bridge.
