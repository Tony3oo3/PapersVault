## Problem

Large Multimodal Models process hundreds to thousands of visual tokens, creating severe computational and memory bottlenecks for deployment. Existing token pruning methods rely on simplistic, uniform strategies—typically attention-based importance maps applied identically across different LMM architectures—leading to substantial performance degradation, especially under aggressive pruning ratios. The paper addresses the lack of model-specific, fine-grained pruning strategies that can preserve performance while drastically reducing token counts.

## Input / Output

The method operates on high-resolution images (up to 1152×1152, yielding up to 7,290 visual tokens for LLaVA-OneVision) or video frame sequences, paired with a text prompt. Pruning is applied at three fixed points within the LLM (before the LLM and after the 9th and 18th layers for LLaVA-OneVision), progressively reducing the visual token sequence while preserving original position IDs. The optimization phase requires only 30 unlabeled samples from the model's training set and runs for 50 Bayesian optimization iterations (~30 minutes), after which inference produces a standard text response on the pruned token sequence.

## Method

VFlowOpt introduces a three-component training-free pruning framework. First, it computes token importance by combining calibrated attention (excluding redundant tokens that disproportionately attend to similar background tokens, identified via a threshold on total received attention) with patch-level information entropy, weighted by a learnable coefficient α. Second, it applies progressive pruning across three LLM stages with stage-specific retention ratios, and recycles pruned tokens by grouping them into spatial grid cells and fusing each cell's tokens via importance-weighted averaging, placing the merged token at the position of the most important pruned token. Third, it formulates the selection of all hyperparameters (threshold sensitivity t, entropy weight α, grid size a, and retention ratios R₁/R₂/R₃) as a Bayesian optimization problem that maximizes the cosine similarity between the final token representation in the last LLM layer with and without pruning, effectively minimizing disruption to the visual information flow that propagates from visual tokens through text tokens to the output position.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, VFlowOpt retains 99.9% of the original average accuracy across 10 benchmarks when pruning 50% of visual tokens, and 85.5% when pruning 90%. At the 90% pruning ratio, VFlowOpt reduces KV-Cache memory by 89% and accelerates inference by 3.8× while degrading performance by only 7.4%. On video understanding (VideoMME + SeedBench), VFlowOpt maintains 100% of original performance at 25% token retention and 95.5% at 10% retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFlowOpt outperforms all five baselines (FastV, FitPrune, PDrop, SparseVLM, VisionZip) at every token retention ratio on LLaVA-OneVision-7B. The gap widens with more aggressive pruning: at 50% retention VFlowOpt leads VisionZip (the strongest baseline) by 1.0% in average accuracy, while at 10% retention the margin grows to 3.4% (85.5% vs. 82.1%). FitPrune and PDrop fail catastrophically on LLaVA-OneVision, dropping to 21.4% and 71.3% at 50% retention respectively, indicating their fixed pruning strategies are incompatible with this architecture. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B, VFlowOpt again achieves the best results (98.8% and 97.8% at 25% retention; 93.4% and 92.8% at 10%), surpassing VisionZip by 1.5–1.9% and FastV by 7.7–8.4% at the 10% setting. On video benchmarks, VFlowOpt matches the unpruned model at 25% retention (100%) and leads VisionZip by 3.1% at 10% retention (95.5% vs. 92.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VFlowOpt builds on the progressive multi-stage pruning framework introduced by PyramidDrop [37] and the attention-based token importance estimation popularized by VisionZip [41] and FastV [7]. It departs from these in three ways: (1) it calibrates attention by excluding redundant tokens that attend to similar background regions, and augments importance with patch-level information entropy; (2) it introduces a grid-based token recycling mechanism that merges pruned tokens via importance-weighted averaging to mitigate information loss; and (3) most distinctively, it formulates pruning hyperparameter selection as a Bayesian optimization problem guided by visual information flow (cosine similarity of the final token representation with and without pruning), thereby automatically tailoring the strategy to each LMM rather than applying a fixed heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 90% token reduction with only ~14.5% average performance loss, 3.8× inference speedup, and 89% KV-Cache reduction make LMM deployment in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings (autonomous driving, robotics) considerably more feasible. The per-model optimization addresses a real gap—FitPrune and PDrop catastrophically fail on LLaVA-OneVision (21.4% and 71.3% at 50% retention) while VFlowOpt retains 99.9%, underscoring that one-size-fits-all pruning is inadequate. Open questions include whether the 30-minute Bayesian optimization step scales to larger models (70B+), whether optimized strategies transfer across model variants, and whether the approach extends to dynamic, input-adaptive pruning ratios. The method remains limited to 7B-scale models in experiments, and the reliance on unlabeled training data for optimization may be a barrier for proprietary models.
