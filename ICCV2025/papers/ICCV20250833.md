## Problem

In diffusion-based Foreground Conditioned Out-painting (FCO), the independently sampled initial noise frequently implies object positions, scales, and poses that conflict with the fixed foreground, producing artifacts and incorrect foreground-background interactions. Existing learning-free and learning-based inpainting methods lack a mechanism to align the synthesized background composition with the given foreground, resulting in backgrounds that exist independently rather than forming a coherent scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB foreground image (encoded by the Latent VAE) and a free-form text prompt (encoded by CLIP) as input, and produces a complete out-painted image where the background is synthesized to interact coherently with the foreground. The IPM predicts an intermediate latent state at a fixed truncation timestep (t^s=800 out of 1000), which is then combined with fresh random noise and denoised via a ControlNet-based FCO model to yield the final image. The foreground pixels remain unchanged throughout the process.

## Method

The core idea exploits the time-frequency coupling of diffusion: low-frequency signals that determine image composition are primarily established in early denoising steps. The Initialization Policy Model (IPM) truncates the diffusion process at timestep t^s and directly predicts the intermediate latent state x_{t^s} conditioned solely on the foreground image and text prompt, thereby decoupling composition from the random initial noise. The IPM is a U-Net initialized from Stable Diffusion with four additional input channels for the condition image and a fixed static noise fed to the original channels. Training combines two losses: (1) an MSE label loss against DDIM-inversion-derived ground-truth low-frequency signals, and (2) a diffusion reward loss that multi-step denoises the predicted state through a frozen reward model and penalizes the discrepancy between the final image and the target, with stop-gradient applied across denoising steps to limit memory. Subsequent denoising from x_{t^s} is performed by a ControlNet trained on the same OpenImage-FCO dataset, preserving the backbone's text-image priors.

## Results

On the OpenImage-FCO test set (250 cases), the proposed IPM achieves the best SAM score of 0.070 (lower is better, measuring foreground-background artifact overlap), the highest user-study interaction score of 4.62/5, the best Image Reward of 0.67, and the best Aesthetic Score of 5.34. It ranks second on CLIP similarity (26.50) behind BrushNet (26.62), which benefits from training on the much larger LAION-Aesthetic dataset. The method outperforms the strongest learning-based baseline (BrushNet) by 0.050 on SAM, 0.56 on INT, 0.07 on IR, and 0.03 on AS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Blended Latent Diffusion (BLD), the proposed method dominates on every metric, with SAM dropping from 0.986 to 0.070 and INT rising from 1.75 to 4.62, confirming that the learning-free paste-and-denoise paradigm fundamentally cannot achieve coherent foreground-background interaction. Against SD-Inpainting, the gap is substantial on SAM (0.317 → 0.070) and INT (3.35 → 4.62), while CLIP scores are nearly identical (26.26 vs 26.50). Against PowerPaint (both original and fine-tuned), the method wins on SAM (0.820/0.433 → 0.070), INT (2.23/3.27 → 4.62), IR (0.34/0.46 → 0.67), and AS (5.05/5.20 → 5.34). Against BrushNet (the strongest competitor), the proposed method wins on SAM (0.190/0.120 → 0.070), INT (4.06/3.94 → 4.62), IR (0.60/0.58 → 0.67), and AS (5.31/5.29 → 5.34), but loses on CLIP (26.62/26.56 vs 26.50), which the authors attribute to BrushNet's training on 120M LAION-Aesthetic images. Against ControlNet† (trained on the same dataset), the method wins on SAM (0.235 → 0.070), INT (3.88 → 4.62), IR (0.52 → 0.67), and AS (5.27 → 5.34), while CLIP is nearly tied (26.46 vs 26.50). Layer Diffusion achieves a lower SAM (0.046) but is considered non-competitive overall due to poor foreground-background coordination (INT 3.50, IR 0.22).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and DDIM inversion/sampling, but departs from all existing FCO methods (Blended Latent Diffusion, SD-Inpainting, PowerPaint, BrushNet, ControlNet) by identifying the random initial noise x_T as the root cause of foreground-background composition conflicts. Rather than modifying the denoising network or the conditioning mechanism, the authors introduce a deterministic Initialization Policy Model that replaces the early denoising steps, exploiting the time-frequency coupling property of diffusion to isolate low-frequency composition signals from random noise. The training paradigm further departs from standard supervised fine-tuning by combining DDIM-inversion-derived labels with a novel multi-step diffusion reward loss that back-propagates through a frozen reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OpenImage-FCO dataset (580K images, 800K masks, 6K tags) fills a notable gap in task-specific FCO data, and the IPM provides a clean, plug-in mechanism for improving composition in any diffusion-based FCO pipeline. However, the method inherently trades output diversity for compositional coherence, and the authors acknowledge failure cases with extreme foreground colors or lighting that dominate background generation. Open questions include whether the IPM approach generalizes to 3D scene composition, whether foreground-background color decoupling can be achieved within this framework, and whether the truncation timestep strategy transfers across different diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models).
