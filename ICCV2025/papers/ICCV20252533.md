## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on unlearned diffusion models cannot simultaneously embed the unlearned concept and reflect an attacker's specific intent (e.g., desired background, style, or context), because they rely solely on target prompts or target images without a mechanism to fuse both. Additionally, each new contextual variation of the same unlearned concept requires a full re-optimization of the adversarial prompt, making iterative attacks computationally prohibitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252533/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ZIUM takes a target attack image (an RGB image generated by vanilla Stable Diffusion 1.4v that contains the unlearned concept) and an optional user-intent text prompt (e.g., 'Japanese comics', 'At the beach'). The target image is encoded into a 768-d visual embedding, transformed through a BLIP-2-style Q-former and projection layer into a 768-d text embedding, then concatenated with the CLIP text embedding of the user-intent prompt to form the conditioning for the unlearned diffusion model. The output is a generated image that embeds the unlearned concept while reflecting the user's intent; in the zero-shot phase, the same optimized modules process a new unseen target image without any gradient updates.

## Method

ZIUM operates in two phases. In the initial attack phase, a visual-text alignment process (CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder → BLIP-2 Q-former image captioning module → linear projection) converts the target attack image's visual embedding into a text embedding that carries the unlearned concept, which is concatenated with the user-intent prompt's text embedding. The optimization process then applies the diffusion classifier mechanism (derived from Bayes' rule) to minimize the denoising error ‖ε − ε_θ(x_t | c_i, p)‖², updating only the Q-former and projection layer parameters θ′ while freezing the visual encoder, thereby identifying the optimal adversarial condition. In the zero-shot attack phase, the optimized Q-former and projection layer are reused as-is to process new unseen target images of the same unlearned concept, eliminating the need for any further optimization and reducing attack time by 45–150× compared to methods that re-optimize per attack.

## Results

ZIUM achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 87.8% across six unlearned concept scenarios (nudity, violence, illegal activity, Van Gogh style, church, parachute) and four unlearned models (ESD, FMN, SLD, AdvUnlearn), outperforming the strongest baseline UnlearnDiffAtk (65.2%) by 22.6 percentage points on average. The improvement ranges from 22.6%p to 62.0%p depending on the scenario. ZIUM also demonstrates superior consistency, with only 39.4%p variation across scenarios compared to 62.0%p for existing methods. In the zero-shot setting (no re-optimization), ZIUM still achieves 84.5% ASR on nudity, 50.0% on Van Gogh, and 48.0% on parachute in just 0.2 minutes, versus 9.0–29.9 minutes for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZIUM outperforms all three baselines across every unlearned concept scenario on average. Against UnlearnDiffAtk (the strongest baseline, a target-image-based method), ZIUM gains the largest margins on style and object concepts: +50.7%p on Van Gogh (80.7% vs 30.0%), +18.0%p on church (74.7% vs 56.7%), and +22.0%p on parachute (75.3% vs 53.3%), while maintaining near-parity on violence (+2.3%p) and illegal activity (+0.4%p). Against the prompt-based methods P4D and Ring-A-Bell, ZIUM's advantage is overwhelming, exceeding 50%p on most scenarios (e.g., 96.4% vs 35.0% on nudity for P4D; 80.7% vs 0.7% on Van Gogh for Ring-A-Bell). The paper notes that at the individual model level, UnlearnDiffAtk occasionally matches or slightly exceeds ZIUM on specific model–scenario pairs (e.g., FMN for church at 96.0% vs 92.0%), but ZIUM's consistency across models and its ability to incorporate user-intent prompts give it a clear overall advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZIUM builds on two key prior works: BLIP-2's Q-former architecture for visual-to-text embedding alignment, and the diffusion classifier mechanism (Li et al., ICCV 2023) that formulates adversarial condition optimization via Bayes' rule. It departs from existing adversarial attacks on unlearned models (UnlearnDiffAtk, P4D, Ring-A-Bell) in two conceptual ways: (1) it introduces a user-intent prompt channel that concatenates with the image-derived text embedding, enabling intent-aware customization absent in prior methods that rely solely on target images or target prompts; and (2) it leverages the image captioning module's generalization to enable zero-shot transfer, eliminating the per-context re-optimization that all prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot attack capability (45–149× speedup over re-optimization) is practically significant for understanding the persistent vulnerability of unlearned models: once an attacker invests in a single optimization, they can pivot to new contexts at near-zero cost. However, the zero-shot ASR drops substantially (e.g., Van Gogh 86%→50%, Parachute 76%→48%), limiting its practical threat. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer diffusion architectures (SDXL, SD3), whether the image captioning module's generalization holds for more diverse unlearned concepts, and whether model-agnostic transferable attacks can further reduce the dependency on white-box access to the unlearned model.
