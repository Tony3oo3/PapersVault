## Problem

Existing generalizable novel view synthesis methods specialize in either small-baseline (large overlap) or large-baseline (small overlap) settings and fail to generalize across both. Small-baseline methods suffer from depth errors due to occlusions and insufficient overlap on large-baseline data, while large-baseline methods lack matching cues on small-baseline data, leading to inconsistent geometry and blurred outputs. MuGS addresses this by unifying both regimes into a single feed-forward Gaussian splatting framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250719/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–3 sparse RGB input views with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, operating in both object-centric (DTU, large overlap) and wide-baseline (RealEstate10K, 128-frame video gap) settings. It produces a rendered target view image, per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH color), and a target-view depth map. The pipeline is hierarchical: a coarse level samples 64 isometric depth candidates, and a fine level refines with 16 candidates, enabling memory-efficient coarse-to-fine inference.

## Method

MuGS employs a two-branch architecture: an MVS branch (cross-view encoder → warped cost volume → depth probability volume) and an MDE branch (pre-trained Depth Anything V2 → per-source-view depth maps and features). The key mechanism is a projection-and-sampling depth fusion: each depth candidate in the target view frustum is projected to every source view to obtain a projected depth d_i^p, while the monocular depth map is sampled at the projected coordinates to obtain d_i^s; a 4-layer 3D U-Net computes a consistency cue from (d_i^p, d_i^s, d_i^s/d_i^p), which is then used as a query in a depth-wise attention over the MVS probability volume to produce a refined volume. Monocular features are also concatenated with MVS features (warped to target-view planes) to build an enhanced feature volume for regressing Gaussian parameters. A novel reference-view loss renders the source views from the same pixel-aligned Gaussians and adds per-source L1 supervision, providing geometric context that accelerates convergence and improves quality beyond target-view-only training.

## Results

On DTU (small-baseline), MuGS achieves 29.34 PSNR / 0.969 SSIM / 0.075 LPIPS in the 3-view setting and 27.56 PSNR / 0.958 SSIM / 0.084 LPIPS in the 2-view setting, outperforming the previous best (MVSGaussian) by over 0.5 dB PSNR in both configurations. On RealEstate10K (large-baseline), MuGS reaches 24.82 PSNR / 0.873 SSIM / 0.153 LPIPS, surpassing MVSplat by 1.5 dB and MuRF by 0.6 dB. Zero-shot on Mip-NeRF 360 yields 24.25 PSNR / 0.845 SSIM / 0.256 LPIPS, the best among all compared methods. Inference time is 2.1 ms per view (rendering), faster than MuRF (1.122 s) and comparable to other 3D-GS methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuGS outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest small-baseline 3D-GS method MVSGaussian, MuGS gains +1.13 dB PSNR (3-view) and +1.78 dB PSNR (2-view) on DTU, with the gap widening in the more challenging 2-view case due to occlusion. Against the strongest large-baseline 3D-GS method MVSplat, MuGS leads by +1.48 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K, confirming that the MDE fusion strategy is critical when view overlap is limited. Against the prior multi-baseline SOTA MuRF (NeRF-based), MuGS achieves +0.58 dB PSNR on DTU 3-view and +0.62 dB on RealEstate10K while being roughly 500× faster at inference (2.1 ms vs. 1.122 s). On zero-shot Mip-NeRF 360, MuGS beats MuRF by +0.27 dB PSNR and +0.045 SSIM, and surpasses MVSGaussian by +3.06 dB PSNR, demonstrating that multi-baseline training transfers better than single-baseline training. NeRF-based baselines (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, MVSNeRF, ENeRF) are consistently 1–10 dB behind the 3D-GS methods, highlighting the rendering speed and quality advantage of explicit Gaussian representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuGS sits at the intersection of three active research threads: feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (MVSplat, MVSGaussian), multi-baseline novel view synthesis (MuRF), and the fusion of MVS with monocular depth estimation (DepthSplat). It departs from prior Gaussian-based methods, which are specialized to either small-baseline (MVSGaussian) or large-baseline (MVSplat) settings, by unifying both through a projection-and-sampling depth fusion mechanism that leverages a pre-trained monocular depth model (Depth Anything V2) to correct MVS cost-volume errors. Unlike DepthSplat, which performs feature-level concatenation of MVS and MDE outputs, MuGS explicitly models the geometric consistency between projected and sampled depths via a 3D U-Net and attention-based probability refinement, and unlike MuRF (NeRF-based), it uses explicit pixel-aligned Gaussians for faster inference and direct multi-view rendering supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first Gaussian-splatting method to handle arbitrary baselines in a single feed-forward model, MuGS removes a practical barrier for real-world applications where input view spacing is unpredictable. The reference-view loss and multi-view rendering supervision offer a simple but effective recipe for improving geometry in sparse-view settings that could transfer to other Gaussian-based pipelines. However, the method inherits the known weaknesses of both MVS (textureless/specular regions) and MDE (scale ambiguity, cross-view inconsistency), and the reliance on a large pre-trained monocular model (Depth Anything V2) raises questions about deployment cost and whether the fusion strategy degrades gracefully when the MDE prior is poorly calibrated for a given scene. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes or very large view counts, and whether the coarse-to-fine hierarchy can be made more adaptive to the actual baseline magnitude.
