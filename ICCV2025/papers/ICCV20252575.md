## Problem

Adversarial training with diffusion-generated synthetic data yields substantial robustness gains on small datasets like CIFAR but has failed to translate to ImageNet, where prior work reported only minimal improvements from 5M synthetic samples. Moreover, scaling model capacity beyond ~500M parameters introduces a previously uncharacterized training instability—loss oscillation—that degrades adversarial robustness and has resisted conventional stabilization techniques.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 (or 336×336 for XL/XXL) RGB images from ImageNet-1K, mixed with 50M REPA-generated synthetic images at a 9:1 synthetic-to-real ratio, and outputs a 1000-way class label. Evaluation is conducted under AutoAttack (APGD-CE, APGD-DLR, FAB, Square Attack) with ε=4/255 under the ℓ∞ norm, following RobustBench protocol without external data.

## Method

The paper combines three scaling strategies—expanding synthetic data to 50M high-fidelity REPA-generated samples, selecting CAFormer over ViT for supervised adversarial training, and extending training with real-data fine-tuning—with a new stabilization mechanism for large models. The key insight is that loss oscillation in >500M-parameter models stems from the fixed step size in PGD's sign operator, which causes weak-gradient components to receive disproportionately large updates, leading to 'bouncing' perturbations. To diagnose this, the authors introduce the Equality Rate (EQ Rate), measuring coordinate-wise equality of adversarial perturbations across iterations. Their Stable-PGD replaces the binary sign operator with an adaptive sign scaling (ASign) that applies quantile-based multi-bin thresholds to downscale updates for weak gradients, preventing overshooting and restoring smooth convergence without degrading performance in already-stable regimes.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K under AutoAttack (ε=4/255, ℓ∞), the proposed CAFormer-XXL48 (1336M params) achieves 71.54% robust accuracy and 83.88% clean accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art among methods without external data. At the mainstream scale, CAFormer-L36 (219M) reaches 69.34% robust accuracy, surpassing the prior best (Swin-L at 59.68%) by +9.66%. The method uses only 50M REPA-generated synthetic samples (9:1 synthetic-to-real ratio) and trains for 400–700 epochs, requiring 250–400 TPU days versus ~4000 epochs and 1B external samples for AdvXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Liu et al. [15] (ConvNeXt-S/B/L baselines), the proposed method wins decisively across all three model scales: +9.42% (Small), +10.42% (Base), and +10.86% (Large) in ℓ∞ robust accuracy, while also improving clean accuracy by 5–4.5 points. Against Xu et al. [30] (Swin-L, MIMIR), CAFormer-L36 outperforms by +9.66% robust accuracy (69.34% vs 59.68%). Against AdvXL [28], which relies on 1B external DataComp samples and ~4000 training epochs, the proposed method achieves higher robust accuracy at both XL (70.78% vs 69.8%) and XXL (71.54% vs 71.0%) scales while using only 50M synthetic samples and 400–500 epochs. Against Singh et al. [22] on supplementary ℓ2/ℓ1 evaluations, CAFormer-L36 leads by +7.18% (ℓ2: 54.18% vs 47.0%) and +2.92% (ℓ1: 25.12% vs 22.2%), though these are not part of the standard RobustBench protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on diffusion-generated synthetic data for adversarial training (Gowal et al., 2021; Bartoldson et al., 2024), which showed strong gains on CIFAR but only modest improvements on ImageNet. It departs from the external-data scaling paradigm (AdvXL, Wang et al.) by relying solely on 50M internally-generated REPA samples, and it modifies the PGD update rule (Madry et al., 2017) by replacing the binary sign operator with an adaptive sign scaling (ASign) mechanism to address a newly identified training instability. The architecture choice (CAFormer over ViT) also contrasts with prior contrastive-pretraining-based robustness work that favored ViT, revealing a different optimal architecture for supervised adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of loss oscillation as a scale-dependent instability in PGD-based adversarial training, along with the EQ Rate diagnostic and Stable-PGD fix, provides a practical tool for anyone training large robust models. Achieving 71.54% robust accuracy without external data while using a fraction of the compute of 1B-sample methods offers a more accessible pathway to strong ImageNet robustness. Open questions include whether Stable-PGD generalizes to other attack formulations or modalities, whether the CAFormer-over-ViT finding holds under different training regimes, and how the approach combines with large-scale pretraining on broader datasets.
