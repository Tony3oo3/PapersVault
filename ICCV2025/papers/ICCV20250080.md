## Problem

State-of-the-art panoptic segmentation methods rely on a fixed set of training classes and fail on novel or out-of-distribution objects, which is critical in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Existing open-world approaches either require OOD data, void-class supervision, or external foundation models (e.g., CLIP, SAM) to handle unknowns, making them impractical when such prior knowledge is unavailable. The paper addresses this by asking whether a mask transformer can be made uncertainty-aware through evidential learning alone, without any assumptions about the distribution of unknown categories.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and produces, for each of N_M mask queries, a per-pixel Beta prior (concentration parameters α and β) rather than a single sigmoid probability, along with a per-mask class assignment. The final segmentation is obtained by weighting class logits by the expected Beta value per pixel. For open-world instance separation, high-uncertainty pixels are clustered via DBSCAN on the pixel decoder's feature embeddings to assign distinct instance IDs to unknown objects, while outlier pixels not belonging to any cluster are reassigned to their original semantic class.

## Method

P2F extends Mask2Former by replacing the sigmoid-based pixel-to-mask assignment with a Beta-prior head that predicts per-pixel concentration parameters α and β for each mask query, computed as the dot product between mask-query embeddings and pixel decoder features (after softplus + 1 to ensure positivity). The mask probability becomes the Beta expectation α/(α+β), and uncertainty is the negative total evidence −(α+β). Training uses a novel symmetric Dice loss that balances updates to α and β to prevent the imbalanced binary mask task from collapsing one parameter, combined with an evidential sampling strategy that allocates 75% of the per-mask pixel budget to the highest-uncertainty regions. At inference, uncertainty is computed by selecting the mask with maximum α per pixel, re-weighting the classification confidence by the Beta expectation, and inverting the product; unknown instances are then separated by DBSCAN clustering on pixel decoder embeddings of thresholded high-uncertainty regions.

## Results

On COCO open-set panoptic segmentation, P2F achieves a closed-world PQ of 41.34 (SQ 78.60, RQ 50.79) and an unseen-class PQ of 10.71 (SQ 79.96, RQ 13.39), outperforming the only other assumption-free baseline U3HS (PQ 22.03 closed, 9.62 unseen) by a wide margin. On Cityscapes, P2F reaches PQ 59.40 in closed-world and 45.25 in open-world settings, versus U3HS's 46.53 and 41.21 respectively. On the OoDIS anomaly instance segmentation benchmark, P2F achieves a mean AP of 3.21 (AP50 8.85), substantially exceeding U3HS (AP 0.19) while remaining assumption-free, though trailing methods that exploit OOD data or external models (M2A: AP 13.73; UGainS: AP 25.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U3HS [20], the only other fully assumption-free method, P2F wins on every metric across all evaluated tasks: COCO closed-world PQ improves from 22.03 to 41.34, COCO unseen PQ from 9.62 to 10.71, Cityscapes closed PQ from 46.53 to 59.40, and OoDIS mean AP from 0.19 to 3.21. Against EOPSN [28] and DDOPS [63], which rely on void-class supervision during training, P2F achieves higher PQ and RQ on unseen COCO classes (10.71/13.39 vs. 0.01/0.01 and 8.39/10.29) but slightly lower SQ (79.96 vs. 81.52 for DDOPS), indicating marginally less precise boundaries on unknown regions. On the OoDIS anomaly instance segmentation benchmark, P2F (AP 3.21) substantially outperforms U3HS (AP 0.19) but remains well below M2A [46] (AP 13.73) and UGainS [42] (AP 25.19), both of which exploit OOD data or external foundation models. On L&F panoptic segmentation (Table 4), P2F (PQ 11.22) surpasses U3HS (7.94) and even M2A with P2F post-processing (9.91), confirming its advantage in instance-level anomaly detection without OOD assumptions. The paper notes that the gap to OOD-data-dependent methods remains large, and the closed-to-open-world performance drop (e.g., Cityscapes PQ 59.40 → 45.25) highlights the difficulty of calibrating uncertainty thresholds using only in-distribution data.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2F extends the Mask2Former (M2F) architecture by replacing its sigmoid-based mask assignment with a Beta-prior head that predicts per-pixel, per-mask concentration parameters, grounding the model in evidential learning (prior networks). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant open-world segmentation paradigm—which relies on OOD data, void-class supervision, or external foundation models—by treating uncertainty estimation as a first-class architectural component rather than a post-hoc heuristic. The closest prior work, U3HS, applies a Dirichlet prior network to a CNN (DeepLabV3+) backbone; P2F is the first to embed evidential learning directly within a mask-transformer decoder, and introduces a symmetric Dice loss and evidence-based pixel sampling tailored to the Beta distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

P2F offers a practical path toward reliable open-world segmentation in safety-critical settings (autonomous driving, robotics) where no OOD data or external models are available, and it handles anomaly segmentation, anomaly instance segmentation, and open-world panoptic segmentation with a single architecture and training procedure. However, the gap to methods that exploit OOD data or foundation models remains substantial (e.g., 3.21 vs. 25.19 mean AP on OoDIS), and the closed-to-open-world performance drop (PQ 59.4 → 45.25 on Cityscapes) highlights that uncertainty calibration and thresholding on known classes alone is still an open problem. Future work on better uncertainty estimation, adaptive thresholding, or combining evidential signals with lightweight external knowledge could close this gap.
