## Problem

Existing 3D talking head methods trained from scratch per identity overfit to limited training data, failing on large head rotations and out-of-distribution audio while requiring hours of training per subject. The paper identifies the root cause as insufficient 3D head priors, which prevents extrapolation beyond the training distribution and makes the technology impractical for widespread use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251735/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an audio sequence (encoded via Wav2Vec 2.0 into 512-dim per-frame tokens) and a single front-facing reference image as primary inputs, plus a 4–6 minute monocular training video at 512×512, 25 FPS for identity-specific adaptation. The output is a photorealistic talking head video where only the head region is synthesized via 3D Gaussian rendering, and a lightweight U-Net Body Inpainter blends the torso and background back in to produce seamless full-frame results indistinguishable from real footage.

## Method

GGTalker employs a two-stage Prior-Adaptation strategy built on FLAME-rigged 3D Gaussians. In the prior stage, a diffusion transformer (8-layer Transformer decoder with FiLM conditioning and classifier-free guidance) learns Audio-Expression Priors from large-scale multi-speaker data, while an Identity-Gaussian Generator predicts a 14-channel UV Gaussian map from a single image, trained self-supervisedly via a source-target rendering paradigm on VFHQ and NeRSemble. In the adaptation stage, both priors are fine-tuned on the target identity's video: the audio encoder is frozen to preserve generalizability while the conditional encoder and decoder adapt to individual speaking style, and the UV Gaussian map plus FLAME parameters are jointly optimized to correct monocular tracking errors. A lightweight color MLP modulates Gaussian SH coefficients by pose and expression for motion-aligned sharp textures, and the Body Inpainter is jointly fine-tuned to eliminate hard blending artifacts at the head-torso boundary.

## Results

On self-reenactment (Table 1), GGTalker achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 35.20, LPIPS 0.0281, SSIM 0.9816, FID 4.62, LMD 2.33, AUE 2.17, and LSE-C 8.21, while training in only 0.3 h and rendering at 120 FPS. On out-of-distribution audio (Table 2), GGTalker also leads in both cross-identity (LSE-D 8.05, LSE-C 6.27) and cross-language (LSE-D 8.92, LSE-C 5.77) lip-sync settings. Training time is roughly 10–100× faster than NeRF-based baselines (e.g., 0.3 h vs. 30 h for AD-NeRF) and inference speed (120 FPS) exceeds all 3DGS competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGTalker outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric in both self-reenactment and OOD-audio evaluations. Against the strongest 3DGS competitors, it improves PSNR by +2.26 over GaussianTalker (35.20 vs. 32.94) and +3.49 over TalkingGaussian (35.20 vs. 31.71), while reducing LPIPS from 0.0531/0.0396 to 0.0281. On lip-sync (LSE-C), GGTalker reaches 8.21 in self-reenactment and 6.27/5.77 in cross-identity/cross-language OOD settings, surpassing the next-best SyncTalk (7.69 / 5.64 / 5.30) and IP-LAP (4.07 / 5.39 / 5.04). NeRF-based methods (AD-NeRF, RAD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, GeneFace, GeneFace++) trail by 2–9 dB in PSNR and 0.03–0.12 in LPIPS, and require 4–30 h of training versus GGTalker's 0.3 h. Inference speed also favors GGTalker: 120 FPS vs. 72.8 (GaussianTalker), 76.3 (TalkingGaussian), and 31.9 (SyncTalk). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is LSE-C in self-reenactment, where Wav2Lip reports 8.87 (marked with an asterisk, likely under a different evaluation protocol), but Wav2Lip is a 2D method that does not produce 3D-consistent output.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGTalker extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting talking head line (GaussianTalker, TalkingGaussian, ER-NeRF) by replacing per-identity from-scratch training with a two-stage Prior-Adaptation strategy: large-scale pre-trained Audio-Expression (diffusion transformer) and Expression-Visual (self-supervised UV Gaussian) priors are fine-tuned on a single identity. The Gaussian-to-FLAME rigging follows GaussianAvatars, and the UV-map sampling of Gaussians adapts the GGHead formulation, but the key departure is treating the prior stage as a generalizable foundation rather than a per-subject optimization. This positions GGTalker between fully generalizable methods (which sacrifice identity fidelity) and fully identity-specific methods (which overfit and lack OOD robustness).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20-minute per-identity adaptation (vs. hours for prior 3DGS/NeRF methods) and 120 FPS rendering make high-fidelity talking heads practically deployable, while the OOD-audio and large-rotation generalization addresses the two most common failure modes in production. Open questions include whether the prior stage can be made fully identity-agnostic (eliminating even the 20-minute fine-tune), how the approach handles multi-person or occluded scenes, and whether the reliance on monocular FLAME tracking (VHAP) remains a bottleneck for extreme poses. The Prior-Adaptation paradigm—learn universal structure at scale, then cheaply specialize—could transfer to other 3D avatar and animation tasks.
