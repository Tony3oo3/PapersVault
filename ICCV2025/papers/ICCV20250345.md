## Problem

Existing image difference captioning (IDC) datasets are either domain-specific (surveillance frames, rendered CLEVR scenes), small-scale (~4K for IER), or large but noisy without human verification (OneDiff at 316K). No prior dataset simultaneously covers all manipulation categories—background change, local object change, text manipulation, and style change—while maintaining high quality at scale, leaving IDC models with poor generalization across real-world editing scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250345/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a pair of RGB images (original I₁ and manipulated I₂) and produces a single natural-language caption T describing only the differences while ignoring commonalities. Over 72.9% of images are 512×512 with the largest at 1024×1024, and captions average 9.72 words (range 33–66). The dataset is organized as triplets (I₁, I₂, T) across four manipulation categories, with text manipulation captions augmented by OCR results from MARIO-10M to specify the exact text content added or removed.

## Method

DiffTell is constructed by aggregating and quality-filtering five source datasets: InstructPix2Pix (synthetic, filtered from 50K to 17.6K), GIER (human-Photoshopped, 6.2K), MagicBrush (manually curated multi-turn edits segmented into single-turn, 8.8K), MARIO-10M (text removal via Firefly Generative Fill inpainting, filtered from 115K to 30.9K), and COCO (object removal via inpainting with size-constrained masks, filtered from 12.9K to 4K). For datasets providing only I₁, the second image is generated by inpainting masked regions with Firefly Generative Fill, and captions are templated as 'add/remove <object/text>' with random ordering. Quality control uses a two-stage human review on LabelBox (annotator + top-30% reviewer), and an automatic filtering pipeline is developed using Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned features fed into an SVM classifier (89.47% accuracy) to reduce annotation costs at scale. The final 67.6K-sample dataset is used as instruction-tuning data for six MLLM baselines (CLIP4IDC, OpenFlamingo-3B, Fuyu-8B, LLaVA-Interleave-8B, Idefics3-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B), with ablations isolating each subset's contribution per manipulation category.

## Results

On the IER testing set, Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned with DiffTell achieves the best overall performance: BLEU@4 17.76, METEOR 22.02, CIDEr 67.71, and ROUGE-L 45.35. All six baselines show consistent improvements across every metric when DiffTell is added to training; for example, CLIP4IDC improves from BLEU@4 5.65 to 8.84 and CIDEr 22.52 to 28.14. On the zero-shot PSBattle benchmark, Qwen2-VL-7B with DiffTell reaches BLEU@4 5.33 and CIDEr 16.66, again outperforming all other models. Ablation studies (Table 4) confirm that each DiffTell subset contributes positively, with the full dataset yielding the highest SPICE score of 21.86 and BertScore-F1 of 90.48 on IER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines—CLIP4IDC, OpenFlamingo-3B, Fuyu-8B, LLaVA-Interleave-8B, Idefics3-8B, and Qwen2-VL-7B—improve on every metric when DiffTell is added to training, confirming the dataset's broad utility. Qwen2-VL-7B is the strongest baseline both with and without DiffTell, reaching BLEU@4 17.76 and CIDEr 67.71 on IER, and it also leads on the zero-shot PSBattle set (BLEU@4 5.33, CIDEr 16.66). Fuyu-8B shows the largest relative CIDEr gain (23.67 → 41.05, +73%), attributed to its lack of a dedicated image encoder, which makes it more reliant on fine-tuning to detect small differences. The IDC-specific CLIP4IDC model, despite its direct image-patch fusion architecture, is outperformed by every MLLM baseline once DiffTell is included, suggesting that scale and data diversity outweigh task-specific architectural design. On PSBattle (zero-shot, out-of-domain), absolute scores drop substantially for all models, but the relative ranking is preserved and DiffTell still yields consistent gains, with Qwen2-VL-7B improving CIDEr from 14.29 to 16.66.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the image difference captioning (IDC) lineage that began with Spot-the-Diff (2018) and progressed through CLIP4IDC, DUDA, and recent MLLM-based approaches. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes instruction-guided image editing datasets (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, GIER) as IDC training data and extends them with generative inpainting (Firefly Generative Fill) on COCO and MARIO-10M to create new image pairs. The key departure from prior IDC datasets is the explicit inclusion of text manipulation as a first-class category and the combination of both real (human-Photoshopped) and synthetic (AI-edited) pairs under unified quality control, addressing the scale-versus-quality tradeoff that limited earlier resources like IER (~4K) and OneDiff (noisy, unverified).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffTell fills a concrete gap in the IDC research ecosystem by providing a 70K-sample, human-verified dataset spanning four manipulation categories, directly supporting the C2PA provenance-verification pipeline where describing differences between a circulated image and its original is the critical human-facing step. The automatic filtering pipeline (SVM on MLLM features, 89.47% accuracy) is a practical contribution that could reduce annotation costs for future dataset scaling. However, the paper acknowledges that the model still struggles with surreal or fantastical compositions (e.g., facial swaps) due to the predominantly real-life training data, and the text manipulation captions remain template-based rather than free-form, limiting linguistic diversity. Open questions include whether preference optimization on the accept/reject labels and Chain-of-Thought augmentation can close the remaining gap on complex multi-change scenes.
