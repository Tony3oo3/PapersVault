## Problem

Anomaly detection in integrated circuit manufacturing is hampered by the scarcity of publicly available, well-annotated datasets for ceramic package substrates, with only one prior dataset (DEEPPCB) existing in the IC domain at roughly 1/60th the scale. Additionally, deploying large vision models like SAM in industrial settings is impractical due to their high computational cost, creating a gap between state-of-the-art segmentation quality and real-world deployment constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250117/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-resolution 2D RGB images of ceramic package substrates (captured at micrometer scale by a 2K CCD camera with a double liquid lens) along with optional prompts: a point sampled within the defect mask, a bounding box, and a pixel-level mask. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask for defect localization and an anomaly score map. The CPS2D-AD dataset comprises 20,781 samples (11,000 normal, 9,781 abnormal) spanning 6 defect types across 40 product variants, with both pixel-level and bounding-box annotations.

## Method

SAIC distills knowledge from a pre-trained SAM with ViT-H encoder (teacher) into a ViT-B encoder (student) via an embedding-based distillation mechanism. A binary spatial mask, derived from the geometric center of the foreground mask prompt, splits patch embeddings into foreground and background components; a custom loss (L_ed) then enlarges the Euclidean margin between foreground and background embeddings while pulling different foreground embeddings closer together in latent space. Only the linear projection layer of ViT-B and the mask decoder are updated during training, drastically reducing trainable parameters. The total loss combines the embedding distillation term (weight 0.2) with a standard cross-entropy prediction loss (weight 1.0), and at inference the foreground/background split is removed, supporting both prompt-free and prompt-based prediction following the original SAM pipeline.

## Results

On the CPS2D-AD fully-supervised segmentation benchmark, SAIC achieves a mean mIoU of 87.03%, the highest among all 14 compared methods, with a mean Dice of 84.02%. The closest competitor on mIoU is DANet (83.75%), while SAM (the base model) reaches 81.02% mIoU and 84.43% Dice. On the unsupervised anomaly detection task, PatchCore leads with a pixel-level AUROC of 0.9051, and on the few-shot segmentation task, DCP achieves the best 1-shot mean mIoU of 34.99% and 5-shot mean mIoU of 36.72%. SAIC also generalizes to external datasets (NEU-Seg, Severstal, Magnetic Tile), achieving a mean Dice of 84.14% and mIoU of 81.63%, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIC outperforms all 13 baselines on mean mIoU (87.03%), with the largest margins over DeepLabV1 (+9.64%), ENCNet (+11.00%), and APCNet (+9.91%). On mean Dice, DANet (86.63%) and SAM (84.43%) slightly exceed SAIC (84.02%), but SAIC dominates on mIoU, indicating more precise boundary delineation. Compared to its base model SAM, SAIC improves mIoU by 6.01 points while sacrificing only 0.41 Dice points, validating the embedding-based distillation mechanism. On the unsupervised task, PatchCore leads pixel-level AUROC (0.9051) but all methods struggle with tiny defects (mean pixel-level AUROC ranges 0.42–0.91). On the few-shot task, DCP achieves the best 1-shot mIoU (34.99%), indicating that even with minimal annotations, CPS2D-AD's defect patterns remain challenging. Cross-dataset generalization (Table 7) confirms SAIC's superiority, beating the next-best method (ENCNet) by 5.85 Dice and 3.81 mIoU on average across NEU-Seg, Severstal, and Magnetic Tile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM (Segment Anything Model) paradigm to industrial IC anomaly detection by introducing an embedding-based knowledge distillation mechanism that compresses a ViT-H teacher into a ViT-B student, building directly on Hinton et al.'s distillation framework. On the data side, it departs from the small-scale, closed datasets that have dominated IC defect detection (e.g., DEEPPCB with only 1,500 samples) by constructing CPS2D-AD with over 20,000 annotated samples captured via a purpose-built AOI system. The benchmarking strategy follows the 'minimum to maximum supervision' logic already established in industrial anomaly detection literature, but applies it systematically across four task settings (unsupervised, few-shot, semi-supervised, fully-supervised) on a single unified dataset, which prior IC-specific works have not done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPS2D-AD fills a critical gap as the first large-scale, open-source anomaly detection dataset for ceramic package substrates, enabling reproducible research in a domain previously locked behind industrial confidentiality. The SAIC model demonstrates that foundation-model distillation can be adapted to micron-scale industrial defects with only linear-layer and decoder fine-tuning, reducing training cost substantially. Open questions include whether the embedding-based distillation mechanism generalizes to other IC substrate types or 3D inspection modalities, and whether the pixel-level performance gap between unsupervised and fully-supervised methods (AUROC ~0.42 vs. Dice ~87) can be closed with better self-supervised pretraining on IC-specific textures.
