## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods focus almost exclusively on the training phase—learning invariant features or augmenting data diversity—while neglecting the testing phase where the domain gap is most acute. Moreover, these approaches degrade significantly when only a few seen domains are available or when the gap between seen and unseen domains is large, because they lack a principled way to align inference-time data with the training distribution. ConstStyle addresses this by introducing a unified domain that serves as a common representation space for both training and testing, with theoretical performance bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for PACS/Digits5, 256×128 for Market-1501/Duke) and produces class labels for image classification or ranked retrieval lists (mAP, R1/R5/R10) for person re-identification. Unusually, at inference time each test image undergoes a partial style transformation—its intermediate-layer feature statistics are interpolated between the original and the unified domain's statistics via a hyperparameter α—before the classifier head produces the final prediction, effectively consuming a style-adjusted version of the input rather than the raw image.

## Method

ConstStyle defines a unified domain whose style distribution is the Wasserstein barycenter of the seen domains' style distributions (estimated via GMM clustering of per-sample channel-wise mean/variance statistics from a pretrained CNN). Training proceeds in two stages: a brief ERM pre-training to obtain style features, followed by unified-style training where each sample's intermediate features are normalized and rescaled to a randomly sampled style statistic from the unified domain, so the model sees E×D style variations all aligned to the same target distribution. At inference, test samples undergo a *partial* alignment (Eq. 5) that interpolates between their original style and the unified domain's style using α∈(0,1), balancing domain-shift reduction against preservation of task-relevant information. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) bounds the empirical loss gap between unified-domain-trained and original-domain-trained models, and between seen and unseen domains, in terms of Fréchet distances between style distributions.

## Results

On the PACS dataset (single unseen domain), ConstStyle achieves 86.77% average accuracy, outperforming the next-best method CSU (85.33%) by 1.44% and ERM (79.86%) by 6.91%. On Digits5, ConstStyle reaches 86.88% average accuracy vs. CSU's 85.65%. In the most challenging setting (trained on a single seen domain, tested on a distant unseen domain), ConstStyle achieves up to 19.82% higher accuracy than CSU (e.g., Sketch→Art). On instance retrieval (Market-1501→Duke), ConstStyle attains 26.1% mAP and 44.7% R1, surpassing CSU by 2.0% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConstStyle outperforms all 8 baselines on the PACS single-unseen-domain benchmark (Table 2), with the largest margins on the Sketch domain (+4.21% over CSU) and Art domain (+0.83% over CSU). On Digits5 (Table 3), ConstStyle leads on 4 of 5 domains, with the biggest gain on MNISTM (+3.26% over EDFMix). In the multi-unseen-domain setting (Table 4, two unseen domains on PACS), ConstStyle achieves 80.81% average vs. CSU's 78.38%, with a particularly large 5.91% advantage when Cartoon and Sketch are both unseen. On CIFAR-10-C corruption (Table 5), ConstStyle's advantage grows with corruption severity, reaching +2.75% over CSU at level 5. For instance retrieval (Table 6), ConstStyle wins on all metrics in both Market→Duke and Duke→Market directions, with mAP gains of 2.0% and 2.1% over CSU respectively. The only case where ConstStyle does not lead is CIFAR-10-C at corruption level 1, where ERM edges it out by 0.74%, consistent with the paper's observation that the unified-domain projection is most beneficial under larger domain gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConstStyle extends the style-statistics-based domain generalization line (MixStyle, DSU, CSU) by introducing a unified domain defined as the Wasserstein barycenter of seen-domain style distributions, and critically, by applying style transformation at test time as well as training time. While prior methods focus exclusively on augmenting or regularizing the training process to capture domain-invariant features, ConstStyle departs from this by explicitly projecting unseen test samples onto the same unified domain before inference, using a partial-alignment strategy (parameter α) to balance domain matching against information preservation. The barycenter formulation draws on optimal transport theory [31], and the style normalization mechanism is inherited from adaptive instance normalization [8] and MixStyle [29].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most practically relevant finding is that in low-domain-count settings (e.g., one or two seen domains), ConstStyle achieves up to 19.82% accuracy gains over the next-best method, addressing a scenario where most existing DG methods degrade sharply. The theoretical bounds (Theorems 1–2) provide a principled justification for the unified-domain choice and the partial-alignment parameter, which is uncommon in style-based DG work. However, the approach is limited to style shift (mean/variance of intermediate features) and does not address feature or semantic shift, as the authors acknowledge. An open question is whether the GMM-based domain clustering and barycenter computation remain reliable when seen domains are highly heterogeneous or when the number of domains is very large.
