## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding methods struggle to bridge the semantic gap between concise textual queries and the rich, redundant context of untrimmed videos, leading to suboptimal cross-modal alignment. Additionally, the absence of explicit target-guided cues makes it difficult for models to extract discriminative features specific to query-relevant moments, resulting in imprecise temporal localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251081/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted clip-level video features (SlowFast or VGG, projected to a shared dimension D) and word-level text features (CLIP or GloVe) from an untrimmed video and a natural language query. It produces start and end timestamps for the grounded moment along with per-clip saliency scores. The video is represented as N clips and the query as L tokens, both in a common D-dimensional space, with the diffusion model operating on a single D-dimensional knowledge vector rather than raw frames or full feature sequences.

## Method

KDA introduces a Background Residual Diffusion Model (BRDM) that starts from a global video knowledge vector (not random noise) and progressively removes query-irrelevant background via a residual injection schedule, generating query-relevant moment knowledge in just 4 denoising steps. A Multi-Layer Video Knowledge Extraction module hierarchically encodes video or moment features through three levels of self-attention with stride-2 downsampling to capture multi-scale temporal semantics. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) learning moment knowledge from annotated target clips, (2) training the BRDM to generate moment knowledge from global video features conditioned on the query, and (3) full inference where DM-generated knowledge replaces ground-truth sampling. The generated moment knowledge is injected as prompt tokens into both cross-modal attention and a Transformer encoder for reasoning, and a Spans-Enhanced Decoder adaptively selects top-K spans from the refined multi-modal features to produce final boundary predictions.

## Results

On QVHighlights (test split, SlowFast+CLIP features), KDA achieves R1@0.5 of 66.70%, R1@0.7 of 50.88%, and mAP (avg.) of 45.67%, surpassing the previous best LLMEPET by 0.94% on R1@0.7 and 1.62% on mAP. On the validation split, KDA reaches R1@0.5 of 69.11%, R1@0.7 of 53.46%, and mAP (avg.) of 47.41%. On Charades-STA (SF+C), KDA attains R1@0.5 of 60.23% and R1@0.7 of 37.63%, and on TACoS it reaches R1@0.5 of 40.13% and R1@0.7 of 24.34%, both setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDA outperforms all 15 compared baselines across all three datasets. On QVHighlights (test), KDA surpasses the closest competitor LLMEPET by +0.94% on R1@0.7 (50.88 vs 49.94) and +1.62% on mAP avg (45.67 vs 44.05), and leads TR-DETR by +1.92% on R1@0.7 and +3.05% on mAP avg. The margin over earlier methods is larger: KDA beats QD-DETR by +5.90% on R1@0.7 and +5.81% on mAP avg, and MomentDiff by +11.22% on R1@0.7. On Charades-STA (SF+C features), KDA edges out UVCOM by +0.98% on R1@0.5 (60.23 vs 59.25) and +0.99% on R1@0.7 (37.63 vs 36.64). On the most challenging long-video TACoS benchmark, KDA achieves a +3.74% relative improvement in R1@0.5 over UVCOM (40.13 vs 36.39) and +1.02% on R1@0.7 (24.34 vs 23.32), confirming consistent gains across video lengths and feature backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

KDA extends the proposal-free Transformer-based VTG paradigm (M-DETR, QD-DETR, TR-DETR) by inserting a diffusion model into the feature space, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based VTG methods (MomentDiff, DiffusionVMR) that generate spans or frames from pure Gaussian noise. The key conceptual departure is the Background Residual Diffusion Model, which reformulates the forward process as progressive removal of query-irrelevant background from a structured global video representation rather than iterative denoising of random noise. The three-stage training strategy, which uses annotated moment guidance to condition the diffusion process before discarding it at inference, is a novel training recipe not seen in prior VTG or diffusion-for-understanding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in VTG—concise queries failing to capture the rich visual context of target moments—by using diffusion to synthesize query-conditioned moment knowledge enriched with video context, a framing that could inform other cross-modal alignment tasks. However, the improvements over strong baselines are modest (1–2% on QVHighlights, ~1% on Charades-STA), and the method still relies on frozen pretrained features (SlowFast, CLIP) and a small number of diffusion steps (4), leaving open whether the generative component is truly necessary or whether a simpler residual projection could capture most of the gain. The approach's applicability to longer, more complex videos beyond TACoS remains untested, and the computational overhead of the diffusion module, while small, adds latency to an already multi-stage pipeline.
