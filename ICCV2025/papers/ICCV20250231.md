## Problem

MambaVC, the first Mamba-based learned image compression method, relies on predefined four-directional scanning that treats spatially adjacent pixels as semantically related, ignoring content structure and causing inefficient redundancy elimination. Additionally, it applies state-space models only to nonlinear transformations while neglecting their potential in the entropy model, where long-range context could substantially improve probability estimation for arithmetic coding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250231/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution (3×H×W) and produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding plus a reconstructed image. The encoder maps the input to a 320-channel latent at 1/16 spatial resolution, which is divided into five uneven channel slices for autoregressive entropy coding. A 192-channel hyperprior latent provides spatial context for distribution parameter prediction. Training uses 256×256 random crops from Open Images with a batch size of 8.

## Method

Cassic introduces two key innovations. First, a Content-Adaptive Selective Scan (CASS) replaces MambaVC's four-directional 2D scanning: at the encoder, a deformable convolution generates a weighted activation map that determines the 1D unfolding order so semantically similar pixels become adjacent before S6 processing; at the decoder, a Bit Allocation Predictor propagates per-position bit costs across upsampling stages to guide the scan, while retaining depthwise convolution for speed. This reduces four S6 passes to one. Second, a Mamba-based channel-wise autoregressive entropy model integrates the content-adaptive VSSB into the slice network, using the bit allocation map from the previously coded slice to modulate CASS in the current slice, thereby exploiting the observed cross-slice consistency in relative bit allocation to sharpen probability estimation.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, Cassic achieves a BD-rate of −15.11% relative to VVC, outperforming the best Mamba-based method MambaVC (−13.63%) by 1.49% and the CNN-based CCA (−13.87%) by 2.17%. On CLIC Professional Validation and Tecnick, Cassic improves over MambaVC by 2.62% and 2.06% respectively, and over CCA by 1.92% and 2.31%. The method uses 67.51M parameters with a coding latency of 261 ms on an RTX 4090, which is faster than MambaVC (282 ms) while delivering better RD performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cassic outperforms MambaVC [31] on all three evaluation datasets (Kodak, CLIC Professional Validation, Tecnick) by 1.49%, 2.62%, and 2.06% BD-rate respectively, while also reducing coding latency from 282 ms to 261 ms. Against the strongest CNN-based baseline CCA [16], Cassic achieves 2.17%, 1.92%, and 2.31% BD-rate gains on the three datasets, though at a higher latency (261 ms vs. 201 ms). Compared to the Transformer-based FTIC [25], Cassic leads by 1.53%, 2.11%, and 1.87% on Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick. MLIC++ [22] is the only method that beats Cassic at low bitrates, but its advantage reverses at higher bitrates, requiring 0.9% and 4.5% more bits than Cassic on CLIC and Tecnick respectively. Cassic also surpasses Mixed [27] (−15.11% vs. −11.88% BD-rate), ELIC [18], Cheng20 [11], and traditional codecs (VVC, BPG, WebP) across all tested datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MambaVC [31], which first introduced selective state-space models (S6/Mamba) into learned image compression, by replacing its fixed four-directional 2D scanning with a content-adaptive selective scan (CASS) guided by deformable-convolution activation maps (encoder) and bit allocation maps (decoder). The gated VSSB structure follows VMamba [29], while the entropy model builds on the channel-wise autoregressive paradigm of Minnen & Singh [30], augmented with CA VSSB blocks and cross-slice bit allocation guidance. The key conceptual departure is treating SSMs not merely as a drop-in replacement for CNN/Transformer blocks in the nonlinear transform, but as a tool whose scanning order should be content-aware and whose long-range modeling capability should also be exploited in the entropy model for probability estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that content-adaptive scanning outperforms fixed multi-directional scanning while reducing computational cost (one S6 pass instead of four), this work provides a practical recipe for integrating SSMs into compression pipelines more effectively than prior attempts. The finding that decoder-side CASS contributes more to RD performance than encoder-side CASS (4.91% vs 2.67% BD-rate degradation in ablation) suggests that reconstruction quality benefits more from semantically coherent context than does latent representation. Open questions include whether the content-adaptive scanning strategy generalizes to video compression or higher-resolution settings, whether the bit allocation predictor's nearest-neighbor interpolation introduces artifacts at extreme scale factors, and whether the approach's gains persist when the underlying SSM is swapped for other linear-complexity sequence models.
