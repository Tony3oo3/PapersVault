## Problem

Leading open-source MLLMs exhibit significant limitations in complex, structured reasoning tasks requiring deep step-by-step problem-solving. Three critical gaps persist: a shortage of high-quality multimodal chain-of-thought training data, insufficient visual representation and cross-modal alignment for accurate reasoning, and a tendency toward over-reasoning on easy tasks and under-reasoning on hard ones during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Corvid takes an RGB image (processed at 384×384 resolution by both the ViT and CNN encoders) and a text question as input, producing a chain-of-thought reasoning response followed by a final answer. During inference, the self-verification strategy generates both a direct response and a CoT response in parallel, then selects the final answer by comparing a weighted combination of cross-modal representation similarity and normalized perplexity (model confidence) between the two paths, with a fixed weight α=0.7 favoring confidence.

## Method

Corvid integrates a hybrid vision encoder (SigLIP ViT-SO400M for semantic features and OpenCLIP ConvNeXt-XXL for multi-stage spatial details) with a novel GateMixer connector that element-wise mixes the two feature streams via a sigmoid-gated selective attention mechanism, preserving token length while enabling cross-modal interaction. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) multi-grained alignment pre-training of GateMixer on MGA-1M with a contrastive regularization loss, (2) CoT-enhanced supervised fine-tuning on Corvid-1M (1M diverse instruction samples including 20.2% CoT-formatted reasoning data), and (3) pure-CoT instruction tuning on o1-320K to strengthen structured reasoning. The key dataset contribution is MCoT-Instruct-287K, a high-quality multimodal CoT instruction-following dataset refined and standardized from diverse public reasoning sources using GPT assistance. At inference, a self-verification strategy mitigates over/under-reasoning by generating both direct and CoT responses, then selecting the answer based on a weighted score of image-text representation similarity and model confidence, eliminating the need for external verifiers.

## Results

Corvid-o1-8B achieves 65.2 on MMStar, 59.7 on MMMU, 72.0 on MathVista, 59.8 on WeMath, 30.1 on MathVision, and 33.2 on DynaMath, setting new state-of-the-art scores on 7 out of 9 multimodal reasoning benchmarks. On comprehensive benchmarks, Corvid-o1-8B reaches 77.0 on SEED-IMG, 76.4 on RWQA, and 57.9 on BLINK, surpassing the previous best by 3.9 and 1.4 points respectively. The self-verification inference strategy (INFER<SV>) yields an average of 59.9 across reasoning benchmarks, outperforming direct inference (48.9) by 11.0 points and CoT-only inference (56.2) by 3.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Corvid-o1-8B outperforms the strongest general MLLM baselines (Ovis2-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MiniCPM-o-2.6-7B) on 7 of 9 reasoning benchmarks, with the largest margins on WeMath (59.8 vs. 36.4 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B), MathVision (30.1 vs. 25.9 for Ovis2-8B), and DynaMath (33.2 vs. 20.4 for Ovis2-8B). It trails only on SQA-IMG (93.8 vs. 98.8 for MiniCPM-o-2.6-7B) and AI2D (85.0 vs. 86.6 for Ovis2-8B). Against o1-like MLLMs, Corvid-o1-8B achieves a 67.5 average across six benchmarks versus 63.1 for LLaVA-o1, 67.3 for LlamaV-o1, and 62.8 for Mulberry-o1-7B, with the most pronounced advantage on MathVista (72.0 vs. 56.1/54.4/57.5). Among Llama3-8B-based models, Corvid-o1-8B leads all competitors by a wide margin, e.g., 65.2 vs. 55.0 (Idefics3-8B) on MMStar and 72.0 vs. 58.7 (Idefics3-8B) on MathVista. On comprehensive benchmarks, Corvid-o1-8B surpasses Ovis2-8B on BLINK (57.9 vs. 54.3) and RWQA (76.4 vs. 72.5), while Corvid-base-8B leads RWQA by 4.5 points over the prior SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

Corvid positions itself within the emerging o1-like MLLM line (LLaVA-o1, LlamaV-o1, Mulberry-o1) that introduced CoT-formatted reasoning to multimodal models, but departs from these in three architectural and procedural ways: a hybrid ViT+CNN vision encoder (SigLIP + ConvNeXt-XXL) rather than a single encoder, a gate-mechanism connector (GateMixer) inspired by LSTM input gates rather than MLP or cross-attention projections, and a self-verification inference strategy that replaces the beam-search or MCTS-based path selection used by prior o1-like MLLMs. The training data pipeline refines and standardizes existing public reasoning datasets into MCoT-Instruct-287K, addressing the quality gap between brief manual CoTs and noisy AI-generated rationales. The three-stage training (alignment pre-training → CoT-enhanced SFT → pure-CoT tuning) follows the standard MLLM recipe but is specifically structured to progressively build reasoning capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-verification inference strategy is a practical and lightweight solution to the over-reasoning/under-reasoning problem that plagues uniform CoT inference, and its 11-point gain over direct inference (and 4-point gain over forced CoT) makes it a technique likely to be adopted by other MLLM pipelines. The MCoT-Instruct-287K dataset and the curated Corvid-1M/o1-320K training sets provide a useful resource for the community working on multimodal reasoning. However, the paper itself acknowledges a key limitation: Corvid still fails on problems requiring world commonsense (e.g., typical lane widths), pointing to the need for retrieval-augmented generation or RL-based approaches. Open questions include whether the self-verification strategy generalizes to longer reasoning chains, whether the hybrid encoder's modest ~1-point advantage over SigLIP alone justifies the added complexity at scale, and how these techniques interact with reinforcement-learning-based reasoning (e.g., GRPO as in DeepSeek-R1).
