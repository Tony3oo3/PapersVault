## Problem

Existing image editing methods cannot simultaneously preserve a subject's identity, adopt the target image's artistic style, and produce a physically plausible insertion with shadows and reflections. Stylization methods (StyleAlign, InstantStyle) lose subject fidelity, photorealistic insertion methods (AnyDoor, ObjectDrop) cannot stylize, and composition-based approaches (TF-ICON) lack realistic contextual cues. This paper formalizes the style-aware drag-and-drop problem and proposes a unified solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject reference image (subject isolated on a plain background) and a target background image that may be in a vastly different artistic style (3D render, cartoon, anime, watercolor, photographic, etc.). It produces a composite image where the subject is rendered in the target's style and inserted with realistic occlusion, shadows, and reflections. The pipeline also accepts optional text prompts for pose modification, accessory changes, or species hybrids, and can be guided by an LLM for automatic scene-interaction affordances.

## Method

Magic Insert decomposes the problem into style-aware personalization and realistic insertion. For personalization, it jointly trains LoRA weight deltas and two learned text-token embeddings on the subject image via diffusion denoising loss, then at inference injects the target image's CLIP style embedding through a frozen IP-Adapter into the UNet upsample block adjacent to the midblock (which most strongly controls style). For insertion, it introduces Bootstrapped Domain Adaptation (BDA): the ObjectDrop removal/insertion model (trained on real photos) is first applied to stylized images to remove subjects and shadows, outputs are filtered for quality, and the model is retrained on this filtered subset to shift its effective domain from photorealistic to stylized. The final insertion uses copy-paste of the segmented stylized subject followed by the BDA-adapted model to generate contextual cues.

## Results

On the SubjectPlop dataset, Magic Insert (with ControlNet) achieves the highest subject fidelity with an overall mean of 0.495 (DINO 0.514, CLIP-I 0.869), outperforming StyleAlign (0.451) and InstantStyle (0.454). For aesthetic quality, Magic Insert (CN) scores -0.147 on ImageReward versus -0.276 (InstantStyle CN) and -0.518 (StyleAlign CN). In a 60-participant user study, Magic Insert is preferred over StyleAlign (85%), InstantStyle (80%), AnyDoor (90%), and TF-ICON (95%) on combined criteria of subject identity, style fidelity, and realistic insertion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magic Insert (CN) outperforms StyleAlign [13] on subject fidelity (0.495 vs. 0.451 overall mean), style fidelity (0.381 vs. 0.345), and ImageReward (-0.147 vs. -0.518), with 85% user preference. Against InstantStyle [51], Magic Insert wins on subject fidelity (0.495 vs. 0.454) and ImageReward (-0.147 vs. -0.276), though InstantStyle edges ahead on style fidelity (0.400 vs. 0.381); user preference still favors Magic Insert at 80%. For non-stylized insertion methods, Magic Insert is strongly preferred over AnyDoor [7] (90%) and TF-ICON [22] (95%), as these baselines fail to stylize the inserted subject or produce missing shadows and reflections (Figure 8). ObjectDrop [55] is used as the base insertion model that BDA adapts rather than a direct end-to-end baseline, but Figure 8 shows that without BDA the insertion quality degrades significantly on stylized inputs. Overall, Magic Insert is the only method that simultaneously achieves high subject fidelity, strong style adherence, and realistic insertion with shadows and reflections across all evaluated baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of subject personalization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, LoRA), style transfer (StyleDrop, InstantStyle, StyleAlign), and photorealistic object insertion (ObjectDrop, AnyDoor), but departs from each by refusing to solve style-aware drag-and-drop as a single inpainting or composition step. Its key methodological lineage is the two-stage decomposition: first generating a style-matched subject via joint LoRA + text-embedding fine-tuning with IP-Adapter style injection into a specific UNet upsample block, then inserting that subject using an ObjectDrop-style model adapted via Bootstrapped Domain Adaptation. The BDA technique itself is a novel lightweight domain-adaptation idea—retraining on a model's own filtered outputs—that has no direct precedent in the cited literature, and the paper explicitly notes that combining adapter-based style injection with personalized models 'remains unexplored in the published literature.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of style-aware drag-and-drop and the release of SubjectPlop (700 subject-background pairs across diverse styles) give the community a standardized benchmark for a task that previously had no dedicated evaluation protocol. The BDA technique, requiring only ~50 filtered samples and one retraining step, is a potentially general-purpose tool for adapting any diffusion-based editing model to a new visual domain, though the paper provides limited quantitative ablation of its effect. A notable limitation is that the two-stage pipeline (copy-paste then contextualize) inherits the segmentation quality of the first stage, and the automated metrics show the method without ControlNet actually trails InstantStyle on both subject and style fidelity, suggesting the ControlNet conditioning is doing substantial work. Open questions include whether BDA can be iterated effectively beyond one step, whether the pipeline can be unified end-to-end, and how it handles multi-subject or heavily occluded scenes.
