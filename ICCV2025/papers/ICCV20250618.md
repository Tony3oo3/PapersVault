## Problem

Existing depth estimation datasets and methods provide only single-layer depth, capturing either the transparent surface or the objects behind it but never both simultaneously. No prior dataset offers multi-layer depth annotations, making it impossible to train or evaluate systems that perceive depth across multiple physical media transitions (e.g., air→glass→air→object). This paper introduces multi-layer depth estimation as a new task and provides the first real-world benchmark and synthetic data generator to support it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes a single RGB image of resolution H×W as input and, for each query pixel, produces an ordered sequence of per-layer depth values {d_1, …, d_n} where n varies spatially and each d_i is the z-axis distance from the camera to the i-th medium transition along that ray. Because absolute multi-layer depth cannot be reliably measured for transparent objects, the real-world benchmark (1,500 images) uses 14.2M relative depth tuples (pairs, triplets, quadruplets) with layer IDs, plus 10% fake tuples to penalize spurious layer predictions. The synthetic dataset provides pixel-aligned multi-layer depth maps with refraction-aware geometry.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully procedural synthetic data generator (LayeredDepth-Syn, 15,300 images) built on Infinigen Indoors, modified with a random material assignment system that can designate any object part as transparent, relaxed spatial constraints to create overlapping/stacked transparent objects, and enhanced lighting including disco-style and close-up camera shots. Multi-layer ground truth is obtained by modifying Blender's ray-tracing source to log depth at every medium transition along each ray, with all non-transparent materials converted to diffuse and transparent materials set to refraction-only to prevent reflection contamination. Three baseline architectures are proposed for multi-layer prediction—Multi-head Output (parallel depth maps), Layer Index Concatenation (4-channel input with layer ID), and Recurrent (iterative prediction conditioned on prior layer depth)—all using NeWCRFs as backbone. Fine-tuning Metric3D V2 on the synthetic data alone boosts quadruplet accuracy on the real benchmark from 55.14% to 75.20%, demonstrating the generator's effectiveness for transfer learning.

## Results

On the LayeredDepth real-world benchmark, fine-tuning Metric3D V2 on the LayeredDepth-Syn synthetic dataset achieves 89.53% pair, 81.71% triplet, and 75.20% quadruplet accuracy, substantially outperforming all existing methods. Among unmodified state-of-the-art models, Depth Pro leads with 87.39% pair and 69.46% quadruplet accuracy, while Depth Anything V2 reaches 85.34% pair and 70.43% quadruplet accuracy. The fine-tuned Metric3D V2 improves quadruplet accuracy from 55.14% (original) to 75.20%, a 20-point gain that surpasses the best prior method (DA V2) by 4.77 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned Metric3D V2 (trained on LayeredDepth-Syn) decisively outperforms all 12 compared methods on all three tuple-accuracy metrics, achieving 75.20% quadruplet accuracy versus 70.43% for the best unmodified model (Depth Anything V2) and 69.46% for Depth Pro. Among methods specifically designed for transparent objects, Depth4ToM (70.61% Q) and Tosi et al. (70.10% Q) are competitive but still trail the fine-tuned model by 4.6 and 5.1 points respectively. The original Metric3D V2 without fine-tuning scores only 55.14% quadruplet accuracy, indicating that the synthetic data is the critical factor rather than the backbone architecture. On the synthetic validation set (Table 3), Depth Pro and Metric3D V2 lead in metric depth, while Depth Pro also leads in affine-invariant depth; all methods show significantly elevated errors on transparent regions compared to standard benchmarks, confirming the difficulty of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Infinigen Indoors for procedural scene generation but fundamentally departs from prior depth estimation work by introducing multi-layer depth as a distinct task formulation. Existing transparent-object datasets (Booster, ClearPose, TransCG) and general depth benchmarks (NYUv2, ScanNet, KITTI) all provide only single-layer depth, either on the transparent surface or on objects behind it, but never both simultaneously. The paper also positions itself against prior synthetic transparent-object datasets (ClearGrasp, LIT, RGB-D LIF) that are limited to desk-bound or planar-surface scenarios, offering instead a fully procedural generator with unlimited scene diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and dataset to provide multi-layer depth ground truth, filling a critical gap for applications requiring perception through transparent materials (autonomous navigation, manipulation, 3D reconstruction). The empirical finding that even the strongest SOTA methods (Depth Anything V2, Depth Pro) achieve only ~70% quadruplet accuracy on transparent objects, and that fine-tuning a mid-tier model (Metric3D V2) on purely synthetic data surpasses all zero-shot methods, suggests that domain-specific synthetic data is a viable path to closing the transparency gap. Open questions include whether the relative-depth annotation paradigm can be extended to metric multi-layer depth, whether the synthetic-to-real gap narrows with more diverse procedural assets, and whether the three baseline architectures (multi-head, index concatenation, recurrent) scale to scenes with many more than 5–6 layers.
