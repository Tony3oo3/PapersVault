## Problem

Controllable traffic simulation for autonomous vehicle testing has relied on manually designed scenarios or post-hoc heuristic guidance functions, limiting scalability and requiring domain expertise. No prior diffusion-based traffic simulation model directly conditions on natural language during training, making it difficult for non-experts to generate diverse counterfactual or safety-critical scenarios. Additionally, standard open-loop training of diffusion models causes distribution shift and error accumulation during closed-loop rollouts.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language prompt (e.g., 'yield to the right'), HD map features, and T_hist steps of historical agent states as input. The language is processed through DistillBERT after rephrasing agent roles (target agent, other agent 1, etc.), producing an agent-specific language-conditioned embedding that is cross-attended in the denoiser. Output is the joint future trajectory of all N agents over a planning horizon T, generated in a scene-independent local coordinate system via iterative diffusion denoising, with optional classifier-based or classifier-free guidance applied at inference time.

## Method

LANGTRAJ is a scene-diffusion model with three components: a query-centric GNN scene encoder operating in local coordinate frames, a DistillBERT-based language encoder fine-tuned with LoRA that produces agent-specific text embeddings, and a stacked transformer denoiser combining query-centric inter-agent attention, agent-context cross-attention, and text-cross attention. The key architectural insight is direct language conditioning during training rather than post-hoc guidance, enabling intuitive behavior specification without hand-crafted objective functions. A novel closed-loop training strategy addresses the distribution shift problem: at each training step, the model performs one-step reverse diffusion to generate M candidate trajectories, selects the closest to ground truth via a distance metric, executes it to update the state, and computes loss in global coordinates—avoiding the computationally prohibitive full iterative denoising loop. A teacher-forcing variant where a subset of agents follows ground-truth states stabilizes training and improves map adherence. The model is trained on INTERDRIVE, a 150k-scene dataset with human-labeled agent-agent interaction types (merging, yielding, passing, etc.) and heuristic single-agent behavior labels from Waymo and NuPlan.

## Results

On the WOSAC benchmark (Table 3), LANGTRAJ achieves a meta-realism score of 0.719, kinematic score of 0.426, interactive score of 0.795, and map adherence of 0.789, placing it competitively among diffusion-based methods (VBD: 0.720 meta; SceneDiffuser: 0.703 meta) but behind non-diffusion baselines UniMM (0.769) and SMART-tiny-CLSFT (0.762). With direct text conditioning on INTERDRIVE, LANGTRAJ reduces mADE by 13.6% (2.65 → 2.29) while maintaining meta-realism at 0.72. In safety-critical simulation, LANGTRAJ with direct text conditioning plus collision guidance achieves a 43% collision rate, outperforming CTG++-style LLM guidance (33%) by 10 percentage points while also improving kinematic (0.41 vs 0.37) and map (0.74 vs 0.72) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-diffusion baselines UniMM (0.769 meta) and SMART-tiny-CLSFT (0.762 meta), LANGTRAJ (0.719 meta) trails by 5 and 4.3 points respectively, reflecting the inherent trade-off of diffusion-based generation. Among diffusion-based methods, LANGTRAJ edges out VBD on kinematic (0.426 vs 0.417) and map (0.789 vs 0.776) but falls slightly behind on interactive (0.795 vs 0.814) and meta (0.719 vs 0.720). Compared to SceneDiffuser, LANGTRAJ wins on meta (0.719 vs 0.703), kinematic (0.426 vs 0.430 is a near-tie), interactive (0.795 vs 0.776), and map (0.789 vs 0.768). Against ProSim (the primary language-conditioned baseline), LANGTRAJ achieves higher meta (0.72 vs 0.69) and interactive (0.80 vs 0.73) scores with text conditioning, while ProSim retains a slight edge on map (0.81 vs 0.78) and mADE (2.35 vs 2.52). In the text-conditioning comparison (Table 4), direct language conditioning outperforms CTG++-style LLM guidance on mADE (2.29 vs 2.70) and interactive score (0.80 vs 0.75), and in safety-critical simulation achieves a 10-point higher collision rate (43% vs 33%) with better kinematic and map fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LANGTRAJ sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: diffusion-based controllable traffic simulation (CTG, SAFE-SIM, SceneDiffuser, VBD) and language-conditioned driving simulation (ProSim, LCTGen). It departs from the former by baking language conditioning directly into the diffusion training objective rather than relying on post-hoc classifier-based or classifier-free guidance functions, and from the latter by adopting a diffusion backbone (for inference-time flexibility and guided sampling) instead of an autoregressive framework. The closed-loop training strategy extends the teacher-forcing idea from autoregressive traffic simulators (TrafficSim, ProSim) to the iterative denoising setting of diffusion models, using a one-step reverse pass to avoid the computational cost of full multi-step denoising during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The INTERDRIVE dataset (150k human-labeled interactive scenarios) fills a notable gap: prior language-conditioned simulation datasets (ProSim-Instruct) rely on LLM-generated prompts and focus on single-agent maneuvers, whereas INTERDRIVE targets agent-agent interactions with expert annotations. The finding that direct language conditioning outperforms LLM-generated guidance functions (CTG++ style) for interaction-level behaviors challenges the assumption that more elaborate post-hoc guidance is always superior, though the evidence is limited to one dataset and one guidance implementation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons, more diverse agent types, or real-time deployment, and whether the closed-loop training strategy generalizes to other diffusion-based generative tasks beyond traffic simulation.
