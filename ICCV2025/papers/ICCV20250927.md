## Problem

Current gait recognition modalities (parsing, 3D mesh, point clouds) rely on unstable upstream estimations with pre-defined designs, and the critical aspect of modality generalization across domains remains underexplored. The paper asks how to develop a gait-specific intermediate modality with strong within- and cross-domain generalization in a stable and flexible manner, without depending on fragile estimation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250927/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB gait video frames (30 frames per sequence) and a pre-computed silhouette mask. Each frame is converted to YCbCr, split into 8×8 patches, and processed through DCT; the final X modality is a masked RGB-like image with DC components removed and AC components progressively suppressed. For the decomposition training branch, each frame is additionally reorganized into 64 (K×K) spectral images that are concatenated along the channel dimension before feeding the backbone. The two branches (X modality and decomposition) produce features that are concatenated along the height dimension after the GaitBase backbone, yielding a final gait embedding for identity matching.

## Method

Gait-X replaces upstream estimation with patch-based DCT (8×8 patches, following JPEG conventions) to construct a gait-specific X modality: it removes all DC (extremely low frequency) components to eliminate brightness and color noise, then applies progressive AC (high frequency) suppression using JPEG-style quantization matrices that impose stronger attenuation on higher frequencies and on chrominance (Cb/Cr) channels, with a global suppression factor γ=1/16. A second contribution is a frequency decomposition training strategy: the DCT coefficients are reorganized so that elements at the same spatial position across all patches form K×K=64 spectral images, which are concatenated along channels and fed to the backbone alongside the X modality, guiding the network to attend to a broader frequency range during early training rather than overfitting to high frequencies. The backbone is GaitBase (ResNet-like) trained with triplet and cross-entropy loss, and the two branches are simply concatenated along the height dimension after feature extraction, requiring no architectural modification to the backbone itself.

## Results

On CCPG (gait evaluation protocol), Gait-X achieves 88.6% mean rank-1, surpassing the best RGB method BigGait (87.2%) by 1.4%, the best silhouette method GaitBase (78.6%) by 10.0%, and the best skeleton method SkeletonGait (50.9%) by 37.7%. On CASIA-B, Gait-X reaches 97.7% mean rank-1 (93.7% under the most challenging CL setting), outperforming BigGait (96.7%) and VPNet (94.9%). On SUSTech1K, Gait-X achieves 96.9% overall, with a notable 85.2% under the cloth-changing condition. In cross-domain evaluation (CCPG→SUSTech1K), Gait-X achieves 73.4% overall, a 17.0-point improvement over BigGait (56.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gait-X outperforms all silhouette-based baselines by a wide margin: +10.0% over GaitBase (78.6→88.6) and +23.0% over GaitSet (65.6→88.6) on CCPG gait protocol, confirming that the X modality retains silhouette's noise robustness while adding discriminative mid-frequency structure. Against the strongest RGB baseline BigGait (87.2%), Gait-X leads by 1.4% on CCPG and by 1.0% on CASIA-B (97.7 vs 96.7), with a particularly large 3.2% gain under the cloth-changing condition (93.7 vs 90.5). On SUSTech1K, Gait-X (96.9%) edges out BigGait (96.2%) and surpasses the best lidar method HMRNet (90.2%) by 6.7%. In cross-domain transfer, Gait-X consistently dominates: CCPG→SUSTech1K overall improves from 56.4% (BigGait) to 73.4% (+17.0), and SUSTech1K→CCPG BG improves from 45.5% to 56.0% (+10.5), demonstrating that frequency-guided denoising yields substantially better domain-invariant representations. The only setting where Gait-X trails is SUSTech1K Night (71.9% vs HMRNet's 90.4%), attributed to poor silhouette segmentation in low-light conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant gait modality exploration paradigm (silhouette → parsing, 3D mesh, point clouds, optical flow) that relies on upstream estimation models, instead repurposing JPEG-style spatial frequency decomposition (DCT with quantization matrices in YCbCr space) as a stable, controllable alternative. It also shifts the frequency analysis in gait recognition from the temporal domain (gait cycle periodicity, step frequency) to the spatial domain, drawing on the frequency principle literature showing CNNs fit low frequencies before high frequencies. The backbone (GaitBase) and evaluation protocols follow recent gait recognition practice, while the frequency decomposition training strategy (concatenating multiple frequency-band images) is a novel application of multi-scale input ideas to gait-specific overfitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gait-X offers a simple, deterministic, and interpretable modality construction that avoids the instability and black-box nature of upstream estimators (parsing, mesh, point cloud), making it attractive for practical deployment where estimation quality varies. The substantial cross-domain gains (+22.6% over RGB on CCPG→CASIA-B) suggest that frequency-domain denoising is a more effective generalization lever than adding semantic/structural information. Open questions include whether the approach extends to mask-free settings (the silhouette mask fails in night conditions), whether learned frequency filters could replace the fixed JPEG quantization matrices, and whether the frequency decomposition training strategy generalizes beyond gait to other video-based recognition tasks.
