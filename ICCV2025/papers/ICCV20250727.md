## Problem

Existing 4D human-object interaction datasets either focus on single-object interactions, lack detailed hand/finger poses, or consist of isolated, purposeless movements that do not reflect natural task progression. This limits the development of models for realistic multi-object manipulation, motion generation, and robotic learning from demonstration. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a high-fidelity dataset with complex, purposeful multi-object interactions captured at full-body and finger-level detail.

## Input / Output

The capture pipeline consumes Rokoko EMF-based smart-suit and glove signals (30 fps full-body + finger articulation), dual-Kinect RGB-D video streams for object tracking, and LLM-generated scene scripts that define the interaction narrative. Outputs include 735 sequences (7,875 s at 30 fps) with Mixamo-rigged skeletal poses, detailed hand/finger poses, 6DoF object poses for 63 artist-modeled objects (72 articulated parts), three-level text annotations (title, short script, long script), and contact maps. Notably, objects are hand-modeled by artists rather than 3D-scanned, and the human skeleton is transferred to a standard Mixamo rig rather than retaining raw mocap joint data.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-modal capture and scripting pipeline. A Scene-Driven LLM Scripting framework hierarchically generates purposeful interaction scripts by grouping objects into conceptual 'rooms' and prompting LLMs to produce cohesive, multi-step task narratives. For capture, a Rokoko electromagnetic-field suit with paired gloves tracks the performer's full body and fingers (immune to visual occlusions), while two Kinect cameras at opposing corners feed FoundationPose for 6DoF object tracking, with SAM2-generated masks and a dynamic reset mechanism to handle occlusions and rapid motion. A custom 12-inch wooden stage minimizes magnetic interference, and a calibration board aligns the mocap and camera coordinate frames. Post-capture, a two-stage quality assurance protocol (artist-led technical refinement followed by independent verification) eliminates foot sliding, object penetration, and joint jitter. The paper also introduces a Motion Signal-to-Noise Ratio (MSNR) metric and a comprehensive benchmark suite (foot sliding, jerk, penetration, contact entropy, state consistency) to quantitatively compare HOI datasets.

## Results

HUMOTO achieves the lowest foot sliding (0.958 cm) and the lowest hand-object penetration (0.0068 cm) among all compared datasets, representing an order-of-magnitude improvement over BEHAVE (0.0606 cm) and OMOMO (0.0602 cm). It also records the lowest object jerk (1.13 m/s³) and the highest motion coherence (0.653), while its MSNR of 9.42 dB approaches the Mixamo reference (10.88 dB). In the perceptual study, HUMOTO received the highest absolute scores across all categories (human motion 4.79/5, object motion 4.88/5, interaction quality 4.75/5, overall 4.78/5) and was preferred in 96% of pairwise comparisons against BEHAVE and 82% against ParaHome.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BEHAVE, HUMOTO wins decisively on every metric: foot sliding is 4.8× lower (0.958 vs. 4.556 cm), penetration is 9× lower (0.0068 vs. 0.0606 cm), and object jerk is 9× lower (1.13 vs. 10.40 m/s³); in the perceptual study, 96% of participants preferred HUMOTO overall and 94% for interaction quality. Versus OMOMO, HUMOTO achieves 2.2× lower foot sliding and 24× lower object jerk, though OMOMO has a higher MSNR (12.37 vs. 9.42 dB) which the authors attribute to OMOMO's lack of detailed hand poses. Against IMHD, HUMOTO has 1.5× lower foot sliding and 21× lower object jerk, while IMHD retains a slight edge in human jerk (1.14 vs. 1.87 m/s³) and MSNR (14.20 vs. 9.42 dB); pairwise preference was 46% HUMOTO vs. 50% tie. Compared to ParaHome, HUMOTO shows 3.1× lower foot sliding and 32× lower penetration, though ParaHome's near-zero object jerk (0.08 m/s³) reflects mostly static objects; HUMOTO was preferred 82% of the time in pairwise comparison. Against the Mixamo reference (no object interaction), HUMOTO's MSNR (9.42 dB) is within 1.5 dB, confirming comparable human motion smoothness while adding detailed hand and object data that Mixamo lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUMOTO extends the line of 4D human-object interaction datasets (GRAB, BEHAVE, OMOMO, IMHD, ParaHome) by addressing two persistent gaps: multi-object interactions with detailed hand articulation, and purposeful task sequences rather than isolated motions. Methodologically, it departs from prior camera-only or single-sensor approaches by combining Rokoko EMF mocap suits (for occlusion-robust body and hand tracking) with dual-Kinect RGB-D cameras running FoundationPose (for 6DoF object tracking), and introduces a Scene-Driven LLM Scripting pipeline to generate coherent multi-object task narratives. Unlike prior datasets that rely on 3D scanning for object geometry, HUMOTO uses artist-modeled meshes with articulated parts, trading scan fidelity for part-level interaction accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUMOTO fills a critical data gap for multi-object HOI research by providing 7,875 seconds of clean, purposeful interactions with 63 objects and detailed finger-level hand poses—data that existing datasets lack. The introduced MSNR metric and comprehensive benchmark suite give the field quantitative standards for evaluating motion and interaction quality that were previously absent. Key limitations include a single performer (body-shape bias) and heavy reliance on manual artist cleaning, raising the open question of whether future automated pose estimation can match this quality at scale. The demonstration that state-of-the-art motion generation (MotionGPT) and pose estimation (4D Humans, TRAM) still fail on HUMOTO's multi-object, occlusion-heavy sequences highlights a substantial gap between current model capabilities and the data's complexity.
