## Problem

Next-scale prediction models (VAR, HART, Infinity) generate high-resolution images by progressively refining token maps across multiple stages, but the final high-resolution stages involve thousands of tokens processed in parallel, creating substantial computational overhead. Existing token reduction strategies—merging via clustering or selection via attention scores—are either computationally prohibitive at this token scale or ineffective due to the strong local attention patterns inherent in autoregressive image generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image, operating as a plug-and-play inference-time modification on top of existing next-scale prediction models (Infinity-2B, HART-0.7B). The text prompt is consumed by the standard VAR pipeline: the model progressively predicts discrete token maps at increasing spatial resolutions, and the final accumulated residual feature map is decoded to the image via a VAE decoder. No architectural changes or retraining are required; SparseVAR only alters which tokens participate in computation at each stage.

## Method

SparseVAR dynamically identifies low-frequency tokens at high-resolution stages (starting from stage P=10) by computing the MSE change in features before and after inference at a specific block (block 16), which empirically focuses on high-frequency regions. Tokens whose MSE values fall below a threshold τ·max are excluded from all subsequent computation. To preserve low-frequency region quality, SparseVAR retains a sparse set of anchor tokens uniformly sampled from the top-left corners of α×α grids (α=4); excluded tokens then copy their logits from the most similar anchor via cosine similarity on interpolated previous-stage logits. This requires no additional training and reduces the effective token count at high-resolution stages, yielding up to 2× speedup on Infinity-2B with minimal quality degradation.

## Results

On GenEval, SparseVAR reduces Infinity-2B inference latency from 2.78s to 1.36s (51% reduction) at τ=0.7, with the overall score dropping only from 0.7260 to 0.7209 (−0.0051). For HART-0.7B, SparseVAR achieves a 25% latency reduction (1.32s → 0.99s) at τ=0.6 while the GenEval overall score actually improves marginally (0.5602 → 0.5606). On DPG-Bench, Infinity-2B's overall score decreases by only 0.0033 (0.8289 → 0.8256) at τ=0.7 with a 53% latency reduction. On human preference metrics, Infinity-2B's HPSv2.1 overall score drops by just 0.47 (30.36 → 29.89) and ImageReward by 0.0266 (0.9212 → 0.8946) at τ=0.7, while latency is halved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseVAR is evaluated as a plug-and-play accelerator on two next-scale prediction baselines: Infinity-2B and HART-0.7B. Against Infinity-2B, SparseVAR at τ=0.7 nearly doubles inference speed (2.78s → 1.36s, 51% reduction) while the GenEval overall score drops by only 0.0051 and DPG-Bench by 0.0033, indicating near-lossless acceleration. Against HART-0.7B, SparseVAR at τ=0.6 achieves a 25% latency reduction (1.32s → 0.99s) with the GenEval overall score actually improving slightly (0.5602 → 0.5606), though at the more aggressive τ=0.7 the quality degrades noticeably (0.5602 → 0.5119), suggesting HART's residual diffusion mechanism is more sensitive to token exclusion. On human preference benchmarks, Infinity-2B + SparseVAR (τ=0.7) retains 98.5% of the HPSv2.1 score (29.89 vs. 30.36) and 97.1% of the ImageReward score (0.8946 vs. 0.9212) while halving inference time. Overall, SparseVAR wins on efficiency across both baselines with minimal quality trade-off, though the optimal τ differs: Infinity tolerates τ=0.7 well while HART is best served by τ=0.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseVAR builds directly on the next-scale prediction paradigm introduced by VAR and extended by HART and Infinity, which progressively refine image resolution across stages. It departs from prior token reduction work (FastV, HiRED, ZipVL, ToMe) that relies on attention-score ranking or similarity-based merging, which the authors argue is infeasible or ineffective for autoregressive image generation due to strong local attention dependencies and prohibitive clustering costs at high token counts. Instead, SparseVAR introduces a frequency-aware exclusion strategy grounded in two empirical observations: high-resolution stages contribute negligibly to low-frequency regions, and specific transformer blocks exhibit distinct regional focus that can be exploited via lightweight MSE metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× inference speedup on Infinity-2B with near-negligible quality loss addresses a practical bottleneck that limits the deployment of next-scale prediction models at 1024×1024 resolution, making high-fidelity autoregressive generation more accessible. However, the method is currently scoped to next-scale prediction architectures and uses a uniform anchor sampling strategy that may not adapt to images with heterogeneous low-frequency content. Open questions include whether the frequency-aware exclusion principle transfers to other autoregressive or diffusion-based generation frameworks, and whether adaptive anchor placement could further close the small quality gap at aggressive sparsity levels.
