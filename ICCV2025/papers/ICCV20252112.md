## Problem

Existing vision-based geolocation methods rely on a decoupled retrieve-then-match paradigm where global retrieval dilutes spatially fine-grained patterns and local re-ranking is bottlenecked by limited candidate coverage, while end-to-end matching approaches incur prohibitive computational cost from symmetric processing of large base images. Additionally, remote sensing images exhibit pronounced areal distribution imbalance of terrestrial features, biasing purely visual models toward overrepresented categories and failing under multi-source or multi-temporal discrepancies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small query image and a pre-indexed database of large georeferenced base images (e.g., 5000×5000 px at 1 m resolution on TZC, 5120×5120 px at 0.5 m on MTGL40-5) as input. Base image features are computed offline and stored as a feature database, so at query time only the small image is processed, enabling an asymmetric-input design that avoids cropping large references into overlapping tiles. The output is the single best-matching base image, pixel-level correspondences between the query and the retrieved reference, and a homography matrix H.

## Method

TopicGeo unifies retrieval and matching in a single asymmetric-input pipeline. A topic-aware encoder distills hierarchical textual semantics (category and attribute prompts generated via GPT-4 and encoded by RemoteCLIP) into visual features through cross-attention, then aligns the resulting topic embeddings with frozen RemoteCLIP vision features via weighted KL-divergence distillation, yielding features robust to intra-class and inter-class distribution shifts. Coarse matching uses dual-softmax bidirectional probabilities with a mutual k-nearest-neighbor (MKNN) filter and a learning-free global geometric verification (GGV) module that scores distance and angle compatibility to re-weight per-pair confidences for retrieval. A center-based adaptive label assignment (replacing AdaMatcher's upper-left grid convention) improves robustness under large rotational misalignment, and a multi-level fine-matching pipeline iteratively refines correspondences from 1/64 to 1/16 to 1/4 resolution. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining topic distillation, focal-loss-based retrieval-coarse matching (weighted by topic-derived category frequency), and expectation-based fine-grained matching.

## Results

On the TZC dataset, TopicGeo achieves 98.9% IoU (n=1) and 99.1% IoU (n=4) for matching, outperforming the best prior method RoMa (92.2%/96.1%) by 6.7 and 3.0 percentage points respectively, while running 8.8× faster per match (0.09s vs. 0.79s). On the multi-temporal MTGL40-5 dataset, TopicGeo reaches 91.1%/92.7% IoU versus RoMa's 86.7%/90.2%, with a 2.5× speed advantage. For retrieval, TopicGeo attains 90.6% R@1 on TZC and 91.6% R@1 on MTGL40-5 with only 1.07s and 0.165s latency, compared to AdaMatcher_Coarse's 87.2%/84.6% at 9,700s/450s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopicGeo outperforms all nine baselines on both matching and retrieval metrics across both datasets. Against the strongest detector-free matcher AdaMatcher [20], TopicGeo gains 7.5 pp IoU on TZC (98.9 vs. 91.4) and 7.8 pp on MTGL (91.1 vs. 83.3) at n=1, while also being 1.7× faster. The dense matcher RoMa [12] is the closest competitor on TZC (92.2 vs. 98.9 IoU) but is 8.8× slower per match and trails by 4.4 pp on MTGL. Among retrieval baselines, AdaMatcher_Coarse [20] leads with 87.2% R@1 on TZC, but TopicGeo achieves 90.6% R@1 at 9,000× lower latency (1.07s vs. 9,700s). Global retrieval methods (Patch-NetVLAD [18], SelaVPR [29], EarthLoc [4]) all fall below 71% R@1 on TZC, highlighting the gap between decoupled retrieve-then-match pipelines and the unified framework. Detector-based approaches (SIFT-SuperGlue [28,42], SP-SuperGlue [42]) lag by 14–17 pp IoU on TZC and 14–21 pp on MTGL, confirming that hand-crafted or GNN-based keypoint pipelines struggle with the large scale and resolution discrepancies inherent to geolocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the previously decoupled retrieval-then-matching paradigm for vision-based geolocation into a single asymmetric-input framework, building directly on LoFTR's detector-free cross-attention matching architecture and AdaMatcher's adaptive label assignment strategy. Its key conceptual departure is the distillation of textual object semantics (topics) from RemoteCLIP's prompt embeddings into the visual feature encoder, providing category- and attribute-aware invariance that neither pure visual matchers (LoFTR, RoMa) nor self-supervised topic models (TopicFM) achieve. The multi-level fine matching pipeline and global geometric verification module extend the coarse matching stage with learning-free spatial consistency checks, positioning the work as a systems-level integration of known components rather than a single algorithmic breakthrough.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~1000× reduction in retrieval latency (1.07s vs. 9.7×10³s for AdaMatcher_Coarse) while simultaneously improving recall, making real-time geolocation from large-scale satellite databases feasible. The topic distillation approach offers a principled way to inject human-interpretable semantic priors into matching features, potentially addressing the long-standing challenge of multi-source and multi-temporal invariance in remote sensing. Open questions include scalability to databases with millions of base images, generalization beyond the two relatively small evaluation datasets (980 and 40 scenes), and whether the GPT-4-generated attribute prompts remain effective for land cover categories not present in the training distribution.
