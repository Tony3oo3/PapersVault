## Problem

State-of-the-art video tokenizers like MagViT-v2 are limited to 4× temporal compression, and naively adding more downsampling/upsampling layers to reach 8× or 16× severely degrades reconstruction quality. The paper asks whether a pretrained 4× model can be leveraged to progressively boost temporal compression while keeping the latent channel dimension fixed, avoiding the difficulty of simultaneously optimizing all parameters for high compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252122/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts 17-frame video clips (sampled at 6, 12, or 24 fps for 4×, 8×, and 16× compression respectively) or single images, and encodes them into 1+N latent frames (5, 3, or 2 latent frames respectively) at 8× spatial compression with a fixed latent channel dimension of 8 or 16. Reconstruction is evaluated at 512×512 and 360×640 resolutions, and the compact latents are further used to train a DiT for text-to-video generation at 192×360, where 16× compression reduces the token budget by 2.5× compared to 4×.

## Method

ProMAG progressively grows a well-trained 4× MagViT-v2-based tokenizer to 8× and 16× temporal compression by adding bottleneck downsampling/upsampling blocks while freezing the original 4× encoder and decoder. The key mechanism is a cross-level feature-mixing module: subsampled keyframes are encoded by the frozen 4× encoder to produce key-frame embeddings, and the full-video intermediate latent is conditioned on these embeddings via AdaNorm before passing through the new downsampling blocks, so the new blocks only learn residual information for in-between frames. The final latent is a 1×1×1 convolution of the sum of key and residual embeddings. Additional modifications include removing mean subtraction from group normalization to eliminate spot artifacts, discarding padding frames in temporal upsampling for efficiency, and using a frozen 2D image encoder for the first frame. During progressive growing, only the new blocks and bottleneck layer are trained with L1 and KL losses (no GAN loss), consuming ~2.7× less training time than full-model training.

## Results

At 8× temporal compression with z_dim=16, ProMAG achieves PSNR 32.06, LPIPS 6.94, and rFVD 63.64 on MCL-JCV, substantially outperforming MagViT-v2 (28.51 / 10.62 / 135.57) and Cosmos-CV (31.05 / 11.79 / 101.36). At 16× compression (z_dim=16), ProMAG reaches PSNR 30.02 and rFVD 115.25 on MCL-JCV, again beating directly-trained MagViT-v2 (28.51 / 135.57). For text-to-video generation, DiT trained on ProMAG-16× latents matches or slightly exceeds 4× compression quality on VBench while reducing per-step inference time from 0.99 s to 0.39 s (2.5× speedup) and enabling 340-frame videos within the same 40-latent token budget that yields only 136 frames at 4×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MagViT-v2 (the primary baseline), ProMAG wins decisively at both 8× and 16× compression across all metrics: at 8×/z=16, ProMAG improves PSNR by +3.55 dB and reduces rFVD by 53% on MCL-JCV; at 16×/z=8, the rFVD gap widens to 183 vs. 256 (MCL-JCV) and 608 vs. 728 (DAVIS). Against Cosmos-CV (the only external baseline with 8× temporal compression), ProMAG at 8×/z=16 achieves higher PSNR (32.06 vs. 31.05), lower LPIPS (6.94 vs. 11.79), and lower rFVD (63.64 vs. 101.36) on MCL-JCV, though Cosmos-CV retains a PSNR edge on DAVIS (28.27 vs. 27.18). At the base 4× compression (Table 1), ProMAG at z_dim=16 matches or slightly exceeds SOTA tokenizers including VidTok, WF-VAE, Wan, and Hunyuan Video on LPIPS and rFVD, while CogVideoX leads on PSNR (32.57 vs. 32.94 for ProMAG on MCL-JCV). The paper also shows that simply adding bottleneck layers without the AdaNorm-guided residual mechanism (progressive training) yields intermediate gains (PSNR 30.35 vs. 32.06 for full ProMAG at 8×), confirming that the key-frame conditioning is essential for the full benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MagViT-v2's 3D causal convolution video tokenizer, adapting it into a continuous-space variant (ProMAG) with modifications for efficiency and artifact reduction. The core methodological idea—progressively growing a model by adding new blocks atop a frozen pretrained one—is borrowed from ProGAN's progressive GAN training, but repurposed here for temporal compression in video tokenizers rather than spatial resolution in generation. It departs from prior high-compression VAE work (DC-AE, UltraPixel, MovieGen, Cosmos) by keeping the latent channel dimension fixed while increasing temporal compression, and from direct-training approaches by introducing a cross-level feature-mixing mechanism (AdaNorm-conditioned residual embeddings) that lets new bottleneck blocks reuse information from the pretrained lower-compression encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 16× temporal compression with competitive reconstruction quality and, crucially, without degrading downstream DiT text-to-video generation quality is a practical milestone: it enables 2.5× longer videos or 2.5× faster generation at fixed token budgets, which is directly relevant to production video generation systems. The finding that the difficulty of high-compression training lies in simultaneously optimizing many downsampling layers rather than in the information bottleneck itself offers a generalizable insight for other compression tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that very high and abrupt motion (e.g., sports) still produces temporal artifacts, and the progressive growing framework has only been validated on a single architecture family (3D causal convolutions), leaving open whether it transfers to transformer-based tokenizers or to spatial compression.
