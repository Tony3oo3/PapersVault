## Problem

Diffusion-based image generation (e.g., ControlNet) produces photorealistic results but lacks the explicit, precise control over geometry, material, and lighting that rendering engines provide. Conversely, synthetic rendering pipelines offer exact intrinsic control but cannot match the photorealism of data-driven models. A further challenge is the distribution gap between synthetic datasets (with accurate intrinsic pairs) and real-world photos (with photorealism but no intrinsic ground truth), which causes domain confusion when naively blended during training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any arbitrary combination of intrinsic images (albedo, normal, depth, metallicity, roughness, semantic segmentation) selected via a matrix switch, an HDR panoramic environment map downsampled to 128×64 and split into HDR/LDR components for lighting control, and a text prompt. All images are processed at 512×512 resolution. The intrinsic inputs are partitioned into a material group (albedo, roughness, metallicity) and a geometry group (normal, depth, segmentation), each routed to a dedicated ControlNet branch, while the lighting condition spans both branches.

## Method

IntrinsicControlNet employs a dual-control architecture with two separate ControlNet branches—one for material and one for geometry—connected by feature cross-injection at higher network layers to preserve each branch's focus while sharing complementary information. A matrix switch enables arbitrary selection of intrinsic modalities, and a tailored encoder using residual blocks (replacing ControlNet's standard convolutions) extracts condition features while preserving fine details. To bridge the synthetic–real distribution gap, a realistic discriminator with two classification heads (target/generated and synthetic/realistic) is trained adversarially, pushing the material branch toward photorealism and the geometry branch toward structural accuracy. Lighting control is achieved through weakly supervised training: real HDR panoramic images from Poly Haven are inverse-cropped using ITTI saliency to produce matched RGB–environment-map pairs, avoiding the bias of predicted panoramas. The diffusion backbone uses V-prediction with Zero Terminal SNR for consistent training–inference behavior.

## Results

On the aggregate FID metric, IntrinsicControlNet achieves 26.40, substantially outperforming Multi-ControlNet (48.69), RGB↔X (42.90), and Ctrl-X (62.63). In user studies, the proposed method receives overwhelming preference (e.g., 100% on Carpark, 89.3% on Car) while baselines score near 0%. On LPIPS, the method wins on 3 of 4 scenes (Carpark 0.206, Bedroom 0.320, Car 0.583) but is slightly outperformed by RGB↔X on Walkway (0.210 vs. 0.177). CLIP score is also best at 0.293 versus 0.283, 0.270, and 0.278 for the three baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntrinsicControlNet decisively outperforms all three quantitative baselines on FID (26.40 vs. 48.69/42.90/62.63) and CLIP (0.293 vs. 0.283/0.270/0.278), confirming superior overall image quality and semantic alignment. Against Multi-ControlNet, the method wins on every LPIPS scene and dominates user-study preference (e.g., 100% vs. 0% on Carpark). Against RGB↔X, the method wins on FID, CLIP, and 3 of 4 LPIPS scenes, though RGB↔X retains a narrow LPIPS edge on the Walkway scene (0.177 vs. 0.210); in user studies, IntrinsicControlNet is preferred 85.7% of the time on Bedroom versus only 10.7% for RGB↔X. Ctrl-X is the weakest baseline, trailing on all metrics by large margins (FID 62.63, LPIPS up to 0.692). For object insertion, qualitative comparison with AnyDoor shows IntrinsicControlNet produces more scene-consistent lighting (highlights and shadows), though no quantitative metric is reported for that specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet framework (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) and Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) by introducing a dual-branch architecture that separates material and geometry conditioning, addressing a cross-distribution problem that prior intrinsic-conditioned generation methods (notably RGB↔X, Zeng et al., 2024) do not handle. While RGB↔X generates images from intrinsic buffers, it is trained solely on synthetic data and produces outputs with a synthetic appearance bias. IntrinsicControlNet departs from this by jointly training on synthetic and real-world data through a material-geometry split, a matrix switch for arbitrary condition selection, and a GAN-based realistic discriminator to close the photorealism gap. The weakly supervised lighting control via HDR environment maps (using StyleLight predictions and inverse cropping from Poly Haven) also distinguishes it from prior shading-image-based lighting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-distribution formulation is practically important for applications that mix real photographs with 3D assets (object insertion, scene compositing, relighting), where prior methods produce visible domain mismatches. The paper demonstrates that separating material and geometry into distinct ControlNet branches, combined with adversarial realism training, is a viable recipe for bridging the synthetic-to-real gap in diffusion-based generation. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in temporal stability, fine-detail control, and offline-only processing speed, leaving open whether the approach can extend to video or real-time interactive use. A key open question is whether the dual-branch design generalizes beyond the specific intrinsic modalities tested, and whether the GAN discriminator component remains necessary as base diffusion models improve in photorealism.
