## Problem

Training 3D diffusion models requires both noisy samples and supervision in the same 3D modality, but large-scale 3D ground truth is scarce. Deterministic feedforward 3D reconstruction models can operate from 2D images alone but produce blurry outputs because they cannot capture the full distribution of plausible 3D structures. The paper addresses the underexplored gap of training 3D diffusion models using only 2D image supervision by decoupling the source of noisy 3D samples from the 2D supervision signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251435/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB source image (128×128 on ShapeNet-SRN, 256×384 on RealEstate10K) and optionally a second view for guidance during inference. It outputs a set of N 3D Gaussian Splats, each parameterized by center, covariance, opacity, and color, which can be differentially rendered to arbitrary novel views. Training supervision comes from k additional target views of the same content, with no 3D ground truth required at any point.

## Method

The core insight is that at noise levels t > t*, adding Gaussian noise to a deterministic teacher's imperfect 3DGS prediction makes it statistically indistinguishable from noise added to the true (unavailable) 3D structure, inspired by SDEdit. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a bootstrapping phase uses the noisy teacher's 3DGS predictions directly as 3D supervision alongside rendered 2D image losses with standard single-step denoising; second, a multi-step denoising fine-tuning phase unrolls the denoiser iteratively from t > t* down to t=0 (using DDIM with 10 steps), supervising only through differentiable rendering of the final clean prediction to target views, thereby propagating gradients to lower timesteps without requiring 3D ground truth. A cycle consistency loss further regularizes both stages by rendering the predicted target view, feeding it back through the teacher (or the model itself in stage 2) to reconstruct the source view, and comparing against the original input image.

## Results

On ShapeNet-SRN single-view reconstruction, SplatDiffusion (Medium) achieves 24.84 PSNR / 0.93 SSIM / 0.077 LPIPS on Cars and 25.21 PSNR / 0.93 SSIM / 0.066 LPIPS on Chairs, outperforming the strongest baselines (Splatter Image Large at 24.00/24.43 PSNR and NeRFDiff at 23.95/24.80 PSNR) by 0.41–0.84 PSNR. On RealEstate10K novel view synthesis, the method surpasses its teacher Flash3D by an average of ~0.5 PSNR across three evaluation settings (5, 10, and U[-30,30] frames), reaching 29.12 PSNR on the 5-frame setting versus Flash3D's 28.46. Notably, the diffusion model uses a smaller U-Net (295 MB, 1.15 GB memory) than the teacher Splatter Image Large (646 MB, 1.71 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatDiffusion outperforms all 13 baselines on both Cars and Chairs splits of ShapeNet-SRN. The largest gains are over the deterministic teacher Splatter Image (Large): +0.84 PSNR on Cars (24.84 vs 24.00) and +0.78 PSNR on Chairs (25.21 vs 24.43). Against the strongest generative baseline NeRFDiff, the improvement is +0.89 PSNR on Cars (24.84 vs 23.95) and +0.41 PSNR on Chairs (25.21 vs 24.80). On RealEstate10K, SplatDiffusion beats Flash3D (its scene-level teacher) by 0.66, 0.60, and 0.47 PSNR on the 5-frame, 10-frame, and U[-30,30] settings respectively, and also surpasses MINE, Splatter Image, SV-MPI, BTS, and Syn-Sin across all settings. The method also achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS in nearly every comparison, indicating superior perceptual quality beyond pixel-level fidelity. A key distinction is that SplatDiffusion uses a smaller model (Medium, 295 MB) than its teacher (Large, 646 MB) while still outperforming it, validating the diffusion training framework rather than mere scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and its differentiable rendering pipeline, using deterministic feedforward predictors (Splatter Image, Flash3D) as 'noisy teachers' to generate 3D training samples. It departs from standard diffusion training—where noisy and clean signals must share a modality—by decoupling the source of noisy 3D samples (the teacher) from the 2D image supervision, inspired by SDEdit's observation that sufficient noise aligns distributions. The closest prior work, Holodiffusion, also trains 3D diffusion with 2D supervision but relies on an additional denoising pass to bridge the distribution gap, whereas this paper uses a multi-step denoising strategy during training to propagate gradients to lower noise levels. The Universal Guidance framework is adopted for incorporating optional additional views during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a fundamental bottleneck in 3D generative modeling: the scarcity of large-scale 3D ground-truth data. By showing that imperfect deterministic predictions, when noised beyond a critical threshold, serve as valid diffusion training inputs, the paper opens a path to scaling 3D diffusion models using only widely available 2D image data. However, the current implementation is tied to pixel-aligned 3DGS, inheriting limitations such as uneven Gaussian coverage in occluded regions that cause oversmoothness in novel views. Open questions include whether the noisy-teacher paradigm generalizes to other 3D representations (NeRF, meshes, point clouds), whether it scales to more complex open-world scenes, and whether the multi-step unrolling can be made more computationally efficient for larger models.
