## Problem

SAM produces coarse segmentation masks that lack the fine-grained boundary precision required for matting (e.g., individual hair strands). Existing matting extensions of SAM fine-tune on public datasets containing only macro-level labels (e.g., entire portraits), causing catastrophic forgetting of micro-level generalization. No prior model simultaneously achieves precise matte output and robust zero-shot generalization across both fine-grained and coarse-grained objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251352/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ZIM takes a 1024×1024 RGB image together with visual prompts—either positive/negative point pairs or a bounding box—and produces a per-pixel alpha matte (soft transparency in [0,1]) at stride-2 resolution. The model also supports an automatic mask generation mode without explicit prompts. Unlike hard segmentation labels, the output encodes sub-pixel boundary softness essential for compositing and inpainting.

## Method

ZIM has two core components. First, a Label Converter (based on MGMatting with a Hierabase-plus backbone) transforms coarse SA1B segmentation labels into high-fidelity matte labels, trained with Spatial Generalization Augmentation (random cut-out to improve generalization to unseen micro-level objects) and Selective Transformation Learning (teaching the converter to skip fine-grained conversion for coarse objects like cars), yielding the SA1B-Matte dataset. Second, the ZIM architecture extends SAM's ViT-B backbone with a Hierarchical Pixel Decoder that fuses multi-resolution feature maps at strides 2, 4, and 8 to eliminate checkerboard artifacts and capture fine structures, plus a Prompt-Aware Masked Attention mechanism that injects binary masks for box prompts and 2D Gaussian soft masks for point prompts into the token-to-image cross-attention layers, directing the model's focus to prompt-specified regions. ZIM is fine-tuned on 1% of SA1B-Matte (~2.2M matte labels) using L1 + gradient loss.

## Results

On the newly introduced MicroMat-3K test set, ZIM achieves a box-prompt SAD of 9.961 and MSE of 1.893 on fine-grained masks, substantially outperforming the next-best method Matte-Any (SAD 34.661, MSE 9.746) and SAM (SAD 36.086, MSE 11.057). On coarse-grained masks, ZIM reaches SAD 1.860 and MSE 0.448, again surpassing SAM (SAD 3.516, MSE 1.044) and Matte-Any (SAD 6.950, MSE 1.983). The gradient error on fine-grained masks drops to 4.813 for ZIM versus 7.021 for Matte-Any and 14.867 for SAM, confirming superior boundary precision. ZIM also outperforms all baselines on the traditional matting benchmarks AIM-500 and P3M-500NP when using dense multi-point prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZIM dominates all four SAM-based matting baselines on MicroMat-3K. Against SAM [18], ZIM reduces fine-grained box-prompt SAD from 36.086 to 9.961 (72% reduction) and coarse-grained SAD from 3.516 to 1.860 (47% reduction), while also cutting gradient error from 14.867 to 4.813 on fine-grained masks. Against Matte-Any [50], the closest competitor, ZIM improves fine-grained SAD by 3.5× (34.661 → 9.961) and coarse-grained SAD by 3.7× (6.950 → 1.860). HQ-SAM [13] and Matting-Any [25] suffer severe zero-shot degradation on fine-grained masks (SAD 124.262 and 246.214 respectively), confirming that fine-tuning on macro-level matting datasets causes catastrophic forgetting of micro-level generalization. On traditional matting benchmarks (AIM-500, P3M-500NP), ZIM with dense multi-point prompting surpasses GFM [23], AIM [21], and SMat [51], even though some of these methods are explicitly trained on similar evaluation domains, demonstrating ZIM's adaptability despite a domain shift from object-level to part-level matting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZIM builds directly on SAM's architecture (ViT-B image encoder, prompt encoder, transformer decoder) but departs from prior matting extensions (Matte-Any, Matting-Any, HQ-SAM) that fine-tune SAM on macro-level matting datasets, which the paper argues causes catastrophic forgetting of micro-level generalization. Instead, ZIM introduces a label converter (based on MGMatting with a Hierabase-plus backbone) to synthesize micro-level matte labels from SA1B's segmentation annotations, sidestepping the need for costly manual matting annotations. Architecturally, it replaces SAM's two-layer transposed convolution pixel decoder with a hierarchical multi-resolution feature pyramid and adds a prompt-aware masked attention mechanism inspired by Mask2Former, targeting the checkerboard artifacts and insufficient spatial resolution of the original decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SA1B-Matte dataset (micro-level matte labels at SA1B scale) and the MicroMat-3K benchmark fill a critical gap: prior matting datasets contain only macro-level labels, making it impossible to properly evaluate or train zero-shot matting at fine granularity. The paper's honest discussion of domain shift—where ZIM underperforms on traditional whole-object matting test sets with box prompts but recovers with dense point prompting—highlights an open tension between object-level and part-level matting that the field has not yet resolved. Inherited limitations from SAM (prompt ambiguity, uncertainty in predictions) remain unresolved, and the authors explicitly call for future work on prompt design and uncertainty-based modeling to address these.
