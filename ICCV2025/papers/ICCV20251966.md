## Problem

Existing bokeh rendering methods either require depth maps and multi-stage pipelines or rely on the limited EBB! dataset, which offers only a single f-stop and suffers from poor alignment and low image quality. This leaves no real-world dataset with varying aperture and focal length settings, and no efficient end-to-end method that provides intuitive, continuous control over bokeh strength while faithfully reproducing the complex PSF of real lenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image captured at a small aperture (e.g., f/22.0) and a scalar f-stop value specifying the desired output aperture, producing a bokeh-rendered image at that aperture without any depth map, segmentation mask, or other auxiliary input. Training and evaluation use 24-megapixel (6000×4000) images from the RealBokeh dataset, with benchmark evaluation performed at 2000×1500 resolution. The f-stop input is encoded via a simple ratio (f_max / f_out) that models the fractional diameter of a physical lens aperture.

## Method

Bokehlicious is a lightweight CNN en-/decoder (based on NAFNet blocks) containing Residual Groups of Aperture Attention Blocks, each embedding an Aperture Aware Attention (AAA) mechanism. AAA extends Manhattan Self-Attention by modulating the 2D Manhattan decay mask rate γ per attention head, where γ is further tuned by the Aperture Encoding so that the receptive field width physically mirrors the lens aperture size for the target f-stop. Each head receives a distinct decay scale, allowing parallel processing of different blur kernel sizes, while the f-stop-dependent modulation ensures the overall attention extent matches the requested aperture. The model is trained end-to-end with an L1 + LPIPS_VGG loss (λ=0.6) on paired small/large-aperture images, requiring only 1.21M parameters (M version) and 5.93 GMACs, substantially less than competing methods.

## Results

On the new RealBokeh_bin benchmark, Bokehlicious-M (1.21M params, 5.93 GMACs) achieves PSNR 30.19, SSIM 0.9288, and LPIPS 0.1126, outperforming the best Bokeh-specific method DMSHN (PSNR 29.18) and the best general IR method Restormer (PSNR 29.02) while using roughly 24× fewer GMACs than Restormer. The scaled-up Bokehlicious-L (13.96M params) further improves to PSNR 31.02, SSIM 0.9342, LPIPS 0.1074. On the aperture-control benchmark (Table 5), Ours-M achieves PSNR 33.05 across all f-stops in 0.28 s, surpassing BokehMe (PSNR 29.01, 0.41 s) and Dr.Bokeh (PSNR 27.75, 22.69 s) by 4+ dB. On the RealDOF defocus deblurring benchmark, Ours-L (Deblur) reaches PSNR 25.86, SSIM 0.797, LPIPS 0.205, edging out DRBNet (PSNR 25.75, LPIPS 0.257).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bokehlicious-M outperforms all five general image-restoration baselines (GRL, SwinIR, MambaIR, NAFNet, Restormer) on RealBokeh_bin by 1.2–2.3 dB PSNR, despite using 1.21M parameters versus 0.91–26.13M for the others; notably, it achieves this with only 5.93 GMACs compared to 141.24 for Restormer. Among dedicated Bokeh methods, Ours-M beats DMSHN (29.18→30.19 PSNR), PyNET (28.75→30.19), BRViT (27.82→30.19), and D2F (25.89→30.19) on the same benchmark. In the aperture-control setting (Table 5), Ours-M surpasses BokehMe by 4.03 dB PSNR (33.05 vs 29.01) while running 1.5× faster (0.28 s vs 0.41 s), and outperforms Dr.Bokeh by 5.30 dB with 81× lower inference time (0.28 s vs 22.69 s). On EBB400, Ours-M leads BokehMe by 0.62 dB PSNR (24.47 vs 23.85). For defocus deblurring on RealDOF, Ours-L (Deblur) edges out DRBNet by 0.11 dB PSNR and 0.052 LPIPS, and surpasses IFANet by 1.15 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bokehlicious builds directly on Manhattan Self-Attention (MaSA) from RMT [13], repurposing its 2D-Manhattan decay mask as a physically interpretable aperture control mechanism, and on NAFNet [7] for a lightweight encoder-decoder backbone. It departs from prior end-to-end bokeh methods (PyNET, DMSHN, BRViT) that implicitly learn the bokeh kernel from fixed-aperture pairs, by making the attention receptive field explicitly modulated by an f-stop encoding. It also contrasts with multi-stage approaches (BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, MPIB) that achieve aperture control but require depth maps and hand-crafted PSF models, positioning itself as the first end-to-end framework with intuitive, parameter-free aperture control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealBokeh dataset (23,000 24-MP pairs with varying f-stops and focal lengths) fills a critical gap that has constrained the field since EBB!'s single-aperture, low-alignment limitations, and will likely become the standard benchmark for controllable bokeh rendering. The aperture-aware attention mechanism demonstrates that a simple physical prior (decay-rate modulation) can outperform more complex implicit learning while reducing parameter count by ~40× over prior SOTA, suggesting that explicit physical structure is more sample-efficient than data-driven kernel learning for this task. Key limitations remain: the method cannot reassign the focus subject (always aligned with the input's optical focus), and the refocusing application via BokehMe integration is preliminary, leaving true single-image refocusing as an open problem.
