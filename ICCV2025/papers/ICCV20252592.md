## Problem

Existing safe semi-supervised learning methods rely on confidence-based pseudo-labeling and OOD detection to reject unseen-class unlabeled data, but deep CNNs are systematically overconfident, causing incorrect pseudo-labels to be accepted and unseen-class instances to be misclassified as seen. No prior work has addressed the joint calibration of both the multiclass classifier and the OOD detector during SSL training, leaving a gap where miscalibrated confidence undermines the reliability of both pseudo-label selection and OOD rejection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset of RGB images with class labels and an unlabeled dataset of RGB images that may contain instances from unseen classes (30% or 60% unseen proportion in experiments). It produces two outputs per unlabeled sample: a multiclass prediction over K known classes and a seen-class score s_i^u (with OOD score u_i^u = 1 − s_i^u) computed as the sum of element-wise products of calibrated classifier and OOD-detector probabilities. The model architecture is a shared CNN encoder (Wide ResNet-28-2 for CIFAR/SVHN/TinyImageNet, ResNet-50 for ImageNet) feeding into a multiclass classifier and a one-versus-rest OOD detector.

## Method

CaliMatch extends FixMatch with an OOD detector and introduces two calibration mechanisms applied during training: (1) adaptive label smoothing, where the smoothing degree for each instance is set to 1 − γ_i (or 1 − δ_i for the OOD detector), with γ_i determined by the empirical accuracy of the model within the confidence bin containing that instance's current prediction confidence; and (2) learnable temperature scaling parameters T_M and T_O (initialized at 1.5) applied to the logits of the multiclass classifier and OOD detector respectively. After a warm-up phase, two additional calibration losses (L_MCal and L_OCal) are added to the standard cross-entropy, OOD binary cross-entropy, and soft consistency losses, driving the scaled probabilities toward the per-instance reference accuracies. Unlabeled samples are selected for FixMatch consistency regularization only if their calibrated seen-class score exceeds τ₁ = 0.5 AND their calibrated confidence exceeds τ₂ = 0.95, ensuring both OOD rejection and pseudo-label quality. A theoretical analysis shows that reducing overconfidence in both components brings the FixMatch gradient closer to the ideal scenario with no incorrect pseudo-labels and no OOD contamination.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet) with 30% and 60% unseen-label data, CaliMatch achieves the highest top-1 accuracy in 7 out of 8 settings, e.g., 96.81% on SVHN (30%), 72.32% on CIFAR-100 (30%), and 47.46% on TinyImageNet (30%). On the large-scale ImageNet dataset with 60% unseen classes, CaliMatch reaches 63.04% top-1 accuracy, outperforming OpenMatch by 1.24% and the supervised baseline by 6.34%. CaliMatch also achieves the lowest ECE across all datasets (e.g., 0.006 on SVHN, 0.025 on CIFAR-100 for classification; 0.036 on SVHN, 0.114 on CIFAR-100 for OOD detection), confirming superior calibration alongside accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaliMatch outperforms all seven baselines on 7 of 8 dataset–κ combinations; the sole exception is CIFAR-10 at 30% unseen data, where IOMatch edges ahead by 0.05% (90.30 vs. 90.25). Against the closest safe-SSL competitor OpenMatch, CaliMatch gains 0.19% on SVHN (30%), 2.67% on CIFAR-100 (30%), and 0.56% on TinyImageNet (30%), with the gap widening to 5.84% on CIFAR-100 (60%). Compared to the non-safe FixMatch, CaliMatch improves by 1.23% on CIFAR-100 (60%) and 0.64% on TinyImageNet (60%), while on SVHN (60%) both methods tie at 96.56%. Against ADELLO (the only calibration-based LTSSL baseline), CaliMatch matches on SVHN (60%) at 96.56% but leads by 3.76% on CIFAR-100 (30%) and 2.74% on CIFAR-100 (60%). On ImageNet (60% unseen), CaliMatch surpasses OpenMatch by 1.24% in accuracy and reduces ECE from 0.041 to 0.028 (classification) and from 0.121 to 0.070 (OOD detection), confirming that the calibration advantage scales to large-scale settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaliMatch extends the FixMatch consistency-regularization framework and the OpenMatch safe-SSL formulation (which adds an OOD rejection mechanism) by introducing adaptive label smoothing and learnable temperature scaling to calibrate both the multiclass classifier and the one-versus-rest OOD detector during training. Unlike prior safe SSL methods (MTC, SafeStudent, IOMatch, SCOMatch) that rely on raw confidence and OOD scores for thresholding, CaliMatch argues these scores are unreliable due to deep CNN overconfidence and instead aligns predicted confidences to empirically measured accuracy per confidence bin. It also departs from standard post-hoc calibration (temperature scaling) and fixed-degree label smoothing by making the smoothing degree adaptive per instance and the temperature a learnable parameter, eliminating manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that overconfidence degrades safe SSL through two distinct channels—poor pseudo-label quality and unreliable OOD rejection—and that calibrating both components is necessary for robust thresholding-based SSL. The adaptive calibration mechanism is practical (no manual smoothing-degree tuning) and the gains are consistent across five datasets including ImageNet, though the margins over OpenMatch are modest (1–3%). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to transformer-based SSL backbones, whether fixed thresholds τ₁ and τ₂ remain optimal under more extreme label distribution shifts, and whether the shared-encoder interaction between classifier and OOD detector (which causes dataset-dependent ablation effects) can be better understood or decoupled.
