## Problem

Existing video stereo matching methods either rely on auxiliary cues like camera poses or optical flow, which degrade under complex camera motion and dynamic object deformation, or are image-based approaches that produce temporally inconsistent disparities with noticeable flickering. The paper addresses how to achieve accurate and temporally consistent stereo matching in dynamic scenes without depending on these unreliable auxiliary inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250891/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified stereo video sequence of T frames at H×W×3 resolution (randomly cropped to 256×512 during training, with T=5 for training and T=20 for evaluation) and outputs a temporally consistent sequence of disparity maps aligned with the left view. The disparity maps can be directly converted into globally aligned metric point clouds for 3D reconstruction. No camera intrinsics, poses, or optical flow are required as additional inputs.

## Method

The architecture integrates a frozen Video Depth Anything (VDA) encoder via a shallow convolutional adapter to provide temporally stable foundation priors alongside trainable convolutional feature and context encoders. For correspondence matching, an all-to-all-pair correlation computes similarity between every pair of potential matching points within two search windows (rather than a fixed one-directional lookup), producing a denser and more robust cost volume that is compacted by an MLP. A 3D-GRU with temporal and spatial attention iteratively refines disparities across spatial and temporal dimensions, and a temporal convex upsampling layer computes each high-resolution pixel as a weighted combination of 27 low-resolution neighbors spanning three adjacent frames. The full pipeline operates in a cascaded 1/16→1/8→1/4 resolution scheme with N=10 (training) or N=20 (evaluation) GRU iterations, trained purely with an L1 loss on SceneFlow and finetuned on Dynamic Replica, Infinigen SV, and Virtual KITTI2.

## Results

In zero-shot generalization (trained only on SceneFlow), Stereo Any Video achieves the best TEPE on Sintel Final (1.07 vs. 1.26 for BiDAStereo) and Dynamic Replica (0.057 vs. 0.080 for DEFOM-Stereo), and the best δ₁px on Sintel (9.98) and Dynamic Replica (0.67). On Virtual KITTI2, it achieves TEPE 0.74 and EPE 1.01, narrowly trailing MonSter (0.73 / 0.67). When allowed to train on mixed datasets (Table 3), the method reaches TEPE 0.77 on Spring and 0.99 on Sintel Final, outperforming all image-based and video-based baselines by at least 15% on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest video-based baseline BiDAStereo, Stereo Any Video wins on all three Sintel metrics (TEPE 1.07 vs 1.26, δ₁px 9.98 vs 11.65, EPE 1.75 vs 1.99) and on Dynamic Replica TEPE/δ₁px (0.057/0.67 vs 0.091/1.04), while BiDAStereo retains a large EPE advantage on Dynamic Replica (0.65 vs 0.25). Against the strongest image-based baseline MonSter, the gap is narrower: MonSter leads on Virtual KITTI2 (TEPE 0.73 vs 0.74, EPE 0.67 vs 1.01) and Infinigen SV EPE (2.27 vs 3.16), but Stereo Any Video matches MonSter on Infinigen SV TEPE (both 1.65) and wins on δ₁px (14.27 vs 15.73). On Dynamic Replica, Stereo Any Video achieves the best TEPE (0.057) and δ₁px (0.67) across all methods, though DEFOM-Stereo retains the best EPE (0.13 vs 0.25). Overall, the method sets new state-of-the-art on Sintel and Dynamic Replica temporal consistency metrics while remaining competitive on spatial accuracy, with the largest margins over image-based methods (e.g., 1.5× TEPE improvement over RAFT-Stereo on Sintel).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video stereo matching lineage (CODD, DynamicStereo, BiDAStereo) by eliminating the dependency on auxiliary cues such as camera poses or optical flow, instead drawing temporal stability from a frozen monocular video depth foundation model (Video Depth Anything). Architecturally, it builds on the cascaded recurrent paradigm of RAFT-Stereo and the 3D-GRU with temporal/spatial attention from DynamicStereo, while introducing two novel components: an all-to-all-pair correlation inspired by point-tracking literature, and a temporal convex upsampling that generalizes RAFT's spatial convex upsampling to the 3D (spatial + temporal) domain. The use of foundation-model features as a plug-in prior follows the recent trend established by FoundationStereo and MonSter, but is the first to exploit a *video* depth model's temporal alignment mechanism for stereo consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization across diverse synthetic and real-world dynamic scenes without requiring camera poses or optical flow, this work removes a key practical bottleneck for deploying video stereo in uncontrolled environments. The finding that a purely image-based L1 loss suffices for strong temporal consistency (obviating dedicated temporal-consistency losses) simplifies training pipelines. However, the high computational cost (303.4T MACs, ~67% more than DynamicStereo) and the 28.4M frozen VDA parameters limit real-time applicability; the authors acknowledge the need for a lightweight model variant. Open questions include whether the foundation-prior approach generalizes to longer sequences or more extreme domain shifts, and whether the all-to-all-pair correlation can be made more efficient at larger search ranges.
