## Problem

Existing hallucination-mitigation methods for LVLMs primarily reinforce unimodal information (e.g., contrasting masked vs. original images) during decoding, but the role of multimodal *interactions*—how image and text jointly shape token decisions—remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether LVLMs implicitly capture such interactions, where in the response they matter, and whether explicitly leveraging them can reduce hallucinations.

## Input / Output

INTER operates as a plug-and-play decoding modification on top of any LVLM: it takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a generated text response (VQA answer or image caption). During autoregressive generation, the method requires four forward passes per token step (original inputs, image-only, text-only, and empty) to compute the Harsanyi dividend interaction contribution for each candidate token in the vocabulary, adding moderate computational overhead to standard decoding.

## Method

INTER is a training-free sampling strategy built on the Harsanyi dividend from cooperative game theory, which decomposes the logit for each candidate token into unimodal (image-only, text-only) and interaction (joint image-text) contributions. The method has two components: (1) the Interactive Guided Locator (IGL) computes the variance of interaction contributions across all candidate tokens at each step; if this variance exceeds a threshold k, the token is flagged as a 'keyword' where multimodal interactions are actively guiding the decision. (2) The Interaction Probability Modifier (IPM) adds the interaction contribution vector I(A) directly to the original logits before softmax, but only for flagged keyword tokens (controlled by a binary indicator β). This selectively amplifies tokens whose selection is most influenced by image-text joint reasoning, suppressing hallucinated content while preserving linguistic coherence in connective tokens.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, INTER achieves a maximum mean F1-score improvement of 7.5% (LLaVA-v1.5 7B: 79.7→85.7 on COCO with Nucleus Sampling). On CHAIR, INTER reduces sentence-level hallucination (C_S) by up to 34.6% and image-level hallucination (C_I) by up to 18.9% compared to baselines. On MME, INTER yields a 343.7-point absolute improvement in total score over Nucleus Sampling, with average gains of 4.1% on CHAIR and 2.6% on MME across five LVLMs. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no additional data or model modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INTER consistently outperforms all four baselines across models and decoding strategies. Against Nucleus Sampling, INTER yields the largest gains (up to +6.0 F1 on LLaVA-v1.5 7B COCO), confirming that interaction-guided sampling is more effective than unguided sampling. Against Beam Search, improvements are smaller but still positive (e.g., +2.7 on InstructBLIP COCO), suggesting Beam Search already partially captures interaction effects. Critically, Nucleus+INTER (85.7) surpasses VCD (84.5) on LLaVA-v1.5 7B COCO, and Beam+INTER (85.5) surpasses OPERA (85.3), demonstrating that multimodal interaction guidance outperforms uni-modal contrastive/penalty approaches. On CHAIR, OPERA†+INTER achieves the lowest C_S (42.2) and C_I (13.6) at 512 tokens for InstructBLIP, outperforming all other methods. The one notable exception is Qwen-VL AOKVQA where OPERA†+INTER drops 0.7 below OPERA† alone, indicating minor instability on certain model-benchmark combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of hallucination-mitigation work (VCD, OPERA, and related contrastive decoding methods) that reinforces LVLMs' attention to unimodal inputs (visual or textual) by instead targeting the cross-modal interaction term. It repurposes the Harsanyi dividend from cooperative game theory—previously applied to DNN interpretability and adversarial robustness—as a tool to explicitly decompose and quantify image-text interaction contributions in the decoding logit space. The key conceptual shift is treating the LVLM inference as a two-player game (image, prompt) and using the interaction dividend as a prior to guide sampling, rather than contrasting against perturbed unimodal inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INTER is a training-free, plug-and-play decoding modification that requires no additional data or model retraining, making it immediately deployable on any LVLM. The empirical finding that LVLMs already implicitly encode multimodal interactions (albeit weakly and concentrated on a few key tokens) reframes hallucination as an under-leveraging problem rather than a missing-capability problem, suggesting future work could focus on amplifying existing internal signals. Open questions include whether the two-player game-theoretic formulation generalizes to more than two modalities (e.g., video, audio), whether the variance-based keyword locator remains reliable for longer or more open-ended generations, and whether the modest 3.4% average gain scales with model size or task complexity.
