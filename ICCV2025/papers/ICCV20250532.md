## Problem

Symmetry detection in natural images remains challenging due to diverse patterns, noise, and the scarcity of large annotated symmetry datasets. Existing deep learning approaches either lack principled equivariance guarantees (PMCNet) or rely solely on equivariant CNNs trained from scratch on limited data (EquiSym), leaving the potential of large-scale pre-trained vision-language models unexplored. The paper asks whether CLIP's representations, trained on internet-scale image-text pairs where ~10% of captions contain shape/symmetry-related words, can be leveraged to improve symmetry detection.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image, resized to 417×417 (aspect-ratio preserved with padding) to match CLIP ViT-B/16's patch grid, and produces a per-pixel symmetry heatmap in [0,1]^{H×W} where each value encodes the probability of that pixel lying on a reflection axis or at a rotation center. A fixed set of 25 text prompts (each containing 4 frequent object-class words, e.g., 'apple cloud table') is fed through CLIP's text encoder and shared across all images, providing a universal semantic anchor for the abstract concept of symmetry.

## Method

CLIPSym fine-tunes CLIP's ViT-B/16 image and text encoders and introduces a rotation-equivariant decoder with three stages: (1) a FiLM block that modulates image patch tokens with text-conditioned scale/shift parameters, (2) a multi-head Transformer that learns global spatial dependencies, and (3) a steerable G-Conv upsampler operating on the roto-translation group Z_M² ⋊ C_n that provides a provable C_4 rotation-equivariance guarantee. The key prompting technique, SAPG, groups multiple frequent object-class words into fixed prompts (25 prompts × 4 tokens) whose embeddings are learned during training, allowing the model to aggregate complementary semantic cues rather than relying on a single symmetry-specific phrase. Training uses α-focal loss to handle the extreme foreground/background pixel imbalance, and both CLIP encoders are jointly fine-tuned to map the abstract symmetry prompts into useful text tokens.

## Results

On the DENDI dataset, CLIPSym achieves a reflection F1-score of 66.5% and a rotation F1-score of 25.1%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art EquiSym (61.7% and 22.0%) by 4.8 and 3.1 points respectively. On the SDRW and LDRS reflection datasets, CLIPSym reaches 51.8% and 39.5% F1-scores, surpassing EquiSym (48.2% and 37.7%) and PMCNet (40.8% and 30.5%). The method also demonstrates superior equivariance consistency (0.082 vs. 0.244 for EquiSym) and robustness (59.7 vs. 57.1) under random rotations in [-45°, 45°].

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIPSym outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor EquiSym [51] (CVPR 2022), CLIPSym gains +4.8 points in reflection F1 (66.5 vs. 61.7) and +3.1 points in rotation F1 (25.1 vs. 22.0) on DENDI, and +3.6 / +1.8 points on SDRW / LDRS respectively. Against PMCNet [50] (ICCV 2021), the margin is larger: +12.7 points in reflection F1 on DENDI and +11.0 points on SDRW. Against the earliest deep baseline SymResNet [12] (ICCV 2017), CLIPSym leads by +35.8 points in reflection and +13.2 points in rotation F1 on DENDI. Notably, even CLIPSym without text conditioning (63.7% reflection F1) already surpasses EquiSym, indicating that CLIP's pre-training alone provides a substantial advantage over ImageNet pre-training used by all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry detection line of work (PMCNet, EquiSym) by replacing the ImageNet-pretrained visual backbone with CLIP's vision-language pre-trained encoders, arguing that natural image captions contain symmetry-relevant semantic cues (e.g., 'circle', 'rectangle') that pure visual pre-training misses. The equivariant decoder directly builds on EquiSym's group-equivariant convolutional design and Steerable CNNs, but is re-architected to operate on CLIP's patch-token representations via a FiLM-conditioned Transformer before lifting to the roto-translation group. The SAPG prompting technique departs from standard CLIP adaptation (which typically uses a single 'a photo of a [CLASS]' prompt) by aggregating multiple object-class prompts, motivated by the observation that symmetry is a universal geometric property not tied to any single object category.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that vision-language pre-training provides a meaningful inductive bias for geometric tasks beyond what ImageNet pre-training offers, even without explicit text conditioning at inference, suggesting CLIP's representations encode structural/geometric regularities from caption statistics. However, the performance gains are modest (2–3% F1 over EquiSym), the datasets are small (≤2500 images), and the computational cost increases by ~30% over EquiSym. Open questions include whether the benefit scales to larger or more diverse symmetry datasets, whether the SAPG technique generalizes to other geometric properties (e.g., periodicity, chirality), and why the larger ViT-L/14 backbone underperforms ViT-B/16—a finding that complicates the 'bigger is better' narrative for CLIP-based adaptation.
