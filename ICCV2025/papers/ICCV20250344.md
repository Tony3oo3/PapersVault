## Problem

Existing dual-teacher adversarial robustness distillation (ARD) frameworks suffer from two inherent issues: (1) the clean and robust teachers have divergent optimization objectives that create conflicting knowledge signals for the student, and (2) the robust teacher's performance degrades as iteratively generated adversarial examples evolve during training. Prior methods like B-MTARD improve robustness at the cost of clean accuracy, revealing an unresolved trade-off between the two objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250344/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) as input and produces class probability distributions over the dataset's label set. During training, each clean image is additionally perturbed via a 10-step PGD attack (ε=8/255, L∞) that jointly targets both the student and clean teacher, so the student simultaneously processes clean and adversarial versions of every input through separate forward passes.

## Method

CIARD introduces a triple-objective loss for the student: a clean-knowledge KL term (student vs. clean teacher on clean inputs), a robust-knowledge KL term (student vs. robust teacher on adversarial inputs), and a novel contrastive push loss that *maximizes* the KL divergence between the student and clean teacher on adversarial inputs (with negative weighting), deliberately decoupling the student from the clean teacher's vulnerable predictions so it can specialize in robust features. To prevent the robust teacher from degrading as the student generates increasingly challenging adversarial examples, CIARD employs Iterative Teacher Training (ITT): the robust teacher is frozen for the first 50 epochs, then continuously retrained with a cross-entropy loss on the evolving adversarial examples at a low learning rate (1e-5), maintaining its defensive decision boundary throughout the 300-epoch training schedule.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 under FGSM attack, CIARD achieves 88.87% clean accuracy and 61.88% robust accuracy (W-R: 75.38%), outperforming the best dual-teacher baseline B-MTARD (88.20%/61.42%/74.81%) and single-teacher methods by ~5% in clean accuracy. On CIFAR-100, CIARD reaches 65.73% clean and 34.47% robust accuracy under FGSM, surpassing B-MTARD (65.08%/34.21%). The paper reports an average 3.53% improvement in adversarial defense rates and a 5.87% increase in clean sample accuracy across all evaluated attack scenarios (FGSM, PGD_SAT, PGD_TRADES, CW∞, Square Attack) on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIARD consistently outperforms the strongest dual-teacher baseline B-MTARD across all white-box attacks: on CIFAR-10 FGSM, CIARD gains +0.67% clean accuracy (88.87 vs 88.20) and +0.46% robust accuracy (61.88 vs 61.42); on CIFAR-100 FGSM, it gains +0.65% clean and +0.26% robust. Against the earlier dual-teacher MTARD, CIARD improves clean accuracy by 1.51% (CIFAR-10) and 1.43% (CIFAR-100) under FGSM, while also surpassing it on robust accuracy by 0.68% and 2.98% respectively. Compared to single-teacher ARD methods (ARD, RSLAD, ABSLD, SCORE, Fair-ARD), CIARD achieves substantially higher clean accuracy (~5% gain) while also matching or exceeding their robust accuracy, effectively resolving the accuracy-robustness trade-off. Under black-box PGD_TRADES attacks, CIARD achieves 77.58% W-R on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), beating B-MTARD by 0.83% and MTARD by 1.27%. Under Square Attack (query-based), CIARD reaches 84.45% W-R on CIFAR-10, outperforming B-MTARD (84.01%) and MTARD (82.97%). The only marginal case is PGD_SAT on CIFAR-100 where CIARD's robust accuracy (28.05%) is slightly below B-MTARD (28.50%), though its clean accuracy and W-R still lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIARD extends the dual-teacher adversarial robustness distillation (ARD) lineage initiated by MTARD (Zhao et al., ECCV 2022) and refined by B-MTARD (Zhao et al., TPAMI 2024), which introduced a min-max framework balancing clean and robust teacher signals. The paper identifies two specific failure modes in this dual-teacher paradigm—conflicting optimization objectives between the clean and robust teachers, and progressive degradation of the robust teacher as adversarial examples evolve during student training—and addresses them with a contrastive push loss (decoupling clean from robust knowledge) and an iterative teacher retraining schedule. This positions CIARD as a targeted refinement of the multi-teacher ARD family rather than a departure from it, while the push-loss mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from contrastive learning's repulsive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical recipe for improving lightweight model robustness on edge devices, where the robustness-accuracy trade-off is most acute. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (B-MTARD) are modest (typically 0.2–0.5% in weighted robustness), and evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, leaving open whether the iterative teacher retraining overhead and push-loss mechanism scale to larger datasets or more complex architectures. A key open question is whether the cyclic teacher-student co-adaptation can be extended to multi-modal or generative settings, and whether the 50-epoch freezing heuristic generalizes across training budgets.
