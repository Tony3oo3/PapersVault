## Problem

Existing Mamba-based pose-lifting methods flatten the graph-structured human skeleton into 1D sequences for scanning, destroying the inherent joint topology, and they process all joint trajectories uniformly, ignoring that limbs exhibit high-flexibility motion while the trunk remains constrained. This results in degraded spatial modeling and insufficient capture of joint-specific motion dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of 2D keypoints (N joints × T frames × 2) produced either by SHNet or ground-truth annotations, and regresses a 3D pose sequence (N joints × T frames × 3). Sequence lengths are 243 or 351 frames on Human3.6M and 81 frames on MPI-INF-3DHP. Notably, no explicit spatial or temporal positional embeddings are added, since the SSM backbone inherently encodes token ordering.

## Method

SAMA alternates two modules built on Mamba2's Structured State Space Duality: a Structure-aware State Integrator (SSI) and a Motion-adaptive State Modulator (MSM). SSI replaces sequential scanning with a learnable, degree-normalized adjacency matrix that simultaneously fuses joint features and hidden states in the state space, so each joint directly attends to its topologically connected neighbors rather than only lower-indexed joints in a scan. MSM learns a joint-specific timescale Δ from adjacent-frame features via a point-wise 1D convolution (kernel size 2), causing high-motion joints (limbs) to receive larger Δ (emphasizing current input) and low-motion joints (trunk) to receive smaller Δ (preserving state continuity). The full network stacks K alternating SSI/MSM layers with interleaved spatial and temporal attention (following Jamba), and is trained end-to-end with a composite loss of weighted MPJPE, MPJVE, and Normalized MPJPE.

## Results

On Human3.6M with 351 input frames, SAMA-L achieves a state-of-the-art MPJPE of 36.5 mm and P-MPJPE of 31.0 mm, outperforming the previous best PoseMamba-X (37.1 mm / 31.5 mm) by 0.6 mm while using fewer parameters (17.3M vs 26.5M) and MACs per frame (234M vs 452M). With ground-truth 2D poses as input, SAMA-L reaches 11.4 mm MPJPE, a 3.4 mm improvement over PoseMamba-X (14.8 mm). On the in-the-wild MPI-INF-3DHP dataset, SAMA achieves 14.4 mm MPJPE, 99.0% PCK, and 88.3% AUC, surpassing PoseMamba (14.5 mm) and PoseMagic (14.7 mm, 98.8% PCK, 87.6% AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMA consistently outperforms PoseMamba at every model scale: SAMA-S beats PoseMamba-S by 1.2 mm MPJPE (40.6 vs 41.8), SAMA-B beats PoseMamba-B by 3.1 mm (37.7 vs 40.8), and SAMA-L beats PoseMamba-X by 0.2 mm (36.9 vs 37.1) at 243 frames, with the gap widening to 0.6 mm at 351 frames (36.5 vs 37.1). Against PoseMagic (14.4M params, 84 MACs/frame), SAMA-B (3.3M params, 48 MACs/frame) achieves a 0.2 mm lower MPJPE (37.7 vs 37.5) with roughly one-third the parameters and MACs. On MPI-INF-3DHP, SAMA edges out PoseMamba by 0.1 mm MPJPE (14.4 vs 14.5) and surpasses PoseMagic by 0.3 mm MPJPE, 0.2% PCK, and 0.7% AUC. The efficiency advantage is notable: SAMA-B uses only 3.3M parameters and 48 MACs/frame yet outperforms the much larger MotionBERT (42.3M params, 719 MACs/frame) by 1.5 mm MPJPE. The generalization study further shows that prepending SSI+MSM to MixSTE, MotionBERT, and MotionAGFormer yields 0.6, 1.2, and 0.9 mm MPJPE reductions respectively, confirming the modules' transferability across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMA builds directly on the Mamba2/SSD formulation (Dao & Gu, ICML 2024) as its state-space backbone and adopts PoseMamba's spatial-temporal decomposition and ZOH discretization for the 2D-to-3D pose-lifting pipeline. It departs from prior Mamba-based pose lifting methods (PoseMamba, PoseMagic) that rely on 2D-to-1D scanning strategies to approximate joint dependencies, instead introducing a learnable adjacency matrix that fuses joint features and hidden states directly in the state space, and a motion-adaptive timescale that replaces the uniform Δ with joint-specific values. The hybrid attention layers follow Jamba's alternating Transformer-Mamba design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a lightweight topology-aware state fusion (SSI) outperforms more complex bi-directional and global-local scanning strategies at lower computational cost, suggesting that explicit structural priors in the state space may be more effective than elaborate scanning heuristics for graph-structured sequential data. The generalization study—showing that prepending SSI+MSM to MixSTE, MotionBERT, and MotionAGFormer consistently improves them—indicates these modules could serve as drop-in enhancements for a broader class of pose-lifting architectures. Open questions include whether the learned adjacency matrix captures meaningful anatomical structure or merely fits dataset-specific correlations, and whether the motion-adaptive timescale generalizes to tasks with more heterogeneous motion (e.g., multi-person or hand pose).
