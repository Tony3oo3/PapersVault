## Problem

Large vision-language-action models have shown strong generalization for robotic manipulation but remain limited to parallel grippers because dexterous grasp data is scarce. Existing dexterous grasp datasets are either semantic-unaware (unable to perform functional, part-specific grasps) or too small to support billion-parameter model training, leaving a gap in language-instructed dexterous grasping at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251359/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-view colored point cloud of a tabletop scene (downsampled to 10,000 points via furthest point sampling) together with a natural-language instruction following the template 'Grasp the {part} of the {object} object, with contacts on {fingers}'. It outputs a full Shadow Hand pose parameterized as wrist translation T∈R³, wrist rotation R∈SO(3), and 22 joint angles θ∈R²², produced by a flow-matching denoising process that generates the pose from Gaussian noise conditioned on the VLM's hidden states.

## Method

DexVLG combines a Uni3D ViT-based point cloud encoder (pretrained via CLIP contrastive alignment) with a Florence-2 LLM backbone, where projected 3D features are concatenated with language token embeddings and processed jointly. A flow-matching denoising head shares cross-attention with the LLM (following the Transfusion/π₀ paradigm) and is trained to predict the velocity field mapping noise to the target grasp pose, with an MLP decoder regressing the final hand parameters. The model is fine-tuned end-to-end (only the tokenizer frozen) on DexGraspNet 3.0—170M part-aligned grasp poses across 174K objects—where each pose is synthesized via part-aware gradient-based optimization (LP force-closure energy, a truncated barrier function enforcing part-specific contact, and distance/regularization terms) and validated in IsaacGym. The key insight is that the large VLM capacity (up to ~1B parameters) is necessary to simultaneously learn high-quality grasp generation and complex instruction following (part selection + contact mode), a trade-off that smaller diffusion-based models cannot achieve without degrading pose quality.

## Results

On the LVIS-Seen benchmark, DexVLG achieves 87.7% simulation success rate, 70.7% part touch accuracy (PTA), and 62.1% part grasp accuracy (PGA), substantially outperforming both baselines. On the Unseen benchmark (84 objects not seen during training), DexVLG retains 79.1% success rate and 36.3% PGA, while on the SamPart3D benchmark (different part segmentation method) it achieves 76.3% success rate and 52.0% PGA. In real-world experiments with a ShadowHand on a UR10e arm, DexVLG achieves 80% grasp success rate and 75% part accuracy on simple tabletop objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DexVLG against DexGraspNet 2.0 (DGN2.0*), a diffusion-based small model retrained on the DexGraspNet 3.0 dataset, and an augmented variant DGN2.0*+CLIP that concatenates CLIP text embeddings with point cloud features for language conditioning. DexVLG wins decisively on all three benchmarks and all three metrics. On LVIS-Seen, DexVLG improves success rate by 16.9 points over DGN2.0* (87.7 vs 70.8) and by 19.9 points over DGN2.0*+CLIP (87.7 vs 67.8), while PGA jumps from 38.5/40.5 to 62.1. On the Unseen benchmark, the success rate gap widens to 21.4 points (79.1 vs 57.7), and PGA improves by 11 points (36.3 vs 25.3). Notably, adding CLIP features to the small DGN2.0 model actually degrades its success rate (70.8→67.8 on LVIS-Seen), suggesting the small model lacks capacity to jointly learn language alignment and pose quality, whereas the large VLM architecture of DexVLG handles both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DexGraspNet/BoDex line of analytic-based dexterous grasp synthesis by introducing part-aware energy functions (part-contact barrier, part-aligned initialization) that make each grasp semantically distinguishable, and scales the pipeline to 170M poses across 174K objects. For the model, it adapts the Transfusion/π₀ architecture pattern—where an LLM shares cross-attention with a flow-matching denoising head—to the dexterous grasp domain, using Uni3D for point-cloud encoding and Florence-2 as the language backbone. The key departure from prior semantic-grasp work (SemGrasp, DexFuncGrasp, FunGrasp) is scale: those methods operate on hundreds to thousands of objects, whereas DexGraspNet 3.0 provides 170M part-captioned poses, enabling end-to-end VLM training rather than small-model fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DexGraspNet 3.0 is the largest dexterous grasp dataset to date (170M poses, 174K objects) and the first to pair every pose with part-level semantic captions, making it a foundational resource for the community. The 76–88% zero-shot simulation success rate and 80% real-world success rate demonstrate that VLM-scale models can handle high-dimensional dexterous hand control, a capability previously limited to parallel grippers. Key limitations include the absence of hand-arm workspace constraints in training (making many generated poses unsafe to execute) and the lack of an effective pose-ranking mechanism for VLM-based generation, both of which are identified as open problems for future work.
