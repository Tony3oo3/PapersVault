## Problem

Existing few-shot video object segmentation (FSVOS) methods are category-centric, associating query objects with support samples based on static object class while ignoring the rich temporal dynamics in videos. This limits applicability to scenarios where the target is defined by a motion pattern (e.g., 'hugging', 'moonwalk') rather than an object category, and text-based referring segmentation fails for novel or fine-grained motions that lack concise verbal descriptions. The paper introduces the MOVE benchmark and a baseline method to establish motion-guided FSVOS as a distinct research direction.

## Input / Output

The method takes K support video-mask pairs (each a short clip demonstrating a target motion with per-frame binary masks) and a query video of T frames as input, producing a per-frame segmentation mask sequence for the query video plus a scalar matching score (cosine similarity between support and query [CLS] tokens) indicating whether the query object performs the same motion. Unlike standard FSVOS where support is a single annotated image, here the support is a full video sequence, making temporal modeling of the support essential. The model operates at 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 input resolutions via an FPN, and is evaluated in 2-way-1-shot and 5-way-1-shot episode settings.

## Method

The core contribution is the Decoupled Motion-Appearance (DMA) module, which extracts two complementary prototypes from 1/4-resolution features: an appearance prototype via mask pooling and a motion prototype via temporal differencing of adjacent frame features followed by 3D convolution and spatial pooling. Two auxiliary classification heads (one for object category, one for motion category) explicitly enforce decoupling so that each branch learns its respective signal. A transformer with learnable queries and a [CLS] token refines both prototypes into a unified representation, which is then fused across support and query branches through a prototype attention module (cross-attention + self-attention over multiple layers). A multi-scale mask decoder guided by the fused prototypes produces final segmentation masks, while the [CLS] token cosine similarity yields the motion matching score. The full pipeline uses a shared encoder (ResNet50 or VideoSwin-T + FPN) and a proposal generator for coarse query masks, trained with cross-entropy and IoU losses over 240K episodes.

## Results

On the MOVE benchmark (OS split, ResNet50 backbone), DMA achieves 50.1% J&F in 2-way-1-shot and 40.2% J&F in 5-way-1-shot, outperforming the strongest baseline TTI (45.2% / 35.6%) by 4.9 and 4.6 points respectively. With the VideoSwin-T backbone, DMA further improves to 51.5% and 41.4% J&F, surpassing DANet (49.8% / 36.1%) by 1.7 and 5.3 points. On the harder NS split, DMA still leads with 46.0% J&F (2-way-1-shot, ResNet50) versus 44.6% for DANet. DMA also achieves the best robustness metrics, reaching 98.6% T-Acc and 11.5% N-Acc (2-way-1-shot, OS, ResNet50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMA outperforms all six baselines across both OS and NS splits and both backbones. Among FSVOS methods, DMA beats TTI (45.2% → 50.1%, +4.9 J&F) and DANet (45.4% → 50.1%, +4.7 J&F) in 2-way-1-shot on OS/ResNet50, with the gap widening in 5-way-1-shot (40.2% vs. 35.6% for TTI, +4.6). HPAN, the strongest FSVOS baseline in 5-way-1-shot (34.0%), is surpassed by 6.2 J&F points. FSS methods (SCCAN at 40.6%, CyCTR at 34.4%) and the RVOS method LMPM (41.8%) trail DMA by 8–16 J&F points, confirming that static image-based or text-guided approaches struggle with motion-centric segmentation. With the VideoSwin-T backbone, the margin over DANet narrows to 1.7 J&F in 2-way-1-shot (51.5% vs. 49.8%) but remains 5.3 points in 5-way-1-shot (41.4% vs. 36.1%), suggesting that stronger temporal backbones partially close the gap for category-centric methods but cannot match DMA's explicit motion modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the category-centric paradigm of existing FSVOS methods (DANet, HPAN, TTI) by redefining the support set from static images to video clips that demonstrate a motion pattern, and from text-based motion descriptions in RVOS (MeViS/LMPM) to video-as-reference. Conceptually, it repurposes the prototype-learning framework of FSVOS but introduces explicit temporal differencing (3D convolutions on frame-to-frame feature differences) and auxiliary motion classification to decouple motion from appearance in the extracted prototypes. The DMA module is the key architectural departure: rather than treating support features holistically, it separates appearance prototypes (mask-pooled) from motion prototypes (temporal-difference-based) and refines them through a transformer with cross-attention to both branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOVE establishes a concrete benchmark for a task that prior work has implicitly assumed would be handled by existing video segmentation or referring segmentation pipelines, but the results show all six baselines struggle (best J&F ~45% vs. DMA's 50%), revealing a genuine gap in motion-centric understanding. The uniformly low N-Acc scores (≤11.5% across all methods) highlight an unsolved challenge in background suppression that no current architecture addresses well. Oracle experiments (63.6% with perfect motion labels, 74.3% with GT masks) indicate substantial headroom, and the authors identify open directions including decomposing complex motions into meta-motions, modeling relational multi-object motions, and learning discriminative background prototypes.
