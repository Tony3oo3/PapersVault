## Problem

3D part assembly is a discrete-continuous optimization problem with a combinatorially large yet sparse solution space, making it difficult for neural networks to learn, especially as part counts grow. Existing methods either rely on geometric priors in a generative fashion (yielding plausible but imprecise assemblies) or assume parts are delivered step-by-step as in LEGO, whereas real furniture manuals (e.g., IKEA) provide diagrammatic instructions whose 2D-to-3D correspondence and assembly order remain unexploited by automated systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes N unordered textureless 3D part point clouds (1,000 sampled points each, normalized to canonical space) and a sequence of N 2D line-drawing step diagrams rendered from the same CAD models, where each diagram shows one additional part attached to the partially assembled furniture. It outputs a permutation matrix giving the assembly order and a 6DoF SE(3) pose (unit quaternion rotation + 3D translation) per part, whose rigid transforms reconstruct the target shape. The number of parts N varies per furniture item, and geometrically equivalent parts (e.g., four table legs) are handled via group-level matching.

## Method

Manual-PA is a two-stage transformer framework. In the first stage, a lightweight PointNet encodes each part and DINOv2 encodes the difference image between consecutive step diagrams (patchified into K patches); an InfoNCE contrastive loss aligns the two modalities, and the Hungarian algorithm on the resulting similarity matrix yields the part-to-step permutation. In the second stage, sinusoidal positional encodings (with RoPE) are added to both modalities, with the part encoding permuted according to the learned order, providing soft sequential guidance rather than a hard constraint. A stack of L transformer decoder layers performs part-to-part self-attention and step-to-part cross-attention (where all diagram patches from the same manual are concatenated along the patch dimension), and a pose head predicts per-part quaternion and translation. The pose loss combines L2 translation error, chamfer-distance rotation error (to handle intrinsic part symmetries), an L2 rotation regularization, and a global chamfer distance between predicted and ground-truth assembled shapes.

## Results

On the PartNet benchmark, Manual-PA achieves SCD of 1.7 (chair), 1.8 (table), and 2.9 (storage), with Part Accuracy of 89.06%, 87.41%, and 56.51% respectively, and Success Rate of 58.03%, 56.66%, and 4.73%. In zero-shot evaluation on the IKEA-Manual dataset, Manual-PA achieves SCD of 11.4 (chair) and 4.8 (table), with PA of 42.51% and 49.72%, substantially outperforming all baselines. The method also demonstrates strong cross-category generalization, with minimal performance drop when trained on chair and tested on table and storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Manual-PA outperforms all nine baselines across every metric and category. Against the closest conditioned baseline, Image-PA (retrained with diagrams), Manual-PA improves Part Accuracy by 26 points on chair (89.06 vs 62.67), 17 on table (87.41 vs 70.10), and 8 on storage (56.51 vs 47.79), while reducing SCD from 5.9 to 1.7 on chair. Compared to the strongest generative method 3DHPA, Manual-PA achieves SCD of 1.7 vs 5.1 (chair) and 1.8 vs 2.8 (table), with PA gains of ~25 points. Against SPAFormer (sequence-conditioned), Manual-PA improves SCD from 6.7 to 1.7 and PA from 55.88 to 89.06 on chair. In zero-shot IKEA-Manual evaluation, Manual-PA reduces SCD from 34.3 to 11.4 (chair) and 37.8 to 4.8 (table) versus 3DHPA, and from 17.3 to 11.4 (chair) and 14.7 to 4.8 (table) versus Image-PA, demonstrating substantially stronger real-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-conditioned 3D part assembly line initiated by Image-PA (Li et al., ECCV 2020), which uses a single rendered image as conditioning, by replacing it with a full sequence of step-by-step instruction diagrams from an assembly manual. It also departs from generative assembly methods (DGL, 3DHPA, SPAFormer, Joint-PA) that rely solely on geometric priors or learned part interactions without any visual guidance. The key architectural novelty is a two-stage pipeline: a contrastive-learning-based permutation module (using InfoNCE loss and Hungarian matching) that establishes part-to-step correspondences, followed by a transformer decoder with RoPE positional encoding that uses the learned order as soft guidance for 6DoF pose prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sequential instruction manuals provide substantially richer guidance than a single final image for 3D part assembly, with the zero-shot IKEA-Manual results (3.5% SR vs. 0% for 3DHPA) suggesting practical viability for real-world furniture assembly automation. Open questions remain around handling sub-module assemblies with tree-like (non-linear) structures, interpreting additional visual elements in real manuals (arrows, labels, close-ups), and bridging the synthetic-to-real rendering gap. The soft-guidance design (positional encoding as a suggestion rather than a hard autoregressive constraint) offers a practical middle ground that avoids error accumulation while still leveraging sequence information.
