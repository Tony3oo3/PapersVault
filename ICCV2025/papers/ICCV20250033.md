## Problem

Existing video summarization methods deterministically regress to a single averaged importance score across multiple annotators, erasing the inherent subjectivity of what constitutes a good summary. This averaging obscures distinct valid summarization perspectives (e.g., one group focusing on early events, another on late events) into a single compromised target. The paper asks whether video summarization can be reframed as a conditional generation task that learns and samples from the full distribution of plausible summaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of N frames, encodes each frame with a pre-trained image encoder (GoogLeNet for TVSum/SumMe, Inception-v3 for Mr. HiSum), and contextualizes the resulting feature vectors via self-attention to produce a condition matrix Z ∈ R^{N×D}. It outputs a continuous importance score vector in (0,1)^N, which is then segmented into clips via Kernel Temporal Segmentation and passed through a binary knapsack solver to yield the final summary. A key distinction is that multiple distinct summaries can be generated for the same video by sampling from different random noise initializations, rather than producing a single deterministic output.

## Method

SummDiff applies a diffusion model to the importance score vector in logit space, where the forward process adds Gaussian noise to the logit-transformed individual annotator score and the reverse process (parameterized by a transformer denoiser) progressively denoises it conditioned on video features. To bridge the scalar score vector and the cross-attention mechanism, scores are quantized into K uniform bins mapped to a learnable codebook of D-dimensional embeddings, which serve as queries attending to the video feature matrix as keys and values. Time step and temporal position are injected via AdaLN-Zero scale-shift operations rather than additive query mixing, preventing information interference. Training minimizes L2 loss on each individual annotator's score (not the average), and inference uses DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance and self-attention guidance for further quality improvement.

## Results

Under the TVT (train/val/test) protocol, SummDiff achieves Kendall's τ = 0.133 and Spearman's ρ = 0.148 on SumMe, and τ = 0.173 and ρ = 0.226 on TVSum, outperforming the strongest baseline CSTA (τ = 0.108/0.168, ρ = 0.120/0.221). On the large-scale Mr. HiSum dataset, SummDiff reaches τ = 0.175, ρ = 0.238, MAP@50% = 65.44, and MAP@15% = 33.83, surpassing CSTA by 4.7 points in τ and 3.4 points in MAP@15%. The novel knapsack-based metrics (CIS, WIR, WSE) further confirm SummDiff's superiority, with the lowest CIS (5.96) and highest WIR (0.61) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SummDiff outperforms all 14 baselines on the TVT protocol across both SumMe and TVSum, with the closest competitor being CSTA [67], which it beats by 0.025 in τ on SumMe and 0.005 in τ on TVSum. On Mr. HiSum, the margin over CSTA widens substantially (τ: 0.175 vs. 0.128; MAP@15%: 33.83 vs. 30.42), demonstrating scalability. The sole exception is TVSum under the 5FCV protocol, where DMASum [81] achieves a slightly higher τ (0.203 vs. 0.195) and ρ (0.267 vs. 0.255), though DMASum underperforms significantly on SumMe (τ = 0.063), indicating poor generalization. Older GAN-based methods (SUM-GAN [50], AC-SUM-GAN [2]) and early attention models (VASNet [17], PGL-SUM [3]) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all datasets. The authors note that the 5FCV protocol inflates scores due to test-set overfitting, and the TVT gap between models is much smaller, suggesting more realistic performance estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in video summarization—deterministic regression to averaged frame-level importance scores—by reframing the task as conditional generation, where the model learns the full distribution of plausible summaries. It is the first work to apply diffusion models to video summarization, building on DDPM/DDIM for the generative mechanism, AdaLN-Zero (DiT) for conditioning on time and position, and classifier-free/self-attention guidance for quality improvement. The knapsack-based evaluation metrics (CIS, WIR, WSE) draw on sensitivity analysis literature from combinatorial optimization (Hifi et al., Belgacem & Hifi), a connection previously unexplored in summarization evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative reframing directly addresses a long-standing limitation: averaging multiple annotator scores obscures distinct valid summaries, and deterministic models can only produce one. The knapsack-grounded metrics offer a theoretically principled alternative to F1 and ranking scores that better reflect how importance scores actually influence the final summary. However, the generative advantage is only fully exercised on small datasets (TVSum: 50 videos, SumMe: 25 videos); on the large-scale Mr. HiSum, the model is trained on a single aggregated label, so the distributional learning benefit is not tested at scale. Open questions include whether individual annotations at scale would further amplify the generative advantage, and whether the approach extends to other sequential selection tasks.
