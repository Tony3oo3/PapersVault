## Problem

Existing remote sensing datasets are roughly 10× smaller than ImageNet-21k and lack fine-grained target diversity, limiting the representational capacity of MIM-based foundation models. Additionally, conventional MIM reconstructs all masked tokens, incurring unnecessary computational cost that is especially wasteful for RS images dominated by redundant background pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes high-resolution visible-light satellite images (e.g., from WorldView, QuickBird, GeoEye) averaging ~199K pixels each, which are split into non-overlapping patches of size p×p. The output is a pre-trained ViT encoder (B or L) whose patch-level feature representations are transferred to downstream heads (Faster-RCNN, Oriented-RCNN, UperNet) for detection, oriented detection, and segmentation. The full OpticalRS-13M corpus contains 13.2M images across 12 top-level categories including an 'Events' class (fire, flood, landslide) not present in prior RS datasets.

## Method

SelectiveMAE replaces MAE's uniform random masking with a two-stage semantic selection strategy. First, HOG features score every patch's semantic richness; only the top 15% of patches are fed to the ViT encoder and the top 25% of masked patches are selected as reconstruction targets (partial reconstruction), so just 40% of patches participate in forward/backward passes. Second, a Progressive Semantic Token Selection (PSTS) module prevents gradient explosion at the high 85% masking ratio by starting training with the highest-HOG patches and, over successive stages, incrementally adding patches ordered by cosine distance from the initial set (near → far → random), thereby following an easy-to-hard curriculum. The decoder is a lightweight cross-attention module borrowed from CrossMAE. This design yields >2× throughput (556k vs 264k images/min on 8×A100) and ~1.8× lower GPU memory while matching or exceeding MAE's downstream accuracy.

## Results

On the full OpticalRS-13M pre-training, SelectiveMAE with ViT-L achieves 97.49/98.52% on AID, 94.73/96.36% on RESISC-45, 78.70 mAP₅₀ on DIOR, 71.75 mAP₅₀ on DIOR-R, 53.92 mIoU on LoveDA, and 79.48 mF1 on SpaceNetv1. The ViT-B variant reaches 97.10/98.28% on AID and 75.80 mAP₅₀ on DIOR. Critically, SelectiveMAE processes 556k images/minute (ViT-B) versus 264k for vanilla MAE, a 2.1× throughput improvement, while also reducing GPU memory by ~1.8× (9570 MB vs 17628 MB for ViT-B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelectiveMAE (ViT-L) substantially outperforms the closest MIM-based RSFMs: it beats SatMAE [33] by +2.47 on AID (97.49 vs 95.02) and +7.81 mAP on DIOR (78.70 vs 70.89), and beats ScaleMAE [37] by +1.05 on AID and +4.89 mAP on DIOR. Against the 914M-parameter OREOLE [43], SelectiveMAE with only 307M parameters surpasses it on DIOR (78.70 vs 77.40) and DIOR-R (71.75 vs 71.31) while trailing by 0.08 mIoU on LoveDA. Versus the MAE baseline on the same ViT-B backbone, SelectiveMAE gains +0.52 on AID, +1.26 on RESISC-45, and +0.40 mAP on DIOR, while achieving 2.1× higher throughput (556k vs 264k images/min). Against CrossMAE [49] (the natural-image method whose decoder architecture is adopted), SelectiveMAE wins on both AID (96.78 vs 96.12) and RESISC-45 (93.35 vs 92.08) with higher throughput (556k vs 475k). On contrastive-learning baselines (SeCo, GASSL, CACo), SelectiveMAE leads by 3–7 points on AID and 5–9 mAP on DIOR, confirming the advantage of MIM-based pre-training on the larger, more diverse OpticalRS-13M dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE framework (He et al., 2022) and the partial-reconstruction idea from CrossMAE (Fu et al., 2024) to the remote sensing domain, where images exhibit sparse foreground objects against dense, repetitive backgrounds. Rather than random masking, SelectiveMAE uses HOG-based semantic richness to decide which patches to encode (≤15%) and which to reconstruct (≤25%), and introduces a Progressive Semantic Token Selection curriculum that shifts from reconstructing semantically similar patches to complementary ones. The pipeline is paired with a newly curated 13-million-image optical RS dataset (OpticalRS-13M), which addresses the scale and diversity gap relative to prior RS pre-training corpora like MillionAID, SeCo, and CACo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×+ training speedup with comparable or slightly better downstream accuracy makes large-scale RS foundation model pre-training substantially more accessible, and the 13M-image dataset is a practical resource the RS community can adopt immediately. However, the approach is limited to visible-light imagery and relies on a hand-crafted HOG descriptor for semantic scoring, raising open questions about whether learned or multi-spectral-aware selectors would generalize better to SAR, multispectral, or temporal settings. The PSTS curriculum, while effective, is a heuristic two-stage schedule whose optimal stage boundaries and similarity thresholds remain unexplored, and the paper does not investigate whether the 40%-patch sufficiency finding holds for higher-resolution or more heterogeneous scenes.
