## Problem

Existing image-to-3D models (e.g., DreamGaussian, TripoSR) are trained on centrally-aligned single objects and re-center inputs during preprocessing, introducing an inherent positional bias that causes multi-object scenes to be reconstructed as fused, monolithic meshes with incorrect spatial relationships. No prior method can simultaneously generate high-fidelity individual 3D assets and recover their correct spatial layout from a single multi-object image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251398/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing 2–6 objects along with their bounding boxes and occlusion masks (obtained via SAM) as input. It outputs a set of independently editable 3D meshes, each with optimized translation and scale parameters in a shared coordinate system, such that the rendered scene matches the input image's spatial arrangement. Objects are produced as separate meshes rather than a single fused geometry, enabling downstream per-object editing (addition, removal, repositioning, replacement).

## Method

REPARO is a two-stage compositional pipeline. Stage 1 isolates each object via SAM segmentation, inpaints occluded regions with a Stable-Diffusion model, crops and re-centers the object image, then generates a 3D mesh using an off-the-shelf image-to-3D model (DreamGaussian or TripoSR). Stage 2 places all meshes in a unified coordinate system and optimizes their translation and scale (rotation is excluded since objects inherit the input image's orientation) via differentiable rendering with Nvdiffrast. The key technical contribution is an optimal-transport-based long-range appearance loss that computes a global one-to-one pixel correspondence between rendered and reference images using a cost combining RGB, depth (from a frozen depth model), and screen-space position, thereby avoiding the local minima that plague pixel-wise L2 losses when objects are initially misaligned. A DINO-v2 feature-matching semantic loss is added to enforce high-level structural coherence, and the two terms are combined with a tunable weight λ.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects multi-object test set (20 scenes, 18 views), REPARO♣ (DreamGaussian-based) achieves the highest CLIP score of 0.833, while REPARO♠ (TripoSR-based) achieves the best PSNR (17.751), SSIM (0.865), and lowest LPIPS (0.216). Both variants substantially outperform the strongest baseline (LRM) on CLIP by 1.1–2.1 points and on PSNR by 3.6–4.1 dB. In a user study with 40 participants, REPARO received 61% preference versus 19% for DreamGaussian, 12% for LRM, and 8% for Wonder3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPARO outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against DreamGaussian, REPARO♣ improves CLIP by 2.6 points (0.833 vs 0.807) and PSNR by 4.0 dB (17.279 vs 13.280), demonstrating that the compositional pipeline with layout alignment resolves the multi-object fusion failures of direct generation. Against TripoSR, REPARO♠ gains 2.7 CLIP points (0.822 vs 0.795) and 0.5 dB PSNR (17.751 vs 17.248), confirming that even a fast feed-forward model benefits from the differentiable layout optimization stage. Against Wonder3D and LRM, both REPARO variants lead on CLIP by 2.1–3.2 points and on PSNR by 3.6–4.1 dB, with LRM being the closest competitor on CLIP (0.812) but still trailing by over 1 point. The user study reinforces these quantitative gains, with REPARO receiving 61% preference compared to 19% for DreamGaussian and 12% for LRM.

## Relation to Prior Work

REPARO extends the differentiable rendering framework of DROT [62], which introduced RGBXY derivatives and optimal transport for scene parameter recovery, by applying it to the novel problem of multi-object layout alignment rather than single-object reconstruction. It departs from the dominant single-object image-to-3D paradigm (DreamGaussian, TripoSR, Wonder3D, LRM) by decomposing the scene into per-object reconstructions via SAM segmentation and inpainting, then reassembling them through a global optimization stage. Among compositional 3D generation approaches that rely on bounding boxes, text prompts, or scene graphs, REPARO is distinctive in directly generating instance-level meshes from a single compositional image while preserving input-aligned spatial arrangements, without requiring post-hoc mesh segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPARO addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: existing image-to-3D models produce fused, low-fidelity assets for multi-object scenes due to center bias and occlusion, forcing users into error-prone manual segmentation. The OT-based long-range appearance loss is a reusable technique for differentiable rendering that mitigates local minima when initial layout estimates are far from the target. However, the paper acknowledges that severe occlusion can produce hallucinated geometry and that semantically implausible scenes cause the optimizer to favor common-sense layouts over the actual image, leaving open how well the approach scales to scenes with many interacting objects or to dynamic/temporal settings.
