## Problem

Existing personalization methods for diffusion models capture either subject or style from reference images but cannot disentangle both from a single input image. Methods like ZipLoRA and B-LoRA combine separately trained LoRAs, while DreamBooth-LoRA overfits when trained independently on the same image. The paper addresses the inverse problem: simultaneously extracting compatible subject and style LoRAs from one image so they can be used independently or recombined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as the sole supervision signal and produces two rank-64 LoRAs (subject and style) for a frozen SDXL base model, trained for 600 steps with batch size 1. The subject LoRA is bound to a trigger phrase of the form 'sks <class>' and the style LoRA to a 2-3 word style descriptor (e.g., 'watercolor painting'). At inference, either LoRA can be applied alone for destylized subject or style-only generation, or both can be arithmetically merged to reconstruct or recontextualize the original image.

## Method

UnZipLoRA trains two LoRAs simultaneously on a single image using three complementary separation strategies. Prompt separation computes cross-attention keys/values with three distinct prompts (base model with the full 'a <c> in <s> style' prompt, content LoRA with only <c>, style LoRA with only <s>) and sums the resulting feature maps, preventing cross-contamination between the two LoRAs. Column separation applies dynamic sparse masks that assign each LoRA weight column to either the subject or style LoRA based on periodic importance re-calibration (using the Cone method), with an orthogonality loss L_⊥ = Σ|m_c^i · m_s^i| enforcing mutual exclusivity. Block separation extends B-LoRA's coarse-grained U-Net block allocation to all upsampling blocks with finer subject/style assignments, relaxing column sparsity in style-sensitive blocks to preserve fine-grained stylistic details.

## Results

UnZipLoRA achieves the highest alignment scores across all four metrics in Table 2: Style-align (CLIP-I) 0.427, Subject-align (DINO) 0.349, Style-align (CSD) 0.265, and Subject-align (CSD) 0.358, outperforming DreamBooth-LoRA, Inspiration Tree, and B-LoRA on every metric. In user preference studies (204 responses each, 34 participants), UnZipLoRA is preferred over DreamBooth-LoRA (91.17% for decomposition, 98.10% for recontextualization), Inspiration Tree (81.53%, 79.17%), and B-LoRA (62.74%, 77.14%). The CSD-based metrics, which are more sensitive to stylistic nuances, show the largest margins over competing methods (e.g., 0.265 vs. 0.245 for style alignment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnZipLoRA outperforms all three baselines on every quantitative metric. Against DreamBooth-LoRA, UnZipLoRA wins on all four alignment scores (e.g., CSD style 0.265 vs. 0.245; CSD subject 0.358 vs. 0.338) and is preferred by 91–98% of users; DreamBooth-LoRA overfits the input image, failing to destylize the subject. Against Inspiration Tree, UnZipLoRA shows the largest gap on subject alignment (DINO 0.349 vs. 0.291) and is preferred by 81.53% of users for decomposition; Inspiration Tree's reliance on textual inversion limits its expressiveness and controllability. Against B-LoRA, UnZipLoRA wins on all metrics (e.g., CSD style 0.265 vs. 0.244; CSD subject 0.358 vs. 0.342) and is preferred by 62.74% for decomposition and 77.14% for recontextualization; B-LoRA retains residual style features and misattributes content attributes (e.g., balloon color) to style due to its coarse block-wise training. Notably, UnZipLoRA trains both LoRAs jointly from a single image, whereas B-LoRA and DreamBooth-LoRA require separate training runs, reducing compute requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

UnZipLoRA positions itself as the inverse of ZipLoRA, which merges independently trained subject and style LoRAs, by instead decomposing a single image into two simultaneously trained, compatible LoRAs. It extends B-LoRA's block-separation strategy (which restricts content and style to specific U-Net blocks) to a finer-grained allocation across more blocks, and borrows the Cone method's column-importance ranking to introduce a dynamic column-separation mechanism. The training objective and prompt format follow DreamBooth, while the orthogonality loss for LoRA compatibility is inherited from ZipLoRA. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior concept-extraction methods (Inspiration Tree, CusConcept) that rely on textual inversion by producing full LoRA weights rather than text embeddings, enabling finer-grained control over generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to extract disentangled, directly-combinable subject and style LoRAs from a single image opens practical workflows for artists and designers who want to recontextualize, destylize, or cross-combine concepts without curating separate training sets. The compatibility guarantee (seamless addition of the two LoRAs) is a non-trivial property that prior separate-training approaches do not provide. Key limitations include failure on highly abstract or shape-distorting styles, dependence on SDXL's U-Net architecture (with only preliminary results on KOALA), and the open question of whether the approach extends to DiT-based models. Future directions noted by the authors include training-free variants and alternative disentanglement techniques for challenging style regimes.
