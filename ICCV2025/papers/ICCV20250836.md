## Problem

Reconstruction-based and distillation-based anomaly detection methods suffer from 'anomaly leakage' (AL): the decoder, optimized by reconstruction or distillation loss, tends to copy encoded inputs verbatim regardless of whether they contain anomalous patterns, degrading localization performance. Existing mitigations (masking anomalous patches or substituting them with normal ones) compromise instance-specific normal patch information. The paper addresses how to remove anomalous information point-to-point without damaging the integrity of neighboring normal features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250836/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 448×448, encoded by a frozen ViT-B/14 (DINOv2) into multi-layer patch features (3 selected layers concatenated and projected to a single H×W×C tensor). At inference, the pipeline produces a pixel-wise anomaly score map computed as the sum of cosine distances between encoded and decoded patch features across three layers, and an image-level score defined as the maximum value of that map. The core anomaly decoupling operates entirely in the feature space, not the pixel space.

## Method

DecAD introduces a globally bounded invertible neural network (INN) that maps channel-expanded encoded features (γ=1.5×) into a prior latent space where normal and abnormal components are disentangled into separate channel groups. The abnormal channels are zeroed out, and the inverse INN mapping recovers a purified feature that is then decoded; the anomaly score is the cosine distance between the original and purified decoded features. To train the decoupling process, the paper synthesizes feature-level anomalies via adversarial perturbations that maximize the entropy of an auxiliary patch-level classifier's logits, initialized from a uniform distribution to ensure broad coverage outside the normal manifold. The INN uses i-DenseNet with Lipschitz-continuous layers (constant η<1) to guarantee global boundedness, ensuring any test input is mapped back to a vicinity of the normal manifold. Training combines three losses: the standard distillation loss, a decoupling reconstruction loss (L1 between purified and ground-truth normal features), and the adversarial synthesis loss.

## Results

On MVTec AD, DecAD achieves 99.0/99.7/98.3 (image-level AUROC/AP/F1-max) and 97.8/60.7/62.5/92.5 (pixel-level AUROC/AP/F1-max/AUPRO), yielding a mean of 87.2 across seven metrics. On VisA, it reaches 94.7/95.5/90.3 and 98.5/43.6/47.3/91.7 (mean 80.2). On the larger and more challenging Real-IAD dataset, DecAD obtains 85.4/83.7/76.1 and 98.3/36.7/42.5/89.0 (mean 73.1), outperforming all baselines on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecAD outperforms all five baselines on the mean of seven metrics across all three datasets. On MVTec AD, it improves over the closest competitor MambaAD [19] by +1.2 in mean, with particularly strong gains in pixel-level AP (+4.4) and F1-max (+3.3), while surpassing ViTAD [52] by +1.8. On VisA, DecAD leads MambaAD by +1.5 and ViTAD by +4.6 in mean, with image-level AUROC improving by +4.2 over ViTAD. On Real-IAD, the gap widens for pixel-level metrics: DecAD improves pixel-level AP by +11.7 and F1-max by +9.8 over RD4AD [9], and by +3.7/+3.8 over MambaAD, reflecting the increased frequency of anomaly leakage on this more diverse dataset. DiAD [20] and UniAD [49] are consistently the weakest baselines, with DecAD exceeding them by +3.2 to +20.5 in mean across datasets, confirming that reconstruction-based methods without explicit anomaly decoupling suffer most from the anomaly leakage problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecAD extends the reverse distillation framework (RD4AD, Tien et al.) by inserting a globally bounded invertible neural network between the encoder and decoder to explicitly decouple normal and abnormal components in a prior latent space. The specific invertible mapping unit follows the monotone-operator formulation of Ahn et al. (NeurIPS 2022), parameterized with i-DenseNet for Lipschitz continuity. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior normalizing-flow-based AD methods (CFlow, HGAD) that use invertible mappings for density estimation, instead repurposing INN as a point-to-point feature surgery tool that removes anomaly channels without disturbing neighboring normal information. The adversarial-perturbation-based anomaly synthesis also distinguishes itself from the Gaussian-noise approach used in prior feature-level anomaly simulation (SimplNet, UniAD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Naming and isolating 'anomaly leakage' as a concrete failure mode in reconstruction/distillation AD provides a clear diagnostic that practitioners can test in their own pipelines, and the INN-based decoupling offers a principled fix that preserves instance-specific normal information better than patch-masking or substitution strategies. The approach is limited to the reconstruction/distillation paradigm and adds forward/inverse INN passes at inference, which may be a concern for real-time industrial deployment. Open questions include whether the channel-splitting decoupling generalizes to non-ViT backbones or 3D/point-cloud settings, and whether the adversarial synthesis step is truly necessary or whether simpler structured perturbations could suffice at scale.
