## Problem

Existing multi-modality medical image generalization methods focus on class-level or modality-level invariance, neglecting individual anatomical and metabolic differences that limit cross-modality and cross-patient generalization. Heterogeneous generalization across structural (MRI, CT) and functional (PET) modalities remains largely unexplored, and prior invariant representations erase modality-specific information, preventing decomposition back into individual modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250931/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical volumes at a fixed 96×96×96 resolution, where all available modalities for a given individual (e.g., T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR for MRI; AC-PET, NAC-PET, CT for PET/CT) are encoded by a shared SwinUNETR encoder whose channel size matches the number of modality types. Outputs are either synthesized target-modality volumes (for modality transfer, evaluated via PSNR/NMSE/SSIM) or per-voxel segmentation masks (for tumor/lesion segmentation, evaluated via DICE). A learnable biological prior tensor is an additional internal component that retrieves missing modality knowledge via attention during pre-training.

## Method

PUIR is a two-stage framework built on the hypothesis that a personalized invariant representation X_h exists for each individual and can be decomposed into modality-specific images. In pre-training, a SwinUNETR encoder produces modality-specific features that attend to a learnable biological prior tensor O to retrieve missing knowledge; the fused features are then constrained by three losses: invariance (aligning X_h across all modalities of the same patient), equivariance (predicting geometric rotation from intermediate features to preserve SO(3) structure), and decomposition (reconstructing all modalities from X_h via a decoder conditioned on modality-specific intermediate representations). A contrastive loss serves as the baseline objective. In fine-tuning, the pre-trained encoder is adapted to downstream tasks (segmentation or generation) using standard task losses plus the invariance loss. Theoretically, the paper shows via a GΔG-divergence bound that learning X_h minimizes the modality-conditional domain gap d(M|H^S, M|H^U) at pre-training, leaving only the patient-level gap d(H^S, H^U) to be addressed at fine-tuning, yielding tighter generalization bounds than prior modality-alignment approaches.

## Results

On BRATS18 missing modality segmentation, PUIR achieves 79.78 mean DICE for Tumor Core, 63.49 for Enhancing Tumor, and 87.63 for Whole Tumor across all settings, outperforming the best baseline (mmFormer) by 3.35, 1.35, and 2.79 points respectively. Under the most challenging MN=3 setting (3 missing modalities), PUIR reaches 74.51/60.19/85.17 DICE for TC/ET/WT, surpassing the next best method by 3.06/11.33/4.72 points. For MRI modality transfer on BRATS23, PUIR achieves 30.76 PSNR (T1→T2) and 32.22 PSNR (T2→Flair), exceeding the best 3D baselines by over 5 dB. On AutoPET-II heterogeneous segmentation, PUIR attains 48.2 DICE, outperforming the best SSL pre-training baseline (VoCo, 46.3) by 1.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUIR consistently outperforms all five missing-modality segmentation baselines (RFNet, mmFormer, SPA, M3AE, M2F) across every setting on BRATS18, with the largest margins appearing under MN=3 where it gains 3.06 DICE on Tumor Core over mmFormer and 11.33 on Enhancing Tumor. Against the SwinUNETR pre-training baseline [44], PUIR improves by 0.59 DICE (TC), 4.02 (ET), and 1.08 (WT) on average, while also reducing standard deviation in most settings, indicating greater robustness. For MRI modality transfer on BRATS23, PUIR surpasses all 2D and 3D GAN/diffusion baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, NICEGAN, RegGAN, ResViT, MD-Diff, MS-SPADE) by 5–8 dB in PSNR, e.g., 30.76 vs. 25.18 for T1→T2 and 32.22 vs. 24.60 for T2→Flair. On heterogeneous PET/CT segmentation (AutoPET-II), PUIR achieves 48.2 DICE, beating VoCo (46.3), SwinMM (44.2), SwinUNETR pre-training (44.1), PCRLv2 (41.9), and from-scratch nnU-Net (33.1) and SwinUNETR (43.5). In the complex cross-domain scenario (HNSCC→NSCLC), PUIR improves SSIM by 0.108 over SwinUNETR for NAC→CT transfer (0.694 vs. 0.585) and by 0.025 for NAC→AC (0.952 vs. 0.927), confirming transferability to unseen patient populations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SwinUNETR self-supervised pre-training framework (Tang et al., CVPR 2022) for its encoder backbone and contrastive learning baseline, but departs fundamentally from prior medical pre-training methods that learn population-level or class-level invariant representations (e.g., anatomical invariance in [25]). The key conceptual departure is the personalized invariant representation hypothesis (X_h): rather than erasing modality-specific information into a shared Z_h, the paper argues that a per-individual representation invariant across modalities and geometric transformations—and decomposable back into any modality—is what drives generalization. The learnable biological prior O, retrieved via attention and fused with encoder features, together with the joint invariance–equivariance–decomposition constraint set, has no direct analogue in prior medical SSL or cross-modality transfer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically critical gap: patients often lack access to all imaging modalities, and models must generalize across both structural (MRI, CT) and functional (PET) modalities as well as across individuals. The theoretical decomposition of the GΔG divergence into patient-level and modality-level components (Theorem 3.2) provides a principled justification for the two-stage design, and the consistent gains across BRATS, AutoPET-II, HNSCC, and NSCLC suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the learnable biological prior O converges to interpretable biological knowledge, how the approach scales to larger patient populations or more modality combinations, and whether the static-biological-profile assumption (Hypothesis 3.1) holds for longitudinal or progressive-disease settings.
