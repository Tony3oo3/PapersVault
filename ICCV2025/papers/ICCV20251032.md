## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based SLAM methods for outdoor RGB-only scenarios face two intertwined challenges: differentiable-rendering-based tracking lacks geometric priors and struggles to converge in complex outdoor environments, while methods that introduce independent tracking modules accumulate scale drift under large rotations and displacements, degrading both localization and reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB video sequences (200–300 frames) with known camera intrinsics and produces per-frame camera poses, a persistent 3D Gaussian scene map, photorealistic novel view renderings, and rendered depth maps. A pre-trained MASt3R pointmap model is invoked per-frame pair to generate 3D pointmaps and confidence scores, but these are used only as a correspondence bridge rather than as direct pose or depth estimates, keeping the 3DGS map's own scale as the single source of geometric truth.

## Method

S3PO-GS introduces a self-consistent 3DGS pointmap tracking module that rasterizes a pointmap from the 3DGS map at the adjacent keyframe's viewpoint, then uses MASt3R to establish pixel-to-pixel correspondences between the keyframe and current frame, propagating them into 2D-3D correspondences between the rendered pointmap and the new image; RANSAC+PnP on these correspondences yields a scale-consistent relative pose that is refined via photometric loss with explicit gradients in the CUDA pipeline, achieving convergence in ~5 iterations versus 50–100 for baselines. For mapping, a patch-based dynamic mapping module segments both the rendered and pre-trained pointmaps into small patches, selects patches with similar mean/std distributions for normalization, identifies 'correct points' to compute a local scaling factor that aligns the pre-trained pointmap to the 3DGS scene scale, and then replaces poorly reconstructed rendered points with the aligned pre-trained points before inserting new Gaussians. The aligned pointmap additionally provides a geometric L1 supervision loss during local bundle-adjustment-style map optimization, combined with photometric loss and isotropic regularization on Gaussian scales.

## Results

S3PO-GS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis across all three datasets, with PSNR of 26.73 (Waymo), 20.03 (KITTI), and 29.97 (DL3DV), surpassing the best 3DGS-based baselines by +2.73, +4.42, and +4.98 dB respectively. For tracking accuracy (ATE RMSE), S3PO-GS achieves 0.622 m on Waymo (comparable to GlORIE-SLAM's 0.589 m), 1.048 m on KITTI, and 0.032 m on DL3DV, the best among all methods on KITTI and DL3DV. The method also requires only 5 pose-optimization iterations to match the accuracy of 100 iterations in MonoGS, demonstrating a 10× reduction in convergence cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest 3DGS-based outdoor competitor OpenGS-SLAM, S3PO-GS reduces tracking error by 67.5% on KITTI (1.048 vs 3.224 m) and 77.3% on DL3DV (0.032 vs 0.141 m), while also improving PSNR by +4.42 and +5.22 dB respectively. On Waymo, S3PO-GS's ATE (0.622 m) is marginally worse than GlORIE-SLAM (0.589 m) but far better than OpenGS-SLAM (0.839 m) and MonoGS (8.529 m); however, S3PO-GS dominates all NVS metrics on Waymo with PSNR 26.73 vs 23.99 (OpenGS-SLAM) and 21.80 (MonoGS). Compared to MonoGS, which relies purely on differentiable rendering for pose estimation, S3PO-GS achieves dramatically better tracking (0.622 vs 8.529 m on Waymo) and converges in 5 iterations where MonoGS needs 100. Against NeRF-based methods (NeRF-SLAM, NICER-SLAM), S3PO-GS is orders of magnitude better in tracking (e.g., 0.622 vs 97.36 m on Waymo) and substantially better in reconstruction quality. Photo-SLAM, which uses an independent tracking module, shows large ATE values (17–20 m) due to scale drift, confirming the paper's motivation for self-consistent scale handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS SLAM lineage initiated by MonoGS [21] and OpenGS-SLAM [41], which either rely on differentiable rendering for pose estimation (lacking geometric priors) or decouple tracking from mapping via external modules (accumulating scale drift). The key conceptual departure is anchoring pose estimation to the 3DGS-rendered pointmap itself, using MASt3R [18] solely as a correspondence bridge rather than a depth source, thereby making scale self-consistent by construction. The patch-based local scale alignment for the mapping module further distinguishes this work from prior global alignment strategies used in methods like OpenGS-SLAM and MGS-SLAM [43].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent failure modes in outdoor RGB-only 3DGS SLAM—scale drift and insufficient geometric priors—with a unified, self-consistent design that reduces pose optimization to ~10% of the iterations required by MonoGS while achieving SOTA tracking and NVS across Waymo, KITTI, and DL3DV. Practically, the efficiency gain (5 iterations vs. 100) is significant for real-time deployment. However, the system is limited to static scenes and short sequences (200–300 frames), and the authors explicitly defer loop closure and large-scale scene handling to future work, leaving open whether the self-consistent scale property holds over much longer trajectories with accumulated drift in the 3DGS map itself.
