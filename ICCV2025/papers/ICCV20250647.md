## Problem

Existing document rectification methods uniformly predict warping flows across the entire image, misaligning optimization with the actual goal of preserving readable foreground content (text, table lines, graphics) that occupies only a small pixel fraction. Foreground-aware approaches like DocGeoNet and FTDR rely on detection models that fail on geometrically distorted text lines and consider only text lines, ignoring other critical foreground elements such as table borders and vector graphics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250647/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single distorted RGB document image resized to 288×288×3 and produces a high-resolution backward deformation field (used to warp the image into its rectified form) along with a pixel-wise binary foreground mask. Notably, training requires only undistorted reference images: foreground labels (character-level masks via fine-tuned Hi-SAM, text lines via PaddleOCR, and straight lines via LSD) are extracted from clean images and then warped by randomly sampled DOC3D distortion fields to synthesize paired training data, eliminating the need for manually annotated distorted-undistorted pairs.

## Method

ForCenNet combines a CNN feature extractor with a three-layer Efficient Transformer Encoder (using overlapping patch embeddings and Spatial Pooling Windows for reduced attention cost), a lightweight foreground segmentation head, and a Mask-Guided Transformer Decoder. The key architectural innovation is the mask-guided self-attention in the decoder, where the predicted foreground mask is expanded sequentially and added to the attention logits (scaled by a learnable factor σ) to bias attention toward readable regions. A curvature consistency loss samples points every 4 pixels along extracted line elements, projects them through both the predicted and ground-truth deformation fields via bilinear interpolation, computes discrete curvature at each point using central differences, and penalizes the L1 curvature mismatch—providing geometric structure supervision that pixel-level L1 loss cannot capture for thin linear elements. The full training objective combines foreground segmentation L1 loss, backward map L1 regression, and the curvature consistency loss, optimized with AdamW and a OneCycle scheduler over 30 epochs on two A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the DocUNet benchmark, ForCenNet achieves the best MS-SSIM (0.582), LD (4.82), AD (0.19), and CER (0.136) among all compared methods, though CGU-Net retains a slight edge on Edit Distance (513.76 vs. 571.40). On DIR300, ForCenNet sets new records across all five metrics: MS-SSIM 0.713, LD 4.65, AD 0.123, ED 390.61, and CER 0.138. For cross-domain generalization, ForCenNet leads on WarpDoc (MS-SSIM 0.54, LD 8.10, AD 0.18, ED 899.67) and DocReal (MS-SSIM 0.595, LD 6.95, AD 0.17), with only a marginal ED gap to DocReal's own method (753.12 vs. 736.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForCenNet outperforms all 16 baselines on the majority of metrics across four benchmarks. Against the strongest foreground-based competitors (FTDR, DocGeoNet, RDGR, LA-DocFlatten), ForCenNet improves MS-SSIM by 0.05–0.09 and reduces LD by 1.6–3.7 points on DocUNet, while on DIR300 it surpasses FTDR (the previous leader) by 0.106 in MS-SSIM and 3.03 in LD. The only metric where ForCenNet trails a prior method is Edit Distance on DocUNet, where CGU-Net achieves 513.76 vs. ForCenNet's 571.40; however, ForCenNet recovers the lead on ED for DIR300 (390.61 vs. CGU-Net's 735.95) and both generalization benchmarks. Weakly supervised methods (PaperEdge, FDRNet, DRNet) are consistently outperformed by large margins (e.g., LD 4.82 vs. 7.42–8.49 on DocUNet). On the cross-domain DocReal benchmark, ForCenNet achieves the best MS-SSIM (0.595) and LD (6.95) but is marginally behind DocReal's own method on ED (753.12 vs. 736.69), suggesting a slight trade-off in OCR fidelity under extreme real-world conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForCenNet sits at the intersection of three lines of document dewarping research: transformer-based deformation field prediction (DocTr, DocGeoNet), foreground-constrained rectification (FTDR, RDGR, DDCP), and weakly supervised training (PaperEdge, FDRNet, DRNet). It departs from prior foreground-aware methods by extracting foreground elements from undistorted reference images rather than relying on detection models applied to distorted inputs, which the authors argue fail under geometric deformation. The curvature consistency loss and mask-guided self-attention in the decoder are novel contributions not present in any single prior work, while the label generation pipeline repurposes Hi-SAM (a hierarchical text segmentation model) and LSD (a classical line detector) in a document-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The foreground-centric label generation strategy is practically significant because it eliminates the need for paired distorted-undistorted training data, enabling rapid model iteration on any collection of clean document scans. The SOTA results across four benchmarks (DocUNet, DIR300, WarpDoc, DocReal) validate the approach, and the cross-domain evaluation without fine-tuning suggests reasonable generalization. However, the ablation showing performance plateauing beyond ~1,000 synthetic deformation fields hints at a ceiling in the current formulation, and the reliance on Hi-SAM for segmentation introduces a dependency on a model fine-tuned for text that may not generalize to all document types. The authors note future integration with scanning and enhancement pipelines, but the broader question of whether foreground-centric supervision can replace full-pixel supervision entirely remains open.
