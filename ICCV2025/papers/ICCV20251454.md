## Problem

Existing physics-based haze synthesis pipelines cannot capture the full complexity of real-world haze and offer limited controllability, causing state-of-the-art dehazing models trained on synthetic data to fail on real hazy images. The paper asks whether pre-trained latent diffusion models, trained on vast natural image distributions, can be adapted to generate realistic, reference-controllable haze in a single step, and whether such generated data can unlock the latent potential of existing dehazing baselines with minimal fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (512×512) and a reference real haze image as inputs, producing a single hazy RGB image (512×512, later resized to 256×256 for downstream dehazing fine-tuning) in one inference step without classifier-free guidance. The reference haze image is consumed through a lightweight 4-residual-block degradation encoder that extracts instance-level haze features, while text conditioning is derived offline from GPT-4o captions of 4,000 real haze images mapped through CLIP and averaged into a single general haze embedding.

## Method

GenHaze adapts SD-Turbo (an adversarially distilled LDM) via a clean-to-haze protocol: instead of adding Gaussian noise, the clean image's VAE latent z_T^l = E(x) is used directly as the noisy input, and the UNet is trained to predict the hazy image latent z_0^l = E(x_hazy) in a single forward pass, supervised by an L1 loss between the decoded output and the target hazy image. For text conditioning, an offline haze database of 4,000 GPT-4o captions is encoded with CLIP and averaged into a general haze embedding, to which a learnable prompt embedding with positional encoding is added for instance-specific adaptation. For haze controllability, a lightweight degradation encoder extracts features from the reference haze image, and StyleGANv2 modulation-demodulation convolutions are inserted after each UNet stage to inject haze-specific style while suppressing background interference—replacing the heavier ControlNet approach. The UNet and VAE are fine-tuned with LoRA (r=8 and r=4 respectively) for 200K steps on 512×512 images, and downstream dehazing baselines are improved by fine-tuning for only 1–2 epochs on the generated paired data without any architectural modifications.

## Results

On the paired Haze4K benchmark, GenHaze achieves PSNR 27.06 dB, SSIM 0.9662, LPIPS 0.0309, FID 15.92, and sFID 47.76, outperforming all baselines by large margins (e.g., 6.19 dB over the next-best Low-Res on PSNR, and 6.92 lower FID). On the unpaired RTTS real-haze benchmark, GenHaze attains FID 57.12 and sFID 105.43, again the best among all methods. For downstream dehazing, fine-tuning baselines for only 1–2 epochs on GenHaze-generated images yields substantial gains across 7 non-reference metrics; for example, MSBDN's FADE score on RTTS drops from 1.58 to 0.69 (≈55% reduction) and BRISQUE from 28.51 to 20.23.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenHaze outperforms every compared baseline on all reported metrics for haze generation. Against the closest competitor Low-Res [6] (regression-based, controllable), GenHaze improves PSNR by 6.19 dB (27.06 vs. 20.87), reduces FID by 7.02 (15.92 vs. 22.94), and lowers sFID by 9.57 (47.76 vs. 57.33) on Haze4K; on RTTS it achieves FID 57.12 vs. 60.65 and sFID 105.43 vs. 111.76. CycleGAN-Turbo [59] (GAN, non-controllable) is second-best on RTTS FID (60.14) but trails GenHaze by 3.02 FID and 3.30 sFID, and is far behind on Haze4K pixel-level metrics. InstructPix2Pix [4] (20-step diffusion, non-controllable) is the weakest baseline, with FID 180.09 on Haze4K and 115.70 on RTTS, roughly 3× worse than GenHaze. RIDCP [79] (physics-based, non-controllable) reports only RTTS scores (FID 59.21, sFID 108.81), still behind GenHaze. For downstream dehazing, GenHaze-enhanced baselines (e.g., MSBDN, DehazeFormer, T³-DiffWeather) surpass the SOTA real-world method KANet [21] on multiple metrics after just 1–2 epochs of fine-tuning, whereas KANet itself shows minimal further improvement (+GenHaze yields only −0.02 FADE on RTTS), confirming that GenHaze unlocks latent capacity in existing architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenHaze repurposes the pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as a degradation synthesizer rather than a from-scratch image generator, building on adversarial diffusion distillation (SD-Turbo) to collapse multi-step sampling into a single inference step. The clean-to-haze protocol—replacing Gaussian noise with the clean image as the diffusion input—draws conceptual inspiration from InstructPix2Pix's image-editing paradigm but diverges by eliminating the noise-concatenation mechanism and operating in one step. For haze controllability, the paper departs from ControlNet's heavy encoder-duplication approach in favor of a lightweight degradation encoder combined with StyleGANv2's modulation-demodulation convolution, injecting reference haze features at every UNet stage rather than only at the decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LDMs can serve as practical, controllable degradation generators, offering a principled alternative to physics-based haze synthesis that struggles with real-world complexity and parameter controllability. The finding that merely 1–2 epochs of fine-tuning existing dehazing baselines with GenHaze-generated paired data yields substantial gains across 7 non-reference metrics suggests that architectural limitations are less binding than training-data quality—a practically important insight for the broader image-restoration community. Open questions include whether the clean-to-degraded protocol generalizes to other degradations (rain, snow, fog, blur), whether reference-free generation is feasible, and how the approach scales to higher resolutions or video.
