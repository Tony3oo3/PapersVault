## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs rely on large numbers of visual tokens from encoders (hundreds for images, thousands for videos), creating prohibitive computational costs that limit deployment on resource-constrained devices and long-context tasks. Existing token reduction methods either require fine-tuning, apply only to image LLMs, or prune tokens at a single fixed layer, leaving no mechanism to adaptively match a desired computational budget while preserving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained multi-modal LLMs during inference, consuming uniformly sampled video frames (e.g., 32 or 192 frames) or a single RGB image along with a text prompt. The visual encoder produces token embeddings that are processed before entering the LLM; for video, tokens are merged only within individual frames to preserve temporal ordering. The output is a standard text response, and the method introduces negligible overhead (0.6% FLOPs for Qwen2-7B, 0.03% for Vicuna-7B) relative to the LLM inference cost it reduces.

## Method

AIM is a training-free, two-stage token reduction pipeline. First, before the LLM, visually similar tokens are iteratively merged by computing pairwise cosine similarity between adjacent token sets and averaging the embeddings of the most similar pairs, reducing the token count to a configurable retention ratio (e.g., 25%). Second, within the LLM, a PageRank algorithm applied to softmax-normalized self-attention weights scores each token's importance at every layer; only visual tokens (never text tokens) are pruned, with a piecewise-linear scheduler controlling the retention ratio—no pruning in early layers, progressive linear reduction in middle layers, and complete removal of visual tokens in later layers. By jointly tuning the merging retention ratio and the scheduler parameters (l₁, l₂), the method spans a 40-fold FLOPs reduction with less than 13% accuracy drop, enabling adaptive inference matched to arbitrary computational constraints.

## Results

On video benchmarks, AIM reduces FLOPs by 6.8× (99.63→14.76 TB) and prefill time by 8.0× (439.58→55.03 ms) relative to the base LLaVA-OV-7B, while maintaining near-identical accuracy (e.g., VideoMME 58.2 vs. 58.2, MVBench 57.1 vs. 56.7, MLVU 63.7 vs. 64.7). On image benchmarks, AIM achieves a 3.7× FLOPs reduction (8.18→2.22 TB) with modest drops (e.g., VQA-v2 75.4 vs. 78.5, GQA 58.6 vs. 62.0). In the long-video setting, AIM with 192 frames at comparable FLOPs (99.27 vs. 99.63 TB) improves MLVU by +4.6 over the 32-frame base model, surpassing SOTA LongVA-7B (56.3). The method spans a 40-fold FLOPs range (99.63→2.51 TB) with less than 13% accuracy drop on VideoMME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIM achieves the lowest FLOPs (14.76 TB) and prefill time (55.03 ms) among all compared methods while matching or exceeding their accuracy on most video benchmarks. Against FastV (21.24 TB), AIM uses 30% fewer FLOPs and gains +2.3 on VideoMME (wo-subs), +1.2 on MVBench, +2.6 on MLVU, and +2.1 on EgoSchema. Against LLaVA-Prumerge (23.65 TB), AIM uses 38% fewer FLOPs and leads on VideoMME (+1.2 wo-subs), MVBench (+0.6), MLVU (+3.1), and NextQA (+0.8), though LLaVA-Prumerge edges ahead on EgoSchema (61.0 vs. 59.6). VTW and PDrop suffer large accuracy drops (e.g., VTW: 41.0 vs. 58.2 on VideoMME; PDrop: 51.7 vs. 58.2) at higher FLOPs, confirming that single-layer or stage-wise pruning is less effective than AIM's progressive approach. On image benchmarks (Table 3), at 2.22 TB FLOPs AIM outperforms LLaVA-Prumerge+ by +2.3 on VQA-v2, +3.3 on GQA, +33.2 on MME, and +3.3 on POPE, while at 1.00 TB it still leads on MMB (+2.3) and POPE (+3.3) over LLaVA-Prumerge. The one notable weakness is TextVQA, where AIM (53.8) trails LLaVA-Prumerge+ (55.2) and FastV (57.3), likely because text-rich images require preserving fine-grained visual tokens that similarity-based merging removes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the token merging concept from Bolya et al. (ICLR 2023, originally for ViTs) to the multi-modal LLM setting by applying it after the visual encoder rather than within it, making it encoder-agnostic. It departs from prior MLLM token-pruning methods (FastV, VTW, PDrop, LLaVA-Prumerge) that prune at a single layer or fixed stage, instead combining pre-LLM similarity-based merging with progressive in-LLM pruning via PageRank on attention weights, creating a continuous efficiency spectrum. The adaptive inference framing draws on the broader dynamic neural network literature but is the first to apply it specifically to multi-modal LLMs in a training-free manner spanning both video and image models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 6.8× FLOPs reduction for video LLMs and 3.7× for image LLMs with minimal accuracy loss, plus the ability to sample 6× more video frames at the same compute budget, directly addressing the long-video understanding bottleneck. The finding that only ~25% of visual tokens are necessary and that LLMs perform cross-modal fusion in early layers while shifting to text-centric reasoning in later layers provides architectural guidance for future efficient MLLM design. Limitations include degraded performance on text-intensive tasks (TextVQA) and the reliance on hand-tuned scheduler parameters (l₁, l₂) rather than learned or input-adaptive scheduling, leaving open whether the pruning schedule can be automatically determined per input.
