## Problem

Video-MLLMs are bottlenecked by the context length of their underlying LLMs, forcing a tradeoff between the number of input frames and spatial resolution. Existing remedies—token compression and streaming inference—either lose fine-grained visual information or incur latency proportional to the context extension. The paper asks whether a single inference pass can achieve comprehensive perception of exponentially longer video contexts without sacrificing either granularity or speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250811/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames sampled at FPS=1 (up to 512 frames, each encoded as 182 vision tokens by the LLaVA-Video vision encoder) together with a text question. The key structural detail is that the long vision token sequence is partitioned into N parallel reference chunks (N=2, 4, or 8 for 2×, 4×, 8× frame expansion), each chunk receiving an identical copy of the system prompt and question. The output is a standard text answer produced by the LLM decoder after the MoRef-attention and optional reference-fusion steps.

## Method

Free-MoRef is a training-free inference-time modification to any Video-MLLM. First, the long vision token sequence is split into N parallel reference chunks via multi-reference partitioning, where each chunk aggregates temporally interleaved fragments from M temporal units to preserve global temporal coverage. Second, the LLM's self-attention is replaced with MoRef-attention: flash-attention is computed independently within each chunk, and the question-token outputs across chunks are aggregated using a query-aware gating weight derived from the cross-modal (question-to-vision) attention map, yielding a unified query activation per layer. Third, at a configurable decoder layer L, a Reference Fusion step prunes the less-important vision tokens (based on the same attention importance scores) and merges the surviving tokens from all chunks into a single global reference, which is then processed by the remaining standard decoder layers. This three-stage design reduces FLOPs to under 27.6% of full-attention inference while enabling 2×–8× context extension on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On a single A100 GPU, Free-MoRef applied to LLaVA-Video-7B achieves 72.8 on MLVU, 59.9 on LongVideoBench, and 66.9 overall on VideoMME (56.0 on the long-video subset), while processing up to 512 input frames (8× the default 64) at only 400% of the baseline FLOPs (vs. 6400% for direct inference). Compared to the unmodified LLaVA-Video baseline (70.2 / 58.2 / 64.3), Free-MoRef yields gains of +2.6 on MLVU, +1.7 on LongVideoBench, and +2.6 on VideoMME overall. The method also reduces inference FLOPs by 72.4% relative to naively doubling the context (110.4% vs. 400% FLOPs at 128 frames).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free-MoRef outperforms all ten compared baselines on every metric where data overlaps. On MLVU, it leads the closest competitors LLaVA-Video (70.2) and RETAKE (69.8) by 2.6 and 3.0 points respectively. On LongVideoBench, it surpasses LLaVA-Video (58.2) by 1.7 points and InternVL2.5 (57.5) by 2.4 points. On VideoMME overall, Free-MoRef (66.9) beats InternVL2.5 (64.5) by 2.4 points and RETAKE (63.9) by 3.0 points. The only near-tie is VideoMME Long, where Free-MoRef (56.0) trails RETAKE (56.2) by a negligible 0.2 points. Notably, Free-MoRef is training-free and applied on top of the standard LLaVA-Video checkpoint, yet it surpasses dedicatedly trained long-video models such as LongVILA, LongVA, and Video-XL by 3–10 points across benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Free-MoRef draws a conceptual analogy from the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm, repurposing the idea of parallel expert routing to partition long vision token sequences into multiple 'reference' chunks that are queried simultaneously. It departs from the two dominant strategies for long video understanding—token compression (FastV, PruneVid, Video-XL) and streaming inference (RETAKE, Inf-MLLM)—by avoiding both information loss from compression and the linear latency penalty of sequential KV-cache reuse. The reference fusion step is grounded in FastV's observation that attention concentrates on query tokens in deep decoder layers, and the overall design is explicitly training-free, distinguishing it from context-extension methods like LongVILA and LongVA that require post-training on long-sequence data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a training-free, single-GPU method that extends context by 2×–8× at under 27.6% of the FLOPs while surpassing dedicatedly trained long-video models offers an immediately deployable solution for practitioners constrained by memory and latency. However, the evaluation is limited to a single base model (LLaVA-Video-7B with Qwen2), and the heuristic gating (max-attention weighting) and fixed hyperparameters (M, N, L) raise open questions about generalizability to other architectures and whether the MoRef-attention mechanism can be learned end-to-end for further gains. The paper also notes that the approach is orthogonal to and composable with streaming inference or token compression, suggesting a path toward hybrid strategies for even longer contexts.
