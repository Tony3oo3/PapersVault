## Problem

Traditional deep networks and, for the first time demonstrated here, Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) exhibit texture bias because their unconstrained expressivity allows them to capture dataset-specific high-frequency details rather than structural shape cues. While neuromanifold theory shows that low-degree function spaces contain shape-fair networks, no prior work has combined this regularization with KANs or explicitly separated local and global shape cue extraction stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (64×64 from Tiny ImageNet, 32×32 from CIFAR-10) and produces class labels via a dual-branch convolutional KAN architecture. Unusually, the network maintains two parallel feature extractors with complementary neuromanifold degree constraints, and shape-fairness is evaluated on a novel cue conflict dataset where images are paired with style-transferred versions, measuring the ratio of correct shape decisions to total correct predictions.

## Method

NMR-KAN introduces two components: (1) Implicit Neuromanifold Regularization (INMR), which extends the neuromanifold degree formula to heterogeneous, layer-wise polynomial degrees (Corollary 1) and assigns complementary degree profiles to two KAN feature extractors—one constraining early layers to low degree for local shape cues while allowing higher degree later, and the other doing the inverse to prevent texture overfitting during global aggregation; (2) a Style Decorrelation Loss (SDL) that penalizes similarity of Gram-matrix style statistics between the two branches at the degree-transition layer, preventing representational collapse. The dual-branch design yields complementary effective receptive fields (Gaussian for short-range, non-Gaussian for long-range correlations), and the total loss combines cross-entropy with the SDL term. The approach is scalable because INMR constrains the function space directly rather than reducing parameter count.

## Results

On the cue conflict dataset, NMR-KAN achieves 57.3% shape bias, a 14.8-point improvement over the VGG-Gram CKAN baseline (42.5%), at the cost of a 7.3-point drop in Top-1 validation accuracy (50.1% vs. 57.4%). Under adversarial attacks on CIFAR-10, NMR-KAN attains 56.2% accuracy under PGD (ε=8/255, 10 steps) and 50.7% under AutoAttack, substantially outperforming ResNet-18 (32.8% and 23.8% respectively). On Tiny ImageNet-C, NMR-KAN achieves the lowest relative mean corruption error (mCE) of 92.5, compared to 105.7 for the baseline VGG-Gram and 96.9 for Top-K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VGG-Gram CKAN baseline, NMR-KAN improves shape bias by 14.8 points (57.3% vs. 42.5%) but sacrifices 7.3 points of Top-1 accuracy (50.1% vs. 57.4%), reflecting the known accuracy–shape-bias trade-off. Compared to Top-K activation sparsity [31], NMR-KAN wins on shape bias (57.3% vs. ~43–44%), PGD robustness (56.2% vs. 46.4%), AutoAttack (50.7% vs. 50.5%), and corruption mCE (92.5 vs. 96.9), while Top-K edges out NMR-KAN on DeepFool (69.4% vs. 79.6%—actually NMR-KAN wins here too at 79.6%). Against ResNet-18, NMR-KAN dominates on PGD (56.2% vs. 32.8%) and AutoAttack (50.7% vs. 23.8%) but trails slightly on DeepFool (79.6% vs. 83.0%). DuFeNet [46], which uses auxiliary edge supervision, achieves only 39.7% shape bias and 41.6% Top-1 on the cue conflict dataset, well below NMR-KAN on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, which learn nonlinearities directly rather than using fixed activations; (2) neuromanifold theory, which characterizes the function space a given architecture can represent and provides a principled way to constrain expressivity; and (3) the shape-vs-texture bias literature in CNNs (Geirhos et al., Li et al. Top-K sparsity, DuFeNet). It departs from prior shape-bias mitigation strategies—data augmentation, sparsity-based activation, and edge-feature injection—by proposing an architectural regularization that directly constrains the polynomial degree of the neuromanifold in a stage-aware manner (low degree for local cues, high degree for global cues, and vice versa in a second branch), combined with a style decorrelation loss to prevent the two branches from collapsing into redundant feature extractors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that KANs' adaptive nonlinearities do not automatically confer shape-fairness, and that unconstrained KANs suffer the same texture-bias pathology as traditional CNNs. The neuromanifold regularization offers a mathematically grounded, scalable alternative to ad-hoc sparsity or augmentation tricks, and the dual-branch design with complementary effective receptive fields (Gaussian vs. non-Gaussian) gives an interpretable mechanism for local-global shape processing. However, the 7.3% Top-1 accuracy drop alongside the 14.8% shape-bias gain highlights the well-known accuracy–shape-bias trade-off, and all shape-bias evaluations are conducted on 64×64 Tiny ImageNet with a single cue-conflict test set, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or larger-scale benchmarks. Future work should investigate whether the neuromanifold degree schedule can be learned end-to-end and whether the style decorrelation loss generalizes beyond the two-branch setting.
