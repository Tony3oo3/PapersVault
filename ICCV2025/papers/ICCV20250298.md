## Problem

Diffusion models are computationally intensive, making deployment on resource-constrained platforms challenging. While two natural acceleration strategies exist—reducing denoising steps or reducing per-step inference cost—there is no systematic study of which is more effective under a post-training (training-free) setting. The paper addresses this gap by investigating the fidelity-efficiency trade-off between these two strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250298/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image (768×768 for SD V1.5/LCM, 1024×1024 for SDXL/PixArt-α) using pre-trained diffusion models without any fine-tuning. A key operational detail is that during early denoising steps, the latent representation operates at a reduced resolution (scaled by factor β, e.g., 0.5 or 0.75), and only after a calibrated transition step sT does the process switch to full resolution. The method is entirely training-free and can be instantly applied to any pre-trained diffusion model.

## Method

PostDiff is a training-free compression framework that reduces redundancy at two levels. At the input level, a mixed-resolution denoising scheme starts from a lower-resolution latent (scaled by β) for the first sT steps to generate low-frequency structure, then upscales the predicted clean image via bilinear interpolation and re-noises it to full resolution for the remaining steps—exploiting the observation that low-frequency components dominate early denoising and that reduced resolution actually enhances them. At the module level, a hybrid caching strategy combines DeepCache-style reuse of U-Net deep skip branches (updated every k steps) with caching of the conditional cross-attention map at step m, which is reused for all subsequent steps to eliminate redundant classifier-free guidance computations. The paper's key empirical finding is that, for preserving high generation fidelity, reducing per-step inference cost via these techniques is generally more effective than simply reducing the number of denoising steps, as fewer steps introduce larger inter-step variability that amplifies compression artifacts.

## Results

On SD V1.5 (20 denoising steps), PostDiff achieves FID 16.65 and Clip Score 30.25 with 11.13T FLOPs (63.14% reduction from the original 30.42T) and 1.14s latency, outperforming all seven compared training-free compression methods on FID while maintaining competitive Clip Score. On PixArt-α, PostDiff achieves FID 25.44 (vs. 29.16 original) with 54.72T FLOPs (36.2% reduction). Across SDXL, LCM, and PixArt-α, PostDiff consistently improves or maintains FID while reducing FLOPs by 36–63%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PostDiff achieves the best FID (16.65) and fastest latency (1.139s) among all eight methods on SD V1.5, outperforming the closest FID competitor ToMe (17.43) by 0.78 FID and the fastest baseline DeepCache (1.737s) by 0.60s in latency. On Clip Score, PostDiff (30.25) trails the original (30.80) and DeepCache (30.75) by 0.55 and 0.50 points respectively, but remains competitive with ToMe (30.55) and ToDo (30.75). On PixArt-α, PostDiff achieves FID 25.44, substantially outperforming TGATE (29.54), DuCa (28.08), and DiTFastAttn (45.46), while matching the original's Clip Score (30.23 vs. 30.41). The key finding is that PostDiff's combined input-level (mixed-resolution) and module-level (hybrid caching) strategy dominates single-technique baselines: module-caching-only methods like DeepCache and TGATE reduce latency but cannot match PostDiff's FID, while token-level methods like ToMe and ToDo preserve quality better but offer less speedup. The paper's central empirical claim—that reducing per-step cost via PostDiff is more effective than simply reducing denoising steps for preserving fidelity—is supported by Table 2, where 20-step PostDiff (FID 16.65) beats 8-step original (FID 20.60) at comparable FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PostDiff sits at the intersection of two lines of diffusion model acceleration work: input-level token compression (ToMe, ToDo) and module-level cross-step caching (DeepCache, TGATE, Cache Me If You Can). It departs from cascade diffusion models, which achieve similar low-then-high-resolution denoising but require multiple separately trained models, by achieving this with a single pretrained model in a fully training-free manner. The hybrid caching strategy unifies U-Net skip-branch caching (DeepCache) with cross-attention caching (TGATE) into a single framework, and the mixed-resolution denoising scheme is a novel input-level technique not previously explored in a post-training setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete, training-free recipe for deploying pretrained diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware, and its central empirical finding—that reducing per-step cost via mixed resolution and caching often outperforms simply reducing denoising steps for fidelity preservation—offers actionable deployment guidance. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (MS-COCO 2014), a binary (two-level) resolution schedule, and no integration with other compression axes like quantization or pruning. Open questions include whether the mixed-resolution insight generalizes to video diffusion, whether more granular resolution schedules yield further gains, and how PostDiff interacts with emerging few-step distilled models where the step-to-step redundancy structure differs.
