## Problem

Existing zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) methods craft or learn text prompts that capture only coarse-grained abnormality semantics (e.g., 'damaged', 'defective'), producing overly homogeneous abnormal patterns that fail to model the diverse, fine-grained deviations from normality observed across different datasets. This limits cross-dataset generalization, particularly for anomalies that deviate subtly from normal patterns rather than matching a single high-level defect category.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (h×w×3) and outputs both a scalar image-level anomaly score in [0,1] and a pixel-level anomaly map of shape h×w with values in [0,1]. The pixel-level map is computed by averaging four segmentation maps (normal/abnormal scores, each with and without the data-dependent prior), while the image-level score combines a cosine-similarity-based classification score with the maximum pixel-level anomaly score. Training requires an auxiliary dataset with image-level labels and pixel-level anomaly masks; at test time, no target-dataset data or fine-tuning is needed.

## Method

FAPrompt builds on a frozen CLIP backbone and introduces two modules. The Compound Abnormality Prompt (CAP) module learns K abnormality prompts, each constructed by concatenating shared learnable normal tokens with unique learnable abnormal tokens followed by a fixed 'object' token, ensuring all abnormal prompts remain semantically anchored to normality while capturing distinct fine-grained deviations; an orthogonal constraint loss on the text-encoder embeddings of the K prompts enforces complementarity, and the final abnormality embedding is their average. The Data-dependent Abnormality Prior (DAP) module selects the top-M patch tokens most similar to the abnormality prototype from each query image, passes them through a small learnable network to produce a sample-wise prior vector, and adds this vector element-wise to every abnormal token in each prompt, dynamically adapting the prompts to the specific test image; a prior loss forces this vector toward zero for normal training images to prevent noise injection. Training optimizes focal and dice losses on pixel-level segmentation maps plus a focal loss on the image-level score, with only the learnable tokens and prompts updated while CLIP parameters remain frozen.

## Results

FAPrompt achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot anomaly detection across 19 industrial and medical datasets. On image-level detection (AUROC, AP), it improves over the best competing methods by up to 3.9% AUROC and 3.0% AP on industrial datasets and 2.3% AUROC and 1.3% AP on medical datasets. On pixel-level detection (AUROC, PRO), it surpasses the best baselines by up to 1.4% AUROC and 4.0% PRO on industrial datasets and 3.2% AUROC and 4.2% PRO on medical datasets. The model is trained on MVTecAD (or VisA for MVTecAD evaluation) and tested zero-shot on all other datasets without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAPrompt outperforms all 10 baselines on the majority of 13 image-level datasets, with the strongest competitors being AnomalyCLIP (ICLR 2024) and FiLO (ACM MM 2024), which also use learnable text prompts. FAPrompt wins on both AUROC and AP on 11 of 13 datasets; it trails FiLO on AITEX (74.1 vs. 78.2 AUROC) and slightly loses on AP for MVTecAD (95.7 vs. 96.2) and VisA (86.8 vs. 87.3). Handcrafted-prompt methods (CLIP, CLIP-AC, WinCLIP, AnoVL) are consistently weaker, with raw CLIP achieving only 34.5 AUROC on BTAD versus FAPrompt's 92.2. General-purpose prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) underperform significantly on anomaly-specific tasks due to their focus on class-level discrimination rather than fine-grained abnormality semantics. BLIP, pretrained on smaller-scale data, is less effective than CLIP-based methods across nearly all datasets. The largest margins over the best baseline appear on BTAD (+3.9 AUROC over AnomalyCLIP), MPDD (+3.1 AUROC), and BrainMRI (+1.3 AUROC over FiLO).

## Relation to Prior Work

FAPrompt extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) line initiated by AnomalyCLIP, WinCLIP, and AnoVL, which learn or handcraft text prompts to separate normal from abnormal semantics. Its key departure is the compound prompting design: rather than learning a single coarse abnormality prompt, CAP builds K complementary abnormal prompts on top of a shared normal prompt with an orthogonal constraint, explicitly modeling abnormality as fine-grained deviations from normality rather than a distinct semantic category. The DAP module repurposes CoCoOp's instance-conditional idea but shifts from image-level token embeddings to patch-level abnormal features, addressing the noise problem when normal images lack anomaly-related information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compound prompting + orthogonal constraint design offers a principled way to capture diverse abnormality patterns without requiring per-defect-type annotations, which is practically important for industrial and medical settings where anomaly types are open-ended. The consistent 3–5% gains across 19 datasets (industrial and medical) suggest the fine-grained decomposition is a meaningful improvement over treating anomaly as a single semantic class. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP backbones, whether K and M can be adapted per dataset without retraining, and whether the auxiliary training requirement (MVTecAD or VisA) limits true deployment in privacy-sensitive settings.
