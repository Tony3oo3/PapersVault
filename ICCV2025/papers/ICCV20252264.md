## Problem

Diffusion models generate high-quality close-up faces and hands but produce distorted local details when these regions occupy a small fraction of a full-body image, because the default global MSE loss allocates insufficient optimization attention to small regions. Existing remedies—separate expert modules (MoLE) or post-hoc inpainting (ADetailer)—address the symptom without resolving the underlying optimization imbalance between global and local generation objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252264/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For general text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a human image via a LoRA module (rank 256) on a frozen SDXL backbone (30 steps, CFG 7.5). For controllable generation, it additionally consumes a depth map and pose skeleton as ControlNet conditioning signals. Training uses ~50k curated images from CosmicMan and BodyHands, each annotated with face/hand positional masks (reshaped to latent resolution) and pose-depth pairs for the controllable variant.

## Method

FairHuman formulates human image generation as a three-objective optimization problem: a global MSE loss plus face- and hand-masked MSE losses, then applies the Minimum Potential Delay (MPD) fairness criterion from network resource allocation to derive a dynamic gradient-weighting scheme. At each step, the update direction d is expressed as a weighted sum of the three gradients, with weights W = (G(θ)ᵀG(θ))^(−2/3)·1 obtained in closed form, ensuring each objective's potential delay (inverse of its projected gradient contribution) is minimized simultaneously. This guarantees convergence to a Pareto-optimal solution where no single region's quality degrades to benefit another. The strategy is deployed by fine-tuning LoRA or ControlNet modules while keeping the SDXL backbone frozen, requiring no architectural changes to the diffusion model itself.

## Results

In the general T2I setting, FairHuman (LoRA) achieves the best scores across all metrics: HPS 32.73%, ImageReward 154.60, CLIP 27.20, FID 46.11, Hand Confidence 94.03%, and Face Confidence 85.31%, outperforming SDXL-base (FID 52.69) and MoLE (FID 48.97). In the controllable generation setting, FairHuman (ControlNet) attains the lowest FID (45.46), highest Hand Confidence (94.48%), highest Face Confidence (85.61%), and best pose alignment (Mean Diff 2.35), while trailing ControlNet-Union by a narrow margin on HPS (32.72 vs. 32.80) and ImageReward (154.74 vs. 155.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL-base, FairHuman (LoRA) improves FID by 6.58 points (52.69→46.11), Hand Confidence by 0.85%, and Face Confidence by 1.64%, while also gaining on HPS (+1.82%) and ImageReward (+13.75). Compared to MoLE, which uses a Mixture-of-Experts strategy with separate LoRA modules for hands and faces, FairHuman achieves better FID (46.11 vs. 48.97), higher Hand Confidence (94.03 vs. 93.32), and higher Face Confidence (85.31 vs. 84.48) while requiring less memory and inference time. In the controllable setting, FairHuman (ControlNet) outperforms the default ControlNet-SDXL on every metric, including FID (45.46 vs. 46.18) and pose alignment (2.35 vs. 3.45). Against the strongest controllable baseline ControlNet-Union, FairHuman wins on FID (45.46 vs. 46.43), Hand Confidence (94.48 vs. 94.28), Face Confidence (85.61 vs. 85.36), CLIP (27.24 vs. 27.21), and pose alignment (2.35 vs. 2.67), but trails slightly on HPS (32.72 vs. 32.80) and ImageReward (154.74 vs. 155.22), which the authors attribute to ControlNet-Union incorporating aesthetic/preference feedback during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work improving human-centric text-to-image generation in diffusion models, particularly MoLE [49] which fine-tunes separate LoRA experts for hands and faces and fuses them via a soft Mixture-of-Experts mechanism, and ControlNet [47] for controllable generation. It departs from these approaches by reframing the problem as a multi-objective optimization task with three simultaneous losses (global, face, hand) and introducing the Minimum Potential Delay (MPD) fairness criterion from communication networks to dynamically allocate gradient weights, rather than relying on fixed scalarization or separate expert modules. The deployment strategy (LoRA for general T2I, ControlNet for controllable generation) follows established practice, but the joint fairness-aware optimization within a single model is the key methodological departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, general-purpose optimization framework for balancing competing objectives in diffusion model fine-tuning, which could extend beyond human image generation to any multi-region quality problem. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines (MoLE, ControlNet-Union) are modest—roughly 0.5–1 point on HPS and 2–3 points on FID—and the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (SDXL) and a single dataset of ~50k images. Open questions include whether the MPD fairness principle scales to more than three objectives (e.g., adding feet, eyes, hair), whether it generalizes to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the hand-crafted positional masks can be replaced by learned or detector-based alternatives for greater robustness.
