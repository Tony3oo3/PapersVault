## Problem

Whole slide images (WSIs) can reach 50,000×50,000 pixels, making direct deep learning infeasible, while the dominant two-stage MIL pipeline disrupts multi-scale information flow by decoupling local feature extraction from global aggregation. Existing end-to-end alternatives like LongViT rely on heavily pruned attention and require 64 A100 GPUs, limiting practicality. The paper addresses how to efficiently model both local inductive biases (e.g., cellular morphology) and long-range dependencies (e.g., tumor heterogeneity across centimeters) in a single end-to-end pass over the full WSI.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full WSI at variable magnification (0.6×–2.5× in experiments, vs. 20× for two-stage baselines) and serializes it into 1×1 pixel-level RGB tokens using a region-based zigzag scan over 224×224 windows, inserting a CLS token per region. The network progressively reduces token count via Region Fusion while expanding each token's spatial receptive field from 1×1 to 32×32, ultimately producing a single averaged slide-level CLS token. This token feeds task-specific heads: a 4-class linear layer for tumor staging, a hazard-function head for survival analysis, or a standard classifier for ImageNet-1K.

## Method

Pixel-Mamba is a 24-layer hierarchical network where each layer contains three cooperating modules: a bidirectional Mamba block (selective SSM with linear complexity) for global context, a Region Fusion module that iteratively merges the top-k most similar regions (by CLS-token cosine similarity) to reduce sequence length, and a Token Expansion module that alternately applies vertical and horizontal shift-merging plus concatenation/averaging to grow the token receptive field from 1×1 to 32×32. The key design insight is that local inductive biases—small adjacent structures combining into larger meaningful units—are captured at every hierarchical level by the expanding tokens, while the Mamba block simultaneously models long-range dependencies across the entire slide. The WSI is serialized with a region-based zigzag scan (224×224 windows) to keep spatially neighboring tokens close in sequence order, preventing the disruption of local structure that naive row-major scanning would cause. Trained end-to-end with only ImageNet pretraining (no pathology-specific pretraining), the 6.2M-parameter model matches or exceeds foundation models pretrained on millions of WSIs or WSI-text pairs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Pixel-Mamba-21M achieves 80.8% top-1 accuracy (21M params, 3.4 GFLOPs), outperforming S4ND-ViT-B (80.4%) and DeiT-S (79.8%), while Pixel-Mamba-6M reaches 77.8% with only 6.2M parameters. On WSI tumor staging (5-fold Macro F1), Pixel-Mamba-Stage at 2.5× magnification achieves 0.5334 (BLCA), 0.3744 (BRCA), and 0.3917 (LUAD), surpassing all two-stage MIL methods using foundation models pre-trained on millions of pathology images. For survival analysis (5-fold C-index), Pixel-Mamba-Surv achieves 0.6507 (BLCA), 0.6707 (BRCA), and 0.6468 (LUAD), again the best in the table. Notably, Pixel-Mamba requires no pathology-specific pre-training—only ImageNet initialization—yet matches or exceeds models pre-trained on 171K WSIs (GigaPath), 1.17M image-caption pairs (CONCH), or 104M pathology patches (HIPT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixel-Mamba achieves the best result on all six WSI metrics (3 tumor staging + 3 survival analysis), outperforming every baseline. Against the strongest two-stage MIL with GigaPath (ILRA-MIL, pre-trained on 171K WSIs), Pixel-Mamba gains +0.0014 F1 on BLCA staging and +0.0354 C-index on BLCA survival. Against CONCH-based MIL (pre-trained on 1.17M image-caption pairs), the advantage is larger: +0.0074 F1 on BLCA staging and +0.0450 C-index on BLCA survival. Against the end-to-end LongViT (pre-trained on 10K WSIs, requiring 64 A100 GPUs), Pixel-Mamba leads by +0.0371 F1 on BLCA staging and +0.0718 C-index on BLCA survival, while using only 6.2M parameters vs. 22M. On ImageNet-1K, Pixel-Mamba-21M (80.8%) beats S4ND-ViT-B (80.4%) and DeiT-S (79.8%), and Pixel-Mamba-6M (77.8%) surpasses Vim-Ti (76.1%) by 1.7% with fewer parameters. The key distinction is that all pathology baselines rely on large-scale pathology-specific pre-training, whereas Pixel-Mamba uses only natural-image (ImageNet) initialization, yet still achieves superior downstream performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pixel-Mamba builds directly on the Mamba state-space model and its bidirectional vision variant (Vision Mamba), adopting the linear-complexity SSM as the backbone for long-range dependency modeling. It departs from the dominant two-stage WSI paradigm (patch extraction via foundation models + MIL aggregation) by proposing a fully end-to-end architecture that processes pixel-level tokens with progressive receptive-field expansion, and from prior end-to-end efforts like LongViT by replacing dilated attention with SSM-based global context modeling. The Region Fusion module and progressive Token Expansion are novel contributions that address the computational infeasibility of pixel-level processing on gigapixel images while preserving local inductive biases analogous to convolutional hierarchies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that competitive WSI analysis may not require the massive pathology-specific pretraining (millions of WSIs or image-caption pairs) that current foundation models demand, lowering the barrier to entry for research groups with limited compute. The linear-complexity, 6.2M-parameter end-to-end design is substantially more memory-efficient than 1B+ parameter two-stage pipelines, making it attractive for deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three TCGA cancer types tested, how it performs at higher magnifications (20×) where foundation models operate, and whether the modest performance margins over strong two-stage baselines hold across more diverse pathological tasks and larger cohorts.
