## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on point clouds to build semantic maps, which are unordered, storage-heavy, and lack explicit occupancy and spatial connectivity information needed for downstream tasks like path planning and text-based object retrieval. Additionally, these methods overlook the inherent inaccuracy of VLMs in segmentation and feature extraction, producing imprecise 3D object segments that degrade scene understanding quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250031/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a posed RGB-D image sequence (T frames) and optional text queries. It produces an Octree-Graph where each node is an adaptive-octree with non-cubic voxels (sized per the object's bounding box, max depth 4 for indoor scenes) carrying semantic features and captions, while edges encode pairwise spatial distances, 3D relative orientation vectors, and semantic relations aligned to the world coordinate system. The representation directly supports LLM-decomposed object retrieval queries (e.g., 'the chair to the right of the table') and grid-free path planning via A* or Jump Point Search, reducing storage from ~71 MB (point cloud) to ~268 KB per scene.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid Octree-Graph representation: each 3D instance is encoded as an adaptive-octree (node) whose voxel dimensions are computed as (b_max − b_min)/2^l per axis, allowing elongated objects like walls to be captured at shallow depth without the cubic-voxel waste of classical octrees. A training-free construction pipeline first extracts 2D masks via CropFormer and visual-language features via CLIP/OVSeg plus TAP captions, then projects them to 3D point cloud segments. A Chronological Group-wise Segment Merging (CGSM) strategy partitions frames into temporal groups of interval I, applies semantic-guided under-segment filtering (removing segments whose contained sub-segments exceed a feature-variance threshold), and iteratively merges with a linearly decaying similarity threshold. An Instance Feature Aggregation (IFA) step fuses per-instance features using a dynamic weight that rewards closeness to the instance's own centroid while penalizing similarity to neighboring instances, yielding features that are both representative and distinctive. The resulting graph edges store semantic relations, Euclidean distances, and 3D direction vectors between node centers, enabling direct spatial reasoning without additional processing.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation, Octree-Graph achieves 0.320 mIoU and 0.414 mAcc on Replica (OVSeg backbone), surpassing the previous best HOV-SG by +8.9% mIoU and +11.0% mAcc. On ScanNet, the gains are even larger: 0.393 mIoU and 0.601 mAcc vs. HOV-SG's 0.222 and 0.431 (+17.1% mIoU, +17.0% mAcc). For zero-shot 3D instance segmentation on ScanNet200, the method reaches 14.3 AP, outperforming the prior best zero-shot method Mask-Clustering (12.0 AP) by 2.3 points. In path planning on HM3DSem, Octree-Graph achieves 97.88% success rate at 1 m threshold versus HOV-SG's 55.25%, and the adaptive-octree representation reduces storage from 18.5 MB (point cloud) to 29.8 KB on Replica.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Octree-Graph outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every evaluated task. In semantic segmentation, it surpasses HOV-SG [44] (the strongest prior scene-graph method) by +8.9% mIoU on Replica and +17.1% on ScanNet, and beats ConceptGraph [7] and ConceptFusion [15] by even wider margins (e.g., 0.320 vs. 0.18 mIoU on Replica). For zero-shot instance segmentation on ScanNet200, the method leads Mask-Clustering [48] (12.0 AP) by 2.3 AP, SAI3D [53] (12.7 AP) by 1.6 AP, and OVIR-3D [25] (9.3 AP) by 5.0 AP; it also matches the supervised Open3DIS [29] variant (23.7 AP) with a 3D-only proposal setting (23.2 AP) while remaining fully zero-shot. In text-based object retrieval on Sr3D, Octree-Graph with LLM-based reasoning achieves 0.26 Acc@0.1, exceeding BBQ [23] (0.23) and ConceptGraph [7] (0.15), while Open-Fusion [47] trails at 0.13. For path planning on HM3DSem, the 97.88% success rate at 1 m threshold dramatically exceeds HOV-SG's 55.25%, attributed to the adaptive-octree's precise occupancy representation versus HOV-SG's pre-computed Voronoi graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical octree structure (Octomap) by introducing an adaptive variant with non-cubic, shape-aware voxels, and combines it with a scene-graph architecture to form the Octree-Graph. It departs from the dominant point-cloud-based 3D mapping paradigm (ConceptFusion, OpenScene) and from prior scene-graph approaches (ConceptGraphs, HOV-SG) that store objects as single points or bounding boxes without explicit per-object occupancy. The CGSM merging strategy and IFA feature aggregation are positioned as improvements over frame-wise and global-wise segment merging, and over simple averaging or clustering-based feature fusion used in OVIR-3D, MaskClustering, and OpenMask3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Octree-Graph offers a compelling storage-efficiency story for embodied agents (42 KB vs. 6.8 M points for the same scene), and the demonstrated deployment on a robotic dog and drone with NVIDIA Orin NX underscores practical relevance. However, the very low mEOR values (≤1.1%) raise questions about how well the adaptive-octree actually captures fine-grained occupancy at the tested depth of 4, and whether the representation scales to larger or outdoor scenes. The path-planning comparison with HOV-SG is somewhat asymmetric (Voronoi-graph waypoints vs. free-form octree queries), so the 42-point success-rate gap may overstate the practical advantage. Open questions include whether the adaptive-octree depth can be increased without prohibitive storage cost, and whether the LLM-based retrieval generalizes beyond the 526-query Sr3D subset.
