## Problem

Existing multi-image benchmarks for MLLMs overlook interleaved textual contexts, fail to evaluate text-to-region and region-to-region reasoning that are essential for capturing deeper vision-language interplay, lack intermediate reasoning step annotations, and suffer from data leakage due to reliance on widely pre-trained public datasets. This paper introduces MIR, a benchmark specifically designed to test joint reasoning over multiple images with interleaved text, where models must associate image regions with corresponding texts and logically connect information across images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250565/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MIR instance consists of an average of six RGB images interleaved with text segments, plus a multiple-choice question (4 options). The expected output is a single MCQ answer accompanied by a structured five-step reasoning chain: Summary (high-level problem interpretation), Caption (per-image description), Text-to-Region (aligning textual references to specific image regions), Region-to-Region (establishing logical relationships between regions), and Conclusion (final answer derivation). The 22,257 instances span three categories—spatial (9,350), sequential (5,455), and analytical (7,821)—further divided into 12 fine-grained subtasks, with each annotation averaging ~3,970 characters.

## Method

The core contribution is a stage-wise curriculum learning strategy built on an adaptive difficulty filter. The filter runs Qwen-VL2.5 on each question 10 times; samples with ≥70% correct rate are labeled simple, the rest challenging. Training first fine-tunes the MLLM on simple samples to establish foundational capability. For challenging samples, 40% are sampled per stage across five progressive stages where reasoning steps are systematically shifted from input to output: Stage 1 concatenates all five reasoning steps to the question (model only generates the answer), Stage 2 moves the final step (conclusion) to the target, and Stages 3–5 progressively remove region-to-region, text-to-region, caption, and summary from the input. By the final stage, the model receives only the raw question and must autonomously generate the full reasoning chain and answer, achieving a transition from externally guided reasoning to independent problem-solving.

## Results

On the in-domain MIR benchmark, the proposed stage-wise curriculum learning method achieves the highest average accuracy of 51.76% with Qwen2-VL, improving from 40.44% (base) and 45.15% (standard fine-tuning). LLaVA-OneVision reaches 47.60% average in-domain accuracy (vs. 39.31% base), and mPLUG-Owl3 reaches 40.36% (vs. 33.13% base). On out-of-domain benchmarks, Qwen2-VL(Ours) attains 54.47% on MIRBENCH, 56.61% on BLINK, and 46.67% on MUIRBench, consistently outperforming both the base models and standard fine-tuning counterparts by 1–5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five open-source MLLMs—Mantis, LLaVA-Next-Interleave, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, and mPLUG-Owl3—each under three conditions: zero-shot base, standard full fine-tuning on MIR, and the proposed stage-wise curriculum learning. The proposed method consistently outperforms standard fine-tuning across all five models on in-domain MIR (e.g., Qwen2-VL: 51.76% vs. 45.15%; LLaVA-OneVision: 47.60% vs. 42.36%) and on out-of-domain benchmarks (e.g., BLINK: 56.61% vs. 52.19% for Qwen2-VL; 52.93% vs. 50.34% for LLaVA-OneVision). The largest absolute gain is observed with Qwen2-VL, where the proposed method adds +6.61% over standard tuning in-domain and +4.28% on BLINK out-of-domain. Notably, Mantis(Ours) shows a slight drop on MIRBENCH (40.82% vs. 43.10% for tuning), suggesting the curriculum strategy benefits stronger instruction-following models more. Sequential tasks show the smallest improvement across all models, which the authors attribute to limitations in representing video content via extracted frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific gap between two existing benchmark families: multi-image QA benchmarks (Mantis, MMIU, MUIRBench) that lack interleaved textual contexts, and interleaved image-text benchmarks (LLaVA-Next-Interleave, ICL benchmarks) that lack structured multi-image reasoning. The Text2Region and Region2Region reasoning framework formalizes a reasoning chain that prior multi-image benchmarks treat implicitly, and the five-stage reasoning annotation (Summary → Caption → Text2Region → Region2Region → Conclusion) extends chain-of-thought prompting from single-image to cross-image settings. The stage-wise curriculum learning strategy draws on educational 'easy-to-hard' progression but applies it specifically to MLLM fine-tuning by progressively removing reasoning-step scaffolding, which is a repurposing of curriculum learning ideas rather than a departure from a specific prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIR provides the first large-scale benchmark (22,257 QA pairs, 138,277 images) that explicitly requires models to associate interleaved text with image regions and reason across regions in multiple images, a scenario pervasive in real-world media but previously unbenchmarked. The structured reasoning steps enable a training paradigm where models learn to generate their own reasoning chains, potentially transferable to other multimodal tasks. However, the modest gains (3–7% in-domain, 1–5% out-of-domain) and the notably weak improvement on sequential tasks (attributed to lossy video-frame extraction) suggest the benchmark may not yet stress-test the hardest aspects of temporal reasoning. Open questions include whether the 70% difficulty threshold generalizes across model scales, whether the curriculum approach outperforms simpler multi-stage fine-tuning, and whether the MCQ format adequately captures open-ended interleaved reasoning.
