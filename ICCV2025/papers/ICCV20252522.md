## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems suffer from two key limitations: they fail to preserve the underlying scene structure during optimization, causing structural degradation in rendered views, and they struggle with abrupt appearance variations (exposure, lighting) across camera poses. While prior work like Photo-SLAM initializes Gaussians from SLAM point clouds, it still underutilizes the latent structure, and appearance embedding methods like NeRF-W require training on each ground-truth test image, which is impractical in SLAM where 80% of views are novel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous image stream (monocular, stereo, or RGB-D) and processes it through ORB-SLAM3 to extract keyframe poses and a structured point cloud. Training operates on keyframe images, their corresponding poses, and the voxelized point cloud (voxel size 0.001 m), with the remaining non-keyframe frames serving as the test set. The output is a structured 3D Gaussian scene representation organized as fixed anchor points each generating k Gaussians via learned offsets, along with per-frame camera poses and photorealistic novel-view renderings at 400 FPS.

## Method

SEGS-SLAM introduces three components. First, SEPM voxelizes the ORB-SLAM3 point cloud into fixed anchor points (borrowing Scaffold-GS's hierarchical structure), so that the strong geometric prior from indirect SLAM is preserved throughout optimization rather than degrading as free Gaussians drift. Second, AfME replaces NeRF-W's per-image learnable appearance vectors with a lightweight MLP that encodes the camera pose (R, t) into a continuous appearance embedding, which is concatenated into the color decoder input; this eliminates the need for training on novel-view ground truth and enables pose-conditioned appearance prediction. Third, FPR applies multi-scale 2D FFT with high-pass filtering to both rendered and ground-truth images, adding a frequency-domain loss that sharpens high-frequency details. The full system is trained by minimizing a weighted combination of L1, SSIM, volume regularization, and frequency regularization losses over 30K iterations, jointly optimizing the Gaussian parameter MLPs and the AfME network.

## Results

On the TUM RGB-D dataset (RGB-D camera), SEGS-SLAM achieves 26.03 PSNR / 0.843 SSIM / 0.107 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline MonoGS (24.11 PSNR) by 1.92 dB and GS-ICP SLAM (17.81 PSNR) by 8.22 dB. On Replica (RGB-D), it reaches 39.42 PSNR / 0.975 SSIM / 0.021 LPIPS, surpassing GS-ICP SLAM (37.14 PSNR) and Photo-SLAM-30K (36.94 PSNR). For monocular cameras on TUM RGB-D, SEGS-SLAM achieves 25.17 PSNR, a 19.86% relative improvement over MonoGS (21.00 PSNR). On EuRoC stereo, it attains 23.64 PSNR, nearly doubling Photo-SLAM's 11.90 PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEGS-SLAM outperforms all rendering baselines across every dataset and camera type. On TUM RGB-D (RGB-D), it beats the closest competitor MonoGS by 1.92 dB PSNR (26.03 vs 24.11) and the depth-based GS-ICP SLAM by a large margin of 8.22 dB (26.03 vs 17.81), indicating that depth-dependent methods degrade severely in cluttered real-world scenes. On Replica (RGB-D), the gap over GS-ICP SLAM narrows to 2.28 dB (39.42 vs 37.14), while Photo-SLAM-30K trails by 2.48 dB. For monocular cameras, SEGS-SLAM achieves 25.17 PSNR on TUM RGB-D versus 21.00 for MonoGS and 21.06 for Photo-SLAM-30K, and on EuRoC stereo it reaches 23.64 PSNR compared to 11.90 for Photo-SLAM and 22.60 for MonoGS. In camera tracking (ATE RMSE), SEGS-SLAM is competitive with ORB-SLAM3 (0.979 cm avg. on RGB-D vs 1.988 cm) and DROID-SLAM (1.169 cm avg. on mono vs 39.145 cm), confirming that the indirect SLAM backbone preserves localization accuracy while the 3D-GS mapping module focuses on rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEGS-SLAM sits at the intersection of two threads: the structured 3D Gaussian representation from Scaffold-GS (anchor points managing local Gaussians) and the indirect visual SLAM pipeline of ORB-SLAM3. The key departure from prior 3D-GS SLAM systems (Photo-SLAM, MonoGS, GS-ICP SLAM, SplaTAM) is the SEPM framework, which repurposes ORB-SLAM3's point cloud to initialize fixed anchor points rather than treating Gaussians as free parameters, thereby preserving scene structure throughout optimization. For appearance handling, AfME replaces the per-image learnable embeddings of NeRF-W with a lightweight pose-conditioned MLP, eliminating the need to train on held-out views—a critical distinction in SLAM where ~80% of frames are test views. The FPR component extends single-scale frequency regularization (FreGS) to a multi-scale pyramid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural insight—that fixing anchor points from a SLAM point cloud prevents the gradual structural degradation seen in free 3D-GS optimization—is simple yet broadly applicable to any 3D-GS-based mapping system, making it a potentially adoptable technique beyond this specific pipeline. AfME's pose-conditioned design addresses a practical gap in SLAM-specific appearance modeling that NeRF-style embeddings cannot handle due to the high test-view fraction. However, the method is limited to static scenes, depends on point cloud quality (poorly structured clouds degrade performance), and the increased Gaussian count for high-frequency details reduces rendering speed. Open questions include whether the structural anchoring generalizes to large-scale outdoor scenes, dynamic environments, and whether the pose-to-appearance mapping can be extended to handle more complex illumination changes.
