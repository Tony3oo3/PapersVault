## Problem

Event-guided HDR reconstruction from a single LDR image and events remains ill-posed in over-/under-exposed regions because events only encode thresholded radiance changes, leaving absolute brightness and color unknown. Regression-based methods (e.g., HDRev) cannot faithfully hallucinate these missing values, while existing diffusion-based approaches (e.g., Sagiri) produce visually appealing but distorted results that fail to preserve fidelity to the original scene. The paper addresses both the conditioning challenge—how to effectively fuse heterogeneous event and LDR modalities as diffusion conditions—and the detail-preservation challenge of suppressing fine-grained distortion introduced by generative sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stream of asynchronous events (converted to a C=5-channel voxel grid tensor) and a single 512×512 LDR image as input, and produces a 512×512 colorful HDR image. During training, LDR images are synthesized by randomly over- or under-exposing 20–50% of pixels in ground-truth HDR frames. Inference runs in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.5 with only 9 DDIM steps and classifier-free guidance (scale 1.5), making it practical for single-GPU deployment on an RTX 3090.

## Method

The core architecture combines a frozen Stable Diffusion V1.5 denoiser with a ControlNet-style control module that receives conditions from a pretrained HDRev event-image encoder. Instead of concatenating raw events and LDR or using HDRev's decoded output, the method extracts multi-level HDR features directly from the encoder and fuses them via a proposed pyramid fusion module, avoiding redundant decoder-encoder computation and preventing the embedding module from misinterpreting unsatisfactory reconstruction artifacts. A refinement decoder (initialized from the VAE decoder) then fuses these HDR features with the denoised latent z₀ to recover fine-grained details. The key training innovation is a histogram-based structure loss: local histogram matching adjusts the ground-truth image's brightness and color distribution to match the diffusion result before supervision, so the refinement module learns to correct structural distortion without introducing the color/brightness artifacts that arise from naively regressing toward a deterministic GT.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (Yang et al. [48]), the proposed method achieves PSNR 25.67 dB, SSIM 0.926, LPIPS 0.099, CIEDE 6.01, FID 27.09, and NIQE 3.86, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, Dille et al. [4], reaches only PSNR 19.73 dB and FID 76.83, a gap of ~6 dB in PSNR and ~50 in FID. On real DSEC data, the method achieves the best NIQE (3.12) and MANIQA (0.693); on HES-HDR it achieves the best MANIQA (0.530) though NeurImg [14] edges it on NIQE (4.61 vs. 4.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all six baselines on the synthetic benchmark, with the largest margins over event-only (EventHDR: +14.6 dB PSNR) and single-image diffusion (Sagiri: +13.2 dB PSNR) approaches. Against the strongest single-image CNN baseline (Dille et al.), it gains +5.9 dB PSNR, +0.106 SSIM, and reduces FID from 76.83 to 27.09, indicating substantially better structural fidelity and perceptual realism. Compared to the closest event-guided regression method HDRev, the improvement is +11.6 dB PSNR and FID drops from 46.23 to 27.09, showing that diffusion priors plus the pyramid-fusion conditioning far outperform direct regression for hallucinating over-/under-exposed content. On real DSEC data, the method achieves the best NIQE (3.12) and MANIQA (0.693), beating HDRev (3.41/0.402) and Sagiri (5.87/0.617). On HES-HDR, NeurImg [14] slightly outperforms on NIQE (4.61 vs. 4.81), but the proposed method leads on MANIQA (0.530 vs. 0.442), and the authors note qualitatively superior recovery even under unnatural color-shifted LDR inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of event-guided HDR reconstruction (HDRev, NeurImg) and conditional diffusion models for image restoration (Sagiri, DiffBIR). It directly repurposes HDRev's pretrained event-image encoder—not its decoded output but its intermediate multi-scale features—as the conditioning signal for a ControlNet-style injection into Stable Diffusion V1.5. The key architectural departure from prior event-guided methods is replacing the regression decoder with a diffusion denoiser to hallucinate missing over-/under-exposed content, and the key departure from prior diffusion-based HDR work (Sagiri) is introducing events as a structured condition rather than relying solely on the LDR image or text prompts. A novel pyramid fusion module bridges the multi-resolution HDR features to the denoiser, and a histogram-based structure loss in a refinement decoder addresses the color/brightness mismatch between deterministic ground truth and stochastic diffusion outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining the differential HDR information from event cameras with the generative prior of diffusion models yields a natural and effective solution to the ill-posed HDR reconstruction problem, outperforming both regression-based event-guided methods and LDR-only diffusion methods on fidelity and perceptual quality. The histogram-matching trick for the refinement target is a practical and generalizable insight for any diffusion-based restoration pipeline where the stochastic output's color/brightness distribution differs from the deterministic supervision signal. A stated limitation is that diffusion uncertainty makes extending the method to temporally consistent video HDR reconstruction challenging, leaving an open question for the event-camera video community. The approach also requires paired (events, LDR, GT) training data, which constrains deployment to settings where such supervision is available.
