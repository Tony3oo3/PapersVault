## Problem

Vecset Diffusion Models (VDM) for 3D shape generation still require over 30 seconds per shape, far behind real-time 2D image generation. The bottleneck is twofold: diffusion sampling needs many steps, and VAE decoding scales cubically with resolution (over 56 million cross-attention queries at 384³). Existing distillation techniques designed for 2D images fail to transfer to VDMs due to fundamentally different latent spaces and optimization landscapes, and the VAE decoding problem has been largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-3D generation, the model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a polygon mesh extracted via marching cubes from a decoded SDF volume at resolution 384. For reconstruction, the input is a 3D mesh point cloud that is encoded into a fixed set of latent tokens (vecset) via cross-attention with query tokens. The decoder then evaluates SDF values at every grid point in the 3D volume using volume queries attending to the shape latents, which is where the cubic computational cost arises.

## Method

FlashVDM accelerates both the diffusion sampler and the VAE decoder. For diffusion, Progressive Flow Distillation first warms up with guidance distillation to stabilize the target model, then applies multi-stage consistency distillation (with EMA updates, Huber loss, and PCM-based phasing), and finally performs adversarial fine-tuning in latent space using a discriminator on intermediate DiT features, achieving comparable quality at only 5 NFE. For VAE decoding, three techniques are combined: Hierarchical Volume Decoding decodes at low resolution first and only refines voxels near the surface (reducing queries by 91.4%), Adaptive KV Selection exploits spatial locality to select only the top-K relevant latent tokens per sub-volume (reducing KV pairs by ~34%), and an Efficient Decoder Architecture narrows the network and removes redundant layers (3.2× per-query speedup). Together these yield a 45× decoding speedup (22.3s → 0.49s) and 32× overall speedup, producing Hunyuan3D-2 Turbo at ~1 second per shape.

## Results

For image-to-3D generation, FlashVDM (Hunyuan3D-2 Turbo) achieves ULIP-I 0.1260 and Uni3D-I 0.3095 in 1.041s, closely matching its teacher Hunyuan3D-2 (ULIP-I 0.1303, Uni3D-I 0.3151) while reducing inference time from 34.85s to 1.041s (≈33× speedup). For VAE reconstruction at 3072 latent tokens, FlashVDM achieves V-IoU 95.55% and S-IoU 93.10% in 0.491s versus 96.11%/93.27% in 22.33s for the unaccelerated decoder (≈45× speedup with <1% IoU drop). The method outperforms all fast 3D generation baselines (TripoSR, SF3D, SPAR3D) by a large margin on both image-mesh similarity metrics while remaining competitive with the slower SOTA Trellis (7.334s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model Hunyuan3D-2 [58], FlashVDM retains 96.7% of ULIP-I and 98.2% of Uni3D-I while achieving a 33× speedup (34.85s → 1.041s), confirming near-lossless distillation. Compared to the other SOTA generator Trellis [46], FlashVDM is slightly lower on ULIP-I (0.1260 vs 0.1267) and Uni3D-I (0.3095 vs 0.3116) but runs 7× faster (1.041s vs 7.334s). Among fast feedforward methods, FlashVDM substantially outperforms TripoSR [42] (ULIP-I 0.1260 vs 0.0642), SF3D [3] (0.1260 vs 0.1156), and SPAR3D [11] (0.1260 vs 0.1149) on image-mesh similarity, while remaining in the same ~1s time bracket. For VAE reconstruction, FlashVDM (V-IoU 95.55%, 0.491s) dominates 3DShape2VecSet [54] (87.88%, 16.43s), Michelangelo [57] (84.93%, 16.43s), and Direct3D [45] (88.43%, 3.201s) on both quality and speed. User studies confirm FlashVDM is preferred over SPAR3D in nearly all test cases and rated comparable to Hunyuan3D-2 at 5 NFE steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashVDM builds directly on the Vecset Diffusion Model (VDM) architecture from 3DShape2VecSet and applies it to the Hunyuan3D-2 foundation model, targeting two bottlenecks that prior fast-3D-generation work (TripoSR, SF3D, SPAR3D) sidestepped by using feedforward reconstruction instead of diffusion. For diffusion acceleration, it departs from 2D consistency distillation by identifying that direct CD fails on 3D VDMs due to target-model instability, and introduces Progressive Flow Distillation with guidance warm-up, EMA stabilization, and adversarial fine-tuning. For VAE acceleration, it is the first work to systematically optimize the VDM decoder, combining hierarchical volume decoding (related to but distinct from octree decoding), adaptive KV selection (related to token merging in DiT inference), and a lighter decoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-shape inference from ~35s to ~1s on a consumer GPU, FlashVDM makes interactive, real-time 3D shape generation from images practically feasible for the first time with a native diffusion model, opening the door to applications like live design tools and AR. The finding that VAE decoding (75.8% of time) dominates over diffusion sampling (23.9%) redirects community attention toward the under-explored decoder bottleneck in VDMs. Limitations include that the framework was validated on a single model (Hunyuan3D-2), the distillation and decoder fine-tuning require non-trivial training, and generalization to other VDM architectures or higher resolutions remains to be demonstrated.
