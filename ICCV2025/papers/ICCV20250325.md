## Problem

Unsupervised object discovery (UOD) methods lack reliable foreground priors to judge whether a discovered region is foreground or background, and have no principled stopping criterion for how many objects to discover. Existing priors are heuristic (e.g., size, location, corner rules) and fixed-iteration discovery leads to under- or over-segmentation because the number of objects per image is unknown and variable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized to 224×224, which is serialized into a 28×28 grid of 8×8-pixel patches whose L2-normalized Key vectors are extracted from a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT-S/8. UnionCut outputs a 28×28 binary mask marking the foreground union; UnionSeg outputs per-patch scalar confidence scores (sigmoid of a 1×1 convolution on ViT patch features) thresholded at 0.5 to produce the same binary mask. The foreground union mask is then consumed by downstream UOD algorithms as a prior for verifying discovered regions and deciding when to stop exploration.

## Method

UnionCut is a parallel ensemble of 784 Unit Voters (UVs), one per patch, where each UV builds a directed graph over all patches with Source/Target terminals, assigns n-link weights from patch-feature similarity and t-link weights from seed/anti-seed patch affinities, and solves a min-cut to segment the region most similar to its seed patch. Aggregating all 784 UV binary masks by summation yields a heat map that is inverted, normalized, and thresholded via Mean-Shift clustering (retaining the top half of clusters) to produce the foreground union, with a corner prior that inverts the mask if it occupies all four image corners. UnionSeg distills this pipeline into an end-to-end ViT (frozen DINO backbone + learnable 1×1 conv + sigmoid) trained on DUTS-TR with a self-correcting label rule that substitutes UnionCut's output only when UnionSeg's own IoU with it falls below 0.5, combined with a binary cross-entropy loss that includes a warm-up term forcing agreement with UnionCut for the first 100 iterations.

## Results

On single object discovery (CorLoc), integrating UnionSeg with TokenCut improves COCO20K from 58.8 to 62.6 (+3.8), and with LOST from 50.7 to 54.9 (+4.2). On saliency detection, CutLER+UnionSeg achieves the largest gains, improving DUT-OMRON maxFβ from 48.1 to 64.1 (+16.0) and DUTS-TE maxFβ from 54.2 to 72.3 (+18.1). For self-supervised instance segmentation, CutLER+UnionSeg raises COCO20K APmask from 9.2 to 10.0 and AP50box from 22.4 to 24.1. UnionSeg also outperforms UnionCut on foreground union detection precision (80.9% vs 70.7%) and runs at 125 FPS vs 0.1 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnionCut/UnionSeg consistently improve all four baseline UOD methods (LOST, TokenCut, FOUND, CutLER) across all three tasks, confirming their role as drop-in foreground priors. The largest absolute gains appear for CutLER on saliency detection: +13.4 Acc, +14.0 IoU, and +16.0 maxFβ on DUT-OMRON, and +12.0 Acc, +14.9 IoU, +18.1 maxFβ on DUTS-TE, because CutLER's original heuristic priors (corner-based, confidence-based) are weakest. TokenCut benefits most on COCO20K single object discovery (+3.8 CorLoc with UnionSeg), while LOST gains +4.2 CorLoc on COCO20K. FOUND, already the strongest learned baseline, sees only marginal improvements (+0.1 to +0.9 CorLoc), suggesting its built-in background detection partially overlaps with UnionCut's function. On instance segmentation, CutLER+UnionSeg improves APmask from 9.2 to 10.0 on COCO20K and from 9.2 to 9.9 on COCO val2017. UnionSeg outperforms UnionCut on precision (80.9% vs 70.7%) and speed (125 vs 0.1 FPS), while UnionCut has higher recall (84.6% vs 78.6%), making UnionSeg better for verifying individual detections and UnionCut better for deciding when to stop exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINO/ViT self-supervised representation learning paradigm that underpins recent UOD methods (LOST, TokenCut, FOUND, CutLER), but shifts the focus from designing better UOD algorithms to improving the foreground prior they rely on. Conceptually, it repurposes min-cut graph segmentation (Boykov & Kolmogorov) as the core operation of each Unit Voter and frames the aggregation of 784 such voters as a parallel ensemble (Dietterich), yielding a mathematically grounded foreground union mask. This departs from prior UOD work, which uses heuristic rules (size thresholds, corner exclusion, semantic similarity to global features) to decide whether a discovered region is foreground, by replacing those assumptions with an ensemble-theoretic construction whose robustness is argued via statistical theory rather than ad-hoc design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that UnionCut/UnionSeg serve as a drop-in replacement for the foreground prior in any existing UOD pipeline, consistently improving single object discovery, saliency detection, and self-supervised instance segmentation across multiple baselines and benchmarks. UnionSeg's 125 FPS inference (vs. 0.1 FPS for UnionCut) makes the approach deployable, though UnionCut's 7-week ImageNet processing time remains a significant barrier for large-scale self-supervised training. Open questions include whether the ensemble-of-min-cut formulation generalizes beyond 2D images (video, 3D), whether the corner prior can be eliminated entirely, and whether the foreground-union concept transfers to open-vocabulary or multi-modal discovery settings.
