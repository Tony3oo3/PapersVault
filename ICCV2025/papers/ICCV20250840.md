## Problem

Standard image tokenizers arrange discrete tokens in raster scan order, forcing autoregressive models to condition on a partial line-by-line reconstruction rather than a holistic view of the image. This sequential ordering is ill-suited to the 2D structure of images and limits the ability to generate at multiple resolutions or perform partial decoding. The paper asks whether tokenizing the image spectrum—specifically discrete wavelet transform coefficients—can yield a naturally coarse-to-fine token sequence that is better aligned with autoregressive modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes an RGB image of any resolution (16×16 up to 512×512 in experiments) and produces a fixed-length sequence of discrete tokens ordered by wavelet scale (coarsest first), with 256 tokens per scale and 4–6 scales total. For generation, AR-SIT accepts a text prompt (or class label) and optionally a low-resolution image whose tokens seed the beginning of the sequence; it outputs a full or partial token sequence that decodes to an image at the corresponding resolution. Because the sequence is scale-ordered, generating only the first k·N tokens yields a valid coarse reconstruction at a lower resolution without any retraining.

## Method

SIT applies a Haar DWT to the input, then patchifies each wavelet scale with the same number of tokens (256), so higher-frequency scales use larger spatial patches and are more heavily compressed—exploiting the fact that natural images concentrate power at low frequencies. A scale-causal attention mask (block-triangular) lets each token attend only to its own and lower scales, enabling the model to encode/decode at any resolution up to the trained maximum. An Approximation-Details Transformer (ADTransformer) uses separate parameters for the lowpass approximation coefficients versus the highpass detail coefficients, and the vector-quantization codebook is split accordingly. The autoregressive generator (AR-SIT) models the joint distribution over the coarse-to-fine token sequence with a standard causal transformer, and can be interrupted after any scale boundary to produce a coarse image or resumed from a given low-resolution token prefix for upsampling and editing.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, AR-SIT-4 achieves FID 6.95 and IS 138.3, outperforming the reproduced AR-ViT-VQGAN baseline (FID 8.37, IS 111.8). With adjusted hyperparameters (AR-SIT-4*), the method reaches FID 4.06 and IS 190.9, matching LlamaGen-L (FID 4.08) but trailing VAR (FID 3.30). For multiscale reconstruction on ImageNet, SIT-SC-5 dramatically outperforms ViT-VQGAN at lower resolutions (e.g., 32×32: PSNR 36.8 vs 23.3; LPIPS 0.029 vs 0.214) and at 512×512 (FID 2.65 vs 6.92), while also being significantly faster (e.g., 2620 vs 627 images/s at 16×16). On MS-COCO text-to-image, AR-SIT-SCD-4 matches Parti350M at 256×256 (FID 12.6 vs 12.4) but is 3–10× faster at lower resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct ViT-VQGAN/Parti baseline (same architecture and training protocol), AR-SIT-4 clearly wins on ImageNet class-conditional generation (FID 6.95 vs 8.37, IS 138.3 vs 111.8), confirming the improved conditioning from coarse-to-fine token ordering. On multiscale reconstruction (Table 1), SIT-SC-5 dominates ViT-VQGAN at every resolution below 256×256, with the gap widening as resolution decreases (e.g., 16×16: PSNR 41.3 vs 24.9), and also wins at 512×512 (FID 2.65 vs 6.92). Against RQ-VAE and DQ-VAE, AR-SIT-4 achieves substantially lower FID (6.95 vs 15.72 and 7.34 respectively). With adjusted hyperparameters, AR-SIT-4* matches LlamaGen-L almost exactly (FID 4.06 vs 4.08) but falls short of VAR (FID 4.06 vs 3.30), which benefits from a convolutional tokenizer trained on larger data and additional architectural improvements (AdaLN, attention normalization) orthogonal to this work's contributions. On MS-COCO text-to-image, AR-SIT-SCD-4 essentially ties Parti350M at the training resolution (FID 12.6 vs 12.4) but offers a unique advantage: it can stop generation early to produce coarse images, a capability none of the baselines possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIT builds directly on the ViT-VQGAN tokenizer architecture and the Parti autoregressive generation framework, but replaces spatial patch embeddings with discrete wavelet transform (DWT) coefficients, introducing scale-causal attention and separate parameters/codebooks for approximation versus detail coefficients. This departs from prior multiscale tokenizers (VQ-VAE-2, RQ-VAE, VAR, STAR) that impose coarse-to-fine ordering on learned latent features, and from handcrafted spectral representations (Nash et al., Mattar et al.) that produce long sequences, by learning a compact spectral tokenizer. The spectral autoregression framing also connects to Dieleman's observation that diffusion models implicitly denoise from low to high frequency, though SIT performs literal spectral autoregression rather than denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multiscale properties of SIT—encoding/decoding at arbitrary resolutions without retraining, partial decoding for rapid coarse previews, and text-guided upsampling/editing—enable applications not available with raster-ordered tokenizers, and the spectral framing offers a principled reason for why high frequencies can be more aggressively compressed. However, at full training resolution the text-to-image FID merely matches Parti (12.6 vs 12.4), and the authors acknowledge that a better tokenizer does not automatically yield a better generator. Open questions include whether scaling AR-SIT to 22B+ parameters (as Parti supports) would unlock larger gains, and whether the spectral approach composes well with recent improvements like AdaLN, 2D RoPE, and larger tokenizer codebooks that VAR and LlamaGen employ.
