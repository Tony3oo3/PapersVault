## Problem

In Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation (OVMM), robots must locate small, often occluded objects in large, cluttered environments and stop at a precise position and orientation for subsequent grasping. Existing rule-based methods offer structured exploration but are rigid and prone to getting stuck, while RL-based methods adapt dynamically yet lack long-term memory, leading to repetitive search. Critically, neither paradigm explicitly addresses the last-mile problem of halting at an optimal manipulation-ready viewpoint.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent consumes egocentric RGB-D frames, its 2D position and heading, a compass reading, and per-frame semantic outputs (bounding boxes, instance masks, and confidence scores) from a fine-tuned YOLO-World detector paired with SAM2 segmentation. The network outputs a single discrete action from {MOVE FORWARD, TURN LEFT, TURN RIGHT, STOP}; during the last-mile phase the turn granularity is halved from 30° to 15° to enable finer angular alignment. A 2D occupancy map built from projected depth and semantic data is also fed as a down-sampled global map (exploration) or a local map (last-mile) to the policy.

## Method

APRR decouples OVMM ObjectNav into an exploration phase and a last-mile navigation phase. In exploration, a rule-guided RL architecture combines a fixed Rule Model (predefined OVMM heuristics) with a learned policy (Feature Encoder → LSTM → Category Net + Critic Net); a Decision Net computes an adaptive weight λ that blends the RL action distribution with the rule-based action, and the whole system is trained in two DD-PPO stages with rewards that penalize stagnation, encourage receptacle visiting, and reinforce systematic area expansion. In last-mile navigation, a separate RL policy receives real-time detection feedback—mask IoU, bounding-box IoU, and confidence changes from the YOLO-World/SAM2 pipeline—as reward signals, effectively using detection quality as an implicit stopping criterion so the agent halts at a viewpoint that maximizes manipulation readiness rather than merely minimizing Euclidean distance.

## Results

On the OVMM ObjectNav benchmark (400 test episodes, 12 unseen scenes), APRR achieves a 64.5% success rate with a fine-tuned YOLO-World + SAM2 detector, improving over the best rule-based baseline Rule* (51.1%) by 13 percentage points. With ground-truth semantic masks, APRR reaches 74.2% SR, a 17-point gain over Rule* (54.4%). APRR also attains the lowest Distance-to-Goal (3.36 m vs. 4.01 m for Rule*) and highest Pick-Goal IoU Coverage (0.0279 vs. 0.0155), confirming both closer approach and better final stopping orientation for manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APRR outperforms all six baselines on success rate under both standard-detector and ground-truth-mask settings. Against the strongest rule-based baseline Rule* (51.1% SR), APRR gains 13 points (64.5%), and against the RL-based RL* (40.8%), the gap widens to 23.7 points. With oracle GT masks, APRR (74.2%) surpasses PoLo (56.7%) by 17.5 points and Rule* (54.4%) by 19.8 points. On Distance-to-Goal, APRR achieves 3.36 m vs. 4.01 m (Rule*) and 4.40 m (RL*), and on PIC it reaches 0.0279 vs. 0.0155 (Rule*) and 0.0223 (RL*), indicating a substantially better final stopping pose for manipulation. The older baselines FBE (10.9%) and PoLo (20.3%) with standard detectors are far behind, confirming that OVMM ObjectNav's small-object, large-environment setting is markedly harder than conventional ObjectNav.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three established threads: rule-based ObjectNav exploration (FBE, PoLo, UniTeam), RL-based navigation (KuzHum, DD-PPO agents), and active object detection (Fang et al., Ding et al., Kotar et al.). Rather than building on a single prior method, APRR repurposes active perception—previously used to improve detection confidence—into a navigation signal that optimizes the agent's stopping pose for manipulation. The rule-guided RL exploration model is a novel fusion that dynamically weights heuristic actions against learned policy outputs via a Decision Net, departing from prior work that treats rules and RL as competing paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-phase decomposition (exploration + last-mile) addresses a practical gap that prior OVMM methods overlook: stopping at a manipulation-ready viewpoint rather than merely reaching the target's vicinity. The 13% SR improvement and substantially better PIC scores demonstrate that explicit last-mile optimization matters for downstream manipulation success. However, the sim-to-real gap (64.5% → 30% in 20 real-world trials) remains a significant open question, and the reliance on ground-truth maps for reward shaping during training limits immediate deployability. Future work on sim-to-real adaptation and reducing dependence on oracle signals would be needed for broader practical impact.
