## Problem

Model-free 6D object pose tracking methods like BundleSDF achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but are bottlenecked by slow neural SDF training (~6.7s per round), limiting tracking to ~0.4 Hz and making them impractical for live dynamic scenarios. The paper addresses this by replacing the neural object field with a computationally efficient differentiable representation that enables simultaneous pose refinement and reconstruction at near-real-time rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251675/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB-D video stream (1080p at 30 FPS in real-world deployment) along with an initial object segmentation mask from SAM2, which is propagated across frames using a fixed-length window. It produces per-frame 6D pose estimates at 3.5 Hz, a continuously updated 2D Gaussian Splat object model (refreshed approximately every 8 seconds), and rendered depth and surface-normal maps from the Gaussian model. The Gaussian Object Field consists of oriented planar 2D Gaussian disks with view-dependent spherical harmonic appearance and per-Gaussian opacity.

## Method

6DOPE-GS builds a 'Gaussian Object Field' using 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) and jointly optimizes the Gaussian parameters and keyframe poses by backpropagating photometric, depth, and normal-consistency losses through the differentiable splatting projection. A dynamic keyframe selection mechanism clusters coarse poses around icosahedron anchor points to maximize spatial coverage, then filters outliers via median absolute deviation of reconstruction loss to prevent erroneous pose updates from destabilizing optimization. An opacity percentile-based adaptive density control prunes the bottom 5% of Gaussians iteratively until the 95th percentile opacity exceeds a threshold, stabilizing particle count without the abrupt fluctuations of vanilla 3DGS densification. Once the Gaussian Object Field converges, all keyframe poses are refined with frozen Gaussians, and an online pose graph optimization using dense pixel-wise reprojection error continuously tracks the object at each subsequent timestep.

## Results

On the HO3D dataset, 6DOPE-GS achieves 95.07% ADD-S and 84.33% ADD pose accuracy with 0.41 cm Chamfer distance reconstruction error at 0.24 s/frame, outperforming BundleSDF (94.86% ADD-S, 0.58 cm CD) while being ~9× faster (0.24 s vs 2.10 s per frame). On YCBInEOAT, it reaches 93.79% ADD-S and 87.82% ADD with 0.15 cm CD at 0.22 s/frame, surpassing BundleSDF (92.82% ADD-S, 0.53 cm CD) with a ~4× speedup. The method substantially outperforms SLAM-based baselines (DROID-SLAM: 64.64% ADD-S on HO3D) and Gaussian Splatting SLAM methods (MonoGS: 2.81% ADD-S; Endo-2DTAM: 18.54% ADD-S) on the challenging HO3D benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor BundleSDF [77], 6DOPE-GS wins on ADD-S (95.07 vs 94.86 on HO3D; 93.79 vs 92.82 on YCBInEOAT) and reconstruction CD (0.41 vs 0.58 cm on HO3D; 0.15 vs 0.53 cm on YCBInEOAT), but loses on absolute ADD accuracy on HO3D (84.33 vs 89.56), which the authors attribute to severe hand occlusion limiting Gaussian supervision. The ~9× speedup on HO3D (0.24 s vs 2.10 s/frame) makes 6DOPE-GS practical for live tracking at 3.5 Hz, whereas BundleSDF's neural SDF training bottleneck limits it to ~0.4 Hz. Against BundleTrack [75], 6DOPE-GS improves ADD-S by 1.11 points on HO3D (95.07 vs 93.96) and adds sub-centimeter reconstruction (0.41 cm) that BundleTrack does not provide. SLAM-based baselines (DROID-SLAM, NICE-SLAM, SDF-2-SDF, KinectFusion) are far behind, with DROID-SLAM reaching only 64.64% ADD-S on HO3D and 46.39% on YCBInEOAT. Gaussian Splatting SLAM methods (MonoGS, Endo-2DTAM) perform poorly on both datasets (≤20.81% ADD-S), likely due to low texture, occlusion, and the absence of pairwise keyframe pose optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the BundleSDF framework [77], which jointly optimizes a neural SDF object field with keyframe poses for model-free 6D tracking, by replacing the computationally expensive neural SDF with a 2D Gaussian Splatting-based 'Gaussian Object Field' for ~5× faster convergence. The tracking backbone (keyframe memory pool, RANSAC coarse pose initialization, online pose graph optimization) is inherited from BundleTrack [75], while the surface-accurate 2D Gaussian representation comes from 2DGS [23] and the joint pose-reconstruction optimization paradigm follows Gaussian Splatting SLAM methods like SplatAM [26]. The paper's algorithmic novelties—dynamic keyframe selection via icosahedron anchor points with MAD-based outlier filtering, and opacity percentile-based adaptive density control—are specific to making the Gaussian Splatting optimization stable and efficient in the object-centric, sparse-view tracking setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

6DOPE-GS is the first method to achieve live (3.5 Hz) joint 6D pose tracking and 3D Gaussian Splat reconstruction from a single RGB-D camera, making model-free object tracking practical for real-time applications in robotics, AR, and autonomous driving where BundleSDF's ~2 s/frame latency is prohibitive. However, the method still lags BundleSDF in absolute pose accuracy on heavily occluded sequences (HO3D), and the optimized 2D Gaussians are not directly integrated into the online pose graph—only the refined poses are transferred—leaving open the question of tighter coupling between the object representation and tracking. The authors also note that rasterization-based rendering may provide weaker gradients than differentiable ray casting, suggesting future work on ray-traced Gaussian representations could close the remaining accuracy gap.
