## Problem

Existing VLM-based open-vocabulary object detectors rely almost exclusively on internal vision-language alignment knowledge, which limits their ability to generalize to novel categories because VLMs encode limited contextual reasoning about how objects relate in scenes. The paper asks whether externally sourced contextual knowledge—specifically object co-occurrence patterns validated by LLMs—can complement VLMs to improve novel category recognition without modifying detector architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, CODet consumes ground-truth bounding boxes and known-category labels from the training set (COCO or LVIS) to extract spatial co-occurrence pairs, and queries an LLM for textual co-occurrence candidates. At inference, the enhanced detector takes a standard RGB image and outputs bounding boxes with category labels spanning both known and novel categories, identical in format to the underlying VLM-based detector (e.g., Faster R-CNN or CenterNet2). The module is plug-and-play: it adds no new network layers and requires no architectural changes to the host detector.

## Method

CODet operates in three stages anchored at each known object. First, it identifies the spatially nearest neighboring proposal via a scoring function combining IoU and normalized center distance, then merges the pair into a minimal co-occurrence bounding box whose visual feature is extracted by the VLM's visual encoder. Second, it queries an LLM with three complementary templates (spatial proximity, functional correlation, hierarchical taxonomy) to generate candidate co-occurring categories, encodes each as a 'A photo of [c_i] and [c_i']' sentence through the VLM's text encoder, and selects the best-matching category by maximizing cosine similarity between the visual and textual co-occurrence features, gated by a confidence threshold. Third, the selected (proposal, category) pairs are injected as pseudo-labels into the detector's classification loss during training, encouraging the visual embedding of the neighbor to align with the co-occurring category's text embedding while repelling unrelated categories. This external knowledge injection is performed iteratively at fixed training intervals to progressively enrich the co-occurrence set.

## Results

On OV-COCO, CODet consistently improves all five VLM-based detectors on both novel and known categories, with the largest gain being BARON-CODet at 35.2 AP Novel (+2.1 over BARON) and VLDet-CODet at 33.9 AP Novel (+1.9). On OV-LVIS, CODet achieves up to +2.3 mAP Novel (ViLD-CODet: 18.9 vs. 16.6; BARON-CODet: 21.5 vs. 19.2) and +1.7 mAP Novel for Detic-CODet (25.6 vs. 23.9) with Swin-B backbone. Zero-shot transfer to PASCAL VOC and LVIS (without fine-tuning) yields +2.6 mAP Novel on PASCAL VOC and +2.3 mAP Novel on LVIS for VLDet-CODet, confirming cross-domain generalization of the co-occurrence knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODet is a plug-and-play module that improves every baseline it is applied to, with no performance degradation on known categories. On OV-COCO, the gains range from +1.6 to +2.1 AP Novel: BARON benefits most (+2.1, reaching 35.2), followed by Detic (+2.0, 29.8) and RegionCLIP (+2.0, 28.8), while ViLD and VLDet gain +1.6 and +1.9 respectively. On OV-LVIS, the pattern holds with gains of +1.6 to +2.3 mAP Novel across RN50 and Swin-B backbones; ViLD and BARON see the largest improvements (+2.3 each), while Detic (Swin-B) gains +1.7 (25.6 vs. 23.9). CODet also improves known-category performance across all baselines (e.g., +1.8 AP Known for ViLD, +2.0 mAP Common for RegionCLIP on LVIS), indicating that co-occurrence pseudo-labels provide useful contextual priors beyond novel categories. In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation, VLDet-CODet outperforms VLDet by +2.6 mAP Novel on PASCAL VOC (64.3 vs. 61.7) and +2.3 mAP Novel on LVIS (12.3 vs. 10), and RegionCLIP-CODet improves over RegionCLIP by +1.7 and +2.0 respectively, confirming that the extracted co-occurrence knowledge transfers across domains without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODet sits at the intersection of two OVOD research threads: VLM-based knowledge distillation (ViLD, VLDet, Detic, RegionCLIP, BARON) and LLM-augmented detection (CuPL, CaFo, MM-OVOD, DVDet). Prior LLM-based methods treat language models as static text generators that enrich category prompts or produce fine-grained descriptors, while VLM-based methods rely on internal image-text alignment for novel category recognition. CODet departs from both by introducing object co-occurrence as an explicit external knowledge source: it mines spatial co-occurrence from training images, validates candidate pairs against LLM-derived semantic dependencies (spatial, functional, hierarchical), and injects the resulting pseudo-labels into any VLM-based detector without architectural changes. The pipeline repurposes standard components (VLM encoders, LLM queries, cosine alignment) in a new combination centered on the co-occurrence signal rather than on category-level prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means CODet can be dropped onto any existing VLM-based OVOD pipeline, making it a low-friction improvement for practitioners already working with ViLD, Detic, VLDet, or similar frameworks. Consistent gains across five detectors and two benchmarks (up to +2.3 AP novel on OV-COCO, +1.7 mAP novel on OV-LVIS) plus zero-shot transfer to PASCAL VOC and LVIS suggest the co-occurrence prior is genuinely complementary to VLM-internal knowledge rather than redundant. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to VLM-based paradigms, co-occurrence quality is bounded by VLM/LLM accuracy (risking bias propagation), and the spatial-proximity extraction is inherently noisy (mitigated but not eliminated by LLM validation). Open questions include whether co-occurrence knowledge can be extended to non-VLM detectors, whether the approach scales to very large category spaces beyond LVIS, and whether the LLM validation step can be replaced by cheaper alternatives without losing the background-filtering benefit.
