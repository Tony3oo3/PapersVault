## Problem

Polarization cameras are expensive and inaccessible, yet polarization-based vision tasks (shape-from-polarization, dehazing, reflection removal) require large-scale labeled polarization datasets that do not exist. Existing simulators like Mitsuba rely on parametric pBRDF models and demand extensive 3D assets (geometry + PBR material maps), making large-scale photorealistic polarization image synthesis impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no 3D geometry, no material maps) and produces a 3-channel polarization property map: two channels for sinusoidally encoded AoLP (cos 2Φ, sin 2Φ) and one channel for DoLP normalized to [−1, 1]. From this map, polarization images at any arbitrary polarizer angle can be analytically recovered via Malus's law. Training images are 1224×1024 polarization captures randomly cropped to 512×512 patches.

## Method

PolarAnything fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.5 as a latent denoising model that predicts the conditional distribution p(cos 2Φ; sin 2Φ; P | I_RGB). The key design choice is to denoise the encoded AoLP–DoLP representation rather than raw polarization images at four angles, which preserves the π-periodicity of AoLP and enforces physical consistency. A ControlNet-style image encoder (convolutional feature extractor + U-Net-matched encoder) extracts hierarchical guidance features from the RGB input and injects them into the denoising U-Net decoder. Unlike ControlNet, all U-Net weights are trainable during fine-tuning. The model is trained on 1,148 real captured polarization images (19 lighting conditions, 100+ objects) plus the Morimatsu dataset, using standard DDPM noise-prediction loss with CLIP text embeddings as an additional condition.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution PN test set (7 objects, different capture environment), augmenting DeepSfP training with PolarAnything-generated polarization data (PSO) reduces mean angular error from 30.69° to 22.93° and improves ≤30° accuracy from 58.26% to 77.47%, outperforming the Mitsuba-rendered augmentation (MSO: 24.22° mean, 72.26% ≤30°). On the in-distribution DP test set, both PSO and MSO yield marginal improvements over the original DeepSfP (19.20° vs. 19.15° vs. 19.38° mean angular error). The ablation on polarization encoding shows that the proposed encoded AoLP representation (cos 2Φ, sin 2Φ) achieves the best MAngE of 25.33° and MAbsE of 0.1075, compared to 45.67°/0.1233 for raw polarization images and 29.46°/0.1100 for unencoded AoLP&DoLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mitsuba [43], the comparison is qualitative (quantitative evaluation is infeasible due to camera calibration misalignment), but PolarAnything produces AoLP and DoLP maps visually much closer to ground truth on both specular (ELEPHANT) and diffuse (MONEY JAR) objects, while requiring only a single RGB image instead of full 3D geometry, environment maps, and PBR parameter maps. On the downstream SfP task, PolarAnything-augmented training (DeepSfP+PSO) clearly outperforms Mitsuba-augmented training (DeepSfP+MSO) on the out-of-distribution PN test set, reducing mean angular error by 1.29° (22.93° vs. 24.22°) and improving ≤30° accuracy by 5.19 percentage points (77.47% vs. 72.26%), indicating that the diffusion-generated polarization data carries more physically faithful cues than the parametric Mitsuba rendering. On the in-distribution DP test set, both PSO and MSO are roughly on par with the original DeepSfP, with differences within ~0.2° mean angular error. Against the Transformer-based Restormer [59] baseline, PolarAnything demonstrates superior AoLP and DoLP estimation quality, attributed to the strong zero-shot priors of the pretrained diffusion model versus Restormer's dependence on large-scale training data that is unavailable for polarization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarAnything repurposes the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the conditional-control architecture of ControlNet for a domain (polarimetric image synthesis) previously dominated by physics-based renderers like Mitsuba and measured-pBRDF simulators. The key conceptual departure is replacing parametric reflectance models—which require 3D geometry and PBR material maps—with a learned generative prior conditioned on a single RGB image, leveraging the zero-shot generalization of diffusion models pretrained on LAION-5B. The choice to denoise encoded AoLP/DoLP rather than raw multi-angle polarization images is a methodological contribution that enforces physical consistency absent in naive image-space generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing polarization data acquisition from a multi-view 3D-asset pipeline to a single RGB photograph, PolarAnything lowers the practical barrier for the polarization-vision community and enables augmentation of existing RGB benchmarks (e.g., Stanford-ORB) with physically plausible polarization counterparts. However, the quantitative comparison with Mitsuba is limited to qualitative assessment due to camera-calibration misalignment, and the downstream SfP gains—while directionally positive—are modest on the in-distribution test set and evaluated on a small out-of-distribution set (7 objects). Open questions include whether the diffusion prior truly encodes physical polarization constraints or merely learns statistical correlations, and how the approach scales to scene-level synthesis with complex multi-object interactions.
