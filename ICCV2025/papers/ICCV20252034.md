## Problem

Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art generation quality but require many sequential denoising steps, causing high sampling latency. Existing solver-based acceleration methods suffer from significant truncation error at low NFE budgets (e.g., < 5), leading to severe image quality degradation. Parallelism-based approaches remain underexplored as a means to improve quality without increasing inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver takes Gaussian noise x_{t_N} ~ N(0, t_N²I) and a pretrained noise-prediction network ε_θ(x,t) as input, and produces a denoised image x_{t_0} after N ODE steps. It operates on pixel-space models (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 FFHQ/ImageNet) and latent-space models (256×256 LSUN Bedroom, 512×512 Stable Diffusion). The key operational detail is that within each step, K additional gradient evaluations are computed in parallel from the same starting state x_{t_{n+1}}, so the effective latency equals that of a single NFE per step regardless of K.

## Method

EPD-Solver approximates the ODE integral in each step by evaluating K gradients at learned intermediate timesteps τ_n^k ∈ (t_n, t_{n+1}) and combining them via a simplex-weighted sum (λ_n^k ≥ 0, Σλ_n^k = 1), grounded in the vector-valued mean value theorem. Each intermediate state x_{τ_n^k} is obtained by a one-step Euler update from x_{t_{n+1}}, making all K gradient computations fully independent and parallelizable. Two additional learnable parameters per step—output scale o_n and timestep shift δ_n^k—mitigate exposure bias. The small parameter set (N(1+3K) total, ranging 6–45) is optimized via distillation: a student trajectory is aligned to a teacher trajectory (generated by DPM-Solver-2 with M=6 intermediate steps) using ℓ₂ loss on noisy states and Inception feature-space loss on the final sample. EPD-Plugin extends the same parallel-gradient idea to existing multi-step solvers like iPNDM by replacing their single gradient estimate with K parallel branches.

## Results

At 5 Para. NFE, EPD-Solver achieves FID 4.33 on CIFAR-10 32×32, 7.84 on FFHQ 64×64, 6.35 on ImageNet 64×64, and 7.52 on LSUN Bedroom 256×256, while EPD-Plugin (applied to iPNDM) achieves 4.47, 7.97, 8.17, and 8.26 respectively. At 9 Para. NFE, EPD-Solver reaches FID 2.49 (CIFAR-10), 3.82 (FFHQ), 4.27 (ImageNet), and 5.01 (LSUN Bedroom). On Stable Diffusion v1.5 (MS-COCO), EPD-Solver achieves FID 12.17 at 20 Para. NFE, outperforming DPM-Solver++(2M) (14.58) and AMED-Plugin (13.24). Inference latency remains essentially unchanged when increasing K from 1 to 2 (e.g., 45.3 ms vs. 47.2 ms on CIFAR-10 at 5 Para. NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPD-Solver (K=2) dominates all eight baselines across every dataset and NFE setting. At 5 Para. NFE on CIFAR-10, EPD-Solver (FID 4.33) outperforms the strongest single-step baseline AMED-Solver (7.59) by 3.26 FID and the strongest multi-step baseline AMED-Plugin (6.61) by 2.28 FID. The gap is even more dramatic on LSUN Bedroom 256×256, where EPD-Solver achieves 7.52 versus AMED-Solver's 13.20 and AMED-Plugin's 25.68 at 5 NFE. At 3 NFE on LSUN Bedroom, EPD-Solver reaches 13.21 FID while the next-best AMED-Solver scores 58.21, a 4.4× improvement. EPD-Plugin (iPNDM + parallel gradients) slightly outperforms EPD-Solver at 7–9 NFE on CIFAR-10 (2.42 vs. 2.49) and FFHQ (3.53 vs. 3.82), consistent with multi-step solvers benefiting from historical gradients at larger step sizes. ParaDiGMS, the only other parallelism-based method, is consistently outperformed by 2–3× in FID across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of ODE solvers for diffusion models (DDIM, EDM, DPM-Solver, AMED-Solver) that approximate the probability flow ODE integral using one or a few gradient evaluations. The key conceptual departure is replacing sequential additional evaluations (as in Heun's method or AMED's single intermediate point) with K independent, fully parallelizable gradient evaluations, grounded theoretically in the vector-valued mean value theorem. The lightweight distillation-based optimization of a small parameter set (6–45 parameters) draws on exposure-bias mitigation ideas from recent work but avoids the heavy training cost of full-model distillation methods. Unlike prior parallelism-based approaches (ParaDiGMS, Distrifusion, AsyncDiff) that trade computation for speed, EPD uses parallelism to improve quality at fixed latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EPD-Solver offers a practical, plug-and-play path to high-quality few-step diffusion sampling: at 5 NFE it achieves FID 4.47 on CIFAR-10 and 7.97 on FFHQ with no latency penalty, and the EPD-Plugin variant can be retrofitted onto existing multi-step solvers. The dramatic gap at 3 NFE on LSUN Bedroom (13.21 vs. 58.21 for AMED-Solver) highlights how severe truncation error is in the extreme low-step regime and how parallel gradients can mitigate it. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether K can be made adaptive per timestep, and how it composes with other acceleration techniques like consistency distillation or rectified flow. The method's reliance on a teacher trajectory for training, while cheap, still introduces a dependency that may limit applicability to newly trained or proprietary models.
