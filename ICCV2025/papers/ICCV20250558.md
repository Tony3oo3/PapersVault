## Problem

Existing text-to-motion methods produce only short sequences (~10 s) due to the scarcity of long-range motion data, while autoregressive approaches suffer from error accumulation (drift, repetition, freezing) over extended durations. Prior in-painting-based composition methods introduce abrupt transitions and boundary conflicts between adjacent segments. The paper addresses the gap of generating arbitrarily long, coherent human motions with smooth inter-segment transitions in a training-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ordered list of free-form textual prompts (e.g., 'a person walks forward a few steps', 'a man is doing a cartwheel') and produces a single continuous 3D human motion sequence of arbitrary length, where each sub-motion segment is semantically aligned with its prompt and 30-frame transition segments bridge consecutive actions. Motions are represented as 263-dimensional pose vectors (joint coordinates, angles, velocities, foot contact) at 20 FPS on HumanML3D, or as joint rotations on BABEL. The sequence length scales with the number of prompts, and the entire output is optimized as a set of differentiable parameters rather than generated autoregressively.

## Method

InfiniDreamer operates in three stages: (1) each sub-motion is generated by a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) conditioned on its text prompt, while transition segments are randomly initialized and gradient masks (Mask_l=0.1 for motion, Mask_h=0.8 for transitions) plus linear interpolation at boundaries regulate where optimization is applied; (2) a sliding window (W=120 frames, stride S=30) iteratively samples overlapping short segments from the coarse sequence, with text conditions assigned probabilistically based on which sub-motions each window covers; (3) the key contribution, Segment Score Distillation (SSD), refines each sampled segment by adding noise, querying the pre-trained MDM in unconditional mode to obtain a predicted clean signal x̂₀, and minimizing an alignment loss ‖x̂₀ − x₀‖² weighted by w(t), augmented with positional, foot-contact, and velocity geometric losses. The entire long sequence is treated as a differentiable parameter optimized for 20,000 iterations with AdamW (lr=0.002), requiring no additional training data or model fine-tuning—only the pre-trained short-clip diffusion prior guides the optimization.

## Results

On HumanML3D, InfiniDreamer achieves R-precision 0.679, FID 0.47, and MultiModal-Dist 3.15 for motion segments, and Transition FID 2.04 with Diversity 8.69 for transition segments, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On BABEL, it reaches R-precision 0.543, FID 0.97, and Transition FID 2.07, again leading all compared methods. The method is training-free, requiring only a pre-trained short-clip motion diffusion model (MDM) and 20,000 optimization iterations on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiniDreamer outperforms all three baselines (DoubleTake, DiffCollage, and TEACH) on every reported metric across both HumanML3D and BABEL. On HumanML3D, it improves R-precision by 0.076 over DoubleTake (0.679 vs 0.603) and 0.074 over DiffCollage (0.679 vs 0.605), while reducing motion FID from 1.36/1.07 to 0.47. The transition-segment FID improvement is especially pronounced: 2.04 vs 3.19 (DoubleTake) and 4.27 (DiffCollage), indicating substantially smoother inter-segment transitions. On BABEL, InfiniDreamer leads TEACH by 0.082 in R-precision (0.543 vs 0.461) and by 2.16 in Transition FID (2.07 vs 4.23), and beats DiffCollage by 0.86 in motion FID (0.97 vs 1.83). Qualitatively, the paper shows that DoubleTake produces frozen or distorted transitions and misses fine-grained actions (e.g., a dodge), whereas InfiniDreamer maintains coherent, contextually-aware motion throughout the sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfiniDreamer adapts Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion's text-to-3D setting to the temporal motion domain, repurposing a pre-trained short-clip motion diffusion model (MDM) as a distributional prior over overlapping segments of a longer sequence. This departs from two dominant paradigms for long motion generation: autoregressive composition (TEACH, Multi-Act), which accumulates drift, and inpainting-based transition filling (PriorMDM/DoubleTake, DiffCollage), which introduces boundary conflicts. The key conceptual shift is treating the entire long sequence as a differentiable parameter optimized via segment-wise score distillation rather than generating content sequentially or patching boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, optimization-based formulation means InfiniDreamer requires no long-sequence training data and is decoupled from any specific short-clip diffusion prior, so future advances in motion diffusion automatically transfer. However, the 20,000-iteration optimization per sequence on a single GPU limits practical throughput, and the approach has only been validated on sequences of a few prompts (tens of seconds), leaving open whether it scales to truly minute- or hour-long compositions. The moderate but consistent gains over inpainting baselines suggest score distillation is a viable alternative to autoregressive and inpainting strategies, but the computational cost and sensitivity to hyperparameters (window size, stride, learning rate) remain practical barriers.
