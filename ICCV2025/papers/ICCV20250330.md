## Problem

Zero-shot depth completion with accurate metric scales remains difficult because monocular depth foundation models, while generalizable, cannot natively consume sparse sensor depth and exhibit systematic scale mismatches relative to sensor measurements. Existing solutions either require additional network architectures to fuse sparse depth into foundation models, demand further supervised training, or rely on computationally expensive test-time optimization of diffusion-based latent representations. The paper asks how to fully exploit depth foundation models for depth completion without extra network design, parameter modification, or dense ground-truth annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a corresponding sparse depth map from an active sensor (LiDAR or depth camera) as input, where the sparse depth provides a validity mask and metric-scale supervision at a subset of pixels. Sparse depth density varies by dataset: 1,000 random points (IBims-1), 1,500 points (VOID), 500 points (NYUv2), multi-frame LiDAR aggregation (KITTI), and 20% random sampling (DDAD). The output is a dense metric depth map at the original image resolution, with the foundation model's parameters remaining frozen throughout inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a pixelwise visual prompt V_φ ∈ R^{3×H×W}, initialized to zero and added element-wise to the input image, which is optimized at test time while the depth foundation model (e.g., UniDepthV2) stays completely frozen. The prompt is trained for 50 AdamW steps (lr=1e-3) by minimizing L1+L2 depth error only at valid sparse depth locations, requiring just 0.25% learnable parameters versus 100% for full fine-tuning. Unlike prior visual prompts that use fixed-size patches at predefined or random positions, this pixelwise design enables localized, spatially-varying corrections that align depth scale region-by-region with sensor measurements. The paper also demonstrates that a simpler fine-tuning baseline (80 steps, lr=1e-6, updating all parameters) already outperforms prior SOTA, establishing that sparse-depth supervision alone is sufficient, but the visual prompt achieves comparable accuracy with lower memory (3991 MB vs 8372 MB) and faster inference (2.93 s vs 2.95 s per frame on NYUv2).

## Results

On five zero-shot depth completion benchmarks, the pixelwise visual prompting method (Ours-VP) achieves the best MAE on all datasets: 0.043 on IBims-1, 0.132 on VOID, 0.041 on NYUv2, 0.343 on KITTI, and 1.482 on DDAD. The method also attains the best RMSE on IBims-1 (0.161), VOID (0.508), and NYUv2 (0.106), and is competitive on KITTI (1.333) and DDAD (5.811). In terms of efficiency, Ours-VP requires only 3991 MB of memory and 2.93 s inference time, compared to 8727 MB / 20.56 s for Zero-DC and 21748 MB / 121.04 s for Marigold-DC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours-VP outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset where they are directly comparable. Against the closest test-time optimization competitors, Zero-DC [24] and Marigold-DC [74], Ours-VP reduces MAE by 45% (0.043 vs 0.078) on IBims-1, 29% (0.132 vs 0.187) on VOID, 41% (0.041 vs 0.069) on NYUv2, 14% (0.343 vs 0.397) on KITTI, and 12% (1.482 vs 1.681) on DDAD. Compared to supervised depth completion methods (NLSPN, CompletionFormer, VPP4DC, BP-Net, OGNI-DC), Ours-VP achieves substantially lower errors in the zero-shot setting, e.g., 0.043 MAE on IBims-1 versus 0.049–0.078 for the supervised baselines. The fine-tuning variant (Ours-FT) also beats all baselines on most datasets, confirming that sparse-depth supervision alone is highly effective. In terms of efficiency, Ours-VP uses roughly 55% less memory and is 7× faster than Zero-DC and 41× faster than Marigold-DC, making it the most practical option for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: monocular depth foundation models (UniDepthV2, Metric3D V2, Depth Anything V2, ZoeDepth) and visual prompt tuning for large-scale model adaptation. Prior depth completion approaches that leverage foundation models (e.g., Depth Prompting, UniDC, Prompting Depth Anything) require carefully designed auxiliary networks to fuse sparse sensor depth into the model, while diffusion-based test-time optimization methods (Zero-DC, Marigold-DC) achieve zero-shot adaptation at the cost of extreme computational overhead. The paper departs from both by showing that no additional architecture is needed—either full fine-tuning or a novel pixelwise visual prompt (extending Bahng et al.'s fixed-patch prompts to per-pixel learnable offsets) suffices to correct the scale mismatch between foundation model predictions and sensor measurements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the pixelwise visual prompt achieves near fine-tuning-level accuracy while using only 0.25% of the parameters and running at ~3 seconds per frame, compared to 121 seconds for the prior best diffusion-based method. This makes zero-shot metric depth completion viable for real-time applications like autonomous driving and robotics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (surface normals, optical flow), how it behaves under extreme sparsity or adversarial sensor noise, and whether the learned pixelwise prompts can be amortized across a sequence to eliminate per-frame test-time training entirely.
