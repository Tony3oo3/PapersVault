## Problem

Purely IMU-based motion tracking is inherently self-referential, producing drift in global translation and providing no spatial reference to other people, which prevents capturing inter-personal dynamics and relative positioning. Prior IMU+UWB methods like UIP and PIP handle only single-person tracking, treating sensors on different individuals as disjoint problems. This paper addresses the gap of estimating multi-human 3D poses and global translations in a shared world frame from sparse wearable sensors without requiring calibrated starting positions.

## Input / Output

Each person wears 6 sparse sensors (head, pelvis, wrists, knees), each combining a 6-DoF IMU (104 Hz) and a UWB radio (40 Hz), yielding per-person acceleration/orientation streams plus pairwise same-person and between-person distance matrices (6x6 per pair of people). The method outputs SMPL joint-angle parameters (24 joints) and a 3D root translation trajectory per person, all expressed in a single shared world frame. A key design constraint is that people may start at arbitrary, uncalibrated positions; the method recovers their initial relative placement purely from the distance observations.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, an SSM-based pose estimator (four S4 modules: SSM-J, SSM-R, SSM-V, SSM-C, plus a GCN for sensor-position prediction and a physics-based optimizer) independently predicts each person's SMPL pose and relative translation from their own IMU signals and same-person distances. Second, an initial-position optimization (L-BFGS) adjusts the second person's starting offset T0_12 to minimize the sum of squared differences between forward-kinematics-predicted and UWB-measured between-person distances over the full sequence, aligning both trajectories into a shared frame. Third, a trajectory optimization simultaneously refines both people's full translation trajectories by minimizing the same distance residual plus first- and second-order smoothness regularizers on velocity and acceleration. The SSM architecture replaces conventional LSTMs, offering better long-range dependency modeling and parallelization, while the two-step optimization (rather than a single joint optimization) is critical for stable convergence to realistic paths.

## Results

On the synthetic InterHuman dataset, GIP reduces full-body joint angle error to 9.94° (vs. 12.89° for UIP and 14.89° for PIP+D) and achieves a dramatic reduction in multi-person distance error, reaching 3.08 cm at 4 s and 1.91 cm at 20 s, compared to 49.61–83.16 cm for UIP and 48.82–63.72 cm for PIP+D. On the real-world GIP-DB dataset, GIP reduces the 20 s distance error by 72% over baselines (20.69 cm vs. 73.70–74.93 cm) and improves joint angle error to 23.34°. The initial position optimizer (which eliminates the need for ground-truth initialization) yields translation errors within 1–3 cm of the ground-truth-initialized version on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIP is compared against two prior IMU-based methods: PIP+D (Physical Inertial Poser augmented with sensor distances, CVPR 2022) and UIP (Ultra Inertial Poser, SIGGRAPH 2024). GIP wins on every metric across both the synthetic InterHuman and real-world GIP-DB datasets. The most striking advantage is in multi-person relative translation: on InterHuman, GIP's 20 s distance error is 1.91 cm versus 63.72 cm (PIP+D) and 81.44 cm (UIP), an order-of-magnitude improvement. On GIP-DB, GIP reduces the 20 s distance error to 20.69 cm versus 73.70 cm (PIP+D) and 74.93 cm (UIP), a 72% reduction. For single-person pose, GIP's SSM-based estimator cuts angle error by 22% over UIP and 33% over PIP on InterHuman. Neither baseline was designed for inter-person translation estimation, so they require ground-truth initial positions; GIP's initial position optimizer closes this gap, achieving translation errors within ~1–3 cm of the ground-truth-initialized setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIP extends the single-person IMU+UWB tracking line (UIP, SIGGRAPH 2024) and physics-based inertial pose estimation (PIP, CVPR 2022) to the multi-person setting by exploiting between-person UWB distances as spatial constraints in a shared world frame. It introduces structured state-space models (S4) as the backbone for sequential inertial pose estimation—a first for this domain—replacing the LSTMs or transformers used in prior sparse-IMU methods. The two-step optimization (initial position alignment followed by trajectory refinement) departs from prior work that either assumes calibrated starting positions or treats each person's translation as an independent, drift-prone problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIP-DB is the first IMU+UWB multi-person motion dataset, and the method demonstrates that sparse wearable sensors alone can recover meaningful inter-person spatial dynamics without any camera infrastructure—opening a path to privacy-preserving, environment-independent interaction capture. Key open questions include scaling beyond four people, reducing the optimization overhead for real-time use, incorporating body-shape variation, and mitigating UWB NLOS noise that currently limits between-person distance accuracy to ~15 cm RMSE. The foot-sliding artifact from trajectory optimization and the assumption of a mean SMPL shape remain practical barriers to deployment.
