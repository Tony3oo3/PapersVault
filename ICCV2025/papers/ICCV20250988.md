## Problem

Pre-trained point cloud models degrade significantly on real-world data affected by sensor noise and occlusion-induced incompleteness. Existing solutions rely on separate denoising and completion models in an ensemble pipeline, which introduces domain gaps with downstream tasks and conflicting objectives that destroy critical geometric features. Meanwhile, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for 3D vision ignore explicit suppression of input noise and defects, leaving them vulnerable to low-quality point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250988/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a noisy and incomplete point cloud of S×3 coordinates (e.g., 1024 points with 24 outlier noise points, 64 surface noise points, and 25% missing regions) and produces a class label for the downstream task. Internally, it also generates a rectified point cloud (noise-filtered via per-point displacement vectors with threshold-based masking) and a completed point cloud (with missing regions reconstructed via a repurposed MAE decoder), both resampled to S points via farthest point sampling before final feature extraction.

## Method

UPP reformulates denoising and completion as point-level prompting within a frozen pre-trained ViT backbone, training only 1.4M parameters (≈6%). A Rectification Prompter uses shallow transformer blocks to predict per-point displacement vectors, masking points whose displacement exceeds a threshold to filter noise while preserving geometry. A Completion Prompter then uses deeper blocks to predict coarse centers for missing regions and reconstructs dense point patches by repurposing the pre-trained MAE decoder. A Shape-Aware Unit is inserted in every transformer block, combining standard self-attention with a spatial-distance-based KNN attention mechanism and LoRA-style low-rank adapters to capture geometric structure robustly. The three components are jointly trained with a unified loss combining rectification L2 loss, Chamfer distance for completion, and cross-entropy for the downstream task.

## Results

On Noisy ModelNet40, UPP with the Point-MAE backbone achieves 92.95% classification accuracy using only 1.4M trainable parameters (6.3% of the full model), surpassing the full fine-tuning baseline by +3.53%. On Noisy ShapeNet55, UPP reaches 90.40%, again exceeding full fine-tuning by +2.27%. On the real-world ScanObjectNN dataset with inherent sensor noise and 25% incompleteness, UPP attains 91.39% accuracy, outperforming the strongest full fine-tuning baseline (Point-FEMAE at 90.71%) and the best PEFT method (DAPT at 89.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPP consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across both synthetic and real-world datasets. Against the strongest full fine-tuning baselines, UPP (Point-MAE backbone) gains +3.53% over Point-MAE FFT (92.95% vs 89.42%), +2.02% over ReCon FFT (91.69% vs 89.67%), and +2.35% over Point-FEMAE FFT (91.94% vs 89.59%) on Noisy ModelNet40. Among PEFT methods, UPP surpasses DAPT by +6.52% (92.95% vs 86.43%) and Point-PEFT by +5.43% (92.95% vs 87.52%), while using comparable or fewer trainable parameters (1.4M vs 1.1M and 0.7M respectively). On the real-world ScanObjectNN benchmark, UPP achieves 91.39%, beating Point-FEMAE FFT (90.71%), DAPT (89.67%), Point-PEFT (89.16%), and IDPT (88.64%). Notably, UPP is the only method that improves upon its own backbone's full fine-tuning accuracy, demonstrating that the point-level prompting mechanism adds genuine robustness rather than merely compensating for limited parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPP sits at the intersection of three research threads: pre-trained point cloud transformers (Point-MAE, ReCon, Point-FEMAE), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Point-PEFT, DAPT, IDPT), and dedicated point cloud enhancement (ScoreDenoise, PointTr, T-CorresNet). Its key conceptual departure is reformulating denoising and completion—traditionally separate preprocessing models in an ensemble pipeline—as point-level prompting mechanisms embedded within the analysis backbone, thereby eliminating the domain gap and conflicting objectives that plague multi-stage approaches. The Shape-Aware Unit further departs from standard PEFT adapters by introducing spatial-distance-based attention alongside feature-similarity self-attention, and the Completion Prompter repurposes discarded MAE decoder weights for local patch reconstruction rather than training a new completion head from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: real-world 3D sensors produce noisy, incomplete point clouds, and existing PEFT methods degrade sharply under such conditions, while ensemble denoise-then-complete-then-analyze pipelines are computationally expensive and suffer from objective conflicts. UPP's unified single-model approach with only 1.4M trainable parameters (≈6% of the backbone) outperforming full fine-tuning by 2–3.5% across three backbones and three datasets is a strong efficiency-robustness tradeoff that could simplify deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dense per-point tasks like segmentation and detection (mentioned but not thoroughly evaluated), how it scales to much larger point clouds (e.g., scene-level), and whether the staged training strategy is necessary or an artifact of the current architecture.
