## Problem

Parameter-level model merging is widely regarded as inferior to prediction-level ensembling, with prior evidence of near-equivalence limited to two ViT-based models fine-tuned from the same checkpoint. This paper asks whether performance consistency between merging and ensembling can be achieved when these restrictions are removed—specifically for multiple models, diverse architectures (ViT and ResNet), and even models trained from random initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250282/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Portland (a single linear layer followed by softmax) takes the concatenated raw outputs of T models on a given data point (z_1 ⊕ z_2 ⊕ … ⊕ z_T) and produces a Cooperative Vector (CoopVec) β ∈ ℝ^T with one non-negative weight per model. The same CoopVec is applied both to parameter-level merging (θ̃ = Σ β_t θ_t) and prediction-level ensembling (f̃ = Σ β_t f_{θ_t}), yielding a unified mechanism. In the semi-supervised variant, no ground-truth labels are required—only unlabeled i.i.d. data for entropy minimization.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows via Taylor expansion that the output gap between ensembling and merging is second-order small if and only if Σ_t β_t ξ_t^T = 0, where ξ_t = θ_t − θ̃ is each model's parameter offset from the merged point. To enforce this condition in practice, NeuLig trains Portland with a two-term loss: a boosting term L_b (cross-entropy in supervised mode, entropy minimization in semi-supervised mode) that drives ensembling quality, and an alignment term L_a = Σ_t (Ψ_φ(ẑ)_t · ξ_t*)² that penalizes first-order parameter-offset misalignment, directly implementing the theoretical condition. The alignment term uses a pre-computed unweighted average as initialization for θ̃ at each step and standard-deviation scaling on offsets for stable convergence. This ensemble-driven merging strategy lets the boosting term discover good ensembling weights while the alignment term pulls the corresponding merging performance up to match, achieving gaps as small as 0.02% across five CLIP-ViT-B/32 models.

## Results

On the 5-model collaboration benchmark with CLIP-ViT-B/32, NeuLig (semi-supervised) achieves 95.44% merging accuracy and 95.46% ensembling accuracy, yielding a mere 0.02% performance gap—nearly perfect consistency between the two paradigms. For CLIP-RN50, the gap is slightly larger at 0.61% (92.07% merging vs. 92.68% ensembling). In contrast, static baselines like Simple-Averaging and Task-Arithmetic show gaps of 11.92% and 11.40% respectively on ViT models, and up to 39.34% on ResNet models. NeuLig also outperforms the best learnable baselines (WeMoE: 1.32% gap, AdaMerging: 1.60% gap) by roughly 60–80% in terms of the merging-ensembling gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuLig decisively outperforms all six baselines on both merging accuracy and the merging-ensembling gap. Against static methods, the advantage is dramatic: Simple-Averaging (78.19% Mer., 11.92% gap) and Task-Arithmetic (78.68% Mer., 11.40% gap) fall 17+ points below NeuLig's merging accuracy, while Ties-Merging (80.80%) and RegMean (83.16%) cannot even be evaluated for ensembling. Among learnable baselines, WeMoE (89.76% Mer., 1.32% gap) and AdaMerging (88.65% Mer., 1.60% gap) are the closest competitors, yet NeuLig still exceeds them by 5.68 and 6.79 points in merging accuracy while reducing the gap by over 60%. Notably, NeuLig is the only method that works across all three architectural settings (ViT, CNN, and diverse-origin/randomly-initialized models), whereas RegMean, AdaMerging, and WeMoE are inapplicable to ResNet-based models, and all baselines degrade to near-random performance under diverse-origin conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-model, ViT-only, same-checkpoint analysis of merging-ensembling equivalence established by [36] to a general multi-model setting with diverse architectures and origins. The theoretical foundation builds on the task vector formalism from Task Arithmetic [15], where parameter offsets (ξ_t = θ_t − θ̃) serve as the natural coordinates for a Taylor expansion linking the merged model's output to individual model outputs. The key departure is the derivation of a clean necessary-and-sufficient condition (weighted sum of offsets equals zero) for second-order performance equivalence, which then motivates the alignment term in the NeuLig loss — a design that no prior merging method explicitly targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If parameter-level merging can reliably match prediction-level ensembling, practitioners gain the storage and inference efficiency of a single model without sacrificing the accuracy typically attributed to ensembles — a significant practical win for multi-task deployment. The result is particularly relevant for privacy-constrained federated or multi-party settings where parameter sharing and output sharing may be available at different stages. Limitations include evaluation only on CLIP-based models (ViT-B/32 and RN-50) across five relatively small datasets, with the ResNet gap (~1%) noticeably larger than the ViT gap (~0.02%), and the framework requires access to model parameters for the alignment term, which may not always be available. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones, more than five tasks, or non-CLIP architectures, and whether the single-layer Portland network remains sufficient as model count grows substantially.
