## Problem

Scaling MLLMs across visual resolution, training data, and model parameters causes computational costs to surge, primarily due to the proliferation of visual tokens. Existing token compression or pruning methods risk losing critical visual information, creating a trade-off between efficiency and performance. The paper identifies that FFN computations on visual tokens induce only marginal feature updates and that most visual information is progressively integrated into later tokens through early causal layers, yet no architecture exploits this redundancy to simultaneously accelerate both training and inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251356/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images processed at resolutions ranging from 224px (CoS pretraining) to 1296 tokens (CoS SFT) or 336–672px (LLaVA-HD dynamic resolution), along with a text prompt. The CLIP ViT-L encoder produces visual tokens that are partitioned into retained (global) and skipped (local) tokens before entering the LLaMA3 8B backbone. During inference, after the pre-fill phase, skipped visual tokens are removed from the KV-cache, so autoregressive decoding operates on a reduced token set (e.g., 25% of original FLOPs for CoS), while the output remains a standard free-form text response.

## Method

Skip-Vision introduces a unified architecture with two complementary acceleration strategies. During training, skipped visual tokens (local/small-window tokens constituting ~80% of total visual tokens) bypass all FFN layers while still participating in self-attention, and a ToMe-inspired token merging step further reduces redundancy among them; adaptive summary tokens generated by a lightweight linear layer are appended at both ends of the skipped sequence to consolidate information for next-token prediction. During inference, a skip KV-cache mechanism removes the K/V pairs of skipped visual tokens after pre-filling, so subsequent decoding steps attend only to retained and summary tokens. The paper provides a theoretical error bound via Lipschitz continuity analysis, showing the cumulative skip error is bounded by ε·(γ^L − 1)/(γ − 1) when γ < 1, and empirically validates this with up to 35% training time reduction, 75% inference FLOPs reduction, and 45% latency reduction while maintaining or improving benchmark performance.

## Results

Skip-Vision achieves up to 35.8% training time reduction, 75% inference FLOPs reduction, and 44.4% inference latency reduction while maintaining or improving performance across all three tested architectures (LLaVA, LLaVA-HD, CoS). In the CoS setting, SV-CoS matches the baseline's average score (46.0) while using only 25% of the inference FLOPs and 90ms latency vs. 160ms. When scaled to SV-9M data, SV-CoS achieves the best scores on MMBench (78.4), MMVet (51.7), MathVista (61.3), and MMStar (52.3) among all 8B-tier open-source models. The LLaVA-HD variant reduces training from 19 to 12.2 hours (−35.8%) and latency from 450ms to 250ms (−44.4%) with only a 1.1% average score drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA [69], SV-LLaVA (Nr=256) improves the average score from 42.0 to 42.6 while cutting inference FLOPs to 60% and latency to 103ms; the more aggressive Nr=100 variant trades 0.7% average accuracy for 75% FLOPs reduction. Compared to other efficiency methods under the LLaVA setting, SV-LLaVA (Nr=100) outperforms FastV [22] (41.3 vs. 40.4 avg), MQT-LLaVA [39] (41.3 vs. 38.6), and Tokenpacker [60] (41.3 vs. 40.5) at comparable or lower FLOPs. In the LLaVA-HD setting, SV-LLaVA-HD [67] matches the baseline within 1.1% average while halving both FLOPs (50%) and latency (250ms vs. 450ms). In the CoS setting, SV-CoS [40] achieves identical average performance (46.0) at only 25% FLOPs and 90ms latency (vs. 160ms), and scaling to SV-1M data pushes the average to 49.3. Against SOTA 8B models in Table 3, SV-CoS (SV-9M) leads on MMBench (78.4 vs. 77.6 Ovis/MiniCPM-V), MMVet (51.7 vs. 40.1 mPLUG-Owl3), MathVista (61.3 vs. 54.3 MiniCPM-V), and MMStar (52.3 vs. 49.5 Ovis), but trails MiniCPM-V2.5 [117] on TextVQA (69.1 vs. 76.6), MMMU (43 vs. 45.8), and OCRBench (69.4 vs. 72.5), and Mini-Gemini-HD [61] on MME (1550 vs. 1606).

## Relation to Prior Work

Skip-Vision builds on the LLaVA/LLaVA-HD/CoS family of MLLM architectures by modifying their internal computation flow rather than compressing or pruning visual tokens as prior efficiency methods (FastV, MQT, TokenPacker, PruMerge) do. The token-merge strategy is directly adapted from ToMe, and the CLS-similarity-based token selection follows VisionZip, while the core observation that FFN induces marginal updates on visual tokens parallels ShortV. The key departure is treating this observation as an architectural design principle—systematically bypassing FFN for a large fraction of visual tokens during training and removing their KV entries during inference—rather than as a post-hoc pruning heuristic, and grounding the approach with a Lipschitz-based error bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% inference FLOPs reduction and 45% latency reduction are practically significant for scaling MLLMs, and the efficiency savings can be reinvested into data scaling (SV-9M), which the paper shows yields competitive or superior performance to SOTA 8B models. However, skip KV-cache introduces measurable degradation on fine-grained tasks (TextVQA, OCRBench), and the Lipschitz-based theoretical bound relies on assumptions (spectral norms ≤ 1) that may not hold in all trained models. Open questions include whether the skip-FFN strategy generalizes to video MLLMs, whether the optimal skip ratio is architecture-dependent, and whether the approach composes with other efficiency techniques like quantization or speculative decoding.
