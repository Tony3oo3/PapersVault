## Problem

Existing diffusion-based blind super-resolution methods rely on synthetic degradation embeddings or text prompts that are not aligned with visual feature maps, limiting their ability to generalize to real-world unknown degradations encountered at inference. The paper addresses this by asking whether degradation characteristics of an unseen LR image can be inferred purely from compositions of attributes learned during training, without any real-world low-quality data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250029/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image with arbitrary (potentially unknown) degradation as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image at a general scaling factor a. No degradation parameters or text prompts are required at inference; the degradation embedding is extracted entirely from the LR image itself via CLIP's vision encoder and non-reference IQA metrics. Evaluation uses 512×512 sub-images from DIV2K (synthetic BSRGAN degradations) and full images from RealSR (real-world camera degradations).

## Method

ZFusion introduces a Compositional Zero-shot Degradation Learning (CZSD) module that decomposes an LR image's degradation into two attribute sets: synthetic attributes derived from CLIP text embeddings of degradation operator descriptions (blur kernel, downsample factor, noise level, JPEG quality) and real-world attributes derived from CLIP text embeddings of non-reference IQA scores (HyperIQA, BRISQUE, TOPIQ, NIMA). Both attribute sets are projected into visual modality via adapters on the CLIP vision encoder, and an alignment loss enforces matching batch-level statistics between them to reduce synthetic-only bias. The fused degradation embedding is then injected at two stages: (1) a SwinIR-based artifact suppression network where residual degradation-aware self-attention blocks (RDSB) recalibrate features via element-wise multiplication with the embedding, and (2) a frozen StableDiffusion latent diffusion model where degradation-aware adapter modules condition the ControlNet at every denoising timestep. Training uses only synthetic BSRGAN degradations on DIV2K/Flickr2K/OutdoorSceneTraining, yet the compositional ZSL mechanism enables zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world degradation operators.

## Results

On the RealSR dataset (real-world unknown degradations), ZFusion achieves the best scores on 5 out of 8 IQA metrics: CLIP-IQA (0.6848), NIQE (4.7152), MUSIQ-AVA (5.1115), MANIQA (0.5687), and CNN-IQA (0.6798), while trailing on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR 21.05, SSIM 0.5788, LPIPS 0.2456). On DIV2K with synthetic degradations, ZFusion similarly leads on 5/8 metrics, including LPIPS (0.2694), CLIP-IQA (0.7882), MUSIQ-AVA (5.7624), MANIQA (0.6141), and CNN-IQA (0.7044). The MANIQA gap on RealSR is particularly large: ZFusion scores 0.5687 versus the next-best DiffBIR at 0.3632, a 56% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZFusion is compared against seven SOTA Blind SR methods: Real-ESRGAN, ResShift, PASD, SeeSR, SinSR, StableSR, and DiffBIR. On RealSR (real-world degradations), ZFusion decisively outperforms all baselines on perceptual/no-reference metrics—most dramatically on MANIQA (0.5687 vs. DiffBIR's 0.3632, a 56% relative gain) and CLIP-IQA (0.6848 vs. DiffBIR's 0.6667). However, ZFusion trails PASD and Real-ESRGAN on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), consistent with the generative diffusion prior producing more realistic but less pixel-accurate reconstructions. On DIV2K (synthetic degradations), ZFusion leads on LPIPS (0.2694 vs. SeeSR's 0.2787) and all four no-reference IQA metrics, but again lags ResShift on PSNR (21.05 vs. 21.88) and SSIM (0.5547 vs. 0.5916). DiffBIR is consistently the strongest runner-up, achieving 4 second-best metrics on RealSR, confirming that the compositional ZSL module provides the key differentiator over existing diffusion-based Blind SR pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based Blind SR (DiffBIR, StableSR, SeeSR) and compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) from the ZSL literature. It repurposes the CZSL paradigm—composing seen attributes to understand unseen concepts—to model unknown real-world degradations as compositions of synthetic degradation attributes (encoded via CLIP text prompts describing blur/downsample/noise/JPEG parameters) and real-world attributes (encoded via CLIP text prompts describing non-reference IQA scores). The degradation suppression backbone follows SwinIR with a novel residual degradation-aware self-attention block, while the generative prior follows the StableDiffusion/ControlNet architecture of DiffBIR and StableSR, with degradation embeddings injected at every diffusion timestep via a degradation-aware adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a mechanism for generalizing to unseen real-world degradations without any real-world training data, which is the central challenge of Blind SR. However, the method trades pixel-level fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS are not best on RealSR) for perceptual quality (MANIQA, CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ-AVA are best), raising the question of whether the CLIP text encoding of degradation parameters captures genuinely informative degradation structure or merely provides a useful conditioning signal. Open questions include whether the alignment loss between synthetic and real-world attribute statistics is the right inductive bias, whether the approach scales to video or multi-frame restoration, and whether the large MANIQA gap over DiffBIR (0.61 vs 0.38 on DIV2K) reflects a genuine perceptual advantage or a metric-specific artifact.
