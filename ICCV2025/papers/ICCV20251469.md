## Problem

Current 3D latent diffusion methods represent every object—regardless of complexity—with a single global latent, causing detail loss on multi-part objects, ignoring the part-by-part structure that 3D designers actually use, and offering only global (text/image) conditioning without local part-level controllability. No existing framework jointly generates coherent, independently detailed 3D parts with explicit inter-part and geometry-appearance consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251469/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt, per-part text descriptions, and per-part 3D bounding boxes (encoded as 6-surface meshes through the 3D VAE encoder to share the geometric latent space) as conditioning. It outputs a set of N ≤ 8 part meshes (decoded via Flexicube-parameter prediction) and multi-view part-centric images, which are assembled into a final textured 3D object. For image-to-3D, a Flux.1-generated image serves as the global visual condition alongside text.

## Method

CoPart represents each 3D part with a hybrid latent: geometric tokens from a fine-tuned 3D part VAE (initialized from CraftMan) and image tokens from a 2D image VAE (Pixart-α), then jointly denoises all part latents via two Transformer-based diffusion branches. The key mechanism is mutual guidance: cross-part attention lets each part attend to all other parts' tokens for inter-part consistency, while cross-modality attention exchanges information between the 3D and 2D branches of the same part for geometry-appearance alignment; a global guidance branch (sharing parameters with part branches via learnable embeddings) further regularizes training. Bounding box conditions are injected by encoding boxes as meshes through the 3D VAE and adding them via cross-attention in the 3D branch, and by rendering 2D wireframes fed through a lightweight ControlNet in the 2D global branch. A refinement stage uses the Trellis enhancer with both generated part images and voxelized part geometry to polish final meshes.

## Results

On part-aware metrics, CoPart achieves the best scores across all three evaluation criteria: CLIP(N-T) 0.1607, CLIP(I-T) 0.1768, and ULIP-T 0.1355, outperforming the strongest holistic baseline Rodin (0.1425 / 0.1571 / 0.1244) by 12.8%, 12.4%, and 9.0% respectively. On whole-aware metrics, CoPart is competitive (CLIP(N-T) 0.2010, CLIP(I-T) 0.2387, ULIP-T 0.1743) but slightly trails Rodin and Trellis. In a user study with 51 participants, CoPart receives 54.9% preference on whole-aware prompts and 60.8% on part-aware prompts, far exceeding Rodin (33.3% / 25.5%) and PartGen (11.8% / 13.7%). Generation time is 65 s (12 s for single-part), substantially slower than holistic methods (3–16 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four holistic 3D generators (Shap-E, Unique3D, CraftMan, Trellis), CoPart wins decisively on all three part-aware metrics, with the largest gap over Shap-E (e.g., CLIP(N-T) Part 0.1607 vs 0.0875, nearly 2×). On whole-aware metrics, CoPart is within 0.003–0.006 of the best holistic methods (Rodin, Trellis), indicating that the part-based decomposition does not sacrifice holistic quality. Rodin remains the strongest holistic baseline on whole-aware CLIP scores (0.2042 / 0.2416 / 0.1785 vs CoPart's 0.2010 / 0.2387 / 0.1743) but trails CoPart on every part-aware metric. In the user study, CoPart dominates both Rodin (54.9% vs 33.3% whole; 60.8% vs 25.5% part) and PartGen (54.9% vs 11.8% whole; 60.8% vs 13.7% part), confirming that the part-based representation is perceived as substantially better by human evaluators. Qualitatively, CoPart also outperforms the part-specific baseline SALAD, which is constrained to PartNet's 24 categories, whereas CoPart generalizes across 175 categories from PartVerse.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPart builds directly on the 3D-native latent diffusion paradigm established by CLAY and CraftMan, which encode 3D meshes into VAE latents and train DiT-based diffusion models, and on BiDiff's bidirectional 2D-3D guidance for cross-modal consistency. It departs from prior part-based generators (SALAD, Part123, PartGen) by adopting a 'bottom-up' strategy that jointly generates coherent parts from diverse Objaverse data rather than a 'top-down' segmentation-then-reconstruction pipeline or category-restricted PartNet data. The mutual guidance mechanism extends BiDiff's two-model synchronization to a multi-part, multi-modality setting via cross-part and cross-modality attention blocks, and the bounding-box-as-mesh encoding is a novel conditioning trick that reuses the 3D VAE encoder to inject spatial constraints into the same latent space as part geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PartVerse dataset (91k parts across 175 categories with text descriptions) fills a notable gap left by PartNet's 24 categories and provides a practical resource for part-aware 3D research. The part-based representation unlocks downstream applications (part editing, articulated object generation, mini-scene generation) without additional training, which is practically valuable for 3D content pipelines. However, the method is constrained to at most 8 parts per object due to GPU memory, assembly quality depends on user-provided bounding boxes, and the whole-aware metrics remain competitive rather than dominant compared to holistic generators like Trellis, suggesting the part decomposition introduces some global coherence cost that future work must address.
