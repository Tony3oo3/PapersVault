## Problem

It is widely assumed that VLMs provide visually grounded and reliable driving explanations, yet this assumption has never been rigorously tested under degraded or missing visual inputs. The paper reveals that VLMs often fabricate plausible driving responses from general knowledge or textual cues rather than true visual grounding, a failure mode concealed by imbalanced datasets and inadequate evaluation metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251157/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes single-frame driving camera images (from nuScenes) in three conditions—clean, corrupted with 15 types (weather, sensor failure, motion blur, transmission errors), or fully absent (text-only)—paired with structured text prompts spanning multiple-choice, open-ended VQA, and visual grounding question types. For the RAU component, a corrupted multi-camera image is fed to a VLM agent that selects and applies a denoising tool, producing a restored image consumed by a downstream BEV 3D detector. Evaluation uses GPT-3.5-turbo as judge with task-specific rubrics (GPT_ctx), alongside accuracy, BLEU-4, and ROUGE-L.

## Method

DriveBench is constructed by re-sampling DriveLM/nuScenes to eliminate the 78.6% 'Going Ahead' label bias, excluding ambiguous cases (occlusion, subtle turn signals), and applying 15 programmatically generated corruptions to 19,200 frames, yielding 20,498 QA pairs across 17 settings. The empirical study evaluates 12 VLMs (commercial, open-source, and driving-specialist) and demonstrates that performance under text-only or corrupted inputs remains comparable to clean inputs, exposing fabrication rather than robustness. The paper further shows VLMs possess inherent corruption awareness but only acknowledge degradation when explicitly prompted, and that fine-tuned driving VLMs (DriveLM, Dolphins) gain primarily from response-template alignment rather than genuine visual grounding. Motivated by these findings, the authors propose Robust Agentic Utilization (RAU), which uses a VLM (InternVL2) as an agentic planner to identify the corruption type at runtime and invoke a corresponding pre-trained denoising tool, improving BEV detector NDS by 10.9–15.6% under corruption without retraining the downstream model.

## Results

On DriveBench (19,200 frames, 20,498 QA pairs), GPT-4o achieves the highest GPT_ctx scores: 35.37 (Perception), 51.30 (Prediction), 75.75 (Planning), and 45.40 (Behavior) under clean inputs. Critically, most VLMs show negligible degradation under 15 corruption types or even text-only (no image) inputs — e.g., GPT-4o scores 36.48 on Perception with text-only vs. 35.37 clean, while human performance drops from 47.67 to 38.32 under corruption. The proposed RAU framework improves 3D detection robustness on RoboBEV, boosting BEVFormer NDS from 30.64 to 35.44 (+10.9%) and DETR3D NDS from 30.76 to 34.12 (+15.6%) under corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all open-source VLMs across every task under clean inputs (e.g., 75.75 Planning vs. 61.30 for Qwen2-VL-72B), but the gap narrows dramatically under corruption and text-only settings, where several open models (Qwen2-VL-7B, Phi-3.5, InternVL2) match or exceed GPT-4o on Behavior (54.48, 39.16, 20.14 vs. 50.03). The most striking comparison is VLMs vs. humans: human Perception drops 9.35 points under corruption (47.67→38.32) while GPT-4o drops only 0.12 (35.37→35.25), and human Behavior drops 15.42 points (69.51→54.09) while GPT-4o actually improves (45.40→50.03). Specialist driving models (DriveLM, Dolphins) underperform general VLMs on most tasks and show the largest text-only collapse (DriveLM Perception: 16.85→8.75), indicating limited generalization beyond their fine-tuning distribution. For the RAU 3D detection task, both BEVFormer+RAU and DETR3D+RAU outperform their un-augmented counterparts under corruption by 4.8 and 3.36 NDS points respectively, with mRR improving from 0.59→0.68 and 0.71→0.79.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DriveLM as its base dataset and QA structure, extending it with 15 corruption types, text-only inputs, and a re-sampled balanced distribution to address inherited biases from nuScenes. It departs from prior driving VLM benchmarks (BDD-X, nuScenes-QA, Talk2Car, DriveVLM) by introducing a dedicated robustness evaluation axis and by critically revisiting the evaluation metrics (accuracy, BLEU, ROUGE, GPT scores) used in those works. The RAU framework repurposes the All-In-One Image Restoration model as a tool within an agentic VLM pipeline, drawing on the OoD robustness literature for BEV perception (RoboBEV) but shifting the robustness decision to a VLM planner rather than a dedicated denoising network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLMs produce near-identical driving answers with no visual input at all fundamentally challenges the field's assumption that VLMs provide visually grounded, interpretable driving decisions — a premise underpinning much of the VLA and end-to-end driving literature. The paper also exposes how dataset imbalance (78.6% 'Going Ahead' labels) and metric limitations (GPT scores varying with prompt phrasing) can mask these failures, calling for more rigorous evaluation protocols. Open questions include whether larger or better-trained VLMs will develop genuine visual grounding, whether the RAU agentic approach scales beyond 3D detection to full-stack driving, and how to design metrics that capture safety-critical reasoning rather than surface-level answer matching.
