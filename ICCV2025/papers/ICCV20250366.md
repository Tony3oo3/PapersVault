## Problem

Existing key-frame-based micro-expression recognition (MER) methods rely on accurate onset, apex, and offset frame indexes to extract partial ME representations, yet key-frame errors are inevitable in practice due to inter-annotator variability and imperfect automatic spotting. The paper demonstrates that these errors severely degrade the performance of state-of-the-art key-frame-based methods, exposing a critical robustness gap that hinders practical deployment of MER systems.

## Input / Output

CausalNet takes two optical flow images (28×28×3, with horizontal, vertical, and optical strain channels) computed over the full onset-to-offset ME sequence: one for the onset-apex phase and one for the apex-offset phase. This complete-range input contrasts with prior methods that use only the apex frame or onset-apex segment. The output is a discrete emotion class label (3-class for CASME II/SMIC/SAMM composite evaluation, 3- or 5-class for MMEW), produced by a fully-connected layer over concatenated short-range and long-range feature tokens.

## Method

CausalNet comprises two modules. The Causal Motion Position Learning Module (CMPLM) exploits the observation that muscle contraction (onset-apex) and relaxation (apex-offset) produce nearly opposite optical flow directions in AU regions; it encodes these directional maps via a direction encoder and applies absolute positional cross attention (adapted from StableMask's pseudo-attention scores) to localize AU-related movement areas, feeding position embeddings into the main branch to suppress redundant regions. The Causal Attention Block (CAB) performs sparse spatial attention followed by causal temporal attention on bidirectionally concatenated onset-apex and apex-offset tokens, generating short-range features (focused on each individual phase) and long-range features (capturing the full onset-offset trend); a Causal Relation Mining step then performs cross-attention between the two long-range features to deepen contraction-relaxation relational learning. The causal masking design ensures that as feature fusion deepens, local representations remain sensitive to key ME information while global representations capture the overall temporal trend, addressing the weakening of local feature perception in deep fusion.

## Results

On the CASME II dataset, CausalNet achieves 97.48% UF1 and 97.82% UAR, surpassing the previous best (HFA-Net at 97.38% UF1). On the composite dataset (CASME II + SMIC + SAMM), CausalNet reaches 89.30% UF1 and 89.41% UAR, outperforming EDMDBN (88.21% UF1) and TGMAE (87.90% UF1). On MMEW, CausalNet attains 93.10% UF1 and 94.44% ACC, leading EDMDBN (92.16% UF1). On SMIC and SAMM individually, CausalNet is competitive but not always the top performer (e.g., μ-BERT leads on SMIC UF1 at 85.50 vs. 84.05, and HFA-Net leads on SAMM UF1 at 90.02 vs. 87.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalNet achieves the best UF1 and UAR on CASME II (97.48/97.82), narrowly beating HFA-Net (97.38/97.54) and SRMCL (96.35/96.49), and leads on the composite dataset (89.30 UF1) over EDMDBN (88.21) and μ-BERT (89.03). On MMEW, CausalNet outperforms EDMDBN by ~1% UF1 (93.10 vs. 92.16) and ~1.7% ACC (94.44 vs. 92.70). However, CausalNet does not dominate every single-dataset metric: μ-BERT leads on SMIC UF1 (85.50 vs. 84.05) and HFA-Net leads on SAMM UF1 (90.02 vs. 87.08), though CausalNet recovers on SAMM ACC (90.83 vs. not reported for HFA-Net). In the robustness evaluation under key-frame index errors (Fig. 6), CausalNet consistently outperforms all seven compared key-frame-based methods (Dual-Inception, Micro-attention, OffApexNet, OffTANet, MMNET, HTNet, SRMCL) across STD levels of 10, 20, and 30 frames, with UF1 improvements of 2.70%, 1.85%, and 1.42% over the second-best method respectively. The paper also notes that video-based methods (SLSTT-LSTM, TGMAE) are inherently more robust to key-frame errors due to full-sequence input, but CausalNet matches or exceeds their accuracy while using a more compact onset-offset OF representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the key-frame-based MER lineage (apex-frame, onset-apex, and onset-offset methods) by adopting the full onset-offset optical flow representation to address a practical gap: prior methods assumed accurate expert-annotated key-frames while ignoring the inevitable errors from manual annotation or automatic spotting. The Causal Motion Position Learning Module draws on StableMask's pseudo-attention mechanism (adapted from NLP) to encode absolute spatial position information via sparse cross-attention, while the Causal Attention Block introduces a bidirectional concatenation strategy with causal temporal masking to simultaneously capture short-range (phase-specific) and long-range (full-sequence) muscle motion features. Conceptually, the paper departs from the 'less is more' philosophy that dominated prior key-frame-based MER by arguing that the complete ME range input, combined with targeted causal relationship learning, offers both robustness and accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most valuable contribution is the systematic robustness evaluation protocol—introducing controlled key-frame index noise (STD 10/20/30) and automatic spotting errors—which exposes a significant practical vulnerability in existing SOTA methods that the field had largely overlooked. However, the method still fails when the expression falls entirely outside the erroneous onset-offset window, and the authors acknowledge that improving spotting algorithms or annotation quality may be more impactful than refining the MER model itself. The causal relationship observation (opposite muscle motion directions in contraction vs. relaxation phases) provides a physiologically grounded inductive bias, but its practical benefit (0.39–0.44% from CRM alone) is modest. Open questions include whether the full-range input strategy generalizes to longer or multi-expression sequences and whether the pseudo-attention mechanism transfers to other spatially-structured recognition tasks.
