## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods either produce small '3D bubbles' that cannot be freely navigated, or rely on 3D latent generators trained on limited domain-specific data that lack the diversity and instruction-following of 2D generators trained on billions of images. The paper addresses how to combine the artistic quality and prompt understanding of 2D generators with the geometric consistency of 3D generators to produce large, coherent, freely navigable 3D worlds from text, without any fine-tuning or inference-time optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-level text prompt describing a world (e.g., a city theme) and optionally tile-specific prompts, which an LLM can auto-expand from the seed prompt. The output is a W×H grid of square 3D tiles represented as 3D Gaussian Splats, seamlessly blended into a single navigable scene. Each tile is generated in an isometric framing with a unit-size square base, and the final world can be explored via arbitrary camera trajectories rather than being restricted to a fixed viewpoint.

## Method

SynCity generates the world tile-by-tile on a grid using three off-the-shelf generators (ChatGPT, Flux, TRELLIS) through novel prompt engineering. For 2D generation, Flux is prompted via inpainting with a fixed isometric base slab and a mask covering the tile region, while previously generated tiles are rendered as context to ensure scale and appearance consistency. For 3D generation, each tile image is foreground-extracted and 'rebased' onto a slightly larger gray slab before being fed to TRELLIS, which produces 3D Gaussian Splats with a detectable base for geometric validation and alignment. The key blending step operates in TRELLIS's latent voxel grid: the boundary between two neighboring tiles is first inpainted in 2D, then the combined latent volume is denoised only in the narrow boundary region using TRELLIS's latent denoiser, with sparse structure kept fixed and structured latents resampled via multi-view conditioned denoising to avoid artifacts from naive interpolation.

## Results

In a human preference study (n=22) comparing SynCity against BlockFusion on a city scene, SynCity achieves a 90.9% overall win rate, with participants preferring its geometry (81.8%), exploration quality (90.9%), diversity (90.9%), and realism (86.4%). Ablations confirm that context-aware 2D prompting, rebasing, and 3D latent blending are each necessary: without rebasing, tile squareness drops from 1.00 to 0.92 and completeness from 1.00 to 0.73; without 3D blending, visible discontinuities appear between tiles. For latent upsampling, the proposed multi-frame conditioning reduces FID from 200.5 (naïve interpolation) to 89.1 and KID from 0.243 to 0.051.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative baseline is BlockFusion [60], a latent tri-plane extrapolation method that also generates expandable 3D scenes. SynCity wins on every evaluated criterion in the human preference study, with the largest margins on overall preference, exploration, and diversity (all 90.9%) and a more modest but still strong lead on geometry (81.8%) and realism (86.4%). The qualitative gap is attributed to SynCity's use of a powerful 2D image generator (Flux) for tile appearance, which BlockFusion lacks, giving SynCity richer textures and greater scene diversity. BlockFusion, by contrast, relies on domain-specific 3D training data and produces untextured meshes, limiting both visual quality and the variety of generated content. No other baselines (e.g., WonderWorld, LT3SD, or image-projection methods) are included in the quantitative comparison, so the evidence base for the superiority claim rests on a single head-to-head evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynCity positions itself at the intersection of two prior lines of work: image-projection-based scene generators (Lucid Dreamer, Text2Immersion, WonderJourney) that produce visually rich but non-navigable '3D bubbles,' and latent-space 3D scene generators (BlockFusion, LT3SD) that can build large scenes but lack the diversity and instruction-following of 2D generators trained on billions of images. The paper's key departure is a training-free, tile-by-tile pipeline that repurposes TRELLIS (an object-level 3D generator) to reconstruct local scene chunks and Flux (a 2D inpainter) to produce consistently framed isometric tile views, connected by a novel latent-space blending operation. Unlike prior compositing approaches that place individual objects into a scene, SynCity generates complete scene tiles that are seamlessly fused, which the authors frame as a fundamentally new approach to scene generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of SynCity is practically significant: it requires no fine-tuning or inference-time optimization, making it immediately deployable with off-the-shelf models. However, the rigid grid structure, single-view conditioning per tile, and reliance on heuristic 3D validation limit robustness, and the evaluation rests on a single baseline (BlockFusion) with a small human study (n=22). Open questions include whether the grid can be relaxed (random shifts, scales), whether a coarse-to-fine hierarchy could improve global coherence, and whether fine-tuning on large-scale scene datasets (currently unavailable) would simplify alignment and rebasing. The approach also raises the broader question of how far prompt engineering alone can push multi-model pipelines before dedicated training becomes necessary.
