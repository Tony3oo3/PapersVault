## Problem

Multi-modal instruction datasets for MLLM fine-tuning suffer from severe data redundancy, making full-data training computationally wasteful. Existing data selection methods—whether rule-based, human-reward, or gradient-based—either lack flexibility across diverse tasks, are prohibitively expensive, or require additional downstream training, and none systematically account for inter-sample relationships (redundancy and outlier noise) in the multi-modal setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-modal instruction dataset where each sample is a triple (image, text query, text answer), extracting unified token-level features from the penultimate layer of a pre-trained MLLM backbone. It outputs a ranked subset of samples with an adaptively determined selection proportion per task category, where the proportion is scaled by the average ratio of the largest singular value to total singular values, reflecting task difficulty. No hand-designed rules or hyperparameter tuning is required; the framework automatically adapts to the given dataset.

## Method

DataTailor scores each sample along three complementary principles: (1) Informativeness is measured by the entropy of normalized singular values from SVD on the unified feature matrix, where higher entropy indicates richer, harder-to-compress information; (2) Uniqueness is computed via Ward hierarchical clustering followed by weighted intra-cluster distance, where samples far from their neighbors receive higher scores; (3) Representativeness is captured by an inter-cluster cosine-similarity coefficient that penalizes samples in isolated clusters (likely noisy or mislabeled). These three values are adaptively combined using a conversation-round-based influence factor—multi-turn instructions weight informativeness more heavily, while single-turn instructions emphasize uniqueness and representativeness. The selection proportion per task is further adapted by the average largest-singular-value ratio, allocating more data to harder tasks to improve training robustness.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-mix-665k, DataTailor achieves 100.1% relative performance of full-data fine-tuning with only 7.5% of the data (50k samples), and surpasses full-data performance (101.3%) at 15% data (100k samples). On MiniGPT4-Instruction, DataTailor reaches 104.6% relative performance using just 0.2k samples (~6% of 3.4k). Notably, DataTailor outperforms full fine-tuning on the challenging MMMU benchmark (33.9 vs. 32.8 on LLaVA-7B; 23.6 vs. 23.4 on MiniGPT4-7B), demonstrating the 'Less is More' principle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DataTailor outperforms all 9 baselines on overall relative performance across both datasets. On LLaVA-1.5-mix-665k (7.5% data), DataTailor (100.1%) beats the strongest MLLM-specific baselines TIVE (94.6%) and ICONS (98.8%, which uses 20% data), as well as LLM-transferred methods InsTag (96.4%), LESS (94.3%), and IFD (87.3%). On MiniGPT4-Instruction (~6% data), DataTailor (104.6%) surpasses EL2N (102.2%), InsTag (100.8%), and IFD (99.8%), while SELF-FILTER (92.8%) and TIVE (92.7%) lag significantly. The gap is most pronounced on general VQA tasks (GQA, TextVQA), where LLM-transferred methods like IFD and LESS drop to ~49% average due to poor representativeness, whereas DataTailor maintains near-full-data levels. At 15% data, DataTailor is the only method to exceed 100% full-data performance (101.3%), while COINCIDE and ICONS at 20% data still fall short (98.0% and 98.8% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms in MLLM data selection—data-specific rules (IFD, InstructionMining), human-reward scoring (InsTag), and gradient-based influence estimation (LESS, TIVE, ICONS)—by reframing the problem around sample *relationships* rather than individual sample quality. It repurposes spectral analysis (SVD entropy) from information theory to quantify informativeness, and introduces Ward hierarchical clustering to capture intra-cluster uniqueness and inter-cluster representativeness, a combination not previously applied to multi-modal instruction data. Unlike gradient-based methods that require costly downstream training, DataTailor operates purely on pre-extracted features, and unlike rule-based methods, it adapts automatically to any dataset without hand-designed criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving over 100% of full-data fine-tuning performance with only 15% of the data (and ~80% wall-clock savings versus TIVE) directly addresses the growing computational bottleneck of MLLM instruction tuning. The transferability results—using LLaVA features to select data for architecturally different models like mPLUG-Owl and Bunny—suggest the selected subsets capture intrinsic data value rather than model-specific artifacts. Open questions include whether the three-principle framework extends to interleaved multi-modal data (video, audio) and whether the SVD-based informativeness proxy remains valid as MLLM architectures evolve beyond the penultimate-layer feature assumption.
