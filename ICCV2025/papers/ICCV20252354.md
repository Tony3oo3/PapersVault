## Problem

Federated learning suffers significant performance degradation under non-IID client data, and while sharing a small portion of client data globally can mitigate heterogeneity, raw data sharing is infeasible under privacy constraints. Existing privacy-preserving alternatives—differential privacy on raw images or generator-based synthetic data—either degrade utility through excessive noise or introduce computational overhead and residual privacy leaks. The paper asks whether one can selectively share only the features that matter for the task, with formal privacy guarantees, to retain the performance benefit of data sharing.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local set of RGB images (H×W×3) with labels; a sparsity level s (fraction of pixels retained) and privacy budget (ε, δ) are set globally. During a one-time warmup phase, a pre-trained model computes per-pixel attribution scores for every client image via a single backward pass. The output is a privacy-protected dataset D_k^p of masked-and-noised images (same spatial dimensions as the originals, with non-relevant pixels zeroed and Gaussian noise added) that is uploaded to the server and redistributed to all clients for joint local training.

## Method

FedXDS first trains a warmup model via standard FedAvg for R_warmup rounds, then computes propagation-based attribution scores (LRP, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, or Gradient×Input) for each client image in a single backward pass. A binary mask retains only the top-s% most relevant pixels per image, and the masked image is perturbed with Gaussian noise calibrated to the sensitivity bound Δ_f ≤ 1 of the masking operator, yielding (ε,δ)-metric differential privacy with less noise than applying DP to the full image. The server aggregates all clients' protected representations into a global dataset, which each client uses alongside its local data in a weighted composite loss (weight λ) during subsequent FedAvg rounds. The key insight is that attribution-guided sparsification is non-expansive in ℓ₂ norm, reducing sensitivity and thus required noise, while LRP's layer-wise relevance conservation produces structurally coherent masks that preserve semantically meaningful regions even at high sparsity—unlike gradient-based methods that yield scattered hotspot pixels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (K=10, α=0.1), FedXDS with LRP (FedXLRP) achieves 83.46% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FedFed (82.58%) and FedAvg (72.90%) by 0.88 and 10.56 percentage points respectively. Under the most challenging setting (K=100, α=0.05), FedXLRP reaches 77.02% on CIFAR-10 versus 55.53% for FedAvg. In terms of communication efficiency, FedXLRP requires only 14 rounds to reach 70% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (K=10) compared to 49 for FedAvg and 15 for FedFed. On real-world LEAF datasets, FedXDS achieves 91.55% on CelebA and 89.03% on FEMNIST, surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedXDS (LRP variant) consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on CIFAR-10 across both client counts and heterogeneity levels, with the largest gaps over FedAvg (e.g., +10.56 pp at K=10, α=0.1) and FedDF (+11.06 pp). The closest competitor is FedFed [30], which uses VAE-based feature distillation; FedXLRP edges it out by 0.88 pp on CIFAR-10 (K=10, α=0.1) and 1.39 pp on CIFAR-100 (K=100, α=0.1), though FedFed narrowly wins on Tiny-ImageNet K=100 α=0.1 (34.89 vs 33.18) and CIFAR-100 K=10 α=0.05 (52.27 vs 52.07). Regularization-based methods (FedProx, FedDyn, SCAFFOLD, FedSAM, FedDISCO) all trail FedXLRP by 4–12 pp on CIFAR-10, with FedDyn being the strongest among them (78.25% at K=10, α=0.1). Data-sharing baselines FedFTG and FedGen, which synthesize pseudo-data via generators, are outperformed by 5–9 pp on CIFAR-10, and FedXDS also requires substantially fewer communication rounds (14 vs 28 for FedFTG to reach 70% accuracy). On real-world LEAF datasets (CelebA, FEMNIST), FedXDS leads FedFed by 0.79 and 0.75 pp respectively, confirming gains under natural distribution shift rather than synthetic Dirichlet partitioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes propagation-based XAI attribution methods (LRP, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, Gradient×Input) from their traditional role in model interpretability to serve as a data-selection mechanism in federated learning. It builds on the FedAvg training loop and the line of data/knowledge-sharing FL methods (FedFed, FedFTG, FedGen, FedAux, FedDF) that address heterogeneity by distributing client information, but departs from them by avoiding generator-based synthesis in favor of attribution-guided masking of raw features. The privacy layer draws on metric differential privacy and the Gaussian mechanism, applying them to the sparsified (attribution-masked) representation rather than to full-dimensional raw data, which the authors argue reduces sensitivity and thus required noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a simple, generator-free pipeline (warmup → attribute → mask → noise → share) that consistently outperforms both optimization-based FL methods and generator-based data-sharing methods in accuracy and communication rounds across multiple datasets and heterogeneity levels. The finding that LRP's structurally coherent relevance maps substantially outperform gradient-based 'hotspot' attributions under high sparsity (83–84% vs. 70–76% at s=70%) suggests explanation quality has direct downstream utility beyond human interpretability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities (text, video, 3D), how the warmup-model dependency affects robustness when the initial model is poorly trained, and whether the metric-privacy guarantee (L2-based) is sufficient for all realistic threat models beyond the tested MIA and feature-inversion attacks.
