## Problem

Existing collaborative BEV semantic segmentation methods transmit dense, scene-level 2D BEV feature maps (~30 Mbps) that exceed practical C-V2X bandwidth limits (10 Mbps), contain substantial redundancy, and inherently lose height information, degrading segmentation accuracy. No prior work has explored sparse, object-centric 3D representations that preserve complete spatial information while enabling selective, bandwidth-efficient transmission between vehicles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250172/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each vehicle in the collaboration (2–7 agents) provides multi-view RGB images from K cameras, encoded by a shared ResNet34 backbone. The ego vehicle outputs a 256×256 BEV semantic segmentation map at 39 cm resolution over a [-50,50]m × [-50,50]m × [-3,1]m region, with three classes: dynamic vehicles, drivable roads, and lane lines. Rather than transmitting full feature maps, collaborative CAVs send only a sparse subset of 3D Gaussians (position, semantic logits, opacity) compressed to 62 Kbps–7 Mbps via V2V links within a 70 m communication range.

## Method

GSCOOP replaces dense BEV feature sharing with sparse 3D Gaussian splatting. A Semantic Gaussian Generator projects multi-view features into a 100×100×4 voxel grid via the LSS lift process, then a 3D CNN decodes each voxel into a Gaussian with position offsets, semantic logits, and opacity (derived from the empty-class logit), supervised by a height-prior regularization loss. A Priority-Based Gaussian Selection module scores each Gaussian by Relevance-Projection Analysis (height-weighted opacity) and Semantic Uncertainty Evaluation (logit entropy), then applies a bandwidth-adaptive threshold to transmit only the most informative subset. A Semantic Gaussian Compression module quantizes semantic logits to 1-byte indices via a learnable 64-entry codebook, reducing per-Gaussian payload. Finally, a BEV Semantic Rasterizer renders the segmentation map by orthogonally projecting the spatially aligned union of local and received Gaussians with alpha-blending, avoiding the heavy computation of BEV feature fusion networks.

## Results

On OPV2V, GSCOOP achieves an average IoU of 56.20% (Vehicle 57.21%, Road 64.67%, Lane 46.71%), surpassing the best baseline CoBEVT (49.90%) by 6.30%. On the real-world V2X-Seq dataset, GSCOOP reaches 60.49% average IoU (Vehicle 47.61%, Road 88.41%, Lane 45.44%), outperforming the strongest baseline ERMVP (56.48%) by 4.01%. Critically, GSCOOP achieves these gains while reducing communication volume by approximately 500× compared to dense BEV feature sharing (from ~30 Mbps to 62 Kbps–7 Mbps). Inference latency scales from 48.92 ms (1 vehicle) to 242.35 ms (7 vehicles), roughly 50% faster than CoBEVT over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSCOOP outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across every category. On OPV2V, the largest gap is against CoBEVT (49.90% avg), the strongest prior method, with GSCOOP leading by 6.30% in average IoU and by 4.26% on the Vehicle category specifically. On V2X-Seq, ERMVP is the closest competitor at 56.48% avg IoU, and GSCOOP extends the lead to 4.01%, with particularly strong gains on Road (+4.65%) and Lane (+3.04%). F-Cooper and AttFuse, which rely on 3D point clouds or simpler fusion, trail by over 10% on OPV2V, highlighting the advantage of the 3D Gaussian representation over 2D BEV feature sharing. DI-V2X, designed for domain-invariant vehicle-infrastructure detection, also lags by 2.93% on OPV2V and 5.79% on V2X-Seq. Notably, GSCOOP achieves these improvements while transmitting roughly 500× less data than the dense BEV feature baselines, and its performance degrades gracefully under bandwidth constraints (Fig. 4), maintaining superiority over CoBEVT at every tested communication volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSCOOP sits at the intersection of two research threads: collaborative BEV perception (CoBEVT, DI-V2X, ERMVP), which transmits dense 2D BEV feature maps between vehicles, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS), originally developed for radiance field rendering and recently adapted to single-vehicle driving tasks (GaussianFormer, GaussianBEV). The paper departs from both: it replaces the continuous, bandwidth-heavy BEV feature representation with a sparse, object-centric set of 3D Gaussians, and it is the first to deploy Gaussians in a multi-vehicle communication setting. The 3D voxelization step borrows the 'lift' mechanism from LSS, while the codebook-based semantic compression draws on vector quantization ideas from compressed 3D-GS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: reducing communication from ~30 Mbps (dense BEV features) to 62 Kbps–7 Mbps (sparse Gaussians) brings collaborative perception within the realistic C-V2X bandwidth budget of ~10 Mbps, a constraint that has limited prior methods. The first evaluation on the real-world V2X-Seq dataset also addresses a gap where most prior work relied solely on the simulated OPV2V benchmark. Open questions remain around robustness to localization errors, packet loss, and adversarial inputs, as well as handling highly dense scenes where ray-sampled Gaussians lose differentiation. Whether the Gaussian-centric formulation extends naturally to other collaborative tasks (e.g., 3D detection, occupancy) is an important direction.
