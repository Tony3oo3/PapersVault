## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models struggle to faithfully align outputs with user prompts, and text conditioning alone cannot specify low-level visual attributes such as color, texture, shape, and sharpness. While prior work has shown that initial noise influences generation within a single model, the cross-model consistency of noise-driven generation and its potential as a reusable, model-agnostic control resource remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts a text prompt and/or one or more generation objectives (semantic, color, texture, shape, sharpness, style) and produces an optimal initial noise sample ε* retrieved from a pre-built library of ~100k Gaussian samples. The retrieval is a simple feature-matching lookup taking ~0.002 seconds, after which ε* is fed into any compatible diffusion model (UNet-based or Transformer-based) with standard sampling. Multiple objectives can be combined via sequential reranking of noise candidates, and the selected noise is compatible with existing enhancement pipelines (DPO, CFG++, ReNO, LaVi-Bridge, ControlNet, Textual Inversion) without modification.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that finite-step noise schedulers (e.g., SD's T=1000 retains √ᾱ_T ≈ 0.068 of the original signal) leave residual image information in the 'pure' Gaussian noise, creating an unintended but architecture-independent link between noise and data that models exploit during training. NoiseQuery exploits this by first generating 'generative posteriors'—images produced with NULL prompts from each noise sample—to reveal the implicit visual content encoded in the noise, then extracting multi-grained features (CLIP/BLIP for semantics, Gram Matrix for style, RGB/HSV/LAB for color, GLCM for texture, Hu Moments for shape, HFE for sharpness) to build a large-scale, model-agnostic noise library offline. At inference, the user's objective features are matched against library entries using goal-specific similarity functions (cosine, MSE, absolute difference, Euclidean distance), and the best-matching noise is selected via argmax. The entire pipeline is tuning-free, requires no gradient updates or model retraining, and adds negligible online cost, making it a drop-in foundational layer for diverse diffusion architectures and enhancement techniques.

## Results

On DrawBench, NoiseQuery consistently improves all four base models: SD 1.5 CLIPScore rises from 30.90 to 31.41, SD 2.1 from 31.13 to 31.68, SD-Turbo from 31.29 to 31.58, and PixArt-α from 30.83 to 31.27. On MSCOCO, gains are similarly consistent (e.g., SD 2.1 HPS v2: 26.58→26.82; SD-Turbo CLIPScore: 31.51→31.76). When stacked with enhancement methods, NoiseQuery adds further improvements (e.g., SD-Turbo + ReNO + NoiseQuery reaches CLIPScore 32.78 vs. 32.55 for ReNO alone). The retrieval overhead is negligible at ~0.002 s per prompt, adding less than 0.002 s to total inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoiseQuery outperforms the base model on every metric across all four architectures and both benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on ImageReward (e.g., SD 1.5 DrawBench: 0.04→0.08, a 100% relative increase) and HPS v2 (SD 2.1 DrawBench: 24.93→25.39). Against Diffusion-DPO on SD 1.5, NoiseQuery alone matches DPO on HPS v2 (25.02) while improving CLIPScore (31.41 vs. 31.19), and the combination (DPO+NoiseQuery) dominates on all four DrawBench metrics. Against CFG++ on SD 2.1, CFG++ alone is essentially neutral (CLIPScore 31.13 vs. base 31.13), but adding NoiseQuery lifts CLIPScore to 31.61 and HPS v2 to 25.55. Against ReNO on SD-Turbo, ReNO achieves the largest absolute gains (CLIPScore 32.55) but at 23.56 s inference cost; NoiseQuery adds a further +0.23 CLIPScore (32.78) at zero additional time. Against LaVi-Bridge on PixArt-α, LaVi-Bridge alone slightly degrades DrawBench ImageReward (0.63 vs. base 0.70), whereas LaVi-Bridge+NoiseQuery recovers to 0.72 and achieves the best MSCOCO CLIPScore (32.01) among all PixArt-α configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the gradient-based initial noise optimization line (ReNO, InitNO, FIND, S2ST) by replacing iterative backpropagation with a tuning-free retrieval from a pre-built noise library, grounded in the observation that finite-step noise schedulers leave residual signal that creates an unintended noise-to-data link. It also positions itself as a complementary foundation layer to existing T2I enhancement methods (Diffusion-DPO, CFG++, LaVi-Bridge, ReNO) rather than a replacement, and extends the scope of noise-based control beyond high-level semantics to low-level visual attributes (color, texture, sharpness) that prior noise optimization work largely ignores. The cross-model consistency finding—same noise yielding similar outputs across UNet- and Transformer-based architectures—repurposes a known scheduler imperfection as a design asset rather than a limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoiseQuery offers a near-zero-cost (0.002 s) plug-in that improves both semantic alignment and, more notably, low-level visual property control in T2I generation—attributes that text prompts and CLIP-based encoders struggle to specify. The model-agnostic, reusable noise library is practically attractive for production pipelines that need fine-grained control without retraining. However, the method remains bounded by the base model's capacity, cannot handle fine-grained spatial control (e.g., Canny edges), and the quantitative gains on standard benchmarks are modest (e.g., +0.4 CLIPScore on SD 1.5). Open questions include whether the noise library generalizes to video generation, 3D synthesis, or significantly different noise schedules, and whether the residual-signal explanation fully accounts for the observed cross-model consistency at scale.
