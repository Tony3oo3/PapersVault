## Problem

Existing document conversion approaches face a trade-off: ensemble pipelines of specialized models (OCR, layout, table recognition) are hard to tune and generalize, while large vision-language models (7B+) are computationally expensive and prone to hallucinations. Additionally, publicly available datasets for multi-modal document understanding lack comprehensive annotations covering all page elements (tables, code, equations, charts) in their natural full-page context, limiting the training of robust end-to-end models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes document page images (processed as 512×512 patches, up to 3 pages per forward pass, evaluated at 144 DPI) along with an optional text prompt, and autoregressively generates a DocTags sequence. DocTags is a new XML-style markup that unifies element type labels, bounding-box coordinates (<loc_x1><loc_y1><loc_x2><loc_y2>), OCR text, table structure via interleaved OTSL tokens, and hierarchical nesting (captions linked to figures/tables, list items nested under parent lists). The same DocTags representation is used for both full-page and cropped-element inputs, ensuring consistent visual-semantic alignment.

## Method

SmolDocling builds on SmolVLM-256M, pairing a SigLIP base patch-16/512 vision encoder (93M) with a SmolLM-2 language backbone (135M), using an aggressive pixel-shuffle strategy that compresses each 512×512 patch into 64 visual tokens (4096 pixels/token). The key architectural contribution is the DocTags output format, which explicitly disentangles textual content from structural markup to minimize token confusion, and embeds OTSL table-structure tokens directly alongside cell text for simultaneous structure and content encoding. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) freeze the vision encoder and train the LLM to produce DocTags tokens; (2) unfreeze the vision encoder and train jointly on pre-training data (DocLayNet-PT, Docmatix) plus task-specific datasets for tables, code, equations, and charts; (3) fine-tune on all datasets including instruction-tuning samples. The paper also contributes five new datasets (DocLayNet-PT, SynthDocNet, SynthChartNet, SynthCodeNet, SynthFormulaNet) totaling over 18M samples to fill annotation gaps in open multi-modal corpora.

## Results

On full-page document transcription (DocLayNet), SmolDocling (256M) achieves an F1-score of 0.80, BLEU of 0.58, and METEOR of 0.67, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL (7B, 27× larger) on every metric (F1 0.72, BLEU 0.46, METEOR 0.57). For equation recognition (Im2Latex-230k), SmolDocling matches GOT (580M) with an edit distance of 0.11 and F1 of 0.95, while substantially surpassing Nougat (350M; F1 0.60) and Qwen2.5-VL (F1 0.89). On table structure (FinTabNet/PubTables-1M), SmolDocling achieves TEDS of 0.72/0.83 (structure-only 0.81/0.88), exceeding Qwen2.5-VL (0.66) and approaching the specialized TableFormer (0.89/0.84). For layout analysis, SmolDocling reaches an overall mAP[0.5:0.95] of 0.231, nearly doubling Qwen2.5-VL's 0.133, though both remain well below the Docling ensemble (0.529) and human baseline (0.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmolDocling (256M) outperforms Qwen2.5-VL (7B, 27× larger) on all full-page OCR metrics (F1 0.80 vs 0.72; BLEU 0.58 vs 0.46) and on layout analysis (mAP 0.231 vs 0.133), as well as on table structure (TEDS 0.72 vs 0.66 on FinTabNet). Against GOT (580M), SmolDocling wins on full-page transcription (F1 0.80 vs 0.69) and matches it on equation recognition (edit distance 0.11, F1 0.95 for both), while Nougat (350M) is clearly behind on all tasks (full-page F1 0.66; equation F1 0.60). The Docling ensemble system remains the strongest reference on full-page OCR (F1 0.91) and layout (mAP 0.529), reflecting the advantage of specialized per-task models. On chart-to-table conversion (ChartQA), SmolDocling achieves TEDS 0.83, trailing Granite Vision (3B, 0.95) but substantially exceeding Molmo-E (1B, 0.54) and the unmodified SmolVLM (2.2B, 0.02). On PubTables-1M table structure, SmolDocling (TEDS 0.83, structure-only 0.88) is competitive with the dedicated TableFormer (52M, 0.84/0.86) despite being a general-purpose VLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmolDocling builds directly on Hugging Face's SmolVLM-256M architecture (SigLIP vision encoder + SmolLM-2 language backbone with pixel-shuffle compression) and extends the OTSL table tokenization vocabulary into a broader document markup format called DocTags. It departs from the dominant paradigm of large VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL at 7B, GOT at 580M) for end-to-end document conversion by demonstrating that a 256M-parameter model, trained with curriculum learning on purpose-built datasets, can match or exceed them. The paper also positions itself against ensemble pipelines (Docling, Marker, MinerU) by offering a single-pass conversion that avoids error accumulation across specialized sub-models, while extending the document scope beyond the scientific-paper focus of prior VLM-based converters like Nougat and DocOwl 2 to include business documents, patents, and forms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 256M model that converts a page in 0.35 s on a single A100 with under 0.5 GB VRAM makes high-quality document conversion feasible on edge or low-cost hardware, and the released datasets (9.3M code renderings, 2.5M charts, 5.5M formulas) fill notable gaps in open multi-modal training resources. However, layout localization remains a clear weakness (0.231 mAP vs. 0.82 human baseline), and performance on low-resolution table crops (72 dpi) degrades noticeably, suggesting the model has not fully internalized robustness to real-world scan quality. Open questions include whether DocTags can serve as a universal interchange format across document-processing stacks, whether the curriculum-learning recipe generalizes to even smaller backbones, and how to close the layout gap without sacrificing the single-pass efficiency advantage.
