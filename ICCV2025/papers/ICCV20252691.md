## Problem

Existing visual SLAM methods assume a clear medium and neglect wavelength-dependent light attenuation and backscattering, causing severe tracking drift and degraded reconstruction in underwater environments. Additionally, degraded image quality amplifies depth estimation outliers, violating geometric consistency and destabilizing bundle adjustment. This paper is also the first to systematically benchmark the performance degradation of existing visual SLAM methods in both real-world and simulated underwater scenes.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream and operates online in two parallel threads: a tracking thread that iteratively estimates SE(3) camera poses and per-pixel inverse depth via recurrent optical flow (RAFT) over a sliding keyframe window, and a mapping thread that incrementally builds a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene. Outputs include the full pose trajectory, depth maps (downsampled during tracking, upsampled for mapping), a hybrid 3DGS + neural scattering medium scene representation, per-pixel depth covariance from Schur complement marginalization, and novel-view renderings. The system runs at ~26.5 FPS with ~9.4 GB peak GPU memory on an RTX 4090.

## Method

DUV-SLAM introduces two core mechanisms. First, depth uncertainty is derived from the block-sparse Hessian of differentiable bundle adjustment via Schur complement marginalization, yielding a per-pixel covariance Σ_D that serves as an adaptive geometric prior in mapping. Second, the scene is represented as adaptive 3D Gaussians coupled with a spectral neural scattering field F^med that predicts wavelength-dependent medium color c^med, direct attenuation σ^att, and backscatter σ^bs as functions of viewing direction, enforcing constancy along each ray. Volume rendering is extended so that transmittance follows the Beer-Lambert law with separate σ^att (for instance occlusion) and σ^bs (for medium backscatter), discretized over depth-ordered Gaussian intervals. The mapping objective jointly minimizes a photometric loss (L1 + DSSIM) and a geometric loss comprising a Mahalanobis distance weighted by Σ_D, a cross-channel depth consistency term, and a KL divergence aligning the α-blended rendered depth histogram with the uncertainty-bounded tracked depth distribution. A gradient-scaling correction compensates for attenuation-induced gradient decay during Gaussian densification, preventing sparse primitive distributions in distant regions.

## Results

On the real-world FLSea dataset, DUV-SLAM achieves 2.40 m ATE and 29.44 PSNR at 26.52 FPS on the TC sequence, outperforming Photo-SLAM (3.37 m ATE, 24.16 PSNR, 23.09 FPS) in both tracking and rendering. On TartanAir-Ocean (Hard split), DUV-SLAM reaches 0.83 m ATE, 27.57 PSNR, 0.67 SSIM, and 0.25 LPIPS, surpassing the best 3DGS baseline Splat-SLAM (1.06 m ATE, 24.13 PSNR). On MIMIR-UW, the method attains 28.68 PSNR and 0.77 SSIM on Sand Pipe t0-d, the highest rendering quality among all baselines. For depth uncertainty quantification, DUV-SLAM achieves a lower average AUSE of 0.21 compared to 0.25 for UncLe-SLAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUV-SLAM outperforms all three 3DGS-based baselines (MonoGS, Photo-SLAM, Splat-SLAM) on rendering quality across all datasets, with the largest gaps on FLSea (e.g., 29.44 vs 24.16 PSNR vs Photo-SLAM on TC) and TartanAir-Ocean Hard (27.57 vs 24.13 PSNR vs Splat-SLAM). Against the NeRF-based NICE-SLAM, DUV-SLAM achieves substantially better rendering (28.86 vs 20.61 PSNR on TartanAir Easy) and comparable or better tracking. On tracking (ATE), DUV-SLAM is competitive with the feature-based ORB-SLAM3 and flow-based DROID-SLAM, achieving 1.26 m on TartanAir Easy (vs 2.03 and 1.15 m respectively) and 6.73 cm on FLSea UC (vs 8.36 and 7.56 cm). UncLe-SLAM, despite using RGB-D input, shows severe degradation underwater (3.20 PSNR on TartanAir Hard), confirming that depth input alone is insufficient without medium modeling. The main acknowledged weakness is long-sequence tracking on MIMIR-UW, where ATE exceeds 10 m on sequences with >6K frames, suggesting room for improvement in global consistency over extended trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUV-SLAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting–based dense SLAM (MonoGS, Photo-SLAM, Splat-SLAM), which it extends by coupling each Gaussian field with a separate neural scattering medium that models wavelength-dependent attenuation and backscattering per the Akkaynak–Treibitz underwater image formation model; (2) differentiable bundle adjustment (DROID-SLAM), from which it derives per-pixel depth covariance via Schur-complement marginalization rather than learning uncertainty in a separate network; and (3) uncertainty-aware SLAM (UncLe-SLAM, CG-SLAM), which it repurposes by feeding tracking-derived depth variance into the mapping loss as a Mahalanobis weight and a KL-divergence regularizer. The key departure from prior underwater reconstruction work (SeaThru-NeRF, WaterSplatting, UW-GS) is that those methods operate offline on fixed image sets, whereas DUV-SLAM performs online state estimation and concurrent mapping in a single real-time pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of existing visual SLAM methods in underwater conditions, revealing that even RGB-D-capable systems (UncLe-SLAM) degrade severely without explicit medium modeling—a finding that should redirect the field toward physics-informed representations for any scattering-medium application. The practical impact is significant for autonomous underwater vehicles, where GPS is unavailable and sonar is bulky; a lightweight monocular system at ~26 FPS with <10 GB GPU memory is deployable on current AUV hardware. However, ATE still exceeds 10 m on long MIMIR-UW sequences (>6K frames), and all real-world data is limited to 3–8 m depth, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to deeper, more turbid environments or to multi-sensor (visual-inertial-sonar) fusion. The gradient-scaling trick for Gaussian densification under exponential attenuation is a small but practically useful insight likely to be adopted by other 3DGS-based underwater methods.
