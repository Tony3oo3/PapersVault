## Problem

Modern VLMs exhibit a strong egocentric bias: they perform well on spatial reasoning from the camera's viewpoint but fail at allocentric reasoning—answering questions from another object's or person's perspective—even when explicitly prompted to shift viewpoints. This limitation is a critical barrier to human-level spatial understanding and to deploying VLMs in collaborative or autonomous agent settings where perspective-taking is essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a spatial reasoning question that specifies a reference perspective (e.g., 'from the man's perspective, is the dog to the left or right?'). It produces a spatial reasoning answer (e.g., left/right, closer/further, visible/not visible, facing direction). Internally, the pipeline generates a transformed scene abstraction in the reference viewer's egocentric frame, which is delivered to the VLM either as numerical 3D coordinates in a text prompt or as a rendered image of colored cubes from the reference viewpoint paired with an abstracted question.

## Method

APC (Abstract Perspective Change) is a three-stage, training-free framework that simulates human mental imagery to enable perspective-taking in VLMs. Stage 1 (Scene Abstraction) uses the VLM to identify objects of interest from the question, then extracts each object's 3D position via GroundingDINO detection, SAM segmentation, and DepthPro metric depth unprojection, and its orientation via OrientAnything, building a coarse 3D scene representation in the camera frame. Stage 2 (Perspective Change) identifies the reference viewer from the question and applies a coordinate transformation to re-express the entire scene abstraction in the reference viewer's egocentric frame, converting the allocentric problem into an egocentric one. Stage 3 (Perspective Prompting) feeds the transformed abstraction back to the VLM in one of two formats: a numerical text prompt listing 3D coordinates, or a visual prompt rendering equal-sized colored cubes at each object's position from the reference viewpoint. The key insight is that abstract scene representations—rather than dense novel-view synthesis—are sufficient for perspective reasoning, are computationally cheaper, and avoid the hallucination and quality issues of reconstruction-based approaches.

## Results

On COMFORT++ (synthetic), APC-Vis achieves 89.67% on left/right, 94.33% on closer, 90.00% on visibility, and 88.33% on facing, substantially outperforming the best baseline (SpatialPIN* at 59.80% on left/right). On 3DSRBench (real images), APC-Vis reaches 72.78% (left/right), 67.44% (visibility), and 66.47% (facing), again leading all baselines. The numerical prompt variant (APC-Num) achieves comparable or higher scores on closer (96.00%) but trails the visual prompt on visibility and facing tasks, where VLMs make fewer logical errors with visual inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APC-Vis outperforms all 11 baselines across every task on both benchmarks. On COMFORT++ left/right, APC-Vis (89.67%) exceeds the best baseline SpatialPIN* (59.80%) by nearly 30 points, while pure VLMs like LLaVA-OneVision (55.33%) and GPT-4o (41.00%) hover near chance. On the closer task, LLaVA-OneVision and Cambrian-1 reach 79.00% by exploiting egocentric distance cues, but APC-Num still leads at 96.00%. On 3DSRBench (real images), APC-Vis leads left/right (72.78% vs. SpatialPIN*'s 50.10%), visibility (67.44% vs. Gemini-2.0-Flash's 57.65%), and facing (66.47% vs. Cambrian-1's 65.03%). Dense reconstruction baselines (SpatialPIN*, ViewCrafter) show that generating novel views is insufficient—ViewCrafter drops to 18.31% on 3DSRBench facing due to generation noise—confirming that the abstraction-based approach is more robust than pixel-level view synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of spatial reasoning in VLMs and visual perspective-taking. Prior spatial-reasoning VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, SpatialPIN) and benchmarks (COMFORT, 3DSRBench) have identified that VLMs are locked into egocentric reasoning, but existing solutions either fine-tune for specific relations (SAT) or attempt dense 3D reconstruction for novel views. APC departs from both by repurposing off-the-shelf vision foundation models (GroundingDINO, SAM, DepthPro, OrientAnything) to build a lightweight scene abstraction, then performing a coordinate transformation to the reference viewer's frame and feeding the result back as a visual or textual prompt—drawing its conceptual motivation from cognitive science literature on mental imagery rather than from prior VLM architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APC addresses a fundamental limitation of current VLMs: the inability to reason from perspectives other than the camera, which is essential for embodied agents, multi-agent collaboration, and human-like spatial understanding. The finding that a coarse abstraction (colored cubes at 3D positions) substantially outperforms dense reconstruction pipelines (SpatialPIN*, ViewCrafter) suggests that perspective reasoning does not require photorealistic novel views, opening a cheaper and more robust path forward. However, performance degrades noticeably on real images (3DSRBench) compared to synthetic scenes (COMFORT++), revealing sensitivity to errors in depth estimation and orientation detection that remain open challenges. Future work could explore richer abstractions, multi-view inputs, or learned scene representations to close this gap.
