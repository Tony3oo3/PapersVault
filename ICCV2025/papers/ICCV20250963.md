## Problem

Existing ensemble-based adversarial attacks focus on optimizing ensemble weights or gradient paths but treat surrogate models as fixed components, overlooking the potential of augmenting the models themselves to increase diversity and reduce adversarial overfitting. Moreover, ensemble-based attacks tailored specifically for Vision Transformers remain largely unexplored despite ViTs' superior performance and greater adversarial robustness compared to CNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250963/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images from ILSVRC 2012 (1000 for attack, 4000 for Bayesian optimization) along with their ground-truth labels and four surrogate ViTs (ViT-B/16, PiT-B, DeiT-B-Dis, Visformer-S). It produces L∞-bounded adversarial images (ε=16) after 10 iterations. A key structural detail is the two-phase design: Phase 1 (one-time) uses Bayesian optimization to tune augmentation parameters per surrogate, while Phase 2 performs the iterative ensemble attack using the pre-computed augmented models with multiple inference passes (loop=2) per step to exploit randomness.

## Method

ViT-EnsembleAttack adversarially augments each surrogate ViT via three structure-specific strategies inspired by data augmentation: Multi-head Dropping (randomly zeroing attention heads with threshold τ), Attention Score Scaling (element-wise random scaling of attention matrices with center s and range ξ), and MLP Feature Mixing (permuting and blending MLP outputs with ratio ρ). Each surrogate yields three parameterized variants, and Bayesian optimization (50 calls, skopt gp_minimize) tunes each parameter by maximizing transfer ASR against the other original surrogates. During the attack, an Automatic Reweighting module dynamically adjusts ensemble weights inversely proportional to per-model loss magnitudes (raised to power b=2) to prevent overfitting to high-loss models, while Step Size Enlargement sets α=(q+ε)/T with q=3 to accelerate convergence. The final update follows MI-FGSM momentum (μ=1.0) over the weighted sum of gradients from all 12 augmented models, with loop=2 inference passes per iteration to accumulate diverse gradient directions.

## Results

On 8 target ViTs (Table 1), ViT-EnsembleAttack achieves an average ASR of 98.4% with DI-FGSM, reaching nearly 100% on CaiT-S/24, TNT-S, LeViT-256, ConViT-B, and DrViT, outperforming the best baseline (SMER) by 4.6% on average. On 8 target CNNs (Table 2), the method achieves 88.3% average ASR with DI-FGSM, surpassing SMER by a substantial 15.3% margin. The method also demonstrates strong cross-structure transferability, e.g., improving I-FGSM ASR from 78.6% (SMER) to 95.4% on LeViT-256 and from 64.9% (SMER) to 90.3% on Inception-v3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViT-EnsembleAttack consistently outperforms all four baselines across every attack setting (I-FGSM, MI-FGSM, DI-FGSM, TI-FGSM) on both ViT and CNN targets. Against the basic ensemble (Ens), the improvement is the largest, e.g., 95.4% vs. 48.6% ASR on LeViT-256 with I-FGSM. Against SVRE, the gap narrows on MI-FGSM (98.5% vs. 95.1% on LeViT-256) but remains substantial on I-FGSM (95.4% vs. 74.3%). Against AdaEA, the advantage is most pronounced on CNNs, where AdaEA lags significantly (e.g., 47.4% vs. 90.3% on Inception-v3 with I-FGSM). Against SMER (the strongest baseline), ViT-EnsembleAttack leads by 4.6% average ASR on ViTs and 15.3% on CNNs, with the largest single-model gap being 16.8% on Inception-v3 (I-FGSM: 90.3% vs. 64.9%). The only near-tie is on the adversarially trained ViT-B/16_AT, where all methods cluster around 97.6–98.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ensemble-based adversarial attack lineage (Liu et al. 2016; Dong et al. 2018 MI-FGSM) by shifting the optimization target from the ensemble combination to the surrogate models themselves. Prior methods such as SVRE, AdaEA, and SMER treat surrogate models as fixed and focus on reducing gradient variance, adaptively reweighting, or applying reinforcement learning to the ensemble path. ViT-EnsembleAttack departs from this by adversarially augmenting each surrogate ViT through three architecture-specific randomization strategies (multi-head dropping, attention score scaling, MLP feature mixing) inspired by data augmentation, with parameters tuned via Bayesian optimization. It is positioned as the first ensemble-based attack specifically designed for Vision Transformers rather than CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—from optimizing how fixed models are combined to optimizing the models themselves—offers a complementary axis of improvement that could be combined with existing ensemble-weight techniques. The 15.3% average ASR gain over the strongest baseline on cross-structure (ViT→CNN) transfer is practically significant for black-box attack scenarios. However, the one-time Bayesian optimization phase (Phase 1) incurs substantial upfront cost (~54P FLOPs, ~2400 s), and the augmentation strategies are tightly coupled to ViT architecture, leaving open whether analogous randomization can be designed for CNNs, diffusion models, or other architectures. The paper also does not explore whether the augmented models themselves become more robust or whether the approach interacts with adversarial training defenses beyond a single ViT-B/16_AT target.
