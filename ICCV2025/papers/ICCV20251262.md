## Problem

Zero-shot monocular depth estimation models are trained on low-resolution images (e.g., 384×384, 518×518) and degrade on high-resolution inputs, while patch-based refinement methods introduce depth discontinuities at grid boundaries and rely on slow test-time ensembling to mitigate them. Additionally, training on synthetic datasets for dense supervision causes overfitting to dataset-specific biases, such as incorrect depth labels for transparent objects in UnrealStereo4K, harming zero-shot generalization to real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 4K resolution) and produces a full-resolution refined depth map. The input image is split into a 4×4 grid of overlapping patches, each resized to 518×518 for the pretrained MDE backbone (DepthAnythingV2), while a resized version of the full image provides a coarse depth map for global context. At inference, each patch is refined exactly once (hence 'One Look'), and the refined patches are reassembled into the final depth map without any test-time ensemble.

## Method

PRO consists of two key strategies. First, Grouped Patch Consistency Training (GPCT) processes four overlapping patches (A, B, C, D) simultaneously in a single backpropagation step, applying a depth consistency loss across all pairwise overlapping regions and comparing the merged depth to ground truth; this enforces boundary consistency at every grid edge in one pass, eliminating the need for multi-patch test-time ensembling and yielding 12× faster inference. Second, Bias Free Masking (BFM) leverages the pretrained MDE model's coarse depth to identify unreliable regions in synthetic ground truth (where normalized coarse and GT depths deviate beyond a threshold), then intersects edge maps from both coarse and GT depths to preserve critical boundary regions, applying supervision only to the resulting reliable mask. The residual prediction network fuses patch-level features with ROI-extracted coarse features via wavelet transform and shallow convolutions, trained with a combined masked L1/L2/gradient loss and the consistency loss (λ=4).

## Results

PRO achieves the best zero-shot depth estimation performance across all four real-world test datasets (Booster, ETH3D, Middlebury 2014, NuScenes) with AbsRel of 0.0304, 0.0422, 0.0287, and 0.104 respectively, while maintaining the fastest inference time of 1.4 s (12× faster than PatchRefiner with 177 patches at 16.2 s). On Middlebury 2014, PRO attains the lowest D³R edge error (0.0803) and the highest δ₁ (0.996). On DIS-5K, PRO achieves a Boundary Recall of 0.156, outperforming PatchRefiner (0.141–0.151) but trailing BoostingDepth (0.170) and PatchFusion (0.189–0.206), which prioritize edge sharpness over overall depth accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO outperforms DepthAnythingV2 (the base model without patch refinement) on every depth metric, confirming that the patchwise refinement pipeline adds meaningful value. Against PatchFusion (both P=16 and P=177), PRO achieves substantially lower AbsRel (e.g., 0.0304 vs. 0.0504 on Booster) and lower D³R (0.0803 vs. 0.1124 on Middlebury 2014), while also being 2.4×–26× faster. Compared to PatchRefiner P=16, PRO improves AbsRel by 12.6% on Booster (0.0304 vs. 0.0348) and 1.7% on Middlebury 2014 (0.0287 vs. 0.0292), and achieves a higher Boundary Recall (0.156 vs. 0.151). Against PatchRefiner P=177 (which uses test-time ensembling over 177 patches), PRO matches or beats all depth metrics while running 12× faster (1.4 s vs. 16.2 s). The only metric where PRO trails is Boundary Recall on DIS-5K, where BoostingDepth (0.170) and PatchFusion (0.189–0.206) score higher, though the authors note these methods sacrifice overall depth accuracy for edge sharpness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the patch-based high-resolution depth estimation line initiated by BoostingDepth, PatchFusion, and PatchRefiner, which all split images into tiles and refine per-patch depth to recover fine details lost at low training resolutions. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) GPCT generalizes the two-patch consistency loss used by PatchFusion and PatchRefiner to a four-patch grouped formulation, providing stronger boundary supervision in a single backward pass and eliminating the need for test-time ensemble; and (2) BFM introduces a novel use of the pretrained zero-shot MDE model's prior to identify and mask unreliable GT regions (e.g., transparent objects in UnrealStereo4K), a problem prior patch-based methods did not explicitly address. The framework is built on top of DepthAnythingV2 as the frozen backbone, following the residual-refinement paradigm established by PatchRefiner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12× inference speedup over PatchRefiner (P=177) while matching or exceeding depth accuracy makes high-resolution patch-based depth estimation practical for real-time or near-real-time applications in autonomous driving and robotics, where prior methods were too slow. The BFM technique offers a general recipe for leveraging synthetic training data without inheriting its annotation biases, which could transfer to other dense-prediction tasks trained on synthetic data. Limitations include the fixed 4×4 grid (no content-adaptive patching), reliance on a single synthetic dataset for training, and the fact that the approach still inherits the resolution ceiling of the backbone model (518×518). Open questions include whether GPCT scales to larger patch counts or irregular tiling, and whether BFM's threshold-based masking generalizes to other synthetic datasets with different failure modes.
