## Problem

Existing person search models achieve strong performance on clean datasets but remain highly vulnerable to environmental corruptions, with relative performance drops up to 63%. This vulnerability is largely unexplored because collecting new person images under diverse corruption scenarios raises significant privacy concerns, leaving no standardized benchmark for evaluating corruption robustness in person search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251370/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query person image and a full scene image (not cropped person crops, as in standard reID) and produces person bounding boxes along with re-identification match scores. A key distinction from conventional reID is that the entire scene is processed, so background regions influence person representations through large receptive fields. The corruption benchmarks independently apply different corruption types and severity levels to query and gallery images, reflecting real deployment where corruption conditions may differ between the two.

## Method

The paper proposes a foreground-aware augmentation strategy that applies AugMix-style augmentations (rotation, shearing, solarization, autocontrast, equalization, posterization, random erasing) exclusively to person regions identified by ground-truth bounding boxes (with random translation to prevent shortcut learning), rather than to the entire scene which would cause severe semantic perturbations. A Robust Proposal Regularizer then enforces invariance by computing a KL divergence between each augmented proposal representation and an IoU-weighted mixture representation from the clean image's proposals, with the mixture detached to encourage invariant features. The total loss combines the standard OIM loss (trained jointly on clean and augmented scenes) with this regularizer, requiring no additional parameters or inference-time cost. The approach is plug-and-play, seamlessly integrating into six state-of-the-art person search models (OIMNet, NAE, OIMNet++, SeqNet, COAT, SAT) and consistently improving corruption robustness while preserving clean-dataset performance.

## Results

The proposed foreground-aware augmentation and robust proposal regularizer consistently improve corruption robustness across six state-of-the-art person search models. On CUHK-SYSU-C, SeqNet's R@1 increases from 43.6 to 66.7 (+53%), and on PRW-C, SAT's R@1 rises from 44.7 to 57.4 (+28%). Clean-dataset performance is largely preserved, with variations within ±2.3% (e.g., SeqNet mAP goes from 93.8 to 94.3 on CUHK-SYSU). The method also outperforms the OADG+CIL combination (robust detection + robust reID) from Table 1, achieving 66.7 R@1 on CUHK-SYSU-C versus 45.6 for OADG+CIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates the proposed method as a plug-in enhancement on six person search baselines (OIMNet, NAE, OIMNet++, SeqNet, COAT, SAT), all of which show substantial corruption-robustness gains. The largest relative improvement is on OIMNet++ (CUHK-SYSU-C R@1: 36.3→58.8, +62%) and NAE (CUHK-SYSU-C R@1: 40.9→62.2, +52%). On PRW-C, COAT benefits most (R@1: 43.6→55.4, +27%) and SAT achieves the highest absolute corrupted R@1 (57.4). The method also surpasses the OADG+CIL two-step combination (robust detector + robust reID) from Table 1, which achieves only 45.6 R@1 on CUHK-SYSU-C and 44.8 R@1 on PRW-C, confirming that task-specific robustness in person search cannot be obtained by simply combining independently robust sub-models. Clean-dataset performance is maintained or slightly improved for all models except COAT (R@1 drops from 94.7 to 92.8), which the authors attribute to COAT's built-in token mixup already providing augmentation-like benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ImageNet-C corruption benchmarking paradigm (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) to the multi-task person search setting, where prior corruption robustness work has been conducted independently for detection (e.g., OADG) and re-identification (e.g., CIL) but never for their joint optimization. The proposed foreground-aware augmentation repurposes AugMix-style transformations restricted to person regions, while the robust proposal regularizer introduces a KL-divergence-based consistency loss between clean and augmented proposals—a mechanism distinct from prior corruption-robust training objectives. The paper departs from the common assumption that composing individually robust sub-models yields a robust pipeline, demonstrating this fails for person search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CUHK-SYSU-C and PRW-C benchmarks fill a critical gap by enabling corruption robustness evaluation without collecting new person images, thus avoiding the privacy issues that led to datasets like DukeMTMC being discontinued. The finding that the re-identification stage and foreground regions are disproportionately vulnerable provides actionable guidance for future robust person search design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multiple simultaneous corruptions, continuous (rather than discrete) severity, and other multi-task vision problems where foreground-background asymmetry exists. The method's simplicity (no extra parameters, no inference cost) makes it broadly applicable, though its gains on clean data are marginal and real-world validation remains limited to four scenarios from BDD100K.
