## Problem

Existing few-shot segmentation methods build image-level correlation between the support target and the entire query image, which inevitably incorporates hard pixel noise from irrelevant background objects, base objects, and—critically—irrelevant novel objects that co-occur with the target. This noise causes overfitting to background regions and misclassification, and post-processing elimination strategies fail to suppress irrelevant novel objects that share the same class-agnostic status as the target.

## Input / Output

The method takes K (1 or 5) support images with binary masks and a single query image, all cropped to 473×473 (PASCAL-5i) or 641×641 (COCO-20i), and produces a binary segmentation mask for the query. A frozen pre-trained backbone (VGG-16 or ResNet-50) extracts mid-level and high-level features; support masks are accessible at both training and test time, while query masks are used only during meta-training. Evaluation follows the standard 4-fold cross-validation episodic protocol with 1,000 test episodes per fold.

## Method

OCNet replaces image-level correlation with object-level correlation by first mining query general objects via the General Object Mining Module (GOMM), which fuses a CAM-derived mask with a high-level cosine-similarity mask to form a general object mask, then learns general object prototypes to produce an initial feature that is cross-attention-fused with the raw query feature. The Correlation Construction Module (CCM) then generates multi-frequency support prototypes, partitions them into foreground and background indices by their distance to the support mask, and uses an Optimal Transport formulation solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm to produce a prototype allocating mask that content-awarely assigns each query pixel to the most relevant prototype. This allocation supervises the construction of the object-level correlation feature, which simultaneously discriminates the target object and suppresses hard pixel noise. Training uses three cross-entropy losses (target segmentation, general object segmentation, and prototype allocation) under the episodic meta-learning paradigm.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet-50, OCNet achieves 71.4% mIoU (1-shot) and 74.5% mIoU (5-shot), surpassing the previous best (ABCB at 70.6% and AENet/HMNet at 74.1%) by 0.8% and 0.4% respectively. With VGG-16, OCNet reaches 68.2% (1-shot) and 71.9% (5-shot) mIoU, improving over HMNet by 0.9% and 0.8%. On the more challenging COCO-20^i benchmark, OCNet with ResNet-50 attains 51.5% (1-shot) and 57.0% (5-shot) mIoU, outperforming ABCB (50.0%/55.1%) and AENet (49.4%/56.7%). FB-IoU also improves, reaching 82.2% (1-shot) and 84.7% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5^i with ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCNet outperforms all 12 baselines across both PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i under both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The closest competitors are HMNet [56] (Mamba-based, NeurIPS'24) and AENet [55] (ECCV'24), which OCNet surpasses by 0.9–1.0% mIoU on PASCAL-5^i (VGG-16) and 0.4–1.6% on COCO-20^i (ResNet-50). ABCB [66] (CVPR'24), the strongest ResNet-50 baseline on PASCAL-5^i, is beaten by 0.8% (1-shot) and 0.9% (5-shot) mIoU. On COCO-20^i, OCNet leads ABCB by 1.5% (1-shot) and 1.9% (5-shot) mIoU with ResNet-50. Earlier methods like PFENet [42], BAM [21], and HDMNet [35] trail by 4–13% mIoU, confirming that the object-level correlation paradigm provides a substantial advantage over image-level correlation with post-elimination. The consistent gains across both backbones (VGG-16 and ResNet-50) and both datasets indicate the improvement is architecture- and dataset-independent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in few-shot semantic segmentation, where methods construct image-level correlation between the support target and the entire query image (via 4D convolutions, Transformers, or dense prototype comparison), and instead proposes object-level correlation between the support target and query general objects. It builds on multi-frequency pooling (Wen et al., 2024) by additionally leveraging background prototypes to suppress hard pixel noise, on CAM (Zhou et al., 2016) for initial general object detection, and on Sinkhorn-based optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) to model content-aware prototype allocation. The biological vision analogy—saliency-based pre-attentive processing followed by task-dependent target selection—provides the conceptual motivation for separating general object mining from target identification, positioning OCNet as a more principled alternative to post-processing approaches like BAM and ABCB that attempt to eliminate background noise after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The object-level correlation framework offers a more fundamental solution to the hard pixel noise problem than iterative post-elimination strategies, and the ablation confirms that both the general object mining and the foreground+background prototype allocation are necessary and complementary. However, the approach adds architectural complexity (GOMM + CCM + OT solver) and still depends on CAM for initial object localization, which can be incomplete; the paper acknowledges that moderately uncompleted information aids generalization but does not fully resolve the dependency. The authors suggest exploring object-level correlations in other few-shot scenarios as future work, and it remains open whether this paradigm extends to denser or more cluttered scenes where general object boundaries are ambiguous.
