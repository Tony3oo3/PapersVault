## Problem

3D Gaussian splatting's discrete primitives make it difficult to apply continuous geometry constraints like SDF regularization, which is critical for robust decomposition of geometry and material in inverse rendering. Existing Gaussian-based methods that introduce a separate SDF network increase memory usage (up to 5×) and require complex multi-stage optimization strategies to balance the two representations. This paper asks how to obtain SDF-level geometry regularization within the Gaussian framework without any extra representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251535/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a single foreground object (synthetic objects from Blender or real objects from NeILF++) and produces a set of 2D Gaussian primitives, each carrying a discretized SDF value, PBR attributes (albedo, roughness), 2D scaling, and a quaternion. Rendering uses deferred shading with a Principled BRDF and split-sum approximation, enabling relighting under arbitrary novel light conditions. The SDF-to-opacity transformation means Gaussian opacity is not directly learned but derived from the SDF value, unifying geometry and appearance in a single representation.

## Method

The core idea is to encode a sampled SDF value directly on each 2D Gaussian primitive and derive its opacity through a bell-shaped SDF-to-opacity transformation T_γ, eliminating the need for a separate SDF network. A median loss (L_γ) guides the learnable parameter γ by linking it to the median unsigned SDF distance across all Gaussians, encouraging the transformation to narrow so Gaussians converge to the surface. Because discrete SDF samples cannot support gradient-based Eikonal regularization, the paper proposes a projection-based consistency loss that projects each Gaussian onto the zero-level set and minimizes the depth difference between the projected point and the alpha-blended surface, proven to approximate the Eikonal condition. A spherical initialization of foreground Gaussians on a unit sphere avoids local minima in early training. The entire framework is built on 2DGS with deferred shading, requiring no extra memory beyond the Gaussians themselves.

## Results

On the Glossy Blender dataset (8 scenes), the method achieves a mean PSNR of 24.52, SSIM of 0.9229, and LPIPS of 0.0762, outperforming the strongest baseline GS-ROR (23.39/0.9140/0.0769) and substantially exceeding GShader (16.95), GS-IR (16.26), and R3DG (17.09). On TensoIR Synthetic, it reaches a mean PSNR of 27.78 vs. GS-ROR's 27.07. Notably, the method uses only 4 GB of GPU memory (vs. 22 GB for GS-ROR, a 5× reduction) and trains in 1 hour, while rendering at 143 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four Gaussian-based baselines on mean PSNR across both synthetic datasets: on Glossy Blender it leads by +1.13 dB over GS-ROR (24.52 vs. 23.39) and by +7.4–8.3 dB over GShader, GS-IR, and R3DG; on TensoIR Synthetic it leads by +0.71 dB over GS-ROR (27.78 vs. 27.07). For geometry quality, the method achieves the best mean normal MAE on Shiny Blender (6.48° vs. GS-ROR's 7.23°) and the best mean Chamfer distance on Glossy Blender (0.0107 vs. GS-ROR's 0.0140), though it trails GS-ROR on three scenes with complex inter-reflection (Toaster, Luyu, Cat). The key efficiency advantage is memory: the method uses 4 GB versus 22 GB for GS-ROR (5× less) and 20 GB for R3DG, while training in 1 hour (vs. 1.5 h for GS-ROR). GS-ROR remains the closest competitor in relighting quality but at substantially higher memory cost and with lost geometry details in some regions; the other three baselines (GShader, GS-IR, R3DG) overfit training appearance and produce erroneous normals, resulting in 7–8 dB lower PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 2DGS for geometrically accurate Gaussian primitives and NeuS-style SDF regularization, but departs from the dominant paradigm in Gaussian-based inverse rendering where an extra continuous SDF network is jointly optimized with Gaussians (as in GS-ROR, DeferredGS, GSDF, GS-Pull). Instead, it encodes a discrete SDF sample directly on each Gaussian primitive and derives opacity via an SDF-to-opacity transformation, unifying geometry and appearance in a single representation. The projection-based consistency loss is a novel substitute for the Eikonal loss, which is infeasible on discrete samples, drawing conceptual inspiration from Neural-Pull but without requiring an auxiliary network or ground-truth point cloud supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching or exceeding GS-ROR's relighting quality at roughly 20% of its GPU memory (4 GB vs 22 GB) and without multi-stage training or warm-up schedules makes high-quality relightable asset creation accessible on consumer hardware. However, the method still fails on scenes with complex inter-reflection (Toaster, Luyu, Cat) because it models only direct lighting, and mesh extraction with BRDF parameters is left for future work. Extending the framework to unbounded scenes and incorporating indirect illumination are the most pressing open questions for broader applicability.
