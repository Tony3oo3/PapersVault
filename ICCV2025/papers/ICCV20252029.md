## Problem

3D object detection degrades significantly in unseen environments due to domain shifts, with distant objects suffering from point sparsity that renders their surfaces featureless and easily confused with surrounding structures. Existing domain adaptation methods—whether augmentation-based or prototype-based—struggle to recover these missing features, leading to persistent false negatives. The paper identifies that first-stage proposals already contain accurate bounding boxes but with low objectness scores, suggesting that densifying the sparse points within these proposals could directly address the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method consumes box proposals (center, size, rotation) and their corresponding BEV spatial features from a first-stage detector, along with the raw LiDAR point cloud. Points inside each proposal are extracted, transformed into Normalized Box View (NBV) coordinates for size-agnostic processing, and voxelized into fixed 32×32×32 binary grids. The output is a densified voxel grid per proposal, which is encoded and cross-attended with the spatial context feature before feeding into a standard second-stage refinement head that produces refined 3D bounding boxes and class labels.

## Method

DiffRefine formulates sparse object points as a noisy version of dense object points and applies a multi-step diffusion denoising process over voxel grids. The forward process removes voxels using a Gaussian-weighted random subsampling strategy that preserves shape similarity to the input, while the reverse denoising network (three Transformer blocks + 3D convolution) predicts continuous 3D offsets from empty grids to their nearest occupied neighbors, enabling differentiable warping instead of discrete voxel classification. The generation target is constructed by accumulating points from multiple ground-truth boxes of the same class in NBV space. To prevent hallucinated objects, a cross-attention module fuses the generated point features with BEV spatial context, and the entire pipeline remains differentiable so that the generation and refinement modules are jointly optimized under standard RPN losses.

## Results

On the Waymo→KITTI adaptation with Second-IoU, DiffRefine achieves 87.81/75.25 AP_BEV/AP_3D, surpassing the prior SOTA GroupExp-DA (86.94/73.70) by 0.87/1.54 and the ST3D baseline (82.19/61.83) by 7.03/9.61. On NuScenes→KITTI with PointPillars, the gains are most dramatic: 86.63/54.16 AP_BEV/AP_3D, exceeding GroupExp-DA by 4.74/1.32 and the ST3D baseline by 26.23/43.06. On Waymo→NuScenes with Second-IoU, DiffRefine reaches 44.89/25.25, outperforming GroupExp-DA by 1.05/0.83. The method also improves on Pedestrian (51.20/48.0) and Cyclist (33.52/24.29) categories in NuScenes→KITTI, exceeding the best prior method by 1.97/1.43 and 1.35/0.81 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffRefine consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three adaptation scenarios and both base detectors. Against the closest competitor GroupExp-DA [26], gains range from 0.87/1.54 (Waymo→KITTI, Second-IoU) to 4.74/1.32 (NuScenes→KITTI, PointPillars) in AP_BEV/AP_3D, with the largest relative improvement on the hardest cross-sensor transfer (32-beam→64-beam). Compared to the self-training baseline ST3D [40], improvements are substantial: 7.03/9.61 on Waymo→KITTI (Second-IoU) and up to 26.23/43.06 on NuScenes→KITTI (PointPillars), where the source-only model is particularly weak. DTS [9] and GPA-3D [15] are also surpassed in every setting, with DTS showing particular weakness on rotation estimation for sparse distant objects. The SN [34] and 3D-CoCo [41] baselines trail by larger margins (10+ AP_BEV points), reflecting their older adaptation strategies. Notably, DiffRefine exceeds the fully-supervised oracle on several metrics (e.g., 87.81 vs. 83.3 AP_BEV on Waymo→KITTI), suggesting the densification provides features even unavailable in the target-domain ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffRefine builds on the ST3D self-training framework for unsupervised domain adaptation in 3D detection and adopts the Normalized Box View (NBV) formulation from DiffuBox for size-agnostic point processing. It departs from prior domain adaptation approaches—data augmentation (DTS, PointDrop) and prototype learning (GPA-3D)—by instead actively generating dense object points within box proposals using a diffusion denoising process over voxel grids. The key conceptual shift is treating sparse object points as a noisy version of dense points recoverable through iterative denoising, rather than training the detector to be invariant to sparsity. The method is designed as a plug-in module before second-stage refinement, making it compatible with existing two-stage detectors like Second-IoU and PointPillars without modifying their architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and well-motivated failure mode in domain adaptive 3D detection: false negatives on distant objects whose surfaces are featureless due to LiDAR sparsity. The plug-in design and compatibility with standard two-stage detectors make the approach broadly adoptable, and the >100% closed gap in some settings suggests the generated points can provide information beyond what the target domain alone offers. Limitations include reliance on ground-truth boxes for constructing generation targets during training, evaluation primarily on the 'car' class (with pedestrian/cyclist as secondary), and added inference cost from multiple denoising steps. Open questions include whether the approach extends to single-stage detectors, whether generation targets can be constructed without ground truth, and whether the method generalizes to more extreme domain gaps or additional object categories.
