## Problem

Existing 3D Spiking Neural Networks struggle with long-range dependencies in irregular point sequences, rely on static temporal encoding that fails to exploit the dynamic nature of spike-driven features, and suffer unaddressed information degradation during spike-based computation. While Mamba's selective state space mechanism offers linear-time sequence modeling, naively adapting it to SNNs is hindered by a temporal dynamics mismatch between continuous state transitions and discrete spike events, and by the ~30% fewer activations per timestep that spikes introduce relative to ANNs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud (2048 points by default, 1024 for ModelNet40) and dynamically encodes it into T=4 time steps of E=256 (or 128) points each via Hierarchical Dynamic Encoding, rather than simply repeating the input. KNN tokens are extracted per time step and processed through spiking layers. Outputs are task-dependent: a single class label for classification, per-point part labels for segmentation on ShapeNetPart, or a reconstructed point cloud (via Chamfer Distance loss) during masked pre-training with a 0.6 mask ratio.

## Method

SPM introduces three key components. First, Hierarchical Dynamic Encoding (HDE) exploits the three stages of farthest point sampling—applying Finite Forward Sliding (decreasing step size) in the early/middle stages and Infinite Backward Extension (growing memory pool) in the late stage—to convert a static point cloud into a dynamic event-based temporal representation. Second, the Spiking Mamba Block (SMB) adapts Mamba's SSM and Gate branches for spike inputs: it reverses only the time dimension (not the token dimension, which is uninformative for sparse spikes) for bidirectional SSM, and applies element-wise average pooling along the token dimension on the gate to preserve feature dimensions while minimizing spike-induced information loss. Third, an asymmetric SNN-ANN pre-training framework stacks SMBs as the encoder and a lighter unidirectional SSM as the ANN decoder for masked point cloud reconstruction, decoupling energy-efficient spike inference from high-capacity ANN decoding to enhance the SNN encoder's generalization.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, SPM achieves 90.2% OA on OBJ-BG, 89.5% on OBJ-ONLY, and 84.2% on PB-T50-RS, surpassing the previous best SNN (Spiking Point Transformer) by +7.4%, +6.1%, and +6.2% respectively. On ModelNet40, SPM reaches 92.3% OA (w/o Vote) and 92.7% (w/ Vote), a +0.9% improvement over SPT. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, SPM attains 82.29% Cls. mIoU and 84.8% Ins. mIoU, outperforming SPT by +0.97% and +1.9%. Energy consumption is at most 12.6× lower than the ANN counterpart (PointMamba) at 1 time step, and 3.5× lower at 4 time steps while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SNN baseline, Spiking Point Transformer (SPT), SPM wins on every dataset: +7.4% on OBJ-BG (90.2 vs 82.8), +6.1% on OBJ-ONLY (89.5 vs 83.4), +6.2% on PB-T50-RS (84.2 vs 78.0), and +0.9% on ModelNet40 (92.3 vs 91.4). SPM also outperforms other SNN baselines by large margins, e.g., +15.1% over Spiking Pointnet (64.1→84.2 on PB-T50-RS) and +9.7% over P2SResLNet-B (74.5→84.2). In the ANN domain, SPM is competitive with PointMamba (90.2 vs 90.2 on OBJ-BG; 92.3 vs 92.4 on ModelNet40) while consuming 3.5× less energy at T=4. For part segmentation, SPM trails PointMamba by 2.0% Cls. mIoU (82.29 vs 84.29) but beats SPT by 0.97% and 1.9% on Cls. and Ins. mIoU respectively. In the self-supervised setting (Table 2), SPM with spike-based pretraining reaches 86.5% on PB-T50-RS, surpassing SPT (82.6%) by 3.9% and approaching PointMamba (88.2%) within 1.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: Mamba-based point cloud backbones (PointMamba, Mamba3D) and spiking neural networks for 3D (Spiking Point Transformer, Spiking PointNet). It departs from prior 3D SNNs by replacing static direct encoding with Hierarchical Dynamic Encoding and by designing a Spiking Mamba Block that explicitly addresses spike-induced information loss in both the gate and SSM branches. It also departs from prior Mamba-for-point-clouds work by making the entire computation spike-driven, and introduces an asymmetric SNN-ANN pretraining scheme (SMB encoder + SSM decoder) that has no direct precedent in the 3D SNN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the energy-efficiency angle: SPM achieves up to 12.6× lower energy consumption than its ANN counterpart (PointMamba) at 1 timestep while retaining comparable accuracy, making it a candidate for neuromorphic hardware deployment where MAC operations are prohibitively expensive. The 6–7% OA gains over the previous SOTA SNN (SPT) on ScanObjectNN are substantial within the SNN domain and suggest that selective SSMs are a natural fit for spike-based sequence modeling. Open questions include whether the theoretical energy model translates to real neuromorphic chips, whether the approach scales to much larger point clouds or dynamic scenes, and whether the asymmetric pretraining strategy generalizes beyond reconstruction to other self-supervised objectives.
