## Problem

Graph-based MIL for WSI survival analysis suffers from two confounding sources: trivial semantic features (staining, sectioning, scanning variations) that bias patch representations, and non-causal topological subgraphs that introduce noise during slide-level aggregation. Existing causal-inference MIL methods rely on multi-stage pipelines, require a predefined cluster number, operate at the patch level without exploiting shared slide-level confounders, and are not adapted to graph-based survival analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes each WSI at two scales: a slide-level thumbnail (for estimating the semantic confounder distribution via soft K-means) and a set of patch features extracted by a pretrained backbone (ViT, ctranspath, or UNI) that form the nodes of a spatial graph. At inference, the model outputs a Cox survival risk score computed from the causal subgraph representation, a soft Bernoulli mask P(C|G) indicating per-patch causal relevance, and interpretable attention heatmaps that align with tumor ground truth.

## Method

C²MIL is grounded in a dual structural causal model (DSCM) that decomposes the backdoor path G←X←T→Y into two interventions. The Cross-scale Adaptive Feature Disentangling (CAFD) module estimates P(T) by projecting thumbnail features into a trivial-feature space, applying Gumbel-Softmax to adaptively determine the effective cluster count K_effective (eliminating the need for a predefined K), computing per-cluster bias vectors, and subtracting the weighted bias from each patch feature to perform backdoor adjustment. The Bernoulli Differentiable Causal Subgraph Sampler uses a two-layer Graph Transformer over the debiased graph to predict per-node inclusion probabilities, then samples hard causal subgraphs via Bernoulli draws with a straight-through estimator for gradient flow during training (soft mask at inference). A joint optimization objective unifies Cox partial-likelihood losses on both the full and causal-subgraph predictions, an MSE loss enforcing invariance of P(C|G) before and after semantic intervention, a contrastive loss aligning causal-subgraph features with the original graph while repelling non-causal features, and a ratio loss penalizing oversized causal subgraphs.

## Results

C²MIL achieves state-of-the-art C-index scores across three TCGA cohorts in five-fold cross-validation: 0.7078 (TCGA-KIRC), 0.6904 (TCGA-ESCA), and 0.6081 (TCGA-BLCA), outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.20–4.04%, 3.85–12.53%, and 3.10–5.54% respectively. In out-of-distribution external validation (single-institution test sets), C²MIL reaches C-index 0.6275, 0.6500, and 0.7015, surpassing all baselines by 2.86–8.26% on average. Kaplan-Meier log-rank tests confirm the most distinct high/low-risk separation (p = 6×10⁻⁴ on TCGA-ESCA; p = 6×10⁻¹¹ on TCGA-KIRC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²MIL outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset in both cross-validation and OOD settings. Against the strongest non-causal graph baseline ProtoSurv (0.6975/0.6194/0.5926 CV), C²MIL gains +1.03%, +7.10%, and +1.55% C-index respectively; against the causal baseline IBMIL (0.6970/0.5893/0.5527), gains are +1.08%, +10.11%, and +5.54%. Among attention-based MIL methods, ABMIL (0.6794/0.6385/0.5771) and RRTMIL (0.6775/0.6196/0.5661) are the closest competitors on KIRC, but C²MIL still leads by 2.84% and 4.04% respectively. In OOD validation, the gap widens: DeepGraphConv drops to 0.5091 on KIRC-CJ (−11.84% vs. C²MIL), and IBMIL falls to 0.5714 on ESCA-L5 (−7.86% vs. C²MIL), highlighting C²MIL's superior domain generalization. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that both the semantic disentangling and topological causal modules contribute additively when plugged into existing baselines (e.g., PatchGCN + semantic + topology reaches 0.7078 on KIRC, matching full C²MIL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in WSI-based survival analysis: causal intervention for semantic confounders (IBMIL, CaMIL) and graph-based topological modeling (PatchGCN, DeepGraphConv, ProtoSurv). Prior causal methods address only bag-level bias via multi-stage pipelines with fixed cluster counts, while graph-based MIL methods capture spatial structure but ignore semantic confounding. C²MIL departs from both by formulating a dual structural causal model that simultaneously performs backdoor adjustment (via cross-scale adaptive feature disentangling with Gumbel-Softmax-based dynamic K) and topological causal discovery (via Bernoulli differentiable subgraph sampling with STE), all within a single end-to-end training loop. The modular design also repurposes standard components—graph transformers, contrastive losses, Cox partial likelihood—under a new causal invariance objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-causal framing offers a principled, interpretable recipe for handling two pervasive sources of bias in multi-institutional pathology data—staining/preparation variation and irrelevant tissue topology—without requiring task-specific hyperparameter tuning (e.g., fixed K). The demonstrated modularity (Table 2) suggests the CAFD and causal subgraph sampler can be grafted onto existing MIL pipelines, broadening practical impact. However, evaluation is limited to three TCGA cohorts with H&E staining and survival labels; generalization to other stain types, classification tasks, or non-graph MIL settings remains untested. Open questions include whether the dual-SCM assumptions hold when causal and non-causal subgraphs are entangled at the cellular level, and whether the Bernoulli sampling strategy scales to very large graphs beyond the current patch counts.
