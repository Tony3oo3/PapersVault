## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models fail at low bit-widths (≤4-bit) due to imbalanced activation distributions where most values cluster near zero but sparse large values are critical for generation quality. While quantization-aware training can handle low-bit settings, it requires resources comparable to training from scratch. The paper identifies this distribution imbalance as the root cause and proposes weight finetuning as an efficient, data-free remedy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252246/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized diffusion model accepts the same inputs as its full-precision counterpart: random Gaussian noise for unconditional generation, class labels for ImageNet, or text prompts for Stable Diffusion, producing 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. For calibration during finetuning, the method is entirely data-free—only random Gaussian noise is fed through the full-precision model at sampled time steps (128–256 samples per step) to construct the training set, requiring no real image data.

## Method

QuEST selectively finetunes only time embedding layers and attention-related layers (≤7% of total parameters) under quantization constraints, leaving all other weights frozen. The training proceeds in two progressive stages: Temporal Layer Alignment (TLA) first adjusts time embedding weights and their activation scaling factors to preserve temporal information, then Critical Module Alignment (CMA) optimizes attention-layer weights alongside all remaining activation quantization parameters. A global MSE loss between the quantized and full-precision model outputs provides network-level supervision. The key mechanism is that finetuning reshapes activation distributions—shrinking value ranges and reducing sparse outliers—making them more amenable to low-bit quantization, which is theoretically justified via a Taylor-expansion analysis showing that weight adjustment compensates for large quantization perturbations that break standard PTQ guarantees.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedrooms (LDM-4, 256×256), QuEST achieves FID 3.03 at W8A8, 3.26 at W4A8, and 5.64 at W4A4, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., FID 3.26 vs. 5.37 for Q-Diffusion and 15.15 for EfficientDM at W4A8). On ImageNet 256×256, QuEST attains sFID 6.07 at W8A8 and 7.93 at W4A4, beating the best PTQ baseline (PTQ-D, sFID 9.01 at W8A8) and EfficientDM (sFID 9.28 at W4A4). On Stable Diffusion v1.4 (512×512), QuEST is the only method that successfully generates at W4A4, achieving a CLIP Score of 28.85 versus Q-Diffusion's complete generation failure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuEST outperforms all four baselines across every bit-width setting where results are available. Against PTQ-D (the strongest PTQ method), QuEST reduces FID from 3.75 to 3.03 at W8A8 and from 5.94 to 3.26 at W4A8 on LSUN-Bedrooms; PTQ-D and Q-Diffusion both fail entirely at W4A4. Against Q-Diffusion, the gap widens at lower bits: FID 3.26 vs. 5.37 at W4A8, and Q-Diffusion cannot generate at W4A4 while QuEST achieves FID 5.64. Against EfficientDM (the prior SOTA efficient finetuning method), QuEST is dramatically better at low bits—FID 3.26 vs. 15.15 at W4A8 and 5.64 vs. 10.60 at W4A4 on LSUN-Bedrooms—while also requiring fewer training iterations (2.2k vs. 32k) and less time (0.45h vs. 2.60h). On ImageNet, QuEST achieves the best sFID at all three bit-widths (6.07, 6.55, 7.93), beating EfficientDM by 1.21–2.07 sFID points, and is the only method besides EfficientDM that succeeds at W4A4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT) for diffusion models, departing from both extremes. Rather than the parameter-free but low-bit-fragile PTQ methods (PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQ-D) or the resource-intensive full QAT approaches, it adopts a parameter-efficient finetuning strategy similar in spirit to EfficientDM but without introducing extra LoRA parameters. The key conceptual departure is identifying two structural properties of quantized diffusion models—temporal embedding sensitivity and attention-layer fragility—that guide *which* weights to finetune, combined with a theoretical argument (via Taylor expansion breakdown under large perturbations) explaining why standard PTQ reconstruction guarantees fail at low bit-widths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QuEST provides a practical, data-free recipe for W4A4 quantization of UNet-based diffusion models at a fraction of the compute cost of prior finetuning methods (0.45 GPU-hours vs. 2.60 for EfficientDM), making low-bit deployment of Stable Diffusion and LDMs accessible on a single 48 GB GPU. The layer-sensitivity analysis (FeedForward activations failing at 6 bits while others survive to 4) offers actionable guidance for future quantization work. Open questions include whether the selective layer identification generalizes to DiT-based architectures (e.g., SD3, FLUX) and whether the theoretical Taylor-expansion argument extends beyond the simplified single-layer analysis presented.
