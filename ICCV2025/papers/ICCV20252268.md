## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting-based head avatar methods apply uniform regularization across all facial regions, ignoring that geometric deviations from the FLAME mesh vary dramatically (e.g., scalp and ears require large offsets while face and lips require minimal ones). This uniform strategy weakens rigging in well-fitted regions and produces severe artifacts during novel animation. Additionally, 3DMMs lack adequate mouth geometry, and prior deformation approaches treat each Gaussian independently without respecting anatomical consistency within mouth parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252268/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video sequence (2–3 minutes per subject in DynamicFace) and per-frame FLAME parameters (shape β∈R³⁰⁰, expression ψ∈R¹⁰⁰, pose Θ∈R¹², rotation, translation) extracted by a FLAME tracker. It produces a rigged 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (local means, rotations, scales, colors, opacities per Gaussian) that can be re-animated with arbitrary novel pose and expression parameters, yielding rendered head avatar images at real-time rates. The spherical harmonics degree is set to zero to suit the monocular setting.

## Method

GeoAvatar introduces three key mechanisms. First, the Adaptive Pre-allocation Stage (APS) runs N unsupervised training steps with all faces in the rigid set, then computes part-wise mean Gaussian-to-mesh distances to partition FLAME faces into a rigid set (small deviation, strict regularization) and a flexible set (large deviation, relaxed regularization) using a data-driven threshold. Second, a novel mouth structure (frontal teeth, molar teeth, palate, and floor) is added to FLAME, and a part-wise deformation network applies a single offset to all vertices within the upper or lower mouth part simultaneously, preserving anatomical relative positions during animation; the network takes FLAME expression/pose parameters plus a positional-encoded timestep for per-frame correction. Third, a polar-coordinate regularization loss penalizes the radial distance r of each Gaussian's local mean from its rigged face center with set-specific thresholds (τ_r=0.1, τ_f=2.0), and an additional angle loss penalizes the azimuthal angle φ when r exceeds the rigid threshold, preventing Gaussians from drifting into neighboring triangles during animation.

## Results

On the SplattingAvatar dataset, GeoAvatar achieves PSNR 32.64, SSIM 0.965, and LPIPS 0.367, outperforming the strongest baseline (GaussianAvatars_0) by ~3 dB in PSNR and ~26% in LPIPS. On the newly introduced DynamicFace dataset, GeoAvatar reaches PSNR 32.76, SSIM 0.919, and LPIPS 0.660, again leading all baselines. In the multi-view NeRSemble comparison, GeoAvatar trained on a single view matches GaussianAvatars (16 views) on MSE and PSNR while surpassing it by 9.7% on LPIPS and 0.6% on SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoAvatar outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both monocular datasets. The largest margins are against the NeRF-based INSTA (−3.5 dB PSNR, +44% LPIPS on SplattingAvatar) and the vanilla 3DGS (−5.1 dB PSNR, +64% LPIPS), which lack any 3DMM-based animation mechanism. Among Gaussian Splatting methods, GeoAvatar leads GaussianAvatars_0 (the strongest baseline) by ~3 dB PSNR and ~26–15% LPIPS on SplattingAvatar and DynamicFace respectively, and beats FlashAvatar by ~3.3 dB PSNR. Notably, on the multi-view NeRSemble benchmark, GeoAvatar trained on a single view achieves comparable MSE/PSNR to GaussianAvatars trained on 16 views while improving LPIPS by 9.7% and SSIM by 0.6%, demonstrating that the adaptive rigid/flexible segmentation and mouth structure yield perceptual gains even when FLAME fitting is already accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoAvatar extends the GaussianAvatars line of work, which rigs 3D Gaussians to a FLAME mesh via local-to-global transformations, by replacing the uniform position regularization with an adaptive rigid/flexible segmentation (APS) that allocates different geometric freedom per facial region. It also departs from prior mouth handling (e.g., GaussianAvatars' frontal-teeth-only structure) by adding molar teeth, palate, and floor geometry, and introduces an anatomically-motivated part-wise deformation that moves upper and lower mouth structures as coherent units. The polar-coordinate regularization loss (penalizing both radial distance r and angular deviation φ) is a new formulation compared to the fixed-threshold position losses used in FlashAvatar and GaussianAvatars.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive rigid/flexible segmentation addresses a practical failure mode in head avatar animation—uniform regularization either over-constrains poorly-fitted regions (scalp, ears) or under-constrains well-fitted regions (face, lips)—and the part-wise mouth deformation is a reusable principle for any articulated sub-structure. The released DynamicFace dataset fills a gap in highly expressive monocular talking-head data, enabling more rigorous evaluation of dynamic animation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hair and clothing (acknowledged limitation), whether it supports relighting, and whether the APS threshold strategy generalizes across subjects without per-subject tuning.
