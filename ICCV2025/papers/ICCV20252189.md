## Problem

Existing video face swapping methods achieve high-quality identity transfer but fail to preserve the target face's dynamic attributes (head pose, expressions, lip-sync), causing flickering and temporal inconsistency. The paper attributes this to the inherent coupling of facial appearance and motion: modifying appearance in the original space inevitably perturbs motion. This gap is compounded by the lack of fine-grained evaluation metrics that capture synchronization and temporal stability specific to video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252189/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source face image and a target video (frames resized to 512×512) as input, and produces a face-swapped video where the source identity replaces the target face while the target's pose, expression, and lip movements are preserved. The VFS benchmark evaluates on 100-frame clips with 4 seconds of paired audio to assess lip-sync. As a byproduct of the motion-appearance decoupling, the framework also supports face animation by optionally transferring the source's expression and pose to the target during the warp-back step.

## Method

CanonSwap decouples motion from appearance by warping each target frame into a unified canonical space (adopting the motion-guided warping from Face vid2vid), where the face has a fixed pose and only appearance information remains. Face swapping is then performed in this canonical space via a Partial Identity Modulation (PIM) module: each PIM block contains a standard convolution branch and a modulated convolution branch (where identity code scales and demodulates the weights), and their outputs are fused by a learned spatial mask that restricts identity modification to facial regions only. A lightweight 3D U-Net refinement module corrects warping artifacts before the swapped features are warped back to the original space. Training is end-to-end and unsupervised, combining ArcFace identity loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, pose/expression motion loss, same-identity reconstruction loss, adversarial loss, and total-variation mask regularization.

## Results

On the proposed VFS benchmark, CanonSwap achieves the best scores across all 10 metrics: ID similarity 0.5748, ID retrieval 99.78%, pose error 0.0159, expression error 0.7592, gaze error 0.0928, EAR 3.742, LSE-D 7.938, LSE-C 6.053, temporal consistency 0.513, and FVD 125.30. On the FF++ dataset, CanonSwap similarly leads with ID similarity 0.5751, ID retrieval 98.29%, pose error 0.0119, and FID 6.21, while expression error (0.7328) is slightly behind FSGAN (0.7184). The method's advantage is most pronounced in lip-sync (LSE-D/LSE-C) and temporal consistency (TC), where it outperforms the next-best method by a wide margin (e.g., TC 0.513 vs. 0.760 for FSGAN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CanonSwap outperforms all six baselines on the VFS benchmark across every metric. Against the strongest GAN-based baseline SimSwap [7], CanonSwap gains +0.059 in ID similarity, +0.91 in ID retrieval, and reduces pose error from 0.0196 to 0.0159, while dramatically improving temporal consistency (TC 0.513 vs. 0.773) and FVD (125.30 vs. 136.78). Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline Face Adapter [20], CanonSwap leads in ID similarity (0.5748 vs. 0.5215), pose (0.0159 vs. 0.0229), and especially in lip-sync (LSE-C 6.053 vs. 4.399) and temporal consistency (0.513 vs. 1.312). FSGAN [35] is the only method that edges out CanonSwap on expression error (0.7525 vs. 0.7592), but it trails severely on identity (ID sim 0.1442) and FVD (322.30). DiffSwap [56] and REFace [2] show the weakest identity transfer (ID sim 0.2461 and 0.4306) and the worst FVD scores (508.16 and 400.88), confirming that diffusion-based inpainting approaches struggle with temporal stability. E4S [30] achieves moderate identity (0.3953) but has the highest pose error (0.0288) and worst TC (1.066) among GAN methods, indicating poor motion preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CanonSwap repurposes the motion-guided warping mechanism from portrait animation (Face vid2vid, FOMM) for a different purpose: instead of transferring pose from a driving video, it uses warping to project faces into a canonical space where identity can be modified without disturbing motion. For the identity modulation step, it departs from the AdaIN paradigm dominant in GAN-based swappers (SimSwap, FSGAN) by introducing Partial Identity Modulation, which borrows StyleGAN's modulation/demodulation idea and adds a learned spatial mask to restrict changes to facial regions. Against diffusion-based video swappers (DynamicFace, VividFace, HiFiVFS) that rely on temporal attention for consistency, CanonSwap achieves temporal stability through the geometric warping round-trip rather than learned temporal modeling, at substantially lower computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The canonical-space formulation offers a principled and computationally efficient alternative to video-diffusion-based face swapping, making high-quality video face swapping more accessible without multi-second GPU inference per frame. The introduction of the VFS benchmark with fine-grained lip-sync (LSE-D/LSE-C), eye dynamics (EAR, gaze), and temporal consistency metrics fills a real evaluation gap that prior image-centric protocols (FID, ID similarity) cannot address. Open questions include how the frame-by-frame warping approach handles very long sequences with cumulative drift, extreme occlusions, and multi-person scenes, and whether the motion extractor's accuracy becomes a hard ceiling on swapping quality.
