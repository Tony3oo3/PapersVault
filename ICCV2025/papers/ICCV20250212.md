## Problem

Standard self-attention in vision Transformers scales quadratically with image size, making it prohibitively expensive for high-resolution image restoration. Existing efficient alternatives—window-based multi-head self-attention and transposed self-attention—either restrict the receptive field to local blocks or lose spatial connectivity entirely, creating a fundamental trade-off between global context modeling and computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image (blurry, rainy, or JPEG-compressed) and produces a restored RGB image of the same resolution. Internally, the input is downsampled to three additional scales (1/2, 1/4, 1/8) via bilinear interpolation, and the U-Net architecture produces outputs at all four scales for multi-scale loss computation. Training uses progressive patch sizes from 128×128 up to 320×320, and inference optionally applies Test-time Local Converter (TLC) for further quality gains.

## Method

Concertormer introduces Concerto Self-Attention (CSA), which decomposes self-attention into a per-block Concertino component (capturing local spatial relationships) and a shared Ripieno component (capturing global relationships across all blocks), achieving linear complexity in image size. A Cross-Dimensional Communication module then aggregates information across the independent attention maps using a 3×3×1 convolution on the Concertino dimension and a linear projection on the Ripieno dimension, replacing the global mean with a local neighborhood mean. The feed-forward network is replaced by a gated-dconv MLP (gdMLP) that fuses the attention output with a depth-wise convolved expansion in a single stage, eliminating the conventional two-stage attention-then-FFN design. CSA is applied to both spatial and channel dimensions, and a simplified channel attention (SCA) balances their contributions. The overall architecture is a U-Net with cross-attention skip connections between encoder and decoder levels, trained with combined spatial and frequency-domain ℓ₁ losses.

## Results

On the GOPRO dataset, Concertormer achieves 34.42 dB PSNR and 0.971 SSIM, while Concertormer-lite achieves 34.18 dB PSNR and 0.970 SSIM, both setting new state-of-the-art results. On HIDE, Concertormer reaches 32.12 dB PSNR and 0.951 SSIM, outperforming the previous best (FFTformer at 31.62 dB) by 0.50 dB. On the real-world REALBLUR-R dataset, Concertormer attains 40.78 dB PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, the highest among all compared methods. The lite model uses 16% fewer FLOPs than the full model while maintaining competitive performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Concertormer outperforms all 22 compared baselines on both GOPRO and HIDE for deblurring. The closest competitors are MISCFilter (34.10 dB on GOPRO) and FFTformer (34.21 dB), with Concertormer exceeding them by 0.32 dB and 0.21 dB respectively; on HIDE the gap over FFTformer widens to 0.50 dB. Against the widely-used Restormer [68], Concertormer gains 1.50 dB on GOPRO and 0.90 dB on HIDE while using fewer FLOPs. On the real-world REALBLUR-R benchmark, Concertormer (40.78 dB) edges out MLWNet (40.69 dB) and MISCFilter (40.61 dB), though on REALBLUR-J, MISCFilter achieves slightly higher PSNR (33.88 vs. 33.51) while Concertormer leads in SSIM (0.945 vs. 0.938). For deraining, Concertormer achieves 34.62 dB average PSNR, surpassing Restormer (34.16 dB) by 0.46 dB. For JPEG artifact removal on REDS-VAL-300, Concertormer (29.33 dB) beats NAFNet (29.09 dB) by 0.24 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

Concertormer positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work on efficient vision Transformers for image restoration: window-based attention (SwinIR) that sacrifices global context, and transposed self-attention (Restormer) that sacrifices spatial connectivity. The Concerto Self-Attention mechanism departs from both by decomposing attention into a local 'Concertino' and a global 'Ripieno' component within a single linear-complexity operation, while the Cross-Dimensional Communication module draws on the talking-heads attention idea to aggregate across the extra dimension this decomposition introduces. The gated-dconv MLP adapts the gated MLP from Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2021) by replacing the large spatial linear layer with depth-wise convolution and dropping layer normalization, and the simplified channel attention is borrowed from NAFNet (Chen et al., ECCV 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for Transformer-based image restoration: quadratic attention cost at high resolutions. The linear-complexity CSA with demonstrated spatial awareness (via diffusion index and local attribution maps) offers a principled middle ground between windowed and transposed attention, and the Restormer-CSA experiment shows the mechanism is drop-in compatible with existing architectures. The finding that a single-stage gdMLP outperforms the conventional two-stage (SA + FFN) design by over 1.6 dB at matched FLOPs challenges the assumption inherited from NLP that the FFN stage is indispensable for vision tasks. Open questions include whether the Concertino/Ripieno decomposition generalizes beyond restoration to higher-level tasks, and whether the modest 0.2–0.4 dB gains over the strongest baselines (FFTformer, GRL) persist at substantially larger model scales or on more diverse degradation types.
