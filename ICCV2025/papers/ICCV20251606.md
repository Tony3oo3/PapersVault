## Problem

Real large-scale face datasets for FIQA training are being discontinued due to GDPR privacy violations, yet existing synthetic face datasets are designed for identity-labeled face recognition rather than quality-annotated FIQA. The core challenge is twofold: generating synthetic faces with precisely controllable degradation factors (pose, blur, occlusion, downsampling) and devising a quality characterization method that leverages the known degradation priors available only in synthetic generation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline consumes pseudo-identity face images (512×512, filtered for yaw <15°, no glasses, neutral expression, inter-class similarity ≤0.3), 3D facial parameters modified via DECA/FLAME, dual text prompts (positive for occlusion/scene control, negative for realism constraints), and explicit degradation parameters (anisotropic Gaussian blur intensity p_b, downsampling factor p_d, yaw angle p_y). It produces 0.5M synthetic face images (5K identities × 100 intra-class samples, 10 high-quality references per identity) with known degradation parameters, from which MR-FIQA derives quality annotations. The resulting FIQA model operates on 112×112 cropped face images and outputs a scalar quality score for recognition utility estimation.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) an unconditional DDPM trained on FFHQ generates pseudo-identity images, which are filtered and deduplicated via ArcFace embeddings; (2) a Stable Diffusion V1.5 model is conditioned via a ControlNet-based module E_C that fuses ArcFace identity embeddings with CLIP ViT-L/14 facial segmentation features through cross-attention, while 3D parameter edits (pose, expression, position, lighting) are rendered as Normal/Albedo/Lambertian maps by DECA and injected as additional conditioning. Occlusion is controlled via positive text prompts (e.g., 'wearing glasses', 'in the rain') and blur/downsampling via post-processing with a 21×21 anisotropic Gaussian kernel. The MR-FIQA quality annotation integrates three domains: recognition embedding similarity to the mean of Z reference embeddings (f_r), a minimum-ranking score across pose/blur/downsampling degradation parameters (s_d), and BLIP image-text similarity between the sample and the reference text prompt (v_l), combined as Q = varpi[(f_r + λ·s_d)/(1−v_l)] with max-min normalization.

## Results

On the primary comparison (Table 1, AdaFace deployed model), SynFIQA++ achieves the best average pAUC of 0.730 and AUC of 0.520 among all synthetic training sets, narrowing the gap to the real-data baseline CASIA-WebFace (pAUC 0.701, AUC 0.474) to just 0.029 and 0.046 respectively. The base SynFIQA (without MR-FIQA annotations) achieves pAUC 0.800 and AUC 0.554, already competitive with DigiFace-1M (0.801/0.620) and DCFace (0.813/0.604). In the quality characterization comparison (Table 2, ArcFace deployed model), MR-FIQA yields the lowest average pAUC of 0.572, outperforming CR-FIQA (0.596), FaceQnet (0.643), and SDD-FIQA (0.652).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynFIQA++ (pAUC 0.730, AUC 0.520) outperforms all seven synthetic baselines across both metrics, with the largest margins over HSFace-10K (pAUC 0.869, AUC 0.717) and IDiff-Face (pAUC 0.873, AUC 0.639), which prioritize inter-class identity diversity over quality variation. Among synthetic datasets emphasizing intra-class quality diversity, SynFIQA++ beats DigiFace-1M (0.801/0.620) by 0.071 in pAUC and 0.100 in AUC, and DCFace (0.813/0.604) by 0.083 in pAUC and 0.084 in AUC. The real-data baseline CASIA-WebFace still leads (0.701/0.474), but the gap is substantially smaller than for other synthetic sets, and combining SynFIQA with CASIA further reduces pAUC, indicating complementary coverage. In the quality characterization comparison (Table 2), MR-FIQA achieves the best average pAUC of 0.572, beating CR-FIQA (0.596), FaceQnet (0.643), and SDD-FIQA (0.652), confirming that multi-domain reference representations yield more precise annotations than recognition-embedding-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion with ControlNet for identity-preserving, quality-controllable face generation, DECA for 3D facial parameter manipulation, and BLIP for vision-language quality scoring. It departs from prior synthetic face datasets (SynFace, DCFace, SFace2, IDiff-Face, GANDiffFace), which are designed to optimize face recognition accuracy via identity labels, by instead targeting the FIQA task with explicit quality annotations. It also departs from prior FIQA quality characterization methods (FaceQnet, SDD-FIQA, CR-FIQA), which restrict reference representations to the recognition embedding domain, by proposing multi-domain references (recognition embedding, spatial degradation parameters, and vision-language) that exploit the known degradation priors inherent in the synthetic generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a pressing practical need: as large-scale real face datasets are discontinued for GDPR compliance, a reliable synthetic alternative for FIQA training is essential. The quality-controllable generation pipeline and the complementary role of synthetic + real data (Fig. 5) offer a viable path forward. However, the results are mixed: SynFIQA with MR-FIQA annotations (SynFIQA++) does not clearly outperform simpler baselines in Table 2, and the overall pAUC gap to real-data training persists. Open questions include whether the controlled degradation spectrum (pose, blur, downsampling, occlusion) adequately captures the full diversity of real-world quality factors, and whether the approach scales beyond 5K identities without quality annotation drift.
