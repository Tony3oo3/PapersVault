## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models consistently fail to render spatial relationships described in prompts (e.g., 'a motorcycle to the right of a bear'). The paper identifies two compounding root causes: existing image-text datasets contain spatially ambiguous annotations due to inconsistent frames of reference, non-spatial usage of spatial terms, and missing reference objects, and commonly used text encoders (CLIP, T5) fail to preserve spatial relationships in their embeddings, making logically equivalent descriptions nearly indistinguishable.

## Input / Output

At inference, the enhanced model accepts only standard natural language text prompts (no bounding boxes, masks, or external layout inputs) and produces a generated image, making it a drop-in replacement for the base model. During training, the SCOP data engine produces image crops (ROIs containing both objects of a spatial pair) paired with text prompts sampled from a predefined template pool, yielding over 28,000 such pairs from 15,000 COCO images. The TENOR module adds no extra parameters or input channels, so the inference interface is identical to the unmodified base model.

## Method

CoMPaSS combines two synergistic components. The SCOP data engine curates spatially unambiguous training data by enumerating all object pairs in COCO images and filtering them through five geometric/semantic constraints (visual significance, semantic distinction, spatial clarity, minimal overlap, and size balance), then decoding surviving pairs into image crops with template-generated text descriptions. The TENOR module is a parameter-free plug-and-play component that injects absolute positional encodings into every text-image cross-attention operation—into K vectors for UNet-based models and into both Q_text and K_text for MMDiT-based models—thereby preserving the sequential token ordering that standard text encoders discard. The key insight is that TENOR provides the structural capacity to distinguish prompts like 'A left of B' from 'B left of A', while SCOP supplies the correct visual ground truth for each, enabling the model to learn accurate spatial associations during a brief fine-tuning phase with negligible inference overhead (~3% time, <1% VRAM).

## Results

On the state-of-the-art FLUX.1 model, CoMPaSS achieves substantial relative gains of +98% on VISOR (37.96% → 75.17% unconditional), +67% on T2I-CompBench Spatial (0.18 → 0.30), and +131% on GenEval Position (0.26 → 0.60). On UNet-based models, SD1.5+CoMPaSS reaches 61.46% on VISOR unconditional and 0.54 on GenEval Position, while SD2.1+CoMPaSS achieves 62.06% VISOR and 0.51 GenEval Position. Importantly, these spatial gains come without sacrificing general generation quality: DPG-Bench overall scores improve (e.g., FLUX.1: 80.63 → 84.42) and image fidelity metrics (FID, CMMD) also improve across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct performance baseline is SPRIGHT [6], a training-based spatial consistency method evaluated on SD2.1. CoMPaSS decisively outperforms SPRIGHT on all spatial metrics: VISOR unconditional (62.06% vs. 43.23%), T2I-CompBench Spatial (0.32 vs. 0.21), and GenEval Position (0.51 vs. 0.11), while also achieving better image fidelity (FID 16.96 vs. 16.15 is slightly worse, but CMMD 0.4083 vs. 0.512 is substantially better). In terms of computational efficiency (Table 3), CoMPaSS incurs only +3.9% training time and +0.7% VRAM overhead, compared to SPRIGHT's +8.1%/+24.5%. The three inference-only baselines—R&B [79], Attention Refocusing [52], and Layout Guidance [13]—are compared solely on inference overhead; CoMPaSS's +2.47% time and +0.6% VRAM overhead is dramatically lower than R&B (+1108.7%/+150.9%), Layout Guidance (+180.9%/+67.4%), and Attention Refocusing (+23.8%/+38.0%), since CoMPaSS requires no test-time optimization or external bounding-box generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMPaSS builds on the Stable Diffusion family (UNet-based SD1.4/1.5/2.1) and FLUX.1 (MMDiT-based), targeting a well-known limitation in spatial relationship generation. It departs from prior spatial control work along two axes: on the data side, it introduces the SCOP engine to curate spatially-unambiguous training pairs from COCO, addressing the ambiguity in web-scale datasets (LAION, CC12M) that prior methods like SPRIGHT inherit; on the architecture side, TENOR is a parameter-free module that injects token-ordering positional encodings into cross-attention, contrasting with SPRIGHT's text-encoder fine-tuning and inference-only methods (R&B, Attention Refocusing, Layout Guidance) that impose heavy test-time optimization. The paper's key conceptual departure is diagnosing two compounding root causes—data ambiguity and encoder failure—rather than treating spatial generation as a single architectural problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMPaSS offers a practical, low-overhead solution (~3% inference time, <1% VRAM, no extra parameters) to a persistent and user-visible failure mode in T2I generation, with very large relative gains across four popular models. The diagnostic showing that even T5-XXL fails to distinguish spatially equivalent prompts >95% of the time is a useful finding for the broader text-encoder community. However, the framework remains viewer-centric and does not handle object-centric frames (e.g., 'to the child's right') or context-dependent spatial language (Qualitative Spatial Relations), and struggles with extreme size disparities—important open questions for truly robust spatial understanding.
