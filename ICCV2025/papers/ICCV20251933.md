## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators are largely confined to controlled indoor environments or narrow scenarios like urban driving, leaving agents unable to develop spatial and social intelligence in the diverse, unstructured open worlds they will encounter in deployment. There is no comprehensive photorealistic virtual world collection that simultaneously offers scene diversity across scales (indoor to 16 km² landscapes), heterogeneous playable entities, and efficient multi-agent interaction tooling for training generalizable embodied agents.

## Input / Output

The system consumes Python API commands and JSON task configuration files to set up scenes, spawn entities, and define reward functions, while agents receive ego-centric observations (RGB, depth, surface normal, instance segmentation masks at 640×480) and output discrete actions or continuous velocities. A key design detail is the batch commands protocol, where the client sends multiple agent commands in a single round-trip and receives a batch of rendered frames, reducing server-client communication overhead for multi-agent scenarios. The Gym interface wraps all of this into a standard reset/step loop with configurable wrappers for environment augmentation, population control, and time dilation.

## Method

UnrealZoo assembles 100 artist-designed photorealistic scenes (sourced from the Unreal Engine Marketplace) and 67 playable entities (humans, animals, vehicles, drones, robots) on UE4/5, each equipped with Smart Locomotion animation and NavMesh-based autonomous navigation. The core engineering contribution is UnrealCV+, which optimizes the original UnrealCV through parallel rendering of object masks and depth maps, a batch commands protocol for simultaneous multi-agent control, and IPC sockets replacing TCP for stable high-load communication, yielding up to 120% FPS gains in multi-agent settings. On top of this, an OpenAI Gym interface with JSON-based task configuration and a toolkit of gym wrappers (environment augmentation, population control, time dilation) standardizes agent-environment interaction. Experiments benchmark distributed online RL (A3C), offline RL, and VLM-based agents (GPT-4o, OpenVLA) on visual navigation and active tracking tasks, revealing that environmental diversity substantially improves cross-scene generalization, that control frequency below 10 FPS degrades tracking performance sharply, and that cross-embodiment transfer (human to robot dog) incurs significant performance drops.

## Results

In the social tracking benchmark (Table 5), the RL agent trained on 8 diverse environments achieves the highest success rate across all distractor levels: 0.76 SR (0D), 0.68 SR (4D), and 0.56 SR (10D), with corresponding episode returns of 321, 280, and 187. In visual navigation (Table 3), the online RL agent reaches 0.52 SR in the Industrial Area and 0.32 SR on the Roof, while GPT-4o achieves only 0.20 and 0.00 SR respectively; human players complete both tasks with 1.00 SR. The UnrealCV+ optimization yields up to 120% FPS improvement for object mask rendering and 100% improvement in 10-agent multi-agent interaction compared to the original UnrealCV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The RL agent (trained on 8 diverse environments) decisively outperforms all baselines in social tracking. Against OpenVLA, the RL agent achieves 0.76 vs. 0.10 SR at 0D (7.6× improvement) and 0.56 vs. 0.00 SR at 10D, where OpenVLA completely fails due to accumulated errors and distraction sensitivity. GPT-4o also underperforms significantly (0.28 SR at 0D, 0.10 at 10D) and suffers from negative episodic returns under distractors, indicating it frequently tracks the wrong target. PID, while more stable than the VLM-based methods, degrades sharply from 0.34 to 0.14 SR as distractors increase, due to delayed reactions when the target is occluded. Cross-embodiment transfer to a quadrupedal robot dog reduces the RL agent's SR from 0.68 to 0.48 (4D) and 0.56 to 0.40 (10D), revealing a 29–29% relative drop that no other method can match in absolute terms, highlighting cross-embodiment generalization as an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own UnrealCV framework into UnrealCV+ with optimized rendering pipelines, batch command protocols, and IPC-based communication, while assembling 100 photorealistic scenes from the Unreal Engine Marketplace into a unified benchmark. Conceptually, it departs from prior simulators (AI2-THOR, Habitat 3.0, CARLA, ThreeDWorld, LEGENT) by broadening scope from indoor rooms or urban driving to genuinely open-world landscapes, villages, temples, and industrial settings, and by introducing heterogeneous multi-agent social interactions with diverse embodiments (humans, animals, robots, vehicles). The experimental baselines draw on standard RL (A3C, offline CQL), classical control (PID), and recent VLM/VLA models (GPT-4o, OpenVLA), positioning the contribution primarily as a new environment and tooling layer rather than a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UnrealZoo fills a notable gap by providing a large-scale, diverse, and practically usable benchmark for open-world embodied AI, with the Gym interface and toolkit lowering the barrier for distributed training and task customization. The experiments confirm that environmental diversity is critical for cross-scene generalization and that current RL and VLM agents still struggle substantially with unstructured 3D terrain, dynamic distractions, and cross-embodiment transfer—challenges previously masked by constrained indoor or urban simulators. Open questions include whether animation-based (non-physics) interactions suffice for sim-to-real transfer, how the benchmark scales to truly large multi-agent social scenarios, and whether the observed latency sensitivity (performance collapse below 10 FPS) will persist as models become more efficient. Licensing restrictions on marketplace content limit full open-sourcing, which may constrain reproducibility.
