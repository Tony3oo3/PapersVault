## Problem

Existing Composed Image Retrieval methods represent bi-modal queries as single overall vectors, overlooking complex multi-schema interactions (e.g., specific spatial relationships between multiple objects and attributes) between reference and target images. Additionally, they assume a strict one-to-one query-target correspondence, but in practice many suitable target images are mislabeled as negatives, introducing noise that degrades retrieval accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image (resized to 224×224 with 1.25 padding ratio) and a free-form text description as the composed query, processed by frozen BLIP-2 image and text encoders respectively. The Q-Former with 32 learnable queries produces a set of composed feature vectors (one per query) rather than a single embedding, which are then matched against target image features extracted by the same frozen encoder. Retrieval is performed by ranking candidate target images by similarity to the composed features.

## Method

MAPNet introduces two key components. First, the Multi-Schema Interaction (MSI) module formulates the assignment of batch samples to learnable Q-Former queries as an entropic optimal transport problem solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations, enforcing balanced and unique query allocation to prevent the sharp query distribution observed in prior work (e.g., SPRC); alignment is then enforced through squared MMD between per-query composed feature distributions and stop-gradient target feature distributions. Second, the Relaxed Proximity Loss (RPLoss) replaces hard binary-label contrastive objectives with a soft, similarity-weighted loss: a Gaussian-kernel confidence score W_ij reweights each pair, and a hinge-based denoising term with threshold γ suppresses the contribution of likely-noisy negatives, yielding separate attracting and repelling terms that adaptively focus on high-confidence relationships.

## Results

On CIRR (test set), MAPNet achieves Recall@1 of 54.65%, Recall@5 of 84.93%, and Recall@10 of 91.44%, surpassing the previous SOTA (SPRC) by 2.69% on R@1 and 2.81% on R@5. On FashionIQ (validation set), MAPNet attains an average Recall@10 of 55.70% across Dress, Shirt, and Toptee categories, exceeding SPRC by 1.53%. On the large-scale LaSCo dataset, MAPNet reaches Recall@5 of 24.62% and Recall@50 of 58.18%, improving over SPRC by 2.41% and 3.08% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPNet outperforms all 22 compared baselines on CIRR, with the most significant margin over the previous SOTA SPRC (+2.69% R@1, +2.81% R@5) and a clear lead over CaLa (+5.54% R@1) and CoVR-BLIP (+4.96% R@1). On FashionIQ, MAPNet surpasses SPRC by 1.53% in average Recall@10 (55.70 vs. 54.17) and leads TG-CIR by 4.38% (55.70 vs. 51.32), while also exceeding FAME-ViL† by 7.41%. On LaSCo, MAPNet improves over SPRC by 2.41% on R@5 and 3.08% on R@50, and outperforms CASE by 6.12% on R@5. Notably, MAPNet's advantage is most pronounced on Recall@K metrics (full gallery) rather than Recalls@K (curated subset), suggesting its multi-schema interaction and denoising mechanisms are most beneficial for complex, diverse retrieval scenarios. The ablation confirms both MSI (+2.06% R@1) and RPLoss (+1.53% R@1) contribute independently, with their combination yielding the full +2.69% gain over the SPRC baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPNet extends the prompt-generation line of CIR methods, building directly on SPRC's multi-query mechanism and BLIP-2's frozen encoder + Q-Former architecture. Its two key departures are conceptual: (1) formulating learnable query assignment as an entropic optimal transport problem (solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp) to prevent the sharp query distribution observed in SPRC, and (2) replacing binary-label contrastive losses with a Relaxed Proximity Loss that uses pairwise similarity to denoise and reweight negative pairs. This positions MAPNet as a refinement of the vision-language foundation model paradigm for CIR, rather than a new architectural family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noisy negative pair problem identified here is a practical data-quality concern that likely affects all CIR benchmarks and methods, making the RPLoss strategy broadly applicable beyond this specific architecture. However, the improvement on Recalls@K (subset-based metric) is minimal, suggesting the multi-schema interaction gains are most relevant for complex, multi-object queries rather than simpler compositional edits. Open questions include whether the optimal transport formulation scales to larger query sets or different backbone architectures, and whether the denoising threshold (γ) generalizes across domains without manual tuning.
