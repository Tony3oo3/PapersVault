## Problem

Autoregressive video generation methods achieve high inference efficiency but produce lower visual quality than diffusion models, primarily because they rely on discrete VQ tokenization (causing quantization errors) and next-token prediction (failing to exploit spatial and temporal dependencies among related tokens). This paper bridges the quality gap by introducing a continuous-domain next-set prediction paradigm that unifies image and video generation under a single decoder-only AR framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251795/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts (encoded via a CLIP text encoder into 1024-dim embeddings), reference images, or video clips as conditioning signals. Visual inputs are tokenized by a 2D VAE (16× downsampling, 2×2 patchification) into continuous latent tokens without vector quantization; a 512×512 frame yields 1024 tokens. Outputs are generated images (256×256 or 512×512) or 32-frame videos at 512×512 resolution, produced in K+T−1 autoregressive steps (e.g., 32 spatial steps for the first frame plus 31 temporal steps for a 32-frame clip).

## Method

The core contribution is a Llama-like decoder-only AR transformer G_θ that predicts continuous probability distributions over groups of tokens (sets) rather than individual discrete tokens. Two partitioning strategies define the sets: Spatial Progressive Partitioning (SPP) groups spatially adjacent image tokens with set sizes following a sine curve (K=32 sets by default), and Temporal Next-Frame Partitioning (TNP) treats each subsequent video frame as a single set conditioned on all prior frames. A lightweight MLP-based denoising sampler D then stochastically draws each token from its predicted continuous distribution via iterative noise-prediction steps, and gradients from the denoising loss backpropagate through D to train G_θ end-to-end. This design eliminates VQ quantization error, reduces inference steps from thousands of tokens to tens of sets, and remains fully compatible with KV-cache acceleration.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 text-to-image generation, the 775M model achieves FID 2.07 and IS 311.7, matching VAR-d24 (FID 2.09, IS 312.09) with fewer parameters. On UCF-101 zero-shot text-to-video, the 775M model achieves FVD 204 and IS 41.47, outperforming VideoPoet (FVD 355, IS 38.44) by a wide margin while using only 23% of its parameters (775M vs. 8B) and generating frames 1.6× faster (1.6s vs. 2.0s per frame). On VBench, the method scores 79.54 average across 9 dimensions, surpassing Emu3 (78.10, 8B) and NOVA (78.48, 0.6B) while remaining competitive with 2B diffusion models like CogVideoX-2B (81.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest AR baseline VideoPoet (8B), the 775M model achieves substantially better FVD (204 vs. 355 on UCF-101; 182 vs. 213 on MSR-VTT), higher IS (41.47 vs. 38.44), and lower latency (1.6s vs. 2.0s per frame) while using only ~10% of the parameters. Compared to the diffusion model SVD (1.5B), the method achieves lower FVD on UCF-101 (204 vs. 242) with faster per-frame inference (1.6s vs. 1.9s). On image generation, the method matches VAR-d24 (FID 2.07 vs. 2.09) with fewer parameters (775M vs. 1.0B) but trails MAR-H (FID 2.07 vs. 1.55), which uses a masked-architecture with continuous sampling. On VBench, the method (79.54 avg, 0.8B) outperforms Emu3 (78.10, 8B) and NOVA (78.48, 0.6B) but remains slightly below the 2B diffusion models CogVideoX-2B (81.49) and T2V-Turbo (79.95), indicating that diffusion models still hold a modest quality edge at larger scale. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that replacing the VQ tokenizer with a continuous VAE improves FVD by 64 points (314→247) and motion smoothness by 0.82%, validating the continuous-domain design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: decoder-only autoregressive visual generation (VideoPoet, EMU3, VAR) and continuous-domain sampling without vector quantization (MAR, NOVA). It departs from prior AR methods by replacing discrete VQ tokens with continuous VAE representations and replacing next-token prediction with next-set prediction (Spatial Progressive Partitioning for images, Temporal Next-Frame Partitioning for video), while departing from MAR/NOVA by adopting a decoder-only Llama-like architecture compatible with KV-cache rather than an encoder-decoder masked framework. The denoising sampler and its training loss formulation are directly inherited from MAR [31] and VAR [54], making the core novelty the architectural combination of next-set grouping with continuous-domain sampling in a causal AR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the demonstrated efficiency-quality tradeoff: a sub-1B parameter model with KV-cache support achieves FVD and VBench scores competitive with 8B+ diffusion and AR models at 1.1s/frame latency, suggesting AR video generation can leverage the mature LLM infrastructure (in-context learning, efficient serving) without sacrificing quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (beyond 32 frames), whether the sine-curve set partitioning is optimal, and how performance holds at much larger model scales where diffusion models have shown continued gains. The paper does not address audio generation, 3D consistency, or interactive/controllable generation, leaving the unified-multimodal vision of the AR paradigm partially unrealized.
