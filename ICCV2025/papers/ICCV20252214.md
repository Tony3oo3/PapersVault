## Problem

Existing feature-based adversarial attacks extract intermediate representations from supervised ConvNets, overlooking the richer, more generalizable features distilled by self-supervised Vision Transformers. The paper asks how to fully exploit the synergy between self-supervised learning (both contrastive learning and masked image modeling) and the Transformer architecture to craft adversarial perturbations with superior black-box transferability across diverse model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet) and produces an adversarial example constrained within an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of ε=10/255. A ResNet generator G_θ maps the input image to the adversarial perturbation, which is clipped at inference. Two frozen self-supervised ViT-B/16 models (DINO for CL and MAE for MIM) serve as feature extractors during training, with features drawn from the penultimate layer (l=10) and attention saliency maps computed from the [CLS] token across all heads.

## Method

dSVA introduces a generative training framework where a ResNet generator is trained to maximize cosine distance between facet-level features (queries, keys, values) of a benign image and its adversarial counterpart, rather than using full intermediate layer outputs. The key architectural insight is targeting internal self-attention components (q, k, v) that are directly associated with the input and provide less noisy, more generalizable features. Self-attention saliency maps from the [CLS] token are extracted and applied as multiplicative regularization (scaling factor γ=100) to guide optimization toward semantically salient regions. The final loss jointly combines feature discrimination from DINO (exploiting the key facet for global structural features) and MAE (exploiting the query facet for local textural features) with a balancing parameter λ=0.5, enabling the crafted perturbation to simultaneously disrupt both shape-level and texture-level representations.

## Results

On ImageNet, dSVA (Joint) achieves an average black-box fooling rate of 78.4% across 12 target models spanning ConvNets, ViTs, and MLP-Mixer, outperforming all compared baselines. Against the strongest generative baseline CDA(ViT-B/16), dSVA (Joint) wins on 9 of 12 targets, losing only on Swin-B (59.83% vs. 62.13%) and PiT-B (50.48% vs. 59.74%). Against adversarially trained defense models (Table 2), dSVA (Joint) exceeds all baselines by an average margin of 32.98%, reaching 88.06% on IncRes-v2_adv and 89.13% on EfficientNet-B0 with AdvProp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generative baseline CDA(ViT-B/16), dSVA (Joint) wins on 9 of 12 targets (e.g., 96.78% vs. 92.75% on VGG-16, 95.32% vs. 87.23% on Eff-B0, 85.38% vs. 80.63% on Mixer), losing only on Swin-B (59.83% vs. 62.13%) and PiT-B (50.48% vs. 59.74%), and essentially tying on LeViT (85.21% vs. 85.20%). Against the best gradient-based ViT attack ATT(ViT-B/16), dSVA (Joint) dominates across all 12 targets, with margins ranging from +2.3% (Swin-B) to +35.3% (Eff-B0). The ConvNet-surrogate baselines (CDA/BIA with VGG-19, Res-152, Den-169) remain competitive only on their structurally matching targets (e.g., CDA(Den-169) reaches 97.03% on Den-121 vs. dSVA's 94.83%) but collapse on ViT and MLP targets (e.g., CDA(VGG-19) scores only 16.53% on Swin-B). The joint exploitation of DINO (CL) and MAE (MIM) features yields a 13.70% average improvement over either single-model variant, confirming the complementary structural and textural feature disruption. Against adversarially trained defense models, dSVA (Joint) outperforms all baselines by an average of 32.98%, with the largest gap over CDA(ViT-B/16) on Inc-v3_adv (79.03% vs. 65.91%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative adversarial attack paradigm established by Poursaeed et al. (2018) and refined by CDA (Naseer et al., 2019) and BIA (Zhang et al., 2022), which use a learned generator to produce transferable perturbations. The key departure is shifting the feature exploitation target from supervised ConvNet intermediate layer outputs to the internal facets (queries, keys, values) of self-supervised ViTs, specifically DINO (contrastive learning) and MAE (masked image modeling). The paper further introduces self-attention saliency maps as a regularization mechanism and proposes joint disruption of both structural (CL) and textural (MIM) features, a combination not explored in prior feature-based attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that self-supervised ViT features yield substantially more transferable adversarial perturbations than supervised ViT features challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that label-aligned representations are the most universal targets for cross-architecture attacks. The joint CL+MIM strategy outperforming single-model variants by ~13.7% on average suggests that complementary feature aspects (global structure vs. local texture) are both necessary for robust adversarial generalization. Open questions include whether these findings extend to larger ViT variants (e.g., DINOv2 trained on web-scale data), whether the facet-level exploitation generalizes to other Transformer architectures beyond ViTs, and whether the observed transferability gains persist under stronger or adaptive defenses beyond the standard adversarial training setups tested here.
