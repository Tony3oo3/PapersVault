## Problem

Existing cache-enhanced test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models select samples solely by low entropy to build class prototypes, assuming intra-class compactness in the feature space. Under distribution shifts, this assumption breaks down: low-entropy samples can be unreliable due to spurious correlations, and class distributions may become too dispersed for prototypes to be representative. The paper identifies a strong positive correlation (r > 0.8) between cache-enhanced performance gains and intra-class compactness, revealing that the caching mechanism's effectiveness is fundamentally limited when classes are not naturally compact.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images (processed through 31 random-resized-crop augmentations, yielding 32 views per sample) and class-specific text prompt descriptions as input, operating entirely in a zero-shot inference setting without any labeled target-domain data. It outputs a class label and probability distribution computed as a weighted sum of three complementary signals: textual semantic matching, visual-prototype contrastive scoring (with negative-cache suppression), and similarity-weighted cache retrieval. Per-class cache capacities are fixed at 10 samples each for the entropy and align caches and 3 for the negative cache, keeping memory overhead minimal relative to the full test set.

## Method

MCP constructs three complementary caches to build more compact and reliable class prototypes: an Entropy Cache that stores the lowest-entropy samples per class to anchor initial representations, an Align Cache that additionally requires proximity to the prototype center (a weighted fusion of visual and textual prototypes with w=0.8 favoring visual) to enforce intra-class compactness, and a Negative Cache that stores calibrated high-entropy samples (via a reflecting mechanism that re-evaluates pseudo-labels against reliable cached features) to suppress uncertain predictions. MCP++ extends this by introducing learnable residual parameters added to both visual and textual prototypes, jointly optimized through a composite loss combining entropy minimization over augmented views, an InfoNCE-based cross-modal alignment loss, and a contrastive loss that pushes prototype centers away from negative-cache features. The final prediction logits aggregate three normalized components—textual matching, visual-prototype contrastive scoring with negative-cache penalty, and attention-weighted cache retrieval—enabling the model to balance global category stability with local adaptability under shifting test distributions.

## Results

On the 10-dataset cross-domain benchmark (ViT-B/16 backbone), MCP++ achieves 72.61% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest cache-based baseline DMN (70.30%) by 2.31% and the zero-shot CLIP baseline (63.58%) by 9.03%. On the OOD benchmark (ImageNet + 4 variants), MCP++ reaches 66.86% average accuracy, surpassing DPE (65.93%) by 0.93% and TDA (65.01%) by 1.85%. MCP (without residual learning) already achieves 71.98% on cross-domain and 66.54% on OOD, confirming the multi-cache mechanism itself provides most of the gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCP++ wins on 9 out of 10 cross-domain datasets, with the largest margins on EuroSAT (+9.21% over DMN) and DTD (+1.12% over DMN), while DMN retains a slight edge on Caltech-101 (95.38 vs. 95.50 is actually MCP++ winning; DMN leads on no dataset). Against prompt-based methods, MCP++ outperforms PromptAlign (66.92%) by 5.69%, MaPle (66.30%) by 6.31%, and TPT (65.10%) by 7.51% in average cross-domain accuracy, while also avoiding the per-sample gradient optimization cost of prompt tuning. Among cache-based methods, MCP++ surpasses DMN (70.30%) by 2.31%, DPE (69.40%) by 3.21%, BoostAdapter (68.68%) by 3.93%, and TDA (67.53%) by 5.08%. On the OOD benchmark, MCP++ leads all baselines with 66.86% average, beating DPE by 0.93%, DMN by 1.37%, and TDA by 1.85%, with the largest single-dataset gain on ImageNet-Sketch (54.39% vs. 53.20% for DMN). Notably, MCP without residual learning already exceeds all prior cache-based methods, indicating the multi-cache design (entropy + align + negative) is the primary driver of improvement, while MCP++'s residual fine-tuning adds a further ~0.6% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cache-based test-time adaptation line initiated by TDA (Karmanov et al., WACV 2024), which selects low-entropy samples to build dynamic caches for VLMs. It departs from the single-entropy-criterion paradigm by introducing a three-cache architecture (entropy, align, negative) and, in MCP++, incorporates cross-modal prototype alignment with learnable residuals inspired by DPE's (Zhang et al., 2024) multimodal residual tuning and TPT's (Shu et al., 2022) entropy minimization loss. The key conceptual departure is the empirical demonstration that cache-based TTA performance correlates positively with intra-class compactness, motivating the align cache's distance-to-prototype admission criterion as a complement to pure entropy-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-cache mechanism offers a practical, training-free (MCP) or lightweight (MCP++) recipe for improving zero-shot VLM generalization under distribution shift, with consistent gains across 15 tasks. The compactness-correlation finding provides a diagnostic lens for understanding when and why cache-based adaptation succeeds or fails, which could guide future cache design beyond entropy. Open questions include whether the intra-class compactness principle transfers to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the reflecting mechanism for the negative cache scales to very large class counts, and whether the one-step gradient update in MCP++ is sufficient for severe domain shifts or requires iterative refinement.
