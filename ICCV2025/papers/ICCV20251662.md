## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction pipelines based on NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting cannot directly process large-FOV fisheye images because they assume perspective projection, forcing a re-projection step that introduces severe peripheral stretching and non-uniform sampling. Traditional parametric distortion models (e.g., Brown-Conrady, equidistant) lack the expressiveness to accurately fit real fisheye lenses at extreme angles, and pre-calibration decouples lens parameters from the reconstruction objective, preventing end-to-end optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251662/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, uncalibrated wide-angle or fisheye photographs (up to 180° FOV) directly, without any pre-calibration, undistortion, or perspective re-projection. It jointly produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation, a continuous lens distortion field, and optimized camera intrinsics and extrinsics, from which novel view renderings and full panoramas can be generated. The photometric loss is computed directly against the original distorted input pixels rather than against rectified or cropped versions.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid distortion field that uses an invertible ResNet (iResNet) to predict displacement vectors on a sparse grid of fixed control points, followed by bilinear interpolation to yield a continuous, differentiable distortion mapping; this combines the inductive bias and smoothness regularization of iResNet with the computational efficiency of grid-based lookup, avoiding the out-of-memory issue of applying iResNet per-Gaussian. To handle extreme FOV without peripheral stretching, the pipeline replaces single-plane perspective projection with a cubemap representation (six 90° faces), where Gaussians are rasterized per-face and a differentiable resampling step maps each output pixel back through the distortion field to the appropriate cubemap face. A distance-from-camera-center sorting strategy replaces the standard depth-based ordering to maintain consistent alpha-blending across cubemap face boundaries. All camera parameters (intrinsics, extrinsics), the distortion field, and the 3D Gaussian scene are optimized end-to-end via L1 + DSSIM photometric loss with native CUDA gradient kernels, eliminating the need for separate calibration or COLMAP-based undistortion.

## Results

On the FisheyeNeRF benchmark (6 real-world scenes, ~120° FOV), the proposed method achieves the best PSNR across all scenes, ranging from 22.01 (Flowers) to 24.88 (Rock), outperforming the strongest baseline Fisheye-GS by 0.7–2.5 dB. SSIM and LPIPS improvements are consistent: e.g., on Rock, Ours reaches SSIM 0.787 / LPIPS 0.145 versus Fisheye-GS's 0.697 / 0.177. On the Mitsuba synthetic 180° FOV scenes, the method with only 100 fisheye views (PSNR 29.01) surpasses vanilla 3DGS with 200 perspective views (PSNR 19.08), and even with just 10 views (PSNR 22.77) it still beats the 200-view baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all six FisheyeNeRF scenes against every baseline. Against the closest competitor Fisheye-GS [45], gains range from +0.7 dB (Flowers: 22.01 vs 20.23) to +2.5 dB (Rock: 24.88 vs 22.38) in PSNR, with the largest relative improvements in peripheral-heavy scenes. ADOP-GS [54] is outperformed by 0.9–2.4 dB in PSNR (e.g., Chairs: 23.45 vs 22.59; Rock: 24.88 vs 22.48). The two 3DGS variants (perspective-only and COLMAP-undistorted) are substantially weaker, trailing by 5–11 dB in PSNR, confirming that both the hybrid distortion field and the cubemap projection are necessary. On the Mitsuba 180° FOV synthetic benchmark, the method with 100 fisheye views (PSNR 29.01) exceeds vanilla 3DGS with 200 perspective views (PSNR 19.08) by nearly 10 dB, and even at 10 views (PSNR 22.77) it still surpasses the 200-view baseline, demonstrating that wide-FOV captures with self-calibration can replace many narrow-FOV shots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to handle uncalibrated wide-angle and fisheye cameras by replacing the standard pinhole projection with a cubemap-based rendering pipeline and a hybrid distortion field. The distortion model combines an invertible ResNet (following NeuroLens) with a sparse control-point grid, borrowing the inductive bias of iResNet while avoiding the per-Gaussian memory cost that makes direct iResNet application infeasible in 3DGS. The cubemap resampling strategy draws on the hemicube/environment-map tradition in computer graphics, and the end-to-end self-calibration of intrinsics, extrinsics, and distortion unifies bundle adjustment with scene optimization in a single differentiable pipeline, departing from prior methods (FisheyeGS, ADOP-GS, SCNeRF) that rely on parametric distortion models or require separate pre-calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for checkerboard calibration and peripheral cropping, this work makes large-FOV cameras (150°–180°) practically usable in 3DGS-based reconstruction pipelines, which is directly relevant to robotics, VR capture, and autonomous driving where wide-angle lenses are standard. The demonstration that 10 wide-angle views can outperform 200 perspective views highlights a significant data-efficiency gain for scene capture. Open questions include how the method scales to very large outdoor scenes, whether the cubemap face-boundary artifacts from the affine covariance approximation can be fully resolved, and whether the approach extends naturally to true 360° omnidirectional capture without stitching.
