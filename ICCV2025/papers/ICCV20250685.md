## Problem

Existing learned world models operate in 2D pixel space, producing incomplete spatial representations with inconsistent object geometry across time steps and no explicit depth or 6-DoF pose information. This limits their utility for robotic manipulation tasks that require precise 3D spatial reasoning, such as grasping or tool use. The paper asks whether a 4D embodied world model that simulates the dynamic evolution of 3D scenes can be learned efficiently and used to improve downstream robotic planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250685/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a text instruction formatted as '[action instruction] + [robot arm name]' (e.g., 'pick up apple google robot') and generates a 49-frame RGB-DN video at 512×512 resolution. The generated depth and normal maps are then refined via normal integration and optical-flow-based temporal consistency optimization to produce a 4D point cloud scene. For embodied action planning, a separate inverse dynamics model (PointNet + MLP) consumes the current and predicted future 4D point clouds along with the text instruction to output a 7-DoF robot action.

## Method

TesserAct fine-tunes CogVideoX's latent diffusion architecture (3D VAE + DiT backbone) to jointly denoise RGB, depth, and normal latents in a single forward pass. Three modality-specific input projectors encode each channel's noisy latent and conditioning frame, whose embeddings are summed and fed to the DiT conditioned on text and timestep; depth and normal outputs are produced by additional Conv3D + projection modules that also receive the predicted RGB denoised output, initialized to zero to preserve pretrained RGB generation. The model is trained on ~200k RGB-DN videos combining synthetic RLBench data (ground-truth depth, DSINE normals) and real-world data (RollingDepth + Marigold-LCM annotations). For 4D reconstruction, the paper introduces two novel losses: a temporal consistency loss that warps the previous frame's refined depth via RAFT optical flow and penalizes mismatches in dynamic and background regions, and a spatial regularization loss that anchors the optimized depth to the generated depth map, both combined with a normal-integration spatial loss for per-frame geometric accuracy.

## Results

On the 4D scene generation task, TesserAct achieves the lowest Point Cloud Chamfer L1 distance in both domains: 0.2030 on real data (vs. 0.2191 for CogVideoX and 0.3013 for OpenSora) and 0.0811 on synthetic data (vs. 0.2884 for CogVideoX and 0.2570 for OpenSora). TesserAct also produces the most accurate depth maps (AbsRel 22.07 real / 16.02 synthetic) and normal maps (Mean 15.74 real / 14.75 synthetic) while maintaining competitive RGB video quality (FVD 21.59 real / 40.01 synthetic). For embodied action planning on 9 RLBench tasks, TesserAct achieves an average success rate of ~65.4%, substantially outperforming Image-BC (~14.4%) and UniPi* (~61.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CogVideoX (fine-tuned without depth/normal annotations), TesserAct wins decisively on depth (AbsRel 22.07 vs 26.17 real; 16.02 vs 19.81 synthetic), normal (Mean 15.74 vs 19.53 real), and final 3D reconstruction (Chamfer 0.2030 vs 0.2191 real; 0.0811 vs 0.2884 synthetic), while CogVideoX retains a slight edge on RGB FVD (20.64 vs 21.59) and PSNR (22.39 vs 20.27) in the real domain. Against OpenSora, TesserAct dominates on depth, normal, and Chamfer distance in both domains (e.g., Chamfer 0.2030 vs 0.3013 real; 0.0811 vs 0.2570 synthetic). The 4D Point-E baseline, which directly generates point clouds, is outperformed by TesserAct on Chamfer L1 in both domains (0.2030 vs 0.2211 real; 0.0811 vs 0.1086 synthetic), and is limited to only 4 frames of 8192 points due to computational cost. For action planning, TesserAct outperforms UniPi* on 7 of 9 RLBench tasks (e.g., close box 88% vs 81%, open drawer 80% vs 67%) but loses on open microwave (70% vs 72%) and weighing off (62% vs 68%). TesserAct also surpasses the Shape of Motion (SoM) novel-view-synthesis baseline on all metrics (PSNR 12.99 vs 10.94, SSIM 42.62 vs 24.02, CLIP Score 83.02 vs 66.67) while running ~120× faster (~1 min vs ~2 hours).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of video-based world models (UniPi, Genie, CogVideoX) from 2D pixel-space prediction to a 4D embodied setting by jointly generating RGB, depth, and normal (RGB-DN) videos as an intermediate representation. Rather than adopting explicit 3D representations (NeRFs, Gaussian splatting, point clouds) used in prior 4D generation work (4D-Fy, DreamGaussian4D, 4D Point-E), it repurposes the architecture of a pretrained video diffusion model (CogVideoX) with lightweight modality-specific projectors, making the 4D prediction problem tractable at scale. The 4D reconstruction pipeline draws on classical normal-integration techniques (Bilateral Normal Integration, ECON) and introduces two novel loss terms (temporal consistency via optical flow and depth regularization) to enforce spatio-temporal coherence, departing from frame-by-frame optimization in prior depth refinement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a compact RGB-DN video representation can outperform both post-hoc depth/normal estimation from 2D video models and explicit 3D point-cloud diffusion for 4D scene reconstruction, this work offers a practical pathway to geometrically grounded world models for robotics without the computational burden of full 3D generation. The demonstrated improvements in action planning (up to 88% success on close-box vs. 81% for UniPi*) and the ~120× speedup in novel view synthesis (~1 min vs. ~2 hours) suggest broad applicability to offline policy training and imagined-rollout planning. Key limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf depth/normal estimators for real data (introducing annotation noise), a relatively small training set (~200k clips), and evaluation confined to tabletop manipulation; open questions include scaling to open-world scenes, real-time inference, and whether the RGB-DN representation remains sufficient for deformable or articulated objects.
