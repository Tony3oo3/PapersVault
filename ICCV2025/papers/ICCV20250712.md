## Problem

Existing facial attractiveness prediction (FAP) datasets are either small-scale or closed-source, and none specifically capture the data distribution of live video faces that have been processed by real-time retouching algorithms. Consequently, FAP models trained on generic or well-edited face images exhibit limited generalization and adaptation in practical live-streaming scenarios where retouching intensity varies with computational resources.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped face image (bounding-box region extracted from a live video frame, potentially with real-time retouching applied) and outputs a continuous attractiveness score on a 1–5 MOS scale. Internally, the image is paired with five fixed text templates ("a photo of a person with {bad/poor/fair/good/perfect} attractiveness") to form a multi-modal representation, but the user-facing interface requires only the face image as input.

## Method

FPEM employs a two-stage training strategy with three modules. In Stage 1, the Personalized Attractiveness Prior Module (PAPM) fuses multi-scale Swin Transformer features with frozen FaceNet embeddings via Cross-Attention to produce a personalized attractiveness feature, while the Multi-modal Attractiveness Encoder Module (MAEM) uses a CLIP-style architecture (ViT-B/16 + GPT-2) with Similarity Regression—computing cosine similarity between visual embeddings and five text-level embeddings, then taking the expectation over the softmax distribution—to predict a preliminary score. In Stage 2, both modules are frozen and a Selective Fusion Module (SFM) adaptively re-weights the visual and prior features channel-wise using a squeeze-and-excitation mechanism, then feeds the refined visual embedding back into the Similarity Regression head for the final score. Training uses L1 loss for PAPM and SFM, and a combined L1 + fidelity ranking + two-direction ranking loss for MAEM to jointly optimize regression and ordinal consistency.

## Results

FPEM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark datasets. On LiveBeauty, it reaches SROCC 0.924, PLCC 0.896, and KROCC 0.773; on SCUT-FBP5500, SROCC 0.930, PLCC 0.932, KROCC 0.774; and on MEBeauty, SROCC 0.783, PLCC 0.795, KROCC 0.593. The most substantial gains appear on MEBeauty, where FPEM outperforms the second-best method (CNN-ER) by +7.7% SROCC and +7.4% KROCC, attributed to the face-prior enhancement being particularly effective under limited training data and high ethnic diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPEM outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is CNN-ER (ensemble of ResNeXt-50 and Inception-V3 with dynamic robust losses), which FPEM surpasses by +1.1% SROCC on LiveBeauty, +7.7% SROCC on MEBeauty, and +2.0% SROCC on SCUT-FBP5500. Among IAA methods (AVA-MLSP, TANet, Dele-Trans, EAT), the best performer EAT trails FPEM by 3.1% SROCC on LiveBeauty and 12.7% on MEBeauty, confirming that generic aesthetic models underperform on face-specific attractiveness. The MEBeauty gap is the largest because that dataset has only 2,550 images and high ethnic diversity, where FPEM's face-prior module (FaceNet features via cross-attention) provides a stronger inductive bias. In the cross-dataset study (trained on LiveBeauty, tested on full MEBeauty/SCUT-FBP5500), FPEM again leads all methods, achieving SROCC 0.630 on MEBeauty and 0.599 on SCUT-FBP5500, further validating generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FAP lineage from CNN-based methods (ComboNet, 2D-FAP, REX-INCEP) and early transformer approaches (FBPFormer) by introducing a multi-modal architecture that repurposes CLIP's vision-language alignment for attractiveness regression via a Similarity Regression strategy. It departs from prior FAP work that uses simplistic feature concatenation for geometric priors by instead integrating FaceNet embeddings through cross-attention with Swin Transformer multi-scale features, and by adding a Selective Fusion Module inspired by Selective Kernel Networks. The LiveBeauty dataset itself represents a new data domain—faces processed by real-time retouching in live streaming—that prior FAP benchmarks (SCUT-FBP5500, MEBeauty) do not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiveBeauty fills a practical gap by providing the first large-scale open-source FAP benchmark where faces have been algorithmically beautified, which is the actual operating condition for live-streaming platforms. The notably larger improvement on MEBeauty (+7.7% SROCC) versus LiveBeauty (+1.1%) suggests face priors are most valuable under data scarcity and demographic diversity, a useful signal for practitioners with limited training data. However, the dataset is restricted to Asian ethnicity and a single platform, and the 20-annotator protocol limits the robustness of the MOS labels; generalization to other ethnicities, retouching styles, and platforms remains an open question.
