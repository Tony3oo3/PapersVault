## Problem

Variational Score Distillation (VSD) for text-to-3D generation suffers from slow and sometimes ill-posed convergence in practice. The paper identifies the root cause as a mismatching problem: the LoRA score model is updated after the 3D model, so it characterizes the distribution of the *previous* 3D state rather than the current one, yielding unreliable gradient corrections. Naively fixing the order (lookahead) introduces a new instability—the LoRA overfits to a single 3D particle and sampled view, causing over-saturation and geometric collapse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D NeRF (geometry plus texture) optimized via differentiable rendering from randomly sampled camera angles. It operates at both high resolution (256×256) and low resolution (64×64), with the low-resolution setting already yielding realistic results in the first optimization stage. The pretrained 2D diffusion prior (SDXL) is frozen; only the LoRA adapter and the NeRF parameters are optimized, and the entire pipeline runs within the threestudio framework.

## Method

L²-VSD (Linearized Lookahead VSD) corrects VSD by (1) swapping the optimization order so the LoRA model looks ahead to the current 3D state before computing the distillation gradient, and (2) Taylor-expanding the updated LoRA prediction to isolate a first-order correction term Δε_first from high-order residuals Δε_high. Empirical analysis shows Δε_first encodes coherent semantic shape information while Δε_high is dominated by high-frequency noise, so L²-VSD retains only the linearized term for gradient correction, which inherently prevents the overfitting that plagues full lookahead. The linearized term is computed as a vector-Jacobian product via a single forward-mode autodiff pass of the LoRA model, adding only one extra forward pass per iteration over vanilla VSD. An optional last-layer approximation further reduces cost by restricting the Jacobian computation to the final LoRA layer, trading minor quality loss for significant speedup.

## Results

On 20 DreamFusion gallery prompts, L²-VSD achieves the best CLIP similarity angle (0.285°) and FID (284.06) among all compared methods. The naive lookahead variant L-VSD, while improving over VSD qualitatively, suffers severe instability with the worst FID (496) and CLIP angle (0.337°), confirming the overfitting risk. L²-VSD outperforms the strongest baseline HiFA by 2.9° in CLIP angle and 8.82 in FID, and surpasses the original VSD by 3.9° and 17.48 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L²-VSD outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP similarity angle and FID. Against SDS (the original score distillation method), L²-VSD improves CLIP angle by 0.020° and FID by 88.29, reflecting substantially better text alignment and image quality. Against VSD (the direct predecessor), the gains are 0.039° in CLIP angle and 17.48 in FID, validating that the linearized lookahead correction addresses VSD's mismatching problem. Against ESD (which tackles the Janus problem via entropy maximization), L²-VSD leads by 0.031° and 31.09 in FID. Against HiFA (the strongest SOTA baseline combining image-space and latent-space distillation), L²-VSD still wins by 0.028° in CLIP angle and 8.82 in FID, though the margin is smaller. Notably, the paper also demonstrates that L²-VSD can be combined with ESD and HiFA for further gains, suggesting orthogonality with these techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Variational Score Distillation (VSD) [29], which itself extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) [21] by introducing a LoRA-adapted score model to correct the distillation gradient. The key departure is a diagnostic finding: the practical VSD implementation has an optimization-order mismatch where the LoRA model characterizes the previous 3D state rather than the current one, violating the theoretical assumptions of [29]. Rather than simply reordering the updates (which causes overfitting instability), the paper linearizes the lookahead correction via a first-order Taylor expansion, retaining only the semantically meaningful term and discarding the high-order noise. The method is explicitly designed as a drop-in module compatible with other VSD-based improvements like ESD [28] and HiFA [37].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, actionable diagnosis of why VSD's practical performance falls short of its theory, and the linearized lookahead fix is computationally cheap (one extra forward pass via forward-mode autodiff), making it easy to adopt in existing pipelines. However, the generation process still requires hours of optimization, and the authors acknowledge they have not yet formulated a principled distribution-based objective underlying the linearized correction, leaving the theoretical grounding incomplete. An open question is the connection to Score Identity Distillation (SiD) [36], which the authors note shares structural similarities but has not been formally explored. The method's plug-and-play nature means it could accelerate progress across the broader VSD-based text-to-3D ecosystem.
