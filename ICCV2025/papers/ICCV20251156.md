## Problem

Most LDM-based outpainting methods impose fixed resolution and aspect-ratio constraints, causing loss of local detail and blurring. While progressive (incremental) outpainting removes these constraints, naive progressive approaches fail to capture global composition context and produce unnatural patterns—issues that are especially damaging for artwork, where artists deliberately pre-design the overall layout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251156/cropped_page0_idx13.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an artwork image of arbitrary aspect ratio and a global text prompt (auto-generated by BLIP or user-provided) as input, and produces a high-resolution outpainted image with no fixed size or aspect-ratio restriction. At each of the 8 progressive steps, the model consumes a local target-window crop, a binary fill mask, a resized hint image of the current intermediate outpainted state, and a location mask indicating where the target window sits within the global canvas. The output is composited back into the growing full image, so the final result can be substantially larger than the input in both dimensions.

## Method

The core contribution is two Composition Planning Modules (CPMs) attached to a frozen Stable Diffusion inpainting backbone, enabling globally-aware progressive outpainting without retraining the base model. The ControlNet-based CPM duplicates the SD control encoder, encodes the hint image and location mask through shallow convolutional layers, and adds the resulting control signal to the frozen U-Net pipeline. The Fusion-based CPM instead uses location-aware patch embeddings (separate positional encodings for the global hint and local target window), a cross-attention operation to fuse local and global context, and concatenation with the current noisy latent representation before encoding through residual blocks to produce timestep-aware control signals. Training updates only the CPM parameters under a standard diffusion denoising loss, with a data augmentation pipeline that simulates arbitrary progressive-outpainting steps by cropping the full image and randomly attaching patches around erased edges.

## Results

On the IconArt dataset (6,528 images), the Fusion-based CPM achieves the best FID of 21.91, pFID-256 of 19.45, pFID-512 of 15.18, CLIP text similarity of 0.293, and CLIP aesthetic score of 6.964. The ControlNet-based CPM follows closely with FID 24.38 and the highest CLIP-AES of 6.997. Both variants substantially outperform the single-step baselines (QueryOTR, PQDiff) and the SD Inpainting baseline without composition planning, with the largest gains in FID (21.91 vs. 30.82 for SD Inpainting) and CLIP text similarity (0.293 vs. 0.278).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QueryOTR (ECCV 2022), the Fusion-based CPM improves FID from 32.51 to 21.91 (−33%), CLIP-S from 0.282 to 0.293, and CLIP-AES from 5.862 to 6.964, while also achieving dramatically lower pFID scores (19.45 vs. 93.99 at 256 px), indicating far sharper local detail. Against PQDiff (ICLR 2024), the gains are even larger: FID drops from 36.25 (gen) / 28.33 (copy) to 21.91, and CLIP-AES rises from 4.605 / 5.677 to 6.964, showing that the progressive approach with composition planning avoids the structural inconsistencies and style breaks of single-step methods. Against the SD Inpainting baseline (the same frozen backbone without CPM), the Fusion variant reduces FID by 28.8% (30.82 → 21.91) and improves CLIP-S by 5.0% (0.278 → 0.293), confirming that the composition planning modules are the source of the quality gain rather than the progressive schedule alone. The ControlNet variant edges out the Fusion variant on CLIP-AES (6.997 vs. 6.964) but trails on all FID and CLIP-S metrics, suggesting the attention-based fusion better preserves global structural coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion inpainting architecture as a frozen generation backbone and adapts two established conditioning frameworks—ControlNet and T2I-Adapter—into Composition Planning Modules (CPMs) that inject global compositional context into each progressive outpainting step. It departs from prior single-step outpainting methods (QueryOTR, PQDiff, VIP) that are constrained to fixed window sizes and aspect ratios by introducing a multi-step, window-by-window expansion strategy, positioning itself as the first to combine progressive outpainting with pre-trained LDMs at arbitrary resolutions. The fusion-based CPM further extends the ControlNet paradigm by incorporating location-aware patch embeddings and cross-attention to explicitly encode where the current local window sits within the global canvas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive formulation removes the resolution and aspect-ratio bottlenecks that plague single-step outpainting, making it practically relevant for high-fidelity applications such as immersive art exhibitions where original aspect ratios must be preserved. However, the multi-step nature introduces two open problems the authors acknowledge: error propagation across windows (a single failed generation can corrupt the entire composition) and increased computational cost from repeated inference. Whether the composition planning modules generalize beyond the artwork domain to natural scenes or architectural imagery, and whether error-correction or rollback mechanisms can be integrated into the progressive loop, remain important directions for future work.
