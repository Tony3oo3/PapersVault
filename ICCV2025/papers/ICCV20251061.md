## Problem

Previous motion forecasting methods introduce future trajectories only after the scene encoding stage, resulting in separate optimization of historical and future states that can produce unrealistic predictions. Additionally, the widely adopted transformer architecture for spatiotemporal modeling scales quadratically with sequence length, creating efficiency challenges in dense traffic scenarios with many interacting agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes agent historical trajectories (sequences of 2D positions over T_h timesteps, with the focal agent as the 0-th agent) and a vectorized lane map (N_l lane segments, each represented by N_pt uniformly sampled 2D points along its centerline). It outputs K potential future trajectories, each consisting of T_f 2D position predictions, along with a probability score per trajectory. On Argoverse 2, T_h=50 and T_f=60 at 10 Hz; on Argoverse 1, T_h=20 and T_f=30 at 10 Hz.

## Method

FINet models potential future trajectories in advance from the focal agent's current motion state, K learnable intention tokens, and an inductive bias, then concatenates them with scene tokens before spatial interaction, enabling joint optimization P(T^fut, ST) rather than the conditional P(T^fut|ST) used by prior methods. Mamba (a selective state space model) replaces transformers for both spatial and temporal processing, achieving linear complexity. To adapt Mamba for unordered spatial interactions, an Adaptive Reorder Strategy sorts scene tokens by distance to a learnable reference point predicted from the focal agent's state, with the focal agent token placed last for maximum influence. The Temporal Enhanced Decoder extends each future trajectory token into a sequential format via interpolation from the current state, then applies cross-attention to aggregate scene information followed by bidirectional Mamba blocks for temporal refinement. Training uses smooth L1 trajectory loss and cross-entropy score loss with a winner-take-all strategy, supplemented by auxiliary losses on intermediate decoder outputs and alignment of the second reference point to the ground-truth trajectory endpoint.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test set, FINet achieves the best minFDE6 (1.27 m), MR6 (0.15), minADE1 (1.60 m), minFDE1 (4.02 m), and MR1 (0.57), while matching or closely trailing the best on minADE6 (0.66 m vs. QCNet's 0.65 m). On Argoverse 1, FINet achieves the best minADE6 (0.59 m, a ~10% improvement over the previous best of 0.66 m) and ties for best minFDE6 (0.95 m). In terms of efficiency, FINet reduces FLOPs by 95% (1.47G vs. 28.0G), latency by 68% (17.72 ms vs. 54.55 ms), model size by 52% (3.7M vs. 7.7M), and GPU memory by 81% (0.55G vs. 2.92G) compared to the transformer-based QCNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest transformer-based baseline QCNet, FINet trades a marginal 0.01 m on minADE6 (0.66 vs. 0.65) for clear wins on minFDE6 (1.27 vs. 1.29), MR6 (0.15 vs. 0.16), minADE1 (1.60 vs. 1.69, ~5% improvement), minFDE1 (4.02 vs. 4.30, ~6% improvement), and MR1 (0.57 vs. 0.59), while using 95% fewer FLOPs and 81% less GPU memory. Against ProphNet, which leads on b-minFDE6 (1.88 vs. 1.93), FINet wins on minFDE6 (1.27 vs. 1.32, ~4% improvement) and MR6 (0.15 vs. 0.18). On Argoverse 1, FINet achieves a ~10% minADE6 improvement (0.59 vs. 0.66) over the previous best methods (ADAPT, SIMPL, HiVT, R-Pred) while tying for best minFDE6 (0.95 m). The paper also notes that K=1 improvements are larger than K=6 improvements, suggesting the interaction-based design particularly benefits single-trajectory accuracy through more diverse and well-scored multi-modal predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINet departs from the dominant transformer-based paradigm in motion forecasting (QCNet, HiVT, MTR) by replacing quadratic-complexity attention with Mamba's linear-complexity state space model for both spatial and temporal modeling. Conceptually, it introduces an 'interaction-based' formulation where potential future trajectories are modeled *before* and jointly with scene encoding, in contrast to prior MLP-based or Query-based methods that introduce future trajectories only after scene encoding is complete. The Adaptive Reorder Strategy is a novel mechanism to bridge Mamba's sequential inductive bias with unordered spatial scene elements, and the Temporal Enhanced Decoder extends Mamba into the trajectory refinement stage for temporal consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% FLOPs reduction and 81% GPU memory reduction relative to transformer-based baselines while maintaining or improving accuracy make this architecture highly relevant for real-time autonomous driving deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The interaction-based paradigm—jointly optimizing current and future states during scene encoding—offers a principled alternative to the sequential encode-then-predict pipeline, though the accuracy gains over strong baselines like QCNet remain modest. Open questions include whether the Adaptive Reorder Strategy generalizes to scenarios with very large agent counts, whether Mamba's sequential processing truly captures spatial relationships as effectively as full attention, and whether the efficiency-accuracy trade-off holds under different hardware (e.g., edge NPU vs. GPU).
