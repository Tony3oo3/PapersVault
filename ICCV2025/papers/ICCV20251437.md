## Problem

Converting raw, colorless 3D point clouds into high-fidelity 3D Gaussian representations with controllable appearance remains unsolved. Existing point-to-Gaussian methods either require pre-colored point clouds as input or produce limited, non-diverse appearances, while mesh-based texture generation suffers from UV unwrapping artifacts and resolution constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw point cloud (100K points for objects, 500K for scenes) with no color attributes, plus a free-form text prompt, and produces a set of 2D Gaussian disk primitives (2DGS) with position, orientation, scale, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients. Inputs can be synthetic point clouds sampled from meshes or real-world partial scans from depth sensors and LiDAR, making the method applicable to both complete and incomplete geometry.

## Method

GAP initializes 2D Gaussian disks at point cloud positions with orientations derived from CAP-UDF normals, then progressively optimizes them across a sequence of viewpoints. At each view, a depth-aware inpainting diffusion model (ControlNet conditioned on rendered depth and mask) synthesizes the target appearance, with a dynamic three-region mask scheme (generate/keep/update) that refines previously processed areas when better viewing angles become available. Per-view Gaussian optimization uses a single-pass scheme with three losses: a rendering loss (L1 + D-SSIM), a surface-anchoring distance loss that constrains Gaussian centers to the UDF zero-level set, and a scale loss preventing oversized primitives. For regions never observed from any viewpoint, a diffuse-based 3D inpainting propagates color, scale, and opacity from visible Gaussians using weights based on spatial proximity, normal alignment, and opacity reliability.

## Results

On the Objaverse subset (410 objects, 100K point cloud input), GAP achieves FID 40.39, KID 5.28, and CLIP Score 27.26, outperforming the best mesh-based baseline SyncMVD (FID 40.85, KID 5.77, CLIP 27.24) on all three metrics. In the user study (30 participants, 1–5 scale), GAP scores 4.21 for overall quality and 4.47 for text fidelity, substantially ahead of the next-best Text2Tex (3.48 / 3.62). When baselines are forced to use reconstructed meshes from the same point clouds (BPA or CAP-UDF), their FID degrades to 52–65, highlighting GAP's advantage of bypassing UV parameterization entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP outperforms all four mesh-based texture generation baselines (TexTure, Text2Tex, SyncMVD, Paint3D) on every metric in Table 1, with the closest competitor being SyncMVD (FID 40.85 vs. 40.39; KID 5.77 vs. 5.28). The advantage is most dramatic in the user study, where GAP's overall quality score of 4.21 exceeds the next-best Text2Tex (3.48) by 0.73 points. When baselines are re-run on meshes reconstructed from the same point clouds via BPA or CAP-UDF, their FID scores jump to 52–65, confirming that mesh reconstruction and UV unwrapping introduce severe artifacts that GAP avoids by operating directly in 3D Gaussian space. For point-to-Gaussian generation on DeepFashion3D (Fig. 6), GAP produces more geometrically accurate and visually appealing results than DreamGaussian (which suffers from over-saturation), TriplaneGaussian, and DiffGS (both limited by low-resolution triplane representations). At scene level on 3D-FRONT, GAP surpasses Paint3D (which fails entirely) and SceneTex (which requires additional VSD + LoRA training), while on real-world partial scans from SRB and DeepFashion3D, GAP robustly completes occluded regions without requiring complete geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAP builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting as its geometric representation and CAP-UDF for learning unsigned distance fields from raw point clouds, but departs from prior point-to-Gaussian approaches (Large Point-to-Gaussian, DiffGS) that either require pre-colored point clouds or rely on limited-resolution triplane representations. Rather than using Score Distillation Sampling (as in DreamGaussian) or feedforward generation, GAP introduces a multi-view progressive optimization loop driven by a depth-aware inpainting diffusion model (ControlNet), treating each viewpoint as a single-pass fitting problem. The surface-anchoring mechanism via UDF distance loss and the 3D-space diffuse-based Gaussian inpainting for unseen regions are novel geometric constraints not present in prior 3DGS generation or texture synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GAP addresses a practical gap: point clouds are the most accessible 3D capture format (LiDAR, depth cameras), yet converting them into high-quality renderable representations has been limited by UV parameterization artifacts in mesh-based pipelines. By operating directly in 3D Gaussian space, the method sidesteps UV unwrapping entirely and scales from objects to large indoor scenes. Open questions include whether the per-viewpoint optimization can be made feedforward for real-time use, how the method behaves on extremely sparse or noisy scans, and whether the text-guided appearance can be extended to image- or video-conditioned generation without the current multi-view inpainting overhead.
