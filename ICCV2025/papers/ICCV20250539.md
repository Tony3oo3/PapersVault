## Problem

Existing music-to-dance methods either generate 3D poses constrained by small multi-view datasets (e.g., AIST++) that exclude head and hand motions, or perform end-to-end audio-to-video synthesis that struggles with long-range full-body kinematics and has been validated primarily on talking heads. No prior framework generates diverse, expressive, full-body dance videos from a single image and music track in 2D space while capturing fine-grained motions and maintaining temporal consistency over extended sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250539/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static human reference image (providing identity, body shape, and background) and a music track as input, and produces a high-resolution dance video at 896×512 resolution and 30 fps. The generated video preserves the reference person's appearance and scene context while animating the full body—including head tilts, hand gestures, and large-scale actions like jumps and rotations—in synchronization with the music's rhythm and style. Extended sequences are generated in 64-frame sliding segments with 12-frame overlap for smooth transitions.

## Method

X-Dancer is a two-stage transformer-diffusion pipeline. First, a compositional multi-part VQ-VAE encodes 2D whole-body poses (60 joints with keypoint confidences) into 5 independent body-part token streams (upper body, lower body, left hand, right hand, head), each using 6 tokens from a 512-entry codebook, enabling fine-grained motion capture. A GPT-based autoregressive transformer then predicts future pose tokens conditioned on per-frame Jukebox/Librosa music embeddings, a global music-style context, and cross-segment motion context, with cross-conditioning across body parts to ensure whole-body coherence. Second, a diffusion model (SD1.5 backbone + AnimateDiff temporal modules + ReferenceNet for identity) animates the reference image using a trainable motion decoder that implicitly translates 1D pose tokens into multi-scale 2D spatial guidance via AdaIN, avoiding the non-differentiable skeleton-map visualization and preserving keypoint confidence information for robust handling of motion blur and occlusions.

## Results

On the In-House dataset, X-Dancer achieves the best motion fidelity (FVD 238.22) and competitive diversity (DIV 28.08) among all methods, while X-Dancer-AIST achieves the highest Beat Alignment Score (BAS 0.26 on AIST++). For video synthesis on the In-House dataset, X-Dancer attains the best FVD (507.06), FID-VID (61.94), and identity preservation (ID-SIM 0.5317), substantially outperforming Hallo (FVD 609.08, ID-SIM 0.4870), Bailando+PG (FVD 583.26, ID-SIM 0.3392), and EDGE+PG (FVD 613.81, ID-SIM 0.3034). The method also demonstrates strong scalability, with performance improving as model size increases from GPT-small (117M) to GPT-medium (345M) and data scales from 10k to 100k videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Dancer outperforms all three baselines on motion fidelity (FVD) across both datasets, achieving 238.22 on In-House versus 249.12 (Hallo), 534.02 (Bailando), and 303.36 (EDGE). On the AIST++ dataset, X-Dancer-AIST matches EDGE's BAS (0.26) while achieving a lower FVD (620.73 vs. 639.46), and the full X-Dancer model still significantly outperforms Bailando and Hallo on beat alignment. For video synthesis, X-Dancer achieves the best FID-VID (61.94) and ID-SIM (0.5317), with a notable 10+ point gap in identity preservation over Bailando+PG (0.3392) and EDGE+PG (0.3034), attributed to the implicit motion token decoder preserving body shape better than explicit skeleton-based pose guiders. Hallo, while competitive on diversity (DIV 28.98), produces noisy video outputs that lead to jittery skeleton motions after pose projection, inflating its diversity score artificially. Bailando and EDGE, trained on the limited AIST++ 3D dataset, generate less diverse motions and lack head/hand modeling, resulting in more rigid dance outputs compared to X-Dancer's 2D multi-part tokenization.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Dancer sits at the intersection of three research threads: music-to-dance motion generation (Bailando, EDGE), which operates in 3D space on limited multi-view datasets; human image animation (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate), which requires a driving video rather than generating motion from scratch; and audio-driven portrait animation (Hallo, EMO), which handles only head-level motion. The key architectural departure is replacing 3D skeleton generation with 2D compositional pose tokenization (VQ-VAE with per-body-part codebooks and keypoint confidence) fed into a GPT-style autoregressive transformer, whose output tokens are then decoded into spatial guidance for a Stable Diffusion + AnimateDiff backbone via AdaIN rather than explicit skeleton maps. This positions the work as a unification of long-context autoregressive sequence modeling and high-fidelity diffusion-based visual synthesis into a single end-to-end pipeline for a task neither thread had addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 2D monocular dance videos (76k clips) can rival or exceed 3D multi-view datasets (AIST++) for music-aligned motion generation, the paper challenges the prevailing assumption that 3D pose is necessary for expressive dance synthesis, opening a much larger and more diverse data regime. The implicit motion token decoder's advantage over explicit pose guiders in identity preservation and jitter reduction is a practical design insight for future pose-conditioned video generation. However, the staged training pipeline (VQ-VAE → GPT → diffusion) is a current limitation rather than a true end-to-end system, and the model's reliance on curated daily-dance data means out-of-domain generalization (stylized characters, professional choreography) remains an open question. Scaling to multi-machine end-to-end training and extending to longer sequences beyond the 64-frame autoregressive window are natural next steps.
