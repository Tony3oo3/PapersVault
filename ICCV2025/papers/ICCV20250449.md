## Problem

One-shot portrait animation methods face a persistent trade-off: sparse-representation approaches (e.g., LivePortrait, Face vid2vid) achieve geometric stability but lack precise local facial control, while dense 3D-feature methods (e.g., Real3DPortrait using 3DMM) enable flexible editing but suffer from limited granularity and facial distortions. No existing one-shot framework simultaneously delivers high rendering fidelity, flexible multi-level editing (global pose, local expression, outline shape), and the interpretability of traditional rendering pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static RGB portrait image and a driving video (or reference image), along with detected camera parameters (rotation R and translation t) and 1,107 dense facial keypoints per frame. The keypoints are converted into Gaussian-like heatmap features with a learnable spread parameter δ before being fed to the network. Output is a rendered head avatar image at the target pose and expression; when applied sequentially to each driving frame, the outputs are reassembled into an animated portrait video. Editing is performed by directly manipulating the skeleton (dragging keypoints for translation/zoom, rotating R for Euler angles, or adjusting local keypoint clusters for expressions).

## Method

OneGT decouples geometry and texture into two sequential stages. The skeleton-anchoring stage uses two lightweight Transformers (~0.1B params each): C_rigid performs explicit rigid transformation (rotation via camera matrices) plus implicit correction of translation via cross-attention, while C_non-rigid transfers expression information from driving to source keypoints using a separate cross-attention Transformer trained on synthetic triplet data. The texture-rendering stage employs a reference-perceptible DiT (~1.2B params, DiT-XL-2 scale) with two parallel branches—a skeleton branch processing the heatmap-encoded keypoints and a texture branch processing the source image latent—whose features are fused via 'mix-calculate-split' joint self-attention modules in every block, enabling bidirectional information exchange. A key data strategy shifts geometric consistency learning entirely to Houdini-rendered synthetic data (~50K groups + 0.5M triplets), reducing the required real-world VFHQ data to only 10–30% of what competing methods consume, while the learnable δ in the Gaussian heatmap conversion lets the model adaptively adjust keypoint sensitivity during training.

## Results

On the TalkingHead-1KH benchmark, OneGT achieves the best scores across all five metrics: PSNR 33.88, SSIM 0.8265, FID 56.15, AED 0.634, and APD 0.0315. On the VFHQ benchmark, it similarly leads with PSNR 33.13, SSIM 0.8066, FID 53.35, AED 0.657, and APD 0.0264. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (X-Portrait) by 0.62 dB in PSNR and 12.3 in FID on TalkingHead-1KH, and by 1.12 dB in PSNR and 4.9 in FID on VFHQ, while also achieving the lowest expression and pose distances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneGT outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Against the closest competitor X-Portrait, OneGT gains 0.62 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 71.46 to 56.15 on TalkingHead-1KH, and gains 1.12 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 58.29 to 53.35 on VFHQ, indicating substantially better identity preservation in cross-reenactment. LivePortrait, the strongest baseline on SSIM and FID before OneGT, is surpassed by OneGT by 0.76 dB PSNR and 1.9 FID on TalkingHead-1KH. Face vid2vid and AniPortrait, which rely on sparse keypoint representations, lag significantly behind on all metrics (e.g., Face vid2vid scores 83.4 FID vs. OneGT's 56.1 on TalkingHead-1KH), confirming the advantage of the dense-keypoint skeleton. Real3dPortrait, built on 3DMM, produces the most blurred results and struggles with large pose differences, reflected in its mid-range scores. The consistent dominance across both self-reenactment (PSNR, SSIM) and cross-reenactment (FID, AED, APD) metrics suggests the geometry-texture decoupled pipeline effectively balances structural fidelity with expression and pose transfer accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneGT extends the diffusion-based portrait animation line (AnimateAnyone, X-Portrait, AniPortrait) by replacing the U-Net backbone with a DiT architecture and introducing a dual-branch 'reference-perceptible' design for texture injection. It departs from sparse-keypoint methods (LivePortrait, Face vid2vid) and 3DMM-based approaches (Real3dPortrait) by adopting dense keypoints with a trainable Gaussian spread, and it mirrors the modular pipeline of traditional rendering software (Maya, Unreal) by explicitly separating geometry (skeleton anchoring via two lightweight Transformers) from texture (diffusion rendering). A notable departure is the use of Houdini-rendered synthetic data to supply geometric consistency, shifting the burden of geometry learning away from real-world data entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-texture decoupling gives practitioners an interpretable, editable pipeline where global pose, local expression, and identity can be manipulated independently without retraining—addressing a long-standing rigidity in one-shot avatar systems. The finding that 10–30% of the real-world data suffices when geometry is learned from synthetic renders is practically significant for teams with limited data budgets. Open questions remain around temporal stability in video generation, inference speed of the 1.2B-parameter DiT, and whether the synthetic-data strategy generalizes beyond head avatars to full-body or multi-character rendering.
