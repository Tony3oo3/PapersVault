## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rasterization produces aliasing, projection distortions, and popping artifacts when rendering from out-of-distribution camera settings (e.g., wider field-of-view, different resolutions, or changed viewing distances), primarily because the pipeline approximates 3D Gaussians as 2D splats and relies on screen-space computations that become unstable or inaccurate under non-standard camera configurations. No prior rasterization-based method simultaneously addresses all these artifact classes in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained 3D Gaussian point cloud (each Gaussian parameterized by 3D mean, anisotropic scaling, rotation quaternion, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color) along with arbitrary camera parameters (position, orientation, focal length, image resolution) and produces a rendered novel-view image in real time (>100 FPS on an RTX 4090). Unlike standard 3DGS, it gracefully handles out-of-distribution settings such as 3× wider FOV, 2× or ½× resolution, and camera positions close to scene geometry without requiring retraining or per-view optimization.

## Method

The paper builds a fully 3D-aware rasterizer on top of hierarchical sorting (StopThePop) and plane-based 3D Gaussian evaluation (Hahlbohm et al.), introducing three key components. First, an adaptive 3D smoothing filter replaces the 2D Mip filter by reformulating the normalization to factor only the scale change perpendicular to the viewing ray (via a directional quadratic form dᵀΣ⁻¹d), preventing excessive transparency for anisotropic Gaussians while using min(v_train, v) to handle both near and far viewing distances. Second, perspective-correct bounding is performed in view space by solving for angles θ and φ of the Gaussian ellipsoid's tangent planes, which remains stable even when the Gaussian extends behind the image plane, eliminating border popping. Third, tile-based culling is elevated to 3D by constructing per-tile frustums from screen-space planes and computing the maximum-contribution point inside each frustum in normalized Gaussian space, culling tiles where ρ(x)² exceeds a threshold—this reduces sorting overhead and enables efficient hierarchical rasterization.

## Results

On in-distribution views (Table 1), the method achieves PSNR 27.84 / SSIM 0.836 / LPIPS 0.188 on Mip-NeRF 360, 23.58 / 0.867 / 0.145 on Tanks & Temples, and 30.49 / 0.913 / 0.222 on Deep Blending, matching or exceeding all baselines except MCMC on Tanks & Temples PSNR (24.64). The headline advantage appears on out-of-distribution large-FOV views (Table 3), where the method retains near-identical quality (e.g., 27.84 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360) while all baselines degrade severely—MCMC drops to 23.35 and Taming 3DGS to 23.30 on Mip-NeRF 360, and Taming 3DGS collapses to 11.55 PSNR on Tanks & Temples. On Deep Blending, the method leads all baselines by a wide margin (30.49 vs. 29.93 for StopThePop). Runtime is 5.8–10.7 ms per frame on an RTX 4090, only slightly slower than MCMC (4.4–8.3 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS [14], the method wins on all three datasets for SSIM and LPIPS, and on PSNR for Mip-NeRF 360 (27.84 vs. 27.44) and Deep Blending (30.49 vs. 29.51), while slightly trailing on Tanks & Temples PSNR (23.58 vs. 23.73). Versus StopThePop [28], the method dominates on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gap on Deep Blending PSNR (30.49 vs. 29.93). Against Mip-Splatting [36], the method leads on all metrics for all datasets, most notably on Deep Blending LPIPS (0.222 vs. 0.243). MCMC [16] is the closest competitor on in-distribution views, edging out the method on Mip-NeRF 360 PSNR (28.03 vs. 27.84) and Tanks & Temples PSNR (24.64 vs. 23.58), but the paper attributes this to MCMC exploiting popping to fake per-view exposure adjustments that break down out-of-distribution. On the large-FOV out-of-distribution evaluation, the method outperforms every baseline by a wide margin—e.g., 27.84 vs. 23.35 PSNR over MCMC and 27.84 vs. 23.30 over Taming 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360—confirming that the artifact-free 3D evaluation is the decisive factor. Hybrid Transparency [11] is only compared on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.17 PSNR) and trails the method by 0.67 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several recent 3DGS artifact-removal lines: it adopts the hierarchical per-ray sorting of StopThePop [28], the screen-space-plane 3D Gaussian evaluation of Hybrid Transparency [11], and the 3D smoothing filter of Mip-Splatting [36], but reformulates each into a fully 3D-aware pipeline. The key conceptual departure is replacing the 2D screen-space Mip filter with an adaptive 3D kernel that normalizes only over the subspace perpendicular to the viewing ray, and moving bounding from screen space to view-space angles to handle Gaussians extending behind the image plane. Prior works each targeted one or two artifacts (aliasing, popping, or projection distortion) in isolation; this paper is the first to address all four simultaneously within a single rasterizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying 3DGS in interactive or VR settings, this paper provides a drop-in rasterizer that eliminates the most visually disruptive artifacts (popping at image borders, flickering under zoom, distortion at wide FOV) while maintaining real-time performance (>100 FPS on consumer GPUs). The finding that 3D Gaussian evaluation is as fast as or faster than 2D splat approximations challenges the common assumption that projection to 2D is inherently cheaper, potentially encouraging broader adoption of 3D evaluation in future 3DGS variants. Open questions include generalization beyond the pinhole camera model (the view-space bounding is tied to it) and whether the stronger view consistency would benefit from more expressive view-dependent encodings, as the authors note.
