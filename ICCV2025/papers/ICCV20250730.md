## Problem

Existing unsupervised fine-grained visual representation learning methods rely solely on contrastive learning from target data, limiting their ability to capture subtle inter-class variations. While pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP offer richer semantic knowledge, they do not consistently outperform SOTA across all fine-grained datasets, and prior distillation approaches transfer knowledge indiscriminately without distinguishing reliable from unreliable predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (standard resolutions for CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVG-Aircraft) during training, augmented with LLM-generated category descriptions capped at 70 words to fit CLIP's text encoder input limit. At inference, only the student encoder f(·) (ResNet18 or ResNet50) is retained, producing a fixed-dimensional visual feature embedding used directly for downstream classification (Top-1/Top-5) and retrieval (R-1, R-5, mAP) without any additional heads or the teacher branch.

## Method

LEAD comprises a teacher branch and a student branch coupled by an entropy-based adaptive distillation mechanism. In the teacher branch, GPT-3 generates fine-grained category-aware descriptions that are encoded by CLIP's frozen text encoder into a prototype matrix; image features from CLIP's frozen visual encoder are then compared against these prototypes via temperature-scaled softmax to yield a category probability distribution. The student branch (MoCo with ResNet backbone) simultaneously learns via InfoNCE contrastive loss on augmented views and KL-divergence distillation toward the teacher's distribution. The key innovation is an information entropy-based adaptive weighting scheme: the normalized entropy of the teacher's prediction dynamically sets the distillation weight (w_distill = 1 − h_i²) and contrastive weight (w_CL = h_i²), so the student emphasizes teacher knowledge when predictions are confident and falls back on self-supervised contrastive learning when the teacher is uncertain.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011, LEAD with ResNet50 achieves 78.44% Top-1 classification accuracy and 44.43 mAP for retrieval, outperforming all prior unsupervised methods. On Stanford Cars, it reaches 88.14% Top-1 and 53.61 mAP; on FGVG-Aircraft, 67.45% Top-1 and 29.54 mAP. Notably, even with the lightweight ResNet18 backbone (1.83 GFLOPs vs. 4.14 for ResNet50), LEAD still surpasses most ResNet50-based competitors, e.g., achieving 39.27 mAP on CUB retrieval versus 24.01 for LCR and 26.31 for PP-SSL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAD (ResNet50) achieves the best Top-1 accuracy on all three datasets, surpassing the strongest prior unsupervised method CMD (76.38% on CUB) by 2.06 points and LoDisc (73.85%) by 4.59 points. In retrieval, the gains are even more pronounced: LEAD reaches 44.43 mAP on CUB versus 24.01 (LCR), 26.31 (PP-SSL), and 22.52 (LoDisc), a margin of 12–22 mAP points. On Stanford Cars, LEAD's 88.14% Top-1 exceeds the teacher CLIP itself (85.77%) by 2.37 points, and its 53.61 mAP nearly doubles the next-best retrieval score (30.04 from CLIP). The only metric where LEAD trails is CUB Top-1 vs. the frozen CLIP teacher (78.44 vs. 79.46), a 1.02-point gap, yet LEAD recovers and surpasses CLIP by 13.57 mAP on CUB retrieval. With ResNet18 (1.83 GFLOPs), LEAD still outperforms all ResNet50-based baselines on retrieval (e.g., 39.27 vs. 26.31 mAP on CUB) and matches or exceeds them on Cars classification (73.40 vs. 67.73 for PP-SSL), demonstrating strong efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MoCo contrastive learning framework for unsupervised representation learning and extends the line of work that integrates pre-trained vision-language models (CLIP) into fine-grained tasks, following PP-SSL [23] and CMD [3]. It departs from prior distillation-based FVRL methods in two key ways: (1) it uses LLM-generated category descriptions to construct a prototype-driven classifier that activates CLIP's multimodal reasoning rather than relying on raw visual features alone, and (2) it introduces an information-entropy-based adaptive weighting mechanism that dynamically balances distillation and contrastive losses per sample, addressing the limitation in PP-SSL of treating all CLIP knowledge equally. The approach sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation, vision-language pre-training, and LLM-assisted semantic enrichment, but applies these in a specifically designed unsupervised fine-grained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a lightweight ResNet18 backbone, augmented with LLM-assisted CLIP knowledge and entropy-adaptive distillation, can match or exceed ResNet50-based SOTA methods, offering a compelling efficiency story for deployment-constrained fine-grained recognition. The entropy-based adaptive weighting is a general principle that could transfer to other teacher-student settings where the teacher's reliability varies across inputs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-world or zero-shot fine-grained settings where the category set is not predefined, how it scales to very large category taxonomies beyond the ~200-400 classes tested here, and whether the 70-word CLIP text encoder limitation constrains the richness of LLM-generated descriptions that could further improve performance.
