## Problem

Deploying pre-trained Vision Transformers across heterogeneous platforms (mobile devices to GPU clusters) typically requires training separate size-specific models, which is computationally expensive and complicates deployment. Existing elastic Transformer methods either introduce elasticity during pre-training (requiring per-submodel fine-tuning downstream) or support only a narrow range of architectural dimensions, leaving the problem of efficiently adapting a large pre-trained ViT into a broadly elastic structure during downstream task adaptation unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT-Base (ImageNet-21K weights) and a scalar normalized MACs budget M_t ∈ [0,1] as input. The router maps this budget to a discrete submodel configuration θ comprising per-layer MLP expansion ratios, per-layer attention head counts, a global embedding dimension, and per-layer binary depth indicators. The selected submodel then processes standard RGB images to produce class labels (classification) or pixel-wise segmentation masks (ADE20K), with the same backbone serving all downstream tasks at different computational budgets.

## Method

EA-ViT operates in two stages. Stage 1 constructs a nested multi-dimensional elastic ViT supporting flexibility along four axes (MLP expansion ratio, attention heads, embedding dimension, and network depth via skip indicators), then applies a curriculum-based adaptation strategy that starts from the full pre-trained model and progressively widens the sampling range of smaller submodels to avoid gradient interference. Stage 2 initializes a lightweight two-layer MLP router using Pareto-optimal submodels discovered by a customized NSGA-II with partitioned selection and crowding-distance enforcement; the router employs Gumbel-Sigmoid with straight-through estimation to make differentiable discrete architectural decisions and is jointly trained with the backbone under a bi-objective loss combining cross-entropy, MACs-constraint penalty, and an annealed reference term toward the initial Pareto front.

## Results

Under an 8 GMACs constraint, EA-ViT achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy on all nine classification benchmarks, ranging from 97.98% on CIFAR-10 to 67.56% on DTD. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (Flextron) appear on fine-grained and texture-heavy datasets: +11.59% on Flowers-102 (85.39 vs. 73.80), +6.35% on DTD (67.56 vs. 61.21), and +4.01% on FGVC Aircraft (83.37 vs. 79.36). On more challenging tasks (ADE20K segmentation, ImageNet-1K, Kvasir medical, UCMerced remote sensing), EA-ViT also consistently outperforms all baselines across the full MACs range (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA-ViT outperforms all four baselines on every one of the nine classification datasets at the 8 GMACs budget. Flextron [3] is the strongest baseline, yet EA-ViT still leads by 0.87–11.59 points (e.g., 97.98 vs. 97.11 on CIFAR-10; 85.39 vs. 73.80 on Flowers-102). MatFormer [9] is competitive on CIFAR-100 (86.18) and SVHN (97.45) but falls 2.02 points behind EA-ViT on CIFAR-100 and 11.59 points behind on Flowers-102. HydraViT [12] struggles most on fine-grained and texture datasets, scoring only 29.27% on Flowers-102 and 43.53% on DTD—far below EA-ViT's 85.39% and 67.56%. DynaBERT [20] is the weakest baseline overall, trailing EA-ViT by 3.74 points on CIFAR-10 and 30.81 points on Flowers-102, reflecting its limited elastic dimensions (width and depth only). The advantage of EA-ViT is most pronounced in the low-MACs regime (Figure 3), where the multi-dimensional elastic space allows the router to select configurations that better match each dataset's capacity needs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the elastic Transformer line of work (DynaBERT, MatFormer, HydraViT, Flextron) in two key ways: it shifts elasticity from the pre-training phase to the downstream adaptation phase of a pre-trained ViT, and it expands elasticity from 1–3 architectural dimensions to four (MLP ratio, attention heads, embedding dimension, depth). The NSGA-II-based Pareto-optimal initialization and the Gumbel-Sigmoid constraint-aware router draw on multi-objective optimization and discrete relaxation techniques, but their combination for ViT submodel selection is new. Unlike Flextron, which targets LLMs and relies on a surrogate model, EA-ViT operates directly on the ViT backbone with joint end-to-end training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: a single adaptation run yields a family of submodels spanning 10^26 configurations, eliminating the need to train and store separate models per device. The t-SNE analysis revealing dataset-specific structural preferences (e.g., texture datasets like DTD favoring different architectures than object-recognition datasets) suggests that a one-size-fits-all compressed ViT is suboptimal, motivating task-aware routing. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ViT-Large or beyond, whether multi-constraint routing (latency, memory, energy) is feasible, and whether the curriculum strategy generalizes to other backbone architectures such as Swin or ConvNeXt.
