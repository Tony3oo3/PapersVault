## Problem

Automated tooth alignment in digital orthodontic planning remains heavily dependent on clinician expertise and is time-consuming. Existing PointNet-based methods have revealed limitations in capturing local point cloud features, and prior approaches lack quantitative modeling of the occlusal relationship between upper and lower jaws, which is a critical clinical constraint for valid alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251873/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds of up to 32 teeth (16 per jaw), each uniformly sampled to 512 points via farthest point sampling, and serializes them into ordered multi-channel textures based on distance from a simulated dental arch line (labial positive, lingual negative). It outputs per-tooth 6DoF transformation parameters (quaternion rotation + 3D translation) that reposition each tooth from its pre-orthodontic state to the aligned target. The network handles missing teeth and wisdom teeth by specifying a maximum of 16 teeth per jaw, with missing slots producing negligible impact on sliding-window feature extraction.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-branch Swin-Transformer encoder: a global branch (SWTBS) processes tooth centroid points through shared Swin-T blocks with positional encoding, while a local branch (SWTP) applies multi-stage hierarchical downsampling with patch partitioning and column-wise feature merging to the serialized tooth point clouds. The two feature streams are pooled, concatenated, and further propagated through SWTBS before an MLP regresses the 6DoF parameters. Two novel occlusal loss functions are introduced: an occlusal projecting overlap loss that enforces binary agreement of projected occlusal contact regions between predicted and ground-truth upper/lower jaws, and an occlusal distance uniformity loss that penalizes variance in inter-tooth occlusal distances (with separate formulations for anterior and posterior teeth). A constrained data augmentation pipeline applies jaw regularization (pulling teeth exceeding 2.2 mm from the arch inward) and BVH-based collision detection to avoid clinically implausible augmented samples.

## Results

On the authors' 837-sample Orthodontic Alignment Dataset, the proposed method achieves ADD of 0.6584, ADD/AUC of 0.89, ME_rotate of 2.77°, and ME_trans of 1.16 mm, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the TADPM dataset, it reaches ADD/AUC of 0.84 and ME_rotate of 2.93°, again leading all competitors. The most striking gain is in rotational error: the method reduces ME_rotate from 7.5–8.7° (other methods) to 2.77° on D_our, a roughly 3× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both datasets. On D_our, the closest competitor is Landmark [40] (ADD/AUC 0.84, ME_rot 7.83°), which the method surpasses by 0.05 in ADD/AUC and by 5.06° in rotational error. TADPM [21], the diffusion-based approach, achieves the best ADD/AUC among baselines on D_TADPM (0.83) but is still beaten by the proposed method (0.84) and trails by 0.41° in ME_rotate. PointNet-based methods (TANet, PSTN, TAligNet) consistently underperform, with PSTN [23] showing the worst results (ADD 1.59, ME_rot 8.69° on D_our). Ptv3 [48] is evaluated only on D_our and sits mid-pack (ADD/AUC 0.78). The authors also note that their method processes each case substantially faster than TADPM during both training and inference, while producing higher-quality alignments under identical hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PointNet-based lineage in automatic tooth alignment (TANet, PSTN, TAligNet) by replacing the point cloud encoder with a Swin Transformer architecture, enabled by a key preprocessing step: serializing each tooth's 512-point cloud into an ordered multi-channel texture along a simulated dental arch line. This serialization converts the permutation-invariant point cloud problem into a structured sequence problem, making the shift-window attention mechanism applicable. The two occlusal loss functions (projecting overlap and distance uniformity) represent a conceptual departure from prior work, which relied on Chamfer distance or transformation parameter losses without explicitly encoding upper-lower jaw occlusal relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a 837-case orthodontic alignment dataset with manually adjusted ground truth (rather than post-treatment scans) addresses a real gap in the field, and the constrained augmentation with BVH collision detection is a practical contribution for generating clinically valid training data. The occlusal losses encode domain knowledge that prior methods lacked, and the ablation confirms both are necessary for the full accuracy gain. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full multi-stage treatment path prediction (the authors only predict the final target), how it performs on larger or more diverse populations, and whether the serialization strategy transfers to other structured 3D prediction tasks.
