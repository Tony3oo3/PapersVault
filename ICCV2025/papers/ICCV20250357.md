## Problem

Existing emotional image generation methods rely on discrete emotion tags (e.g., 'happy', 'sad'), which cannot capture the nuanced, continuous spectrum of human affect, and they generate images from emotion labels rather than free-text prompts, making content control impractical. This paper introduces the task of continuous emotional image content generation (C-EICG), where both the scene content and the emotional tone are specified independently by the user.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-text prompt describing the desired scene content and a pair of continuous Valence-Arousal values, each in the range [-3, 3], specifying the target emotional tone. The output is a standard RGB image generated by Stable Diffusion XL that simultaneously matches the prompt's semantic content and evokes the specified V-A emotion. Notably, the V-A values can override emotional cues embedded in the prompt text, and the model supports fine-grained control with increments as small as 0.2 in either dimension.

## Method

The core architecture is an Emotion Injection Transformer (EIT), a modified GPT-2 with 12 sequential Emotion Injection Blocks that fuse V-A features into neutral prompt features via cross-attention at each layer. Two separate MLPs encode the scalar Valence and Arousal values into feature vectors, which are then injected through cross-attention modules alongside standard self-attention and feedforward operations in each block. The network predicts a residual between emotional and neutral prompt features (rather than the full emotional feature), which is added to the neutral feature before being fed into SDXL's cross-attention for image generation. A novel loss function combines scaled residual learning (amplifying the target residual by α=1.5 to emphasize emotional shifts) with V-A density weighting via Gaussian kernel density estimation to counteract the imbalanced distribution of training samples in V-A space. The emotion-embedding network is trained on a GPT-4-generated dataset of paired neutral/emotional prompts derived from 39,843 human-annotated images from OASIS, EMOTIC, and FindingEmo, using AdamW for 200 epochs on two A800 GPUs.

## Results

On 3,300 generated images (132 prompts × 5 V values × 5 A values), EmotiCrafter achieves the lowest A-Error (1.828) and V-Error (1.510), indicating the most accurate emotion control among all methods. It scores 23.067 CLIPScore and 0.881 CLIP-IQA, slightly below the best baselines on prompt fidelity and image quality but with the critical advantage of actually producing emotionally distinct images. In the continuity metric (LPIPS-Continuous), EmotiCrafter achieves 0.220 versus 0.361 for GPT-4+SDXL, demonstrating smoother emotional transitions across V-A values. User studies confirm statistically significant improvements over GPT-4+SDXL in V/A ranking consistency, emotion consistency, and smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Cross Attention baseline (IP-Adapter-based), EmotiCrafter wins decisively on emotion accuracy (A-Error 1.828 vs. 1.923; V-Error 1.510 vs. 2.080) but loses on CLIP-IQA (0.881 vs. 0.949); however, the paper notes that Cross Attention produces nearly identical outputs regardless of V-A variation, making its higher image quality score misleading for the emotion-control task. Against the Textual Inversion baseline, EmotiCrafter dominates on every metric, with Textual Inversion showing the worst CLIP-IQA (0.370) and a persistent purple tint artifact. The closest competitor is GPT-4+SDXL, which achieves slightly better CLIPScore (25.907 vs. 23.067) and CLIP-IQA (0.906 vs. 0.881), but EmotiCrafter wins on both A-Error (1.828 vs. 1.860) and V-Error (1.510 vs. 1.517), and substantially on continuity (LPIPS-Continuous 0.220 vs. 0.361) and all user-study metrics (e.g., A-Ranking Consistency 0.759 vs. 0.165; Emotion Smoothness 4.240 vs. 3.195 on a 5-point scale). Overall, EmotiCrafter trades a modest drop in prompt fidelity and image quality for the only method that reliably produces continuous, controllable emotional variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EmoGen's discrete emotion-tag-based image generation to a continuous Valence-Arousal formulation with free-text prompt control, building on the V-A circumplex model from affective psychology. Architecturally, it repurposes a GPT-2 backbone as an Emotion Injection Transformer that fuses V-A features into CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention, then feeds the modified features into Stable Diffusion XL. The loss function draws on kernel density estimation to handle V-A distribution imbalance and introduces scaled residual learning to amplify emotional signal. Baselines span UNet-level injection (IP-Adapter, time embedding), prompt-level techniques (textual inversion), and an LLM-rewriting pipeline (GPT-4+SDXL), positioning the contribution between feature-level and prompt-level emotion control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EmotiCrafter is the first model to enable fine-grained, continuous emotional control over text-to-image generation, opening a path toward affective content creation tools where creators can dial in precise emotional intensity rather than choosing from a fixed set of labels. Key limitations remain: arousal control lags behind valence (consistent with the lower inter-annotator agreement in V-A prediction literature), the model over-generates human activities due to training data bias, and the GPT-4-generated prompt pairs may introduce systematic biases in what 'emotional' means visually. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame generation, whether a 3D V-A-D model would improve expressiveness, and whether the GPT-2-based emotion injection can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for real-time use.
