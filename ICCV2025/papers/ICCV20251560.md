## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for semantic segmentation under adverse weather suffer from two key limitations: they are weather-unaware, failing to explicitly capture the distinct style characteristics (illumination, contrast, visibility) that differentiate fog, rain, snow, and night scenes, and they treat all weather conditions uniformly despite the severe heterogeneity in visual distortions across weather types. This paper addresses both gaps by explicitly aggregating weather-specific knowledge from the frequency domain and then homogenizing features across diverse weather conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from a labeled source domain (CityScapes, 2048×1024, normal weather) and unlabeled target domain images under adverse conditions (ACDC at 1920×1080 covering fog/rain/snow/night, NightCity, or DarkZurich for night), rescaled and randomly cropped to 512×512 during training. Beyond the raw RGB input, the network internally computes 2D Fourier amplitude characteristic maps per image as an intermediate weather representation. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask over 19 classes, produced by a teacher-student network where the teacher generates pseudo-labels for the target domain.

## Method

WA²Net comprises two coupled modules. First, Amplitude Prompt Aggregation extracts weather characteristics by computing the 2D Fourier transform of each image, fixing the phase to a constant, and inverse-transforming to obtain an amplitude characteristic map encoded by a shared ResNet-18; a bank of N=16 learnable prompts then interacts with these amplitude features via cross-attention to distill weather-aware prompts, regularized by a diversity loss that encourages each prompt to capture distinct weather attributes. Second, Weather Heterogeneity Adaptation uses the learned prompts to modulate the current image's amplitude features (prompt-guided modulation via softmax-weighted projection), then applies a custom homogenization attention that computes the pixel-wise squared L2 difference between the MiT-B5 vision features and the modulated weather characteristics, using this disparity map as a pixel-wise weight to suppress weather-induced interference while preserving target-relevant information. Training combines supervised cross-entropy on the source domain, unsupervised pseudo-label loss on the target domain via an EMA-updated teacher, and the prompt diversity loss.

## Results

WA²Net achieves the best overall mIOU across all three adverse-weather benchmarks: 73.8 on CityScapes→ACDC, 56.5 on CityScapes→NightCity, and 62.8 on CityScapes→DarkZurich. On the most challenging ACDC benchmark (which includes fog, rain, snow, and night), WA²Net outperforms the strongest prior method CoDA by 1.2 mIOU points and MIC by 3.4 points. Gains are particularly pronounced on fine-grained classes such as Person (72.8 vs. 71.4 for CoDA), Rider (50.2 vs. 47.6), and Traffic Light (68.5 vs. 69.9 for CoDA, but 68.5 vs. 62.0 for MIC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WA²Net outperforms all nine baselines on every benchmark. Against the closest competitor CoDA [12] (ECCV 2024), WA²Net gains +1.2 mIOU on ACDC (73.8 vs. 72.6), +1.6 on NightCity (56.5 vs. 54.9), and +1.4 on DarkZurich (62.8 vs. 61.4). Against MIC [18] (CVPR 2023), the margin is larger: +3.4 on ACDC, +2.7 on NightCity, and +2.6 on DarkZurich. HALO [8] (ICML 2024) trails by 1.9, 2.3, and 2.8 mIOU on ACDC, NightCity, and DarkZurich respectively. Earlier UDA methods show substantially larger gaps: DAFormer [16] is 8.4 mIOU behind on ACDC, HRDA [17] is 5.8 behind, and SSAC [20] is 14.2 behind. The oldest baselines ADVENT [53] and MGCDA [44] lag by over 25 and 25 mIOU points on ACDC, respectively, highlighting the progress of weather-aware adaptation over generic domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

WA²Net extends the unsupervised domain adaptation framework for adverse-weather segmentation (DAFormer, MIC, HRDA) by explicitly modeling weather characteristics in the Fourier frequency domain, building on the observation from FDA that amplitude spectra encode style information. It repurposes the prompt-learning paradigm (Visual Prompt Tuning) as a medium for aggregating cross-weather knowledge, but departs from prior UDA methods that treat all weather conditions as a single generic domain shift by introducing a dedicated prompt bank with diversity regularization. The homogenization attention mechanism, which computes pixel-wise squared differences between vision features and modulated amplitude characteristics, is a novel design that goes beyond standard cross-attention or masked attention for domain alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing UDA methods for adverse-weather segmentation treat heterogeneous weather types (fog, rain, snow, night) as a monolithic domain shift, whereas WA²Net explicitly disentangles weather-specific characteristics from scene content. However, the approach is limited to the specific Fourier amplitude representation and a fixed set of weather conditions seen during training; generalization to truly unseen weather types or mixed-weather scenes remains untested. The modest but consistent gains (1–2 mIOU over CoDA/MIC across three benchmarks) suggest the weather-aware decomposition is a useful inductive bias, but whether it scales to more diverse or dynamic weather scenarios is an open question.
