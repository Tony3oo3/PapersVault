## Problem

Existing vector quantization methods for visual tokenization suffer from codebook collapse when scaling up codebook size or code dimension, because their partial-update strategy only optimizes selected codes during backpropagation, progressively widening the distribution gap between non-activated codes and encoder features. This bottleneck prevents training large-scale codebooks (e.g., 2^18 codes at 256 dimensions) with high utilization, limiting the representational capacity of discrete visual tokenizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images and produces a 16×16 grid of discrete tokens (256 tokens total) via 16× downsampling, with codebook sizes up to 262,144 and code dimensions up to 256. For generation, the autoregressive transformer consumes the flattened raster-scan token sequence along with a class label (serving as both start token and AdaLN condition) to produce 256×256 images. The tokenizer itself is trained on ImageNet 256×256, while reconstruction quality is evaluated on the 50k validation set and generation is evaluated at 256×256 resolution.

## Method

IBQ replaces the standard straight-through estimator on selected codes with one applied to the full categorical (softmax) distribution between each visual feature and all codebook embeddings, making every code differentiable and receiving gradient updates in every backward pass. Concretely, it computes logits via dot product, applies softmax to get a soft one-hot distribution, then uses the reparameterization trick (Ind = Ind_hard − sg[Ind_soft] + Ind_soft) to copy gradients from the soft distribution to the hard one-hot index, so that z_q = Ind^T C backpropagates to all K codes simultaneously. This global updating keeps the codebook distribution aligned with the encoder feature distribution throughout training, maintaining ~96% utilization even at 16,384 codes and 84% at 262,144 codes. Training combines reconstruction, double quantization, LPIPS perceptual, PatchGAN adversarial, and entropy losses. For generation, a vanilla Llama-based autoregressive transformer (with RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, AdaLN) performs next-token prediction over the IBQ token sequence, scaling from 342M to 2.1B parameters.

## Results

On 256×256 ImageNet reconstruction, IBQ with a 16,384 codebook (256-dim) achieves 1.37 rFID and 96% codebook utilization, while scaling to a 262,144 codebook (256-dim) reaches a state-of-the-art 1.00 rFID with 84% utilization. For class-conditional generation, IBQ-XXL (2.1B parameters) achieves 2.05 gFID and 286.73 IS, outperforming all vanilla autoregressive baselines at comparable scales. The method demonstrates consistent scaling gains: increasing codebook size from 1,024 to 262,144 improves rFID from 2.24 to 1.00, and scaling the AR model from 342M to 2.1B parameters improves gFID from 2.88 to 2.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBQ decisively outperforms VQGAN on reconstruction: at 16,384 codebook size and 256-dim, IBQ achieves 1.37 rFID vs. VQGAN's 3.98 rFID, with codebook utilization jumping from 5.3% to 96%. Against LlamaGen at the same 256-dim setting, IBQ improves rFID from 9.21 to 1.37 and utilization from 0.29% to 96%, exposing LlamaGen's severe codebook collapse at high dimensions. At the 262,144 codebook scale, IBQ (1.00 rFID) surpasses Open-MAGVIT2 (1.17 rFID), which uses zero-dimensional codes (LFQ). In generation (Table 7), IBQ-XXL (2.1B) achieves 2.05 gFID, beating LlamaGen-3B* (2.18 gFID) and Open-MAGVIT2-XL (2.33 gFID), and outperforming the diffusion-based DiT-XL/2 (2.27 gFID). IBQ is competitive with AR variants VAR-d30 (1.92 gFID) and MAR-H (1.55 gFID), which employ specialized multi-scale or non-quantized architectures, suggesting the tokenizer gains could be further amplified with such designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBQ builds directly on the VQGAN encoder-quantizer-decoder framework and the straight-through estimator for gradient propagation through discrete operations, but departs fundamentally from the partial-update strategy inherited by VQ-VAE, VQGAN, and LlamaGen, where only the selected code receives gradient. Instead of reducing code dimension (LlamaGen, MAGVIT-v2) or freezing the codebook with a trainable projector (VQGAN-LC) to sidestep collapse, IBQ repositions the straight-through estimator onto the full categorical distribution over all K codebook embeddings, rendering every code differentiable in each backward pass. This is a minimal architectural change—replacing a single argmin-based gradient copy with a softmax-based one-hot reparameterization—that addresses the root cause of codebook collapse rather than its symptoms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying codebook collapse as a gradient-flow problem rather than a capacity problem, IBQ unlocks a previously inaccessible regime: 2^18 codebook size at 256 dimensions with 84% utilization, achieving 1.00 rFID on ImageNet. The well-documented scaling curves across codebook size, code dimension, and model depth provide a clear roadmap for future tokenizer design. Open questions remain: whether the 84% utilization at extreme scale indicates a residual collapse mechanism, whether IBQ combined with advanced AR architectures (VAR, MAR) yields further gains, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities (audio, video) where codebook collapse is equally problematic.
