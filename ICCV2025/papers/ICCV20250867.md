## Problem

Existing LMM benchmarks are rapidly saturating—GPT-4o already scores above 90% on MGSM, HumanEval, and MMLU—leaving insufficient headroom to distinguish frontier model capabilities. Chart and figure interpretation, a critical analytical skill, lacks a benchmark challenging enough for current and next-generation LMMs, with prior works like FigureQA, PlotQA, and ChartQA no longer providing adequate difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250867/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs a graph image (synthetically rendered at 2251×2171 resolution via Matplotlib/Seaborn, or hand-drawn and photographed for the Real task) with a text question asking for a specific graph property. The expected output is a single numerical value at integer, one-decimal-place, or nearest-10 precision, or a full function equation. Evaluation uses strict exact string matching with only leading-whitespace removal, so verbose or reformatted answers are marked incorrect even if numerically correct.

## Method

GRAB is generated synthetically using Matplotlib and Seaborn, producing 3284 questions across five tasks (Properties, Functions, Series, Transforms, Real) that cover 23 graph properties. For each property, the pipeline either samples a target value and fits generation parameters to match it, or randomly samples parameters and computes the property from the resulting data, then downsamples to ensure uniform answer distribution and avoid convergence toward zero. The Real task (1114 questions) introduces realism through hand-drawn figures on paper and whiteboards, figures embedded in digital screenshots, and figures with added visual noise (artefacts, flips, blurs). Evaluation employs greedy decoding with a minimal single-turn prompt and exact string matching, jointly measuring visual analytical reasoning and instruction-following ability across 20 open- and closed-source LMMs.

## Results

On GRAB (3284 questions), the highest-performing model, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, achieves only 21.0% overall accuracy, with GPT-4o at 14.9% and Gemini 1.5 Pro at 18.8%. The Properties task (single-function analysis) is the easiest, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet reaching 41.8%, while the Transforms task is the hardest at just 10.0% for the same model. No model correctly answers a single question in the Functions category (deriving full function equations), and the best open-source model (TransCore-M) scores only 7.9% overall. On the lighter GRAB-Lite (500 questions), reasoning models push scores higher: Gemini 2.5 Flash reaches 28.4% and o1 reaches 24.2%, compared to Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 19.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads all 20 evaluated models at 21.0% overall, outperforming GPT-4o (14.9%) by 6.1 points and Gemini 1.5 Pro (18.8%) by 2.2 points. Among closed-source models, the Claude 3.5 Sonnet advantage is most pronounced on the Properties task (41.8% vs. 34.2% for Gemini 1.5 Pro and 24.7% for GPT-4o), while GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro are more competitive on the Real task (17.3% and 20.3% respectively vs. 19.6%). All closed-source models substantially outperform the best open-source model (TransCore-M at 7.9%), with a gap of 13+ points over the top closed-source model. On GRAB-Lite, reasoning models close the gap: Gemini 2.5 Flash (28.4%) surpasses Claude 3.5 Sonnet (19.6%) by 8.8 points, and o1 (24.2%) also exceeds it, suggesting that extended chain-of-thought reasoning provides meaningful gains on this benchmark. Notably, no model—closed or open—answers a single Functions-category question correctly, indicating a shared fundamental limitation in deriving exact function parameters from visual plots.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAB belongs to the family of synthetic figure-interpretation benchmarks (FigureQA, DVQA, PlotQA, ChartQA, ChartBench) and LMM evaluation suites (MMBench, MMMU, MathVista), but deliberately carves out a harder niche: prior chart benchmarks are saturated or too easy for current frontier models, while GRAB targets the specific sub-task of deriving quantitative graph properties (intercepts, stationary points, correlations, bounded areas, function equations) under controlled complexity. The synthetic generation pipeline via Matplotlib/Seaborn follows established practice, but the addition of multi-function/series averaging, sequential geometric transforms, and a 'Real' split with hand-drawn and noisy figures extends the scope beyond what prior synthetic benchmarks covered. The paper explicitly positions itself as a difficulty uplift rather than a methodological departure from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With GPT-4o already scoring 90%+ on MGSM, HumanEval, and MMLU, the field urgently needs benchmarks with sufficient headroom; GRAB's 21% best score (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) provides exactly that, and the 0% across all 20 models on the Functions category (deriving full function equations) reveals a concrete, measurable capability gap in current LMMs. The finding that visual noise and screenshot distractors in the Real task do not significantly degrade performance suggests the bottleneck is analytical reasoning rather than perceptual robustness, redirecting future research effort. Open questions include whether the synthetic aesthetic limits ecological validity, whether the exact-matching protocol unfairly penalizes models that reason correctly but format differently, and whether GRAB will retain its difficulty as next-generation reasoning models (e.g., o1, Gemini 2.5 Flash) close the gap — the GRAB-Lite subset is a pragmatic hedge for the inference-time-scaling era.
