## Problem

SAM's zero-shot interactive segmentation degrades on complex scenarios such as camouflaged or multi-part objects, and existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods update a single model monolithically with all user clicks at once. This monolithic process induces conflicting gradient signals when clicks target distinct semantic regions, preventing the model from capturing fine-grained details. The paper asks whether partitioning clicks into coherent subsets and adapting specialized sub-models can resolve these conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252147/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at 1024×1024 resolution and a sequential stream of user clicks, each specified as (x, y, s) where s ∈ {0,1} denotes positive (foreground) or negative (background). It produces a binary segmentation mask per iteration. Notably, the model is re-initialized from pretrained SAM (ViT-B) weights for every test image, and only the prompt encoder and mask decoder are optimized during TTA (one AdamW step at η=1e-5 per iteration), keeping the image encoder frozen.

## Method

DC-TTA partitions the accumulated positive clicks into segmentation units, each encapsulating a coherent click subset, its predicted mask, and independently adapted SAM parameters. A new positive click is assigned to an existing unit if its SAM-predicted mask (using only that click plus all negative clicks) has non-zero IoU with the unit's current mask; otherwise a new unit is created. Each affected unit undergoes TTA using a mask-level BCE loss (comparing the previous mask against an intermediate mask that includes the new click) plus a pixel-level loss at all clicked locations. A global unit always retains all clicks to preserve overall context. Finally, unit-specific models are merged via task arithmetic—adding scaled task vectors (θ_k − θ₀) to the base parameters with a global-unit weight γ and per-unit weight γ²—followed by one fine-tuning step using the pixel-wise union of unit masks as pseudo ground truth.

## Results

DC-TTA is evaluated on eight interactive segmentation benchmarks using NoC (Number of Clicks) and FR (Failure Rate) at 85% and 90% IoU thresholds with a 20-click budget. On the most challenging camouflaged-object datasets, DC-TTA reduces NoC90 from 10.45 to 9.58 on CAMO and from 13.30 to 12.05 on TRASHCAN, while cutting FR90 from 31.20% to 25.60% and 52.29% to 38.80% respectively. On standard benchmarks, gains are also consistent: COCOMVal FR90 drops from 10.38% to 5.00%, and PascalVOC FR90 drops from 3.04% to 1.99%. The DC-only variant (without TTA) already improves over the SAM baseline, confirming that the divide-and-conquer partitioning itself contributes meaningfully to performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-TTA outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against the SAM zero-shot baseline [19], DC-TTA reduces NoC90 by 0.6–1.25 clicks and FR90 by 2–13 percentage points depending on the dataset, with the largest gains on TRASHCAN (FR90: 52.29→38.80) and COCOMVal (FR90: 10.38→5.00). TENT [49] provides negligible improvement over the SAM baseline (e.g., CAMO NoC90: 10.42 vs 10.45) and is consistently outperformed by DC-TTA. AdaptSAM [41], the closest TTA competitor, improves over SAM but still trails DC-TTA on all eight datasets (e.g., TRASHCAN FR90: 49.33 vs 38.80). When DC-TTA is applied as a plug-in to conventional IS methods, it further reduces NoC90 and FR90 for RITM [44] (e.g., TRASHCAN: 11.10→10.60 NoC90), FocalClick [3] (e.g., TRASHCAN: 11.03→10.16 NoC90), and MFP [22] (e.g., TRASHCAN: 8.80→8.73 NoC90), confirming its generality beyond SAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-TTA builds on the SAM-based interactive segmentation paradigm and the test-time adaptation line of work exemplified by AdaptSAM, which uses result masks as pseudo-labels to refine SAM per sample. Its key conceptual departure is the Divide-and-Conquer strategy: rather than updating a single monolithic model with all accumulated clicks (as in naive TTA or AdaptSAM), it partitions clicks into coherent subsets, adapts a dedicated model per subset, and merges them via task-vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al.). The model merging component draws directly from the task arithmetic literature, repurposing it to consolidate specialized per-unit adaptations into a unified predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap—SAM's degradation on camouflaged, multi-part, or domain-specific objects—by reducing signal conflicts among diverse user clicks, and the DC strategy proves broadly applicable beyond SAM (validated on RITM, FocalClick, MFP). A notable open question is the computational overhead of maintaining and merging multiple adapted models as the number of units grows, which the paper does not analyze in depth. Future work could explore adaptive unit creation thresholds, hierarchical merging for very long click sequences, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D interactive segmentation.
