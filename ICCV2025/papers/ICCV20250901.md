## Problem

Foundation model features (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) achieve strong zero-shot semantic correspondence but remain ambiguous for symmetric objects and repeated parts. Prior supervised methods that resolve these ambiguities require manual keypoint annotations or dataset-specific label definitions, making them non-scalable. The paper asks whether self-generated pseudo-labels, enhanced with weak 3D-aware filtering, can provide sufficient supervision to refine foundation features without any manual keypoint labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250901/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a source image and a target image (resized to 960² and 840² to extract DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion features at 60×60 resolution) plus a query point on the source image, and outputs the corresponding point on the target image via nearest-neighbor search in the adapter-refined feature space. Notably, the 3D poses, instance masks, and azimuth annotations used during training for pseudo-label generation are not required at test time, making the method applicable to arbitrary image pairs.

## Method

DIY-SC trains a lightweight adapter (4 bottleneck layers, 5M parameters) on top of concatenated DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion features using pseudo-labels generated in a two-stage filtering pipeline. First, zero-shot nearest-neighbor matches are computed along a K=4 image chain where consecutive pairs have <90° azimuth difference, then filtered by a relaxed cyclic consistency constraint (allowing one-patch deviation) and a canonicalized spherical 3D prior that rejects matches mapping to dissimilar regions on a shared sphere. The adapter is trained with a CLIP-inspired sparse contrastive loss on matched points and a dense window soft-argmax loss with Gaussian noise, which propagates gradients to unlabeled regions. This design avoids the need for dataset-specific keypoint definitions and the performance degradation from weighted-averaging spherical features, instead using the sphere purely as a rejection filter for pseudo-label quality.

## Results

On SPair-71k, DIY-SC achieves PCK@0.1 of 71.6 (per-image) and 74.4 (per-keypoint), setting a new state-of-the-art among weakly-supervised methods. This represents a +6.2 point gain over the previous SOTA (TLR, 65.4 per-image) and +4.8 points over TLR per-keypoint (69.6). On AP-10k, the method achieves PCK@0.1 of 70.6 (intra-species), 69.1 (cross-species), and 57.8 (cross-family), outperforming TLR by 1.9, 4.5, and 5.1 points respectively, despite never being trained on AP-10k. Pre-training on the larger ImageNet-3D dataset further improves SPair-71k performance to 72.2 PCK@0.1 (per-image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIY-SC outperforms all seven compared baselines on both SPair-71k and AP-10k. Against the zero-shot baselines, the gains are substantial: SD+DINOv2 [62] scores 59.9 PCK@0.1 (per-image) on SPair-71k versus 71.6 for DIY-SC (+11.7), while DINOv2 alone [40] and DIFT [49] achieve only 55.6 and 57.7 per-keypoint average respectively versus 74.4 for DIY-SC. Among weakly-supervised methods, DIY-SC beats TLR [63] (65.4) by 6.2 points and SphMap [35] (64.4) by 7.2 points on SPair-71k PCK@0.1 per-image; both TLR and SphMap require additional information at test time (keypoint definitions or spherical feature weighting) that DIY-SC does not. DistillDIFT [9] in its weakly-supervised variant (65.3) is also outperformed by 6.3 points, and its unsupervised variant (60.8) trails by 10.8 points. Notably, on AP-10k where DIY-SC was never trained, it still surpasses TLR by up to 5.1 points (cross-family), approaching the performance of a fully supervised TLR model (70.1 vs. 70.6 I.S.), suggesting the pseudo-label training generalizes better than fixed keypoint annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adapter-based refinement of concatenated Stable Diffusion and DINOv2 features introduced by Zhang et al. (TLR, CVPR 2024), but replaces their reliance on dataset-specific keypoint definitions and test-time geometric flipping with a fully self-supervised pseudo-labeling pipeline. It repurposes the spherical mapper from Mariotti et al. (SphMap, CVPR 2024) not as a weighted feature regularizer (which degrades performance on non-rigid objects) but purely as a rejection filter for wrong pseudo-labels, and adapts the cyclic consistency constraint from Aberman et al. (TOG 2018) into a relaxed form suitable for noisy zero-shot matches. The CLIP-inspired sparse contrastive loss from Luo et al. (NeurIPS 2023) provides the training objective, while the 3D-aware chaining strategy is a novel combination of these components into a scalable pseudo-label generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual keypoint annotations and dataset-specific label conventions, this work makes strong semantic correspondence learning scalable to large, diverse object-centric datasets (demonstrated on ImageNet-3D with ~86k images), which is a practical bottleneck for prior SOTA methods. The finding that pre-training on ImageNet-3D without ever seeing SPair-71k outperforms the previous SOTA trained directly on SPair-71k suggests the pseudo-label signal is dense and generalizable in ways that sparse manual annotations are not. Key limitations remain: the spherical rejection prior is less effective for non-rigid or topologically complex objects, and the method still requires 3D pose annotations (albeit coarser than keypoint labels), leaving open whether fully annotation-free pseudo-labeling can match this performance.
