## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models adapted for Generalized Zero-Shot Classification (GZSC) exhibit systematic prediction bias toward base classes because fine-tuning on seen-class data distorts decision boundaries in a unified label space. Existing calibration methods (e.g., GatingAE, DUS) perform only a single-stage binary base/novel split, leaving intra-domain class confusion unresolved, particularly on fine-grained datasets. No prior framework simultaneously eliminates base-class dependency and hierarchically disentangles ambiguous classes within each domain.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB test image and routes it through a two-level hierarchical classifier: first to a base or novel subspace (via a multi-modal domain score), then to one of two sub-subspaces within that domain (via text-embedding similarity), before producing a final class label from the corresponding subspace-specific linear head. For novel classes lacking real images, the pipeline synthesizes 50 images per class at 1024×1024 resolution using Stable Diffusion XL conditioned on 10 GPT-4-generated diverse prompts, then filters them by comparing image-to-text similarity against novel versus base class embeddings. The frozen CLIP ViT encoder processes all images; only lightweight linear projection layers are trained per subspace.

## Method

HDG decomposes the joint label space hierarchically in two stages. First, inter-domain grouping computes a domain score combining image-to-image and image-to-text distances to base versus novel reference sets, with the optimal threshold T selected by maximizing Normalized Mutual Information between ground-truth domain labels and predicted assignments—eliminating heuristic thresholding. Second, intra-domain grouping applies K-means (K=2) on CLIP text embeddings of class names within each domain to create two sub-subspaces, routing test samples by the sign of the text-similarity difference to the two cluster anchors (threshold fixed at 0). For novel-class data synthesis, GPT-4 generates multi-perspective class descriptions that condition Stable Diffusion XL, and a concept-selection step retains only generated images whose CLIP image embedding is more similar to novel-class text than to base-class text. Each leaf subspace is assigned a frozen CLIP encoder plus a trainable linear classifier trained exclusively on in-subspace samples, preventing cross-domain interference. Depth=2 (one intra-domain split) yields the best trade-off; deeper recursion (Depth=3) degrades performance due to overfitting on smaller subsets.

## Results

HDG-CLIP (Depth=2, Tuning) achieves the best harmonic mean (H) across all three GZSC benchmarks: 94.3% on AwA2, 78.2% on CUB, and 77.1% on SUN. These results surpass the second-best competitors by 4.7% (vs. CLIP) on AwA2, 3.9% on CUB, and a substantial 21.4% (vs. VADS) on SUN in terms of H. Even the frozen variant (Depth=1, Frozen) outperforms the original CLIP on all datasets (e.g., H 91.6 vs. 89.6 on AwA2, 63.5 vs. 55.0 on CUB, 54.0 vs. 44.3 on SUN), confirming that hierarchical decomposition alone resolves label-space ambiguity without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDG-CLIP (Depth=2, Tuning) outperforms all 18 baselines on the harmonic mean metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest VLM-based competitors, it surpasses CLIP by 4.7% H on AwA2 (94.3 vs. 89.6) and 21.4% H on SUN (77.1 vs. 55.7 for VADS, the best visual-only method there). Compared to MaPLe, which fine-tunes prompts on base classes only, HDG-CLIP achieves near-40% novel-class accuracy (N=94.1 on AwA2 vs. 55.4 for MaPLe) while maintaining comparable base-class performance (B=94.5 vs. 94.7), demonstrating that domain-specific fine-tuning eliminates the base-novel trade-off inherent in unified prompt learning. Against SHIP, which combines generative prototypes with prompt tuning, HDG-CLIP improves H by 5.3% on AwA2 (94.3 vs. 89.0) and 21.1% on CUB (78.2 vs. 57.1), showing that hierarchical subspace partitioning resolves the intra-domain confusion that SHIP's flat calibration cannot address. Even without any fine-tuning, HDG-CLIP (Depth=1, Frozen) already beats CLIP on all three datasets, confirming that the grouping strategy itself—rather than the fine-tuning—drives the primary gains. The only case where performance degrades is at Depth=3, where excessive granularity leads to overfitting (e.g., H drops from 94.3 to 91.9 on AwA2), indicating an optimal hierarchy depth of 2 for these benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GZSC paradigm of flat, single-stage base/novel calibration (GatingAE, DUS) by introducing a hierarchical, multi-level decomposition of the label space. It repurposes three off-the-shelf components—CLIP for multi-modal feature extraction, GPT-4 + Stable Diffusion XL for novel-class data synthesis, and K-means on text embeddings for intra-domain clustering—into a unified divide-and-conquer framework that no prior GZSC method combines. Unlike prompt-learning approaches (MaPLe, CoOP) or generative-prototype methods (SHIP, HSVA) that fine-tune within a unified label space and inherit base-class bias, HDG trains subspace-specific linear classifiers in isolation, explicitly removing base-class dependency from the adaptation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc threshold calibration that has been the standard remedy for GZSC prediction bias, and the NMI-guided automatic threshold selection removes a manual hyperparameter that has plagued prior methods. However, the approach is constrained: depth=3 degrades performance (suggesting a narrow optimal granularity), the pipeline depends on expensive LLM and diffusion-model calls for every novel class, and evaluation is limited to three mid-scale benchmarks (50–717 classes). Open questions include whether the hierarchical depth can be made adaptive per dataset, whether the method scales to open-vocabulary settings with thousands of classes, and whether the LLM+diffusion synthesis bottleneck can be replaced by cheaper alternatives without sacrificing the diversity that drives the gains.
