## Problem

While egocentric pose estimation has seen significant progress, 3D human mesh recovery from head-mounted fisheye cameras remains underdeveloped, with existing methods like EgoHMR relying on diffusion models that are unsuitable for real-time applications and failing to explicitly address fisheye lens distortions. Annotated egocentric datasets are also scarce and tend to lack naturalistic head movements and the self-occlusions characteristic of real-world daily activities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251217/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 180-degree fisheye RGB image from a head-mounted camera without any prior rectification or undistortion, and produces SMPL shape and pose parameters (from which the 3D mesh is directly derived), camera translation, global orientation, and 3D/2D key joints. The key design choice is that the raw distorted fisheye image is fed directly into the network, with distortion handled internally via the position embedding rather than through a preprocessing correction step.

## Method

Fish2Mesh employs a Swin Transformer backbone augmented with a novel Egocentric Position Embedding (EPE) that projects each fisheye pixel onto a sphere via equirectangular coordinates, discretizes the resulting 3D spherical coordinates, and uses them as indices into a learnable position table, replacing standard sinusoidal or grid-based embeddings and removing the need for relative position bias. Three task-specific heads jointly predict SMPL parameters, camera transformation, and global orientation, trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining SMPL parameter regression, orientation, and auxiliary 3D-to-2D joint consistency terms. The training data is augmented by extending the ECHP dataset with a prompt-based collection system that captures naturalistic movements and self-occlusions, using the 4D-Human model as a weak supervisor for labeling.

## Results

Fish2Mesh achieves the best PA-MPJPE and PA-MPVPE across all three evaluation datasets. On ECHP, it reaches 57.67 mm PA-MPJPE and 75.32 mm PA-MPVPE, outperforming EgoHMR (64.11 / 79.03 mm) by 11.2% and 4.9% respectively. On Ego4D, the gap widens dramatically: Fish2Mesh scores 41.93 mm PA-MPJPE and 54.76 mm PA-MPVPE versus EgoHMR's 114.42 / 129.00 mm. On the authors' extended ECHP dataset, Fish2Mesh achieves 37.24 mm PA-MPJPE and 51.58 mm PA-MPVPE, while EgoHMR degrades to 127.55 / 144.18 mm, indicating strong generalization to naturalistic motion and self-occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fish2Mesh outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and every reported metric. Against EgoHMR (the closest competitor and the prior SOTA for egocentric HMR), Fish2Mesh improves PA-MPJPE by 11.2% on ECHP (57.67 vs. 64.11 mm), by 63.3% on Ego4D (41.93 vs. 114.42 mm), and by 70.7% on the extended ECHP dataset (37.24 vs. 127.55 mm), showing that EgoHMR's diffusion-based approach degrades sharply on out-of-distribution and occlusion-heavy data. Against 4D-Human, Fish2Mesh reduces PA-MPJPE by roughly 36–68% depending on dataset, and against FisheyeViT (a pose-estimation method without mesh output) the PA-MPJPE gap is 36–60%. The largest relative gains appear on the authors' own extended ECHP dataset, where EgoHMR's PA-MPJPE nearly triples compared to its performance on the original ECHP, while Fish2Mesh remains stable—attributed to the prompt-based data augmentation that captures natural head motion and self-occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of egocentric vision and 3D human mesh recovery, explicitly noting that while pose estimation has been applied to egocentric settings (EgoCap, EgoGlass, Mo2Cap2), HMR techniques have lagged behind. It departs from EgoHMR's diffusion-based approach by adopting a Swin Transformer backbone with a novel Egocentric Position Embedding that encodes equirectangular spherical geometry directly into the position table, rather than correcting fisheye distortion via explicit reprojection (as in Panoformer) or patch extraction (as in FisheyeViT). The prompt-based dataset augmentation extends ECHP by using 4D-Human as a weak supervisor, moving beyond the scripted, in-place actions of the original dataset toward naturalistic motion with realistic self-occlusions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EPE mechanism offers a lightweight, learnable alternative to explicit fisheye correction that could generalize to other spherical-projection vision tasks, and the ablation (PA-MPJPE jumping from 57.7 mm to 92.4 mm without EPE) suggests the geometric prior is genuinely critical rather than a marginal contributor. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with only three baselines, the extended ECHP dataset details are deferred to supplementary material, and the paper does not address real-time inference speed—a key requirement for the XR and assistive-robotics applications it cites. Open questions include whether EPE generalizes to non-180° fisheye lenses, different Swin variants, or multi-frame temporal settings, and whether the prompt-based collection protocol scales beyond a single lab setup.
