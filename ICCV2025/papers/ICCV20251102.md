## Problem

Medical language-guided segmentation methods require paired image-text reports during both training and inference, creating 'textual reliance' that underutilizes the majority of image-only data in medical datasets and confines deployment to retrospective analysis where reports already exist. This misaligns with most clinical workflows where segmentation must precede reporting for preoperative planning, diagnosis, and real-time procedural guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251102/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single medical image (chest X-ray, CT slice, or colonoscopy image) and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask without any textual input. During training, it accepts both paired image-text samples (used only once to initialize the prototype space) and image-only samples (used for standard supervised training). The textual reports are consumed exclusively in a one-time initialization phase via BioMedCLIP encoding and cross-attention-based token selection, after which no text is needed.

## Method

ProLearn introduces a Prototype-Driven Semantic Approximation (PSA) module that replaces the need for textual input with a discrete, compact prototype space. During a one-time initialization, segmentation-relevant tokens are extracted from clinical reports using cross-attention weights from a trained Language-guided U-Net, then clustered hierarchically (HDBSCAN for N semantic surrogate labels, K-means for M sub-clusters per label) to form N×M query-response prototype pairs, where each pair links a visual query prototype to a textual response prototype. At training and inference, an image feature encoded by BioMedCLIP queries the prototype space via cosine similarity; the top-k most similar query prototypes are selected, and their linked response prototypes are aggregated via a softmax-weighted sum to produce an approximate semantic guidance vector. This guidance vector is fed into the decoder of a Language-guided U-Net to refine feature maps and produce the segmentation mask, achieving O(1) inference with only ~1M parameters in the PSA module compared to O(n) autoregressive generation with 1.5B–7B parameters in LLM-based alternatives.

## Results

On QaTa-COV19, ProLearn achieves 0.8667 Dice at 50% text availability and retains 0.8566 Dice even at just 1% text, compared to SGSeg (0.7307) and LViT (0.7006) at the same 1% setting. On MosMedData+, the gap widens dramatically: ProLearn maintains 0.7218 Dice at 1% text while SGSeg collapses to 0.3452 and LViT to 0.1677. On Kvasir-SEG, ProLearn reaches 0.8983 Dice at 50% text and 0.8718 at 1%, substantially outperforming all language-guided baselines. In the image-only setting (Table 2), ProLearn with only 1% paired text surpasses all unimodal and VLP baselines, e.g., 0.857 Dice on QaTa-COV19 vs. 0.836 for Swin-UNet and 0.799 for GLoRIA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProLearn outperforms all three language-guided baselines (LViT, GuideSeg, SGSeg) across every dataset and every text-availability level in Table 1. The advantage is most pronounced at extreme text scarcity: on MosMedData+ at 1% text, ProLearn's 0.7218 Dice exceeds SGSeg (0.3452) by 37.7 points and LViT (0.1677) by 55.4 points. In the image-only setting (Table 2), ProLearn with only 1% paired text surpasses all unimodal CNN/Transformer baselines (U-Net 0.819, U-Net++ 0.823, Attention U-Net 0.822, TransUNet 0.806, Swin-UNet 0.836 Dice on QaTa-COV19) and VLP methods (CLIP 0.798, GLoRIA 0.799). It also beats the MedSAM foundation model (0.730 Dice) and BiomedParse (0.781 Dice) on QaTa-COV19, and outperforms BiomedParse on Kvasir-SEG (0.872 vs. 0.828 Dice). Compared to SGSeg specifically, ProLearn achieves a 1000× reduction in parameters (1M vs. 1.5B for GPT-2) and 100× faster inference (4 ms vs. 136 ms), while maintaining superior segmentation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the language-guided medical segmentation line (LViT, GuideSeg, MAdaptor, LGA) by replacing the explicit text-encoding pipeline with a compact prototype space that approximates semantic guidance from image features alone. It departs most directly from the authors' own prior work SGSeg, which used an LLM to generate synthetic reports at inference; ProLearn replaces that autoregressive generation with a one-shot cosine-similarity lookup over a pre-constructed prototype matrix, reducing parameters by ~1000×. Conceptually, it repurposes prototype learning (originally from few-shot classification) as a mechanism for cross-modal semantic transfer, leveraging the observation that clinical report semantics occupy a finite, standardized vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for clinical deployment: segmentation in most workflows (preoperative planning, real-time procedural guidance) occurs before a report is written, so requiring text at inference is fundamentally misaligned. ProLearn's 4 ms inference and 1 M parameter footprint make it viable on edge devices where LLM-based alternatives are infeasible. However, the prototype space is initialized once from a fixed set of paired samples and is not updated online, raising open questions about adaptability to new pathologies or distribution shifts. The authors acknowledge the work is limited to single-label 2D segmentation, leaving multi-label, 3D volumetric, and other language-guided vision tasks as future directions.
