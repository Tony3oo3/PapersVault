## Problem

No unified model exists for egocentric vision that jointly handles multiple modalities (RGB, depth, gaze, camera pose) and multiple tasks (tracking, gaze estimation, depth estimation, video synthesis). Existing approaches are either task-specific specialist models requiring geometry-based test-time optimization, or third-person video foundation models that fail to capture the dynamic camera motion and human-intention signals inherent to first-person capture. Additionally, egocentric datasets are heterogeneous with missing modalities, making standard multimodal supervised learning infeasible at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2-second clips at 30 FPS (videos downsampled to 8 FPS, 16 frames) at 256×256 resolution across four modalities: RGB video, inverse-depth video, 2D gaze coordinates (T×2), and camera-to-world poses parameterized as 6D rotation + translation (T×9). Any subset of modalities can be provided as input, with missing modalities represented as masked placeholders. The model outputs discrete tokens for any target modality, decoded back to the native format (e.g., a 60-frame camera trajectory in 0.18 s, or a 16-frame depth video in 0.8 s), enabling any-to-any cross-modal prediction in a single feed-forward pass.

## Method

EgoM2P builds a 4-billion-token multimodal database from eight egocentric datasets, tokenizing RGB and depth via the Cosmos video tokenizer (4× temporal, 8× spatial compression) and gaze/camera trajectory via a unified Transformer-based VQ-VAE with 12 blocks and cosine-similarity quantization. The main model follows T5-Base, with modality-specific embedding layers, sine-cosine positional encodings (1D for gaze/camera, 3D for video), and a learnable modality category embedding shared between input and target tokens. Training extends 4M's massively multimodal masked modeling to the video domain by handling missing modalities through placeholder masking, balancing highly imbalanced token counts (170× more video than gaze tokens) via dataset-proportional sampling followed by uniform token sampling, and increasing the visible-token budget from 256 to 2048. At inference, variable masking rates enable parallel decoding: the model predicts n target tokens over s steps using classifier-free guidance (ω-weighted combination of conditional and unconditional distributions) and nucleus sampling, achieving 300+ FPS for camera tracking without any geometry inductive bias.

## Results

On egocentric camera tracking (EgoExo4D), EgoM2P achieves ATE 0.017, RTE 0.004, and RRE 0.429 in 0.18 s per sequence, matching or beating specialist methods (DROID-SLAM, ACE-Zero, Align3R) that require 2.7–426 s of test-time optimization. For gaze estimation, EgoM2P attains the lowest MSE of 0.0162, outperforming Huang et al. (0.0255) and Lai et al. (0.0175). On monocular depth estimation (H2O), EgoM2P reaches Abs Rel 0.055 and δ₁.₂₅ 96.0% in 0.8 s, surpassing RollingDepth (0.087 / 90.5%) and Align3R (0.074 / 91.8%) while being 46–112× faster. For depth-to-RGB video synthesis on HoloAssist, EgoM2P achieves FVD 0.759, SSIM 0.592, and PSNR 15.16, substantially outperforming ControlVideo (FVD 1.363) and Control-A-Video (FVD 2.309).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On camera tracking, EgoM2P beats DROID-SLAM on ATE (0.017 vs 0.018) and RTE (0.004 vs 0.005) on EgoExo4D and on RTE (0.006 vs 0.010) on ADT, while trailing on RRE (0.490 vs 0.316 on ADT); it dominates ACE-Zero on all three EgoExo4D metrics (ATE 0.017 vs 0.028, RTE 0.004 vs 0.007, RRE 0.429 vs 0.672) and on ATE/RTE for ADT, and outperforms Align3R on ATE and RTE for EgoExo4D but loses on RRE for both splits. The speed advantage is dramatic: 0.18 s per sequence versus 2.7 s (DROID-SLAM), 372 s (Align3R), and 426 s (ACE-Zero). For gaze estimation, EgoM2P (MSE 0.0162) outperforms both Huang et al. (0.0255) and Lai et al. (0.0175). On depth estimation, EgoM2P wins on H2O (Abs Rel 0.055 vs 0.087 for RollingDepth, 0.074 for Align3R) but trails Align3R on the unseen HOI4D set (0.061 vs 0.045 Abs Rel), while being 46–112× faster. For video synthesis, EgoM2P clearly wins on HoloAssist across all four metrics (FVD 0.759 vs 1.363/2.309, SSIM 0.592 vs 0.235/0.185, PSNR 15.16 vs 8.18/9.25, LPIPS 0.336 vs 0.653/0.677) and on FVD for unseen ASE (1.336 vs 1.392/2.226), though it lags on PSNR for ASE (6.92 vs 10.46/11.11), suggesting the diffusion-based baselines produce sharper but less distributionally faithful frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoM2P extends the Massively Multimodal Masked Modeling (4M) paradigm from static images to egocentric video, addressing challenges 4M was not designed for: temporal dynamics, vastly larger token counts per sample, severe modality imbalance (170× more video tokens than gaze tokens), and inherently missing annotations that cannot be pseudo-labeled. For the model backbone it adopts T5-Base, for video tokenization it leverages the Cosmos tokenizer, and for non-video modalities (gaze, camera trajectory) it introduces a unified Transformer-based VQ-VAE tokenizer. This positions the work at the intersection of image foundation models (4M, ImageBind), video foundation models (VideoMAE, diffusion-based generators), and the nascent egocentric foundation model literature (EgoVLM, MMEgo), which has so far focused on video QA rather than 4D geometric understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoM2P demonstrates that a single feed-forward model trained without any 3D inductive bias or geometry constraints can match specialist SLAM and depth-estimation pipelines while being 15–2000× faster, suggesting that large-scale multimodal pretraining can subsume explicit geometric reasoning for egocentric perception. This has direct practical implications for AR/robotics where real-time processing on constrained hardware is essential. Key limitations include video synthesis quality bounded by the Cosmos tokenizer's quantization error, the absence of hand-motion, audio, and text modalities, and evaluation on relatively small test sets (200 clips for tracking, 236 for depth). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, whether a diffusion decoder over discrete tokens can close the visual-quality gap, and how the model performs on embedded GPUs for true real-time deployment.
