## Problem

Existing model and image watermarking methods for text-to-image generation can only trace a single role in the GenAI ecosystem—either the business that deploys the model or the consumer who generates content—but not both simultaneously. In a layered developer→business→consumer pipeline, this single-level limitation prevents hierarchical attribution of responsibility for model misuse or harmful content generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (T2I) or an input image plus editing instruction (I2I) and produces a 512×512 RGB image carrying two independent 32-bit binary fingerprints (64-bit total payload). At extraction time, two separate EfficientNet-B0-based decoders independently recover the business fingerprint m_b and consumer fingerprint m_c from the image, each yielding a 32-bit binary string compared against registered fingerprints with a matching threshold τ=28 out of 32 bits.

## Method

The core contribution is 2-head Fingerprint-Informed Low-Rank Adaptation (FI-LoRA), a pluggable module inserted into the VAE decoder of a pre-trained latent diffusion model. Each LoRA head introduces a fingerprint encoding network f(m) that maps a binary fingerprint to an r×r matrix Z, so the weight update becomes B·Z·A rather than the standard B·A, allowing the fingerprint to modulate every element of ΔW. The two heads share a common B matrix but have separate A_b, A_c matrices and encoding networks f_b, f_c; the developer merges the business head into the model weights while reserving the consumer head for the business to activate at generation time, preventing the business from knowing or tampering with m_b. Fine-tuning jointly optimizes LPIPS+MSE reconstruction loss and per-bit binary cross-entropy for both fingerprint decoders, with an attack-simulation augmentation layer (JPEG, Gaussian noise, blur) applied to generated images during training to improve robustness. The U-Net is left unmodified, so the method is model-agnostic across T2I and I2I tasks.

## Results

FI-LoRA embeds two independent 32-bit fingerprints (64 bits total) into generated images, achieving >99.9% Bit Acc for both the business fingerprint (m_b) and consumer fingerprint (m_c) across all four datasets (COCO, ImageNet, MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix), with 0.0% FPR at threshold τ=28. Under JPEG compression (qf=50), m_c retains 99.1–99.7% Bit Acc, while Sta.Sig drops to 80.2–81.5%; under 50% cropping, m_b retains 85.8–87.1% versus 76.5–79.5% for WOUAF. Image quality is well preserved: FID_r matches or slightly beats the no-fingerprint baseline (e.g., 23.272 vs. 23.265 on COCO, 19.979 vs. 19.922 on MagicBrush), and CLIP Score is unchanged at 0.658–0.659.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines embed a single 32-bit fingerprint, whereas FI-LoRA simultaneously embeds two 32-bit fingerprints (64 bits total), making the comparison particularly favorable to the proposed method. On clean Bit Acc, FI-LoRA outperforms Per. Norm. [14] by 0.14–0.29 pp, Sta. Sig. [20] by 0.39–0.44 pp, and WOUAF [24] by 0.55–0.65 pp across the four datasets. The gap widens substantially under image-level attacks: under JPEG (qf=50), Sta. Sig. degrades to 80.2–81.5% while FI-LoRA's m_c stays above 99.1%; under 50% cropping, WOUAF drops to 76.5–79.5% whereas FI-LoRA's m_b remains at 85.8–87.1%. Per. Norm. [14] is the closest competitor on crop robustness (84.4–85.3%) but still trails FI-LoRA's m_b by 1–3 pp. On image quality (Table 1), FI-LoRA achieves the best PSNR_b and LPIPS_b across all datasets and matches or slightly beats the no-fingerprint baseline on FID_r, indicating that the dual-fingerprint overhead does not degrade generation quality relative to single-fingerprint baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the single-fingerprint model watermarking line (Sta. Sig., WOUAF, Per. Norm.) and the in-generation image watermarking line (Tree-ring, Gaussian Shading, WM-Adapter) by unifying both into a dual-fingerprint framework for hierarchical attribution. Architecturally, it repurposes LoRA — originally a parameter-efficient fine-tuning tool — as a fingerprint encoding mechanism, inserting a fingerprint-derived matrix Z between the standard A and B factors so that different binary fingerprints can be swapped in without retraining. The 2-head design (shared B, separate A_b/A_c and encoding networks f_b/f_c) is the key structural novelty, enabling the developer to bake in a business fingerprint while reserving a second head for the business to embed consumer fingerprints at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in the commercial GenAI ecosystem: no prior method can simultaneously attribute content to both the licensed business and the end consumer, which is essential for legal accountability when model ownership and content creation are separated. The hierarchical control model (developer → 2 fingerprints, business → 1, consumer → 0) is a clean formalization that could generalize to deeper supply chains. Key limitations include the 32-bit-per-fingerprint capacity ceiling (64-bit single fingerprints fail to converge), the lack of full collusion-attack robustness, and evaluation limited to two model families (SD2-base, InstructPix2Pix). Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two attribution levels and how it performs under adaptive attacks that specifically target the LoRA structure.
