## Problem

Existing unsupervised part discovery methods either reconstruct images directly with part descriptors (which fails because descriptors capture high-level semantics rather than low-level appearance) or cluster foreground features (which misidentifies near-outlier features as separate parts). Moreover, most methods are restricted to a single category and assume all parts appear in every image, making them unable to handle occlusion or diverse object categories without labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, randomly masks 90% of its patches, and feeds only the visible patches into a ViT encoder. It produces K+1 pixel-level part masks (K foreground parts plus background) at the original image resolution by bilinearly interpolating the low-resolution similarity maps between part descriptors and a dense feature map from a frozen pretrained ViT. The part descriptors themselves (K+1 vectors of dimension C) are learned jointly and serve as mask prototypes at inference time.

## Method

MPAE (Masked Part Autoencoder) replaces direct image reconstruction with a descriptor-filling strategy: it computes softmax-normalized similarity maps between K+1 learned part descriptors and a dense feature map, then uses these maps to assign weighted descriptor combinations to fill masked regions before decoding. This restoration implicitly clusters unmasked patch features (carrying low-level appearance like boundaries and textures) with the high-level part descriptors in latent space, aligning descriptor semantics with actual part shapes. Three optimized constraints enforce robustness: a looser presence loss requiring parts to appear only within mini-groups rather than every image (enabling cross-category generalization and occlusion handling), an ArcFace-style angular margin loss ensuring semantic consistency among present parts, and a total-variation-plus-entropy distribution loss that produces connected, sharply-bounded part regions without the square-shape bias of prior concentration losses.

## Results

On PartImageNet OOD (K=50), MPAE achieves 53.65% NMI and 74.22% ARI, outperforming the best weakly-supervised method PDiscoFormer+ViT-B/14† (46.29% NMI, 62.21% ARI) by 7.36 and 12.01 points respectively, despite using no labels. On the more challenging PartImageNet Segmentation dataset (158 categories, K=50), MPAE reaches 55.10% NMI and 73.52% ARI, surpassing PDiscoFormer+ViT-B/14† (44.06% NMI, 60.10% ARI) by 11.04 and 13.42 points. On CUB (K=16), MPAE attains 79.00% NMI and 66.56% ARI, exceeding PDiscoFormer+ViT-B/14† (73.38% NMI, 55.83% ARI) by 5.62 and 10.73 points. On CelebA (K=16), MPAE achieves 70.21% NMI and 60.33% ARI, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline Xia et al. (58.35% NMI, 46.78% ARI) by 11.86 and 13.55 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPAE outperforms all seven baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest weakly-supervised competitor PDiscoFormer+ViT-B/14† (which uses category labels and a partially fine-tuned ViT-B/14), MPAE wins by 7.36% NMI on PartImageNet OOD and 11.04% NMI on PartImageNet Segmentation, despite being fully unsupervised with a frozen backbone. Among unsupervised methods, MPAE surpasses DINO [1] by 15.84% NMI (53.65 vs. 37.81) on PartImageNet OOD at K=50, and by 25.78% NMI (55.10 vs. 29.32) on PartImageNet Segmentation. The prior unsupervised method Xia et al. [40] is outperformed by 25.80% NMI on PartImageNet OOD and 29.93% NMI on PartImageNet Segmentation, with the gap widening on the more diverse Segmentation dataset. On CUB, MPAE beats PDiscoFormer+ViT-B/14† by 5.62% NMI and 10.73% ARI at K=16, and on CelebA it exceeds GANSeg [19] (K=8) by 2.93% NMI while also reporting K=16 results that no other unsupervised baseline provides. The consistent advantage across both multi-category and single-category settings confirms that the masked restoration paradigm and optimized constraints generalize beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPAE builds directly on the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE) for its random masking and encoder-decoder restoration pipeline, and on the descriptor-extraction approach from Xia et al. (TPAMI 2024) that replaces ViT class tokens with K+1 part embeddings. The key conceptual departure is that prior reconstruction-based methods (SCOPS, Liu et al.) feed part descriptors directly into a decoder to reconstruct the full image, which fails because those descriptors encode high-level semantics rather than low-level appearance. MPAE instead uses similarity maps between descriptors and a dense ViT feature map to selectively fill only masked regions, then restores using both the filled descriptors and unmasked patch features—implicitly clustering them and aligning semantics with appearance. The looser presence constraint (allowing parts to be absent per image) also departs from prior methods that enforce all parts in every image, enabling cross-category generalization without labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for category labels and allowing individual parts to be absent, MPAE addresses a practical bottleneck in part discovery: most prior methods are restricted to single-category datasets or require category labels to determine which parts should appear. The approach provides a foundation for handling occlusion and for leveraging part similarity across categories, which are important for real-world applications like fine-grained recognition and behavior analysis. Open questions include whether the method scales to very large part counts (K >> 50), whether the reliance on a frozen pretrained ViT backbone limits adaptability to novel domains, and whether the paradigm extends naturally to video or 3D part discovery. The evaluation is limited to four relatively small benchmarks, leaving scalability to large-scale datasets untested.
