## Problem

Existing joint diffusion methods for long-form generation (MultiDiffusion, MAD, SyncDiffusion) rely on a step-wise averaging operator to merge overlapping subview latents, which excessively suppresses high-frequency components in mel-spectrogram VAE latents, causing spectrum aliasing (visual blurriness, auditory distortion, monotonous tails) in audio generation. Additionally, these methods either require high overlap rates, expensive gradient-based optimization (LPIPS loss), or architecture-specific attention merging to achieve cross-view consistency, leaving no efficient, architecture-agnostic solution for both long-form audio and panorama generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pretrained text-to-audio or text-to-image diffusion model (U-Net or DiT) as input, operating entirely in the VAE latent space without any additional training. For audio, it produces long-form mel-spectrograms (e.g., 24s at 16kHz from three 10s segments with 0.2 overlap) that are converted to waveforms via a HiFi-GAN vocoder; for images, it produces 512×3200 panoramas from 512×640 subviews. The method is feed-forward and training-free, requiring only two swap operators applied during the standard denoising loop.

## Method

SaFa replaces the standard averaging merge in joint diffusion with two binary latent swap operators. First, the Self-Loop Latent Swap performs frame-level bidirectional swaps in overlapping regions of adjacent subviews at every denoising step (swap interval w=1), alternately selecting the right slice of subview i or the left slice of subview i+1 rather than averaging them; this acts as an adaptive high-pass filter that preserves the high-frequency variability inherent to spectrum VAE latents. Second, the Reference-Guided Latent Swap applies a unidirectional frame-level swap in non-overlapping regions during the first r_guide×T denoising steps (r_guide=0.3), periodically replacing each subview's non-overlapping latent with a shared reference trajectory to enforce cross-view consistency without repetition. The key insight is that spectrum VAE latents exhibit high-frequency variability (validated via Fourier analysis and a channel-wise linear connectivity-inheritance argument) that the averaging operator destroys, while the hard binary swap preserves subcomponents from step-wise differentiated trajectories that share a common initial overlap state. The approach is modality-agnostic (audio and image), architecture-agnostic (U-Net and DiT), and requires no additional training or test-time optimization.

## Results

On long-form audio generation (24s, 16kHz), SaFa achieves FD 6.84 / FAD 4.91 / KL 0.73 / CLAP 0.54 with DiT and FD 7.88 / FAD 4.27 / KL 1.11 / CLAP 0.53 with U-Net, approaching reference-level quality (FD 2.92, FAD 0.22 for DiT) while significantly outperforming all joint diffusion baselines. On panorama generation (512×3200), SaFa achieves FID 22.54 / KID 4.53 with SD 3.5 and FID 34.71 / KID 9.91 with SD 2.0, while running 2–20× faster than MAD and SyncDiffusion (19.15s vs. 41.82s and 390.63s for U-Net). SaFa also maintains stable performance at 48s and 72s audio lengths with negligible metric degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaFa outperforms MultiDiffusion (MD) by a large margin on all quality metrics: FD drops from 11.31 to 6.84 (DiT) and 14.29 to 7.88 (U-Net), while CLAP improves from 0.51 to 0.54, confirming that the latent swap operator avoids the spectrum aliasing caused by MD's averaging. The enhanced MD* (triangular window) narrows the gap (FD 9.79 DiT) but still trails SaFa by ~30% on FD and FAD. MAD performs worst among joint diffusion baselines (FD 12.77 DiT, 16.33 U-Net) due to position-embedding repetition in DiT and block-wise averaging exacerbating aliasing. On panorama generation, SaFa achieves FID 22.54 (SD 3.5) vs. MD's 24.50 and MAD's 65.10, while running 2× faster than MD (49.54s vs. 103.85s) and 12.6× faster than SyncDiffusion (49.54s vs. 623.59s); the only metric where SaFa trails is I-LPIPS vs. SyncDiffusion (0.56 vs. 0.57), which directly optimizes LPIPS loss. Against training-based methods (AudioGen, Stable Audio, Make-An-Audio2), SaFa outperforms across various generation lengths per Appendix Table 5, demonstrating that the training-free latent swap approach matches or exceeds dedicated long-form audio models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint diffusion framework for long-form generation (established for panoramas by MultiDiffusion, MAD, and SyncDiffusion) to the audio spectrum domain, where it previously failed due to a modality-specific artifact the authors term 'spectrum aliasing.' The core methodological departure is replacing the step-wise averaging operator in overlap regions with a binary latent swap operator, motivated by a Fourier analysis showing that averaging excessively suppresses high-frequency components in mel-spectrogram VAE latents. The Reference-Guided Latent Swap further departs from SyncDiffusion's gradient-based LPIPS optimization by achieving cross-view consistency through a feed-forward unidirectional swap with an independent reference trajectory, eliminating test-time optimization entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SaFa fills a practical gap: joint diffusion methods were previously unusable for long-form audio generation due to spectrum aliasing, and training-based alternatives (AudioGen, Stable Audio) incur high training costs and prompt sensitivity. The 2–20× speedup with fewer subviews and architecture-agnostic applicability (U-Net and DiT) make it a drop-in replacement for the averaging step in existing joint diffusion pipelines. Open questions remain regarding applicability to 1D wave-based VAE latents and discrete token-based representations, and the method's reliance on the step-wise differentiated trajectory property (Eq. 7) has not been formally proven to hold across all diffusion schedules or noise levels.
