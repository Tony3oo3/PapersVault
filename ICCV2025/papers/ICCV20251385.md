## Problem

Most machine unlearning (MU) methods rely on model-based weight adjustments to erase data influence, with little exploration of how data-level modifications could proactively facilitate the unlearning process. This paper asks whether digital watermarking—a controlled, imperceptible data perturbation—can be strategically designed to make unlearning easier, and how watermarking interacts with existing MU algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251385/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18 for classification, Stable Diffusion v1.5 for generation), a training dataset with a designated forget set (random 10% of data points, 10% of classes, or specific text prompts), and an L-bit binary watermark message. It produces watermarked training images (visually near-identical to originals via the HIDDEN encoder) and an unlearned model whose weights have been adjusted to remove the forget-set influence. Evaluation is performed on both watermarked and unwatermarked test sets, measuring unlearning accuracy, MIA efficacy, retain accuracy, and testing accuracy (or FID for generation).

## Method

WATER4MU formulates a bi-level optimization where the upper level (leader) optimizes the watermarking encoder-decoder parameters (ψ, φ) to minimize a combined objective of unlearning validation loss on the original unwatermarked data plus watermark reconstruction/decoding loss, while the lower level (follower) solves the GradDiff unlearning objective on the watermarked forget and retain sets. The two levels are coupled through ψ, which determines the watermarked data seen by the lower-level unlearning. The implicit gradient of the upper-level objective with respect to ψ is computed via the Implicit Function Theorem, with a diagonal Hessian approximation (∇²_θθ ℓ_mu ≈ λI) that reduces the computation to first-order derivatives of two scalar functions, enabling standard gradient descent. The framework is agnostic to the specific MU method (GA, FT, Sparse, IU, Retrain) and extends naturally to prompt-wise forgetting in diffusion models and to watermark message selection as an additional optimization variable.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, random data forgetting), WATER4MU improves unlearning accuracy (UA) by 7.2–16.0 points across approximate methods: GA gains the most (37.29→53.25 UA, +15.96), followed by FT (41.84→49.03, +7.19), Sparse (87.09→94.60, +7.51), and IU (92.03→98.49, +6.46). MIA efficacy improves by 0.7–13.3 points, with GA showing the largest gain (55.96→69.24). On UnlearnCanvas (Stable Diffusion), WATER4MU achieves the best balance between unlearning and retainability: style unlearning UA 93.49 with IRA 92.75 and CRA 94.01, and object unlearning UA 82.24 with IRA 85.60 and CRA 81.52, while maintaining FID of 86.62 (vs. 65.55 for ESD, 131.37 for FMN, 182.01 for UCE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WATER4MU is not a standalone unlearning method but a watermarking augmentation that enhances any existing MU method. Compared to Retrain (exact unlearning), WATER4MU-integrated Retrain maintains perfect UA/MIA (100%) while incurring a small TA drop (91.20→89.95), confirming that watermarking does not degrade the gold standard. Against approximate methods, WATER4MU consistently improves UA and MIA: GA benefits most (+15.96 UA, +13.28 MIA), FT gains +7.19 UA and +6.31 MIA, Sparse gains +7.51 UA and +8.09 MIA, and IU gains +6.46 UA and +0.68 MIA. The trade-off is a modest utility cost: RA drops 0.1–4.3 points and TA drops 0.1–1.3 points depending on the method. For image generation on UnlearnCanvas, WATER4MU outperforms ESD, FMN, and UCE on retainability (IRA/CRA >80% in both style and object settings) while maintaining competitive UA (93.49 style, 82.24 object) and the best FID among methods with high retainability (86.62 vs. 131.37 for FMN and 182.01 for UCE). On the worst-case 'challenging forgets' benchmark, WATER4MU still provides consistent gains (e.g., GA on ImageNet class-wise: UA 42.49→50.21, +7.72), demonstrating robustness in the hardest unlearning scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously disconnected areas: digital watermarking (building on HIDDEN [31] as the encoder-decoder backbone) and machine unlearning (comparing against Retrain, GA, FT, Sparse, and IU). The authors explicitly note that the relationship between watermarking and MU 'has remained largely unexplored,' positioning WATER4MU as the first systematic study of their interaction. Methodologically, the bi-level optimization framework draws on standard BLO techniques (implicit function theorem, Hessian diagonalization) to couple a watermarking design objective with an unlearning objective, repurposing a well-known optimization paradigm for a new data-model co-design problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a data-centric lever for MU that complements the dominant model/weight-centric approaches, and the finding that watermarking is largely orthogonal to unlearning (P1) provides useful reassurance for practitioners who watermark data for provenance tracking. However, the practical gains are modest (typically 5–15 percentage points in UA/MIA on standard forgets, and small absolute improvements on challenging forgets), and the utility tradeoff (RA/TA drops of 1–4 points) is consistent with the known unlearning-utility tension. Open questions include whether the BLO watermark design generalizes beyond HIDDEN to other watermarking architectures, whether it extends to non-image modalities (text, audio), and whether the watermark message selection variant can be made more scalable than the current 20-epoch optimization.
