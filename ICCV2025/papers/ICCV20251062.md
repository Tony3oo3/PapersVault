## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: effective methods (CA, MACE) maintain image quality but are vulnerable to adversarial regeneration attacks (CCE, UnlearnDiff), while robust methods (STEREO) resist attacks at the cost of severely degraded image quality (FID nearly doubled). No prior method achieves both robustness and effectiveness simultaneously for narrow concepts—specific instances like celebrities, copyrighted characters, or fine-grained subclasses—where the target concept sits close to non-target neighbors in embedding space, requiring finer-grained manipulation than NSFW erasure.

## Input / Output

The erasure procedure takes a target concept (e.g., 'Grumpy Cat', 'Elon Musk', 'Van Gogh style') and an original Stable Diffusion model (SD1.4 or SD1.5) as inputs, and produces a modified diffusion model whose cross-attention weights have been fine-tuned. During the Subspace Construction stage, the method consumes iterative Textual Inversion tokens (500-step TI runs) from the concept-erased model to build subspace bases at each U-Net layer. At inference time, the modified model accepts standard text prompts and generates 512×512 RGB images, with the target concept replaced by its reference concept (e.g., 'Grumpy Cat' → 'Cat', 'Elon Musk' → generic person).

## Method

SuMa operates in two stages. First, Subspace Construction iteratively applies Concept Ablation to erase the target concept, then runs Textual Inversion on the erased model to collect tokens: final-step TI tokens form the Target Subspace basis, while early-step (step 50) TI tokens form the Reference Subspace basis, both transformed through cross-attention weights W_k at each U-Net layer. Second, Subspace Elimination fine-tunes W_k to minimize a projection loss L_proj that maps all Target Subspace basis vectors onto the Reference Subspace, combined with a regularization term L_reg that anchors the Reference Subspace in place to prevent drift. The key insight is that a concept is best represented as a subspace (validated by showing ≥3 TI tokens suffice to span the concept space with cosine similarity >0.6), and mapping to a nearby reference subspace—rather than pushing the target away—minimizes weight modifications and preserves image quality. The final objective combines the subspace mapping loss with the standard CA loss, which redirects the target concept's noise prediction trajectory toward its general term, ensuring the SDE trajectory is properly altered for multi-token erasure.

## Results

Across four erasure tasks (subclass, identity, artistic style, instance), SuMa achieves an Attack Success Rate (ASR) below 0.2 under both CCE and UnlearnDiff attacks, matching the robustness of STEREO (ASR 0.00–0.05) within a margin of 0.02–0.14. On image quality, SuMa reports FID scores of 17.18–22.34 and CLIP scores of 0.29–0.33, comparable to E-CEM baselines (CA: FID 18.0–18.4) and roughly 9 points lower than STEREO (FID 26.3–28.0). The Instance category is the hardest to erase, requiring 750 fine-tuning steps and yielding a slightly higher FID (22.34) than the other categories (17.2–18.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuMa is compared against six baselines spanning both E-CEMs (CA, MACE) and R-CEMs (RACE, AdvUnlearn, DUO, STEREO). CA and MACE, while maintaining good image quality (FID 16.6–19.3), remain highly vulnerable to CCE attacks with ASR of 0.78–0.97 across all categories, confirming that effective-only erasure is insufficient. RACE and AdvUnlearn defend against UnlearnDiff (ASR 0.03–0.52) but fail under CCE (ASR 0.74–0.95), because they only erase tokens identified by UnlearnDiff rather than the full concept subspace. DUO, the only prior method combining robustness and effectiveness, is limited to NSFW concepts and fails on narrow concepts (ASR 0.65–0.92). STEREO achieves the lowest ASR (0.00–0.05) but at a severe quality cost, with FID roughly double that of E-CEMs (26.3–28.0 vs. 17–19). SuMa strikes the best trade-off: its ASR (0.05–0.19) is within 0.02–0.14 of STEREO while its FID (17.2–22.3) is 9–11 points lower, matching or exceeding E-CEM quality on three of four categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuMa sits at the intersection of two generations of concept erasure methods: it adopts the iterative Textual Inversion procedure from STEREO to construct a target subspace, and uses Concept Ablation (CA) as its base erasure loss, but departs from both by introducing a subspace-mapping objective that projects the target subspace onto a nearby reference subspace rather than pushing it away. This mapping strategy is motivated by the observation that CA-style mapping preserves image quality better than ESD-style pushing, and the reference subspace is derived from early TI training tokens that naturally converge toward general forms. The paper positions itself as complementary to DUO, which handles broad NSFW concepts via Diffusion-DPO, by targeting the harder regime of narrow concepts (specific characters, celebrities, artistic styles) where editing-based approaches fail due to the fine-grained similarity between target and non-target concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve adversarial robustness (ASR < 0.2 under both CCE and UnlearnDiff) and image quality comparable to E-CEMs (FID within ~1.3 of the original model) for narrow concepts, addressing a real gap in copyright and legal compliance for generative AI. The mechanistic insight that three or more TI iterations suffice to span the target concept subspace (cosine similarity > 0.6) provides a clear, interpretable criterion for when erasure is complete. Key limitations include the iterative construction cost (multiple TI + CA cycles), the method's reduced effectiveness on NSFW concepts (where DUO is preferable), and the reliance on a fixed early-TI step (50) for reference subspace selection that may not generalize across all concept types. Open questions include whether the subspace construction can be accelerated, whether the approach extends to video diffusion models, and how it interacts with emerging safety alignment methods.
