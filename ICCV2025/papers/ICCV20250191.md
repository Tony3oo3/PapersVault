## Problem

Although LoRA fine-tuning of pre-trained perspective diffusion models empirically succeeds at text-to-panorama generation, the underlying mechanism enabling this transfer across the substantial domain gap between perspective and equirectangular images remains unexplained. The paper asks: which specific trainable components within the attention modules are actually responsible for learning panoramic structure, and which merely preserve pre-trained perspective knowledge?

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a living room with a fireplace') and produces a single equirectangular 360-degree panoramic image with a 2:1 aspect ratio, at 512×1024 resolution by default and up to 1024×2048 when scaled with Stable Diffusion 3. The output encodes a full 360° horizontal and 180° vertical field of view in a single image, with characteristic spherical distortion at the poles.

## Method

The paper first isolates and ablates each trainable weight matrix (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) in the cross-attention blocks during LoRA fine-tuning, revealing that W_q and W_k fail to capture equirectangular distortion when trained in isolation and, when jointly trained, learn only domain-shared semantic knowledge, while W_v and W_o are responsible for the spherical structure. Based on this analysis, UniPano freezes W_q and W_k entirely, applies standard LoRA to W_v, and replaces the W_o LoRA with a Mixture-of-Experts module (4 experts, top-2 routing) to boost its representational capacity for panoramic-specific adaptation. This uni-branch design eliminates the dual-branch architecture of PanFusion, reducing peak GPU memory by ~45% and training time by ~48% relative to PanFusion while achieving state-of-the-art FAED (5.90) and horizontal 8-view FID (17.74) on Matterport3D.

## Results

On 512×1024 text-to-panorama generation (Matterport3D), UniPano achieves the best FAED (5.90) and best 8-view FID (17.74), while tying PanFusion on panorama FID (46.47) and 20-view FID (17.09 vs. 17.04). UniPano requires only 32.59 GB peak GPU memory and 3.43 hours of training, compared to PanFusion's 60.12 GB (+89.7%) and 6.61 hours. The method also scales end-to-end to 1024×2048 panorama generation using Stable Diffusion 3, which PanFusion's dual-branch architecture cannot support at that resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPano outperforms PanFusion (the prior SoTA dual-branch method) on FAED (5.90 vs. 6.04) and 8-view FID (17.74 vs. 19.88), while matching it on panorama FID (both 46.47) and 20-view FID (17.09 vs. 17.04). Against the SD+LoRA baseline, UniPano improves FAED from 7.19 to 5.90 and 8-view FID from 20.68 to 17.74, at only a 2.8% memory overhead and ~1.2 hours additional training. Compared to Pano Only (which adds circular padding to SD+LoRA), UniPano reduces FAED from 7.90 to 5.90 and 8-view FID from 20.56 to 17.74. MVDiffusion, which generates 8 perspective views and stitches them, reports only 8-view metrics (FID 25.27) and is substantially worse than UniPano (17.74). The key practical advantage is that UniPano's single-branch design uses roughly half the GPU memory of PanFusion (32.59 vs. 60.12 GB), enabling end-to-end generation at 1024×2048 resolution without a separate super-resolution stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the empirical observation from PanFusion (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024) that standard LoRA fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion on limited panoramic data yields reasonable text-to-panorama generation, and asks *why* this works despite the structural gap between perspective and equirectangular images. It departs from the dual-branch architecture of PanFusion and the multi-view stitching approaches (MVDiffusion, MultiDiffusion) by proposing a uni-branch solution grounded in a mechanistic analysis of which LoRA components (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) actually encode panoramic-specific structure. The work is positioned as an interpretability study of parameter-efficient fine-tuning in diffusion models, with UniPano serving as a simple, efficient baseline rather than a complex architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that W_q and W_k in cross-attention learn only shared perspective/panorama semantics while W_v and W_o carry the equirectangular distortion provides a practical recipe: freeze Q/K LoRAs and invest capacity (e.g., MoE) in V/O, reducing memory by ~87% relative to PanFusion and enabling end-to-end 1024×2048 generation. However, the analysis is confined to a single dataset (Matterport3D, ~10K images) and one base model family (Stable Diffusion 2/3), leaving open whether the Q/K-irrelevance finding generalizes to other domain shifts (e.g., 3D, video, or different projection geometries). The paper also acknowledges persistent failure modes (invalid room layouts, missing entrances) shared with PanFusion, and notes that the MoE hyperparameter choices are far from optimal, suggesting room for further refinement.
