## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for monocular 3D object detection only optimize semantic uncertainty (prediction entropy) while ignoring geometric uncertainty (unstable depth/spatial localization), which is a critical distinction from 2D tasks. The paper identifies two specific failures: entropy minimization neglects low-score objects due to foreground-background imbalance, and direct depth uncertainty minimization causes multi-head depth estimator collapse. No prior TTA framework jointly addresses both uncertainties in a label-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and produces 3D bounding boxes parameterized by center position (P∈R³), dimensions (D∈R³), and orientation (O∈[-π,π]) along with semantic class labels. During TTA, the model operates online: each incoming test image triggers both a detection forward pass and a parameter update via the DUO loss, requiring no ground-truth labels. Internally, the depth map is upsampled to full image resolution and converted to a surface normal field via Sobel operators, which feeds the geometric constraint branch.

## Method

DUO comprises two complementary branches. The semantic branch derives a Conjugate Focal Loss (CFL) by reformulating the standard focal loss in Legendre-Fenchel convex structure, then applying higher-order approximation to obtain a label-agnostic surrogate that dynamically reweights all classes via an inverse matrix term encoding inter-class prediction relationships—this resolves the low-score object neglect inherent in vanilla entropy minimization. The geometric branch computes a surface normal field from the predicted depth map using Sobel operators and enforces an edge-aware Normal Consistency Loss (NCL) that penalizes local normal discontinuities, but only within regions selected by low semantic uncertainty (via an EMA threshold on CFL-derived scores), preventing the multi-head depth collapse observed with direct uncertainty regression. The two branches form a feedback loop: confident semantic regions bootstrap geometric feature learning, while improved spatial perception in turn sharpens semantic predictions. Hyperparameters α and γ are inherited directly from the source focal loss training, requiring no additional tuning.

## Results

On KITTI-C (severity level 5) with MonoFlex as the base model, DUO achieves an average AP_3D|R40 of 22.97 for Car, 7.19 for Pedestrian, and 5.10 for Cyclist across 13 corruption types, yielding a +2.2 AP improvement over the strongest baseline (MonoTTA) in the Car category. With MonoGround, DUO reaches 24.73 average AP for Car. On real-world nuScenes scenarios (Daytime↔Night, Sunny↔Rainy), DUO delivers an average gain of +18% over existing methods, e.g., 12.38 AP for Sunny→Rainy with MonoFlex versus 11.01 for MonoTTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every category and both base models. On KITTI-C (MonoFlex, Car category), DUO achieves 22.97 average AP, surpassing MonoTTA (20.87, +2.10), DeYO (20.30, +2.67), EATA (20.03, +2.94), and TENT (19.68, +3.29). The margin is most pronounced under noise-type corruptions (e.g., Gaussian: 7.30 vs. 5.93 for MonoTTA) and motion blur (6.89 vs. 3.21), where geometric uncertainty is most severe. On nuScenes real-world shifts, DUO leads MonoTTA by +2.13 AP on Daytime→Night (MonoFlex) and by +1.37 AP on Sunny→Rainy (MonoFlex), confirming that the dual-uncertainty mechanism generalizes beyond synthetic corruptions to natural domain shifts. No baseline matches DUO on any individual corruption type or scenario, indicating a consistent rather than selective advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm from 2D classification/segmentation to monocular 3D object detection by identifying a task-specific 'dual uncertainty' structure (semantic + geometric) that prior TTA methods (TENT, EATA, DeYO, MonoTTA) overlook. Theoretically, it builds on the conjugate pseudo-label framework of Goyal et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and the focal loss of Lin et al. (ICCV 2017), reformulating the latter through a Legendre-Fenchel structure to derive a label-agnostic Conjugate Focal Loss. The geometric branch departs from prior uncertainty-aware depth estimation (which typically requires additional training or data) by introducing a lightweight Sobel-based normal field constraint that operates purely on the predicted depth map at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-uncertainty framing provides a principled decomposition that is specific to multi-task 3D perception and does not reduce to the single-entropy minimization used in 2D TTA, offering a template for other multi-attribute 3D tasks. The model-collapse finding (Observation 2) is a useful caution: naively minimizing predicted depth uncertainty degenerates the multi-head estimator, so geometric regularization must be indirect. Limitations include evaluation primarily on synthetic corruptions (KITTI-C) and only four real-world nuScenes scenarios; the complementary-loop claim rests on visualization rather than controlled ablation of cross-branch gradients, and the method inherits the base model's architectural assumptions (multi-head depth). Extending the framework to BEV-based detectors or multi-camera settings remains open.
