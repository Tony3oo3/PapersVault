## Problem

CLIP-based vision-language models excel at zero-shot classification but struggle to establish spatial dependencies among scene objects due to their holistic image-level pre-training objective, causing sub-optimal performance in dense perception tasks like open-vocabulary semantic segmentation. Meanwhile, diffusion models encode rich scene spatial priors (e.g., distributional associations between objects) but have only been used as data generators or alternative feature extractors, leaving their intrinsic scene-structure knowledge untapped for perception.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (cropped to 640×640 during training) and free-form text category descriptions as input, producing a pixel-level segmentation mask with per-pixel class labels. Unusually, during training the image's latent code is treated as a noisy sample fed into a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 denoising process (3 iterations at timesteps [50, 100, 200]), while at inference all diffusion-related steps are omitted entirely, keeping the pipeline as efficient as a standard CLIP + Mask2Former decoder. The backbone is CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 with a Mask2Former decoder augmented by cost aggregation.

## Method

DEDOS introduces learnable proxy queries that interact with features at every layer of the diffusion model's noise predictor via cross-attention, progressively constructing the 'scene skeleton' (implicit spatial dependencies among objects) across iterative denoising steps. To capture multi-level scene structure, proxy queries are decomposed into a shared basic kernel and stage-specific diffusion kernels combined via Kronecker product, with global queries aggregated from max, mean, and per-stage components. A consistency loss aligns CLIP feature similarity matrices before and after denoising (which differ mainly in texture), forcing proxy queries to prioritize scene layout over texture details. Finally, the optimized proxy queries refine CLIP features through dot-product similarity maps and serve as an interface for a text aggregation module that integrates object embeddings with text embeddings for classification, enabling multi-level cross-modal interaction.

## Results

On standard open-vocabulary semantic segmentation benchmarks, DEDOS achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all six evaluation settings. With CLIP ViT-L/14, DEDOS reaches 25.6 mIoU on the challenging PC-459 (459-class Pascal Context), 39.4 on A-150 (ADE20K), 65.7 on PC-59, and 97.6 on PAS-20, outperforming the previous best (CAT-Seg) by 1.8–2.4 mIoU. With the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, DEDOS achieves 21.3 mIoU on PC-459, surpassing even ViT-L/14-based methods such as SAN (15.7) and ODISE (14.5). On the multi-domain MESS benchmark, DEDOS attains a mean mIoU of 38.8 (ViT-L/14) and 35.9 (ViT-B/16), leading all competitors by 3.1 and 3.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEDOS consistently outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark and both backbone scales. The closest competitor, CAT-Seg (ViT-L/14), is surpassed by DEDOS by 1.9 mIoU on A-847, 1.8 on PC-459, 1.5 on A-150, 2.4 on PC-59, 0.6 on PAS-20, and 2.1 on PAS-20b. With the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, DEDOS (21.3 on PC-459) exceeds ViT-L/14-based methods including SAN (15.7), ODISE (14.5), and OVSeg (12.4), demonstrating that the diffusion-derived scene skeleton compensates for backbone capacity. On the multi-domain MESS benchmark, DEDOS leads CAT-Seg by 3.9 mIoU (ViT-B/16: 35.9 vs 32.0) and 4.1 mIoU (ViT-L/14: 38.8 vs 34.7), with particularly large gains in the General (+3.9) and Agriculture/Biology (+2.1) domains. The only domain where DEDOS does not lead is Engineering (23.5 vs CAT-Seg's 20.2, but still best overall), where the authors note diffusion models may have limited understanding of multispectral and electromagnetic imagery. DEDOS also uses fewer learnable parameters (23.57M vs CAT-Seg's 70.27M) and achieves faster inference (0.12s vs 0.37s), indicating superior efficiency alongside accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work simultaneously. On the diffusion-model side, prior approaches (ODISE, DiffusionDet, DGInStyle) treat pre-trained diffusion models as feature extractors or synthetic data generators; DEDOS instead mines the *internal* scene priors encoded in the denoising process by injecting learnable proxy queries into the frozen U-Net layers. On the open-vocabulary segmentation side, prior methods (OpenSeg, MaskCLIP, ZegCLIP, SAN, CAT-Seg) focus on refining CLIP's image-level representations to pixel-level granularity; DEDOS instead augments CLIP features with a scene-skeleton signal extracted from diffusion, positioning the diffusion model as a spatial-relationship prior rather than a feature source. The Kronecker-product decomposition of proxy queries into a shared basic kernel and stage-specific diffusion kernels is a new mechanism not seen in prior learnable-query designs (DETR, Mask2Former, MDETR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'image as noisy label' reframing offers a principled way to extract spatial/contextual priors from generative models for perceptual tasks, potentially generalizable beyond segmentation to detection or depth estimation. Practically, DEDOS achieves SOTA on five benchmarks while using fewer learnable parameters (23.6M vs. 70.3M for CAT-Seg) and substantially faster inference (0.12s vs. 0.37s), since the diffusion model is only invoked during training. A key open question is whether the scene-skeleton signal extracted from a single frozen diffusion model captures sufficient diversity for truly open-world scenes, and the authors themselves note degraded performance on multispectral (medical, engineering) domains where the diffusion prior may be weak. Future work could explore whether multiple or domain-adapted diffusion models yield complementary scene priors.
