## Problem

Existing input-level backdoor defenses for text-to-image diffusion models rely on the Trigger Dominance assumption, which fails when backdoors target only specific image patches, objects, or styles rather than the entire output. Moreover, the two prior T2I-specific defenses (T2IShield, UFID) incur 2–5× the computational cost of normal generation, making real-time deployment impractical. The paper asks whether a more general and efficient signal exists for detecting malicious prompts without executing the full diffusion pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (up to 77 tokens) and white-box access to a T2I diffusion model (UNet or DiT). For each non-stopword token it performs a single forward pass at the first diffusion step only (not the full 50–100 denoising iterations), computing layer-wise activation differences. The output is a per-token activation-variation score vector, a scalar detection score, and a binary malicious/benign decision against a threshold derived from Gaussian fitting on local clean data.

## Method

NaviT2I exploits the newly identified Early-step Activation Variation phenomenon: trigger tokens induce disproportionately large neuron activation changes in the initial diffusion steps, a property theoretically grounded by Theorem 4.1, which bounds the condition-dependent prediction difference by O(1/α · exp(−1/2σ_t²)) that decays exponentially as t decreases. Concretely, the method masks each non-stopword token, computes a layer-wise 1-norm activation variation (spatially averaged for convolutional layers, element-wise for linear/attention layers) at the first denoising step, and normalizes by the embedding-space distance between the original and masked prompts. A scoring function takes the maximum per-token variation divided by the mean of non-outlier tokens (75th-percentile cutoff), and a backdoor-agnostic threshold is set via Gaussian fitting on clean-data scores. Because only the first diffusion step is executed per masked token (≈7 forward passes for a typical 6-token prompt), the total cost is roughly 7–14% of a full image generation, versus 50–200 iterations for baselines.

## Results

On MS-COCO, NaviT2I achieves an average AUROC of 96.3% across eight diverse T2I backdoor attacks, compared to 78.0% for the best baseline (T2IShield_CDA) and 69.3% for UFID. Detection accuracy (ACC) reaches 89.3% on average versus 67.6% (T2IShield_CDA) and 59.0% (UFID). In terms of efficiency, NaviT2I requires only ≈7 diffusion iterations per sample (1.242 s), compared to 50 iterations (7.445 s) for T2IShield and 200 iterations (33.041 s) for UFID, representing a 16.7% and 3.8% time cost relative to those baselines respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaviT2I outperforms both baselines across all eight backdoor attack types in AUROC, with the largest gaps on attacks that violate the Trigger Dominance assumption: BadT2I_Tok (97.0 vs. 62.1 for T2IShield_CDA), BadT2I_Sent (89.7 vs. 70.7), PersonalBKD (99.8 vs. 68.5), and EvilEdit (85.5 vs. 57.8). T2IShield_CDA is the strongest baseline, reaching 98.0% AUROC on VillanBKD_mul, but still trails NaviT2I (99.9%) by ~2 points and degrades to near-random (50–62%) on patch-level and object-level backdoors. UFID is the weakest baseline overall (69.3% avg AUROC) and requires 200 diffusion iterations per sample, making it 28.6× slower than NaviT2I in wall-clock time (33.041 s vs. 1.242 s). On the DiT architecture (Stable Diffusion v3.5), T2IShield cannot be applied at all (requires UNet attention maps), UFID drops to random-guessing levels (48.3–54.5% AUROC), while NaviT2I retains 82.8–99.9% AUROC, confirming broader architectural generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Neuron Coverage concept from software testing (DeepXplore) and neuron-based interpretability tools to create an input-level backdoor detector for T2I diffusion models, departing fundamentally from prior T2I defenses (T2IShield, UFID) that rely on the 'Trigger Dominance' assumption—i.e., that the trigger alone determines the output regardless of other input content. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) formalizes why text conditioning only differentiates model predictions at early diffusion steps, providing a principled basis for restricting activation measurement to the first iteration. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between neuron-activation-based model analysis (used for safety auditing in LLMs) and practical backdoor defense, showing that the same activation-variation signal that reveals concept activation can also expose trigger tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's 7–14% time overhead relative to full generation (vs. 100–400% for baselines) makes input-level backdoor screening feasible in real-time deployment pipelines for resource-constrained third-party users—the primary victims of T2I backdoor attacks. However, detection degrades noticeably for sentence-length triggers (AUROC 89.7 vs. 97.0 for single-token), and the DiT generalization is validated on only one attack type, leaving open whether the Early-step Activation Variation phenomenon holds as strongly in transformer-based denoisers. The finding that style-based implicit triggers fail to converge on U-Net T2I models (ASR 28.5%, FAR 16.3%) suggests an architectural resistance that may not persist in DiT-based models, an important open question as the field shifts toward transformer architectures.
