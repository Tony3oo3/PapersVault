## Problem

Diffusion models for text-to-image generation suffer from slow inference due to iterative denoising and large model sizes. Existing acceleration methods either reduce sampling steps (degrading quality at low step counts) or modify internal network structure (limiting generalizability across architectures and downstream tasks). The paper seeks a model-agnostic, training-free strategy that accelerates inference without altering the denoising network or sacrificing text-image alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250572/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (and optionally spatial conditions like edge maps for ControlNet) as input and produces a standard RGB image as output, operating entirely at inference time on pretrained models (SD 1.5, SD 2, DreamBooth, ScaleCrafter, ControlNet). It requires no retraining or architectural modification—only the classifier-free guidance schedule is adjusted. On average, the number of function evaluations drops from 100 to ~68.76 per sample, with the exact truncation point varying per image based on semantic convergence speed.

## Method

TCFG exploits the observation that text conditioning becomes redundant once visual semantics are established during denoising: after a dividing time t′, the conditional noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, c) is dropped and only the unconditional prediction ε_θ(x_t, ∅) is computed. The dividing time is determined online by tracking the L2 distance between bottleneck features (with and without text) and applying Bayesian changepoint detection to identify when semantic convergence decelerates. To enable earlier truncation, CFG is reformulated as a gradient descent step where the guidance scale λ serves as the learning rate; a warmup phase (λ=1) stabilizes the initial trajectory, followed by a decay schedule (starting at C·λ₀, multiplying by γ every k steps, floored at λ₁) that accelerates semantic establishment. This rescheduling is fully model-agnostic and composes with existing downstream modifications like ControlNet's spatial branch, which is also truncated at t′.

## Results

On SD 1.5 evaluated on MS-COCO, TCFG achieves a 1.70× speedup (reducing average NFE from 100 to 68.76) while maintaining FID of 66.43 (vs. 65.18 for the DDIM-50 baseline) and KID of 3.02 (identical to baseline). TCFG also improves sample-level quality metrics over the baseline: MUSIQ rises from 5.17 to 5.53, LAION-Aes from 5.25 to 5.42, and HPSv2 from 28.31 to 28.97, while CLIPScore remains essentially unchanged (30.49 vs. 30.54). The method is further validated on Pick-a-Pic and HPD v2 datasets and on downstream tasks (DreamBooth, ScaleCrafter, ControlNet), where it achieves up to 1.72× speedup on ControlNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCFG matches DeepCache's 1.70× speedup but dramatically outperforms it on all quality metrics: FID 66.43 vs. 82.16, KID 3.02 vs. 3.52, MUSIQ 5.53 vs. 4.85, and HPSv2 28.97 vs. 27.22. Against TokenMerge (1.58× speedup), TCFG is faster and superior on every metric, with FID 66.43 vs. 101.52 and HPSv2 28.97 vs. 24.63. Compared to the step-reduction baselines DDIM-35 and DPM-Solver-35 (both at 70 NFE), TCFG achieves comparable or better speed while maintaining near-identical FID (66.43 vs. 77.86 and 70.92) and KID (3.02 vs. 3.51 and 3.38). The only metric where TCFG does not lead is CLIPScore, where it scores 30.49 versus 30.54 for the DDIM-50 baseline — a negligible 0.05 difference. Overall, TCFG is the only method that simultaneously achieves ≥1.7× acceleration and preserves (or improves) set-level and sample-level image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by Ho and Salimans, but introduces a novel theoretical lens by reformulating CFG as a gradient descent optimizer where the guidance scale acts as the learning rate. It departs from the two dominant acceleration paradigms—sampling-step reduction (DDIM, DPM-Solver, distillation) and architectural modification (DeepCache, TokenMerge, pruning)—by operating purely on the guidance schedule without touching the model's internal structure or the number of denoising steps. The use of online changepoint detection on U-Net bottleneck features to dynamically identify semantic convergence is a repurposing of a time-series tool into a diffusion inference context, and the warmup/decay schedule borrows directly from standard deep learning optimization practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TCFG's model-agnostic design means it can be dropped into any CFG-based diffusion pipeline—including downstream tasks like DreamBooth, ScaleCrafter, and ControlNet—without architectural conflicts, a practical advantage that structural methods lack. The finding that truncating text conditioning after semantic convergence not only preserves but slightly improves text-image alignment (CLIPScore, HPSv2) challenges the assumption that text guidance must be active throughout denoising. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion, multi-modal conditioning (e.g., image+text), and whether the changepoint-detection overhead becomes non-negligible at very high resolutions or with longer denoising schedules.
