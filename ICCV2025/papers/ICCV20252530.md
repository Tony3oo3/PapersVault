## Problem

Relation-based knowledge distillation (KD) methods have fallen significantly behind instance-matching counterparts in performance, despite being theoretically more robust to architectural and task variations. The authors identify two root causes through pilot studies: (1) relation matching is a weaker constraint than instance matching, making students more prone to overfitting, and (2) spurious predictions in a batch cause gradient diffusion to all other samples via the relational computational graph. Additionally, prior relation encoders rely on Gram matrices that collapse inter-class knowledge, limiting the diversity of transferred signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252530/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of RGB images processed through both a pre-trained teacher and a student network, yielding per-sample logit vectors of dimension C (number of classes). Each image is also passed through a RandAugment transformation to produce a virtual view, doubling the effective batch to 2B predictions. The distillation operates entirely on these batch-level prediction vectors (not feature maps), constructing B×B×C and C×C×B edge matrices that are matched between teacher and student. For classification the output is a class label; for the MS-COCO detection variant, bounding boxes are produced via a Faster R-CNN backbone.

## Method

VRM constructs two types of affinity graphs from batch predictions: an inter-sample graph where each edge encodes the L2-normalized difference between two samples' logit vectors (preserving class-wise structure along the secondary dimension, unlike Gram matrices), and an inter-class graph with the roles transposed. Virtual views generated via RandAugment extend these graphs to include real-real, virtual-virtual, and real-virtual relation edges, compactly integrating three types of inter-view knowledge. Two pruning stages then sparsify the graphs: redundant edge pruning removes the symmetric half and intra-view edges (halving edge count twice), while unreliable edge pruning dynamically detaches edges whose student-prediction joint entropy exceeds a batch percentile, suppressing spurious gradient propagation. The final objective matches the pruned teacher and student edge matrices via Huber loss, combined with a cross-entropy term: L_total = L_ce + α·L_ISV + β·L_ICV.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, VRM achieves 78.76% top-1 accuracy for ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4 and 72.30% for ResNet50→MobileNetV2, surpassing all prior relation-based and instance-matching methods. On ImageNet, VRM reaches 74.16%/91.78% (Top-1/Top-5) for ResNet50→MobileNetV2, the first method to exceed 74% Top-1 in this setting, and 72.67%/90.68% for ResNet34→ResNet18. On MS-COCO object detection, VRM achieves 35.46 AP (ResNet101→ResNet18) and 32.67 AP (ResNet50→MobileNetV2), outperforming all logit-based methods. For cross-architecture distillation, VRM improves DeiT-T by 14.44% over the student baseline on CIFAR-100 with a ResNet56 teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRM decisively outperforms all relation-based baselines: on CIFAR-100 it beats DIST (the strongest prior RM method) by 1.16% (RN32×4→RN8×4: 77.47 vs 76.31) and 2.22% (VGG13→VGG8: 72.30 vs 68.66), and surpasses RKD, CC, SP, and ICKD by 2–4% margins. Against top instance-matching methods, VRM exceeds FCFD by 1.04% (77.47 vs 76.43) and CRLD by 1.02% (77.47 vs 76.45) on the same-model CIFAR-100 pair, and outperforms FCFD by 0.63% on the heterogeneous ResNet50→MobileNetV2 pair (78.62 vs 77.99). On ImageNet, VRM achieves 74.16% Top-1 for ResNet50→MobileNetV2, beating FCFD (73.37%) by 0.79% and CRLD (73.53%) by 0.63%, while also surpassing DIST (73.24%) by 0.92%. On MS-COCO, VRM (35.46 AP) trails the best feature-based methods FCFD (37.37 AP) and ReviewKD (36.75 AP) by 1.9 and 1.3 AP respectively, but outperforms all logit-based methods (best: DKD at 35.05 AP). For cross-architecture distillation, VRM leads all baselines including AutoKD (78.58%) by 0.94% on DeiT-T and surpasses LG (78.15%) by 1.37%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRM builds directly on the foundational KD framework of Hinton et al. (2015) and the relation-matching paradigm of DIST (2022), but departs from both in critical ways. Rather than using Gram matrices or angular distances to encode inter-sample and inter-class relations, VRM preserves raw pairwise differences along the secondary dimension, retaining class-wise or batch-wise information that prior encoders collapse. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of inter-view virtual relations: by generating augmented views of each sample and constructing affinity graphs over the combined real-virtual batch, VRM adds a third type of relational knowledge (real-virtual edges) that no prior RM method has exploited. The pilot studies identifying overfitting and spurious gradient diffusion as the two main failure modes of RM methods provide the diagnostic motivation for the richer relation construction and the joint-entropy-based edge pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most significant contribution is repositioning relation-based KD as competitive with (and in many settings superior to) instance-matching methods, which had dominated the field for several years. This challenges the implicit assumption that per-sample matching is the most effective distillation signal and suggests that structured, batch-level relational knowledge—when properly constructed and regularised—can provide stronger guidance. Open questions include whether the virtual-view mechanism generalises beyond classification and detection to tasks like segmentation or generation, whether the computational overhead of extra forward passes for virtual views limits practical adoption at scale, and whether the dynamic pruning criterion (joint entropy threshold) remains stable under very large batch sizes or highly imbalanced class distributions.
