## Problem

Current human motion generation and editing methods are fragmented into task-specific solutions, forcing practitioners to train and maintain multiple models. Existing unified frameworks merely treat different modalities as generation conditions, lacking true editing capabilities, fine-grained trajectory control, and cross-task knowledge sharing. The paper asks whether generation and editing can be effectively unified within a single framework that also achieves competitive performance and inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252379/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts a source motion (or none for pure generation) and a task-specific condition—text prompt (encoded via CLIP), joint coordinate trajectories, style motion, or keyframe poses—and outputs a 3D human motion sequence in HumanML3D format (22 joints, 263-dimensional features at 20 fps). For generation tasks the source motion is empty; for editing tasks it is a full motion sequence. Inference uses 50 rectified-flow ODE steps, and the model supports classifier-free guidance by also learning unconditional generation and full reconstruction.

## Method

MotionLab formulates all tasks under a Motion-Condition-Motion paradigm where a target motion is predicted from a source motion and condition via rectified flow velocity-field regression. The core architecture, MotionFlow Transformer (MFT), extends MM-DiT with joint attention across five modality paths (source motion, target motion, text, trajectory, style) and per-modality adaLN modulation, eliminating task-specific modules and posterior guidance. Aligned 1D Rotational Position Encoding explicitly synchronizes corresponding frames between source and target sequences, while Task Instruction Modulation uses CLIP text embeddings (e.g., 'edit source motion by given style') to flexibly route a single modality to different tasks. Training follows a two-stage Motion Curriculum Learning strategy: self-supervised masked pre-training (randomly masking joint trajectories from zero to all frames) followed by supervised fine-tuning that introduces tasks in an easy-to-hard order (single-modality generation → editing → multi-modality trajectory/in-between), with probability-weighted replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On text-based motion generation (HumanML3D), MotionLab achieves FID 0.167, R@3 0.810, MModality 2.912, and AITS 0.068 s, outperforming most baselines on diversity and retrieval while being among the fastest (second only to MotionLCM at 0.045 s). On trajectory-based generation, MotionLab achieves the lowest FID (0.095 for pelvis, 0.126 for all joints) and lowest average error (0.0286 / 0.0334 m) across all compared methods. On motion editing (MotionFix), MotionLab reaches R@1 of 56.34% (text) and 72.65% (trajectory), substantially surpassing the only editing baseline TMED (38.69% and 60.01%) while being ~160× faster in inference (0.16–0.19 s vs. 26.57–30.56 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionLab outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (MDM, MotionDiffuse, MLD, GMD, PriorMDM, OmniControl) by large margins on FID and average error, and is 100–1000× faster in inference (e.g., 0.068 s vs. 26.04 s for MDM, 0.133 s vs. 137.0 s for GMD). Against GPT-based methods (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT), MotionLab wins on R@3, MModality, and AITS but trails T2M-GPT on FID (0.167 vs. 0.116) and MotionCLR on R@3 (0.810 vs. 0.831) and MM Dist (2.830 vs. 2.806). On trajectory-based generation, MotionLab achieves the best FID and average error, beating OmniControl (FID 0.095 vs. 0.212; error 0.0286 vs. 0.3226 m) and MotionLCM (FID 0.095 vs. 0.531; error 0.0286 vs. 0.1897 m), though MotionLCM is faster (0.035 vs. 0.133 s). On motion editing, MotionLab dominates the sole editing baseline TMED by 17.65 points on text-edit R@1 (56.34 vs. 38.69) and 12.64 points on trajectory-edit R@1 (72.65 vs. 60.01), while reducing inference time from ~27–31 s to 0.16–0.19 s. The unified framework also surpasses its own task-specific specialist models across all six tasks (Table 6), confirming the benefit of multi-task knowledge sharing via Motion Curriculum Learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionLab builds on rectified flows (Lipman 2022, Liu 2022) as its generative backbone and extends the MM-DiT architecture (Esser 2024) from image synthesis to multi-modal human motion, adding source motion, trajectory, and style as additional modality paths with joint attention. It departs from prior unified motion frameworks (FLAME, MotionCLR, Everything2Motion) that treat diverse inputs merely as generation conditions, by introducing the Motion-Condition-Motion paradigm that explicitly models a source-to-target mapping, thereby enabling editing alongside generation. The masked pre-training strategy extends MoMask (Guo 2024) from frame-level to joint-trajectory-level masking, and the Aligned ROPE modifies standard rotary position encoding (Su 2024) to enforce temporal synchronization between source and target sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying six motion tasks (text/trajectory generation, text/trajectory editing, in-between, style transfer) in a single model that outperforms per-task specialists, MotionLab offers a practical path toward reducing the model zoo problem in motion production pipelines for games, film, and VR. The very low inference time (~0.07s per sample vs. 26–57s for diffusion-based baselines) makes real-time interactive editing feasible. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to additional modalities (audio, scene geometry), whether the curriculum learning strategy generalizes to much larger or more heterogeneous motion datasets, and whether the SMPL/20fps constraint limits applicability to higher-fidelity or multi-character settings.
