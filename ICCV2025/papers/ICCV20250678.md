## Problem

Single-view gaze target estimation (GTE) methods fail when the subject's face is occluded, the gaze target lies outside the frame, or multiple candidate targets exist, because they are confined to one camera's field of view. No prior work has explored GTE using multiple calibrated third-person camera views, despite multi-camera setups being common in real-world environments like lecture halls and retail spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of RGB images (scene at 512×384, head crops at 224×224) from a primary and reference view, along with per-view head bounding boxes and full camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters. It produces a 64×64 gaze target heatmap and a scalar in/out probability for each view. A distinctive capability is cross-view GTE, where the subject appears only in the reference view and the target only in the primary view, requiring absolute depth recovery via a pre-reconstructed 3D scene (Dust3R) to generate the FoV heatmap in the target's view.

## Method

The architecture extends a transformer-based single-view GTE backbone to process image pairs through three novel modules: (1) Head Information Aggregation (HIA) uses cross-attention where queries come from one view's ResNet-18 head features and keys/values come from the other view's features concatenated with the relative camera rotation R₂₁, enhancing head embeddings with geometric context; (2) Uncertainty-based Gaze Selection (UGS) trains the gaze estimator with an aleatoric uncertainty loss, then selects the lower-uncertainty 3D gaze vector and transforms it into the other view's coordinate frame via camera rotation; (3) Epipolar-based Scene Attention (ESA) inserts two cross-attention layers into a shared ViT-base encoder where each token attends to 48 features sampled along its epipolar line in the other view. Training follows a transfer-learning protocol: pretraining on GazeFollow, fine-tuning on three MVGT scenes, and leave-one-scene-out validation. The cross-view extension adds a feature-transform MLP and a learnable 'outside embedding' to generate the head embedding for the view lacking the subject, and relies on a one-time Dust3R reconstruction to calibrate monocular depth to absolute scale.

## Results

On the MVGT dataset (leave-one-scene-out evaluation), the multi-view method achieves a normalized L2 distance of 0.129 and AP of 0.909 when the reference view shows both the head and target, outperforming the best single-view baseline (Miao [42], Dist 0.141, AP 0.886) by 8.5% in distance. The improvement is most pronounced when the reference view contains the head but not the target (Dist 0.122 vs. 0.140 for Miao), where the multi-view advantage is largest. For the cross-view GTE task (subject visible only in reference view), the method achieves Dist 0.188 and AP 0.820, substantially outperforming the adapted Recasens baseline (Dist 0.271, AP 0.542) by 30% in distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The multi-view method outperforms all single-view baselines across all four visibility categories in Table 1. Against the strongest single-view baseline Miao [42] (WACV 2023), the multi-view method reduces distance error from 0.141 to 0.129 (Head Visible, Target Visible) and from 0.140 to 0.122 (Head Visible, Target Not Visible), while also improving AP from 0.886 to 0.909 and 0.892 to 0.912 respectively. Tafasca [54] (ICCV 2023) is competitive on AP but lags in distance (0.149 vs. 0.129), and Chong [9] (CVPR 2020) is the weakest learned baseline at 0.159 distance. When the head is not visible in the reference view, the multi-view advantage shrinks (0.161 vs. 0.164 for Miao), as expected since little additional head information is available. For the cross-view GTE task (Table 3), the method achieves Dist 0.188 and AP 0.820, beating the adapted Recasens [50] baseline (Dist 0.271, AP 0.542) by 30% in distance and 28 points in AP, and outperforming the saliency-based DeepGazeIIE [39] (Dist 0.248) by 24% in distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-view gaze target estimation (GTE) to a multi-view setting, building on transformer-based GTE frameworks (e.g., Tafasca's FoV heatmap approach, Miao's patch-level prediction) and repurposing epipolar attention from multi-view 3D reconstruction and pose estimation literature. It departs from prior multi-view gaze *direction* estimation works (which require rectified faces, visible eyes, and no head turning) by handling wild, uncontrolled multi-view GTE with occluded or absent faces. The cross-view estimation capability—predicting a target in one view from a person visible only in another—has no direct precedent in the GTE literature, and the use of a pre-reconstructed 3D scene (via Dust3R) to resolve absolute depth for cross-view FoV generation is a novel adaptation of multi-view stereo to this problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method and dataset for multi-view GTE, opening a research direction that addresses practical limitations of single-view approaches (face occlusion, out-of-frame targets) in real multi-camera environments like supermarkets and lecture halls. The cross-view GTE capability is something no single-view method can attempt, and the large margin over adapted baselines (0.188 vs. 0.271 Dist.) suggests the geometric multi-view formulation is essential rather than merely helpful. Key limitations include the requirement for calibrated cameras, a pre-reconstructed 3D scene for cross-view cases, and processing only view pairs (not arbitrary numbers of views simultaneously). Open questions include whether geometric-aware features can be learned without explicit camera parameters, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes where the 3D reconstruction becomes stale.
