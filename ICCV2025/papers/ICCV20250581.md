## Problem

Explicitly disentangling style and content in vision models is fundamentally limited by the semantic overlap and subjective nature of these concepts, making clean ground-truth separation criteria unavailable. Existing generative and discriminative approaches all operate within a separation paradigm that struggles with this inherent ambiguity. The paper asks whether disentanglement can emerge implicitly by learning an invertible merging process instead of enforcing explicit separation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in CLIP embedding space (768-d vectors), not pixel space. For forward merging, it takes an asymmetric pair: a content reference z_{c_i, s_*} (content i with arbitrary style) and a style reference z_{c_*, s_j} (style j with arbitrary content), and outputs a single merged embedding z_{c_i, s_j}. For reverse disentangling, it takes one merged embedding (duplicated to match the pair dimension) and outputs two disentangled embeddings: a content proxy z_{c_i, s̄} and a style proxy z_{c̄, s_j}, where s̄ and c̄ approximate dataset-mean style and content. Inference requires only 1 NFE by default via ODE solving.

## Method

SCFlow uses flow matching with a linear (rectified) schedule (α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t) to learn a deterministic velocity field v_θ(x,t) that maps between the disentangled distribution p_0 (pairs of content and style references) and the merged distribution p_1 (repeated mixed embeddings). The key insight is that training solely on the merging direction p_0→p_1 suffices: the invertibility of the learned ODE flow automatically yields the disentangling direction p_1→p_0 without any explicit separation supervision. The asymmetric triplet construction (c_i s_*, c_* s_j, c_i s_j) with full combinatorial coverage (51 styles × 10,000 contents = 510,000 samples) forces the model to discard irrelevant style/content variations and retain only the invariant attributes, making disentanglement an emergent property of the merging task. The model is a neural network trained with the standard flow-matching velocity-matching loss, and all operations occur in the compact CLIP embedding space to avoid pixel-level spatial biases.

## Results

On the curated SCFlow dataset, SCFlow achieves the highest NMI scores across all cluster sizes: 0.9202 for 25 styles and 0.8374 for 25 contents, with FDR values of 3.7271 (style) and 1.7998 (content), substantially outperforming CLIP, DEADiff, and CSD. On zero-shot ImageNet-1k kNN classification, SCFlow reaches 66.25% (k=1) accuracy, within 0.85% of CLIP's 67.10%. On WikiArt style retrieval, SCFlow achieves 65.34% Recall@1, surpassing CSD-S (64.56%), DEADiff (61.24%), and CLIP (59.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCFlow outperforms all three baselines on every NMI and FDR metric in Table 1, with the largest margins over CLIP (e.g., 0.9202 vs. 0.5838 NMI for 25 styles; 3.7271 vs. 0.3066 FDR) and DEADiff. CSD is the strongest competitor on style NMI (0.8229 at 25 styles) but is explicitly trained for style representation and collapses on content (0.1888 NMI at 25 contents vs. SCFlow's 0.8374). On ImageNet-1k kNN, SCFlow (66.25% at k=1) is slightly below CLIP (67.10%) but clearly ahead of DEADiff (62.81%) and CSD-C (56.54%), confirming that the learned content embeddings retain strong discriminative power. On WikiArt style retrieval, SCFlow leads all baselines at every k (65.34% Recall@1 vs. CSD-S 64.56%, DEADiff 61.24%, CLIP 59.40%), demonstrating that the implicitly learned style representations generalize to a completely unseen style dataset without any style-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCFlow repurposes flow matching (Lipman et al., Liu et al., Ma et al.) from its standard role in generative modeling (mapping noise to data) toward bidirectional mapping between two real data distributions—disentangled style/content pairs and their merged form. This departs from prior style-content disentanglement work (DEADiff, CSD, B-LoRA) that relies on explicit separation criteria, contrastive objectives, or generative conditioning; instead, SCFlow trains only on the merging direction and lets disentanglement emerge from invertibility. The use of CLIP as a shared semantic encoder space, combined with a combinatorial dataset of 510K ControlNet-stylized images, distinguishes it from pixel-space or diffusion-based approaches that require Gaussian priors at one end of the mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core conceptual contribution—that invertible merging alone, without any explicit disentanglement loss, yields usable style and content representations—offers a simpler training recipe for a task that has historically required carefully designed separation objectives. Zero-shot generalization to ImageNet-1k and WikiArt suggests the learned representations capture transferable semantics, though the method is confined to CLIP's embedding space and relies on a synthetic dataset whose quality is bounded by ControlNet's stylization fidelity. Open questions include whether the framework extends beyond style/content to other entangled attributes, whether it scales to pixel-space or higher-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the implicit disentanglement aligns with human perceptual boundaries in more nuanced cases.
