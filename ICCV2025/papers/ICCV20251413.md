## Problem

Existing VSR methods struggle to propagate high-quality information over long temporal distances because alignment errors accumulate and dilute features across frames. Additionally, state-of-the-art VSR methods are locked to a single fixed scaling factor (typically ×4) and cannot flexibly incorporate high-resolution reference images from arbitrary positions in a sequence. This paper addresses all three limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of low-resolution video frames and optionally any number of high-resolution reference images that can be injected at arbitrary temporal positions (not just at the start or end). It produces high-resolution video frames at an arbitrary scaling factor (trained on ×1.5 to ×4.5, evaluated up to ×8), rather than being restricted to a single fixed factor. The LRRF module is applied only to a sparse set of key-frames to manage compute and memory, with the HRIP module propagating refined information to all remaining frames.

## Method

The core contribution is the Long Range Reference Fusion (LRRF) module, which operates on high-resolution feature maps after upscaling and enables information injection from any frame to any other frame. It works in two stages: Ref-Align decodes HR features into images, computes dense matching via optical flow, and warps reference features to the anchor's coordinate frame; Ref-Fusion then uses pixel-wise attention (queries from the anchor, key-value pairs from aligned references) to refine the anchor feature map. Because LRRF is expensive, it is applied only to sparse key-frames, and a lighter High Resolution Iterative Propagation (HRIP) module with first-order propagation distributes the refined information to neighboring frames. The entire pipeline is built on top of pre-trained VSR backbones (BasicVSR++ or IART), with the pixel-shuffle upscaling layer replaced by an Arbitrary Scaling Module (ASM) that encodes sub-pixel sampling offsets as additional channels, enabling arbitrary scaling factors with less than 24 hours of additional training.

## Results

On fixed ×4 VSR, the method with IART backbone achieves the best LPIPS across all three datasets: 0.097 on REDS, 0.059 on Vimeo, and 0.134 on Vid4, outperforming IART (0.105, 0.067, 0.158) and BasicVSR++ (0.119, 0.072, 0.162). PSNR is slightly lower than IART on REDS (32.83 vs. 33.20), which the authors attribute to PSNR's poor correlation with perceptual quality. For arbitrary scaling on REDS, the method (IART backbone) achieves 32.36 PSNR / 0.103 LPIPS at ×4, substantially beating ST-AVSR (30.55 / 0.189) and LIIF (28.91 / 0.261), with similar margins at ×2.5, ×3.25, ×5.5, and ×8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed ×4 VSR baselines (BasicVSR, IconVSR, BasicVSR++, RVRT, IART), the method with IART backbone wins on LPIPS across all three datasets (e.g., 0.097 vs. 0.105 on REDS, 0.059 vs. 0.067 on Vimeo, 0.134 vs. 0.158 on Vid4) but trails IART on PSNR by 0.37 dB on REDS. With the lighter BasicVSR++ backbone, the method still beats all baselines on LPIPS for Vimeo (0.065 vs. 0.072) and Vid4 (0.134 vs. 0.162) but not REDS. For arbitrary scaling, the method dominates both ST-AVSR and LIIF on every tested scaling factor (×2.5 through ×8) on both PSNR and LPIPS; at ×4 it gains +1.81 dB PSNR and −0.086 LPIPS over ST-AVSR, and +3.45 dB PSNR and −0.158 LPIPS over LIIF. The authors note that ST-AVSR's publicly released checkpoint was trained without anti-aliasing; after re-training with anti-aliasing, ST-AVSR improves substantially but still falls well short of the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative-propagation VSR lineage (BasicVSR, BasicVSR++, IART) by inserting a Long Range Reference Fusion (LRRF) module that injects information from temporally distant HR feature maps, going beyond the local second-order propagation those backbones rely on. It also generalizes the reference-based SR line (RefVSR, C2-Matching) by decoupling reference injection from any fixed camera geometry, allowing an arbitrary number of HR references at any point in the sequence. The arbitrary scaling module (ASM) follows the implicit-function approach of LIIF but is designed to drop into a pre-trained VSR backbone, contrasting with ST-AVSR which trains a dedicated architecture from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design—plugging LRRF and HRIP on top of frozen pre-trained backbones—substantially lowers the training cost for achieving long-range propagation and arbitrary scaling, making the approach accessible without retraining entire pipelines. A key open question the authors flag is intelligent key-frame and reference selection for long video sequences, where their current regular-interval strategy becomes suboptimal and the LRRF module becomes a computational bottleneck. The consistent LPIPS gains across backbones and datasets suggest the long-range fusion mechanism is a broadly applicable component, but the PSNR trade-off (slightly lower than IART in some settings) and the reliance on a separate optical-flow model for alignment leave room for more unified or efficient formulations.
