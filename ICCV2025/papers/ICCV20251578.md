## Problem

Existing diffusion model quantization methods apply uniform or time-step-aware bit precision but ignore the text prompt as a source of information for quantization decisions. The paper observes that detailed text prompts produce high-quality images requiring higher precision, while vague prompts yield simpler images where lower precision suffices, yet no prior work exploited this input-adaptive dynamic quantization for text-guided diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input, which is encoded into a CLIP text embedding (dimension C_clip) and consumed by both the diffusion model (for generation) and the T2Q module (for quality prediction). The output is a 512×512 RGB image generated by the quantized diffusion model. Unusually, the text prompt serves a dual role: it conditions the denoising process and simultaneously determines the per-layer, per-timestep activation bit precision (chosen from {b_low, b_med, b_high}) through the Q2B module, while weights remain fixed at 4-bit across all layers and timesteps.

## Method

QLIP consists of two modules: a Text-to-Quality (T2Q) module, a 3-layer MLP trained with MSE loss to predict normalized GIQA quality scores from CLIP text embeddings, and a Quality-to-Bit (Q2B) module that maps the predicted quality to per-layer, per-timestep bit assignments. The Q2B module computes probability vectors via sigmoid functions over learnable parameters (s, o for quality-dependent selection; u_m^t, u_h^t for timestep-dependent selection, shared across groups of M=200 timesteps), then combines them into low/med/high bit probabilities whose argmax determines each layer's precision. A straight-through estimator makes the non-differentiable argmax amenable to backpropagation by weighting quantized activations by their selection probabilities during the backward pass. The Q2B module is trained for 5000 iterations on a small calibration set with a loss balancing noise-prediction error against a bit-cost penalty (λ_bit), while all other components (T2Q, diffusion weights, quantization clipping parameters) remain frozen. QLIP is designed as a plug-in that layers on top of existing PTQ methods (Q-diffusion, PTQD) without modifying their core quantization procedures.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4 at 512×512, QLIP reduces the feature average bit-width (FAB) from 8.00 to 7.86 on COCO2017 and 7.77 on Conceptual Captions while simultaneously improving FID. Specifically, PTQD+QLIP (W4A{6,8,10}) achieves FID 21.35 on COCO2017, a 4.98-point improvement over PTQD (W4A8) at FID 22.75, and FID 20.42 on Conceptual Captions versus PTQD's 25.40. Q-diffusion+QLIP also improves FID from 23.40 to 21.61 on COCO2017 while reducing FAB. CLIP scores remain nearly unchanged (e.g., 0.3120 vs. 0.3126), confirming that text-image alignment is preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QLIP is evaluated as a plug-in on two existing diffusion quantization baselines: Q-diffusion [24] and PTQD [13], both using W4A8 fixed precision. Against Q-diffusion, QLIP reduces FAB from 8.00 to 7.86 (COCO2017) / 7.77 (Conceptual Captions) while improving FID by 1.79 (23.40→21.61) and 0.85 (20.74→19.89) respectively, and sFID by 2.25 and 0.70. Against PTQD, the gains are larger: FID improves by 1.40 (22.75→21.35) on COCO2017 and by 4.98 (25.40→20.42) on Conceptual Captions, with sFID improving by 2.82 and 6.18 respectively. CLIP scores remain essentially unchanged across all comparisons (differences ≤0.0006), indicating that the dynamic bit allocation does not degrade text-image alignment. On the lighter BK-SDM-Tiny-2M model (Table 1), QLIP similarly reduces FAB from 16.00 to 12.14 while matching or slightly improving FID and sFID over both Q-diffusion and PTQD baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

QLIP sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) post-training quantization of diffusion models (Q-diffusion, PTQD, TDQ), which have so far applied uniform or time-step-only bit precision, and (2) input-adaptive dynamic quantization in super-resolution (CADyQ, AdaBM, RefQSR), which adjusts bit widths based on image content but has not been explored for diffusion models. The paper departs from prior diffusion quantization by treating the text prompt as a first-class signal for bit allocation, using CLIP text embeddings to predict expected image quality and then mapping that quality to per-layer, per-timestep bit choices via a lightweight learnable Q2B module. It is explicitly designed as a plug-in on top of existing baselines (Q-diffusion, PTQD) rather than a standalone quantization pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt QLIP without re-deriving quantization schedules, and the demonstrated FAB reductions (e.g., 8.00 → 7.86 on Stable Diffusion) with equal or better FID offer a practical efficiency win for deployment on resource-constrained hardware. However, the method is limited to text-conditioned generation; the authors note that extending to image or segmentation-map conditions remains open. A broader open question is whether the text-prompt-to-quality mapping generalizes across model scales and architectures beyond the two tested (BK-SDM-Tiny-2M and SD v1.4), and whether the straight-through estimator for non-differentiable bit selection introduces training instability at larger bit-width ranges.
