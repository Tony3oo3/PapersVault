## Problem

Most light field spatial super-resolution (LFSSR) methods concentrate on encoder design while treating the decoder upsampling as a trivial transpose convolution or pixel shuffle operation, which fails to exploit the 4D geometric structure of light fields and cannot generalize to unseen scale factors. Directly transferring single-image implicit image functions to light fields is non-trivial because macro-pixel representations are incompatible with pixel-based continuous decoding, and per-SAI application discards angular cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution light field of size U×V×H×W (5×5 angular resolution, Y channel) along with a continuous scale factor α, and produces a high-resolution light field of size U×V×αH×αW. Features are extracted by any existing LFSSR encoder (without its upsampling module) and fed into SEIIF as the sole decoder; the scale factor can be any real value, enabling out-of-distribution inference at factors like ×6 or ×8 without retraining. Training crops 32α×32α HR patches and bicubically downsamples to 32×32 LR inputs, with α randomly sampled from discrete values in (1,4] at 0.5 intervals per iteration.

## Method

SEIIF comprises two complementary implicit image function branches: a Spatial Implicit Image Function (SIIF) that decodes intra-view spatial context from sub-aperture images, and an Epipolar Implicit Image Function (EIIF) that decodes inter-view spatial-angular correlation from horizontal and vertical epipolar plane images. Both branches perform two-step upsampling (horizontal then vertical) so that after the first step, intermediate features from both branches plus bilinearly interpolated features are concatenated and fused by a 1-layer MLP, enabling cross-branch feature interaction before the final vertical decoding step. For EIIF, the paper introduces an oriented line sampling strategy that determines the correct epipolar line slope at each query coordinate by minimizing feature variance across three candidate sampling points, then aggregates features along that line to capture disparity-consistent inter-view information. The final HR light field is obtained by averaging the pixel predictions from SIIF and EIIF branches, each implemented as 3-layer MLPs with LIIF-style cell decoding.

## Results

On fixed-scale LFSSR, replacing the original upsampling module with SEIIF yields average PSNR improvements of 0.12–0.39 dB for 2× SR and 0.23–0.51 dB for 4× SR across eight encoder backbones on five benchmark datasets (EPFL, HCInew, HCIold, INRIA, STFgantry). The largest gains appear on 4× SR, e.g., LF-InterNet improves from 31.61 to 32.12 dB (+0.51) and HLFSR from 32.29 to 32.75 dB (+0.46). For arbitrary-scale LFSSR, EPIT+SEIIF achieves 39.60/35.25/32.76/29.61/27.68 dB at ×2/×3/×4/×6/×8, matching or exceeding CiaoSR while using only 8.4 G FLOPs at ×2 versus CiaoSR's 57.0 G, and avoiding out-of-memory failures at ×8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEIIF is evaluated as a drop-in decoder replacement for eight existing LFSSR encoders (LF-InterNet, LF-DFnet, LF-IINet, LFT, DistgSSR, HLFSR, EPIT, LF-DET). In nearly all cases, swapping the original transpose-convolution or pixel-shuffle upsampler for SEIIF improves average PSNR by 0.12–0.39 dB at 2× and 0.23–0.51 dB at 4×, with the largest absolute gains on STFgantry (up to +0.94 dB at 4× for LF-InterNet) and HCIold (up to +0.55 dB at 4×). Two minor exceptions exist: HLFSR drops 0.05 dB on EPFL 2× and LF-DET drops 0.02 dB on EPFL 2×, but both still improve on every other dataset and at 4×. For arbitrary-scale SR, SEIIF (as EPIT+SEIIF) achieves the best or near-best PSNR at all tested scales (×2 through ×8), edging out DIIF by ~0.05–0.10 dB and matching or slightly exceeding CiaoSR (e.g., 32.76 vs 32.72 dB at ×4), while CiaoSR runs out of memory at ×8. Critically, SEIIF uses only 8.4 G FLOPs at ×2 versus 57.0 G for CiaoSR and 20.4 G for DIIF, making it the most computationally efficient arbitrary-scale decoder in the comparison. The additional parameter overhead is negligible (~0.05–0.20 M), confirming SEIIF's plug-and-play compatibility with existing encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the implicit image function paradigm from single-image arbitrary-scale super-resolution (LIIF, LTE, CiaoSR, DIIF) and adapts it to the 4D light field domain, where prior LFSSR work has overwhelmingly focused on encoder design while treating upsampling as a trivial transpose-convolution or pixel-shuffle step. The key departure is the introduction of two complementary 2D implicit functions—SIIF operating on sub-aperture images for intra-view spatial context and EIIF operating on epipolar plane images for inter-view angular correlation—unified through a shared two-step upsampling schedule that enables cross-branch feature interaction. The oriented line sampling strategy is a novel EPI-specific mechanism that replaces the generic nearest-neighbor or local-region sampling used in single-image implicit functions, exploiting the line-structure geometry unique to epipolar representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEIIF offers a practical, plug-and-play upsampling decoder that can be swapped into any existing LFSSR encoder with negligible parameter overhead, consistently yielding 0.1–0.5 dB PSNR gains across eight diverse architectures, and it extends the capability to arbitrary (including non-integer) scale factors without retraining. The finding that local-region sampling in EPIs is actually worse than oriented line sampling challenges the assumption that more neighborhood context always helps, and the two-phase discrete-scale training strategy reveals that continuous uniform scale sampling is detrimental when angular correlation shifts with scale. Open questions include whether the per-pixel MLP decoding bottleneck can be further reduced for real-time deployment at large angular resolutions (beyond 5×5), and whether the approach generalizes to 4D angular super-resolution or joint spatial-angular SR.
