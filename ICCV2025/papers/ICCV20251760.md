## Problem

Existing active mapping methods for 3D indoor exploration are trained on fewer than 100 homogeneous scenes, rely on handcrafted heuristics (information gain, layout priors, frontier rules), and use simplified training protocols such as centralized starting positions. These limitations collectively prevent generalization to unseen complex indoor scenes with diverse layouts, high clutter, and complex inter-room connectivity, as demonstrated by the paper's experiments showing prior SOTA methods failing even on simpler 5-room scenes after training on 10-room scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent (a CrazyFlie UAV) receives 256×256 depth maps with 90° FOV and its own pose at each time step. These observations are back-projected and integrated into a 128×128 probabilistic global occupancy map (10 cm cells) and a 128×128 egocentric semantic map with four task-specific states (occupied, free, unknown, frontier). The policy outputs a long-term SE(2) goal (Δx, Δy, Δθ) that is verified for collision-free reachability by an A* planner before execution, operating under a keyframe budget of T=50.

## Method

GLEAM is a PPO-based exploration policy that achieves generalization through three key designs: (1) a semantic egocentric map encoding four task-relevant states (occupied, free, unknown, frontier) alongside a probabilistic global map, both processed by a lightweight LocoTransformer encoder that distills spatial and trajectory history into task-aware embeddings; (2) a long-horizon action space where the policy predicts distant reachable goals rather than short motion primitives, with an A* verifier ensuring collision-free path connectivity on the global map, thereby decoupling high-level exploration from local navigation; and (3) randomized training strategies including random initial poses in any navigable area and a scene-updating mechanism that swaps training environments during episodes to prevent overfitting. The policy is trained end-to-end on 1,024 diverse scenes from ProcTHOR, HSSD, and Gibson in NVIDIA Isaac Gym, achieving 66.50% coverage on 128 unseen scenes without fine-tuning.

## Results

On 128 unseen complex indoor scenes from GLEAM-Bench, GLEAM achieves 66.50% average coverage, 57.63% AUC, and 0.80m Chamfer Distance, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On synthetic scenes (ProcTHOR & HSSD), GLEAM reaches 76.01% coverage and 0.38m CD; on real-scan scenes (Gibson & Matterport3D), it maintains 47.04% coverage and 1.67m CD. The policy is trained on 1,024 scenes and evaluated zero-shot with 50 keyframes per episode, requiring no fine-tuning or scene-specific optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLEAM outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (coverage, AUC, and Chamfer Distance) across both synthetic and real-scan splits. Against the previous state-of-the-art ANM [51] (57.01% Cov, 49.56% AUC, 1.02m CD), GLEAM improves coverage by +9.49%, AUC by +8.07%, and reduces CD by 0.22m. The best heuristic baseline, FBE [50] (56.80% Cov), is surpassed by +9.70% coverage and +12.56% AUC. Among RL-based methods, GLEAM leads OccAnt [35] (53.61% Cov) by +12.89% coverage and ANS [7] (48.86% Cov) by +17.64% coverage. Notably, GLEAM's advantage is largest on synthetic scenes (76.01% vs. 63.98% for ANM) and smallest but still positive on real-scan scenes (47.04% vs. 42.73% for ANM), confirming cross-domain generalization without per-scene optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based active mapping work (ANS, OccAnt, ExploreNav) and information-gain-driven methods (ANM, UPEN) by addressing their core generalization failure: prior policies were trained on fewer than 100 homogeneous scenes with centralized starting positions and heuristic action spaces, limiting transfer to unseen layouts. GLEAM departs from this lineage on three axes—scaling training data to 1,024+ diverse scenes across four datasets, replacing motion primitives with A*-verified long-term goals, and introducing randomized initialization—while retaining the PPO optimization framework and occupancy-grid mapping formalism inherited from classic active SLAM. The LocoTransformer encoder is repurposed from quadrupedal locomotion to encode egocentric semantic maps with frontier states, a cross-domain transfer not previously explored in the active mapping literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GLEAM-Bench (1,152 scenes) fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark offered sufficient scene diversity and complexity to meaningfully evaluate generalization in active mapping, and the paper demonstrates that prior SOTA methods collapse when tested outside their narrow training distribution. The practical impact is significant for embodied AI practitioners who need exploration policies that transfer across building types without per-scene retraining. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor or multi-floor environments, how it performs under real sensor noise and partial observability beyond simulation, and whether the 50-keyframe budget is sufficient for the largest real-scan scenes where coverage plateaus around 47%.
