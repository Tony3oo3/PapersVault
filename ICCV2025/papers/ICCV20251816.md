## Problem

Medical image segmentation inherently contains uncertainty from both image quality and context-dependent labeling preferences (e.g., over-segmentation for diagnosis vs. under-segmentation for radiotherapy). Existing uncertainty-aware models generate numerous similar predictions that burden users with tedious selection, while interactive segmentation models require pixel-level clicks or boxes without modeling uncertainty. No prior method efficiently adapts to diverse clinical preferences with minimal user interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (RGB fundus, 2D CT slice, or MRI slice) plus an initial point prompt (analogous to SAM), and at each subsequent iteration the user selects one of K=4 representative segmentation candidates via multi-choice rather than pixel-level interaction. The model outputs an aggregated preference-aware segmentation mask along with the 4 candidates, with pixel-wise differences between the aggregated prediction and each candidate highlighted in distinct colors to aid selection. Internally, N=48 latent-conditioned predictions are generated per iteration and clustered into the 4 candidates.

## Method

SPA models user segmentation preferences as a Gaussian Mixture Model (M=16 components) over latent variables z, where each component captures a distinct preference for handling ambiguous pixels. A pre-trained SAM-ViT/B encoder produces image embeddings that are concatenated with N=48 sampled latent variables, processed through convolutional layers, and decoded by a SAM-style mask decoder conditioned on an interaction embedding from the SAM prompt encoder. At inference, K-means clustering over the 48 predictions yields 4 representative candidates; the user's selection is encoded and fed to a neural 'Preference Distribution Generator' that uses amortized inference to update GMM parameters (means, variances, weights) in a single forward pass. Training simulates multi-rater interaction by stochastically combining annotations from multiple clinicians as ground truth and randomly selecting point prompts, jointly optimizing the segmentation decoder, interaction encoder, and preference distribution generator via cross-entropy and GMM E-step supervision.

## Results

SPA achieves an average Dice Score of 89.68% after three interaction iterations across seven segmentation tasks (REFUGE2, LIDC-IDRI, and four QUBIQ tasks), outperforming the next-best interactive method SAM (84.10%) by 5.6 points. After just one iteration, SPA already reaches 89.22% average Dice, surpassing SAM (83.29%) and SAM-U V2 (83.09%). The improvement is most dramatic on the LIDC lung CT task, where SPA reaches 88.56% Dice versus 66.71% for SAM — a ~22-point gain. In a human user study, SPA required on average 4.34 s and 2.46 iterations per image versus 6.62 s and 4.33 iterations for MedSAM, confirming its efficiency advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA outperforms all 11 baselines across every task and iteration count. Against deterministic methods (UNet 74.96%, TransUNet 75.19%, SwinUNet 75.69% 1-Iter Ave), SPA's 89.22% represents a 13–14 point margin, confirming that multi-rater preference modeling far exceeds majority-vote training. Among uncertainty-aware methods, SPA surpasses the strongest (LS-Unet, 79.10%) by 10.1 points and the weakest (ProbUNet, 70.96%) by 18.3 points on 1-Iter Ave. For interactive baselines, SPA leads SAM (84.10% 3-Iter) by 5.6 points, MedSAM (81.95%) by 7.7 points, and MSA (75.91%) by 13.8 points; the gap is largest on LIDC where SPA reaches 88.56% versus SAM's 66.71%. Against the closest uncertainty-interactive competitor SAM-U V2 (82.96% 3-Iter), SPA gains 6.7 points on average and 23.1 points specifically on LIDC. In the human study, SPA also required fewer iterations (2.46 vs. 4.33 for MedSAM) and less time per image (4.34 s vs. 6.62 s), validating its efficiency claims beyond automated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: uncertainty-aware segmentation (ProbUNet, MRNet, ensemble methods) that generates many stochastic predictions, and interactive segmentation (SAM, MedSAM, MSA) that refines outputs via pixel-level prompts. It departs from both by replacing the 'choose from many similar outputs' paradigm with a small set of K-means-clustered representative candidates, and by replacing pixel-level clicking with a multi-choice selection that feeds a GMM-based preference distribution updated via amortized inference. The segmentation backbone follows SAM's ViT encoder + mask decoder architecture, while the preference distribution generator (a small neural network predicting GMM parameters from interaction embeddings) is the paper's core architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical reduction in user effort (~40% fewer iterations and seconds per image in the human study) addresses a real bottleneck in clinical deployment where radiologists cannot spend minutes per image on pixel-level refinement. The preference-distribution formulation also opens a path toward personalizing segmentation to individual clinicians or institutional protocols without retraining. Open questions include whether the GMM with M=16 components adequately captures the full diversity of clinical preferences, how the approach extends to 3D volumetric segmentation, and whether the one-point-prompt initialization requirement limits usability in fast triage settings.
