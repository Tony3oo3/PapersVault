## Problem

SAM-based medical segmentation methods (e.g., SAMed, H-SAM) rely solely on limited labeled data for prompt-free fine-tuning, leaving the abundant unlabeled medical images and their inherent hierarchical multi-scale structure (global anatomy, regional features, fine-grained tissue details) largely untapped. The paper asks whether complementary self-supervised objectives at image, patch, and pixel levels can be jointly distilled into a SAM encoder to substantially improve segmentation under few-shot and fully supervised regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252668/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 medical CT or MRI slices (no user prompts required) and outputs per-organ binary segmentation masks. Pre-training uses 100,000 unlabeled CT images from AMOS22, LiTS, KiTS, and the Medical Segmentation Decathlon; fine-tuning uses small labeled subsets (10% of Synapse, 5% of LA, 7.5% of PROMISE12). All LoRA experts share rank 4, matching SAMed/H-SAM conventions, and the frozen SAM ViT-B or SAM2 hiera-base-plus backbone is shared across both stages.

## Method

SAMora follows a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, three LoRA experts are pre-trained on unlabeled data with level-specific SSL objectives: image-level via SimCLRv2 contrastive distillation (ResNet-50 2X+SK teacher → SAM+LoRA student), patch-level via MAE reconstruction distillation (ViT-Large teacher → SAM+LoRA student), and pixel-level via a denoising autoencoder (SAM encoder + U-Net decoder); the image- and patch-level teachers undergo continual pre-training on 100K medical images before distillation. In Stage 2, a novel HL-Attn module sequentially fuses the three frozen LoRA outputs using cross-attention (higher-level LoRA as query, lower-level as key/value) in a pixel→patch→image order, and only HL-Attn plus the decoder are fine-tuned on labeled data with a combined Dice + cross-entropy loss. This hierarchical cross-attention preserves each expert's distinct characteristics better than linear arithmetic composition or gating-based fusion, and the entire framework is plug-and-play across SAM, SAM2, SAMed, and H-SAM backbones.

## Results

On the Synapse dataset with only 10% of training data, H-SAMora achieves a Mean Dice of 84.34% and HD of 11.63, outperforming H-SAM (80.35% Dice, 15.54 HD) by 3.99% Dice. In the fully supervised setting, H-SAMora reaches 88.59% Mean Dice and 7.09 HD, surpassing the strongest non-SAM baseline MERIT (84.90% Dice) by 3.69%. Across the LA (5%) and PROMISE12 (7.5%) datasets, H-SAMora achieves 92.46% and 90.14% Mean Dice respectively, setting new SOTA. Notably, SAMora variants require only 10% of the fine-tuning epochs (20 vs. 200 for SAMed; 30 vs. 300 for H-SAM) while delivering superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest SAM-based baselines, SAMora consistently wins: on 10% Synapse, SAMora (79.41%) beats SAMed (75.57%) by 3.84% Dice, and H-SAMora (84.34%) beats H-SAM (80.35%) by 3.99% Dice. In the fully supervised setting, H-SAMora (88.59%) surpasses H-SAM (86.49%) by 2.10% and the non-SAM SOTA MERIT (84.90%) by 3.69%. Against semi-supervised methods (SS-Net, MC-Net), SAMora and its variants lead by 18–23% Dice on 10% Synapse, 4–9% on 5% LA, and 2–5% on 7.5% PROMISE12. On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 6), H-SAMora outperforms MA-SAM and I-MedSAM on AMOS22 (91.84 vs. 82.70/86.26) and BTCV (92.51 vs. 83.12/85.67), while SAMora achieves the lowest inference time (3.1 s) among all compared methods. The paper also demonstrates that SAMora requires only 20 fine-tuning epochs versus 200 for SAMed and 30 versus 300 for H-SAM, a 90% reduction, while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMora extends the prompt-free SAM fine-tuning line (SAMed, H-SAM) by introducing a self-supervised pre-training stage that was previously absent from these methods, which relied solely on labeled data. It draws on three distinct SSL paradigms—contrastive learning (SimCLRv2), masked autoencoding (MAE), and denoising autoencoders—and assigns each to a different spatial hierarchy (image, patch, pixel), a combination not explored in prior medical SSL work. For multi-LoRA fusion, the paper departs from linear arithmetic composition (LAC) and gating-based approaches (MOLE, LoRAHub) by proposing a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism (HL-Attn) that respects the scale ordering of the experts rather than treating them as interchangeable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% reduction in fine-tuning epochs (20 vs. 200 for SAMed, 30 vs. 300 for H-SAM) while simultaneously improving Mean Dice is practically significant for medical imaging pipelines where labeled data is scarce and compute budgets are constrained. The framework's compatibility with multiple SAM backbones (SAM, SAM2, SAMed, H-SAM) increases its adoptability. Open questions include whether the specific SSL-to-level assignment (contrastive→image, MAE→patch, denoising→pixel) is optimal or merely one of many viable configurations, whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation without slice-wise processing, and whether the continual pre-training step on 100k unlabeled images is necessary or whether domain-adaptive SSL could be achieved with fewer samples.
