## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods require multi-exposure LDR inputs (multiple exposures per viewpoint), which are labor-intensive to capture and highly susceptible to motion blur and calibration/alignment errors. No prior method can reconstruct an HDR scene representation from single-exposure multi-view LDR images, making HDR-NVS inapplicable to standard multi-view datasets. This paper addresses the resulting ill-posed problem of recovering unbounded scene radiance from a single exposure level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed multi-view LDR images all captured at a single, uniform exposure time (e.g., 35 viewpoints with one exposure each from the HDR-NeRF dataset) and produces an HDR 3D Gaussian representation whose spherical harmonic coefficients encode view-dependent radiance in linear space. Rendered novel views are in HDR linear radiance and are tone-mapped (via PhotoMatix or the μ-law operator) for evaluation and visualization. In real-world settings, no HDR ground truth is required during training; the method is fully self-supervised on the LDR inputs.

## Method

SEHDR first trains a base 3D Gaussian Splatting representation whose SH coefficients parameterize colors in a linearized radiance space aligned with the input exposure time. It then synthesizes Bracketed 3D Gaussians by scaling the linear radiance values according to sampled exposure-time ratios (c_i^{l_j} = c_i^{l_1} · Δt_j/Δt_1), preserving identical geometry (means, covariances, opacities) while varying only the color. A Differentiable Neural Exposure Fusion (NeEF) module fuses these bracketed variants into a unified HDR Gaussian by computing per-channel well-exposedness scores via a Gaussian RBF centered at 0.5 in tone-mapped space, refining them with three channel-specific MLPs, and performing a softmax-weighted sum over the exposure dimension within the SH coefficient space. Training combines a standard LDR reconstruction loss (L1 + D-SSIM) with a self-supervised linear loss that aligns the base Gaussian's linear radiance rendering against the HDR-fused rendering scaled by the input exposure time, ensuring physically consistent radiance estimation without HDR supervision.

## Results

On the HDR-NeRF synthetic dataset (8 scenes, 5 exposure levels, single-exposure training), SEHDR without HDR ground truth achieves 25.371 PSNR, 0.810 SSIM, and 0.117 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest multi-exposure baseline HDR-GS (w/o GT) by 14.36 dB in PSNR (25.371 vs. 11.008). With HDR ground truth supervision, SEHDR reaches 37.054 PSNR and 0.032 LPIPS, slightly surpassing HDR-GS (w/ GT) at 36.821 PSNR and 0.046 LPIPS. All single-image HDR + 3DGS baselines (DrTMO, SingleHDR, IntrinsicHDR) perform poorly, with PSNR ranging from 4.6 to 14.3, confirming that naively combining 2D HDR reconstruction with 3DGS introduces severe multi-view inconsistencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEHDR (w/o GT) decisively outperforms all baselines in the single-exposure, no-HDR-supervision setting: it beats HDR-GS (w/o GT) by 14.36 dB PSNR (25.371 vs. 11.008), 0.294 SSIM, and 0.325 LPIPS; beats HDR-NeRF by 12.79 dB PSNR; and beats the best single-image HDR+GS baseline (SingleHDR+GS) by 11.12 dB PSNR. In the supervised setting (w/ GT), SEHDR marginally surpasses HDR-GS (w/ GT) by 0.23 dB PSNR and 0.014 LPIPS, while matching its SSIM at 0.965. The three single-image HDR reconstruction methods combined with 3DGS (DrTMO, SingleHDR, IntrinsicHDR) all fail substantially, with PSNR below 14.3, because per-image HDR predictions introduce multi-view inconsistencies that manifest as floaters and blurs in rendered novel views. HDR-NeRF, designed for multi-exposure inputs, degrades to 12.581 PSNR when forced onto single-exposure data, confirming that existing HDR-NVS methods cannot transfer to the single-exposure regime without the proposed bracketing strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEHDR extends the HDR-NVS line of work initiated by HDR-NeRF (Huang et al., 2022) and accelerated by HDR-GS (Cai et al., 2024), both of which require multi-exposure LDR inputs to reconstruct HDR scenes. The key departure is removing the multi-exposure requirement by drawing on the 2D exposure-bracketing principle from Endo et al.'s Deep Reverse Tone Mapping (2017) and adapting Mertens et al.'s (2007) weighted exposure-fusion paradigm into a differentiable 3D module (NeEF) operating in SH coefficient space. The paper also explicitly rejects the seemingly straightforward alternative of chaining single-image HDR reconstruction (DrTMO, SingleHDR, IntrinsicHDR) with standard 3DGS, demonstrating that multi-view inconsistencies from per-image HDR estimation produce severe floater artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for multi-exposure capture, SEHDR makes HDR novel view synthesis applicable to any standard multi-view dataset, removing a significant practical barrier (precise registration, motion blur, calibration errors) that has limited real-world deployment of HDR-NVS. The 14.3 dB PSNR gain over the strongest multi-exposure baseline (HDR-GS) under single-exposure training, without any HDR ground-truth supervision, demonstrates that the bracketing-and-fusion strategy is a viable prior for this ill-posed problem. Open questions include scalability to larger or dynamic scenes, sensitivity to the number of bracketed exposures, and whether the NeEF module's per-channel MLPs generalize across diverse camera response functions beyond the DoRF database.
