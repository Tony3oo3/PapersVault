## Problem

While continuous diffusion models have been successfully distilled into one-step generators, Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) resist this due to two fundamental obstacles: the non-differentiable discrete sampling operation makes intermediate-step gradients intractable, and the deterministic all-mask initial state lacks the entropy needed to avoid mode collapse. Prior MDM distillation methods (di4c, Deschenaux et al.) require multi-round progressive distillation and still need multiple inference steps, leaving one-step MDM generation unexplored.

## Input / Output

The one-step generator takes a conditioning signal (class label for ImageNet-256 or a text prompt for text-to-image) plus an initialized token sequence of length L=hw (where h×w is the VQ-VAE grid) and produces a complete discrete token sequence in a single forward pass, which is decoded to an RGB image. The initialization is a hybrid of r_init fraction mask tokens and (1−r_init) fraction random image tokens, with Gaussian perturbation added to all token embeddings. Because the generator is trained with conditioning, Classifier-Free Guidance is unnecessary at inference, further reducing computation.

## Method

Di[M]O performs on-policy distillation by matching token-level conditional distributions between the frozen teacher φ and the student θ at pseudo-intermediate states. The student generates tokens in one step from the initialized input; forward masking is then applied to create pseudo-intermediate states x̃_t, at which the teacher's and an auxiliary model ψ's per-token categorical distributions are compared via a generalized Jeffreys divergence. The auxiliary model ψ (initialized from the teacher) is trained with cross-entropy on the student's generated tokens to approximate the intractable student conditional, and the gradient is backpropagated through ψ's logits combined with the student's one-step output logits under a consistency assumption. A hybrid token initialization strategy (partial masking + random tokens + Gaussian perturbation at level σ_init) injects entropy to prevent mode collapse while preserving input-distribution similarity to the teacher's training regime.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation, Di[M]O achieves FID 6.91 and IS 214.0 in a single step, closely matching the MaskGit teacher's 16-step FID of 6.60 while the teacher degrades to FID 10.73 at 4 steps. On the HPSv2 text-to-image benchmark, the one-step Di[M]O generator (distilled from Meissonic) scores 28.11 averaged, surpassing the teacher's 4-step score (24.66) and approaching its 16-step score (27.90). On GenEval, Di[M]O achieves 0.43 overall, outperforming the teacher at 4 steps (0.09) and 16 steps (0.37), though trailing the 48-step teacher (0.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MaskGit teacher, Di[M]O (1 step, FID 6.91) nearly matches the 16-step teacher (FID 6.60) and substantially outperforms the 4-step teacher (FID 10.73) and 2-step teacher (FID 91.35). Compared to the θ-trapezoidal high-order sampler, Di[M]O achieves a lower FID (6.91 vs. 7.1) with 32× fewer steps (1 vs. 32). Against the prior discrete distillation method di4c (4 steps, FID 6.79), Di[M]O achieves a slightly higher FID (6.91) but with 4× fewer steps and a higher IS (214.0 vs. 209.2); di4c-d (FID 6.57) remains marginally better on FID but also requires 4 steps. On HPSv2 for text-to-image, Di[M]O (28.11) outperforms all one-step continuous diffusion distillation baselines—SD Turbo (27.48), SwiftBrush v2 (27.15), and InstaFlow (26.01)—and exceeds the Meissonic teacher at 4 steps (24.66) and 8 steps (26.46), though it trails the 48-step teacher (28.83) and SDXL (28.25). On GenEval, Di[M]O (0.43 overall) beats the Meissonic teacher at 4 steps (0.09) and 16 steps (0.37) but falls short of the 48-step teacher (0.54) and SDXL (0.55), indicating that semantic composition remains a challenge for one-step discrete generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the on-policy distillation paradigm from NLP and robotics to the discrete masked diffusion setting, combining the auxiliary-model gradient approximation strategy from continuous diffusion distillation (Diff-Instruct, DMD) with a token-level distribution matching objective that is native to MDMs. It departs from prior MDM distillation work (Hayakawa et al., Deschenaux et al.) by eliminating the multi-round progressive distillation pipeline and directly targeting one-step generation, and from continuous diffusion distillation by replacing PF-ODE-based regression or full-latent score matching with per-token conditional divergence. The consistency property assumption from Song et al. is repurposed to justify substituting the intractable intermediate-state Jacobian with the one-step output, and the generalized Jeffreys divergence extends the RKL-only objective used in prior VSD-style methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that one-step generation from masked diffusion models is achievable at competitive quality, which is significant because MDMs are increasingly used in multimodal and discrete-data pipelines where the multi-step inference cost is a practical bottleneck. The token initialization strategy (hybrid mask + random tokens + Gaussian perturbation) addresses a fundamental structural difference from continuous diffusion that had no prior solution. Open questions include whether the approach scales to stronger MDM teachers (e.g., larger vocabulary, higher resolution), whether incorporating real data during distillation can push the student beyond the teacher, and whether more general f-divergences yield further gains beyond the Jeffreys family explored here.
