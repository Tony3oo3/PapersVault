## Problem

In zero-shot learning, semantic-unrelated visual details (background clutter, lighting, surrounding objects) dilute the visual-semantic alignment needed to recognize unseen classes. Existing methods suppress this noise post hoc—either by disentangling features in the feature space or pruning tokens in the model space—but cannot fully prevent extraneous information from contaminating learned representations. The paper asks whether semantic-unrelated patches can be identified and repurposed at the input stage, before they propagate through the network.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images partitioned into 49 patches (each a 2×2 aggregation of ViT-base's native 196 patches) and class-level attribute vectors derived from GloVe word embeddings of attribute names. At inference, it produces a predicted attribute vector per image, which is matched against unseen-class attribute vectors via cosine similarity to yield the final class label. The patch classifier additionally outputs a binary semantic score per patch, and the W2P module converts K attribute word embeddings into a single learnable patch-level embedding used for contextualization.

## Method

SVIP is a ViT-base framework with three components operating at the input stage. First, Self-Supervised Patch Selection (SSPS) aggregates multi-head self-attention matrices across all L transformer layers via iterative matrix multiplication, then uses the class-token row of the aggregated matrix as pseudo-labels to train a binary patch classifier with BCE loss, identifying semantic-unrelated patches before they enter the transformer. Second, Patch Semantic Contextualization (PSC) replaces the identified semantic-unrelated patches with a learnable embedding initialized from GloVe attribute word embeddings via a Word-to-Patch (W2P) projection, preserving object structure while injecting semantic priors into the input sequence. Third, Attribute Localization maps the top-M semantic-related patch outputs through a Patch-to-Attribute (P2A) projection and max-pools over patches to predict attribute values, rather than relying solely on the class token. Training involves two forward passes (original and contextualized patches) with cross-entropy on both, a Jensen-Shannon divergence term to stabilize the contextualized branch, and the patch-classifier BCE loss, all optimized jointly.

## Results

SVIP achieves state-of-the-art GZSL harmonic mean (H) on all three benchmarks: 75.0% on CUB, 74.9% on AwA2, and 50.7% on SUN. In the ZSL setting, it reaches 79.8% top-1 accuracy on CUB, 69.8% on AwA2, and 71.6% on SUN. The largest gains over the closest ViT-based competitor ZSLViT are on SUN (+3.4 H, +7.8 U) and CUB (+1.4 H, +2.7 U), while on AwA2 the improvement is more modest (+0.7 H).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest ViT-based competitor ZSLViT (CVPR'24), SVIP wins on CUB (H 75.0 vs 73.6; U 72.1 vs 69.4) and SUN (H 50.7 vs 47.3; U 53.7 vs 45.9), but slightly trails on AwA2 T1 (69.8 vs 70.7) and U (65.4 vs 66.1). Among ResNet101-based methods, SVIP surpasses the strongest baseline HAS (MM'23) by +3.2 H on CUB, +1.6 H on AwA2, and +9.9 H on SUN, and outperforms MSDN (CVPR'22) by +6.9 H on CUB and +9.4 H on SUN. Against language-driven ViT methods (CLIP, CoOp, I2DFormer, I2MV), SVIP achieves substantially higher GZSL harmonic means, e.g., 75.0 vs 55.0 on CUB and 74.9 vs 73.8 on AwA2. The largest single-dataset gain is on SUN, where SVIP improves unseen accuracy by +7.8 over ZSLViT and +11.0 over DUET, indicating that input-stage patch contextualization is particularly effective for scene recognition with many semantic-unrelated background patches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVIP departs from two established lines of work for mitigating semantic misalignment in ZSL: feature-space disentanglement methods (RFF, SDGZSL, Dis-VAE) that suppress irrelevant information post-hoc, and model-space pruning methods (ZSLViT) that progressively remove semantic-unrelated tokens during forward propagation. Rather than operating in either of these spaces, SVIP intervenes at the input stage by training a self-supervised patch classifier—supervised by attention scores aggregated across all transformer layers—to identify and replace semantic-unrelated patches before they enter the network. The replacement strategy, using learnable embeddings initialized from GloVe attribute word embeddings, repurposes the concept of positional-embedding injection to carry semantic context into otherwise irrelevant patches, a design not seen in prior ZSL or ViT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By filtering semantic-unrelated patches at the input level, SVIP offers a cleaner inductive bias for visual-semantic alignment that could be adapted to other fine-grained recognition or attribute-based tasks beyond ZSL. However, the approach is tightly coupled to attribute-based ZSL with a fixed patch grid (49 patches from 2×2 aggregation), and the self-supervised signal relies on the assumption that aggregated attention faithfully reflects semantic relevance—an assumption that may not hold for all architectures or datasets. Open questions include whether the input-stage filtering generalizes to document-based or embedding-based ZSL, whether the method scales to larger ViT variants with more patches, and whether the semantic contextualization can be made adaptive per-class rather than using a single aggregated word embedding.
