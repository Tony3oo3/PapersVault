## Problem

Existing Text-to-Motion methods rely on Action Texts containing explicit action labels (e.g., 'walk, bend'), which limits practicality in real-world scenarios where users provide scene descriptions without direct action commands. This paper introduces the Arbitrary Text to Motion task, where Scene Texts describing events or situations (e.g., 'a person sees a beautiful sunset while camping') can yield multiple valid motion patterns, creating a multi-solution problem that existing one-to-one datasets, frameworks, and evaluation metrics cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrary natural language inputs spanning both Action Texts (containing explicit action labels) and Scene Texts (describing events/situations without action labels). The LLM Think stage converts the input text into one or more action instruction sets, which the Act stage then processes autoregressively to produce a sequence of discrete motion tokens from a VQ-VAE codebook. The final output is a continuous 3D human motion sequence decoded from these tokens, with the multi-solution nature meaning a single Scene Text can produce several distinct but contextually valid motion sequences.

## Method

TAAT (Think and Act) decouples scene comprehension from motion generation into two phases. In the Think phase, a LLaMA-based LLM fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8) maps arbitrary text to multiple plausible action instruction sets, leveraging the LLM's zero-shot scene interpretation to handle the multi-solution nature. In the Act phase, a transformer (18 layers, 1024 hidden dim, 16 heads) autoregressively predicts VQ-VAE motion token indices conditioned on the LLM's extracted action instructions, with parameters of the Think model frozen during Act training. For multi-action sequences, each action segment is generated sequentially, where subsequent actions are conditioned on the last n indices of the prior action to ensure temporal continuity. The VQ-VAE tokenizer follows T2M-GPT's approach, and the full token sequence is decoded into a smooth 3D motion. This architecture avoids the deterministic one-to-one mapping constraint of prior T2M methods while ensuring complete action execution without omission.

## Results

On the Scene Text to Motion task (the paper's novel benchmark), TAAT achieves the best Hit Accuracy (79.9%) and lowest Mean Hit Distance (1.075) among all trained models, outperforming the closest competitor T2M-GPT (79.6% HA, 1.081 MHD). In the zero-shot setting (trained only on Action Texts, tested on unseen Scene Texts), TAAT also leads with 75.1% Hit Accuracy and 0.488 FID, demonstrating strong generalization. On the standard Action Texts to Motion task (Table 4), TAAT achieves the highest Diversity (10.038) and competitive FID (0.461), though T2M-GPT attains a lower FID (0.140) and MLD/MotionDiffuse achieve higher R-Precision (0.772/0.782 vs. 0.696).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAAT outperforms all five baselines on Hit Accuracy and Mean Hit Distance in both zero-shot and trained settings for Scene Text to Motion, with the closest competitor being T2M-GPT (79.6% vs. 79.9% HA; 1.081 vs. 1.075 MHD in trained mode). On FID, T2M-GPT achieves a lower value (0.316 vs. 0.379) in the trained setting, indicating slightly better sample realism, while TAAT leads in zero-shot FID (0.488 vs. 0.516). MotionDiffuse attains the highest MModality (3.191) but suffers from a substantially worse FID (2.688), suggesting mode collapse or distribution mismatch. MLD shows the largest degradation when retrained on Scene Texts (FID jumps to 9.408), indicating poor adaptability to the multi-solution setting. On the standard Action Texts to Motion task, TAAT achieves the best Diversity (10.038) but trails MLD (0.772) and MotionDiffuse (0.782) on R-Precision, while T2M-GPT achieves the best FID (0.140), suggesting that the autoregressive discrete-token approach of T2M-GPT remains strong for deterministic one-to-one text-motion alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Text to Motion (T2M) line of work (T2M-GPT, MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse) by shifting the input from deterministic Action Texts (containing explicit action labels) to arbitrary Scene Texts that describe events or situations without specifying actions. Architecturally, TAAT decouples scene comprehension (a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA LLM) from motion synthesis (a VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer similar to T2M-GPT), whereas prior coupled frameworks treat text-to-motion as a single end-to-end mapping. The multi-solution evaluation metrics (Hit Accuracy, Mean Hit Distance) depart from the single-GT assumption baked into R-Precision and MM-Dist from the HUMANML3D benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—enabling NPCs or virtual humans to react to open-ended scene descriptions rather than rigid action commands—is compelling for games, robotics, and film production. However, the dataset is anchored to HUMANML3D's 15k motions and 344 subjects, limiting motion diversity, and the LLM front-end introduces inference latency and cost that may hinder real-time deployment. Open questions include how to handle genuinely ambiguous scenes with no dominant action, whether the multi-solution evaluation generalizes beyond the HUMANML3D++ test split, and whether the Think-Act decoupling scales to longer, multi-character interactions.
