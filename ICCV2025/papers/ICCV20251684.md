## Problem

High-quality 3D data is orders of magnitude scarcer than 2D image-text pairs, causing 3D diffusion models to underperform their 2D counterparts. Existing approaches either train 3D diffusion models from scratch on limited 3D data or bolt frozen 2D diffusion models onto 3D pipelines via score distillation or multi-view generation followed by 2D-to-3D lifting, neither of which directly leverages the rich priors in pretrained 2D generators. This paper asks whether a pretrained 2D diffusion model can be directly fine-tuned to generate 3D content.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (encoded by CLIP) and produces a set of 16,384 3D Gaussians, each carrying five attributes (3D location, albedo, opacity, scale, and quaternion rotation), renderable in under 5 seconds. Internally, the generation target is a Gaussian Atlas: a 128×128×16 dense 2D grid where each pixel encodes all Gaussian attributes (3-channel attributes are padded with the 1-channel opacity to form 4-channel groups, repeated four times to match the UNet's native 4-channel latent interface). The atlas is standardized using pixelwise mean and standard deviation computed over the full GaussianVerse dataset rather than passed through the VAE encoder/decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is Gaussian Atlas, a deterministic 3D-to-2D transformation that maps unstructured 3D Gaussians onto a dense square grid via three stages: (1) sphere offsetting via Optimal Transport to place Gaussians on a unit sphere surface, (2) equirectangular projection to flatten sphere coordinates to 2D, and (3) plane offsetting via a second OT to snap projected points onto a 128×128 grid. Because the mapping is deterministic and object-agnostic, the OT indices from stage 3 are computed once and reused for all objects, ensuring consistent spatial patterns that a 2D diffusion model can learn. A pretrained Latent Diffusion UNet (initialized from LAION-5B) is fine-tuned on 205K GaussianVerse fittings using a combined diffusion loss (v-parameterization) and photometric losses (L1 RGB, accumulated opacity mask, LPIPS) between rendered denoised Gaussians and multi-view references. The approach avoids VAE-based auto-encoding of the atlas (unlike Marigold-style repurposing) because Gaussian attribute distributions differ fundamentally from natural image latents; instead, simple pixelwise standardization aligns the atlas distribution with the UNet's expected input range.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-3D generation evaluated with 100 diverse prompts, GaussianAtlas achieves a CLIP score of 23.20 and VQA score of 0.61, outperforming all baselines while using only 16K Gaussians (the minimum among all methods). Compared to the strongest baseline GaussianCube (CLIP 22.31, VQA 0.52, 33K Gaussians), the proposed method improves CLIP by 0.9 points and VQA by 17% while halving the number of Gaussians and training steps. In user studies with over 2,500 valid responses, 65% of participants preferred GaussianAtlas over GaussianCube, and 88% preferred it over TriplaneGaussian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianAtlas outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP and VQA scores. Against the closest competitor GaussianCube (the SOTA 3D Gaussian diffusion model), it achieves +0.9 CLIP and +0.09 VQA while using half the Gaussians (16K vs. 33K) and half the training steps on 3D data. Against TriplaneGaussian, which also uses 16K Gaussians, the improvement is +2.1 CLIP and +0.15 VQA, with a strong 88% user preference rate. Against DreamGaussian (optimization-based) and LGM (multi-view 2D model), the gains are larger in absolute terms (+2.7 and +2.9 CLIP respectively), though these methods use more Gaussians (40K and 66K). The paper notes that direct comparison with other 2D-representation methods (GIMDiffusion, PI3D, HexaGen3D) is not possible due to lack of open-source implementations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: repurposing pretrained 2D diffusion models for non-image tasks (Marigold for depth, GeoWizard for geometry) and generating 3D Gaussians via diffusion (GaussianCube, GVGen, L3DG). It departs from prior 3D Gaussian generators that either train 3D-native diffusion models from scratch or use frozen 2D models as external guidance (DreamFusion, GIMDiffusion) by directly fine-tuning a 2D UNet on a structured 2D representation of 3D Gaussians. The Gaussian Atlas—combining optimal transport onto a unit sphere, equirectangular projection, and a second OT onto a dense grid—differs from Triplane-based approaches (NFD, CRM, TriplaneGaussian) and UV-map methods (Omegas, GIMDiffusion) by using a universal, deterministic, per-object-agnostic flattening that preserves 3D continuity without requiring per-geometry unwrapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that the 2D-to-3D data gap can be bridged by a simple geometric reparameterization rather than complex multi-stage pipelines, potentially lowering the barrier to high-quality 3D generation. The GaussianVerse dataset (205K high-quality 3DGS fittings) is a significant community resource. Open questions include whether the unit-sphere parameterization generalizes well to elongated or non-convex objects, whether the approach extends to scene-level generation, and whether the 16K Gaussian budget is sufficient for high-frequency detail. The reliance on pre-fitted ground-truth Gaussians and the compute cost of the fitting pipeline (>3.8 A100 GPU-years) remain practical limitations.
