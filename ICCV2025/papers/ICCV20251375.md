## Problem

Publicly available abdominal CT datasets lack paired radiology reports, and per-voxel tumor annotations remain scarce, severely limiting the development of accurate report-generation AI. No existing public dataset combines 3D CT volumes, expert-reviewed reports, and tumor segmentation masks, and the few available report-generation models rely on text-similarity metrics that are insensitive to diagnostic correctness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RadGPT consumes 3D CT scans in NIfTI format (2,789,975 slices across 9,262 volumes), per-voxel tumor and organ segmentation masks (radiologist-revised or from DiffTumor/nnU-Net), and 10 example narrative reports from a target institution for style transfer. It outputs three report types—structured (template-based with WHO-standard diameters, volume, HU attenuation, Couinaud/SMA-based sub-segment location, and PDAC T-stage), narrative (free-text in the target hospital's style), and enhanced (fusing structured tumor data with 66-diagnosis clinical notes)—all paired with per-voxel masks for liver, kidney, and pancreatic tumors plus organ sub-segments and peripancreatic blood vessels.

## Method

RadGPT is a three-stage, anatomy-aware pipeline that deliberately avoids end-to-end learning in favor of deterministic, rule-based algorithms for full explainability. Stage I segments 26 anatomical structures (tumors via DiffTumor, organs/vessels via nnU-Net fine-tuned on AbdomenAtlas 1.1, liver sub-segments via HU-offset training on LiTS, pancreas sub-segments via an SMA-landmark algorithm) with radiologist revision. Stage II extracts tumor attributes—WHO-standard diameters, volume, attenuation, sub-segment location, and tumor-vessel contact angle for T-staging—through deterministic algorithms that fill a radiologist-designed template, guaranteeing reports are fully coherent with the segmentation. Stage III uses Llama 3.1 70B (AWQ) with diagnosis-conditioned in-context learning (10 matched example reports) to adapt structured reports into a target hospital's narrative style, followed by an LLM consistency check that verifies no diagnoses or quantitative details diverge from the structured source. This segmentation-assisted design lets RadGPT also operate fully automatically (skipping radiologist revision), and its deterministic core makes errors traceable to specific segmentation or rule failures rather than opaque network outputs.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution (OOD) UCSF test set, fully-automated RadGPT achieves 76.9% sensitivity for small pancreatic tumors, 92.0%/97.3% for small/large kidney tumors, and 79.6%/89.4% for small/large liver tumors—dramatically surpassing all five end-to-end VLM baselines. On the in-distribution (IID) test set, RadGPT leads in sensitivity for large tumors across all three organs (81.5% pancreatic, 93.3% kidney, 96.8% liver). RadGPT also achieves 75.6% tumor detection precision and 93.5% size measurement accuracy on 23 external CTs, and 71.43% accuracy in automated PDAC T-staging (T1–T3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadGPT decisively outperforms all five baselines on the OOD UCSF set, where end-to-end models struggle to detect tumors (sensitivity rarely exceeding 30%). CT-CHAT [20] is the strongest baseline, reaching 27.5% sensitivity for small pancreatic tumors and 24.3% for small kidney tumors, yet RadGPT nearly triples these (76.9% and 92.0%). CT2Rep [21] and RadFM [54] are near-zero on OOD sensitivity for most organs, indicating poor generalization. Merlin [8] achieves 100% specificity on OOD but at the cost of very low sensitivity (7.5–19.2%), making it clinically uninformative for tumor detection. M3D [4] shows moderate kidney sensitivity (14.8% small) but remains far below RadGPT. The trade-off is that RadGPT's higher sensitivity comes with lower specificity (e.g., 73.4% liver vs. 100% for Merlin), suggesting a more aggressive detection profile that may require radiologist review.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own AbdomenAtlas 1.1 (organ-only segmentation masks) into a full image-text-mask triplet dataset by adding tumor segmentation, sub-segmentation, and radiology reports. Conceptually, RadGPT departs from the dominant end-to-end VLM paradigm (M3D, Merlin, CT2Rep, CT-CHAT, RadFM) by inserting explicit per-voxel segmentation as an intermediate representation and using deterministic, rule-based algorithms to extract clinically meaningful attributes before any LLM is invoked. The LLM (Llama 3.1 70B) is used only for style adaptation via in-context learning, not for content generation, which is a deliberate architectural choice for interpretability and coherence with ground-truth masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AbdomenAtlas 3.0 is the first public abdominal CT dataset providing paired CT scans, per-voxel tumor masks, and expert-reviewed radiology reports, filling a critical gap that has limited report-generation research. The 4.2× expansion of tumor annotations and the inclusion of pancreas sub-segments, peripancreatic vessels, and PDAC T-stage labels make it a uniquely rich resource for segmentation-assisted report generation. Open questions include whether the deterministic pipeline generalizes beyond the three tumor types covered, whether the LLM-based diagnostic evaluation (96% validated accuracy) is sufficient for broader clinical claims, and whether the 71.43% staging accuracy is clinically acceptable without radiologist revision. The cancer-centric focus and limited enhanced-report count (240) are acknowledged limitations.
