## Problem

Existing semi-supervised learning methods rely exclusively on visual features and one-hot categorical labels, causing them to fail on fine-grained classification where visually similar classes (e.g., different cake varieties, or 'boy' vs. 'man') are frequently confused. This leads to large performance gaps between SSL and fully supervised learning on datasets like Food-101, where FreeMatch achieves only 60.4% accuracy versus 98.8% for supervised training. The rich semantic information embedded in label names is entirely unused by prior SSL approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled images with categorical class labels, a large pool of unlabeled images, and the set of class label names (short text strings). Label names are encoded once by a frozen text encoder at initialization and cached, so during training only images are processed by the visual backbone while text embeddings are retrieved from a pre-computed bank. The output is a standard class probability vector over C classes, with the visual model trained from scratch using a ViT-Small backbone under the USB benchmark protocol.

## Method

SemiVisBooster augments the FreeMatch SSL framework with a boosting branch that aligns visual features with text embeddings derived from label names. A learnable projector (single-layer MLP) maps frozen text encoder outputs into a text embedding bank, and two novel losses are introduced: (1) TEDS, which penalizes the absolute cosine similarity between projected text embeddings of different classes to counteract the high inherent similarity of raw text embeddings (often >0.92), and (2) CAViTA, a class-aware contrastive alignment loss that uses a binary mask to correctly identify positive pairs when multiple images in a batch share the same class text embedding—avoiding the false-negative penalty that NT-Xent would impose. The total training objective combines the standard supervised cross-entropy, the FreeMatch pseudo-label consistency loss, the CAViTA alignment loss (weight λ_c=1, temperature 0.07), and the TEDS loss (weight λ_TEDS=0.01), with the text encoder kept frozen and offloaded from VRAM for efficiency.

## Results

SemiVisBooster achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all five SSL benchmarks. The most striking result is on Food-101 (404 labeled images), where it reaches 73.6%, surpassing the second-best method (EPASS, 64.8%) by 8.8 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 (200 labels) it achieves 81.8% (vs. AdaMatch's 79.4%), on STL-10 (40 labels) 95.4% (vs. EPASS's 91.5%), on Semi-Aves (3959 labels) 70.3% (vs. AdaMatch's 68.4%), and on EuroSAT (20 labels) 96.8% (vs. DeSSL's 96.3%). The method also outperforms CLIP-ViT-B and CLIP-ViT-L zero-shot on CIFAR-100 and EuroSAT despite using a smaller ViT-Small backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiVisBooster outperforms all 13 prior SSL baselines on every dataset and label budget. The largest margin is on Food-101 (404 labels), where it beats EPASS (64.8%) by 8.8 points and FreeMatch (60.6%) by 13.0 points, confirming the paper's central claim that label semantics are critical for fine-grained SSL. On CIFAR-100 (200 labels), it surpasses AdaMatch (79.4%) by 2.4 points and FreeMatch (78.9%) by 2.9 points. On STL-10 (40 labels), it leads EPASS (91.5%) by 3.9 points. On EuroSAT (20 labels), the margin is smaller (96.8% vs. DeSSL's 96.3%), reflecting the coarser-grained nature of the task. Against CLIP zero-shot, SemiVisBooster wins on CIFAR-100 (81.8% vs. 65.1% for ViT-B) and EuroSAT (96.8% vs. 49.4%), but CLIP-ViT-L outperforms it on Food-101 (92.9% vs. 73.6%) and STL-10 (99.3% vs. 95.4%), likely due to CLIP's exposure to 400M image-text pairs during pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FreeMatch's self-adaptive thresholding framework for semi-supervised learning, adding a 'boosting branch' that injects label-name semantics into the training loop. Conceptually, it repurposes the visual-text alignment paradigm from CLIP-style pre-training (NT-Xent contrastive loss over image-caption pairs) into the SSL setting, where pseudo-labels replace ground-truth captions and multiple images share the same text embedding. The two proposed components—TEDS to decorrelate similar text embeddings and CAViTA to handle many-to-one positive pairs—address challenges specific to this SSL adaptation that do not arise in standard vision-language pre-training. The work also connects to the broader SSL literature on pseudo-label quality (FlexMatch, SoftMatch, EPASS) by providing an orthogonal signal that stabilizes pseudo-label assignment for visually confusable classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~13.6% improvement on Food-101 with only 404 labeled images demonstrates that label-name semantics can close a substantial portion of the SSL-to-supervised gap on fine-grained tasks, a setting where prior SSL methods plateau. The method's practicality is high: the text encoder is frozen, embeddings are precomputed, and the added overhead is a single MLP projector plus two loss terms, making it a drop-in enhancement for any existing SSL pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to datasets with non-descriptive or multilingual label names, whether it helps at larger label budgets where visual features already separate classes well, and whether the alignment signal can be extended beyond classification to detection or segmentation. The finding that a simple FastText word-embedding model nearly matches CLIP's text encoder suggests the semantic signal needed is relatively shallow, which both broadens applicability and raises questions about what level of linguistic understanding is truly required.
