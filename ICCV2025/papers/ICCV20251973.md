## Problem

Existing GCD methods built on pre-trained ViT backbones suffer from a hidden stumbling block: distracted attention, where the model's self-attention on unlabeled data spreads to task-irrelevant background regions rather than concentrating on key objects. This degrades feature quality and downstream classification accuracy, yet no prior work had identified or quantified this phenomenon. The paper is the first to systematically investigate and address this issue in the GCD setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images processed by a ViT backbone, where each image is divided into P×P patches forming a token sequence of length N+1 (including the [CLS] token). AF is inserted as a plug-and-play module within the ViT blocks: it prunes a variable number of non-informative patch tokens before the final block, and the surviving tokens (plus [CLS]) are average-pooled to produce the image representation fed into any existing GCD head. The number of retained tokens is image-specific and adaptive, determined by a threshold on multi-scale importance scores rather than a fixed count.

## Method

AF comprises two cascading components: Token Importance Measurement (TIME) and Token Adaptive Pruning (TAP). TIME inserts a learnable query vector Q into each ViT block (except the last) that computes cross-attention scores over all patch tokens, trained exclusively on labeled data via an auxiliary classifier with cross-entropy loss; a stop-gradient isolates the classifier from the backbone. TAP aggregates the per-block importance scores into a single multi-scale vector, then applies an adaptive threshold τ to prune the least informative patch tokens, retaining only those whose cumulative importance exceeds τ. The remaining tokens pass through the final ViT block and are average-pooled for the GCD head. A key design choice is single-view TAP (applied to only one of two augmented views), which acts as a form of non-regular cropping augmentation and avoids over-constraining the model's generalization.

## Results

On the three fine-grained GCD benchmarks, SimGCD+AF achieves 69.0% / 67.0% / 59.4% clustering accuracy (All) on CUB, Stanford Cars, and FGVC-Aircraft respectively, improving over the SimGCD baseline by +8.7, +13.2, and +5.2 points. The largest gain appears on Stanford Cars New classes (+15.4 points, from 45.0% to 60.4%), where background interference is most severe. On generic datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100) and Herbarium-19, gains are smaller (+0.7 to +2.4 on All accuracy), consistent with simpler backgrounds offering less room for attention correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Stanford Cars, SimGCD+AF (67.0% All) decisively outperforms all baselines, beating the next-best MOS (64.6%) by 2.4 points and AptGCD (62.1%) by 4.9 points, with the largest margin on New classes (60.4% vs MOS 56.7%). On CUB, SimGCD+AF (69.0%) is competitive but trails AptGCD (70.3%) and MOS (69.6%) by 1.3 and 0.6 points respectively, while surpassing InfoSieve (69.4%) is a near-tie and CMS (68.2%) by 0.8 points. On FGVC-Aircraft, SimGCD+AF (59.4%) is slightly below AptGCD and MOS (both 61.1%) but edges out SPTNet (59.3%). On generic datasets, gains are modest: CIFAR-10 improves by only +0.7% (97.8% vs 97.1%), CIFAR-100 by +2.1% on All but actually drops 1.3% on New classes, and ImageNet-100 by +2.4%. The paper notes that AF's advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained datasets with complex backgrounds, while on simpler datasets the baseline already performs near-saturation, leaving little room for attention correction to help.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SimGCD baseline for Generalized Category Discovery and borrows the FFN-based Aggregator design from Token Cropr, but repurposes the token-pruning paradigm from an efficiency tool into an accuracy mechanism. While prior token-pruning work (EVit, SPVit, ToMe, Cropr) targets inference speed and high-resolution recognition methods (IPS, Monkey) target supervised settings, AF is the first to use adaptive, multi-scale token pruning specifically to correct distracted attention in the semi-supervised GCD setting. Two concurrent works (MOS, AptGCD) also address background interference in GCD, but AF differs in motivation (attention correction via pruning vs. object-scene modeling or prompt tuning) and in its plug-and-play, backbone-agnostic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying distracted attention as a previously overlooked bottleneck in GCD, the paper reframes part of the performance gap on fine-grained datasets as an attention-allocation problem rather than a representation-learning problem, opening a new diagnostic axis for the field. The plug-and-play nature of AF (validated across SimGCD, CMS, SelEx, and GET) makes it immediately adoptable, and the finding that training the importance queries on unlabeled data actively degrades performance highlights a subtle pitfall in semi-supervised query learning. A key open question the authors acknowledge is that AF suppresses background noise but does not improve the model's ability to extract more discriminative features from the key object itself, suggesting complementary work on feature sharpening within the retained tokens.
