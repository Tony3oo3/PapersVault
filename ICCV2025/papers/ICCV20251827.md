## Problem

Federated learning under combined local non-IID and global long-tailed data distributions suffers a large performance gap relative to centralized training, primarily due to poor feature representations and biased classifiers. Existing neural-collapse-inspired methods (FedETF, FedLoGe) construct synthetic ETF classifiers but fail to reach neural collapse optima under severe heterogeneity, leaving a substantial centralized-federated gap (up to 20%) unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Each client processes local RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet) through a ResNet-8 or ResNet-50 backbone. Each sample is consumed twice per training step as a weakly augmented view (RandomCrop, HorizontalFlip, Rotation) and a strongly augmented view (RandAugment), and the model outputs a class probability vector computed via a distribution-fused balanced softmax rather than standard softmax. The server aggregates client models via FedAvg and also aggregates estimated class distributions to form an approximate global prior.

## Method

FedYoYo introduces two co-designed components. Augmented Self-bootstrap Distillation (ASD) uses the logits of a weakly augmented sample as a self-teacher to distill knowledge into the strongly augmented view via KL divergence, but unlike BYOL it learns logits (the unified prediction space) rather than features and uses a single model as its own teacher rather than separate online/target networks; only correctly classified weak samples serve as teachers to avoid noise. Distribution-aware Logit Adjustment (DLA) calibrates the classifier with a balanced softmax weighted by a fused distribution π_mix = (1−γ)·π_g + γ·π_k, where the local prior π_k is estimated via Pearson correlation coefficients over batch features and the global prior π_g is approximated by server-side FedAvg aggregation of client priors. The total loss is L_DLA + λ·L_ASD, combining supervised classification on both augmented views with the distillation objective, enabling the method to close the centralized-federated gap from 20% to 1.3% and even surpass centralized logit adjustment by 5.4% under global long-tailed settings.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100, α=0.5), FedYoYo achieves 81.45% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best federated baseline Fed-Grab (70.63%) by 10.82% and even exceeding centralized training with logit adjustment (77.27%) by 4.18%. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), it reaches 46.13%, outperforming FedLoGe (42.63%) and centralized LA (41.57%). On ImageNet-LT (α=0.1), FedYoYo attains 31.41% overall accuracy with 15.44% on few-shot classes, an 8.04% improvement over the best baseline. Under vanilla non-IID (α=0.1), the centralized-federated gap shrinks from ~20% (FedAvg) to just 1.21% (88.82% vs. 90.03% centralized on CIFAR-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedYoYo outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and setting. The closest competitor is Fed-Grab [32], which FedYoYo surpasses by 10.82% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100), 12.60% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), and 1.34% on SVHN-LT (IF=100). Among neural-collapse-inspired methods, FedETF [16] and FedLoGe [31] are the strongest baselines; FedYoYo exceeds FedETF by 13.20% on CIFAR-10-LT and 3.50% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), and beats FedLoGe by 10.91% and 3.50% respectively. On vanilla non-IID CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), FedYoYo (88.82%) surpasses FedETF (83.10%) and FedLC (82.03%) by 5.72% and 6.79%, approaching the centralized upper bound of 90.03%. Notably, FedYoYo exceeds centralized training with logit adjustment [21] by 4.18% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100) and by 5.4% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), demonstrating that the federated method can surpass the centralized oracle under long-tailed conditions. Heterogeneity-only methods (FedProx, CCVR) and imbalance-only methods (τ-norm, LWS, BalanceFL) all lag significantly, confirming that addressing both local non-IID and global long-tailed heterogeneity simultaneously is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedYoYo repurposes the BYOL self-bootstrap paradigm from self-supervised learning for federated settings, but makes two key departures: it distills logits (the unified prediction space) rather than features, and uses a single local model as its own teacher rather than BYOL's online/target pair. The distribution-aware logit adjustment component extends Menon et al.'s logit adjustment from centralized long-tail learning to the federated setting by fusing locally estimated distributions (via Pearson-correlation-based effective priors, inspired by AREA) with a server-aggregated global distribution. The paper explicitly positions itself against neural-collapse-inspired methods (FedETF, FedLoGe), arguing they fail to reach neural collapse optima under severe heterogeneity, and instead pursues a self-supervised representation-learning angle that prior FL work has largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Closing the centralized-federated gap from ~20% to 1.3% and actually surpassing centralized logit adjustment by 5.4% under global long-tailed settings is a strong practical result, suggesting that the representation-learning bottleneck in FL can be addressed without extra datasets, models, or server-side calibration. The co-design insight—that self-bootstrap distillation and logit adjustment must be jointly optimized rather than applied independently—offers a reusable recipe for other heterogeneous FL scenarios. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with ResNet backbones, a brief privacy discussion, and reliance on estimated (not ground-truth) global distributions, leaving open whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities or more extreme client counts.
