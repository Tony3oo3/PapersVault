## Problem

In Source-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection, the Mean Teacher's EMA mechanism updates all teacher parameters synchronously with a single smoothing coefficient, creating an inherent conflict between preserving learned source knowledge and adapting to target-domain shifts. This lag causes the teacher's pseudo-labels to progressively contradict the student's adaptation, impeding acquisition of domain-specific features and amplifying error accumulation from inaccurate labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB target-domain images (processed via weak, strong, and masked augmentations) together with the weights of a pre-trained source detector (Deformable DETR); no source-domain data is accessed during adaptation. It outputs standard object detection predictions—bounding boxes with class labels—evaluated via mAP@0.5 on traffic-scene benchmarks (Cityscapes, FoggyCityscapes, BDD100K, Sim10K). The teacher and student share identical architecture, and the teacher's role is purely to generate pseudo-labels that guide student optimization.

## Method

DDT introduces Asynchronous EMA (AEMA), which decouples teacher parameter updates into two groups based on gradient magnitude through the teacher detection loss: the top 10% of parameters (highest gradient) are designated target-responsive and updated with a low smoothing coefficient (α₁≈0.997) for rapid adaptation, while the remaining stability-preserving parameters use a high coefficient (α₂≈0.9996) to retain historical knowledge. Pseudo-labels from both teacher and student are fused via NMS to form integrated supervision, compensating for each model's complementary weaknesses. The student is optimized with detection loss on strong and masked (MIC) images against these integrated pseudo-labels, while the teacher is updated asynchronously via the group-wise AEMA rule. Built on Deformable DETR with weak-strong augmentation and Masked Image Consistency inherited from DRU, the framework achieves state-of-the-art SF-DAOD performance across three adaptation tasks.

## Results

On the Normal-to-Foggy adaptation task (Cityscapes → FoggyCityscapes), DDT achieves 45.5 mAP, outperforming the previous SOTA SF-DAOD method DRU (43.6 mAP) by 1.9 points and SF-UT (45.0 mAP) by 0.5 points. On Cross Scene Adaptation (Cityscapes → BDD100K), DDT reaches 39.6 mAP, surpassing DRU (36.6 mAP) by 3.0 points and LPU (33.5 mAP) by 6.1 points. On Synthetic-to-Real Adaptation (Sim10K → Cityscapes), DDT attains 60.6 AP for the car class, exceeding DRU (58.7) and even the DAOD method MTM (58.1) by 1.9 and 2.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDT is compared against 23 baselines spanning both DAOD (source-data-accessible) and SF-DAOD (source-free) paradigms across three adaptation tasks. Among SF-DAOD methods using the same DefDETR backbone, DDT outperforms DRU [17] by 1.9 mAP on Foggy Adaptation (45.5 vs 43.6), 3.0 mAP on Cross Scene (39.6 vs 36.6), and 1.9 AP on Sim10K→Cityscapes (60.6 vs 58.7). Against FRCNN-based SF-DAOD methods, DDT leads SF-UT [10] by 0.5 mAP (45.5 vs 45.0) on Foggy and LPU [4] by 6.1 mAP (39.6 vs 33.5) on Cross Scene. Notably, DDT also surpasses DAOD methods that have access to source data: it exceeds MTM [44] by 2.5 AP on Sim10K→Cityscapes (60.6 vs 58.1) and approaches MRT [44] (51.2 mAP) on Foggy Adaptation despite operating in the more constrained source-free setting. The generalization study further shows AEMA improves TransDA [36] from 83.0 to 84.2 average accuracy on VisDA (SFDA classification) and MRT [44] from 51.2 to 51.6 mAP in DAOD mode, confirming the mechanism's transferability beyond the primary SF-DAOD setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) for source-free domain adaptive object detection by replacing the uniform exponential moving average with an Asynchronous EMA (AEMA) that applies group-specific smoothing coefficients. It builds directly on DRU (Le Ba Khanh et al., ECCV 2024) for the overall training pipeline (weak-strong augmentation, masked image consistency, Deformable DETR backbone), but departs from the synchronous EMA paradigm that all prior SF-DAOD methods inherit. The parameter grouping strategy draws conceptual inspiration from the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, repurposing the idea that only a sparse subset of parameters drives learning to identify which parameters should adapt quickly versus which should remain stable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, previously underexplored tension in Mean Teacher-based domain adaptation: the single EMA coefficient cannot simultaneously preserve historical knowledge and track emerging domain shifts. The empirical finding that target-responsive parameters shift hierarchically from backbone to encoder/decoder during training provides useful mechanistic insight into how domain adaptation unfolds. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (Deformable DETR), only three adaptation tasks, and the added cost of computing teacher gradients for parameter grouping each step. Open questions include whether the dual-rate strategy generalizes to other teacher-student paradigms (e.g., multi-teacher frameworks like PETS) and whether the 10% selection ratio and coefficient values transfer across architectures and domain gaps without re-tuning.
