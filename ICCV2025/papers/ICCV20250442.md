## Problem

Existing panoramic image generation methods rely on joint diffusion with heuristic crop-connection strategies (weighted averaging, gradient guidance, attention merging) that struggle to achieve multilevel coherence across both low-level features (color, edges) and high-level features (semantics, layout). Meanwhile, visual autoregressive models that naturally model sequential dependencies are limited to fixed-size generation (typically 512×512) and cannot produce arbitrarily long panoramas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250442/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded into a conditional embedding via a text encoder) and optionally a reference image (tokenized for multi-guidance) as input, and produces a panoramic RGB image of arbitrary dimensions. In experiments, panoramas are generated at 512×5120 (10× wider than the base LlamaGen model's 512×512 output), with the output structured as a sequence of spatially-ordered token blocks decoded by the image tokenizer. The expansion stride u = c/√p controls the overlap between adjacent crops, trading off coherence against generation speed.

## Method

PanoLlama redefines panoramic generation as a next-crop prediction task, modeling the panorama as a product of conditional probabilities P(x_i | x_1,...,x_{i-1}) that captures both local and global dependencies. The key mechanism is a training-free token redirection strategy applied to a pre-trained VAR model (LlamaGen): for vertical expansion, when the token limit p is reached, generation restarts at position p−r√p using the last p−r√p tokens as conditioning; for horizontal expansion, an interleaved row-wise scheme redirects the last √p−c tokens of each preceding row as the starting condition for the corresponding row in the next block. This extends the fixed-size model to arbitrary panorama dimensions without any fine-tuning, and the concatenated token sequence is decoded into pixels via the image tokenizer decoder. The approach also enables mask-free layout control (by varying rows, columns, and prompts per region) and multi-guidance synthesis (text + image conditioning) that joint diffusion methods cannot support.

## Results

On the PanoLlama Prompt Benchmark (1,000 prompts, 512×5120 panoramas), PanoLlama achieves the best scores across all four evaluation aspects: coherence (LPIPS 0.410, DISTS 0.196, TV 0.021, SSIM 0.305), fidelity & diversity (FID 40.09, IS 5.97), compatibility (CLIP 31.56, CLIP-aesthetic 6.97), and competitive efficiency (726 s per batch of 50). Compared to the strongest coherence baseline MAD, PanoLlama improves TV by 47.50%, LPIPS by 21.15%, DISTS by 6.22%, and SSIM by 13.81%. The FID of 40.09 represents only a +2.27 relative degradation over the reference LlamaGen model (37.82), the smallest gap among all PIG methods, indicating minimal quality loss during panoramic expansion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoLlama outperforms all six baselines on every coherence metric (LPIPS, DISTS, TV, SSIM), with the largest margin over BLD (LPIPS 0.410 vs. 0.829) and the closest competition with MAD (LPIPS 0.410 vs. 0.520, a 21% relative improvement). On fidelity, PanoLlama achieves the best FID (40.09) and IS (5.97), substantially outperforming BLD (FID 89.51) and SMD (FID 89.64), and narrowly beating MD (FID 39.30) while MAD trails significantly (FID 59.23). In terms of aesthetic quality (CLIP-aesthetic), PanoLlama scores 6.97, edging out SyncD (6.94) and MAD (6.90), and clearly surpassing BLD (5.76). On efficiency, PanoLlama (726 s) is 3–10× faster than MD, TD, SyncD, and MAD, but slower than the real-time SMD (241 s); however, SMD sacrifices coherence and fidelity to achieve that speed. Overall, PanoLlama is the only method that simultaneously leads in coherence, fidelity, and aesthetics, whereas each baseline excels in at most one dimension (e.g., MAD in coherence but poor fidelity; SMD in speed but poor quality).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant joint diffusion paradigm for panoramic image generation (MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion, TwinDiffusion, MAD, SMD) and the inpainting-based approach (BLD), reframing PIG as a next-token prediction task aligned with autoregressive modeling. It builds directly on LlamaGen as the fixed-size VAR reference model, extending its generation capability to arbitrary-length panoramas through a training-free token redirection strategy that exploits the raster-scan token ordering. The conceptual lineage draws from LLM next-token prediction applied to visual tokens, bridging the gap between sequential language modeling and spatial image extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the token redirection strategy makes this immediately practical—no retraining of the base VAR model is required, lowering the barrier to adopting AR-based PIG. The 1,000-prompt benchmark across 100+ themes addresses a real gap in standardized evaluation that prior PIG works (typically using 5–20 prompts) left unaddressed. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that global conditioning is approximated by a partial token window due to the fixed token capacity of pre-trained VAR models, leaving full-context autoregressive panorama generation as an open problem. Whether the AR paradigm generalizes beyond LlamaGen to other VAR architectures or to higher-resolution base models remains to be explored.
