## Problem

Generating multi-view consistent RGB images from depth-only data in complex multi-room environments remains unsolved: autoregressive warp-and-inpainting pipelines accumulate style drift and geometric errors over long trajectories, panorama-based methods cannot handle multi-room layouts, and prior diffusion-synchronization approaches are limited to constrained viewpoint variations. No existing training-free method enforces cross-view coherence under arbitrary camera trajectories with extensive perspective changes and spatial discontinuities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N overlapping depth images with known camera poses (collected by an autonomous robot along planned trajectories) and a single text prompt describing scene content/style, and produces N photorealistic RGB images that are mutually consistent in overlapping 3D regions. The depth views must satisfy an overlap constraint (each view shares area with at least one other), and the generated images can be fused via a standard TSDF pipeline into a textured 3D mesh. Captions are auto-generated by GPT-4o from rendered depth maps, and the entire pipeline runs in minutes per scene without any additional training.

## Method

MOSAIC formulates multi-view generation as a joint denoising process where N depth-conditioned views share a single latent channel set and are refined simultaneously by a pre-trained LDM (SDXL + ControlNet). At every denoising step, a depth-weighted projection loss back-projects each view's estimated clean latent into a common scene representation and penalizes pairwise disagreement in overlapping regions, with closer views (smaller depth) receiving higher weight to prioritize more reliable RGB estimates. A pixel-space refinement loss in the final denoising stages decodes latents, warps and depth-weighted-fuses them across views, re-encodes the blended result, and pulls the latent predictions toward it to correct VAE non-linearity. The paper proves that conditioning on more overlapping views monotonically reduces the expected variance of the reconstructed scene (via the law of total variance), explaining why MOSAIC avoids the error accumulation inherent in sequential generation. The entire procedure is training-free: it requires no fine-tuning and scales to arbitrary numbers of views at inference time.

## Results

On the full 21-scene benchmark (HM3D + MP3D), MOSAIC achieves the best KID (0.03391↓), CLIPScore (0.7166↑), CLIQA+ (0.6526↑), and CLIPConsistency (30.85↑) among all baselines. In terms of geometric consistency, MOSAIC reaches a Warped PSNR of 25.30 and Warped Ratio of 0.99, nearly matching ground truth (25.45 / 1.00) and far exceeding MVDiffusion (13.58 / 0.53) and Warp-Inpaint (22.00 / 0.86). On the 8-scene subset where SceneTex is feasible, MOSAIC still leads on KID (0.06547 vs. 0.10536) and CLIQA+ (0.7322 vs. 0.6308).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOSAIC outperforms all five baselines on every perceptual metric in Table 1. Against the closest competitor MVDiffusion, MOSAIC improves KID from 0.03640 to 0.03391, CLIQA+ from 0.6025 to 0.6526, and CLIPConsistency from 29.41 to 30.85; the gap is most dramatic in geometric consistency, where MOSAIC's Warped PSNR (25.30) is nearly double MVDiffusion's (13.58) and its Warped Ratio (0.99) is close to ground truth versus MVDiffusion's 0.53. Warp-Inpaint is the second-strongest baseline (Warped PSNR 22.00, Ratio 0.86) but still trails MOSAIC by 3.3 dB in Warped PSNR and 0.03 in Warped Ratio, reflecting its error-accumulation problem over sequential generation. SceneCraft and Text2Room lag substantially on all metrics (KID 0.077 and 0.086 respectively; CLIQA+ below 0.46), with SceneCraft's NeRF backbone producing pronounced blur and Text2Room exhibiting patchwork seams. On the 8-scene subset where SceneTex is evaluated, MOSAIC wins on KID (0.06547 vs. 0.10536), CLIPScore (0.7141 vs. 0.7045), and CLIQA+ (0.7322 vs. 0.6308), while also completing in minutes versus SceneTex's >20 hours per scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSAIC builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM/SDXL) backbone with ControlNet depth conditioning, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-view generation pipelines. Where MVDiffusion trains an additional correspondence-aware module and warp-and-inpainting methods (Text2Room, SceneCraft) sequentially extend a panorama, MOSAIC performs simultaneous multi-channel inference-time optimization on a frozen pre-trained model, enforcing cross-view consistency through a depth-weighted projection loss at every denoising step. The theoretical framing—modeling inter-view dependency via an explicit projection conditional and proving variance reduction with more overlapping views—extends the diffusion synchronization literature (SyncTweedies, SyncDiffusion) from constrained settings (orthogonal transforms, evenly spaced cameras) to arbitrary scene-level trajectories with large perspective changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The privacy-preserving angle (depth-only capture in hospitals, elder-care facilities, factories) gives the work immediate practical relevance for robotics and digital-twin deployment where RGB collection is restricted. The training-free, inference-time-optimization design means the approach can be grafted onto any future pre-trained diffusion model without retraining, and the variance-reduction theorem provides a principled explanation for why more overlapping views help rather than merely an empirical observation. Open questions include scalability to outdoor or dynamic scenes, the computational cost of the multi-channel optimization at very large N, and whether the pixel-space refinement can be made differentiable through the VAE to avoid the current re-encoding workaround.
