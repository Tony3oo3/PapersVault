## Problem

Existing Visual Semantic Embedding (VSE) models for image-text matching learn embeddings with limited information capacity because they are trained on short, sparse text descriptions, making them vulnerable to negative samples with similar local semantics. The paper argues that the information density gap between rich visual content and brief textual annotations is the root cause of the multi-view description problem, and that enhancing the information capacity of both visual and textual embeddings is the key to robust matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251434/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a short sparse text description (e.g., 5–15 words from Flickr30K or MS-COCO) and produces a cosine similarity score in a 512-dimensional shared embedding space for retrieval ranking. During training, dense text descriptions (~500 tokens) generated by LLaVA are additionally consumed by a frozen teacher encoder for distillation, but these are not required at test time, incurring no extra inference cost. The image encoder uses ViT or Swin Transformer with GPO pooling, and the text encoder uses BERT-base with GPO pooling.

## Method

D2S-VSE is a two-stage framework that aligns information capacity between vision and language. In Stage I (pre-training), LLaVA generates dense captions for each image, and the VSE model is trained on image–dense-text pairs via triplet contrastive loss, forcing the visual encoder to extract richer features and produce embeddings with higher information capacity. In Stage II (fine-tuning), the model is trained on standard image–sparse-text pairs for alignment while simultaneously performing dense-to-sparse feature distillation: a 4-layer Transformer decoder with 100 learnable mask tokens (placed in a 'surround' position around the sparse word embeddings) reconstructs the missing context of the sparse text, and the final sparse embedding is the sum of the original sparse embedding and the decoder output. The distillation loss is negative cosine similarity between the frozen dense-text teacher embedding and the reconstructed sparse embedding, jointly optimized with the triplet alignment loss. Notably, D2S-VSE achieves state-of-the-art results without any fine-grained cross-modal alignment module, attributing its gains purely to the information-capacity alignment mechanism.

## Results

On Flickr30K with ViT-Base-224 + BERT-base, D2S-VSE achieves rSum of 531.9 (Text R@1: 82.8%, Image R@1: 68.5%), outperforming the closest competitor AVSE (rSum 512.3) by 19.6 points. On MS-COCO 1K, D2S-VSE reaches rSum 533.7 (Text R@1: 80.1%, Image R@1: 68.1%), and on MS-COCO 5K it achieves rSum 445.6. With the larger ViT-Base-384 backbone, D2S-VSE further improves to rSum 537.9 on Flickr30K and 535.8 on MS-COCO 1K, maintaining consistent gains over all baselines across both datasets and backbone sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2S-VSE outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and backbone configuration. The closest competitor is AVSE [26], which D2S-VSE surpasses by 6.8% in text R@1 (82.8 vs 76.0) and 5.8% in image R@1 (68.5 vs 62.7) on Flickr30K with ViT-Base-224, and by 1.3% in text R@1 (80.1 vs 79.8) and 1.1% in image R@1 (68.1 vs 67.0) on MS-COCO 1K. LAPS [11], the strongest fine-grained method, trails D2S-VSE by 8.8% in text R@1 and 6.0% in image R@1 on Flickr30K. Earlier global-matching baselines (VSE++ [9], SCAN [17], SGR [7], CHAN [29]) are outperformed by 11–13% in text R@1 on Flickr30K. On the Swin-Base-384 backbone (Table 2), D2S-VSE achieves rSum 553.2 on Flickr30K, beating AVSE (548.2) by 5.0 points and LAPS (545.3) by 7.9 points. Notably, D2S-VSE achieves these gains without fine-grained cross-modal alignment (FG=✗), whereas methods like LAPS and CHAN rely on it, suggesting the dense-to-sparse distillation mechanism provides a more effective route to handling multi-view descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Semantic Embedding (VSE) lineage initiated by VSE++ (Faghri et al., 2018), which established the shared-embedding-space + triplet-loss paradigm for image-text matching. It departs from the set-of-embeddings approaches (PCME, DivE, AVSE, MV-VSE) that learn multiple embeddings per image/text to handle multi-view descriptions, arguing instead that the core bottleneck is the limited information capacity of a single embedding. The dense-to-sparse distillation mechanism borrows the masked-signal-reconstruction decoder design from MAE (He et al., 2022), repurposing learnable tokens to reconstruct missing semantics in sparse text rather than visual patches. Unlike long-text pre-training methods (DreamLip, Long-CLIP, LoTLIP), D2S-VSE uses a small amount of LLaVA-generated dense text solely as a pre-training signal and a distillation teacher, requiring no dense text at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical two-stage recipe—pre-train with MLLM-generated dense captions, then fine-tune with sparse data plus a lightweight distillation decoder—that improves retrieval SOTA without increasing inference cost, making it directly adoptable for existing VSE pipelines. The 'information capacity' framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for why set-based methods underperform: splitting semantics across multiple embeddings dilutes each one's capacity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond English Flickr30K/COCO-scale data, how sensitive performance is to the quality of LLaVA-generated dense text, and whether the distillation decoder can be replaced by lighter alternatives. The reliance on an external MLLM for dense text generation also raises reproducibility and compute-cost concerns for the pre-training stage.
