## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D Gaussian Splatting methods lack a sufficiently large-scale, high-fidelity, and culturally diverse real-world dataset for evaluation. Existing benchmarks are either synthetic (MatrixCity, suffering domain shift) or scene-homogeneous (GauU-Scene, mostly uniform outdoor environments), leaving a gap for testing reconstruction on complex, architecturally intricate cultural heritage sites at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 41,006 raw 48MP (8064×6048) drone images captured with a DJI Mini 3 (f/1.7, ISO 100–3200) across 20 culturally significant scenes worldwide, alongside COLMAP-generated sparse point clouds serving as ground truth. For benchmarking, all methods consume identical inputs—high-resolution images plus the COLMAP sparse point cloud—and produce rendered novel-view outputs evaluated against held-out ground truth images. The full dataset also ships dense point clouds (10B points total), textured meshes, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and reconstructed 3D assets in standard .ply/.pcd and COLMAP formats for downstream VR, navigation, and AI applications.

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale data collection and reconstruction pipeline: systematic drone flight paths (orbit and grid patterns with ~15° overlap) capture both outdoor landmarks and indoor museum spaces, followed by COLMAP and Reality Capture photogrammetry to generate sparse/dense point clouds and textured meshes. The paper then establishes a unified benchmarking protocol where six Gaussian-based and photogrammetry methods (3DGS, SuGaR, Wild Gaussian, GOF, HoGS, City GS) are trained under identical settings (8×RTX A6000, 30k iterations, standardized learning rates) on five representative scenes. Evaluation uses PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS against ground truth, revealing that even the best method (City GS) achieves only ~17–22 dB PSNR, while several methods encounter out-of-memory errors or outright reconstruction failures—demonstrating that current Gaussian Splatting approaches are not yet robust to the scale and detail of real cultural heritage environments.

## Results

On the CULTURE3D benchmark, City GS achieves the best overall performance across all five scenes, reaching SSIM up to 0.6998 (Petra Treasury Face) and PSNR up to 21.86 dB (Petra), while avoiding OOM errors that plague other methods. 3DGS is the most stable baseline (no failures) but yields the lowest quality, with SSIM ranging 0.2861–0.6268 and PSNR 11.63–18.24 dB. GOF achieves the highest single-scene SSIM (0.7167 on Trinity St East) and PSNR (19.87 on Petra) among non-City GS methods but fails entirely on the Pyramid scene. Training time varies dramatically: 3DGS completes in 0.48–0.95 hours, while SuGaR requires up to 23.95 hours and City GS 5.84–12.16 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

City GS (CityGaussian) dominates the benchmark, achieving the highest SSIM and PSNR on four of five scenes (e.g., SSIM 0.6998 and PSNR 21.86 on Petra; SSIM 0.6245 and PSNR 14.37 on Gallery Hall) while being the only method besides 3DGS to complete all scenes without OOM or failure. GOF achieves the best single-scene SSIM (0.7167) and LPIPS (0.0260) on Trinity St East and the highest PSNR (19.87) on Petra among non-City GS methods, but it fails entirely on the Pyramid scene. 3DGS is the most robust baseline—completing all five scenes in under one hour—but produces the lowest quality (SSIM 0.29–0.63, PSNR 11.6–18.2). SuGaR, Wild Gaussian, and HoGS all crash with OOM on Cambridge Graduation Square, and HoGS additionally fails on Pyramid, revealing severe scalability limits. Wild Gaussian achieves the best SSIM on Petra (0.6074) among non-City GS methods but produces the worst LPIPS on Gallery Hall (0.8256) and Pyramid (0.8193), indicating poor perceptual quality despite structural similarity. Overall, the results confirm that even state-of-the-art Gaussian Splatting methods struggle with the scale and detail of cultural heritage scenes, with City GS emerging as the current best but still showing a large quality gap compared to results on smaller datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale, high-resolution (48MP) real-world dataset specifically targeting culturally significant landmarks for 3D Gaussian Splatting evaluation. It departs from prior large-scale datasets like MatrixCity (synthetic, domain-shifted) and GauU-Scene (homogeneous outdoor scenes) by combining diverse indoor/outdoor cultural heritage environments with ultra-high-resolution drone imagery. The benchmarking framework repurposes existing reconstruction methods (3DGS, SuGaR, GOF, Wild Gaussian, CityGaussian, HoGS) and photogrammetry tools (COLMAP, RealityCapture) as baselines rather than proposing a new algorithm, positioning the contribution squarely in dataset and benchmark design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CULTURE3D exposes a significant scalability gap: even state-of-the-art Gaussian Splatting methods (SuGaR, Wild Gaussian, HoGS) encounter out-of-memory failures on large cultural heritage scenes, and all methods show substantially lower PSNR/SSIM than on smaller benchmarks, indicating current approaches are not yet robust for real-world heritage-scale reconstruction. The dataset's 10-billion-point scale, 48MP resolution, and 20 diverse scenes (from the Pyramids to Cambridge) make it a natural stress-test for next-generation methods. Open questions include how to handle memory constraints at this scale, reconstruct thin structures and complex decorative patterns, and extend evaluation beyond the 5 scenes tested in the paper to the full 20-scene collection.
