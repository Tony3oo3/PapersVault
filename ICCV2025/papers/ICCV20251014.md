## Problem

Open-world 3D part segmentation is hindered by two issues: (1) existing methods that leverage 2D foundation models require per-shape optimization over multi-view renders, leading to runtimes of minutes to hours and multi-view inconsistencies; (2) the notion of a 'part' is inherently multi-scale and inconsistent across datasets (semantic, geometric, mobility-based), making it difficult to train a single model without committing to a fixed template. The paper seeks a fast, feedforward, class-agnostic approach that learns a versatile, hierarchical concept of 3D parts from large-scale noisy supervision.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud of 100,000 points (sampled from meshes, Gaussian splats, or other representations) normalized to [-1,1]³ and produces a continuous 448-dimensional feature field encoded as a 512² triplane with 128 channels, queryable at any spatial location including shape interiors. At inference, a single forward pass yields the triplane in under one second; hierarchical part segmentation is then obtained by applying agglomerative clustering to face-averaged features on mesh connectivity, or k-means for point-cloud outputs. An auxiliary SDF decoding branch (MLP on triplane features) is trained with L2 loss to improve overall feature quality.

## Method

The architecture uses a PVCNN encoder to extract per-point features, orthogonally projects them onto three axis-aligned 2D planes (triplane), then processes the triplane through a 2D CNN, a 6-layer transformer (operating on 128² downsampled tokens), and a transposed 2D CNN to produce the final 512² triplane. Training employs a relative triplet contrastive loss (inspired by BYOL) that only enforces sim(f(p_a), f(p_b)) > sim(f(p_a), f(p_c)) for positive/negative point pairs within a part proposal, sidestepping conflicts between multi-scale proposals without explicit scale conditioning. Part proposals are sourced from SAM2 masks projected from multi-view renders of ~340k Objaverse shapes (2D) and hierarchical PartNet labels (3D, including interior via tetrahedral meshing). Hard negative sampling—mixing uniform, 3D-Euclidean-hard, and feature-space-hard negatives near part boundaries—sharpen segmentation boundaries. The feedforward design trained at scale on noisy, inconsistent proposals yields a feature space that is robust to label noise and exhibits emergent cross-shape consistency, enabling co-segmentation and correspondence as bonus applications.

## Results

On PartObjaverse-Tiny, PartField achieves 79.18 mIoU, outperforming the second-best method SAMesh (56.86) by 22.3 percentage points, while running in ~10 seconds versus 7 minutes to 1.5 hours for competing methods. On PartNetE, PartField reaches 59.10 mIoU, surpassing SAMPart3D (56.17) and all other baselines. PartField is the only evaluated method that is simultaneously feed-forward, multi-scale, and text-input free, combining the best accuracy with the fastest inference (comparable to Find3D's ~10s but with 58 points higher mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartField decisively outperforms all five baselines on both evaluation datasets. On PartObjaverse-Tiny, it beats SAMesh (56.86) by 22.3 points, SAMPart3D (53.47) by 25.7 points, Ultrametric (46.39) by 32.8 points, PartSLIP (31.54) by 47.6 points, and Find3D (21.28) by 57.9 points in mIoU. On PartNetE, the margin narrows somewhat: PartField (59.10) leads SAMPart3D (56.17) by ~3 points, SAMesh (26.66) by ~32 points, PartSLIP (34.94) by ~24 points, and Find3D (21.69) by ~37 points. The speed advantage is dramatic: PartField's ~10-second feedforward inference is 42× faster than SAMPart3D (~15 min), 42× faster than SAMesh (~7 min), and 540× faster than Ultrametric (~1.5 h). The text-based methods (PartSLIP, Find3D) consistently underperform the class-agnostic approaches, confirming that open-world 3D part detection via text prompts remains unreliable. Among per-shape optimization methods, SAMesh produces the most competitive accuracy but suffers from over-segmentation and lacks flexible multi-scale control, whereas PartField's feedforward design yields both higher accuracy and crisper boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartField carves new ground by being among the first to train a feedforward 3D model that learns a continuous part feature field for open-world part decomposition, departing from the dominant per-shape optimization paradigm (Ultrametric Feature Fields, SAMPart3D, SAMesh) that renders multi-view images, segments with 2D foundation models, and fuses results. Conceptually, it repurposes the contrastive embedding learning and image-space distillation ideas from concurrent works [12, 30, 68, 72] but adapts them into a single forward pass via a PVCNN-triplane-transformer architecture, using a triplet-based loss inspired by SimCLR to sidestep the multi-scale granularity conflicts that plague pull/push formulations. The training data strategy—distilling SAM2 masks from ~340k Objaverse shapes plus PartNet 3D annotations—follows the 2D-to-3D distillation philosophy but at a scale and with a feedforward objective that prior per-shape methods could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartField's combination of a 22% mIoU improvement over the second-best method and a 10-second inference time (versus minutes to hours for per-shape baselines) makes high-quality open-world 3D part decomposition practically deployable for editing, simulation, and robotics pipelines. The emergent cross-shape feature consistency—enabling co-segmentation and correspondence without explicit cross-shape supervision—is a promising but underexplored property that could seed a new line of work on 3D shape foundation features. Key limitations include the extrinsic triplane architecture (weakly correlated with 3D position, requiring consistent orientation for cross-shape tasks), object-scale-only scope, and the fact that cross-shape applications remain small-scale demonstrations rather than rigorously validated capabilities.
