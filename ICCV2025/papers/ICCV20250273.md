## Problem

2D slice-based MRI segmentation methods consistently underperform 3D models because they ignore the spatial correlations between adjacent slices, yet full 3D convolutions impose prohibitive computational costs for practical deployment. The paper identifies that the gradual variation in lesion size and location across consecutive slices constitutes "temporal-like" data analogous to video frames, but existing sequential modules in medical imaging are limited to patch sequences within single images and have not been applied to inter-slice volumetric context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250273/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T multimodal MRI slices (T1, T1c, T2, FLAIR), each cropped to 160×160×4, where T equals the full volumetric depth (155 slices per subject). Unlike standard 2D segmentation that processes one slice at a time, M-Net ingests the entire slice stack as a temporal sequence, and unlike 3D methods it never forms a 3D tensor. Output is a per-slice segmentation mask for three nested regions—Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET)—produced independently for each slice in the sequence.

## Method

M-Net is a modular encoder-decoder (UNet-style with skip connections) whose core novelty is the Mesh-Cast Sequential Module: it flattens 2D feature maps into 1D sequences, processes them along the temporal axis (T slices) with a pluggable sequential unit (LSTM, xLSTM, ConvLSTM, Transformer, or Mamba SSM), then transposes the tensor so the channel dimension C becomes the new sequence length, runs a second sequential pass over channels, and transposes back—thereby capturing both inter-slice and inter-modality correlations without any 3D convolution. A separate Vision Sequential Module applies VMamba's CrossScan to serialize each 2D slice in four diagonal directions before feeding it into the same sequential interface. Training follows a Two-Phase Sequential (TPS) curriculum: Phase 1 globally shuffles slice order across all sequences to force the model to learn modality- and position-invariant lesion features, then Phase 2 restores the true anatomical ordering for fine-tuning on genuine spatial continuity. The architecture is fully plug-and-play: swapping the Sequential Unit changes the backbone from RNN to SSM to Transformer without altering the Mesh-Cast propagation logic.

## Results

On BraTS 2019, M-Net (with Mamba SSM) achieves Dice scores of 88.38% (WT), 90.52% (TC), and 91.43% (ET), with Hausdorff95 distances of 1.2869, 0.8154, and 0.6571, respectively. On BraTS 2023, it further improves to 91.33% (WT), 93.55% (TC), and 93.42% (ET) Dice, with H95 of 1.1534, 0.7069, and 0.6600. M-Net attains the best scores across all six metrics on both datasets while requiring only 91.29G FLOPs and 15 min 33 s inference time—roughly 15% of nnUNet's 97 min runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-Net achieves the best Dice and Hausdorff95 scores across all three tumor regions (WT, TC, ET) on both BraTS 2019 and BraTS 2023, outperforming all 12 baselines. The closest competitor is Mamba UNet, which M-Net surpasses by 0.17% WT Dice and 0.57% ET Dice on BraTS 2019, and by 0.30% WT Dice and 0.23% TC Dice on BraTS 2023. On BraTS 2023, M-Net also edges out Swin UNETR (91.11% WT Dice) and UKAN (93.14% ET Dice) while maintaining a lower inference time (15 min vs. 21 min and 19 min, respectively). Compared to the 3D baseline nnUNet, M-Net improves WT Dice by 0.57% on BraTS 2019 and 0.99% on BraTS 2023 while reducing inference time from 97 min to 15 min. Transformer-based methods (TransUNet, UNETR, TransNorm) consistently underperform M-Net by 2–4% Dice, suggesting that the Mesh-Cast sequential mechanism captures inter-slice correlations more effectively than self-attention alone. The ablation further confirms that the TPS training strategy contributes an additional ~0.3–0.7% Dice gain over single-phase training for every sequential backbone tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-Net repurposes sequential models (LSTM, Transformer, Mamba SSM) that were previously applied to patch-level sequences within single images, extending them to process entire MRI slice sequences as 'temporal-like' data analogous to video frames. The Vision Sequential Module directly adopts VMamba's CrossScan mechanism for intra-slice feature serialization, while the novel Mesh-Cast mechanism is the key departure: it enables any sequential model to alternately process temporal (slice) and channel (modality) dimensions through a transpose-based propagation scheme, something no prior medical segmentation architecture has systematically addressed. The TPS training strategy draws on curriculum learning principles but is specifically designed for the sequential input paradigm rather than standard 2D or 3D segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Mesh-Cast mechanism offers a practical, plug-and-play way to inject volumetric context into 2D slice-based pipelines without the computational cost of full 3D convolutions, which is valuable for deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The framework's modularity—swapping in LSTM, xLSTM, Transformer, or Mamba SSM—provides a useful empirical comparison of sequential backbones in a medical imaging context. However, the approach is validated only on brain tumor MRI with strong structural consistency across slices; whether the 'temporal-like' framing generalizes to modalities or tasks with weaker inter-slice correlations (e.g., CT, ultrasound, or non-sequential imaging) remains an open question. The modest Dice improvements (~1–2%) over strong baselines like Mamba UNet and UKAN suggest the gains, while real, are incremental rather than transformative.
