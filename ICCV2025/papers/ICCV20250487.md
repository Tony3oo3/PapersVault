## Problem

Recovering uncalibrated cameras from a set of fundamental matrices on a general viewing graph remains an open problem: existing methods (GPSFM, GPSFM-SYNCH, SINHA, COLOMBO) are restricted to special graph topologies such as those covered by real or virtual triplets, and cannot handle the most general solvable graph structure. Moreover, even in the noiseless case, the underlying polynomial system has no known efficient global solver, making the noisy recovery task particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a solvable viewing graph whose edges are noisy (and possibly outlying) 3×3 fundamental matrices, along with an initial estimate for a subset of cameras (obtained from GPSFM or set to the canonical camera [I₃ 0]). It outputs n uncalibrated 3×4 projective camera matrices, defined up to a global 4×4 projective transformation. The graph need not be complete; a 'holes density' parameter controls the fraction of missing edges, and the method handles arbitrary solvable topologies, not just triplet-covered ones.

## Method

The core mechanism is an alternating optimization (block relaxation) scheme that solves for one camera at a time while fixing all others. The key algebraic insight is that the skew-symmetry constraint P_i^T F_ij P_j = 0, when vectorized via Kronecker products and a commutation matrix, reduces to a homogeneous linear system A_j p_i = 0 for a single unknown camera, where A_j ∈ ℝ^{16×12} is built from the known neighbor camera and fundamental matrix. Two solvers are proposed: LS-SVD, which stacks the A_j matrices and extracts the null vector via SVD, and ANGLE-IT, which minimizes the sum of angular distances between p_i and its projections onto the multi-dimensional null spaces of each A_j, solved by a fixed-point iteration. Robustness to outliers is achieved by embedding the scheme in an IRLS loop with Huber loss, where edge weights are updated from residuals between input and re-computed fundamental matrices. This is the first method capable of recovering 100% of cameras on a general solvable viewing graph, including nodes that do not belong to any triplet.

## Results

On 25 real-world VGG projective SfM sequences, the proposed ANGLE-IT-IRLS method achieves the lowest mean reprojection error (before Bundle Adjustment) in 20 out of 25 sequences compared to GPSFM and in 21 out of 25 compared to GPSFM-SYNCH, with notable gains on challenging sequences (e.g., 64.10 px vs. 13.85 px for GPSFM on Toronto University, 87.53 px vs. 122.82 px for GPSFM on Smolny Cathedral). On synthetic general viewing graphs (n=25, ρ=0.75), the method recovers 100% of cameras while triplet-based baselines miss ~20% on average, and it achieves lower angular error across all noise levels. On triplet-covered synthetic graphs, both variants (LS-SVD and ANGLE-IT) significantly outperform all four baselines in angular error under increasing noise, holes, and up to 40% outlier density (with IRLS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPSFM [15], the proposed ANGLE-IT-IRLS achieves lower reprojection error on 20 of 25 real sequences, with particularly large margins on sparse or noisy graphs (e.g., Alcatraz Courtyard: 13.37 vs. 36.73 px; Smolny Cathedral: 87.53 vs. 122.82 px), though GPSFM wins on a few dense, low-noise sequences (De Guerre, Dome, Toronto University, Tsar Nikolai I). Against GPSFM-SYNCH [18], the proposed method wins on 21 of 25 sequences, with the largest gaps on sequences with high holes density (Nijo: 17.38 vs. 25.83 px; Alcatraz Water Tower: 36.54 vs. 19.76 px where SYNCH actually wins). Against SINHA [28], the proposed method dominates across all synthetic experiments: SINHA is outperformed in noise, holes, and outlier settings, and on general graphs SINHA recovers fewer cameras than the proposed method (which achieves 100% recovery). Against COLOMBO [9], the proposed method is consistently superior; COLOMBO's sequential triplet-based approach accumulates errors and yields the worst angular error in all synthetic experiments. On general (non-triplet-covered) viewing graphs, only the proposed method recovers all cameras, while GPSFM, GPSFM-SYNCH, and COLOMBO miss ~20% of nodes on average, and SINHA (which handles virtual triplets) still falls short.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera-recovery-from-fundamental-matrices task, previously addressed only for viewing graphs covered by real or virtual triplets (Sinha et al. 2004, Colombo et al. 2021, GPSFM 2019, GPSFM-SYNCH 2024), to the most general solvable graph topology. Conceptually, it departs from the compatibility-constraint refinement strategy used by GPSFM and instead exploits the algebraic linearity that emerges when all but one camera are fixed, casting the problem as an alternating (block-relaxation) optimization over individual cameras. The preprocessing step relies on recent finite-solvability checks [2, 5], and the robustness layer borrows the IRLS framework standard in geometric estimation, but the core iterative scheme and the angular-distance-to-null-spaces subproblem (ANGLE-IT) are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method capable of recovering 100% of cameras on a general solvable viewing graph, closing a gap that has persisted because prior approaches silently assumed triplet coverage—a property absent in many practical SfM graphs. The main limitation is the dependence on GPSFM for initialization, which itself requires triplet coverage, so the method cannot bootstrap from scratch on graphs with no triplets at all. Open questions include whether the initialization requirement can be relaxed, whether the alternating scheme converges globally (only local convergence insights are provided), and whether the approach extends to calibrated or partially calibrated settings. For the projective SfM community, this provides a practical tool to handle sparse, irregular viewing graphs that were previously intractable.
