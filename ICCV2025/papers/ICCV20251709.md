## Problem

Existing single domain generalized object detection methods rely on predefined data augmentation to diversify source features, but these augmentations may fail to cover the true target domain distribution, and training across multiple augmented domains introduces unmodeled domain discrepancies that degrade detection performance. This paper asks whether a diffusion-based framework can explicitly model the distribution shift between augmented and source domains to learn a source-biased detector that generalizes to any unseen domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251709/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image from an unseen target domain, extracts its feature representation via a ResNet-101 backbone, and passes it through a T-step (default T=10) reverse diffusion process conditioned on CLIP text embeddings before feeding the resulting source-style features into a Faster-RCNN detection head. The output is standard object detection predictions: bounding box coordinates and class labels for seven (Urban Scene) or 20 (Real-to-Artistic) categories. The text modality enters only as frozen CLIP text embeddings with learnable prompts (e.g., 'in a daytime clear scene') that condition the denoising U-Net via cross-attention, rather than as a direct input to the detector.

## Method

SDG-DiffDet replaces the standard Gaussian-noise diffusion process with a structured, memory-guided forward process and a text-conditioned reverse process. In the forward direction, two learnable memory banks store channel-wise mean and standard deviation statistics of local parts (per-object regions and background) across the dataset; at each training step, randomly sampled statistics are applied via AdaIN to source features to produce augmented features that preserve content structure while shifting style, and these augmented features—rather than isotropic Gaussian noise—serve as the noise schedule in the forward diffusion, enabling the model to capture practical inter-domain distributional differences. In the reverse direction, a frozen CLIP text encoder generates embeddings from learnable prompts encoding class names and a source-style descriptor; cross-attention between the noised features (queries) and text embeddings (keys/values) yields conditions that steer a U-Net denoiser to reconstruct source-style features from any unseen-domain input. Training jointly optimizes the Faster-RCNN detection loss, a diversity loss on memory elements, an MSE reconstruction loss for the denoiser, and a pixel-text matching loss that aligns source visual features with text embeddings at the bounding-box level.

## Results

On the Urban Scene dataset, SDG-DiffDet achieves 37.1% average mAP across four unseen weather domains (Daytime Foggy, Dusk Rainy, Night Clear, Night Rainy) without CLIP initialization, and 38.9% with CLIP initialization, outperforming the strongest baseline AFDA (35.5%) by 1.6 mAP. On the Real-to-Artistic dataset, the method reaches 45.1% average mAP (46.4% with CLIP), surpassing AFDA (43.2%) by 1.9 mAP. The method also achieves the best source-domain performance (60.2% mAP on Daytime Clear), demonstrating that training with source-style features alone does not sacrifice in-domain accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDG-DiffDet outperforms all 15 baselines on both primary benchmarks. Against the strongest data-augmentation baselines, it beats AFDA [3] by 1.6 mAP (37.1 vs. 35.5) on Urban Scene and by 1.9 mAP (45.1 vs. 43.2) on Real-to-Artistic, and surpasses UFR [23] by 3.9 mAP on Urban Scene. Among feature-normalization methods (FR, IBN-Net, SW, IterNorm, ISW), the gap is largest, with SDG-DiffDet exceeding the best (FR at 29.4) by 7.7 mAP on Urban Scene. The feature-disentanglement method CDSD [42] trails by 8.4 mAP, and the CLIP-based methods CLIPGap [39] and PDOC [19] are outperformed by 5.5 and 4.5 mAP respectively. In the zero-shot domain adaptation setting (Table 3), SDG-DiffDet achieves 39.8% average mAP, exceeding PODA [5] by 2.7 mAP, confirming that the source-biased transfer strategy generalizes beyond the single-domain generalization setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in single domain generalized object detection, where prior methods (AFDA, UFR, CLIPGap, OA-Mix) aim to learn an 'unbiased' detector by augmenting source data to cover diverse target distributions. Instead, SDG-DiffDet reframes the problem as learning a 'source-biased' detector that explicitly transfers any unseen domain distribution back to the source distribution via a diffusion process. The technical foundation draws on denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al.), AdaIN for style transfer (Huang & Belongie), and CLIP text encoders for conditioning (Radford et al.), with the pixel-text matching and diversity losses adapted from prompt distribution learning (Lu et al.). The key architectural departure is using memory-sampled feature statistics (via AdaIN) as structured noise in the forward diffusion process rather than Gaussian noise, and using CLIP text embeddings as inference-time conditions to guide denoising toward the source style.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply diffusion models to single domain generalized object detection, offering a principled framework that unifies feature augmentation and target-to-source distribution transfer in a single generative process. The 'source-biased' framing challenges the assumption that a detector must be trained on diverse augmented domains to generalize, suggesting instead that explicitly modeling the transfer back to source is more effective. However, the practical impact is tempered by the iterative denoising cost (198 ms vs. 151 ms for Faster-RCNN at T=10) and ~7.8M extra parameters, and the numerical gains over strong baselines like AFDA are modest (~1.6–1.9% mAP). Open questions include whether the approach scales to more extreme domain shifts, whether fewer diffusion steps suffice with better schedulers, and whether the memory-based augmentation can be replaced by learned generative noise to reduce the reliance on ground-truth bounding boxes during training.
