## Problem

Hierarchical diffusion models for large-scale 3D scene generation typically sample fine details from pure noise conditioned on a coarse scene, which causes semantic and detail misalignment between scales. Additionally, standard transformer architectures with global self-attention are prohibitively memory-intensive for the large, sparse voxel grids that represent realistic outdoor environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250483/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on multi-level sparse semantic voxel grids (e.g., 64²×4 → 128²×8 → 256²×16 for CarlaSC; 64²×16 → 128²×32 → 256²×64 for KITTI-360), where each level's voxels are strictly contained within the coarser level. For unconditional generation, the coarsest level is sampled from uniform noise; for conditional generation, a ground-truth coarse scene is provided as the initial diffusion state. The output is a high-resolution sparse 3D semantic voxel scene where the majority of positions are empty, with non-empty voxels assigned one of N semantic class labels.

## Method

The core contribution is Cube-Absorb Discrete Diffusion (CADD), which uses the coarse scene as the stationary distribution rather than pure noise: fine voxels are partitioned into cubes matching the coarse resolution, and during forward diffusion each fine voxel either remains unchanged or transitions to the same semantic state as its corresponding coarse voxel with a time-dependent probability, guaranteeing cross-scale alignment. For the backbone, the Sparse Cube Diffusion Transformer (SCDT) replaces DiT's global self-attention with a sparse cube attention operator that performs local attention only between neighboring cubes, and substitutes all empty cubes with a single shared learnable embedding to avoid wasting computation on the predominantly empty 3D space. Training proceeds level-by-level independently using the SEDD framework with a diffusion-weighted denoising score entropy loss, and sampling uses Tweedie τ-leaping from coarsest to finest level.

## Results

On CarlaSC, CADD achieves 78.13 mIoU(S) and 55.08 mIoU(P), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline XCube (70.96 / 40.75) and PDD (68.02 / 33.89) on segmentation metrics, while also attaining the best F3D (0.259) and MMD (0.124) among all methods. On the sparser, higher-resolution KITTI-360 dataset, CADD reaches 62.25 mIoU(S) and 71.44 MA(S), surpassing PDD (54.01 / 64.07) by over 8 points, and dramatically reduces F3D from 0.347 to 0.085. The method also excels in conditional generation, achieving 89.47 mIoU on CarlaSC and 87.71 mIoU on KITTI-360 when given a coarse ground-truth scene as input, far exceeding PDD's 47.89 and 57.46 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADD decisively outperforms all four baselines on CarlaSC across every metric. Against the closest competitor XCube [46], CADD gains +7.17 mIoU(S), +14.33 mIoU(P), and +10.78 MA(P), while also improving F3D from 0.354 to 0.259. PDD [28] is the strongest on MA(S) (85.66 vs. 87.06 for CADD) but lags significantly on mIoU(P) (33.89 vs. 55.08), indicating CADD produces more semantically consistent fine-grained details. On KITTI-360, CADD beats PDD by +8.24 mIoU(S) and +7.37 MA(S), with a 4× reduction in F3D (0.085 vs. 0.347), though mIoU(P) and MA(P) are nearly tied (41.34 vs. 41.29; 53.58 vs. 53.10). The single-layer baselines DiscDiff [2] and LatentDiff [22] are far behind on all metrics, confirming the necessity of hierarchical generation for large-scale scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical discrete diffusion framework for 3D scene generation (PDD, XCube) by replacing the standard 'sample fine details from pure noise' paradigm with a cube-absorb process that uses the coarse scene as the diffusion's initial state, drawing on the absorb diffusion mechanism from Austin et al. and the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework of Campbell et al. The architectural contribution adapts the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie) to sparse 3D voxels via a local sparse cube attention operator, departing from DiT's global self-attention which is infeasible at scene scale. The training and sampling procedures follow the SEDD framework (Lou et al.), making the paper a targeted recombination of existing discrete diffusion and transformer components around a single conceptual shift in the coarse-to-fine generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cube-absorb formulation offers a principled fix for the semantic misalignment problem in hierarchical 3D generation, and the SCDT architecture makes large-scale sparse voxel generation feasible on consumer GPUs (6.38 GB vs. OOM for global-attention DiT), which is practically important for the autonomous driving and VR communities. Open questions include whether the cube-absorb idea generalizes beyond semantic voxel scenes (e.g., to geometry or appearance), how the method scales to even larger or dynamic scenes, and whether the coarse-to-fine alignment benefit persists when the coarse input is itself generated (compounding errors across levels). The paper does not address temporal consistency or dynamic elements, which are natural next steps for driving applications.
