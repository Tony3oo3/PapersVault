## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs inherit social biases from training data, producing stereotypical associations (e.g., linking 'nurse' predominantly to women), yet existing debiasing efforts are limited in two ways: counterfactual datasets cover too few social concepts (mostly occupation only), and no debiasing strategy is designed for the autoregressive training paradigm of MLLMs (prior methods target CLIP-style contrastive models). This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a richer dataset and a counter-stereotype debiasing method tailored to autoregressive MLLMs.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a single RGB face image with a structured text prompt of the form 'Based on the race, gender, and age of the face <image>, do you think the person is {SCs}?', where {SCs} is a list of candidate social concepts from one of three groups (personality, responsibility, education). The model outputs a single-word text response selecting one concept. The CMSC dataset provides 60K generated images, each annotated with three social-attribute labels (gender, race, age) and one social-concept label, enabling 198 distinct SA-SC bias pairs to be measured via Skew metrics.

## Method

The Counter-Stereotype Debiasing (CSD) strategy operates on two axes. First, a bias-aware dataset resampling step uses the per-instance Skew(P_i) value (the maximum-absolute Skew across all social attributes for that instance) to down-weight over-represented cases (Skew > 0) via probabilistic exclusion and up-weight under-represented cases (Skew ≤ 0) via an accumulative over-resampling mechanism with threshold τ₂. Second, the standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss is rescaled into a Social Fairness Loss (SFLoss) that multiplies each instance's loss by e^{−Skew(P_i)}, so that instances the model already over-predicts receive smaller gradients while overlooked instances receive amplified gradients. The accompanying CMSC dataset is generated with SDXL using an intersectional pipeline: four base images are produced per gender-age combination, then Prompt-to-Prompt editing varies race while preserving visual consistency, followed by CLIP-Score filtering and expert review. Experiments across LLaVA-7B/13B, Qwen-VL-7B, and Bunny-8B show CSD reduces MaxSkew@C by over 50% relative to naive fine-tuning without degrading general multi-modal benchmark scores by more than 0.5%.

## Results

Across four MLLM architectures (LLaVA-7B/13B, Qwen-VL-7B, Bunny-8B), CSD achieves the lowest social bias (closest-to-zero MinS@C and MaxS@C) on all three evaluation datasets. On the CMSC dataset (intra-dataset evaluation), LLaVA-13B+CSD reaches MinS@C of −0.8167 and MaxS@C of 0.4192, a 46% improvement over direct fine-tuning (−1.5107 / 0.4605). On FairFace (cross-dataset), Qwen-VL-7B+CSD attains MaxS@C of 0.2607, a 0.6 absolute reduction versus the original model (0.8469). Critically, CSD preserves general multi-modal performance with less than 0.5% degradation on VQAv2, MMBench, and TextVQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD is compared against two baselines: direct fine-tuning (FT) on a balanced counterfactual dataset [Wang & Russakovsky, ICCV 2023] and POPE [Li et al., EMNLP 2023], a training-free hallucination-mitigation method adapted here as a debiasing baseline. CSD consistently outperforms FT across all four architectures and all three social-bias evaluation datasets; for example, on CMSC, LLaVA-13B+CSD achieves MinS@C of −0.8167 versus −1.5107 for FT (46% improvement), and Qwen-VL-7B+CSD reaches MaxS@C of 0.2607 on FairFace versus 0.6916 for FT. POPE, being training-free, underperforms FT on debiasing in most settings (e.g., Qwen-VL-7B: POPE MaxS@C = 0.9207 vs. FT 0.6916 on FairFace), confirming that test-time prompt adjustments cannot adequately counteract biases baked into pre-training. Notably, CSD's gains in fairness come at virtually no cost to general capabilities: VQAv2, MMBench, and TextVQA scores shift by less than 0.5% compared to the original models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Skew metric originally developed for fairness-aware ranking in search systems (Geyik et al., KDD 2019) as the core diagnostic and optimization signal for social bias in autoregressive MLLMs, and adopts the intersectional image generation strategy from SocialCounterfactuals (Howard et al., CVPR 2024) to construct its CMSC dataset. It departs from the dominant line of VLM debiasing work (DEAR, FairerCLIP, biased-prompt methods) that targets CLIP-style contrastive models, arguing that their objectives are incompatible with autoregressive MLLMs. The 'counter-stereotype' framing—using the opposite of a prevalent stereotype as a corrective signal via data resampling and exponential loss rescaling—is a conceptual departure from prior data-balancing or adapter-based approaches, drawing an explicit analogy to acid-base neutralization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs are increasingly deployed in applications involving sensitive human attributes (hiring, healthcare, education), a practical, training-time-only debiasing method that preserves general multi-modal capabilities (sub-0.5% degradation on VQAv2, MMBench, TextVQA) is directly useful to practitioners. The CMSC dataset (60K images, 18 social concepts) fills a concrete gap left by prior single-concept or small-scale counterfactual resources. Open questions remain: whether the Skew-based resampling generalizes to more complex intersectional structures beyond the three binary/categorical attributes studied, whether synthetic (SDXL) images introduce their own distributional biases that limit real-world transfer, and whether the method extends to test-time or inference-time settings without fine-tuning.
