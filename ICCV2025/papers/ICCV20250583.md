## Problem

Existing generative models for autonomous driving cannot simultaneously support temporal extrapolation (predicting future scenes) and spatial novel view synthesis (NVS) in a feed-forward manner. Temporal video generation methods are locked to predefined trajectories and lack NVS, while NVS methods either require costly per-scene optimization or lack temporal prediction entirely. The paper identifies the missing piece as an efficient geometric representation that is both learnable by generative models for forecasting and sufficient for view-consistent rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250583/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DiST-T takes a single historical frame of 6-camera RGB images plus control signals (BEV semantic maps, 3D bounding boxes, ego trajectory, camera poses) and produces 17-frame future multi-view RGB videos at 424×800×6 resolution along with corresponding metric depth sequences. DiST-S takes sparse RGB-D projections (reprojected point clouds from the generated sequences) at arbitrary novel viewpoints and synthesizes dense RGB-D outputs. The depth is metric (in meters), not relative, and is generated jointly with RGB rather than estimated post-hoc, which is critical for accurate point cloud projection into novel views.

## Method

DiST-4D decomposes 4D scene generation into two disentangled diffusion processes bridged by metric depth. DiST-T uses an STDiT backbone with a ControlNet-style conditioning branch to generate future multi-view RGB-D latents via rectified flow with v-prediction loss, trained progressively from low-resolution RGB to high-resolution RGB-D. DiST-S employs a Stable Video Diffusion UNet modified for multi-modal (RGB+depth) generation, conditioned on sparse point cloud projections via a trainable condition encoder. The key training innovation is a Self-supervised Cycle Consistency (SCC) strategy: after initial training on real trajectories, novel lateral-shifted trajectories are generated, DiST-S synthesizes RGB-D at those viewpoints, and the results are reprojected back to original viewpoints to form self-supervised training pairs, closing the generalization gap to out-of-distribution camera poses.

## Results

On nuScenes, DiST-T achieves FID 6.83 and FVD 22.67 for multi-view RGB video generation, setting a new SOTA on FVD (previous best: UniScene at 71.94) and matching the best FID (UniScene 6.45). For spatial NVS, DiST-S with SCC achieves FID 10.12 and FVD 45.14 at ±1 m lateral shift, outperforming the best per-scene baseline (StreetGaussian, FID 32.12) by over 3×. For depth generation, DiST-T (Ours-D) attains Abs.Rel. 0.20 and RMSE 5.33 with multi-frame LiDAR GT, surpassing M2Depth (0.25/6.02) and SurroundDepth (0.27/6.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For temporal video generation (Table 2), DiST-T achieves FVD 22.67, a 3.2× improvement over the previous best UniScene (71.94), while its FID of 6.83 is within 0.4 of UniScene's 6.45; all other baselines (MagicDrive 217.94, Drive-WM 122.70, Vista 89.40) are substantially worse on FVD. For spatial NVS (Table 5), DiST-S(+SCC) dominates all per-scene optimization methods (PVG, EmerNeRF, StreetGaussian, OmniRe) by 2–3× in FID at every shift magnitude, and also outperforms the feed-forward FreeVS* by 5× in FID at ±1 m. In depth generation (Table 4), DiST-T(Ours-D) with reference depth beats both M2Depth and SurroundDepth on Abs.Rel. and RMSE under multi-frame LiDAR GT (5.33 vs 6.02 vs 6.50), while the RGB-only variant (Ours) remains competitive. For downstream perception (Table 3), DiST-T-generated videos yield NDS 32.44 and mAP 15.63 with UniAD, surpassing MagicDrive (28.36/12.92) and DriveArena (30.03/16.06) on detection while maintaining competitive L2 planning error (0.56 m at 1.0 s). The SCC ablation confirms a consistent ~20% FID reduction across all shift magnitudes, validating the cycle-consistency strategy's contribution to generalization on out-of-distribution trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiST-4D builds on the STDiT video diffusion backbone (Open-Sora) for temporal generation and Stable Video Diffusion's UNet for spatial synthesis, while adopting control-signal embedding strategies from MagicDriveDiT. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by disentangling temporal forecasting and spatial NVS into two separate diffusion processes bridged by metric depth, rather than attempting a monolithic 4D generation or relying on per-scene reconstruction (3DGS, PVG, EmerNeRF). The self-supervised cycle consistency strategy repurposes cycle-consistency ideas from image translation and correspondence learning to address the trajectory-diversity gap in driving NVS, and the metric depth curation pipeline integrates MVS, LiDAR fusion, and generative depth completion in a way not previously combined for driving scene supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feed-forward framework to jointly support temporal extrapolation and spatial NVS for driving scenes without per-scene optimization, which is a practical bottleneck for scalable simulation and data generation in autonomous driving. The use of metric depth as a geometric bridge is both a methodological choice and a practical asset, since dense metric depth has direct utility for perception training and annotation. Key limitations include dependence on pseudo-GT depth quality (especially for distant structures) and evaluation restricted to a single dataset (nuScenes); extending to more diverse environments and integrating with embodied/robotic planning pipelines are natural next steps.
