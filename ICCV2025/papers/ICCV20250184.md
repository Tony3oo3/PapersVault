## Problem

Absolute pose regression (APR) methods suffer from poor generalization because they implicitly memorize training views rather than learning appearance-invariant scene representations. Previous data augmentation approaches (e.g., LENS, DFNet) enriched training with NeRF-synthesized novel viewpoints but neglected photometric/appearance variation, and the limited quality of rendered images introduced artifacts that degraded feature learning. The paper identifies appearance diversity as the missing key and shows that a learning gap—rendering artifacts and the syn-to-real domain gap—prevents APR from benefiting from diverse synthetic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB query image and directly regresses a 6-DoF camera pose (3D translation vector and rotation) via a Transformer-based regressor, requiring no retrieval or iterative refinement. The optional refinement variant (RAP_ref) additionally renders an RGB-D image from the trained 3DGS at the predicted pose and uses MASt3R for 2D-3D correspondences followed by RANSAC-PnP to produce a refined pose. Training consumes paired real images with ground-truth poses plus online-synthesized 3DGS renders at perturbed poses and blended appearance weights, with the discriminator operating on intermediate feature maps rather than raw pixels.

## Method

RAP builds a two-branch joint training pipeline on top of an appearance-varying, deblurring 3DGS data engine. Branch 1 coarsely trains an EfficientB0 + Pose Transformer regressor on both real and same-pose synthetic images, while an LSGAN-inspired adversarial discriminator (operating on adjusted feature maps) aligns synthetic and real feature distributions to close the syn-to-real gap. Branch 2 progressively generates novel image-pose pairs by perturbing poses (δt, δr) and randomly blending appearance weights ω in the 3DGS, feeding them as additional supervision to the same regressor every 20 epochs. The Pose Transformer introduces dedicated global Trans and Rot tokens (analogous to CLS tokens) that aggregate long-range dependencies via multi-head self-attention before a two-layer MLP regression head produces the final pose. The 3DGS itself extends vanilla 3DGS with per-Gaussian appearance features sampled from input images (following GS-W) and a motion-blur model that blends multiple time-step renders (following Deblur-GS), enabling high-quality synthesis under varying lighting and weather.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks, RAP achieves a median translation/rotation error of 56 cm / 1.28° on average across four scenes, reducing errors by 53% and 56% compared to DFNet (119 cm / 2.90°) and by 38% and 44% compared to PMNet (90 cm / 2.27°). On the 7-Scenes indoor dataset, RAP (SfM GT) reaches 5 cm / 2.18° average, halving the translation error of PMNet (10 cm / 3.24°). On the Aachen Day-Night benchmark with extreme lighting changes, RAP reduces rotation error from 85.80° (DFNet) to 13.70°, and with one-shot refinement (RAP_ref) further drops to 67 unit / 4.64°. On MARS driving scenes, RAP_ref achieves sub-10 cm median translation error (8.3 cm / 0.13°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP outperforms all Pure APR baselines on Cambridge Landmarks: it beats DFNet (119→56 cm, 2.90→1.28°) and PMNet (90→56 cm, 2.27→1.28°) by over 50% in translation, and surpasses LENS (39 cm / 1.20°) in rotation while matching its translation. On 7-Scenes, RAP (SfM GT) achieves 5 cm / 2.18° average, outperforming PMNet (10 cm / 3.24°), DFNet (12 cm / 3.71°), and CoordiNet+LENS (9 cm / 3.07°). With refinement (RAP_ref), the method surpasses SCR baselines (GLACE: 1.2 cm / 0.36°) and PPR baselines (ACE+GS-CPR: 0.8 cm / 0.25° on 7-Scenes; on Cambridge, RAP_ref at 12 cm / 0.27° beats ACE+GS-CPR at 15 cm / 0.33°). On Aachen Day-Night, RAP dramatically outperforms DFNet (174/85.80°) and PoseNet (217/74.30°), achieving 130/13.70°, and RAP_ref (67/4.64°) even surpasses SCR methods ACE (914/90.50°) and GLACE (482/36.4°). On MARS, RAP (28 cm / 0.60°) and RAP_ref (8.3 cm / 0.13°) substantially outperform PoseNet (121 cm / 1.67°). The only scenario where RAP is not best is the Heads scene on 7-Scenes (rotation 6.02° vs. PMNet's 4.23°), attributed to limited training sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of APR data augmentation work (LENS, DFNet, PMNet) that used NeRF to synthesize additional posed views, but makes two key departures: switching to 3D Gaussian Splatting for efficient, appearance-controllable rendering, and introducing an adversarial discriminator to close the syn-to-real feature gap. The authors identify a 'learning gap' that explains why LENS's earlier attempt at appearance perturbation via NeRF-W yielded minimal gains — the training pipeline itself, not the data, was the bottleneck. The Pose Transformer architecture draws on ViT's global token mechanism, while the deblurring and appearance-varying 3DGS components are adapted from Deblur-GS and GS-W respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing narrative that APR is fundamentally limited to image-based memorization, showing that generalizability can emerge when diverse appearance data is paired with adversarial feature alignment. The void-zone experiments and the finding that 20% of real data with augmentation outperforms 100% without it provide concrete evidence of this shift. Open questions remain about scaling to much larger scenes, covering the full SE(3) manifold (the sharp performance cliff at 30 cm/1.5° rotation perturbation suggests limited rotational generalization), and whether the adversarial component generalizes beyond the specific 3DGS rendering artifacts it was designed to address.
