## Problem

Existing concept-based explanation methods can identify which concepts influence a prediction or localize where a concept appears, but few simultaneously reveal what concepts a classifier learns, which spatial regions correspond to them, and how each contributes to the final prediction. Dissection-based approaches that associate concepts with individual activation-map channels suffer from a many-to-many relationship, making the mapping ambiguous, and most require fine-grained annotated datasets to define concepts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and an optional set of concept text labels (e.g., 'blue feather', 'hair') as input. It produces three outputs per concept: a spatial region map (a ReLU-weighted sum of activation maps from the most relevant CNN layer), a scalar contribution score (AUC of classification-score decrease under channel masking), and a concept prediction score (dot product in VLM embedding space). Because concept texts are not required during training, the system operates in a zero-shot regime for arbitrary unseen concept labels at inference time.

## Method

CE-FAM trains a lightweight branched network that shares all intermediate activation maps with a frozen image classifier (ResNet or ViT). The branch concatenates global-average-pooled embeddings from every CNN layer, projects them into a VLM's (CLIP or SigLIP) embedding space via a small MLP translator, and is trained with an MSE loss to match the VLM's image embedding (optionally augmented with a similarity loss against VLM text-embedding dot products). At inference, a concept's region is obtained by backpropagating its prediction score through the translator to yield per-channel gradients, which weight the activation maps in a Grad-CAM-style sum; the most relevant layer is selected by an association score measuring the AUC of concept-score decrease under sequential channel masking. Concept contribution is quantified analogously by measuring the AUC of the class-prediction-score decrease when masking the same channels, enabling contributions from any layer rather than only the penultimate one. This design establishes a one-to-one correspondence between each concept label, its spatial region, and its contribution, and the authors introduce a Normalized Region Accuracy (NRA) metric to evaluate region quality against segmentation ground truth.

## Results

On the Broden dataset with ResNet50, CE-FAM achieves an average NRA of 0.361 and Hit Rate of 0.247, outperforming CLIP-Dissect (NRA 0.334, Hit Rate 0.199) and WWW (NRA 0.278, Hit Rate 0.114). On ImageNet-S with ViT-B/16, CE-FAM reaches NRA 0.671 and Hit Rate 0.783, substantially exceeding CLIP-Dissect (NRA 0.171, Hit Rate 0.045) and WWW (NRA 0.448, Hit Rate 0.038). The method also achieves the highest EPG scores across most settings, e.g., 0.666 on ViT-B/16 ImageNet-S versus 0.437 for WWW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CE-FAM consistently outperforms both CLIP-Dissect and WWW across all three evaluation metrics (EPG, NRA, Hit Rate) on the Broden dataset for both ResNet50 and ResNet18. The advantage is most pronounced in Hit Rate: on ResNet50 Broden, CE-FAM achieves 0.247 versus 0.199 (CLIP-Dissect) and 0.114 (WWW), indicating it correctly localizes a substantially larger fraction of concepts. On ImageNet-S with ViT-B/16, the gap widens dramatically—CE-FAM's Hit Rate of 0.783 dwarfs CLIP-Dissect's 0.045 and WWW's 0.038, suggesting that single-neuron association methods fail severely on Transformer architectures. The one exception is on ResNet50 ImageNet-S, where WWW slightly edges CE-FAM on EPG (0.625 vs. 0.619) and Hit Rate (0.674 vs. 0.744 is actually CE-FAM winning), while CLIP-Dissect is competitive on EPG (0.614 vs. 0.619). Overall, CE-FAM wins on NRA and Hit Rate in nearly all settings, with the largest margins on ViT and on the Hit Rate metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

CE-FAM extends the Grad-CAM formulation (weighted sum of activation maps) from class-level saliency to concept-level region localization, replacing class-score gradients with concept-prediction-score gradients. It departs from dissection-based methods (CLIP-Dissect, WWW) that enforce a one-to-one mapping between a concept and a single neuron, instead representing each concept as a learned fusion across multiple channels and layers. For concept learning, it follows the VLM-driven paradigm (Label-free CBM, CounTEX) of mimicking CLIP/SigLIP embeddings to avoid annotated concept datasets, but uniquely backpropagates through the translator to obtain spatial gradients for region extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in concept-based XAI: prior methods either identify concepts without localizing them, or localize regions without quantifying contribution to the final prediction. The zero-shot capability (no concept labels required at training time) makes the framework broadly applicable to arbitrary concept vocabularies, which is practically valuable for deployment. Key open questions remain: the expressiveness of concepts is bounded by VLM quality (CLIP struggles with fine-grained attributes), there is no ground-truth metric for concept contributions, and the optimal concept set size is unclear. The proposed NRA metric is a useful step toward standardized evaluation of concept regions.
