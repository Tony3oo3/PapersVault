## Problem

Existing regularization strategies for generalizable blind super-resolution (e.g., dropout, feature alignment) apply uniform constraints across all degradation types, implicitly assuming models overfit equally to blur, noise, and JPEG. Through frequency-domain and feature-similarity analysis, this paper reveals that noise is the dominant overfitting source due to its full-spectrum spectral spread, which progressively degrades the signal-to-noise ratio in gradient updates as training shifts toward high frequencies. No prior method explicitly targets this noise-specific feature corruption.

## Input / Output

The TFD framework is a plug-and-play module inserted into the intermediate feature space of any existing SR backbone (CNN-based like SRResNet/RRDB or transformer-based like SwinIR/HAT), requiring no architectural modification. It takes a degraded low-resolution image processed through the backbone encoder, operates on the resulting feature maps (C×H×W), and outputs denoised features that are passed to the decoder for final super-resolution. The noise detection gate activates denoising only when predicted noise confidence exceeds 0.75, otherwise features pass through an identity shortcut, making the module effectively zero-cost on clean inputs.

## Method

TFD comprises two modules: (1) a noise detection module that applies 2D FFT to intermediate features, convolves the real and imaginary spectral components with independent learnable filters, performs inverse FFT, global average pooling, and a lightweight classifier to output binary noise/clean logits; and (2) a frequency-spatial denoising module with a dual-path design where the frequency branch reuses pre-computed FFT results to generate a per-pixel noise attention mask via sigmoid-activated learnable filters, while the spatial branch performs hierarchical reconstruction through an encoder-bottleneck-decoder with residual attention units (layer norm → 1×1 conv → depthwise 3×3 conv → channel attention → 1×1 conv + skip). The two paths are fused by element-wise multiplication of the frequency mask with spatial features. Training uses a tri-objective loss (L1 reconstruction, cross-entropy noise classification, L1 feature consistency against clean-reference features) with a dynamic 0.75 confidence threshold that defers aggressive denoising to later training stages, adding only ~9% MACs to the base model.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (×4 blind SR, 8 degradation types), TFD applied to SRResNet achieves average PSNR gains of +1.86 dB (Set5), +0.85 dB (Set14), +0.41 dB (BSD100), and +0.77 dB (Urban100) over the unmodified baseline, with the largest improvements under noise-corrupted settings. On real-world datasets, TFD (on SRResNet) reaches 25.72 dB PSNR on Canon and 25.31 dB on Nikon, outperforming both Dropout and Feature Alignment by 0.59–0.94 dB. The method also improves LPIPS by up to 0.020 (e.g., 0.315 vs. 0.332 on Canon), indicating better perceptual quality. Across four architectures (SRResNet, RRDB, HAT, SwinIR), TFD consistently improves average PSNR by 0.2–0.8 dB depending on the backbone and dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFD is compared against two regularization-based baselines: Reflash Dropout (Kong et al., CVPR 2022) and Feature Alignment (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), both applied to the same SRResNet backbone. TFD wins on every real-world dataset across all five metrics. On the DSLR Canon subset, TFD achieves 25.72 dB PSNR, surpassing Dropout (24.86 dB, +0.86 dB) and Alignment (25.13 dB, +0.59 dB); on Nikon, the margins are +0.94 dB over Dropout and +0.57 dB over Alignment. On the DIV2K validation tracks, TFD leads by 0.36–0.43 dB over Dropout and 0.29–0.38 dB over Alignment. TFD also achieves the best LPIPS on Canon (0.315 vs. 0.332 for Dropout and 0.328 for Alignment), confirming superior perceptual quality. Notably, Dropout actually worsens LPIPS relative to the unmodified SRResNet on Canon (0.332 vs. 0.327), while TFD improves it, suggesting that uniform regularization can degrade perceptual fidelity whereas targeted denoising preserves content structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the generalizable blind SR line of work, specifically departing from prior regularization strategies (Reflash Dropout [19], Feature Alignment [31]) that apply uniform constraints across all degradation types. The authors' key conceptual departure is the empirical and theoretical argument that noise is the dominant overfitting source, justifying a targeted rather than generic intervention. The noise detection module draws on the Fourier-based noise detection principle of Dosselmann and Yang [14], while the dual-path denoising (frequency + spatial encoder-decoder with channel attention) repurposes standard restoration building blocks into a plug-and-play feature-level module compatible with CNN and transformer backbones alike.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of noise as the primary overfitting bottleneck, supported by both feature-similarity tracking and a Frequency Principle-based SNR argument, provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why generalizable SR models struggle, and the plug-and-play design makes the technique immediately adoptable across architectures. However, the SSIM values in Table 1 appear identical between baseline and +TFD for SRResNet across all degradation types, which raises questions about whether the reported PSNR gains fully reflect genuine perceptual improvements. Open questions include whether the 0.75 confidence threshold for activating denoising generalizes to structured or sensor-specific noise, and whether the framework extends naturally to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) where similar frequency-domain entanglement occurs.
