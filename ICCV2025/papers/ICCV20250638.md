## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods rely solely on low-resolution inputs and cannot leverage reference information, while reference-based SR methods require manually selected HR auxiliary images whose identification remains a critical challenge in real-world scenarios. The paper addresses this gap by proposing an image-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (iRAG) framework that automatically retrieves relevant references from a database using a trainable hashing function, eliminating the need for exact reference matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250638/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image as query and retrieves the most relevant 512×512 HR reference patch from a pre-built database (comprising 25% of DF2K-OST real images augmented 1:1 with SDEdit-generated synthetic images) via 16-bit binary hash codes. The output is a high-resolution super-resolved image produced by a two-stage pipeline: an intermediate reference-based SR reconstruction followed by latent diffusion refinement. The hashing-based retrieval reduces storage from ~828 MB (VGG features) to ~52 KB and retrieval time from 4338 ms to 68 ms for 80,000 queries against 26,500 references.

## Method

The iRAG framework comprises three key components. First, a data augmentation stage generates synthetic HR references via SDEdit with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, applying a variance-based hallucination filtering metric to reject out-of-distribution samples. Second, an unsupervised contrastive hashing network (VGG encoder + learnable binary hash function trained with InfoNCE-style loss on augmented positive pairs) enables efficient nearest-neighbor retrieval of relevant reference patches in a compact 16-bit code space. Third, a two-stage SR pipeline first produces an intermediate reconstruction using a reference-based SR network (DATSR/TTSR) that fuses the LR input with the retrieved reference, then refines it through a latent diffusion U-Net conditioned on both LR and intermediate features via Spatial Feature Transform (SFT) layers that compute per-block affine parameters to modulate the denoising process.

## Results

On the RealSR benchmark, iRAG achieves PSNR 23.95, SSIM 0.717, LPIPS 0.263, CLIP-IQA 0.678, and MUSIQ 70.52, outperforming all diffusion-based baselines (LDM, StableSR) on perceptual quality metrics while trailing non-diffusion RefSR methods (TTSR, DATSR) on PSNR/SSIM. On DIV2K-Valid, the method reaches CLIP-IQA 0.702 and MUSIQ 68.25, the highest perceptual scores among all compared methods. On DRealSR, iRAG attains PSNR 27.14 and MUSIQ 64.86, surpassing StableSR (PSNR 27.05, MUSIQ 64.34) and LDM (PSNR 24.23, MUSIQ 69.90 on RealSR). In the reference-provided CUFED5 setting, iRAG achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.693) and MUSIQ (73.57) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-diffusion RefSR methods (TTSR, DATSR), iRAG consistently trails on PSNR and SSIM (e.g., 23.95 vs. 31.14/31.55 PSNR on RealSR) but substantially outperforms them on perceptual quality: CLIP-IQA improves from 0.458/0.454 to 0.678 and MUSIQ from 64.29/63.49 to 70.52 on RealSR. Compared to GAN-based real-world SR methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, DASR), iRAG achieves higher CLIP-IQA and MUSIQ across all three benchmarks while DASR retains an edge on LPIPS (0.140 vs. 0.263 on RealSR). Among diffusion-based baselines, iRAG outperforms LDM on PSNR (23.95 vs. 24.23 on RealSR is a loss, but 27.14 vs. 24.23 on DRealSR is a gain), CLIP-IQA, and MUSIQ, and beats StableSR on PSNR (23.95 vs. 23.70) and MUSIQ (70.52 vs. 70.20) on RealSR. In the reference-provided CUFED5 setting (Table 2), iRAG achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.693 vs. 0.536 for DASR) and MUSIQ (73.57 vs. 69.15 for DATSR) but lower PSNR (20.56 vs. 26.71 for DATSR), reflecting the diffusion model's perceptual-over-fidelity trade-off. When integrated into CoSeR's diffusion RefSR pipeline, hash-retrieved references yield consistent gains over generated references (PSNR 20.30 vs. 20.23, MUSIQ 57.69 vs. 56.42), validating the retrieval component's transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of reference-based super-resolution (RefSR) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), a framework previously applied to NLP and text-to-image generation (Re-Imagen) but not to SR. It builds directly on latent diffusion models (LDM/Stable Diffusion) as the generative backbone, adapts SFT conditioning from StableSR, and uses DATSR/TTSR as an intermediate reference-alignment stage. The key conceptual departure from prior RefSR work (C2-Matching, MASA-SR, DATSR) is automating reference selection via a trainable neural hashing function rather than assuming a well-matched reference is provided, and augmenting the reference database with hallucination-filtered synthetic images generated via SDEdit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical gap: real-world SR rarely comes with a perfectly matched reference, and manually curating one from large datasets is infeasible. The hashing-based retrieval (16-bit codes, ~68 ms lookup vs. ~4.3 s for VGG matching) makes large-scale reference databases tractable, and the hallucination-filtering pipeline for generated references is a reusable component for any diffusion-based data augmentation. However, the method's gains are confined to perceptual metrics (CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ); PSNR and SSIM remain well below non-diffusion RefSR baselines like TTSR and DATSR, and the multi-stage pipeline (hashing → intermediate SR → diffusion refinement) adds considerable inference complexity. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large or domain-shifted reference databases, whether the 1:1 real-to-synthetic augmentation ratio is optimal, and whether the perceptual gains translate to downstream tasks.
