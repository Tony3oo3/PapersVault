## Problem

Medical image segmentation models suffer from distribution shifts caused by varying imaging protocols and patient populations across institutions. Existing domain adaptation methods require multiple passes over a pre-collected target dataset, which is impractical in clinical streaming settings, while standard test-time adaptation methods (entropy minimization, pseudo-labeling) fail to exploit the strong anatomical shape priors inherent in segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a single medical image (cardiac MRI at 256×256, spinal cord MRI at 144×144, or chest X-ray at 128×128) along with a pretrained segmentation network and a frozen region-based energy model. It produces a refined pixel-wise segmentation map and a K×K patch-wise energy map where each scalar value indicates the plausibility of the corresponding anatomical region. Only BatchNorm layer parameters are updated during adaptation, with weights restored after each batch to prevent catastrophic drift.

## Method

The core contribution is a region-based shape energy model (a fully convolutional discriminator) that assigns per-patch energy scores to segmentation maps, where low energy indicates in-distribution anatomical shapes and high energy signals erroneous predictions. Since only source data is available, negative (OOD) training examples are curated by applying FGSM adversarial perturbations to the segmentation network's inputs, generating diverse implausible shapes; patch labels are then derived by thresholding the distance between perturbed and ground-truth patches. At test time, the frozen energy model guides progressive adaptation by minimizing the predicted energy of the segmentation network's output patches toward a uniform zero reference (Eq. 8), updating only BatchNorm statistics. This discriminative, patch-level formulation avoids the generative steps required by prior energy-based TTA (TEA) and provides localized, interpretable updates that correct specific erroneous regions without disturbing already-accurate predictions.

## Results

On cardiac segmentation (ACDC→LVQuant, UNet), the proposed method achieves 76.93% DSC for LV and 59.43% for Myo, outperforming the best baseline TEA (67.96% / 54.10%) by ~9 and ~5 percentage points respectively. On spinal cord segmentation (GMSC), the method reaches 80.9% average DSC across six cross-site transfers, surpassing TEA (77.3%) and CoTTA (76.4%). On lung X-ray segmentation, it achieves 96.2% average DSC, marginally ahead of InTENT (95.9%) and TEA (95.6%). The region-based energy model detects erroneous segmentation regions with >92% accuracy across all three tested architectures (UNet, MedNeXt, SwinUNETR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all seven baselines (TENT, CoTTA, TEA, SAR, FSeg, MEMO, InTENT) across all eight datasets and three architectures. The largest gains are against TEA on cardiac segmentation: on ACDC→LVQuant with UNet, the method improves LV DSC by ~9 points (76.93 vs 67.96) and Myo DSC by ~5 points (59.43 vs 54.10), attributed to the region-based energy model's finer-grained shape discrimination versus TEA's global energy. Against TENT and CoTTA, improvements are more moderate on already well-segmented targets (e.g., MyoPS LV: 86.06 vs 85.88/85.64) but substantial on harder cross-domain transfers (M&M LV: 61.84 vs 57.01/52.98). On spinal cord (GMSC), the method leads TEA by 3.6 points in average DSC (80.9 vs 77.3) and InTENT by 10.2 points (80.9 vs 70.7), with the gap most pronounced on harder site transfers (1↦3: 77.7 vs 66.5 for TEA). On lung X-ray, margins are smaller (96.2 vs 95.9 for InTENT) due to the simpler single-class task, but the method still achieves the best average. The method is model-agnostic, with the same energy model transferring across UNet, MedNeXt, and SwinUNETR without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based test-time adaptation framework of TEA [63] from classification to medical image segmentation, making two key conceptual departures: it repurposes the energy model as a *discriminative* shape classifier (assigning patch-wise energy to in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution anatomy) rather than a generative component, and it uses adversarial perturbations (FGSM) to curate OOD training examples instead of a separate generative model. Within the broader TTA literature for segmentation (TENT, CoTTA, FSeg, MEMO, InTENT), the paper's distinguishing contribution is the explicit shape prior encoded through the region-based energy model, which localizes adaptation to erroneous patches rather than applying global entropy or consistency objectives. The progressive, single-pass update of BatchNorm layers positions the method as more clinically practical than domain adaptation approaches that require multiple passes over a pre-collected target set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass, model-agnostic design addresses a practical gap in clinical deployment where target patient data cannot be anticipated in advance, and the patch-wise energy maps provide a degree of interpretability (localizing where the model is uncertain or wrong) that entropy-based methods lack. The consistent improvements across eight datasets, three architectures, and multiple source-to-target transfer directions suggest the shape-energy prior generalizes well, though the paper acknowledges it assumes covariate shift and does not handle concept shift (e.g., two-chamber vs. four-chamber cardiac views). Open questions include extension to 3D volumes, whether the adversarial perturbation strategy scales to more complex multi-organ tasks, and how the method behaves when source and target distributions differ in semantics rather than just imaging protocol.
