## Problem

Neural mesh models (e.g., NOVUM) represent objects as 3D meshes with learned vertex features and have shown strong out-of-distribution robustness, but they have only been demonstrated on 10–12 object classes because their per-vertex contrastive learning scales quadratically with the number of classes. It remains unclear whether these models can be efficiently scaled to hundreds of classes while retaining their robustness advantages over standard deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (640×800 for ResNet50 or 644×812 for ViT-B/14) and simultaneously produces an object class label from 188 categories and a 3D camera pose (azimuth, elevation, in-plane rotation). Classification is performed by matching extracted feature-map vectors against learned vertex features via a fast convolution-like operation, while pose estimation uses a render-and-compare procedure over 144 predefined candidate poses followed by gradient-based refinement.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level contrastive loss that decouples the original O(Y²) per-vertex contrastive learning into an in-class term (contrasting all vertex features within the same category plus background) and a cross-class term that only samples N=32 vertex features from each other category. On top of this, a dynamically weighted scheme recomputes a confusion matrix on calibration data every two epochs and sets the cross-class weight ω_{y,y'} to zero for pairs with confusion below 0.05, effectively skipping contrastive loss between rarely-confused classes in a supervised hard-negative-mining fashion. This reduces contrastive FLOPs by up to 96% and training time by 7× relative to NOVUM, while scaling linearly rather than quadratically with the number of classes. The probabilistic model uses von Mises–Fisher distributions over unit-norm vertex and image features, with vertex features updated via momentum (σ=0.9) and background features resampled from each training batch.

## Results

On ImageNet3D+ (188 classes), 3D-CompNets with a ViT-B/14 backbone achieves 93.5% classification accuracy on IID data, outperforming DINOv2 (92.4%) and NOVUM (85.7%). Under occlusion (average across L1–L3), it reaches 43.7% classification accuracy vs. 41.7% for DINOv2 and 38.5% for NOVUM. For 3D pose estimation (accuracy at π/6), 3D-CompNets scores 57.6% on IID and 45.7% under corruption, consistently leading all baselines. On synthetic-to-real transfer, it achieves 74.9% classification accuracy vs. 72.3% for DINOv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 3D-CompNets against three baselines: NOVUM (the direct neural-mesh predecessor without the proposed grouped/dynamic-weighted training), ResNet50 (standard CNN with dual classification+pose heads), and DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (strong self-supervised transformer with the same dual-head setup). 3D-CompNets outperforms NOVUM by 7.8% on IID classification (93.5 vs. 85.7) and by 5.2% on corruption-averaged classification (59.2 vs. 55.9), while also being 7× faster to train (13.3h vs. 66.8h). Against DINOv2, the gains are more modest but consistent: +1.1% IID classification, +2.0% occlusion-averaged classification, +6.8% corruption-averaged classification, and +0.7% to +1.9% on pose estimation across settings. The largest margin over DINOv2 appears under corruption (fog, snow, frost, brightness), where 3D-CompNets leads by 6.8 points on average, suggesting the 3D compositional representation provides meaningful robustness beyond what a strong 2D transformer alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Neural Mesh Model family (NOVUM, NEMO, Compositional CNNs) that represents objects as 3D meshes with learned vertex features trained via supervised contrastive learning. The key departure is algorithmic: prior neural mesh models were limited to 10–12 object classes because per-vertex contrastive loss scaled quadratically with the number of classes, whereas here the authors decouple the loss into in-class and cross-class components and apply dynamic, confusion-matrix-driven weighting (analogous to hard-negative mining) to the cross-class term. This transforms the training from an O(Y²) problem into one that scales approximately linearly, enabling 188-class training with 96% fewer FLOPs and 7× faster convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 3D compositional models, previously confined to toy-scale benchmarks, now scale to a realistic 188-class setting and outperform strong DNN baselines (DINOv2, ResNet50) on both IID classification/pose estimation and OOD scenarios (occlusion, weather corruption, real-to-synthetic shift, zero-shot unknown categories). A notable conceptual shift is that these models, which previously showed no IID advantage over standard DNNs, now achieve modest but consistent IID gains, suggesting the 3D inductive bias contributes beyond just OOD robustness. Open questions include whether replacing the coarse cuboid geometry with tighter per-class meshes and moving beyond factorized vertex features would yield further gains, and whether the approach generalizes to even larger class counts or to 6D pose estimation at this scale.
