## Problem

Dynamic scene reconstruction from sparse-view videos (e.g., four cameras 90° apart with limited cross-view overlap) remains unsolved: dense multi-view methods rely on geometric correspondences that are absent in sparse setups, while monocular methods are under-constrained and produce inconsistent geometry across views. This paper targets the practical in-the-wild capture scenario of reconstructing skilled human behaviors (piano, CPR, bike repair) from a small set of portable static cameras, a regime that is neither well-posed enough for classic SfM nor under-constrained enough for purely monocular approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB video from four equidistant inward-facing static cameras (90° apart) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, at 512×288 resolution and 30 fps over 10-second clips, plus a per-frame foreground-background mask (obtained via SAM). It produces a canonical 4D Gaussian scene representation (18k foreground + 1.2M background Gaussians, 28 SE(3) motion bases) that can be rasterized to yield novel-view RGB images, depth maps, foreground silhouettes, and 32-dim feature maps. Because cameras are stationary, temporal correspondence for background pixels is trivially given by 2D pixel index, which the method exploits for denoising.

## Method

MonoFusion initializes a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation by fusing two complementary geometry sources: DUSt3R provides a metric multi-view consistent pointmap at a reference timestamp (constrained to known camera poses), while MoGe provides more accurate per-view monocular depth. Monocular depth is aligned to the DUSt3R reference via per-view, per-frame scale-and-shift least-squares fitting restricted to background pixels, then temporally denoised by averaging background depth across time. For motion, instead of clustering noisy 3D tracks (as in Shape of Motion), the method extracts DINOv2 pixel features, reduces them to 32 dimensions via PCA, and runs k-means to define 28 rigid motion bases whose weights are set by L2 distance to cluster centers. A joint differentiable-rendering optimization then refines geometry and motion using photometric, feature, mask, and depth reconstruction losses plus a rigidity regularizer on neighboring Gaussians, with Gaussian scales initialized from projected pixel area rather than k-NN to avoid over-large splats.

## Results

On Panoptic Studio (held-out views), MonoFusion achieves 28.01 PSNR / 0.899 SSIM / 0.117 LPIPS on full frames and 27.52 PSNR / 0.944 SSIM / 0.022 LPIPS / 0.965 IoU on the dynamic foreground, outperforming all baselines. On the in-the-wild ExoRecon dataset, MonoFusion reaches 30.43 PSNR / 0.927 SSIM / 0.061 LPIPS (full frame) and 29.71 PSNR / 0.947 SSIM / 0.017 LPIPS / 0.963 IoU (dynamic only). For 45° extreme novel-view synthesis on Panoptic Studio, MonoFusion achieves 25.73 PSNR and 0.943 IoU, substantially ahead of the next-best method (Dyn3D-GS at 23.31 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoFusion outperforms all baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the monocular Shape of Motion (SOM), MonoFusion gains over 10 PSNR on Panoptic Studio (28.01 vs. 17.86) and over 15 PSNR on ExoRecon (30.43 vs. 14.73), reflecting the severe under-constraint of single-view 4D reconstruction. Against Dynamic 3D Gaussians (Dyn3D-GS), MonoFusion improves by 2.6 PSNR on Panoptic Studio and 6.2 PSNR on ExoRecon, with a particularly large gap in LPIPS (0.117 vs. 0.266 on Panoptic; 0.061 vs. 0.539 on ExoRecon), indicating far better perceptual quality. The closest competitor is the authors' own multi-view extension MV-SOM (four independently-initialized SOM instances optimized jointly), which MonoFusion beats by 1.7 PSNR on Panoptic Studio and 3.5 PSNR on ExoRecon; even when MV-SOM is augmented with DUSt3R-consistent depth (MV-SOM-DS), MonoFusion still leads by 2.1 PSNR on ExoRecon, confirming that the DUSt3R+MoGe alignment strategy and feature-based motion bases are critical beyond simply providing multi-view-consistent depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and the motion-bases formulation from Shape of Motion, but departs from both dense multi-view dynamic reconstruction (Dynamic 3DGS, NeRF-based methods requiring hundreds of cameras) and naive multi-view extensions of monocular 4D methods. Its core methodological contribution is a careful alignment pipeline: using DUSt3R to establish a global metric reference frame, then aligning per-view MoGe monocular depth predictions (which are more accurate for humans than multi-view predictors) to that frame via background-only scale/shift optimization. For motion initialization, it replaces Shape of Motion's noisy 3D-track-based clustering with DINOv2 feature clustering, arguing that semantic grouping is more robust in the sparse-view regime where cross-view correspondences are limited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 4-camera inward-facing setup is orders of magnitude cheaper and more deployable than the 480-camera Panoptic Studio, making dynamic scene reconstruction accessible for in-the-wild skilled-activity capture (the Ego-Exo4D use case). The ExoRecon benchmark and the finding that monocular priors outperform multi-view geometry for human-centric scenes offer a generalizable recipe for sparse-view 4D reconstruction. Limitations include the requirement for fixed cameras with known poses, foreground-background masks, and a human-centric focus; open questions include whether the alignment strategy extends to moving cameras, non-human dynamics, or longer sequences, and whether the 28-basis motion model generalizes beyond the tested scene complexity.
