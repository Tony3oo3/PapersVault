## Problem

CLIP-based video learners for open-vocabulary action recognition inherit a static bias from CLIP's image pre-training, causing them to overfit on shortcut static features that help in-context generalization but severely degrade out-of-context performance (e.g., when video backgrounds are replaced). Existing regularization methods like Open-VCLIP and FROSTER curb deviation from CLIP but remain tethered to these static cues, leaving a gap in achieving robust generalization across both in-context and out-of-context open-vocabulary settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips and text prompts formatted as predefined templates (e.g., 'a video of {action}') as input, with the text encoder kept frozen throughout training and inference. At test time, 3 temporal and 1 spatial views per video are extracted and their recognition results are linearly aggregated to produce a final action class prediction. Evaluation spans both in-context protocols (base-to-novel, cross-dataset) and out-of-context protocols (UCF-SCUBA, UCF-HAT) where video backgrounds are synthetically replaced, making the output sensitive to whether the learner captures motion cues versus static appearance.

## Method

Open-MeDe replaces CLIP regularization with a cross-batch meta-optimization scheme: consecutive training batches are treated as support (meta training) and query (meta testing) sets, where the learner obtains fast weights via one gradient step on the support batch and is then virtually evaluated on the query batch. The outer loop jointly minimizes the support loss and the query loss computed at the fast weights, explicitly encouraging the learner to quickly generalize to arbitrary subsequent data using only its own learned knowledge rather than CLIP's static generalization. To stabilize the optimization trajectory, a Gaussian Weight Average (GWA) self-ensemble assigns epoch-wise weights following a Gaussian prior (peaking at middle epochs, tapering at both ends) and deliberately excludes the original CLIP weights, yielding a generic optimum that balances video-specific knowledge with zero-shot plasticity. The framework is model-agnostic and can be plugged into adapter-based, prompt-based, partially-tuned, or fully-tuned CLIP video learners without architectural modification.

## Results

Under the in-context base-to-novel setting (Table 1), Open-MeDe achieves harmonic means of 69.9% on K400, 63.9% on HMDB, 85.9% on UCF-101, and 14.3% on SSv2, surpassing the strongest CLIP-regularization baselines (Open-VCLIP: 68.6/58.5/85.0/12.9; FROSTER: 68.3/58.3/84.7/12.4) across all four datasets. Under the more challenging out-of-context setting (Table 3), Open-MeDe reaches 33.5% Top-1 on UCF-SCUBA and 64.5% on UCF-HAT, outperforming Open-VCLIP by +4.6% and +4.9% respectively, with a harmonic mean of 52.4% vs. 47.4% for Open-VCLIP. In the in-context cross-dataset protocol (Table 2), Open-MeDe leads on UCF (83.7%), HMDB (54.6%), and K600 (73.7%), exceeding the previous best (Open-VCLIP: 83.3/53.8/73.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Open-MeDe outperforms all 14 compared baselines on the in-context base-to-novel HM metric across K400, HMDB, UCF, and SSv2, with the largest margins over FROSTER (+5.6% HM on HMDB) and Open-VCLIP (+1.3% on K400, +1.0% on UCF). On the out-of-context UCF-SCUBA and UCF-HAT benchmarks, Open-MeDe leads Open-VCLIP by +4.6% and +4.9% Top-1 respectively, and FROSTER by +8.3% and +5.9%, confirming that CLIP-regularization methods suffer severe static-bias degradation when backgrounds are replaced. In the in-context cross-dataset protocol, Open-MeDe edges out Open-VCLIP on UCF (+0.4%), HMDB (+0.8%), and K600 (+0.7%), and surpasses FROSTER by +0.8%, +1.2%, and +2.6% on the same datasets. Partially fine-tuned X-CLIP and prompt-based VPT show the weakest generalization, with VPT dropping to 37.6% novel accuracy on K400 (vs. 63.8% for Open-MeDe), while ST-Adapter and ViFi-CLIP remain competitive on UCF but trail by 1.0–2.6% HM. The model-agnostic ablation (Table 4) further shows that integrating Open-MeDe with ST-Adapter, Vita-CLIP, X-CLIP, and VCLIP yields consistent gains of +0.9 to +12.5% across datasets, with the largest improvements for fully-tuned and partially-tuned learners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes MAML's inner/outer-loop meta-optimization (Finn et al., 2017) for open-vocabulary action recognition, but departs from the standard few-shot formulation by using consecutive training batches as support/query rather than sampling N-way K-shot tasks, thereby avoiding meta-overfitting from limited task diversity. It also draws on Gaussian Weight Average from prompt learning (Khattak et al., 2023) and model patching (Ilharco et al., 2022) for self-ensemble stabilization. Conceptually, the key departure from prior OVAR work (Open-VCLIP, FROSTER) is replacing CLIP regularization—which anchors the learner to static image features—with an explicit meta-learning objective that trains the learner to generalize to 'unknown' data without any CLIP weight interpolation or distillation loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a critical failure mode: CLIP regularization methods that excel in in-context OVAR actually degrade out-of-context generalization (e.g., UCF-SCUBA) because they entrench static bias, a finding that reframes how the community should think about preserving CLIP's generalization in video. The model-agnostic framework (validated across adapter, prompt, partial, and full fine-tuning) offers a practical drop-in improvement for existing CLIP-based video pipelines. Open questions include whether the cross-batch meta-optimization scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/H) or longer training, whether the GWA schedule generalizes beyond the tested epoch ranges, and whether the out-of-context evaluation protocols (SCUBA, HAT) capture the full spectrum of distribution shift encountered in deployment.
