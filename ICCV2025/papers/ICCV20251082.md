## Problem

Existing image-based normal estimators, whether discriminative or diffusion-based, produce flickering and temporally inconsistent results when applied frame-by-frame to video. Naively repurposing video diffusion models for normal estimation yields over-smoothed predictions because their intermediate features lack sufficient high-level semantic cues to preserve fine-grained geometric details. The paper addresses both the temporal coherence gap and the spatial detail loss simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251082/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video clip of arbitrary length (F×W×H×3, short edge resized to 576) and produces a corresponding temporally consistent normal sequence of the same shape. It can also operate on single images by setting frame length to one. Training uses only synthetic data (Replica, 3D Ken Burns, Hypersim, MatrixCity, Objaverse) with ground-truth normals, while evaluation is zero-shot on real-world benchmarks (NYUv2, iBims-1, ScanNet, Sintel).

## Method

NormalCrafter builds on Stable Video Diffusion, adapting it by replacing the single-image conditioning with frame-wise concatenation of the noisy normal latent and the conditional video latent. The key insight is that SVD's intermediate features are semantically ambiguous, causing over-smoothed normals; to fix this, Semantic Feature Regularization (SFR) aligns U-Net decoder features (at the Up1 layer) with DINOv2 patch embeddings via a cosine-similarity loss, steering the model toward geometric detail without adding inference cost. A two-stage training protocol balances temporal context with spatial fidelity: Stage 1 trains the full U-Net in latent space with denoising score matching plus SFR over variable-length sequences (1–14 frames), while Stage 2 fine-tunes only the spatial layers in pixel space with an angular loss plus SFR over shorter clips (1–4 frames), preserving long-sequence inference capacity. The VAE decoder is additionally fine-tuned on normal maps to improve reconstruction quality from 5.75° to 4.07° mean angular error.

## Results

On the Sintel video benchmark (characterized by large camera motion and fast-moving objects), NormalCrafter achieves a mean angular error of 30.7° and median of 23.9°, outperforming the second-best method (Lotus-D, 32.3°/25.5°) by 1.6° on both metrics. On ScanNet, it reaches 13.3° mean / 6.8° median, beating Marigold-E2E-FT (14.1°/6.3°) by 0.8° in mean error. On single-image NYUv2, NormalCrafter attains 15.4° mean angular error (best among all methods), while on iBims-1 it remains competitive at 16.1° mean, slightly behind Marigold-E2E-FT (15.8°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NormalCrafter achieves state-of-the-art results on both video benchmarks. On Sintel, it outperforms the second-best Lotus-D by 1.6° in mean angular error (30.7° vs 32.3°) and by 2.6/3.1 points on the 22.5°/30° thresholds, with all other baselines trailing by 3–8° in mean error. On ScanNet, it beats Marigold-E2E-FT by 0.8° in mean error (13.3° vs 14.1°) and by 1.2/1.5 points on the 22.5°/30° thresholds, though it is slightly behind on median (6.8° vs 6.3°). On the single-image NYUv2 benchmark, NormalCrafter achieves the best mean error (15.4°) and best 22.5°/30° thresholds, edging out Marigold-E2E-FT (16.2°) and Lotus-D (16.2°). On iBims-1, however, NormalCrafter (16.1° mean) is marginally behind Marigold-E2E-FT (15.8°) and GenPercept (16.3°), indicating that its video-oriented design does not fully dominate in the single-image regime. Overall, the gains are most pronounced on video sequences with significant camera motion, confirming the value of the video diffusion prior and SFR for temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

NormalCrafter extends the video diffusion model paradigm (established by SVD for video generation and adapted by DepthCrafter for depth estimation) to surface normal estimation, a task where prior diffusion-based methods (Marigold, StableNormal, Lotus) operated exclusively on single images and produced temporally inconsistent results. The key conceptual departure is identifying that SVD's intermediate features are semantically over-smoothed for normals (unlike depth), and addressing this by borrowing the feature-alignment loss from REPA as a lightweight Semantic Feature Regularization. The two-stage training protocol adapts Marigold-E2E-FT's pixel-space fine-tuning insight to the video setting, where naive end-to-end pixel-space training is infeasible due to VAE decoder memory costs over long sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve state-of-the-art video normal estimation by directly leveraging video diffusion priors rather than bolting temporal layers onto image-based estimators, offering a cleaner path to temporal consistency. The SFR mechanism is attractive for its zero inference overhead and generality (applicable to any diffusion-based dense prediction task with over-smoothed features). The main limitation acknowledged is the large parameter size inherited from SVD, which precludes mobile deployment; the authors suggest pruning, quantization, and distillation as future directions. An open question is whether the SFR + two-stage recipe transfers cleanly to other video dense-prediction tasks (e.g., optical flow, segmentation) beyond normals and depth.
