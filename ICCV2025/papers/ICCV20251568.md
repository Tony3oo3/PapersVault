## Problem

DMesh, a probabilistic differentiable mesh representation, encodes connectivity into continuous point features but relies on Weighted Delaunay Triangulation (WDT) for its tessellation function, incurring O(N) time complexity that becomes prohibitive for complex shapes with 100K+ points. This computational bottleneck limits DMesh's practical use for detailed 2D and 3D reconstruction, while data-driven alternatives suffer from self-intersections, outlier sensitivity, and their own high costs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D point clouds (sampled from spline curves at 1,000 points per curve), dense 3D point clouds (200K points via Poisson disk sampling, optionally with normals), or 512×512 diffuse and depth maps from 64 viewpoints with known Phong shading. It produces 2D or 3D triangular meshes, and when per-point color features are jointly optimized, textured 3D meshes with barycentric-interpolated vertex colors. The formulation handles both open and closed surfaces, as well as non-orientable geometries, without requiring oriented input.

## Method

DMesh++ replaces WDT with a Minimum-Ball condition: a face F belongs to the tessellation if its unique minimum bounding ball contains no other point from the set, making it a self-intersection-free subset of Delaunay Triangulation faces. Each point is a (d+1)-dimensional vector (position + real value ψ), and face existence probability is the product of a sigmoid-gated signed distance to the nearest point outside F (Λ_min) and a differentiable min over vertex ψ values (Λ_real). The key efficiency gain comes from reducing the per-face evaluation to a parallelizable nearest-neighbor search with O(log N) complexity, achieving up to 32× speedup over DMesh in 3D. Reconstruction proceeds by iteratively optimizing point positions and real values under modality-specific losses (Chamfer Distance for point clouds, rendering loss for images), with periodic mesh subdivision to increase detail and nearest-neighbor caching to further accelerate optimization.

## Results

On 3D point cloud reconstruction (50 models, Table 2), DMesh++ achieves CD of 0.033×10⁻³ and F1 of 0.480 with oriented input, outperforming DMesh (CD 0.154, F1 0.289) by 4.7× in CD while processing 4.2× more faces (55,546 vs 13,088) in 76% less time (282s vs 1147s). On 3D multi-view reconstruction (50 models, Table 4), DMesh++ achieves the best CD (0.342×10⁻³), F1 (0.360), NC (0.923), and zero self-intersections, while running 3.8× faster than DMesh (205s vs 772s). The tessellation function itself is up to 32× faster in 3D and uses up to 75% less GPU memory compared to DMesh's WDT-based approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMesh++ dominates its direct predecessor DMesh across all tasks: on 3D point cloud reconstruction it improves CD by 4.7× (0.033 vs 0.154) and F1 by 66% (0.480 vs 0.289) while generating 4.2× more faces in 76% less time; on multi-view reconstruction it halves CD (0.342 vs 0.697) and runs 3.8× faster. Against PSR and VoroMesh, DMesh++ achieves dramatically lower CD (0.033 vs 10.164 and 19.591) and far better aspect ratios (1.814 vs 5.218 and 145.7), as these methods struggle with open surfaces. PoNQ is the closest competitor on oriented point clouds (CD 1.578 vs 0.033), but DMesh++ still wins on CD by 48×, F1 (0.480 vs 0.402), and EF1 (0.116 vs 0.090). In multi-view reconstruction, DMesh++ outperforms Remeshing, DMTet, FlexiCubes, and GShell on CD, F1, NC, and EF1, while uniquely achieving zero self-intersections (Remeshing has SI=0.220, GShell 0.047, FlexiCubes 0.011). The only metric where a competitor edges ahead is Remeshing's aspect ratio (1.562 vs 1.639), though Remeshing's self-intersection problem makes it less practical for downstream use.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMesh++ is a direct algorithmic successor to DMesh [42], replacing its Weighted Delaunay Triangulation (WDT) tessellation with a Minimum-Ball condition that is a strict subset of standard Delaunay Triangulation. This eliminates the O(N) sequential WDT computation (which is inherently non-parallelizable due to race conditions) in favor of a GPU-parallelizable nearest-neighbor search with O(log N) complexity. In the broader landscape, it occupies the 'probabilistic differentiable mesh' niche distinct from data-driven connectivity predictors (SpaceMesh, MeshGPT, MeshAnything) and implicit-surface-based methods (NeuS, DMTet, FlexiCubes, GShell), retaining DMesh's key advantages of self-intersection avoidance and open-surface support without requiring learned connectivity models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32× speedup in 3D and up to 96% memory reduction make differentiable mesh optimization practical for complex shapes with 200K+ points, a regime where DMesh was infeasible. This opens the door to applying probabilistic differentiable meshes in generative models and physics simulation pipelines that were previously blocked by computational cost. However, non-manifoldness errors persist (albeit reduced), sparse point clouds remain challenging, and the multi-view pipeline currently requires known lighting/rendering conditions, limiting real-world applicability. The authors identify integration with Gaussian Splatting and training generative models over mesh connectivity as natural next steps.
