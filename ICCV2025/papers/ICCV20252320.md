## Problem

Existing human behavior simulation work is limited to either short-horizon linguistic task planning or low-level human-scene interaction, with no system capable of achieving high-level, long-horizon, abstract goals (e.g., 'prepare for work') in realistic 3D environments. A competent agent must decompose such goals into sequences of activities, adapt to environmental dynamics (e.g., an occupied bathroom), and navigate a vast behavior space guided by value priorities—none of which prior approaches address jointly. Additionally, no comprehensive scene-aware benchmark exists for evaluating such agents.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a high-level goal as free-form language (concrete or abstract, e.g., 'feel the call of nature'), a 3D household scene with textured meshes and object states (e.g., fridge: opened), the agent's current SMPL-X pose, and optional language commands encoding personality or value preferences. It outputs a hierarchical tree-structured plan where intermediate nodes are semantic activities and leaf nodes are executable actions, each grounded to a scene-conditioned 3D whole-body motion sequence of ~83 frames on average, with hand-grasp refinement for object interactions.

## Method

ACTOR operates in a perceive-plan-act cycle where an LLM (GPT-4) controller performs active tree search over the behavior space rather than one-shot planning. Node expansion samples candidate behaviors via LLM prompting constrained by a hierarchical prior that groups low-level actions into interchangeable activity units. Each candidate is scored by a product of the LLM's commonsense likelihood and a set of customizable value functions—real-valued (e.g., shortest-path distance) and language-based (e.g., 'how likely is a neat person to do this')—that ground the plan in environment state and agent beliefs. MCTS is used as the search algorithm with window size 5 and depth 3. The BEHAVIORHUB dataset (10k samples, 1.5k scenes) is generated by bootstrapping LLM commonsense into goal-plan trees via in-context learning, then aligning motions from AMASS/GRAB to ScanNet/HM3D scenes using MOVER collision-contact optimization and Transformer-based sequence blending.

## Results

On the BEHAVIORHUB Main Set, ACTOR achieves a Goal Success Rate (GSR) of 0.472 and GSRPL of 0.351, nearly doubling the GSR of the strongest baseline HuggingGPT (0.317 / 0.161). On the more challenging Dynamic Subset, ACTOR maintains a GSR of 0.306 and GSRPL of 0.212, again roughly doubling HuggingGPT (0.164 / 0.073). In terms of motion quality, ACTOR achieves a FID of 2.087 (Main) and 3.141 (Dynamic) versus 5.386 and 9.116 for HuggingGPT, and a motion recognition accuracy of 0.773 and 0.697 respectively. Human evaluation on the Dynamic Subset confirms these trends, with ACTOR scoring 3.05/5 on completeness and 3.47/5 on rationality versus 2.71 and 2.89 for HuggingGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTOR is compared against two LLM-based baselines: LLMaP (a procedural planner that operates purely on text) and HuggingGPT (a general tool-use agent). Against LLMaP, ACTOR wins on both planning metrics—S-BLEU improves from 0.089 to 0.170 (Main) and 0.069 to 0.135 (Dynamic), and BERT-S from 0.825 to 0.879 and 0.821 to 0.862—while LLMaP cannot produce motion sequences at all. Against HuggingGPT, ACTOR dominates across every metric: GSR nearly doubles (0.472 vs. 0.317 on Main; 0.306 vs. 0.164 on Dynamic), GSRPL more than doubles (0.351 vs. 0.161; 0.212 vs. 0.073), and FID drops from 5.386 to 2.087 (Main) and 9.116 to 3.141 (Dynamic), indicating substantially more natural motion. The gap widens on the Dynamic Subset, where HuggingGPT's ungrounded planning fails to account for environmental state changes (e.g., an occupied bathroom), while ACTOR's value-driven search adapts its plan accordingly. For downstream motion generation, pre-training on BEHAVIORHUB improves both a scene-aware method (MPJPE 242.50→201.56) and MDM (FID 0.544→0.471, R-Precision 0.611→0.705), confirming the dataset's utility beyond the primary agent task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perceive-plan-act lineage from computational cognition (ICARUS, Generative Agents) and game AI into realistic 3D household environments, departing from VirtualHome's environment-agnostic procedural planning and LLMaP's purely linguistic zero-shot planning by grounding an LLM controller in a value-driven tree search over a hierarchical behavior space. The BEHAVIORHUB dataset departs from human-authored benchmarks like ActivityPrograms by leveraging LLM commonsense to synthesize goal-plan trees and automatically aligning them with 3D motions (AMASS, BABEL, GRAB) and scenes (ScanNet, HM3D, Replica) via collision/contact constraints. The architecture borrows the LLM-as-controller paradigm from HuggingGPT and the grounded-decoding idea from robotics (RT-1, Grounded Decoding) but replaces retraining-based grounding with pluggable real-valued and language-based value functions evaluated during MCTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEHAVIORHUB provides the first large-scale, scene-aware, long-horizon benchmark for evaluating holistic human behavior simulation, filling a gap the authors identify as barrier (iv) that has hampered agent development. The significant remaining gap to human performance (GSR 0.306 vs. human-level plans) underscores that current LLM-based agents still struggle with environmental dynamics and value-conditioned reasoning, pointing to open questions around richer perception, multi-agent interaction, and outdoor generalization. The demonstrated utility of BEHAVIORHUB for downstream motion generation tasks (scene-aware and language-conditioned) suggests the dataset will serve as a training resource beyond its primary evaluation role, potentially accelerating progress in human-scene interaction research.
