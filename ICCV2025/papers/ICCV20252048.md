## Problem

Existing two-stage fashion editing pipelines (mask generation followed by diffusion-based editing) suffer from two critical limitations: coarse-grained human masks restrict edits to predefined zones while fine-grained clothes masks prevent style or length customization, and mask generators fail under articulated poses or miss rare anatomical regions like waist. No existing method can dynamically interpret user-defined anatomical instructions (e.g., 'belly-length') and produce pose-robust, anatomy-aware masks in open-world scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-world fashion image (RGB) and a natural-language anatomical instruction (e.g., 'belly-length', 'short → long sleeves') as input. A language model (DeepSeek) parses the instruction into a body-structure group, which drives the mask generation pipeline. The output is a fine-grained human mask (initially at 16×16 latent resolution, upsampled to 32×32 for edge refinement) that is plugged into off-the-shelf editors such as IDM-VTON or PowerPaint to produce the final edited image. The entire framework is training-free and requires no fine-tuning of the downstream editor.

## Method

Pose-Star is a training-free, three-stage mask generation framework. (1) Localization: Star tokens are extracted from Stable Diffusion's cross-attention layers via DDIM inversion and Null-text optimization, then their centroids are calibrated to OpenPose skeletal keypoints to ensure robust localization under extreme articulation; a radial constraint suppresses noise outside biomechanically plausible regions. (2) Aggregation: The paper identifies a three-phase attention evolution across 100 diffusion steps (convergence → stabilization → divergence) and designs thresholded mask averaging (β=0.3) combined with a time-weighted 3×3 sliding-window consensus to fuse multi-step token maps while suppressing phase-specific noise. (3) Optimization: Cross-attention maps (spatial focus) and self-attention maps (edge coherence) are merged via pixel-wise averaging, and the resulting boundaries are aligned to Canny edges from the input image to produce anatomically consistent, pose-aware mask contours.

## Results

On a user-feedback survey (5-point scale) across three dimensions, Pose-Star integrated with PowerPaint achieves 4.85±0.33 (User-Defined Flexibility), 4.74±0.41 (Pose Robustness), and 4.63±0.62 (In-the-Wild Generalizability), while IDM-VTON+Pose-Star reaches 4.89±0.21, 4.82±0.04, and 4.55±0.38 respectively. The paper reports improvements of +3.11, +1.81, and +0.81 points over the strongest prior fashion-specific baselines on the three axes, uniquely advancing all three simultaneously. CLIP-Score comparisons (Fig. 8b) further confirm superior text-image alignment for edited outputs versus general-purpose editors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pose-Star (with either PowerPaint or IDM-VTON) outperforms all ten baselines on all three survey dimensions simultaneously. Against the strongest general-purpose editor InstructPix2Pix (3.57/2.01/3.96), Pose-Star gains +1.28/+2.73/+0.67 on User-Defined/Pose/Wild respectively. Among fashion-specific methods, the closest competitor CatVTON (1.73/4.43/4.10) is surpassed by +3.12/+0.31/+0.53, while TexFit (1.22/3.82/3.00) and IDM-VTON+SCHP (1.30/3.78/4.29) trail by even wider margins. In the ablation (Fig. 9), replacing the Localization module with open-vocabulary detectors (T-Rex2, DINO 1.6, YOLO-World) causes failures on rare anatomical regions (e.g., chest, waist) under articulated poses; replacing Aggregation with SAM introduces 62% more boundary jitter and identity loss, while SCHP retains only 48.2% valid region coverage versus Pose-Star's 92.5%; replacing Optimization with DIS/U²-Net increases hand/foot distortions by 35% relative to the Cross-Self Attention Merge + Canny alignment pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pose-Star repurposes diffusion model internals (cross-attention token maps from Null-text inversion, following the Prompt-to-Prompt lineage) as a training-free anatomical segmentation tool, a conceptual departure from the standard two-stage fashion editing pipeline that relies on dedicated human parsers (SCHP, OOTDiffusion) or clothes segmenters (TexFit). It further departs from open-world detectors (YOLO-World, T-Rex2, DINO) and segmenters (SAM, U2-Net) by showing that their noun-centric or foreground-background paradigms fail on rare anatomical regions under articulated poses, whereas diffusion attention—calibrated with OpenPose skeletal keypoints—achieves finer anatomical granularity. The framework is explicitly plug-and-play, slotting its generated fine-grained human masks into existing editors (IDM-VTON, PowerPaint) without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap in fashion editing: the inability of existing mask generators to support user-defined anatomical regions (e.g., 'belly-length', 'waist-length') in open-world, articulated-pose scenarios, which is a practical bottleneck for industrial virtual try-on and clothes design. The three-phase attention dynamics (convergence → stabilization → divergence) and the phase-aware sliding-window fusion offer a reusable recipe for extracting temporally stable spatial information from diffusion processes beyond fashion. Limitations include the 3.6 s/image inversion overhead, reliance on a single diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion U-Net), and evaluation primarily through subjective user ratings on limited datasets; acceleration via DPM-Solver++ and extension to video editing are noted as future directions.
