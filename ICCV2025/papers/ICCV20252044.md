## Problem

Diffusion models are vulnerable to copyright infringement backdoor attacks where poisoned samples injected into training data cause the model to reproduce infringing content under specific trigger prompts. While general backdoor defense methods exist for diffusion models, none specifically target the copyright infringement mechanism, which exploits the model's overfitting to the correspondence between infringing features, their spatial positions, and associated captions via cross-attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252044/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset of image-caption pairs, an infringing output image generated by the poisoned model under a trigger prompt, and the poisoned caption text. GroundingDINO and SAM are used to produce segmentation masks that isolate the infringing feature region from both the training samples and the infringing output. The output is a per-sample copyright attribution score (used with a threshold of 0.35 to flag poisoned samples) and a retrained diffusion model whose loss has been modified to suppress memorization of the detected infringing features.

## Method

CopyrightShield has two components. First, for poisoned sample detection, it constructs a copyright attribution score by adapting TRAK's kernel-based data attribution with a spatially masked SSCD similarity objective: segmentation masks (from GroundingDINO + SAM) restrict gradient computation to the infringing feature region, so the attribution score τ_c(z_i, x_0) = ∇_θ f_spatial(z_i, x_0; θ)ᵀ · Δθ(z_i) quantifies how strongly each training sample drives the model to memorize infringing features at specific spatial positions. Second, for defense, it introduces a dynamic penalty term into the standard DDPM training loss: L = L_0 + (1/N) Σ τ_i · L_mse(ε_θ(z_t, y, t), ε(z_d, t)), where the attribution scores τ_i weight the penalty to selectively reduce the model's reliance on detected poisoned samples' features and prompts while preserving overall generative quality (measured by FID and CLIP scores).

## Results

CopyrightShield achieves an average F1-score of 0.665 for poisoned sample detection (0.671 on Pokemon, 0.691 on COYO+Midjourney at threshold 0.35), outperforming the best data-attribution baseline D-TRAK (F1 0.534/0.533) by roughly 25%. In defense, CopyrightShield reduces the Copyright Infringement Rate (CIR) by an average of 56.7% and delays the First-Attack Epoch (FAE) by 115.2% compared to no defense. For example, at a 10% poisoning rate on COYO+Midjourney, CIR drops from 0.519 (no defense) to 0.217, while FAE increases from 31.28 to 86.42 epochs. Generative quality is largely preserved, with FID and CLIP scores remaining close to the original Stable Diffusion v1.4 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CopyrightShield is compared against two SoTA backdoor defense methods (T2IShield and TERD) on the full defense pipeline (CIR/FAE) and three data-attribution methods (TRAK, Journey TRAK, D-TRAK) on poisoned-sample detection (F1). On defense, CopyrightShield consistently outperforms both T2IShield and TERD across all six dataset/rate combinations: at the 10% poisoning rate on COYO+Midjourney, it achieves CIR 0.217 vs. 0.420 (T2IShield) and 0.374 (TERD), and FAE 86.42 vs. 37.85 and 41.71 respectively — roughly a 25% improvement over the best baseline. On detection, CopyrightShield's spatially-guided attribution score yields F1 of 0.671 (Pokemon) and 0.691 (COYO+Midjourney), surpassing D-TRAK (0.534/0.533), Journey TRAK (0.479/0.484), and TRAK (0.443/0.473) by 25–50% in F1. The ablation confirms that the detection component contributes more to overall defense than the adaptive optimization alone, indicating that accurate poisoned-sample identification is the critical bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data attribution framework of TRAK by incorporating spatial masking (via GroundingDINO + SAM) and SSCD similarity to construct a copyright-specific attribution score that quantifies how much a training sample drives memorization of infringing features at specific spatial positions. It departs from general backdoor defense methods for diffusion models (T2IShield, TERD, UFID) by targeting the unique mechanism of copyright infringement attacks—overfitting to prompt-feature-spatial-position correspondences via cross-attention—rather than generic trigger-based backdoors. The adaptive optimization training component introduces a dynamic penalty term weighted by attribution scores, which is a new loss formulation not present in prior diffusion model defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As commercial text-to-image models face active copyright litigation, this is the first defense method explicitly designed for copyright infringement backdoor attacks, providing a concrete 'detect-clean-retrain' pipeline with measurable metrics (CIR, FAE). However, the evaluation is limited to a single attack method (SilentBadDiffusion), virtual datasets (Pokemon, Midjourney v5) rather than real copyrighted works, and only SD v1.x, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures, more sophisticated attacks, or real-world copyright scenarios. The ablation showing detection accuracy is the dominant factor (more than the adaptive loss) suggests that improving poisoned sample identification is the critical bottleneck for future work.
