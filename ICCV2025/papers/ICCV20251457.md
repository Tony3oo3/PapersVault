## Problem

Existing VLN benchmarks evaluate models in idealized simulators with point-based agents, ignoring physical embodiment constraints such as locomotion dynamics, camera height differences across robot types, falls, collisions, and lighting variations. No prior work has systematically quantified how much performance degrades when transferring VLN-CE models to physically realistic settings across humanoid, quadruped, and wheeled robots, leaving the 'embodied gap' between simulation and deployment largely unmeasured.

## Input / Output

All evaluated methods consume a single ego-centric RGB-D pinhole camera observation (matching real robot hardware) plus a natural language instruction, deliberately excluding panoramic sensors used by prior VLN-CE SoTA. Outputs vary by pipeline: Seq2Seq/CMA predict a single discrete action (forward, turn left/right, stop) per step; RDP generates a dense sequence of T continuous relative displacements (Δx, Δy, Δyaw) via iterative denoising; NaVid outputs actions in natural language parsed by regex; and VLMaps produces executable Python code for path planning on a semantic map. The platform also tracks physical-specific metrics—Fall Rate and Stuck Rate—beyond standard VLN metrics.

## Method

VLN-PE is built on GRUTopia (NVIDIA Isaac Sim) with RL-based locomotion controllers for Unitree H1/G1 humanoids, Unitree Aliengo quadrupeds, and Jetbot wheeled robots, enabling physically realistic data collection and evaluation. The paper's new architectural contribution is RDP, a recurrent diffusion policy for VLN that encodes RGB and instructions via LongCLIP, depth via ResNet50, fuses them through multi-head cross-modal attention, and uses a Transformer diffusion decoder conditioned on fused features to generate T-step continuous waypoints, with a GRU maintaining historical context and an auxiliary MLP stop-progression head to address diffusion models' difficulty with stopping decisions. The empirical study systematically isolates the effects of physical controller engagement (training vs. evaluation consistency), cross-embodiment co-training (humanoid + quadruped + wheeled data), and lighting conditions (daytime, nighttime, camera-light) on five representative methods, revealing a 34% SR drop in physical settings, strong sensitivity to camera height, and that RGB-only models degrade far more than RGB+depth models under low light.

## Results

On the R2R dataset with the Unitree H1 humanoid robot in VLN-PE, zero-shot transfer from VLN-CE causes a 34% relative SR drop: NaVid (7B) achieves the best zero-shot SR of 21.58% (val-seen) and 22.42% (val-unseen), while CMA-Full and Seq2Seq-Full drop to 15.50% and 13.83% respectively. Training from scratch on VLN-PE data, the proposed RDP (diffusion-based) achieves SR 23.86% / SPL 17.35% (val-seen) and SR 21.98% / SPL 16.44% (val-unseen), outperforming CMA (21.12%) and Seq2Seq (19.60%) trained under the same conditions. Fine-tuning CMA with VLN-PE data (CMA+) reaches SR 28.72% / SPL 24.24% (val-seen), surpassing NaVid's zero-shot performance. The train-free VLMaps LLM approach achieves SR 20.00% / SPL 12.70% on val-unseen without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VLN-CE baselines (Seq2Seq and CMA from Krantz et al. 2020), the paper demonstrates that zero-shot transfer to physical VLN-PE causes severe degradation: CMA-Full drops from its VLN-CE SR of ~31% to 15.50%, and Seq2Seq-Full to 13.83%. Training from scratch on VLN-PE-collected data recovers performance (CMA: 21.12%, Seq2Seq: 19.60%), and the proposed RDP further improves to 23.86% SR (val-seen), establishing diffusion-based dense waypoint prediction as a stronger baseline than discrete action classification. Against NaVid (Zhang et al. 2024), the 7B MLLM achieves the best zero-shot transfer (22.42% SR val-unseen) and notably the lowest Fall Rate (8.61%) and Stuck Rate (0.45%), but CMA+ fine-tuned on VLN-PE data surpasses it on val-seen (28.72% vs. 21.58% SR) and val-unseen (23.31% vs. 22.42% SR). Against VLMaps (Huang et al. 2023), the train-free map-based LLM achieves 20.00% SR on val-unseen, competitive with trained small models but trailing CMA+ (23.31%) and RDP (21.98%), while maintaining zero stuck rate. Cross-embodiment co-training (humanoid + quadruped + wheeled) yields the best overall result: CMA reaches SR 26.87% / SPL 23.54% on val-unseen, demonstrating that multi-robot data enables a single model to generalize across embodiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLN-CE line of work (Krantz et al., ECCV 2020) by replacing idealized Habitat-based pseudo-motion with physically realistic locomotion via RL-based controllers on GRUTopia, supporting humanoid, quadruped, and wheeled embodiments. It introduces RDP as the first diffusion-policy-based VLN method, adapting the action-diffusion framework from manipulation (Chi et al., IJRR 2023) to continuous navigation with cross-modal attention and recurrent memory. The map-based evaluation leverages VLMaps (Huang et al., ICRA 2023) augmented with VLFM frontier exploration, positioning train-free LLM navigation as a complementary pipeline to end-to-end action prediction. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior VLN benchmarks that assume point-based or smooth wheeled agents by explicitly modeling falls, collisions, stuck states, and embodiment-specific camera heights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic revelation that existing VLN-CE models suffer a ~34% SR drop under physical deployment, and that camera height alone can cause near-total failure on quadrupeds, exposes a critical blind spot in current evaluation protocols. The finding that a 36M-parameter CMA fine-tuned on 441 in-domain episodes outperforms the 7B NaVid zero-shot challenges the prevailing assumption that MLLM scale guarantees VLN robustness, while NaVid's complete failure on 3DGS-rendered scenes (5.81 SR) highlights fragility to perceptual noise. Open questions include how to build truly embodiment-invariant representations, whether diffusion-based dense waypoint prediction can scale to long-horizon VLN, and how to integrate multi-modal sensing (depth, radar) to mitigate lighting sensitivity. VLN-PE as a platform could become a standard for evaluating physical VLN deployment, but its current scope (R2R, limited scene diversity, no stair navigation) constrains its immediate applicability.
