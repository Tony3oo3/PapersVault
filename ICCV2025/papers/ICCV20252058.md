## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from sensitivity to Gaussian initialization, limited spatial awareness, and weak inter-Gaussian correlations that degrade rendering quality. While prior work has partially leveraged NeRF properties (e.g., initialization or attribute embedding), these approaches treat NeRF and 3DGS as independent paradigms and fail to systematically address the structural differences between the two representations, leaving their complementary potential underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252058/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and an SfM point cloud, but replaces uniform SfM-based Gaussian initialization with edge-weighted ray sampling (8:2 ratio of edge rays to random rays) to place initial Gaussians at scene contours. At inference, only the GS branch is retained, producing a standard 3D Gaussian splatting scene renderable in real-time (122–134 FPS) with smaller storage than vanilla 3DGS, while the NeRF branch and shared hash grid are discarded.

## Method

NeRF-GS integrates a Hash-based NeRF network into 3DGS training through three mechanisms: (1) a sharing mechanism where the pre-trained NeRF hash grid provides continuous spatial features to the GS branch, which decodes Gaussian attributes via a tight MLP; (2) residual vectors (Δf for features, Δp for positions) that explicitly model the representational gap between NeRF and GS, allowing each Gaussian to personalize shared information; (3) joint optimization via GS-Rays—rays from the camera through high-opacity Gaussian centers—on which NeRF renders and is aligned with GS outputs through opacity and RGB losses, while NeRF's continuous opacity field drives adaptive Gaussian growth in under-represented regions. The overall loss combines single-branch reconstruction (L1+SSIM for GS, L1+entropy for NeRF), cross-branch alignment, and L2 regularization on residuals, with the GS branch remaining independently deployable after training.

## Results

On real-world datasets, NeRF-GS achieves PSNR of 30.70 (DeepBlending), 28.32 (Mip-NeRF360), and 24.44 (Tanks&Temples), surpassing vanilla 3DGS by 1.28, 0.83, and 0.75 dB respectively. Under sparse-view conditions (12 views on Blender), NeRF-GS reaches 26.34 PSNR, outperforming the best baseline SplatFields (25.80) by 0.54 dB. In terms of efficiency, NeRF-GS renders at 122–134 FPS with smaller model sizes (526–564 MB) than 3DGS (672–729 MB), while requiring ~50–60 min training time versus 36–42 min for 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRF-GS outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the primary baseline 3DGS [27], it gains +1.28 dB PSNR on DeepBlending, +0.83 dB on Mip-NeRF360, and +0.75 dB on Tanks&Temples, while also improving SSIM and LPIPS consistently. Among NeRF-augmented GS variants, VDGS [36] and Hash-GS [8] are the closest competitors (within ~0.7–1.2 dB), but NeRF-GS still leads by a clear margin, demonstrating that joint dual-branch optimization is more effective than simply transferring NeRF features. Scaffold-GS [35] and C3DGS [44] trail by 0.5–1.5 dB, confirming that structural GS improvements alone are insufficient. In the sparse-view setting (Table 2), NeRF-GS surpasses SplatFields [40]—a method specifically designed for sparse views—by 0.54 dB at 12 views and 0.06 dB at 8 views, while beating 2DGS [24] by 0.72 dB and Mip3DGS [65] by 1.48 dB at 12 views. The INGP [42] and SparseNeRF [57] baselines, being pure NeRF methods, are outperformed by 4–11 dB, highlighting the rendering speed and quality advantage of the GS branch.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRF-GS builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al.) and Instant-NGP-style hash encoding (Müller et al.), integrating a pre-trained NeRF branch into the 3DGS training loop. It departs from prior NeRF-to-3DGS transfer methods (RadSplat, VDGS, Hash-GS, SplatFields, Scaffold-GS) that incorporate isolated NeRF features or initialization strategies into independently optimized 3DGS variants. The key conceptual departure is treating the two representations as jointly optimizable partners with explicit residual vectors modeling their formal differences, rather than as a one-way feature donor relationship.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that NeRF's continuous spatial awareness and 3DGS's discrete rendering efficiency are complementary, offering a practical path to improve 3DGS quality without sacrificing real-time inference (the GS branch is separable post-training). The mutual regularization effect is particularly notable for sparse-view settings, where NeRF-GS matches or exceeds SplatFields without being purpose-built for that regime. However, the added complexity of dual-branch training and the increased training time (51.7 min vs. 36.1 min for 3DGS on DeepBlending) raise open questions about whether a more compact integration could achieve similar gains, and whether the framework extends naturally to dynamic or 4D scenes.
