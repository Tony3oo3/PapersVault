## Problem

Rectified flow models (RFMs) are state-of-the-art in generative modeling but lack efficient controlled generation capabilities for tasks like inverse problems and image editing. Existing approaches rely on computationally expensive backpropagation through ODE solvers or inversion strategies, requiring minutes per image and large memory footprints that prevent scaling to billion-parameter models like Flux. The paper asks whether the straight-trajectory and smooth-vector-field properties inherent to RFMs can enable gradient-free, inversion-free steering of the denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method starts from arbitrary random Gaussian noise (no inversion of a reference image is needed) and, depending on the task, also consumes a degraded observation (for inverse problems), a text prompt with a mask (for editing), or a classifier (for guidance). It operates in both pixel space (64×64) and latent space via a VAE (512×512 for SDv1.5/InstaFlow, 1024×1024 for Flux), producing a restored, edited, or guided RGB image. For latent-space inverse problems, the cost function is computed in latent space for inpainting but in pixel space for super-resolution and deblurring to avoid VAE nonlinearity.

## Method

FlowChef exploits the straight-line trajectories and smooth Jacobian of rectified flow models to derive a gradient relationship (Lemma 4.2) showing that ∇_{x_t} L ≈ (I + t·J_{u_θ})^T ∇_{x̂_0} L, which reduces to a simple scaling as t→0. This yields the core update rule (Theorem 4.3): at each timestep, estimate x̂_0 = x_t + t·v, compute the loss gradient ∇_{x̂_0} L directly (no ODE-solver backpropagation), apply it to x_t, then advance with x_{t−Δt} = x_t + Δt·v. The entire procedure is training-free, inversion-free, and requires only a forward pass of the pretrained flow model plus a single loss gradient per step. This design scales to billion-parameter models (Flux) within 30 NFEs, achieving 7× latency reduction and 2× performance improvement over prior diffusion-based SOTA on inverse problems.

## Results

On pixel-space inverse problems (64×64, CelebA/AFHQ-Cat), FlowChef achieves PSNR 26.32/27.69/26.00 for box inpainting, deblurring, and super-resolution respectively, outperforming the best baseline DPS (23.61/22.49/23.94) by 2.7–5.2 dB. FlowChef is also the fastest method at 4.31 s per image with only 0.43 GB VRAM, compared to 80.42 s and 6.44 GB for D-Flow. On latent-space models (512×512), FlowChef (Rectified Diffusion) reaches PSNR 25.96 on inpainting in 18 s, while PSLD requires ~3 min per image. For image editing on PIE-Bench, FlowChef-Edit (Flux, HA) achieves the best CLIP Edited score (22.96) and lowest LPIPS (0.0845) among all flow-based and inversion-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowChef decisively outperforms all pixel-space baselines across every task and metric: it beats DPS (the strongest gradient-based baseline) by 2.7 dB PSNR on inpainting and 5.2 dB on deblurring, while also being 3× faster (4.31 s vs. 12.8 s) and using less memory (0.43 vs. 1.16 GB). Among flow-based methods, FlowChef surpasses PnP-Flow by 4.2 dB PSNR on inpainting and 5.7 dB on deblurring, and outperforms D-Flow by 5.95 dB on inpainting PSNR while being 18× faster (4.31 s vs. 80.42 s). OT-ODE and FreeDoM (diffusion-based methods adapted to RFMs) trail FlowChef by 7.2 and 5.45 dB respectively on inpainting PSNR. On latent-space models, FlowChef (Rectified Diffusion) leads all flow-based baselines (D-Flow, RectifID, InstaFlow) but remains below the pure diffusion baselines PSLD and Resample, which require 3–5 min per image versus FlowChef's 18 s. For image editing on PIE-Bench, FlowChef-Edit (Flux, HA) achieves the best CLIP Edited score (22.96) and lowest LPIPS (0.0845) among all methods, outperforming RF-Inversion (22.55, 0.1888) and the concurrent FlowEdit (22.67, 0.1154), while also being inversion-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowChef builds directly on the straight-trajectory and smooth-vector-field properties of rectified flow models (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), but departs sharply from prior controlled-generation methods that require backpropagation through ODE solvers (DPS, D-Flow) or expensive inversion (P2P, NT-P2P, RF-Inversion). The key conceptual departure is showing that the straightness of RFM trajectories makes the gradient at the intermediate state x_t an affine function of the gradient at the denoised estimate x̂_0, eliminating the need for ODE-solver backpropagation entirely. This unifies inverse problems, image editing, and classifier guidance under a single gradient-free steering framework, whereas prior work treated these as separate problems requiring distinct pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowChef's ability to scale to billion-parameter models like Flux for both inverse problems and inversion-free editing within 30 NFEs is a practical breakthrough, since no prior flow-based method could handle such models due to memory and compute constraints. The 7× latency reduction over prior SOTA on inverse problems (18 seconds vs. 1–3 minutes) makes controlled generation feasible in interactive settings. Limitations remain: a performance gap persists versus pure diffusion baselines (PSLD, Resample) in latent space, and Flux experiments show slight color degradation from the non-linear vector field. Open questions include whether the Jacobian-constancy assumption degrades for more complex or multi-step flow architectures, and whether the approach extends cleanly to video generation or 3D tasks at scale.
