## Problem

Existing semi-dense feature matching pipelines (LoFTR, ELoFTR) perform a global search across the entire 1/8-scale feature map to establish coarse matches, making the matching stage a dominant runtime bottleneck at higher resolutions. Simply removing the dual-softmax operator to save computation, as ELoFTR does, causes a notable accuracy drop because it discards global confidence information. The paper addresses this by proposing a cascaded matching pipeline that defers primary operations to a coarser scale while preserving matching reliability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of grayscale images resized so the longer side matches the target resolution (e.g., 1152 px for high-resolution evaluation) and produces one-to-one feature correspondences at 1/8 scale refined to sub-pixel accuracy via a two-stage homography module. Internally, the pipeline operates across five scales (1/2 through 1/32), with the matching stage split between 1/16 (one-to-many priors) and 1/8 (one-to-one assignment). The final output is a set of matched point pairs with confidence scores, filtered by a threshold and mutual-nearest-neighbor criterion before refinement.

## Method

CasP decomposes the matching stage into two progressive phases: first, top-k one-to-many correspondence priors are selected at the 1/16 scale from a score matrix; second, one-to-one matches at 1/8 scale are determined by restricting the search to tokens within those prior areas. A Region-Based Selective Cross-Attention (RSCA) mechanism bridges the two phases, allowing each 1/8-scale token to attend only to its k prior candidates in the cross-view, enhancing feature discriminability at relevant positions. During inference, a partial softmax is applied only over prior tokens rather than the full feature map, replacing the expensive dual-softmax while retaining global confidence. The feature backbone uses a lightweight RepVGG (reduced channels) for low-level cues and a context-cluster (self-CoC) mechanism for high-level global features, with a hybrid interaction module combining aggregated attention at 1/32 and cross-CoC at 1/16. A training-inference decoupling strategy applies the full dual-softmax during training for supervision but omits it at inference, and a two-stage rigid-homography refinement module achieves sub-pixel accuracy from the coarse matches.

## Results

On MD-1500 (outdoor), Ours-full achieves AUC@20° of 83.9, comparable to AffineFormer (84.0) but with 2.4× lower runtime (147.2 ms vs ≥347.6 ms). On SN-1500 (indoor, cross-domain), Ours-full reaches AUC@20° of 58.7, outperforming ELoFTR (53.6) by 5.1 points and LoFTR (50.6) by 8.1 points. The lite model achieves a ~2.2× speedup over ELoFTR and ~3.2× over LoFTR at resolution 1152 (108.1 ms vs 238.3 ms vs 347.6 ms on MD-1500) while retaining competitive accuracy (AUC@20° = 83.3 on MD-1500, 57.0 on SN-1500). On HPatches homography estimation, Ours-full achieves AUC@3px of 71.8, surpassing ELoFTR (66.5) and approaching the dense method DKM (71.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest semi-dense baseline ELoFTR, Ours-full wins on SN-1500 AUC@20° by 5.1 points (58.7 vs 53.6) and on MD-1500 AUC@5° by 0.7 points (57.1 vs 56.4), while running 1.6× faster (147.2 vs 238.3 ms); the lite model extends the speed advantage to ~2.2×. Against AffineFormer (the most accurate semi-dense method), Ours-full trades 0.1 AUC@20° on MD-1500 (83.9 vs 84.0) for a 2.4× runtime reduction and a 0.7-point gain on SN-1500 (58.7 vs 58.0). Compared to LoFTR, both Ours variants improve AUC@20° by 2.7–3.1 points on MD-1500 and 6.4–8.1 points on SN-1500, with 2.2–3.2× speedup. Against sparse methods (SP+SG, SP+LG), Ours-full leads by 3.3–3.8 AUC@20° on MD-1500 and 7.5–9.2 points on SN-1500, though sparse methods have lower absolute runtime due to fewer keypoints. On HPatches homography estimation, Ours-full (71.8 AUC@3px) surpasses ELoFTR (66.5) and LoFTR (65.9) by over 5 points, matching the dense DKM (71.3). For visual localization on Aachen Day-Night, Ours-full is competitive with ELoFTR (89.2 vs 89.6 at 0.25m/2° day) while offering 2–3× faster matching, and on InLoc DUC2 it matches ELoFTR at 0.5m/5° (80.2 vs 80.9) with a notable gap at 0.25m/2° (55.0 vs 58.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

CasP extends the semi-dense matching lineage initiated by LoFTR and refined by ELoFTR, but shifts the focus from improving feature extraction or interaction modules to redesigning the matching pipeline itself. It adopts ELoFTR's RepVGG backbone and aggregated attention, and borrows EcoMatcher's context-cluster mechanism for high-level feature extraction, but introduces a fundamentally different matching strategy: a two-phase cascaded process (one-to-many priors at 1/16 scale, then one-to-one matching at 1/8 scale) with a novel region-based selective cross-attention (RSCA) bridge. The key conceptual departure from prior semi-dense methods is replacing the single global search with a prior-guided restricted search, and from ELoFTR specifically, replacing the removal of the dual-softmax operator with a partial-softmax that preserves global confidence while reducing computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies the matching stage as the primary efficiency bottleneck in semi-dense pipelines at high resolution, a diagnosis that prior work (focused on feature extraction or attention efficiency) had not addressed directly. The ~2.2× speedup over ELoFTR at 1152 resolution while maintaining or improving accuracy makes CasP practically relevant for latency-sensitive applications like SLAM and UAV systems. The cross-domain generalization gains (notably on SN-1500 and ETH3D[I]) suggest the cascaded structure provides a form of implicit regularization that benefits out-of-domain robustness, though the paper does not deeply analyze why. Open questions include whether the 1/16→1/8 decomposition is optimal, how the approach scales to even higher resolutions or video sequences, and whether the training-inference decoupling strategy transfers to other matching architectures.
