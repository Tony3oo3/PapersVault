## Problem

Existing image fusion methods are largely restricted to directly placing a foreground object into a background scene, failing to handle interactive scenarios such as holding, wearing, logo printing, or style transfer. Moreover, the lack of high-quality multi-scene fusion datasets and the trade-off between background consistency and foreground harmony remain unresolved challenges for realistic, adaptive fusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a foreground image with its mask, a background image, and an optional text prompt as input; both images are scaled to a 512-pixel short side. The foreground's target position and size in the background are specified by a centroid and bounding box derived from the fused mask. The output is a harmonious fused image at the same resolution, and when a text prompt is provided, the model can additionally edit attributes of the foreground object (e.g., changing a car's color to gold) within the fused scene.

## Method

DreamFuse builds on the Flux DiT architecture and introduces three key mechanisms. First, a shared attention layer concatenates the key/value components of the foreground, background, and noisy fused image so that all three interact through a single attention computation, establishing identity consistency. Second, a positional affine transformation maps the foreground's 2D position indices to the target region in the background via an inverse affine matrix, injecting position and scale information without physically resizing or compressing the foreground. Third, Localized Direct Preference Optimization (LDPO) applies the DPO loss only within a dilated bounding-box region around the foreground while using a self-consistency term outside it, preserving background consistency while improving foreground harmony. Training is two-stage: flow-matching supervised learning on 80k curated samples, followed by LDPO on 15k manually selected pairs. The 80k dataset itself is produced by an iterative human-in-the-loop pipeline that fine-tunes a Flux model with shared attention and LoRA to generate diverse multi-scene fusion samples, filtered by GPT-4o.

## Results

On the DreamFuse test set (500 samples covering placement, wearing, logo printing, handheld, and style transfer), DreamFuse achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP 35.52, AES 6.10, ImageReward 1.19, and VisionReward 5.56. On the out-of-domain TF-ICON benchmark (332 samples across photorealism, sketch, oil painting, and cartoon), DreamFuse also leads with CLIP 31.93, AES 6.55, IR 0.35, and VR 3.27. The most notable margin is in VisionReward, where DreamFuse surpasses the second-best method (Anydoor) by 1.42 points on the DreamFuse test set and by 0.90 points over TF-ICON on the TF-ICON benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamFuse outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings. Against Anydoor (the strongest baseline on the DreamFuse test set), DreamFuse gains +0.48 CLIP, +0.09 AES, +0.18 IR, and +1.42 VR, with the VisionReward gap being the most substantial, reflecting better alignment with human perceptual preferences. Against TF-ICON, the margins are +0.31 CLIP, +0.04 AES, +0.33 IR, and +1.68 VR. MADD is the weakest baseline, scoring negative on VisionReward (−0.08) and trailing DreamFuse by over 5.6 VR points, indicating poor fusion quality. On the out-of-domain TF-ICON benchmark, DreamFuse still leads all methods, with a 0.90 VR advantage over TF-ICON (3.27 vs. 2.37), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution. MimicBrush, a reference-based fusion method, ranks mid-pack and is outperformed by DreamFuse by +1.06 VR on the DreamFuse test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamFuse builds on the Flux DiT architecture (with its RoPE positional encoding) and Flow Matching training objective, extending the shared attention mechanism from DreamLayer to jointly attend over foreground, background, and noisy fused image tokens. It departs from prior image fusion methods (TF-ICON, AnyDoor, MADD, ControlCom, MimicBrush), which are largely restricted to object placement with mask-based blending, by formulating fusion as a generative denoising problem conditioned on both images and a text prompt, and by introducing a positional affine transformation to encode foreground placement without spatially restricting the editable region. The Localized DPO loss extends Diffusion-DPO with a region-specific mask that decouples background consistency from foreground harmony, addressing a trade-off that prior inversion-based or mask-based methods handle poorly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80k multi-scene fusion dataset (covering placement, handheld, wearable, logo printing, and style transfer) fills a notable gap, as prior fusion methods relied on segmentation-plus-inpainting pipelines that introduce artifacts. The positional affine mechanism is a lightweight, general-purpose way to inject spatial placement into DiT-based generators that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object or video fusion, whether the heavy data-generation pipeline (iterative LoRA fine-tuning, GPT-4o filtering, manual curation) can be automated further, and whether the text-driven attribute editing capability (e.g., recoloring a car) can be made more controllable without additional training.
