## Problem

Existing methods for transferring multimodal understanding from MLLMs to T2I models either require massive training resources (e.g., 1600+ GPU hours with large-scale image-text datasets), modify the base generation model's weights (reducing plug-and-play and zero-shot capability), or are restricted to U-Net backbones. There is no efficient, lightweight framework that endows DiT-based T2I models with multimodal comprehension while preserving the teacher model's generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251956/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, X2I accepts any combination of multilingual text (29+ languages), reference images, video clips, audio clips, or multi-image sets as conditioning, and produces a single generated image. Notably, training requires only a 100K English text corpus with no image inputs—the multimodal capability is inherited from the frozen MLLM encoder, while the DiT student is aligned purely through text-conditioned attention distillation during the reverse denoising process. The model operates within the Flux.1 DiT architecture, with the MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL, or MiniCPM-o) replacing the original T5/CLIP text encoders.

## Method

X2I introduces two key mechanisms: (1) an AlignNet that ingests hidden states from all layers of the MLLM and uses a small CNN to learn cross-layer weighted aggregation, mapping the result to the dimensions expected by the DiT's dual text-encoder slots (c and c_p); and (2) attention distillation performed at the self-attention output position (x_A, c_A) within each MM-DiT block, using reverse KL divergence to match the student's attention distributions to the teacher's during the denoising step. This distillation location was chosen because it avoids the confounding effects of AdaLN regression scaling factors (α, β, γ) that corrupt feature distributions at block or FF outputs. The entire student is trained on 100K English text pairs in ~160 GPU hours (8 A100s, 24K steps), with only AlignNet parameters updated. A separate LightControl module (a 19-layer ResNet in parallel with the DiT) is optionally trained to inject fine-grained reference-image conditioning for high-fidelity instruction editing.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, X2I achieves a 99.21% average Performance Ratio (PR) relative to the Flux.1 teacher model across five sub-evaluation sets (Complex, Non-spatial, Color, Texture, Numeracy), with per-set PRs ranging from 99.12% to 99.77%. On DreamBench for image-to-image generation, X2I attains the best DINO score (0.817) among all 13 compared methods, a competitive CLIP-I of 0.826, and a CLIP-T of 0.304. Training converges rapidly, reaching 98.2% of the teacher's performance with only 8K steps out of 24K total, using just 100K text samples and 160 GPU hours on 8 A100s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Flux.1 teacher model on T2I-CompBench++, X2I preserves 99.21% of performance on average, with several individual metrics (e.g., HPSv2 on Complex and Color, BV on Numeracy) actually exceeding the teacher. On DreamBench for IT2I, X2I achieves the highest DINO score (0.817) among all 13 methods, narrowly edging out UNIC-Adapter (0.816) and substantially outperforming OminiControl (0.740), IP-Adapter/Flux.1 (0.768), and KOSMOS-G (0.694). However, X2I trails on CLIP-I (0.826) behind KOSMOS-G (0.847) and UNIC-Adapter (0.841), and on CLIP-T (0.304) behind OminiControl (0.329) and IP-Adapter/Flux.1 (0.322), indicating room for improvement in faithfully following image semantics. For multilingual T2I, the paper compares against PEA-Diffusion and Sana, reporting consistent English performance with only a ~1% gap, though detailed multilingual numbers are deferred to the appendix. The ablation on distillation loss shows RKL (99.70% PR) outperforms KL (99.26%) and JS (99.26%) by ~0.44 percentage points, validating the choice of reverse KL divergence from MiniLLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

X2I positions itself at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) transferring MLLM understanding into diffusion-based T2I models (KOSMOS-G, Easyref, GlueGen, PEA-Diffusion) and (2) knowledge distillation within DiT architectures (SSKD, Efficient-VDiT). It departs from prior alignment methods in two key ways: rather than distilling U-Net block outputs (PEA-Diffusion) or training a VLM+aligner from scratch (KOSMOS-G), it distills specifically at the MM-DiT attention-output position using reverse KL divergence borrowed from MiniLLM, and it replaces the typical single-layer MLP projector with a CNN-based AlignNet that fuses hidden states from all MLLM layers. The LightControl module draws on ControlNeXt's ResNet-based conditioning pathway but is trained jointly with the distillation objective rather than as a separate adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving <1% T2I performance degradation while unlocking multilingual, image, video, and audio conditioning with only 100K text samples and 160 GPU hours (roughly 10× less than PEA-Diffusion) makes multimodal DiT alignment accessible to smaller labs. The plug-and-play design—where only AlignNet parameters change and the DiT backbone remains frozen—means the approach composes with the existing Flux.1 ecosystem (LoRA, ControlNet, IP-Adapter), which the paper validates empirically. Open questions include whether the text-only alignment strategy generalizes to video or 3D generation, whether the attention-output distillation position finding holds for non-MM-DiT architectures, and how to better exploit MLLM reasoning and few-shot capabilities that the current pipeline leaves largely untapped.
