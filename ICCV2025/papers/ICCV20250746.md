## Problem

Flow-based generative models like SiT learn velocity exclusively from the final transformer layer, under-utilizing the rich inter-layer representations and leading to slow convergence and suboptimal generation quality. Existing remedies such as REPA rely on external self-supervised models for feature alignment, overlooking the opportunity to internally rectify velocity misalignment across transformer layers using the intrinsic properties of flow-based models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250746/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes random Gaussian noise in the latent space of a Stable Diffusion VAE encoder, along with a class label (ImageNet) or text prompt (MS-COCO), and a scalar time-step t ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a velocity vector in the same latent dimension, which is integrated via ODE/SDE sampling to produce a clean image (256×256 or 512×512). During training, adjacent transformer branches are conditioned on deliberately different time-steps to enable second-order dynamics, while at inference all layers share a single time-step.

## Method

DeepFlow partitions the DiT transformer into k equal-sized branches, each ending with an auxiliary velocity layer trained under deep supervision to predict the same ground-truth velocity. Between consecutive branches, a lightweight VeRA (Velocity Refiner with Acceleration) block explicitly aligns intermediate velocity features: it first generates an acceleration feature via a small MLP trained with a second-order ODE loss that reconstructs the clean image, then concatenates this with the previous velocity feature and applies time-gap-conditioned AdaLN-Zero modulation, and finally refines the result through cross-space attention between the modulated velocity tokens (as key/value) and the original patchified image tokens (as query). Adjacent branches are conditioned on distinct time-steps during training (with a bounded maximum gap α), enabling the VeRA block to model temporal acceleration. The total training objective combines the deep-supervised velocity loss across all branches with a weighted acceleration loss, requiring no external pretrained models.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256 (XLarge, lognormal sampling, no CFG), DeepFlow-XL/2-3T achieves FID 7.2 at 400 epochs, matching SiT-XL/2's 800-epoch FID of 9.8 while halving training time; with DINOv2 SSL alignment, DeepFlow reaches FID 5.0 at 400 epochs vs. SiT's 5.7 at 800 epochs. On ImageNet-512×512, DeepFlow-XL/2-3T+SSL attains FID 1.96 at 200 epochs, outperforming SiT-XL/2+SSL (FID 2.08) at the same budget. For text-to-image on MS-COCO, DeepFlow-3T with DINOv2 achieves FID 4.18 and GenEval 0.3458, surpassing SiT (FID 4.24, GenEval 0.3166) across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepFlow consistently outperforms SiT [28] across all model sizes and training budgets: at 80 epochs on ImageNet-256 (XLarge), DeepFlow-XL/2-3T reduces FID from 13.8 to 10.3 (no SSL) and from 7.2 to 6.5 (DINOv2), and at 400 epochs it reaches FID 7.2 vs. SiT's 9.8 at 800 epochs—an 8× training efficiency gain. Against REPA/SSL-aligned SiT [52], DeepFlow+SSL achieves FID 1.77 at 400 epochs on ImageNet-256 (with CFG), beating SiT+SSL's 1.80 at 800 epochs, and FID 1.96 at 200 epochs on ImageNet-512 vs. SiT+SSL's 2.08 at 200 epochs. On text-to-image (MS-COCO), DeepFlow-3T surpasses SiT on every metric (FID 4.18 vs. 4.24, GenEval 0.3458 vs. 0.3166 with DINOv2). Against SOTA generators in Table 4, DeepFlow-XL/2-3T (FID 1.97, 400 epochs) beats SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06, 1400 epochs) and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 1400 epochs), and is competitive with masked generative models like MAGVIT-v2 (FID 1.78) and TiTok-S-128 (FID 1.97) while using far fewer epochs. On ImageNet-512, DeepFlow-XL/2-3T (FID 2.22, 400 epochs) outperforms SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.62, 600 epochs) and DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04, 600 epochs), and with SSL alignment (FID 1.96, 200 epochs) it surpasses SiT+SSL (FID 2.08, 200 epochs) and LDM-4-G (FID 3.60). The only comparisons where DeepFlow trails are against the strongest masked/autoregressive SOTA (MAGVIT-v2 FID 1.78, TiTok-S-128 FID 1.97 on ImageNet-256) and SiT+SSL on IS score (284.0 vs. 271.3), though FID and sFID remain superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepFlow extends the SiT/DiT flow-based transformer architecture by introducing deep supervision (borrowed from classification) across partitioned transformer branches, combined with a novel VeRA block that uses a second-order ODE to model acceleration between adjacent branches. It departs from REPA's approach of aligning internal features with external self-supervised models (DINO/MAE) by instead performing purely internal velocity alignment across layers, arguing that the flow-based model's own inter-layer representations contain sufficient signal for self-correction. The method also builds on the MMDiT architecture for text-to-image, integrating the same deep supervision and VeRA mechanism into that setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× training efficiency gain (matching SiT-XL/2 at 800 epochs in just 100 epochs) is a substantial practical win for flow-based model training, and the finding that DeepFlow without any external SSL alignment matches or exceeds SiT with DINOv1 alignment suggests that internal feature alignment can partially obviate the need for expensive pre-trained teacher models. Open questions include whether the second-order ODE acceleration formulation generalizes to video or higher-dimensional generation, whether the time-gap conditioning strategy interacts well with very long training schedules, and whether the approach extends naturally to autoregressive or masked generative settings. The simplicity of the VeRA block (a small MLP plus AdaLN-Zero plus cross-attention) makes it an easy drop-in for existing flow-based pipelines.
