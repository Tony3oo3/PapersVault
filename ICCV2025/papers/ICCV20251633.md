## Problem

CLIP-based class-incremental learning relies on matching visual embeddings to simple class-name templates, which overlooks fine-grained discriminative features (e.g., 'whiskers,' 'tail,' 'fur' for a cat). During sequential updates, these sub-features are progressively overwritten, causing catastrophic forgetting and a growing mismatch between cross-modal representations. The paper asks how to inject external, class-specific knowledge to compensate for lost detail while preventing forgetting across incremental stages.

## Input / Output

At training, the method consumes RGB images (processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP visual encoder) and class-name template text ('a photo of a [CLASS]'), augmented with GPT-4-generated visual feature descriptors and AutoAugment-transformed image views. At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and produces a class label over all seen classes; the final score is the sum of injected-feature matching logits and a post-tuning re-ranking score computed from GPT-4-generated pair-wise discriminative descriptors among the top-k candidate classes. The setting is exemplar-free: no historical instances are stored, only per-class visual prototypes with Gaussian noise.

## Method

ENGINE introduces a dual-branch knowledge injection framework built on frozen CLIP encoders. The textual branch appends a linear injection unit u_t after the text encoder, trained via cosine similarity to pull template-text embeddings toward GPT-4-generated class descriptors; the visual branch appends a linear unit u_i trained to align original image embeddings with AutoAugment-augmented views. For continual learning, a new pair of injection units is initialized per incremental task while all previous units are frozen, and the final representation is the sum across all units, keeping per-unit parameter cost at d×d. Forgetting is mitigated by replaying class prototypes (mean visual embeddings plus Gaussian noise) in the contrastive loss. At inference, a post-tuning stage extracts the top-k predictions, queries GPT-4 for pair-wise discriminative features among those k classes, and re-ranks via zero-shot CLIP matching, adding the resulting local logits to the primary prediction.

## Results

ENGINE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all nine benchmark datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline (RAPF) by 3–10% in last-stage accuracy (A_B). On the most challenging fine-grained dataset Aircraft (B0 Inc10), ENGINE reaches 58.69% A_B versus 23.61% for RAPF and 48.09% for SimpleCIL. On CIFAR-100 (B0 Inc10), ENGINE attains 79.22% A_B and 86.92% average accuracy, surpassing the next-best method (RAPF) by 1.18% and 0.78% respectively. Notably, ENGINE uses zero exemplars yet outperforms exemplar-based methods like iCaRL (20/class) by 14.71% on Aircraft and 11.24% on SUN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENGINE outperforms all ten compared baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the strongest CLIP-based CIL method RAPF [25], ENGINE gains 35.08% on Aircraft (58.69 vs 23.61 A_B), 1.18% on CIFAR-100 (79.22 vs 78.04), and 9.89% on ImageNet-R (80.37 vs 70.48). Visual-prompt methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) are consistently weaker, with CODA-Prompt [53] trailing ENGINE by 31.0% on Aircraft and 17.2% on CUB, reflecting their inability to leverage textual information. The textual-prompt method CoOp [88] suffers severe forgetting (7.14% on Aircraft vs ENGINE's 58.69%), while the training-free SimpleCIL [84] baseline is the closest competitor on easier datasets (e.g., 76.63 vs 79.22 on CIFAR-100) but falls far behind on fine-grained tasks. In the exemplar-based comparison (Table 2), ENGINE with zero exemplars surpasses iCaRL [51] (20/class) by 14.71% on Aircraft and 11.24% on SUN, and outperforms PROOF [86] (20/class) by 5.10% on Aircraft, demonstrating that external knowledge injection compensates for the absence of stored samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENGINE builds directly on CLIP's frozen dual-encoder architecture and the contrastive matching paradigm, extending the line of CLIP-based CIL methods that append lightweight modules (prompts in L2P/DualPrompt/CODA-Prompt, adapters in RAPF/MOE-Adapter). Its key conceptual departure is replacing data-driven prompt learning with externally sourced knowledge: GPT-4-generated discriminative descriptors for the textual branch and data augmentation for the visual branch, encoded through per-task linear injection units rather than shared prompt pools. The prototype-replay mechanism for preventing forgetting of injection units draws on exemplar-based CIL ideas but operates purely on frozen visual embeddings, maintaining the exemplar-free setting. The post-tuning re-ranking step is a novel inference-time calibration that leverages GPT-4 pair-wise discriminative features without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting LLM-generated class knowledge into CLIP's frozen encoders via simple linear units can substantially close the gap between zero-shot CLIP and fine-tuned CIL, achieving 3–10% improvements over strong baselines across nine datasets while remaining exemplar-free. This is practically significant because it suggests that the bottleneck in CLIP-based CIL is not model capacity but the informativeness of the textual matching target, and that external knowledge can compensate for sequential forgetting without storing historical data. Key limitations include the hard dependency on GPT-4's coverage of class concepts (acknowledged by the authors), the fixed ViT-B/16 backbone, and the linear injection units that may not capture complex cross-modal interactions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class spaces (thousands of classes), whether smaller or open-source LLMs can substitute for GPT-4, and whether the injection framework generalizes to other pre-trained vision-language models beyond CLIP.
