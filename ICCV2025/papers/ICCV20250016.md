## Problem

Existing VLM personalization methods require costly training procedures—contrastive fine-tuning with multiple reference images, large-scale instruction tuning, or test-time adaptation—making them expensive and impractical for individual users. This paper asks whether the internal world knowledge already present in pre-trained VLMs can be exploited to perform personalization in a fully training-free manner, eliminating all training overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single reference image per personal concept (along with its name and semantic category) to build the personal database, with no negative samples needed—unlike prior work that demands multiple positive and negative examples. At inference, a query image and a free-form textual prompt are consumed; the system outputs the recognized concept name, a personalized caption, or a VQA answer. The personal database is created once and supports flexible addition, deletion, or modification of concepts without retraining.

## Method

R2P operates in two phases. In the database-creation phase, a pre-trained VLM (MiniCPM-o-2.6) is prompted with each reference image and its category to extract distinctive fingerprint attributes (e.g., fur color, shirt logo) and a discriminative caption; CLIP ViT-L/14-336 encodes both images and captions for retrieval. At inference, multimodal retrieval (averaging cosine similarity between the query image embedding and stored visual and textual embeddings) selects top-K candidates, which are then disambiguated via attribute-focused Chain-of-Thought reasoning where the VLM compares shared fingerprint attributes across candidates. To mitigate hallucination, a cross-modal attribute verification step uses CLIP to check whether the attributes the VLM relied on actually align with the query image; if the attribute-based prediction disagrees with the CoT output, a more exhaustive pairwise multimodal reasoning step compares the query directly against each candidate's reference image and full textual description, using the VLM's Yes/No logits to select the final concept.

## Results

R2P achieves 97.4% weighted recognition accuracy on MyVLM, 94.4% on Yo'LLaVA, and 91.8% on the newly introduced PerVA dataset, while remaining entirely training-free. On captioning, R2P reaches 91.4% recall (MyVLM), 87.1% (Yo'LLaVA), and 72.5% (PerVA), outperforming the next-best method by 5.5% and 8.4% respectively on the latter two. On personalized VQA (Yo'LLaVA), R2P scores 96.5% accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art RAP (93.2%) by 3.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the training-based MyVLM [4], R2P improves weighted recognition by +3.6% on MyVLM (97.4 vs. 93.8) and by a large +29.6% on PerVA (91.8 vs. 62.2), while also achieving 91.4% captioning recall versus MyVLM's near-zero (0.02%). Compared to Yo'LLaVA [29], R2P gains +2.0% weighted accuracy on Yo'LLaVA (94.4 vs. 92.4) and +19.8% on PerVA (91.8 vs. 72.0), with a dramatic captioning recall improvement (87.1% vs. 0.2%). Versus the retrieval-augmented RAP [13], R2P is training-free yet outperforms it on weighted recognition on both Yo'LLaVA (+2.2%) and PerVA (+2.8%), and leads on captioning recall by 7.4% and 8.4% respectively; RAP retains a slight edge on positive accuracy on PerVA (92.9 vs. 90.2). Against the prompting-based MiniCPM-o + prompt [47], R2P wins on weighted accuracy across all three datasets (97.4, 94.4, 91.8 vs. 96.1, 92.4, 82.7) and on captioning recall everywhere. On personalized VQA (Yo'LLaVA), R2P achieves 96.5% accuracy, beating RAP (93.2%), Yo'LLaVA (92.9%), LLaVA + prompt (92.5%), and GPT-4V + Vprompt (86.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM personalization (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, RAP), which assumes that training—whether contrastive fine-tuning, instruction tuning, or large-scale pretraining—is necessary to ground user-specific concepts. Instead, R2P repurposes the retrieval-augmented generation framework of RAP and the attribute-based recognition ideas from zero-shot learning literature, combining them with chain-of-thought reasoning and cross-modal verification to show that a pre-trained VLM's internal world knowledge suffices for personalization without any gradient updates. The 'fingerprint attributes' mechanism is a novel repurposing of machine-generated descriptive attributes (as in [26, 33, 35]) specifically for disambiguating visually similar personal objects, while the cross-modal attribute verification draws on hallucination-mitigation techniques from the VLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that training-free personalization is feasible and competitive with or superior to training-based methods, R2P removes a significant practical barrier (costly fine-tuning per concept, need for multiple reference images and negative samples) that has limited real-world adoption of VLM personalization. The PerVA benchmark is a meaningful contribution, exposing the visual-ambiguity challenge (folded clothes, similar bottles, different poses) that prior datasets with highly distinctive objects failed to capture. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of personal concepts, how it handles dynamic or evolving objects, and whether the single-reference-image assumption generalizes; the authors also note computational overhead from pairwise reasoning as a limitation for deployment.
