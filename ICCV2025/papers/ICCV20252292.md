## Problem

Existing fairness-aware deepfake detection methods rely on predefined demographic attributes (race, gender, age) and corresponding annotations, yet unfairness in deepfake detectors is multifaceted, varies substantially across different architectures, and often manifests through non-demographic factors (e.g., background bright spots) that have not been previously documented. No prior approach can dynamically discover and mitigate arbitrary, detector-specific bias patterns without requiring attribute labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252292/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing deepfake detector (CNN backbones like Xception, ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B3, or ViT-based models like UIA-ViT) by inserting a lightweight Unfairness Regulator into each backbone block. Input is a face image (32 frames sampled per video for evaluation); output is a binary real/fake forgery classification. No additional demographic or attribute annotations are required at training or inference time—only the standard forgery labels. At inference, only the original detector is used; the UR and unfairness discoverer are discarded.

## Method

AdvOU alternates between two modules in an adversarial training loop. The Open-Unfairness Discovery (OUD) module inserts a LoRA-inspired bottleneck (down-projection → BN → ReLU → up-projection → BN → ReLU) into the frozen detector to extract unfairness sub-representations, then amplifies them via Unfairness Amplification Learning: a group-level Equal Opportunity Violation loss separates unfairness-positive/negative samples, while a fine-grained pairwise loss magnifies subtle unfairness by comparing nearest/farthest samples in forgery-feature space. The Unfairness Adversarial Mitigation (UAM) module then counteracts these biases at two levels: Global Relation Debiasing rotates unfairness-closest samples outward around a random pivot to disrupt biased relational structure, and Local Adversarial Learning perturbs the mean and standard deviation of shallow features in the gradient direction that reduces unfairness classification, followed by a KL-divergence consistency constraint forcing the detector to produce identical predictions on original and perturbed features. Training alternates: the detector is frozen while UR is optimized via OUD, then the detector is unfrozen and optimized via UAM, repeating over 50 epochs after a 10-epoch warmup.

## Results

On the video-level cross-dataset generalization benchmark (trained on FF++ c23, tested on unseen datasets), AdvOU with a ViT backbone achieves 93.9% AUC on CDF, 88.6% on DFDCP, 79.1% on DFDC, and 95.4% on DFD, outperforming the strongest single-ViT baseline UDD (which uses three ViT networks) by 0.8% on CDF and 0.5% on DFDCP. In the open-unfairness fairness evaluation (Table 1), AdvOU reduces F_FPR by at least 14.57% compared to the original detectors across three unseen datasets, and reduces average F_FPR by 12.68% and F_MEO by 8.5% over UCF and LSDA with the Xception backbone. On demographic fairness, AdvOU achieves F_FPR of 18.81% on DFDC with EfficientNet-B3 versus 22.89% for FG-DD, while also improving detection AUC from 60.67% to 65.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UDD [9], the strongest multi-ViT baseline, AdvOU wins on CDF (93.9 vs 93.1) and DFDCP (88.6 vs 88.1) but trails slightly on DFDC (79.1 vs 81.2) and DFD (95.4 vs 95.5), which the authors attribute to using a single ViT with lightweight LoRA adapters rather than three full ViT networks. Compared to TALL++ [34] (single ViT), AdvOU gains 1.9% on CDF and 0.6% on DFDC. Against UIA-ViT [43], AdvOU improves CDF by 11.5% and DFDCP by 12.8%, though UIA-ViT retains a marginal edge on DFD (94.7 vs 95.4 is actually a win for AdvOU). In the fairness evaluation (Table 1), AdvOU substantially outperforms FG-DD [20] on demographic fairness (e.g., F_FPR 18.81 vs 22.89 on DFDC with EfficientNet-B3) without requiring attribute annotations, and dominates UCF [36] and LSDA [38] on open-unfairness metrics, reducing F_FPR by 12.68% on average with Xception. The transferability study (Table 4) confirms that integrating AdvOU boosts F3Net [23] by 5.19% and UIA-ViT [43] by 3.09% in average AUC across four datasets, demonstrating plug-and-play effectiveness across CNN, frequency-based, and inconsistency-based detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alternate debiasing paradigm from Li et al. (ECCV 2022), which identified and reweighted unknown biased groups in general classification, to the specific and more challenging setting of deepfake detection where forgery artifacts are subtle. Architecturally, the Unfairness Regulator borrows the low-rank bottleneck design from LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022), while the Local Adversarial Learning module adapts the mean/std perturbation strategy from StyleAdv (Fu et al., CVPR 2023). The key departure from prior deepfake fairness work (DAW-FDD, FG-DD) is the rejection of predefined demographic attributes in favor of an adversarial discovery mechanism that uncovers arbitrary, detector-specific bias patterns—what the authors term 'open-unfairness.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As deepfake detectors are increasingly deployed in legal and forensic contexts, the finding that the most impactful biases can be non-demographic (e.g., background bright spots for inconsistency-based detectors) challenges the assumption that fairness auditing limited to race and gender is sufficient. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can retrofit existing detectors without retraining from scratch, lowering the barrier to fairer deployment. Open questions include whether the adversarial discovery generalizes to video-level temporal biases, whether the method remains effective when the underlying detector is itself adversarially trained, and how the approach scales to multi-modal deepfake detection (audio-visual).
