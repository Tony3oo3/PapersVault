## Problem

Existing multimodal place recognition methods require the query and database to share the same modalities (e.g., Image+PointCloud → Image+PointCloud), necessitating a comprehensive multimodal retrieval database that is impractical for UAV geo-localization with its broad retrieval range. This paper introduces a more practical compositional retrieval paradigm where multimodal data (point cloud, depth, text) augments only the drone-view query while the satellite-view database remains unimodal (image-only), avoiding the need for an extensive multimodal database.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a drone-view RGB image (resized to 384×384), a point cloud (4096 colorless points), a dense relative depth map (384×384, derived by top-down projection of the point cloud followed by K×K weighted convolution), and a GPT-4o-generated text description (padded/truncated to 77 tokens) as the compositional query, and retrieves the best-matching satellite-view image tile from a geotagged reference database. The satellite-view reference is image-only; its missing modalities are filled with learnable [SUB] tokens of configurable length (default 500). Output is a ranked list of satellite-view tiles with similarity scores, from which top-K retrieval and spatial distance metrics are computed.

## Method

MMGEO employs a unified architecture with modality-specific pretrained encoders (ViT-Base+RoPE from Game4Loc for image, Uni3D/EVA02-Base for point cloud, ViT-Base for depth, CLIP-ViT-Base for text) connected through a cross-attention compositor where image tokens serve as Query and auxiliary modality tokens as Key/Value. To preserve pretrained representations under limited training data, adapter-based fine-tuning (serial + parallel adapters per transformer block, following SelaVPR) is applied to all non-image encoders while the image encoder is fully trainable. The key architectural insight is the introduction of learnable [SUB] substitution tokens on the satellite-view side to replace missing modalities, enabling bi-directional symmetric contrastive learning (I+M ↔ I+[SUB]) in a shared compositional representation space. Training uses a weighted-InfoNCE loss (extended from Game4Loc) where the positive weight α is a sigmoid function of the IoU between query and reference regions, allowing partial matches to contribute proportionally to the loss.

## Results

On GTA-UAV-MM (cross-area), MMGEO with image+text (I+T→I) achieves the best R@1 of 55.34% and AP of 66.11%, outperforming the strongest unimodal baseline Game4Loc (52.03% R@1, 64.39% AP) by 3.31% in R@1. The image+depth variant (I+D→I) attains the best SDM@3 of 79.39% and lowest Dis@1 of 297.45 m. On the real-world UAV-VisLoc-MM dataset (cross-area), MMGEO I+T→I reaches 53.47% R@1 and 62.54% AP, improving over Game4Loc (47.56% R@1, 56.99% AP) by 5.91% in R@1, while also reducing Dis@1 from 1038.59 m to 821.33 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMGEO outperforms all six unimodal baselines across every metric on both datasets. The closest competitor is Game4Loc [20], the best vision-only method, which MMGEO surpasses by 2.72–3.31% in R@1 and 1.69–1.72% in AP on GTA-UAV-MM cross-area, and by 5.22–5.91% in R@1 on UAV-VisLoc-MM cross-area. Sample4Geo [10] (45.72% R@1) and TransGeo [55] (36.71% R@1) trail Game4Loc by 6–15% in R@1, while SALAD [19] (29.12%) and AnyLoc [21] (22.63%, zero-shot) are substantially weaker. SelaVPR [32] performs poorly in the cross-area setting (18.26% R@1 on GTA-UAV-MM, 3.82% on UAV-VisLoc-MM), indicating limited generalization to unseen areas. Notably, MMGEO also demonstrates a 'free lunch' effect: even when tested with image-only input (replacing missing modalities with [SUB] tokens), the multimodally-trained model still exceeds the unimodal Game4Loc baseline, suggesting that multimodal training improves the underlying image representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UAV cross-view geo-localization line (Game4Loc, Sample4Geo, DenseUAV) by introducing a multimodal compositional retrieval formulation where the drone-view query is multimodal (image + point cloud/depth/text) while the satellite-view database remains unimodal image. It departs from prior multimodal place recognition (CrossLoc, LCPR, AdaFusion) that requires matching multimodal-to-multimodal databases, and from cross-modal retrieval (GeoText-1652, CVG-Text) that struggles with fine-grained localization. The architectural design draws on Uni3D for point cloud encoding, CLIP for text encoding, and adapter-based fine-tuning (following SelaVPR) to preserve pretrained representations under limited training data, while the [SUB] learnable token mechanism for maintaining symmetric contrastive learning under modality asymmetry is a novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional retrieval formulation is practically significant because it avoids the need to build a comprehensive multimodal satellite database, making multimodal geo-localization feasible with existing image-based reference maps. The 'free lunch' finding—that multimodal training improves image-only retrieval performance at test time—suggests a pathway to enhance single-sensor systems without requiring multimodal hardware at deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that the method remains fundamentally vision-based, the datasets are limited in scale, and the per-modality parameter overhead (each encoder comparable in size to the visual model) makes the comparison to unimodal baselines not entirely fair. How to more efficiently leverage multimodal information in compositional retrieval, and whether these gains hold at larger scale, remain open questions.
