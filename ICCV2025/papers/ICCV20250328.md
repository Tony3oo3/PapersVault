## Problem

Despite the rapid adoption of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), no prior work has systematically evaluated their robustness when one or more input modalities are absent, nor has any retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach been applied to compensate for missing modalities in MLLMs. This paper establishes the first benchmark for MLLM robustness under missing modality conditions and proposes a solution that avoids the computationally prohibitive fine-tuning of billion-parameter LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system receives audio clips, video clips, and/or text as input, with one or more modalities potentially absent (simulated at 100% missing rate). When a modality is missing, the framework queries a pre-built prototype pool (constructed from the training set of each dataset) using ImageBind contrastive embeddings of the available modalities, retrieves top-k substitute samples, and injects their modality tokens into the LLM input. The output is a text answer (QA), a text caption, or a sentiment label, depending on the downstream task. A key design detail is that text has no separate modality tokens (being the LLM's native modality), so multiple retrieved texts are concatenated rather than averaged.

## Method

MissRAG is a zero-shot multimodal RAG framework that substitutes missing modalities with retrieved prototypes from a contrastive embedding space built by ImageBind. For each missing modality, the system computes dot-product similarity between the embeddings of available modalities (queries) and stored prototype embeddings (keys), selects the top-k most similar prototypes, and averages their modality tokens to reconstruct the missing input. This retrieval mechanism is the first to operate concurrently across three modalities (audio, video, text), handling all possible combinations of single or multiple missing inputs. The framework is augmented with a tailored prompt engineering strategy that appends to the system message (i) a modality status descriptor (Present/Prototype/Missing for each input) and (ii) a Compensation Strategy for Missing Modalities (CSMM) prompt that instructs the model to infer the likely context of absent modalities from available ones. Both components work at independent levels—MissRAG improves input quality while PE conditions the model's generation behavior—without requiring any parameter updates to the underlying MLLM.

## Results

MissRAG+PE improves ChatBridge in 22/23 experiments, OneLLM in 21/23, and VideoLLaMA 2 in 17/23 across five datasets. The largest gains appear in the most challenging missing-modality scenarios: OneLLM achieves +30.03 accuracy on MOSI (Missing Audio) and +28.36 on MOSEI (Missing Audio), while ChatBridge gains +15.93 on MUSIC-AVQA (Missing Video) and OneLLM gains +13.23 CIDEr on VALOR-32K (Missing Video). On the Complete scenario, improvements are modest (e.g., +0.03 to +1.47), confirming that the method primarily targets the missing-modality gap rather than boosting already-complete inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ChatBridge, MissRAG+PE wins in 22 of 23 settings, with the most dramatic improvement on MUSIC-AVQA Missing Video (27.25 → 43.18, +15.93) and MOSEI Missing Video (21.76 → 40.95, +19.19). Against OneLLM, the method wins in 21 of 23 settings, delivering the paper's largest absolute gains on sentiment analysis: MOSI Missing Audio jumps from 48.54 to 78.57 (+30.03) and MOSEI Missing Audio from 30.82 to 59.18 (+28.36), while VALOR-32K Missing Video CIDEr rises from 3.34 to 16.57 (+13.23). Against VideoLLaMA 2, MissRAG+PE wins in 17 of 23 settings; it consistently improves audio-visual tasks (e.g., VALOR-32K MV: 7.02 → 10.30) but underperforms the baseline on several MOSI/MOSEI sentiment scenarios (e.g., MOSI MA: 46.06 → 33.38, −12.68; MOSI MAV: 74.34 → 40.52, −33.82), which the authors attribute to VideoLLaMA 2's text-only input being its optimal operating mode rather than a flaw in the retrieval mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the missing modality problem, previously studied almost exclusively in encoder-only architectures (SMIL, tag-encoding, multimodal Transformers), to the MLLM setting where fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive. It repurposes the RAG paradigm—previously applied to text-only or image-text knowledge augmentation (Lewis et al., VisualRAG)—as a mechanism for substituting absent modalities, using ImageBind's shared embedding space as the retrieval backbone. The prompt engineering component builds on zero-shot instruction tuning and chain-of-thought prompting but is specifically tailored to inform the model of modality status and induce compensatory reasoning, a use case not previously explored in the MLLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of MLLM robustness under missing modality conditions, providing a much-needed evaluation protocol for real-world deployments where sensor failures, privacy constraints, or transmission issues cause modality dropout. The parameter-free nature of MissRAG+PE makes it immediately deployable without the compute cost of fine-tuning billion-parameter models. However, the sentiment analysis results reveal that current MLLMs still struggle with genuine cross-modal reasoning (VideoLLaMA 2 performs best with text-only input), suggesting that retrieval-based compensation cannot fully substitute for the model's own multimodal understanding. Open questions include whether the prototype-pool approach scales to open-world settings without a curated training set, and whether the method generalizes beyond the three modalities tested here.
