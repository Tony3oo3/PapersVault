## Problem

Existing feed-forward human Gaussian methods predict Gaussians independently per frame, failing to exploit complementary information across timesteps, and lack alignment with the SMPL mesh, preventing pose-driven animation. Optimization-based alternatives achieve high quality but require hours of per-instance training, making them impractical for real-time or generalizable applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252314/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either four ground-truth views (front, left, back, right) per frame from a multi-view video, or a single view per frame where a fine-tuned Wonder3D diffusion model synthesizes the remaining views. It outputs a single set of SMPL-aligned 3D Gaussians (temporal-invariant) that can be re-posed via LBS to arbitrary SMPL poses and rendered at over 120 FPS. Inference takes 0.9 s for multi-view input or 9.7 s for single-view input (including 8.8 s of diffusion) on an A800 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is the Human Gaussian Graph (HGG), a dual-layer graph where per-frame Gaussians (predicted by a frozen LGM U-Net) form first-layer nodes and SMPL mesh vertices form second-layer nodes, with edges defined by spatial proximity (Gaussian-to-nearest-vertex) and mesh topology (vertex-to-neighbor). Two complementary operations process the graph: an intra-node operation uses cross-attention to aggregate Gaussian features from all frames connected to the same SMPL vertex (temporal information), and an inter-node operation performs attention-based message passing between topologically adjacent vertices (spatial information). Stacking L=6 blocks of intra+inter operations progressively widens each node's receptive field. The final SMPL-aligned Gaussians are obtained by cross-attending the initial-frame Gaussians against the enriched vertex queries, then trained with MSE+LPIPS on RGB and MSE on alpha rendered to 8 views, optimizing only the HGG modules.

## Results

In the single-view setting, the method achieves 23.11 PSNR and 0.920 SSIM for novel view synthesis, surpassing the best optimization-based baseline ExAvatar (21.45 PSNR) by 1.66 dB and the generalizable LGM (21.27 PSNR) by 1.84 dB. For novel pose animation in single view, it reaches 21.86 PSNR, a 2.53 dB improvement over ExAvatar (19.33 PSNR). In the multi-view setting, the method achieves 26.54 PSNR for novel views and 24.01 PSNR for novel poses, outperforming GPS-Gaussian (19.13 PSNR) by over 7 dB while running in 0.9 s per frame versus 8.5 min for 4DGS and 2.9 h for ExAvatar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GART (CVPR 2024), the method wins on all metrics in single view: +3.0 dB PSNR on novel views (23.11 vs 20.11) and +2.9 dB on novel poses (21.86 vs 18.97), while running ~12× faster (9.7 s vs 1.9 min). Against ExAvatar (ECCV 2024), the method gains +1.66 dB PSNR on novel views and +2.53 dB on novel poses, with a dramatic speed advantage of 9.7 s versus 2.9 hours of per-instance optimization. Against LGM (ECCV 2024, fine-tuned), the method improves novel-view PSNR by 1.84 dB and novel-pose PSNR by 2.30 dB, demonstrating the value of cross-frame aggregation that LGM's single-frame design cannot exploit. In the multi-view setting, the method outperforms GPS-Gaussian (CVPR 2024) by 7.4 dB PSNR on novel views (26.54 vs 19.13) and 4DGS (CVPR 2024) by 7.5 dB, while also being the only method supporting novel pose animation in this setting. The method is the only one that is simultaneously generalizable (feed-forward) and animatable (SMPL-aligned), a combination none of the five baselines achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LGM's feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting backbone (kept frozen) and departs from the per-frame independent prediction paradigm of generalizable Gaussian methods (LGM, GPS-Gaussian, HumanSplat) by introducing a dual-layer Human Gaussian Graph that links Gaussians across all frames to SMPL mesh vertices. It also contrasts with optimization-based animatable Gaussian methods (GART, ExAvatar) that require hours of per-instance training, positioning its contribution as the first feed-forward pipeline producing SMPL-aligned, pose-drivable Gaussians at inference time. The graph formulation—using nearest-vertex assignment for Gaussian-to-mesh edges and mesh topology for vertex-to-vertex edges—is a natural but previously unexploited structure for cross-frame feature aggregation in the 3DGS human reconstruction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing animatable, SMPL-aligned Gaussian avatars in under 10 seconds without per-instance optimization, this work removes a major practical barrier to deploying realistic human avatars in VR, gaming, and social media pipelines. The authors acknowledge a 3.4 dB PSNR gap between single-view and multi-view settings (attributed to the absence of a strong open-source human diffusion model) and a 1.5–2.5 dB gap between novel-view and novel-pose quality (attributed to inaccurate SMPL parameters in the dataset), both of which are open problems. A key open question is whether the graph-based cross-frame aggregation generalizes beyond the MvHumanNet training distribution and whether the SMPL alignment can be refined to close the pose-quality gap.
