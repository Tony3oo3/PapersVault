## Problem

In Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning with ViT backbones, direct fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting and overfitting due to massive parameter shifts, while freezing the backbone creates a growing inconsistency between the static feature extractor and the evolving classifier. Existing parameter-importance methods (e.g., EWC) require gradient computation over all parameters, which is computationally prohibitive for large-scale vision transformers. The paper asks: how can one identify the minimal set of parameters best suited for new knowledge and update them with the least possible perturbation to preserve prior knowledge?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images through a pre-trained ViT-B/16 backbone, where each image is split into N patch tokens plus a CLS token. During knowledge localization, hidden states (MHSA and MLP outputs) from all L encoder layers are extracted and perturbed with Gaussian noise to compute per-layer sensitivity scores. During incremental editing, only 5 samples per new class are used to construct target hidden states. The final output is a class label produced by a Nearest Neighbor Classifier over class prototypes, which remains decoupled from backbone updates. In the keypoint detection extension, the output is 2D keypoint coordinates for hand landmarks.

## Method

Lark operates in two stages. First, Knowledge Localization computes a Fisher Information Matrix per category by injecting Gaussian perturbations into hidden states and measuring the resulting cosine-similarity change in the final CLS token, avoiding expensive full-parameter gradient computation; the Lowest-K criterion then selects the parameter matrices (across MHSA and MLP modules) with minimal sensitivity to previously learned classes. Second, Incremental Editing applies Rank-One matrix updates to only the selected parameters: for MHSA, only W_Value is modified (justified by showing attention distributions remain nearly unchanged after training), and for MLP, the second linear layer is edited. Each token's contribution is expressed as an outer product ν⊗μᵀ/‖μ‖², and the aggregate update is reduced to a single Rank-One matrix via SVD, retaining only the largest singular value. This ensures the parameter perturbation is minimal-norm while still encoding the new class information.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Lark integrated into CLOSER-ViT achieves 86.03% average accuracy with a 7.74% performance drop (PD), while Lark in OrCo-ViT reaches 86.36% Avg with 13.50% PD. On mini-ImageNet, Lark in CLOSER-ViT attains 88.15% Avg and 9.43% PD, and on CUB-200 it reaches 82.00% Avg with 9.77% PD. These results surpass the strongest non-prompt baselines (CLOSER-ViT, OrCo-ViT, LRT, MTE-FSCIL) by 2–5% in average accuracy and reduce PD by 4–10 percentage points across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lark in CLOSER-ViT outperforms the original CLOSER-ViT by +2.84% Avg on CIFAR-100 (86.03 vs 83.19), +4.04% on mini-ImageNet (88.15 vs 84.11), and +3.21% on CUB-200 (82.00 vs 78.79), while reducing PD by 6.36, 6.36, and 4.28 points respectively. Similarly, Lark in OrCo-ViT improves over OrCo-ViT by +4.28% Avg on CIFAR-100 (86.36 vs 82.08) and +5.33% on mini-ImageNet (86.15 vs 80.82). Against the strongest prompt-based baseline CPE-CLIP, Lark in CLOSER-ViT achieves higher Avg on CIFAR-100 (86.03 vs 83.42) and mini-ImageNet (88.15 vs 86.13), though CPE-CLIP retains a slightly lower PD on CIFAR-100 (7.31 vs 7.74) due to its reliance on CLIP's auxiliary semantic information. Lark also decisively surpasses LRT (TPAMI'24) by 14.83% Avg on CIFAR-100 and 12.21% on mini-ImageNet, and outperforms MTE-FSCIL, WaRP, SV-T, CEC, and LIMIT across all three datasets in both Avg and PD.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lark synthesizes two distinct research threads: the Fisher Information Matrix framework from EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) for measuring parameter importance, and the rank-one matrix editing paradigm from ROME/MEMIT (Meng et al., 2022/2023) originally developed for NLP model editing. It departs from EWC by replacing per-parameter gradient computation with hidden-state-based sensitivity estimation to handle ViT-scale models, and departs from ROME/MEMIT by extending rank-one updates from single-fact editing to multi-class incremental learning in vision. The method is positioned as a plug-in for existing FSCIL frameworks (OrCo, CLOSER) rather than a standalone pipeline, and its selective editing of only W_Value in MHSA represents a structural analysis not present in prior model-editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe for ViT-based FSCIL that avoids both catastrophic forgetting (from full fine-tuning) and backbone-classifier misalignment (from freezing), which are the two dominant failure modes practitioners face when scaling FSCIL beyond CNNs. The finding that W_Value alone suffices for MHSA editing, supported by attention-matrix similarity analysis, simplifies the editing target and reduces the search space. Open questions include the computational cost of the localization phase at scale (the paper acknowledges this but does not provide wall-clock comparisons), whether the method extends to larger ViT variants or non-classification tasks like segmentation, and how the approach behaves when the number of incremental sessions grows substantially beyond 8–10.
