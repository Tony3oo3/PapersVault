## Problem

Existing intuitive 3D segmentation methods based on NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting require extensive scene-specific training (780–8220 seconds) to obtain accurate 3D priors, making them impractical for real-time or in-the-wild use. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a feed-forward approach that segments arbitrary 3D objects from sparse 2D views without any per-scene optimization, while also tackling the accumulation of 3D alignment errors that degrades segmentation accuracy in such feed-forward pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of sparse RGB views (3–9 images tested) and a user 2D prompt (click or scribble) on one reference view. During an offline pre-processing stage, SAM2 generates consistent object masks across all views stored in a mask cache, and MASt3R predicts per-pixel pointmaps. At query time, the system outputs a 3D segmentation point cloud in a globally aligned world coordinate frame, with interaction latency of 5–20 ms after the ~30 s one-time scene reconstruction.

## Method

WildSeg3D operates in three stages. First, SAM2's video-tracking capability produces panoptic masks on a reference view, which are then propagated to all other views and cached offline as soft masks. Second, Dynamic Global Aligning (DGA) refines the standard MASt3R global alignment by multiplying pointmap confidence with soft masks to suppress background, then applying a dynamic adjustment function A(x) that amplifies the weight of hard-to-match points (those with confidence near 0.5) while attenuating well-matched ones, using a matching map from point pre-matching to set positive/negative adjustment factors. Third, Multi-view Group Mapping (MGM) retrieves the relevant cached masks for the prompted object, computes their union across all views, and projects the masked pixels into the aligned world coordinate system via per-view transform matrices learned by DGA, yielding the final 3D segmentation point cloud in real time.

## Results

On the NVOS dataset, WildSeg3D achieves the best mIoU of 94.1% and mAcc of 99.0%, outperforming all scene-specific baselines while requiring only 30 seconds for full scene reconstruction and segmentation (vs. 780 s for SA3D and 8220 s for OmniSeg3D). On SPIn-NeRF, WildSeg3D reaches 94.0% mIoU and 98.6% mAcc with an interaction latency of 5–20 ms, compared to 1–2 s for OmniSeg3D. The method eliminates scene-specific training entirely, delivering a 40× speedup over the fastest SOTA baseline (SA3D) while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildSeg3D outperforms all seven NVOS baselines on both mIoU and mAcc, with the largest gaps over NVOS (+24.0 mIoU) and ISRF (+10.3 mIoU). Against the strongest SOTA methods, it beats FlashSplat by 2.3 mIoU (94.1 vs. 91.8) and OmniSeg3D by 2.4 mIoU (94.1 vs. 91.7), while being 50× and 274× faster respectively. On SPIn-NeRF, WildSeg3D (94.0 mIoU) is marginally below OmniSeg3D (94.3 mIoU) but surpasses SA3D (92.4) and MVSeg (90.9), with interaction latency of 5–20 ms versus 1–2 s for OmniSeg3D and 120–600 s for SA3D. The key differentiator is that all competing methods require scene-specific training (ranging from 13 to 137 minutes), whereas WildSeg3D operates in a fully feed-forward manner with no per-scene optimization, completing the entire pipeline in under 30 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm established by DUSt3R and MASt3R (pointmap prediction + global alignment) to the task of intuitive 3D segmentation, a domain previously dominated by scene-specific NeRF and 3DGS training pipelines (SA3D, SAGA, FlashSplat, OmniSeg3D). The key departure is addressing the alignment-error accumulation that degrades segmentation accuracy in feed-forward reconstruction, via a Dynamic Global Aligning loss that reweights hard-to-match points using soft masks from SAM2. The mask cache and Multi-view Group Mapping modules repurpose SAM2's video tracking to decouple 2D segmentation from 3D alignment, enabling real-time prompt-to-segmentation without per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating scene-specific training, WildSeg3D makes high-quality intuitive 3D segmentation deployable in arbitrary, previously unseen environments—a practical barrier that has limited adoption of NeRF/3DGS-based segmentation in real-time applications (AR/VR, robotics, auto-labeling). The 40× speedup (30 s reconstruction + 5–20 ms interaction vs. 780–8220 s for SOTA) is a substantial efficiency gain, though the quantitative evaluation is confined to small-scale datasets (NVOS: 8 scenes, SPIn-NeRF: 10 scenes), leaving open questions about scalability to large, unbounded scenes and robustness to dynamic or heavily occluded objects. Future work could explore whether the DGA strategy generalizes to video sequences or streaming settings where the mask cache must be updated online.
