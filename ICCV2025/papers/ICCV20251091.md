## Problem

Existing audio-visual localization (AVL) research operates on single-frame inputs and assumes simplified scenarios with a single visible sound source, failing to capture temporal dynamics or handle real-world complexity such as multiple concurrent sources, visually similar entities, and off-screen emitters. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a video-centric benchmark with high temporal resolution and a model that explicitly models spatiotemporal audio-visual alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251091/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second video (640×360, 20–40 fps) as an RGB frame sequence and a corresponding audio spectrogram. The audio encoder uses a rectangular 2D CNN kernel whose width is set to ⌊W_a/T⌋ so that each audio segment aligns precisely with one visual frame, producing per-frame audio features. The output is a per-timestamp localization heatmap of size H×W, computed as the cosine similarity between the frame-level audio representation and each spatial location's visual feature, yielding a T×H×W spatiotemporal map.

## Method

TAVLO employs a two-stream encoder (ResNet-18 for visual, 2D CNN for audio) with modality-specific positional encoding: spatial PE is added element-wise to visual features while temporal PE is concatenated to both modalities, ensuring spatiotemporally consistent representations. The core architectural contribution is the Audio-Spatial-Temporal (AST) Attention Block, which factorizes 3D self-attention into Spatial Attention (across 1+H·W positions within each frame, capturing cross-modal audio-visual interaction) and Temporal Attention (along the T axis, modeling long-range dependencies), avoiding the quadratic cost of full video self-attention. Training uses a cross-modal multiple-instance contrastive loss adapted from EZ-VSL, with two key modifications: visual bags are defined at the frame level (each audio segment paired with its corresponding frame's spatial features) rather than globally, and negative-bag similarity is computed as a mean rather than a max to prevent outlier-dominated optimization.

## Results

On the AVATAR benchmark, TAVLO(10k) achieves 13.42% CIoU and 14.08% AUC on Single-sound, 14.13% CIoU and 14.52% AUC on Mixed-sound, and 12.08% CIoU and 12.69% AUC on Multi-entity, outperforming all baselines in the latter two scenarios. On the Cross-event subset, TAVLO shows only a −0.33 CIoU drop (13.37→13.04) and −0.37 AUC drop (13.98→13.61), whereas baselines degrade by up to −5.36 CIoU and −5.00 AUC, demonstrating the critical role of temporal modeling. On Off-screen, TAVLO achieves 91.18% TN, with the paper noting that re-evaluating with a stricter top-5% threshold (†TN) yields comparable performance across methods (95.02% for TAVLO vs. 94.55–96.45% for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAVLO(10k) is compared against three baselines trained on VGGSound: SLAVC(144k), EZ-VSL (at 10k, 144k, and full scale), and SSL-TIE(144k). On Mixed-sound, TAVLO achieves 14.13% CIoU, nearly doubling the best baseline (EZ-VSL 10k at 8.16%), and on Multi-entity it reaches 12.08% CIoU versus 6.96% for the best baseline (EZ-VSL full). On Single-sound, TAVLO slightly trails SSL-TIE(144k) by 0.15% in AUC (14.08 vs. 14.23) but leads in CIoU (13.42 vs. 13.10), making it essentially a tie on this simpler scenario. The most striking gap appears on Cross-event: TAVLO drops only 0.33 CIoU from its overall score, while SSL-TIE(144k) drops 5.36 and EZ-VSL(full) drops 5.24, confirming that frame-level baselines cannot track shifting sound sources. On Off-screen, raw TN% favors baselines (96.46–96.91% vs. 91.18%), but the authors attribute this to thresholding artifacts; under the stricter †TN protocol (top-5% threshold on off-screen-only videos), all methods converge to 94.55–96.45%, with TAVLO at 95.02%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised audio-visual localization line (EZ-VSL, SLAVC, SSL-TIE) by shifting the evaluation paradigm from single-frame image-level associations to video-centric, temporally-resolved localization. The training loss is directly adapted from EZ-VSL's cross-modal multiple-instance contrastive objective, with two modifications: frame-level visual bags instead of global bags, and mean (rather than max) similarity for negative bags. Architecturally, TAVLO borrows the factorized spatial-then-temporal attention pattern from video transformers (ViViT, Space-Time Attention) and applies it to a two-stream audio-visual encoder, departing from prior AVL models that treat audio as a single global vector and visual input as a single frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVATAR provides the first video-level AVL benchmark covering four realistic scenarios (single-sound, mixed-sound, multi-entity, off-screen) with per-frame annotations, filling a gap left by image-level benchmarks like Flickr-SoundNet and VGGSS. The empirical finding that image-level baselines lose 3–5 CIoU points when the sound source changes over time, while TAVLO loses less than 0.4, makes a concrete case for temporal modeling in AVL. However, absolute performance remains very low (~13% CIoU), and the paper explicitly leaves fully off-screen sound localization as an open problem, as the current framework still assumes at least partial audio-visual alignment within the frame.
