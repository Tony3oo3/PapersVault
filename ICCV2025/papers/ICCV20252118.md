## Problem

Existing person search methods rely on ImageNet pre-trained backbones that lack the spatial context and fine-grained identity cues needed for complex scenes with multiple overlapping individuals. Additionally, sharing a single backbone for both person detection and re-ID creates conflicting optimization objectives, as detection requires general human characteristics while re-ID demands highly discriminative identity features, causing competing gradients that degrade both sub-tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped query person image and a gallery of uncropped scene images (e.g., CUHK-SYSU at 384×128, PRW at 256×128) and produces person bounding boxes via Faster R-CNN along with a ranked list of detected person crops matched to the query identity. The frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 UNet processes each gallery image at a fixed timestep (t=0 for optimal performance), and CLIP text embeddings for body-part tokens ("head", "shirts", "pants", "shoes") are pre-computed once and used as spatial attention queries during inference.

## Method

DiffPS freezes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2-1 UNet backbone and extracts task-specific features from its up-stage, completely eliminating the optimization conflict between detection and re-ID. Three specialized modules maximize diffusion priors: (i) DGRPN thresholds the cross-attention map for the "person" token, constructs Gaussian maps from detected peaks, and modulates the detection feature map to guide region proposals; (ii) MSFRN applies a hierarchical Discrete Wavelet Transform across multi-scale up-stage features (7 maps from levels 1–3), refining high-frequency sub-bands with channel-wise attention to counteract the shape bias inherent in diffusion features; (iii) SFAN computes cosine similarity between refined features and CLIP body-part text embeddings, producing spatially adaptive semantic maps that are aggregated with learnable weights to emphasize informative body regions. Training uses standard Smooth-L1 and cross-entropy losses for detection and OIM loss for re-ID, with the backbone parameters held fixed throughout.

## Results

On CUHK-SYSU, DiffPS achieves 97.8% mAP and 98.4% Top-1 accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art and surpassing the previous best (SEAS with ConvNeXt) by 0.7% mAP and 0.6% Top-1. On the more challenging PRW dataset, DiffPS reaches 62.0% mAP and 91.0% Top-1, improving over SEAS (ConvNeXt) by 1.5% mAP and over SOLIDER (Swin-S) by 2.2% mAP. Notably, DiffPS uses a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 backbone without any fine-tuning, while all competing methods rely on ImageNet-pretrained discriminative backbones (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt, Swin-S, PVTv2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPS outperforms all 16 compared baselines on CUHK-SYSU mAP, with the largest margins over ResNet-50-based methods (up to +22.3% over OIM) and a clear lead over the strongest ConvNeXt-based competitors: +0.7% mAP over SEAS and +1.4% over SeqNeXt+GFN. On PRW, DiffPS achieves the best mAP (62.0%), beating SEAS (ConvNeXt) by 1.5%, SOLIDER (Swin-S) by 2.2%, and SeqNeXt+GFN (ConvNeXt) by 3.7%. However, DiffPS does not achieve the best Top-1 on PRW: SeqNeXt+GFN (ConvNeXt) edges it out with 92.4% vs. 91.0%, though DiffPS's mAP advantage of 3.7 points indicates substantially better ranking quality across the full retrieval list. The paper also demonstrates that DiffPS eliminates the optimization conflict between detection and re-ID: unlike DMRNet and DMRNet++ which show 0.4–1.5% performance drops when jointly optimizing both tasks, DiffPS achieves identical performance whether trained jointly or separately, thanks to its frozen diffusion backbone. All competing methods rely on ImageNet-pretrained discriminative backbones (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt, Swin-S, PVTv2-B2), whereas DiffPS is the first to use a pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2-1) as a frozen backbone for person search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in person search, where ImageNet-pretrained CNNs (ResNet50, ConvNeXt) serve as shared, jointly-optimized backbones for both detection and re-ID. It repurposes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet as a frozen feature extractor, drawing on the broader trend of leveraging diffusion model internals for downstream vision tasks (segmentation, classification, semantic correspondence) but applying it specifically to the multi-task person search setting. Conceptually, it extends the decoupled design of DMRNet/DMRNet++ by going further: rather than merely separating detection and re-ID heads on a shared backbone, it freezes the backbone entirely, extracting task-specific features from different up-stage layers and cross-attention maps, thereby eliminating gradient interference at the source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen diffusion backbone achieves identical performance under joint and individual task optimization (Table 2) provides a clean resolution to the long-standing multi-task conflict in person search, and the substantial margins over ResNet50-based SOTA (up to 22.3% mAP) suggest that diffusion priors encode spatial and identity information that category-level ImageNet pretraining simply does not capture. However, the approach is validated on only two datasets with a single diffusion model (SD v2-1), and the computational cost of running a full UNet forward pass per image is not analyzed, raising questions about practical deployment. Open questions include whether the t=0 (noise-free) feature extraction strategy generalizes to other diffusion architectures, whether the text-aligned semantic aggregation (SFAN) transfers to other person-centric tasks, and whether lighter-weight diffusion backbones can achieve comparable results.
