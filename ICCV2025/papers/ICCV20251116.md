## Problem

Existing benchmarks for vision-language reward models (VLRMs) typically evaluate only a single capability, such as pairwise comparison of two reasoning processes, and predominantly focus on the language domain. This singular evaluation fails to reveal the full range of flaws in VLRMs across process understanding, outcome judgment, and critique generation, thereby limiting their development in the visual-language domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251116/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For step-based tasks, the model receives an image (or multiple images) alongside a modified reasoning process and must output a binary 0/1 sequence indicating which steps contain errors, redundancies, or hallucinations. For outcome-based tasks, the model receives either a pair of reasoning processes for the same question (MJ) or a truncated prefix of a reasoning process (FF) and outputs a quality score or correctness prediction. For criticism-based tasks, the model receives an erroneous reasoning step and produces free-text error analysis (ERA) or a corrected reasoning process (EC). Evaluation uses weighted F1-score for step tasks, accuracy for outcome tasks, and GPT-4o-baselined win rate for criticism tasks.

## Method

VLRMBench is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting 16,550 samples from 8 datasets spanning mathematical reasoning, hallucination understanding, and multi-image understanding; (2) joint filtering with Qwen2VL-7B, retaining only samples the model answers incorrectly both with and without image input (yielding 6,715 samples); and (3) generating reasoning processes with QVQ-72B-preview, performing step-level segmentation with GPT-4o, and conducting manual verification by three PhD volunteers, ultimately yielding 1,000 high-quality samples. From these, 12 tasks are designed across three themes: 8 step-based tasks (Step Correctness, Redundancy Detection, Confidence Misdirection, Existential/Attribute Hallucination, Detail Error, Spatial Relationship, Image Confusion) where specific error types are injected into reasoning steps; 2 outcome-based binary classification tasks (Multi-solution Judgment, Forecasting Future); and 2 criticism-based tasks (Error Reason Analysis, Error Correction). The evaluation follows the 'LVLM-as-a-Judge' paradigm, testing 21 open-source and 5 closed-source models, with the key finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 62.4% average F1 on step tasks and 66.3% accuracy on outcome tasks, while Qwen2.5VL-72B matches or exceeds GPT-4o on several tasks.

## Results

On VLRMBench's 12 tasks, even the strongest closed-source model GPT-4o achieves only 62.4% weighted F1-Score on the eight step-based tasks and 66.3% average accuracy on the two outcome-based binary classification tasks. Notably, the open-source Qwen2.5VL-72B matches or exceeds GPT-4o on step-based tasks (62.6% vs 62.4%) and outperforms it on MJ (72.1% vs 58.4%) and FF (80.2% vs 76.0%), while achieving a 74.1% win rate in the ERA criticism task where GPT-4o scores 0.0%. Small open-source models (<10B) average only 29.8% F1 on step-based tasks, and outcome-based tasks remain near random-guessing (~50%) for most open-source models, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLRMBench is positioned against two prior vision-language reward model benchmarks: VLRewardBench [Li et al., 2024] and Multimodal RewardBench [Yasunaga et al., 2025]. Both prior benchmarks evaluate only a single task (pairwise comparison of two reasoning processes) without step-level labels, whereas VLRMBench covers 12 tasks across three themes (process, outcome, critique) with step-level evaluation, scaling from 1,250 (VLRewardBench) or 5,211 (MultimodalRewardBench) samples to 12,634. The paper does not re-run models on the prior benchmarks; instead, the comparison is structural, demonstrating that VLRMBench subsumes the evaluation scope of both. In terms of model-level findings, Qwen2.5VL-72B surpasses GPT-4o on step-based F1 (62.6% vs 62.4%), MJ accuracy (72.1% vs 58.4%), and ERA win rate (74.1% vs 0.0%), while Claude-3.5-Sonnet leads on MJ (82.2%) and ERA (60.6%). GPT-4o's 0.0% win rate on ERA/EC suggests it fails to produce usable free-text critiques under the evaluation protocol, a failure mode not captured by the single-task pairwise design of the prior benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward model evaluation paradigm from the NLP domain (MR-GSM8K, ProcessBench, PRMBench) to the vision-language domain, while simultaneously broadening the scope from single-capability assessment to a unified multi-task framework. Prior VL-domain benchmarks (VLRewardBench, MultimodalRewardBench) are limited to pairwise comparison without step-level labels, whereas VLRMBench integrates process, outcome, and critique evaluation across 12 tasks. The data construction pipeline repurposes the 'LVLM-as-a-Judge' paradigm and leverages QVQ-72B-preview for reasoning generation and GPT-4o for step segmentation, building on the collaborative filtering approach seen in prior data curation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLRMBench fills a critical gap by providing the first comprehensive evaluation platform for VLRMs, revealing that even GPT-4o achieves only 62.4% average F1 on step-based tasks and 66.3% on outcome tasks, indicating substantial room for improvement. The finding that scaling LVLMs yields diminishing returns for RM tasks (Large group gains only 0.4% over Middle group) challenges the assumption that larger models are automatically better judges, and suggests dedicated VLRM training at mid-scale may be more effective. Open questions include how to train purpose-built VLRMs, how to handle long reasoning chains where performance degrades significantly, and how to best integrate VLRM feedback into LVLM inference pipelines for maximum correction gain.
