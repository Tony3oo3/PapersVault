## Problem

Existing diffusion-based character animation methods (e.g., Animate Anyone) rely solely on motion signals to drive character animation, producing no meaningful spatial or interactive relationship between the animated character and its surrounding environment. This limits them to simple gestures or dances and fails to capture complex human-object interactions or coherent scene integration, a gap that prior attempts (MovieCharacter, MIMO, AnchorCrafter) address only through cascaded pipelines or suboptimal depth-based decompositions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference character image (center-cropped and composited onto a random background) and a driving video as input. From the driving video it extracts three conditional streams: an environment sequence (video with character regions removed via a shape-agnostic mask), a motion sequence (skeleton + structured depth), and an object sequence (interactive objects segmented by SAM2). The output is a 16-frame animated character video that preserves the reference character's identity while populating the environment and interacting with objects as specified by the driving video; long videos are handled by sequential clip inference with the last frame of each clip serving as a temporal reference for the next.

## Method

Built on Stable Diffusion's 3D UNet (with AnimateDiff temporal layers) and trained self-supervisedly, the framework disentangles character and environment from source videos using a shape-agnostic mask that propagates patchwise maximum values to deliberately disrupt the correspondence between mask boundaries and character outlines, preventing shape leakage during inference. A lightweight fully-convolutional object guider downsamples object latents four times to produce multi-scale features, which are injected into the denoising decoder via spatial blending (an alpha-weighted combination computed by a zero-initialized Conv2D-Sigmoid layer) applied after each spatial-attention stage. For motion, a depth-wise pose modulation strategy augments skeleton features with structured depth (depth values extracted only within the skeleton mask) through cross-attention, enabling each limb to incorporate spatial context from other limbs, while Conv3D layers model temporal consistency to mitigate per-frame pose estimation errors. Appearance consistency is maintained via a ReferenceNet whose features are merged exclusively in the midblock and upblock of the decoder through spatial attention, reducing computational overhead compared to full-depth injection.

## Results

On the TikTok benchmark, Animate Anyone 2 with pretraining (Ours*) achieves the best FVD of 144.65, SSIM of 0.812, PSNR of 30.82, and LPIPS of 0.223, surpassing the strongest baselines UniAnimate* (FVD 148.06) and Champ* (FVD 160.82). On the proposed 100-video generalization test set, the method achieves FVD 172.54, SSIM 0.809, and LPIPS 0.259, substantially outperforming the next-best method Champ (FVD 205.79) by over 33 FVD points. The character-environment affordance evaluation further shows improvement over a compositing baseline (FVD 186.17 vs. 195.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the TikTok benchmark, Animate Anyone 2 with pretraining (Ours*) achieves the best FVD (144.65) and LPIPS (0.223), narrowly beating UniAnimate* (FVD 148.06) and clearly surpassing Champ* (FVD 160.82), Animate Anyone (FVD 171.90), and MagicAnimate (FVD 179.07). Without pretraining, the method still outperforms MagicAnimate and Animate Anyone but trails the pre-trained Champ* and UniAnimate*, indicating the benefit of the larger custom dataset. On the proposed generalization test set, the gap widens dramatically: Ours achieves FVD 172.54 versus Champ's 205.79, MimicMotion's 212.48, and Animate Anyone's 228.53, with SSIM improving from 0.771 (Champ) to 0.809. The qualitative comparison with MIMO shows that MIMO's reliance on depth-based spatial decomposition and separate inpainting introduces preprocessing artifacts and erroneous character-background relationships, whereas Animate Anyone 2's end-to-end formulation produces more coherent scene integration. Overall, the method wins on all quantitative benchmarks against all baselines when pretraining is included, and the largest margins appear on the out-of-domain generalization set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Animate Anyone [15] framework (ReferenceNet + 3D UNet + pose guider) by adding two new conditional streams: an environment sequence (the driving video with character regions removed) and an object sequence (interactive objects extracted via SAM2). It departs from prior character animation methods that treat the background as static or absent, and from human-environment affordance methods (MovieCharacter, MIMO) that rely on cascaded pipelines or depth-based decomposition, instead learning character-environment fusion end-to-end within a single diffusion model. The pose modulation strategy occupies a middle ground between Animate Anyone's pure skeleton representation and Champ's dense 3D SMPL mesh, augmenting skeletons with structured depth to capture inter-limb spatial relationships without sacrificing cross-character generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making environment a first-class conditional input rather than an afterthought, this work addresses a practical gap that limits current character animation to simple gestures and dances in isolation. The shape-agnostic mask strategy is a clean solution to the shape-leakage problem inherent in self-supervised training with precise masks, and the spatial blending mechanism for object injection is a lightweight, generalizable design choice. Limitations remain for small-region hand-object interactions, large shape discrepancies between source and target characters, and dependence on SAM segmentation quality. Open questions include whether the environment affordance can be made more controllable (e.g., user-specified scene changes) and whether the approach generalizes to multi-character or non-human subjects.
