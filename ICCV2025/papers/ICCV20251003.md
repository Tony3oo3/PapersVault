## Problem

Text-to-image models struggle to generate the exact number of objects specified in a prompt because object quantity is a global constraint that existing conditional training and attention-based guidance methods cannot directly enforce. Prior counting models are either non-differentiable (detection-based) or rely on ambiguous density maps with arbitrary kernel placement and radius, making them unsuitable as gradient-based guidance signals for generative pipelines.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 640×640×3 RGB image and a text category prompt (encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder) and produces a dense 80×80 cardinality map whose cell values sum to the predicted object count, alongside a pixel-wise binary classification map for category localization. In the T2I generation setting, the target object count Q_req is additionally provided, and the system outputs an optimized learnable text token embedding that steers SDXL-Turbo toward the desired quantity via single-step inference.

## Method

YOLO-Count replaces the standard density map regression target with a cardinality map: for each object instance with mask M_i of area N_i, a uniform value of 1/N_i is assigned to all mask pixels, and contributions are summed and downsampled to a grid, yielding an unambiguous representation whose total sum equals the true count. The architecture builds on YOLO-World with a YOLOv8l backbone, a VLPAN enhanced with T-CSPLayers (sigmoid attention modulated by CLIP text embeddings) and an extended top-down fusion path to preserve fine-grained spatial details, and parallel classification and counting heads. Training uses a hybrid strong-weak scheme: strong supervision on LVIS instance segmentation masks (cardinality regression + SigLIP-style binary classification for representation alignment), followed by weak-supervision finetuning on FSC147 using sparse point annotations and a total-count consistency loss. For T2I control, a learnable counting token is iteratively optimized (up to 150 steps) by backpropagating the absolute deviation between the predicted count and the target Q_req through the fully differentiable YOLO-Count model.

## Results

On the FSC147 test set (seen categories), YOLO-Count achieves MAE 14.80 and RMSE 96.14, closely approaching the non-differentiable CountGD (MAE 12.98) while remaining fully differentiable. On the LVIS benchmark, YOLO-Count dramatically outperforms all baselines with MAE 1.65 and RMSE 6.08, compared to the next-best DAVE (MAE 5.29, RMSE 11.48). On the newly introduced unseen-category benchmarks, YOLO-Count achieves the best MAE on both OImg7-New (3.72) and Obj365-New (3.28), surpassing even non-differentiable detection-based methods like CountGD (6.09 and 3.53 respectively) and DAVE (5.31 and 4.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among differentiable (regression-based) baselines, YOLO-Count dominates across all five benchmarks. On FSC147, it reduces MAE by 1.08 over CounTX (14.80 vs. 15.88) and by 2.98 over CLIP-Count (14.80 vs. 17.78). On LVIS, the gap is dramatic: YOLO-Count's MAE of 1.65 is 3.2× lower than DAVE (5.29) and 6.5× lower than CLIP-Count (10.81). On the unseen-category benchmarks, YOLO-Count outperforms even the non-differentiable detection-based methods: on OImg7-New it beats CountGD by 2.37 MAE (3.72 vs. 6.09) and DAVE by 1.59 (3.72 vs. 5.31); on Obj365-New it beats CountGD by 0.25 (3.28 vs. 3.53) and DAVE by 1.61 (3.28 vs. 4.89). The only setting where YOLO-Count trails is FSC147 seen categories, where non-differentiable CountGD achieves a 1.82 MAE advantage (12.98 vs. 14.80), though YOLO-Count still leads all differentiable methods. In T2I quantity control experiments (Fig. 5), YOLO-Count substantially reduces the gap between requested and generated object counts compared to guidance baselines [7, 25] and token-optimization with CLIP-Count or CountGD, particularly at large counts (25–100 objects).

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLO-Count is architecturally grounded in YOLO-World (CVPR 2024), inheriting its VLPAN with T-CSPLayers and YOLOv8 backbone, but repurposes the detection pipeline into a regression-based counting model by replacing the detection head with a cardinality regression head and a contrastive classification head. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant density-map paradigm (Gaussian kernels at object centers) by introducing the cardinality map, which distributes quantity uniformly over object masks, and from detection-based counters (CountGD, DAVE) by maintaining full differentiability. For T2I integration, it adopts the iterative textual-inversion token optimization framework of Zafar et al. [50], substituting their counting signal with YOLO-Count's differentiable output. The hybrid strong-weak training strategy—pretraining on LVIS instance segmentation and finetuning on FSC147 point annotations—is a novel data-efficiency mechanism that avoids the heavy CLIP/GroundingDINO visual encoders used by prior open-vocabulary counters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Precise object quantity control remains a recognized gap in controllable T2I generation, where existing methods handle layout and attributes but struggle with global numerical constraints; YOLO-Count provides a lightweight, differentiable mechanism that can be plugged into any diffusion pipeline via gradient-based guidance. The cardinality map offers a clean, reusable regression target that eliminates the kernel-placement and radius ambiguities of density maps, and the size-bias analysis (Fig. 8) provides concrete evidence of why density-based methods fail at scale. Open questions include whether the iterative token optimization (up to 150 steps per image) scales to real-time or batch generation, how the approach behaves for very large counts beyond 100, and whether the cardinality formulation transfers to video or 3D counting. The new OpenImg7-New and Obj365-New benchmarks also fill a gap in open-vocabulary counting evaluation beyond FSC147's limited 89 training categories.
