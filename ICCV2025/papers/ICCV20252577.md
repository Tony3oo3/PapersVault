## Problem

Conventional convex-lens endoscopes are limited to millimetre-scale thickness, impeding navigation through narrow anatomical structures, while ultra-thin metalenses (sub-micron) introduce severe chromatic aberration from wavelength-dependent focal lengths that no existing algorithm addresses. This paper is the first to tackle metalens endoscopy from both data and algorithmic perspectives, identifying two physically grounded imaging challenges—color/position-dependent intensity loss and color-dependent PSF misalignment—that must be corrected for reliable clinical segmentation and visualization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252577/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB metalens endoscopic image at 1024×1024 resolution (captured through a 2.6 mm GaN metalens with 10 mm focal length) and simultaneously produces a semantic segmentation mask and a restored high-quality image. Training uses triplet data {metalens image, convex-lens ground truth, label} generated by projecting public endoscopic datasets onto a screen and re-capturing with the metalens camera, so the model learns a restoration function that generalizes to real in-vivo scenes with varied depth and different metalens hardware.

## Method

MetaScope builds on a NAFNet backbone and injects two optics-informed modules into the encoder. Optics-informed Intensity Adjustment (OIA) encodes simulated optical priors—a color-dependent efficiency vector and a spatial vignetting map—into channel and spatial embeddings that modulate attention maps, correcting wavelength- and position-dependent attenuation. Optics-informed Chromatic Correction (OCC) models the implicit PSF distribution via a Multi-expert Gaussian (MeG) paradigm: a VAE samples a latent offset space, K=3 Gaussian experts each learn a linear projection to produce spatial offsets, and a router aggregates them; the resulting offsets drive a deformable convolution that re-aggregates chromatically dispersed pixels. A gradient-guided distillation loss, weighted by the restoration-loss gradient amplitude map, transfers DINOv2 features from the convex-lens image to focus learning on under-optimized regions. The overall objective jointly optimizes segmentation (CE), restoration (PSNR), distillation, and KL divergence terms under multi-task supervised training.

## Results

On the five metalens endoscopy benchmarks, MetaScope achieves an average of 80.51% mIoU and 87.67% mDICE for segmentation, and 33.37 dB PSNR with 0.9724 SSIM for restoration. For segmentation, MetaScope outperforms the strongest baseline EDAformer by 6.81% mIoU and 5.01% mDICE on average, and wins on every individual dataset. For restoration, MetaScope surpasses the best metalens-specific method DRMI by 0.74 dB PSNR and 0.0081 SSIM, while general-purpose restorers (SwinIR, MambaIR) lag by 7–8 dB PSNR, underscoring the unique degradation pattern of metalens imaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaScope wins on all five datasets for both segmentation and restoration against every baseline. In segmentation, the closest competitor is EDAformer (73.70% mIoU / 82.66% mDICE average), which MetaScope surpasses by 6.81% mIoU and 5.01% mDICE; Mask2Former (75.19% mIoU) and U-KAN (69.62% mIoU) trail further behind. In restoration, the metalens-specific method DRMI (32.63 dB PSNR) is the nearest rival, with MetaScope gaining 0.74 dB; NAFNet (32.41 dB) and Restormer (32.10 dB) are also outperformed. Notably, general-purpose restorers SwinIR (24.93 dB) and MambaIR (25.15 dB) underperform by roughly 8 dB, and the de-raining method NeRD-Rain (28.23 dB) also lags significantly, confirming that metalens chromatic aberration is a distinct degradation not addressed by existing restoration pipelines. The paper further shows that MetaScope's metalens system outperforms a conventional convex-lens system using high-quality ground-truth images with SOTA segmentation, demonstrating that the optics-informed correction fully compensates for the physical limitations of the metalens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of meta-optics and medical image analysis. Prior metalens imaging work (e.g., DRMI [80], neural nano-optics [81]) focused exclusively on image restoration, while medical segmentation methods (U-Net variants, Mask2Former, EDAformer) assumed conventional convex-lens imaging with no wavelength-dependent degradation. MetaScope repurposes the NAFNet backbone and injects physics-derived optical priors (channel efficiency vectors and spatial vignetting maps from simulation) into attention mechanisms, and adapts the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm into a Multi-expert Gaussian (MeG) module for implicit PSF estimation — a conceptual departure from both the data-driven restoration literature and the purely learned segmentation literature. The gradient-guided distillation from DINOv2 borrows from knowledge distillation but applies it selectively via restoration-loss gradient maps, a use case not seen in prior medical imaging work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of metalens endoscopy as a joint restoration-and-segmentation problem, and the five introduced benchmarks (Meta-CVC-Clinic, Meta-CVC-Colon, Meta-Kvasir-Seg, Meta-EndoVis-17, Meta-EndoVis-18) provide the community with the first paired data for this modality. The practical impact is significant for micro-robotics and minimally invasive surgery, where sub-milligram, sub-micron-thickness optics could enable access to anatomical structures unreachable by conventional lenses. Key open questions remain: the datasets are constructed via a proxy method (projecting images onto a screen and re-capturing with a metalens camera), so generalization to true in-vivo metalens imaging with variable depth, motion, and tissue scattering is only partially validated (Supp. C); whether the optical priors transfer across substantially different metalens designs (materials, geometries, focal lengths) is untested; and the computational overhead of the MeG module and dual-branch decoder for real-time clinical deployment is not discussed.
