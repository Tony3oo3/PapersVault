## Problem

Sparse-view 3D reconstruction remains challenging because SDF-based methods capture global geometry but miss fine details, while 3DGS-based methods preserve high-frequency detail but produce fragmented, globally incoherent surfaces. No existing approach effectively leverages the complementary strengths of both dense (SDF) and sparse (3DGS) representations under sparse-view conditions, where both degrade significantly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5–20 sparse RGB images with known camera poses (10 views with 10% overlap on MobileBrick; 5 uniformly sampled frames on DTU) and produces a watertight 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from a 256³ voxel SDF grid, along with photorealistic novel-view renderings. COLMAP sparse points are used as a background initialization complement, and Metric3D monocular normal predictions provide geometric supervision during coarse SDF training. A full optimization cycle takes approximately 1 hour on a single GPU.

## Method

SurfaceSplat performs a cyclic optimization between a Voxurf-based SDF representation and a 3DGS renderer. In the SDF→3DGS step, a coarse 96³ voxel SDF is trained with a normal consistency loss (supervised by Metric3D predictions) to suppress floaters; the resulting mesh is cleaned by removing non-main connected components, and surface points are sampled via a depth-based visibility strategy to initialize 3DGS with globally consistent geometry. In the 3DGS→SDF step, the trained 3DGS (7k iterations) renders 10 novel-view images from expanded camera poses (via position perturbation or cubic spline interpolation), which are combined with the original inputs to refine the SDF at 256³ resolution with a dual-color network and hierarchical geometry features. The two steps can be iterated, though a single cycle typically suffices, yielding state-of-the-art F1 and Chamfer Distance on both MobileBrick and DTU.

## Results

On MobileBrick (10 input views), SurfaceSplat achieves the best F1 score of 68.97% at σ=2.5mm and 86.65% at σ=5mm for mesh reconstruction, while also attaining the highest foreground PSNR of 20.45 dB for novel view rendering. On DTU (5 input views), it achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.45 mm, substantially outperforming the next-best SDF method MonoSDF (1.70 mm) and all 3DGS-based baselines (≥2.84 mm). A single optimization cycle takes approximately 1 hour, comparable to Voxurf (55 min) and GOF (50 min), while a two-cycle variant yields marginal further gains (F1 69.14%, PSNR-F 20.55 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurfaceSplat outperforms all SDF-based baselines in reconstruction: it beats Voxurf by +6.55 F1 points at σ=2.5mm (68.97 vs. 62.42) and reduces Chamfer Distance from 13.3 mm to 9.7 mm on MobileBrick, and from 2.43 mm to 1.45 mm on DTU. Against 3DGS-based methods, it surpasses GOF by +14.01 F1 points (68.97 vs. 54.96) and 2DGS by +21.87 F1 points in reconstruction, while also achieving the best rendering PSNR-F of 20.45 dB—exceeding 3DGS (19.12 dB) and SparseGS (18.74 dB). On DTU with only 5 views, SurfaceSplat (1.45 mm CD) nearly halves the error of MonoSDF (1.70 mm) and is 2× better than 2DGS (2.84 mm) and GOF (3.55 mm). GSDF, which jointly optimizes SDF and 3DGS, degrades severely in sparse settings (5.40 mm CD on DTU with 10 views), confirming that the paper's sequential complementary strategy is more effective than joint optimization under limited supervision. The method's key advantage is that it simultaneously achieves the best reconstruction AND the best rendering, whereas prior methods excel at one or the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurfaceSplat sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: SDF-based implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS, Voxurf) and 3D Gaussian Splatting for rendering (3DGS, 2DGS, GOF). Prior hybrid methods such as NeuSG, GSDF, and 3DGSR jointly optimize SDF and 3DGS within a single framework, enforcing geometric consistency through shared losses. SurfaceSplat departs from this joint-optimization paradigm by adopting a sequential, complementary pipeline: SDF first provides a coarse global mesh to initialize 3DGS, and 3DGS then renders additional views to refine the SDF. This decoupled, cyclic design is specifically motivated by the observation that joint optimization degrades under sparse-view conditions, whereas the sequential approach lets each representation operate in its strength regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple two-stage cyclic pipeline can outperform both pure SDF and pure 3DGS methods on sparse-view reconstruction, offering a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt without re-architecting either component. However, the current implementation is tied to Voxurf and 3DGS specifically, is limited to object-level scenes, and struggles with extremely sparse inputs (e.g., two images), leaving open the question of whether the cyclic strategy generalizes to other SDF/3DGS variants or to unbounded scene reconstruction. The diminishing returns observed beyond 10 rendered views (Fig. 7) also suggest a natural ceiling on how much synthetic supervision can compensate for genuinely missing geometric information.
