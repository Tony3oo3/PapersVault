## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion transformers rely on cross-attention to inject fixed text embeddings, which limits multimodal fusion efficiency, introduces unidirectional positional bias from causal LLM encoders, and requires architecture modifications for task expansion. Additionally, the absence of a dedicated captioning system tailored for T2I generation results in inaccurate and insufficiently detailed image-text training pairs, constraining both prompt adherence and model expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form text prompts at multiple granularities (detailed, medium, short, tag-based) and in multiple languages (English, Chinese, and emergent multilingual support via Gemma), and for unified multi-image generation tasks it takes composite grids of conditional inputs (depth maps, pose maps, canny maps, sketches) concatenated with paired images. Output is a 1024×1024 RGB image synthesized via flow-matching, with the architecture also supporting multi-image generation where multiple output images are produced in a single forward pass. Training uses 200M text-image pairs re-captioned by UniCap at native image resolution rather than fixed low-resolution crops.

## Method

Unified Next-DiT replaces all zero-initialized gated cross-attention blocks with a single-stream joint self-attention mechanism that concatenates caption embeddings (from Gemma) and noised latents into one sequence, with lightweight text and image processors preceding the main blocks to bridge the modality gap and mRoPE encoding text length plus image height/width in three dimensions. A key analytical insight reframes text-to-image attention as a dynamic feed-forward network whose hidden dimension equals the caption length, meaning longer captions effectively scale model capacity without additional parameters. UniCap, a unified captioning system, generates multi-granularity, multi-perspective, and bilingual descriptions by processing images at native scale across diverse domains, and is trained on GPT-4o-generated detailed captions summarized into multiple levels. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule (256→1024→1024 high-quality tuning) over a hierarchically filtered dataset (100M→10M→1M samples) with domain-specific system prompts and an auxiliary flow-matching loss on 4×-downsampled latents to preserve low-frequency structure. Inference combines CFG-Renormalization (rescaling velocity magnitude to prevent over-saturation) with CFG-Truncation (skipping conditional evaluation after a timestep threshold) to achieve >20% speedup without quality loss.

## Results

On the DPG benchmark, Lumina-Image 2.0 (2.6B) achieves the best Overall score of 87.20, outperforming all compared models including Sana-1.5 (85.00), Sana-1.6B (84.80), and SD3-medium (84.08), with particularly strong Relation (94.85) and Entity (91.97) sub-scores. On GenEval, it achieves an Overall score of 0.73, ranking second only to Janus-Pro-7B (0.80) among all autoregressive and diffusion models. On T2I-CompBench, it attains the best Color score (0.8211) among all models. On human-rated ELO arenas, Lumina-Image 2.0 ranks third overall on Artificial Analysis (982) and Rapidata (969), trailing only FLUX Pro variants, but achieves the second-highest prompt-alignment score (1031) on Rapidata, ahead of Imagen 3 and DALL-E 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lumina-Image 2.0 achieves the best DPG Overall score (87.20) among all 16 compared models, surpassing the nearest competitors Sana-1.5 (85.00) and Sana-1.6B (84.80) by 2.2 and 2.4 points respectively, with a particularly large margin on the Relation sub-metric (94.85 vs. 91.90 for Sana-1.6B). On GenEval Overall, it trails Janus-Pro-7B (0.80 vs. 0.73) but leads all diffusion-based models, including SD3-medium (0.62) and Sana-1.5 (0.72). On T2I-CompBench Color, it edges out DALL-E 3 (0.8211 vs. 0.8110), though DALL-E 3 retains advantages on Shape (0.6750 vs. 0.6028) and Texture (0.8070 vs. 0.7417). In human-rated ELO arenas, Lumina-Image 2.0 consistently ranks third overall behind FLUX 1.1 Pro and FLUX 1 Pro on both Artificial Analysis and Rapidata, but its prompt-alignment score (1031 on Rapidata) exceeds Imagen 3 (1003) and DALL-E 3 (1022), validating the effectiveness of the Unified Next-DiT architecture and UniCap captioning system. Compared to its direct predecessor Lumina-Next, the model improves DPG Overall from 75.66 to 87.20 and GenEval Overall from 0.46 to 0.73, demonstrating the combined impact of the unified architecture, UniCap, and efficient training strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Lumina-Next's Next-DiT architecture by replacing zero-initialized gated cross-attention with a unified single-stream joint self-attention mechanism, positioning itself between the cross-attention paradigm (PixArt, Lumina-Next) and the double-stream MMDiT used in SD3/FLUX. The design is conceptually closest to OmniGen's single-stream causal DiT, but deliberately retains adaLN and avoids LLM initialization to preserve diffusion-specific inductive biases. On the data side, UniCap generalizes the DALL-E 3 insight that better captions improve generation into a multi-granularity, multi-domain, multilingual captioning system, and the FFN reinterpretation of text-to-image attention builds on prior work showing FFN layers as key-value memories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-stream architecture offers a practical path to task expansion (multi-image generation, depth/pose/canny/sketch conditioning) without per-task architectural modifications, which is a meaningful engineering advantage for open-source T2I ecosystems. The FFN-as-dynamic-capacity interpretation of caption length provides a conceptual handle on inference-time scaling, though the paper does not yet exploit this for concrete test-time compute strategies. A notable limitation is that on human-rated ELO arenas the model still trails FLUX Pro, suggesting the architectural unification has not yet closed the gap with the strongest closed-source systems, and the academic benchmark advantage (especially DPG) may partly reflect caption-length alignment rather than genuine generative superiority.
