## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods produce static, non-interactive models plagued by blurry textures, missing semantic details (e.g., luggage racks, door seams), redundant artifacts, and a fundamental lack of functional decomposition. The authors argue the primary bottleneck is not the 3D generator itself but the semantic understanding and functional decomposition of generated objects, which current pipelines entirely overlook.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a structured 3D mesh composed of individually generated functional modules, each annotated with a connection type (rotational, flipping, translational, surface, or no connection) and motion parameters (axis, velocity, angle/distance range). The intermediate 2D reference image is generated at 512×512 resolution, and the final 3D assembly uses outer-contour point sequences as positional noise to ensure geometric alignment between the full model and its subcomponents.

## Method

LEGO-Maker reformulates text-to-3D generation into a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Stable Diffusion v1 model fine-tuned on 5,000 ShapeNet/PartNet renderings generates a semantically clean reference image; (2) a fine-tuned G-SAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) performs text-driven segmentation into functional modules, with the SAM mask decoder specifically adapted via dynamic convolution and a BCE+Dice loss; (3) each module undergoes diffusion-based texture enhancement, is converted to 3D via a replaceable image-to-3D generator (SPAR3D or TRELLIS), and is assembled through structured spatial composition using outer-contour positional noise. A motion-driven mechanism then maps each semantic component to a motion category (rotation, flipping, translation) with predefined kinematic parameters derived from annotated spatial-relationship data, enabling interactive behaviors like opening doors or rotating wheels in the final fused model.

## Results

On T³ Bench (Multiple Objects), LEGO-Maker achieves the highest multi-view quality score of 52.90 and text consistency of 43.60, outperforming the next-best method Meshy (49.33 / 39.82). On Toys4K, it attains the best CLIP score of 27.80 and the lowest Fréchet Distance (FD_dinov2) of 210.23, while completing inference in 3 minutes—comparable to Meshy and far faster than NeRF-based methods (15–180 min). The text consistency margin is the most pronounced: LEGO-Maker is the only method exceeding 40, with a gap of 3.78 points over Meshy and 17.47 points over ProlificDreamer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGO-Maker outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both benchmarks. On T³ Bench, it leads Meshy 2.0 by +3.57 in Quality and +3.78 in Text Consistency, and surpasses ProlificDreamer by +7.34 in Quality and +16.22 in Text Consistency. On Toys4K, it achieves the best CLIP (27.80 vs. Meshy's 27.10) and the lowest FD_dinov2 (210.23 vs. Meshy's 257.08), while matching Meshy's 3-minute inference time. HunYuan 2.0 is the fastest at 1 minute but trails LEGO-Maker in both CLIP (26.16) and FD (249.75). NeRF-based methods (DreamFusion, Latent-NeRF, ProlificDreamer) are dominated on all quality metrics and are 5–60× slower in inference. The largest relative gains appear in text consistency, where LEGO-Maker's semantic decomposition pipeline yields a clear separation from all competitors, particularly over score-distillation methods like DreamFusion (16.13) and ProlificDreamer (27.38).

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGO-Maker departs from the monolithic text-to-3D paradigm (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, Magic3D) by decomposing generation into semantically functional modules, drawing an explicit analogy to LEGO assembly. It fine-tunes Stable Diffusion for 3D-appropriate image generation and G-SAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) for text-driven semantic segmentation, then delegates per-module 3D reconstruction to off-the-shelf image-to-3D generators (SPAR3D, TRELLIS). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the introduction of a motion-driven binding mechanism that maps semantic components to predefined kinematic parameters (rotation, flipping, translation), enabling interactive 3D models rather than the static meshes produced by all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: current text-to-3D outputs require extensive manual post-processing to be usable, and none support functional interactivity. The modular decomposition strategy is a reasonable architectural choice that could generalize to more complex scenes, though the current implementation is limited to 50 object categories, 5,000 training samples, and three simple motion types with hand-tuned parameters. Open questions include whether the motion dynamics can be learned end-to-end rather than predefined, how the approach scales to multi-object scenes with complex inter-module constraints, and whether the semantic decomposition generalizes beyond the curated ShapeNet/PartNet categories. The finding that the choice of image-to-3D generator (TRELLIS vs. SPAR3D) matters far less than the diffusion retraining step is a useful practical insight for practitioners assembling similar pipelines.
