## Problem

Existing cross-modal UDA methods for 3D semantic segmentation treat modality bias and domain shift as separate, static optimization objectives, overlooking the intrinsic semantic coupling between heterogeneous data. This decoupled paradigm fails under dynamic-coupled shifts (e.g., simultaneous layout and LiDAR density changes) and produces semantically ambiguous representations. The paper asks whether a single unified discrete representation can simultaneously address both modality and domain distribution shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250978/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized 2D RGB images and 3D LiDAR point clouds from both a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, where 3D points are restricted to those within the camera's field of view. A shared encoder (U-Net/ResNet34 for 2D, SparseConvNet with 5 cm voxels for 3D) maps both modalities into a common feature space, which is then quantized into a 128×128 codebook. The output is per-point semantic class predictions from both the 2D and 3D branches, with the cross-modal ensemble (mean of softmax outputs) used as the primary evaluation metric.

## Method

UniDxMD introduces a unified discrete latent space via two key mechanisms. First, a Cluster-Based Soft Quantization Mechanism (CSQM) replaces the non-differentiable argmin of standard VQ with a Gaussian-kernel affinity matrix that selects ⌊K/C⌋ local code vectors per feature (where C is the number of classes), then computes the latent code as a weighted cluster center of those vectors—preserving gradient flow while avoiding codebook collapse. Second, Latent Space Regularization (LSR) learns joint prototypes from all four modality/domain combinations (2D/3D × source/target) through an MLP, constrained by a semantic relation consistency loss that enforces cosine-similarity structure preservation across local prototypes. These joint prototypes serve as learnable anchors that pull code vectors toward semantically coherent clusters via a minimum distribution discrepancy loss with entropy regularization. The overall training objective combines cross-entropy segmentation loss (source only), CSQM quantization and commitment losses, LSR regularization, and a cross-modal mutual learning loss, enabling end-to-end optimization of encoder, codebook, and decoder.

## Results

UniDxMD achieves state-of-the-art cross-modal (xM) mIoU across all four UDA scenarios: 74.3% on nuScenes USA/Sing., 74.6% on nuScenes Day/Night, 58.7% on v.KITTI/Sem.KITTI, and 52.5% on A2D2/Sem.KITTI. The method outperforms the strongest prior cross-modal UDA baseline (FtD++) by 4.5%, 3.6%, 6.1%, and 1.4% in xM mIoU, respectively, and surpasses the original xMUDA by margins of 4.9%–10.5%. On the 3D branch specifically, UniDxMD reaches 68.5% (USA/Sing.) and 71.8% (Day/Night), exceeding all prior methods by at least 1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDxMD outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the xM (cross-modal ensemble) metric across all four UDA scenarios. Against the most competitive cross-modal UDA methods, it leads FtD++ [38] by 4.5% (USA/Sing.), 3.6% (Day/Night), 6.1% (v.KITTI), and 1.4% (A2D2) in xM mIoU, and surpasses MM2D3D [4] by 1.9%, 2.5%, 2.2%, and 6.3% respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on the synthetic-to-real v.KITTI/Sem.KITTI scenario, where UniDxMD gains 10.5% over xMUDA [15] and 9.8% over SSE-xMUDA [47], indicating the unified discrete representation is particularly effective under large distribution gaps. On the 3D branch, UniDxMD achieves the best 3D mIoU in all four scenarios (68.5%, 71.8%, 55.2%, 52.2%), exceeding the next-best 3D scores by 1.7–10.2%. In the VFM-based comparison (Table 4), UniDxMD with a CLIP ViT-Large backbone (UniDxMD-ViT) achieves 63.3% xM on v.KITTI/Sem.KITTI, outperforming dedicated VFM adaptation methods like UniDSeg [36] (62.0%) and VFMSeg [41] (61.0%), while on A2D2/Sem.KITTI it reaches 57.0% xM versus 57.5% for UniDSeg, showing competitive but not dominant performance in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cross-modal UDA for 3D semantic segmentation, where modality adaptation and domain adaptation are treated as separate multi-objective problems solved by tailored modules (adversarial alignment, mutual learning, distillation). Instead, it draws on vector quantization (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017) to argue that both objectives share a common goal—extracting semantically equivalent information from heterogeneous data—and can be unified through a shared discrete latent space. The key methodological departure from standard VQ is the replacement of non-differentiable argmin single-code assignment with a cluster-based soft quantization mechanism (CSQM) that uses weighted local code vectors, coupled with latent space regularization (LSR) that structures the codebook via joint prototypes. The overall framework builds directly on xMUDA's cross-modal mutual learning setup and loss formulation, extending it with the discrete representation layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of cross-modal and cross-domain adaptation into a single discrete representation offers a cleaner conceptual framework than the current patchwork of separate alignment objectives, and the consistent SOTA gains (4.9–10.5% mIoU over xMUDA across four scenarios) validate this framing. The scalability study showing competitive results with a frozen CLIP ViT-Large backbone suggests the discrete representation layer is architecture-agnostic and could be plugged into future foundation-model-based pipelines. Open questions include whether the codebook approach scales gracefully to larger semantic taxonomies (beyond 6–10 classes), how it behaves under more extreme or compound domain shifts, and whether the pseudo-label-based joint prototype sampling remains reliable without post-processing in noisier target distributions.
