## Problem

High-resolution (4K) imagery increasingly produces large pixel displacements even for moderate motion, yet existing optical flow datasets are confined to sub-1K resolutions and displacements under 500 pixels, leaving long-range flow estimation underexplored. Moreover, simply training on high-resolution data does not improve long-range accuracy because the increased number of matching candidates introduces severe ambiguity in cost volume estimation, and no suitable training strategy addresses this challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes two 4K (3840×2160) RGB frames and outputs a dense per-pixel optical flow field. During training, intermediate frames between the source and reference are additionally available and used exclusively for cost volume distillation, but are not required at test time. The RelayFlow-4K dataset also provides stereo image pairs with disparity, match maps (forward-backward consistency), and distance maps (Euclidean distance from occluded pixels to nearest matched pixels) at full 4K resolution.

## Method

The paper introduces RelayFlow-4K, a 4K optical flow dataset with cross-frame annotations up to 4 skips, stereo pairs, and match/distance maps, generated via Blender with temporal stretching to produce large displacements. The core training framework combines three components: (1) matching cost distillation, where an aggregated cost volume is computed as the product of adjacent-frame cost volumes (masked by the match map to suppress occluded regions) and distilled into the direct long-range cost volume via KL divergence; (2) incremental time-step learning, a curriculum that progresses from 0-skip to 4-skip training stages, using a frozen copy of the previous-stage model as the teacher for distillation; and (3) a matched-region distance loss that weights the per-pixel flow loss by the Euclidean distance from unmatched pixels to their nearest matched neighbors, enabling effective learning from occluded regions without discarding them. The approach is plug-and-play, applied on top of RAFT, GMA, GMFlow, and GMFlowNet backbones, reducing long-range EPE by over 40% without requiring intermediate frames at inference.

## Results

On the RelayFlow-4K test set (4K resolution, 3840×2160), GMA-Relay achieves the best overall EPE of 7.96, reducing the base GMA error of 22.08 by 64%. In the most challenging long-range regime (s160+, displacements >160 px), GMA-Relay drops to 173.57 EPE versus 557.18 for GMA, a 69% reduction. RAFT-Relay similarly improves from 18.54 to 9.81 overall EPE and from 466.83 to 230.32 in s160+. On HS-Sintel, RAFT+Ours reaches 1.574 ALL EPE (vs. 2.567 for RAFT alone), while on CVO it achieves 4.310 (vs. 4.445), all without requiring intermediate frames at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Relay training strategy is applied on top of five base architectures (RAFT, GMA, GMFlow, GMFlowNet, and implicitly CRAFT/SKFlow as standalone baselines) and consistently outperforms each base model on RelayFlow-4K: GMA-Relay (7.96 EPE) beats GMA (22.08) by 64%, RAFT-Relay (9.81) beats RAFT (18.54) by 47%, and GMFlow-Relay (8.92) beats GMFlow (15.44) by 42%. Against the two accumulation-based long-range methods that require intermediate frames at inference, the picture is more nuanced: on HS-Sintel, AccFlow (Acc+MF) achieves 1.383 ALL EPE with RAFT, slightly better than RAFT+Ours (1.574), but on CVO the gap widens in favor of AccFlow (2.634 vs. 4.310). However, AccFlow suffers OOM at 4K resolution (3840×2160) and requires 7 frames at inference (1567 ms at 1440p), whereas RAFT+Ours runs in 310 ms with only 2 frames and 15.5 GB memory. The Lim (MF) baseline, which also uses intermediate frames, performs substantially worse than both RAFT+Ours and AccFlow on all three benchmarks (e.g., 23.34 vs. 4.310 on CVO Clean). Overall, Relay matches or exceeds accumulation-based methods on non-occluded regions while being the only approach that scales to 4K without intermediate-frame overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the cost-volume + iterative refinement paradigm established by RAFT and extended by GMA, applying its proposed training framework (matching cost distillation + incremental time-step learning) as a plug-in on top of these and other architectures (GMFlow, GMFlowNet, CRAFT, SKFlow). It departs from prior long-range flow approaches (AccFlow, SlowFlow, Lim) that require intermediate frames at inference by using them exclusively during training via knowledge distillation of aggregated cost volumes. The RelayFlow-4K dataset extends the Spring dataset concept to 4K resolution with cross-frame flow annotations (up to 4 skips) and stereo pairs, filling a gap where existing optical flow benchmarks remain below 1K resolution. The matched-region distance loss introduces a distance-weighted occlusion handling mechanism that goes beyond binary occlusion masks used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As sensor and display technologies push toward 4K and beyond, the inability of current flow models to handle large pixel displacements (>500px) becomes a practical bottleneck for video frame interpolation, tracking, and action recognition. The RelayFlow-4K dataset provides a much-needed high-resolution benchmark with diverse displacement magnitudes, and the architecture-agnostic training framework (KD + curriculum) offers a reusable recipe for any cost-volume-based flow method. A key limitation is that intermediate frames are still required during training, which constrains applicability to real-world video where such frames may not exist. Open questions include whether the curriculum strategy transfers to other temporal prediction tasks and whether the approach generalizes to real (non-CGI) 4K footage with sensor noise and compression artifacts.
