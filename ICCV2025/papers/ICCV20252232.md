## Problem

Glass surface detection remains challenging because glass lacks distinct visual semantic features and is highly context-dependent, yet existing methods rely on small training datasets and limited model capacity, restricting generalization to unseen scenes. No unified foundational model with multi-modal glass recognition capabilities (RGB, RGB-D, RGB-T) had been developed, and standard foundation models like SAM perform poorly on this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image (optionally paired with a depth or thermal map) and a learned text-condition embedding as input. The RGB image is encoded into a latent code via the pre-trained VAE encoder and concatenated channel-wise with an all-zero latent vector (replacing the usual noisy latent) before being fed to the U-Net. The output is a binary glass surface segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution, produced in a single forward pass without iterative denoising.

## Method

GlassWizard adapts Stable Diffusion 2.0 for GSD via a two-stage pipeline. Stage I freezes all SD parameters and trains only a newly introduced text token embedding (initialized from the word 'glass') to build a text-based content prior that captures the implicit semantics of glass surfaces. Stage II trains the full model end-to-end with a one-step formulation: the timestep is fixed at T=999, the input latent is an all-zero vector (eliminating stochastic noise), and a lightweight Cascade Predictor head replaces the VAE decoder to directly output segmentation masks, optimized with weighted IoU and BCE losses. For multi-modal extension, a Modality-Customized Adaptation module fuses the RGB latent with a modality-specific latent (depth or thermal) via cross-attention, with LoRA layers injected into the U-Net to enable rapid adaptation without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the four standard GSD benchmarks, GlassWizard achieves IoU of 0.933 (GDD), 0.928 (Trans10K-Stuff), 0.904 (GSD), and 0.879 (HSO), outperforming the strongest baseline DDP by 2.5–3.9 IoU points across all datasets. In zero-shot evaluation on the unseen VGSD-D video glass detection dataset, the method reaches IoU 0.942 and MAE 0.031, surpassing the best supervised baseline VGSD-Net (IoU 0.802) by 14 IoU points. For multi-modal adaptation, the framework attains IoU 0.667 on RGB-D (TROSD) and MAE 0.022 on RGB-T (RGBT), both exceeding their respective best baselines. Inference runs in 0.177 s per 512×512 image on a single RTX 4090, roughly 80× faster than Marigold's multi-step pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlassWizard outperforms all 26 compared baselines on the four primary GSD benchmarks (GDD, Trans10K-Stuff, GSD, HSO), with the largest margins over the diffusion-based Marigold (IoU gap of 15–30 points) and the strongest non-diffusion baseline DDP (IoU gap of 2.5–3.9 points). On the zero-shot VGSD-D video glass detection task, the method achieves IoU 0.942 versus VGSD-Net's 0.802 (trained on VGSD-D), a 14-point advantage that demonstrates strong generalization. On the RGBP polarization dataset, GlassWizard (IoU 0.792, RGB-only input) is slightly below PGSNet-Polar (IoU 0.811, which uses polarization cues), but still exceeds all RGB-only baselines. For multi-modal adaptation, the framework beats TROSNet by 9.5 IoU points on RGB-D (TROSD) and RGBT by 0.002 MAE on RGB-T (RGBT). On mirror detection (PMD/MSD), GlassWizard surpasses the best baseline SATNet by 8.4 and 5.7 IoU points respectively, confirming transferability to other implicit-surface tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pre-trained Stable Diffusion (SD) architecture for glass surface detection, departing from both traditional GSD methods (Trans2Seg, GSDNet, PGSNet, VBNet) that rely on hand-crafted visual cues and small training sets, and from prior diffusion-for-segmentation approaches (SegDiff, MedSegDiff, DMP) that retain multi-step stochastic denoising. The key architectural departures are: (1) replacing the stochastic multi-step diffusion with a deterministic one-step formulation using a zero-latent initialization, (2) introducing a learnable text embedding as a glass-specific content prior in the SD text encoder space, and (3) substituting the VAE decoder with a lightweight Cascade Predictor head for mask output. The Modality-Customized Adaptation module, combining cross-attention fusion with LoRA, extends the framework to RGB-D and RGB-T settings without retraining the full model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization results—outperforming methods trained specifically on VGSD-D and RGBP by large margins—suggest that the rich image priors in diffusion models capture fundamental visual properties of glass (transparency, reflection, context-dependence) that task-specific training on small datasets fails to learn. The one-step formulation (0.177s inference) makes diffusion-based segmentation practically viable for real-time applications, addressing a key barrier to adopting generative priors in dense prediction. Open questions include whether the one-step deterministic formulation is truly optimal versus a small number of steps, whether the text-based prior approach generalizes beyond glass to other 'implicit' material classes, and whether the framework scales to higher resolutions or video settings without the temporal modeling that dedicated video GSD methods employ.
