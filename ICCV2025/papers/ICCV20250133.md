## Problem

Existing language-conditioned manipulation benchmarks (RLBench, Calvin, LIBERO) focus primarily on skill learning with template-based instructions and fail to evaluate the capabilities that foundation-model-based approaches actually require: world knowledge transfer, implicit semantic understanding, long-horizon multi-step reasoning, and cross-category generalization. VLABench addresses this by introducing 100 task categories specifically designed to stress-test pretrained VLA models, foundation-model workflows, and VLMs on dimensions previously unmeasured in robotics benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250133/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For VLA evaluation, the model receives multi-view RGB images (four camera views) and natural language instructions containing implicit human intentions (e.g., 'I'm thirsty' rather than 'pick up the cola'), producing continuous end-effector action sequences. For VLM evaluation, four-view images are augmented with segmented numerical annotations and fed alongside instructions; the model outputs an operation sequence in a domain-specific language (DSL) that serves as a callable skill API. Evaluation produces three metrics: Success Rate (SR), Intention Score (IS, measuring proximity to the correct target object with ε=0.15m), and Progress Score (PS, fraction of completed sub-tasks in long-horizon episodes averaging 500+ timesteps).

## Method

VLABench comprises 60 primitive and 40 composite tasks across six capability dimensions (mesh/texture recognition, spatial reasoning, common sense/world knowledge, semantic understanding, physical law, and logical reasoning), with 2000+ 3D objects and strong domain randomization. An automated data collection framework replaces teleoperation by leveraging a task-specific skill library, RRT-based motion planning with slerp quaternion interpolation, Bezier curve trajectory smoothing, and prior information (point clouds, annotated grasp points, target entities) to generate 200+ trajectories per task. The evaluation protocol defines six tracks for VLAs (from in-distribution transfer to cross-domain and long-horizon generalization), a zero-shot protocol for foundation-model workflows (VoxPoser, CoPA), and a DSL-mediated pipeline for VLMs that compares generated operation sequences against ground truth using four weighted metrics. Task difficulty is quantified via a composite score combining variant count, sub-step count, and reasoning-step count, yielding a difficulty level of 75.96—substantially higher than prior benchmarks.

## Results

Across six evaluation tracks, the best-performing VLA (π₀-Base) achieves only 13.2% success rate on Track 1 (in-distribution) and drops to 0% on Track 5 (cross-domain transfer), with long-horizon composite tasks (Track 6) yielding just 2.0% SR. The intention score (IS) peaks at 59.6% for π₀-Base on Track 1, indicating the model can often identify the correct target but fails to execute the action. All five evaluated VLAs (Octo, OpenVLA, RDT-1B, π₀-Base, π₀-Fast) achieve 0% SR on cross-domain transfer, and the overall difficulty level of VLABench (75.96) far exceeds prior benchmarks (e.g., RLBench 28.86, LIBERO 17.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five fine-tuned VLAs, π₀-Base leads on Tracks 1–3 (13.2%, 6.0%, 5.2% SR) and Track 6 (2.0% SR), while π₀-Fast outperforms it on Track 4 (6.8% vs. 3.6% SR), suggesting the flow-matching variant handles semantically rich instructions better. Octo is the weakest, achieving only 2.0% SR even on the in-distribution Track 1. All models uniformly fail on Track 5 (cross-domain transfer) with 0% SR, indicating no meaningful behavioral generalization beyond the training task distribution. For zero-shot workflow baselines, Voxposer (with GPT-4V) reaches progress scores of 30–40 on basic tasks but degrades sharply on long-horizon and physical-reasoning tasks; CoPA shows similar limitations in task decomposition. Among VLMs evaluated via the DSL pipeline, GPT-4o is the strongest overall but still scores only ~20 on the reasoning dimension for non-GPT models; notably, the open-source Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct surpasses GPT-4o on certain dimensions (e.g., spatial understanding), yet all VLMs struggle with long-horizon task decomposition and implicit semantic goals.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLABench carves new ground in the language-conditioned manipulation (LCM) benchmarking landscape by departing from the skill-centric, template-instruction paradigm of RLBench, Calvin, and LIBERO. Rather than building on a single prior method, it synthesizes the evaluation needs of two parallel research threads—pre-trained VLA models (RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, RDT-1B, π₀) and foundation-model workflows (VoxPoser, CoPA)—into a unified benchmark that tests dimensions those threads have not jointly addressed: world-knowledge transfer, implicit semantic goal extraction, and long-horizon multi-step reasoning. The automated data-collection pipeline (RRT + Bezier smoothing + skill library) extends the teleoperation-based collection of ARNOLD and LIBERO into a fully scalable, prior-information-driven framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's central finding—that even the strongest current VLAs (π₀, OpenVLA) achieve near-zero cross-domain transfer (Track 5) and success rates below 13% even on the easiest track—challenges the assumption that VLA pre-training on large-scale robotics data yields LLM-like emergent generalization. This opens a critical question: whether the gap is fundamentally architectural (discrete action spaces, limited multimodal fusion) or a data-scaling problem, and whether the 'emergent capabilities' narrative from NLP transfers to embodied control. For practitioners, VLABench provides the first standardized 6-track protocol to diagnose *which* capability dimension (vision, language, knowledge, spatial, physical, reasoning) a given model fails on, making it a likely reference point for future VLA development.
