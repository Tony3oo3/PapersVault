## Problem

Existing content-aware layout generation methods rely on a single-step reasoning paradigm where the model produces a final layout in one forward pass. This lack of a feedback-based self-correction mechanism causes performance to degrade sharply as the number of elements to arrange grows, particularly in complex scenes with more than 8 elements. The paper addresses this by introducing a hierarchical, iterative refinement strategy inspired by human fast/slow thinking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252274/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a background image and a conditional constraint specifying which element types to place, and produces a set of element bounding boxes (type, left, top, width, height) along with a natural-language evaluation of the layout's design quality. Unusually, the fine-level refinement module also receives a rendered intermediate image (the background with the coarse layout visually overlaid) as a visual prompt, so the model can 'see' its own draft before correcting it. At inference, the refinement step can be iterated multiple times, though one pass is the default setting.

## Method

SEGA decomposes layout generation into a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a Coarse-level Estimation (CE) module built on LLaVA-1.5 first predicts a rough layout from the background image and instruction, then a Fine-level Refinement (FR) module—initialized from the CE weights—iteratively improves it. The FR module is trained on a curated mixture of CE outputs and perturbed ground-truth layouts to cover the full quality spectrum, and is supervised to first emit an Evaluation Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) that checks the current layout against four explicit design principles (no element overlap, proper underlay coverage, no background-object occlusion, and alignment) before predicting the corrected coordinates. This auto-regressive evaluation-then-prediction structure injects design prior knowledge directly into the generation process. The authors also contribute GenPoster-100K, a 105K-poster dataset with layer-parseable, artifact-free components and multi-grained metadata, which serves as a pre-training corpus that improves cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On the most challenging Crello dataset (7B model), SEGA achieves Und_s of 0.8978 and Ove of 0.0040, substantially outperforming PosterLlama (Und_s 0.7378, Ove 0.0238) and FlexDM (Und_s 0.5034, Ove 0.1139). SEGA also leads on all four GPT-4V aesthetic scores, reaching S_Mean of 5.941 vs. 5.542 (PosterLlama) and 4.950 (FlexDM). On PKU and CGL, SEGA 7B attains Und_s of 0.9731 and 0.9782 respectively, surpassing the best non-LLM baseline RALF (0.8981 and 0.9652) by 8.5 and 1.3 percentage points. The 13B variant further improves to Und_s 0.9270 on Crello, confirming scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEGA dominates all five baselines on the Crello dataset across nearly every graphic metric: it reduces overlay (Ove) from 0.0238 (PosterLlama) and 0.1139 (FlexDM) to 0.0040 (7B) / 0.0025 (13B), and lifts strict underlay accuracy (Und_s) from 0.7378 (PosterLlama) to 0.8978 (7B) / 0.9270 (13B). Against the strongest LLM baseline PosterLlama, SEGA wins on 10 of 12 rule-based and aesthetic metrics on Crello, losing only marginally on Occ (0.3964 vs 0.4041) and S_IO (5.708 vs 5.819). On PKU and CGL, SEGA 7B outperforms RALF on Ove (0.0033 vs 0.0095 on PKU; 0.0017 vs 0.0059 on CGL) and Und_s (0.9731 vs 0.8981; 0.9782 vs 0.9652), but RALF retains an edge on content metrics Read and Occ for CGL (0.0180 vs 0.0294; 0.1263 vs 0.2430), which the authors attribute to inpainting artifacts in CGL backgrounds. The non-LLM baselines CGL-GAN and LayoutDM are consistently outperformed on all available metrics across both PKU and CGL. SEGA also surpasses its own ensemble baseline (Ens-2) on 11 of 12 metrics, demonstrating that the iterative refinement strategy is more effective than simple multi-sample voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEGA departs from the dominant single-step paradigm in LLM-based layout generation (PosterLlama, RALF) by introducing a hierarchical coarse-to-fine architecture inspired by Kahneman's fast/slow thinking distinction. The CE module follows the standard MLLM instruction-tuning recipe (built on LLaVA-1.5), while the FR module is initialized from CE weights and trained on intermediate outputs plus perturbed layouts, effectively creating a self-correction loop. The incorporation of explicit design principles as an Evaluation Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) mechanism draws on the broader CoT literature for LLMs but applies it specifically to graphic layout critique. The GenPoster-100K dataset extends prior poster benchmarks (CGL, PKU, Crello) by providing layer-parseable, artifact-free components with structured multi-grained metadata.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode of current MLLM-based layout generators—significant performance degradation when element counts exceed 8—and demonstrates that a simple two-stage refinement loop with design-principle feedback can recover much of the lost quality. The GenPoster-100K dataset (105K posters with structured metadata) is a practical resource that shows cross-dataset generalization advantages over CGL pre-training, potentially reducing data collection costs for downstream layout tasks. Open questions include whether the iterative refinement paradigm generalizes to other sequential design tasks (web pages, slides), whether more than one refinement round ever becomes beneficial at scale, and whether the approach extends to unconstrained layout generation where element types are not pre-specified.
