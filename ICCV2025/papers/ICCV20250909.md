## Problem

Existing music-driven dance generation methods, particularly those built on Bailando's cross-conditional causal attention, capture only local music-pose correspondences and fail to leverage global rhythmic structures, resulting in poor beat alignment and unnatural motion dynamics. Explicit beat-synchronization approaches like Beat-It impose rigid control signals that constrain pose diversity. The paper seeks a balanced framework that achieves precise rhythmic alignment without sacrificing movement naturalness and expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a music audio sequence and an initial pose code as input. The music is processed via STFT to extract phase angles, which are center-cropped to match the downsampled temporal length T' = T/λ. Upper and lower body poses are separately encoded into discrete code tokens via two pretrained Pose VQ-VAEs. The output is a 20-second 3D dance pose sequence in SMPL format at 60 FPS, generated autoregressively by predicting pose codes from separate upper-body and lower-body codebooks, then decoded back to continuous poses.

## Method

Danceba introduces three modules: (1) Phase-Based Rhythm Extraction (PRE) applies STFT to music features, extracts phase angles via Angle(·), center-crops them to the target temporal length, and linearly projects them into rhythm-aware embeddings that are added to the original music/pose features, decoupling rhythm from semantic content. (2) Temporal-Gated Causal Attention (TGCA) computes the element-wise product of standard cross-conditional causal attention output with a gating signal SiLU(Linear(X)), explicitly prioritizing global rhythmic patterns and preventing the next-token prediction from losing historical beat context. (3) Parallel Mamba Motion Modeling (PMMM) employs three parallel Mamba blocks (for music, upper body, lower body) each with a GateMlp residual, separately modeling distinct upper/lower-body dynamics while incorporating rhythm-aware representations. Training uses supervised cross-entropy loss on predicted pose code probabilities at each timestep for both body halves.

## Results

On the AIST++ dataset, Danceba achieves the best FIDk of 11.67 (48.68% lower than the previous best Bailando++ at 22.74), the highest DIVk of 8.52 (7.0% above E3D2's 7.96, even surpassing Ground Truth at 8.19), the highest DIVg of 7.55 (16.3% above E3D2's 6.49), and the best Beat Align Score of 0.2714 (12.0% above LODGE's 0.2423, exceeding Ground Truth's 0.2374). On FIDg, Danceba scores 11.90, ranking third behind E3D2 (8.94) and Bailando++ (11.58), but compensates with substantially higher geometric diversity. Averaged over five independent runs, Danceba maintains FIDk of 15.48 and BAS of 0.2779, confirming robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Danceba decisively outperforms all 10 baselines on FIDk, DIVk, DIVg, and BAS, with the largest margin over Bailando++ (48.68% lower FIDk: 11.67 vs. 22.74) and LODGE (12.0% higher BAS: 0.2714 vs. 0.2423). On FIDg, Danceba (11.90) trails E3D2 (8.94) and Bailando++ (11.58), placing it third, but it more than compensates with 19.0% higher DIVg (7.55 vs. 6.34) over Bailando++. Against the most recent baselines—E3D2 (AAAI 2024) and LODGE (CVPR 2024)—Danceba wins on every metric except FIDg, where E3D2 leads by 3.0 points. Notably, Danceba surpasses the Ground Truth on both diversity metrics (DIVk 8.52 vs. 8.19; DIVg 7.55 vs. 7.45) and BAS (0.2714 vs. 0.2374), indicating that the generated dances are more diverse and rhythmically aligned than the original recordings. Older baselines (Li et al., DanceRevolution, DanceNet, FACT) are far behind on all metrics, with FIDk values ranging from 35 to 86 compared to Danceba's 11.67.

## Relation to Prior Work

Danceba extends the Bailando [30] autoregressive framework (Pose VQ-VAE + cross-conditional causal attention) by inserting three targeted modifications: Phase-Based Rhythm Extraction (STFT phase angles) to decouple rhythm from semantic music features, a Temporal-Gated Causal Attention (TGCA) that multiplies C³ Attention outputs by a learned gate to enforce global beat alignment, and Parallel Mamba Motion Modeling that replaces Transformer layers with separate Mamba blocks for upper and lower body. It explicitly departs from Enhancing-Bailando's reliance on generic pretrained audio encoders (MERT) and from Beat-It's rigid explicit beat-synchronization, arguing that implicit rhythm-aware gating achieves alignment without sacrificing diversity. The Mamba integration follows the broader trend of state-space models for motion generation (Motion Mamba, InfiniMotion) but is the first to apply parallel body-part Mamba specifically to music-driven dance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gating-enhanced rhythm representation offers a practical, lightweight mechanism for beat-dance alignment that could transfer to other music-conditioned generation tasks (e.g., audio-driven full-body animation, instrument performance). However, the method still relies on a simple linear projection of raw music features rather than pretrained audio encoders, and inherits Bailando's VQ-VAE quantization bottleneck, both of which the authors acknowledge as limiting fine-grained musical understanding and motion expressiveness. Open questions include whether pretrained music encoders (MERT, CLAP) combined with the PRE gating would yield further gains, whether hierarchical or adaptive codebooks could close the remaining FIDg gap, and whether the parallel Mamba design generalizes to multi-person or style-conditioned dance synthesis.
