## Problem

Current Large Vision-Language Models are bottlenecked by low input resolution (typically 224–448), preventing them from matching task-specific experts in dense, fine-grained tasks like multi-object detection and counting. Additionally, existing object-referring approaches rely on either singular visual prompts (lacking conversational flexibility) or textual descriptions (suffering from ambiguity in complex scenes), leaving no unified mechanism for user-friendly, precise object specification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252651/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a high-resolution RGB image (resized to 1022×1022, yielding 1369 visual tokens after downsampling), an optional visual referring image (a cropped screenshot of the target object, encoded via its EVA-CLIP [CLS] token), and free-form text including natural-language descriptions or explicit top-left/bottom-right coordinates. Outputs are bounding box coordinates expressed as plain textual numerical values (no special tokens or dedicated heads), natural-language descriptions, and integer object counts, all generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

Griffon v2 replaces image-partitioning strategies with a direct high-resolution pipeline: a trainable EVA2-CLIP-L/14 encoder (upscaled from 336 to 1022 via bilinear position-embedding interpolation) extracts full-image features, which are compressed by a single strided convolution (kernel 3, stride 2) followed by a linear projection into the LLM's word-embedding space, preserving global context while reducing token redundancy. For visual-language co-referring, a frozen EVA-CLIP region encoder produces a [CLS] token for the user-provided referring image, projected into the same embedding space and inserted at a <region> placeholder in the instruction, enabling category-level visual grounding alongside textual and coordinate-based references. The system is trained in three stages: (I) projector-only alignment on 558K image-text pairs, (II) full-network multi-task pre-training on 12M instances spanning detection, REC/REG, visual grounding, counting, and non-existence judgment, and (III) instruction tuning on 900K diverse prompts with the visual encoders frozen to prevent forgetting. The down-sampling projector consumes only 461 MB of memory versus 416 GB for a Flamingo-style resampler, while achieving substantially higher detection mAP under identical training budgets.

## Results

On MSCOCO val2017 object detection, Griffon v2 achieves mAP 38.5 (AP50 54.3, AP75 41.2), surpassing specialist detectors Faster R-CNN-FPN (mAP 37.9) and DAB-DETR (mAP 38.0) with only 1 training epoch at 1022 resolution. On FSCD-LVIS object counting, it achieves MAE 20.3 and NAE 0.5, outperforming the specialist Counting-DETR (MAE 23.5, NAE 0.6). On RefCOCO/+/g referring expression comprehension, Griffon v2 reaches 90.0 average ACC@0.5, and on ODINW visual grounding it achieves 42.0 average mAP, exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 5.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Griffon v2 outperforms all specialist detection baselines (Faster R-CNN-FPN at mAP 37.9, DAB-DETR at 38.0) with a single training epoch, though it trails the heavily trained Pix2Seq (mAP 43.0, 300 epochs) and DETR (mAP 42.0, 500 epochs). Among generalist LVLMs, it surpasses Lumen by 3.2 mAP points and dramatically exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-7B (16.2) and InternVL2.5-8B (11.9), whose partition-based high-resolution designs lose edge context. On object counting (FSCD-LVIS), Griffon v2 (MAE 20.3, NAE 0.5) beats the specialist Counting-DETR (MAE 23.5, NAE 0.6) and FSDetView (MAE 29.0), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL2.5-8B struggle in dense scenarios. For REC, Griffon v2 (90.0) outperforms G-DINO-L (86.6), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (86.6), and Ferret-13B (85.6); on ODINW grounding it leads all generalists (42.0 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 37.0) but remains below the specialist G-DINO-L (52.4). In the ablation against partition-based methods, Griffon v2 exceeds Monkey by 6.7 mAP on detection and matches or slightly beats LLaVA-NeXt on VQA benchmarks (VQAv2 81.9 vs. 80.9, ChartQA 69.8 vs. 62.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

Griffon v2 directly extends Griffon v1 (Zhan et al., 2024) by replacing its 448-resolution two-layer linear projector with a high-resolution visual encoder (EVA2-CLIP-L/14@336 upscaled to 1022) paired with a strided-convolution down-sampling projector, and by adding a plug-and-play visual tokenizer for co-referring. Architecturally it follows the LLaVA paradigm (visual encoder → projector → LLM) but departs from the partition-based high-resolution strategies of Monkey, LLaVA-NeXt, and Sphinx, arguing that direct encoding with token compression preserves global context and edge details that patch-splitting loses. The visual-language co-referring mechanism builds on CLIP's [CLS] token for category-level visual prompting and extends prior singular-prompt approaches (arrows, marks, textual coordinates) into a unified multi-modal referencing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generalist LVLM to quantitatively surpass specialist models on both object detection (mAP 38.5 vs. 38.0 for DAB-DETR) and dense object counting (MAE 20.3 vs. 23.5 for Counting-DETR), suggesting that resolution-aware unified architectures can close the gap with task-specific experts. The ablation showing a simple strided-convolution projector (28.4 mAP) dramatically outperforming the Flamingo resampler (9.6 mAP) challenges the assumption that complex learnable-token resampling is necessary for high-resolution token compression. Open questions include whether the 1022-resolution ceiling (imposed by Llama2's 4096-token limit) can be pushed further with longer-context LLMs, whether the co-referring paradigm generalizes to video or multi-image settings, and whether the counting advantage holds across more diverse dense scenes beyond FSCD-LVIS.
