## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based open-vocabulary semantic methods supervise 3D semantic fields by projecting them into 2D views and comparing against CLIP features extracted from SAM masks. However, occlusion, motion blur, and view-dependent variations cause the same object to receive inconsistent semantic features across viewpoints, and these inconsistencies propagate into the 3D field, producing artifacts and degraded segmentation in novel views. Prior work enforces geometric consistency but overlooks explicit alignment of 2D semantic features across views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images and a text query at inference. SAM is prompted with a uniform 32×32 grid to generate dual-scale masks (subpart/part and whole/part), which are then tracked across views by SAM2 for cross-view association. The output is a 3D Gaussian semantic field where each Gaussian carries an 8-dimensional language feature vector; at query time, rendered per-pixel features are decoded through an MLP+softmax into an N-class codebook distribution, and the retrieved prototype is compared against the text embedding via cosine similarity to produce a pixel-wise relevance map that is thresholded into a segmentation mask.

## Method

CCL-LGS introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) SAM generates two aggregated mask sets per view, (2) SAM2 propagates K reference masks to all frames and assigns each mask a category label via IoU > 0.5 matching, and (3) a Contrastive Codebook Learning (CCL) module distills consistent semantics. The CCL module maintains a learnable codebook of N prototypes and applies three losses: a matching loss (L_max) pulling each feature toward its nearest prototype, a pull loss enforcing cosine similarity between prototypes of same-category features, and a push loss (with margin m=0.7) separating prototypes of different-category features. These low-dimensional codebook indices then supervise the 3DGS semantic field: per-Gaussian 8-dim features are alpha-blended into 2D maps, decoded by a lightweight MLP+softmax into an N-way distribution, and trained against the codebook index map via cross-entropy. This design replaces autoencoder-based feature compression with explicit contrastive structure, ensuring intra-class compactness and inter-class distinctiveness even when input masks are incomplete or noisy.

## Results

On the LERF dataset (four indoor scenes with severe occlusion and motion blur), CCL-LGS achieves a mean IoU of 65.6, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art 3D VL-GS (62.0) by 3.6 points and the next-best LangSplat (51.4) by 14.2 points. On the 3D-OVS dataset (four outdoor scenes with minimal occlusion), CCL-LGS achieves 95.2 mIoU, slightly trailing 3D VL-GS (96.9) by 1.7 points, which the authors attribute to 3D VL-GS's data-enrichment strategy being more advantageous on simpler scenes. The method ranks first or second across all four LERF scenes individually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL-LGS outperforms all six baselines on the LERF dataset, with the largest margin over LangSplat (+14.2 mIoU) and Feature-3DGS (+19.9 mIoU), and a clear 3.6-point advantage over the strongest competitor 3D VL-GS. On 3D-OVS, CCL-LGS (95.2) is surpassed only by 3D VL-GS (96.9), while still beating LangSplat (93.2) by 2.0 points and GOI (90.5) by 4.7 points. The gap over 3D VL-GS on 3D-OVS is attributed to that method's data-enrichment pipeline being more effective on the simpler, less occluded outdoor scenes. Among the earlier 3DGS-based baselines (Feature-3DGS, LEGaussians, GS-Grouping), CCL-LGS leads by 17–20 mIoU on LERF, highlighting the benefit of explicit cross-view semantic alignment over direct CLIP feature projection. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both the pull and push contrastive losses contribute: removing either degrades performance by 3–6 mIoU relative to the full model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based language field paradigm established by LangSplat and subsequent works (LEGaussians, GOI, 3D VL-GS), which all supervise 3D semantic Gaussians by projecting them to 2D and matching against CLIP features. The key departure is explicitly addressing cross-view semantic inconsistency caused by occlusion, blur, and view-dependent variation—issues that prior methods handle only implicitly through 3D geometric constraints. CCL-LGS introduces a SAM2-based zero-shot tracker for cross-view mask association and a Contrastive Codebook Learning module that enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class distinctiveness, moving beyond the direct CLIP-on-mask supervision used by all prior 3DGS semantic methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-view semantic inconsistency problem is a genuine bottleneck for all projection-supervised 3D semantic methods, and the CCL module offers a plug-in-style solution that could benefit other 3DGS semantic pipelines. However, the gains are moderate (3.6 mIoU on LERF) and the method actually underperforms 3D VL-GS on the simpler 3D-OVS dataset, suggesting the benefit is most pronounced in challenging real-world conditions. A key open question is how much of the improvement comes from the contrastive codebook versus the SAM2-based mask association, and whether the approach scales to scenes with many more object categories than the codebook capacity N allows.
