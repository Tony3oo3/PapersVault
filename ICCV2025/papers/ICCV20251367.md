## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors rely on forgery-specific artifacts and global features, causing them to overfit to known manipulation types and miss subtle local inconsistencies such as blending boundaries. This limits cross-domain generalization when encountering unseen deepfake techniques. The paper addresses both the local sensitivity gap (inability to capture fine-grained patch-level anomalies) and the global generalization gap (overfitting to specific forgery domains).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251367/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes face video clips of T=12 consecutive frames, each cropped to 224×224 via RetinaFace, and produces a video-level real/fake binary prediction alongside per-patch forgery probabilities. Four clips are sampled per video for both training and testing. The patch-level output is structured as P independent binary classification scores per frame, derived from dividing each frame into non-overlapping patches of size H/√P × W/√P, with a threshold on manipulated pixel count determining ground-truth patch labels.

## Method

DeepShield fine-tunes CLIP-ViT with a parameter-efficient ST-Adapter and introduces two complementary components. Local Patch Guidance (LPG) uses Spatiotemporal Artifact Modeling (SAM) to synthesize blended fake videos with precise manipulation masks, then applies patch-level binary cross-entropy supervision so each patch token learns to discriminate local forgery traces, strengthening the class token's aggregation of fine-grained semantics. Global Forgery Diversification (GFD) synthesizes diverse forgery embeddings via Domain Feature Augmentation: Domain-Bridging Feature Generation (DFG) mixes channel-wise mean/std from different forgery types using AdaIN with Beta(0.1,0.1) sampling, while Boundary-Expanding Feature Generation (BFG) scales per-channel standard deviation by α=1.1 to push features beyond known domain boundaries. The combined training objective is ω·L_LPG + L_cls + υ·L_supCon, where the supervised contrastive loss enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class dispersion across original, SAM-blended, and DFA-synthesized samples.

## Results

On cross-dataset evaluation (trained on FF++ HQ, tested on four unseen datasets), DeepShield achieves the best AUC on three of four unseen datasets: 92.2% on CelebDF v2, 93.2% on DFDC Preview, and 82.8% on DFDC, outperforming the previous best methods (TALL at 90.8%, SeeABLE at 86.3%, and VB-StA at 77.8%) by 1.4%, 6.9%, and 5.0% respectively. On DFD it ties StyleFlow at 96.1%, while on the in-domain FF++ test set it scores 99.2% (slightly below TALL's 99.9%). In cross-manipulation evaluation, DeepShield achieves the highest average AUC across all four training manipulation types (e.g., 95.54% average when trained on FaceSwap), surpassing the second-best method WATCHER by up to 15.95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepShield outperforms all 17 compared baselines on the three most challenging unseen datasets: it beats SeeABLE (86.3%) by 6.9% on DFDCP, VB-StA (77.8%) by 5.0% on DFDC, and TALL (90.8%) by 1.4% on CelebDF v2. On DFD, DeepShield ties StyleFlow at 96.1% AUC, while on the in-domain FF++ test it trails TALL (99.9%) by 0.7%. Among frame-based methods, LSDA is the strongest competitor (91.1% on CDF, 77.0% on DFDC, 95.6% on DFD), but DeepShield surpasses it on every dataset where both report results. In cross-manipulation evaluation (Table 2), DeepShield dominates DCL, UIA-ViT, and WATCHER across all four manipulation types; the largest margin is when trained on FaceSwap, where DeepShield achieves 95.54% average AUC versus WATCHER's 79.59% (a 15.95% gap). The consistent advantage over CLIP-based VB-StA (the closest architectural competitor) highlights the benefit of the proposed LPG and GFD components over simple spatiotemporal adapter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepShield builds on the CLIP-ViT backbone adapted for video via ST-Adapter (Pan et al., NeurIPS 2022), following the trend of leveraging pre-trained vision-language models for deepfake detection (e.g., VB-StA). Its Spatiotemporal Artifact Modeling (SAM) directly extends the image-level self-blending strategy of SBI (Shiohara & Yamasaki, CVPR 2022) to the video domain, adding temporal consistency constraints. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior feature-space augmentation methods like LSDA, which rely on simple linear interpolation between domain features, by introducing distribution-based mixing (AdaIN) and boundary expansion to generate more diverse forgery representations. The local-to-global paradigm—combining patch-level binary supervision with global contrastive training—is the paper's core architectural departure from both frame-level detectors and prior video-level approaches that treat the clip as a monolithic input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain generalization remains the central unsolved problem in deepfake detection, and DeepShield's simultaneous treatment of local artifact sensitivity and global domain invariance offers a practical recipe that outperforms prior SOTA by 5–7 AUC points on the hardest unseen datasets (DFDCP, DFDC). The parameter-efficient CLIP + ST-Adapter backbone makes the approach relatively lightweight compared to training large 3D CNNs from scratch. However, the method still depends on synthetic blending (SAM) and feature-space augmentation (DFA) that may not capture artifacts from rapidly evolving generators (e.g., diffusion-based face editing), and the evaluation is limited to face-manipulation datasets without testing on full-body or non-facial forgeries. An open question is whether the patch-level supervision signal from SAM generalizes to forgeries that lack clear spatial blending boundaries, such as those produced by end-to-end generative models.
