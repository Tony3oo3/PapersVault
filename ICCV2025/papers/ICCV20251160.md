## Problem

Existing 3D shape editing methods are either slow optimization-based approaches with noisy gradients and topology constraints, or multi-view diffusion + LRM pipelines that suffer from view-consistency artifacts and occlusion ambiguity. No prior method can perform fast, controllable, topology-unconstrained edits (e.g., genus changes) on an existing mesh from a single 2D image edit in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6–8 masked multi-view renders (512×512) of the source mesh, where a randomly generated 3D rectangular occluder is rendered from each camera to produce multi-view-consistent masks, plus one clean edited image from a canonical viewpoint serving as the conditional signal. Camera poses are encoded as 6-channel Plücker ray grids at the same spatial resolution as the RGB images. The output is a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from a predicted SDF, along with volumetric RGB renders and normal maps estimated from SDF gradients.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional LRM trained with a 3D-consistent multi-view masking strategy: a random rectangular mesh is rendered from all input cameras, and patches containing occluded pixels are replaced with a learnable mask token (rather than dropped), preserving 3D spatial context. The architecture patchifies images and Plücker rays into tokens, adds Plücker tokens after masking so the model can distinguish occluded patches, and uses a clean conditional image as cross-attention input while masked views and triplane tokens form the self-attention sequence. The transformer body iterates self-attention and cross-attention blocks, and the output triplane tokens are upsampled and decoded via MLPs into SDF and RGB values for volumetric rendering. Training proceeds in two stages (256→384 resolution, 128→512 samples/ray) with L2 reconstruction, LPIPS, and normal-map losses, where geometric supervision is introduced only in stage 2 to prevent texture-based degenerate solutions. At inference, a user defines a 3D editing region, edits one canonical view, and the model propagates the 2D edit into 3D in a single forward pass, enabling topology-changing edits impossible for optimization-based methods.

## Results

On the ABO dataset, MaskedLRM (8 views) achieves 28.65 PSNR, 0.947 SSIM, and 0.078 LPIPS, outperforming MeshLRM (26.09 PSNR, 0.898 SSIM, 0.102 LPIPS) by 2.56 dB in PSNR. On GSO, MaskedLRM (8 views) reaches 27.58 PSNR and 0.933 SSIM, slightly trailing MeshLRM's 27.93 PSNR but matching or exceeding it on SSIM and LPIPS. In terms of runtime, MaskedLRM completes editing in under 3 seconds, making it 2–10× faster than LRM-based editing methods (InstantMesh at 30 s, PrEditor3D at 80 s) and orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods (TextDeformer ~20 min, MagicClay ~1 hr).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeshLRM (the SoTA LRM for mesh reconstruction), MaskedLRM wins decisively on ABO (28.65 vs 26.09 PSNR, +2.56 dB) and is competitive on GSO (27.58 vs 27.93 PSNR, −0.35 dB) while matching or exceeding SSIM and LPIPS. Against InstantMesh, MaskedLRM substantially outperforms on GSO (27.58 vs 22.79 PSNR for mesh output), as expected since InstantMesh reconstructs from a single view. In editing quality (Figures 5–6), MaskedLRM produces multi-view-consistent, realistic edits that optimization methods (TextDeformer, MagicClay) fail to achieve due to noisy SDS gradients and topology constraints, and that concurrent multi-view diffusion methods (Instant3DiT, PrEditor3D) produce with visible artifacts and missing detail. On runtime, MaskedLRM (<3 s) is ~10× faster than InstantMesh (30 s), ~27× faster than PrEditor3D (80 s), and ~400–1200× faster than optimization baselines, while still matching SoTA reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) line of work (MeshLRM, InstantMesh) by introducing a conditional branch and a 3D-consistent multi-view masking strategy during training, adapting the masked autoencoder paradigm from 2D image modeling to 3D geometry inpainting. It departs from optimization-based editing methods (MagicClay, TextDeformer) that rely on noisy SDS gradients and cannot perform topology changes, and from multi-view diffusion + LRM pipelines (InstantMesh, PrEditor3D, Instant3DiT) that suffer from view-consistency artifacts in occluded regions. The key conceptual departure is replacing random MAE-style patch dropout with structured occlusions from a randomly rendered 3D mesh, forcing the model to learn 3D-consistent inpainting rather than 2D patch completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-forward-pass editing (sub-3 seconds) with SoTA-level reconstruction quality offers a practical, controllable alternative to both slow optimization loops and artifact-prone multi-view diffusion pipelines, and uniquely enables topology-changing edits (genus changes) that are fundamentally inaccessible to deformation-based methods. Limitations include dependence on the quality of the 2D edit in the canonical view, Marching Cubes resolution capping geometric detail, and blurry artifacts on fine structures like faces. Open questions include improving spatial localization of edits, merging edited regions with the original triangulation to avoid seams, and whether the 3D-consistent masking strategy generalizes to more complex or non-rectangular editing regions.
