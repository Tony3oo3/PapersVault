## Problem

SAM 2, while highly effective for video object segmentation, cannot be directly applied to referring video object segmentation because (1) its architecture has no mechanism to translate textual descriptions into effective segmentation prompts, and (2) its online memory-based decoding only provides a historical view, lacking the global context awareness required for offline RVOS where the full video is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of T frames (resized to 1024×1024 for SAM 2's image encoder and 224×224 for the BEiT-3 multimodal encoder) and a natural-language description tokenized via XLM-RoBERTa. It produces per-frame binary segmentation masks at the target object's spatial resolution. The mask prior generator operates at patch resolution (H_m/p × W_m/p) and upsamples the pseudo masks via linear interpolation to align with SAM 2's feature dimensions before feeding them as dense prompts.

## Method

MPG-SAM 2 adapts SAM 2 for RVOS through two key modules. First, a mask prior generator (MPG) leverages a BEiT-3 multimodal encoder to jointly encode video and text, then applies spatiotemporal self-attention on unfolded video patch embeddings and cross-attention between [CLS] tokens and video embeddings to produce frame-specific pseudo masks (dense prompts) and a global video feature. Second, a hierarchical global-historical aggregator (HGA) injects global context into SAM 2's online decoder at two levels: at the pixel level, the current frame feature undergoes memory attention followed by global multi-head attention with the global video feature; at the object level, mask tokens perform cross-attention with the global video feature and then with historical mask tokens from the memory bank. The multimodal [CLS] tokens are additionally projected through an MLP to form sparse prompts, and a novel mask-text similarity loss enforces pixel-level consistency between the cosine similarity of text embeddings and predicted masks versus ground-truth masks.

## Results

On Ref-YouTube-VOS, MPG-SAM 2 achieves 73.9% J&F (71.7% J, 76.1% F), surpassing the previous best DsHmp (67.1% J&F) by 6.8% and LoSh (67.2% J&F) by 6.7%. On Ref-DAVIS17, it reaches 72.4% J&F, outperforming VISA (70.4%) by 2.0% and VD-IT (69.4%) by 3.0%. On the more challenging MeViS dataset, MPG-SAM 2 attains 53.7% J&F, exceeding DsHmp (46.4%) by 7.3% and VISA (44.5%) by 9.2%, demonstrating consistent superiority across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPG-SAM 2 outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset. On Ref-YouTube-VOS, it leads DsHmp (67.1%) by 6.8% J&F and LoSh (67.2%) by 6.7%, and even surpasses MUTR (68.4%), which uses additional training data, by 5.5%. On Ref-DAVIS17, it beats VISA (70.4%)—itself trained on extra datasets—by 2.0% J&F and VD-IT (69.4%) by 3.0%. On MeViS, the margin is largest: MPG-SAM 2 (53.7%) exceeds DsHmp (46.4%) by 7.3% and VISA (44.5%) by 9.2%, indicating the framework's particular strength in handling motion-centric expressions. The gap over earlier Transformer-based methods (ReferFormer 62.9%, SgMg 65.7%, SOC 66.0%) is 7–11% J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS, confirming that the mask prior generator and hierarchical global-historical aggregator provide substantial gains over prior query-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts SAM 2 (Ravi et al., 2024) to referring video object segmentation by borrowing the early vision-language fusion strategy from EVF-SAM (Zhang et al., 2024), which uses a unified BEiT-3 encoder to jointly encode video and text. It departs from prior SAM-based RVOS attempts (RefSAM, AL-Ref-SAM 2) that relied on independently encoded text prompts or multi-stage GPT-4/Grounding DINO pipelines, instead generating frame-specific mask priors directly from aligned multimodal features as dense prompts. The hierarchical global-historical aggregator extends SAM 2's online memory mechanism with a global context channel, addressing a gap that prior RVOS methods (ReferFormer, SgMg, DsHmp) handled through custom temporal modeling architectures rather than through the SAM 2 memory paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.8–7.3% J&F gains over prior SOTA on Ref-YouTube-VOS and MeViS are substantial for this field, and the mask-prior-as-dense-prompt strategy offers a reusable recipe for injecting semantic positional guidance into any promptable segmentation backbone. The mask-text similarity loss provides an additional training signal that directly constrains segmentation to be text-consistent, which could transfer to other referring tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences without the BEiT-3 embedding-size constraint, whether the 21M-parameter overhead is acceptable for real-time deployment, and whether the hierarchical aggregation can be replaced by lighter-weight global context injection.
