## Problem

Physical cameras cannot capture every memorable moment, yet existing sketch-to-image methods fail to faithfully reconstruct real-world scenes from the multi-concept freehand sketches and textual descriptions that humans naturally produce from memory. Training-based approaches require large-scale paired data and generalize poorly to idiosyncratic sketches, while training-free 'snapshot' methods that integrate all concepts simultaneously suffer from mutual interference, missing concepts, and misaligned spatial relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250238/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual description followed by a sequence of N freehand sketches (512×512), where each sketch encodes a distinct concept and the processing order mirrors the user's natural readout sequence. The output is a single photorealistic RGB image that integrates all concepts with correct spatial layout and appearance consistency. The framework is entirely training-free, operating on a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) without any fine-tuning or paired data.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sequence-Aware Sketch-Guided Diffusion framework that processes subjective readouts individually and sequentially rather than jointly. It begins with text-reward optimization to establish an initial latent and extract an appearance prior via style encoding. Each sketch is then individually encoded through VAE encoding followed by iterative diffusion-inversion optimization to bridge the domain gap between abstract line drawings and realistic forms. During sequential concept integration, three loss terms guide optimization: a spatial loss using cross-attention maps from the optimized sketch latent for layout control, an appearance loss preserving the style established by text-reward optimization, and a preserve loss with exponential decay (γ=0.9) that maintains previously incorporated concepts. This sequential, concept-wise strategy mirrors human step-by-step scene construction and avoids the least-squares degradation inherent in simultaneous multi-concept integration.

## Results

On the CMC dataset, Subjective Camera 1.0 achieves the best FID (11.56), CLIP-T (66.64), and HPS (0.850) among all methods. On the FMC dataset, it achieves the best FID (10.18), CLIP-T (74.25), HPS (0.862), and CLIP-I (0.797), outperforming the closest training-free competitor FreeControl (FID 11.17, CLIP-I 0.775) and all training-based baselines. The method is entirely training-free, requiring no paired data or fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Subjective Camera 1.0 outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among training-free methods, FreeControl [17] is the closest competitor, achieving FID 11.17 and CLIP-I 0.775 on FMC, but our method leads by 0.99 FID, 7.58 CLIP-T, 0.103 HPS, and 0.022 CLIP-I. PNP [27] shows competitive HPS (0.840/0.852) but trails significantly in FID (17.01/16.26) and CLIP-I (0.593), indicating poor spatial alignment. Multi-Sketch [4] achieves the second-best FID on FMC (11.59) but lags in CLIP-T (72.02 vs. 74.25) and CLIP-I (0.690 vs. 0.797). Training-based methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet) consistently underperform, with FID values 3–8 points higher and CLIP-I scores 0.08–0.18 lower on FMC, confirming that fine-tuning on paired data introduces overfitting to sketch lines and poor generalization to freehand inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on reward-based latent noise optimization (RENO, PARROT) for text alignment and training-free spatial control methods (FreeControl, Multi-Sketch, PNP) for sketch conditioning, but departs from both by rejecting the 'snapshot' paradigm of jointly optimizing all concepts simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is a sequence-aware, concept-wise sequential optimization that mirrors human step-by-step scene construction, combined with a diffusion-inversion-based sketch encoding to bridge the domain gap between rough sketches and realistic imagery. Unlike training-based approaches (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet) that require large-scale paired data, the framework is entirely training-free, and unlike prior training-free sketch-to-image methods, it explicitly handles multi-concept interference through ordered latent propagation and a decay-weighted preserving loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'subjective camera' framing opens a practical direction for preserving unrecorded experiences without physical cameras, and the training-free sequential optimization strategy is broadly applicable to any multi-condition generation task where concepts arrive incrementally. However, the authors acknowledge significant limitations: reconstruction fidelity still falls short of ground truth due to the gap between diverse T2I priors and specific scenes, and results are sensitive to sketch order and quality. Open questions include whether personalizing priors with user-specific galleries can close the fidelity gap, and whether computational tools can guide users toward more effective readout sequences—both deferred to future iterations.
