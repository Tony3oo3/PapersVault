## Problem

Existing multi-body human-object interaction (HOI) synthesis methods are restricted to specific configurations (single hand+object, one person+object, two hands+one object) and cannot generalize to arbitrary numbers of bodies. Even when a single diffusion model is used to jointly model all body motions, synchronization is only implicitly captured, leading to interpenetration, contact loss, and asynchronous interactions. Additionally, high-frequency semantic interactions (e.g., periodic frictions) are overshadowed by low-frequency large-scale movements in time-domain denoising.

## Input / Output

The method takes action labels, object categories, object geometries (encoded via Basis Point Sets), and skeleton shape parameters β as conditions, and produces a complete multi-body motion sequence without requiring predefined object trajectories or HOI keyframes. The internal representation is a high-order vector concatenating all individual motions (3D joint positions for articulated skeletons; translation + quaternion for rigid bodies) and all pairwise relative motions, with dimension D_sum = 7m + 3Dn + 7m(m−1) + 3Dmn. Only the individual motion components are used as final output; relative motions serve as auxiliary synchronization signals.

## Method

SyncDiff formulates multi-body HOI synthesis as a diffusion model on a graphical model where nodes are individual body motions and edges are pairwise relative motions. Two synchronization mechanisms are introduced: (1) during training, a mathematically derived alignment loss L_align penalizes the discrepancy between generated relative motions and those computed from individual motions, acting as alignment scores analogous to data sample scores; (2) during inference, an explicit synchronization step is performed every s steps (s=50, T=1000), applying a closed-form maximum-likelihood update that blends the predicted mean with relative-motion-derived estimates for both individual and relative motions, with a reduced noise scale σ′. A frequency decomposition via FFT separates all motions into low-frequency (dc) and high-frequency (ac) components; the model denoises the high-frequency part in the frequency domain (supervised in time domain) to prevent semantically important periodic interactions from being lost. The backbone is a latent diffusion transformer conditioned on CLIP text features, BPS geometry features, padding masks, timestep embeddings, and shape parameters.

## Results

On TACO (2 hands + 2 objects), SyncDiff achieves 73.00% CSIoU and 73.28% RA on the seen test set, outperforming MACS (56.81% CSIoU, 58.40% RA) and DiffH2O (62.29% CSIoU, 61.40% RA). On CORE4D (2 humans + 1 object), SyncDiff reaches 92.89% RA versus 68.02% (OMOMO) and 70.05% (CG-HOI), with FID of 6.45 vs. 13.22 and 12.16. On OAKINK2 (2 hands + 1–3 objects), SyncDiff scores 72.14% CSIoU and 74.83% RA, beating MACS (57.52%, 54.91%) and DiffH2O (55.50%, 50.48%). The paper reports an average RA improvement of over 15% over state-of-the-art methods across all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncDiff outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and every reported metric. Against MACS [70] and DiffH2O [13] on hand-object tasks (TACO, GRAB, OAKINK2), SyncDiff leads by 10–20 percentage points in CSIoU and 10–25 points in RA; for example, on OAKINK2 it achieves 72.14% CSIoU vs. 57.52% (MACS) and 55.50% (DiffH2O). On the human-object task (CORE4D), SyncDiff's 92.89% RA substantially exceeds OMOMO [42] (68.02%) and CG-HOI [20] (70.05%), while also halving their FID (6.45 vs. 13.22/12.16). On GRAB's post-grasping phase, SyncDiff matches DiffH2O-UNet's diversity (OD 0.188 m) while achieving higher CSR (7.2% vs. 6.4%) and RA (88.9% vs. 87.5%), and clearly surpasses MACS (CSR 3.9%, RA 76.4%) and IMoS [24] (CSR 5.0%, RA 58.8%). The consistent wins across all configurations—ranging from 1 hand + 1 object to 2 humans + 1 object—support the paper's claim of a unified framework that generalizes across arbitrary body counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of configuration-specific HOI synthesis methods (DiffH2O for two hands + one object, MACS for bimanual manipulation, CG-HOI/OMOMO for human-object) under a single diffusion model operating on a graphical model where nodes are individual body motions and edges are relative motions. It draws on motion synchronization theory from control (graph-based coordination, SE(3) attitude synchronization) to derive alignment scores and an explicit inference-time synchronization step, and borrows frequency-domain decomposition from GID to handle high-frequency interaction semantics. The key departure from prior diffusion-based HOI work is the explicit, mathematically grounded synchronization mechanism rather than relying solely on implicit correlations learned by a single high-dimensional denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By handling arbitrary numbers of humans, hands, and rigid objects in one framework, SyncDiff removes the need for configuration-specific architectures and priors, which is a practical step toward general-purpose interaction synthesis for VR/AR and robot learning. The finding that frequency decomposition without proper synchronization actually degrades contact quality (worse than removing all components) highlights a non-trivial coupling between representation choices and synchronization that the field has not previously addressed. Open questions include scalability to very large body counts, extension to deformable objects, and whether the explicit synchronization step can be made real-time for interactive applications.
