## Problem

Burst super-resolution has proven effective for reducing noise and enhancing resolution in standard RGB images, but its application to polarization imaging remains unexplored due to the absence of dedicated training datasets and reliable ground-truth noise statistics. Polarization cameras employ double Bayer-patterned sensors that halve light efficiency and spatial resolution, amplifying noise in derived polarization properties (DoLP, AoLP) far beyond what RGB noise models capture. Without a physically grounded noise characterization and a purpose-built benchmark, polarization-aware super-resolution models cannot be trained or evaluated.

## Input / Output

The input consists of 14 low-resolution hand-held burst frames captured simultaneously with a FLIR BFS-U3-51S5PC-C polarization camera (35 mm lens), each frame containing four linear polarization channels (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) in a double Bayer pattern. The output is a 2× super-resolved polarization image decomposed into three maps: s0 (total intensity), DoLP (degree of linear polarization), and AoLP (angle of linear polarization). Ground truth for PolarBurstSR is the per-pixel mean of 10,000 static burst captures from PolarNS, yielding a noise-suppressed reference at full sensor resolution; the 160-scene dataset is split 112/16/32 for training, validation, and testing.

## Method

The paper contributes two datasets and an analytical noise model. PolarNS captures 54 darkroom objects (10,000 frames each) and 190 indoor/outdoor scenes (~1,000 frames each) under static illumination to compute per-pixel mean and variance of the raw polarization channels. The noise model, built on a shot-plus-read Gaussian sensor model, derives that Stokes vector component variance scales linearly with s0, that DoLP follows a Rician distribution parameterized by true DoLP and the s0-to-noise ratio, and that AoLP follows a symmetric Gaussian-like distribution shaped by the s_pol-to-noise ratio—validated against 10,000-frame histograms in the darkroom. For benchmarking, five state-of-the-art BSR architectures (MFIR, BSRT, Burstormer, FBANet, BurstM) are retrained on PolarBurstSR with polarization-specific channel handling (Bayer-pattern-aware demosaicking, s0-based optical flow) and compared against their RGB-pretrained counterparts, demonstrating that polarization-tailored training yields substantial gains in DoLP and AoLP fidelity even when s0 PSNR improvements are modest.

## Results

On the real PolarBurstSR dataset, polarization-trained models substantially outperform their RGB-pretrained counterparts: p-BSRT achieves 45.69 dB PSNR and 0.979 SSIM on s₀, while p-FBAnet leads on polarization metrics with 26.92 dB DoLP PSNR and 18.14 dB AoLP PSNR. On the synthetic dataset, p-BSRT attains the highest overall PSNR (43.75 dB) and s₀ SSIM (0.979), with a +11.80 dB PSNR gain over its RGB-pretrained version. The PolarNS noise dataset achieves a PSNR of 80.50 dB against the noise-reduced reference, far exceeding other polarization datasets (e.g., Spectro-polarimetric at 67.37 dB), confirming its superior noise characteristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five burst SR architectures (MFIR, BSRT, Burstormer, FBANet, BurstM) under two training regimes: RGB-pretrained vs. polarization-trained (p-). Polarization training consistently improves performance: p-BSRT gains +11.80 dB PSNR on synthetic and +1.54 dB on real data over RGB-BSRT, while p-FBAnet gains +5.07 dB PSNR and +2.64 dB DoLP PSNR on real data. Among polarization-trained models, p-BSRT achieves the best s₀ PSNR (45.69 dB real, 43.75 dB synthetic) and s₀ SSIM (0.979), but p-FBAnet dominates on polarization-specific metrics (26.92 dB DoLP PSNR, 18.14 dB AoLP PSNR on real data), outperforming p-BSRT by +1.75 dB and +1.08 dB respectively. Burstormer and BurstM show the weakest polarization-trained results, with p-BurstM actually degrading AoLP PSNR by −0.32 dB on real data relative to its RGB version. The paper also compares noise levels across five existing polarization datasets (KAUST, RSP, MCubeS, Spectro-polarimetric), finding PolarNS has the lowest noise (0.056×10⁻³ mean std.dev., 80.50 dB PSNR) by a wide margin over the next best (Spectro-polarimetric at 0.346×10⁻³, 67.37 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the burst super-resolution (BSR) framework established by Bhat et al. [7] to the polarization imaging domain, where no dedicated training or evaluation dataset previously existed. It departs from prior polarization noise analysis, which relied on first-order Taylor expansions or assumed additive white Gaussian noise in Stokes components, by deriving a physically grounded noise model from the shot-and-read noise characteristics of snapshot polarization sensors. The BSR models evaluated (MFIR, BSRT, Burstormer, FBANet, BurstM) are all originally designed for RGB images and are adapted here to handle the four-channel polarization Bayer pattern, highlighting the absence of polarization-native architectures in the BSR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale polarization noise statistics dataset (PolarNS) and the first polarization BSR benchmark (PolarBurstSR), this work removes a critical barrier to developing and evaluating polarization-aware super-resolution methods. The derived noise model (Rician for DoLP, Gaussian-like for AoLP) offers a physically interpretable framework for quantifying measurement confidence in downstream polarization tasks such as shape-from-polarization and reflection removal. Key limitations include the restriction to static scenes and static illumination, which precludes evaluation on dynamic content, and the reliance on a single camera model (FLIR BFS-U3-51S5PC-C), leaving open whether the noise model generalizes across hardware. The finding that polarization-specific training yields substantially larger gains than simply scaling RGB training data suggests that future work should focus on polarization-native architectures rather than direct transfer from RGB BSR methods.
