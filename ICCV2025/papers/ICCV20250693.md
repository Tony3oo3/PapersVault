## Problem

Standard adversarial distillation for Vision-Language Models relies on untargeted adversarial examples that only push inputs away from their original class, exploring a limited portion of the teacher's decision space and failing to transfer out-of-distribution (zero-shot) robustness to lightweight students. The paper identifies a fundamental trade-off: untargeted adversaries improve in-distribution robustness while targeted adversaries improve out-of-distribution robustness, but naive targeted optimization collapses into local optima within a single category's decision region, limiting diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 for CLIP) paired with text prompts in the format 'This is a photo of a [CLASS]' as input, with perturbations constrained to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε_I = 2/255. The teacher is a large CLIP ViT-L/14 model and the student is a lightweight ViT-B/32 or ResNet-50/101 model. Outputs include zero-shot class predictions across 15 datasets, image-text retrieval scores (Flickr30k), and image captions (Nocaps), evaluated under PGD, CW, and AutoAttack adversaries at multiple perturbation radii.

## Method

MOO-AD formulates adversary generation as a two-objective optimization problem where F1 = Σ c·[p_S(x̂)]_c (expected predicted class) and F2 = C − F1 (its complement), with constraints ensuring misclassification by both teacher and student. A decomposition-based solver using augmented Tchebycheff scalarization with diverse preference vectors generates a set of n=10 Pareto-optimal adversaries per sample, each traversing a distinct path across decision boundaries. The distillation loss (MOPA) aligns student and teacher predictions via KL divergence on both clean samples and MOO-adversaries, while a Group-wise Distribution Matching term using MMD with a kernel metric enforces higher-order statistical consistency across the adversarial prediction set. Theoretically, the paper proves that objective-space diversity implies sample-space diversity under Lipschitz continuity (Theorem 1) and that incorporating MOO-adversaries into training minimizes a tighter upper bound on robust risk when the boundary-risk gain κ ≥ R_nat(D).

## Results

On the primary zero-shot classification benchmark (ViT-L teacher → ViT-B student, averaged over 15 datasets), MOO-AD achieves 58.96% clean accuracy and 35.70% robust accuracy under 20-step PGD (ε=2/255), outperforming the strongest adversarial fine-tuning baseline FARE (56.68% clean, 30.94% robust) by +2.28 and +4.76 points respectively. Against the vanilla CLIP baseline (64.90% clean, 3.85% robust), MOO-AD closes the clean-accuracy gap to within 6 points while boosting robust accuracy by over 9×. Across ResNet-50 and ResNet-101 students, MOO-AD similarly leads all baselines on both clean and robust metrics (e.g., ResNet-101: 49.82% clean, 29.91% PGD-robust).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOO-AD consistently outperforms all three adversarial fine-tuning baselines (TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE) across every architecture and attack type. On ViT-B, MOO-AD leads FARE (the strongest baseline) by +2.28% clean and +4.76% PGD-robust accuracy, and leads TeCoA by +10.13% clean and +8.37% PGD-robust. The gap over FARE narrows on ResNet-101 (+1.66% clean, +7.59% PGD-robust), where FARE's clean accuracy is closer but its robustness degrades sharply (22.32% PGD vs. MOO-AD's 29.91%). Against vanilla CLIP (no adversarial training), MOO-AD sacrifices ~6 points of clean accuracy (58.96 vs. 64.90) but gains 31.85 points of PGD-robust accuracy (35.70 vs. 3.85), demonstrating that the distillation framework transfers robustness without the catastrophic clean-accuracy collapse seen in direct adversarial fine-tuning of the student.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adversarial knowledge distillation [22, 26] to the VLM setting, departing from prior adversarial fine-tuning methods (TeCoA [34], FARE [38], PMG-FT [43]) that rely solely on untargeted adversaries and dataset one-hot guidance. The key conceptual departure is formulating adversary generation as a multi-objective optimization problem using augmented Tchebycheff scalarization [51], combining the disruptive capability of untargeted adversaries with the decision-boundary-spanning property of targeted adversaries. The distillation loss (MOPA + GDM) builds on standard KL-divergence-based knowledge distillation [25] but extends it to align predictions on diverse MOO-generated adversaries and enforce higher-order distributional matching via MMD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: scaling laws tie adversarial robustness to model size, making lightweight VLMs inherently less robust, yet the paper shows that standard adversarial distillation fails to transfer zero-shot (out-of-distribution) robustness. The MOO-AD framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism (robust risk upper bound minimization) for closing this gap, with consistent gains across ViT, ResNet, BLIP, and medical imaging settings. Open questions include whether the MOO formulation generalizes beyond CLIP-style contrastive VLMs to generative VLMs or LLMs, whether the Lipschitz continuity assumption in Theorem 1 holds in practice for deep networks, and whether the training-time overhead of generating n diverse adversaries per sample limits scalability to very large teacher-student pairs.
