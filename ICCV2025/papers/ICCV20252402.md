## Problem

Comics and manga present a significant accessibility barrier for visually impaired readers because their narratives rely heavily on visual storytelling. Prior manga-to-text systems (Magi, Magiv2) could extract dialogue transcripts and link them to speakers, but they omitted scene descriptions, character actions, and emotional cues that visually impaired readers explicitly desire. This paper addresses the gap by generating rich, literary prose that captures the full narrative depth of manga pages, not just their spoken content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252402/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-resolution manga page image (resized to 768×768 px) paired with a task-specific text prompt as input. Magiv3 outputs a sequence of autoregressive tokens encoding bounding boxes (quantized to 1000 bins) for panels, characters, texts, and speech-bubble tails in manga reading order (top-to-bottom, right-to-left), followed by binary association scores between element pairs. Downstream, the pipeline produces per-panel captions with interleaved character-grounding boxes and a final page-level literary prose narrative, with style and length controllable via zero-shot prompting of the LLM.

## Method

Magiv3 is a unified multimodal encoder-decoder transformer (12-layer encoder and decoder, 1024 hidden dim, 16 heads) initialized from Florence-2 weights, using a DaViT dual-attention vision transformer as the image encoder and a word-embedding layer for task prompts. Detection is formulated as graph generation: the model autoregressively predicts box location tokens and class tokens as nodes, while three lightweight MLP association heads compute binary edge scores (text–character, character–character, text–tail) from paired class-token embeddings, avoiding the quadratic token blowup of explicit edge prediction. OCR and character grounding are handled by re-running the same weights with different prompts, with grounding boxes linked back to global character identities via IoU-based bipartite matching. Captioning and prose generation are performed zero-shot by off-the-shelf VLMs and LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o for captions, DeepSeek-R1 for prose), which receive the structured Magiv3 outputs—transcript, captions, and grounded character references—as organized context. The entire pipeline thus requires only two models: Magiv3 for all structural/relational tasks and a single instruction-tuned VLM/LLM for all generative tasks.

## Results

On detection, Magiv3 achieves the best F1 scores across most element types: 0.85 on Characters (PM-S), 0.93 on Texts (PM-S), and 0.88 on Tails (PM-S), outperforming Magiv2 (0.81, 0.93, 0.87) and Florence-2 (0.70, 0.62, –). For association, Magiv3 leads on character clustering (0.6884 AMI on PM-S) and text-to-tail (0.9911 AP on PM-S), though Magiv2 retains a slight edge on speaker diarisation (0.7499 vs. 0.7241 AP). On the PopCaptions benchmark, Magiv3-cg achieves 0.74 F1 for character grounding (vs. 0.28 for Florence-2), and the full pipeline scores 3.61/5 on LLM-judged prose quality, marginally surpassing GPT-4o's end-to-end baseline (3.57/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magiv3 outperforms all prior unified or specialised manga-analysis models on detection: it beats Magiv2 by 4 F1 points on Characters (0.85 vs. 0.81 on PM-S) and by 1–2 points on Tails, while matching Magiv2 on Texts (0.93) and trailing by 1 point on Panels (M109: 0.92 vs. 0.93 for Magi). Florence-2, a general-purpose VLM, is substantially weaker on manga-specific detection (0.70 F1 on Characters vs. 0.85), confirming the need for domain-specific models. In association tasks, Magiv3 leads on character clustering (0.6884 AMI vs. 0.6745 for Magiv2) and text-to-tail linking (0.9911 vs. 0.9838 AP), but Magiv2 retains a 2.6-point advantage on speaker diarisation (0.7499 vs. 0.7241 AP). For character grounding on PopCaptions, Magiv3-cg (0.74 F1) more than doubles Florence-2's grounding score (0.28 F1). In the end-to-end prose comparison, the structured pipeline (3.61/5) slightly edges out GPT-4o's direct end-to-end generation (3.57/5), while GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro remain the strongest individual captioners (3.66 and 3.62/5 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manga transcript generation line of work (Magi, Magiv2) by unifying detection, association, OCR, and character grounding into a single Florence-2-inspired encoder-decoder (Magiv3), replacing the prior multi-model pipeline of separate DETR, crop-embedding, OCR, and topological-sorting components. It then extends the output from dialogue transcripts to full literary prose by adding zero-shot panel captioning and character-grounded descriptions, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior transcript-only approaches that miss scene context and emotional cues. The character grounding step, which links caption phrases to bounding boxes via IoU-based bipartite matching, is a new sub-task not present in earlier manga analysis systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical accessibility motivation (making manga readable to visually impaired audiences) gives the work clear real-world impact, and the released PopCaptions dataset (3,374 character-grounded panel captions plus page-level prose) provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating VLMs on stylized, narrative-driven media. However, character grounding accuracy drops significantly in complex multi-character scenes due to training data imbalance and annotation quality issues, and the zero-shot captioning/prose pipeline remains dependent on proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini) for best results. Open questions include whether domain-specific fine-tuning could close the gap with proprietary models, whether the unified architecture generalizes to non-manga comic styles (American, European), and how to improve grounding in crowded scenes without large-scale annotated data.
