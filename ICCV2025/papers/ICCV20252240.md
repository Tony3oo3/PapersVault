## Problem

Zero-shot instance segmentation requires producing class-specific pixel-level masks for objects never seen during training, yet existing approaches either demand retraining for new instances or lack the ability to assign specific object identities. Prior model-based methods (e.g., CNOS, SAM-6D) decouple region proposal generation from template matching in a two-stage pipeline, which limits their ability to jointly reason about instance separation and object identity. This paper asks whether a conditional generative diffusion process can unify instance disambiguation and template matching end-to-end.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a set of rendered object templates (N_T views per object, rendered from an icosphere around the CAD model and encoded with a frozen DINOv2 backbone) as conditioning. It outputs per-object binary instance segmentation masks at the original image resolution. The architecture is resolution- and object-count-agnostic: the coarse model processes the full image with up to 12 objects, while the refine model operates on a single-object crop at potentially higher resolution (e.g., 420×560). At inference, test-time ensembling averages masks across multiple stochastic diffusion passes (N_ensemble × N_aug forward passes) with optional spatial augmentations.

## Method

OC-DiT performs latent diffusion over a β-VAE-compressed representation of binary masks, sidestepping the mismatch between Gaussian diffusion assumptions and Bernoulli-distributed segmentation targets. The denoiser is a DiT-style transformer where each query token (noisy latent + DINOv2 image features) cross-attends only to the template tokens of its assigned object, then self-attends across all query tokens after a reshape—this token rearrangement is key to learning per-object commitment while reducing cross-attention cost. Noise-level conditioning uses adaLN modulation with Fourier-embedded log-noise values. Two dedicated model variants are trained: a coarse model (multi-object, biased toward positive samples) for initial proposals and a refine model (single-object, exposed to false positives) for precision on cropped regions. Training uses only synthetic renders (GSO and Objaverse meshes via BlenderProc, ~1M samples total) with a log-uniform noise schedule over σ ∈ [0.002, 80], and a random-interval positional encoding strategy enables extrapolation to more objects at test time than were seen during training.

## Results

On the BOP challenge benchmark for model-based 2D segmentation of unseen objects, OC-DiT (refined) achieves an Average Precision (AP) of 71.7 on YCBV, 59.4 on TUDL, 40.1 on LMO, and 61.5 on HomebrewedDB (HB). The coarse model alone already reaches 68.6 AP on YCBV, surpassing all prior methods, while the refinement stage adds further gains particularly on TUDL (+26.9 AP) and LMO (+10.5 AP). The method is evaluated with 12 conditioning objects at test time (trained on 8) and uses 18 diffusion steps with test-time ensembling (8 ensembles × 5 augmentations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OC-DiT (refined) clearly outperforms all three primary baselines on YCBV, achieving 71.7 AP versus NIDS (65.0), SAM6D (60.5), and CNOS (59.9) — a margin of 6.7 AP over the strongest competitor. On TUDL, OC-DiT also leads with 59.4 AP, edging out NIDS (55.6) and SAM6D (56.9) by 3.8 and 2.5 AP respectively. However, the method underperforms on LMO (40.1 AP vs. SAM6D's 46.0 and NIDS's 43.9), which the authors attribute to training-data bias toward larger objects. On HB, OC-DiT (61.5) trails NIDS (62.0) and LDSeg (62.2) by a narrow margin while still beating SAM6D (59.3) and CNOS (51.1). Against the two additional baselines MUSE (67.2/56.5/47.8/59.7) and LDSeg (64.7/58.7/47.8/62.2), OC-DiT wins on YCBV and TUDL but loses on LMO and HB, indicating that the diffusion-based approach excels on datasets with fewer, more distinct objects but struggles with the smaller, more cluttered LMO scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OC-DiT extends the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion to the domain of object-conditioned instance segmentation, adapting the Gaussian-diffusion formulation to binary masks via a β-VAE latent space. It departs from prior zero-shot instance segmentation methods (CNOS, SAM6D, NIDS) that follow a two-step pipeline of region proposal extraction followed by template matching, instead unifying hypothesis generation and feature matching into a single end-to-end generative process. The cross-attention design, where each object query attends only to its own template tokens before global self-attention, is a key architectural departure from standard DiT that enforces per-object commitment during the denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a conditional latent diffusion model, trained exclusively on synthetic data, can achieve state-of-the-art zero-shot instance segmentation on real-world BOP benchmarks without any target-domain retraining, opening a new methodological direction for the task. The two-stage coarse/refine design and test-time ensembling provide practical recipes, but the multi-second-to-minute inference times (up to 400+ seconds at higher resolutions) severely limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the diffusion formulation can be made more efficient (fewer steps, distillation), how to scale to larger object counts without the quadratic attention cost, and whether the strong dependence on training data diversity can be mitigated with better synthetic scene generation.
