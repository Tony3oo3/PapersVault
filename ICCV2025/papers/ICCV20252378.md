## Problem

Autoregressive image generation faces a fundamental trade-off: discrete token models (e.g., RAR, VAR) are easy to optimize but suffer quantization-induced information loss that caps reconstruction fidelity, while continuous token models (e.g., MAR, FlowAR) avoid quantization but struggle with the unbounded, high-dimensional continuous space, leading to out-of-distribution artifacts and higher generation FID. No existing AR method simultaneously achieves low reconstruction FID and low generation FID.

## Input / Output

At training, the model consumes pre-tokenized 256×256 ImageNet images: discrete tokens from a MaskGIT VQ tokenizer and continuous tokens from a VA-VAE (LightningDiT) tokenizer, with a random subset of continuous tokens masked for prediction. At inference, the input is a sequence of discrete tokens produced by a pre-trained RAR-XXL model (with classifier-free guidance), which conditions the continuous AR transformer; the output is a 256×256 RGB image decoded from the predicted continuous tokens. The discrete tokens are never decoded directly—they serve purely as structural conditioning signals.

## Method

DisCon decomposes continuous image generation into two sequential subproblems via the factorization p(x_c) = Σ p(x_c|x_d) p(x_d): a discrete AR model (cross-entropy trained) predicts structural discrete tokens, and a continuous AR model (built on MAR-L, modified to accept discrete token conditioning) predicts fine-grained continuous tokens via an intermediate latent z refined by a lightweight diffusion head. The key insight is that discrete tokens capture most essential image structure, so the conditional distribution p(x_c|x_d) is far simpler to model than the marginal p(x_c), reducing optimization difficulty while preserving the representational richness of continuous tokens. Training uses a diffusion loss on the continuous branch conditioned on ground-truth discrete tokens; inference chains RAR-XXL (discrete generation) followed by only 16 AR steps of the continuous model, yielding a 5× speedup over MAR's 256 steps. The modular design allows independent swapping of tokenizers, the continuous AR transformer, and the discrete AR model.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, DisCon-L (558M params) achieves a gFID of 1.38 and rFID of 0.28, setting the new state-of-the-art among all autoregressive image generation models. DisCon-B (427M params) also achieves gFID 1.41 with the same rFID of 0.28. Both variants outperform the strongest discrete AR model (RAR-XXL, gFID 1.48 with 1.5B params) and the strongest continuous AR model (MAR-H, gFID 1.55 with 943M params), while requiring only 16 AR steps versus 256 for MAR, yielding roughly 5× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCon-L (gFID 1.38, 558M) outperforms the best discrete AR model RAR-XXL (gFID 1.48, 1.5B) by 0.10 gFID while using roughly 3× fewer parameters, and surpasses the best continuous AR model MAR-H (gFID 1.55, 943M) by 0.17 gFID with fewer parameters. Against other continuous AR baselines, DisCon-L beats HART-d30 (gFID 1.77, 2.0B) by 0.39, FlowAR-H (gFID 1.65, 1.9B) by 0.27, and ACDIT-H (gFID 2.37, 954M) by 0.99. Among diffusion models, DisCon-L trails only LightningDiT (gFID 1.35, 675M) by 0.03, while surpassing REPA (1.42), MDTv2 (1.58), SiT (2.06), and DiT (2.27). Notably, DisCon-L achieves the best recall (0.64) among all AR models and matches LightningDiT's recall, indicating strong diversity. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that conditioning quality matters: using RAR-XXL as the discrete token generator yields gFID 1.38, whereas weaker RAR-B or RAR-L produce only marginal gains (1.87–1.91), confirming that the discrete AR model's strength directly translates to final generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCon extends the continuous autoregressive framework of MAR (Li et al., 2025) by introducing discrete tokens as high-level conditional signals rather than generation targets, and leverages a pre-trained discrete AR model (RAR-XXL, Yu et al., 2024) to produce those conditioning tokens at inference. This departs from both the discrete AR line (RAR, VAR, MaskGIT), which treats quantized tokens as final outputs and suffers fidelity loss, and the continuous AR line (MAR, FlowAR, ACDIT), which directly models unbounded continuous distributions and faces optimization difficulty. The key conceptual shift is reframing the joint distribution p(x_c) as a mixture over discrete modes p(x_c|x_d)·p(x_d), turning a hard continuous density estimation problem into a simpler conditional one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisCon achieves the best gFID (1.38) among autoregressive image generators on ImageNet-256 while requiring only 16 AR steps versus 256 for MAR, yielding roughly a 5× inference speedup. Its autoregressive formulation preserves native compatibility with LLMs, a practical advantage over diffusion-based competitors for multimodal integration. Open questions include whether the discrete-conditioning strategy scales to higher resolutions or other modalities, how sensitive performance is to the quality of the discrete AR backbone (ablations show weaker RAR variants yield diminishing returns), and whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified into a single end-to-end model.
