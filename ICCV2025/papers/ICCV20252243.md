## Problem

Consistent editing across multiple in-the-wild images remains challenging because existing training-free methods rely on implicit attention features that are unstable and inaccurate under diverse poses, lighting, and backgrounds, while learning-based approaches lack proper regularization and struggle with paired data. No prior method effectively enforces edit uniformity across uncontrolled real-world images in a training-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images captured under arbitrary real-world conditions (varying poses, lighting, occlusions) along with a text prompt describing the desired edit, and produces consistently edited versions of all input images. Correspondence between image pairs is pre-computed by external extractors (e.g., DIFT, Dust3R) and cached via MD5 hashing to avoid redundant computation. The editing is performed through BrushNet or ControlNet reference networks on top of Stable Diffusion with a DDIM scheduler over 50 steps, with the correspondence-guided manipulations applied only from the 4th to 40th denoising steps and from the 8th attention layer onward to preserve generative priors.

## Method

Edicho is a training-free, plug-and-play method that injects explicit image correspondence into the diffusion denoising process at two levels. First, Corr-Attention warps query features from a source image to the target based on pre-computed correspondence, then queries the source's keys and values instead of the target's own, effectively borrowing attention features to enforce consistency. Second, Corr-CFG modifies the unconditional branch of classifier-free guidance by fusing the source's unconditional noise prediction into the target's via a correspondence-guided injection function (inspired by Null-text Inversion), with tunable parameters λ and γ balancing generative prior preservation against edit consistency. The key insight is that explicit correspondence from dedicated extractors is substantially more accurate than implicit attention-based matching, as demonstrated by the paper's comparison showing implicit methods query unreasonable regions. The entire pipeline requires no training and is compatible with any diffusion-based editing framework.

## Results

Edicho achieves the best scores on both text alignment (TA) and editing consistency (EC) across local and global editing tasks. On local editing, it reaches TA 0.3176 and EC 0.8931, outperforming the strongest baseline (Adobe Firefly) by 0.0094 in TA and 0.0362 in EC. On global editing, it attains TA 0.3228 and EC 0.9355, surpassing the closest competitor MasaCtrl by 0.0088 in TA and 0.0097 in EC. All metrics are CLIP-based feature similarities evaluated on in-the-wild image pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edicho outperforms all six baselines on both TA and EC across both editing tasks. In local editing, it leads Adobe Firefly (TA 0.3082, EC 0.8569), AnyDoor (0.2981, 0.8320), and Paint-by-Example (0.2969, 0.8683) by margins of 0.009–0.021 in TA and 0.025–0.061 in EC, with the largest gap over AnyDoor in consistency. In global editing, the advantage over MasaCtrl (TA 0.3140, EC 0.9258) is the narrowest (≈0.009 on both metrics), while the gap over Cross-Image-Attention (0.2914, 0.8912) is the widest (0.031 TA, 0.044 EC), suggesting that methods relying purely on implicit attention features suffer the most from inconsistent cross-image matching. The paper also reports user studies in the supplementary material that corroborate the quantitative gains in perceived editing consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Edicho builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion) and its classifier-free guidance mechanism, extending both with explicit correspondence information. It departs from prior training-free editing methods (MasaCtrl, Cross-Image-Attention, StyleAligned) that rely on implicit attention features for cross-image consistency, instead injecting pre-computed explicit correspondences (from DIFT, Dust3R, etc.) into both the self-attention computation and the CFG denoising process. The method is designed as a plug-and-play layer over existing editing backbones like BrushNet and ControlNet, and draws on Null-text Inversion's insight that modifying only the unconditional branch preserves generative quality while enabling controlled feature transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play design makes Edicho broadly applicable across diffusion-based editing pipelines without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners working with diverse editing tools. The demonstration of downstream applications—customized generation via DreamBooth/LoRA and 3D reconstruction via Dust3R—suggests consistent editing as a useful intermediate step in broader creative and geometric pipelines. Key limitations include dependence on correspondence quality (misalignment causes texture inconsistency) and inherited distortions from the underlying editing models; open questions include scaling to more than two images simultaneously, handling heavily occluded or viewpoint-diverse pairs, and whether learned correspondence extractors can be jointly optimized with the editing process.
