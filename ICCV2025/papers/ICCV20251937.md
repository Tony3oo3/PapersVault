## Problem

Solar flare prediction remains challenging because heuristic physical-feature methods (e.g., SHARP parameters) lack direct representation learning from solar images, while end-to-end deep learning approaches struggle to model the long-range temporal dependencies that span the solar rotation period. Additionally, existing benchmarks cover only short, non-representative windows of solar activity, introducing evaluation bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of k time steps (1-hour cadence), each comprising one SDO/HMI line-of-sight magnetogram and nine SDO/AIA extreme-ultraviolet images at different wavelengths, yielding 10 channels per frame; missing channels are zero-padded if under 25% are absent. The output is a 4-way probability vector over flare classes (X, M, C, O) representing the maximum flare expected within the next 24 hours. During pretraining, a longer history of m > k frames is used, and the model operates on compressed (not full-resolution) solar images.

## Method

Deep SWM combines three components: (1) a Solar Spatial Encoder (SSE) that hierarchically downsamples 10-channel image sequences via 3D convolutions, applies a Depth-wise Channel Selective Module (DCSM) to weight channels by informativeness using parallel 2D/3D convolutions and sigmoid-gated channel descriptors, and captures spatio-temporal dependencies via an S5-based Spatio-Temporal State Space Model (ST-SSM); (2) a Long-range Temporal State Space Model (LT-SSM) that stacks multiple S5-based SSM Blocks over the pretraining features to model dependencies exceeding the solar rotation period; and (3) a Sparse Masked Autoencoder for self-supervised pretraining that uses a two-phase masking strategy—spatial-level masking applies a lower ratio to high-variance patches (sunspots) and a higher ratio to the rest, followed by feature-level masking—to preserve critical sparse regions during reconstruction. The SSE and LT-SSM outputs are concatenated along the sequence dimension and passed through a final SSM Block and feedforward network for classification, trained with cross-entropy plus GMGS and BSS losses.

## Results

On FlareBench (a full 11-year solar cycle, evaluated via time-series cross-validation), Deep SWM achieves GMGS 0.582±0.032, BSS≥M 0.334±0.299, and TSS≥M 0.543±0.074. These scores surpass the strongest baseline (Flare Transformer with physical features: GMGS 0.503, BSS 0.082, TSS 0.530) and, notably, exceed human expert performance (GMGS 0.48, BSS 0.16, TSS 0.50) on all three metrics, marking the first reported superhuman result in solar flare prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep SWM outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the strongest learned baseline, Flare Transformer (full, with physical features), Deep SWM gains +0.079 GMGS, +0.252 BSS≥M, and +0.013 TSS≥M; the BSS gap is particularly large, indicating substantially better calibration for ≥M-class flares. Compared to the MLP-based DeFN (GMGS 0.375) and DeFN-R (GMGS 0.302), Deep SWM improves GMGS by 0.207 and 0.280 respectively, while also achieving far more reliable BSS scores (0.334 vs. 0.022 and 0.036). Against the CNN-LSTM baseline (the only method re-evaluated on the same FlareBench test period), Deep SWM leads by +0.267 GMGS, +0.062 BSS≥M, and +0.213 TSS≥M, with the GMGS difference being statistically significant (p<0.05). Most strikingly, Deep SWM surpasses the reported human expert performance (Kubo et al., 2017; 16-year operational record) by +0.102 GMGS, +0.174 BSS≥M, and +0.043 TSS≥M, making it the first end-to-end model to achieve superhuman scores on both weighted accuracy and reliability simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep state space model lineage (S5, Mamba) to the domain of multi-channel solar image sequences, addressing a gap where prior end-to-end solar flare predictors (e.g., Flare Transformer) relied on Transformers with quadratic complexity or limited temporal windows. It also builds on the MAE pretraining paradigm, adapting it with a two-phase masking strategy (Sparse MAE) specifically for images where critical information is spatially sparse (sunspots). The loss formulations for GMGS and BSS are adopted directly from the Flare Transformer, while the architectural core departs from both heuristic-feature methods (DeFN, SHARP parameters) and standard CNN-LSTM pipelines by replacing attention with S5-based SSM blocks for both spatial and temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration that an end-to-end model can surpass human expert performance on both GMGS and BSS for 24-hour solar flare prediction is a meaningful milestone for space weather forecasting, with direct implications for protecting critical infrastructure. FlareBench, covering a full 11-year solar cycle with time-series cross-validation, addresses a long-standing evaluation gap where prior datasets spanned only 2–4 years and risked period-specific bias. Key limitations include the use of compressed (not full-resolution) solar images and the fact that the human-expert comparison draws on 2000–2015 operational forecasts while the model is tested on 2019–2022 data, so the superhuman claim should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Open questions include whether the SSM-based architecture scales to full-disk, full-resolution observations and whether the Sparse MAE pretraining strategy generalizes to other sparse-signal imaging domains.
