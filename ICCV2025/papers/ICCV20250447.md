## Problem

Existing deepfake detection methods extract handcrafted, deep, or frequency-domain features that inevitably mix semantic image content with forgery traces, degrading both detection accuracy and cross-model generalization. This paper identifies that forged images are more easily assimilated into specific distributions than real images, and proposes modeling the latent diffusion distribution itself to isolate forgery traces free from semantic interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250447/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and feeds it through a pretrained Latent Diffusion Model to extract 20 latent representations (each 3×64×64), which are average-pooled to 3×8×8 and flattened into 192-dimensional vectors, forming a 20×192 sequential token sequence. The output is a binary real/forged classification label produced by the spatial correlation module, with no spatial localization map or confidence score beyond the scalar prediction.

## Method

STFT maps an image into the latent diffusion space via a pretrained LDM and extracts forgery traces from the temporal evolution of latent representations across 20 diffusion steps. Three modules operate on the resulting 20×192 token sequence: (1) a Temporal Prior Correlation module computes a Gaussian-kernel-based prior association matrix P between time steps with learnable scale parameters, exploiting the observation that forged images exhibit smoother temporal transitions; (2) a Spatial Correlation module applies self-attention over transposed latent features (treating each spatial position's temporal trajectory as a token) to capture abnormal spatial similarities or inconsistencies; (3) a Frequency-Enhanced Attention module performs FFT on query and key matrices, computes their conjugate product to capture cross-scale frequency interactions, and applies IFFT plus Softmax to produce frequency-weighted attention that accelerates forgery trace localization. Training uses a KL-divergence-based association difference (AssD) between the prior P and frequency-domain association A, combined with cross-entropy loss, where fake images are penalized for small AssD (encouraging the model to find distributional irregularities) while real images are rewarded for large AssD (emphasizing their non-stationary temporal structure).

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark, STFT achieves the highest in-domain accuracy (Score1) on 7 of 8 generative-model subsets and the best cross-subset generalization accuracy (Score2) on all 8 subsets, reaching 92.3% on Midjourney and 87.0% on GLIDE. On DeepFaceGen, STFT attains an average AUC of 95.21%, exceeding 90% AUC on every one of the 12 forgery categories. In the non-face generalization experiment (LSUN Cat/Horse/Bedroom), STFT maintains AUC scores of 89.7–91.5%, while reconstruction-based baselines DIRE and DRCT drop to 55.0–74.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STFT's most consistent advantage is in cross-subset generalization (Score2): it outperforms the previous SOTA DRCT by 3.0 pp on Midjourney (92.3 vs 89.3), 2.5 pp on SDv1.4 (89.1 vs 86.6), 1.3 pp on SDv1.5 (90.4 vs 89.1), and 4.6 pp on GLIDE (87.0 vs 83.8). On in-domain accuracy (Score1), DRCT narrowly edges STFT on Midjourney (99.9 vs 99.4) and SDv1.4 (99.7 vs 99.6), but STFT leads on the remaining six subsets. On the non-face generalization test (Table 2), STFT achieves 90.32/89.70/91.51 AUC across Cat→Horse/Bedroom, Cat→Cat/Bedroom, and Cat→Cat/Horse splits, surpassing DRCT (74.48/68.78/67.62) and DIRE (73.01/65.45/55.04) by 15–36 pp, confirming that reconstruction-error-based methods degrade severely when the test domain differs from the pre-training domain. On DeepFaceGen, STFT outperforms all 10 baselines (Xception, EfficientNet-B0, F3-Net, RECCE, DNADet, DIRE, DRCT, UnivFD, NPR, FreqNet) across all 12 forgery subsets, with the largest margins on DALL-E 1, DALL-E 3, Midjourney, and Wenxin-generated faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the association discrepancy framework from temporal anomaly detection (Anomaly Transformer [48]) for forgery image identification, adapting the Gaussian-kernel prior association and KL-divergence-based association difference to model temporal evolution in latent diffusion space. It departs from the dominant reconstruction-error paradigm (DIRE, De-fake, LaRE2, AEROBLADE) by arguing that reconstruction errors conflate semantic content with forgery traces, and instead extracts forgery signals from the temporal distribution patterns of latent features during a forward diffusion pass. Frequency-domain features are repurposed from direct detection signals (as in F3-Net, FreqNet) into attention weighting factors that guide spatial-temporal correlation computation, a conceptual shift from prior frequency-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized weakness in forgery detection—semantic information contaminating forgery traces—and demonstrates that working in the latent diffusion distribution space can decouple the two, yielding consistent generalization across 8 generative models on GenImage and 12 on DeepFaceGen. However, the method's reliance on a pretrained LDM forward pass makes it computationally expensive, and it explicitly underperforms on GAN-based forgeries (BigGAN), leaving a gap for the broader AIGC detection landscape. Open questions include whether the temporal-distribution framing can be made efficient enough for real-time deployment and whether it can be extended to video or GAN-generated content without architectural rework.
