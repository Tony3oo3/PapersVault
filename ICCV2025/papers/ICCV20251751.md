## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models require expensive expert-annotated concept labels for training, and their concept saliency maps frequently misalign with the actual input features that drive predictions. The semi-supervised setting—where only a small fraction of concept labels are available—remains largely unexplored, and existing unsupervised alternatives rely on unreliable LLM-generated concepts without standard evaluation metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images as input; labeled samples additionally carry a class label and a binary concept vector of length k, while unlabeled samples carry only the class label. At inference, the model outputs a class prediction, a binary concept vector, high-dimensional concept embeddings (a positive and negative m-dimensional vector per concept, combined via a learned probability), and per-concept spatial saliency heatmaps over the feature map grid.

## Method

SSCBM builds on the Concept Embedding Model architecture (ResNet-50 backbone, per-concept positive/negative embedding generators, and a differential scoring function) and introduces two pseudo-labeling mechanisms for unlabeled data. First, a KNN-based strategy assigns soft concept labels by computing cosine distances in a visual encoder's feature space and taking a weighted average of the k nearest labeled neighbors' concept vectors. Second, concept heatmaps are generated by computing cosine similarity between each concept embedding and the spatial feature map, then thresholded to produce a second set of pseudo-labels. An alignment loss (BCE between the KNN pseudo-labels and the heatmap-derived pseudo-labels) forces the concept encoder to ground its predictions in the correct spatial regions of the input. The total objective combines task cross-entropy, concept BCE on labeled data, and the alignment loss, trained jointly over both labeled and unlabeled batches.

## Results

With only 10% labeled data, SSCBM achieves concept and task accuracy on average across four datasets that are merely 2.44% and 3.93% lower, respectively, than the best fully supervised baseline (CEM). On the AwA2 dataset, SSCBM at 10% labeled ratio (96.48% concept, 89.77% task) actually surpasses fully supervised CEM (95.91%, 87.00%). In the semi-supervised setting, SSCBM consistently outperforms all baselines; for example, on CUB with only 1 labeled sample per class (K=1), SSCBM reaches 88.99% concept and 66.72% task accuracy, exceeding CEM+SSL by 6.63% and 7.37% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fully supervised CEM, SSCBM at 10% labeled data trails by 2.44% (concept) and 3.93% (task) on average, but notably surpasses CEM on AwA2 (89.77% vs 87.00% task) and is competitive on CUB task accuracy (67.67% vs 79.82% is a larger gap, but SSCBM beats CBM's 67.33%). Against fully supervised CBM, SSCBM matches or exceeds task accuracy on CUB (67.67% vs 67.33%) and AwA2 (89.77% vs 88.71%). In the semi-supervised setting, SSCBM dominates all baselines: on CUB at 10% labeled, it achieves 90.88% concept accuracy vs. 84.96% (CBM+SSL) and 84.03% (CEM+SSL), and 67.67% task vs. 9.84% and 63.12% respectively. Label-free CBM and LaBo, which rely on GPT-3-generated concepts, perform poorly on WBCatt (41.92% and 92.26% task) and 7-point (55.44% and 64.97% task) due to their small class counts, while SSCBM reaches 99.68% and 70.09% on those datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., ICML 2020) and Concept Embedding Models (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., NeurIPS 2022) into a semi-supervised setting where concept annotations are scarce. It departs from unsupervised alternatives like Label-free CBM and LaBo, which rely on LLMs to generate concepts and lack evaluation metrics, by instead leveraging a small set of expert-annotated concepts with KNN-based pseudo-labeling for the remaining data. The key methodological departure is the introduction of an alignment loss that enforces consistency between KNN-derived pseudo-concept labels and similarity-based concept heatmaps, addressing the known misalignment between concept saliency maps and input features that plagues existing CBM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: expert concept annotation is expensive, and the semi-supervised setting is more realistic than the fully supervised or fully unsupervised extremes that prior CBM work assumes. The alignment loss provides a principled mechanism to ensure concept predictions correspond to relevant image regions, which is critical for the interpretability claims of CBMs. Limitations include evaluation on only four datasets with a single backbone (ResNet-50), and the KNN pseudo-labeling strategy, while effective, may not scale to very large or highly heterogeneous concept sets. Open questions include whether the alignment loss generalizes to other modalities (e.g., medical imaging, graphs) and whether more sophisticated pseudo-labeling (e.g., iterative refinement) could further close the gap to fully supervised performance.
