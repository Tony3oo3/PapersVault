## Problem

Previous evaluations of concept unlearning for text-to-image diffusion models narrowly focus on whether target concepts are removed and whether image quality is preserved, neglecting broader concerns such as unintended over-erasure of related concepts, multilingual robustness, adversarial attack resistance, and computational efficiency. No existing benchmark provides a unified, multi-faceted framework to systematically compare unlearning methods across these diverse dimensions, making it difficult to assess their true effectiveness and limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250626/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 16,000 LLM-curated prompts per target concept (generated via a two-step attribute-extraction and prompt-composition pipeline) and feeds them into unlearned Stable Diffusion v1.5 models to produce generated images. Concept presence in each image is then detected using either dedicated classifiers (Q16 for NSFW, GIPHY for celebrities) or a VLM-based framework (InternVL2.5-8B or Qwen2.5-VL-7B) employing in-context learning with three reference images followed by chain-of-thought reasoning. The final output is a structured set of scores across 15 tasks spanning six perspectives—faithfulness, alignment, pinpoint-ness, multilingual robustness, attack robustness, and efficiency—for each of 33 concepts in four categories.

## Method

HUB introduces a comprehensive evaluation framework built around three key technical components. First, an LLM-based prompt curation pipeline extracts high-level attributes per concept category and randomly combines up to three attributes to generate diverse, realistic prompts (~10,000 per concept), far exceeding the 1–50 prompts used in prior work. Second, a concept-agnostic detection framework leverages vision-language models with in-context learning (three reference images from the pre-unlearning model) and chain-of-thought reasoning to identify target concepts without requiring dedicated classifiers, achieving 83–85% accuracy on IP and Style validation sets. Third, the benchmark defines six evaluation perspectives decomposed into 15 concrete tasks, including novel metrics such as selective alignment (QG/A-based verification of non-target entities in prompts containing the target concept), pinpoint-ness (measuring over-erasure of 100 CLIP-nearest WordNet lexicons), and multilingual robustness (translating 1,000 prompts into five languages). The framework is applied to seven unlearning methods (SLD, AC, ESD, UCE, SA, RECELER, MACE) across 33 concepts in Celebrity, Style, IP, and NSFW categories, revealing that no single method dominates all criteria.

## Results

Across 33 concepts and 16,000 prompts per concept, no single unlearning method dominates all six evaluation perspectives. RECELER achieves the lowest overall target proportion (0.086) and best multilingual robustness (0.030), while UCE preserves image quality best (FID 13.822 vs. original 13.203) and runs in under 1 minute. SA degrades general image quality most severely (FID 32.572) and requires ~476 GPU hours, whereas MACE achieves the lowest FID-SD (3.203) indicating minimal alteration to generation capabilities. For NSFW concepts, all methods struggle: even the best (RECELER) retains a 0.272 target proportion, and UCE is nearly as vulnerable as the original model (0.603 vs. 0.647).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECELER leads in target proportion removal (0.086 overall, 0.026 for IP) and multilingual robustness (0.030), outperforming ESD (0.143, 0.071) and all other methods, but sacrifices selective alignment (0.497 vs. AC's 0.601) and general image quality (FID 15.190). UCE is the most efficient (0.1 min via closed-form solution) and best preserves general image quality (FID 13.822) and general alignment (0.193, exceeding the original model's 0.172), yet fails on NSFW (target proportion 0.603, nearly matching the original 0.647). AC achieves the highest selective alignment (0.601) and second-best FID (14.203), but its target proportion (0.301) is barely better than SLD (0.228) and far worse than RECELER. MACE attains the lowest FID-SD (3.203), indicating minimal disruption to the generation process, and competitive target proportion (0.148), but shows the worst general alignment (−0.345 ImageReward). SA is the clear outlier: it degrades FID to 32.572 (2.5× the original), requires ~476 GPU hours, and achieves the lowest pinpoint-ness (0.131), indicating severe over-erasure of related concepts. SLD requires no training (0 min) and achieves moderate target proportion reduction (0.228), but offers no advantage over fine-tuning methods on any other metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a comprehensive evaluation framework that departs from prior unlearning benchmarks (I2P, Ring-A-Bell, CPDM, UnlearnCanvas), which typically assess only 1–2 of the 6 perspectives covered here and use at most 50 prompts per concept. Rather than proposing a new unlearning method, HUB repurposes existing tools—VLMs (InternVL, Qwen) for concept detection, LLMs for prompt generation, and CLIP embeddings for pinpoint-ness analysis—into a unified multi-faceted evaluation protocol. It sits at the intersection of the concept-erasure literature (SLD, AC, ESD, UCE, SA, RECELER, MACE) and the growing safety/alignment evaluation literature, extending the narrow 'did the concept disappear?' question into a broader assessment of side effects, multilingual behavior, and adversarial vulnerability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no existing unlearning method simultaneously achieves strong concept removal, image quality preservation, pinpoint accuracy, and adversarial robustness, exposing tradeoffs that prior narrow evaluations masked. The particularly poor NSFW results—where even the best methods leave target proportions above 0.27 and prompt-optimization attacks recover concepts at rates up to 0.78—suggest that current unlearning is insufficient for the most safety-critical use cases. A key open question is whether truly pinpoint unlearning (removing Pikachu without degrading banana color) is achievable with current architectures, and whether the reliance on VLMs for concept detection introduces systematic biases that could affect method rankings.
