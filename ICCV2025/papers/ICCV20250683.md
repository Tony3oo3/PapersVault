## Problem

DNN-based image compression methods achieve superior quality but face a standardization bottleneck, while JPEG remains deeply embedded in ISP pipelines and cannot be easily replaced. Existing JPEG enhancement approaches improve either the encoder or decoder in isolation without sharing learned information, and none provide a fully backward-compatible codec that can flexibly swap neural and conventional components at both stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder (JENO) takes an RGB image plus coordinate maps for Y/CbCr components (resolution determined by the chroma subsampling mode: 4:2:0, 4:2:2, or 4:4:4) and produces a JPEG-compatible bitstream via the standard JPEG pipeline. The decoder (JDNO) takes 8×8 DCT spectra for luma and chroma along with the quantization matrix and outputs a full-resolution reconstructed image. A key constraint is that any encoded file must remain decodable by a conventional JPEG decoder, and any existing JPEG file can be decoded by JDNO, ensuring full interoperability in both directions.

## Method

JPNeO comprises three modules: a JPEG Encoding Neural Operator (JENO), a trained quantization matrix (Q_ψ), and a JPEG Decoding Neural Operator (JDNO). JENO uses an EDSR-baseline feature extractor, coordinate-based sampling, and a Galerkin-Attention neural operator to learn high-frequency components lost during chroma subsampling, effectively rewriting the input as a sum of high-pass and low-pass components before standard JPEG encoding. JDNO employs a DCT-based group embedding, a SwinV2 feature extractor, and a Cosine Neural Operator (CNO) that formulates reconstruction as a product of learned amplitude coefficients and continuous cosine basis functions via Kronecker and Hadamard products, followed by a Galerkin-Attention layer. The quantization matrix is trained with a single linear layer on the standard QF=50 map, using a 3rd-order polynomial approximation of the non-differentiable round operation. The core insight is that mutual information embedded in the trained parameters of both encoder and decoder allows each stage to exceed the information ceiling of standard JPEG, with JENO raising the upper quality bound at high bit rates and JDNO raising the lower bound at low bit rates.

## Results

On LIVE-1 and BSDS500, JPNeO (29.7M params) achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all quality factors (0–40) compared to JPEG, QGAC, FBCNN, and JDEC. At extreme compression (q=0), JPNeO reaches 23.15 dB PSNR / 0.631 SSIM on LIVE-1, outperforming the next-best FBCNN (21.70 dB / 0.577) by ~1.5 dB and JPEG (20.89 dB / 0.540) by ~2.3 dB. At high quality (q=40), JPNeO attains 32.83 dB / 0.929 SSIM on LIVE-1, edging out JDEC (32.50 dB / 0.925) and FBCNN (32.34 dB / 0.923). The gains are consistent on BSDS500, where JPNeO leads by 0.3–0.5 dB PSNR at q=40 and by over 1.4 dB at q=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JPNeO outperforms all four baselines (JPEG, QGAC, FBCNN, JDEC) on both PSNR and SSIM at every quality factor tested (0–40) on both LIVE-1 and BSDS500. The margin is largest at extreme compression (q=0), where JPNeO gains ~2.3 dB PSNR over vanilla JPEG and ~1.4 dB over FBCNN on LIVE-1, while QGAC actually degrades below JPEG at this setting. At moderate-to-high quality (q=30–40), JPNeO still leads by 0.2–0.5 dB PSNR over the strongest baselines (JDEC and FBCNN), with the gap narrowing as quality increases. A key differentiator is chroma restoration: Table 2 shows JPNeO's chroma PSNR advantage over JDEC reaches ~1.5 dB at q=0 (32.30 vs. 28.75 on LIVE-1), confirming that the JENO encoder's chroma-aware neural operator is the primary source of the overall gain. In terms of efficiency, JPNeO uses only 29.7M parameters—roughly 1/9 of QGAC (259.4M) and 1/2.4 of FBCNN (70.1M)—while a lightweight variant (JPNeO⁻, 8.0M params) still surpasses FBCNN at q=0 (22.98 vs. 21.70 dB on LIVE-1) with lower memory (0.09 GB vs. 0.61 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of JPEG research—encoder-side improvements (trainable quantization matrices à la Strümpler et al., pre-editing à la Talebi et al.) and decoder-side artifact removal (QGAC, FBCNN, JDEC)—into a single backward-compatible codec by casting both stages as neural operator problems. The decoder (JDNO) extends the INR-based JPEG decoding of JDEC [22] to support all chroma subsampling modes via a Cosine Neural Operator, while the encoder (JENO) repurposes the Galerkin-attention neural operator [25, 46] as an auxiliary pre-encoder that learns to compensate for chroma subsampling loss. The mutual information framing (Eqs. 4, 6, 7) provides a theoretical lens absent from prior JPEG restoration work, arguing that learned parameters θ and φ inject additional information beyond what the quantized symbols carry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because JPEG is hardwired into ISP pipelines and billions of existing files, a codec that drops in without changing the bitstream format has immediate practical value that end-to-end learned codecs (Balle et al., Minnen et al.) cannot offer. The decoupled architecture (encoder and decoder can be independently swapped) and the lightweight JPNeO⁻ variant (8 M params, 0.09 GB memory) make this deployable in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the neural operator formulation offers genuine inductive-bias advantages over simpler CNN/Transformer decoders at scale, whether the mutual information gains generalize beyond the small Kodak validation set, and whether the approach extends to other legacy codecs (JPEG 2000, HEVC intra). The paper does not address temporal compression or perceptual metrics beyond PSNR/SSIM.
