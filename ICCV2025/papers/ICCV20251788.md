## Problem

Existing MER methods apply AU annotations superficially without exploiting the anatomical coordination structure between upper and lower facial muscle groups, limiting the network's understanding of ME dynamics. Additionally, real-world MER deployment faces a dual challenge: biometric data privacy prevents cross-client aggregation, while individual clients lack sufficient samples for robust training—a combination that remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes onset-apex optical flow (128×128 pixels, containing horizontal/vertical OF components and optical strain) cropped into 65 ROIs of 5×5 pixels centered on refined facial landmarks, along with AU labels that define the adjacency structure for the GAT. It produces a primary emotion class label (3-class on CAS(ME)³, 7-class on DFME) and auxiliary multi-label AU predictions used as intermediate supervision at two stages (AFE and GAT). The AU adjacency matrix is constructed from both a psychological coordination study (intrinsic prior) and statistical co-occurrence patterns (extrinsic prior), with a dynamic weight β blending the two during attention computation.

## Method

FED-PsyAU employs a hierarchical local-to-global architecture: a Local ROI Modeling module (3-layer conv LFE + 3-head Transformer SSE) extracts spatially-aware ROI features; an AU Feature Relationship module groups ROIs by AU via Group Squeeze-and-Excitation, then applies a Dynamic Prior Knowledge GAT (DPK-GAT) that constrains attention to psychologically-derived adjacency neighborhoods and blends self-attention with a data-driven co-occurrence matrix via a tunable weight β, operating separately on upper/lower face and then globally for cross-regional interactions. A dual-stream InceptionNet fuses the global AU relationship features with full-face optical flow for final classification. For privacy-preserving multi-client training, the framework uses P-FedProx, which personalizes FedProx by initializing each client's model as 90% its own previous parameters plus a 10% weighted average of other clients' models, combined with a proximal regularization term to stabilize local updates under data heterogeneity.

## Results

On DFME (7-class), FED-PsyAU achieves UF1=0.3853 and UAR=0.3978, outperforming the strongest prior method FR (0.3559/0.3814) by 2.94% and 1.64% respectively. On CAS(ME)³ (3-class, LOSO), it reaches UF1=0.6221 and UAR=0.6226, surpassing the previous best HSTA (0.593/0.618) by 2.91% in UF1 and 0.46% in UAR. In federated settings, P-FedProx outperforms FedAvg, FedProx, FedRep, and ELLP, with particularly large gains on clients with challenging data distributions (Clients 6–7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FED-PsyAU outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both datasets. On DFME, the largest margins are over handcrafted methods (LBP-TOP: +15.17% UF1; MDMO: +13.64% UF1) and 3D-CNN baselines (I3D: +9.30% UF1; R3D: +16.89% UF1), while the closest competitor FR is surpassed by 2.94% UF1 and 1.64% UAR. On CAS(ME)³, the method exceeds the strongest recent baselines—HSTA by 2.91% UF1, Micro-BERT by 6.17% UF1, and HTNet by 4.54% UF1—demonstrating consistent gains across all evaluated metrics. In the federated setting, P-FedProx beats FedAvg and FedProx on all clients, surpasses FedRep and ELLP, and achieves performance competitive with FedAS while using a simpler weighted-initialization strategy rather than architectural modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAT architecture (Veličković et al., 2017) by injecting two forms of prior knowledge—psychological priors from a behavioral experiment on upper/lower facial AU coordination and data-driven AU co-occurrence statistics—into the attention mechanism, creating the DPK-GAT. For the federated component, it builds on FedProx (Li et al., 2018) by modifying only the server-side initialization strategy (P-FedProx) rather than altering the client training loop, positioning itself as a simpler alternative to more complex personalized FL methods like FedRep and ELLP. In the MER literature, prior GCN-based approaches (Lei et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2020) use AU labels to construct adjacency matrices but do not incorporate anatomical coordination structure or cross-regional (upper vs. lower face) modeling, which this paper addresses through its hierarchical local-to-global GAT design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical barriers to MER deployment—small per-client sample sizes and biometric data privacy—by combining structured psychological priors with a lightweight personalized FL scheme, making the approach more deployable in sensitive settings like interrogation and counseling. However, the FL experiments rely on artificially splitting existing datasets into clients rather than truly distributed data, and the psychological study (n=30) provides only coarse-grained coordination patterns; whether these priors generalize across populations, cultures, or the full 48-AU FACS space remains open. The limited use of only 12 AUs and two evaluation datasets also constrains the generality of the claims.
