## Problem

Existing adversarial patch attacks either fail to achieve high success rates in targeted scenarios or produce visually salient patches that are easily detected by human examiners and automatic patch defenses. The paper asks whether targeted attack goals are achievable with visually imperceptible adversarial patches, arguing that the key is not constraining perturbation magnitude but strategically placing and curating large perturbations using human visual perception insights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, a target class label, and full victim model parameters (white-box setting), and produces an adversarial image with a localized patch (default 84×84, covering 14% of the image) placed at an optimized location. The patch is initialized with the original pixel values at the chosen region and supports arbitrary shapes (square, circular, etc.). Optimization runs until the target class confidence reaches 0.9 or up to 1000 iterations, with up to 3 step-size reinitializations on failure, averaging ~379 iterations and ~19 seconds per sample on an A100 GPU.

## Method

IAP is a two-stage gradient-based optimization framework. First, it computes a perturbation priority index G(x; i,j) that divides a Grad-CAM class localization map (model vulnerability) by a sensitivity map (reciprocal of local pixel standard deviation, penalizing axis-aligned edges), then slides a patch-sized window to find the location maximizing G, balancing attack susceptibility with human visual insensitivity. Second, it iteratively updates the patch by minimizing a composite loss (target cross-entropy minus source cross-entropy plus a perception-aware L1 distance weighted by the sensitivity map) using a color-constancy gradient update rule: the gradient is averaged across the three RGB channels and element-wise multiplied by the ratio of current patch values to the sensitivity map, ensuring the base hue of each pixel remains unchanged while directing larger updates to low-sensitivity (high-variance) regions. This design allows large, unbounded perturbations to be stored in textured areas where humans are least sensitive, achieving high targeted ASR while remaining imperceptible and evading saliency-based patch defenses.

## Results

On ImageNet (target: 'Toaster'), IAP achieves 99.5% ASR on ResNet-50 with a local LPIPS of 0.12 and SSIM of 0.94, while on Swin Transformer Base it reaches 99.4% ASR with LPIPS of 0.07. On VGG Face, IAP attains 99.44% ASR (VGG16) and 98.20% ASR (Swin-B) with LPIPS values of 0.21 and 0.28 respectively. In a human perceptibility study with 28 participants, IAP patches are detected at only 4.2% rate versus 94.5% for MPGD. Against six state-of-the-art patch defenses, IAP maintains 78.6–100% ASR, whereas all other baselines drop below 12% on at least one defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAP matches or slightly exceeds the ASR of Google Patch and LaVAN on ImageNet (99.5% vs 99.1% and 100.0% on ResNet-50) while reducing local LPIPS by roughly 6× (0.12 vs 0.74/0.78), making patches far less perceptible. Against GDPA, IAP wins on both axes: higher ASR (99.5% vs 93.7% on ResNet-50 ImageNet; 98.2% vs 72.4% on Swin-B VGG Face) and substantially lower LPIPS (0.12 vs 0.57). Compared to MPGD (the strongest imperceptibility baseline), IAP achieves higher ASR (99.5% vs 97.8% on ResNet-50 ImageNet; 98.2% vs 91.2% on Swin-B VGG Face) and lower LPIPS (0.12 vs 0.24; 0.28 vs 0.38). The most dramatic gap appears on Swin Transformer Base for ImageNet, where MPGD drops to 70.5% ASR while IAP maintains 99.4%. In the defense-evasion setting (Table 2), IAP achieves 99.8–100% ASR against all six defenses (Jedi, Jujutsu, SAC, DW, DIFFender, DiffPAD), whereas every other baseline fails catastrophically on at least one (e.g., Google Patch drops to 0.0% against Jujutsu, GDPA to 1.3% against DW).

## Relation to Prior Work

IAP builds on two distinct research threads: (1) adversarial patch attacks (Brown et al. 2017, Karmon et al. 2018) that modify localized regions without magnitude constraints, and (2) perceptibility-aware perturbation methods (Luo et al. 2018, Croce & Hein 2019) that exploit human visual system insensitivity to high-variance textures. The key departure is that prior stealthy patch methods (PS-GAN, GDPA, VRAP) either produced highly visible patches with strong targeted ASR or achieved invisibility only in untargeted settings. IAP unifies these by using Grad-CAM-based class localization to find model-vulnerable regions and a sensitivity map to ensure those regions can absorb large perturbations without being visually salient, combined with a color-constancy-preserving gradient update rule that had not been applied to the patch attack setting before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper affirmatively answers whether targeted adversarial goals are achievable with visually imperceptible patches, challenging the assumed trade-off between attack efficacy and stealth. The practical impact is underscored by IAP's ability to bypass six state-of-the-art patch defenses (achieving 100% ASR against SAC where the best competitor reaches only 11.6%), suggesting current defense paradigms built on saliency detection are fundamentally insufficient. Limitations include the white-box assumption, computational overhead from the localization search, and the absence of local pixel-context modeling during updates. A critical open question the authors raise is whether defenses can be redesigned to align machine perception with human vision, which would be necessary to counter such stealthy attacks.
