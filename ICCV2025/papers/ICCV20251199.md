## Problem

Existing 2D camera motion estimation methods are limited to either single-plane homography models or grid-based local homographies (meshflow) that struggle with complex non-linear transformations. The paper identifies that superposing homography-derived flow fields produces inherently non-linear motion that cannot be represented by any single homography, contradicting the linear 8-dimensional basis assumption in prior work like BasesHomo. This gap prevents accurate modeling of multi-plane, depth-varying camera motion in real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB video frames, randomly cropped to 320×576 patches and converted to grayscale, which are projected into a shallow feature space and organized into a 3-layer feature pyramid for multi-scale processing. It produces bidirectional dense motion fields (m_ab and m_ba) as linear combinations of 24 predefined motion bases, along with per-pixel confidence masks (d_ab and d_ba) that flag dynamic objects and unreliable regions for exclusion from the loss.

## Method

CamFlow represents camera motion as a linear combination of 24 hybrid motion bases: 12 physical bases derived from second-order Taylor expansion of homographic transformations (capturing rotation, translation, scaling, and perspective), and 12 stochastic bases obtained via SVD decomposition of randomly sampled Gaussian homographies (capturing higher-order residual patterns). A Motion Estimation Transformer (MET) predicts separate weight vectors for physical and stochastic bases in both directions, and a mask network generates uncertainty maps from warped feature concatenations. Training uses a hybrid probabilistic loss that models motion residuals with conditionally independent Laplace distributions, combining pseudo-label motion supervision (NLL_m) with photometric consistency (NLL_p) under adaptive scale balancing. The key insight is that the non-linearity of flow addition motivates expanding beyond the 8D linear basis, while the Laplace-based formulation simplifies loss design and stabilizes optimization without complex weighting schemes.

## Results

On the CAHomo benchmark (PME, lower is better), CamFlow achieves an average of 0.32, outperforming the best unsupervised method HomoGAN (0.39) by 17.95% and the best feature-based method SPSG+MAGSAC (0.63) by 49.2%. On the zero-shot GHOF-Cam benchmark (EPE), CamFlow achieves 1.10 average, beating BasesHomo (1.74) by 36.8% and MeshFlow (2.15) by 48.8%. On the zero-shot GHOF benchmark (PME), CamFlow reaches 1.23 average, a 28.5% improvement over the best supervised method RealSH (1.72) and 36.9% over HomoGAN (1.95). In perceptual quality (Table 4), CamFlow achieves 32.09 dB PSNR on average, approaching ground-truth homography (32.78 dB) with only 0.69 dB gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamFlow outperforms all 18 baselines on the CAHomo benchmark. Against the strongest unsupervised competitor HomoGAN, CamFlow reduces average PME from 0.39 to 0.32 (17.95% improvement), with particularly large gains in low-light (0.57→0.39, 31.6%) and low-texture (0.41→0.32, 22.0%) scenarios. Against the best supervised method DMHomo (0.31), CamFlow is competitive on average but notably superior in foreground-heavy scenes (SF: 0.38→0.39 is a tie, but LF: 0.28→0.31 shows DMHomo slightly ahead there). Feature-based methods (SIFT, ORB, SPSG, LoFTR) are dominated by CamFlow across all scenarios, with SPSG+MAGSAC (0.63) being the strongest at 2x the error. On zero-shot GHOF-Cam (EPE), CamFlow (1.10) beats BasesHomo (1.74) by 36.8%, MeshFlow (2.15) by 48.8%, and HM_Mix (4.35) by 74.7%, with the largest margin in foggy conditions (0.74 vs. 0.97 for BasesHomo). On zero-shot GHOF (PME), CamFlow (1.23) surpasses RealSH (1.72) by 28.5% and HomoGAN (1.95) by 36.9%, with a dramatic 69.2% error reduction over HomoGAN in snowy conditions (0.93 vs. 3.02).

## Relation to Prior Work

CamFlow extends the motion-basis paradigm introduced by BasesHomo [44], which represented homography as a linear combination of 8 basis flows, by expanding to 12 physically interpretable bases (via second-order Taylor expansion of the homographic transformation) plus stochastic bases derived from SVD of randomly sampled homographies. It departs from the meshflow line of work [27, 43, 31] that partitions images into grids of local homographies, instead modeling complex multi-plane motion through a single global hybrid basis. The probabilistic loss formulation adapts the Laplace-distribution modeling of dense correspondences from Truong et al. [42] to the camera-motion setting, combined with a confidence-mask architecture borrowed from GyroFlow [22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that superposing homography-derived flow fields yields non-linear motion that no single homography can represent provides a principled justification for moving beyond the 8D linear basis assumption, potentially reframing how motion-basis methods are designed. The GHOF-Cam benchmark, which isolates pure camera motion by masking dynamic objects via SAM, addresses a long-standing evaluation gap where optical flow datasets conflate camera and object motion. Open questions include whether the randomly initialized stochastic bases can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D camera pose estimation, and whether the fixed 24-basis configuration generalizes to arbitrarily complex scenes without further expansion.
