## Problem

Roadside infrastructure cameras in V2X systems drift from their calibrated positions due to weather and vibration, yet traditional calibration methods require manual targets, road closures, or pre-prepared reference images that are impractical at scale. Existing deep-learning LiDAR-camera calibration methods designed for single vehicles fail in V2X scenarios because the vehicle's LiDAR covers only a small, shifting region within the roadside camera's wide field of view, making single-frame cross-modal matching unreliable. This paper presents the first target-less V2X-based infrastructure camera calibration method that leverages vehicle-side LiDAR data without any physical reference objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes vehicle LiDAR point clouds, roadside camera RGB images, and known initial extrinsic parameters (K_init, R_init, t_init) as input. Point clouds are projected onto the camera plane using the *inaccurate* initial parameters to produce erroneous depth maps, which are then paired with the RGB images as network inputs. The output is a rotation deviation quaternion R_error that, when composed with the initial extrinsics, yields the calibrated LiDAR-to-camera transformation. Notably, only rotational deviation is estimated (translation is assumed negligible in V2X), and multi-frame sequences are required for the temporal fusion stage, with distance filtering applied to select vehicle-infrastructure pairs within a threshold.

## Method

MamV2XCalib combines three key mechanisms: (1) three parallel ResNet-18+FPN branches extract multi-scale features from the RGB image, the projected depth map, and an image context channel, producing H/8×W/8×256 feature maps; (2) an inner-product 4D correlation volume between image and depth features is iteratively queried via a GRU-based LookUp loop (10 iterations, inspired by RAFT) to refine pixel-level correspondences called 'calibration flow'; (3) a bidirectional Mamba block fuses calibration flow fields across multiple frames and iterations by patchifying the T×i×h×w tensor, adding spatiotemporal embeddings, and scanning with a learned state-space model, with an additional summary token regressing the final quaternion. Training proceeds in two stages—first single-frame flow estimation without Mamba, then multi-frame Mamba fusion with frozen preceding layers—while inference cascades multiple networks trained under different noise levels to handle large initial deviations.

## Results

On the V2X-Seq dataset with an initial deviation range of (−20°, +20°), MamV2XCalib achieves a mean calibration error of 0.6313° (Roll 0.1867°, Pitch 0.2409°, Yaw 0.4780°) and a standard deviation of 0.3211°, compared to LCCNet's 1.0741° mean and 2.5336° std. With a tighter (−10°, +10°) range, the mean error drops to 0.5858° and std to 0.2428°. On TUMTraf-V2X with (−5°, +5°) deviation, MamV2XCalib reaches 0.267° mean error and 0.280° std, outperforming LCCNet (0.389° / 0.608°). The method uses only 47M parameters versus LCCNet's 70M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: LCCNet [21], a single-vehicle LiDAR-camera calibration method using 3D cost volumes, and Calib-anything [20], a zero-training method leveraging Segment Anything. MamV2XCalib consistently outperforms LCCNet on both datasets: on V2X-Seq (−20°, +20°), it reduces mean error from 1.0741° to 0.6313° (−41%) and standard deviation from 2.5336° to 0.3211° (−87%), indicating far fewer catastrophic calibration failures. On TUMTraf-V2X (−5°, +5°), the mean error improves from 0.389° to 0.267° and std from 0.608° to 0.280°. Calib-anything fails entirely in V2X scenarios with large deflections (−20°, +20°), producing no valid calibration, while MamV2XCalib succeeds. Notably, MamV2XCalib achieves these gains with fewer parameters (47M vs. 70M for LCCNet), and the ablation shows that replacing Mamba with a Timesformer [3] module increases parameters to 55M while slightly worsening both mean (0.6683°) and std (0.3808°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-vehicle LiDAR-camera calibration line (LCCNet, CalibNet, RegNet) to the V2X infrastructure setting, where the calibration target is a fixed roadside camera and the LiDAR is mounted on a moving vehicle. It borrows the iterative GRU + LookUp refinement mechanism from RAFT for optical flow and repurposes it as a 'calibration flow' estimator, while upgrading LCCNet's 3D cost volume to a multi-scale 4D correlation volume. For temporal fusion across frames, it adopts the Mamba state-space model (informed by VideoMamba's patching and embedding strategy) rather than the more common Transformer or LSTM, motivated by the large token count produced by iterative flow estimation. The key departure from prior work is the target-less V2X formulation: no checkerboards, no pre-prepared reference images, and no reliance on detection or trajectory accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first target-less method for calibrating roadside infrastructure cameras using only passing vehicles' LiDAR, which is practically significant for large-scale intelligent transportation systems where manual calibration is infeasible. The order-of-magnitude reduction in standard deviation (2.53° → 0.32°) demonstrates that temporal fusion via Mamba is critical for robustness in the sparse, viewpoint-varying V2X setting, not merely for accuracy. Limitations include dependence on vehicle LiDAR quality, strict time-synchronization requirements between vehicle and infrastructure, and the need for fine-tuning when transferring to new datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dense multi-camera intersections, how it handles the small but non-zero translational drift over long periods, and whether the two-stage training protocol can be simplified.
