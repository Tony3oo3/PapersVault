## Problem

Efficient video quality assessment for long-duration, high-resolution videos remains challenging because CNNs and Transformers struggle to balance long-range modeling with computational cost, and existing resampling strategies (e.g., fragment sampling) preserve local distortion details but discard global semantic information critical for accurate quality prediction. No prior work has applied state space models, which offer linear complexity in sequence length, to the VQA domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video tensor of shape C×T×H×W (e.g., 32 frames at arbitrary resolution) and applies USDS to produce a fixed 224×224 sampled frame per timestep, where each 32×32 block contains three 16×16 high-resolution distortion patches and one 16×16 low-resolution semantic patch. The output is a single scalar quality score obtained by normalizing the regression token after the Mamba encoder and passing it through a two-layer MLP head.

## Method

MVQA consists of two key components: (1) Unified Semantic and Distortion Sampling (USDS), which samples 16×16 high-resolution fragments on a uniform grid for distortion details and simultaneously downsamples each frame to 112×112 for semantic context, then fuses them via a binary mask that assigns the bottom-right quadrant of each 32×32 block to the semantic patch and the remaining three quadrants to distortion patches—adding no extra computational cost; and (2) a fully Mamba-based encoder that applies 1×16×16 3D convolution for patch embedding, prepends a learnable regression token, adds spatial and temporal position embeddings, and processes the sequence through stacked bidirectional Vision Mamba blocks using a spatial-first, temporal-next scanning strategy. Training uses a combined monotonicity-induced and linearity-induced loss, with the backbone pre-trained on Kinetics-400 before supervised fine-tuning on LSVQ.

## Results

On the LSVQ pre-training benchmark, MVQA-middle achieves SROCC 0.898 / PLCC 0.899 on LSVQ_test, 0.812 / 0.846 on LSVQ_1080p, 0.885 / 0.887 on KoNViD-1k, and 0.852 / 0.873 on LIVE-VQC, outperforming the best single-branch baseline (FasterVQA) by 2.86–5.18% SROCC and the best multi-branch baseline (MBVQA) by 0.34–2.16% SROCC across all four test sets. The lighter MVQA-tiny (7M params, 34G FLOPs) reaches SROCC 0.882 on LSVQ_test while running 2× faster and using 1/5 the GPU memory of FAST-VQA. After fine-tuning on small datasets, MVQA-middle achieves average SROCC 0.901 / PLCC 0.908 across LIVE-VQC, KoNViD-1k, and YouTube-UGC, surpassing the best multi-branch method (CLiF-VQA) by 1.69% SROCC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVQA outperforms all classical handcrafted-feature baselines (BRISQUE, TLVQM, VIDEVAL) by large margins (e.g., +0.33 SROCC over VIDEVAL on LSVQ_test). Among CNN-based single-branch methods, MVQA-tiny surpasses VSFA by 0.081 SROCC and PVQ by 0.055 on LSVQ_test, while MVQA-middle extends these gaps further. Against the most efficient Transformer-based single-branch baselines, MVQA-tiny beats FAST-VQA by 0.010 SROCC and FasterVQA by 0.009 on LSVQ_test, and MVQA-middle leads FasterVQA by 0.025 SROCC on LSVQ_test and 0.022 on KoNViD-1k. In the multi-branch category, MVQA-middle exceeds DOVER by 0.017 SROCC on LSVQ_test, CLiF-VQA by 0.012, and MBVQA by 0.003, while also achieving the best cross-dataset transfer (LIVE-VQC SROCC 0.852 vs. MBVQA's 0.806, a +0.046 gap). Notably, MVQA is a single-branch model yet matches or surpasses all multi-branch competitors, which typically require additional semantic or aesthetic branches. On fine-tuning (Table 3), MVQA-middle leads CLiF-VQA by 0.015 SROCC on KoNViD-1k and by 0.012 on YouTube-UGC, confirming consistent gains across datasets and training regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply state space models (specifically Mamba) to video quality assessment, building directly on Vision Mamba's bidirectional scanning blocks and VideoMamba's spatial-first, temporal-next scanning strategy for video. On the sampling side, USDS extends FastVQA's Fragments grid sampling by fusing low-resolution semantic patches via a binary mask, addressing the known limitation that grid-based distortion sampling loses global semantic context. The architecture replaces the CNN or Transformer backbones used in prior efficient VQA methods (FastVQA, FasterVQA) with a purely SSM-based encoder, while the loss function combines monotonicity and linearity terms common in the VQA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains—2× faster inference and roughly 5× less GPU memory compared to the current fastest baseline (FasterVQA)—are meaningful for deploying VQA on long, high-resolution video at scale, a setting where Transformer-based methods become prohibitive. The finding that a single-branch Mamba model can match or slightly exceed multi-branch Transformer/CNN models (DOVER, CLiF-VQA, MBVQA) suggests that the semantic-quality separation previously thought necessary may be subsumed by a unified SSM encoder with appropriate sampling. Open questions include whether this advantage persists at much larger training scales or on more diverse in-the-wild content, and whether the USDS mask ratio is optimal or could be learned end-to-end.
