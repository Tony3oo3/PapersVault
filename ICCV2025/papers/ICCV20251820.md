## Problem

Pre-trained vision foundation models (e.g., DINO) suffer severe performance degradation on extreme cross-domain few-shot tasks (medical imaging, radiology, satellite imagery) due to large domain shifts from pre-training data and the scarcity of annotated target samples. Existing adaptation strategies—LoRA, visual prompt tuning, linear probing—fail to bridge this gap, and even full fine-tuning remains suboptimal because limited supervision is insufficient to overcome the model's overconfidence in source-domain representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images that are split into ViT patches, with a random subset masked during training (masking ratio σ = I/(J+I)). During training, the foundation ViT processes both the full image and the masked version while the task-specific expert ViT processes only the full image; at inference, only the fine-tuned foundation ViT and its linear classifier are retained, producing a single class label per image. The evaluation uses an All-way K-shot protocol on extreme cross-domain benchmarks (ISIC, ChestX-ray8, EuroSAT, CropDisease) where all categories participate, as well as standard N-way K-shot M-query setups on traditional CD-FSL and in-domain benchmarks.

## Method

The proposed absorption adaptation learning framework jointly trains two structurally identical ViTs: a foundation ViT (F-ViT) initialized from DINO and a task-specific expert ViT (T-ViT) trained from scratch on the few target-domain samples. Two novel mechanisms regularize F-ViT's fine-tuning using T-ViT: (1) Masked Cross-model Unidirectional Reconstruction (MCR), where a lightweight decoder reconstructs T-ViT's masked patch features from F-ViT's masked features, explicitly injecting task-relevant semantic knowledge while the masking encourages local pattern relationships; and (2) Decision Graph Association (DGA), which constrains the cosine-similarity matrix between each model's class token and patch tokens to be consistent across the two models, transferring high-order relational structure without forcing logits alignment. The total loss combines cross-entropy for both models, the MCR reconstruction loss, and the DGA association loss, optimized end-to-end. This design avoids the pitfalls of direct feature/logits distillation (which collapses into a trivial compromise) and standard MAE (which overfits in few-shot settings) by providing goal-oriented, unidirectional knowledge transfer at both intermediate feature and decision levels.

## Results

On the extreme cross-domain few-shot learning (CD-FSL) benchmarks (All-way K-shot), the proposed method achieves an average accuracy of 43.35% (1-shot) and 65.57% (5-shot) across ISIC, Chest, EuroSAT, and CropDisease, outperforming the strongest baseline RotNet (41.11% / 63.73%) by 2.24% and 1.84% respectively. The largest absolute gain is on ISIC 1-shot (37.49% vs. 34.39% for RotNet), where the domain gap is most severe. On traditional 5-way CD-FSL, the method reaches 59.13% (1-shot) and 75.72% (5-shot) average accuracy, surpassing FLoR (58.90% / 74.75%) and StyleAdv-FT (58.57% / 74.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across every dataset and both 1-shot/5-shot settings. Against the strongest transfer-learning baselines (RotNet, BYOL, MaskCLIP), the gains are 2.24% (1-shot) and 1.58% (5-shot) in average accuracy, with the largest margin on ISIC 1-shot (+3.10% over RotNet). Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, VPT) and simple probing (Linear Probing, ProtoNet) are substantially weaker, confirming that partial parameter updates cannot bridge the extreme domain gap. On traditional 5-way CD-FSL (Table 2), the method edges out FLoR by 0.23% (1-shot) and 0.97% (5-shot), and surpasses StyleAdv-FT by 0.56% and 1.66% respectively. In-domain FSL results (Tables 5–7) show the method also leads on mini-ImageNet (94.68% vs. 93.10% for PMF, 1-shot), tiered-ImageNet (82.78% vs. 79.67% for HCT, 1-shot), and CIFAR-FS (87.64% vs. 81.10% for PMF, 1-shot), demonstrating that the absorption mechanism does not sacrifice in-domain performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of foundation model fine-tuning and cross-domain few-shot learning by proposing an 'absorption adaptation' framework that regularizes foundation model fine-tuning with a from-scratch expert model. It deliberately departs from parameter-efficient adaptation methods (LoRA, VPT) and standard knowledge distillation, arguing that direct feature/logit alignment is insufficient when the two models have vastly different representational capacities. The masked cross-model unidirectional reconstruction repurposes the MAE paradigm as a cross-model knowledge injection mechanism, while the decision graph association offers a higher-order alternative to conventional distillation losses. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between self-supervised pre-training (DINO, MAE) and task-specific adaptation, positioning the expert model not as a teacher to be imitated but as a complementary knowledge source to be selectively absorbed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: foundation models that achieve near-saturation on in-domain FSL benchmarks (e.g., 94%+ on mini-ImageNet) collapse to 20-40% on extreme cross-domain tasks like medical imaging (ISIC) and radiology (ChestX-ray8), where deployment is most needed. The dual-model training requirement and ViT-specific design limit immediate adoption, and the absolute accuracy on extreme domains remains modest, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger foundation models or non-ViT architectures. A key open question is whether the expert model can be replaced by cheaper alternatives (e.g., synthetic data, self-generated pseudo-labels) to reduce the training overhead, and whether the absorption principle generalizes beyond few-shot classification to other extreme cross-domain tasks.
