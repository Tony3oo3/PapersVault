## Problem

MLLMs struggle with rarely encountered domain-specific tasks because their built-in knowledge and visual perception are not exposed to such domains, yet existing multimodal knowledge graph (MMKG) benchmarks are built from web or VQA data already embedded in model training, making them unsuitable for evaluating true knowledge retrieval. Additionally, training effective graph retrievers is data-intensive and infeasible for low-resource domains or closed-source models, leaving a gap in how MLLMs can autonomously access structured external knowledge.

## Input / Output

Each query Q = (q, d, z) pairs a text question with visual reference d (video or image frames, resized to 1K due to token limits) and auxiliary context z (monster name plus non-inferable details). The MH-MMKG stores entities with attributes including 60fps 4K video clips, human-selected keyframes (≤10 frames), and human-written captions, since current MLLMs cannot process raw video. The model outputs both a predicted answer ŷ and a retrieved knowledge subgraph Î, with three task variants (Knowledgeable, Perceptive, Unaided) controlling how much perception and knowledge are provided externally.

## Method

The paper proposes a training-free multi-agent retriever that autonomously navigates the MMKG via breadth-first search using three MLLM-based agents: a topic selection agent identifies the root entity from the question, an expansion agent retrieves plausible neighboring entities for each open node by judging path utility, and a validation agent checks whether each root-to-node path provides sufficient knowledge to answer the query. A perceiver first converts visual input into textual captions to reduce computational cost, and a summarizer generates the final answer from at most 5 retrieved paths. The entire pipeline requires no additional training, making it applicable to both open- and closed-source MLLMs, and the BFS strategy is shown to outperform DFS because benchmark answers are reachable within few hops.

## Results

On the MH Benchmark (238 queries), GPT-4o achieves the highest accuracy among MLLMs: 0.3122 (Vanilla), 0.8565 (Knowledgeable), 0.7383 (Perceptive), and 0.5105 (Unaided-Online). The multi-agent retriever substantially boosts performance over the Vanilla setting (e.g., GPT-4o: 0.31→0.51 in Unaided-Online; Claude 3.7 Sonnet: 0.28→0.44). A knowledgeable human reaches 0.9033 accuracy in the Unaided-Online setting with 0.9207 precision and 0.8535 recall, while a non-player human achieves 0.6092. Online captioning (context-aware) improves over offline captioning for GPT-4o (+0.105) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (+0.076), confirming that query-aware perception enhances retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 13 MLLMs (8 closed-source, 5 open-source) plus 2 human baselines on the MH Benchmark. GPT-4o leads among MLLMs in most settings (e.g., 0.5105 Unaided-Online Acc. vs. Claude 3.7 Sonnet's 0.4388 and Gemini 1.5 Pro's 0.4050), while Claude 3.7 Sonnet achieves the highest Knowledgeable accuracy (0.8987). Closed-source models consistently outperform open-source ones: the best open-source model (InternVL2.5-78B) reaches only 0.3080 Unaided-Online Acc. versus GPT-4o's 0.5105. The multi-agent retriever provides a clear gain over Vanilla for all models (e.g., GPT-4o: 0.31→0.51; Ovis2-16B: 0.16→0.35), and online captioning further improves over offline for GPT-4o (+0.105) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (+0.076) but not for all models. A knowledgeable human (0.9033 Acc., 0.9207 Pre.) substantially outperforms the best MLLM (GPT-4o: 0.5105 Acc., 0.2756 Pre.), indicating a large remaining gap in both visual perception and knowledge retrieval precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-searching paradigm on knowledge graphs (Think-on-Graph, StructGPT) from text-only KGs to multimodal KGs, introducing a training-free multi-agent retriever (topic selection, expansion, validation) that navigates MMKGs via BFS. It departs from prior MMKG construction work, which typically relies on VQA datasets or web resources already embedded in MLLMs' training data, by curating a domain-specific KG (MH-MMKG) from a video game with imaginary creatures and cultures that MLLMs have not seen. The multi-agent design also contrasts with heuristic mRAG pipelines that ground multiple modalities into a single primary modality, instead preserving the graph structure and allowing the model to autonomously decide which paths to explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first benchmark specifically designed to test MLLMs on domain-specific tasks where built-in knowledge is useless and visual perception is the primary bottleneck, with the finding that models achieve ~0.86–0.90 accuracy given perfect knowledge but drop to ~0.31–0.51 without it. The observation that online (task-aware) captioning consistently outperforms offline captioning suggests that knowing what to look for enhances visual perception, pointing toward a promising direction for perception-reasoning co-adaptation. Key limitations include the small benchmark size (238 questions), single-game domain, and the BFS-over-DFS result indicating shallow multi-hop reasoning, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more complex KGs and truly long-horizon reasoning chains.
