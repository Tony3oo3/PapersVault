## Problem

Existing video world models based on attention mechanisms have inherently limited temporal memory bounded by their context window, causing previously observed environments to become inconsistent or vanish when revisited. Extending this memory by training on longer contexts is impractical because attention training scales quadratically and per-frame inference scales linearly with sequence length, making real-time infinite-length generation infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of context video frames (encoded in latent space via a 3D VAE) along with per-frame action signals—either continuous camera positions processed through an MLP or discrete action embeddings (e.g., forward, turn left/right, jump)—and autoregressively generates subsequent video frames one at a time. Actions are injected via adaptive normalization layers alongside noise-level embeddings. Experiments use low-resolution synthetic video from Memory Maze (2000-frame trajectories) and TECO Minecraft (150-frame trajectories), with evaluation on spatial retrieval (backtracking a path) and spatial reasoning (continuing into previously seen regions).

## Method

The architecture replaces full attention with a hybrid of block-wise SSM scanning (Mamba2) and frame local attention. The block-wise scan partitions spatial tokens into blocks of size (b_h, b_w) along the spatial dimensions while keeping the full temporal axis T, performing an independent unidirectional SSM scan per block; different layers use different block sizes to trade off temporal memory against spatial coherence, and the per-block state allocation effectively increases SSM state dimensionality. A frame local attention block following each SSM scan applies bidirectional attention within a window of k previous frames to preserve short-term visual fidelity and temporal consistency. Training employs a modified diffusion forcing scheme where a random-length prefix of frames is kept completely clean (t_i = 0) while later frames receive independent noise, forcing the model to extract long-range dependencies from distant clean context rather than relying solely on nearby noisy frames. At inference, the model maintains constant memory (a fixed KV-cache for k frames plus one SSM state per block per layer) and constant per-frame compute, enabling indefinite autoregressive generation without degradation.

## Results

On the Memory Maze retrieval task (400 generated frames after 400 context frames), our method achieves SSIM 0.898, LPIPS 0.069, and PSNR 30.8, approaching the full-context causal transformer (SSIM 0.914, LPIPS 0.057, PSNR 32.6) while using only linear training complexity and constant per-frame inference cost. On the 224-frame reasoning task, our method scores SSIM 0.855, LPIPS 0.099, PSNR 28.2, again close to the full-context baseline (0.860, 0.089, 28.8). On the TECO Minecraft reasoning task (50 frames after 100 context frames), our method achieves SSIM 0.454, LPIPS 0.259, PSNR 17.8, outperforming both DFoT and a 25-frame-context causal transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Our method decisively outperforms both pure Mamba2 (SSIM 0.747, PSNR 20.4) and Mamba2 + Frame Local Attention (SSIM 0.735, PSNR 19.3) on the retrieval task, with gaps of roughly 0.15 SSIM and 10+ dB PSNR, indicating that the block-wise SSM scan is critical for long-range memory. Against the causal transformer limited to a 192-frame sliding window (SSIM 0.829, PSNR 26.4), our method gains 0.069 SSIM and 4.4 dB PSNR, showing that the SSM state preserves information beyond the attention window. The full-context causal transformer remains the strongest baseline (SSIM 0.914, PSNR 32.6), but requires quadratic training complexity and linearly growing inference memory, whereas our method achieves near-parity at constant cost. On the Minecraft reasoning task, our method edges out DFoT (SSIM 0.454 vs. 0.450, PSNR 17.8 vs. 17.1) and clearly beats the 25-frame-context causal transformer (SSIM 0.417, PSNR 15.8), confirming the advantage extends beyond synthetic mazes to more complex game environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of autoregressive video diffusion (diffusion forcing [10], causal transformers [87], DFoT [60]) and state-space models (Mamba [20], Mamba2 [13]). It departs from prior SSM-in-vision work (Matten, LinFusion, etc.) that applies bidirectional scanning over the full token sequence, instead exploiting SSMs' unidirectional autoregressive inference for temporal dynamics. The key architectural departure is the block-wise SSM scan, which reorders spatio-temporal tokens into independent scanning blocks to balance temporal memory against spatial coherence, combined with a frame-local causal attention window to recover short-range fidelity that pure SSMs lack. The long-context training scheme (keeping a random clean prefix) extends the diffusion forcing paradigm to explicitly incentivize reliance on distant context frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a hybrid SSM + local-attention architecture can match full-context causal transformers on long-term spatial memory while maintaining constant per-frame inference cost is practically significant for interactive world models (gaming, robotics) where unbounded rollout without degradation is essential. However, the paper acknowledges that it does not yet reach interactive frame rates, cannot extrapolate beyond its training context length, and is validated only on low-resolution synthetic data (Memory Maze, Minecraft), leaving open whether the approach scales to high-fidelity, high-resolution real-world video. The block-wise scanning trade-off (block size as a knob between memory and spatial coherence) is a reusable design principle that could inform other long-sequence generative architectures.
