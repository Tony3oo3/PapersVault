## Problem

Existing VIO/SLAM benchmarks rely on controlled, small-scale environments with motion patterns tailored to algorithms, and their ground-truth poses (mocap, synthetic, or LiDAR-based) are either limited in scale or insufficiently accurate to measure improvements without saturation. Egocentric data from wearable devices introduces unique challenges—extreme low light, moving platforms, time-varying calibration, and kilometer-long trajectories—that no prior dataset captures with centimeter-accurate pose annotations at city scale.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multi-sensor streams from Project Aria glasses-like devices: two synchronized 640×480 grayscale global-shutter cameras at 20 FPS, a 1408×1408 rolling-shutter RGB camera at 10 FPS, two IMUs (1 kHz and 800 Hz), plus GNSS, magnetometer, barometer, and WiFi/Bluetooth signals, all factory-calibrated and timestamped in the VRS format. Evaluation produces a 6-DoF trajectory scored via sparse Sim(3) alignment against 483 surveying control points (≈1.5 cm horizontal, 3 cm vertical uncertainty), yielding a piecewise-linear score (0–100), CP recall at 1 m, and pseudo-GT recall at 5 m. A dense pseudo-GT trajectory is also provided via joint multi-sensor optimization for fine-grained analysis.

## Method

The paper introduces LaMAria, a 63-sequence dataset (22 h, 70 km) recorded over 6 months in a European city center using Project Aria devices, with ground truth derived from 483 surveying control points (public CPs plus additional GNSS-RTK measurements) marked with AprilTag fiducials for automatic detection in Aria imagery. Evaluation uses a sparse alignment protocol: CPs are triangulated from multi-view marker detections, then a Sim(3) transformation is jointly optimized with proxy 3D points weighted by triangulation and CP covariances to compute per-CP alignment errors. A dense pseudo-GT is produced by fusing Aria's SLAM initialization with a multi-factor non-linear least-squares optimization over CP triangulation, CP alignment, feature reprojection, and inertial pre-integration residuals. A controlled experimental set with four difficulty levels (platform-based controlled motion → unrestricted egocentric motion) bridges the gap between academic benchmarks and the full dataset, enabling systematic failure analysis. Nine open-source systems (DSO, ORB-SLAM3, DPVO, DPV-SLAM, Kimera VIO, OpenVINS, OpenVINS+Maplab, DM-VIO, OKVIS2) plus Aria's proprietary SLAM are evaluated under monocular, mono-inertial, and multi-camera-inertial configurations.

## Results

On the main LaMAria benchmark (63 sequences, ~70 km), Aria's closed-source SLAM achieves a mean score of 90.7 on short sequences, 78.5 on medium, 70.8 on long, 84.2 on low-light, and 53.6 on moving-platform challenges. The best academic mono-inertial system, ORB-SLAM3, scores 28.3 (short), 20.3 (medium), 14.2 (long), 6.2 (low-light), and 15.7 (moving platform). The best multi-camera-inertial academic system, OpenVINS+Maplab, scores 26.0 (short), 21.3 (medium), 12.6 (long), 16.5 (low-light), and 13.0 (moving platform). All academic methods degrade substantially on challenge sequences, with scores dropping to single digits on low-light and moving-platform categories, while Aria's SLAM retains scores above 50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Aria's closed-source SLAM dominates all academic baselines by a wide margin: it scores 90.7 on short sequences versus 28.3 for the best academic method (ORB-SLAM3, mono-inertial), a gap of over 60 points. Among academic systems, ORB-SLAM3 leads in the mono-inertial category on short (28.3) and medium (20.3) sequences, while OpenVINS+Maplab (multi-camera-inertial) is competitive on medium (21.3) and long (12.6) sequences. OKVIS2 (multi-camera-inertial) achieves the best academic score on the low-light challenge (15.4) but collapses on long sequences (3.6). All academic methods suffer severe degradation on moving-platform sequences, with scores dropping to 2.4–15.7, whereas Aria's SLAM retains 53.6. The controlled experimental set (Table 2) further shows that all methods perform well on Level I (controlled motion, ATE < 1 m) but most fail or drift beyond 10 m by Level III–IV (natural egocentric motion), confirming that the gap is driven by uncontrolled head-mounted dynamics rather than sensor quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting surveying-grade control points (CPs) with fiducial markers into the VIO/SLAM evaluation pipeline, a technique borrowed from photogrammetry but previously unused in SLAM benchmarks. It departs from existing datasets (EuRoC, TUM-VI, TartanAir, Hilti-Oxford) that rely on mocap, LiDAR, or synthetic GT limited to small or controlled environments, and from the only prior egocentric SLAM benchmark (LaMAR) whose GT is too imprecise for VIO/SLAM evaluation. The controlled experimental set with four difficulty levels explicitly bridges the gap between the constrained motions of academic datasets and unrestricted head-worn motion, a design choice absent from prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that all evaluated academic VIO/SLAM systems (ORB-SLAM3, OpenVINS, Kimera, OKVIS2, DPVO, DPV-SLAM, DSO, DM-VIO) degrade dramatically under egocentric conditions—low light, moving platforms, time-varying calibration, and kilometer-scale trajectories—while Aria's commercial SLAM API, with online calibration and full VI bundle adjustment, still struggles on moving-platform sequences (score 53.6 vs. 90.7 on short sequences). This identifies concrete open problems: online time-varying intrinsic calibration, robust loop closure over long open-loop trajectories, and tailored strategies for moving platforms. The dataset's centimeter-accurate sparse GT is orders of magnitude more precise than current system errors, ensuring the benchmark will not saturate for years and enabling meaningful evaluation of next-generation multi-sensor algorithms.
