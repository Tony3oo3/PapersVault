## Problem

Multi-modal contrastive learning for human activity understanding has been dominated by RGB-centric visual modalities (ImageBind, IMU2CLIP, MotionCLIP), leaving LiDAR point clouds largely unexplored as a binding visual modality in joint embedding spaces. This limits privacy-preserving applications in healthcare and surveillance where RGB cameras cannot be deployed, and prevents novel cross-modal tasks such as matching IMU signals to point cloud sequences or retrieving temporal moments across sensing modalities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 24-frame sequences: LiDAR point clouds subsampled to 256 points per frame via farthest point downsampling, IMU time series with C channels per frame, and skeleton poses with 24 joints in 3D. Text descriptions are optional and handled via a boolean mask, since only a subset of training samples (from the Babel/AMASS overlap) carry natural language annotations. The output is a 512-dimensional embedding per modality in a shared space; downstream, these embeddings yield activity class labels (HAR), cross-modal matching assignments via argmax cosine similarity, or temporal moment indices via top-k retrieval within a 10-frame tolerance.

## Method

DeSPITE learns a joint embedding space across point cloud, skeleton, IMU, and text modalities using a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss with a learnable temperature. Each modality has a dedicated encoder—PST-Transformer with a SimCLR projection head for point clouds, a 2-layer LSTM for IMU, the ACTOR transformer for skeletons, and a frozen CLIP text encoder—trained jointly for 145 epochs (batch size 1024, Adam lr=1e-4). The total loss combines two terms equally weighted (α=β=0.5): a text-binding loss that aligns sensing embeddings to CLIP text embeddings (masked for text-free samples) and a pairwise sensing-modality loss that directly optimizes all three pairs (PC↔IMU, PC↔Pose, IMU↔Pose) to be close in embedding space. A key design choice is that text acts as an anchor modality rather than a primary alignment target, and the paper trains all 2⁴−1 modality subsets as ablations, revealing that text-free variants (e.g., DeSPIE) outperform text-inclusive ones for matching and retrieval, while text-inclusive variants (DeSPITE, DePITE) yield better HAR fine-tuning performance.

## Results

On MSR-Action3D, fine-tuning DeSPITE/DeSPIE/DePITE embeddings with PST-Transformer achieves 95.47% clip-level accuracy, surpassing PvNext (94.77%) and MAMBA4D (93.38%) while nearly matching KAN-HyperpointNet (95.59%). On HMPEAR, the same pre-training strategy yields a new state-of-the-art segment accuracy, improving PST-Transformer by nearly 4% over its from-scratch baseline (65.94%). On LIPD-Babel-v2, DeSPIE pre-training lifts the IMU encoder (LSTM) from 65.62% to 69.21% and the skeleton encoder (ACTOR) from 68.23% to 70.64% segment accuracy. For cross-modal matching and temporal moment retrieval, text-free variants (DeSPE, DeSPIE, DeSIE) consistently outperform text-inclusive variants (DeSPITE, DePITE, DeSPTE), with DeSIE achieving the best IMU↔Skeleton retrieval performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSR-Action3D, DeSPITE/DeSPIE/DePITE (95.47%) outperforms all prior point cloud HAR pre-training methods including PvNext (94.77%), MAMBA4D (93.38%), LEAF (93.84%), and PST-Transformer (93.73%), but falls 0.12% short of KAN-HyperpointNet (95.59%). On HMPEAR, the method establishes new SOTA across all categories, beating the best prior multi-modal approach PEAR-Proj BestAR (66.0%), the best point cloud method PST-Transformer (65.94%), and all RGB-only methods (SlowFast 62.2%, Uniformer 61.6%, TimeSformer 56.3%, I3D 55.5%). On LIPD-Babel-v2, DeSPIE pre-training improves the IMU LSTM encoder by +3.59% (65.62→69.21) and the skeleton ACTOR encoder by +2.41% (68.23→70.64) over from-scratch baselines. For cross-modal matching and temporal moment retrieval, the paper's key finding is that text-inclusive models (DeSPITE, DePITE, DeSPTE, DeSITE) perform worse than text-free variants (DeSPE, DePIE, DeSIE, DeSPIE), indicating that CLIP text embeddings act as a binding modality that slightly degrades pure sensor-to-sensor alignment. The most challenging retrieval task is IMU↔Pointcloud, while IMU↔Skeleton (DeSIE) and Pointcloud↔Skeleton (DeSPIE/DeSPE) achieve near-perfect matching for small subject counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeSPITE extends the multi-modal contrastive binding paradigm established by ImageBind and IMU2CLIP, which align sensor modalities (IMU, skeleton, audio) into a shared embedding space using pairwise InfoNCE losses. The key departure is replacing RGB as the visual anchor with LiDAR point clouds, motivated by privacy constraints in healthcare and surveillance settings where cameras cannot be deployed. The LIPD-Babel dataset is constructed by merging LIPD (LiDAR + IMU + skeleton) with Babel's text annotations over AMASS, creating the first large-scale resource for privacy-preserving multi-modal contrastive pre-training. Unlike prior works that use text or RGB embeddings as a 'binding' modality to anchor other sensors, DeSPITE treats all four modalities as co-equal participants in the joint space, with text serving only as a partial supervisory signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practical pathway for privacy-preserving human activity understanding by demonstrating that LiDAR point clouds can serve as the primary visual modality in contrastive multi-modal learning, enabling cross-modal matching, temporal moment retrieval, and HAR without any RGB data. The finding that text embeddings improve HAR fine-tuning but degrade matching and retrieval performance is a useful practical insight for practitioners choosing which modality combinations to train. Open questions include whether these results generalize beyond AMASS-derived synthetic data to purely real-world LiDAR sequences, how the approach scales to truly multi-person scenes with occlusion, and whether the LIPD-Babel dataset (largely synthetic) is sufficient to bridge the domain gap to real LiDAR HAR benchmarks like HMPEAR.
