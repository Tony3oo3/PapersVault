## Problem

Existing text-to-video editing methods fail to correctly interpret negative text prompts (e.g., "without glasses"), causing undesired content to persist in the edited video. Additionally, these methods struggle to maintain temporal consistency across frames, resulting in visible inter-frame flickering. The root cause is that end embeddings in the text encoder output contain coupled positive and negative semantic information that existing pipelines do not disentangle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252444/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (divided into m=4 video groups of F frames each) and a text prompt that may contain negative instructions, and produces an edited video. The text prompt is encoded into a 77×1024 embedding matrix by the text encoder of Stable Diffusion 2.1, and the video is processed through DDIM inversion and sampling with T=200 steps. The method is training-free, operating entirely within the pre-trained diffusion model's inference pipeline.

## Method

SUV consists of two modules. First, Semantic Modulation Based on Text Embeddings (SMTE) identifies that end embeddings (EE) carry coupled negative semantic information; it constructs a target matrix from negative embeddings and EE, applies SVD to decouple them, and uses an exponential suppression operator (f(σ)=e^{-σ}·σ) to shrink the singular values of negative components. Two attention-map-based constraints (EOAM) then further push negative embeddings apart and pull positive embeddings closer via cross-attention maps at each timestep. Second, a Fuzzy Feature Selection (FFS) strategy computes fuzzy membership degrees for cross-frame features, selects the most similar feature pairs via cosine similarity, and fuses them at both local (within a video group) and global (across groups) scales before self-attention, then decomposes the fused features back to per-frame representations to enforce temporal consistency.

## Results

On approximately 36 text-video pairs from DAVIS, SUV achieves the best scores across all six metrics in Table 1. The headline automatic metrics are Temporal Consistency of 0.9896 and Frame Accuracy of 0.8681, both substantially ahead of the next-best baselines (TokenFlow at 0.9824 and ControlVideo at 0.7278, respectively). In the user study, SUV leads in Editing Accuracy (4.35/5), Aesthetics Quality (4.18/5), Temporal Consistency (4.46/5), and Overall Impression (4.31/5), with the largest margin over the strongest baseline (TokenFlow) in Editing Accuracy (+0.99) and Frame Accuracy (+0.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUV outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. The largest gap is in Frame Accuracy (CLIP-based), where SUV scores 0.8681 versus ControlVideo's 0.7278 and TokenFlow's 0.6361, reflecting SUV's superior ability to suppress undesired content (e.g., removing 'glasses' or 'hat') while preserving the rest of the scene. In Editing Accuracy (user study), SUV leads by 0.52 over ControlVideo (4.35 vs 3.83) and by 0.99 over TokenFlow (4.35 vs 3.36), confirming that negative-prompt understanding is the key differentiator. On Temporal Consistency (automatic), SUV (0.9896) edges out TokenFlow (0.9824) and ControlVideo (0.9809), with the FFS strategy contributing the most to this margin. In the user study, SUV also leads in Overall Impression (4.31 vs TokenFlow's 4.05) and Aesthetics Quality (4.18 vs TokenFlow's 3.75), indicating that the suppression of negative content does not come at the cost of visual quality. FateZero and Control A Video trail significantly on all metrics, particularly in Editing Accuracy (2.28 and 2.52 respectively), underscoring the difficulty existing methods have with negative prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUV positions itself within the training-free text-to-video editing lineage (TokenFlow, COVE, FLATTEN, FateZero), which all operate on pre-trained latent diffusion models without additional training. Its key conceptual departure is shifting the intervention point from attention/feature manipulation to the text embedding space itself, specifically targeting the end embeddings (EE) that prior work had not explicitly analyzed. The SVD-based decoupling of negative from positive embeddings repurposes a standard linear algebra tool in a novel semantic context, while the fuzzy feature selection strategy extends token-merging ideas (ToMe, VidToMe) with a fuzzy membership formulation for cross-frame consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored gap: existing video editing methods fail on negative prompts (e.g., 'without glasses'), which limits real-world usability. The EE analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for this failure that could inform future prompt engineering and model design. However, the evaluation is limited to ~36 text-video pairs and a single base model (Stable Diffusion 2.1), leaving open whether the SVD-based suppression generalizes across architectures, prompt complexities, or longer videos. The fuzzy feature selection, while effective, adds computational overhead and its interaction with other consistency mechanisms (e.g., cross-frame attention) remains unexplored.
