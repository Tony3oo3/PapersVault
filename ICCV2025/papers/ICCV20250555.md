## Problem

CLIP systematically fails to interpret negation in text prompts (e.g., confusing 'parking' with 'no parking'), achieving only 60.8% balanced accuracy on a simple binary negation classification task. The paper traces this failure to the pre-training data: in LAION-400M, only 0.70% of captions contain negation terms, and even those are frequently misaligned with the visual content, providing no meaningful learning signal for exclusionary semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250555/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with text prompts containing negation terms (e.g., 'no', 'not', 'without') and produces image-text similarity scores via the contrastive CLIP framework. Because only the text encoder is fine-tuned while the vision encoder remains frozen, the resulting NegationCLIP text encoder is drop-in compatible with downstream models: it can be swapped into Stable Diffusion for text-to-image generation or into CLIPSeg for referring image segmentation without any additional training. The data generation stage itself takes existing image-caption pairs (COCO) and image-question-answer triplets (VQAv2) as input and outputs 229k augmented negation-inclusive captions.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-pipeline data generation strategy that produces visually grounded negation-inclusive captions. Pipeline 1 (P1) uses an LLM (Llama-3-8B) to identify plausible objects from a caption, an MLLM (LLaVA-1.6) to verify those objects are actually absent from the image, and the LLM again to rewrite the caption incorporating the negation. Pipeline 2 (P2) draws on VQA pairs with 'no' answers to introduce negation over actions, attributes, and other non-object dimensions. The pre-trained CLIP text encoder is then fine-tuned on the combined 229k generated pairs using InfoNCE loss (lr 1e-6, AdamW) with the vision encoder frozen, preserving the original embedding geometry. The paper also introduces NegRefCOCOg, a text-to-image retrieval benchmark built on RefCOCOg that pairs each negation-inclusive prompt with a positive patch and a hard negative patch of the same object category, enabling fine-grained evaluation of negation comprehension beyond simple object existence.

## Results

On the proposed NegRefCOCOg benchmark, NegationCLIP achieves 64.09 (ViT-B/32) and 62.95 (ViT-L/14), outperforming original CLIP (57.73 and 57.27) and CoN-CLIP (55.45) by 6–9 points. On VALSE Existence, NegationCLIP reaches 80.15 (ViT-B/32) and 79.59 (ViT-L/14), surpassing CLIP-bnl (76.78) and CoN-CLIP (71.72/65.73). Crucially, general performance is preserved: ImageNet accuracy stays within 0.5–1.0 points of original CLIP, while COCO retrieval recall@5 actually improves (e.g., 68.00 vs. 54.78 for ViT-B/32). In T2I generation, swapping in the NegationCLIP text encoder raises the negation score from 0.295 to 0.449 on SD-1.4 and from 0.308 to 0.421 on SDXL-1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NegationCLIP decisively outperforms all three CLIP-based negation baselines on both negation benchmarks: it beats CLIP-bnl by 3.4 points on VALSE and 2.0 on NegRefCOCOg (ViT-B/32), and beats CoN-CLIP by 8.4 and 8.6 points respectively. On general benchmarks, NegationCLIP matches or exceeds original CLIP (e.g., COCO recall@5 jumps from 54.78 to 68.00 on ViT-B/32), whereas CLIP-bnl degrades ImageNet accuracy by 8.7 points. In T2I generation, replacing the text encoder with NegationCLIP improves the negation score by 0.154 on SD-1.4 (0.295→0.449) and 0.113 on SDXL-1.0 (0.308→0.421), while CoN-CLIP's encoder actually worsens SD-1.4's negation score (0.295→0.243). For referring image segmentation, NegationCLIPSeg improves mIoU on the negation subset of RefCOCOg from 0.267 (CLIPSeg) to 0.288, while CoN-CLIPSeg drops to 0.123, confirming that image-grounded negation data generation is critical for downstream transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's contrastive pre-training framework, fine-tuning only the text encoder with negation-inclusive data generated via LLM (Llama-3) and MLLM (LLaVA) pipelines. It departs from CoN-CLIP (Singh et al., 2024), which generates negated captions first and then retrieves semantically similar images, by instead starting from images and generating captions grounded in verified visual absence—addressing a misalignment issue the authors identify in CoN-CLIP's approach. The paper also positions itself against existing negation benchmarks (CREPE Negate, CC-Neg, VALSE), which either bias against negation or restrict it to object existence, motivating the new NegRefCOCOg benchmark built on RefCOCOg's richer negation expressions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (freezing the vision encoder, fine-tuning only the text encoder) makes NegationCLIP immediately applicable to downstream models like Stable Diffusion and CLIPSeg without retraining, which has practical value for practitioners deploying CLIP-based systems. The NegRefCOCOg benchmark fills a genuine evaluation gap by covering negation across actions, attributes, and adverbs rather than just object existence. Limitations include the relatively small fine-tuning set (229k pairs), the slight T2I quality drop in SDXL (likely due to dual-encoder replacement), and the question of whether this data-driven approach generalizes to other underrepresented linguistic phenomena beyond negation.
