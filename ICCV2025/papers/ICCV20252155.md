## Problem

Existing neural video representations face a trade-off between reconstruction quality, training efficiency, and temporal consistency. NeRF-based methods are slow and memory-intensive, while recent Gaussian Splatting approaches like Splatter-a-Video depend on expensive external supervision (optical flow, depth, segmentation, and O(N²) pairwise point correspondences) that is difficult to compute and can mask representation design flaws. The paper seeks a self-contained, memory-efficient video representation that captures semantically coherent Gaussian motion without any auxiliary supervisory signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view video clip (e.g., 540p at 30/60 FPS for 5–10 s on DL3DV, or 480p at 30 FPS on DAVIS) as input, with no precomputed camera parameters or auxiliary signals required. It produces a set of 400K 3D Gaussians parameterized by B-spline position trajectories, polynomial covariance, constant color (spherical harmonics), and constant opacity, along with a learned camera model (constant intrinsics and Neural ODE extrinsics). The resulting representation renders at ~93 FPS on an A40 GPU and supports downstream tasks such as frame interpolation and spatial resampling.

## Method

GaussianVideo models each Gaussian's position over time with clamped cubic B-splines (avoiding the overfitting of low-order polynomials and the instability of high-order ones), while deliberately constraining color and opacity to be constant and covariance to a 3rd-order polynomial to prevent Gaussians from becoming insignificant. A spatio-temporal hierarchical learning strategy progressively refines the representation: spatially via a Gaussian pyramid (coarse-to-fine resolution, adding 100K Gaussians at 15K steps) and temporally via progressive frame sampling with optional B-spline knot refinement. Camera motion is learned end-to-end using a Neural ODE for extrinsics (rotation and translation) with constant intrinsics, implemented as custom CUDA kernels tightly integrated with the rasterization pipeline. Training uses the Adan optimizer with an exponential learning-rate decay over 50K steps and a standard L2 reconstruction loss, with periodic warping of unused Gaussians toward high-error regions to improve coverage.

## Results

On the DL3DV dataset (high camera motion, 540p), GaussianVideo achieves a PSNR of 43.21, SSIM of 0.99, and LPIPS of 0.013 with a training time of ~45 minutes, substantially outperforming the next-best method GaussianImage (PSNR 38.68) and NeRV (PSNR 28.50). On the DAVIS dataset (lower motion, 480p), it reaches PSNR 37.38, SSIM 0.96, and LPIPS 0.021, again leading all baselines including Splatter-a-Video (PSNR 28.63) and CoDeF (PSNR 26.17). The method also reports a flow warping error of 1.21 on DL3DV and 0.23 on DAVIS, indicating strong temporal consistency. On the longer UVG dataset (~3–4× DAVIS length), it maintains a comparable average PSNR of 37.87.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianVideo outperforms all baselines on both datasets across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. On DL3DV, it leads GaussianImage by 4.53 dB in PSNR (43.21 vs. 38.68) and NeRV by 14.71 dB (43.21 vs. 28.50); Splatter-a-Video fails to converge entirely on this high-motion dataset due to its O(N²) pairwise point-tracking dependency. On DAVIS, the margin over the closest competitor GaussianImage narrows to 1.13 dB (37.38 vs. 36.25), while the gap over Splatter-a-Video widens to 8.75 dB (37.38 vs. 28.63) and over CoDeF to 11.21 dB. In terms of temporal consistency (warping error), GaussianVideo achieves 0.23 on DAVIS, marginally better than GaussianImage (0.24) and far superior to NeRV (1.41) and HNeRV (3.66). Training time is competitive at ~45 minutes, compared to ~8.5 hours for Splatter-a-Video and >24 hours for RoDyF and OmniMotion. In a separate comparison on the NVIDIA dataset, GaussianVideo achieves PSNR 44.14 versus SplineGS's 27.21, though the comparison is not directly equivalent (single-view vs. multi-view input).

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianVideo extends 3D Gaussian Splatting from static scenes to video by introducing B-spline-based motion trajectories and Neural ODE-driven camera modeling, departing from the polynomial motion representations used in Spacetime GS and 4DGS. Its most significant conceptual departure is from Splatter-a-Video, which relies on extensive external supervision (optical flow, depth, segmentation, and O(N²) dense point correspondences); GaussianVideo achieves competitive or superior reconstruction entirely within a self-contained, unsupervised pipeline. The hierarchical learning strategy draws on multigrid methods and Gaussian pyramids, progressively refining spatial and temporal resolution to accelerate convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive auxiliary signals and their O(N²) point-tracking bottleneck, GaussianVideo makes GS-based video representation practical for longer videos and high-camera-motion scenes where prior methods struggle or fail to converge. The 45-minute training time and 93 FPS rendering at 960×540 are practically competitive, and the B-spline motion parameterization offers a clean, extensible foundation for downstream tasks like frame interpolation and editing. Key limitations remain: per-video fitting is still slow at scale, learned camera trajectories miss fine details, and the absence of semantic supervision yields imperfect motion coherence. Open questions include whether NURBS or learned knot placement can further improve motion flexibility, and whether the framework can be extended to generative video models.
