## Problem

Existing distribution-matching dataset distillation methods rely on MMD, which the authors argue fails to capture geometric structure in feature distributions, leading to a consistent performance gap versus optimization-based approaches (gradient/trajectory matching) that are too expensive for large datasets like ImageNet-1K. The paper asks whether a more geometrically faithful distributional metric can close this gap while retaining the computational efficiency of distribution matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled RGB image dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K at 224×224) and a pretrained ResNet-18 classifier whose penultimate-layer features define the working space. It produces a compact synthetic dataset of m_k images per class (1–100 IPC) in the original pixel space, along with per-sample optimal-transport weights that are used during the FKD fine-tuning stage. The barycenter computation operates entirely in the pretrained model's feature space, so the synthetic images are optimized to match barycenter points in that space rather than in pixel space.

## Method

WMDD computes a per-class Wasserstein barycenter of the pretrained classifier's penultimate-layer features using an alternating scheme: weights are updated via projected subgradient descent on the dual variables of the optimal transport problem, and positions are updated via a single Newton step (closed-form given the transport plan). Synthetic images are then optimized by gradient descent on a combined loss: an L2 feature-matching term pulling each synthetic image's features toward its assigned barycenter point, plus a Per-Class BatchNorm (PCBN) regularization that matches per-class, per-layer BN mean and variance statistics (weighted by the transport weights) rather than global BN statistics. This PCBN design is the key differentiator from prior BN-based regularization, as it avoids the conflict between class-specific matching objectives and global BN alignment. The entire pipeline avoids bi-level optimization and second-order derivatives, scaling to ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-21K with only ~10 barycenter iterations.

## Results

WMDD achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all four datasets. On ImageNet-1K at 100 IPC, WMDD reaches 60.7% (vs. 58.0% for CDA and 52.8% for SRe²L), approaching the 63.1% of a ResNet-18 trained on the full dataset. On Tiny ImageNet at 50 IPC, WMDD attains 59.4%, surpassing the next-best method (CDA at 48.7%) by over 10 points. On ImageNet-21K at 20 IPC, WMDD achieves 29.3%, outperforming CDA (26.4%) and SRe²L (21.8%) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WMDD outperforms all seven baselines in the majority of settings. Against the strongest distribution-matching baselines (SRe²L, CDA, G-VBSM, SCDD), WMDD leads by 4–12 points at 50–100 IPC on ImageNet-1K (e.g., 60.7% vs. CDA's 58.0% and SRe²L's 52.8% at 100 IPC) and by 10+ points on Tiny ImageNet (59.4% vs. CDA's 48.7% at 50 IPC). The only exceptions are the 1 IPC setting on ImageNette (MTT: 47.7% vs. WMDD: 40.2%) and Tiny ImageNet (MTT: 8.8% vs. WMDD: 7.6%), where trajectory-matching MTT edges out WMDD, likely because a single image cannot capture distributional geometry. At 10 IPC and above, WMDD consistently dominates. On ImageNet-21K, WMDD surpasses CDA by ~2 points and SRe²L by ~6–8 points, confirming scalability to very large datasets. The MMD-based DM baseline [60] is substantially weaker (e.g., 12.9% vs. 41.8% on Tiny ImageNet at 10 IPC), and the authors note that simply swapping MMD for Wasserstein in their pipeline yields near-random performance, underscoring the geometric advantage of the Wasserstein metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

WMDD extends the distribution-matching paradigm for dataset distillation (Zhao & Bilen [60], SRe2L [53]) by replacing the Maximum Mean Discrepancy metric with the Wasserstein distance and its barycenter operator from optimal transport theory [1, 8]. The feature-space embedding strategy and the use of a pretrained classifier as a representation backbone are inherited directly from prior distribution-matching work, while the Per-Class BatchNorm regularization draws on batch-normalization variants developed for feature heterogeneity [17] and long-tail classification [5]. The key conceptual departure is the argument that MMD's kernel-induced feature space lacks the geometric structure needed to produce meaningful synthetic images, whereas the Wasserstein barycenter naturally interpolates distributional geometry (illustrated in Fig. 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WMDD closes much of the performance gap between distribution-matching methods and more expensive bi-level optimization approaches (gradient/trajectory matching) while retaining the computational efficiency that makes large-scale distillation (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-21K) feasible. The ablation showing that standard global BatchNorm regularization actively degrades performance when paired with the Wasserstein loss (26.8% vs. 38.1% on ImageNet-1K at 10 IPC) highlights a subtle but important interaction between class-specific objectives and global statistics that prior methods overlooked. Open questions include whether the approach can be combined with generative models to produce more diverse synthetic samples, whether the Wasserstein advantage persists in other modalities (audio, video), and whether the reliance on a single pretrained teacher limits the method's applicability when no suitable pretrained model exists.
