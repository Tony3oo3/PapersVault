## Problem

Egocentric video understanding models have focused predominantly on action-level recognition, overlooking the multi-level hierarchical structure inherent in human behavior (actions → activity threads → routines). Existing procedure learning approaches either require per-frame annotations, multiple scripted demonstrations of the same task, or consider only a single level of aggregation, limiting their applicability to unscripted, in-the-wild videos. The paper asks whether the hierarchy of human behavior can emerge naturally from weakly-supervised video-narration alignment and be exploited to improve reasoning across diverse downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

During training, HiERO consumes fixed-length video segments (encoded by a pre-trained backbone such as EgoVLP, Omnivore, or LAVILA) organized as a graph whose edges encode temporal proximity, paired with timestamped textual narrations from EgoClip (3.8M clip-text pairs from Ego4D). At inference, the model produces enriched segment-level features that encode functional dependencies at multiple temporal scales; these features are then partitioned via spectral graph clustering to yield procedure step assignments, temporal boundaries, and step labels (matched against a 514-label taxonomy) without any task-specific fine-tuning. For video-text alignment tasks, the same features feed a lightweight VSLNet grounding head.

## Method

HiERO is a hierarchical encoder-decoder architecture inspired by Graph U-Net, operating on a video graph where nodes are fixed-length segments and edges reflect temporal distance. The Temporal Encoder stacks TDGC layers with progressive temporal subsampling to aggregate information over expanding local neighborhoods, while the Function-Aware Decoder inserts a Cut & Match module at each stage that performs spectral graph clustering to group functionally similar (but potentially temporally distant) nodes, then applies temporal reasoning within each partition separately. Training combines two contrastive objectives: a window-based video-narration alignment loss that makes co-occurring actions close in feature space, and a functional threads loss that regularizes deeper-layer features to be more similar within each spectral cluster and dissimilar across clusters. The key insight is that functional threads emerge as strongly connected regions in the graph, and the hierarchical clustering at increasing depths captures multi-scale activity patterns (e.g., individual actions → sub-steps → full procedure steps) without explicit procedural supervision.

## Results

On EgoProceL (procedure learning), HiERO achieves 44.5% average F1 and 25.3% IoU with EgoVLP features in zero-shot, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art OPEL (32.0% F1) by +12.5% F1 without any task-specific training. On EgoMCQ, HiERO with LAVILA features reaches 94.6% inter and 64.4% intra accuracy, surpassing EgoVLPv2 (91.0/60.9) by +3.6/+3.5 points. On EgoNLQ, HiERO with LAVILA achieves 13.35 R@1@0.3, beating EgoVLPv2 (12.95) by +0.4. On Ego4D Goal-Step Step Localization, HiERO in zero-shot reaches 8.7 average mAP, approaching the supervised EgoVLP baseline (11.1) without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiERO's headline result is on EgoProceL, where it outperforms the previous SOTA OPEL (NeurIPS'24) by +12.5% average F1 (44.5 vs 32.0) in a fully zero-shot setting, while OPEL requires multiple scripted demonstrations of the same task. Compared to earlier self-supervised methods CnC (ECCV'22, 22.0 F1) and GPL (WACV'24, 25.6 F1), HiERO gains +22.5% and +18.9% F1 respectively. Even the raw backbone features without HiERO (Omnivore: 39.1 F1, EgoVLP: 40.0 F1) already exceed OPEL, but HiERO adds a further +4.9% and +4.5% F1 on top, confirming the value of the hierarchical graph architecture. On EgoMCQ, HiERO with LAVILA beats EgoVLPv2 (ICCV'23) by +3.6% inter and +3.5% intra accuracy, and with EgoVLP it surpasses the EgoVLP backbone alone by +1.0% inter. On EgoNLQ, HiERO with LAVILA improves R@1@0.3 by +1.3% over LAVILA alone but slightly underperforms EgoVLPv2 on R@5@0.3 (21.12 vs 23.80), suggesting the hierarchical features are strongest for precise top-1 localization rather than broader candidate retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiERO builds on the video-language pretraining line (EgoVLP, LAVILA, EgoVLPv2) by using their pre-extracted features as node embeddings, and on the Graph U-Net architecture with TDGC layers for hierarchical temporal reasoning on video graphs. It departs from prior procedure learning methods (CnC, GPL, OPEL) that require multiple scripted demonstrations of the same task with shared key-steps, instead learning functional threads from unscripted egocentric videos using only narrations as weak supervision. The spectral graph clustering in the decoder, inspired by normalized-cut segmentation, is repurposed to identify functionally similar but temporally distant segments, a use distinct from prior clustering-based action segmentation (TW-FINCH, Kumar et al.) that assumed repetitive isolated tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the hierarchical structure of human behavior—functional threads at multiple abstraction levels—can emerge without explicit procedural supervision, challenging the assumption that procedure learning requires either per-frame annotations or multiple demonstrations of the same task. The zero-shot outperformance of fully-supervised methods (+12.5% F1 on EgoProceL) suggests that enriching features with multi-scale functional context is a more generalizable signal than task-specific supervision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer videos where the number of nodes grows combinatorially, whether the fixed K in spectral clustering can be made adaptive, and whether the reliance on pre-extracted backbone features limits the ceiling of what HiERO can capture.
