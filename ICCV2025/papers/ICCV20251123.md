## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary multi-object tracking (OVMOT) methods, such as OVTrack, simply concatenate an open-vocabulary detector and a tracking head as independent modules, ignoring tracking-specific object states (occlusion, motion blur) during detection and failing to exploit the abundant raw video data without tracking-ID annotations. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating OVMOT from a video-centric perspective that incorporates temporal object states into detection and leverages unannotated video for association learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames processed through a ResNet-50 FPN backbone, CLIP text embeddings for category names, and four pairs of tracking-state-aware adjective prompts (e.g., 'unoccluded/occluded') encoded via the CLIP text encoder. For association training, it additionally ingests 534.1K frames of raw TAO video without any tracking-ID or category annotations, using only the detector's own predictions. Output consists of per-frame bounding boxes with open-vocabulary class labels (base and novel categories) and persistent track IDs maintained across frames via appearance-similarity matching with a 30-frame track memory.

## Method

VOVTrack comprises three modules: a Faster R-CNN localization head, a CLIP-distilled classification head with separate text and image branches, and a self-supervised association head. The key detection innovation is a tracking-state-aware prompt-guided attention mechanism that encodes M pairs of opposite-state adjectives through CLIP, computes per-candidate attention weights w_r via cosine similarity with the text-branch embedding, and applies a piecewise weighting strategy (filter w_r < 0.3, retain 0.3–0.6, boost > 0.6 to 1) to modulate the classification loss, thereby down-weighting occluded or blurred objects during training. For association, the paper formulates a self-supervised objective over raw video segments: it enforces appearance self-consistency via pairwise symmetry (S_ij · S_ji ≈ I) and triplet cyclicity (S_ik · S_ki ≈ I) losses on normalized similarity matrices, plus a mutual-consistency BCE loss aligning appearance similarity with spatial IoU assignment. Category consistency is enforced by K-means clustering of predicted class distributions to restrict consistency learning within same-category intervals, eliminating the need for ID annotations entirely.

## Results

On the TAO validation set, VOVTrack achieves 39.6% base TETA and 35.3% novel TETA, outperforming the strongest baseline SLAck (37.2% / 31.1%) by 2.4 and 4.2 points respectively. The novel-class classification accuracy (ClsA) jumps to 6.5%, a 5× improvement over SLAck's 1.3%. Notably, VOVTrack trains its association head on raw TAO video without any tracking annotations, while SLAck uses fully annotated TAO data. On the TAO test set, VOVTrack maintains its lead with 38.1% base TETA and 29.8% novel TETA versus SLAck's 34.7% and 27.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOVTrack outperforms all six baselines on TETA for both base and novel classes on the TAO validation set. Against the closest competitor SLAck [28], VOVTrack gains +2.4% base TETA and +4.2% novel TETA, with a particularly large +5.2% gain in novel ClsA (6.5 vs 1.3), despite training its association head on unannotated raw video while SLAck uses fully annotated TAO data. Compared to the direct baseline OVTrack [27], VOVTrack improves base TETA by +4.1% and novel TETA by +7.5%, with novel AssocA rising from 33.6 to 40.9. The only metric where VOVTrack trails a baseline is base ClsA (19.1 vs OVTrack's 20.2), which the authors attribute to VOVTrack detecting more objects overall, diluting per-object classification precision. Closed-set trackers (QDTrack, TETer) that see novel classes during training are still surpassed by VOVTrack, which never sees novel-class annotations, confirming the effectiveness of the CLIP-distilled classification head and self-supervised association.

## Relation to Prior Work

VOVTrack extends the OVMOT framework established by OVTrack [27], which first defined the task and proposed a simple detection-then-association pipeline using an off-the-shelf OVD model (DetPro [13]) and a tracking head trained on hallucinated image pairs. The key departure is twofold: (1) integrating tracking-state awareness (occlusion, blur, etc.) into the detection head via CLIP-based prompt-guided attention rather than treating detection as a frame-independent task, and (2) replacing the supervised association training with a self-supervised consistency-learning objective that exploits raw unlabeled video sequences. The classification head design follows the CLIP distillation paradigm from ViLD [18], and the overall architecture remains a tracking-by-detection pipeline built on Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50/FPN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practical contribution is demonstrating that 534K frames of unlabeled TAO video can be leveraged for association learning without any tracking ID annotations, outperforming the supervised SLAck baseline that uses the same dataset's annotations. This directly addresses the annotation bottleneck that has limited OVMOT research. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (TAO), the detection head still requires supervised training on LVIS and TAO annotations, and the self-supervised association relies on K-means clustering of category distributions whose quality is unverified. Open questions include whether the self-supervised consistency objective generalizes to longer videos, more diverse domains, or settings where no annotated detection data is available at all.
