## Problem

Training segmentation models for rare MRI modalities is hindered by the scarcity of manual mask annotations, and existing generative approaches either focus on augmenting well-annotated modalities (X-ray, CT, brain MRI) or require registered image pairs for translation. No prior method can controllably synthesize MRI data for modalities that have no segmentation annotations at all, leaving a gap for 'zero-shot' segmentation in annotation-scarce clinical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250533/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MRGen takes two conditioning signals: a templated text prompt encoding modality label, tissue signal attributes (e.g., 'fat high signal, muscle intermediate signal, water low signal'), anatomical region, and organ names, plus a multi-class organ segmentation mask where distinct intensity values denote different organs. The output is a synthetic 512×512 single-channel MRI 2D slice. At inference, 20 candidates are generated per mask and an automatic SAM2-based filter retains only samples whose predicted segmentation achieves IoU ≥ 0.80 and confidence ≥ 0.90 against the conditioning mask.

## Method

MRGen is a latent diffusion model with three components: a VAE autoencoder (compression ratio 8, latent dimension 16) trained on MRGen-DB, a UNet denoiser conditioned on BiomedCLIP text embeddings via cross-attention, and a mask condition controller whose encoder is initialized from the UNet encoder weights and whose outputs are injected as residuals into the UNet decoder blocks. Training follows a two-stage strategy: first, text-guided pretraining on ~250K image-text pairs across 100+ MRI modalities teaches the model to synthesize diverse MR appearances; second, mask-conditioned finetuning on ~18K mask-annotated samples (mixed with unmasked data to prevent overfitting) teaches the model to respect organ masks while keeping all other parameters frozen. The key insight is that this decoupling lets mask controllability generalize to modalities seen only during text pretraining, enabling synthesis for modalities with zero mask annotations. GPT-4 is used to convert coarse modality labels into fine-grained tissue signal descriptions, and a SAM2-based filtering pipeline ensures generated images faithfully match the conditioning masks.

## Results

On the primary segmentation task (DSC score), MRGen achieves an average of 43.76% (UMamba) and 44.71% (nnUNet) across 10 cross-modality transfer experiments, winning 8 out of 10 individual settings. For generation quality (FID), MRGen achieves an average of 82.18, substantially lower than CycleGAN (178.18), UNSB (194.78), and DualNorm (290.37). The largest gains appear in cross-dataset transfers (e.g., LiQA T1 → CHAOS-MRI T2-SPIR: 5.14% DSC with MRGen vs. 11.05% with CycleGAN under UMamba), while same-dataset modality swaps (e.g., PanSeg T1↔T2) show smaller absolute differences due to lower baseline difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRGen decisively outperforms all three baselines on both generation quality and downstream segmentation. On FID (lower is better), MRGen achieves 82.18 average versus CycleGAN (178.18), UNSB (194.78), and DualNorm (290.37), indicating far more realistic target-modality synthesis. On segmentation DSC (higher is better), MRGen's average of 43.76% (UMamba) / 44.71% (nnUNet) surpasses CycleGAN (27.54% / 26.39%), UNSB (24.19% / 23.85%), and DualNorm (10.28% / 10.48%) by 16–34 percentage points. CycleGAN and UNSB occasionally match or slightly exceed MRGen on individual same-dataset settings (e.g., MSD-Prostate ADC→T2: CycleGAN 62.00 vs. MRGen 64.05 under UMamba), but MRGen wins 8 of 10 experiments overall. DualNorm, which only augments source-domain images without modality translation, performs worst, confirming that simple style augmentation cannot bridge the intensity gap between MRI modalities. The advantage is most pronounced in cross-dataset transfers (LiQA↔CHAOS-MRI, MSD-Prostate↔PROMISE12), where baselines struggle most due to both modality and domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the latent diffusion model architecture (Stable Diffusion) to medical MRI synthesis, but departs from prior medical generative work in two key ways. First, whereas prior methods (DiffTumor, FreeTumor, brain MRI diffusion models) focus on data augmentation within well-annotated modalities, MRGen targets the harder setting of synthesizing data for modalities with no mask annotations at all. Second, unlike image translation approaches (CycleGAN, UNSB) that require registered pairs or are limited to specific modality conversions, MRGen uses a two-stage strategy—text-guided pretraining on diverse modalities followed by mask-conditioned finetuning on partial annotations—to achieve controllable generation that generalizes across modalities. The curation of MRGen-DB, combining Radiopaedia free-text labels with GPT-4-derived modality attributes and BiomedCLIP region classification, is a novel dataset construction pipeline not seen in prior medical generative work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a clinically significant gap: MRI modalities are expensive, highly heterogeneous, and often lack the manual annotations needed for segmentation training, yet are critical for diagnosis. The two-stage training strategy (text pretraining + mask finetuning) offers a reusable recipe for extending controllable generation to any modality seen during pretraining, potentially reducing annotation costs for new scanning protocols. Limitations include the restriction to 2D abdominal slices, reliance on source-domain masks to drive target-domain generation, and evaluation on only five dataset pairs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric generation, broader anatomical regions, and whether synthetic data can fully substitute for real data in clinical deployment.
