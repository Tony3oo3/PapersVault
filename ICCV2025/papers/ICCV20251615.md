## Problem

While Single Transformer-based MLLMs that eliminate pretrained vision encoders have been explored architecturally, their fundamental properties—data scalability, cross-modal information flow patterns, and visual representation quality—remain poorly understood. A key open question is whether a Single Transformer trained end-to-end can match modular MLLMs at scale and simultaneously emerge as a competitive vision encoder without any pretrained ViT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251615/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SAIL consumes raw RGB images partitioned into 14×14 patches (projected via a linear layer) and text tokens (via the Mistral-7B tokenizer), concatenated into a single sequence delimited by <vision>/</vision> special tokens. During pretraining, images are processed at 224×224 (Stage 1) or native any-resolution (Stage 2); the model outputs autoregressive text with language modeling loss computed only on text tokens. For vision-encoder evaluation, the frozen Stage-1 checkpoint's patch embeddings are fed into a linear classifier (ImageNet-1K) or a ViT-Adapter + UperNet decoder (ADE20K segmentation at 512×512).

## Method

SAIL is a 7B single Transformer (initialized from Mistral-7B-v0.1) that processes image patches and text tokens in one unified architecture, removing the separate ViT encoder and projector. Two key micro-designs enable this: (1) a mixed attention scheme applying full bidirectional attention among image patches within the same image while preserving causal attention for text tokens, and (2) Multimodal RoPE (M-RoPE) that encodes 2D spatial coordinates for image tokens and uniform 1D positions for text tokens with sequential cross-modal indexing. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—512M image-text pairs at 224×224 followed by 86M any-resolution pairs—interleaved with pure text data (SlimPajama) to preserve language capability, then 6M instruction-tuning pairs for SFT. The paper's core contribution is an empirical analysis showing SAIL achieves steeper data-scaling gains than a matched modular MLLM (closing the gap at 512M samples), allocates 60–80% of attention to image tokens during prediction (vs. 10–30% for modular models), and attains 84.06% ImageNet Top-1 and 55.30% ADE20K mIoU as a frozen vision encoder, rivaling ViT-22B.

## Results

On 13 vision-language benchmarks, SAIL (7B, 600M pretraining samples) achieves 53.1 on MMStar, 70.1 on MMBench-EN, 72.9 on SEEDBench-IMG, 46.3 on MMVet, 1719 on MME, 63.9 on RealWorldQA, 77.1 on TextVQA, and 783 on OCR-Bench, outperforming all prior Single Transformer-based MLLMs (Fuyu, SOLO, EVE, EVE2, MonoInternVL, Emu3, Chameleon). On vision representation tasks, SAIL reaches 84.06% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K and 55.30% mIoU on ADE20K segmentation, matching ViT-22B's segmentation score with only 0.5B training samples versus 3B. On the ARO benchmark, SAIL achieves near-perfect scores (100.0/99.5/100.0/100.0) for relation, attribute, and ordering tasks, surpassing all prior vision backbones including CapPa and NegCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIL decisively outperforms all prior Single Transformer-based MLLMs: it surpasses EVE2 (the strongest prior encoder-free model) by 6.8 points on MMBench (70.1 vs 66.3), 1.5 on SEEDBench (72.9 vs 71.4), 1.3 on MMVet (46.3 vs 45.0), and 2.3 on TextVQA (77.1 vs 74.8), while also leading on OCR-Bench (783 vs 702) and HallusionBench (54.2 vs not reported). Against modular MLLMs, SAIL narrows the gap substantially: it matches or exceeds LLaVA-OneVision on MMStar (53.1 vs 60.9 is a loss, but SAIL leads on SEEDBench 72.9 vs 74.8 is close) and RealWorldQA (63.9 vs 65.5), yet still trails on MMBench (70.1 vs 81.7) and MMVet (46.3 vs 58.8), indicating remaining gaps in world-knowledge and integrated-reasoning tasks. SAIL outperforms LLaVA-1.5/1.6 across nearly all benchmarks (e.g., MMStar 53.1 vs 33.1/37.6, TextVQA 77.1 vs 54.8/66.6) and surpasses Cambrian-1 on MMBench (70.1 vs 75.9 is a loss) but leads on TextVQA (77.1 vs 73.0). On vision representation, SAIL's 84.06% ImageNet Top-1 and 55.30% ADE20K mIoU match ViT-22B's segmentation score (55.3) with 6× less training data (0.5B vs 3B samples) and 3× fewer parameters (7B vs 22B), while its ARO scores (100/99.5/100/100) exceed CapPa (86.7/85.7/98.8/99.2) and NegCLIP (81.0/71.0/86.0/91.0) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAIL extends the single-transformer MLLM line initiated by Fuyu, SOLO, EVE, and MonoInternVL, which process raw image patches and text tokens in a unified architecture without a separate ViT encoder. Its key architectural departure from all prior single-transformer MLLMs is the introduction of bidirectional attention among image patches (combined with M-RoPE from Qwen2-VL), whereas previous works uniformly applied causal attention to the entire sequence. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as an empirical study rather than an architectural proposal, explicitly stating it does not introduce novel components but instead systematically analyzes the fundamental properties—scalability, cross-modal information flow, and visual representation quality—of this unified design relative to modular MLLMs like LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SAIL exhibits a steeper data-scaling curve than its modular counterpart (catching up at 512M samples despite starting behind at 32M) provides concrete evidence that unified architectures may be more data-efficient at scale, which has direct implications for how the field allocates pretraining budgets. The demonstration that a 7B single transformer trained on 0.5B image-text pairs matches a 22B ViT on ADE20K segmentation (55.30% mIoU) suggests that multimodal pretraining can yield transferable visual representations without dedicated vision training, potentially simplifying model pipelines. However, SAIL still lags modular MLLMs on world-knowledge-heavy tasks (celebrity recognition, posters), and the remaining gap to LLaVA-OneVision on several benchmarks indicates that data diversity and quality remain open challenges for the single-transformer paradigm.
