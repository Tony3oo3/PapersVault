## Problem

Existing lane graph generation methods rely on Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) formulations that cannot represent loops or bidirectional lanes prevalent in real-world road networks. Generative approaches like RNTR and LaneGraph2Seq either over-deconstruct the intrinsic graph structure during serialization or decouple node and edge learning, losing holistic topological understanding. The paper asks how to formulate lane graph construction as a natural, incremental process that preserves full directed connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes surrounding camera perspective-view images (6 cameras for nuScenes, 7 for Argoverse 2) and produces a token sequence encoding the complete lane graph. Coordinates are discretized at 0.5 m resolution into distinct token ranges: 0–200 for node positions, 200–350 for node indices, 350–570 for Bezier control points, and 571–575 for special tokens (<to>, <SEP>, <EOS>, <BOS>, <N/A>). The BEV target range spans −48 m to 48 m (x) and −30 m to 30 m (y) on nuScenes, and −30 m to 30 m (x) and −15 m to 15 m (y) on Argoverse 2.

## Method

SeqGrowGraph models lane graph construction as a chain of incremental graph expansions: at each step a new node is introduced with its position, the adjacency matrix grows from n×n to (n+1)×(n+1) encoding incoming ('from') and outgoing ('to') edges, and a geometric matrix stores quadratic Bezier control points for each centerline. Nodes are introduced in depth-first search order, and the full graph is serialized as S_N = S_{N−1} + (v_N + F_N + T_N), where F_N and T_N enumerate connections to previously introduced nodes. A 6-layer transformer decoder autoregressively generates the token sequence conditioned on BEV features extracted by an LSS encoder (ResNet50 backbone), trained with maximum-likelihood loss that assigns double weight to point-position tokens to prioritize accurate node localization. Unlike DAG-based methods, this formulation natively supports loops and bidirectional lanes without requiring acyclicity constraints or complex pre/post-processing.

## Results

On nuScenes (default split), SeqGrowGraph achieves Landmark F1 of 56.4 and Reachability F1 of 67.8, outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging PON split (no train/test lane overlap), it maintains the lead with Landmark F1 of 37.7 and Reachability F1 of 46.7. On Argoverse 2, SeqGrowGraph reaches Landmark F1 of 56.4 and Reachability F1 of 55.7, surpassing the strongest baseline (LaneGraph2Seq) by 2.5 and 5.3 points respectively. For centerline-level metrics on nuScenes, SeqGrowGraph achieves Mean F1 of 70.6 and Connectivity F1 of 65.8, compared to 64.1 and 63.2 for LaneGraph2Seq.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeqGrowGraph outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets. Against TopoNet [10], the gains are most dramatic on Reachability (R-F: 67.8 vs. 17.5 on default split), reflecting TopoNet's inability to produce continuous centerlines. Against LaneGAP [15], SeqGrowGraph wins on Landmark Precision (63.6 vs. 49.9), Landmark F1 (56.4 vs. 53.2), and Reachability F1 (67.8 vs. 47.5), though LaneGAP retains a slight edge on Landmark Recall (57.0 vs. 50.8). Against RNTR [17], SeqGrowGraph leads on every metric in both the default split (e.g., L-F: 56.4 vs. 48.9; R-F: 67.8 vs. 52.8) and the PON split (L-F: 37.7 vs. 34.9; R-F: 46.7 vs. 35.7). Against LaneGraph2Seq [21], the closest competitor, SeqGrowGraph achieves consistent improvements: on Argoverse 2 it gains +3.5 L-P, +1.7 L-F, +2.2 R-P, +5.6 R-R, and +5.3 R-F, confirming that the incremental graph-expansion formulation captures topology more holistically than the vertex-then-edge encoding used by LaneGraph2Seq.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeqGrowGraph builds on the generative lane-graph pipeline established by RNTR and LaneGraph2Seq (LSS BEV encoder + autoregressive transformer decoder) but departs fundamentally from their DAG-based graph-to-sequence conversion. While prior generative methods require converting the lane graph into a Directed Acyclic Graph—necessitating complex pre/post-processing and precluding loops and bidirectional lanes—SeqGrowGraph instead serializes the graph as a chain of incremental adjacency-matrix expansions (n×n → (n+1)×(n+1)), guided by depth-first search ordering. This formulation is inspired by how humans draw maps node-by-node, and it allows the model to represent arbitrary directed topologies without the acyclicity constraint that limits RNTR and LaneGraph2Seq.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to natively represent loops and bidirectional lanes without DAG conversion removes a structural limitation that has constrained prior generative approaches, and the consistent gains across nuScenes (default and PON splits) and Argoverse 2 validate the formulation. The centerline re-segmentation experiment (Table 4) offers a practical insight: ground-truth segmentation at non-visual breakpoints degrades learning, suggesting the community should adopt junction-only segmentation standards. Open questions include whether the quadratic Bezier approximation suffices for highly curved centerlines, how the method scales to city-scale maps with hundreds of nodes, and whether the DFS ordering advantage persists under different graph densities.
