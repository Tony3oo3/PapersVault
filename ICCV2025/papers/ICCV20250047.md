## Problem

Vision foundation models pre-trained for single-image understanding exhibit a critical misalignment when applied to image feature matching, which requires cross-image relational understanding. Contrastive-learning based models (e.g., DINOv2) prioritize global semantics and lose instance-level details, while no effective mechanism exists to convert single-image understanding into cross-image understanding. This misalignment causes significant failures in multi-instance matching scenarios where multiple objects of the same class must be correctly paired across views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250047/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and produces sub-pixel feature matches through a two-stage pipeline. Coarse features are extracted at 1/8 spatial resolution (640-dim) from a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet at timestep t=0, while fine features are extracted at 1/2 resolution via a ResNet encoder. The coarse stage yields pixel-to-pixel correspondences at 1/8 scale, which are then refined to sub-pixel accuracy by cropping local feature patches around each coarse match and computing a softmax-weighted expectation over a 3×3 neighborhood.

## Method

IMD replaces the standard contrastive-learning backbone with a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet, exploiting its generative pre-training to retain explicit instance-level representations that contrastive objectives discard. The key architectural innovation is the Cross-image Interaction Prompt Module (CIPM), which encodes both images with a frozen CLIP encoder, applies cross-attention between them to produce personalized prompt embeddings for each image, and feeds these into the diffusion UNet's text-conditioning pathway—repurposing the prompt mechanism as a natural tunnel for bidirectional cross-image information flow. Coarse matching is performed via interleaved self- and cross-attention on the diffusion features followed by dual-softmax mutual nearest neighbor selection, and a specialized ResNet fine encoder fuses with transformed coarse features for sub-pixel refinement. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on MegaDepth with focal loss for coarse matching and a combination of log-likelihood and L2 losses for fine matching, while the diffusion UNet and CLIP encoder remain frozen.

## Results

On the outdoor MegaDepth benchmark, IMD achieves 61.2% AUC@5°, 76.0% AUC@10°, and 85.8% AUC@20°, setting a new state-of-the-art among all semi-dense and sparse methods. On the indoor ScanNet benchmark, IMD reaches 29.8% AUC@5°, a 24.6% relative improvement over the best MegaDepth-trained semi-dense method (PRISM, 23.9%). On the newly introduced IMIM multi-instance benchmark, IMD achieves 88.7% matching accuracy, a 12% absolute improvement over the previous best (CasMTR, 79.2%), demonstrating effective mitigation of the foundation-model-to-matching misalignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MegaDepth (outdoor pose estimation), IMD (61.2% AUC@5°) outperforms the strongest prior semi-dense methods PRISM (60.0%) and CasMTR (59.1%) by 1.2 and 2.1 points respectively, and surpasses all sparse methods including OmniGlue (47.4%) and SP+LightGlue (49.9%). On ScanNet (indoor), IMD (29.8% AUC@5°) substantially exceeds PRISM (23.9%) and CasMTR (22.6%), though the sparse method OmniGlue (31.3%) retains a slight edge at the strictest threshold. On the new IMIM multi-instance benchmark, IMD (88.7%) leads all methods by a wide margin over CasMTR (79.2%), OmniGlue (77.6%), and DIFT (61.2%), confirming that diffusion-based features with cross-image interaction are critical for distinguishing same-class instances. On HPatches homography estimation, IMD (73.9% AUC@3px) beats the best prior semi-dense method PRISM (71.9%) by 2.0 points. On InLoc visual localization, IMD achieves the best DUC2 score (60.3/82.7/88.5) and competitive DUC1 performance (54.6/77.3/88.2), surpassing all listed baselines including SP+SuperGlue and AspanFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIFT's demonstration that frozen diffusion model features serve as powerful matching descriptors, and on Efficient LoFTR's two-stage coarse-to-fine matching pipeline. However, it departs sharply from the dominant paradigm of using contrastive-learning foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP) as feature extractors, arguing instead that generative diffusion models preserve the instance-level detail that contrastive objectives discard. The Cross-image Interaction Prompt Module (CIPM) extends the prompting mechanism explored in SD4Match and SD+DINO by replacing shared or concatenated prompts with personalized, cross-attention-derived prompts for each image in a pair, explicitly modeling inter-image dependencies during feature extraction rather than post-hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'misalignment' between single-image understanding and cross-image matching provides a useful diagnostic lens for the broader question of how to adapt foundation models to relational tasks. The IMIM benchmark, while small (100 pairs), fills a genuine gap: existing matching benchmarks rarely contain multiple instances of the same class, masking the failure mode that motivates this work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to smaller diffusion backbones (the paper shows SD-tiny still helps but with reduced gains), whether the frozen-UNet-at-t=0 recipe transfers to video or 3D matching, and whether the 1.3B-parameter SD backbone is a practical deployment constraint that limits real-world adoption.
