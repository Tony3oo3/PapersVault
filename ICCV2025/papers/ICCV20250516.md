## Problem

Low-light image enhancement is inherently ill-posed because many training pairs (especially heavily degraded ones) cannot be fully fitted by any single network, leaving residual knowledge unexploited. Existing reference-based approaches rely on external knowledge—event cameras, multi-modal predictions, pre-trained features, or quantized codebooks—while overlooking the valuable feature-level information already present within the training dataset itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard low-light RGB image and produces an enhanced normal-light image, operating as a plug-and-play module inserted into the encoder of any existing LLIE backbone. At inference, only 16 learnable 4×4 feature patches and 1×1 vectors (0.017M parameters total) are retained; the Sample Updater and Mutual Updater networks used during training are discarded. The module is resolution-agnostic and works with both supervised and unsupervised LLIE objectives.

## Method

The core idea is to learn a compact set of Learnable Feature Patches and Vectors (LFPVs) that explicitly capture the common knowledge from training samples the LLIE network has not fully fitted (the D_n subset). LFPVs are updated iteratively by two lightweight networks: a Sample Updater (SU) that extracts useful features from each training sample and produces residual updates for individual LFPV nodes, and a Mutual Updater (MU) that propagates information between randomly paired LFPV nodes to enforce inter-node consistency and prevent redundancy. At inference, an attention-based query-and-fusion procedure computes dense softmax-weighted correlations between each input feature and all LFPV nodes (rather than selecting only the nearest), then additively fuses the queried vector-level and patch-level references back into the encoder features. Training uses a dual-loss objective where both the plain network output and the LFPV-augmented output are supervised toward the ground truth, creating an explicit divide-and-conquer signal that pushes residual knowledge into the LFPVs.

## Results

As a plug-and-play module adding only 0.017M parameters, LFPVs consistently improves all tested LLIE backbones across four datasets. The largest gains appear on weaker baselines: MIRNet on SID improves from 20.84 to 21.98 PSNR (+1.14), Restormer on SID from 22.27 to 23.60 PSNR (+1.33), and LLFlow on SDSD-Indoor from 26.51 to 28.44 PSNR (+1.93). Even the strongest baseline, RetinexFormer, benefits: SDSD-Indoor rises from 29.77 to 31.52 PSNR and SDSD-Outdoor from 29.49 to 31.61 PSNR. On LOL-real, SNR+LFPVs reaches 24.38 PSNR / 0.864 SSIM with the fewest extra parameters (0.017M) among all reference-based methods compared.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFPVs is a plug-and-play module, so it does not compete against baselines but rather augments them; every backbone tested (MIRNet, SNR, Restormer, LLFlow, RetinexFormer, Diff-L, Event) shows consistent PSNR/SSIM gains of 0.2–2.0 dB across all four datasets. When compared directly against other reference-based strategies on LOL-real (Table 3), LFPVs (0.017M extra params, 24.38 PSNR with SNR) outperforms SKF (2.15M, 23.05), SMG-depth (16.76M, 24.12), and ACCA (0.088M, 24.00), but trails SMG-semantic (16.76M, 24.84) and Pretrain/CLIP (0.67M, 25.50) on PSNR. The parameter-efficiency advantage is stark: LFPVs achieves near-competitive quality with roughly 40× fewer extra parameters than SMG and 40× fewer than SKF. Importantly, LFPVs is composable with existing reference methods (Table 4), further boosting SNR+SMG from 24.84 to 25.60 PSNR and SNR+Pretrain from 25.50 to 25.77 PSNR, confirming it captures complementary knowledge. For unsupervised settings (Table 2), LFPVs improves ZeroDCE, RUAS, and SCI by 0.3–1.1 PSNR on LOL-real and SID, demonstrating generality beyond supervised training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the LLIE reference-based enhancement paradigm by repurposing the idea of learnable feature stores (codebooks, lookup tables) into a dataset-derived formulation. Rather than relying on external hardware (event cameras), multi-modal predictions (segmentation, depth), or frozen pre-trained features (CLIP), the authors learn a compact set of feature patches and vectors (LFPVs) directly from the training data's residual knowledge — specifically the subset D_n that the backbone network has not fully fitted. The Sample Updater and Mutual Updater mechanism, which treats LFPV nodes as a graph with mutual information propagation, departs from nearest-neighbor codebook retrieval by using attention-based querying over all nodes, and the dual-path training objective explicitly divides labor between the backbone and the reference store.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with only 0.017M additional parameters makes LFPVs a practical add-on for any existing LLIE framework, and the consistent (if modest) gains across CNN, Transformer, flow, and diffusion backbones suggest broad applicability. However, the improvements are typically 0.5–2 dB in PSNR, and the method still requires paired training data and an offline training phase for SU/MU. Open questions include whether the D_f/D_n framing generalizes to other restoration tasks, whether the sequential-model variants (LSTM for SU/MU) unlock substantially larger gains, and whether the approach transfers to unpaired or in-the-wild settings where the 'unfitted subset' is harder to characterize.
