## Problem

Projection-based Zero-Shot CIR methods project image embeddings into the text token space and concatenate them with conditioning text, but the pre-trained CLIP text encoder was never trained to handle such composed inputs, creating a task discrepancy between its original text↔image alignment objective and the image+text→image retrieval task. Existing remedies either require expensive CIR triplets (I_r, T_c, I_t) to fine-tune both encoders or rely on costly inference-time LLM/captioner calls, leaving an efficient post-hoc solution unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252441/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, RTD takes the same inputs as standard projection-based CIR: a reference image and a conditioning text, producing a ranked list of retrieved target images. The key distinction is in training: instead of image triplets, RTD consumes automatically generated text triplets (reference caption T_r, conditioning text T_c, target caption T_t) that can be produced in under 10 minutes for 1M samples via rule-based templates or LLMs. The image encoder and projection module φ remain frozen throughout, so pre-extracted CLIP image embeddings in the retrieval database are unchanged.

## Method

RTD introduces a frozen-target text contrastive loss (FCL) that updates a learnable copy of the text encoder ψ_T^tr to align the embedding of a concatenated caption T_{r+c} with the embedding of the target caption T_t extracted from a frozen copy of the text encoder, using a symmetric InfoNCE objective. Two enhancements further close the training-inference gap: (1) a refined batch sampling strategy that co-samples (T_{r+c}, T_t) pairs with (T_r, T_r) pairs so that the reference caption acts as a hard negative, encouraging the model to capture the modification in T_c; and (2) a refined concatenation scheme that formats the training input as 'a photo of [φ(t_r)] that [T_c]' with Gaussian noise injected into the textual token before projection, mimicking the inference-time prompt and bridging the text-image modality gap. Only the text encoder is updated (image encoder and φ stay frozen), making training text-only and extremely fast—23 minutes on 4 A100 GPUs for ViT-L/14—while the inference pipeline is identical to the base projection-based method.

## Results

RTD consistently improves all tested projection-based CIR methods by an average of over 2.78 points across CIRR, CIRCO, and FashionIQ. With the CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, LinCIR+RTD achieves CIRR R@1 26.6, R@5 56.2, R@10 69.0; CIRCO mAP@5 17.1, mAP@10 18.1, mAP@25 20.1; and FashionIQ R@10 30.2, R@50 51.1. Scaling to ViT-G/14, LinCIR+RTD reaches CIRR R@1 36.3, R@5 67.5, R@10 78.3 and FashionIQ R@10 46.2, R@50 67.3, surpassing MagicLens (ViT-L/14) on CIRR and FashionIQ while remaining within 1% on CIRR R@1 and 0.1% on FashionIQ R@10 when combined with FTI4CIR. All gains are achieved with only 23 minutes of additional training on 4 A100 GPUs for ViT-L/14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With the CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, RTD variants remain below MagicLens on CIRR (e.g., FTI4CIR+RTD R@1 29.1 vs. 30.1) and CIRCO (mAP@5 16.2 vs. 29.6), but nearly match on FashionIQ (R@10 30.6 vs. 30.7, R@50 51.7 vs. 52.5). Scaling to ViT-G/14, LinCIR+RTD clearly outperforms MagicLens (ViT-L/14) on CIRR (R@1 36.3 vs. 30.1, R@5 67.5 vs. 61.7) and FashionIQ (R@10 46.2 vs. 30.7, R@50 67.3 vs. 52.5), and also surpasses LDRE (ViT-G/14) on CIRR R@5/R@10 and FashionIQ, while still trailing on CIRCO mAP. Against training-free methods, LinCIR+RTD (ViT-G/14) beats CIReVL by 9.6 points on CIRR R@1 and 14.0 on FashionIQ R@10, but CIReVL and LDRE retain advantages on CIRCO mAP. Compared to other synthetic-triplet methods (Compodiff, CoVR, CASE), RTD+LinCIR (ViT-G/14) leads on CIRR and FashionIQ while Compodiff (ViT-G/14) is the closest competitor on CIRCO. The consistent 2–5 point average gain over all projection-based baselines (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR, Context-I2W, FTI4CIR) confirms RTD's plug-and-play compatibility across different projection module designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTD positions itself as a post-hoc complement to the family of projection-based zero-shot CIR methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR, Context-I2W, FTI4CIR), which all share the architecture of projecting an image embedding into CLIP's text token space via a learned module φ. The key conceptual departure is identifying and addressing the 'task discrepancy' between CLIP's pre-training objective (text↔image alignment) and the CIR inference task (image+text→image), a gap that prior task-discrepancy methods (Combiner, FashionERN, MT-CIR) attempt to close but only by training on expensive CIR triplets (I_r, T_c, I_t). RTD instead uses cheap, automatically generated text triplets (T_r, T_c, T_t) and updates only the text encoder via a frozen-target contrastive loss, inheriting the language-only training efficiency of LinCIR while introducing a new loss formulation and hard-negative batch sampling strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: RTD adds only ~23 minutes of training on 4 A100 GPUs to match or exceed methods requiring 128 GPUs for hours (CompoDiff, MagicLens), and it is a drop-in module compatible with any existing projection-based CIR method without modifying the image encoder or retrieval database. An important open question is whether the text-only training paradigm fundamentally limits how much task discrepancy can be resolved, since the image encoder and projection module remain frozen. The paper also leaves open whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based architectures or to non-English settings, and whether the gains saturate with larger text triplet scales beyond the 2.5M used here.
