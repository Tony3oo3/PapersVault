## Problem

Existing learning-based 3D reconstruction methods like DUSt3R and MASt3R are trained primarily on static outdoor scenes and fail to accurately reconstruct articulated, self-occluded human bodies. Concurrent approaches that jointly reconstruct humans and their environment rely on multi-stage optimization pipelines (iterative bundle adjustment, pre-computed HMR, SLAM), limiting scalability and practicality. HAMSt3R addresses this by providing a fully feedforward architecture that jointly recovers scene geometry and dense human semantics in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of uncalibrated RGB images (downscaled to a maximum dimension of 518 pixels) and produces, per image, a pixel-aligned 3D point map in the first camera's coordinate frame, a confidence map, a matching descriptor, an instance segmentation mask per person, a 4-channel DensePose output (RGB SMPL-template color + binary validity mask), and a binary SMPL-projection mask. For scenes with more than two views, the network is run independently on every image pair and results are aggregated via MASt3R's global alignment, with 2D-overlap-based ID resolution for consistent person labeling and confidence-weighted averaging for DensePose. The 3D point map can be post-processed with SMPL fitting (via MvSMPLFitting) to extract parametric body poses.

## Method

HAMSt3R extends the MASt3R architecture (Siamese ViT encoder + dual ViT decoder with cross-attention) by replacing the encoder with DUNE, a ViT-B/14 backbone distilled from three teachers—DINOv2, Multi-HMR, and MASt3R itself—via the UNIC projection mechanism, yielding features simultaneously robust for scene geometry and human bodies. Three new prediction heads are added: a Mask2Former-inspired instance segmentation head for human silhouettes, a DensePose head producing continuous RGB SMPL-template mappings (supervised by L2 loss rather than discrete part classification), and a binary mask head isolating SMPL-projected body regions from hair and clothing. Training freezes the DUNE encoder and fine-tunes the decoders and all heads with a weighted sum of the MASt3R point-map/matching loss, segmentation loss, DensePose L2 loss, and mask cross-entropy, using a 50/50 mix of the original MASt3R dataset and human-centric data. To supply large-scale multi-view human supervision, the authors generate 524k synthetic images by placing HumGen3D humans (with AMASS poses) into procedurally generated Infinigen Indoors scenes, supplemented by BEDLAM, HuMMan, and EgoBody.

## Results

On EgoExo4D, HAMSt3R achieves the best world-coordinate human pose error (W-MPJPE = 0.51 m), outperforming HSfM (0.56 m) and UnCaliPose (2.90 m), though it trails HSfM on Procrustes-aligned error (PA-MPJPE = 0.09 m vs. 0.06 m). On EgoHumans, HAMSt3R achieves GA-MPJPE = 0.42 m, beating UnCaliPose (0.67 m) and Multi-HMR (0.99 m), but is outperformed by HSfM (0.21 m) which benefits from global optimization. For camera pose on EgoExo4D, HAMSt3R achieves the best translation error (TE = 0.60 m) and rotation accuracy (RRA@10 = 0.99) across all baselines. On traditional depth estimation, HAMSt3R's average relative error (4.26) and inlier ratio (61.0%) are lower than MASt3R (3.13 / 73.7%) but competitive with DeepV2D (8.60 / 39.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HSfM (CVPR 2025), the closest concurrent method, HAMSt3R wins on EgoExo4D W-MPJPE (0.51 m vs. 0.56 m) but loses on PA-MPJPE (0.09 m vs. 0.06 m); on EgoHumans, HSfM dominates all three metrics (W-MPJPE 1.04 vs. 3.80 m), largely due to its global bundle-adjustment optimization step. Against UnCaliPose, HAMSt3R wins on GA-MPJPE for EgoHumans (0.42 vs. 0.67 m) and on EgoExo4D W-MPJPE (0.51 vs. 2.90 m), but trails slightly on PA-MPJPE (0.14 vs. 0.13 m). Against the monocular Multi-HMR baseline, HAMSt3R substantially outperforms on all multi-view metrics (e.g., EgoExo4D W-MPJPE 0.51 vs. 2.88 m). For camera pose on EgoExo4D, HAMSt3R beats all baselines including DUNE (TE 0.60 vs. 1.03 m, AE 2.85° vs. 6.45°), while on EgoHumans DUNE remains superior overall, with HAMSt3R performing best in small-scale scenes (s-TE 0.12 m, RRA@10 1.00). On traditional tasks, HAMSt3R degrades relative to MASt3R on depth (avg. rel. 4.26 vs. 3.13) and CO3Dv2 pose regression (RRA@15 90.7 vs. 94.6), but slightly improves on RealEstate10K mAA30 (77.4 vs. 76.4), indicating the human-centric training has limited negative transfer on non-human scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAMSt3R extends the DUSt3R/MASt3R paradigm of feedforward 3D pointmap regression from static scenes to human-centric environments by swapping in the DUNE distilled encoder (which fuses DINOv2, Multi-HMR, and MASt3R representations) and adding human-specific heads for instance segmentation, DensePose, and SMPL-projection masking. It departs from concurrent joint human-scene reconstruction methods (JOSH, HSfM, SynCHMR) that rely on multi-stage optimization pipelines by performing all predictions in a single forward pass. The training data pipeline, combining Infinigen Indoors procedural scenes with HumGen3D human generation, is a new synthetic data contribution not present in prior MASt3R or HMR training setups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully feedforward method that produces dense 3D point maps enriched with per-instance human semantics (body-part-level DensePose, instance IDs) from sparse uncalibrated views, removing the optimization bottleneck that limits scalability of prior joint reconstruction approaches. However, clear trade-offs remain: depth accuracy degrades noticeably relative to MASt3R, camera pose estimation suffers on large-scale outdoor scenes (EgoHumans), and Procrustes-aligned human pose accuracy still lags behind the optimization-based HSfM. Open questions include whether the method extends to video and dynamic scenes, whether the encoder trade-off (human understanding vs. structural depth) can be resolved, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap in human-centric training can be further closed.
