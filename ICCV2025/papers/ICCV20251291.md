## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) models, such as ACE0, degenerate when training images provide insufficient multi-view constraints (e.g., texture-poor walls, repetitive structures), producing dispersed point clouds, camera poses that penetrate geometry, and artifacts in downstream tasks. Prior SCR frameworks incorporate only very weak regularization (e.g., an initialization loss bounding depth), leaving no principled mechanism to inject high-level reconstruction priors that enforce plausible scene geometry during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB (or RGB-D) images of a scene together with their known camera poses; the diffusion prior is additionally pre-trained offline on ~700 ScanNet point clouds (5,120 points per scene, normalized to [-1,1]). At query time, the trained SCR model takes a single RGB image and outputs per-pixel 3D scene coordinates, which are fed to RANSAC+PnP to recover a 6-DoF camera pose; an explicit point cloud can also be extracted by running the model over all mapping images. The depth distribution prior is parameterized by a Laplace distribution (μ=1.73 m, b=60 cm) fitted to ScanNet training depths, and the RGB-D variant uses a narrow Laplace (b′=10 cm) centered at each measured depth value.

## Method

The paper reformulates SCR training as maximum-likelihood estimation: the reprojection loss becomes −log p(h*, I_M | y) and a new regularization term L_reg = −log p(y) acts as a prior over scene coordinates, optimized jointly with the reprojection loss. Three priors are proposed: (1) a Laplace depth-distribution prior applied either as per-pixel negative log-likelihood or as a Wasserstein distance between the mini-batch depth histogram and the target Laplace; (2) an RGB-D depth prior substituting the broad Laplace with a narrow one centered at measured depth; and (3) a 3D point-cloud diffusion prior built on a PVCNN noise estimator trained via DDPM on ScanNet scenes, whose noise prediction serves as the score function ∇_x log p(x) to nudge predicted coordinates toward plausible scene layouts. The diffusion prior is activated only after 5,000 ACE mapping iterations, with the diffusion timestep linearly interpolated from T/20 down to 0, and is skipped for points whose reprojection error is below 30 pixels. All priors are applied during training only, leaving test-time inference cost unchanged.

## Results

On 7Scenes relocalization (5 cm/5° threshold), ACE+Diffusion achieves 97.7% average accuracy, outperforming ACE alone (97.1%) and GLACE (95.6%), with the largest gain on the Stairs scene (86.2% vs. 81.9%). For SfM on ScanNet, ACE0+LaplaceNLL improves registration rate to 98.9% (vs. 98.1% for ACE0) and reduces ATE/RPE from 26.6/4.0 cm to 25.4/3.5 cm. On the larger Indoor6 scenes, the diffusion prior boosts registration rate by +4.7% (61.8% vs. 57.1%) and PSNR by +1.1 dB (14.6 vs. 13.5). The RGB-D depth prior yields the largest single improvement on ScanNet, reducing ATE/RPE to 18.3/3.5 cm and median error to 12.8 cm/4.4°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SCR baselines, ACE+Diffusion (97.7% avg) outperforms ACE alone (97.1%) by 0.6% and GLACE (95.6%) by 2.1% on 7Scenes, with the largest per-scene gain on Stairs (+4.3% over ACE). The Laplace NLL prior also improves over ACE (97.3% vs. 97.1%) and reduces mapping time from 5 min to 4.5 min. Among SCR-with-3D-supervision methods, ACE+Laplace NLL (97.4%) trails DSAC* (98.2%) and FocusTune (97.9%) but does so with 15× less mapping time than DSAC* and without requiring depth input. Against feature-matching baselines, the SCR methods with priors (97.4–97.7%) are competitive with AS(SIFT) (98.5%) while being orders of magnitude faster (~5 min vs. ~1.5 h) and producing far smaller maps (4 MB vs. ~200 MB). On Indoor6 SfM, the diffusion prior gives the clearest win over the ACE0 baseline: +4.7% registration rate and +1.1 dB PSNR, while the RGB-D depth prior dramatically reduces pose error (ATE/RPE 18.3/3.5 cm vs. 29.2/6.0 cm for the DSAC*-loss baseline). EGFS [50] on Indoor6 (56.1%) outperforms both ACE and ACE-Diff at N=5120, but the code is not publicly available and no direct comparison at N=51200 is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ACE/ACE0/GLACE family of scene coordinate regression (SCR) frameworks by reformulating their training objective as maximum likelihood estimation, which naturally admits a prior term over scene coordinates. Prior SCR methods (ACE, ACE0, GLACE) relied solely on reprojection loss and a simple initialization constraint, making them vulnerable to degeneracy in texture-poor or repetitive scenes. The learned prior draws on 3D point cloud diffusion (PVCNN-based, following DDPM training), conceptually connecting to DiffusioNeRF's use of diffusion as a NeRF regularizer but operating directly in 3D space rather than through 2D rendering. The hand-crafted depth distribution prior (Laplace NLL / Wasserstein distance) parallels depth regularization terms used in sparse-view novel view synthesis (Mip-NeRF 360, RegNeRF), repurposed here for SCR training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a lean 3D diffusion model trained on only ~700 indoor scenes provides a useful plausibility signal for regularizing scene reconstruction, sidestepping the need for high-fidelity 3D generation. The probabilistic reformulation is general and could accommodate other priors (e.g., semantic, physical) without architectural changes. Limitations include the indoor-only evaluation, modest absolute gains (a few percentage points in registration rate, ~1 dB PSNR), and the added ~3 min mapping cost from the diffusion prior. Open questions include extending to outdoor scenes (requiring broader depth distributions and more diverse diffusion training data), improving 3D point cloud encoder expressiveness, and exploring conditional diffusion signals for scene-specific guidance.
