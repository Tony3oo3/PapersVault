## Problem

Existing low-light light field (LF) enhancement methods are trained for fixed illumination levels and cannot adapt to unknown degradation in real-world scenes. More critically, they ignore the disparity constraint that illumination adjustment maps must satisfy across views, breaking the inherent view-consistency of the enhanced LF and degrading downstream tasks like depth estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4D low-light LF tensor (U×V×H×W, e.g., 5×5 or 8×8 views at 642×432 spatial resolution) and produces an enhanced LF image at the same angular and spatial resolution. A key design choice is that the illumination estimator (VCAIA) is a lightweight plug-in module (0.04 s per LF) that can be prepended to any existing LF enhancement or denoising network, outputting a per-view illumination adjustment map that is element-wise multiplied with the input before the recovery stage.

## Method

VCNet decomposes enhancement into illumination adjustment (VCAIA) and corruption recovery (VCRM). VCAIA estimates a global illumination matrix via a log-based Retinex formula over the view-averaged image, then refines it per-view with cascaded 3D spatial convolutions. The core novelty is a self-supervised view-consistent loss (ℓ_vc): for each pixel, it warps all views toward the center view under N discrete disparity candidates, builds a cost volume of angular variances, selects the disparity with minimum variance, and then minimizes that minimum variance—enforcing cross-view consistency of illumination maps without requiring ground-truth disparities. VCRM is a multi-scale decoder that applies deformable convolutions on EPIs with uniform offsets in both V-H and U-W spaces to preserve epipolar geometry during detail recovery. Training minimizes a hybrid loss (0.5·ℓ_vc + 1·ℓ_rec + 0.5·ℓ_ssim) on the L3F dataset under both fixed and dynamic illumination protocols.

## Results

On the L3F-Dynamic task (5×5 views), VCNet achieves 24.58/24.06/23.09 PSNR on L3F-20/50/100, outperforming the best single-frame baseline RFormer* (27.29/23.81/22.51) on L3F-20 but leading on L3F-50 and L3F-100, while achieving substantially higher view-consistency AP scores (79.07/78.46/76.80 vs. 67.26/42.31/34.84 for RFormer*). On the real-world L3F-Wild dataset, VCNet attains the best no-reference quality scores (NIQE 14.22, BRISQUE 25.98) with a runtime of 6.33 s per LF. Adding the VCAIA illumination module (𝓔) to existing LF methods (DRLF, MSP) yields ~3 dB PSNR improvement without architectural changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-frame Retinex-based methods (RFormer*, Pydiff*, DarkIR) fine-tuned on L3F-Dynamic, VCNet achieves the best LPIPS (0.089 vs. 0.163 for RFormer* on L3F-20) and dramatically higher AP scores (79.07 vs. 67.26), confirming that per-view processing breaks view consistency even when pixel-level quality is competitive. Among LF-based methods on the dynamic task (Table 1), VCNet surpasses MSP (21.37 PSNR) by 3.2 dB and DRLF (19.10) by 5.5 dB on L3F-20, with similar margins on L3F-50 and L3F-100. On the fixed-illumination task (Table 4, 5×5 views), VCNet leads all LF baselines (L3F-Net, FER-Net, LRT, LFLL-DCU, DRLF, MSP) by 0.6–1.6 dB PSNR across all three illumination levels. On the real-world L3F-Wild dataset, VCNet achieves the lowest NIQE (14.22 vs. 15.67 for RFormer*) and BRISQUE (25.98 vs. 29.42 for MSP+𝓔), while running in 6.33 s—faster than DRLF (16.22 s) but slower than MSP (3.89 s). The plug-and-play VCAIA module (𝓔) consistently adds ~3 dB PSNR and improves AP for DRLF and MSP without modifying their architectures, validating its generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Retinex-based low-light enhancement to the light field domain by explicitly addressing view consistency in illumination map estimation, a constraint that prior LF methods (L3F-Net, DRLF, MSP, LFLL-DCU) largely ignore. It departs from the fixed-illumination paradigm of most LF enhancers by adopting a scene-adaptive formulation inspired by single-image Retinex methods (Zero-DCE, RFormer), while introducing a self-supervised disparity-consistency loss that operates without ground-truth depth. The global illumination estimation directly adapts the adaptive local tone mapping formula from Ahn et al. [1], and the recovery module borrows deformable convolution on EPIs from LF super-resolution literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

View consistency in enhanced LF images is critical for downstream tasks like depth estimation and 3D reconstruction, yet prior methods silently break it through per-view or single-gain illumination adjustments. The plug-and-play VCAIA module, which adds only ~0.04s of computation and improves all tested baselines by ~3 dB, offers a practical drop-in solution for existing LF pipelines. Open questions include whether the relaxed candidate-disparity formulation generalizes to LFs with larger angular baselines or occluded scenes, and whether the approach extends to video light fields where temporal consistency adds another dimension.
