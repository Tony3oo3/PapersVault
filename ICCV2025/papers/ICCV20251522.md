## Problem

Existing 3D open-vocabulary scene understanding methods distill from a single 2D teacher model, inheriting its category-specific misidentifications (e.g., confusing a picture for a wall). Integrating multiple 2D models to correct these errors is hindered by two obstacles: their image-text feature spaces are unaligned, and per-category model capabilities cannot be directly measured without test annotations, which are unavailable in the zero-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (indoor RGB-D scans at ~148k points or outdoor LiDAR at ~34k points) along with multi-view RGB-D images for pixel-point correspondence, and a text query specifying the target category. It outputs a per-point feature vector in CLIP's text-aligned embedding space; at inference, the label is obtained by cosine similarity against CLIP text embeddings of candidate categories with no post-processing. For downstream tasks, the same distilled encoder produces per-Gaussian labels (3DGS) or per-instance semantic assignments (combined with Mask3D proposals).

## Method

SAS aligns heterogeneous 2D models (e.g., LSeg and SEEM) into a shared CLIP text space by generating rich per-mask captions via TAP and encoding them with a common text encoder, then projecting back to per-pixel features. It constructs per-category model capabilities annotation-free by synthesizing images with Stable Diffusion, extracting pseudo-masks via cross-attention maps refined by SAM, and computing mIoU between each 2D model's predictions and these pseudo-masks. The constructed capability vectors serve as category-aware weights in a softmax-gated fusion of the aligned 2D point features, so that for each point the model with higher demonstrated capability on the predicted category contributes more. A MinkowskiNet18A 3D encoder is then distilled from the fused 2D features using superpoint-level cosine loss, followed by temporal ensembling self-distillation (EMA-smoothed pseudo-labels with cross-entropy) to further refine the 3D model without a trainable teacher.

## Results

SAS achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation with mIoU of 47.5 on nuScenes, 61.9 on ScanNet v2, and 48.6 on Matterport3D, outperforming the previous best method OV3D by +1.4%, +4.6%, and +2.8% respectively. On the long-tail evaluation (up to 160 categories on Matterport3D), SAS maintains a consistent advantage over OpenScene (e.g., 28.5 vs. 22.9 at K=40). In downstream tasks, SAS reaches 63.9 mIoU on 3D Gaussian segmentation (vs. 60.7 for Semantic Gaussians) and 25.1 mAP on 3D instance segmentation (vs. 19.9 for OpenIns3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAS outperforms all zero-shot baselines across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor OV3D (CVPR 2024), SAS gains +1.4 mIoU on nuScenes (47.5 vs. 44.6), +4.6 on ScanNet v2 (61.9 vs. 57.3), and +2.8 on Matterport3D (48.6 vs. 45.8). Compared to OpenScene (CVPR 2023), the original 2D-to-3D distillation framework, SAS improves by +15.4, +7.7, and +5.2 mIoU on nuScenes, ScanNet, and Matterport3D respectively. GGSD (ECCV 2024) is beaten by +1.4 on nuScenes and +8.5 on Matterport3D, while Diff2Scene (ECCV 2024) is surpassed by +13.3 on ScanNet and +3.1 on Matterport3D. In the long-tail setting (Table 2), SAS consistently exceeds OpenScene by 5.6, 4.7, and 2.3 mIoU at K=40, 80, and 160 categories. For downstream tasks, SAS surpasses Semantic Gaussians by +3.2 mIoU on Gaussian segmentation and OpenIns3D by +5.2 mAP on instance segmentation, confirming the generalizability of the integrated 2D prior approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAS extends the 2D-to-3D distillation paradigm established by OpenScene (CVPR 2023) and the superpoint/self-distillation framework of GGSD (ECCV 2024), but departs from both by integrating multiple 2D open-vocabulary teachers rather than relying on a single one. The key conceptual departure is the explicit modeling of per-category model capability: features from different 2D models are first aligned into a shared CLIP text space via generated captions (Model Alignment via Text), and then fused using capability scores computed on diffusion-model-synthesized images (Annotation-free Model Capability Construction). This positions SAS as the first method to treat multi-teacher integration as a first-class design problem in 3D open-vocabulary distillation, rather than simply picking the best single 2D model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in open-vocabulary 3D understanding: no single 2D foundation model is uniformly best across all categories, and the distilled 3D model inherits the teacher's blind spots. The annotation-free capability construction via Stable Diffusion + SAM is a clean solution to the chicken-and-egg problem of evaluating 2D models without test annotations. However, the approach is still bounded by the ceiling of the individual 2D models, the capability scores are computed on a fixed pre-built vocabulary (limiting true open-vocabulary generalization), and the fusion strategy—while effective—remains a relatively simple softmax-weighted combination that may not capture more complex inter-model interactions. Open questions include scalability to many more 2D models, whether the diffusion-based capability proxy transfers to truly novel categories, and whether the approach can be made more efficient for real-time applications.
