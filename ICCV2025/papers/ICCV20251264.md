## Problem

Existing VAE-based zero-shot skeleton action recognition methods produce coarse feature representations that fail to capture fine-grained semantic distinctions between visually similar actions (e.g., 'drinking' vs. 'eating'), and rigid cross-modal alignment treats all skeleton-text pairs as equally reliable despite inherent ambiguities from occlusions and viewpoint variations. This paper addresses how to enrich skeletal semantics through frequency-domain modeling and how to perform robust cross-modal alignment that accounts for skeleton noise and ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences of shape J×3×F×M (joints, coordinates, frames, subjects) extracted via Shift-GCN, along with GPT-4-generated text descriptions comprising an action label, a local description (fine-grained body-part motions), and a global description (overall movement patterns), all encoded through CLIP's text encoder. The output is a predicted action class label for both seen and unseen categories, with evaluation under both standard zero-shot (unseen-only) and generalized zero-shot (seen + unseen) protocols on NTU-60 and NTU-120.

## Method

FS-VAE applies a Discrete Cosine Transform to decompose skeleton joint trajectories into frequency coefficients, then selectively amplifies low-frequency components (global motion structure) with a progressively diminishing scaling factor and adaptively attenuates high-frequency components (fine-grained details) to suppress skeletal jitter while preserving micro-movements, before reconstructing via IDCT. A Semantic-based Action Description concatenates CLIP-encoded action label, local description, and global description into a unified text embedding that captures both localized dynamics and holistic action patterns. The Calibrated Cross-Alignment Loss uses a sigmoid-based pairwise distance measure that dynamically balances positive and negative skeleton-text pairs, exploiting the symmetric property ℓ(a)+ℓ(−a)=1 to naturally counterbalance mismatched pairs caused by ambiguous skeletons. Training minimizes the sum of VAE ELBO reconstruction losses (for both skeleton and text branches) and the calibrated alignment loss, with hyperparameters α and λ controlling the trade-off and sensitivity respectively.

## Results

On the NTU-60 dataset (55/5 split), FS-VAE achieves 86.9% zero-shot accuracy, surpassing the previous best (GZSSAR* at 83.3%) by 3.6 points. On NTU-120 (96/24 split), it reaches 62.5% accuracy, outperforming GZSSAR* (60.7%) by 1.8 points. In the Generalized Zero-Shot setting, FS-VAE attains the highest H-score of 75.7% on NTU-60 (55/5) and 63.3% on NTU-120 (110/10), exceeding the next-best methods by 6.3 and 2.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FS-VAE outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and split. The closest competitor is GZSSAR* [27] (the VAE-based baseline the authors build upon), which FS-VAE surpasses by 3.6 points on NTU-60 55/5, 7.4 points on NTU-60 48/12, 2.4 points on NTU-120 110/10, and 1.8 points on NTU-120 96/24. Among recent 2024–2025 methods, SA-DVAE [28] (ECCV 2025) and PURLS [49] (CVPR 2024) are the strongest alternatives; FS-VAE leads SA-DVAE by 4.5 points on NTU-60 55/5 and by 16.4 points on NTU-120 96/24, while beating PURLS by 7.7 and 10.5 points on the same splits respectively. Older baselines (ReViSE, JPoSE, CADA-VAE) are outperformed by 10–30+ points, reflecting substantial progress in the field. In the GZSL setting, FS-VAE's H-score advantage over GZSSAR* is even more pronounced (e.g., +6.3 on NTU-60 55/5, +5.3 on NTU-120 96/24), indicating that the frequency-enhanced and calibrated alignment components particularly benefit balanced seen/unseen classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based zero-shot skeleton action recognition line established by SynSE [15], GZSSAR [27], and SA-DVAE [28], which align skeleton and text features in a shared latent space. It departs from these by introducing DCT-based frequency decomposition (previously explored only in supervised skeleton recognition [4, 43] and motion prediction [12, 26]) to enrich skeletal semantics, and by proposing a multi-level semantic description (action label + local + global descriptions via CLIP) rather than relying solely on action labels or temporal motion descriptions. The calibrated cross-alignment loss draws on symmetric loss theory from noisy-label learning [5, 14] to handle skeleton-text ambiguities, a concern prior VAE-based ZSSAR methods addressed only through rigid positive-pair alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain perspective offers a principled way to separate global motion structure from fine-grained details in skeleton sequences, which is particularly relevant for distinguishing semantically similar actions (e.g., drinking vs. eating) in zero-shot settings. The calibrated loss provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for robust cross-modal alignment under skeleton ambiguity, addressing a practical limitation of prior rigid alignment approaches. However, the method remains confined to skeleton-based recognition and relies on GPT-4-generated descriptions, raising open questions about whether the frequency-enhancement strategy transfers to video-based ZSSAR or whether simpler text prompts could achieve comparable gains. The moderate improvements (3–7% over the strongest baseline) suggest the approach is effective but not a paradigm shift in how zero-shot skeleton recognition is formulated.
