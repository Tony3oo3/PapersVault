## Problem

Existing certifiably optimal rotation averaging methods minimize the isotropic chordal distance, discarding the anisotropic uncertainties naturally present in relative rotation estimates. Naively substituting anisotropic costs into the standard SDP relaxation (which optimizes over O(3)) fails catastrophically: the relaxation never yields a rank-3 solution because indefinite cost matrices cause the O(3) minimizer to be a reflection rather than a rotation. The paper addresses this by deriving a stronger relaxation that enforces membership in conv(SO(3)), enabling certifiably global anisotropic rotation averaging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise relative rotation estimates (from two-view relative pose) together with their 3×3 Hessian matrices H_ij, obtained by marginalizing nuisance parameters (camera position, 3D points) via the Schur complement from a Gauss-Newton two-view solve. The Hessian is converted to a cost matrix M_ij = tr(H_ij)/2 · I − H_ij, which is generally indefinite when one rotation direction is much better constrained than the others. The output is a set of absolute camera rotation matrices R_i ∈ SO(3), certified globally optimal when the SDP solution X has rank 3, with the gauge fixed by aligning to ground truth via a global rotation.

## Method

The key insight is that anisotropic cost matrices M_ij are frequently indefinite (one eigenvalue negative), which makes the O(3) minimizer of each loss term a reflection (det = −1) rather than the true rotation, breaking the standard SDP relaxation. The paper proves this via a KKT analysis (Theorem 1) showing the O(3) optimum is strictly lower than the SO(3) optimum by 2|λ₃|, and shows that restricting to conv(SO(3)) restores correctness (Corollary 1). The proposed relaxation (SDP-cSO(3)) is derived by introducing auxiliary variables Q_ij = R_i R_j^T, forming the Lagrangian dual over Q_ij ∈ SO(3), and taking the bidual, which yields the constraint X_ij ∈ conv(SO(3)) for all off-diagonal blocks. This constraint is implemented as a 4×4 linear matrix inequality using the orbitope SDP characterization, making the full problem a standard semidefinite program solvable by general-purpose conic solvers.

## Results

On 11 real SfM datasets (69–157 cameras), the proposed SDP-cSO(3) method achieves certifiably optimal rank-3 solutions in all cases, while the anisotropic variant without conv(SO(3)) constraints (SDP-O(3)-ANISO) yields rank 6–7 solutions with rotation errors 100–300× larger. Compared to the standard isotropic baseline (SDP-O(3)-ISO), SDP-cSO(3) reduces the rotation error √(Σᵢ‖Rᵢ−Rᵢ*‖²) in 10 of 11 datasets, with improvements ranging from 7% (Vercingetorix: 0.3146→0.2910) to 54% (Tsar Nikolai I: 0.1170→0.0534). On synthetic experiments (2–100 cameras, 0–90% missing data), the proposed method consistently produces lower median rotation errors and faster solver runtimes than the standard relaxation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SDP-O(3)-ISO, the standard isotropic chordal-distance SDP relaxation from Eriksson et al. [18]. The proposed SDP-cSO(3) outperforms this baseline on 10 of 11 real datasets in rotation error, with the largest gains on Tsar Nikolai I (0.0534 vs. 0.1170, a 54% reduction) and King's College (0.0796 vs. 0.1656, a 52% reduction). The sole exception is Kronan, where SDP-cSO(3) (0.3892) is worse than SDP-O(3)-ISO (0.2149), which the authors attribute to the single-noise-realization limitation and potential bias in the SfM ground truth. The intermediate method SDP-O(3)-ANISO (anisotropic objective without conv(SO(3)) constraints) catastrophically fails on all real datasets, producing rank-6 or rank-7 solutions with rotation errors of 14.8–30.3, confirming the theoretical analysis that dropping the determinant constraint is insufficient when cost matrices M_ij are indefinite (77–100% of pairs in the tested scenes). On synthetic data, SDP-cSO(3) also matches or beats SDP-O(3)-ISO in solver runtime, suggesting the local conv(SO(3)) constraints reduce oscillations in the first-order SDP solver.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certifiably optimal rotation averaging framework of Eriksson et al. [18], which established tight SDP relaxations for the isotropic chordal distance, to the anisotropic setting where per-edge uncertainty (from two-view Hessian matrices) is explicitly modeled. It leverages the SDP characterization of conv(SO(3)) from Saunderson et al. [41] to construct a stronger relaxation, and builds on the anisotropic chordal distance concept introduced empirically by Zhang et al. [51]. The key departure from prior work is the demonstration that the standard O(3) relaxation fundamentally fails for anisotropic costs when the cost matrix M_ij is indefinite—a common scenario in image-based estimation—requiring the additional conv(SO(3)) constraint for correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation to yield tight (rank-3) semidefinite relaxations for anisotropic rotation averaging, providing certifiably globally optimal solutions that incorporate per-edge uncertainty. The practical impact is more accurate SfM and SLAM reconstructions, with consistent improvements across 11 real datasets. However, the paper relies on general-purpose conic solvers rather than dedicated algorithms, and the authors acknowledge that no theoretical guarantee of tightness exists (only empirical evidence). Designing efficient dedicated solvers for the stronger conv(SO(3)) relaxation, and extending the framework to handle outliers or non-Gaussian noise, are important open directions.
