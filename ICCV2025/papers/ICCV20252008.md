## Problem

NeRF reconstruction suffers from shape-radiance ambiguity, especially with sparse input views, and existing depth regularization methods rely on fixed depth predictions that are unreliable for unbounded outdoor scenes. The paper addresses this by replacing pointwise depth estimates with view-consistent distributions that implicitly regularize sampling without requiring accurate 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of posed RGB images (10–110 views per scene) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics. DINOv2 features are pre-extracted and distilled to 32 dimensions per pixel, and normalized RGB values serve as low-level features. The view-consistent sampling is activated only during the first 5,000 of 30,000 training iterations, after which standard Nerfacto sampling resumes. The output is a trained NeRF (MLP + feature grid) that renders novel views via volume rendering.

## Method

VS-NeRF distills DINOv2 features (384-dim) to 32-dim geometric features using a lightweight ResNet bottleneck block trained with a CLIP-style contrastive loss on Megadepth correspondences. During training, pre-sampled 3D points along each ray are projected onto all visible views, and a view-consistency score is computed by checking whether both the color and distilled feature similarities exceed a threshold (δ=0.4) across views. These scores form a per-ray distribution from which importance sampling (via Nerfstudio's PDF sampler) draws the actual rendering samples, concentrating them near surface-consistent points. A depth-pushing loss (−log(expected depth + ε), weighted at 0.0001) is added to the color loss to prevent background collapse in unbounded scenes. The entire pipeline is built on Nerfacto with all other settings unchanged, isolating the contribution of the sampling scheme and depth-pushing loss.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, VS-NeRF achieves PSNR 21.40, SSIM 0.625, and LPIPS 0.400, outperforming all baselines including Nerfacto (PSNR 19.05) and multi-view depth regularization (PSNR 20.15). On Tanks & Temples, it reaches PSNR 19.45, SSIM 0.714, and LPIPS 0.373, again leading all competitors. The method requires approximately 39 minutes of training on a single A100 GPU, roughly 3× longer than Nerfacto (11–12 min) due to the view-consistency computation, but the view-consistent sampling is only active for the first 5000 of 30000 iterations. When applied to the slower Mip-NeRF360 base, VS-NeRF improves PSNR from 22.54 to 23.37, though 3D Gaussian Splatting still leads at 23.53 PSNR with far faster training (27 min vs. 32 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VS-NeRF consistently outperforms its Nerfacto base by +2.35 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and +1.16 dB on Tanks & Temples, confirming the effectiveness of the view-consistent sampling and depth-pushing loss. Against the strongest depth-regularization baseline (multi-view depth via MVSFormer++), VS-NeRF leads by +1.25 dB on Mip-NeRF360 and +0.17 dB on Tanks & Temples; the gap widens in sparser-view settings where multi-view depth becomes unreliable. Monocular depth (ZoeDepth + SparseNeRF ranking loss) degrades severely on Tanks & Temples (PSNR drops to 13.81 vs. 19.45 for VS-NeRF), confirming the paper's claim that pseudo-depth is inconsistent across views in unbounded scenes. NeRFAcc provides a solid improvement over Nerfacto but is surpassed by VS-NeRF on both datasets, with the advantage growing as the number of training views decreases. TensoRF lags significantly (PSNR 15.68 vs. 21.40 on Mip-NeRF360), attributed to tensor-based representation limitations in complex unbounded scenes. In the supplementary comparison (Table 3), 3D Gaussian Splatting still achieves the highest PSNR (23.53 vs. 23.37 for Mip-NeRF360+Ours), but the authors note that NeRF-based representations remain preferable for downstream tasks like surface reconstruction and inverse rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Nerfacto implementation from the Nerfstudio project, replacing its sampling scheme with a view-consistency-based importance sampling strategy while retaining the Mip-NeRF360 proposal network. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant depth-regularization paradigm (DS-NeRF, SparseNeRF, MVSFormer++) by substituting fixed depth predictions with a distribution computed from view-consistent features, thereby avoiding the generalization failures of depth estimators on unbounded scenes. The use of DINOv2 features distilled via a lightweight contrastive-trained ResNet bottleneck (inspired by Diffusion Hyperfeatures) to form a compact geometric representation is a novel repurposing of foundation model features for NeRF training, distinct from prior feature-field distillation approaches that lift 2D features into 3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view-consistent sampling approach offers a more robust alternative to depth-based regularization for challenging outdoor and unbounded scenes, where monocular and multi-view depth estimators are known to fail. However, the method introduces significant computational overhead (~38 min vs. ~11 min for Nerfacto) and is only active during the first 5000 of 30000 training iterations, limiting its practical adoption. The authors acknowledge that 3D Gaussian Splatting still outperforms their NeRF-based method on pure view synthesis quality, and they identify extending the regularization to Gaussian Splatting and developing more efficient implementations as key future directions. An open question is whether the binary thresholding in the view-consistency metric (Eq. 4) is optimal or whether a soft, continuous weighting would yield further gains.
