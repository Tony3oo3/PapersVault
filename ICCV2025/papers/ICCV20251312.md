## Problem

Existing semantic watermarking methods for latent diffusion models (Tree-Ring, RingID, Zodiac) embed patterns in the latent Fourier domain but discard the imaginary component after inverse Fourier transform, destroying frequency integrity and degrading both detection accuracy and generative quality. Additionally, these methods apply the Fourier transform to the full latent spatial matrix, making them vulnerable to cropping attacks that remove watermark information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251312/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a text prompt and embeds the watermark directly into the 64×64 latent noise before Stable Diffusion v2-1-base synthesis at 512×512 resolution, producing a watermarked image with zero additional processing time (merged-in-generation scheme). For detection, a clean or attacked image is inverted via DDIM (50 steps) to recover the latent query, from which the watermark key region is extracted. The Fourier transform is applied only to the central 44×44 region of the latent space, and detection computes L1 distance in the complex Fourier key region to yield a verification decision (TPR@1%FPR) or a message index (QR code content for HSQR, tree-ring pattern index for HSTR).

## Method

The core contribution is Hermitian Symmetric Fourier Watermarking (SFW), which enforces the constraint F[M−k, N−l] = conjugate(F[k,l]) so that the inverse Fourier transform yields a real-valued signal without discarding the imaginary component, preserving full frequency integrity for detection. The watermark is embedded in the free half of the frequency domain (offset one pixel from the DC axis to avoid numerical instability), with the other half determined by symmetry. A center-aware embedding strategy restricts the Fourier transform to the central 44×44 region of the 64×64 latent space, concentrating watermark energy in a spatially resilient area to resist cropping. Two instantiations are provided: HSTR applies SFW to the Tree-Ring Gaussian-radius pattern, and HSQR splits a version-1 QR code (21×21 cells, 2×2 pixel per cell, error correction level H, up to 72 bits) into two halves mapped to the real and imaginary components respectively, using signed amplitude encoding (±|F|) with Λ=45. Detection leverages both real and imaginary components via L1 distance, and the approach requires no model fine-tuning or external encoder/decoder.

## Results

On MS-COCO, HSQR achieves 0.997 average verification (TPR@1%FPR) and 0.985 average identification accuracy, matching RingID's verification while surpassing it in identification. HSQR also attains the best generative quality with FID 24.895 and CLIP score 0.330, outperforming RingID (FID 27.052, CLIP 0.324) and HSTR (FID 25.062, CLIP 0.329). HSTR achieves 0.971 verification and 0.889 identification, dramatically outperforming the original Tree-Ring baseline (0.655 / 0.114) while maintaining the merged-in-generation scheme with zero additional processing time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSQR matches RingID's verification performance (0.997 avg TPR@1%FPR) but surpasses it in identification (0.985 vs. 0.964) and generative quality (FID 24.895 vs. 27.052; CLIP 0.330 vs. 0.324), indicating RingID's high-energy ring pattern introduces visible artifacts that degrade image fidelity. HSTR outperforms the original Tree-Ring by a wide margin in both verification (0.971 vs. 0.655) and identification (0.889 vs. 0.114), confirming that enforcing Hermitian symmetry recovers lost frequency information. Against Zodiac, which achieves 0.962 verification but 0.000 identification (it is designed solely for verification), HSQR dominates on identification while matching verification. Compared to bitstream-based methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN, S.Sign.), both HSTR and HSQR achieve substantially higher scores under regeneration attacks (e.g., HSQR 0.992–1.000 vs. RivaGAN 0.552–0.570 on VAE attacks), confirming the robustness advantage of semantic latent-domain embedding over pixel-level perturbation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic watermarking line of work in the latent Fourier domain initiated by Tree-Ring (Wen et al.) and RingID (Ci et al.), both of which embed geometric patterns into the Fourier representation of latent noise using a merged-in-generation scheme. The key departure is identifying that all prior methods discard the imaginary component after inverse Fourier transform, violating Hermitian symmetry and destroying frequency integrity; the proposed SFW enforces this symmetry to preserve both real and imaginary frequency components. Additionally, the paper introduces a center-aware spatial restriction on the Fourier transform region for cropping robustness and extends the encoding paradigm to QR codes (HSQR), which is a new information-theoretic strategy not present in prior tree-ring or ring-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that enforcing Hermitian symmetry simultaneously improves detection accuracy and generative quality (FID/CLIP) addresses a previously unacknowledged trade-off in latent Fourier watermarking, and the observation that latent-space watermarking need not be confined to low-mid frequency bands challenges assumptions inherited from pixel-domain watermarking. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model (Stable Diffusion v2-1-base) at 512×512 resolution, and robustness against adversarial or learned attacks remains untested. Open questions include whether the full-spectrum encoding strategy generalizes to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), higher resolutions, and whether the QR code approach scales gracefully when the embedding region grows.
