## Problem

Existing VQA datasets requiring external knowledge are either entirely synthetic, depend heavily on costly manual annotation, or lack control over reasoning complexity (number of hops). There is no large-scale, scalable benchmark that systematically integrates structured encyclopedic knowledge with multi-hop reasoning over real images while maintaining verifiable quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251464/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an RGB image (sourced from Visual Genome or Google Landmark Dataset v2) with a question text that references a specific object in the image and requires 1–3 hops of external knowledge to answer. Outputs are evaluated in two formats: free-text (scored via exact match, substring, or semantic similarity using all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and four-option multiple choice (one correct answer plus three generated distractors). The full dataset spans 598K images and 4.2M questions across 20 predefined knowledge domains.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages: (1) External Knowledge Integration links annotated objects in images to Wikidata entities by canonicalizing Visual Genome labels to WordNet synsets and querying Wikidata via SPARQL; (2) Template-based Question Generation constructs 1-hop questions by filling a property template with the object's class name, then builds 2-hop and 3-hop questions by recursively nesting sub-clause templates (e.g., 'the architect of _') and optionally incorporating scene-graph relations for visual grounding; (3) Dataset Construction applies iterative answer-distribution balancing (removing over-represented answers to flatten the distribution) and stratified image-level splitting to ensure train/test answer distributions match. False choices are generated per answer type: random selection from fixed sets, ±10-year perturbations for dates, scaled numeric ranges for numbers, and same-property retrieval for literals. The entire pipeline is designed to be scalable with respect to input images, requiring only new image annotations and Wikidata links to expand.

## Results

In the zero-shot open-ended setting, GPT-4o achieves the highest accuracy on ReasonVQA-U (62.8%) and ReasonVQA-B (60.8%), followed by Qwen2.5-VL (59.3% / 58.1%) and PaliGemma-2-Mix (43.7% / 41.8%). All 13 evaluated models show a significant accuracy drop on ReasonVQA compared to standard VQA benchmarks: for example, PaliGemma-2-Mix scores 90.4% on VQAv2 but only 43.7% on ReasonVQA-U, and GPT-4o drops from 71.8% on OK-VQA to 62.8% on ReasonVQA-U. In the multiple-choice setting, GPT-4o leads with 76.6% (R-U) and 73.4% (R-B), while fine-tuning yields the largest relative gain for PaliGemma-2-3B-Mix (+66.5%, from 40.1% to 66.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 13 vision-language models on ReasonVQA alongside OK-VQA, VQAv2, and GQA to demonstrate the dataset's difficulty. GPT-4o is the strongest model on ReasonVQA (62.8% open-ended, 76.6% multiple-choice) but still lags well behind its 71.8% on OK-VQA, confirming that multi-hop external-knowledge questions remain hard even for frontier models. Qwen2.5-VL is the best open-source model (59.3% R-U) and also leads on OK-VQA (84.9%), yet drops ~26 points from its VQAv2 score (88.6%). PaliGemma-2-Mix, which achieves 90.4% on VQAv2 and 86.8% on OK-VQA, collapses to 43.7% on ReasonVQA-U — a 47-point drop — illustrating that high performance on standard VQA does not transfer to knowledge-intensive multi-hop reasoning. Older models (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2) show the weakest performance, with mPLUG-Owl2 scoring only 22.1% on R-U. Fine-tuning experiments show that PaliGemma-2-3B-Mix gains the most from training on ReasonVQA (+66.5%), suggesting smaller models have the most room to improve when exposed to the dataset's knowledge-intensive questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-based VQA line of work (OK-VQA, KVQA, CRIC, LORA, Encyclopedic-VQA) by introducing a fully automated, template-based construction pipeline that links Visual Genome and GLDv2 images to Wikidata entities via SPARQL, then generates controlled multi-hop questions by nesting property-specific templates. Unlike prior datasets that rely heavily on human annotation (OK-VQA, Encyclopedic-VQA) or restrict to commonsense facts (CRIC) or synthetic scenes (LORA), ReasonVQA leverages structured encyclopedic knowledge at scale while maintaining scene-graph grounding for visual context. The multi-hop mechanism—filling 1-hop templates with sub-clause templates—generalizes KVQA's multi-relation reasoning to arbitrary hop depths over a rich knowledge graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 4.2M questions over 598K images, ReasonVQA is one to two orders of magnitude larger than existing knowledge-based VQA benchmarks, making it a practical standard for evaluating multi-hop reasoning in VLMs. The dramatic performance collapse on 3-hop questions (GPT-4o drops from ~72% on 1-hop to ~31% on 3-hop in open-ended) reveals a clear reasoning-depth bottleneck in current models that prior smaller benchmarks could not expose. Limitations include template-based linguistic diversity (83% rated natural, 2.5% grammatical errors), 4% factual errors in the user study, and the fact that questions are generated from a fixed set of Wikidata properties, which may not capture the full breadth of real-world multi-hop reasoning. Open questions include whether the template structure inadvertently provides models with exploitable patterns, and whether the framework's scalability to new image sources preserves the same difficulty profile.
