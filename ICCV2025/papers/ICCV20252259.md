## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for large-scale scene reconstruction rely on partition-and-merge strategies that introduce boundary inconsistencies, require time-consuming post-merging on a single GPU, and hit out-of-memory limits beyond ~5 million Gaussians. Additionally, the unstructured Gaussian representation struggles to capture accurate geometric surfaces, and the lack of efficient multi-GPU parallelism makes training and rendering prohibitively slow for scenes with thousands of images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252259/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes SfM-initialized sparse point clouds, a collection of RGB images (up to 5,000+), and corresponding camera poses/intrinsics, plus off-the-shelf monocular depth estimates from Depth Anything as geometric priors during training. It produces a distributed 3D Gaussian representation (stored as voxel embeddings + shared decoder weights) that supports real-time novel-view RGB rendering at up to 4K resolution, along with depth maps, normal maps, and extractable surface meshes. Unlike prior methods that must merge all blocks onto one GPU before rendering, CityGS-X renders directly from the multi-GPU distributed representation.

## Method

CityGS-X introduces PH2-3D, a K-level hierarchical voxel structure where each voxel carries a learnable embedding, scaling factor, and offsets, and a shared MLP Gaussian Decoder converts these into view-dependent Gaussian attributes. Voxels are distributed across GPUs via spatial average sampling for automatic load balancing, and the decoder is synchronized through DDP gradient communication, eliminating the partition-and-merge paradigm entirely. Rendering is parallelized at the batch level: images are patchified into 16×16 tiles and assigned to GPUs with adaptive load balancing, each patch triggering a View-Dependent Gaussian Transfer that gathers intersected voxels across all LoDs and GPUs before tile-based rasterization of RGB, depth, and normal simultaneously. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: (1) batch-level RGB loss to generalize the shared decoder across views, (2) enhanced depth-prior regularization using scale-recovered and multi-view-consistency-filtered pseudo-depth from Depth Anything, and (3) batch-level geometric training with normalized cross-correlation photometric constraints between paired views to refine fine geometric details.

## Results

On the Mill-19 and UrbanScene3D datasets (Table 1), CityGS-X achieves the best PSNR in the geometric-optimization group across all four scenes: 22.76 dB (Building), 26.15 dB (Rubble), 22.44 dB (Residence), and 22.77 dB (Sci-Art), with the lowest LPIPS (0.179–0.210). On the MatrixCity surface-reconstruction benchmark (Table 2), CityGS-X attains 27.58 dB PSNR and 0.581 F1, surpassing CityGS-V2 by 0.35 dB in PSNR and 0.025 in F1. Training a 5,000+ image scene completes in ~5 hours on 4×RTX 4090 GPUs, roughly 3× faster than the next-best geometric method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the geometric-optimization group, CityGS-X dominates every baseline: it outperforms CityGaussianV2 by 3.69 dB PSNR on Building (22.76 vs 19.07), 2.40 dB on Rubble (26.15 vs 23.75), 1.29 dB on Residence (22.44 vs 21.15), and 2.11 dB on Sci-Art (22.77 vs 20.66), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS in all four scenes. Against the strongest non-geometric method Momentum-GS, CityGS-X is competitive or superior on most metrics (e.g., +0.22 dB PSNR on Rubble, −0.026 LPIPS on Sci-Art) though slightly trails on Building PSNR (22.76 vs 23.23). On the MatrixCity surface-reconstruction benchmark, CityGS-X achieves the best PSNR (27.58) and F1 (0.581), beating CityGS-V2 (27.23 / 0.556) and 2DGS (21.35 / 0.270) by large margins, while SuGaR and NeuS either OOM or fail entirely. The paper also reports that CityGS-X trains a 5,000+ image scene in ~5 hours on 4×4090 GPUs, whereas competing methods either OOM or require significantly longer wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

CityGS-X sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) hybrid 3D representations that replace unstructured Gaussian sets with voxel/anchor hierarchies (Scaffold-GS, Octree-GS), (2) distributed training systems for 3DGS (Grendel-GS), and (3) planar-based surface reconstruction losses (PGSR). Its key architectural departure is from the dominant partition-and-merge paradigm used by VastGS, CityGaussian, and CityGaussianV2, which train spatial blocks independently and then merge on a single GPU. Instead, CityGS-X distributes LOD voxels across GPUs from the outset in a DDP-like fashion, eliminating the merge step entirely and enabling parallel novel-view rendering. The progressive RGB→Depth-Prior→Geometric training strategy extends single-view geometric losses to a batch-level multi-view consistency formulation, leveraging off-the-shelf monocular depth (Depth Anything) with confidence-aware filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical bottleneck this paper addresses—single-GPU memory capping the number of Gaussians to ~5M and forcing time-consuming merge steps—is a real constraint for urban-scale and aerial reconstruction pipelines. Demonstrating 5-hour training on 5,000+ images with 4×4090 while competitors OOM is a meaningful efficiency win for practitioners. However, open questions remain: the paper only validates up to 8 GPUs, the method still depends on SfM initialization quality and off-the-shelf depth priors that degrade in long-range or textureless regions, and the progressive training adds complexity that may not transfer cleanly to dynamic or sparse-view settings. Whether the DDP-like voxel distribution scales gracefully to dozens of GPUs or multi-node clusters is untested.
