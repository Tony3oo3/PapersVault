## Problem

Existing VLA models discretize robot actions via simple per-dimension binning or task-specific VQ-VAE tokenizers trained on small, single-task datasets, limiting both generalizability and long-horizon planning performance. No prior work has explored whether action tokenizers can be scaled with large amounts of synthetic data, nor has the domain gap between simulated and real action trajectories been quantified for this purpose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250922/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA model consumes RGB observation images (from a fixed third-person RealSense D435 camera) and a natural language instruction, and outputs a sequence of discrete codebook indices from N_q residual VQ layers, which are decoded by the frozen VQ-VAE into a chunk of K=5 continuous 7-DoF end-effector actions (3D position, quaternion orientation, gripper state). Unlike OpenVLA's uniform 256-bin discretization, each VQ layer's tokens occupy a non-overlapping ID range (layer i maps to [256(i−1), 256i−1]), preserving semantic separation across quantization stages.

## Method

The paper proposes a convolutional residual VQ-VAE where both encoder and decoder use 2D temporal convolutional layers (replacing the MLPs in prior VQ-VAE action tokenizers) to capture hierarchical temporal dependencies in action sequences. Sinusoidal time embeddings and learnable per-dimension action-type embeddings are prepended to the input to encode temporal frequency and the distinct semantics of each action component. Training follows a progressive strategy: the tokenizer is first trained on noisy real-world trajectories from Open X-Embodiment, then gradually co-trained with cleaner synthetic data from LIBERO and ManiSkill, expanding the training corpus by over 100×. The trained VQ-VAE is frozen and plugged into OpenVLA as a drop-in replacement for bin-based tokenization, with the VLM fine-tuned via LoRA to predict the new discrete tokens. A key empirical finding is that the domain gap between synthetic and real action trajectories is marginal, so scaling with simulated data yields linear improvements in downstream VLA success rates without degrading real-world performance.

## Results

On the LIBERO-90 simulation benchmark, VQ-VLA with the VQ_{M+R} tokenizer (trained on ManiSkill + RLBench) achieves 80.98% success rate, a 7.45% absolute improvement over the OpenVLA baseline (73.53%). On six real-world Franka Research 3 tasks, the best model VQ_{O+L+M} (trained on Open X-Embodiment + LIBERO + ManiSkill) achieves an average success rate 23.25% higher than the baseline, with up to 30% gains on long-horizon tasks (e.g., 50% vs. 15% on 'Put all cups into the basket'). Inference speed nearly triples from 4.16 Hz (OpenVLA) to 11.84 Hz (VQ-VLA) due to the 5× action compression ratio of the VQ-VAE tokenizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original OpenVLA model with its uniform 256-bin per-dimension action discretization. VQ-VLA consistently outperforms OpenVLA across all evaluated settings: on LIBERO-90, VQ_{M+R} reaches 80.98% vs. 73.53% for the baseline; on real-world short-horizon tasks, the average success rate rises from 23% to 46.25%; and on long-horizon tasks, VQ_{O+L+M} achieves 50% and 30% success where the baseline manages only 15% and near 0%. Inference frequency improves from 4.16 Hz to 11.84 Hz (≈2.8× speedup). The paper also ablates against an autoregressive action-chunking variant of OpenVLA, which degrades performance (e.g., 66.53% vs. 74.76% on LIBERO-90, and 0% vs. 20% on 'Put toy into basket'), confirming that VQ-based chunking is superior to sequential autoregressive prediction for multi-step actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE-based action tokenization line initiated by Lee et al. [28] and Mete et al. [32], which applied residual vector quantization to robot actions but relied on task-specific MLP encoders trained on single-task datasets. The key architectural departure is replacing MLPs with 2D temporal convolutional encoders/decoders (inspired by pyramidal flow matching [25]) and scaling training data by over 100× across Open X-Embodiment, LIBERO, and ManiSkill. The VLA backbone is OpenVLA [26], whose uniform 256-bin discretization is replaced by the learned VQ-VAE tokenizer, while the residual quantization mechanism itself traces back to Soundstream [52]. Unlike FAST [35], which uses a fixed DCT transform, this approach learns a data-driven tokenizer that generalizes across tasks in a zero-shot manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is a 3× inference speedup (4.16 Hz → 11.84 Hz) combined with improved success rates, making VLA models more viable for real-time robotic deployment. The finding that the domain gap between synthetic and real action trajectories is marginal opens a cheap scaling pathway: one can train action tokenizers on abundant simulation data without degrading real-world performance, decoupling tokenizer quality from the cost of collecting real robot data. However, the evidence base is narrow—experiments use a single Franka arm, one camera, 20 Hz control, and only six real-world tasks—so generalizability to other morphologies, higher frequencies, and more diverse environments remains untested. The catastrophic failure of VQ_M (14.38% vs. 80.98% for VQ_M+R) also suggests that data diversity, not just volume, is critical, a nuance the paper does not fully dissect.
