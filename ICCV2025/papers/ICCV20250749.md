## Problem

Existing referring multi-object tracking methods encode the entire referring sentence into a single embedding for vision-language alignment, failing to exploit fine-grained linguistic cues that distinguish objects with similar visual characteristics. This coarse sentence-level matching cannot properly separate static spatial descriptions from motion states, which play fundamentally different roles at detection versus association stages, leading to incorrect target identification in complex scenes with multiple similar objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video sequence of arbitrary length (T frames) and a natural language expression of L words. Visual features are extracted via ResNet50 while text features come from a frozen RoBERTa encoder; the word-level features are then split into localized descriptions and motion states via a part-of-speech-based binary mask. Output consists of per-frame bounding boxes for referred objects with persistent identity tracklets, where objects are retained as foreground if their classification score exceeds 0.6 and are declared lost after five consecutive frames below 0.4.

## Method

DKGTrack decouples the referring sentence into localized descriptions (static attributes like color, category, position) and motion states (dynamics) using a POS-tag-derived binary mask, routing each to a dedicated tracking branch. The Static Semantic Enhancement module first injects the sentence embedding into object queries via scaled attention, then applies a recurrent text guidance strategy that iteratively fuses image features with word-level features through bidirectional attention, deformable cross-attention, and similarity-based query refinement to produce discriminative region-level representations. The Motion Perception Alignment module performs Hungarian matching between current detections and existing tracklets, aligns motion-state embeddings with the matched queries via temperature-scaled attention, adds sinusoidal temporal positional encodings, and refines queries through a cross-attention memory bank that stores embeddings from previous frames. The architecture is built on a Deformable DETR backbone initialized from COCO pre-training, trained end-to-end with focal losses for classification and referring plus L1/GIoU losses for localization.

## Results

On Refer-KITTI, DKGTrack achieves a HOTA of 52.08%, DetA of 41.10%, and AssA of 66.04%, setting new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On the more challenging Refer-KITTI-V2, DKGTrack reaches HOTA 35.26% and DetA 23.04%, outperforming the previous best (TempRMOT) by 4.35% in HOTA and 10.29% in DetA. The method also achieves the highest DetRe (57.57%) and LocA (90.54%) on Refer-KITTI, indicating superior detection recall and localization precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKGTrack outperforms the strongest prior language-guided method TempRMOT by 3.52% in HOTA (52.08 vs. 50.31) and 10.29% in DetA (41.10 vs. 38.48) on Refer-KITTI, and by 4.35% in HOTA on Refer-KITTI-V2. Against TransRMOT, the improvement is even more pronounced at +14.09% HOTA. The two-stage method iKUN achieves a slightly higher AssA (66.80 vs. 66.04) on Refer-KITTI due to its tracklet-selection paradigm, but DKGTrack surpasses it by 6.63% in overall HOTA and by a large margin (35.26 vs. 10.32 HOTA) on Refer-KITTI-V2, where complex language expressions defeat iKUN's coarse sentence-matching strategy. Among non-language MOT baselines (FairMOT, ByteTrack, TrackFormer, etc.), DKGTrack leads by 18–30 HOTA points, underscoring the value of fine-grained language guidance. On Refer-KITTI-V2, DKGTrack also achieves the best DetRe (37.81%) and LocA (91.65%), confirming gains in both detection recall and spatial precision over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DKGTrack builds on the query-based detection framework of Deformable DETR and the cross-modal fusion paradigm established by TransRMOT and TempRMOT, which treat the entire referring sentence as a single embedding for vision-language alignment. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of language into localized descriptions and motion states via part-of-speech tagging, rather than relying on coarse sentence-level similarity. This repurposes the standard Transformer decoder architecture with two specialized modules (SSE for static localization, MPA for temporal motion alignment) that operate on different linguistic sub-components, a design not present in prior RMOT methods like iKUN's two-stage tracklet selection or TransRMOT's unified cross-modal encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language decoupling strategy offers a practical and interpretable way to handle the distinct roles of static attributes (color, position, category) versus dynamic states (moving, parking) in referring expressions, which prior methods conflate. However, the approach is validated only on KITTI-derived driving datasets, leaving open whether POS-based decoupling generalizes to more diverse scenes, languages, or complex multi-clause expressions. The reliance on a binary mask for splitting language also raises questions about robustness to ambiguous or compound descriptions where static and motion cues are entangled. Broader applicability to other video domains (pedestrian tracking, sports) remains untested.
