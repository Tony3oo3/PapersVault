## Problem

Generative CAD models can produce sketch geometry but fail to align their constraint outputs with design intent, resulting in under-constrained or over-constrained sketches that distort when parameters are modified. This paper establishes 'design alignment' as a formal problem and demonstrates that alignment techniques from LLM post-training can be adapted to make constraint generation preserve geometric relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252035/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D sketch's geometric primitives (points, lines, arcs, circles) as input and autoregressively generates a token sequence of constraints (coincident, parallel, perpendicular, tangent), dimensions (horizontal, vertical, radial), and pointer tokens referencing specific primitives. Generated sequences are validated by the Autodesk Fusion constraint solver, which classifies each sketch as fully-constrained, under-constrained, over-constrained, unsolvable, or unstable based on whether geometry shifts beyond a discretized grid after solving.

## Method

Starting from the Vitruvion transformer base model, the paper applies a three-stage post-training pipeline: supervised fine-tuning on solver-verified fully-constrained sketches, followed by either preference-based optimization (DPO, Expert Iteration) or policy-gradient RL (RLOO, GRPO, ReMax) using the constraint solver as a verifiable reward signal. The key insight is that sketch solvability and geometric stability are mechanically checkable, eliminating the need for a learned reward model and enabling direct optimization of shaped rewards (percentage of fully-constrained curves/points, penalties for instability, over-constraint, and unsolvability). RLOO and GRPO, which use group-relative baselines over multiple sampled constraint sequences, achieve 93.05% and 91.59% fully-constrained rates versus 8.87% for the base model, outperforming offline preference methods that rely on fixed ranking or filtering criteria.

## Results

On SketchGraphs (2.8M sketches, ≤16 primitives, ≤64 constraints), RLOO achieves the highest fully-constrained (FC) rate at 93.05%, followed closely by GRPO at 91.59%, compared to 8.87% for the base Vitruvion model and 34.24% for SFT. RLOO also has the lowest under-constrained rate (3.55%) and not-solvable rate (1.25%), while maintaining 89.16% stability. In the Pass@K setting (Table 2), RLOO reaches 83.57% Pass@1 and 84.96% Pass@8, showing minimal degradation with additional samples compared to other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baselines are the base Vitruvion model (8.87% FC) and a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) variant trained only on solver-verified fully-constrained sketches (34.24% FC). RLOO outperforms the base model by 84.2 percentage points and SFT by 58.8 points on FC rate, while also reducing the under-constrained rate from 71.38% to 3.55%. Among the alignment methods, the RL-based approaches (RLOO, GRPO, ReMax) consistently outperform the preference-optimization methods (Iterative DPO at 64.91% FC, Expert Iteration at 71.70% FC), which the authors attribute to the online exploration advantage of policy gradient methods over offline ranking/filtering. ReMax achieves competitive FC (79.84%) but suffers the lowest stability (75.77%), while RLOO and GRPO maintain stability above 88%. In the Pass@K evaluation, RLOO's 83.57% Pass@1 already approaches its 84.96% Pass@8, indicating that the aligned policy is near-deterministic in producing valid outputs, unlike the base model which improves from 8.53% to 20.47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transfers the post-training alignment toolkit developed for LLMs—DPO, Expert Iteration (ReST), ReMax, RLOO, and GRPO—into the parametric CAD domain, using a commercial constraint solver (Autodesk Fusion) as a verifiable reward signal in place of human preference annotations. The base model is Vitruvion, a Transformer-based autoregressive constraint generator, which prior CAD generation work (SketchGen, DaVinci, CADVLM) trains only via supervised fine-tuning on ground-truth constraint sequences. The key conceptual departure is treating constraint generation as an alignment problem where the 'design intent' is operationalized through solver-verified conditions (fully-constrained, stable, not over-constrained), rather than as a pure sequence-prediction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that LLM-style alignment (preference optimization and policy-gradient RL) can be applied to a parametric design task, establishing 'design alignment' as a tractable sub-problem for AI-assisted CAD. The practical impact is significant: moving from 8.9% to 93% fully-constrained sketches could reduce manual refinement in real design workflows. However, the scope is limited to moderate-complexity sketches (≤16 primitives, ≤64 constraints), a single solver, and a single base architecture; the paper also documents reward hacking (excessive dimensioning) that partially undermines the gains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to complex multi-body assemblies, whether human design preferences can be incorporated alongside solver feedback, and whether the findings generalize beyond the Vitruvion architecture.
