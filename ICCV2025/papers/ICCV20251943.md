## Problem

Heatmap regression methods for facial landmark detection universally rely on Soft-argmax to make the non-differentiable argmax operation trainable end-to-end. This paper demonstrates that Soft-argmax is fundamentally flawed: a bimodal heatmap can produce the same Soft-argmax expectation as a correct unimodal one, yielding zero loss while the actual argmax prediction is wrong, which causes slow and inconsistent optimization. The paper asks whether a principled alternative training objective can eliminate this approximation entirely.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB facial image (face region cropped and resized) and produces N 2D landmark coordinates (98 for WFLW, 68 for COFW, 29 for 300W) by taking the argmax over each of N predicted heatmaps from a four-stacked hourglass network. Unlike prior heatmap regression methods that require a differentiable coordinate prediction (Soft-argmax) during training, this method trains directly on the heatmap scores without any differentiable coordinate extraction step, and at inference simply applies hard argmax.

## Method

The paper replaces Soft-argmax + L2 loss with a training objective derived from the deep structured prediction framework (Chen et al., 2015). The score function F is decomposed per-landmark as F_n(y_n, X; θ) = Ĥ_n[y_n] (the predicted heatmap value at candidate location y_n), and the margin term Δ is a smooth-L1 distance between candidate and ground-truth landmark positions. The non-differentiable max in the structured hinge loss is relaxed via the log-sum-exp trick with a temperature parameter ε, yielding a fully differentiable objective that directly pushes up the correct heatmap peak and pushes down all others. To handle annotation ambiguity, the authors introduce image-aware label smoothing: they construct a smooth distribution G around each ground-truth landmark by combining a pseudo edge heatmap (from boundary-line distance transforms), a centered Gaussian, and a covariance-estimated Gaussian, then approximate the expected loss via Monte Carlo sampling. The architecture remains the four-stacked hourglass from STAR, with only the Soft-argmax layer removed and the new loss substituted.

## Results

On WFLW (full set), the proposed method achieves NME 3.99, FR₁₀% 1.84, and AUC₁₀% 0.606, outperforming the previous SOTA STAR (NME 4.02, FR 2.32, AUC 0.605) by 0.02 NME and 0.32 FR. On COFW, it reaches NME 4.58 and AUC 0.544, and on 300W it achieves NME 2.87 (Full) and 4.27 (Challenging). The method also converges 2.2× faster during training than STAR, reaching NME 4.50 on WFLW in 20 epochs versus 44 epochs for STAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WFLW, the proposed method outperforms the strongest heatmap-regression baseline STAR [98] by 0.02 NME and 0.32 FR, and beats coordinate-regression methods like DTLD [45] (NME 4.08) and SLPT [86] (NME 4.14) by a wider margin. On COFW, it achieves the best NME (4.58) and AUC (0.544), though HIH [43] reports a lower FR (0.39 vs. 0.79) while having a higher NME (4.63). On 300W, the method ties STAR on the Full set (NME 2.87) and slightly trails on the Common subset (2.53 vs. 2.52) but leads on the Challenging subset (4.27 vs. 4.32). Across all three benchmarks, the method also converges approximately 2.2× faster than STAR during training, reaching equivalent NME in roughly 20 epochs versus 44. The gains over STAR are statistically significant, with a reported NME standard deviation of only 0.003 across three training runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard heatmap regression pipeline (stacked hourglass + Soft-argmax + regression loss) by replacing the Soft-argmax differentiable relaxation with a training objective derived from the deep structured prediction framework (Chen et al., ICML 2015; Schwing & Urtasun, ICCV 2015 tutorial). The architecture and data pipeline are inherited directly from STAR (Zhou et al., CVPR 2023), with only the loss function and an image-aware label smoothing technique changed. Conceptually, the paper repositions landmark detection as a structured prediction problem where the score function is the per-landmark heatmap channel, rather than treating it as a regression problem requiring a differentiable argmax surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Soft-argmax is not necessary for effective heatmap regression challenges a widely-adopted design assumption in the field and offers a principled, structured-prediction-based alternative that converges 2.2× faster. However, the absolute performance gains over STAR are modest (0.02 NME on WFLW), and the image-aware label smoothing remains a hand-crafted heuristic whose design is acknowledged as ad-hoc. Open questions include whether the structured prediction framing generalizes to other structured-output tasks (pose estimation, segmentation), whether data-driven smoothing can replace the heuristic edge-based approach, and whether the convergence advantage persists with different architectures or larger datasets.
