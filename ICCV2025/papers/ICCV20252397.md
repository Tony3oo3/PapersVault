## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of LLMs with LoRA suffers from aggregation noise (bias from separately averaging A and B matrices) and aggregation misalignment (rank and feature mismatch when clients have heterogeneous LoRA ranks due to resource constraints). Existing alternating-freeze methods that eliminate noise assume a uniform initial rank across all clients, contradicting the core resource-heterogeneity assumption of real-world federated learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client trains a LoRA adapter (matrices A ∈ R^{r_k×l} and B ∈ R^{d×r_k}) on local instruction data partitioned via a Dirichlet distribution (α=0.5), with per-client ranks drawn from [64, 32, 16, 16, 8, 8, 4, 4, 4]. Due to alternating freezing, only one module (A or B) is uploaded per round, halving communication cost. The server outputs a single aggregated module of shape R^{d×r_max} (or R^{r_max×l}) distributed back to all clients for the next round, where r_max is the maximum rank among participating clients.

## Method

Te-LoRA combines three strategies: (1) an alternating-freeze schedule where odd rounds train and upload only B (A frozen) and even rounds train and upload only A (B frozen), eliminating aggregation noise without extra server computation; (2) a Pre-Aggregation Alignment (PAA) strategy that uses Optimal Transport via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to map each client's heterogeneous-rank module to the maximum rank with minimal information loss, by computing a regularized distance matrix and solving for the optimal transport plan G that transforms B_k to B̃_k ∈ R^{d×r_max}; (3) a Tensor-to-Matrix (T2M) strategy that stacks the K aligned modules into a 3D tensor B̂ ∈ R^{d×r_max×K} and decomposes it via mode-i tensor-matrix multiplication, with three variants (T2M₁ weighting clients, T2M₂ averaging along the client axis, T2M₃ combining both). T2M₂, which simply averages the aligned matrices along the client dimension, achieves the best empirical performance while preserving inter-client dependencies.

## Results

In the heterogeneous LoRA setting (ranks [64,32,16,16,8,8,4,4,4] across 10 clients), Te-LoRA achieves MMLU scores of 23.71 (Wizard), 28.37 (Dolly), and 46.16 (Alpaca-GPT4), along with an MT-Bench score of 3.33, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In the homogeneous setting (rank 16 for all clients), Te-LoRA scores 23.35, 28.44, 45.86 on MMLU and 3.31 on MT-Bench. Te-LoRA also achieves the lowest per-round communication cost among all compared methods, as it transmits only one LoRA module (A or B) per round via the alternating-freeze strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Te-LoRA outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. Against the strongest baseline FlexLoRA [3], Te-LoRA gains 0.22, 0.19, and 0.68 MMLU points on Wizard, Dolly, and Alpaca-GPT4 respectively in the heterogeneous setting, plus 0.05 on MT-Bench. FLoRA [43] (stacked aggregation with matrix multiplication) trails Te-LoRA by 0.92–1.22 MMLU points, while Zero-padding [8] (the simplest baseline) lags by 1.85–2.22 MMLU points. Notably, Zero-padding and FLoRA both degrade when moving from homogeneous to heterogeneous ranks (by up to 0.41 and 0.21 points), whereas Te-LoRA and FlexLoRA actually improve slightly, confirming Te-LoRA's robustness to rank heterogeneity. Te-LoRA also achieves the lowest communication cost per round, transmitting only one module (A or B) versus both modules required by FlexLoRA and Zero-padding, and the full BA product required by FLoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

Te-LoRA extends the alternating-freeze federated LoRA framework introduced by Koo et al. (LoRA-A2), which assumed a uniform global LoRA rank across all clients, to the more realistic setting where clients possess heterogeneous ranks due to resource constraints. For aligning these heterogeneous ranks, the paper repurposes the Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport algorithm (Pauwels & Vaiter) in a novel way: rather than aligning data distributions or prototypes as in prior FL-OT work (FedOTP, FedAli), it computes a soft column-matching between source and target LoRA matrices to produce a rank-aligned transformation with minimal information loss. The T2M-strategy then draws on classical tensor decomposition (Kolda & Bader) to aggregate the aligned matrices into a unified tensor before decomposing back to per-client matrices, departing from the merge-then-SVD approach used by FLoRA and FlexLoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real federated deployments will inevitably involve clients with different computational budgets and thus different LoRA ranks, yet prior methods either assumed homogeneity or used lossy zero-padding/SVD truncation. The OT-based alignment is a conceptually clean solution to the rank-mismatch problem, and the alternating-freeze design halves per-round communication. However, the experimental scope is narrow (single 7B model, 10 clients, 2 per round, 200 rounds), and the performance gains over the strongest baseline (FlexLoRA) are modest (≤0.7 MMLU points, ≤0.05 MT-Bench). Open questions include whether the PAA/T2M strategies scale to hundreds of clients, larger models, or non-LoRA PEFT variants, and whether the tensor decomposition overhead becomes prohibitive at larger ranks.
