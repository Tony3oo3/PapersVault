## Problem

Visual room rearrangement requires an embodied agent to restore a shuffled scene to its goal state, but object changes span three heterogeneous types—movement, appearance, and existence—whose joint reasoning space grows multiplicatively (O(d_m × d_a × d_e)), making unified handling intractable. Existing modular methods address only movement changes, while end-to-end RL methods struggle with the excessive state-space complexity. The paper seeks a unified framework that processes all three change types without combinatorial explosion.

## Input / Output

The agent operates in a two-stage POMDP: during the walkthrough stage it collects egocentric RGB-D observations of the goal-state room, and during the unshuffle stage it receives the same modality from the shuffled room while executing discrete actions (move_ahead, turn_right, turn_left, look_down, look_up, pick, put, open, remove, push, stop). The goal specification is an ExperienceGoal—the agent's own prior observations of the goal state—rather than an explicit image or language description. Output is a sequence of these discrete actions terminated by a stop signal, with the implicit target being full restoration of all object poses and states.

## Method

TOR maintains a dynamic differential point cloud (diff-cloud) that records moved and protruding geometry relative to the goal configuration, updated in real time during both exploration and interaction modes. A PointNet++-based geometric feature extractor and weighted bipartite graph matching classify each protruding cluster as movement, appearance, or existence change. The core innovation is an iterative trial-assessment loop: a score function combining geometric matching (φ_match) and a RoI-pooled visual context network (φ_context) prioritizes candidate operations, and an assessment module measures each action's effectiveness by the reduction in diff-cloud magnitude (number of differing points) against a threshold τ. Failed trials prune physically infeasible actions, converting the reasoning complexity from multiplicative to additive O(d_m + d_a + d_e). The context network is trained on a static dataset of randomly spawned agent-object interactions with success/failure labels.

## Results

On the AI2THOR Rearrangement Challenge (RoomR test set), TOR achieves 18.5% Success, 40.6% Fixed Strict, 0.633 Misplaced, and 0.639 Energy Remaining, outperforming the strongest baseline CAVR (8.9% Suc, 24.2% FS, 0.797 Mis, 0.800 E) by 9.6 points in success rate and 16.2 points in object-level restoration. On the more challenging VIR dataset (test set), TOR reaches 11.3% Success and 27.1% Fixed Strict, more than doubling the success rate of CAVR (4.5% Suc, 16.5% FS) and substantially outperforming TIDEE (0.3% Suc) and MaSS (0.5% Suc). The Misplaced metric drops from ~0.85–1.0 in baselines to 0.752 for TOR on VIR, indicating fewer newly introduced errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOR is compared against three modular baselines: TIDEE (2D occupancy map + object tracking), MaSS (voxel-based semantic map + search), and CAVR (differential point cloud for scene change detection). TOR wins on all four metrics (Success, Fixed Strict, Misplaced, Energy Remaining) against every baseline on both RoomR and VIR. The gap is most pronounced on VIR, where TOR's 11.3% Success rate is 2.5× that of CAVR (4.5%), 22.6× that of MaSS (0.5%), and 37.7× that of TIDEE (0.3%), reflecting the advantage of the trial-oriented mechanism when handling existence and appearance changes that the baselines cannot address. CAVR, the closest competitor and the method TOR builds upon, benefits from the same diff-cloud representation but lacks the iterative trial-assessment loop, resulting in a consistent 9–11 point success rate gap. TIDEE and MaSS, which rely on semantic maps or scene graphs, struggle with fine-grained appearance and existence changes, yielding Misplaced scores above 1.0 on VIR (i.e., they introduce more errors than they fix).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CAVR [24], which introduced differential point clouds for category-agnostic scene change detection in visual rearrangement, by making the diff-cloud dynamic (updated during interaction rather than built once) and adding a trial-and-assessment loop that unifies handling of movement, appearance, and existence changes. It departs from prior modular methods (TIDEE, MaSS, CAVR) that treat only movement changes and from end-to-end RL approaches that struggle with the combinatorial state space, by reframing the joint reasoning problem as an iterative discrimination task whose complexity scales additively rather than multiplicatively across change types. The POMDP formulation and ExperienceGoal specification follow the AI2THOR Rearrangement Challenge framework established by Weihs et al. [35].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VIR dataset fills a practical gap by introducing multi-room scenes, unpickable (push-only) objects, and existence changes (new objects appearing) that the RoomR benchmark lacks, making it a more realistic evaluation for embodied rearrangement. The main limitation identified is that undetected scene changes (diff-cloud coverage gaps) remain the dominant failure mode, suggesting that improving perception fidelity—rather than planning—would yield the largest gains. Open questions include whether the trial mechanism generalizes beyond simulation to real-world settings with noisier depth and partial observability, and whether the approach scales to scenes with many simultaneous changes or long-horizon dependencies.
