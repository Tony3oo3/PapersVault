## Problem

Semantic scene completion (SSC) requires predicting both 3D geometry and semantics from limited image observations, but all prior approaches rely on expensive 3D annotations or at least 2D semantic labels. No method has tackled SSC in a fully unsupervised setting, which is challenging because semantic taxonomies are inherently human-defined and a single image only reveals a partial view of the scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, SceneDINO takes a single RGB image and produces a continuous 3D feature field mapping world coordinates to a volumetric density and a 768-dimensional DINO feature vector, from which pseudo-semantic labels are obtained via distillation and k-means clustering. During training, it consumes n+1 multi-view images (8 views for KITTI-360, 3 for RealEstate10K) with known camera poses, rendering 32 sample points per ray in inverse depth over [3 m, 80 m]. The 2D encoder-decoder produces per-pixel embeddings at full resolution, while the MLP decoder predicts 64 intermediate features that are upprojected to the full 768-dim DINO space.

## Method

SceneDINO combines a DINO-B/8 2D encoder-decoder with a 2-layer MLP decoder that, given a 3D position, queries the per-pixel embedding via bilinear interpolation and predicts both density and 3D features using positional encoding. Training is fully self-supervised via multi-view consistency: photometric loss (L1 + SSIM) regularizes geometry, while a cosine-similarity feature loss with a learned constant decomposition of ViT positional encodings enforces view-consistent 3D features. The key novelty is a 3D feature distillation stage where a pointwise head maps 768-dim features to a 19-dim space, trained with a contrastive correlation loss (STEGO-style) using a novel depth-stratified 3D sampling strategy: visible surface points are sorted by depth, partitioned into chunks to select center points, and neighborhood samples within a 0.5 m radius are drawn to capture local semantic structure. A cross-scene feature buffer provides k-nearest-neighbor and random negative pairs for the distillation loss, and cosine-similarity k-means on the distilled features yields the final pseudo-semantic predictions.

## Results

On SSCBench-KITTI-360 (51.2 m range), SceneDINO achieves 8.00% mIoU for unsupervised semantic scene completion, outperforming the S4C+STEGO unsupervised baseline (6.60%) by 1.4 points and reaching within 2.2 points of the 2D-supervised S4C (10.19%). Geometrically, SceneDINO attains 37.60% IoU, 41.59% Precision, and 79.67% Recall at 51.2 m. For 2D unsupervised semantic segmentation on KITTI-360, SceneDINO achieves 25.81% mIoU and 77.74% accuracy, surpassing STEGO with DINO features (23.57% mIoU) and U2Seg (23.43% mIoU). In multi-view feature consistency, SceneDINO reduces L1 distance from 16.06 (raw DINO) to 6.45 and raises cosine similarity from 0.70 to 0.93 on KITTI-360.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unsupervised SSC baseline S4C+STEGO, SceneDINO wins on both semantic mIoU (8.00% vs 6.60% at 51.2 m) and geometric IoU (37.60% vs 36.39%), with a notable recall improvement (79.67% vs 73.43%). Compared to the 2D-supervised S4C, SceneDINO trails by 2.19 mIoU points at 51.2 m (8.00% vs 10.19%) but surpasses it in 2D segmentation (25.81% vs 21.67% mIoU) and domain generalization on Cityscapes (22.81% vs 14.04% mIoU). In 2D unsupervised segmentation, SceneDINO outperforms STEGO with DINO features by 2.24 mIoU points (25.81% vs 23.57%) and U2Seg by 2.38 points (25.81% vs 23.43%). For multi-view consistency, SceneDINO dramatically improves over raw DINO features (cos-sim 0.93 vs 0.70, L1 6.45 vs 16.06) and also beats FiT3D's 3D-refined features (cos-sim 0.93 vs 0.82 on KITTI-360). Using DINOv2 as target features further boosts SceneDINO's consistency to cos-sim 0.96 and mIoU to 9.08%, closing the gap to S4C to just 1.11 points at 51.2 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDINO combines two established lines of work: the feed-forward 3D geometry prediction architecture of Behind the Scenes (BTS) [108], which uses a 2D encoder-decoder and MLP decoder with volume rendering for single-image scene reconstruction, and the feature distillation framework of STEGO [33], which uses contrastive correlation loss to extract unsupervised semantics from DINO features in 2D. The key conceptual departure is lifting self-supervised 2D features into a continuous 3D feature field and performing distillation in 3D, rather than in the 2D image plane. This positions SceneDINO as the first method to approach semantic scene completion without any ground-truth annotations (no 3D labels, no 2D labels, no LiDAR), whereas all prior SSC methods (S4C, SelfOcc, RenderOcc) rely on some form of supervised signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive 3D annotations, SceneDINO opens a path toward scalable semantic scene understanding that is not bottlenecked by human labeling. The 3D feature field is general-purpose and shows strong multi-view consistency (cos-sim 0.93–0.97 vs. 0.70 for raw DINO) and domain generalization to Cityscapes and BDD100K, suggesting it could serve as a foundation for broader 3D scene-understanding tasks beyond SSC. However, absolute performance remains modest (8.0% mIoU at 51.2 m range), and the evaluation is limited to driving scenes; whether the approach transfers to indoor or more diverse 3D environments remains an open question. The relatively weak unsupervised baseline (S4C+STEGO) also leaves room for stronger comparisons as the field matures.
