## Problem

Existing 3D VAEs such as VecSet rely on direct cross-attention mappings between point clouds and latent vectors, which limits the achievable compression ratio and forces downstream diffusion models to operate on large sets of latent vectors (512–1024), incurring substantial computational cost. This paper asks whether a far smaller set of latent vectors (as few as 64) can still support high-quality 3D reconstruction and generation if the encoder and decoder are redesigned to achieve higher compression.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud sampled from an object's surface (N=2048 points for ShapeNet, N=8192 for Objaverse) and encodes it into M=64 one-dimensional latent vectors of dimension D=32, regularized as a multivariate Gaussian via KL divergence. Decoding produces dense triplane features at resolution 128 (ShapeNet) or 256 (Objaverse), from which continuous occupancy values are retrieved at arbitrary query points through bilinear interpolation and a shallow MLP, yielding a 128³ or 256³ occupancy grid. The compact latent set is then used as the target space for a transformer-based latent diffusion model (EDM formulation) that generates new shapes.

## Method

COD-VAE replaces VecSet's single-stage cross-attention encoder/decoder with a two-stage autoencoder that uses intermediate representations as bridges. The encoder progressively compresses high-resolution point features (N) into intermediate point patches (L=512) via cross-attention, processes them with self-attention, then further compresses into M=64 compact vectors, with a feedback path that enriches high-resolution features from the compact vectors. The decoder reconstructs dense triplane embeddings from the latent vectors using ViT-style transformer blocks rather than directly querying neural fields, reducing per-query cost to bilinear interpolation plus a shallow MLP. An uncertainty-guided token pruning mechanism predicts per-token reconstruction error at the start of decoding and prunes the lowest-uncertainty 75% of triplane tokens, concentrating computation on complex regions. Training is two-stage: first the full autoencoder is trained, then the KL block and latent decoder are trained separately to specialize in channel compression/decompression while the rest of the network is frozen.

## Results

On ShapeNet class-conditioned generation, COD-VAE with M=64 achieves a Rendering-FID of 37.05 and Surface-FPD of 0.460, outperforming VecSet with M=512 (Rendering-FID 44.18, Surface-FPD 0.521) while using 16× fewer latent vectors. Full-pipeline throughput reaches 16.09 sample/s versus 1.16 sample/s for VecSet (M=512), a 20.8× speedup. On Objaverse, COD-VAE with M=64 attains 79.9% IoU reconstruction, matching VecSet with M=1024 (79.8%), while delivering 21.6× higher generation throughput (4.97 vs. 0.23 sample/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COD-VAE with M=64 decisively outperforms VecSet across all FID-based generation metrics: Rendering-FID drops from 44.18 (VecSet M=512) to 37.05, and Surface-FPD from 0.521 to 0.460, while full-pipeline throughput improves by 20.8× (16.09 vs. 1.16 sample/s). Against 3DILG, COD-VAE achieves a 27.8-point lower Rendering-FID (37.05 vs. 64.83) and a 5.6× higher Surface-FPD advantage (0.460 vs. 2.585), though 3DILG has slightly lower MMD. Compared to GEM3D, COD-VAE reduces Rendering-FID by 10.9 points (37.05 vs. 47.98) and Surface-FPD by 0.537 (0.460 vs. 0.997), while matching or exceeding coverage (COV 86.03% vs. 66.64%). On Objaverse reconstruction, COD-VAE (M=64) matches VecSet (M=1024) in IoU (79.9% vs. 79.8%) but surpasses it in CD (0.046 vs. 0.051) and F1 (88.0% vs. 87.0%), with 21.6× higher throughput. The key finding is that reducing VecSet's latent vectors from 512 to 64 causes severe quality degradation (Rendering-FID jumps from 44.18 to 54.47), whereas COD-VAE's two-stage autoencoder maintains quality at the same 64-vector budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

COD-VAE builds directly on VecSet (3DShape2VecSet), which established the paradigm of encoding 3D shapes into sets of 1D latent vectors via cross-attention, and on triplane-based neural field representations from EG3D and K-Planes. The key architectural departure is replacing VecSet's direct point-to-latent and latent-to-query cross-attention mappings with a two-stage autoencoder scheme: the encoder uses intermediate point patches as a learnable patchifier for progressive compression, and the decoder reconstructs dense triplanes rather than directly decoding neural fields. This design is inspired by 2D compression work (TiTok, DC-AE) that demonstrated highly compressed 1D token spaces can accelerate diffusion models, now extended to the 3D domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 64 latent vectors suffice to match or exceed VecSet with 512–1024 vectors challenges the field's implicit assumption that high-quality 3D reconstruction requires large latent sets, and the resulting 20.8× generation speedup makes latent diffusion for 3D shapes substantially more tractable at scale. The compact latent space also enables larger batch sizes and lower memory footprints, which is critical for scaling to more complex datasets like Objaverse. Limitations include increased VAE parameter counts and encoder computation relative to VecSet, and the authors note that multi-modal encoding (images, videos) remains an open direction. The uncertainty-guided pruning mechanism, while effective, is currently tuned to a fixed 75% ratio and could benefit from adaptive or learned scheduling.
