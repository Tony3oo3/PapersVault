## Problem

Standard space-time patch tokenization for video transformers produces a number of tokens proportional to video duration rather than scene complexity, leading to memory bottlenecks and computational waste. Existing token-reduction strategies (merging, pruning, run-length encoding) either degrade accuracy or fail when the camera is moving, leaving no content-aware tokenization that is both efficient and robust to viewpoint changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252679/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (uniformly sampled 16 frames for training, up to several hundred frames at inference) or a single RGB image, processed at 512×512 resolution through a segmentation-and-tracking pipeline. It produces a variable number of fixed-size trajectory tokens—one per distinct panoptic sub-object trajectory—whose count reflects scene complexity rather than frame count. Images are handled natively by treating each segmented region as a trajectory of length one, enabling joint image-video pretraining without architectural changes.

## Method

TrajViT replaces space-time patches with panoptic sub-object trajectories as tokens. A parallelizable pipeline detects key frames via color-histogram and luminance thresholds, panoptically segments each key frame with DirectSAM (84M params), tracks objects within each clip using SAM2 hiera-small (46M params), and merges identities across clips via IoU matching (>0.8). A lightweight two-branch trajectory encoder (20M params) converts each variable-length trajectory into a single token: the appearance branch applies masked feature pooling over ResNet-18 hierarchical features per frame and aggregates with a one-query perceiver resampler using rotary embeddings, while the position branch encodes per-frame bounding-box coordinates via sinusoidal embeddings and a second perceiver resampler; the two embeddings are summed. The resulting trajectory tokens feed a standard ViT-Large transformer, trained end-to-end with a CLIP contrastive objective on Panda-70M video-caption pairs (2M–8M scales) with optional joint training on DataComp-50M images.

## Results

On zero-shot video-text retrieval (R@5), TrajViT achieves 38.6/38.4 on ActivityNet, 36.2/61.0 on VATEX, 31.7/61.0 on MSR-VTT, and 14.9/14.8 on Charades (txt2vid/vid2txt), outperforming ViT3D by an average of ~6% while using 10× fewer tokens. On action classification (top-1 accuracy), TrajViT reaches 55.6% on K400 and 84.5% on UCF-101, surpassing ViT3D (53.4%, 83.8%). When connected to Llama-3 as a VideoLLM, TrajViT-LLM improves average accuracy by 5.24% over ViT3D-LLM across six VideoQA benchmarks (e.g., 65.1 vs 49.2 on NextQA OE, 32.2 vs 19.2 on MLVU) while training 4.2× faster and consuming 18× fewer inference FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajViT decisively outperforms ViT3D (space-time patch ViT) across all retrieval benchmarks by 2–6 R@5 points (e.g., 38.6 vs 35.3 on ActivityNet txt2vid, 61.0 vs 56.5 on MSR-VTT vid2txt) while using 10× fewer tokens, and also leads on K400 (55.6 vs 53.4) and UCF-101 (84.5 vs 83.8) classification. Among token-merging baselines, TokenLearner is the closest competitor on retrieval (36.9 vs 38.6 on ActivityNet txt2vid) but still trails TrajViT by 1.7–4.5 points; RLT and ToMe underperform ViT3D on most metrics, confirming that naive patch merging degrades quality. On the VideoLLM front, TrajViT-LLM beats ViT3D-LLM on every one of the six QA benchmarks (e.g., NextQA OE 65.1 vs 49.2, MLVU 32.2 vs 19.2, VideoMME 32.0 vs 28.9), with the largest gaps on tasks requiring fine-grained object reasoning. The only task where TrajViT slightly underperforms ViT3D is SSV2 action classification (45.8 vs 46.1), attributed to over-compression in simple egocentric scenes with very few trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant space-time patch tokenization paradigm (ViViT/ViT3D) and the line of token-merging/reduction methods (ToMe, RLT, TokenLearner) that operate on fixed patches. Rather than merging or dropping similar patches post-hoc, it repurposes off-the-shelf panoptic segmentation (DirectSAM) and video tracking (SAM2) to define tokens as semantically coherent object trajectories, grounded in Gestalt and Spelke's perceptual principles. The trajectory encoding module borrows the perceiver resampler architecture and rotary positional embeddings, while the overall training follows the standard CLIP contrastive objective. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to intermediate-layer token compression and VideoLLM token pruning methods, operating entirely at the tokenization stage before the transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrajViT is the first token-reduction video encoder to consistently outperform the full-token ViT3D baseline across retrieval, classification, and VideoLLM QA tasks, challenging the prevailing assumption that fewer tokens necessarily means worse performance. The 10× token reduction with 6% retrieval improvement and 18× inference FLOPs reduction for VideoLLMs makes long-video understanding substantially more tractable. Key limitations include the tracking pipeline being the inference bottleneck (only faster than ViT3D for >64 frames), slight underperformance on SSV2 due to over-compression in simple scenes, and the perceiver resampler acting as a potential information bottleneck. Open questions include whether faster tracking or learned trajectory generation can remove the pipeline overhead, and whether the approach scales to hour-scale videos or 3D/egocentric settings.
