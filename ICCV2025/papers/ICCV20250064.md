## Problem

Existing scene-aware motion synthesis and forecasting methods rely on explicit 3D scene representations (point clouds, meshes, SDFs) that are impractical to obtain in real-world egocentric settings with limited field of view and frequent occlusions. No prior work has addressed egocentric motion generation, and egocentric motion forecasting remains limited to simple actions, leaving a gap in scene-aware first-person motion modeling from accessible RGB inputs alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250064/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts egocentric RGB video frames (8-second clips at 10 fps, N=80) paired with a 6-DOF inertial SLAM trajectory for reconstruction, a truncated prefix (first 2 s) for forecasting, or a single egocentric image for generation. Output is a full 3D motion sequence parameterized by SMPL-X, but re-expressed in a head-centric canonical frame: per-frame SE(3) transforms for all 22 joints with pitch, roll, and height removed, and trajectory encoded as frame-to-frame residuals. During generation, missing trajectory and image inputs are replaced with learnable mask tokens rather than requiring any 3D scene geometry.

## Method

UniEgoMotion is a transformer-decoder conditional diffusion model that denoises noisy motion via cross-attention on image features (from a frozen DINOv2 ViT encoder) and additive trajectory embeddings, unifying reconstruction, forecasting, and generation through classifier-free guidance with randomly masked conditioning during training. A key design choice is a head-centric motion representation that replaces pelvis-centric SMPL-X parameters with canonicalized SE(3) joint transforms, eliminating kinematic-chain dependencies and aligning the motion frame with the egocentric camera. Forecasting is achieved at inference via diffusion inpainting (Repaint-style overwriting of known past frames), while generation uses the unconditional branch of classifier-free guidance. The model is trained on EE4D-Motion, a 110+ hour dataset derived from EgoExo4D with pseudo-ground-truth SMPL-X annotations obtained through multi-view fitting, sequence smoothing, and quality filtering.

## Results

On egocentric motion reconstruction (Table 1), UniEgoMotion achieves the best MPJPE of 0.100 m (vs. 0.116 for AvatarPoser, 0.130 for EgoEgo, 0.163 for EgoAllo), the lowest head rotation error (0.260 vs. 0.531 for EgoAllo), and the highest semantic similarity (0.918) and lowest FID (0.027). On the novel forecasting task (Table 2), UniEgoMotion achieves the lowest MPJPE of 0.206 m over the 2–4 s prediction window and the lowest foot sliding (2.60), outperforming the LSTM baseline (0.238 m, FS 7.23) and the two-stage baseline (0.253 m, FS 3.55). For generation from a single egocentric image, UniEgoMotion achieves the best foot contact (0.025 m) and foot sliding (2.89) among all methods, confirming physically plausible motion synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniEgoMotion outperforms all three reconstruction baselines across every reported metric. Against AvatarPoser [39], it reduces MPJPE by 14% (0.100 vs. 0.116 m) and cuts foot sliding nearly in half (3.62 vs. 7.85), while improving semantic similarity from 0.872 to 0.918. Against EgoEgo [45], the gains are similar: 23% lower MPJPE (0.100 vs. 0.130 m) and 34% lower hand-joint error (0.180 vs. 0.272 m). Against EgoAllo [85], which uses a pelvis-centric representation, UniEgoMotion halves the head rotation error (0.260 vs. 0.531) and reduces MPJPE by 39% (0.100 vs. 0.163 m), confirming the benefit of the head-centric formulation. For the novel forecasting and generation tasks, the LSTM baseline achieves slightly lower MPJPE-PA (0.066 vs. 0.071) but suffers severe foot sliding (7.23 vs. 2.60) and higher FID (0.058 vs. 0.047), indicating it produces averaged, physically implausible motions. The two-stage baseline [7,78] achieves the best FID on forecasting (0.038) but has higher MPJPE (0.253 vs. 0.206 m) and worse hand-joint accuracy (0.361 vs. 0.308 m), showing that the trajectory-to-motion decomposition introduces distribution mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional diffusion framework (Ho et al. 2020; Tevet et al. 2022) and repurposes classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans 2022) and diffusion inpainting (Lugmayr et al. 2022) to unify three egocentric motion tasks in a single transformer-based model. It departs from prior scene-aware motion generation/forecasting, which relies on explicit 3D scene representations (point clouds, SDFs, meshes), by extracting scene context solely from egocentric RGB images via a DINOv2-initialized ViT encoder. It also diverges from prior egocentric reconstruction methods (EgoEgo, EgoAllo, AvatarPoser) that discard image semantics, and from the standard pelvis-centric SMPL-X parameterization, adopting instead a head-centric canonicalized representation better aligned with ego-device conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing egocentric motion generation from a single image and egocentric motion forecasting as formal tasks, the paper opens a practical pathway for AR/VR, assistive technology, and healthcare applications where 3D scene reconstruction is infeasible. The EE4D-Motion dataset (110+ hours from EgoExo4D) is a valuable resource, though its pseudo-ground-truth annotations via a fitting pipeline introduce uncertainty that limits the strength of quantitative claims. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 8-second clips, how it handles longer-horizon forecasting, and whether multimodal conditioning (text, audio) can further improve scene-relevant generation. The finding that a simple unified diffusion model with strong image features outperforms specialized architectures suggests the field may over-invest in task-specific motion backbones.
