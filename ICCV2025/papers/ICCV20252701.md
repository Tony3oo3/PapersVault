## Problem

Existing human grasp synthesis methods produce object-centric grasps that ignore surrounding scene context and downstream task objectives, leading to collisions with nearby objects and failures during task execution (e.g., placing or stacking). No prior approach simultaneously models both the environmental clutter and the intended manipulation goal when generating a grasp pose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two scene point clouds (initial and goal, each containing the target object and surrounding obstacles) and produces a task-oriented human grasp parameterized by MANO (10-dim shape β, 51-dim joint rotations and root translation θ). The target object is sampled to 2048 points and scene obstacles to 6000 points; point-to-point shortest distances are computed and rescaled via a sigmoid transform (α=30 for scene, α=100 for hand) to form normalized distance maps in [0,1]. The intermediate output is a task-aware contact map of shape N×1 encoding the distance from each object point to its closest hand mesh vertex in the grasp pose.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage diffusion pipeline. ContactDiffuser (a Transformer adapted from SceneDiffuser) denoises a task-aware contact map conditioned on a 5D point cloud formed by concatenating each object point's 3D coordinates with its initial and goal scene distances; PointNet++ encodes local features which are injected as positional embeddings to overcome point-order invariance. GraspDiffuser then denoises noisy MANO parameters using self-attention over joints and over task-aware object features (contact map concatenated with PointNet++ object features), followed by cross-attention to align hand geometry with object geometry. Training uses the standard diffusion objective for ContactDiffuser and adds reconstruction, penetration, and contact-map consistency losses for GraspDiffuser. The key insight is that a task-aware contact map—encoding where the hand should contact the object given both scene clutter and the goal configuration—carries substantially more task-relevant information than an object-centric contact map that only reasons about the object surface in isolation.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best Task Score (TS) across all three tasks: 0.563 on Placing, 0.687 on Stacking, and 0.550 on Shelving. In Placing, it attains a Qualified Ratio of 64.61% with Init OPP of 6.82% and Goal OPP of 6.35%, outperforming the next-best FLEX (TS=0.511). In Stacking, the method reaches 84.54% QR with the lowest Goal OPP (4.55%), while F-GraspTTA and FLEX fail entirely (TS=0). The method also achieves the highest QR in Placing (64.61%) and Shelving (66.94%), demonstrating superior grasp quality in terms of physical plausibility and stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on the proposed Task Score (TS) metric across all three tasks. Against GraspTTA [24], the method achieves substantially higher TS (0.563 vs. 0.382 in Placing; 0.687 vs. 0.236 in Stacking; 0.550 vs. 0.428 in Shelving), primarily due to much lower obstacle penetration (Init OPP 6.82% vs. 21.36% in Placing) and higher QR. Against ContactGen [32], the method wins in Placing (TS 0.563 vs. 0.366) and Shelving (0.550 vs. 0.376), and leads in Stacking (0.687 vs. 0.638), though ContactGen achieves lower PV and higher diversity. Against SceneDiffuser [22], the method dominates on TS in all tasks (e.g., 0.563 vs. 0.362 in Placing), with SceneDiffuser suffering from high Init OPP (20.88%) due to lack of scene modeling. Against FLEX [45], the method wins in Placing (0.563 vs. 0.511) and Stacking (0.687 vs. 0, where FLEX fails entirely on small bricks), while FLEX is competitive in Shelving (0.524 vs. 0.550) thanks to its lower Goal OPP (4.46% vs. 10.06%). Notably, F-GraspTTA achieves the lowest Init OPP in Placing (0.54%) but still lags in Goal OPP (15.41%) due to ICP transformation inaccuracies, and completely fails in Stacking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human grasp synthesis line (GraspTTA, ContactGen, FLEX, SceneDiffuser) by introducing a two-stage diffusion framework that explicitly models both scene context and downstream task objectives, which prior methods treat as object-centric problems. It builds on SceneDiffuser's Transformer-based diffusion architecture and PointNet++ encoding, but departs from object-centric contact maps (ContactGen, ContactOpt) by proposing task-aware contact maps that encode distance to the hand in a grasp pose conditioned on both initial and goal scene geometry. The dataset construction leverages DexGraspNet's differentiable force-closure estimator for prior poses, filtered through PyBullet simulation for physical plausibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a clearly defined new task (task-oriented grasp synthesis) with a large-scale dataset (~1.38M grasps across three tasks) and a composite Task Score metric, providing a benchmark the community can build on. Key open questions remain: the method fails on small objects (Stacking), assumes initial and goal grasps are coherent, and is validated only on synthetic scenes where the goal point cloud is given—real-world deployment would require estimating the goal scene. The gap between ground-truth and predicted contact maps (e.g., TS 0.660 vs 0.570 in Placing) suggests the contact map prediction stage is the current bottleneck, pointing to a promising direction for future work.
