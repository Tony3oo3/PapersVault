## Problem

Existing methods for analyzing articulated object motion parts and attributes rely on dense multi-view RGB(D) images, 3D point clouds, or annotated training data, making them impractical for real-world robot scenarios with limited fields of view and complex backgrounds. No prior work can parse motion parts and their motion attributes (type and axis) at scene level from a single monocular video in a zero-shot, annotation-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250497/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence as its sole input, with no camera calibration, depth sensors, or multi-view captures required. Internally it leverages off-the-shelf depth estimation (Depth Anything V2), optical flow (RAFT/FlowDiffuser), and camera pose estimation (MASt3R) as preprocessing, then segments motion parts via FlowI-SAM on the flow map. Outputs include per-part motion type labels (translation, rotation, or rotation+translation), motion axis parameters (3D direction vector and 3D position point), and a fully optimized 2D Gaussian scene representation that can render the articulated object in any state.

## Method

MonoMobility initializes a 2D Gaussian splatting scene from first-frame point clouds, assigning each segmented motion part an initial axis estimated via point cloud registration between the two frames with the largest displacement. All parts are initially assumed to undergo composite rotation+translation motion, with per-frame translational distances and rotational angles as optimizable scalars. An end-to-end optimization then jointly refines axis parameters, motion quantities, and Gaussian attributes by randomly sampling frame pairs, transforming Gaussians to those states, and minimizing a combined rendering loss (L1 + D-SSIM), a normal-map consistency loss, and a novel motion loss that enforces pattern consistency between Gaussian transformations and point-cloud displacements. After a fixed number of iterations, each part's total translational and rotational movement is compared against thresholds to classify its true motion type, and parts with negligible motion are pruned back into the static region.

## Results

On the MonoMobility dataset, MonoMobility achieves a mean angle error (AE) of 1.262° and mean position error (PE) of 4.843 cm for motion axis prediction, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, DGMarbles*, achieves 12.930° AE and 26.336 cm PE — roughly 10× worse on both metrics. The PARIS variants (scene and object-level) perform even worse, with mean AE of 37.873° and 17.000° respectively, and additionally fail entirely on translation and rotation-translation categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoMobility decisively outperforms all three baselines on both AE and PE across nearly every category. Against DGMarbles* (the closest competitor, also using monocular video input), MonoMobility reduces mean AE from 12.930° to 1.262° (≈10× improvement) and mean PE from 26.336 cm to 4.843 cm (≈5.4× improvement). The PARIS variants, which require dense multi-view images of initial and final states, perform worst overall (mean AE 37.87° for scene-level, 17.00° for object-level) and completely fail on translation and rotation-translation categories, likely due to their limited field of view and object-level design. Notably, even MonoMobility without its optimization module (w/o optim) already matches or beats DGMarbles* on most categories, indicating that the initialization stage alone is strong, but the full optimization pipeline is critical for achieving the best results (e.g., Faucet AE drops from 12.60° to 0.30°). The ablation also shows that the optimization module is essential: removing it degrades mean AE from 1.262° to 12.054°, nearly matching DGMarbles*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of articulated object motion analysis (Shape2Motion, OPD, PARIS, Ditto) and dynamic scene reconstruction via Gaussian splatting (4DGS, DGMarbles, Shape of Motion). It departs from prior articulated-object methods by requiring only a monocular video rather than multi-view images, RGB-D data, or annotated training sets, and from prior Gaussian-based dynamic reconstruction by unifying motion-part Gaussians under explicit kinematic constraints (axis, type) rather than treating each Gaussian independently. The choice of 2D Gaussian Splatting over 3D is notable, motivated by its explicit surface representation that facilitates integrating rigid-body motion attributes, and the self-supervised optimization with a dedicated motion loss is specifically tailored to articulated kinematics rather than general dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, monocular-video formulation directly addresses a practical bottleneck for embodied intelligence: robots equipped with a single RGB camera need to identify how articulated objects in their environment move without any prior annotation or multi-view setup. The constructed dataset (15 synthetic + 11 real-world scenes) provides a useful benchmark for this underexplored setting. Key limitations include per-scene optimization (no amortized inference), dependence on the quality of offline depth and flow estimators, and the current inability to handle occluded or highly textured-free motion parts robustly. Open questions include whether the optimization can be made real-time or amortized, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-joint or non-rigid articulated structures.
