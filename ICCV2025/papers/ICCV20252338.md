## Problem

Visual data tokenization via methods like VQ-VAE produces regular grids where uniform regions (e.g., blue sky) consume the same number of tokens as complex foreground regions, wasting sequence length on low-information content. While Byte Pair Encoding effectively compresses 1D text sequences by merging frequent token pairs, applying it to images only along a single axis misses the spatial structure of 2D/3D data. The paper asks whether truly multidimensional BPE can produce shorter, more information-dense sequences that improve transformer-based generation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any discrete token grid as input—VQ-VAE/VQGAN codebook indices (e.g., 16×16 latent grids for 32×32 or 128×128 images), color-quantized pixels, or binary/latent voxel grids (8³ or 32³). It outputs a compressed token sequence where each token is anchored at a single grid position but may span multiple positions in non-convex shapes, reducing sequence length by 50–90% while expanding the vocabulary by a user-specified number of extra tokens. The resulting sequences are fed to a standard autoregressive transformer for generation, with the constraint that predicted tokens must fit within grid boundaries and not overlap already-generated tokens.

## Method

Multidimensional Byte Pair Encoding (MDBPE) iteratively counts constellations of neighboring token pairs across multiple spatial directions (horizontal and vertical, or three axes for 3D), where each constellation is identified by a tuple of (token class A, token class B, offset vector between their anchor points). The most frequent constellation is replaced by a new token, and the process repeats until the target vocabulary size is reached. To handle the resulting irregular sequences, the paper introduces three positional encoding strategies: next-token position encoding, integrated positional encoding (summing positional encodings over all grid positions a token covers, inspired by Mip-NeRF), and generation-time constraints that zero out probabilities for tokens exceeding boundaries or overlapping existing tokens. A lossy variant further amplifies compression by collapsing the VQ-VAE/VQGAN codebook via farthest-point-sampling-initialized K-means, merging semantically similar tokens before MDBPE runs, enabling 20% additional compression on top of the lossless step.

## Results

MDBPE consistently improves generative FID across all tested datasets: MNIST drops from 89.07 to 4.23 (256 extra tokens), CIFAR-10 from 38.32 to 33.93 (1024 extra tokens), SVHN from 49.79 to 43.65 (512 extra tokens), and CelebA from 65.99 to 50.87 (512 extra tokens). On ShapeNet 3D generation, MDBPE achieves 70.47% Coverage and 0.0019 MMD on Planes (32³ voxels) versus 47.47% and 0.0031 for the Octree Transformer baseline. Against Taming Transformers [21] on CelebA, MDBPE reaches FID 13.35 versus 15.48, converging roughly 20 epochs earlier. Compression rates range from 54% (MNIST greyscale) to 92% (CIFAR-10 VQ-VAE 256), with the lossy codebook-collapse variant pushing compression to 73% at K=64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 1D BPE (Razzhigaev et al. [53]), MDBPE wins on both FID and compression: on CIFAR-10 with VQ-VAE, MDBPE achieves FID 33.93 vs 36.53 at 89% compression vs 91%; on CelebA, FID 50.87 vs 52.15 at 86% vs 87%. Against the Octree Transformer [29] on 32³ voxel grids, MDBPE substantially outperforms on both Coverage and MMD: for Planes, 70.47% Coverage and 0.0019 MMD vs 47.47% and 0.0031; for Rifles, MDBPE achieves 69.62% Coverage while the Octree Transformer runs out of VRAM entirely. Against Taming Transformers [21] (VQGAN recipe on CelebA), MDBPE converges to a better optimum (FID 13.35 vs 15.48) and reaches comparable scores roughly 20 epochs earlier, with the lossy codebook-collapse variant further improving to FID 17.85 at 73% compression (K=64). Overall, MDBPE wins all three head-to-head comparisons, with the largest margins on 3D shape generation where the quadratic attention cost of uncompressed voxel grids is most prohibitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Byte Pair Encoding from 1D text sequences to n-dimensional visual data, building directly on Razzhigaev et al.'s pixel-level BPE for linearized images and the VQ-VAE/VQGAN discrete tokenization framework from van der Oord et al. and Esser et al. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of anchor points and constellation offset vectors to handle non-convex, multi-pixel tokens in 2D and 3D, rather than restricting merges to a single linear axis. It also extends the Taming Transformers autoregressive generation recipe with novel positional encodings (next-token position and integrated positional encoding for token shape) and a lossy codebook-collapse variant that amplifies compression by merging semantically similar codewords.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for compute-constrained settings: shorter sequences reduce quadratic attention cost, enable larger batch sizes, and make 3D voxel grids (e.g., 32³) feasible on consumer hardware where naive transformers run out of memory. As a lossless, content-adaptive preprocessing step, MDBPE is drop-in compatible with existing VQ-VAE/VQGAN pipelines and aligns with the trend of integrating visual tokens into multimodal LLMs where token count directly affects cost. Open questions include whether the gains persist at much larger scales (e.g., full ImageNet training), how the method interacts with 2D rotary position embeddings, and whether the lossy codebook collapse can be fine-tuned to recover the small quality gap it introduces in the decoder.
