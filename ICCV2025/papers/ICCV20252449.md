## Problem

Long video understanding is hampered by the low and uneven information density across video segments: uniform frame sampling wastes computation on redundant frames while missing critical details, and existing agent-based methods (e.g., VideoTree) rely on expensive per-frame captioning or image-similarity retrieval that scale poorly to hour-long videos. The paper asks whether a single VLM can autonomously and efficiently locate the most informative segments without any auxiliary tools or training.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a long video (180 s on EgoSchema, 30+ min on LVBench) and a natural-language question, and outputs a single text answer. Rather than ingesting the full video, it consumes frames in small batches (N per segment) through a fixed-size memory buffer of 8–16 frames, so the effective visual context at any reasoning step is bounded regardless of video length. Discarded frames are compressed into short text descriptions retained in the exploration history, allowing the reward model to reference earlier content without re-observing pixels.

## Method

VCA treats the video as a tree-search environment where each segment is a node and the agent iteratively samples N frames from a chosen segment, forming finer sub-segments. A self-generated intrinsic reward model (the same VLM, prompted with chain-of-thought) scores each sub-segment's relevance to the query, and these scores guide—but do not force—the agent's next selection, enabling backtracking and balancing exploitation with exploration. A fixed-size memory buffer retains only the highest-scoring frames and converts evicted frames into textual summaries, keeping inference cost constant across exploration depth. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, demonstrated with both GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL-72B, and outperforms uniform sampling and prior agent baselines (VideoAgent, VideoTree) while observing fewer frames.

## Results

On EgoSchema (180s videos), VCA achieves 73.6% accuracy using only 7.2 frames on average, outperforming GPT-4o with 32 uniform frames (70.4%) by 3.2 points while observing fewer than 25% of the frames. On LVBench (30+ min videos), VCA reaches 41.3% average accuracy with 20.0 frames, surpassing GPT-4o with 64 frames (34.7%) by 6.6 points. The largest gains appear in Event Understanding (+10.0 over GPT-4o on LVBench) and Reasoning (+12.7 over GPT-4o), confirming that curiosity-driven segment exploration captures critical temporal information that uniform sampling misses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCA outperforms all nine baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest video-LLM baselines on LVBench, VCA (41.3%) beats InternVL2-40B (39.8%, 16 frames) by 1.5 points and GLM4V-Plus (38.3%, 20 frames) by 3.0 points, while using comparable or fewer frames. On EgoSchema, VCA (73.6%, 7.2 frames) surpasses GPT-4o with 32 uniform frames (70.4%) by 3.2 points and LVNet (68.2%, 12 frames) by 5.4 points. Among agent-based systems, VCA leads VideoAgent (63.2% on EgoSchema, 29.3% on LVBench) by 10.4 and 12.0 points respectively, and VideoTree (67.0% on EgoSchema, 28.8% on LVBench) by 6.6 and 12.5 points, while VideoTree requires 103.2 frames on LVBench versus VCA's 20.0. With the open-source Qwen2-VL-72B, VCA (75.2%, 16.9 frames) still edges out Qwen2-VL with 32 uniform frames (74.2%) and substantially outperforms VideoTree (71.0%, 85.4 frames), confirming the framework generalizes beyond GPT-4o.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCA builds on the agent-based paradigm for long video understanding (VideoAgent, VideoTree) but departs from them in two key ways: it replaces frame-level image-similarity retrieval with segment-level tree-search exploration, and it eliminates the need for dense captioning or auxiliary tools by using a single VLM to generate its own intrinsic reward scores. The tree-search structure draws on classical search algorithms, while the curiosity-driven framing and memory buffer are inspired by human selective attention and working-memory mechanisms. Unlike prior methods that require traversing or captioning the entire video, VCA adaptively allocates its exploration budget, making it a more efficient and self-contained agent framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCA demonstrates that a training-free, single-VLM agent can outperform both uniform-sampling baselines and more complex agent systems (VideoAgent, VideoTree) while observing substantially fewer frames, offering a practical and scalable recipe for long video QA. The main limitation is that the intrinsic reward model is not always reliable, and the agent can be misled by inaccurate scores or miss subtle visual details, capping absolute accuracy (e.g., 41.3% on LVBench). Open questions include whether specialized or synthetic-data-trained reward models could close this gap, and whether the segment-level exploration strategy generalizes to even longer or more heterogeneous video content beyond the 30-minute LVBench setting.
