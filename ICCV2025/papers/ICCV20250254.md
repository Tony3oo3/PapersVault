## Problem

Prior work on enhancing CLIP's fine-grained visual representations with generative models (e.g., DIVA) relies on heavy pre-trained diffusion models and does not explore the underlying principle of when and how generative models transfer useful knowledge. This paper asks whether perfect reconstruction is actually necessary for representation enhancement, and identifies that the key challenge is extracting useful fine-grained visual knowledge from generative models while suppressing irrelevant information such as the feature-space gap between CLIP and the denoiser.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (processed at 224×224, 336×336, or 384×384 depending on the CLIP backbone) and uses only the [CLS] token of the CLIP ViT as the conditional input to the denoiser, deliberately dropping all local tokens to prevent information leakage. The output is a post-trained CLIP ViT with enhanced fine-grained visual representations, which can be used directly as a vision-language retriever or plugged into MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5) as a drop-in replacement for the original visual encoder.

## Method

GenHancer is a two-stage post-training framework that uses a lightweight, randomly-initialized denoiser (a FLUX-like DiT with ~1/10 the parameters for continuous rectified flow, or a 6-layer Perceiver for discrete VQ-GAN token prediction) to perform self-supervised reconstruction conditioned solely on the CLIP [CLS] token. Stage 1 trains the projector and denoiser while freezing the ViT, learning basic reconstruction and bridging the feature-space gap (reducing irrelevant information G₂); Stage 2 fine-tunes the ViT via LoRA (rank 16) to maximize useful fine-grained knowledge transfer (I(V;G₁)). For continuous denoisers, a scaled LogitNormal timestamp sampling strategy concentrates training on intermediate t≈0.5 where the velocity target is most challenging, amplifying the learning signal. The theoretical framing decomposes the generative model's knowledge into useful (G₁) and irrelevant (G₂) components, formalizing the enhancement objective as max I(V;G₁) − λI(V;G₂), which the two-stage design and class-token-only conditioning jointly optimize.

## Results

On the MMVP-VLM benchmark, GenHancer with a lightweight denoiser achieves 31.9% average on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L-14 @224, outperforming DIVA (25.9%) by 6.0% and the original CLIP (19.3%) by 12.6%. Across all six CLIP backbones tested, GenHancer consistently surpasses DIVA, with gains of +4.4% to +6.0% on OpenAI/MetaCLIP variants and +1.5% on SigLIP variants. When plugged into LLaVA-1.5 MLLMs, the enhanced CLIP yields 6.0% improvement on MMVP-MLLM and 4.5% on CV-Bench 3D over the original CLIP. Zero-shot classification and retrieval performance is preserved with <0.3% degradation, confirming that fine-grained enhancement does not compromise global semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DIVA [58], which enhances CLIP via diffusion feedback using a heavy pre-trained Stable Diffusion denoiser. GenHancer outperforms DIVA on all six CLIP backbones evaluated on MMVP-VLM, with the largest margin of +6.0% on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L-14 @224 (31.9 vs. 25.9) and +5.1% on MetaCLIP ViT-H-14 (37.0 vs. 31.9). The advantage is most pronounced on backbones with weaker original fine-grained perception (OpenAI and MetaCLIP), while gains on SigLIP are smaller (+1.5%) due to indirect information leakage through its pooler output. Notably, GenHancer achieves these improvements with a ~10× smaller denoiser (1.31B vs. 11.90B parameters) and 10× faster training, whereas DIVA relies on the full FLUX.1-dev model. In MLLM evaluations (Table 2), GenHancer-enhanced CLIP also surpasses DIVA-enhanced CLIP on MMVP-MLLM (30.7 vs. 31.3 for Vicuna-7B is close, but 36.7 vs. 35.3 for Vicuna-13B) and CV-Bench 3D (63.2 vs. 60.2 for Vicuna-7B), while maintaining comparable performance on conventional benchmarks like POPE and ScienceQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on enhancing CLIP's fine-grained visual representations through generative reconstruction, most directly building on DIVA (Wang et al., 2025), which uses a pre-trained Stable Diffusion denoiser conditioned on CLIP features. The key departure is a systematic empirical investigation into *what* in the generative model actually helps representation learning, revealing that the prior reliance on heavy pre-trained denoisers and local visual tokens as conditions is not only unnecessary but actively harmful. GenHancer replaces the heavy pre-trained denoiser with a lightweight randomly-initialized one (FLUX-like DiT or Perceiver), restricts conditioning to the class token alone, and introduces a two-stage training schedule to separate useful visual knowledge from irrelevant feature-space gaps. The method is further generalized to both continuous (rectified flow) and discrete (VQ-GAN + Perceiver) generative paradigms, whereas prior work focused exclusively on diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that visually perfect reconstruction does not yield optimal representations, and that a 1/10-parameter randomly-initialized denoiser outperforms a 12B pre-trained one—challenges the prevailing assumption that stronger generative models are needed for representation enhancement. This has direct practical implications: practitioners can enhance CLIP for MLLMs with a fraction of the compute (10× faster training, 1/3 memory) in a plug-and-play manner without retraining the full MLLM. Limitations include the relatively small evaluation benchmarks (MMVP-VLM has only 9 patterns with ~15 samples each), the modest improvement on SigLIP (attributed to indirect information leakage through its pooler output), and the fact that the two-stage training and class-token-only strategy, while well-motivated, have not been validated on architectures beyond ViT-based CLIP variants.
