## Problem

Existing gradient-based multi-task learning methods (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL) focus exclusively on finding a common descent direction that reduces empirical losses across tasks, but ignore the geometrical properties of the loss landscape. This leaves models vulnerable to overfitting and sharp minima, which is further exacerbated in MTL where sharp minima of different tasks may appear at different locations. The paper is the first to address this by seeking flat minima jointly across all tasks within a gradient-based MTL framework.

## Input / Output

The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into any gradient-based MTL method operating on a shared backbone with task-specific heads. For image classification (Multi-MNIST, CelebA), inputs are standard RGB images and outputs are class labels or multi-label attribute predictions. For scene understanding (NYUv2, CityScapes), inputs are RGB-D images processed by a SegNet backbone, producing per-pixel segmentation masks, depth maps, and surface normal vectors. The method requires no changes to input format or output structure; it only modifies how gradients are computed and aggregated during training.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each task's SAM-style perturbed gradient into a loss component (gradient of the empirical loss w.r.t. the shared backbone) and a flat component (the difference between the perturbed and unperturbed gradients), avoiding expensive Hessian computation. These two components are then aggregated separately across tasks using any existing gradient-based MTL method (e.g., PCGrad, CAGrad), and the two aggregated gradients are simply summed for the final shared-parameter update. Non-shared task heads are updated with standard single-task SAM. Theoretically, the paper proves a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound (Theorem 1) showing that minimizing the worst-case perturbation loss over both shared and non-shared parameters controls the true risk of each task, using a more general bounded-loss bound than prior SAM theory. This decomposition into loss and flat sub-objectives is shown empirically to reduce gradient conflict more effectively than directly aggregating full SAM gradients, because it handles intra-task conflict (between loss and flatness) and inter-task conflict separately.

## Results

On the Multi-MNIST benchmarks, flat-based methods consistently improve over their vanilla counterparts by ~1–1.5% average accuracy (e.g., F-CAGrad reaches 95.95% on MultiMNIST vs. 95.01% for CAGrad, and 93.54% on MultiFashion+MNIST vs. 92.68%). On CelebA (40-task multi-label classification), F-CAGrad achieves the lowest average error of 8.22%, outperforming CAGrad (8.52%) and PCGrad (8.69%). On NYUv2, F-IMTL attains the best relative task improvement of −4.77% Δm% (vs. −0.76% for IMTL), and on CityScapes, F-IMTL reduces Δm% from 11.10% (IMTL) to 1.87%, while F-MGDA drops from 44.14% to 0.67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed flat-based framework is applied on top of six gradient-based MTL methods (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL, NashMTL, FairGrad) and consistently outperforms each vanilla counterpart across all four benchmark suites. On Multi-MNIST, F-CAGrad achieves the best average accuracy (95.95% on MultiMNIST, 93.54% on Fashion+MNIST), surpassing CAGrad by ~0.9–0.86% and even exceeding the single-task learning (STL) baseline by 1.2% on MultiFashion and MultiMNIST. On CelebA, F-CAGrad (8.22% avg error) beats CAGrad (8.52%), PCGrad (8.69%), and IMTL (8.88%), narrowing the gap between different gradient aggregation strategies. On NYUv2, F-IMTL delivers the largest relative improvement (−4.77% Δm% vs. −0.76% for IMTL), while F-MGDA flips MGDA's negative improvement (1.38%) to positive (−0.33%). On CityScapes, the gains are most dramatic: F-MGDA reduces Δm% from 44.14% to 0.67%, and F-IMTL from 11.10% to 1.87%, indicating that seeking flat minima substantially mitigates the gradient conflicts that plague vanilla gradient-based MTL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: sharpness-aware minimization (SAM), which seeks flat minima in single-task optimization, and gradient-based multi-task learning methods (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL, Nash-MTL, FairGrad), which manipulate task gradients to find common descent directions but ignore loss-landscape geometry. The key departure is extending SAM's worst-case perturbation principle to the multi-task setting with shared and task-specific parameters, requiring a non-trivial theoretical treatment because the shared perturbation ε_sh is common across all tasks while ε_ns^i is task-specific. Theoretically, the authors replace SAM's PAC-Bayesian bound (restricted to 0-1 loss) with a more general bounded-loss bound, and practically they decompose the joint objective into separate loss-minimization and flatness-minimization sub-problems, aggregating each independently before combining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first work to explicitly target flat minima in gradient-based MTL, this paper provides a principled, model-agnostic plug-in that consistently improves all tested gradient-aggregation baselines across four diverse benchmarks, while also reducing gradient conflict from >50% to near 0% and improving calibration and robustness. The finding that flatness-seeking narrows the performance gap between different MTL methods (e.g., CelebA errors compress from 8.52–8.88 to 8.22–8.24) suggests flat regions act as a 'leveler' that mitigates method-specific biases. Open questions include scalability to larger architectures and datasets (experiments use ResNet-18 and SegNet on moderate-scale benchmarks), the interaction with other regularization strategies, and whether the perturbation radii ρ_sh and ρ_ns require careful per-dataset tuning in practice.
