## Problem

Existing generative self-supervised methods for point clouds (e.g., Point-MAE) perform masked self-reconstruction within a single view, which is inherently less challenging and informative than two-view learning paradigms proven effective in 2D. The key unaddressed challenges are: (1) how to construct two meaningfully decoupled views from unstructured 3D point cloud data, and (2) how to perform cross-reconstruction between them, which requires learning both intra-view spatial structure and inter-view positional relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method takes a 3D point cloud (1024 points sampled via FPS), crops it into two partial views using a random-center nearest-neighbor mechanism (minimum crop ratio 0.6), applies min-max normalization centered on each view's geometric center plus random rotation, then divides each view into 64 patches of 32 points via FPS+KNN. The model outputs the reconstructed point coordinates of the other view (n×k×3 patches), trained with Chamfer Distance loss. For downstream transfer, the frozen encoder features are fed to classification or segmentation heads to produce class labels or per-point part/semantic labels.

## Method

Point-PQAE introduces a cross-reconstruction generative framework where two decoupled point cloud views are generated via a novel 3D crop mechanism (random center selection + nearest-neighbor expansion) followed by independent normalization and rotation to isolate coordinate systems. A lightweight PointNet + 12-block Transformer encoder processes each view into latent representations, and a 4-block Transformer decoder reconstructs the target view. The key architectural innovation is the Positional Query Block: a View-Relative Positional Embedding (VRPE) is computed via sinusoidal encoding of the concatenated group-center coordinates and inter-view relative displacement, then used as the Query in a cross-attention mechanism where the source view's latent features serve as Key and Value. This allows the model to condition reconstruction on explicit 3D relative geometry rather than learnable positional codes, and the bidirectional cross-reconstruction (view 1→2 and view 2→1) is optimized jointly with Chamfer Distance loss.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (the most challenging real-world 3D classification benchmark), Point-PQAE achieves 95.0% / 93.6% / 89.6% accuracy on OBJ-BG / OBJ-ONLY / PB-T50-RS under the FULL protocol, outperforming the self-reconstruction baseline Point-MAE by 2.4%, 1.7%, and 1.2% respectively. Under the frozen-representation MLP-LINEAR protocol, the gains are substantially larger: +6.5%, +7.0%, and +6.7% over Point-MAE, demonstrating that cross-reconstruction learns more transferable features. On ModelNet40, Point-PQAE reaches 94.0% (1K) and 94.3% (8K) under FULL, and on ShapeNetPart it achieves 84.6% Cls.mIoU and 86.1% Inst.mIoU, matching or exceeding prior self-supervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary self-reconstruction baseline Point-MAE, Point-PQAE wins on every ScanObjectNN variant under all three protocols, with the largest margins under MLP-LINEAR (+6.5% to +7.0%) and MLP-3 (+4.4% to +5.3%), confirming that cross-reconstruction yields more informative frozen representations. Against the strongest cross-modal competitor ReCon (which uses a 2D image teacher and 43.6M parameters vs. Point-PQAE's 22.1M), Point-PQAE is competitive: it trails by only 0.2% on OBJ-BG (FULL) and 0.5% on PB-T50-RS (MLP-3), while matching on OBJ-ONLY (FULL) and leading on ModelNet40 MLP-LINEAR (92.0 vs. 92.5 is a 0.5% gap). Against ACT, Point-PQAE consistently outperforms by 1.7–4.1% on ScanObjectNN across protocols. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, Point-PQAE ties Point-MAE on Inst.mIoU (86.1%) and leads on Cls.mIoU (84.6% vs. 84.2%), but trails ReCon by 0.2–0.3%. On S3DIS scene segmentation, Point-PQAE improves over Point-MAE by 0.7% mAcc and 0.6% mIoU but remains slightly below ACT (71.1% mAcc). In few-shot learning on ModelNet40, Point-PQAE achieves the best or near-best results among single-modal methods, e.g., 96.9% (5-way 10-shot, FULL) vs. 96.4% for Point-MAE, and 93.0% (5-way 10-shot, MLP-LINEAR) vs. 91.1% for Point-MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative self-supervised learning paradigm for point clouds (initiated by Point-MAE's masked self-reconstruction) by replacing single-view masked reconstruction with a two-view cross-reconstruction objective, drawing conceptual inspiration from the two-view contrastive learning paradigm (SimCLR, MoCo) in 2D. It builds on Point-BERT's FPS+KNN patching strategy and Point-MAE's asymmetric Transformer encoder-decoder, but introduces two novel components: a 3D random crop mechanism (first applied to point cloud SSL) for generating decoupled views, and a View-Relative Positional Embedding (VRPE) with a cross-attention positional query block to encode inter-view geometry. The key departure from prior work is that cross-reconstruction requires the model to learn both intra-view spatial structure and inter-view positional relationships, making the pre-training task substantially harder than self-reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the two-view paradigm, long established in contrastive learning, transfers effectively to the generative/MIM side of 3D pre-training, yielding consistent 2–7% improvements over Point-MAE across classification, few-shot, and segmentation benchmarks. The finding that simply adding crop augmentation to Point-MAE yields negligible gains (<0.5%) while the full cross-reconstruction framework delivers large improvements highlights that the synergy between decoupled view generation and cross-view reconstruction is what drives the benefit, not the crop alone. Open questions include whether this paradigm extends to larger-scale or multi-modal settings, whether the fixed sinusoidal VRPE generalizes to non-rigid or dynamic point clouds, and whether the approach can be combined with contrastive objectives (as ReCon does) for further gains.
