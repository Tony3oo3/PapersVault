## Problem

3D geometric shape reconstruction from sparse, uncalibrated (unposed) images remains underexplored compared to radiance field learning in the same setting. Existing approaches either require dense calibrated image grids, rely on multiple external monocular geometry priors that are cumbersome and costly, or demand lengthy multi-stage optimization. This paper targets the gap of achieving accurate surface reconstruction from just a few unposed images within minutes on a consumer GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of unposed RGB images (3, 6, or 12 views) with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics provided. MASt3R is run at 512-pixel resolution to produce initial point maps and correspondences. The output is a 3D triangle mesh extracted via TSDF from the converged 2DGS rendered depth, along with refined camera poses and the ability to render novel views. The full pipeline (MASt3R inference + 1000 iterations of 2DGS optimization) completes in roughly 146 seconds on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

## Method

The pipeline bootstraps a bundle-adjusting 2D Gaussian Splatting model using MASt3R's feedforward point maps (for primitive positions), PCA-computed normals (for orientation), and dense pixel correspondences. Two key losses differentiate the approach: (1) a correspondence reprojection loss that back-projects splatted depth at one view and checks consistency with the corresponding pixel in another view, guiding joint camera and Gaussian optimization; and (2) a novel splatted color variance loss derived by interpreting alpha-compositing as a color expectation over ray-terminated fragments, then minimizing the second moment (variance) of color along each ray via a modified CUDA splatting kernel that renders squared colors. This variance reduction sharpens geometry and improves multi-view coherence. The entire optimization is single-stage (unlike multi-stage SPARF), jointly updating camera extrinsics and all 2DGS parameters with Adam, using a cosine-annealed weight for the variance term.

## Results

On the DTU 3-view reconstruction benchmark, Sparfels achieves a mean Relative Error of 4.82 and Normal Consistency of 0.857, outperforming the next-best method InstSplat2DGS (Rel 5.73, NC 0.832) and MASt3R alone (Rel 7.34, NC 0.830). For camera pose estimation, the method achieves a mean ATE of 0.0186, a 13.1% reduction over the best baseline InstSplatGOF (0.0214). Training completes in ~146 s on a single A6000 GPU for 1000 iterations, comparable to InstantSplat-XL (~128 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sparfels outperforms all seven baselines on both Rel and NC across the majority of DTU scans. The closest competitor, InstSplat2DGS [22], achieves Rel 5.73 and NC 0.832, while Sparfels reaches 4.82 and 0.857 respectively — a ~16% relative improvement in Rel. MASt3R [44] used as a standalone baseline (Rel 7.34, NC 0.830) shows that the 2DGS refinement and correspondence loss contribute substantially over the raw foundation-model output. Generalizable methods UFORecon [56] (Rel 42.77) and CasMVSNet [26] (Rel 17.10) degrade severely under noisy MASt3R-initialized poses, as do Spurfies [67] (Rel 21.70) and SparseCraft [95] (Rel 6.50). For camera pose (ATE), Sparfels (0.0186) beats InstSplatGOF [21] (0.0214) by 13.1% and MASt3R [44] (0.0269) by 31%, confirming that the correspondence-guided bundle adjustment effectively corrects initial pose errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D foundation models (MASt3R/DUSt3R) and differentiable surface rendering (2D Gaussian Splatting), building directly on both as its two pillars. It departs from the broader sparse-view reconstruction literature (SparseNeuS, Spurfies, SparseCraft, UfoRecon) that typically relies on multiple external geometric priors (monocular depth, normals, learned SDFs) by harnessing a single foundation model for all initialization signals. Most closely related to InstantSplat, which also pairs MASt3R with Gaussian Splatting, this work extends that recipe by (1) using 2DGS planar primitives instead of 3DGS for surface accuracy, (2) introducing a correspondence reprojection loss that leverages MASt3R's dense pixel matches to refine cameras during training, and (3) deriving a novel splatted color variance loss from a statistical moment analysis of volume rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is a simple, single-model pipeline that achieves state-of-the-art sparse unposed reconstruction in under 3 minutes on a consumer GPU, removing the need to deploy multiple auxiliary networks for depth, normals, or geometry priors. The variance regularization loss, while theoretically straightforward, provides a clean mechanism for sharpening reconstructions and improving multi-view coherence without additional data or architecture. Limitations remain for highly complex geometry, thin structures, semi-transparent objects, and dynamic scenes, and the method assumes consistent lighting. An open question is whether the variance loss generalizes beyond 2DGS to other splatting or implicit representations, and whether the single-foundation-model strategy scales as MASt3R-class models improve.
