## Problem

Existing image generation and editing methods lack precise control over 3D geometric features of objects in 2D image space. Purely 2D dragging-based editing cannot guarantee geometry-aware modifications because it has no access to real-world dimensions and proportions, while 3D editing workflows are too slow and skill-intensive for rapid concept design. No integrated framework provides accurate, efficient, and training-free geometric conditioning that enforces structural constraints with metric precision.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a single class-specific 3D reference object (e.g., a car model from ShapeNet or Objaverse) loaded in Blender, a text prompt describing the desired style, geometric target parameters (e.g., length, height, wheelbase), and a viewpoint defined by camera distance, elevation, and azimuth angles. Source keypoints are defined once per 3D object in 3D space and projected into the 2D image plane using the camera intrinsics. The output is a 512×512 RGB image where the object's geometric features match the specified 3D constraints while reflecting the user's style prompt, with source-target keypoint pairs driving the spatial modifications.

## Method

GeoDiffusion is a four-step training-free pipeline: (1) a point translation function D encodes parametric relationships between 3D keypoints (e.g., extending a car's length simultaneously shifts wheel centers and overhangs), computing target keypoints from user-specified geometric parameters; (2) Plug-and-Play Diffusion performs text-conditioned style transfer via DDIM inversion and feature injection, after which Diffusion Hyperfeatures re-detect the source keypoints in the restyled image; (3) GeoDrag, the core editing mechanism, improves upon GoodDrag by introducing a point fixation mechanism that excludes keypoints within a lower threshold of their target from the optimization loss and applies a gradient mask to prevent drift, plus a two-stage copy-and-paste refinement that directly transfers latent patches to target positions and blurs the source to avoid duplication; (4) SDXL-refiner polishes the final image. The key insight is that projecting 3D geometric differences into viewpoint-accurate 2D source-target pairs ensures structural consistency across viewpoints, something purely 2D methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On the Geometry Guidance Benchmark, GeoDrag achieves a mean distance (MD) of 10.80 pixels, outperforming the next best method GoodDrag (11.77 px) by 8.24% while also being 9.46% faster (60.03s vs. 66.30s). On DragBench, GeoDrag attains the lowest MD of 20.86 px (14.12% better than GoodDrag's 24.29 px) with comparable image fidelity (IF = 0.872 vs. 0.871). CLIP and HPSv2 scores are on par with GoodDrag (25.31 vs. 25.21 CLIP; 25.71 vs. 25.93 HPSv2), confirming that the accuracy gains do not come at the cost of prompt alignment or perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDrag is compared against seven dragging-based methods: DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion, SDE-Drag, FreeDrag, EasyDrag, DragNoise, and GoodDrag. GeoDrag decisively outperforms all methods on dragging accuracy (MD), achieving 60.90% lower MD than DragonDiffusion and 54.02% lower than SDE-Drag on the Geometry Guidance dataset, and 14.12% lower than GoodDrag on DragBench. Against its closest competitor GoodDrag, GeoDrag wins on both MD and inference time (60.03s vs. 66.30s) while matching on CLIP score and trailing by only 0.22 points on HPSv2. On DragBench, EasyDrag achieves a marginally higher IF (0.910 vs. 0.872) but at the cost of 11.69 px higher MD, indicating a fidelity-accuracy trade-off that GeoDrag resolves in favor of geometric precision. The paper notes that DragonDiffusion scores higher on CLIP/HPSv2 for the geometry guidance set but fails to preserve object integrity and viewpoint, a discrepancy the authors attribute to limitations of the HPSv2 metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDiffusion sits at the intersection of drag-based image editing (GoodDrag, DragDiffusion, SDEDrag) and 3D-informed generation frameworks (Image Sculpting, MVDrag3D, 3D-DST). It departs from 3D reconstruction-based approaches by using a single class-specific 3D reference object as a geometric prior rather than reconstructing geometry from images, and it departs from purely 2D drag methods by projecting parametric 3D keypoint relations into the image plane. The core editing mechanism, GeoDrag, extends GoodDrag's alternating optimization-and-denoising scheme with a point fixation mechanism and a copy-and-paste refinement strategy inspired by SDE-Drag, while the style transfer stage reuses Plug-and-Play Diffusion Features without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in early-phase product design where engineers need rapid 2D concept images with precise dimensional constraints (e.g., wheelbase, overhang) without full 3D modeling. Its training-free nature (only <10 s LoRA per object) and the introduced Geometry Guidance Benchmark make it accessible and reproducible. However, multi-view consistency remains unresolved since each viewpoint is generated independently, large dragging distances still degrade geometric precision, and the method inherits all limitations of the underlying Stable Diffusion backbone. Future work on integrating GeoDiffusion into multi-view diffusion models could close the gap toward consistent 3D object modification.
