## Problem

Existing space-time superpixel methods that propagate labels across video frames suffer from wall-clock runtimes exceeding one second per frame, making them impractical for real-time or deep-learning pipeline integration. While single-image superpixel methods like BASS achieve high quality at very low cost, they lack temporal coherence. The paper addresses the gap of achieving state-of-the-art space-time superpixel quality at dramatically reduced runtime without sacrificing temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252469/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

BIST consumes a video sequence of RGB frames (typically 480×320 or 480×960 resolution), a pre-computed dense optical flow field per frame pair (obtained from external DNN methods like RAFT or SpyNet rather than estimated internally), and a set of hyperparameters controlling superpixel size, appearance variance, Potts strength, merge threshold, temporal coherence strength, and relabeling thresholds. It produces per-frame superpixel label maps where each pixel is assigned a contiguous, deformably-shaped cluster ID that is propagated forward in time. The number of superpixels adapts automatically per frame, and the method processes strictly forward in time (no backward refinement).

## Method

BIST extends the single-image BASS method to video through three iterative steps: a Shift-and-Fill step that propagates superpixels via averaged optical flow and fills holes with a watershed algorithm, GPU-parallel boundary updates that reclassify boundary pixels using a Potts-regularized likelihood, and split/merge/relabel steps that adapt the superpixel count. The core novelty is a temporal coherence term (γρ_s) added to the split-step Hastings ratio, where ρ_s is the fraction of propagated (valid) labels within each superpixel after the shift step. This term raises the acceptance threshold for splitting when a superpixel is well-propagated (ρ_s ≈ 1), suppressing unnecessary label churn, while automatically deactivating in disoccluded regions (ρ_s ≈ 0) to allow unconstrained adaptation. The number of iterations adapts to scene motion (4, 8, or 12), and the method is Bayesian-inspired rather than strictly Bayesian, incorporating heuristic modifications to the theoretically correct DP-GMM sampler.

## Results

BIST processes 480×320 frames at over 60 fps and averages ~0.015 s/frame on SegTrackv2, making it over 30× faster than TSP (~2 fps) and over 2× faster than BASS. On standard superpixel benchmarks (SA-2D, SA-3D, UE-2D, UE-3D, EV, Pool dB), BIST achieves state-of-the-art quality comparable to TSP, with only a slightly shorter temporal extent (TEX). The temporally coherent split term (γρs) yields higher-quality superpixels than controlling superpixel count via the merge hyperparameter α alone, as shown at matched superpixel counts of ~1065, ~1180, and ~1300.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TSP [3] (the prior fastest open-source space-time method), BIST is over 30× faster in wall-clock runtime while achieving comparable SA-2D/SA-3D and UE-2D/UE-3D scores; BIST's TEX is slightly shorter, attributed to its smaller, more irregular superpixel shapes. Compared to BASS [32] (its single-image parent), BIST is over 2× faster because it requires only ~8 iterations on average versus BASS's fixed 20, while maintaining similar per-frame superpixel quality. Against Streaming-GBH [38], BIST is orders of magnitude faster (milliseconds vs. tens of seconds per frame). BIST is 15× faster than CCS [12] and ~13× faster than PPM [13] (0.015 s vs. 0.20 s per frame on SegTrackv2). On single-image quality metrics (SA, UE, EV), BIST outperforms SLIC [1], ERS [17], ETPS [39], and SEEDS [33], consistent with its BASS heritage. The ablation in Table 2 confirms that the proposed γρs split term produces better SA-3D and lower UE-3D than the α-only alternative at every matched superpixel count.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIST is a direct space-time extension of BASS [32], which itself applies the DP-GMM deferred-sampling framework of Chang & Fisher [2] to single-image superpixel estimation. It departs from prior space-time superpixel methods (TSP [3], Streaming-GBH [38], TCS [25], CCS [12], PPM [13]) that formulate the problem as minimizing a heuristic energy function; instead, BIST modifies the theoretically correct Hastings ratio of a DP-GMM sampler with a single temporal coherence term (γρ_s) in the split step. Unlike TSP, which jointly refines optical flow via a forward-backward process, BIST consumes an external dense flow (RAFT or SpyNet) and processes strictly forward in time, trading flow accuracy for speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× wall-clock speedup over the next fastest open-source space-time superpixel method (TSP) and 60+ fps throughput make temporally coherent superpixels practical inside real-time deep-learning pipelines, a regime previously infeasible. The temporal coherence hyperparameter γ requires no per-dataset tuning, which lowers the barrier to adoption. A noted limitation is that BIST's irregular superpixel shapes yield a shorter temporal extent (TEX) than TSP, and the method remains 'Bayesian-inspired' rather than rigorously Bayesian due to several heuristic modifications (dropped NIG terms, fixed appearance variance treated as unknown), leaving open whether a fully principled sampler could match or exceed this quality-speed tradeoff.
