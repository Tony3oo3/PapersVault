## Problem

Nuclei instance segmentation in histopathology images remains difficult due to ambiguous boundaries, dense overlap, morphological heterogeneity, and staining variations. Existing encoder-decoder architectures blur critical high-frequency boundary details through repeated downsampling/upsampling, while frequency-based methods focus on global spectral characteristics rather than localized, boundary-specific information. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the spectral blurring and the contextual ambiguity of overlapping instances in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 RGB histopathology patches normalized to [0,1] and produces a per-pixel nuclei instance segmentation mask along with a predicted uncertainty map (S^u ∈ R^{H×W}). Training additionally supervises auxiliary foreground, background, and trilateral-decomposition predictions. The target uncertainty map used for training is derived from ground truth via gradient magnitude, centroid detection, and morphological erosion/dilation to delineate boundary and overlap regions.

## Method

WeaveSeg is a U-Net-variant encoder-decoder whose decoder integrates two synergistic modules in a closed loop. The Adaptive Spectral Detail Refinement (ASR) module learns spatially-varying low-pass filters for intra-nuclei semantic consistency and employs Local Similarity-Guided Resampling (LSGR)—using a cosine-similarity map to predict sub-pixel offsets—for content-aware upsampling that preserves high-frequency boundary cues; a parallel high-pass branch enhances fine details from low-level features, and the two are fused by element-wise addition. The Iterative Contrast-Weaving (ICW) module then performs three refinement iterations, each combining a dense block for feature propagation, a local contrastive attention mechanism that computes softmax weights over a K×K neighborhood using downsampled foreground/background context maps to amplify nuclei-specific features, and an Adaptive Gated Linear Unit (AGLU) with learnable κ and λ parameters for dynamic, non-linear gating. A residual connection feeds each iteration's output back, and the final ICW output is fed back into ASR, creating an ASR↔ICW feedback loop. Training uses a multi-task loss combining BCE for the segmentation mask and auxiliary fg/bg predictions, a trilateral decomposition loss, and a novel uncertainty-aware MSE loss that penalizes over-confident predictions in ambiguous boundary regions.

## Results

On MoNuSeg, WeaveSeg achieves an AJI of 0.648 and PQ of 0.644, setting a new SOTA on AJI (previous best: Mudslide at 0.642). On CoNSeP, it reaches AJI 0.563 and PQ 0.548, outperforming the next-best methods (CellViT-B and Mudslide at AJI 0.547). On CPM17, WeaveSeg attains the highest AJI (0.751) and PQ (0.733), surpassing TopoSeg (0.743/0.705) and CellViT-B (0.737/0.713). Ablation studies confirm that the ICW module contributes more to performance than ASR (Dice drops 0.017 without ICW vs. 0.008 without ASR on CoNSeP), and 3 ICW iterations yield optimal results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeaveSeg achieves the best AJI across all three datasets: 0.648 on MoNuSeg (vs. Mudslide 0.642, CellViT-B 0.639), 0.563 on CoNSeP (vs. CellViT-B and Mudslide at 0.547), and 0.751 on CPM17 (vs. TopoSeg 0.743, Mudslide 0.740). On PQ, WeaveSeg leads on CoNSeP (0.548 vs. Mudslide 0.542) and CPM17 (0.733 vs. NucSeg-B 0.726), but trails CellViT-B on MoNuSeg (0.644 vs. 0.654), indicating that the transformer-based CellViT-B retains a slight edge in panoptic quality on that particular dataset. Compared to the classic U-Net baseline, WeaveSeg improves AJI by 0.051 on MoNuSeg, 0.062 on CoNSeP, and 0.096 on CPM17. Against the strong Hover-Net baseline, gains are 0.030 AJI on MoNuSeg, 0.027 on CoNSeP, and 0.046 on CPM17. The largest relative improvements are seen on CoNSeP, where the dense overlap and small nuclei make boundary delineation most challenging, suggesting the ICW module's contrastive attention is particularly effective in those scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeaveSeg extends the encoder-decoder (U-Net) paradigm for nuclei instance segmentation by introducing two complementary refinement pathways: a frequency-domain module (ASR) that builds on adaptive frequency filter work (FcaNet, Guo et al.) but localizes it to boundary-aware, per-pixel spectral adaptation, and an iterative contrastive module (ICW) that departs from global or pixel-level contrastive strategies used in prior medical segmentation by operating on local neighborhoods guided by foreground/background/uncertainty context maps. The closed-loop ASR-ICW interplay, where ICW outputs feed back into ASR for re-refinement, is a structural departure from the one-pass feature fusion typical of U-Net variants. The uncertainty-aware loss and AGLU gating mechanism are novel additions not present in prior boundary-based, distance-based, or topology-aware nuclei segmentation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nuclei instance segmentation underpins quantitative pathology for cancer diagnosis and prognosis, and WeaveSeg's SOTA results on three standard benchmarks (MoNuSeg, CoNSeP, CPM17) provide a stronger baseline for downstream clinical applications. However, the paper acknowledges that severely clustered nuclei with near-indistinguishable boundaries and cross-staining generalization remain unresolved, and the modest margins over recent competitors (e.g., +0.006 AJI over Mudslide on MoNuSeg) suggest the field is approaching a performance plateau on these datasets. Open questions include whether the iterative weaving strategy generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation or whole-slide image scales, and whether the uncertainty loss can be extended to handle inter-observer annotation variability.
