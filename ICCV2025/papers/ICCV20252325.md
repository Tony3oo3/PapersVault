## Problem

Unified image generation models like OmniGen, which handle diverse editing and generation tasks in a single diffusion framework without separate text encoders, frequently overlook text instructions—especially when a prompt contains multiple sub-instructions. This paper identifies the root cause as significant conflicts in cross-attention maps between the activation regions of neglected sub-instructions and the dominant input image tokens, and proposes a remedy that requires no retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252325/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on OmniGen-v1 at 512×512 resolution with 50 flow-matching denoising steps. For instruction-based editing, the input is an RGB image plus a free-form text instruction (potentially containing 2–4 sub-instructions); for visual conditional generation, the input is a depth map or segmentation map plus a text prompt. SaaS intervenes only during the first 20 denoising steps, after which standard OmniGen sampling resumes, and the output is a single 512×512 edited or generated image.

## Method

SaaS (Self-Adaptive Attention Scaling) is a training-free inference-time technique that exploits two empirical observations: (1) neglected sub-instructions have cross-attention activation regions that conflict with and are suppressed by the input image's activations, and (2) cross-attention maps remain highly consistent across adjacent timesteps. At each denoising step t, the method extracts a spatial mask per sub-instruction by Gaussian-smoothing and summing token-level cross-attention maps (averaged over vital layers and heads), min-max normalizing, and thresholding; it then computes a scaling factor as the ratio of input-image activation to sub-instruction activation within that mask. At step t−1, the cross-attention values for that sub-instruction's tokens are multiplied by the scaling factor within the masked region, effectively boosting under-represented instructions while leaving well-handled ones largely untouched. The entire procedure is applied only to the first 20 of 50 steps and to deeper transformer layers, requiring no gradient updates, additional parameters, or test-time optimization.

## Results

On instruction-based image editing (Table 1), SaaS achieves the best CLIP-T (0.282 single / 0.315 multi) and PickScore (0.462 single / 0.513 multi) among all methods, substantially outperforming OmniGen (PickScore 0.233 / 0.244) while remaining competitive on image-similarity metrics (CLIP-I 0.900 / 0.892 vs. OmniGen's 0.915 / 0.911). On visual conditional image generation (Table 3), SaaS surpasses OmniGen on all four metrics, e.g., PickScore 0.658 vs. 0.342 for depth-map generation and 0.683 vs. 0.317 for segmentation-map generation. A user study with 32 participants confirms a strong preference for SaaS (56.3% single-instruction, 65.2% multi-sub-instruction) over OmniGen (21.8% / 21.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaaS is compared against three baselines: IP2P, MagicBrush, and OmniGen. Against OmniGen (the primary baseline and the model SaaS augments), SaaS wins decisively on instruction-following metrics—CLIP-T improves from 0.256→0.282 (single) and 0.276→0.315 (multi), while PickScore nearly doubles from 0.233→0.462 (single) and 0.244→0.513 (multi)—but slightly trails on image-similarity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-v2), which the authors attribute to OmniGen's instruction neglect making outputs closer to the input. Against IP2P and MagicBrush, SaaS dominates on all four metrics across both editing sub-tasks, with the largest gaps on PickScore (e.g., 0.513 vs. 0.112 for IP2P on multi-sub-instruction). In the visual conditional generation setting (Table 3), SaaS outperforms OmniGen on every metric, with PickScore gains of +0.316 (depth) and +0.366 (segmentation). The user study further confirms SaaS's superiority, receiving 56.3% and 65.2% preference votes versus OmniGen's 21.8% and 21.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OmniGen, a unified image generation model that replaces the traditional LDM pipeline (text encoder + U-Net) with a single VAE + transformer architecture accepting interleaved text and image tokens. It departs from prior instruction-following work on diffusion models (Attend-and-Excite, Prompt-to-Prompt, Focus-on-Your-Instruction) that operated on explicit cross-attention in LDM-based architectures; here, cross-attention is extracted from joint self-attention in a unified sequence, and the diagnosed failure mode—activation conflicts between input image tokens and sub-instruction tokens—is specific to this unified formulation. The method itself (SaaS) is a training-free, test-time-optimization-free inference-time scaling technique that leverages cross-attention consistency across adjacent denoising timesteps, positioning it as a lightweight diagnostic-and-fix contribution rather than a new model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unified image generation models represent a shift toward simpler, more user-friendly architectures, but the newly identified instruction-neglect problem threatens their practical utility for multi-instruction editing. SaaS offers a practical, zero-cost remedy that could be adopted as a default inference-time patch for OmniGen and potentially adapted to other unified models. However, the evaluation is confined to a single model (OmniGen-v1) at 512×512 resolution with 50 denoising steps, leaving open whether the activation-conflict diagnosis and the timestep-consistency assumption hold for other unified architectures, higher resolutions, or longer instruction sequences. A key open question is whether the self-adaptive scaling principle generalizes beyond the specific cross-attention extraction scheme used here.
