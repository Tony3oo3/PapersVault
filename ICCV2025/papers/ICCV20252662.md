## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (Foley) models either extract sound solely from visual cues without user control, or incorporate text conditioning that is too weak to override or supplement the video's implied semantics. When the text prompt intentionally contradicts or extends the visual content—e.g., describing a T-Rex stomp for a scene showing only a shaking glass—current TV2A models fail to produce coherent, temporally aligned audio that follows the textual instruction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second video clip and a free-form text prompt as input, producing stereo audio at 44.1 kHz (up to 47 s with Stable Audio Open, 30 s with TangoFlux; outputs are truncated to 8 s for evaluation). Video is encoded by AV-CLIP over 0.64-second overlapping segments (and optionally CLIP at 5 FPS for semantic context), while text is processed by the backbone's native encoder (T5 for Stable Audio Open, CLAP for TangoFlux). A duration embedding controls the target audio length within the T2A model.

## Method

CAFA freezes a pretrained T2A diffusion backbone (Stable Audio Open or TangoFlux, both DiT-based) and attaches a trainable modality adapter inspired by ControlNet but adapted for the temporal domain and DiT architecture. Video features from AV-CLIP are passed through Zero Fully-Connected layers and injected both into the noisy latent z_t and into the hidden states of every DiT block, while only the adapter parameters are trained (48k steps on VGGSound + 33k fine-tuning on VisualSound on a single A100, ~48 GPU-hours). The key architectural difference from standard ControlNet is the use of zero-initialized FC layers rather than zero convolutions, reflecting the DiT backbone's token-based connectivity. To balance text and video conditioning strength, the paper introduces Asymmetric Classifier-Free Guidance (A-CFG), which scales the adapter contribution in the unconditional path by a factor α (0 ≤ α ≤ 1), effectively amplifying the video signal relative to text during inference without retraining.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set with semantically aligned text-video conditions, CAFA achieves FAD 12.60, IS 13.45, DeSync 0.96 s, and CLAP 0.23, ranking second in temporal alignment and text-audio similarity while maintaining competitive audio quality. Under the more challenging semantically misaligned setting (VGGSound-Sparse), CAFA dramatically outperforms all baselines in text controllability, reaching CLAP 0.21 and binary accuracy 0.87, whereas MMAudio scores only CLAP 0.10 and Acc 0.36. In the human study, CAFA wins 72% of prompt-adherence comparisons against MMAudio under unaligned captions (p < 10⁻³), while losing on quality (28%) and alignment (30%) in the aligned setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMAudio (the strongest baseline), CAFA loses on audio quality (FAD 12.60 vs 6.89; IS 13.45 vs 20.44) and temporal alignment (DeSync 0.96 vs 0.76 s), but matches it on CLAP (0.23 vs 0.25) and substantially wins on text controllability under misaligned conditions (CLAP 0.21 vs 0.10; Acc 0.87 vs 0.36). Against FoleyCrafter, CAFA wins on every metric: FAD 12.60 vs 22.17, DeSync 0.96 vs 1.31, CLAP 0.23 vs 0.18, and in the misaligned setting FAD 27.33 vs 57.00 with DeSync 0.81 vs 1.30. Against ReWaS, CAFA dominates across all dimensions (FAD 12.60 vs 14.71, IS 13.45 vs 8.45, DeSync 0.96 vs 1.18, CLAP 0.23 vs 0.18). Against MultiFoley, CAFA is competitive on CLAP (0.23 vs 0.23) and slightly better on DeSync (0.96 vs 1.04), but trails on FAD (12.60 vs 13.51) and IS (13.45 vs 15.89); the human study shows no statistically significant difference (p = 0.205 for prompt adherence). Against VATT and Frieren (V2A-only), CAFA achieves comparable or better FAD and substantially better CLAP (0.23 vs 0.15 and 0.11), confirming the benefit of text conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAFA extends the ControlNet paradigm (originally for text-to-image) into the audio domain by attaching a trainable modality adapter to a frozen pretrained T2A backbone (Stable Audio Open or TangoFlux), rather than jointly training video, text, and audio representations from scratch as MMAudio and MultiFoley do. It departs from prior TV2A methods like FoleyCrafter and ReWaS, which rely on spectrogram-based generation plus a vocoder or simplified energy/onset features, by operating directly in the latent diffusion space with expressive AV-CLIP video features. The proposed Asymmetric CFG is a targeted modification of standard classifier-free guidance that selectively scales the adapter's contribution in the unconditional path, a mechanism not present in prior TV2A or V2A work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight adapter trained in ~48 A100 GPU-hours can match or exceed much more expensive jointly-trained TV2A models on audio quality and temporal sync while substantially outperforming them on text controllability (0.87 vs. 0.36 accuracy under conflicting prompts). This modular, adapter-based recipe lowers the barrier to building controllable Foley systems and makes it straightforward to swap in new T2A backbones or video encoders. Open questions remain around synthesizing multiple simultaneous audio sources, capturing fine-grained motion cues, and whether the controllability advantage persists on more naturalistic (non-cross-category) text-video mismatches at scale.
