## Problem

Current WSI-level multimodal large language models suffer from two critical limitations: they produce brief answers without morphological reasoning that clinicians can validate, and they map pathology images directly to diagnostic outcomes while overlooking the morphological features that pathologists rely on for accurate diagnosis. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates morphological understanding capabilities in WSI analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251759/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a gigapixel whole slide image (processed as 256×256 pixel patches by a tile-level encoder and as a global sequence by a slide-level encoder) together with a free-form text query. Outputs are structured as morphological findings followed by the final answer, spanning 11 tasks including open-ended and closed-ended VQA, report generation, treatment recommendations, and prognosis. For comparison models with input size constraints, WSIs are resized to 1024×1024 pixels.

## Method

WSI-LLaVA integrates a patch-level encoder (Prov-GigaPath's DINOv2-pretrained tile encoder) and a slide-level encoder (LongNet) to capture both local detail and global context from gigapixel WSIs, bridging the cross-modal gap to text via a three-stage training strategy. Stage 1 performs contrastive alignment between the combined WSI features and Bio_ClinicalBERT text embeddings on WSI-report pairs. Stage 2 adds a projection layer and LLM, freezing all pretrained components and training only the projection layer. Stage 3 freezes the WSI encoder and jointly fine-tunes the projection layer and LLM on all 11 WSI-Bench tasks. The key design choice is training on VQA pairs where answers include detailed morphological descriptions before the diagnostic conclusion, which forces the model to articulate its reasoning and produces explainable outputs.

## Results

On WSI-Bench, WSI-LLaVA achieves the highest average score of 0.754 across morphological analysis, diagnosis, and treatment planning, outperforming Quilt-LLaVA (0.721), WSI-VQA (0.590), and GPT-4o (0.507). On the report generation task, WSI-LLaVA leads all metrics including WSI-Precision (0.380), WSI-Relevance (0.429), BLEU-1 (0.480), and ROUGE-L (0.490). In zero-shot external validation, WSI-LLaVA achieves 0.721 on CPTAC-NSCLC and 0.553 average on SlideBench-VQA(BCNB), surpassing all baselines, with particularly large margins on histological grading (46.3% vs. 30.0% second-best) and molecular subtyping (29.2% vs. 18.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSI-LLaVA outperforms all six baselines on the primary WSI-Bench evaluation. Against Quilt-LLaVA (the closest competitor at 0.721 average), WSI-LLaVA gains the most on open-ended morphological analysis (WSI-Relevance: 0.610 vs. 0.447) and diagnosis (WSI-Precision: 0.610 vs. 0.586), while matching on closed-ended treatment planning (both 1.000). Against WSI-VQA (0.590) and GPT-4o (0.507), the advantage is substantial across every capability, with GPT-4o particularly weak on open-ended morphological tasks (WSI-P: 0.220). In report generation, WSI-LLaVA surpasses MI-Gen (WSI-Precision 0.380 vs. 0.310) and Hist-Gen (0.380 vs. 0.300), and the paper demonstrates that NLU metrics mask clinical errors—Hist-Gen scores nearly identical BLEU-1 (0.531 vs. 0.521) to WSI-LLaVA yet receives a WSI-Precision of only 0.233 due to factual inaccuracies. On external zero-shot validation, WSI-LLaVA beats SlideChat on SlideBench-VQA(BCNB) average (0.553 vs. 0.436) and achieves 0.721 on CPTAC-NSCLC where no other WSI model is reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WSI-level MLLM line of work (WSI-Caption/MI-Gen, HistGen, WSI-VQA, SlideChat) by addressing two gaps those models leave open: explainability and morphological understanding. Architecturally, WSI-LLaVA follows the LLaVA-style three-stage training paradigm (contrastive alignment → projection → instruction tuning) but adapts it to gigapixel WSIs by combining a DINOv2-based tile encoder (from Prov-GigaPath) with a LongNet slide-level encoder, aligned to Bio_ClinicalBERT text features. The key conceptual departure is treating morphological reasoning as a first-class output requirement rather than an implicit intermediate step, and introducing WSI-specific evaluation metrics (WSI-Precision, WSI-Relevance) to replace NLU metrics that the authors demonstrate are misleading in pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WSI-Bench (180k VQA pairs, 9,850 WSIs, 30 cancer types) is the first large-scale morphology-aware WSI benchmark, filling a clear gap where prior benchmarks (WSI-VQA, SlideBench) assessed diagnosis and report generation but never systematically evaluated morphological understanding. The WSI metrics expose a practical problem: standard NLU scores (BLEU, ROUGE) can be nearly identical for two models while their clinical accuracy differs by 3× (Table 5), which has direct implications for how the field evaluates pathology MLLMs. Open questions include whether the morphological-reasoning-first output format genuinely improves downstream clinical decision-making (vs. just sounding more explainable), how well the approach generalizes to non-TCGA imaging protocols and rarer cancer types, and whether the GPT-4o-assisted data construction introduces systematic biases that limit real-world applicability.
