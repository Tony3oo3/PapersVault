## Problem

Diffusion Policies have achieved strong results in robot manipulation via imitation learning, but their deployment on resource-constrained mobile platforms is hindered by two compounding bottlenecks: the iterative denoising process requires many forward passes (e.g., 100 steps for DP-T), and the transformer-based denoiser carries a large parameter count. This paper is the first to systematically address on-device deployment of diffusion policies, targeting real-time action prediction on smartphones and embedded hardware.

## Input / Output

For DP-T, the input is a hybrid of an 84×84 RGB image and a low-dimension robot state, with an observation sequence length of 2; for MDT-V, the input comprises two RGB images from different camera views plus a language instruction as the goal. The output is a single robot action vector produced after iterative denoising. In practice, the trained PyTorch models are converted to Core ML (.mlpackage) format and executed on an iPhone 13 (A15 Bionic, 16-core Apple Neural Engine), where the full pipeline must complete within a few milliseconds for real-time control.

## Method

LightDP accelerates diffusion policies through two jointly applied strategies. First, a learnable layer-pruning mechanism uses SVD-based importance scores to initialize Bernoulli gate variables, which are then jointly optimized with the network weights via Gumbel-Softmax sampling during training (a single-stage approach that avoids the suboptimality of conventional prune-then-retrain pipelines). The pruning follows an N:M local block scheme (from TinyFusion), where groups of M consecutive transformer layers are reduced to N layers, with pruned layers acting as identity mappings during training. Second, consistency distillation trains the pruned student model to satisfy the self-consistency property across noise levels, reducing the required denoising steps from 100 to 4 while preserving action prediction accuracy. The student is initialized from the teacher, a target model is maintained via EMA (decay 0.95→0.999), and the DDIM solver with a skip interval of 10 generates the paired noised actions for the consistency loss.

## Results

On Push-T, LightDP compresses the 8-layer DP-T (90.6 ms, 100 NFE) down to a 2-layer model (0.97 ms, 4 NFE) achieving 0.730 success rate vs. 0.754 for the original — a 93× speedup with only a 2.4-point drop. On CALVIN (ABCD→D), the MDT-V/E3-D3 variant (8.7 ms, 4 NFE) matches the original MDT-V's average rollout length (4.50 vs. 4.52) while cutting latency by 2.6×. On LIBERO, MDT-V/E3-D3 achieves 73.2 average score vs. 74.3 for the full model, with significantly reduced model size (17.50M vs. 22.52M parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DP-T baseline (8 layers, 100 denoising steps, 90.6 ms on iPhone 13), LightDP achieves near-parity success rates across all compression levels: the 6-layer variant matches at 0.752 vs. 0.754, the 4-layer variant is within 0.7 points (0.747), and even the 2-layer variant retains 0.730 — all while reducing inference latency by 19× to 93×. Against the original MDT-V baseline on CALVIN ABCD→D (22.25 ms, avg. chain length 4.52), the MDT-V/E3-D3 compression (8.7 ms) preserves performance almost exactly (4.50 avg. length), while more aggressive E1-D1 compression (3.39 ms) degrades to 3.44. On LIBERO, MDT-V/E3-D3 (73.2 avg.) trails the full MDT-V (74.3 avg.) by only 1.1 points across all four task suites. The paper does not compare against other compression methods (e.g., SnapFusion, BK-SDM, MobileDiffusion) on the same robot manipulation benchmarks, so the competitive positioning is limited to the original unpruned baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightDP extends the diffusion policy lineage (DP-T, MDT-V) by integrating two compression strategies—learnable layer pruning and consistency distillation—into a unified training pipeline, departing from the conventional two-stage prune-then-finetune paradigm. The pruning mechanism borrows the N:M local block scheme from TinyFusion and initializes gate scores via SVD-based importance, while the step reduction leverages consistency distillation from the EDM framework. Unlike prior diffusion model compression work (SnapFusion, BK-SDM, MobileDiffusion) that targeted image generation, this is the first effort to apply such techniques specifically to robot manipulation policies and validate them on mobile hardware (iPhone 13 via Core ML).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that diffusion-based robot policies can achieve real-time inference on consumer mobile hardware (~1 ms per action on Push-T), a practical milestone for deploying learned manipulation on resource-constrained platforms like mobile robots and drones. The MDT-V results, however, reveal that long-horizon language-conditioned tasks are significantly more sensitive to depth reduction (average chain length drops from 4.52 to 3.44 at 1-layer), suggesting that compression strategies may need task-specific tuning. The authors explicitly note that newer VLA architectures (e.g., π₀, OpenVLA) remain unexplored, leaving open whether the same pruning-plus-distillation recipe transfers to larger, multi-modal vision-language-action models.
