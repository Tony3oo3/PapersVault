## Problem

iToF cameras produce noisy depth measurements due to multi-path interference, low SNR, and phase wrapping, and existing data-driven denoising methods rely on simulated training data that limits generalization across different camera systems and frequencies. This paper asks whether neural scene representations, which exploit multi-view consistency to denoise, can be adapted to reconstruct accurate 3D geometry directly from raw multi-view iToF amplitude and phase measurements without any ground-truth supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250549/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view iToF amplitude and phase maps (e.g., 320×240 from a Texas Instruments OPT8241 sensor at 30 MHz, giving a 5 m unambiguous range) along with camera poses. The phase is inherently wrapped to [0, 2π], so depth beyond c/2f is ambiguous; the method must resolve this periodicity during optimization. Output is a continuous SDF-based 3D scene representation from which depth maps and a 3D mesh can be extracted, with no external RGB or GT depth required.

## Method

The core contribution is an SDF-based rendering pipeline tailored to iToF amplitude imaging: instead of standard NeRF volume rendering, the radiance integral incorporates inverse-square geometric attenuation and a neural lighting function F_θ(v_c, E_j) that models direction-dependent infrared source intensity via a learnable per-view encoding. A phase-guided sampling strategy places sample points in narrow windows around each wrapped phase interval θ+2πk, concentrating capacity where the true surface likely lies. Training is fully self-supervised via three key losses: a photometric amplitude loss, a wrapping-aware phase-to-depth loss that subtracts the nearest multiple of the unambiguous range d=c/2f before penalizing depth error, and a noise-weight loss that down-weights rays with large residuals to prevent over-smoothing of locally noisy views. An Eikonal regularization enforces valid SDF gradients, and the network is trained in three staged phases (photometric-only → wrapping-aware → noise-weight) over 20K iterations.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (6 indoor scenes at 60 MHz), the proposed method achieves MAE values ranging from 0.0337 (Bedroom) to 0.0820 (Living-room), with δ₁ scores consistently above 0.977 across all scenes. This represents a 70.3% average MAE reduction compared to TöRF, the strongest iToF-based baseline. The method also outperforms NeuS-RGB (trained on RGB images without iToF data) in most scenes, demonstrating that the iToF-specific rendering and loss design provides advantages over standard RGB-based neural surface reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against TöRF [3], the closest prior iToF-based neural scene representation method. The proposed method outperforms TöRF (iToF-only) by a large margin on all 6 scenes, with MAE reductions ranging from ~52% (Veach-bidir: 0.0393 vs 0.0811) to ~87% (Living-room: 0.0820 vs 0.6170), averaging 70.3% overall. Even TöRF†, which is augmented with additional RGB supervision, is outperformed on 5 of 6 scenes (e.g., Bathroom2: 0.0427 vs 0.1470; Bedroom: 0.0337 vs 0.1400), though TöRF† edges ahead on Veach-ajar (0.2180 vs 0.0428 is actually a win for Ours — correction: Ours wins on all 6). The NeuS-ToF baseline (applying standard NeuS rendering to iToF amplitude) performs poorly (MAE 0.05–2.34), confirming that the proposed SDF-based active-illumination rendering scheme is critical. NeuS-RGB, trained purely on RGB images, achieves moderate results but is surpassed by the proposed method in most scenes, validating the benefit of the iToF-specific phase and amplitude supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scene representation lineage (NeRF → NeuS → TöRF) to the specific domain of iToF imaging, replacing the density-based volume rendering of TöRF with an SDF-based formulation that naturally supports surface extraction. It draws on RawNeRF's insight that multi-view consistency can denoise measurements without explicit denoising modules, but adapts this principle to the unique challenges of iToF data: active infrared illumination (addressed via a neural lighting function), phase wrapping (addressed via a wrapping-aware loss), and the periodic nature of phase measurements (addressed via phase-guided sampling). Unlike data-driven iToF denoising methods that require training on simulated datasets, the approach is entirely self-supervised and camera-agnostic, and unlike TöRF it does not require RGB images as auxiliary supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free pipeline for high-quality 3D reconstruction from commodity iToF cameras, which are widely deployed in robotics, AR, and autonomous driving but produce inherently noisy depth due to MPI, low SNR, and phase wrapping. The 70.3% MAE reduction over TöRF (static mode) and the demonstrated ability to handle wrapped phase and local noise in real-world captures make this directly applicable to existing iToF hardware. Key limitations remain: the method requires calibrated camera poses, struggles with strong specular reflections and complex multi-path interference, and has only been validated on relatively small indoor scenes. Open questions include scalability to larger or outdoor scenes, handling of dynamic objects without RGB, and whether the neural lighting function generalizes across different iToF sensor models and modulation frequencies without retraining.
