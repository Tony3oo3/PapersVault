## Problem

Existing adversarial perturbation methods for object detection are either restricted to CNN-based detectors or perform poorly against transformer-based detectors, leaving no unified attack framework that is both architecture-agnostic and effective across the full spectrum of modern detection models. This gap is critical because transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR, Swin, EVA) now significantly outperform CNN-based ones, yet their vulnerabilities to adversarial perturbations remain underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen victim detector (any architecture: transformer or CNN) and a single RGB image from the test set, standardized to [0,1] pixel values. It produces a pixel-level adversarial perturbation map constrained by a maximum L∞ budget ε (typically 0.031), which when added to the original image yields a visually imperceptible perturbed image that causes the detector to produce erroneous bounding boxes and/or class predictions. The attack completes in just 10 iterations, and the learned attention map (same spatial resolution as the input) is an additional byproduct indicating which pixels were most vulnerable.

## Method

AFOG (Attention-Focused Offensive Gradient) generates adversarial perturbations via iterative projected gradient descent where two components are jointly optimized: a learnable attention map A (initialized to all-ones) and a perturbation map P (initialized uniformly in [-ε, ε]). At each iteration, the perturbed image is constructed as the element-wise product A ⊙ P added to the original image, then projected onto an L∞ ball of radius ε. The attack loss combines a bounding-box feature loss and a class-label feature loss, both computed as the difference between benign and adversarial model outputs; gradients of this loss update A (via a normalized gradient step) and P (via a sign-based gradient step) simultaneously. The key insight is that the attention map dynamically learns which pixels are most susceptible to perturbation, adapting across iterations rather than relying on static priors about foreground, background, or region proposals. Two special variants—AFOG-V (vanishing, targeting objectness) and AFOG-F (fabrication, targeting false positives)—modify the loss formulation to target specific failure modes.

## Results

On COCO 2017, AFOG reduces the benign mAP of all 12 detection transformers by a factor of 2.5× to 37.8×, with adversarial mAP dropping from 42.1 to 4.1 on DETR-R50, from 56.8 to 7.3 on Swin-L, and from 62.1 to 12.2 on EVA (1B+ params). AFOG achieves this with only 10 iterations and a perturbation budget of L∞=0.031, yielding an 82.7% improvement over the next strongest attack on Swin. On PASCAL VOC 2007 CNN-based detectors, AFOG achieves adversarial mAP of 2.38 on Faster R-CNN, 0.50 on SSD-300, and 2.28 on YOLOv3, with SSIM scores of 0.788–0.801 indicating high visual imperceptibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFOG decisively outperforms all 11 compared attacks on both DETR-R50 and Swin-L in Table 2, achieving adversarial mAP of 4.1 and 7.3 respectively, versus the next-best GARSDC (6.0 on DETR) and GHFD (42.3 on Swin). Among surrogate-based attacks (RAD, GHFD, UEA, DAG, RAP, GALD, GARSDC), AFOG reduces mAP by 3–8× more while using fewer iterations (10 vs. 50–3000+) and a smaller perturbation budget (0.031 vs. 0.05–0.063). Among victim-based attacks, AFOG beats EBAD (34.9), AttentionFool (21.0), and OATB (26.6) on DETR-R50, and outperforms DBA (56.7) on Swin by a factor of ~8×. On CNN-based detectors (VOC 2007), AFOG outperforms TOG, UEA, RAP, and DAG on Faster R-CNN (mAP 2.38 vs. 2.64/5.0/4.78/3.56) and SSD-300 (mAP 0.50 vs. TOG's 0.86), but loses to TOG on YOLOv3 (AFOG mAP 2.28 vs. TOG 0.56). AFOG also achieves lower L₂ distortion than TOG on SSD-300 and FRCNN while using the same L∞ budget, indicating better imperceptibility at equivalent attack strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFOG carves new ground at the intersection of adversarial attack methods and transformer attention mechanisms. It departs from surrogate-based attacks (RAD, UEA, GHFD, DAG, RAP) that rely on transferability from a proxy model, and from prior victim-based attacks (EBAD, OATB, DBA, TOG, AttentionFool) that either target only one architecture family or use static region priors. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a learnable, dynamically updated attention map that co-optimizes with the perturbation via projected gradient descent, drawing inspiration from transformer self-attention but repurposing it to identify vulnerable pixels rather than model internal representations. This makes AFOG architecture-agnostic in a way that prior methods (e.g., AttentionFool for transformers only, or CNN-specific surrogate attacks) are not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even billion-parameter detection transformers (EVA, DETA) remain highly vulnerable to visually imperceptible perturbations, with mAP reductions up to 37.8×, raising practical security concerns for deployed detection systems. The worst-case analysis reveals a meaningful limitation: the learnable attention can converge on the wrong regions (e.g., distant background objects), causing the attack to fail, which suggests the mechanism is not yet fully reliable. Open questions include whether the attention-guided perturbation strategy transfers to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection), how to design defenses that specifically counter attention-focused perturbations, and whether the 10-iteration budget is sufficient for all model scales or whether larger models require more iterations.
