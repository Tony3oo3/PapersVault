## Problem

Existing multi-person motion prediction methods are designed for moderate motions (walking, handshaking) where per-person prediction in isolation suffices, but they fail on extreme collaborative motions like Lindy-hop dancing where large cross-person dependencies dominate. The prior work XIA models interactions only in historical poses and predicts future poses independently, neglecting cross-person coupling in the future, and its architecture is difficult to extend beyond two persons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes T historical 3D pose frames per person (18 joints × 3 coordinates each), encoded via DCT into frequency-domain representations before a linear projection to model dimension D=128. It outputs K future pose frames for both persons simultaneously in a non-autoregressive fashion, decoded through a GCN (treating joints as graph nodes) followed by IDCT. A gravity loss constrains the predicted center-of-gravity trajectory to remain physically plausible, with separate learnable joint-weight vectors per person.

## Method

PGformer is a non-autoregressive encoder-decoder Transformer (L=4 layers, D=128) whose core novelty is a Cross-Query Attention (XQA) module where both persons' embeddings serve as queries sharing a single attention map, enabling bidirectional cross-dependency learning in one step rather than two separate attention passes. A proxy unit—built from M=3 learnable template vectors that aggregate spatial semantics from both persons—produces a symmetric D×D matrix P that modulates the shared attention score (A = Q^l · P · [Q^f]^T), subtly recalibrating spatial information flow; the decoder's proxy is predicted from the encoder's via a standard attention operation. The decoder query is learned from the last M input frames and broadcast across all K future steps, and a gravity loss (penalizing frame-to-frame center-of-gravity displacement with learnable joint weights) stabilizes long-term predictions. The architecture generalizes to n>2 persons by concatenating other persons' embeddings as the 'follower' query and iterating with shared parameters.

## Results

On the ExPI common action split (JME, mm), PGformer achieves 53 / 108 / 156 / 230 at 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 / 1.0 s, outperforming the strongest baseline XIA (55 / 112 / 162 / 238) by 3–4% across all horizons. On the unseen action split, PGformer reaches 60 / 169 / 243 (0.2 / 0.6 / 1.0 s) versus XIA's 61 / 172 / 250, confirming generalization to held-out actions. On CMU-Mocap (3 persons), PGformer attains 31 / 108 / 179 / 377 / 591 mm at 0.2–5.0 s, matching TBIFormer at short horizons but leading by 21–35 mm at 3.0–5.0 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGformer outperforms all ten baselines on the ExPI common action split at every prediction horizon. The closest competitor, XIA [13] (the first method to model cross-person interactions on ExPI), is beaten by 2–8 mm (≈3–4%) in average JME, with the gap widening at longer horizons (8 mm at 1.0 s). TBIFormer [24] and HRI [20], both designed for moderate multi-person motions, trail PGformer by 8–11 mm at 0.2 s and 19–21 mm at 1.0 s, confirming that moderate-motion architectures struggle with extreme Lindy-hop dynamics. On the unseen action split, PGformer maintains its lead over XIA (60 vs 61 at 0.2 s; 243 vs 250 at 1.0 s), demonstrating robust generalization. On CMU-Mocap (3 persons), PGformer ties TBIFormer at 0.2 s (31 vs 30 mm) but surpasses it by 14–21 mm at 3.0–5.0 s, and beats MRT [30] by 5–72 mm across all horizons. JRTransformer [32] is competitive at short horizons on CMU-Mocap (178 vs 179 mm at 1.0 s) but does not report long-horizon results, leaving PGformer's 377 mm at 3.0 s unchallenged.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGformer extends the non-autoregressive Transformer decoder architecture from Pose Transformers (POTR) [22] to the multi-person setting, and builds directly on XIA [13], which first introduced cross-interaction attention for extreme multi-person motion prediction on the ExPI dataset. The key departure from XIA is twofold: (1) cross-person interaction is modeled in both the encoder (historical poses) and decoder (future poses) via a shared attention map, rather than only in the encoder with separate per-person attention modules; and (2) a lightweight proxy unit (M=3 learnable template vectors) is introduced to recalibrate spatial dependencies between body joints, a mechanism absent in prior multi-person methods. The method also generalizes the two-person formulation to N persons by treating one person as the query and concatenating all others as the key/value, avoiding the fixed-person-count constraint of prior GCN-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: prior multi-person motion prediction methods (MRT, TBIFormer) were designed for moderate motions and fail on highly interactive extreme actions like Lindy-hop, while XIA only modeled cross-person dependencies in historical poses. PGformer's shared-attention XQA and implicit proxy provide a simple, extensible mechanism that generalizes to arbitrary group sizes without architectural changes, making it a practical baseline for future multi-person interaction modeling. Open questions include whether the proxy concept scales meaningfully to larger groups or more complex interaction topologies, and whether the gravity loss heuristic generalizes beyond the specific ExPI dance domain. The paper also notes that for very long-term prediction (>5s), integration with motion synthesis methods would be needed, but no established benchmarks exist for such comparisons.
