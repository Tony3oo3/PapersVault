## Problem

Existing camera trajectory generation methods are constrained by either geometric optimization, human-centric tracking biases (CCD, E.T.), or the absence of trajectory-level textual captions (Director3D), none of which produce artistically coherent, free-moving camera paths aligned with directorial intent. The paper addresses this by introducing both a large-scale dataset of free-moving cinematic trajectories with rich captions and an auto-regressive generation framework that treats camera motion as a sequential token prediction problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250407/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (either a Motion caption describing camera movement alone, or a Directorial caption describing movement plus scene interaction and intent) and optionally an RGBD image from the first frame at 512×512 resolution. It outputs a fixed-length sequence of N=60 camera poses, each encoded as a 10-dimensional integer vector (quaternion components, normalized translation, focal lengths, and scale) with values in [0, 256], representing a scale-invariant, canonical-normalized trajectory in the camera's local coordinate frame.

## Method

GenDoP tokenizes each camera pose into a 10-element integer vector via canonical normalization (aligning the first pose to identity, relativizing subsequent poses, and projecting translations to unit scale), then trains a 12-layer OPT decoder-only transformer to predict the next pose token autoregressively. Conditioning is achieved by prepending latent codes—extracted from a trainable SD2.1 text encoder and trainable CLIP vision encoders (for RGB and depth, with depth expanded to 3 channels)—before the BOS token in the input sequence. The model is trained with cross-entropy loss plus L2 regularization on the latent code, converging in ~8 hours on a single A100 GPU. The key architectural insight is that the inherently sequential nature of camera trajectories (each pose depends on its predecessor) makes next-token prediction a natural and effective formulation, avoiding the discontinuities common in diffusion-based trajectory generation.

## Results

On the DataDoP test set (3K samples), GenDoP achieves the best performance across all metrics under both Motion and Directorial caption conditions. For Motion captions, GenDoP scores 0.400 F1-Score, 36.179 CLaTr-CLIP, 0.872 Coverage, and 22.714 CLaTr-FID, outperforming the strongest baseline (Director3D trained on DataDoP) by 4.5 in CLaTr-CLIP and 9.3 in CLaTr-FID. In user studies (AUR, 1–5 scale), GenDoP leads with 4.680/4.720/4.753 on Alignment/Quality/Complexity for Motion captions, versus 3.800/3.313/3.520 for Director3D+DataDoP. The RGBD & Text-conditioned variant maintains competitive scores (0.388 F1, 30.231 CLaTr-CLIP, 33.653 CLaTr-FID) while adding geometric grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenDoP outperforms all three baselines (CCD, E.T., and Director3D) across every metric and both caption conditions. Against the strongest baseline, Director3D retrained on DataDoP, GenDoP gains +4.5 CLaTr-CLIP (36.179 vs. 31.689) and reduces CLaTr-FID by 9.3 (22.714 vs. 31.979) under Motion captions, and gains +8.9 CLaTr-CLIP (32.408 vs. 23.505) under Directorial captions. The pre-trained Director3D is particularly weak, scoring 0.000 CLaTr-CLIP (no text-trajectory alignment) and the worst CLaTr-FID (542.385), confirming that object/scene-centric training without trajectory-level captions fails to produce text-aligned free-moving trajectories. CCD and E.T., designed for character tracking, show moderate alignment (CLaTr-CLIP ≈ 5–8) but poor trajectory quality (CLaTr-FID > 94) and low user-study scores (AUR ≈ 2–3), reflecting their structural bias toward character-relative motion. In user studies, GenDoP's AUR scores (4.6–4.8) exceed the next-best method (Director3D+DataDoP at 3.3–3.9) by roughly 0.8–1.4 points on a 5-point scale, indicating a clear perceptual advantage in alignment, smoothness, and motion complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for camera trajectory generation (CCD, E.T., Director3D) by reformulating the task as auto-regressive next-token prediction, drawing on the success of AR transformers in image, video, and 3D generation (VAR, VideoPoet, MeshGPT). It builds directly on E.T.'s motion tagging methodology for the DataDoP construction pipeline, but extends the problem from character-tracking or object/scene-centric trajectories to free-moving, artistically motivated camera paths conditioned on both text and RGBD geometry. The dataset itself is positioned against existing trajectory datasets (MVImgNet, RealEstate10K, DL3DV-10K, CCD, E.T.) by uniquely combining long-shot duration (avg. 14.4s), free-moving motion, and three-level captions (motion, scene interaction, directorial intent).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DataDoP provides the first large-scale dataset specifically targeting artistic, free-moving camera trajectories with directorial-intent captions, filling a gap that tracking-centric and object-centric datasets leave for narrative-driven cinematography. The auto-regressive formulation offers a practical advantage over diffusion for sequential trajectory generation, yielding smoother and more instruction-aligned paths. However, the approach currently relies only on first-frame RGBD (the extracted 4D point cloud remains underexplored), and the pipeline does not yet unify trajectory planning with actual video synthesis, leaving the full 'automated artistic film production' vision as future work. The dataset's demonstrated ability to dramatically improve Director3D's performance (CLaTr-CLIP from 0 to 30+) suggests it will serve as a useful benchmark for the broader camera-control community.
