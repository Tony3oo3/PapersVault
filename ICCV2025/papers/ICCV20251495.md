## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation methods either rely on single-modality foundation models or combine segmentation and multimodal foundation models in an off-the-shelf manner where the models operate independently without meaningful interaction. This decoupled strategy fails to transfer cross-modal alignment knowledge from ImageBind to SAM2's segmentation framework due to their different feature spaces, and segmentation loss alone provides insufficient supervision for learning meaningful audio-visual association.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed audio signal and corresponding video frames (resized to 224×224 or 1024×1024) as input. The audio is processed through ImageBind's audio encoder to produce a trunk feature and cls token, while video frames pass through SAM2's image encoder with audio-guided adaptors at each stage. The output consists of N pixel-wise segmentation masks (one per decomposed sound source) for binary segmentation, and in semantic/zero-shot settings, class labels are predicted via cosine similarity between audio/image embeddings and pre-computed text embeddings rather than a fixed MLP classifier.

## Method

TAViS couples SAM2 (for segmentation) and ImageBind (for cross-modal alignment) through three key components. First, an ImageBind-guided Query Decomposition (IBQD) module uses two-stage multi-head cross-attention to decompose the mixed audio trunk feature into N object-level queries while preserving ImageBind's aligned feature space via additive bias on the cls token. Second, a text-bridged hybrid prompting mechanism generates a sparse prompt by concatenating pseudo-text embeddings (produced by passing audio queries through ImageBind's text encoder) with audio-specific prompts, and a dense prompt by adding ImageBind's image cls token to every pixel location in SAM2's image features. Third, text-bridged alignment supervision introduces two cross-entropy losses (L_a2t and L_i2t) that align audio queries and highlighted image regions to text embeddings via Hungarian matching, using text as an intermediate bridge to extract shared semantic concepts and mitigate intra-class diversity from modality-specific noise. The overall training loss combines these alignment losses with per-object and binary segmentation losses (BCE + IoU), and the similarity-based classification design enables zero-shot generalization to unseen classes.

## Results

On the S4 dataset (224×224), TAViS achieves M_J=84.8 and M_F=0.912, surpassing the previous best COMBO (84.7/0.919) on M_J and SAMA-AVS (83.2/0.901) by 1.6 points. On MS3, TAViS reaches M_J=68.2 and M_F=0.759, outperforming SAMA-AVS (66.9/0.754) and GAVS (63.7/0.774). On the semantic AVSS benchmark, TAViS achieves M_J^I=44.2, exceeding COMBO (42.1) and AVSegFormer (36.7). At 1024×1024 resolution, TAViS further improves to M_J=87.0/0.926 on S4 and M_J=71.2/0.796 on MS3, establishing new state-of-the-art across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAViS outperforms all 14 compared baselines on the primary metrics across all three benchmarks. On S4, TAViS (224) edges out COMBO by 0.1 M_J (84.8 vs 84.7) and surpasses SAMA-AVS by 1.6 M_J; at 1024 resolution the gap widens to 3.8 M_J over SAMA-AVS. On MS3, TAViS leads SAMA-AVS by 1.3 M_J (68.2 vs 66.9) and GAVS by 4.5 M_J, while also achieving the best M_F (0.759) among all methods. On the semantic AVSS benchmark, TAViS achieves 44.2 M_J^I, beating COMBO (42.1) by 2.1 and AVSegFormer (36.7) by 7.5. In the fair SAM-based comparison (Table 5), TAViS with SAM2-L (255G MACs) outperforms SAMA-AVS with SAM2-L (221G MACs) by 1.3 M_J on S4 and 1.9 M_J on MS3, confirming the gains come from the text-bridged design rather than the backbone alone. For zero-shot generalization on AVSBench-OV, TAViS achieves 28.21 M_J^I with only 54.9M trainable parameters, surpassing OV-AVSS (22.20) by 6.0 points while using roughly one-third the parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary foundation models—SAM2 for segmentation and ImageBind for cross-modal alignment—and departs from prior AVS methods that either rely on single-modality foundation knowledge or combine these models in an off-the-shelf, decoupled manner (e.g., [2, 3]). The key conceptual departure is the text-bridged design, which uses pseudo-text embeddings as an intermediate semantic bridge to organically couple ImageBind's alignment space with SAM2's segmentation framework, rather than treating them as independent modules. The IBQD module further extends prior audio-prompting approaches by decomposing mixed audio into object-level queries while preserving ImageBind's aligned feature space, addressing a limitation of earlier SAM-based AVS methods that treat the entire audio feature as a single prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-bridged design offers a generalizable recipe for coupling heterogeneous foundation models whose feature spaces are misaligned, a problem that extends beyond AVS to other multimodal tasks. The finding that direct audio-visual alignment loss actually degrades performance compared to text-mediated alignment challenges the intuitive assumption that closer modality-to-modality coupling is always better. Limitations include reliance on frozen pre-trained encoders (limiting adaptability to domain shifts), evaluation confined to AVSBench-scale datasets, and the zero-shot setting still achieving only moderate absolute accuracy (28.21 M_J^I). Open questions include whether the text-bridged paradigm transfers to other segmentation tasks (e.g., referring expression segmentation) and whether it can be extended to video-level temporal reasoning beyond frame-level processing.
