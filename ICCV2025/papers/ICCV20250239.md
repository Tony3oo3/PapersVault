## Problem

Most robust image watermarking methods embed watermarks via irreversible perturbations, permanently distorting the cover image. While robust reversible watermarking (RRW) aims to recover the cover losslessly, existing RRW schemes rely on complex two-stage designs with high computational cost and large auxiliary bitstreams. Furthermore, flow-based invertible networks used in watermarking operate in real-valued space, so quantizing outputs to discrete pixel values breaks the invertibility needed for perfect cover recovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB cover image (256×256 for the learning-based pipeline; 512×512 grayscale for traditional RRW baselines) and a binary watermark vector (64 bits for learning-based methods, 256 bits for grayscale baselines) and produces a stego image that, in a lossless channel, can be perfectly inverted to recover both the original cover and watermark, while in a lossy channel the watermark is robustly extracted via inverse mapping. The stego image carries a compact auxiliary bitstream (arithmetic-coded latent variable plus under/overflow map, averaging ~1962 bits) embedded via PEE-based reversible data hiding, which is orders of magnitude shorter than the ~57K–62K bits required by existing RRW methods.

## Method

CRMark's core contribution is the Integer Invertible Watermark Network (iIWN), a normalizing-flow architecture of 5 integer discrete coupling layers that performs invertible mappings entirely in the integer domain, eliminating the quantization-induced information loss that plagues real-valued flow-based watermarking. Training follows an encoder–noise-layer–decoder framework: the forward pass maps the cover–watermark pair through iIWN to an overflowed stego image and a latent variable, a differentiable noise layer applies one of six random distortions (JPEG, Gaussian blur/noise, salt-and-pepper, median filter, dropout), and the inverse pass extracts the watermark from the noised stego image. A novel under/overflow penalty loss constrains pixel values toward [0,255] to minimize the auxiliary bitstream, while a latent-variable regularization loss prevents numerical overflow during inference. At inference, the overflowed stego is clipped to [0,255], and the arithmetic-coded overflow map plus latent variable are embedded into the clipped image via PEE-based reversible data hiding, enabling exact cover recovery in lossless channels and robust watermark extraction in lossy channels.

## Results

CRMark achieves a PSNR/SSIM of 40.95/0.9995 on the USC-SIPI and Kodak24 test sets, surpassing STDM (36.54/0.9603), PZM (38.17/0.9859), FIN (39.74/0.9988), and MBRS (40.73/0.9996) in imperceptibility. In terms of robustness, CRMark reaches 98.70% extraction accuracy under Gaussian blur (σ=5.0), 95.53% under salt-and-pepper noise (p=0.7), and 97.52% under median filtering (w=15), while maintaining 99.27% under JPEG compression (Qf=50). The auxiliary bitstream length averages only 1962 bits, a 31.5× reduction over PZM (61,897 bits) and 29.0× over STDM (56,969 bits), and embedding time is 4.51 s versus 248.65 s (PZM) and 184.26 s (STDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two robust reversible watermarking baselines, CRMark dominates on every distortion except JPEG at Qf=50, where STDM and PZM achieve a perfect 100% while CRMark reaches 99.27%. The gap is dramatic on Gaussian blur (CRMark 98.70% vs. STDM 53.76% and PZM 61.92%), salt-and-pepper noise (95.53% vs. 49.48% and 50.09%), and median filtering (97.52% vs. 63.76% and 68.18%), reflecting a peak improvement of ~46 percentage points. Against the irreversible flow-based method FIN, CRMark wins on all six distortions, with the largest margins on Gaussian blur (+50.7 pp) and median filtering (+48.0 pp). Against the CNN-based MBRS, CRMark leads on Gaussian blur (+11.7 pp), salt-and-pepper (+13.0 pp), median filtering (+28.5 pp), and JPEG Qf=50 (+1.6 pp), but trails slightly on Gaussian noise σ=0.25 (88.87% vs. 90.18%) and is essentially tied on dropout (92.35% vs. 96.61%). Crucially, CRMark is the only method that guarantees lossless cover-image recovery for all test images, a capability neither STDM nor PZM can fully achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRMark sits at the intersection of two research threads: integer discrete flows (IDFs) for lossless discrete-data modeling and flow-based robust image watermarking. It directly adopts the integer discrete coupling layer architecture from Hoogeboom et al. (NeurIPS 2019) and the encoder-noise-layer-decoder training paradigm from Fang et al. (AAAI 2023), but departs from both by operating entirely in the integer domain to eliminate the quantization-induced irreversibility that has been a fundamental blocker for applying real-valued normalizing flows to reversible watermarking. Compared to traditional two-stage RRW methods (STDM, PZM) that rely on hand-crafted histogram shifting or template-based embedding, CRMark replaces the first stage with a learned invertible network, while retaining reversible data hiding (PEE) only for the small auxiliary bitstream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing practical limitation: existing RRW methods (PZM, STDM) cannot guarantee lossless recovery for all test images, whereas CRMark achieves perfect recovery across the entire test set. The 55× embedding speedup and 31.5× auxiliary bitstream reduction make the approach far more practical for real-time or high-throughput copyright protection pipelines. Open questions include scalability to higher-resolution images or video, robustness against geometric attacks (cropping, rotation) that the current framework does not address, and whether the integer-domain constraint limits the expressiveness of the watermark embedding compared to real-valued flows. The reliance on FLOAT64 precision during inference for guaranteed losslessness is a practical caveat for deployment on standard hardware.
