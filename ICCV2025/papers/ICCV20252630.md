## Problem

Standard SMPL-based 3D human reconstruction assumes all surface points follow the same Linear Blend Skinning warping as skin, making it unable to model hand-held objects or loose-fitting clothing whose motions are decoupled from the body. Existing work either requires time-consuming case-specific manual rigging or relies on implicit auxiliary bones that prevent explicit, part-specific animation of these complicated regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 24 synchronized multi-view video sequences (100 frames each) along with pre-extracted SMPL skeleton parameters, and produces a canonical-space 3D Gaussian avatar augmented with an extended joint tree stored in an 'extra joint book' (parent indices, canonical positions, per-frame SE(3) rotations). The output supports both novel view synthesis and explicit animation: users can either replay recorded joint transformations or directly override them to customize motion trajectories of grown joints independently of the SMPL body pose.

## Method

ToMiE adaptively grows the SMPL joint tree by first localizing parent joints that need children via gradient accumulation, where each Gaussian's backpropagation gradient is assigned to joints using a hybrid weight combining LBS blending weights and proposed Motion Kernels (MK) that measure relative motion between Gaussians and joints across all frames based on rigid-body distance invariance. This hybrid assignment corrects the misclassification that pure nearest-neighbor LBS produces for hand-held objects in canonical space. Once parent joints are identified by a gradient threshold, child joints are initialized and their positions (time-invariant) and rotations (time-varying) are decoded by two shallow MLPs with positional encoding, forming an explicit extra joint book. A non-rigid deformation network additionally corrects Gaussian rotation and scale during LBS warping to handle non-rigid clothing dynamics. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase with the original SMPL skeleton, followed by adaptive growth and joint optimization with the extended skeleton, using adaptive density control throughout.

## Results

On the DNA-Rendering dataset (complicated scenarios with hand-held objects and loose-fitting clothing), ToMiE achieves the best full-image PSNR of 31.28 dB and SSIM of 0.966, while ranking second only to non-animatable Im4D on LPIPS (0.0374 vs. 0.0308). On the masked evaluation targeting only the complex regions, ToMiE leads all methods with a PSNR of 19.75 dB, outperforming the next-best GauHuman (18.26) by over 1.5 dB. On the ZJU-MoCap tight-fitting clothing benchmark, ToMiE still achieves the highest PSNR (31.10 dB), confirming that the adaptive growth framework does not degrade performance in standard scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToMiE outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and SSIM for the full image on DNA-Rendering, with the largest margin over 3DGS-Avatar (+2.35 dB PSNR) and GART (+2.03 dB PSNR). On the masked evaluation of complex regions, ToMiE leads GauHuman by 1.49 dB and GART by 2.63 dB, confirming that the adaptive joint growth specifically benefits the hardest-to-model parts. The only metric where ToMiE trails is LPIPS, where non-animatable Im4D achieves a lower (better) score of 0.0308 vs. 0.0374, which the authors attribute to Im4D's lack of structural constraints allowing higher visual fidelity at the cost of animatability. On ZJU-MoCap (tight-fitting clothing), ToMiE achieves the best PSNR (31.10) but is slightly behind 3DGS-Avatar on SSIM (0.963 vs. 0.970) and LPIPS (0.0312 vs. 0.0296), indicating comparable but not dominant performance in standard scenarios. Critically, ToMiE is the only method that simultaneously supports explicit animation of both the body and complex garments/objects, whereas GART's implicit auxiliary bones are still coupled to SMPL poses and cannot be independently controlled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMPL + 3DGS pipeline (building on SMPL [36], 3DGS [25], and GauHuman's learnable LBS weights [17]) by introducing an adaptive joint growth mechanism that explicitly adds child joints to the SMPL skeleton for hand-held objects and loose-fitting clothing. Unlike GART [29], which adds implicit global auxiliary bones still controlled by SMPL poses, ToMiE decouples external parts into independently animatable SE(3) joints via an extra joint book decoded by MLPs. The Motion Kernels concept—assigning gaussians to joints based on temporal pairwise distance invariance in the observation space—departs from prior work that relies solely on canonical-space LBS weights or nearest-neighbor search, which the paper shows fails for decoupled objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This addresses a practical gap in 3D human avatar pipelines: SMPL-based methods fundamentally cannot represent hand-held objects or dynamic clothing as independently controllable parts, limiting downstream animation and editing. The explicit joint growth enables part-specific animation (e.g., waving a feather duster while the body stays still), which is infeasible with implicit auxiliary bones. The stated limitation—topology changes like removing clothing or opening a book—remains open, and the method's reliance on multi-view synchronized video (24 training views) limits its applicability to in-the-wild single-video settings. Whether the adaptive growth strategy generalizes to more complex multi-object interactions or longer sequences is an open question.
