## Problem

Generalized few-shot 3D point cloud segmentation (GFS-3DSeg) must segment both base and novel classes with only K support samples per novel class, but existing prototype-based methods suffer from two bottlenecks: novel-class prototypes derived from sparse supports lack representativeness, and the matching stage treats each category independently, ignoring inter-class structural relationships that could disambiguate geometrically similar classes (e.g., table vs. chair).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes query point clouds partitioned into 1 m × 1 m blocks (2,048 points per block, 9-dim features: XYZ, RGB, normalized XYZ) together with K (1 or 5) support point clouds per novel class. Textual knowledge is injected offline: GPT-3.5-turbo generates 24 descriptions per category via four prompt templates, which are encoded by CLIP ViT-B/32 and cached. The output is a per-point semantic label for every point in the query cloud, covering all base and novel classes simultaneously.

## Method

LARM comprises two synergistic modules. The LLM-Assisted Multimodal Fusion (LAM) module first aligns the DGCNN backbone with CLIP text features via a contrastive distillation loss during base-class training, then uses a text-guided meta-feature selector (channel-wise cross-attention with novel text features as queries and base prototypes as keys/values) to transfer relevant base-class meta-features into novel prototypes, and finally fuses text features into both base and novel prototypes through a shared attention module. The Hyper-Relation Matching (HRM) module replaces point-to-prototype cosine similarity with a probabilistic ranking comparison: for each query point, the top-K most similar multimodal prototypes are selected as 'agents,' a Plackett-Luce-style permutation probability distribution over all K! orderings is computed from the point-agent scores, and the final label is the prototype whose own agent-ranking distribution has the highest cosine similarity to the query's distribution, thereby exploiting inter-class structural context to reduce misclassification among confusable categories.

## Results

On S3DIS, LARM achieves mIoU-N of 54.08% (5-shot) and 44.52% (1-shot), outperforming the strongest baseline PE by 3.85% and 4.74% respectively, while also leading on mIoU-B (75.64/75.68) and harmonic mean (63.06/56.04). On the more challenging ScanNet dataset, LARM reaches mIoU-N of 28.61% (5-shot) and 21.50% (1-shot), surpassing PE by 5.27% and 5.93%. The method also demonstrates lower standard deviation across all metrics (e.g., 2.29 vs. 2.76–3.34 for mIoU-N), indicating greater robustness to support-set variability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LARM outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both S3DIS and ScanNet. The strongest competitor, PE (Pseudo-Embedding, ECCV 2024), is surpassed by 3.85% mIoU-N on S3DIS 5-shot and 4.74% on 1-shot, with even larger margins on ScanNet (5.27% and 5.93%). GW (Geometric Words, ICCV 2023), which serves as the architectural baseline, is outperformed by 10.82% mIoU-N on S3DIS 5-shot and 14.86% on 1-shot. CAPL (CVPR 2022) and PIFS (arXiv 2020) trail by 18.9% and 34.4% mIoU-N respectively on S3DIS 5-shot, reflecting the gap between generalized and non-generalized few-shot paradigms. AttMPTI (CVPR 2021), the earliest few-shot 3D segmentation method, is outperformed by a wide margin (38% mIoU-N on S3DIS 5-shot), underscoring the difficulty of extending novel-only methods to the generalized setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GFS-3DSeg formulation introduced by Xu et al. (GW [38]) and extended by Tsai et al. (PE [31]), both of which adopt a prototypical learning paradigm where base-class prototypes are learned from abundant data and novel-class prototypes are derived from sparse support samples. LARM departs from these methods in two key ways: it introduces LLM-generated textual descriptions (via GPT-3) fused with visual prototypes through contrastive distillation and attention-based fusion, and it replaces the standard point-to-prototype cosine matching with a permutation-distribution-based hyper-relation matching strategy inspired by the Plackett-Luce model. The text-guided meta-feature selection module also draws on the meta-feature transfer idea from Wu et al. [36] and Wei et al. [34], but repurposes it with LLM text as the bridging signal between base and novel classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM prior knowledge can meaningfully compensate for the data scarcity that plagues novel-class prototypes in 3D few-shot segmentation, achieving 4–6% mIoU-N gains over the strongest prior methods on both S3DIS and ScanNet. The hyper-relation matching strategy offers a principled alternative to naive nearest-prototype assignment that explicitly models inter-class structure, which could be applicable beyond point clouds. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes with many more categories, whether the offline LLM dependency (GPT-3.5-turbo) is practical for rapidly evolving taxonomies, and whether the permutation-based matching scales efficiently when the number of classes grows substantially beyond the 13–20 tested here.
