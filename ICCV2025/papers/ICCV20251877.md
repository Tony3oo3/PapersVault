## Problem

3D Gaussian inpainting via pretrained 2D diffusion models suffers from inherent multi-view inconsistency because each view is processed independently. Existing remedies—fine-tuning with task-specific conditions, joint-view grid processing, or iterative dataset updates—either fail to generalize beyond narrow scenarios, still produce artifacts in challenging regions, or cause texture fading in the final 3D scene. The paper addresses how to achieve globally consistent, high-fidelity inpainting across many views without modifying the pretrained diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D Gaussian scene with masked regions, its multi-view renderings at 512×512, corresponding SE(3) camera poses, and binary inpainting masks. It iteratively produces inpainted multi-view images (using SD2 with reference-guided cross-attention) and an optimized 3D Gaussian scene, where each iteration alternates between inpainting a small set of perspective-adjacent views and re-optimizing the Gaussians with L1 + D-SSIM loss (excluding masked pixels). Depth maps are estimated on-the-fly via ZoeDepth to enable geometric projection and consistency scoring.

## Method

PAInpainter builds a perspective graph over all views using LoFTR feature-matching confidence as edge weights, capturing view similarity beyond raw 6-DoF camera distance. In each iteration, an anchor view is adaptively sampled from the graph (with spatial separation constraints to avoid local trapping), inpainted by SD2, and its result is projected onto k adjacent views via depth-based back-projection and camera-pose transformation, supplying pixel-level visual priors to the diffusion model. For each adjacent view, SD2 generates four inpainting candidates conditioned on the anchor as a reference image; a dual-feature consistency verifier (cosine similarity of ResNet-18 features on both RGB and ZoeDepth depth maps, weighted η=0.7) selects the most coherent candidate. The selected inpainted images then drive one round of 3D Gaussian optimization, and the cycle repeats, progressively refining both global consistency and local texture fidelity.

## Results

PAInpainter achieves the best performance across all three datasets and all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID). On the NeRFiller dataset, it reaches 29.51 dB PSNR and 0.94 SSIM, surpassing the strongest baseline (GridPrior+DU) by 2.54 dB in PSNR. On SPIn-NeRF, it achieves 26.03 dB PSNR and 0.81 SSIM, and on NeRF Blender, 24.19 dB PSNR with the lowest FID of 101.8. The method also attains the best FID scores on NeRFiller (96.1) and SPIn-NeRF (121.7), indicating superior perceptual quality and distribution alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAInpainter outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against SD2 (independent per-view inpainting), PAInpainter gains 3.77 dB PSNR on NeRF Blender and 9.15 dB on NeRFiller, though SD2's large-scale pretraining gives it a marginally competitive FID on NeRF Blender (102.2 vs. 101.8). Against MVInpainter (fine-tuned reference-guided inpainting), PAInpainter leads by 4.77 dB PSNR on NeRF Blender and 8.38 dB on NeRFiller, confirming that fine-tuning for specific tasks generalizes poorly. Against GridPrior+DU (2×2 grid joint-view inpainting with dataset update), PAInpainter achieves a 2.54 dB PSNR advantage on NeRFiller (29.51 vs. 26.97) and a 25.2-point FID improvement (96.1 vs. 121.9), addressing the texture degradation issue of DU. Against NeRFiller (progressive joint-view inpainting), PAInpainter wins by 7.16 dB PSNR on NeRFiller (29.51 vs. 22.35) and 0.83 dB on SPIn-NeRF (26.03 vs. 25.20), with substantially lower FID scores indicating better perceptual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAInpainter sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for real-time 3D representation and pretrained Stable Diffusion 2 (SD2) for generative inpainting, following the established two-stage paradigm of inpainting multi-view renderings then optimizing the 3D scene. It departs from prior multi-view consistency strategies—fine-tuning-based methods (MVInpainter) that are task-specific, joint-view grid processing (NeRFiller) that still produces artifacts, and Dataset Update (DU) that suffers texture fading—by introducing a perspective graph built via LoFTR feature matching to guide adaptive view sampling, depth-based content propagation from anchor to adjacent views, and a dual-feature (RGB + depth) consistency verification using ResNet-18. Crucially, the method avoids fine-tuning the diffusion model entirely, relying instead on SD2's cross-attention mechanism for reference-guided generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core bottleneck in diffusion-based 3D inpainting—multi-view inconsistency—without modifying the pretrained model, making it broadly applicable across diverse inpainting scenarios (object removal, large missing regions, disjoint holes) as validated on three datasets. The perspective graph + propagation + verification pipeline is a practical, modular design that practitioners can adopt or extend. Open questions remain around sparse-view settings, unbounded outdoor scenes, and whether the components (LoFTR matching, ZoeDepth, ResNet-18 verification) can be integrated end-to-end into the diffusion model for improved efficiency and performance, as the authors themselves note.
