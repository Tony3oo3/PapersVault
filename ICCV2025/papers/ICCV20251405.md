## Problem

Generating 3D facial expressions conditioned on complex situational contexts (e.g., distinguishing 'unexpectedly meeting an old friend' from 'cherishing childhood memories', both labeled 'happy') remains unexplored. Prior MLLM-based emotion work produces textual labels or explanations but does not synthesize actual 3D facial geometry, and no existing dataset jointly annotates images with emotions, situations, and quotes to support such a task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either an RGB image (336×336, processed by CLIP ViT-L/14) paired with a text prompt containing a situation description or a subject quote, or a text-only prompt. It produces a natural-language response (inferred situation and emotion category from a 7-class set) followed by a special <FACE> token whose hidden state is projected to a 100-dimensional FLAME expression coefficient vector; this vector is then rendered into a 3D facial mesh via INFERNO matched to a reference photo. The same architecture supports speculative generation, where the model infers the expression of an occluded or absent person through interactive multi-turn prompting.

## Method

ContextFace builds on LLaVA-llama2-13B with LoRA fine-tuning and introduces a two-layer MLP face projection head (5120→5120→100) that maps the hidden state of a learnable <FACE> token to FLAME expression parameters. To overcome the absence of context-annotated emotion data, the authors use Claude-3.5-Sonnet to augment SFEW and CAER-S with plausible situation descriptions and subject quotes, releasing SFEW-C (1,321 samples) and CAER-S-C (63,134 samples). Training uses visual instruction tuning across three jointly sampled task datasets—Situation Generation (image+quote → situation+emotion), Expression Generation (situation/quote → emotion+<FACE>), and LLaVA-Instruct-150K VQA—with a combined loss of cross-entropy on text and L2 on FLAME coefficients (weighted λ=10). The key architectural insight is that the <FACE> token's contextualized hidden state already encodes the situational reasoning needed to disambiguate subtle expression differences within the same emotion category, requiring only a lightweight projection to recover 3D geometry.

## Results

On the SFEW-C dataset (zero-shot evaluation), ContextFace achieves the highest UAR and WAR across both context types: 90.13% UAR / 90.68% WAR with situation context and 93.21% UAR / 94.89% WAR with quote context. For facial expression prediction, ContextFace reduces L2 error to 0.07 (situation) and 0.10 (quote) versus 0.34/0.31 for random and 0.14/0.12 for mean baselines, and achieves FD scores of 0.81 and 1.95 respectively, far below the mean baselines (14.38 and 11.76). The model also outperforms all baselines on fine-grained emotion categories such as Disgust (85.71% F1 with situation) and Fear (84.31% F1 with situation), where other MLLMs struggle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextFace outperforms all four baselines on both UAR and WAR in both evaluation settings. With situation context, ContextFace leads by 3.25 UAR points over the strongest baseline (LLaVA-NEXT-13B at 86.88) and by 3.60 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (86.08). With quote context, the margin narrows slightly—Qwen2.5-VL-7B is the closest competitor at 90.20 UAR / 91.33 WAR—but ContextFace still wins by 3.01 UAR and 3.56 WAR points. BLIP-13B is dramatically weaker (UAR 51.11 with situation, 26.35 with quote), confirming that general-purpose VLMs without emotion-specific tuning struggle with contextual emotion tasks. Notably, ContextFace achieves the best per-category F1 on Surprise (85.45 with situation, 91.74 with quote) and Disgust (85.71 with situation, 95.65 with quote), categories where baselines show the largest gaps, suggesting the instruction-tuning pipeline specifically strengthens recognition of less frequent emotional states.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextFace extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (LLaVA, LISA, GLaMM, ChatPose) from tasks like segmentation and pose estimation to 3D facial expression generation, repurposing the instruction-response format to map textual contexts to FLAME parameters. It departs from prior MLLM emotion work (Emotion LLaMA, EMER, DialogueLLM) that stops at predicting emotion labels or generating textual explanations, by adding a face projection head that outputs 100-dimensional FLAME expression coefficients. On the 3D face reconstruction side, it builds on FLAME and INFERNO but is the first to condition expression generation on free-form situational contexts rather than speech or single-image inputs, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as 'underexplored.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to close the loop from textual emotional context to a renderable 3D face, which is directly relevant to virtual avatars, interactive assistants, and human-robot interaction where expressions must reflect nuanced situational understanding. The LLM-assisted context augmentation pipeline (using Claude-3.5-Sonnet to generate situations and quotes) offers a practical recipe for creating training data where none exists, and the released SFEW-C and CAER-S-C datasets lower the barrier for follow-up research. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the seven discrete emotion categories, how it handles multi-person or culturally diverse expression norms, and whether the qualitative speculative-generation capability (occluded faces, interactive prompting) can be validated quantitatively at scale.
