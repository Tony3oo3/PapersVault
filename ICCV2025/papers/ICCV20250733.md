## Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement learning for MLLM reasoning suffers from two underexplored failure modes: (1) low data utilization, where difficult samples yield all-incorrect outputs and thus zero advantages, rendering them useless for gradient updates; and (2) text-bias, where the MLLM progressively learns to ignore the image and reason solely from the text query, degrading performance on problems that genuinely require visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text query (e.g., a geometry problem statement) and produces a structured response containing explicit reasoning steps enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags. During training, a partial sequence of correct reasoning steps (the hint) is prepended to the model's generated output, so the model only needs to complete the remaining steps. At test time, the text-bias calibration requires two forward passes per token—one with the image and one without—to compute the calibrated logit.

## Method

Hint-GRPO addresses low data utilization by adaptively injecting partial correct reasoning steps as the beginning of the model's answer (not the query), so the MLLM continues from the hint to produce a full solution. An adaptive hint strategy samples M groups with progressively increasing hint ratios (0, 1/M, …, (M−1)/M) and selects the first group that yields at least one correct output, balancing data utilization against over-hinting. Text-bias calibration, inspired by classifier-free guidance in diffusion models, computes token logits both with and without the image at test time and combines them as π_calibrated(o_t|q_img) = π(o_t|q_img) + γ·(π(o_t|q_img) − π(o_t)), where γ (set to 0.8) amplifies the image-conditioned signal to counteract the learned text-only shortcut.

## Results

On geometry reasoning with Qwen2-VL-7B, Debiased Hint-GRPO achieves an average accuracy of 38.55% across seven datasets, outperforming the original model (30.40%), SFT (25.50%), Mulberry (32.08%), R1-V (33.19%), and vanilla GRPO (33.92%). On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the method reaches 41.95% average, a +9.78 point gain over the original model (32.17%). For universal multimodal reasoning on Llama-3.2-11B-Vision, Debiased Hint-GRPO achieves 65.8% average across six benchmarks, surpassing LLaVA-o1 (63.5%) and the original model (56.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PRM baseline Mulberry, Debiased Hint-GRPO wins by a substantial margin on Qwen2-VL-7B (38.55% vs. 32.08% average), with the largest gap on Math-Vision (14.99% vs. 6.06%) and MM-Math (14.61% vs. 10.69%). Against the two existing GRPO baselines, Debiased Hint-GRPO outperforms R1-V by +5.36 points (38.55% vs. 33.19%) and Open-R1-Multimodal by +6.99 points (38.55% vs. 31.56%) on Qwen2-VL-7B, and by +5.40 and +6.85 points respectively on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. The gains are consistent across all seven geometry datasets, with the most pronounced improvements on harder benchmarks like Math-Vision and MM-Math where vanilla GRPO suffers most from low data utilization. On universal multimodal reasoning, Debiased Hint-GRPO beats LLaVA-o1 (SFT-based PRM) by +2.3 points average (65.8% vs. 63.5%), with the largest gains on MMVet (+3.9) and HallusionBench (+2.9), though the authors note the improvement is less dramatic than in geometry due to accuracy-estimation challenges in tasks like bounding-box localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) paradigm introduced by DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath for LLM reasoning to the multimodal setting, building directly on prior MLLM-GRPO efforts such as R1-V and Open-R1-Multimodal. It departs from the PRM lineage (Mulberry, LLaVA-o1, MSTAR) that supervises intermediate reasoning steps, instead identifying and addressing two specific failure modes of ORM-based GRPO on MLLMs: low data utilization on hard samples and a text-bias where the model learns to ignore the image. The text-bias calibration mechanism is a creative repurposing of classifier-free guidance from diffusion models, applied here to token-level logit calibration at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-bias finding is practically important: it reveals that RL fine-tuning can silently degrade a multimodal model's reliance on visual input, a failure mode that would go undetected without explicit w/o-image ablations. The adaptive hint strategy offers a straightforward, low-overhead fix for the well-known sparse-reward problem in RL training of weak base models. However, the gains on universal (non-geometry) multimodal reasoning are notably smaller, and the authors themselves flag that brittle exact-match accuracy estimation limits GRPO's applicability to tasks like bounding-box localization—suggesting a more robust reward signal remains an open problem for broader MLLM reasoning.
