## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation models rely on raster-order next-token prediction, requiring n² sequential forward passes for an n×n token image, which is far slower than diffusion-based alternatives. Existing acceleration strategies either degrade quality when predicting spatially distant tokens in parallel, require specialized multi-scale tokenizers that lengthen sequences, or demand extensive hyperparameter tuning. No prior method simultaneously preserves spatial/temporal locality, enables parallel decoding, and remains compatible with standard single-scale tokenizers.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 image (ImageNet) or a 16×128×128 video clip (UCF-101); for text-to-image, it takes a text prompt encoded by FLAN-T5 and produces a 512×512 image. Images are tokenized with a VQVAE at downsampling factor 16 (yielding 16×16 tokens for 256×256), and videos with a dedicated tokenizer mapping 16×128×128 clips to 4×16×16 tokens. The key structural difference from vanilla AR is that generation completes in 2n−1 steps for n×n images and 2n+t−2 steps for t×n×n videos, rather than n² or tn² sequential steps.

## Method

NAR reframes autoregressive visual generation as progressive outpainting: starting from a single initial token, all remaining tokens are decoded in ascending order of their Manhattan distance from the start, so that every token is conditioned only on its spatially/temporally closer neighbors. To handle the fact that multiple equidistant tokens exist along different axes at each step, the model employs dimension-oriented decoding heads—one per orthogonal spatial dimension (two for images: row and column; three for videos: time, row, column)—each a small Transformer block plus linear layer that predicts the next token along its specific axis. Training uses a proximity-aware causal attention mask (causal across distance levels, bidirectional within the same level) and standard cross-entropy loss, remaining fully compatible with existing single-scale VQ tokenizers. At inference, all tokens adjacent to the current decoded boundary are generated in parallel in a single forward pass, with overlapping predictions from different heads combined via logit mixing, reducing generation to 2n−1 steps for images and 2n+t−2 for videos.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, NAR-L (372M params) achieves FID 3.06 in 31 forward steps, outperforming LlamaGen-XXL (1.4B params, FID 3.09) while using 87.8% fewer steps and delivering 13.8× higher throughput (195.4 vs 14.1 img/s). On UCF-101 video generation, NAR-LP (694M) achieves FVD 71.1 in 34 steps, reducing generation latency by 97.5% compared to LARP-L-Long (FVD 102, 1280 steps). On GenEval text-to-image, NAR-XL (0.8B, 6M training pairs) scores 0.43 overall, matching SDv1.5 (0.9B, 2B pairs) and surpassing Chameleon (7B, 1.4B pairs, 0.39) while using only 0.4% of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against raster-order AR (LlamaGen), NAR consistently achieves lower FID at every model size while reducing forward steps from 256 to 31 (e.g., NAR-L FID 3.06 vs LlamaGen-XXL FID 3.09 with 4× fewer parameters). Against parallel raster-order AR (PAR-4X), NAR improves FID by 0.44 at the XL scale (3.06 vs 3.50) and by 0.62 at XXL (2.58 vs 3.20), without requiring hyperparameter tuning for parallelism. Against scale-order AR (VAR), NAR-M (290M) beats VAR-d16 (310M) on FID (3.27 vs 3.30) with 1.92× higher throughput, though VAR-d24 (1.0B) still leads on FID (2.09 vs 2.70) due to its stronger 108M tokenizer. On text-to-image (GenEval), NAR-XL (0.8B, 6M data) ties SDv1.5 (0.9B, 2B data) at 0.43 overall and beats Chameleon (7B, 1.4B data) at 0.39, while delivering 4.4× higher throughput than LlamaGen-XL. On video (UCF-101), NAR-LP (694M) achieves FVD 71.1 in 34 steps, outperforming LARP-L-Long (343M, FVD 102, 1280 steps) by 30.9 FVD and 97.3% fewer steps, and beating PAR-XL-4X (792M, FVD 99.5, 323 steps) by 28.4 FVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAR departs from the dominant raster-order next-token prediction paradigm used by LlamaGen and extends the parallel-decoding ideas explored by PAR and SJD, but reframes the problem as progressive outpainting guided by Manhattan distance rather than block-constrained or random-position parallelism. It also contrasts with VAR's next-scale prediction, which requires multi-scale tokenizers and longer sequences; NAR retains standard single-scale tokenizers and shorter sequences while achieving comparable or better quality. The dimension-oriented decoding heads are a new architectural component not present in any prior AR visual generation method, specifically designed to handle the distinct conditional distributions that arise when predicting multiple equidistant tokens in parallel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 91.8% reduction in generation steps for images and 97.3% for video makes autoregressive visual generation substantially more competitive with diffusion-based approaches in wall-clock time, while the data-efficiency result (0.8B model with 6M pairs outperforming Chameleon-7B trained on 1.4B pairs) suggests the near-to-far ordering provides a stronger inductive bias for visual generation. Key limitations include the use of a moderate-size tokenizer (72M, rFID 2.19) for fair comparison, which caps the achievable FID, and video experiments restricted to the small UCF-101 benchmark. Open questions include whether NAR scales to large-scale video datasets, whether combining it with stronger tokenizers (e.g., VAR's 108M tokenizer) closes the remaining FID gap, and whether the paradigm extends naturally to unified multimodal generation.
