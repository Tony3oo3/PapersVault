## Problem

Real-world humanoid egocentric navigation remains unsolved because existing methods either assume static environments, target wheeled/legged robots with vastly different action spaces, or ignore the critical human behavior of active information gathering via head turning. Additionally, no scalable pipeline exists for collecting multi-modal labeled navigation data from humanoid-form-factor agents in the real world.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250479/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a posed egocentric RGB video of T₁=8 frames (downsampled to 224×224) as its sole visual input; the corresponding head poses are used only to unproject features into 3D and are not fed to the network as a separate input. It outputs a sequence of T₂=8 future 6D head poses, each parameterized as [3D translation | 6D continuous rotation], defined in a head-centered canonical frame that is parallel to the ground and aligned with the current heading. At 20 fps, each prediction window covers 4.5 seconds of future motion.

## Method

LookOut extracts per-frame 2D feature maps using a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 encoder, then applies parameter-free unprojection: a fixed voxel grid (96×32×96) in the canonical frame is projected into each frame's pixel space, and 2D DINO features are bilinearly sampled at each projected location to build a 3D feature volume. These volumes are temporally aggregated via average pooling, then projected to a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representation by squeezing the vertical axis with an MLP. A BEV Net of 11 sequential 2D-conv/LayerNorm/MLP modules (hidden dim 384→1540, spatial 96×96→3×3) processes the BEV map, and a final 3-layer MLP with spatial average pooling regresses the future 6D head pose sequence. Training uses combined L1 losses on translation and rotation (via the 6D-to-matrix conversion) with AdamW and a OneCycle scheduler over 700k steps on the 4-hour Aria Navigation Dataset.

## Results

On the held-out test set of the Aria Navigation Dataset, LookOut achieves L1 translation error of 0.17 and L1 rotation error of 0.16, roughly halving the error of the closest learned baseline EgoCast (0.34 / 0.63). For collision avoidance, LookOut attains 97.2% non-collision at 15 cm for dynamic obstacles and 91.3% for static obstacles, approaching ground-truth levels (97.7% / 92.7%). When provided with a goal position, LookOut further improves to L1trans = 0.11 while maintaining strong dynamic collision scores (97.2% at 15 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three types of baselines: simple kinematic extrapolation (Const_Vel, Lin_Ext), the closest learned prior work EgoCast [8] (adapted to predict head poses rather than full-body poses), and a goal-conditioned planner (A*+Lin_Ext). LookOut outperforms EgoCast on every metric, reducing L1 translation error by 50% (0.17 vs. 0.34) and L1 rotation error by 75% (0.16 vs. 0.63), while also improving dynamic collision avoidance by 2.6 points at 15 cm (97.2% vs. 94.6%). The A*+Lin_Ext baseline achieves near-perfect static collision scores (98.8–100%) because it explicitly searches around the SLAM point cloud, but it fails badly on dynamic obstacles (61.9% at 35 cm) since pedestrians and vehicles are absent from the static point cloud. LookOut with a goal input (Ours +goal) further narrows the gap to ground truth on translation (0.11 vs. 0) while maintaining the best dynamic collision scores among all methods. Overall, LookOut is the only method that simultaneously achieves low pose error and strong collision avoidance for both static and dynamic obstacles without requiring a goal or explicit occupancy grid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the parameter-free unprojection strategy from Harley et al. (2019, 2023) and the BEV processing pipeline from Philion & Fidler (2020) with DINOv2 semantic features, repurposing a scene-representation architecture for the novel task of egocentric navigation trajectory prediction. It departs from the closest prior works—EgoCast (full-body pose forecasting) and EgoNav (trajectory prediction from chest-mounted RGBD)—by predicting 6D head poses (translation + rotation) in dynamic environments with pedestrians and vehicles, rather than assuming static scenes. The data collection pipeline using Project Aria glasses also represents a practical departure from prior approaches that rely on teleoperated robots or custom sensor suites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Aria Navigation Dataset (4 hours, 18 locations, 36k clips) fills a clear gap: no prior dataset captures real-world egocentric navigation with both static and dynamic obstacles and 6D head pose annotations. The learned behaviors—waiting, rerouting, looking for traffic—suggest the model captures genuinely useful navigation heuristics, and the near-GT collision scores (97.2% vs 97.7% dynamic at 15 cm) indicate strong generalization. Key limitations remain: the non-generative formulation struggles with multi-modal futures (e.g., choosing left vs. right around a pedestrian), and the training set lacks certain obstacle types (rails, stairs). Future work on generative (diffusion-based) prediction and broader environmental diversity would address these gaps.
