## Problem

Existing 3D scene tokenizers produce compact representations but cannot model temporal dynamics, while 4D tokenizers embed motion in the token space at the cost of prohibitive computational overhead for downstream autoregressive or diffusion models. This paper tackles the joint optimization of tokenization efficiency and dynamic fidelity for 4D occupancy forecasting in autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a current 3D occupancy volume (H×W×Z at 0.4 m voxel resolution over a 80 m × 80 m × 6.4 m spatial range) plus G historical frames (2 s at 2 Hz) and ego-motion actions (speed, trajectory commands). It autoregressively produces K future occupancy frames (3 s) at the same voxel resolution, along with a predicted 4×4 rigid transformation matrix per timestep that can be externally overridden for fine-grained spatial-temporal control. A camera-based variant (I²-World-STC) replaces ground-truth occupancy with STCOcc predictions, operating at 4.21 FPS end-to-end.

## Method

The I²-Scene Tokenizer decouples compression into an Intra-Scene component (S-scale hierarchical residual quantization with a single shared 512-entry, 128-dim codebook, inspired by RQ-VAE and VAR) and an Inter-Scene component (G-step temporal residual quantization over ego-pose-aligned historical features), yielding tokens with 3D-tokenizer compactness but 4D-tokenizer expressiveness. The I²-Former is an encoder-decoder (not decoder-only GPT-style) architecture: the Intra-Encoder uses deformable spatial self-attention and multi-head cross-attention with a plan embedding across L=3 multi-scale layers, fuses features via FPN, and regresses a 4×4 transformation matrix; the Inter-Decoder conditions on this matrix and a memory queue of historical tokens, using SSA plus a lightweight channel-concatenation-and-MLP temporal fusion to predict the next latent token. Training is two-stage: first a VAE objective (focal + Lovász + VQ loss) trains the tokenizer, then the frozen tokenizer feeds the I²-Former, which is supervised by weighted MSE on latent tokens plus L2/cosine losses on the transformation matrix's translation and quaternion components.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nus validation set, I²-World-O (occupancy input) achieves 39.73% mIoU and 49.80% IoU (averaged over 1s/2s/3s forecasting), outperforming the previous best method UniScene (31.76% mIoU) by 25.1% and DOME (36.36% IoU) by 36.9%. The method requires only 2.9 GB of training memory and runs at 37.04 FPS on an RTX 4090, far exceeding DOME's 6.54 FPS. In the end-to-end camera-input setting (I²-World-STC), the method reaches 18.97% mIoU and 28.77% IoU, surpassing DOME-STC (14.53% mIoU) by 30.3% and OccWorld-STC (7.53% mIoU) by 152%. Zero-shot transfer to Occ3D-Waymo at 10 Hz yields 43.73% mIoU and 60.97% IoU, substantially above the copy-paste baseline (28.34% / 40.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

I²-World-O dominates all six occupancy-input baselines on both mIoU and IoU across every forecasting horizon (1s, 2s, 3s). The closest competitor, UniScene [18], achieves 31.76% mIoU but trails I²-World-O by 7.97 points (25.1% relative); DOME [11] leads in IoU among baselines at 36.36% yet is surpassed by 13.44 points (36.9% relative). Among LLM-based methods, Occ-LLM [49] (20.99% mIoU) and OccLLaMA-O [46] (19.93% mIoU) are outperformed by roughly 19–20 mIoU points, while the non-autoregressive DFIT-OccWorld [55] (22.71% mIoU) and the original OccWorld [59] (17.14% mIoU) lag even further. In the camera-input (STC) setting, I²-World-STC (18.97% mIoU, 28.77% IoU) beats DOME-STC (14.53% / 23.33%) by 4.44 mIoU and 5.44 IoU points, and OccWorld-STC (7.53% / 17.14%) by a wide 11.44 mIoU margin, while also running faster (4.21 vs. 2.75 FPS). Notably, I²-World-O achieves all gains with only 2.9 GB training memory and 37.04 FPS inference, compared to DOME's 6.54 FPS, making it the only method in the table that is both the most accurate and the fastest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of 3D scene tokenizers (OccWorld, RenderWorld) and 4D scene tokenizers (DOME, OccSora), arguing that both extremes are suboptimal: 3D tokenizers lack temporal expressiveness while 4D tokenizers impose prohibitive computational cost. It draws the multi-scale residual quantization design from RQ-VAE and VAR for the Intra-Scene Tokenizer, and adopts the BEV encoder-decoder backbone from OccWorld. The key departure is the decoupling of spatial and temporal tokenization into separate, sequentially applied stages, and the replacement of decoder-only GPT-style autoregression with an encoder-decoder architecture conditioned on a predicted transformation matrix rather than trajectory-based guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-real-time 4D occupancy forecasting (37 FPS, 2.9 GB memory) at a level of accuracy that surpasses heavier 4D-tokenizer-based methods, making occupancy world models viable for latency-critical autonomous driving deployment. The transformation-matrix conditioning offers a principled controllable-generation interface that could support corner-case simulation and auto-labeling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unseen ego-motion distributions (the paper acknowledges a failure case with reversing), whether the discrete codebook bottleneck limits further accuracy gains, and whether the two-stage training pipeline can be unified for end-to-end optimization.
