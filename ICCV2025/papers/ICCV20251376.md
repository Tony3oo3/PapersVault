## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods extract pseudo-samples from ID data to regularize decision boundaries, but the unpredictable quality of these samples and the need for large ID data volumes leave the model without adequate knowledge of OOD distributions. As a result, detectors fail to learn compact decision boundaries that reliably separate known from unknown categories, leading to high-confidence misclassifications on unseen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes detected region images cropped from raw RGB frames (C×H×W) along with region-level text prompts that encode location coordinates and category labels. It outputs object detection boxes with a binary ID/OOD classification per region, where all OOD categories are collapsed into a single 'background' class. Notably, only ID images come from the dataset; ID prompts, OOD prompts, and OOD images are all virtually synthesized during training.

## Method

APLGOS comprises a Prompt Learning Module and a Text-Image Alignment Module. The PLM uses ChatGPT to iteratively standardize predefined Q&A pairs (with location templates) into a diverse statements set, from which prompts are sampled to initialize learnable ID prompts containing location tokens and description tokens. For OOD, virtual prompts and images are synthesized by sampling in the low-likelihood tail of class-conditional Gaussian distributions fitted over ID prompt/image embeddings in hidden space, with a learnable noise term to expand the sampling region. The TAM computes cosine similarity between normalized image and prompt embeddings and applies a contrastive alignment loss across three training phases (ID alignment, OOD alignment, and joint optimization), combined with a location regression loss that implicitly grounds prompts to spatial coordinates.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC (ID) with RegNetX4.0, APLGOS achieves FPR95 of 45.96% (MS-COCO2017) and 47.10% (OpenImages), AUROC of 89.19% and 88.49%, AUPR of 99.00% and 98.30%, and mAP of 49.4%. On BDD-100k (ID) with RegNetX4.0, FPR95 drops to 39.48% (MS-COCO2017) and 19.79% (OpenImages), with AUROC of 87.47% and 93.59%, AUPR of 99.79% and 99.94%, and mAP of 37.6%. These results represent state-of-the-art performance, reducing FPR95 by up to 7.76% over the closest baseline (VOS) on BDD-100k with OpenImages as OOD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APLGOS outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric across both ID datasets. The closest competitor is VOS [6] (virtual outlier synthesis in hidden space); APLGOS reduces FPR95 by 4.57% on PASCAL VOC/MS-COCO2017 and by 7.76% on BDD-100k/OpenImages (both with RegNetX4.0), while also improving AUROC by 1.09–1.48 points and mAP by 0.3–0.6 points. Traditional score-based methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy score, Mahalanobis, Gram matrices) are substantially worse, with FPR95 values 10–50 points higher than APLGOS. GAN-synthesis [18] and CSI [36] also trail APLGOS by 10–20 FPR95 points. On BDD-100k, Dynamic Prototypes [38] achieves FPR95 of 45.72/35.05, which APLGOS (RegNetX4.0) improves to 39.48/19.79, a 6.24/15.26 point reduction, while also matching its mAP (37.6 vs. 31.5). Notably, APLGOS achieves these gains while using a 1:1 ID-to-OOD data ratio, whereas VOS uses 2:1, indicating better data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

APLGOS extends the virtual outlier synthesis paradigm of VOS (Du et al., ICLR 2022) by relocating the synthesis from pixel or hidden feature space into the prompt embedding space, where class-conditional Gaussian distributions are fitted and low-likelihood regions are sampled to generate OOD pseudo-prompts. It also adapts the CoOp-style learnable prompt framework (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) to the OOD detection setting—a combination the authors note has not been explored before—while introducing region-level location tokens and ChatGPT-standardized Q&A templates to produce more diverse and spatially grounded prompts. The method departs from prior extraction-based OOD pseudo-sample approaches (outlier exposure, GAN synthesis) by making all non-image training signals (ID prompts, OOD prompts, OOD images) fully virtual and synthesized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that prompt-space Gaussian outlier synthesis can regularize decision boundaries more effectively than pixel-space or feature-space synthesis, achieving SOTA FPR95 and AUROC on four standard detection benchmarks with a 1:1 ID:OOD data ratio (versus 2:1 for the VOS baseline). However, the reliance on ChatGPT for prompt standardization introduces an external dependency and reproducibility concern, and the Gaussian assumption in 1024-dimensional embedding space is validated only on relatively small-scale detection datasets (20–91 ID classes). Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or truly open-world settings with hundreds of ID classes, whether the tied-covariance approximation remains valid at larger scale, and whether the ChatGPT standardization step can be replaced by a learned or rule-based alternative.
