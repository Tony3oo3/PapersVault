## Problem

Vision-language contrastive models like CLIP and SigLIP learn global representations via instance-wise contrastive learning, which treats all different instances as negatives regardless of semantic similarity and causes OCR pairs to overshadow object-centric understanding. Existing cluster discrimination methods assign pseudo-labels to whole images, limiting their ability to capture localized region-level semantics needed for dense prediction tasks such as grounding, OCR, and segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (minimum 336px edge length in training data) and produces both a global CLS embedding from standard ViT layers and fixed-length region class embeddings from Region Transformer layers. Regions are adaptively sampled to a fixed count N per image to enable batched processing, and a region-specific visibility mask handles variable spatial sizes within the same batch. The final output is a (L, B, D) tensor of region embeddings alongside the global embedding, all in a shared space aligned to 1M cluster centers.

## Method

RICE integrates standard Vision Transformer layers for global semantics with novel Region Transformer layers that apply a region-specific visibility mask (0 inside, −∞ outside) to restrict attention to tokens within each designated region, enabling parallel processing of variable-sized regions in a single forward pass. Supervision comes from a unified region cluster discrimination loss: an Object Region Loss (single-label, one positive center per region) and an OCR Region Loss (multi-label, multiple positive token embeddings per region), both using margin-based classification with L2-normalized features and a negative sampling ratio ρ=0.1. The training data comprises 400M images with 2B SAM-generated object regions (labeled via k-means into 1M centers) and 50M images with 400M PaddleOCR-extracted text regions (labeled via tokenization), totaling 13B samples trained with batch size 32K on 64 GPUs.

## Results

As a vision encoder in LLaVA-NeXT with Qwen2.5-7B, RICE (ViT-L/14-336px) achieves an OCR average of 56.18 and a general vision average of 73.03, surpassing CLIP-336px by +3.91 and +3.20 respectively, and outperforming SigLIPv2-384px (OCR Avg 55.98, Other Avg 71.70) at a comparable resolution. On detection (Cascade Mask R-CNN, ViT-B/16-512px), RICE attains 38.9% COCO Det AP and 26.5% LVIS Det AP, exceeding SigLIP by +3.9% and +4.7% respectively. For referring segmentation in LLaVA-NeXT, RICE achieves 83.5/85.3/81.7 IoU on refCOCO val/testA/testB, improving over CLIP by +1.7/+1.3/+2.6 and over MLCD by +0.7/+0.7/+1.5. On video tracking (OSTrack), RICE leads all baselines on LaSOT (60.24 success), TrackingNet (76.30 success), and GOT-10k (55.40 AO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RICE consistently outperforms CLIP across all evaluated tasks: on MLLM benchmarks it gains +3.91 OCR Avg and +3.20 Other Avg at 336px, and on detection it improves COCO Det AP from 35.0% to 38.9%. Against SigLIPv2 (the strongest vision-language baseline), RICE-378px wins on OCR Avg (+2.04) and DocVQA (+3.45) but trails slightly on general vision (Other Avg −0.14 at 560px); at 336px RICE leads on both OCR and general vision. RICE surpasses MLCD (the closest cluster-discrimination competitor) on nearly all metrics: +2.61 OCR Avg, +0.72 Other Avg at 336px, and +1.30 average IoU on referring segmentation. On detection and tracking, RICE dominates DINOv2 by large margins (e.g., +7.3% COCO Det AP, +5.13 LaSOT success) and SigLIP by +3.9% COCO Det AP. Among referring segmentation baselines (GLaMM, VisionLLMv2, LLaVA-G, GSVA, PixelLM, OMG-LLaVA, Sa2VA), RICE with LISA achieves the highest refCOCO val IoU (83.5%) in the LLaVA-NeXT setting, exceeding the previous best (Sa2VA 82.5%) by 1.0 points. The one area where RICE does not lead is at 560px resolution against Qwen2.5-ViT's specialized backbone, which achieves higher OCRBench (66.2 vs 60.7) and Other Avg (75.50 vs 73.46), though RICE still surpasses it on DocVQA (87.38 vs 85.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cluster discrimination paradigm from UNICOM (single-label) and MLCD (multi-label) from the image level to the region level, unifying both formulations in a single training framework. It departs from prior region representation methods (RegionCLIP, CLIM) that require descriptive text annotations for regions, instead using k-means cluster centers and OCR token embeddings as supervision signals. The Region Transformer layer with its region-specific visibility mask is a new architectural component that enables fixed-length region embeddings in a single forward pass, building on standard ViT attention while constraining it spatially. The negative sampling strategy is inherited directly from Partial FC, and the data curation pipeline leverages SAM and PaddleOCR as off-the-shelf tools for generating region proposals and text labels at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely felt limitation: global visual encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2) produce features that are insufficient for dense prediction tasks like segmentation, grounding, and OCR, which are increasingly critical for MLLM applications. The unified object+OCR cluster discrimination loss is a practical contribution that avoids the need for region-level text annotations, making the approach scalable to billion-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the reliance on SAM and PaddleOCR for data curation introduces systematic biases that propagate into the learned representations, whether the region attention mechanism is optimal compared to alternatives like multi-scale pooling or cross-attention with region queries, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D settings. The compute cost (13B samples, 64 GPUs) also limits immediate reproducibility for smaller groups.
