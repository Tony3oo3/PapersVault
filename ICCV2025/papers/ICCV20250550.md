## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video depth estimation methods produce only affine-invariant depth maps because compressing unbounded depth values into a VAE's fixed input range causes significant information loss, especially in distant regions. This scale-and-shift ambiguity, combined with the absence of camera intrinsics, prevents accurate 3D reconstruction and other metrically grounded downstream tasks. Meanwhile, image-based geometry estimators that do recover camera parameters suffer from temporal flickering when naively applied to video sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video of arbitrary length (processed via stitching inference for sequences beyond the trained window of up to 110 frames) along with per-frame geometry priors from MoGe, which are encoded into latent space via the point map VAE to provide camera-intrinsic cues. It outputs a temporally consistent point map sequence in each frame's camera coordinate system (up-to-scale, normalized by a shared factor across frames) plus a valid mask excluding undefined regions like sky. The decoupled representation (log-space depth + diagonal FoV) enables generalization to varying resolutions and aspect ratios despite training at a fixed resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is a point map VAE with a dual-encoder architecture: the native SVD encoder captures normalized disparity (primary depth cues), while a newly designed residual encoder embeds remaining geometric information as a latent offset applied only to the mean, preserving the original variance. The point map is decoupled into log-space depth and diagonal field of view—both shift-invariant quantities—rather than the cuboid representation used by prior work, making the latent space compatible with the translation-invariant video VAE. An identity loss explicitly penalizes deviation of the combined latent from the original SVD distribution, enabling direct reuse of pretrained video diffusion priors. A diffusion UNet (EDM pre-conditioning, multi-stage training on 14 synthetic RGBD datasets totaling 1.85M frames) is then conditioned on both video latents and per-frame MoGe geometry priors to generate point map latents, with a deterministic single-step variant also provided for faster inference.

## Results

On point map estimation (Table 1), GeometryCrafter (diffusion variant, Ours(G)) achieves an average rank of 1.9 across seven datasets, with Rel^p of 8.52% and δ^p of 94.3% on GMU Kitchen, 20.5%/75.5% on Monkaa, and 25.6%/64.9% on Sintel. The deterministic variant (Ours(D)) achieves a near-identical average rank of 2.0 while running 1.1× faster (3.7 vs 4.1 FPS at 448×768). On depth map estimation (Table 2), Ours(D) attains the best average rank of 1.9, with Rel^d of 8.28% on GMU Kitchen, 12.0% on Monkaa, and 5.60% on KITTI, outperforming the strongest depth-only baseline DAV (avg. rank 3.1) by a substantial margin on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeometryCrafter outperforms all point-map baselines (DUSt3R, MonST3R, UniDepth, DepthPro, MoGe) on the majority of seven evaluation datasets, achieving the best average rank of 1.9. The largest gains appear on the challenging dynamic synthetic datasets: on Monkaa, Ours(G) reduces Rel^p from 23.0% (UniDepth) to 20.5% and raises δ^p from 65.6% to 75.5%; on Sintel, Rel^p drops from 30.9% (UniDepth) to 25.6% and δ^p rises from 50.6% to 64.9%. UniDepth remains the strongest single competitor, winning on KITTI (Rel^p 4.24% vs. 6.34%) likely due to training on DrivingStereo with a shared LiDAR sensor, and on ScanNet where it benefits from in-domain training. MoGe is competitive on DIODE (Rel^p 13.5% vs. 13.1%) but lags substantially on video datasets due to flickering. In the depth-map comparison (Table 2), Ours(D) beats the best depth-only method DAV by 2.5–13.7 points in Rel^d across datasets (e.g., 12.0% vs. 19.0% on Monkaa, 16.3% vs. 35.7% on Sintel), and surpasses DepthCrafter and ChronoDepth by even wider margins, confirming that the point-map VAE's handling of unbounded depth values eliminates the distant-region errors that plague affine-invariant depth methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based video depth estimation (DepthCrafter, DAV, ChronoDepth) and monocular point map estimation for static images (DUSt3R, MoGe, UniDepth). It departs from the former by replacing the native VAE's bounded disparity encoding with a purpose-built point map VAE that handles unbounded 3D coordinates, and from the latter by extending point map estimation to temporally coherent video sequences via a video diffusion prior. The dual-encoder design is conceptually inspired by LayerDiffuse's steganographic embedding of auxiliary information into a pretrained latent space, adapted here to preserve the SVD latent distribution while encoding geometric residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing metrically grounded (up-to-scale) point maps with temporal consistency from open-world videos, this work unlocks downstream tasks—3D/4D reconstruction, camera pose estimation, and depth-conditioned video generation—that were previously limited by the scale-and-shift ambiguity of affine-invariant depth. The point map VAE with its decoupled log-depth/diagonal-FoV representation is a reusable component for any diffusion-based pipeline needing to encode unbounded geometric quantities. The main limitation acknowledged is the high computational and memory overhead of the large diffusion model, and the approach remains up-to-scale rather than fully metric, leaving open the question of absolute scale recovery without additional sensors or priors.
