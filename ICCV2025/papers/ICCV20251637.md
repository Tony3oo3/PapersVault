## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generation methods produce meshes in a partial-to-complete fashion, so any intermediate stopping point yields an incomplete mesh with missing faces rather than a valid, coarser mesh. This means a single generation run offers only one fixed level of detail, with no mechanism to obtain a valid mesh at arbitrary resolutions. The paper asks whether mesh generation can be reformulated so that every intermediate step produces a geometrically valid mesh with progressively increasing detail.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes no input and autoregressively emits a token sequence that first encodes a coarse base mesh M_0 (averaging ~18 faces, only 5.68% of total sequence length) followed by a sequence of vertex-split records that progressively refine it to the full mesh (~457 faces on average). For shape-conditioned generation, 4,096 sampled points with normals from a dense reference mesh are encoded by a pre-trained point-cloud encoder and projected into the token embedding space as a prefix. The output is a valid 3D triangle mesh at any halting point: stopping after k vertex splits yields a valid mesh M_k with k fewer faces than the final output, enabling continuous level-of-detail control without post-processing.

## Method

VertexRegen reframes mesh generation as the reversal of Hoppe's progressive mesh edge-collapse operations, learning to generate vertex splits autoregressively. The key technical contribution is a token-efficient parameterization: each vertex split is uniquely specified by the target vertex v_s, two ring neighbors v_l and v_r, and the new vertex position v_t (12 tokens, or 10 for boundary edges), with the half-edge data structure resolving the ambiguity of which half of the vertex ring belongs to v_s versus v_t. The coarse base mesh M_0 is tokenized in the MeshXL style (9 tokens per face), while the full sequence is trained with standard next-token prediction on an OPT-350M backbone. A geometry-guided decoding state machine enforces validity constraints (v_s must exist in the current mesh, (v_s,v_l) and (v_s,v_r) must be valid edges, at most one of v_l/v_r can be nil) and performs vertex splits on the fly, preventing invalid splits from breaking the generation chain. This yields a compression ratio of 0.73 relative to MeshXL (theoretical limit 0.67) while producing meshes of comparable quality to state-of-the-art methods.

## Results

On unconditional mesh generation (Objaverse-XL subset, ~18k meshes), VertexRegen achieves COV 51.03%, MMD 8.29×10⁻³, 1-NNA 50.22%, and JSD 2.89, matching or approaching the best baseline on each individual metric while delivering the unique capability of continuous level-of-detail generation. Under a 400-face constraint, VertexRegen substantially outperforms MeshXL with face-count conditioning (FCC), achieving COV 50.92% vs. 41.20%, MMD 8.31 vs. 10.03, and JSD 2.88 vs. 5.19, demonstrating that its coarse-to-fine strategy captures overall structure far better at low face budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VertexRegen is compared against three autoregressive mesh generation baselines: MeshXL, MeshAnything V2, and EdgeRunner. On the full unconditional generation benchmark, VertexRegen achieves the best JSD (2.89 vs. 3.22 for EdgeRunner and 3.81 for MeshXL), indicating the closest distributional match to the reference set, while remaining within ~0.7% COV of MeshXL (51.03 vs. 51.76) and within ~0.5×10⁻³ MMD of EdgeRunner (8.29 vs. 7.81). No single baseline dominates all four metrics, and VertexRegen is competitive across the board. The decisive advantage emerges under face-count constraints: at 400 faces, VertexRegen's COV (50.92%) exceeds MeshXL+FCC (41.20%) by nearly 10 points, and its JSD (2.88) is less than half that of MeshXL+FCC (5.19), confirming that the progressive coarse-to-fine strategy preserves global shape far better than truncating a partial-to-complete sequence. EdgeRunner, despite its more efficient EdgeBreaker tokenization, follows the same partial-to-complete paradigm and thus does not benefit from early stopping, showing a similar degradation trend to MeshXL under face constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

VertexRegen reframes autoregressive mesh generation by building on Hoppe's progressive mesh formulation (1996), where a mesh is represented as a coarse base plus a sequence of vertex split records that reverse edge collapse operations. For the coarse mesh M_0, it adopts MeshXL's 9-token-per-face coordinate discretization, while the vertex split subsequences are parameterized using a half-edge data structure to resolve neighbor-assignment ambiguity with only 12 tokens per split. This departs fundamentally from the partial-to-complete paradigm of prior autoregressive mesh generators (MeshGPT, MeshXL, MeshAnything, EdgeRunner), where intermediate generation states are incomplete meshes with missing faces, by ensuring every intermediate state is a valid mesh at a coarser level of detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime-generation property—halting at any step to obtain a valid mesh at a chosen level of detail—addresses a practical gap in current mesh generation pipelines where users must commit to a full generation to get any usable output. This is particularly relevant for interactive applications, streaming, and adaptive rendering where LOD control is essential. Limitations include a compression ratio (0.73) that is notably worse than adjacency-aware schemes like MeshAnything V2 (0.46) and EdgeRunner (0.47), evaluation restricted to unconditional generation and shape-conditioned examples without quantitative comparison, and a face-count ceiling of ~8,000 that limits applicability to high-poly assets. Open questions include whether the progressive mesh formulation scales to much higher face counts, how it interacts with texture/material generation, and whether the coarse-to-fine inductive bias helps or hurts at very high resolutions.
