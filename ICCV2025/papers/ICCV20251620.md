## Problem

Traditional multi-modal models rely on separate encoders and decoders per modality, preventing unified joint representation learning and slowing inference. Existing end-to-end alternatives (autoregressive, hybrid, or mixed diffusion-AR) still suffer from quadratic Transformer complexity for high-resolution images and long text, and from conflicting multi-objective optimization goals that impede convergence. No prior architecture unifies multi-modal encoding and decoding under a single objective while scaling linearly with sequence length.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts (tokenized via SentencePiece Unigram BPE), RGB images (split into patches via patchify), and text questions, all of which are mapped into a shared noisy latent space by a VAE encoder. A key distinction is that MDM generates images and text simultaneously in a single forward pass—for example, producing a 256×256 image alongside its caption—rather than treating generation as separate sequential tasks. Three learnable padding tokens (time, class, pad) are inserted into the noise sequence to encode diffusion step, data category, and sequence boundaries.

## Method

MDM replaces the standard Markov-chain denoising objective with a Score Entropy Loss (SE) that directly learns the probability density ratio between noisy and clean states, providing a single unified training signal for both image and text modalities. The decoder is a multi-step selection diffusion model built on Mamba-2: image and text scan switches (4 and 2 directional sequences, respectively, following DiM) create multiple temporal orderings of patches/tokens, and the Mamba selective state-space structure then selects which elements to focus on at each denoising step by approximating the score ratio s_θ ≈ p_data(y)/p_data(z_{n,t}). Denoising uses a second-order DPM-Solver update (Eq. 9) for fast sampling, and the entire pipeline achieves O(MLN²) complexity versus O(ML²N/G) for Transformer-based end-to-end models like MonoFormer. The VAE serves as both encoder (mapping inputs to a noisy latent space) and decoder (reconstructing images via L2 loss and text via cross-entropy), with KL divergence regularizing the latent space.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 image generation, MDM (7B) achieves FID 2.49, IS 281.4, Precision 0.86, and Recall 0.59, ranking second among all models on three of four metrics and outperforming all end-to-end multi-modal baselines. On COCO text-to-image, MDM achieves FID 5.91 and Gen Eval 0.68, matching the SOTA Gen Eval score of SD-3 and Show-O while substantially outperforming other end-to-end models (e.g., Chameleon FID 26.74, Transfusion FID 6.78). On image captioning (CIDEr), InstructMDM scores 122.1 on COCO and 75.2 on Flickr30K, and on VQA it reaches 66.7% on VQAv2, 46.3% on VizWiz, and 51.6% on OKVQA. In text comprehension and reasoning, InstructMDM surpasses MonoFormer and Chameleon on 7 of 10 datasets but remains below GPT-4V and Gemini Pro on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet, MDM (FID 2.49) outperforms all end-to-end multi-modal baselines: MonoFormer (FID 2.57), LlamaGen (FID 2.81), and Chameleon (no ImageNet result), while trailing only the single-modality DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) by 0.22 FID. On COCO text-to-image, MDM's FID of 5.91 beats Transfusion (6.78), NExT-GPT (10.07), Chameleon (26.74), and Show-O (9.24), though it is surpassed by LlamaGen (4.19) and SDXL (4.40); MDM ties SD-3 and Show-O at Gen Eval 0.68. In image captioning, InstructMDM (CIDEr 122.1 COCO, 75.2 Flickr) outperforms MonoFormer and Chameleon but falls short of NExT-GPT (124.9 COCO) and mPLUG-Owl (119.3 COCO). On VQA, InstructMDM (VQAv2 66.7%, VizWiz 46.3%, OKVQA 51.6%) surpasses InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl, DREAMLLM, and Emu, but remains well below GPT-4V (77.2% VQAv2) and JanusFlow (79.8% VQAv2). In text comprehension/reasoning, InstructMDM beats MonoFormer on all 10 datasets and Chameleon on 7 of 10, yet lags behind Mistral-7B and GPT-4V by 5–20 points on most benchmarks, reflecting the cost of abandoning dedicated language encoders in the end-to-end design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDM sits at the intersection of three research threads: Mamba-2's selective state-space model for linear-time sequence processing, DiM's scan-switch mechanism for multi-directional image patch scanning in diffusion, and the Score Entropy Loss for discrete diffusion via probability-ratio estimation. It departs from prior end-to-end multi-modal models (MonoFormer, Transfusion, Chameleon, LlamaGen) by replacing the Transformer backbone with Mamba-2 and unifying the training objective under a single Score Entropy Loss rather than separate autoregressive and diffusion losses. The VAE serves as both encoder and decoder, eliminating the modality-specific en/decoder separation that characterizes traditional LMMs like Flamingo and LLaVA, though the overall pipeline (patchify → latent → denoise → decode) follows the latent diffusion paradigm established by LDM and DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-in-sequence-length complexity (O(MLN²) vs. O(ML²N/G) for MonoFormer) is a genuine practical advantage for long-sequence and high-resolution generation, and the simultaneous image+text output in a single forward pass is architecturally clean. However, the empirical results are mixed: MDM (7B) trails smaller dedicated diffusion models (DiT-XL/2 at 675M) on ImageNet FID, underperforms SDXL and LlamaGen on COCO text-to-image, and lags significantly behind GPT-4V and JanusFlow on VQA. The paper acknowledges that end-to-end models suffer 'deviations in multimodal fusion' relative to traditional multi-encoder architectures, and the gap on text comprehension tasks (e.g., HellaSwag 70.6 vs. Mistral's 81.3) suggests the unified objective may not yet fully capture language modeling quality. Open questions include whether the selection mechanism scales to higher resolutions (beyond 256×256), whether the Score Entropy Loss advantage over standard DDPM objectives holds at larger scale, and whether the Mamba-based denoising can match Transformer-based diffusion at equivalent parameter budgets.
