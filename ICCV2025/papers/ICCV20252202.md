## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP and EVA-CLIP suffer from a pronounced 'foreground bias' in dense prediction: background regions are systematically misclassified as co-occurring foreground object classes because these models are trained on global image-text pairs that overemphasize foreground semantics. Existing self-distillation approaches (e.g., CLIPSelf, MaskEmbed) improve local alignment but remain constrained by the teacher model's own bias and do not explicitly separate foreground from background semantics during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images resized to 512×512 pixels and a set of category text prompts (316 categories from COCO annotations, or up to 794 generated by NXTP), with no region or mask annotations required. Dense visual features are extracted by modifying the final residual attention block of a ViT (removing SoftMax and adding mapping layers), then partitioned into a random m×n grid (m,n ∈ {2,…,6}) and pooled via RoIAlign to produce region-level representations. The output is a set of region-category alignment scores that explicitly distinguish foreground (Thing) from background (Stuff) classes, which can directly replace the original VLM in downstream detection and segmentation pipelines.

## Method

DenseVLM is an end-to-end framework with two components: a frozen powerful VLM (P-VLM, ViT-L/14) that retrieves the most relevant category for each unlabeled region via cosine similarity over a broad category set, and a trainable unbiased VLM (U-VLM, ViT-B/16) that learns region-language alignment through a decoupled contrastive strategy. The key mechanism is asymmetric contrastive decoupling: background (Stuff) regions are contrasted against both Thing and Stuff text embeddings, while foreground (Thing) regions are contrasted only against Thing embeddings, preventing foreground categories from dominating the background representation. A region denoising step discards any region whose P-VLM matching probability falls below a threshold (θ=0.3) to reduce misalignment from partially-covered objects. Training is performed via KL-divergence between the P-VLM and U-VLM probability distributions over categories, with only the U-VLM image encoder updated using pre-extracted text embeddings, avoiding costly image cropping and repeated feature extraction.

## Results

On the COCO dense prediction benchmark, DenseVLM achieves 72.3% Top-1 box classification accuracy, 70.1% Top-1 thing-mask accuracy, and 44.9% Top-1 stuff-mask accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior method CLIPSelf (69.1%, 66.7%, 41.7%) by 3.2, 3.4, and 3.2 points respectively. On ADE20K (zero-shot), DenseVLM reaches 51.0% Top-1 box accuracy and 57.0% Top-1 stuff-mask accuracy, again leading all baselines. In downstream open-vocabulary object detection, replacing the frozen VLM with DenseVLM in F-ViT yields a +12.5 AP₅₀ gain on OV-COCO (47.4 vs. 34.9) and +6.0 mAP on OV-LVIS (21.4 vs. 15.4). For open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, DenseVLM plugged into CAT-Seg achieves 34.1 mIoU on ADE-150, surpassing the prior best (CAT-Seg+FineCLIP at 32.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseVLM outperforms all 19 baselines on the primary dense-representation benchmark (Table 1). Against the closest competitor CLIPSelf [57], DenseVLM gains +3.2/+3.4/+3.2 Top-1 on COCO Boxes/Mask-T/Mask-S and +2.9/+1.8/+3.3 on ADE20K, with the largest relative improvement on stuff (background) masks, directly addressing the foreground bias. RegionCLIP [69], trained on region-text pairs, shows strong COCO box accuracy (68.5) but collapses on stuff masks (22.0 Top-1) and fails to generalize to ADE20K (34.0 Mask-T vs. DenseVLM's 49.3). FineCLIP [23] and EVA-CLIP [53] trail DenseVLM by 7.6 and 28.0 points respectively on COCO box Top-1. In downstream segmentation, SAN+DenseVLM beats SAN [62] by +2.1 mIoU on ADE-150 and +2.6 on PC-459, while CAT-Seg+DenseVLM surpasses CAT-Seg [5] by +2.7 mIoU on ADE-150 and also edges out CAT-Seg+FineCLIP [23] (34.1 vs. 32.4). For detection, F-ViT+DenseVLM [27] achieves 47.4 AP₅₀ on OV-COCO, beating RO-ViT [25] (41.5 with ViT-B/16) and BARON-Cap [56] (49.1 with RN50, but DenseVLM uses a lighter ViT-B/16 backbone), and 21.4 mAP on OV-LVIS, substantially exceeding F-VLM [27] (24.2 with RN50) and VLDet [37] (30.1 with RN50) when accounting for backbone size. The only baseline that matches or exceeds DenseVLM on a single metric is PACL [48] (31.4 mIoU on ADE-150 vs. SAN+DenseVLM's 29.5), though CAT-Seg+DenseVLM (34.1) still surpasses it.

## Relation to Prior Work

DenseVLM extends the self-distillation line of work initiated by CLIPSelf [57] and the dense feature extraction approach from [72], but departs fundamentally from the self-distillation paradigm by using a separate, more powerful frozen VLM (P-VLM) as a category retrieval oracle rather than relying on the student model's own representations. It also moves beyond region-text contrastive learning methods like RegionCLIP [69] that require expensive annotations, and addresses a specific failure mode—foreground bias—that prior self-distillation methods (CLIPSelf, FineCLIP) inadvertently exacerbate. The decoupled alignment strategy, which structurally separates Thing and Stuff category sets during contrastive training, is the key conceptual departure from prior work that treats all categories uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and mitigating foreground bias through a clean architectural intervention (decoupled foreground/background alignment), DenseVLM provides a practical drop-in replacement for VLMs in open-vocabulary detection and segmentation pipelines, with consistent gains across ViT and CNN backbones. The scalability results on SA-1B (100K → 5.5M samples) showing continued improvement where CLIPSelf plateaus suggest the approach benefits from larger unlabeled data, which is promising for real-world deployment. However, the method still depends on a powerful frozen VLM (ViT-L/14) as the retrieval oracle, and the binary Thing/Stuff categorization limits its applicability to more nuanced scene structures. Open questions include whether the decoupling strategy generalizes beyond the binary foreground/background split and whether the retrieval oracle can be replaced by lighter models without significant performance loss.
