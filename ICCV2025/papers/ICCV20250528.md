## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation methods primarily enhance interaction efficiency but overlook two fundamental issues: modality representation imbalances (visual features dominate while sparse audio is marginalized) and the lack of semantics in audio leading to erroneous matches in complex multi-source scenes. Current approaches design problem-specific functional components that risk capturing spurious statistical correlations rather than true causal associations between sound and its visual source.

## Input / Output

The method takes video frames (resized to 224×224) and audio waveforms (converted to Mel-spectrograms via STFT) as input, processed by ResNet-50/PVT-v2 and VGGish backbones respectively. During training, it additionally generates multi-granularity implicit text from both modalities using VideoCLIP, CLIP, and CLAP, and produces counterfactual text via a latent diffusion model. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask (binary for AVS-Object, class-conditional for AVSS) decoded by a Multi-Scale Deformable Attention Transformer; all text and counterfactual components are training-only and not required at inference.

## Method

The Implicit Counterfactual Framework (ICF) comprises three training-time modules. First, Multi-granularity Implicit Text (MIT) retrieves best-matching textual representations at video, segment, and frame levels via foundation models, then adaptively fuses them with learned weight queries to form a composite factual text. Second, Semantic Counterfactual (SC) conditions on visual features, adds Gaussian noise to the factual text in a latent diffusion forward process, orthogonalizes partial representations in the noise space via Gram-Schmidt with per-sample controllable ratios (α·m, α·s), and denoises to generate diverse counterfactual text samples that progressively diverge from the factual. Third, Collaborative Distribution-aware Contrastive Learning (CDCL) models T-frame modality features as Gaussian distributions, measures inter-distribution distance via Wasserstein distance augmented with continuous information entropy, and constructs tri-modal (visual↔audio, visual↔text, audio↔text) contrastive losses with soft sample partitioning based on audio-text anchors and weighted counterfactual negatives.

## Results

On the PVT-v2 backbone, ICF achieves J&F of 90.07 (S4), 69.89 (M3), and 48.16 (AVSS), outperforming the second-best method COMBO by 1.77%, 4.69%, and 4.06% respectively. On the ResNet-50 backbone, the method reaches 85.90 (S4), 66.93 (M3), and 39.00 (AVSS), surpassing CPM by 1.95% on M3 and 1.95% on AVSS. The gains are most pronounced on the multi-source M3 and semantic AVSS benchmarks, where complex audio-visual interactions demand stronger cross-modal alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICF outperforms all 15 baselines on the PVT-v2 backbone across all three datasets. The largest margin is over COMBO (CVPR 24), the strongest prior method, by 4.69% J&F on M3 and 4.06% on AVSS, while the gap on S4 is smaller (1.77%) due to simpler single-source audio. Against SEIM (ACM MM 24), ICF gains 3.06% on M3 and 4.06% on AVSS. Compared to DiffusionAVS (arXiv 23), which also uses latent diffusion but conditions on audio rather than text, ICF improves by an average of 4.82% J&F, attributed to better decoupling and distribution-aware contrast. On the ResNet-50 backbone, ICF edges out CPM (ECCV 24) by 1.53% on M3 and 1.95% on AVSS, while matching CPM on S4 (85.90 vs. 85.92). The method also surpasses AVSegFormer (AAAI 24) by 4.06% on M3 and 8.83% on AVSS, and outperforms the original AVSBench (ECCV 22) baseline by 10.82% on M3 and 15.66% on AVSS, demonstrating substantial gains over earlier work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual segmentation (AVS) task introduced by Zhou et al. (ECCV 2022) by introducing a causal-inference perspective that prior AVS methods largely overlooked. It builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) for generating counterfactual text samples in a continuous latent space, and on textual inversion (Gal et al., 2022) for retrieving implicit text representations from visual and audio features via foundation models (VideoCLIP, CLAP). Conceptually, it departs from prior counterfactual learning in VQA and segmentation, which typically rely on explicit attribute modification or generative model outputs, by operating entirely in an implicit feature space with Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. It also shifts contrastive learning from the feature level to the distribution level (Gaussian parameters + Wasserstein distance), distinguishing it from prior AVS contrastive approaches like CAVP and CPM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ICF framework's plug-and-play nature—demonstrated by consistent 3–4% J&F gains when integrated into AVSBench and AVSegFormer (Table 3)—makes it a practical module for the broader AVS community without requiring architectural redesign. The causal-inference framing (counterfactual 'if not... then...') offers a principled lens for addressing the well-known visual-dominance bias in cross-modal tasks, potentially transferable to other audio-visual or multi-modal settings. However, the O(n²) Gram-Schmidt complexity, the large number of manually tuned hyperparameters (α, m, s, k^c, s^d, r^a, r^v), and the reliance on multiple foundation models (VideoCLIP, CLAP, CLIP, Latent Diffusion) limit scalability and ease of adoption. Open questions include whether automated parameter selection (e.g., MoE) and lower-complexity orthogonalization (e.g., SVD) can preserve the gains, and whether the counterfactual strategy generalizes beyond the three evaluated datasets.
