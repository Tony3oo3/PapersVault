## Problem

Event-based monocular depth estimation is constrained by the scarcity and high cost of annotated event data, limiting generalization to real-world scenes. While the RGB-based depth foundation model DAM (Depth Anything V2) achieves strong zero-shot depth estimation, its direct application to sparse event data fails due to the fundamental density gap between dense RGB images and asynchronous event streams. The paper addresses how to transfer DAM's knowledge to an event-based model in an annotation-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only sparse event data as input, converted into a 3-channel frame-like representation (252×504 pixels) by aggregating positive and negative event counts over 5 temporal bins spanning 50 ms. Paired RGB images (same spatial resolution) are used exclusively during training to provide teacher features and auxiliary supervision. The output is a dense pixel-wise depth map, normalized per-sample via affine-invariant scaling (median translation and mean absolute deviation).

## Method

EventDAM fine-tunes DAM V2's ViT encoder (S/B/L) with LoRA (rank 4) while freezing the depth decoder, using three sparsity-aware components to bridge the dense-to-sparse gap. (1) Sparsity-aware Feature Mixture (SFM) computes a patch-level sparsity metric M_i = 1 − N_i/(P×P), selects event features from patches below the median sparsity, mixes them with image features from sparser regions, and feeds the mixed features through the decoder to produce an auxiliary depth prediction that supervises the student via scale-invariant log and gradient-matching losses. (2) Sparsity-aware Feature Distillation (SFD) applies a sparsity-weighted cosine similarity loss, (1−M_i)(1−cos(f_i^evt, f_i^img)), to prioritize feature alignment in denser regions. (3) Sparsity-invariant Consistency Module (SCM) randomly extracts a single temporal bin to create a sparser augmented event frame, then enforces feature and depth consistency between the original and augmented inputs. The total loss is L_SFM + 2·L_SFD + 0.2·L_SCM, requiring no ground-truth depth annotations.

## Results

On EventScape, EventDAM-L (event-only input) achieves absolute mean depth errors of 0.56 m / 1.52 m / 2.30 m at 10 m / 20 m / 30 m, outperforming the best E+I baseline SRFNet (1.27 / 1.68 / 2.76) by up to 16.7% at 30 m. In the zero-shot setting on DENSE, EventDAM-L reduces the 30 m error to 3.451 m versus 6.116 m for SRFNet and 19.113 m for RAMNet. On MVSEC Night1 (zero-shot), EventDAM-S with event input (3.36 m at 30 m) surpasses DAM-S with event input (4.25 m) but remains slightly behind DAM-S with image input (3.06 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventDAM (event-only) decisively outperforms the event-only baseline E2Depth [16] on EventScape (0.56 vs. 1.79 m at 10 m) and on MVSEC Night1 (1.39 vs. 3.38 m at 10 m). Against the four E+I baselines (RAMNet [11], HMNet [14], ER-F2D [6], SRFNet [30]), EventDAM-L wins on all three distance metrics on EventScape (2.30 vs. 2.76 m best at 30 m) and on DENSE zero-shot (3.451 vs. 6.116 m at 30 m), despite using only event data. On MVSEC, EventDAM does not uniformly beat all E+I methods (e.g., SRFNet achieves 0.96 m at 10 m on Day1 vs. 1.12 m for EventDAM-L), which the authors attribute to suboptimal DAM predictions on grayscale images in certain scenes. In the zero-shot MVSEC Night1 comparison against DAM-S [41], EventDAM-S with event input (3.36 m at 30 m) improves over DAM-S with event input (4.25 m) but trails DAM-S with image input (3.06 m), confirming that the distillation preserves DAM's zero-shot capability while adapting to the sparser event modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal knowledge distillation paradigm to the event-camera domain, building directly on Depth Anything V2 (DAM) as a frozen teacher and adapting it via LoRA fine-tuning of the ViT encoder. It is conceptually inspired by EventSAM and OpenESS, which repurposed SAM and CLIP for event-based segmentation, but departs from prior event-based depth methods (E2Depth, RAMNet, ER-F2D, SRFNet) by eliminating the need for ground-truth depth annotations entirely. The key methodological departure is the explicit modeling of the density gap between dense RGB and sparse event data through three sparsity-aware components (SFM, SFD, SCM), which prior distillation work for event data has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on annotated event depth data, EventDAM addresses a fundamental bottleneck that has limited the generalization of event-based depth estimation to real-world scenes. The zero-shot transfer to DENSE (3.45m vs. 19.11m for RAMNet at 30m) demonstrates that foundation-model distillation can substantially outperform task-specific supervised methods on unseen datasets. However, the approach inherits DAM's limitations (e.g., degraded performance on grayscale/night scenes in MVSEC) and struggles with event-free regions, leaving open the question of how to handle truly sparse or static scenes where event data carries minimal information. Future work could explore whether the sparsity-aware distillation framework generalizes to other event-based tasks beyond depth estimation.
