## Problem

Existing 3D parametric curve reconstruction methods follow a two-stage pipeline (3D edge point cloud reconstruction followed by parametric curve fitting), which accumulates errors between disconnected optimization stages and produces redundant, fragmented curves. The fundamental challenge is that parametric curves inherently lack differentiable rendering capability, preventing direct one-stage optimization from multi-view edge maps while preserving geometric continuity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view single-channel edge maps (produced by detectors such as PiDiNet or DexiNed) along with known camera poses as input, and outputs a set of 3D parametric curves represented as cubic Bezier curves (4 control points each) and first-order Bezier line segments (2 endpoints each), each with an opacity and thickness parameter. Each curve is internally coupled to N=12 edge-oriented Gaussian primitives whose positions, orientations, and scales are analytically derived from the curve's control points, so the optimization variables are only the curve parameters rather than free Gaussian attributes.

## Method

CurveGaussian establishes a bi-directional coupling between parametric Bezier curves and edge-oriented 3D Gaussians: each curve uniformly samples N=12 Gaussians whose positions lie on the curve, whose principal axes align with the curve tangent, and whose scales encode local arc-length and a shared thickness parameter. Differentiable rendering is achieved by splatting these curve-bound Gaussians (a modified 3DGS pipeline without color, using alpha-weighted values for single-channel edge maps), and rendering losses are backpropagated to update only the curve control points. A dynamic topology optimization framework adaptively refines curve structure during training through four operations: linearizing near-straight cubic Beziers into line segments, merging collinear or geometrically compatible adjacent curves, splitting curves at points of geometric abrupt change or low mask occupancy (via de Casteljau's algorithm), and pruning curves whose opacity or mask values fall below thresholds. The training objective combines an edge-aware rendering loss that adaptively balances edge and non-edge pixel contributions, with regularization terms for endpoint connectivity, curve smoothness, opacity conciseness, and Gaussian masking.

## Results

On ABC-NEF with DexiNed edge detection, CurveGaussian achieves 8.2 mm accuracy, 7.5 mm completeness, 69.6% recall@5mm, 79.0% precision@5mm, and 73.7% F-score@5mm, outperforming all learning-based baselines across every metric. In terms of efficiency and compactness, the method trains in 3 minutes (vs. 1.5–2.5 hours for NeRF-based methods) and produces only 28.9 average curves/lines per object, a 70.5% reduction compared to EdgeGaussians (97.9). On MV2Cyl real-world objects, CurveGaussian achieves 73.1% F10 with just 70.5 curves versus EdgeGaussians' 56.7% F10 with 1952.5 curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurveGaussian outperforms all three learning-based two-stage baselines (NEF, EMAP, EdgeGaussians) on every metric in Table 1. Against the closest competitor EdgeGaussians (DexiNed), the proposed method improves F5 from 45.2% to 73.7% (+28.5 pp), P5 from 49.1% to 79.0% (+29.9 pp), and accuracy from 9.6 mm to 8.2 mm, while also reducing the average curve count from 97.9 to 28.9. Against EMAP (DexiNed), gains are F5 59.1%→73.7% and F10 88.9%→94.0%. Against NEF (DexiNed), improvements are dramatic (F5 10.8%→73.7%), reflecting NEF's severe edge omissions from its two-stage fitting. Compared to the SfM-based LIMAP (SOLD2), CurveGaussian trades a small precision drop (P5: 79.0% vs. 88.1%) for substantially better completeness (Comp: 7.5 mm vs. 29.6 mm) and recall (R5: 69.6% vs. 64.2%), since LIMAP cannot represent curves and omits many edges. On real-world MV2Cyl objects, CurveGaussian achieves 73.1% F10 with 70.5 curves versus EdgeGaussians' 56.7% F10 with 1952.5 curves, demonstrating both superior accuracy and a 27× reduction in curve count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for 3D parametric curve reconstruction (NEF, EMAP, EdgeGaussians), which first reconstructs a 3D edge point cloud and then fits parametric curves via RANSAC or clustering. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for differentiable rendering, adapts the edge-aware loss from NEF, and borrows the learnable importance mask from Compact 3D Gaussian, but the core novelty is the bi-directional coupling between Bezier curve control points and edge-oriented Gaussians, which enables direct gradient-based optimization of curve parameters without an intermediate point cloud. The adaptive topology operations (linearization, merging, splitting, pruning) during training represent a new training-time structural refinement strategy not present in prior curve or Gaussian-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the optimization gap between edge reconstruction and curve fitting, the method achieves a 70.5% reduction in generated curve count while simultaneously improving accuracy by 14.5% over the best two-stage baseline, which is practically significant for downstream CAD and reverse-engineering applications where compact, clean parametric representations are essential. The main limitation acknowledged is dependence on 2D edge map quality, which constrains performance in real-world scenes with lighting variation; the authors suggest that integrating 2D edge-detection foundation models could mitigate this. An open question is whether the one-stage formulation generalizes to more complex curve types (e.g., NURBS, splines) or to scene-level reconstruction with thousands of curves, where the current per-curve Gaussian coupling may face scalability challenges.
