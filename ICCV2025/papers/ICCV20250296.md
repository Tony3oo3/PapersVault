## Problem

Generating immersive, navigable 3D worlds from a single image is highly ambiguous: multiple 3D scenes project to the same 2D view, and existing video-diffusion-based methods (e.g., WonderJourney, DimensionX) lack 3D consistency, produce noticeable 360° stitching artifacts, and struggle to outpaint in all directions. The paper tackles this by decomposing the task into two individually tractable subproblems—2D panorama synthesis and 3D lifting—rather than attempting direct 3D-consistent video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250296/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (real photo or AI-generated) and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene navigable within a 2-meter cube on a VR headset. Internally, the pipeline generates a 2048×4096 equirectangular panorama, then renders 1024×1024 perspective views from 14 translation × 14 rotation camera poses on the cube grid. The final 3DGS is trained for only 5k steps and rendered in real time at 1024×1024 with a 60° field of view.

## Method

The core recipe has two stages. First, panorama synthesis is framed as a zero-shot in-context learning task for a pre-trained T2I inpainting diffusion model with ControlNet: the input image is embedded into equirectangular space, then progressively outpainted using an 'anchored' strategy (duplicating the input to the backside to anchor sky/ground synthesis before rotating the camera), with prompts generated by Llama 3.2 Vision for scene atmosphere, sky, and ground separately. Second, the panorama is lifted to 3D using MoGE monocular depth (scale-aligned to Metric3Dv2 via quantile matching), and occluded regions in translated views are filled by a ControlNet inpainting model fine-tuned for only 5k iterations on DL3DV-10K using a forward-backward warping strategy that produces self-consistent conditioning masks. The final 3DGS (Splatfacto) is initialized from the lifted point cloud and augmented with a trainable per-image distortion MLP to absorb residual multi-view inconsistencies, yielding sharper reconstructions.

## Results

On the final 3D world quality (Table 3), the full pipeline (Ours+ControlNet+RefinedGS) achieves BRISQUE 33.9, NIQE 4.6, and Q-Align 3.6 on WorldLabs inputs, and BRISQUE 33.9, NIQE 4.5, Q-Align 3.5 on Tanks and Temples, substantially outperforming WonderJourney (BRISQUE 51.0/45.1) and DimensionX (BRISQUE 64.8/63.1). For panorama synthesis (Table 1), the training-free in-context approach achieves the best CLIP-I alignment (81.9 vs. 79.4 for MVDiffusion) and lowest BRISQUE (36.3 vs. 51.5 for MVDiffusion) across both datasets. The forward-backward warping strategy for point cloud-conditioned inpainting improves PSNR from 12.0 to 15.9 on ScanNet++ over forward warping alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's full pipeline decisively outperforms both 3D world baselines: WonderJourney and DimensionX. On WorldLabs inputs, the proposed method reduces BRISQUE from 51.0 (WonderJourney) and 64.8 (DimensionX) to 33.9, and raises Q-Align from 1.9 and 1.7 to 3.6, indicating substantially sharper and more coherent 3D scenes. DimensionX produces the worst quantitative scores across all metrics on both datasets, while WonderJourney is intermediate but still lags by a wide margin. For panorama synthesis, the training-free in-context approach beats MVDiffusion (BRISQUE 36.3 vs. 51.5, CLIP-I 81.9 vs. 79.4), Diffusion360 (BRISQUE 36.3 vs. 81.9), and DiffusionLight (BRISQUE 36.3 vs. 85.7, CLIP-I 81.9 vs. 59.1) on all four metrics. The ablation replacing the ControlNet inpainter with ViewCrafter shows a small but consistent drop (BRISQUE 43.5 vs. 41.1), confirming that the simpler point cloud-conditioned inpainting is more effective for this task than a video diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes single-image-to-3D-world generation into two subproblems—panorama synthesis and 3D lifting—departing from the dominant video-diffusion paradigm (DimensionX, WonderJourney) that attempts to synthesize 3D-consistent novel views in a single generative pass. For panorama generation, it repurposes pre-trained inpainting models via visual in-context learning rather than fine-tuning diffusion backbones (as in Diffusion360, MVDiffusion), and for 3D lifting it introduces a forward-backward warping strategy to train a ControlNet on point-cloud-derived masks. The 3D representation builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Splatfacto from NerfStudio), augmented with a trainable per-image distortion model to absorb residual multi-view inconsistencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The recipe demonstrates that a carefully decomposed pipeline using off-the-shelf components (inpainting diffusion, metric depth estimation, Gaussian Splatting) can outperform purpose-built video-diffusion systems for VR-ready 3D scene generation, with minimal training (5k steps for ControlNet, 5k for Splatfacto). Key limitations constrain broader impact: the navigable volume is capped at a 2-meter cube, occluded backsides of objects remain ungenerated, and the pipeline relies on a proprietary inpainting model due to legal constraints, limiting reproducibility. Open questions include whether the in-context panorama strategy generalizes to other inpainting backbones, whether the 2-meter constraint can be relaxed without degrading inpainting quality, and whether real-time synthesis is achievable with distilled or flow-matching variants.
