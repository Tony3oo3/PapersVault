## Problem

When training VLM agents with RL using only outcome-based rewards, the agent's chain-of-thought reasoning rapidly loses diversity and degenerates into state-irrelevant, templated outputs—a phenomenon the authors term 'thought collapse.' This is especially severe in complex environments with long episodes and large state spaces (e.g., the 24-points card game, ALFWorld), where sparse final-action rewards provide insufficient signal to sustain meaningful intermediate reasoning, causing the agent to produce identical thoughts across different states and ultimately fail.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a visual observation (e.g., an image of four poker cards with the current formula, or a rendered ALFWorld scene) concatenated with textual context including the current state/formula and historical action sequence. Notably, the authors deliberately remove ALFWorld's textual scene descriptions to force reliance on visual input. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning segment followed by an action token (a number/operator for 24-points, or an environment command like 'go to cabinet 1' for ALFWorld), with the action extracted via a post-processing function that searches for the keyword 'action: a' and falls back to random legal action if the format is invalid.

## Method

GTR augments standard PPO-based RL fine-tuning of a VLM agent with an automated thought-correction loop: at each training step, an external VLM corrector (GPT-4o with tool function calling) evaluates the agent's CoT for visual recognition accuracy and reasoning correctness, then produces a refined thought. The agent's thought tokens are trained with an SFT loss against the corrector's output while action tokens receive the standard PPO policy-gradient update, so reasoning and action are optimized simultaneously. To counteract distribution shift as the agent's policy evolves, DAgger aggregates all historical (observation, corrected-thought) pairs into a growing dataset sampled uniformly during SFT updates. Additional stabilizers include a token-level repetition penalty, a format reward for valid output structure, and episode truncation guided by the corrector's solvability judgments. The ablations confirm that process guidance must be maintained throughout training (cosine-annealing the SFT weight causes collapse to return), that the corrector must have task-specific tool access, and that cloning only thoughts (not full responses) avoids interference between corrector hallucinations and environment-grounded action learning.

## Results

On the Points24 task, GTR achieves a 17.5% success rate and -2.17 episode return, representing a 3–7× improvement over all baselines. GTR surpasses the corrector model itself (GPT-4o + Tool, 13.5% SR) and the 10× larger Qwen2-VL-72B (4.5% SR). On ALFWorld (without text observations), GTR reaches 18% average success rate versus 4% for RL4VLM, and on simpler gym_cards tasks (Numberline, EZPoints, Blackjack), GTR matches or exceeds Qwen2-VL-72B performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR (17.5% SR) decisively outperforms RL4VLM (2.5% SR, -12.95 ER), the prior SoTA RL baseline, by a factor of ~7 in success rate and substantially in episode return, confirming that outcome-only rewards cause thought collapse. GTR also surpasses the corrector model itself (GPT-4o + Tool, 13.5% SR), demonstrating that RL exploration under process guidance enables the agent to exceed the teacher. Against the 10× larger Qwen2-VL-72B (4.5% SR) and GPT-4o (2.5% SR), GTR achieves 4–7× higher success rates. On ALFWorld without text observations, GTR (18% avg) outperforms RL4VLM (4% avg) by 4.5×, while matching or exceeding models that receive textual scene descriptions. On simpler gym_cards tasks where thought collapse is less severe, GTR still improves over RL4VLM (e.g., 94.5% vs 48.0% on EZPoints) and matches Qwen2-VL-72B on Numberline (100% vs 100%). The SFT-only ablation (11.0% SR) shows that thought cloning alone is insufficient—RL exploration under guidance adds another 6.5 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RL4VLM [60], which first demonstrated that PPO-based RL can fine-tune VLM agents for sequential decision-making, but identifies a fundamental limitation: in complex, long-horizon tasks, outcome-only rewards cause 'thought collapse' where the agent's CoT degenerates into state-irrelevant, templated outputs. The GTR framework departs from prior process-supervision approaches (PRMs requiring human annotation, Monte Carlo credit assignment, LLM-as-a-judge numerical scores) by using an off-the-shelf VLM (GPT-4o) as an automated corrector that rewrites the agent's thoughts at each step, combined with SFT on thought tokens and PPO on action tokens. The use of DAgger to aggregate historical corrections and mitigate distribution shift draws from interactive imitation learning, adapting it to the on-policy PPO setting where prior work (e.g., TD3+BC) operated in off-policy contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of thought collapse as a systematic, scale-invariant failure mode in RL training of VLM agents provides a concrete diagnostic that explains why prior RL approaches plateau on complex tasks, and the finding that process guidance must be maintained throughout training (not just early) challenges the common curriculum of 'imitate first, explore later.' The practical impact is significant: a 7B LLaVA model trained with GTR outperforms GPT-4o with tool calling on the 24-points task, suggesting that targeted process supervision can unlock capabilities in much smaller models. However, the framework's reliance on GPT-4o as a corrector raises questions about cost and whether the approach scales to domains where no sufficiently capable corrector exists; the authors also note they have not explored o1-style long CoT reasoning and have only validated on 7B-scale models and two environments.
