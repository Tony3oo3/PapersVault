## Problem

Single-view strand-based hair reconstruction is challenging because existing methods trained purely on synthetic data suffer a significant domain gap with real hair (which contains over 100,000 strands versus ~10,000 in synthetic datasets), while multi-view approaches require hours to days of processing. The paper addresses this by learning a hairstyle prior from a mixture of synthetic and real data, enabling fast one-shot reconstruction that captures both internal 3D structure and realistic outer appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB photograph and internally extracts orientation maps (via Gabor filters), hair/body segmentation maps (via Segment Anything), and depth maps (via Depth Pro) as auxiliary inputs to the network. The output is a strand-based 3D hair geometry parameterized as a 64×64 PCA hair map (64 coefficients per strand) on the FLAME head model's scalp UV space, with each strand represented as a polyline of L segments. The full pipeline (prior prediction + 400-step fine-tuning) takes approximately 10 minutes on an A100 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid training strategy for a transformer-based hairstyle prior that combines 3D geometry supervision from synthetic data (PERM, 21k hairstyles) with self-supervised rendering losses from real data (Hairstep, 1,250 photos) using 3D Gaussian Splatting differentiable rasterization. The architecture uses a coarse-to-fine design: a coarse branch predicts the first 10 PCA components and a baldness mask, while a fine branch at 2× resolution predicts the remaining 54 PCA components, with real-data gradients restricted to the coarse branch to avoid noisy high-frequency artifacts. At inference, the prior regresses an initial hairstyle from the input image, which is then refined via 400 steps of single-view optimization using segmentation, direction, depth, and penetration losses, with strand count increased through interpolation following HAAR.

## Results

On synthetic data (10 hairstyles rendered from a hemisphere), Im2Haircut achieves the lowest chamfer distance on both points (0.000246) and directions (0.502), outperforming NeuralHDHair (0.000251 / 0.627) and Hairstep (0.001279 / 0.605). On 50 real images from the Hairstep test set, the method attains the best silhouette IoU (0.97 vs. 0.80 for Hairstep and 0.76 for PERM) and the lowest undirected orientation error L_undir (0.07 vs. 0.12 and 0.128 respectively). The full single-view reconstruction pipeline takes approximately 10 minutes on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Im2Haircut outperforms all five baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against NeuralHDHair [35], it achieves a lower chamfer_angle (0.502 vs. 0.627) and marginally lower chamfer_pts (0.000246 vs. 0.000251); NeuralHDHair's hair-growing procedure produces overly smooth geometry that lacks strand-level detail. Against Hairstep [41], the gains are substantial: chamfer_pts drops by over 5× (0.000246 vs. 0.001279), chamfer_angle improves by ~17% (0.502 vs. 0.605), and on real data IoU jumps from 0.80 to 0.97 while L_undir nearly halves (0.07 vs. 0.12); Hairstep's errors stem from inaccurate length predictions, especially on the back of the head. Against PERM [10], the method achieves 45% lower L_undir (0.07 vs. 0.128) and 27% higher IoU (0.97 vs. 0.76), with PERM struggling on wavy and complex hairstyles due to its expensive rendering-based initialization. Qualitatively, HairNet [42] fails to capture wavy structures, and in the multi-view setting Gaussian Haircut [37] requires 10 hours of optimization versus ~45 minutes for Im2Haircut while producing less accurate geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hair-map-based strand representation (following PERM and HairNet) and the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline (following Gaussian Haircut), but departs from prior work by introducing a hybrid training strategy that mixes synthetic data with full 3D supervision and real in-the-wild data with self-supervised rendering losses. Unlike hair-growing methods (NeuralHDHair, Hairstep) that reconstruct a volumetric orientation field before extracting strands, Im2Haircut operates directly at the strand level via a PCA-parameterized hair map, and unlike PERM's expensive retrieval-based initialization, it uses a lightweight transformer regressor. The coarse-to-fine PCA prediction architecture and the finding that real-data gradients should only flow into the coarse branch represent the key architectural departures from prior single-view hair priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid synthetic+real training strategy directly addresses the long-standing domain gap that limits the realism of synthetic-trained hair priors, and the 10-minute single-view reconstruction time makes strand-based hair capture practical for avatar pipelines and CG production. However, the method remains dependent on off-the-shelf estimators (Depth Pro, Segment Anything, Hairstep direction maps) whose errors propagate, and it struggles with highly curly hairstyles and hair parting due to limited synthetic dataset diversity and fixed root positions. Open questions include whether the approach can be made robust to estimator failures, whether dynamic root positions and densification can resolve parting artifacts, and whether the hybrid training recipe generalizes to other strand-based reconstruction tasks beyond hair.
