## Problem

Existing masked skeleton modeling methods reconstruct low-level joint coordinates or motion differences, incurring heavy decoder computation (e.g., 17.70G FLOPs) while providing no explicit supervision for higher-level spatiotemporal semantics. This creates both a computational bottleneck and a semantic gap between pretraining and downstream tasks like recognition and retrieval that require holistic action understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence X ∈ R^{T×V×C} (e.g., 100 frames × 25 joints × 3 channels), temporally patchified into non-overlapping 4-frame patches, with 90% of patches randomly masked. During pretraining, the decoder outputs hierarchical semantic features at four temporal granularities (5-, 10-, 30-frame spans, and a global action-level vector) rather than per-joint coordinates. For downstream use, the frozen encoder produces a single action representation vector per sequence, used directly for linear classification or cosine-similarity retrieval.

## Method

GFP replaces the standard MAE reconstruction target with a set of 251 hierarchical high-level semantic features predicted by a progressive decoder that cascades temporal average pooling with single-layer transformer decoders at increasing receptive fields (t₁=5, t₂=10, t₃=30, then global aggregation). Supervision targets are generated online by a lightweight Target Generation Network (TGN) of 3-layer MLPs that processes motion-transformed inputs (frame-difference of patch embeddings) to avoid bias toward frequently unmasked joints. To prevent the co-optimization from collapsing to constant outputs, the framework applies VICReg-style variance and covariance regularization on all TGN target features, enforcing batch-wise feature diversity and inter-dimensional decorrelation. This design reduces decoder FLOPs from 17.70G to 1.57G (6.2× speedup) while simultaneously capturing local motion patterns and global action semantics in a single unified objective.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60, GFP achieves 85.9% top-1 accuracy (x-sub) and 92.0% (x-view), outperforming S-JEPA (85.3%/89.8%) and MAMP (84.9%/89.1%) while training 6.2× faster than SkeletonMAE (3h14m vs. 20h27m). On NTU-120, GFP reaches 79.1% (x-sub) and 80.3% (x-setup); on PKU-MMD II it attains 56.2% (x-sub), the best among all masked-modeling methods. For action retrieval on NTU-60, GFP scores 70.9% (x-sub) and 87.1% (x-view), surpassing MAMP by 8.9 and 17.1 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFP outperforms all three direct masked-modeling baselines on NTU-60: it beats SkeletonMAE by 11.1 points (x-sub) and 14.3 points (x-view), MAMP by 1.0 and 2.9 points, and S-JEPA by 0.6 and 2.2 points, while training 6.2× faster than SkeletonMAE and 28× faster than S-JEPA. On NTU-120, GFP slightly trails S-JEPA on x-sub (79.1 vs. 79.6) but leads on x-setup (80.3 vs. 79.9); on PKU-MMD II it achieves the best masked-modeling result (56.2%) ahead of MAMP (53.8%) and S-JEPA (53.5%). Against contrastive and other pretext-task baselines (HiCo, UmURL, USDRL, HaLP, CMD, ISC, P&C), GFP leads on NTU-120 x-setup (80.3%) and PKU-II (56.2%), though UmURL edges it by 0.4 points on NTU-60 retrieval x-sub (71.3 vs. 70.9). In the semi-supervised setting (1% and 10% labels), GFP sets new state-of-the-art on all four NTU-60 splits, exceeding the previous best (S-JEPA/MAMP) by 3.3–4.3 points at 1% labels and by 0.3–0.7 points at 10% labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked skeleton modeling lineage (SkeletonMAE → MAMP → S-JEPA) by replacing low-level reconstruction targets with hierarchical high-level semantic feature prediction. It borrows the motion-aware masking strategy from MAMP and the VICReg-style variance-covariance regularization to prevent feature collapse in the co-optimization of the decoder and a lightweight Target Generation Network (TGN). The key conceptual departure from S-JEPA is the use of an online, lightweight TGN (0.64G FLOPs) instead of a heavy EMA-updated encoder (28.32G FLOPs) for target generation, and the multi-granularity temporal pyramid (5/10/30-frame + global) instead of single-level patch features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.2× training speedup while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy makes masked skeleton modeling substantially more practical for research groups with limited GPU budgets, and the large retrieval gains (8.9–17.1% over MAMP) demonstrate that low-level reconstruction objectives fundamentally fail at global semantic tasks. An open question is whether the hierarchical multi-granularity structure is truly necessary or whether a single global-level target with the TGN would suffice, since the ablation shows each level contributes only marginally. The approach also raises the question of whether similar high-level feature prediction with lightweight target networks could replace reconstruction in other masked modeling domains (e.g., point clouds, audio) where decoder cost is a bottleneck.
