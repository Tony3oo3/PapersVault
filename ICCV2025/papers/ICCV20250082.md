## Problem

Event-based small object detection (EVSOD) for anti-UAV tasks lacks a dedicated large-scale benchmark: existing event datasets feature large targets in simple backgrounds, provide only bounding-box annotations, and do not cover the extremely small scales (averaging 6.8×5.4 pixels) and cluttered real-world scenes that anti-UAV scenarios demand. Additionally, frame-based detection pipelines that convert events into synchronized images discard the temporal sparsity and continuity that make small moving targets distinguishable in raw event streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8-second sparse event streams from a DAVIS346 camera (346×260 spatial, up to 10⁶ Hz temporal resolution), voxelized into a 3D XYT grid at 1 px × 1 px × 1 ms resolution. Output is an event-by-event binary segmentation label (target vs. background/noise) for every individual event in the stream, rather than a per-frame bounding box or mask. This event-level granularity enables microsecond-precision localization and can be degraded to bounding-box annotations for compatibility with frame-based algorithms.

## Method

EV-SpSegNet is a U-shaped encoder-decoder built entirely on sparse convolution, exploiting the observation that small moving targets trace continuous elongated curves in spatiotemporal event point clouds, unlike the surfaces of static backgrounds or the scattered points of noise. The core GDSCA module first applies Grouped Dilated Sparse Convolution with four branches at dilation rates (1,2,3,4) to capture multi-scale local curve features, fuses them via a Sparse Squeeze-and-Excitation block, then performs Patch Attention over downsampled sub-regions to model long-range global context without quadratic cost. Training uses a Spatiotemporal Correlation (STC) loss that modulates the per-event cross-entropy weight by the sigmoid of the summed prediction confidence in a k×k×τ neighborhood (k=3, τ=5, γ=2), so events embedded in high-confidence continuous trajectories are penalized more heavily when misclassified, while isolated noise events receive reduced penalty. The full model has only 4.0 M parameters and infers 8 seconds of events in 35.9 ms on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the EV-UAV test set, EV-SpSegNet achieves 55.18% IoU, 65.02% ACC, 77.53% probability of detection (Pd), and a false alarm rate of 1.63×10⁻⁴, outperforming all 13 baselines across every metric. The method uses only 4.0M parameters and processes 8 seconds of event data in 35.9 ms, which is roughly 10× faster than the nearest point-cloud segmentation baseline (RandLA-Net at 353 ms) and over 50× faster than frame-based detectors (up to 3962 ms for Faster R-CNN). The closest competitor, COSeg (CVPR 2024), reaches 51.89% IoU and 60.93% ACC but has a false alarm rate 5.6× higher (9.21 vs. 1.63 ×10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EV-SpSegNet outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric (IoU, ACC, Pd, Fa). Against the four frame-based detectors (SSD, Faster R-CNN, Deformable DETR, YOLOv10-S), the proposed method gains 22.6–29.9 points in IoU and reduces the false alarm rate by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 1.63 vs. 486.63–689.68 ×10⁻⁴), reflecting the fundamental limitation of converting sparse events into dense frames for tiny targets. Among the six event-based methods, the closest competitor is Spike-YOLO (43.94% IoU, 55.38 Fa), which EV-SpSegNet surpasses by 11.2 IoU points and a 34× lower false alarm rate; RVT (43.21% IoU) and GET (40.31% IoU) trail by similar margins. The three point-cloud segmentation baselines (KPConv, RandLA-Net, COSeg) are the strongest competitors, with COSeg reaching 51.89% IoU and 60.93% ACC, yet EV-SpSegNet still leads by 3.3 IoU points, 4.1 ACC points, and a 5.6× lower false alarm rate (1.63 vs. 9.21 ×10⁻⁴). In terms of efficiency, EV-SpSegNet's 35.9 ms inference on 8 s of events is roughly 10× faster than RandLA-Net (353 ms) and COSeg (364 ms), and over 50× faster than the slowest frame-based baseline (Faster R-CNN at 3962 ms), while using fewer parameters than all but RandLA-Net and EMS-YOLO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of event-based vision and small object detection, a combination largely absent from prior literature. While event-based object detection methods (RVT, SAST, GET, RED) typically convert sparse events into dense image-like representations for frame-based detectors, and point cloud segmentation methods (KPConv, RandLA-Net, COSeg) operate on LiDAR data, this work repurposes sparse convolution and attention architectures from the point cloud domain to directly segment target events in spatiotemporal event point clouds. The key conceptual departure is the observation that small moving targets form continuous elongated curves in event point cloud space—distinct from background surfaces and noise points—which motivates both the GDSCA module (multi-scale dilated sparse convolution + patch attention) and the novel STC loss that explicitly rewards spatiotemporal continuity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EV-UAV is the first large-scale benchmark with event-level annotations for anti-UAV small object detection, filling a critical gap where prior event-based datasets (NeRDD, F-UAV-D) only covered large targets in simple or indoor scenes. The 35.9 ms inference time for 8 seconds of event data makes real-time anti-UAV deployment practically feasible, a significant advantage over frame-based methods requiring 1.6–4 seconds. A key limitation is that event cameras produce no output for stationary or very slow targets, making the approach inherently complementary to frame-based sensors; the authors identify multi-modal (event + RGB/IR) fusion as the natural next step. The dataset's single-camera, single-location collection also limits generalizability claims, and the absence of a public test set evaluation protocol (test set held out) constrains independent verification.
