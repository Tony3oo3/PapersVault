## Problem

Existing zero-shot industrial anomaly detection methods either process each test image in isolation (ignoring the inherent rarity of defects) or require batch processing of all test images simultaneously (introducing prohibitive latency for real-time applications). No prior framework supports sequential, real-time zero-shot anomaly detection that dynamically exploits inter-image rarity without requiring prior domain knowledge or batch access to the full test set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250052/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB images (resized to 518×518) from a single product category, with no prior domain-specific training data. It must emit a detection result for each image before the next one arrives. Outputs include a pixel-level anomaly map (computed on a √M×√M patch grid, upsampled to original resolution, and Gaussian-smoothed) and a refined image-level anomaly score that incorporates re-scoring against the B most similar historical images.

## Method

RareCLIP combines a Text Prompt Branch (CLIP ViT-L-14-336 with lightweight learnable projections aligning multi-layer patch features to normal/abnormal text prompts, trained with focal + Dice + MSE loss) with a Patch-level Rarity Branch that maintains two memory banks: a prototype patch feature bank (M_F) and a patch-image similarity bank (M_S). For each incoming test patch, rarity is estimated indirectly by retrieving K nearest neighbors in M_F and aggregating their stored patch-image similarity vectors, weighted by retrieval similarity. Two key mechanisms enable efficiency and robustness: Sequential Coreset Sampling (SCS) prunes redundant features by retaining those with the smallest maximum similarity to previously kept features, and Loose Similarity (LS) calibrates multiplicative similarity estimates by normalizing against a Y-percentile threshold to prevent gradual underestimation. An Image-level Rarity Branch and an Image-level Re-scoring strategy (temperature-weighted averaging over B most similar historical images) further refine the final anomaly score.

## Results

In online mode, RareCLIP achieves 98.2% I-AUC and 97.7% P-AUC on MVTec AD, and 94.4% I-AUC and 98.8% P-AUC on VisA, while maintaining a latency of only 59.4 ms per image. In offline mode, RareCLIP is competitive with the best offline zero-shot methods (e.g., VCP-CLIP at 92.1% I-AUC on MVTec AD vs. RareCLIP's 91.5%), but the online mode yields substantial gains of 6–7% I-AUC over offline. RareCLIP also outperforms the batch zero-shot method MuSc (97.7% I-AUC on MVTec AD) while operating sequentially with far lower memory (4.3 GB vs. 7.3 GB at N_I,max=200).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RareCLIP in online mode outperforms the strongest batch zero-shot baseline MuSc (ICLR'24) by 0.5% I-AUC on MVTec AD (98.2 vs. 97.7) and 1.8% on VisA (94.4 vs. 92.6), while operating sequentially at 59.4 ms latency versus MuSc's prohibitive pairwise-computation cost. Against the online baseline MuSc* (a reproduced sequential version of MuSc), RareCLIP gains 2.2% I-AUC on MVTec AD (98.2 vs. 96.0) and 4.4% on VisA (94.4 vs. 90.0), with similar margins on P-AUC. The other online method, O-InReach (ECCV'24), is substantially weaker, trailing RareCLIP by 11.1% I-AUC on MVTec AD and 16.4% on VisA. Among offline zero-shot methods, VCP-CLIP (ECCV'24) leads with 92.1% I-AUC on MVTec AD; RareCLIP's offline mode (91.5%) is slightly below but its online mode surpasses all offline methods by 6+ points. In the few-/full-shot comparison (Table 2), RareCLIP in 4-shot online mode (98.7% I-AUC on MVTec AD) approaches full-shot methods like PatchCore (99.1%) and RD++ (99.4%) without requiring any normal training samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new middle ground between offline zero-shot AD (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP), which processes each image in isolation, and batch zero-shot AD (ACR, MuSc), which requires all test images simultaneously. It repurposes CLIP's zero-shot text-prompt alignment as a semantic prior and augments it with a dynamic rarity mechanism inspired by PatchCore's memory-bank philosophy, but adapted for sequential, single-pass processing. The Sequential Coreset Sampling strategy is a sequential analogue of PatchCore's greedy coreset, and the Loose Similarity calibration addresses a multiplicative-error accumulation problem specific to indirect similarity estimation through a memory bank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The online zero-shot formulation directly mirrors real production-line deployment where images arrive sequentially, no domain-specific training data is available, and latency budgets are tight. The 16× latency reduction over the closest online competitor (MuSc*) while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes the approach practically deployable. Open questions include: performance sensitivity to test-image ordering in online mode, generalization beyond single-category streams, and whether the memory-bank approach degrades under distributional drift (e.g., lighting changes, seasonal variation). The single-category assumption and evaluation on only two datasets limit confidence in broader applicability.
