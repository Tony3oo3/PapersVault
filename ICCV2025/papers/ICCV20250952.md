## Problem

Single-photon lidar (SPL) is traditionally restricted to low-flux operation (< 0.05 photons per pulse) to avoid dead-time effects, yet high-flux operation is desirable for practical sensing. While synchronous detectors suffer from pile-up distortion that can bury the histogram peak, free-running detectors have less severe distortion but lack efficient estimation methods due to statistically dependent detection times. This paper closes that gap by showing free-running SPL outperforms synchronous SPL across all flux levels and providing the first computationally efficient joint ML estimators for both modes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only histograms of quantized relative detection times (mod t_r) per pixel, scan angles (polar and azimuthal), and a pretrained 3D point cloud score model; it does not require absolute detection times or separate background calibration. Per-pixel, it outputs joint estimates of signal flux S, background flux B, and depth z. For multi-pixel 3D reconstruction, the SSDR algorithm additionally produces a regularized depth map and 3D point cloud, with the score model operating on Cartesian coordinates derived from the per-pixel depth estimates and scan angles.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form log-likelihoods for ideal, synchronous, and free-running detectors and shows that joint ML estimation of (S, B, z) can be solved via alternating maximization: L-BFGS-B for the concave (S, B) subproblem and a matched-filtering correlation for the nonconcave depth subproblem. For free-running mode, the depth filter is h⊕u + g⊕v where g is the histogram cyclically shifted by the dead time, making the computation as fast as synchronous matched filtering. For 3D regularization, SSDR projects the 3D Stein score from a pretrained point cloud denoiser (Luo & Hu, ICCV 2021) onto each pixel's line-of-sight to obtain a scalar depth score, then runs Plug-and-play Monte Carlo (Langevin dynamics) where the SPL log-likelihood gradient provides data fidelity and the hard-thresholded depth score provides the learned prior. Outlier depths are initialized via K-nearest-neighbor median smoothing in scan-angle space before iterative refinement.

## Results

In 3D simulation (Greyc 3D Colored Meshes), free-running pixel-wise ML estimation achieves a depth MAE of 0.007 m versus 0.022 m for synchronous mode, while SSDR further reduces the signal flux RMSE from 2.4×10⁻² to 1.8×10⁻². In experiments under high-flux illumination, synchronous ML estimates fail entirely (depth clusters near zero due to pile-up), whereas free-running estimates closely match the low-flux reference, and SSDR corrects remaining out-of-bound points. Across simulations, free-running maintains depth-independent RMSE (unlike synchronous, which degrades with depth) and outperforms synchronous across all tested dead times up to 90 ns (90% of the repetition period) and signal fluxes from 0.01 to 10 photons per pulse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Rapp et al. [40] (dead-time compensation via stationary distribution of a Markov chain), the proposed free-running ML estimator matches depth RMSE accuracy while running significantly faster, and additionally requires only relative detection times (histograms) rather than absolute detection times for joint flux estimation. Against Coates's correction [11] (the standard pile-up mitigation for synchronous mode), the proposed synchronous ML estimator achieves lower depth RMSE in the moderate-to-high flux regime (S ∈ [0.6, 1.2] at SBR = 0.5), confirming that Coates's correction, while ML-optimal for the transient, is not ML-optimal for depth. The free-running mode itself outperforms the synchronous mode (with Coates correction) across all tested flux levels, depths, and dead times, with the advantage being most pronounced at high flux where synchronous pile-up buries the histogram peak.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint ML estimation framework of Kitichotkul et al. [24] (which applied only to ideal, dead-time-free detectors) to both synchronous and free-running SPAD modes, deriving matched-filtering formulations for each. For free-running detection, it builds on Rapp et al.'s [40,41] Markov-chain model of dead-time-dependent detection times but replaces the computationally expensive stationary-distribution computation with a histogram-based matched filter. The SSDR regularization algorithm adapts the Plug-and-play Monte Carlo framework of Laumont et al. [25] and Sun et al. [47] to SPL, with the key novelty of projecting a pretrained 3D point cloud score model (Luo & Hu [28]) onto the detector's line-of-sight to obtain depth scores—making it the first SPL regularizer to operate directly on 3D point clouds rather than 2D depth maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper definitively challenges two entrenched assumptions in SPL: that the 5% flux rule is a hard operational constraint (the optimal flux is substantially higher) and that synchronous mode's simpler statistics make it the preferred default (free-running consistently outperforms it across depths, fluxes, and dead times up to 90% of the repetition period). The SSDR algorithm opens a path toward learned 3D priors for SPL reconstruction, but its current limitation—reliance on a score model trained on isolated objects—means it underperforms on complex multi-depth scenes, and only depth (not signal/background flux) is regularized. Open questions include the Cramér-Rao bound for free-running measurements, the distribution of the number of active periods in synchronous mode, and whether better score models or diffusion-based techniques can close the gap on complex scenes.
