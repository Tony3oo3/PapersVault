## Problem

Vanilla Rectified Flow models learn velocity fields with unconstrained neural networks that fail to satisfy the theoretically derived boundary conditions (v(x,1)=x and v(x,0)=E[X₁]−x). This violation is especially critical during stochastic sampling, where the score function estimated via Tweedie's formula becomes unbounded as t→1, causing oversmoothing and loss of fine detail in generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard Gaussian noise vector z₀~N(0,I) and produces a synthetic RGB image by numerically integrating the learned ODE (or augmented SDE) from t=0 to t=1. Experiments are conducted at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) and 256×256 / 512×512 (ImageNet) resolutions using a DiT backbone. The Subtraction-based variant requires an additional forward pass at t=1 for each velocity evaluation, roughly doubling inference cost compared to the single-pass Mask-based variant.

## Method

The paper proposes a Boundary-enforced Rectified Flow Model that modifies the velocity field parameterization to satisfy boundary conditions by construction. The Mask-based variant structures the velocity as v(x,t) = g(t)·(C−x) + f(t)·x + h(t)·m_θ(x,t), where scalar functions f, g, h (default: sin/cosine) are chosen so that v(x,1)=x and v(x,0)=C−x hold identically regardless of the neural network m_θ. The Subtraction-based variant simplifies this to v(x,t) = x + m_θ(x,t) − m_θ(x,1), enforcing only the t=1 boundary without auxiliary scalar functions. The key insight is that enforcing v(x,1)=x prevents the score function ∇log ρ_t = (t·v(x,t)−x)/(1−t) from diverging near t=1, stabilizing Langevin-based stochastic samplers. Training uses the standard flow-matching MSE loss with a DiT architecture, requiring only a minimal code change to the output layer.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the Subtraction-based Boundary RF Model achieves a FID of 6.32 (Euler) and 6.38 (SDE), outperforming the vanilla RF baseline (6.87/6.79) by 8.01% and 6.04% respectively. On CIFAR-10, the same model reaches FID 3.47 (Euler) and 3.04 (SDE) versus 3.94/3.63 for vanilla RF. With classifier-free guidance on ImageNet 256×256, the Mask-based variant achieves FID 4.94 (Euler), a 19.1% improvement over vanilla RF (6.11). The Subtraction-based model also attains the best IS scores across all settings, reaching 149.00 (SDE) on ImageNet 256×256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: vanilla Rectified Flow (Flow Matching [21]) and RF with Mode Sampling [7]. Boundary RF Model consistently outperforms vanilla RF on FID across all datasets and sampler types: on ImageNet 256×256, the Subtraction-based variant improves FID from 6.87 to 6.32 (Euler) and the Mask-based variant from 6.79 to 6.18 (SDE). Against RF with Mode Sampling, Boundary RF Model also wins decisively on ImageNet (6.32 vs. 7.48 Euler FID), showing that explicit boundary enforcement is more effective than merely re-weighting timestep sampling toward boundaries. The one exception is ImageNet 256×256 with CFG, where RF + Mode Sampling achieves a slightly better Euler FID (5.60) than the Subtraction-based model (5.68), though the Mask-based variant still leads (4.94). Overall, Boundary RF Model wins on FID in 10 out of 12 head-to-head comparisons against vanilla RF and in all 6 comparisons against RF + Mode Sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rectified Flow / Flow Matching framework [Lipman et al., Esser et al.] by identifying that the unconstrained neural network parameterization of the velocity field systematically violates the theoretically derived boundary conditions at t=0 and t=1. It is positioned as complementary to prior training refinements such as noise schedule design, loss re-weighting, and mode sampling, which address different aspects of the training objective but do not enforce the velocity field's boundary behavior. The proposed boundary-aware parameterization (mask-based and subtraction-based) is a minimal architectural modification to the velocity network, distinct from the broader class of flow/diffusion training enhancements surveyed in the related work section.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The boundary condition violation is a fundamental issue affecting all vanilla RF implementations, and the proposed fix is a minimal code change that integrates with existing U-Net and DiT architectures across model scales (DiT-B through DiT-XL) and resolutions (32×32 to 512×512). The stabilization of the score function near t=1 has practical implications for stochastic sampling, enabling larger noise scales without the over-smoothing artifacts that plague vanilla RF. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image and video generation at scale, and whether adaptive or learned boundary enforcement strategies could further improve performance.
