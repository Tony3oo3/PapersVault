## Problem

Existing forgery localization methods process spatial and frequency-domain cues separately due to computational concerns, leaving no unified attention mechanism to jointly leverage both for capturing subtle manipulation artifacts. Additionally, decoder upsampling loses fine detail in challenging regions, and CNN-based approaches suffer from limited representation power and poor generalization to unseen forgery types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250042/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 256×256 as input and produces two outputs: a pixel-level binary forgery mask (the primary prediction) and a global prior map at 1/32 resolution that encodes forgery cues and drives difficulty estimation. The global prior map is not a standalone prediction but an internal signal whose curvature is computed to classify each sample as 'hard' or 'easy,' which then modulates channel-wise attention in the decoder.

## Method

M2SFormer uses a PVT-v2-B2 encoder-decoder with a novel M2S attention block inserted in the skip connections. The M2S block first concatenates four encoder-stage features (after 1×1 channel reduction and resizing) into a cross-scale map, then applies multi-spectral attention via 2D DCT basis images with top-K frequency selection to produce a channel attention map, followed by multi-scale attention using a SIFT-inspired dilated-convolution feature pyramid with learnable foreground/background gating at each level. In the decoder, an Edge-Aware Difficulty Calculator computes the second-order curvature of the global prior map using anisotropic Sobel filters, averages curvature only over edge regions, and thresholds the result into a 'hard'/'easy' text label. This label is embedded via BPE and passed through a two-layer 1×1 convolution to generate a channel-wise attention gate that modulates each decoder stage's features, directing more capacity toward challenging samples. Training is end-to-end with a combined BCE loss on both the final mask and the upscaled global prior map, using Adam with cosine annealing over 100 epochs at batch size 32.

## Results

On the CASIAv2 training scheme, M2SFormer achieves 58.8 DSC / 50.8 mIoU on the seen domain and leads all six unseen test datasets, reaching 69.1 DSC / 56.9 mIoU on MISD and 58.4 DSC / 50.1 mIoU on CASIAv1. Under the DIS25k training scheme, it scores 87.7 DSC / 81.5 mIoU on the seen domain and 40.1 DSC / 34.5 mIoU on CASIAv1 (unseen). The model requires only 14.0G FLOPs and 27.4M parameters, making it more efficient than dual-encoder baselines like EITLNet while outperforming them on cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2SFormer outperforms the strongest baseline PIMNet on all six unseen datasets under the CASIAv2 training scheme, with the largest margin on Columbia (+9.9 DSC: 42.4 vs 32.5) and CASIAv1 (+8.7 DSC: 58.4 vs 49.7). Against EITLNet, which uses a dual-encoder Transformer with frequency cues, M2SFormer wins on every unseen domain (e.g., +10.1 DSC on Columbia, +10.3 on CASIAv1) while using fewer parameters and FLOPs. Compared to the frequency-based FBINet, M2SFormer gains +24.2 DSC on Columbia and +20.9 on CASIAv1, demonstrating that unified spatial-frequency attention in skip connections is more effective than applying 2D DCT to raw input images. On the DIS25k training scheme, M2SFormer trails EITLNet slightly on the seen domain (87.7 vs 90.6 DSC) but surpasses it on all unseen datasets, particularly CoMoFoD (+12.0 DSC: 31.9 vs 19.9) and IMD2020 (+1.9 DSC). General-purpose segmentation models (UNet, SegNet, SegNeXt) perform poorly on forgery localization, confirming the need for task-specific architectures. The ablation confirms that the Edge-Aware Difficulty Calculator + DGA decoder contributes the largest single gain (+10.7 DSC on unseen) over a no-DGA baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

M2SFormer carves new ground by unifying multi-spectral (2D DCT-based channel attention) and multi-scale (SIFT-inspired spatial attention) mechanisms into a single attention block placed in skip connections, whereas prior forgery localization methods (FBINet, ObjectFormer, EITLNet) process spatial and frequency cues in separate branches or encoders. The architecture repurposes PVT-v2 as both encoder and decoder to exploit global Transformer context, and introduces a curvature-based difficulty metric converted to a BPE text token that drives a channel-wise attention gate in the decoder—neither of which has appeared in prior forgery localization work. Conceptually, the paper bridges the frequency-domain generalization literature (FCANet, FBINet) with the Transformer-based global-attention line (TransForensic) and adds a perceptually-motivated difficulty signal inspired by curvature processing in the human visual system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to localize forgeries across unseen domains without fine-tuning is practically critical as manipulation techniques evolve faster than labeled datasets can be collected; M2SFormer's consistent gains across six external benchmarks under two training schemes make it a strong reference point for the field. However, the binary 'hard/easy' difficulty classification via a single curvature threshold is a coarse heuristic that may not capture the full spectrum of manipulation subtlety, and the approach has not been evaluated on generative forgeries (e.g., diffusion-model edits or deepfakes) where spatial-frequency artifacts differ fundamentally from cut-and-paste traces. Open questions include whether the difficulty-guided attention generalizes to multi-class manipulation types, whether the BPE text encoding adds meaningful capacity over a simple scalar gate, and how the method performs under heavy real-world compression pipelines beyond the mild corruptions tested.
