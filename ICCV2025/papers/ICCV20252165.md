## Problem

Extremely sparse ToF depth sensors (providing as few as 64 noisy points per frame) cannot be directly integrated into dense SLAM pipelines, as low-resolution noisy depth degrades tracking and mapping accuracy. Existing work relies on single-image depth completion, which generalizes poorly across noise levels and domains, and no 3D Gaussian Splatting-based SLAM system has been designed to exploit such sparse active depth measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252165/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized RGB frames and an extremely sparse ToF depth map (e.g., 8×8 = 64 points, ~0.02% of a 640×480 image) along with a buffer of N=4 keyframes and their estimated relative poses. It produces per-frame camera poses, a global 3DGS scene representation, dense metric depth maps per frame, and final 3D meshes extracted via TSDF integration and marching cubes. The multi-frame integration module operates on a sliding window of keyframes rather than the full trajectory, keeping computation local.

## Method

ToF-Splatting is a frame-to-model 3DGS SLAM system with three tightly coupled modules: a tracking frontend that optimizes camera poses via backpropagation on the 3DGS model (two-stage: photometric-only, then photometric+depth), a multi-frame integration module that extends Depth on Demand (DoD) to fuse sparse ToF points, multi-view stereo geometry from a keyframe buffer, and monocular cues from Depth Anything v2 into dense metric depth maps, and a mapping backend that seeds and optimizes 3D Gaussians with depth, normal, SSIM, and isotropy losses. A key design choice is a two-pass outlier filtering strategy: DoD first predicts depth using only multi-view geometry (no ToF input), then sparse measurements exceeding a quantile threshold of the ℓ₁ error are discarded before a second DoD pass integrates the cleaned depth. Keyframe selection is driven by rendered opacity (low opacity indicates unmapped or unstable regions), and the mapping step back-propagates gradients to keyframe poses, acting as a local bundle adjustment.

## Results

On the ZJUL5 sparse ToF benchmark, ToF-Splatting achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.051 m, nearly halving the error of the closest competitor ToF-SLAM (0.107 m) and outperforming MonoGS (0.087 m) by 41%. For mapping, it attains a mean F-score of 0.664 and accuracy of 0.043 m, surpassing ToF-SLAM (F-score 0.604, accuracy 0.087 m) and all other baselines. On TUM RGB-D, with only 0.04% depth density (128 points), ToF-Splatting reaches 0.022 m ATE, approaching the 0.015 m of MonoGS at 100% density while using 2500× fewer depth points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToF-Splatting outperforms all seven ZJUL5 baselines on tracking (ATE RMSE), with the largest margins over iMAP (0.647 m) and NICE-SLAM (0.319 m), which struggle with the extreme sparsity of 64-point ToF input. The closest competitor, ToF-SLAM [19], relies on a single-image depth completion model (DELTAR) and achieves 0.107 m ATE—more than double ToF-Splatting's 0.051 m—while also losing on mapping F-score (0.604 vs. 0.664). MonoGS [22], adapted with OGNI-DC depth completion, reaches 0.087 m ATE but still trails ToF-Splatting by 41%, and its mapping F-score (0.318) is less than half. Traditional methods (KinectFusion, BundleFusion, ElasticFusion) either fail entirely on challenging sequences (Kitchen, Living Room) or show 2–4× higher ATE. On TUM RGB-D, ToF-Splatting at 0.04% density (0.022 m ATE) approaches MonoGS at 100% density (0.015 m) and surpasses DROID-VO (0.077 m), though it remains slightly behind ORB-SLAM2 with loop closure (0.010 m) and the 100%-density NeRF-based methods ESLAM/CG-SLAM (0.020 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM line (MonoGS, GaussianSLAM, SplaTAM) by addressing the previously unhandled regime of extremely sparse ToF depth (as few as 64 points per frame), where all prior 3DGS-SLAM systems assumed dense depth input. The multi-frame integration module directly extends Depth on Demand (DoD) from two-view to multi-view processing and augments it with monocular cues from Depth Anything v2 and a multi-view-geometry-based outlier filter. The key conceptual departure from the only prior sparse-ToF SLAM system (ToF-SLAM) is rejecting single-image depth completion (DELTAR) in favor of multi-view geometric reasoning over a keyframe buffer, which the authors argue is necessary to handle the noise and sparsity that completion models cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that consumer-grade sparse ToF sensors (8×8, ~200 mW) can drive a full dense SLAM pipeline, opening a path toward always-on depth sensing in mobile AR/VR devices where power budgets preclude high-resolution depth cameras. The multi-frame integration strategy—fusing sparse active depth, multi-view geometry, and monocular priors—is a generalizable recipe applicable beyond ToF to any low-rate active sensor. However, the 1.5 fps runtime (614 ms tracking + 865 ms mapping) and absence of loop closure limit immediate deployment, and the reliance on backpropagation through the 3DGS model for tracking remains a significant compute bottleneck that fast 3DGS renderers have not yet addressed in the SLAM context.
