## Problem

The widely accepted 'gold standard' for machine unlearning—re-training a model after removing undesirable data—fails for score-based generative models (SGMs), which still generate samples resembling the unlearned content due to unintended generalization. The paper shows this failure occurs because re-training does not alter the original score function, leaving the model's generative trajectory unchanged in the regions corresponding to the forgotten data. No prior unlearning framework existed specifically for SGMs, and existing DDPM unlearning methods cannot be directly transferred due to differences in optimization objectives.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a Gaussian noise sample and produces an image via the reverse SDE; for text-to-image (Stable Diffusion v1.4), it takes a text prompt plus noise; for inpainting, it takes a partially masked image plus noise; for reconstruction, it takes an original image and recovers it through a short SDE trajectory (t=0.02). The unlearning constraint is enforced at the score-function level during the continuous-time SDE process rather than at the data level, meaning the model's learned score field is explicitly deformed to avoid NSFG regions while preserving SFG fidelity. Evaluation uses 10,000 sampled images scored by external classifiers or CLIP to compute the Unlearning Ratio.

## Method

MSGM introduces a moderated score-matching objective that simultaneously approximates the SFG score (L_g, standard denoising score matching on retained data) and deforms the score away from NSFG data (L_f). Two variants are proposed: Orthogonal-MSGM minimizes the dot product between the learned score and the NSFG score, steering sampling into the orthogonal complement space of the NSFG score direction; Obtuse-MSGM relaxes this to a negatively correlated (obtuse) half-space, which is more effective when SFG and NSFG distributions overlap closely (e.g., facial attributes). The combined loss is α·L_g + (1−α)·L_f with α=0.99 by default, and an alternative updating schedule (L_f updated every 4th iteration) is used to balance convergence. The framework is plug-and-play across VP SDE, VE SDE, DDPM, and latent diffusion architectures, and supports both from-scratch training and fine-tuning of pre-trained models, enabling zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks like inpainting and reconstruction.

## Results

On MNIST (VE SDE), Orthogonal-MSGM reduces the Unlearning Ratio for digits 3&7 from 26.8% (Standard) to 1.2%, while increasing the NLL of the NSFG data from 2.78 to 13.23, compared to only 3.01 for Unseen by Re-training. On CIFAR-10 (VP SDE), MSGM achieves UR of 11.9% (Ort) and 12.4% (Obt) for automobile+dog forgetting versus 24.6% (Standard) and 14.2% (Unseen). On CelebA, Obtuse-MSGM achieves the lowest UR of 0.7% for 'bangs' feature removal, outperforming EraseDiff (1.2%) and Unseen (6.7%). For text-to-image unlearning on Imagenette (SD v1.4), MSGM achieves FID 3.08 and CR 0.00, matching or slightly exceeding ESD and EraseDiff (both FID 3.09, CR 0.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSGM consistently outperforms Unseen by Re-training (the claimed 'gold standard') on both UR and NLL across all four unconditional-generation datasets; the NLL gap on D_f is especially stark (e.g., 13.23 vs 3.01 on MNIST, 8.94 vs 2.32 on STL-10), confirming that re-training alone fails to suppress NSFG generation. Against EraseDiff, MSGM achieves lower UR on MNIST (1.2% vs 2.5%), STL-10 (2.4% vs 2.6%), and CelebA (0.7% vs 1.2% for Obtuse-MSGM), and substantially higher NLL on D_f (13.23 vs 3.74 on MNIST). On CIFAR-10, MSGM also beats EraseDiff on UR (11.9% vs 17.9%) and NLL (5.94 vs 4.10). For text-to-image unlearning on Imagenette, MSGM matches ESD and EraseDiff on CR (all 0.00) while achieving a marginally better FID (3.08 vs 3.09); crucially, MSGM requires no pseudo-labels, whereas EraseDiff with a semantically similar pseudo-label ('cyprinoid' for 'tench') fails to fully erase the target class (CR 0.47). Overall, MSGM wins or ties on every reported metric against both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SGM-SDE formulation of Song et al. [42], modifying the score-matching objective to introduce a moderated score adjustment (orthogonal complement or obtuse half-space) that steers the reverse SDE away from NSFG data. It departs from the broader machine unlearning literature for generative models (VAEs, GANs, DDPMs) by showing that the widely-adopted 'gold standard' of Unseen by Re-training is fundamentally ineffective for SGMs, and that existing DDPM-specific unlearning methods (EraseDiff, ESD) cannot be directly transferred due to the continuous-time score estimation objective. The work carves new ground as the first MU framework purpose-built for the SGM setting, while remaining architecturally agnostic across VP/VE SDEs, DDPMs, and latent diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that re-training without the target data fails to alter the learned score function in SGMs challenges a core assumption in the machine unlearning community and has implications beyond SGMs for any model where the data distribution's score is smooth and the removed data lies in a high-density overlap region. The plug-and-play design and zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks (inpainting, reconstruction) make the framework practically useful, but open questions remain about scalability to very large models (e.g., multi-billion parameter video diffusion), the behavior under adversarial re-learning attacks, and whether the orthogonal/obtuse score constraints generalize to non-Gaussian or highly multimodal data distributions. The authors note future extensions to skeletal time-series and video as natural next steps.
