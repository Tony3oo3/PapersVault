## Problem

Skeleton-based motion data in VR settings leaks personally identifiable information (body proportions, gait signature, limb lengths) that enables re-identification, linkage, and sensitive attribute inference attacks. Prior anonymization methods either require explicit knowledge of the attacker model, operate at the frame level limiting generalization, or severely degrade motion utility. This paper addresses the gap of achieving full-video-level anonymization through motion retargeting without prior knowledge of the attack model while preserving action-recognition utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence (25 joints × 75 frames × 3 coordinates) from the original user and a dummy skeleton sequence from a target actor, both normalized to fixed length T=75. At inference, the original skeleton is encoded by the motion encoder and the dummy by the privacy encoder; the decoder cross-combines these embeddings to produce an anonymized skeleton that retains the original action but adopts the dummy's body shape and movement style. No additional computational cost is incurred at inference beyond standard two-encoder retargeting, as the adversarial classifiers and discriminator are training-only components.

## Method

PMR employs a two-encoder (motion E_M, privacy E_P) / one-decoder (D) architecture augmented with cooperative-adversarial classifiers and a GAN-style quality controller. The motion classifier M cooperates with E_M (predicting action labels) while adversarially attacking E_P (forcing it to discard action cues); symmetrically, the privacy classifier P cooperates with E_P (predicting actor ID) while adversarially attacking E_M (forcing it to discard PII). Training proceeds in four stages: autoencoder warm-up, classifier pre-training on frozen encoders, unpaired cooperative-adversarial fine-tuning, and paired cross-reconstruction where E_M(original) is combined with E_P(dummy) to generate the anonymized output. The paired stage adds cross-reconstruction, triplet, latent consistency, and end-effector velocity losses to enforce clean motion/identity disentanglement and prevent foot-sliding artifacts.

## Results

On NTU-60 (Cross-View), PMR achieves 7.78% re-identification Top-1 accuracy (near random chance for 40 subjects) while retaining 35.69% action recognition Top-1, compared to DMR's 25.66% re-ID and Moon's UNet/ResNet methods' 2–3% action recognition. On NTU-120, PMR reduces re-ID Top-1 to 8.40% (vs. DMR's 13.55%) while maintaining 28.21% action recognition Top-1. The MSE between anonymized and original skeletons is 0.0138 (NTU-60) and 0.0122 (NTU-120), indicating modest geometric distortion that preserves motion semantics. Gender classification accuracy drops to 7.78% and linkage attack accuracy to ~50% (random), confirming broad privacy protection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMR [1,2], PMR trades a modest increase in MSE (0.0138 vs. 0.0071 on NTU-60) and a ~13-point drop in action recognition Top-1 (35.7% vs. 49.1%) for a dramatic reduction in re-identification Top-1 from 25.7% to 7.8%—a 3.3× improvement in privacy. Against Moon's UNet/ResNet frame-level anonymizers [13], PMR achieves comparable or slightly higher re-ID rates (7.8% vs. 3.0–9.1%) but preserves 10–20× more action recognition utility (35.7% vs. 1.8–2.6% Top-1), making the anonymized skeletons far more usable for downstream tasks. On NTU-120, PMR similarly outperforms DMR on re-ID (8.4% vs. 13.6%) while retaining reasonable action recognition (28.2% vs. 39.7%). Overall, PMR occupies the best privacy–utility Pareto frontier: it is the only method that simultaneously keeps re-ID near random chance and action recognition above 25%, whereas DMR leaks identity and Moon's methods destroy motion semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Motion Retargeting (DMR) framework of Aberman et al. [1,2], which uses a two-encoder/one-decoder architecture to transfer motion between skeletal structures, by adding adversarial and cooperative classifiers that explicitly disentangle motion from identity. It departs from prior skeleton anonymization work (Moon et al. [13], Carr et al. [3]) that performs frame-level adversarial perturbation and requires knowledge of a specific attack model; instead, PMR operates at the video level via retargeting onto a dummy skeleton and does not assume any prior knowledge of the attacker. The conceptual bridge between graphics-oriented motion retargeting and privacy-preserving data transformation is the paper's main positioning move.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Skeleton data in VR and motion-capture pipelines is widely collected yet its privacy risks (re-identification, linkage, sensitive-attribute inference) remain under-addressed; PMR offers a practical, inference-time-free anonymization mechanism that practitioners can plug into existing retargeting pipelines. Key limitations include the assumption of complete fixed-length buffers, the modification of motion style (e.g., standing-to-sitting) that may be unacceptable for some applications, and the still-modest action-recognition accuracy (~35% Top-1) that limits downstream utility. Open questions include real-time streaming, handling missing or noisy joints, and whether the approach generalizes to other skeleton sources or higher-fidelity capture systems.
