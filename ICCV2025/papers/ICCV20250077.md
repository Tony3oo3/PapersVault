## Problem

Vision-Language Models for autonomous driving operate as opaque black boxes that lack transparent, step-by-step reasoning, making them untrustworthy in complex or long-tail traffic scenarios. Existing CoT-based driving methods rely on unstructured open-ended generation and static single-frame inputs, limiting their ability to predict future events and produce controllable, interpretable decisions in dynamic environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes N_f consecutive frames across 6 camera views (front, front-left, front-right, back, back-left, back-right) at 70° FoV, each compressed to 336×336 pixels, alongside ego-vehicle state (position, speed) and a target point. Output is a structured four-step CoT chain (scene description → spatial risk reasoning → dynamic trajectory prediction → driving decision) whose final decision is fused with an E2E model's spatiotemporal features to produce waypoints and low-level control signals for the next timestep.

## Method

VLR-Driver builds on LLaVA-NeXT-Video 7B with an EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 visual encoder and introduces a hierarchical Spatiotemporal CoT (ST-CoT) that decomposes driving into a perception level (spatial object/risk identification plus temporal motion tracking across consecutive frames) and a decision level (risk-point recognition → driving-intention prediction → decision selection). Training follows a dual-phase strategy: first, LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning on the newly constructed VLR-Driver Dataset (20,000 multi-frame multi-view sequences with human-annotated CoT reasoning and decisions); second, Step-GRPO, an extension of GRPO that assigns per-step rewards to each of the four CoT reasoning stages and uses KL-divergence-based group comparison to update the policy, yielding fine-grained credit assignment that accelerates convergence and reduces erroneous reasoning.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive benchmark (44 closed-loop routes in CARLA), VLR-Driver achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 75.01, Route Completion (RC) of 86.09, and Success Rate (SR) of 50.00%, outperforming the best E2E baseline InterFuser (DS=63.81, SR=40.90%) by 17.5% in DS and 22.2% in SR. In advanced driving ability, VLR-Driver scores a mean of 55.82 (vs. 42.46 for InterFuser and 33.51 for LeapAD), with particularly strong Emergency Brake (72.72) and Traffic Sign (72.22) scores. The driving experience score (DE) reaches 125.22, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLR-Driver outperforms all 10 baselines on the primary Driving Score metric, achieving 75.01 vs. the best E2E method InterFuser (63.81, +17.5%) and the best VLM method LeapAD (55.18, +35.9%). On Success Rate, VLR-Driver reaches 50.00% compared to InterFuser's 40.90% (+22.2%) and LeapAD's 36.36% (+37.5%), indicating substantially more completed routes. In advanced driving ability (Table 2), VLR-Driver leads in OverTaking (37.50 vs. 25.00 for TCP/LMDrive), Emergency Brake (72.72 vs. 54.54 for InterFuser), and Traffic Sign compliance (72.22 vs. 61.11 for InterFuser), with a mean of 55.82 vs. 42.46 for the best E2E and 33.51 for the best VLM. Notably, VLR-Driver uses only camera and text modalities (C+T), yet surpasses all camera+LiDAR (C+L) E2E methods, suggesting the reasoning process compensates for the absence of depth sensors. The paper attributes the gap to E2E methods' inability to handle blocked-lane scenarios (e.g., illegally parked vehicles), where they stall indefinitely, while VLR-Driver's ST-CoT reasoning enables timely lane-change decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLR-Driver sits at the intersection of three research threads: VLM-based driving (DriveVLM, Senna, DriveGPT4), VLA models that unify perception and action (CoVLA, RT-2), and CoT reasoning applied to AD (DriveCoT, LanguageMPC, Sce2DriveX). It departs from prior CoT-based AD methods in two key ways: (1) introducing a hierarchical spatiotemporal CoT that operates over multi-frame sequences rather than static snapshots, and (2) structuring the reasoning into explicit perception-level and decision-level stages with step-wise rewards via Step-GRPO. The framework is built on LLaVA-NeXT-Video as the backbone VLM and EVA-CLIP as the visual encoder, with LoRA for efficient fine-tuning, positioning it as a reasoning-augmented VLA rather than a pure VLM or pure VLA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical trust gap in VLM-based AD: the inability to explain *why* a decision was made, which is essential for safety certification and driver confidence in long-tail scenarios. The 17.5% driving score and 22.2% success rate improvements over the best E2E baselines on Bench2Drive, combined with the structured reasoning output, make this a practically relevant step toward interpretable embodied driving. However, the evaluation is confined to CARLA simulation, and the authors themselves note that deploying LLM-scale models on real vehicles with limited onboard compute remains an open challenge. The VLR-Driver Dataset (20K annotated multi-frame sequences with CoT labels) is a useful resource for the community, though its generalizability beyond CARLA's 30+ scenarios is untested.
