## Problem

Dynamic dexterous grasping for human-to-robot handover lacks both a high-quality real-world dataset and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark. Existing datasets either focus on static object grasping or rely on synthetic handover motions that diverge significantly from real-world robot motion patterns, and most target two-finger grippers rather than five-finger dexterous hands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252315/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a k-frame observation window containing RGB images, depth images, the object point cloud, and the current dexterous hand state (22-DoF articulation pose plus global rotation and translation), and outputs a sequence of m future hand states that reconstruct the full hand mesh. The dataset provides 18 RGB views, 6 depth views, and 2 egocentric views per trial, with 3D annotations of human hands (MANO parameters), object meshes, and the ShadowHand across 4,282 handover trials.

## Method

DynamicGrasp decomposes the handover into three stages inspired by human receiving behavior: (1) Grasping Pose Preparation uses a cVAE conditioned on the object point cloud (encoded via PointNet) to generate diverse goal-pose candidates, which are filtered by a six-direction stability test in Isaac Gym and a collision check against the human hand; (2) Approaching Motion Generation is evaluated under two paradigms—an autoregressive MotionNet that predicts per-step hand state deltas from hand point clouds, velocities, and historical interactions, and diffusion-policy variants (2D RGB-based DP and 3D point-cloud-based DP3) that denoise trajectory distributions over m future frames; (3) Goal Pose Alignment applies linear interpolation between the current hand pose and the regenerated goal pose once the translation distance drops below a threshold (5 or 10 cm), iteratively updating until convergence below 1 cm. The cVAE is pre-trained on DexGraspNet (1.32M poses) and fine-tuned on DexH2R, while the motion generation models are trained via supervised imitation on the teleoperated dataset.

## Results

On the DexH2R test set (803 trajectories with unseen objects/subjects), DP3 achieves the best balance of success and safety in Hard Mode (U=5 cm): 27.1% success rate with 33.7% safety rate, outperforming MotionNet (26.6% success, 15.4% safety) and DP (8.3% success, 38.5% safety). In Easy Mode (U=10 cm), MotionNet leads in success rate (71.1%) but at the cost of the lowest safety (25.9%), while DP3 achieves 66.3% success with 50.1% safety. For grasp pose generation, DexgraspAnything with pretraining achieves 52.81% six-direction success rate and 1.64 cm penetration depth, substantially outperforming cVAE (35.00% and 2.23 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three approaching-motion baselines (MotionNet, Diffusion Policy, Diffusion Policy 3D) and one grasp-pose baseline (DexgraspAnything vs. cVAE) within the DynamicGrasp pipeline. In Easy Mode, MotionNet achieves the highest success rate (71.1%) but the lowest safety rate (25.9%) and highest penetration frames (8.3), reflecting its aggressive strategy that neglects collision avoidance. DP3 achieves a strong compromise (66.3% success, 50.1% safety, 5.0 pen frames), while DP is overly conservative (39.4% success) due to its slow approach. In Hard Mode, DP3 matches MotionNet's success rate (27.1% vs. 26.6%) while nearly doubling its safety rate (33.7% vs. 15.4%), and DP collapses to only 8.3% success. For grasp pose generation, DexgraspAnything with pretraining outperforms cVAE by 17.8 points on six-direction success (52.81% vs. 35.00%) and reduces penetration depth from 2.23 cm to 1.64 cm, confirming the benefit of physics-aware pose generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human-to-robot handover line of work (HandoverSim, GenH2R, MobileH2R) from two-finger parallel grippers in simulation to five-finger dexterous hands in real-world settings, and extends dexterous grasping methods (RealDex/MotionNet, Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy) from static object grasping to dynamic handover scenarios. The grasp pose generation stage builds on cVAE-based approaches from RealDex and DexgraspAnything, while the approaching motion generation stage adapts both autoregressive (MotionNet) and diffusion-policy (DP, DP3) paradigms to the dynamic setting. A key departure from prior synthetic handover datasets is the use of teleoperated ShadowHand data collection, which captures authentic human-like motion patterns rather than parametric 3D animation trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DexH2R fills a critical gap by providing the first real-world H2R dataset for dexterous hands, addressing the well-recognized sim-to-real gap that limits applicability of synthetic handover benchmarks. The benchmark's dual-mode evaluation (Easy/Hard) and safety-focused metrics (penetration depth, safety rate) offer a more realistic assessment framework than prior work. Open questions include whether the 4,282-trial dataset scales to broader object categories, whether the teleoperation-based data collection generalizes to autonomous deployment without human-in-the-loop, and whether the linear interpolation strategy in Goal Pose Alignment remains effective for faster or more erratic human handover motions.
