## Problem

Existing 3D scene captioning methods describe objects at only a single level of detail, capturing coarse scene-level relations but failing to model the object-part hierarchy or describe fine-grained localized properties such as material, color, and function of individual parts. This limits their utility for applications requiring both coarse identification (e.g., navigation) and precise manipulation (e.g., robotic interaction or accessibility descriptions).

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh M=(V,F) with vertex coordinates and RGB colors, which is voxelized into a sparse grid (limited to ~2 cm resolution by the sparse convolutional backbone). For each detected object instance, it outputs a bounding box, semantic class, binary instance mask, and two complementary text captions: a high-level object description and a fine-grained collective part-level description. The object-level caption is explicitly conditioned on the part-level caption, making the two outputs structurally dependent rather than independent.

## Method

ExCap3D uses Mask3D (a sparse convolutional UNet + transformer mask module) for instance segmentation, producing refined instance-aware queries and dense voxel features. Two separate transformer-based captioning heads generate object- and part-level captions, both attending via cross-attention to 3D backbone features aggregated over precomputed mesh segments. The key architectural insight is part→object information sharing: the object captioner's cross-attention keys are augmented with the part captioner's last-layer hidden states (projected and concatenated), so the object description is directly informed by the part description. Training is regularized by two consistency losses applied to the captioners' hidden states: a semantic consistency loss (symmetric cross-entropy over projected fine-grained latent class distributions) and a textual consistency loss (distance between sequence-aggregated hidden state vectors), ensuring the two captions remain semantically and lexically coherent.

## Results

On the ScanNet++ benchmark, ExCap3D achieves a CIDEr score of 32.9 for object-level and 32.8 for part-level captions, outperforming the strongest baseline PQ3D by 17% (32.9 vs. 27.9) and 124% (32.8 vs. 14.5) respectively. ExCap3D also leads on ROUGE (16.7 object, 21.9 part) and METEOR (18.0 object) across both detail levels. The joint multi-level captioning with consistency losses is the key driver, as the ablation shows the full model reaches 32.8 part-level CIDEr versus 17.9 for two independent captioning heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExCap3D decisively outperforms all six baselines on CIDEr, the metric most aligned with human perception, for both object-level (32.9 vs. 27.9 for PQ3D, the runner-up) and part-level (32.8 vs. 19.5 for Vote2Cap-DETR++). On ROUGE, ExCap3D leads at both levels (16.7 object, 21.9 part), surpassing Vote2Cap-DETR (12.9 / 20.3) and PQ3D (11.6 / 16.0). The method also wins on METEOR for object-level (18.0 vs. 17.2 for Vote2Cap-DETR) and BLEU-4 for part-level (4.8 vs. 4.3 for PQ3D). However, ExCap3D trails PQ3D on object-level BLEU-4 (1.2 vs. 1.9) and Vote2Cap-DETR on part-level METEOR (21.1 vs. 21.7), suggesting slightly less n-gram overlap in those specific settings. Cap3D and LLaVA-3D, which rely on VLM-based multi-view aggregation without 3D geometry, produce the weakest results overall, with CIDEr scores below 6 at both levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D dense captioning line (Scan2Cap, D3Net, Vote2Cap-DETR, PQ3D) by introducing a multi-level formulation that jointly produces object-level and part-level descriptions, a dimension absent from all prior 3D captioning work. Architecturally, it repurposes Mask3D's sparse-convolutional UNet and transformer instance queries as a shared backbone, then attaches two transformer captioners with a novel cross-attention pathway that feeds part-level hidden states into the object-level captioner. The dataset generation pipeline (multi-view VLM captioning + LLM summarization) follows the Cap3D paradigm but extends it to part-level via pseudo-segmentation from MaskClustering and SAM. The consistency losses (semantic and textual) are a new regularization idea not present in prior 3D captioning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ExCap3D Dataset (190k multi-level captions over 34k objects) fills a clear gap: no prior 3D scene dataset provides part-level descriptions, and the multi-level structure enables applications ranging from coarse navigation to fine-grained manipulation and accessibility descriptions. The 124% CIDEr gain on part-level captioning is striking, though baselines were not originally designed for that level, so the gap partly reflects task novelty rather than a fundamental architectural breakthrough. Key limitations include the ~2 cm sparse-convolutional resolution (restricting small/thin objects), class imbalance in ScanNet++, and the two-captioner design that limits information flow. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two detail levels, whether a single unified captioner could replace the two-head design, and whether the consistency losses transfer to other multi-granularity generation tasks.
