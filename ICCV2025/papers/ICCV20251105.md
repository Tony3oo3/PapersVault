## Problem

Current MLLM training applies auto-regressive supervision only to text tokens, treating visual embeddings from the vision projector as mere contextual cues without equivalent direct visual supervision. This asymmetric supervision, inherited from LLM training paradigms, fails to fully exploit rich visual information and limits fine-grained alignment between visual and linguistic representations, causing questions in early LLM layers to perceive inaccurate visual embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the standard vision encoder–vision projector–LLM architecture, taking RGB images (e.g., 336×336 processed by CLIP-ViT-L/14) and text questions as input, and producing text responses. The vision projector generates initial visual embeddings (m×d) that are consumed by the LLM alongside tokenized text embeddings; during training, the LLM's own shallow-layer hidden states at visual positions are extracted as supervisory signals, requiring no external models or annotations.

## Method

BASIC constructs a supervisory visual embedding by quadratically weighted summation of refined visual embeddings from the LLM's shallow layers (e.g., layers 1–16 of a 32-layer model), then applies two direct supervision losses to guide the vision projector: (i) a directional alignment loss that minimizes the L2 distance between normalized initial and supervisory embeddings on the unit hypersphere, and (ii) a semantic distribution loss that minimizes KL divergence between the softmax logit distributions obtained by projecting each embedding against the full LLM vocabulary. The supervision degree per image patch is modulated by mean attention scores from text tokens to that patch across shallow layers, so more important patches receive stronger guidance. The total training loss combines the standard auto-regressive LM loss with these two terms (weighted by λ₁=1 and λ₂=0.01), applied during both pre-training and instruction-tuning stages without requiring additional supervisory models or annotations.

## Results

On the 7B LLM setting (CLIP-ViT-L/14-336px + Vicuna-7B), BASIC achieves 79.2 on VQA-v2, 63.5 on GQA, 70.6 on ScienceQA-IMG, 68.8 on MMBench-EN, and 52.5 on VizWiz, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (same architecture and data) by +0.7, +1.5, +3.8, +4.5, and +2.5 respectively. The only benchmark where BASIC slightly underperforms LLaVA-1.5 is TextVQA (58.0 vs. 58.2), attributed to the semantic-focused supervision potentially blurring fine-grained textual content. On the 13B setting, BASIC similarly improves over LLaVA-1.5 across 7 of 8 benchmarks, with the largest gain on ScienceQA-IMG (+1.5) and MMBench-EN (+1.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BASIC's most direct comparison is against LLaVA-1.5 (same architecture, data, and training pipeline), where it wins on 7 of 8 benchmarks with the largest margins on ScienceQA-IMG (+3.8 for 7B, +1.5 for 13B) and MMBench-EN (+4.5 for 7B), while losing by a negligible 0.2–0.3 points on TextVQA. Against Qwen-VL-Chat, BASIC leads on 7 of 8 shared benchmarks (e.g., GQA 63.5 vs. 57.5, MMB-EN 68.8 vs. 60.6, VizWiz 52.5 vs. 38.9) but trails on TextVQA (58.0 vs. 61.5), reflecting Qwen-VL's stronger OCR capability. Compared to VW-LMM, BASIC surpasses it on all 8 benchmarks, with notable advantages on ScienceQA-IMG (70.6 vs. 68.1) and MMBench-EN (68.8 vs. 65.9). Against the discrete-tokenization approaches (LaVIT-v2, Emu1) and the early-fusion model (Fuyu), BASIC achieves substantially higher scores across all shared benchmarks (e.g., GQA 63.5 vs. 48.0 for LaVIT-v2, VQA-v2 79.2 vs. 74.2 for Fuyu), confirming the superiority of the vision-encoder–projector–LLM architecture with direct visual supervision. InstructBLIP and BLIP-2, which use Q-Former-based projectors, are outperformed by large margins on every shared benchmark (e.g., MMB-EN 68.8 vs. 36.0 for InstructBLIP-7B). IDEFICS, the weakest baseline, is surpassed by 20+ points on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP vision encoder + MLP projector + Vicuna LLM) and its two-stage training paradigm, but departs from the standard auto-regressive-only supervision by introducing two direct visual supervision losses (directional alignment via angular distance and semantic distribution via KL divergence on logit projections). Conceptually, it bridges mechanistic interpretability techniques (logit lens, cosine-similarity token matching) with self-distillation, using the LLM's own shallow-layer refined visual embeddings as a teacher signal for the projector. It positions itself against both discrete-tokenization approaches (Chameleon, LaVIT, SEED-LLaMA) that sacrifice visual fidelity, and continuous-regression approaches (Emu1/Emu2) that prioritize generation over comprehension, arguing that the vision-encoder-projector-LLM route still dominates for understanding tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental asymmetry in MLLM training: text tokens receive direct auto-regressive supervision while visual embeddings are treated as passive context, potentially leaving rich visual information underutilized. The method's simplicity (no extra models, no annotations, two additional loss terms) makes it broadly applicable across the vision-encoder-projector-LLM family, as demonstrated across multiple LLMs and vision encoders. However, the modest gains (typically 1–4 points) and the slight degradation on TextVQA (tiny-text recognition) suggest the shallow-layer semantic refinement may blur fine-grained spatial/textual details. Open questions include whether the quadratic layer-weighting and attention-based supervision degree generalize to much larger models or to video/multi-image settings, and whether the observed shallow-layer refinement pattern is an artifact of the specific Vicuna architecture.
