## Problem

Existing Referring Video Segmentation methods are constrained to object-centric benchmarks built from tracking datasets, limiting them to a narrow subset of what language can describe in video. While video diffusion models learn universal visual-language mappings at Internet scale, prior attempts to repurpose them for RVS (e.g., VD-IT) extract intermediate features and train new decoder heads, disrupting the pre-trained alignment and losing generalization to dynamic, non-object concepts like smoke or shattering glass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence at 512×512 resolution and a natural language referring expression as input. The video is encoded into latent space by a frozen VAE, and the text is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. Output is a binary segmentation mask produced in a single non-iterative forward pass: the denoising network predicts mask latents at timestep t=0, which are decoded by the frozen VAE into 3-channel masks, then binarized at a fixed 0.5 threshold. Ground-truth masks are broadcast from 1 to 3 channels for VAE encoding during training.

## Method

REM's key insight is to preserve the entirety of a pre-trained video diffusion model's architecture by shifting its objective from predicting Gaussian noise to predicting mask latents, rather than extracting intermediate features and training a new decoder head. The full denoising network (U-Net in ModelScope-1.4B or DiT in Wan-14B) is fine-tuned end-to-end, with the frozen VAE decoder reused to convert predicted latents into pixel-space masks. Training uses an L2 loss between predicted and VAE-encoded ground-truth mask latents at the minimum timestep t=0, on small-scale RVOS data (Ref-COCO + Ref-YTB). This design preserves the universal visual-language mapping learned during Web-scale generative pre-training, enabling zero-shot generalization to rare objects, 'Stuff' categories, and dynamic processes that object-centric methods cannot handle.

## Results

On the standard RVOS benchmarks, REM (Wan-14B) achieves 75.0 J&F on Ref-DAVIS and 71.7 J&F on Ref-YTB, setting new state-of-the-art. On the MeViS motion-expression benchmark, REM (Wan-14B) reaches 60.3 J&F, surpassing the previous best (GLUS, 51.3) by 9 points. Most strikingly, on the new Ref-VPS benchmark for dynamic processes, REM (Wan-14B) achieves 50.0 J zero-shot, outperforming the strongest baseline UNINEXT (28.7 J) by 21.3 points (74.2% relative improvement). On out-of-domain object tracking (BURST), REM (Wan-14B) scores 40.9 J versus 30.2 for UNINEXT, and on 'Stuff' segmentation (VSPW) it reaches 18.5 J versus 10.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REM outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark. On in-domain RVOS (Ref-DAVIS/Ref-YTB), REM (Wan-14B) beats UNINEXT [60] by 2.5 J&F points on Ref-DAVIS and 1.6 on Ref-YTB, despite UNINEXT being trained on 10+ localization datasets versus REM's single image and single video dataset. Against VD-IT [71], which shares the same ModelScope 1.4B backbone, REM (MS-1.4B) gains 3.2 J&F on Ref-DAVIS and 1.9 on Ref-YTB, demonstrating that preserving the full generative architecture (rather than extracting intermediate features) is critical. On MeViS, REM (Wan-14B) surpasses GLUS [26] (51.3 J&F) by 9.0 points and VISA-13B [61] (44.5) by 15.8 points. The largest margins appear out-of-domain: on Ref-VPS, REM (Wan-14B) scores 50.0 J versus 28.7 for UNINEXT and 37.9 for VD-IT; on BURST it reaches 40.9 J versus 30.2 for UNINEXT; and on VSPW 'Stuff' it achieves 18.5 J versus 10.1 for UNINEXT and 12.7 for VD-IT. ReferFormer [53] and MUTR [62], representing earlier query-based and bottom-up paradigms, are consistently outperformed by 10+ J&F points on in-domain benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes video diffusion models (ModelScope, Wan) for referring video segmentation by shifting the denoising objective from noise prediction to mask latent prediction while preserving the full generative architecture, including the VAE decoder. It departs sharply from the dominant prior approach (VD-IT, ODISE) of extracting intermediate features from the denoising network and training a new task-specific decoder from scratch. The paper also extends the RVS task beyond its object-centric formulation (RVOS) by introducing Ref-VPS, a benchmark for segmenting dynamic, non-object processes like smoke, shattering glass, and blooming flowers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that preserving the entire generative architecture (rather than isolating the denoiser as a feature extractor) is the key to maintaining Internet-scale visual-language generalization provides a clear, actionable design principle for repurposing diffusion models for perception tasks. The Ref-VPS benchmark fills a critical gap by testing RVS methods on dynamic processes that no existing dataset covers, and the 21-point gap over the best RVOS method on this benchmark highlights how object-centric training limits current approaches. Open questions include whether this architecture-preservation principle generalizes to other generative-to-perception transfers (e.g., audio, 3D), and whether the limited training data (Ref-COCO + Ref-YTB) is sufficient for truly open-world deployment or whether scaling training data further would close the remaining gap.
