## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods struggle with a fundamental trade-off: enhancing cross-modal alignment (fusibility) inevitably diminishes modality-specific complementarity, leading to over-fusion that destroys unique modal characteristics. Prior approaches lack a principled information-theoretic mechanism to systematically control this trade-off and fail to account for contextual variations where different modalities should contribute differently depending on the scenario.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of modality-specific inputs—audio spectrograms (224×224 or 128×128) with 3–4 video frames, RGB images with text descriptions, or 80-dimensional genomic CNA features with histopathology ROI images—each encoded by a dedicated unimodal encoder (ResNet-18/50, ViT-B/16, BERT-base) into fixed-dimensional embeddings. A semantic category label serves as the conditioning variable T fed into the NCE critic. The output is a single class label produced by a linear classifier over the concatenated unimodal feature vectors, with no explicit fused representation required.

## Method

InfoBridge reframes multimodal fusion as conditional mutual information (CMI) maximization between modality-specific embeddings given a context condition T. A learnable critic h(z^{m1}, z^{m2}, T) is trained via a conditional noise-contrastive estimation (NCE) loss to distinguish positive (same-sample) from negative (cross-sample) pairs, and the paper proves this NCE objective constitutes a lower bound on CMI with an inherent protective margin log(N1/N0) that prevents over-alignment. Training is adversarial: the critic is maximized to sharpen discrimination while unimodal encoders are optimized to push up the CMI lower bound, all combined with a standard cross-entropy task loss weighted by α. The context T (set to semantic category labels) focuses alignment on task-relevant features, allowing non-discriminative modality-specific information to remain intact. This yields a fusion-agnostic, plug-and-play regularization that can be attached to any encoder–classifier pipeline without modifying the fusion architecture.

## Results

InfoBridge achieves top-1 accuracy of 62.85% on CREMA-D, 68.19% on AVE, 84.03% on UPMC Food-101, and 92.15% on GBMLGG, outperforming all compared baselines on every dataset. The largest gains appear on GBMLGG (+1.48% over the best baseline InfoNCE) and CREMA-D (+1.26% over ReconBoost), while the margin on AVE is tighter (+0.14% over ReconBoost). The method consistently surpasses the unimodal baseline by 6–14 points across datasets, confirming effective multimodal fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoBridge outperforms all eight baselines on all four datasets. Against the strongest competitors, it leads ReconBoost [10] by +1.26% on CREMA-D, +0.14% on AVE, +0.77% on Food-101, and +2.27% on GBMLGG, and beats D&R [35] by +1.50%, +0.71%, +0.67%, and +1.71% respectively. The direct InfoNCE baseline [52] (which maximizes cross-modal MI without the protective margin or context conditioning) is surpassed by +2.24% on CREMA-D, +0.67% on AVE, +1.48% on Food-101, and +1.48% on GBMLGG, validating the benefit of the conditional formulation. Gradient-modulation approaches OGM-GE [55] and PMR [17] trail InfoBridge by 1.69–1.75% and 1.75–3.09% across datasets, suggesting that explicit information-theoretic control of fusion is more effective than post-hoc gradient balancing. Notably, InfoBridge is fusion-agnostic (works with any classifier or fusion operator), whereas OGM-GE and PMR require additional modules tied to specific architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoBridge extends the noise-contrastive estimation (NCE) framework for mutual information estimation by introducing a conditional context variable T and reframing the standard NCE bias-correction term log(N₁/N₀) as a 'protective margin' against over-fusion. It builds on the information-theoretic multimodal learning line (Yang et al.'s holistic interaction view, information bottleneck approaches, and disentanglement methods) and departs from heuristic gradient-modulation strategies (OGM-GE, PMR, D&R) by grounding fusion control in a principled conditional MI lower bound with excess-risk guarantees. The context-conditioning mechanism connects to prior work on adaptive fusion and modality-aware learning, but the specific formulation—tying the NCE estimator to a task-relevant condition and deriving the protective margin from Bayes' theorem rather than imposing it as a hyperparameter—is the paper's distinguishing contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical lens for the well-known complementarity-vs-fusibility trade-off in multimodal learning, and the protective margin interpretation gives practitioners a principled knob (via N₀/N₁ ratio) to control fusion aggressiveness. However, the practical gains over strong baselines are modest (1–1.5 points), the context T in the main experiments is set to ground-truth class labels (limiting applicability to unsupervised or label-free settings), and the framework is demonstrated only on two-modality classification with relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the conditional MI formulation scales to more than two modalities, whether the protective margin remains meaningful when N₀ is large (making the margin negligible), and whether the approach transfers to generative or retrieval-based multimodal tasks beyond classification.
