## Problem

Monocular 3D scene reconstruction remains underexplored because a single image provides far less geometric and contextual information than multi-view setups that exploit cross-view correspondences. Existing single-view 3DGS methods like Flash3D rely solely on visual features from the image and a 2D depth map, leaving the network without rich scene semantics or continuous 3D structural cues, which limits generalizability to unseen scenes and distant novel views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252219/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and camera intrinsics (focal length) as input. A pre-trained UniDepth model estimates a metric depth map, and LLaVA generates a one-sentence scene description whose text embeddings are extracted before linguistic decoding. The output is a set of per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (center, opacity, covariance, spherical harmonics coefficients) forming a 3D radiance field, from which novel views are rendered via rasterization in a single forward pass without scene-specific optimization.

## Method

CATSplat is a multi-resolution transformer (three layers at three resolutions) that enhances depth-conditioned image features from a ResNet encoder through two cross-attention mechanisms. First, contextual cross-attention injects text embeddings from LLaVA as keys/values, providing scene-level semantics (object identities, spatial relationships, scene type) that act as shared semantic anchors across similar scenes. Second, spatial cross-attention uses PointNet-encoded features from a backprojected 3D point cloud (unprojected from the depth map via camera intrinsics) as keys/values, supplying continuous x-y-z geometric structure beyond what a 2D depth grid offers. A learnable ratio γ in the residual connection balances the original visual features against the injected prior information, and self-attention refines the combined features before ResNet decoders predict per-pixel Gaussian parameters. Training is supervised with a composite loss (L1 + SSIM + LPIPS) between rendered novel views and ground-truth target frames.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, CATSplat achieves state-of-the-art single-view novel view synthesis: PSNR 29.09 / SSIM 0.907 / LPIPS 0.094 at n=5 frames, PSNR 26.44 / SSIM 0.866 / LPIPS 0.125 at n=10 frames, and PSNR 25.45 / SSIM 0.841 / LPIPS 0.151 at random frames (±30 range). In the extrapolation protocol (target views up to 45 frames away), CATSplat reaches PSNR 25.35 / SSIM 0.837 / LPIPS 0.159, surpassing all two-view 3DGS baselines. Cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation (trained on RE10K, tested on NYUv2, ACID, KITTI) confirms consistent gains over Flash3D across indoor, aerial, and driving domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATSplat outperforms all single-view baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor Flash3D [44], CATSplat gains +0.63 dB PSNR at n=5, +0.50 dB at n=10, and +0.52 dB at random frames, with consistent SSIM and LPIPS improvements. Older single-view methods (MPI, BTS, Splatter Image, MINE) trail by 1–5 dB PSNR, with the gap widening at larger frame distances. In the interpolation/extrapolation protocol (Table 2), CATSplat surpasses Flash3D by +1.36 dB (interpolation) and +1.25 dB (extrapolation) PSNR. Notably, in the extrapolation setting where target views are 45+ frames away, CATSplat (PSNR 25.35) exceeds all two-view 3DGS methods including MVSplat (23.04), pixelSplat (21.84), and latentSplat (22.62), despite using only a single input image. Two-view NeRF-based methods (pixelNeRF, Du et al.) are substantially weaker, scoring 20–22 dB PSNR in both protocols. Cross-dataset results (Table 3) confirm that CATSplat maintains its lead over Flash3D on NYUv2, ACID, and KITTI in zero-shot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATSplat extends the generalizable 3DGS feed-forward paradigm established by pixelSplat, MVSplat, and latentSplat to the single-view setting, where cross-view correspondences (epipolar geometry, cost volumes) are unavailable. Its closest predecessor is Flash3D, which already uses a pre-trained depth estimator to predict per-pixel Gaussians from one image; CATSplat departs from Flash3D by injecting two additional priors—text embeddings from a VLM (LLaVA) and 3D point-cloud features from a PointNet encoder—into the image feature stream via iterative cross-attention layers. The architectural choice to treat both textual and spatial cues as external 'key-value' sources in a multi-resolution transformer, rather than as simple channel concatenations, is the key methodological departure from prior single-view 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-view 3D scene reconstruction remains fundamentally underconstrained, and this paper demonstrates that combining semantic (VLM text) and geometric (back-projected 3D points) priors can meaningfully narrow the gap to multi-view methods, particularly in extrapolation where target views are far from the source. The ablation showing that VLM text embeddings outperform strong pre-trained visual encoders (DINOv2, ConvNeXt V2) as cross-attention priors suggests that language-driven scene semantics provide complementary information not captured by pixel-level visual features. However, the method is trained exclusively on RE10K (indoor home walkthroughs), and the authors acknowledge limitations with occluded or truncated regions; whether the text-guidance mechanism transfers well to domains where VLM descriptions are less discriminative (e.g., abstract or highly novel scenes) remains an open question.
