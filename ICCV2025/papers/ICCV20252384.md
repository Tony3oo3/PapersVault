## Problem

Simulating photorealistic, controllable weather effects (rain, snow, fog, clouds, puddles, snow coverage) in real-world videos remains challenging: physics-based methods require accurate 3D reconstructions that introduce blending artifacts, while general video editing models lack the realism for transient phenomena and offer no precise control over weather type or intensity. A further obstacle is the scarcity of paired training data showing the same dynamic scene under different weather conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-world video (encoded into Stable Video Diffusion's VAE latent space, spatially compressed 8× with C=4 channels) and a 6-dimensional continuous weather strength vector s ∈ R⁶ (one value each for cloud, fog, rain, snow, puddle, snow coverage). The synthesis model outputs a weather-affected video; the removal model outputs a clear-day video. The strength vector is broadcast into a spatial-temporal condition map S ∈ R^{l×h×w×6} that is concatenated with the source and noisy latents as UNet input. The two models can be chained sequentially for arbitrary weather editing (e.g., remove fog, then add rain at a chosen intensity).

## Method

WEATHERWEAVER decomposes weather simulation into two complementary conditional video diffusion models—a Weather Removal Model (w→c) and a Weather Synthesis Model (c→w)—both initialized from Stable Video Diffusion and sharing the same UNet architecture with zero-initialized extra channels to accept the concatenated source latent and weather strength map. The key design insight, borrowed from graphics engines, is to route all weather editing through a canonical weather-free intermediate rather than performing direct weather-to-weather translation, which simplifies each model's task and enables the removal model to serve as a pseudo-labeling engine. Training uses a denoising score matching objective in VAE latent space, with a staged curriculum: the removal model is first trained on simulation (Unreal Engine, 20.8k video pairs) and generation (SDXL + Prompt-to-Prompt, 1.15M image pairs) data, then used to auto-label 4.6k real-world video pairs, and finally the synthesis model is jointly trained on all three sources to combine precise controllability, scene diversity, and photorealism.

## Results

On weather synthesis, WeatherWeaver achieves the highest alignment scores (Align. VLM 77.29, Align. CLIP 0.22) and PickScore (20.75) among all methods, outperforming the next-best Text2Live (70.45 Align. VLM, 20.41 PickScore) by a clear margin. On weather removal, it leads in Align. VLM (71.61 vs. 66.39 for TokenFlow) and PickScore (19.10 vs. 19.07), while maintaining competitive structure preservation (DINO Struct. 2.09). Temporal consistency and motion smoothness are on par with all baselines (0.96–0.99 range) across both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeatherWeaver outperforms all four general video-editing baselines (Text2Live, AnyV2V, TokenFlow, FRESCO) on both weather synthesis and removal in alignment and preference metrics, with the largest gap over TokenFlow (62.38 vs. 77.29 Align. VLM on synthesis). For weather removal, the specialized methods WeatherDiffusion and Histoformer achieve near-zero DINO Struct. scores (0.26 and 0.05) because they fail to actually modify the input—producing outputs nearly identical to the source—resulting in very low alignment scores (22.79 and 13.30 Align. VLM) despite high structure preservation. WeatherWeaver's DINO Struct. of 2.09 on removal is slightly higher than TokenFlow (2.20) and FRESCO (2.71), indicating it modifies the scene more substantially while still preserving structure. In user studies, WeatherWeaver is preferred over all baselines in 52–94% of comparisons (human evaluators), with the strongest margins against TokenFlow and AnyV2V. The method also matches or exceeds baselines on temporal consistency and motion smoothness, confirming that the added weather effects do not introduce flicker or jitter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its base generative model but departs fundamentally from the 3D-reconstruction-based weather simulation line (ClimateNeRF, 3DGS-based approaches) by formulating weather editing as a pure video-to-video translation task in latent space. Conceptually, it draws from modern graphics engines' treatment of weather as an additive effect on a canonical scene, splitting the problem into a removal model and a synthesis model — a decomposition not present in prior general video editing methods (TokenFlow, FRESCO, AnyV2V) or specialized weather removal networks (WeatherDiffusion, Histoformer). The data curation strategy, combining Unreal Engine simulation, Prompt-to-Prompt image generation, and VLM-assisted pseudo-labeling of real videos, is the paper's most distinctive methodological contribution, addressing the paired-data scarcity that has limited prior weather editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, geometry-free pipeline for controllable weather simulation and removal in arbitrary real-world videos, with direct applications to autonomous driving perception, AR/VR content creation, and film production. The pseudo-labeling loop (using the removal model to generate training pairs for the synthesis model) is a generalizable data strategy that could transfer to other video editing domains lacking paired data. Key limitations include dependence on Stable Video Diffusion's quality ceiling (poor text/face preservation, nighttime struggles) and its offline, short-video constraint; integrating more efficient and higher-fidelity base models is the most obvious path forward. An open question is whether the 6-dimensional continuous strength vector generalizes to weather phenomena beyond the six modeled effects, or to more complex coupled atmospheric dynamics.
