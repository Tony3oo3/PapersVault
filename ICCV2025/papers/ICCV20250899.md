## Problem

Existing motion-controllable image animation methods struggle to achieve collaborative camera and object motion control with fine-grained control granularity. Pixel-offset-based representations (e.g., optical flow) lack 3D awareness and cause conflict control when camera and object motions co-occur, while reconstruction-based representations (e.g., point clouds) offer spatial consistency for camera control but are too rigid for fine-grained object motion editing. The paper identifies the need for a motion representation that is simultaneously 3D-aware, spatially consistent, and flexible enough to disentangle local object components from global viewpoint changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image at 768×512 resolution and two spatially aligned motion control signal layers: a camera control layer (a rendered checkerboard-textured world envelope showing viewpoint changes) and an object control layer (rendered colored unit spheres with perspective-scaled radii showing object displacements). These control signals are produced by rendering a simplified 3D scene constructed from user-specified trajectories or extracted from a source video. The output is a 16-frame video at 768×512 that preserves the reference image's appearance while exhibiting the prescribed camera and object motions.

## Method

The core contribution is a 3D-aware motion representation that simplifies the scene into unit spheres (key object parts) and a world envelope (a checkerboard cube for the static background), transforming both camera and object motions into unified, spatially aligned visual changes. A data curation pipeline constructs this representation from in-the-wild videos using SpaTracker for 3D point tracking and TartanVO for camera pose estimation. The animation framework is a U-Net-based diffusion model (initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5) with ReferenceNet for appearance preservation; two lightweight encoders process camera and object control signals separately to avoid RGB-level interference, then a convolutional fusion module merges them before injection into the denoising network. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) camera-only training on RealEstate-10K to learn envelope-to-scene correspondence, (2) collaborative motion training on WebVid-10M with dense spheres to align spheres with object parts, and (3) dense-to-sparse fine-tuning where the model adaptively determines each sphere's control range from a small set of 1–16 spheres selected by saliency and trajectory length.

## Results

On the RealEstate-10K test set for camera motion control, Perception-as-Control achieves FID 9.675 and FVD 52.421, substantially outperforming CameraCtrl (FID 14.538, FVD 178.497) and MotionCtrl (FID 17.081, FVD 122.570). On the WebVid-10M test set for collaborative object motion control, the method achieves FID 16.465, FVD 161.076, ObjMC 23.32, and SubCon 0.968, leading all baselines on every metric. The 16-frame variant (Ours14) further reduces FVD to 73.449 on RealEstate, demonstrating strong temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Perception-as-Control outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against CameraCtrl (Plücker embedding-based camera control), the proposed method reduces FID from 14.538 to 9.675 and FVD from 178.497 to 52.421 on RealEstate, while also achieving lower translation error (0.235 vs. 0.203 is actually slightly higher for TransErr, but RotErr is comparable at 0.757 vs. 0.648). Against MotionCtrl (flattened camera parameter control), the gap is even larger: FID drops from 17.081 to 9.675 and FVD from 122.570 to 52.421. For object motion control on WebVid, the method beats Motion-I2V (optical flow-based) by a wide margin (FID 16.465 vs. 60.643, FVD 161.076 vs. 367.524) and MOFA-Video (dense flow-based) by FID 16.465 vs. 26.895 and ObjMC 23.32 vs. 30.26. The paper also shows that incorporating its 3D-aware motion representation into CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl (denoted ++ versions) improves those baselines but still falls short of the full Perception-as-Control framework, confirming the synergy between the representation and the dedicated network design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-to-video animation line of work by introducing a 3D-aware motion representation (unit spheres for object parts + a world envelope for camera context) that unifies camera and object motion into spatially aligned visual control signals. It builds on Animate Anyone's ReferenceNet for appearance preservation and departs from prior motion representations—optical flow (Motion-I2V, MOFA-Video), Plücker embeddings (CameraCtrl, CamCo), and sparse point trajectories (DragNUWA, ImageConductor)—by arguing that a simplified rendered 3D scene avoids the conflict-control issues inherent in 2D pixel-offset representations when camera and object motions co-occur. The three-stage training strategy (camera-only → collaborative → dense-to-sparse) is a practical departure from prior single-stage or two-stage training protocols for motion-controllable diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in motion-controllable video generation: prior methods either handle camera or object motion well but struggle with fine-grained collaborative control, and the conflict between the two signal types remains unresolved in optical-flow or Plücker-based approaches. The 3D-aware representation is architecturally orthogonal (demonstrated by improving CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl when plugged in), making it a reusable component for future systems. Open questions include scalability beyond 16 frames at 768×512, sensitivity to errors in off-the-shelf 3D tracking and camera pose estimation, and whether the simplified sphere-plus-envelope abstraction generalizes to highly dynamic or deformable scenes where rigid unit spheres are insufficient.
