## Problem

Frame-based video LMMs suffer from temporal sparsity due to low frame rates, causing discontinuous motion representation and misalignment between textual timestamps and available frames. This limits fine-grained spatiotemporal reasoning, as the model cannot reliably infer scene content at arbitrary positions and times. The paper addresses this by introducing event cameras, which provide temporally dense but spatially sparse boundary signals, to complement the spatially dense but temporally sparse frame modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252599/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a frame video sequence (5–10 s), a synchronized event stream from an event camera, and a text instruction containing explicit spatiotemporal coordinates (bounding-box positions and duration intervals). The text coordinates are tokenized into learnable position and duration vectors that are additively embedded into every visual token before LLM processing. Outputs include detailed captions, VQA answers, and spatiotemporal grounding results expressed as spatial bounding boxes paired with temporal intervals, evaluated via s-IoU and t-IoU.

## Method

LLaFEA builds on LLaVA-1.5-7B with a CLIP-ViT-L-336px frame encoder and a ViT-based event encoder. A hierarchical fusion module first applies cross-attention spatial fusion (frame spatial-dense features as query, event spatial-sparse as key/value) and cross-attention temporal fusion (event temporal-dense as query, frame temporal-sparse as key/value), then a self-attention matching layer over the concatenated spatial and temporal features establishes global spatiotemporal associations. An MLP projects the fused features into the language embedding space, where learnable position and duration tokens are additively embedded into all visual tokens to enforce coordinate alignment. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: projector-only content alignment, fusion/matching module training, coordinate-embedding refinement, and full instruction fine-tuning on a real frame-event dataset (DSEC + CoSEC) with GPT-4V-generated annotations.

## Results

On the RealEvent-ST benchmark, LLaFEA achieves 3.65 DC, 4.82 VQA, 46.27 s-IoU, and 52.80 t-IoU, substantially outperforming the strongest frame-event baseline (Qwen2.5-VL w/ FI: 3.14 DC, 4.05 VQA, 13.29 s-IoU) by 16% on DC, 19% on VQA, and 3.5× on s-IoU. On the synthetic SynEvent-ST set, LLaFEA reaches 56.82 s-IoU and 68.35 t-IoU, nearly doubling the best competitor (VTimeLLM w/ FI: 39.09 t-IoU). On traditional frame-only video grounding benchmarks (VidSTG, HC-STVG), LLaFEA attains 57.5 m_tIoU and 42.4 m_vIoU on VidSTG, surpassing CG-STVG (51.4 m_tIoU) and CSDVL (33.7 m_vIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaFEA outperforms all 10 baselines across every reported metric on both SynEvent-ST and RealEvent-ST. The largest margins appear in spatiotemporal grounding: on RealEvent-ST, LLaFEA's s-IoU of 46.27 exceeds Qwen2.5-VL w/ FI (13.29) by 3.5× and GroundingGPT w/ FI (7.97) by 5.8×, while its t-IoU of 52.80 nearly doubles VTimeLLM w/ FI (23.55). Among frame-only baselines, Video-ChatGPT and Video-LLaVA report only DC/VQA scores and trail LLaFEA by 0.67 and 0.83 on RealEvent-ST DC respectively. On the traditional frame-only grounding benchmarks (Table 3), LLaFEA (adapted with optical flow) beats CG-STVG by 6.1 m_tIoU on VidSTG (57.5 vs 51.4) and by 3.4 m_tIoU on HC-STVG (56.2 vs 52.8), while surpassing CSDVL by 8.7 m_vIoU on VidSTG (42.4 vs 33.7). The paper notes that frame-event methods consistently outperform frame-only methods, confirming that event data compensates for temporal sparsity, but LLaFEA's hierarchical cross-attention fusion and self-attention matching yield substantially better grounding than simpler frame-event fusion approaches like GroundingGPT or Grounded-VideoLLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-1.5-7B as its base LMM and extends the line of video understanding LMMs (Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaVA, GroundingGPT) by introducing event cameras as a complementary modality to frame cameras. It departs from prior event-frame fusion networks (EvT, EST) that integrate features for motion or low-light robustness by proposing a structured hierarchical fusion—cross-attention spatial fusion, cross-attention temporal fusion, followed by self-attention matching—explicitly designed to produce spatiotemporal-dense representations for coordinate alignment. The spatiotemporal coordinate embedding mechanism (injecting textual position and duration tokens into visual tokens) extends prior point-to-point grounding approaches by operating in a temporally densified feature space rather than raw frame indices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of frame-based video LMMs—temporal sparsity causing misalignment between linguistic timestamps and visual frames—by leveraging the high temporal resolution of event cameras, a practical and increasingly accessible sensor. The constructed real-world frame-event dataset with spatiotemporal coordinate annotations (LLaFEA-RealEvent-ST) fills a gap in the literature and provides a resource for future event-assisted multimodal research. However, the method's reliance on brightness-change-triggered events limits its applicability to smooth, textureless scenes, and the authors acknowledge the need for complementary sensors (LiDAR, RGB-D) to handle such cases. Open questions include whether the hierarchical fusion generalizes to longer video sequences, higher-resolution event streams, or multi-camera event setups.
