## Problem

Combining LoRA with Federated Learning introduces two coupled challenges that existing methods address only in isolation: (1) Server-Side Aggregation Bias, where independently averaging LoRA matrices A and B at the server yields a product (Σp_k B_k)(Σp_k A_k) that diverges from the ideal weighted sum of local updates Σp_k B_k A_k, and (2) Client-Side Initialization Lag, where reinitializing LoRA modules each round causes slow, uninformative early gradients that degrade local updates under limited epochs. No prior federated fine-tuning method tackles both issues simultaneously while preserving communication efficiency.

## Input / Output

Each client receives 224×224 RGB images from a single domain (e.g., clipart, painting, sketch from DomainNet; autumn, dim, grass from NICO++) and trains rank-16 LoRA modules on a frozen ViT-B/16 or MLP-Mixer-B/16 backbone. The server outputs a corrected LoRA B matrix (B̄′ = B̄ + ΔB) and the averaged LoRA A matrix (Ā) to all clients each round, incurring no additional communication beyond standard FedIT. The final model produces class labels over 100 (DomainNet) or 60 (NICO++) categories.

## Method

LoRA-FAIR introduces a server-side residual correction: after computing the standard weighted averages Ā and B̄, the server computes the ideal global update ΔW = Σp_k B_k A_k and solves a small optimization for ΔB that minimizes the cosine-similarity gap between (B̄ + ΔB)Ā and ΔW, regularized by λ‖ΔB‖ to keep the corrected matrix close to the original average. This single correction simultaneously reduces aggregation bias (the product now approximates the true weighted sum of local updates) and mitigates initialization lag (the regularization preserves continuity of the LoRA B matrix across rounds, implementing the Avg-Initial strategy). Clients simply initialize their local LoRA as A_k = Ā, B_k = B̄′ and fine-tune as usual, requiring no extra computation or communication. The residual is applied only to B (not A), consistent with the observation that A captures general information benefiting from stable averaging.

## Results

On DomainNet (feature non-IID, 6 clients), LoRA-FAIR achieves 77.07% average accuracy with ViT and 65.87% with MLP-Mixer, outperforming the best baseline FlexLoRA (76.02% and 64.79%) and approaching the centralized upper bound (77.77% and 66.64%). On NICO++ (feature non-IID), LoRA-FAIR reaches 91.24% (ViT) and 83.56% (MLP-Mixer), again surpassing all federated baselines and closing the gap to centralized (91.51% and 84.50%). Under the more challenging feature-and-label non-IID setting (30 clients, 18 participating per round), LoRA-FAIR maintains its lead with 74.99% (DomainNet-ViT) and 90.04% (NICO++-ViT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-FAIR outperforms all four baselines across every dataset-model combination. FFA-LoRA, which freezes matrix A and trains only B, consistently yields the worst results (e.g., 72.82% vs. 77.07% on DomainNet-ViT), confirming that restricting trainable parameters severely limits optimization capacity. FedIT (direct FedAvg on LoRA modules) and FLoRA (stacked module transmission) perform comparably to each other (75.75% vs. 75.53% on DomainNet-ViT), indicating that FLoRA's fix for aggregation bias is negated by its reinitialization strategy (Challenge 2). FlexLoRA, which applies SVD to the summed local updates, is the strongest baseline but still trails LoRA-FAIR by 1.05% on DomainNet-ViT and 0.31% on NICO++-ViT. LoRA-FAIR's advantage is most pronounced on DomainNet-MLP-Mixer, where it gains 1.08% over FlexLoRA, suggesting the residual correction is particularly beneficial for architectures with weaker pre-trained representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition (ΔW = BA) and the standard FedAvg weighted aggregation protocol, positioning itself within the emerging line of federated LoRA fine-tuning methods. It departs from prior approaches—FedIT (naive FedAvg+LoRA), FFA-LoRA (freeze A, update only B), FLoRA (stack and redistribute all client modules), and FlexLoRA (SVD-based redistribution)—by arguing that each addresses only one of two identified challenges (server-side aggregation bias or client-side initialization lag) in isolation. LoRA-FAIR's key conceptual departure is the introduction of a server-side residual correction term ΔB optimized to simultaneously approximate the ideal global update and preserve the averaged initialization, unifying both concerns in a single lightweight objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical insight—that fixing aggregation bias alone (as FLoRA does) yields performance comparable to the uncorrected FedIT baseline—reframes how the community should think about federated PEFT: initialization continuity is as important as aggregation accuracy. The method is communication-efficient (no extra overhead vs. FedIT) and simple to implement, making it a practical drop-in improvement. However, the evaluation is limited to two vision foundation models (ViT, MLP-Mixer) on two datasets, and the absolute gains over the best baseline (FlexLoRA) are modest (≈1–1.5% average accuracy). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to language models, whether it handles heterogeneous client LoRA ranks, and whether the SGD-based ΔB optimization scales to very large rank or matrix dimensions.
