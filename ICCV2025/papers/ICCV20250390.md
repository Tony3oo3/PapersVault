## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for image super-resolution networks fail to adequately handle activation outliers: clipping them causes noticeable color distortion, while retaining them consumes bit-width that would otherwise represent normal activations. This paper identifies that these outliers are strongly correlated with image color information and that different network layers exhibit varying quantization sensitivities, both of which prior PTQ approaches overlook.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision SR network (EDSR, RDN, or SRResNet) and a calibration set of 100 low-resolution images sampled from DIV2K (no ground-truth HR images required). It outputs a quantized SR network with per-layer quantization parameters (weight bounds, activation bounds, and breakpoints) that can then be deployed to upscale LR inputs to HR outputs at 4× scale. The finetuning loss uses only LR images, making the entire pipeline data-free with respect to ground truth.

## Method

The core contribution is a piecewise linear quantizer that partitions each layer's activation range into a symmetric dense region [−bp, bp] and two outlier regions [la, −bp) ∪ (bp, ua], applying independent uniform quantization to each region so that bit-width is balanced between normal and extreme values. The breakpoint bp is initialized at the 99th percentile and refined via sensitivity-aware finetuning, which computes per-layer sensitivity scores from feature-map variance and weights a layer-wise cosine-similarity loss accordingly, directing optimization toward the most quantization-sensitive layers. Parameters are updated in a staged cycle (weight bounds → activation bounds → breakpoints) over 10 epochs using Adam, requiring only LR calibration images and completing in under 2 minutes—achieving performance comparable to QAT methods with at least a 75× speedup.

## Results

Under the challenging W4A4 setting, the proposed method achieves 31.54 dB PSNR on Set5 and 25.61 dB on Urban100 with EDSR, and 31.80 dB on Set5 and 25.93 dB on Urban100 with RDN, consistently outperforming all PTQ baselines. On the larger Test4K dataset, the method reaches 28.83 dB PSNR (EDSR) and 28.80 dB (SRResNet) under W4A4, surpassing the next-best PTQ method by 0.44 and 0.55 dB respectively. Compared to QAT methods, the approach achieves comparable PSNR (within 0.21 dB of the best QAT baseline ODM) while requiring no ground-truth supervision and delivering a 75× speedup in processing time (73 sec vs. 75×–185× for QAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three generic PTQ baselines, the proposed method dominates: it outperforms MinMax by 4.71 dB (EDSR) and 5.89 dB (RDN) on Set5 under W4A4, surpasses MSE by 3.80 dB and 6.25 dB respectively, and exceeds Percentile by 7.51 dB and 12.97 dB, confirming that naive clipping or percentile-based range selection severely harms SR quality. Among SR-specific PTQ methods, the approach beats PTQ4SR [36] by 1.03 dB (EDSR) and 3.48 dB (RDN) on Set5, and outperforms AdaBM [10] by 0.52 dB and 3.09 dB respectively, with the gap widening on detail-rich Urban100 (0.50 dB over AdaBM for EDSR, 2.55 dB over AdaBM for RDN). Against QAT baselines, the method achieves near-parity: it trails ODM [11] by only 0.21 dB on Set5 and 0.05 dB on Urban100, is within 0.06–0.10 dB of DDTB [52] and DAQ [13], and surpasses PAMS [21] by 0.20 dB on Set5, all while completing quantization in 73 seconds versus 75×–185× longer for QAT methods and without requiring ground-truth images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ-for-SR line initiated by PTQ4SR (Tu et al., CVPR 2023) and AdaBM (Hong et al., CVPR 2024), both of which addressed activation range issues through clipping or dynamic bit allocation but largely overlooked the role of outliers. The piecewise linear quantizer adapts the dual-region idea from Fang et al. (ECCV 2020) for general DNNs to the SR-specific problem of preserving color-critical outliers, while the sensitivity-aware finetuning loss is a new contribution that reweights layer-wise quantization error by measured variance. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior PTQ4SR/AdaBM by reframing the outlier problem not as a range-clipping issue but as a bit-allocation trade-off between two structurally distinct activation regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-QAT performance (within ~0.1–0.3 dB) without retraining or ground-truth supervision, with a 75× speedup, making high-quality SR quantization accessible for edge deployment. The empirical finding that activation outliers encode color information provides a mechanistic explanation for why naive clipping fails in SR (unlike classification tasks), which could inform quantization design for other color-sensitive restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the dual-region strategy generalizes to transformer-based SR architectures, higher scale factors (×8, ×16), and whether the sensitivity-aware loss can be extended to handle channel-wise or spatially-varying sensitivity rather than per-layer scalar weights.
