## Problem

Existing Lang-GS methods (e.g., LangSplat) require computationally intensive offline preprocessing with SAM+CLIP to generate pixel-wise language features, taking minutes per frame and limiting them to static, predefined environments. No prior work achieves online, near real-time, open-vocabulary 3D language mapping within a SLAM-GS system, which is essential for dynamic applications like service robotics and AR where immediate scene understanding is required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251596/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous stream of 640×640 RGB-D images and produces a 3D Gaussian map where each Gaussian carries a 15-dimensional compressed language code alongside RGB color. At inference, a text query is encoded via CLIP and compared against the rendered 2D language map (decoded back to 768-dim) or against 3D voxelized language features (via TSDF fusion) to localize target objects. The full pipeline runs at 0.6–0.8 s per frame on an RTX-3090, with the language-specific modules (CLIP encoding + SRD + online compression) contributing only 21 ms.

## Method

The framework integrates three key modules into a MonoGS-based SLAM pipeline. First, a ConvNeXt-based pixel-wise CLIP encoder produces a coarse 24×24×768 feature map, which a lightweight Super-Resolution Decoder (SRD) upsamples to 192×192×768 in 2 ms using hierarchical feature supervision. Second, a two-stage autoencoder compresses the 768-dim CLIP features: a generalized 8-layer MLP (trained on COCO) reduces to 32 dims, then an Online-Learned AutoEncoder (OLAE) with a 2-layer MLP further compresses to 15 dims while dynamically adapting to the current scene's dominant variance via incremental keyframe updates. Third, a color-language disentangled optimization assigns separate scale, rotation, and opacity parameters to color and language modes (while sharing position μ), preventing the homogeneous language features from distorting the texture-sensitive color Gaussians and improving both rendering quality and localization accuracy.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, Online Language Splatting (with Omnidata-trained SRD + OLAE) achieves mIoU 0.487 and localization accuracy 0.826, surpassing the best offline baseline LangSplat (mIoU 0.417, Loc 0.720) while running at 0.8 s/frame versus 2.8 min/frame (≈210× speedup). On TUM RGB-D, the method reaches Loc 0.917 (Scene 1) and 0.791 (Scene 2), outperforming LangSplat (0.850 / 0.783) at 0.6 s/frame. For 3D language localization, the online method achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.38 and EMD of 0.97, compared to LangSplat's 0.43 and 5.63. In SLAM-GS rendering, the method matches MonoGS (PSNR 35.81 vs 35.72, LPIPS 0.072 vs 0.075) while adding open-vocabulary language mapping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three offline Lang-GS baselines on Replica, Online Language Splatting wins on both mIoU and Loc: it beats LangSplat by +0.070 mIoU and +0.106 Loc, Feature3DGS by +0.128 mIoU and +0.071 Loc, and LEGaussian by +0.242 mIoU and +0.144 Loc, while being 40–210× faster. On TUM RGB-D, the method surpasses LangSplat in localization accuracy (0.917 vs 0.850 on Scene 1; 0.791 vs 0.783 on Scene 2) but trails slightly in mIoU (0.599 vs 0.646 on Scene 1), which the authors attribute to motion blur and lower image quality that favor offline global optimization. In the SLAM-GS rendering comparison (Table 3), the method matches MonoGS (its SLAM backbone) in PSNR (35.81 vs 35.72) and LPIPS (0.072 vs 0.075), slightly trails RTG-SLAM in ATE (0.397 vs 0.182 cm) due to RTG-SLAM's additional classical SLAM modules, and outperforms SplaTAM in PSNR (35.81 vs 33.39). For 3D language localization, the online method achieves a lower average EMD (0.97 vs 5.63) and comparable CD (0.38 vs 0.43) to LangSplat, with particularly large gains on objects like cushions (EMD 0.03 vs 0.10) and lamps (EMD 4.08 vs 38.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MonoGS (Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for the online tracking and mapping backbone, and on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the core scene representation. It departs from the offline Lang-GS line (LangSplat, Feature3DGS, LEGaussian), which all require minutes-per-frame SAM+CLIP preprocessing to generate pixel-wise language features, by replacing that pipeline with a real-time ConvNeXt-based CLIP encoder plus a Super-Resolution Decoder. It also extends prior SLAM-GS methods (SplaTAM, RTG-SLAM) that handle only geometry and texture by introducing open-vocabulary language channels into the online mapping loop, a combination the authors frame as the first of its kind.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for robotics and AR: a service robot or AR headset can now build a language-queryable 3D map in near real-time without any pre-computed annotations, enabling immediate natural-language interaction with novel environments. The 40–200× efficiency gain over offline baselines makes this feasible on embedded or mobile hardware. Limitations include the current 0.6–0.8 s/frame bottleneck inherited from MonoGS tracking, degraded performance on real-world sequences with motion blur (TUM RGB-D), and the fact that the online autoencoder's generalization to highly out-of-domain scenes remains untested. Open questions include scaling to large multi-room environments, handling dynamic objects, and whether the disentangled optimization strategy transfers to other multi-modality GS settings.
