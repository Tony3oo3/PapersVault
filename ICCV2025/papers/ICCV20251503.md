## Problem

Negative label-based zero-shot OOD detection methods (NegLabel, CSP) suffer from three failure modes: subcategory labels of in-distribution classes leak into the negative set, proper nouns from WordNet receive spuriously high similarity scores for certain in-distribution images, and images matching both in-distribution and negative labels are misclassified as OOD. These issues cause in-distribution samples to be incorrectly flagged as out-of-distribution, degrading detection reliability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a fixed set of in-distribution class labels (e.g., 1000 ImageNet-1K labels) as input, with no training data or fine-tuning required. It produces a scalar in-distribution score S(x) that is compared against a threshold γ to yield a binary in-distribution/OOD decision. The negative label set is pre-computed offline using WordNet and an LLM (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct), and the multi-matching score evaluates top-k=5 labels from both the in-distribution and negative sets per image.

## Method

NegRefine introduces two components on top of the CSP negative-label framework. First, NegFilter uses an LLM to iteratively remove proper nouns and subcategory labels from the initial negative set, ensuring cleaner semantic separation between in-distribution and negative labels. Second, a multi-matching score S_MM concatenates each top-k in-distribution label y_i with each top-k negative label ỹ_j into the text 'y_i and ỹ_j', then computes a softmax ratio between the concatenated text similarity and the negative label similarity alone; a high ratio indicates the in-distribution label genuinely contributes to describing the image. The final score combines the original NegLabel softmax score with the multi-matching adjustment: S(x) = S_NegLabel(x) + α·S_MM(x), with α=2. This design leverages CLIP's caption-like training to distinguish true multi-object/multi-aspect matches from spurious negative label matches.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K benchmark with four reliable OOD datasets (iNaturalist, OpenImage-O, Clean, NINCO), NegRefine achieves an average AUROC of 91.79% and average FPR95 of 30.12%, outperforming the state-of-the-art CSP by 1.82% in AUROC and 4.35% in FPR95. The method wins 7 out of 8 individual metrics, with the sole exception being a 0.03% AUROC deficit on iNaturalist. The largest gains appear on NINCO (AUROC 81.92 vs. 77.88, FPR95 61.96 vs. 68.65) and OpenImage-O (AUROC 95.00 vs. 94.09, FPR95 23.85 vs. 28.94), where the multi-label matching and proper-noun issues are most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NegRefine outperforms all six baselines on average AUROC and FPR95. Against the closest competitor CSP, it gains 1.82% AUROC and 4.35% FPR95 on average, with the most substantial improvements on NINCO (+4.04% AUROC, −6.69% FPR95) and OpenImage-O (+0.91% AUROC, −5.09% FPR95). Compared to NegLabel, the improvement is 2.46% AUROC and 4.99% FPR95. The gap widens considerably against earlier methods: CLIPN trails by 3.18% AUROC, GL-MCM by 4.25%, MCM by 5.75%, and ZOC by 16.55% in average AUROC. The only metric where NegRefine does not lead is iNaturalist AUROC, where CSP edges it by 0.03% (99.60 vs. 99.57), a negligible difference. On the harder ImageNet-99 (in) vs. ImageNet-10 (OOD) subset, NegRefine also outperforms CSP (FPR95 40.99 vs. 44.35), confirming the gains are not limited to the primary benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

NegRefine extends the negative label-based OOD detection paradigm introduced by NegLabel [16] and refined by CSP [6], which mine WordNet to construct a set of negative (OOD) labels and define a softmax-based in-distribution score using CLIP similarities. The key departure is twofold: (1) an LLM-driven filtering step (NegFilter) that removes subcategory labels and proper nouns from the negative set—issues that prior methods overlooked because they relied solely on semantic similarity cutoffs—and (2) a multi-matching score that concatenates in-distribution and negative labels into caption-like text, drawing on CLIP's training on multi-object captions. The paper also positions its multi-matching approach as more general than GL-MCM's local-features strategy, which only addresses multi-object images but not single-object images matching multiple labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode in negative-label-based OOD detection—subcategory overlap, proper nouns, and multi-label matching—that causes in-distribution samples to be falsely flagged as OOD, a problem with direct safety implications in deployed systems. The LLM-based label filtering is a practical, reusable technique that could benefit other label-construction pipelines beyond OOD detection. However, the improvements are modest (1.82% AUROC, 4.35% FPR95 over CSP), the method adds an LLM dependency to the pipeline, and the evaluation is confined to the CLIP ViT-B/16 model and the negative-label paradigm; whether the multi-matching score generalizes to other VLM architectures or to few-shot/supervised settings remains open.
