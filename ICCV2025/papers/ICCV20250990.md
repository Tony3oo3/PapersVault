## Problem

Existing video-based human pose estimation methods rely on a single modeling structure (convolutions or self-attention) that inherently biases toward either global motion contexts or local keypoint details, and attention-based approaches incur quadratic complexity that is prohibitive for high-resolution spatiotemporal sequences. Meanwhile, Mamba's selective scan, while offering linear-complexity long-range modeling, is restricted to 1D sequential data and lacks the multi-directional scanning needed for dense video analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250990/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a top-down cropped video clip of 5 frames (temporal span δ=2) of a single human bounding box enlarged by 125%, and produces a 2D pose heatmap for the center frame from which 15 keypoint locations are extracted. The ViT-Pose backbone (frozen, pretrained on COCO) extracts spatial features at 1/4 resolution (H/4 × W/4), and the entire spatiotemporal modeling operates on this high-resolution sequence of 15,360 tokens, a setting where standard Transformer blocks run out of memory.

## Method

GLSMamba is a decoupled, pure Mamba-based framework with two sequential components. The Global Spatiotemporal Mamba (GSM) flattens the 3D feature tensor along six tailored spatiotemporal routes (unified, space-wise, and time-wise, each in forward and backward directions) and applies independent S6 selective scans to each, then adaptively merges the multi-directional outputs via Spatial- and Temporal-Modulated scan Merging (STMM), which uses deformable convolutions to perform spatial- and temporal-guided feature compensation. The Local Refinement Mamba (LRM) partitions the GSM output into non-overlapping windowed 3D tubelets (e.g., 8×6×T) and performs frame-wise bidirectional selective scans within each tubelet to capture high-frequency local keypoint dynamics while preserving a sequence-size receptive field. The entire trainable module contains only 9.8M parameters (with the ViT-Pose backbone frozen), is trained end-to-end with a standard L2 heatmap loss, and achieves state-of-the-art mAP on four benchmarks with substantially fewer FLOPs than comparable Transformer-based methods.

## Results

On PoseTrack2017, GLSMamba-B (ViT-B backbone, 9.8M trainable params) achieves 86.9 mAP, surpassing the previous SOTA TDMI (85.7) by +1.2 and DiffPose (86.4) by +0.5. With the larger ViT-H backbone, GLSMamba-H reaches 88.0 mAP. On PoseTrack2018 and PoseTrack21, GLSMamba-H achieves 84.9 and 84.7 mAP respectively, and on Sub-JHMDB, GLSMamba-B attains 97.9 mAP. The method uses only 9.8M trainable parameters and 138.9 GFLOPs, compared to 71.1M params and 210.5 GFLOPs for PoseWarper, while a Transformer baseline at the same 1/4×T resolution runs out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLSMamba-B outperforms all CNN-based baselines by substantial margins: +5.7 mAP over PoseWarper (81.2), +4.1 over DCPose (82.8), +3.1 over DetTrack (83.8), and +2.1 over FAMI-Pose (84.8) on PoseTrack2017. Against Transformer-based methods, GLSMamba-B beats DiffPose (86.4) by +0.5 mAP and DSTA (85.6) by +1.3 mAP, while using only 9.8M trainable parameters versus 46–71M for the baselines. On PoseTrack2018 and PoseTrack21, GLSMamba-B similarly leads all competitors (84.2 and 84.1 mAP respectively). On Sub-JHMDB, GLSMamba-B (97.9 mAP) surpasses FAMI-Pose (96.0) by +1.9 but trails the post-processing method DeciWatch (98.8) by 0.9 mAP. Notably, GLSMamba achieves these results with a frozen ViT-Pose backbone (no fine-tuning), reducing trainable parameters by 86.2% compared to methods that fine-tune their backbones on VHPE datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba's 1D selective state space scan (S6) into a 6D spatiotemporal scanning mechanism (STS6D) and a windowed local scan (WSTS), positioning itself as the first pure Mamba-based framework for video-based human pose estimation. It departs from CNN-based VHPE methods (TDMI, FAMI-Pose, PoseWarper) whose fixed receptive fields limit global context, and from Transformer-based methods (DiffPose, DSTA) whose quadratic attention cost becomes prohibitive at high resolution. Conceptually, it also differentiates from VideoMamba's frame-by-frame bidirectional scanning by introducing multi-directional (unified, space-wise, time-wise) scanning routes with adaptive deformable-convolution merging, and by explicitly decoupling global and local modeling into separate Mamba modules rather than relying on a single unified structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled global-local Mamba design offers a practical path to high-resolution spatiotemporal modeling with linear complexity, as evidenced by the Transformer baseline OOM-ing at 15,360 tokens while GLSMamba-B processes the same resolution at 138.9 GFLOPs with only 9.8M trainable parameters. This efficiency advantage could make high-resolution video pose estimation more accessible in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the 6D scanning scheme generalizes beyond pose estimation to other dense video tasks (the authors mention 3D pose and video segmentation as future work), whether the windowed local refinement can be replaced by simpler alternatives, and how the approach scales to longer temporal spans or higher frame rates.
