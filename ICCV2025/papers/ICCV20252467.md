## Problem

Existing interactive head generation methods face two fundamental limitations: clip-wise paradigms (e.g., INFP) cannot achieve real-time inference because they require future audio to form a complete clip, and explicit speaker/listener generator-switching methods (e.g., DIM) handle complex conversational states (interruptions, pauses, feedback) poorly due to abrupt role transitions. Additionally, prior approaches rely on short audio-only context, limiting their ability to capture long-range conversational semantics and non-verbal visual cues from both participants.

## Input / Output

At frame T, the model consumes the agent's current audio A_T^a plus the user's and agent's audio and head motions from frame T-1 (all historical data is cached as compressed features), along with a reference face image. The output is a per-frame 70-dimensional motion vector (64-dim expression + 6-dim pose in 3DMM space) rendered to video via LivePortrait's optical-flow warping at 25 fps, with inference running at 31 fps to meet real-time constraints. The autoregressive formulation means only the latest frame's signals are explicitly processed each step; all prior context is maintained in sliding-window caches (chunk size c=6, context window w=512).

## Method

ARIG is a frame-wise autoregressive framework with three modules. The Interactive Behavior Understanding (IBU) module first merges dual-modal (audio + visual motion) signals per frame via an MLP, then performs bidirectional-integrated learning over short chunks (c=6 frames) using a dual-channel modulation-attention structure inspired by MMDiT to capture mutual interaction, compressing each chunk into a single summary token; these summaries are aggregated over a long range (w=512) via a decoder-only causal transformer to produce a contextual interaction summary (cis-token). The Conversation States Understanding (CSU) module combines binary VAD signals for both parties with the cis-token through cross-attention to predict one of seven conversational states (speaking, listening, interruption, feedback, pause, wait, overlap), yielding a state guidance vector. The Progressive Motion Prediction (PMP) module first predicts a coarse motion from audio, then refines it with state and context features through temporal layers, and finally samples the continuous motion via a DiffusionMLP (MLP with AdaLN) that models p(M_T^a | z) as a denoising diffusion process rather than a discrete categorical distribution, preserving micro-expression fidelity that VQ-based AR would lose.

## Results

On the RealTalk dataset (primary interactive head generation task), ARIG achieves RPCC 0.125, SyncScore 7.218, PSNR 29.67, SSIM 0.827, FID 21.64, SID 2.428, and Var 2.397, substantially outperforming DIM (RPCC 0.186, SyncScore 4.192, FID 26.29). On ViCo (listening head generation), ARIG attains the lowest FD (18.39 exp / 0.06 pose) and highest SID (4.82 exp / 3.94 pose) among all baselines. On HDTF (talking head generation), ARIG achieves the best FID (18.32), CSIM (0.876), and SyncScore (7.424), while matching EchoMimic on PSNR (28.63 vs 28.65) and SSIM (0.806 vs 0.805).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIM [26] on RealTalk, ARIG wins on every metric: RPCC drops from 0.186 to 0.125 (33% lower), SyncScore jumps from 4.192 to 7.218 (72% higher), and FID improves from 26.29 to 21.64. On ViCo (listening head generation), ARIG slightly edges out INFP [38] on FD (18.39 vs 18.63 for expression) and SID (4.82 vs 4.78), while clearly surpassing DIM, L2L [17], and RLHG [35] on all metrics (e.g., FD 18.39 vs 23.88 for DIM, 33.93 for L2L, 39.02 for RLHG). On HDTF (talking head generation), ARIG beats SadTalker [32] by a wide margin on FID (18.32 vs 23.46) and SyncScore (7.424 vs 6.792), and surpasses both Hallo [30] and EchoMimic [2] on FID and CSIM, while matching EchoMimic on PSNR/SSIM within 0.02. INFP [38] is not open-source, so quantitative comparison is limited to ViCo (where ARIG wins narrowly) and qualitative comparison on DyConv (where ARIG shows more natural lip movements and eye gaze). Overall, ARIG achieves the best or near-best result across all three task settings and all evaluated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARIG extends the interactive head generation (IHG) line initiated by DIM (explicit role-switching generators), AgentAvatar (LLM-driven motion tokens), and INFP (unified clip-wise generator) by replacing the clip-wise paradigm with a frame-wise autoregressive process, eliminating the inherent one-clip delay that prevents real-time operation. For motion modeling, it departs from the dominant VQ-based discrete AR approach (used in [24, 37, 18]) by adopting the non-quantized continuous AR formulation from recent image/video generation work [4, 13], where a diffusion procedure replaces the categorical codebook-index prediction. The dual-track dual-modal (audio + visual motion) input and the conversational-state understanding module are novel architectural additions not present in prior IHG methods, which typically condition on audio alone or use explicit role labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time interactive head generation is a practical bottleneck for deploying virtual agents in face-to-face conversations, and the frame-wise AR formulation directly addresses the latency problem that clip-wise methods cannot solve. The conversational-state insight—that identical audio produces different motions depending on whether the agent is speaking, listening, pausing, or being interrupted—provides a structured inductive bias that could generalize to richer multi-party or multi-turn settings. Limitations include reliance on LivePortrait for rendering (constraining visual quality), evaluation limited to dyadic conversations at 25 fps, and the need for manual state annotations during training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person scenes, whether the 15-step diffusion sampling can be further reduced without quality loss, and how well the model handles out-of-distribution conversational dynamics.
