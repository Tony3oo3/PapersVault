## Problem

Existing video customization methods can personalize subject appearances and single-object motions but fail at relational video customization, where a model must precisely capture interactions between two subjects and generalize them to novel subject categories. The core difficulty is that relations involve intricate spatial arrangements, layout variations, and nuanced temporal dynamics, causing current models to overemphasize irrelevant appearance details rather than the meaningful interaction itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250428/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of exemplar videos (approximately 20 per relation type) depicting a <subject, relation, subject> pattern along with a text prompt specifying the desired relation on new subjects, and produces a 61×480×848, 30-fps generated video. During training, Grounding DINO and SAM are used to derive per-subject masks that are temporally averaged to match the 3D VAE compression; during inference, Subject LoRAs are deliberately excluded so that only relational information transfers, preventing appearance leakage to the novel subjects.

## Method

DreamRelation builds on the Mochi MM-DiT base model and decomposes relational customization into two concurrent processes. In Relational Decoupling Learning, a relation LoRA triplet injects Relation LoRAs into the query and key matrices and two Subject LoRAs into the value matrix of full attention, guided by a hybrid mask training strategy that randomly selects which LoRA branch to update per iteration and amplifies the diffusion loss within mask-defined regions. The QKV placement is justified by an empirical subspace-similarity analysis showing that Q and K matrices share high Grassmann-distance similarity while remaining nearly orthogonal to V, making them natural carriers of relational versus appearance information. In Relational Dynamics Enhancement, a space-time relational contrastive loss (RCL) computes pairwise frame-difference features as anchors, pulls them toward positive samples from other videos of the same relation, and pushes them away from single-frame appearance features, using an InfoNCE objective with a FIFO memory bank to enlarge the sample pool. The total training loss combines the mask-weighted reconstruction loss with the RCL term, and the full pipeline is trained for 2400 steps with LoRA rank 16.

## Results

On the NTU RGB+D relational video customization benchmark (26 relation types, 260 evaluation prompts with novel subject pairs), DreamRelation achieves a Relation Accuracy of 0.4452±0.01, substantially outperforming the base Mochi model (0.2623) and all adapted baselines. It also attains the best CLIP-T (0.3248), Temporal Consistency (0.9954), and FVD (2079.87) among all customization methods, while remaining close to the base model's FVD (2047.37). The evaluation uses QwenVL-Max as a VLM judge for relation accuracy, repeated 10 times for robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamRelation decisively outperforms the base Mochi model on Relation Accuracy (0.4452 vs. 0.2623, a 69% relative improvement) while maintaining comparable FVD (2079.87 vs. 2047.37), confirming that the relation LoRA triplet and contrastive loss add relational capability without degrading video quality. Against ReVersion (an image-based relation inversion method adapted to video), DreamRelation wins on every metric, with the largest gap in FVD (2079.87 vs. 2682.69), indicating that image-based relational cues are insufficient for capturing temporal dynamics. Compared to MotionInversion (a motion-transfer method), DreamRelation achieves a 41% relative gain in Relation Accuracy (0.4452 vs. 0.3151) and superior Temporal Consistency (0.9954 vs. 0.9855), while FVD is nearly identical (2079.87 vs. 2084.51). Direct LoRA finetuning, though not a published method, improves Relation Accuracy over the base model (0.3258) but suffers from appearance leakage that degrades CLIP-T (0.2966) and FVD (2229.08), highlighting the necessity of the decoupling strategy. A user study with 15 annotators further confirms DreamRelation is most preferred across Relation Alignment, Text Alignment, and Overall Quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of video subject customization (DreamVideo, CustomCrafter), motion customization (MotionInversion, Motion Director), and relational image customization (ReVersion, RelationBooth), arguing that none of these adequately handle the joint spatial-temporal dynamics of two-subject interactions in video. It builds directly on the Mochi Video DiT and its MM-DiT full-attention architecture, using LoRA-based adaptation rather than inversion or full fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is the empirical QKV subspace analysis in MM-DiT, which motivates placing Relation LoRAs in query/key matrices and Subject LoRAs in the value matrix—a design choice grounded in the observed non-overlap between Q/K and V subspaces across multiple DiT architectures (Mochi, HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing relational video customization as a distinct task with a dedicated evaluation protocol (VLM-based relation accuracy rather than biased classifiers), the paper opens a new research direction for multi-subject interaction generation. The space-time relational contrastive loss is a reusable component that improves even unrelated baselines (MotionInversion), suggesting broader applicability to any video customization setting where temporal dynamics matter. However, the evaluation is limited to 26 human interaction types from NTU RGB+D and a single base model (Mochi), leaving open questions about scalability to more complex multi-subject scenes, longer temporal horizons, and whether the QKV-based LoRA placement generalizes to future DiT architectures with different attention designs.
