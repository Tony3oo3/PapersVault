## Problem

Diffusion models frequently fail to comply with complex text prompts (e.g., spatial relations, multiple objects) even at high classifier-free guidance scales. Existing negative prompting approaches either require users to manually craft negative prompts or rely on external captioning models (as in DNP) that estimate a single negative prompt once, ignoring that the optimal negation changes as the latent evolves through the diffusion chain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251840/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single positive text prompt as input—no negative prompt, no external captioning model, and no retraining are required—and produces a synthesized RGB image. It is a training-free sampling procedure that plugs into any CFG-compatible latent diffusion model (evaluated on SD and SDXL), operating entirely in the model's latent space with a tunable hyperparameter K controlling the number of DNS sub-steps per diffusion iteration.

## Method

ANSWER replaces the static negative prompt of DNP with an adaptive negative noise estimated at each diffusion step by running K_t steps of the Diffusion-Negative Sampling (DNS) chain from the current latent z_t. Rather than translating the negative image into a text caption (which loses information), the negative conditioning is performed directly in noise space: the DNS chain produces a noise estimate ε^{t_n} that is normalized to match the noise statistics at the current timestep and fed into the negative prompting equation. A scheduler gradually decays K_t from K at t=T down to 0, and for later steps (t < T/2) the method falls back to standard CFG to avoid disrupting the Markov chain. This reduces computational complexity from O(T²) (as in CNP) to O(KT) while eliminating all external resources, and the key insight is that the optimal negative condition is step-dependent, so a single fixed negative prompt is fundamentally suboptimal.

## Results

On four text-to-image benchmarks (Attend&Excite, Pick-a-Pic, DrawBench, PartiPrompts), SDXL+ANSWER achieves the highest CLIP score (32.91–34.09), Image Reward (0.79–1.20), Pick Score (22.39–22.88), and HPSv2 (28.97–30.95) across all datasets, while SDXL+DNP leads on Inception Score. On ImageNet, SDXL+ANSWER attains the best FID (67.67 vs. 69.8 for CFG and 70.11 for DNP) alongside the highest CLIP (30.64), IS (40.83), Image Reward (0.48), and HPSv2 (26.87). In win-rate terms, SDXL+ANSWER is preferred 41–52% of the time—roughly 2× more often than either baseline—and human AMT evaluation confirms a 46–61% preference rate for ANSWER over 21–32% for DNP and 8–20% for CFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CFG baseline (SDXL with classifier-free guidance only), SDXL+ANSWER wins on CLIP, Image Reward, Pick Score, and HPSv2 across all four benchmarks (e.g., CLIP 33.62 vs. 32.97 on Attend&Excite; HPSv2 30.92 vs. 30.07), while losing slightly on Inception Score (11.15 vs. 11.24). Against DNP (diffusion-negative prompting with external captioning), ANSWER similarly dominates on CLIP, Image Reward, Pick Score, and HPSv2 (e.g., Image Reward 1.20 vs. 1.03 on Attend&Excite; HPSv2 30.95 vs. 30.47 on Pick-a-Pic), but DNP retains a small edge on IS (11.65 vs. 11.15 on Attend&Excite; 15.34 vs. 15.19 on DrawBench). On ImageNet, ANSWER achieves the best FID (67.67) and IS (40.83), outperforming both CFG (FID 69.8, IS 27.26) and DNP (FID 70.11, IS 34.55), indicating it improves diversity rather than sacrificing it. In the AMT human study, ANSWER is preferred 46–61% of the time versus 21–32% for DNP and 8–20% for CFG, confirming the ~2× win-rate advantage seen in automated metrics. The one consistent loss for ANSWER is Inception Score on the four prompt-based benchmarks, where DNP's full-chain negative sampling produces slightly higher IS, though the gap is small (≤0.5 points) and ANSWER recovers the lead on IS for PartiPrompts and ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANSWER extends the negative prompting framework introduced by Liu et al. (ECCV 2022) and the diffusion-negative sampling (DNS) chain from DNP (Desai & Vasconcelos, 2024), both of which build on classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022). The key departure is from DNP's assumption that a single optimal negative prompt, estimated once via an external captioning model at t=T, suffices for all diffusion steps. ANSWER demonstrates that the optimal negative is time-dependent (n*(p,t) ≠ n*(p,T)) and replaces the text-domain captioning with a short K-step DNS chain executed in latent space at each step, yielding an adaptive negative noise estimate without any external model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play sampling modification for any CFG-compatible model, ANSWER offers a practical path to improved prompt compliance without retraining or auxiliary captioning models. The 2× human preference win rate across four diverse benchmarks and the elimination of the 'lost in translation' problem from text captions make it immediately deployable. Open questions include automatic selection of the K hyperparameter (currently fixed at 5), whether the t > T/2 cutoff is optimal for all prompt types, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D diffusion where the Markov chain is longer and the negative may drift more substantially.
