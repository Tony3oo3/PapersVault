## Problem

Despite rapid progress in video diffusion models, there is no objective, quantifiable, and interpretable method to assess how well AI-generated videos simulate the 3D visual world. Existing evaluations rely on subjective user studies or general-purpose metrics that do not specifically target 3D visual coherence, while 3D reconstruction—a natural verification approach—fails on in-the-wild videos due to unreliable camera pose estimation and absent multi-view cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

L3DE takes a video clip and internally extracts three monocular 3D proxies: per-frame DINOv2 appearance features, RAFT optical flow between adjacent frames, and UniDepth metric depth maps. It outputs four confidence scores in [0, 1]—one each for appearance, motion, geometry, and a fused overall 3D coherence score—along with Grad-CAM localization maps that highlight spatial and temporal regions responsible for the model's real-vs-synthetic decision. Training uses paired 4-second clips at 1024×576 and 7 FPS, with real videos from Pexels and synthetic counterparts generated by Stable Video Diffusion conditioned on the same first frame.

## Method

L3DE trains a 3D convolutional network with a combined classification and contrastive loss on paired real/synthetic videos, where the contrastive term pushes each synthetic video's penultimate-layer feature away from its nearest real counterpart in Euclidean space, enhancing discriminative power. The key design choice is replacing full 3D reconstruction with monocular 3D cues from foundation models (DINOv2, RAFT, UniDepth) as reliable proxies for appearance, motion, and geometry, making evaluation feasible at scale on in-the-wild content. A feature fusion module concatenates the three aspect-specific features to produce a holistic score, and Grad-CAM backpropagation to the last convolutional layer yields interpretable localization maps of 3D simulation failures. The approach requires no manually labeled defects or quality annotations, relying solely on the real/synthetic pairing for supervision.

## Results

L3DE Fusion achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.7566 with 3D reconstruction quality (PSNR-based) and 0.6460 with human ratings, substantially outperforming its individual aspect variants (Appearance: 0.7181/0.5643, Motion: 0.6669/0.4617, Geometry: 0.3142/0.3479). In direct comparison with existing video evaluation metrics, L3DE Fusion Score reaches a Spearman's ρ of 64.84 with human ratings, far exceeding VBench's Subject Consistency (3.90), Background Consistency (20.68), Motion Smoothness (19.99), and EvalCrafter's Temporal Consistency (13.85). On the 3D visual simulation benchmark, Kling 2.1 (0.8904) and Sora (0.8895) achieve the highest fusion scores, while all generative models remain well below the real-video upper bound of 0.9999, with motion (0.58–0.67) and geometry (0.68–0.76) scores notably lower than appearance (0.50–0.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L3DE against two existing video evaluation benchmarks: VBench [18] and EvalCrafter [26], using Spearman correlation with human ratings as the evaluation criterion. L3DE Fusion Score (64.84) outperforms all individual VBench metrics by a wide margin—Subject Consistency (3.90), Background Consistency (20.68), and Motion Smoothness (19.99)—as well as EvalCrafter's Temporal Consistency (13.85). The gap is particularly stark: L3DE's correlation is roughly 3× higher than the best VBench metric and nearly 5× higher than EvalCrafter's. Beyond correlation, L3DE uniquely provides spatially localized failure regions via Grad-CAM, a capability absent from both VBench and EvalCrafter, enabling interpretable diagnosis of specific 3D inconsistencies. The paper also validates L3DE against 3D reconstruction quality (3D-GS/SC-GS PSNR), where the Fusion variant achieves the strongest correlation (0.7566), confirming that its scores track objective geometric fidelity rather than merely perceptual similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the natural but impractical route of using full 3D scene reconstruction (NeRF, 3D-GS) to evaluate generative videos, arguing that unreliable pose estimation on in-the-wild content makes large-scale reconstruction-based evaluation infeasible. Instead, it repurposes monocular 3D cues from foundation models (DINOv2 for appearance, RAFT for optical flow, UniDepth for metric depth) as proxies for 3D structure, drawing on the 'three varieties of realism' framework from Ferwerda and the projective-geometry failure analyses of Sarkar et al. In the video-evaluation landscape, it positions itself as complementary to general-purpose benchmarks like VBench and EvalCrafter, which measure spatial/temporal consistency but do not specifically target 3D visual coherence, and introduces a contrastive-learning 3D CNN trained on paired real/synthetic data as a lightweight discriminative alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L3DE provides the first scalable, interpretable metric specifically targeting 3D visual coherence in generative videos, filling a gap between subjective user studies and impractical reconstruction-based evaluation. The benchmarking results reveal that even top models (Sora, Kling 2.1) achieve only ~0.89 fusion scores versus 0.9999 for real videos, with motion and geometry being the persistent weak points across all models while appearance is the primary axis of differentiation. Limitations include reliance on the quality of upstream foundation models (RAFT, UniDepth, DINOv2), training on SVD-generated videos of relatively low quality, and 3D reconstruction validation restricted to a small subset where pose estimation succeeds. Open questions include whether the monocular-cue approach captures all dimensions of 3D coherence (e.g., long-range geometric consistency, physical dynamics), how it generalizes to non-photorealistic or stylized content, and whether the contrastive training paradigm scales to the rapidly evolving landscape of generative models.
