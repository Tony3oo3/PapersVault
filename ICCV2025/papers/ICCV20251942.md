## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models suffer from prohibitive computational cost because long visual token sequences (e.g., 728 tokens per image) undergo full self-attention and FFN propagation through every decoder layer, despite empirical evidence showing their representations remain nearly unchanged across depth. Existing visual token pruning methods (FastV, VTW, PruMerge) either discard critical visual information or require manually tuned pruning ratios and exit layers, offering limited efficiency gains while degrading accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 384×384 by SigLIP SoViT-400M/14 into 728 visual tokens) and a text question as input, producing an autoregressively generated text response. A key architectural detail is that the vision sequence is consumed only as static K/V projections at each decoder layer rather than as a propagated hidden state, so the effective sequence length through the FFN is just the language token count (typically 32–200 tokens) rather than the combined vision+language length.

## Method

LVIDA replaces standard self-attention in each decoder layer with Disentangled Attention: only the language sequence produces Query projections, while both vision and language produce Key and Value projections, so attention is computed as Q_l · K_vl^T with a partial causal mask. This eliminates the quadratic (n+m)² self-attention term, reducing it to (n+m)·m, and removes the vision sequence from the FFN entirely (FFN cost drops from 8(n+m)d² to 8md²). To compensate for the vision sequence no longer propagating through layers, Layer-wise Vision Injection re-injects the original encoder features at every decoder layer via dedicated W_v^K and W_v^V projections, ensuring visual information is accessible at all depths. The net effect is a ~90% reduction in decoder FLOPs (e.g., 1750G→161G for TinyLlama-1.1B) with comparable benchmark performance, validated across 1B–7B decoders and nine multimodal benchmarks.

## Results

LVIDA achieves approximately 90% reduction in language decoder FLOPs across all tested models (1750G→161G for TinyLlama-1.1B, 10800G→1310G for Vicuna-7B) while maintaining comparable or slightly improved performance on most benchmarks. Time-to-first-token (TTFT) improves by 2.0×–4.0× depending on model scale, and peak GPU memory drops by 3.5–7.1 GB. On the Vicuna-7B decoder, LVIDA matches or exceeds the vanilla baseline on POPE (87.4 vs 86.6), MMMU (38.3 vs 36.4), and STVQA (53.7 vs 50.6), with only minor drops on TextVQA (61.4 vs 63.7) and MM-Vet (37.5 vs 40.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla LLaVA-1.5-SigLIP baseline (Vicuna-7B), LVIDA trades small drops on TextVQA (61.4 vs 63.7) and MM-Vet (37.5 vs 40.0) for gains on POPE (87.4 vs 86.6), MMMU (38.3 vs 36.4), and STVQA (53.7 vs 50.6), while reducing decoder FLOPs by 88%. Compared to visual token compression methods, LVIDA substantially outperforms LLaVA-Prumerge (GQA 62.4 vs 51.6, MM-Vet 37.5 vs 21.1, POPE 87.4 vs 76.3) and LLaVA-Prumerge+ (GQA 62.4 vs 56.4, MM-Vet 37.5 vs 25.0), indicating that aggressive token pruning causes significant information loss. In the homogeneous framework comparison (Table 4), LVIDA on Vicuna-7B achieves 62.4 GQA and 61.4 TextVQA at 1310 GFLOPs, versus FastV (K=3, R=75%) at 3970 GFLOPs with 58.8/58.1 and VTW (K=16) at 5710 GFLOPs with 59.2/52.7—LVIDA delivers both lower compute and higher accuracy. Against FastV and PyramidDrop on the cross-method table, LVIDA leads on GQA (62.4 vs 61.0/61.9), TextVQA (61.4 vs 58.4/58.5), and POPE (87.4 vs 85.2/86.0). The only method that slightly edges LVIDA on MME is the vanilla LLaVA-1.5 (1510.7 vs 1498.9), a marginal 0.8% difference, while LVIDA surpasses it on POPE and MMMU.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVIDA departs from the dominant paradigm in efficient LVLMs—visual token pruning (FastV, VTW, LLaVA-PruMerge, PyramidDrop)—by eliminating visual token removal entirely and instead restructuring the decoder's interaction mechanism. Conceptually, it extends the cross-attention fusion idea from Flamingo's dense blocks into a per-layer injection scheme, where the full visual sequence is re-injected at every decoder layer via disentangled attention rather than propagated through self-attention and FFN. The empirical motivation draws on FastV's observation that visual tokens become less informative in deeper layers, but LVIDA pushes this to its logical extreme: visual features need no layer-wise refinement at all, only repeated cross-modal access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~90% reduction in decoder FLOPs with near-parity performance across nine benchmarks and four LLM scales (1B–7B) makes LVIDA a practically significant efficiency technique for deploying LVLMs on resource-constrained hardware. The cosine-similarity diagnostic (visual sequences barely change across decoder layers) provides a reusable analytical tool for identifying redundant computation in other multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video inputs, whether it can be made plug-and-play without retraining, and whether the modest drops on TextVQA and MM-Vet indicate a fundamental information bottleneck in single-pass visual injection that deeper architectural changes might resolve.
