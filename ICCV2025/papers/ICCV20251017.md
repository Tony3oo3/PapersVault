## Problem

Existing BEV-based multi-modal 3D object detection methods rely on depth estimators (sensitive to depth errors) or transformer encoders (computationally expensive) for view transformation, and both suffer from ray-directional misalignment due to insufficient geometric guidance. This misalignment causes unintended information capture along the ray direction, degrading BEV representation quality and limiting detection accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surround-view camera images (1600×900, processed at 704×256 with ResNet-50 or 1600×640 with V2-99 + FPN) and a 32-beam LiDAR point cloud (20 fps, voxelized at 0.075×0.075×0.2 m over a 108×108 m × 8 m ROI) as input. It produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels for 10 traffic categories, using 900 object queries organized into 6 size-based groups (150 queries each) and a 180×180 multi-modal BEV feature map as the intermediate representation.

## Method

EVT introduces ASAP (Adaptive Sampling and Adaptive Projection) for view transformation: AS uses LiDAR BEV features to generate per-cell 3D sampling heights and adaptive weights, projecting them onto the image plane to sample multi-scale features without depth estimation; AP then refines the resulting BEV map with per-cell channel-wise kernels derived from LiDAR, correcting ray-directional misalignment. For detection, group-wise mixed query selection initializes query positions from heatmap keypoints while sharing group-level learnable feature embeddings (rather than instance-wise), and geometry-aware cross-attention in the transformer decoder relocates sampling points to predicted bounding-box corners aligned with heading (corner-aware sampling) and decodes them via sinusoidal position encoding of the transformed offsets followed by adaptive channel and spatial mixing (position-aware feature mixing). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end for 10 epochs with CBGS and query denoising, achieving 75.3% NDS on nuScenes test at real-time speed without ensemble or TTA.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, EVT (LiDAR+Camera) achieves 75.3% NDS and 72.6% mAP, setting a new state-of-the-art without model ensemble or test-time augmentation. On the validation set, EVT reaches 74.6% NDS and 72.1% mAP with V2-99 backbone, and 74.1% NDS with ResNet-50 at 8.3 FPS. The LiDAR-only variant EVT-L achieves 71.7% NDS and 66.4% mAP on validation at 12.1 FPS, outperforming other LiDAR-only baselines such as TransFusion-L (70.1% NDS) and CMT-L (68.6% NDS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVT outperforms all LiDAR+Camera baselines on the nuScenes test set, surpassing the closest competitor FusionFormer (75.1% NDS, 72.6% mAP) by 0.2% NDS while using only single-frame data without temporal fusion. Against other LC methods, EVT leads UniTR by 0.8% NDS and 1.7% mAP, MSMDFusion by 1.3% NDS and 1.1% mAP, and SparseFusion by 1.5% NDS and 0.6% mAP. The LiDAR-only variant EVT-L also beats TransFusion-L by 1.9% NDS and 2.2% mAP, and CMT-L by 2.0% NDS and 2.4% mAP on the test set, validating the query-based detection framework independently of camera input. Notably, EVT gains 3.2% NDS and 4.9% mAP from adding camera data over EVT-L, substantially larger than TransFusion's 1.5% NDS gain, indicating that the ASAP view transformation more effectively exploits complementary camera information. On the validation set, EVT (74.6% NDS) also surpasses FusionFormer (74.1% NDS) and UniTR (73.3% NDS) by 0.5% and 1.3% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVT positions itself within the explicit multi-modal fusion paradigm for BEV-based 3D object detection, building directly on deformable cross-attention (Deformable DETR) for the decoder while departing from both depth-based view transformation (BEVDet, BEVDepth) and query-based VT (BEVFormer, FusionFormer) by replacing them with LiDAR-guided adaptive sampling and projection. In the detection head, it extends the query-based framework lineage (PETR, TransFusion, CMT) by introducing group-wise shared embeddings for query initialization and geometry-aware cross-attention that relocates sampling points to object corners, a departure from center-based deformable attention. The paper explicitly contrasts with implicit fusion methods (TransFusion, CMT, DeepInteraction) that rely on iterative cross-attention over full sensor features, arguing that explicit BEV-space alignment is more efficient and effective for complementary feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVT demonstrates that LiDAR-guided view transformation can match or exceed depth-based and attention-based VT methods while eliminating their key weaknesses (depth estimation fragility and transformer encoder overhead), yielding a practical real-time multi-modal detector. The geometry-aware cross-attention's transferability to Stream-PETR suggests it could benefit other camera-only or multi-modal detectors beyond the BEV paradigm. However, the method's reliance on LiDAR for both sampling point generation and adaptive kernel derivation limits its applicability to camera-only settings, and temporal fusion—exploited by several competing methods (BEVFusion4D, FusionFormer)—remains unexplored. Validation is limited to nuScenes, leaving open whether the LiDAR-guided VT strategy generalizes to other sensor configurations or driving scenarios.
