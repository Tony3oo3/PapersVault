## Problem

Existing part segmentation methods treat each part as an independent category, ignoring the structural adjacency relationships among parts within an object and the coarse-grained distinctions between different objects. This coarse-to-fine cognitive gap means models lose critical spatial and hierarchical context, limiting their ability to produce accurate fine-grained part boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251238/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a set of text descriptions for both object-level and part-level categories as input. It produces two outputs: a coarse-grained object-level segmentation mask and a fine-grained part-level segmentation mask, both at full image resolution. A pre-constructed knowledge graph (built offline via GPT-4 Q&A on part adjacency) is also consumed as structured guidance during inference, and no data augmentation is applied during training.

## Method

KPS builds on the CAT-Seg cost aggregation architecture and introduces three knowledge-guided modules. The Structural Knowledge Guidance Module (SKGM) uses a 2-layer TAGConv GCN to embed part adjacency relationships (extracted from GPT-4) into CLIP text features, enriching them with structural context. The Coarse-grained Object Guidance Module (COGM) encodes object-level segmentation predictions through a multi-scale mask encoder and fuses the resulting features with structural-knowledge-embedded text features via cosine similarity to produce visual guidance. The Fine-grained Perception Module (FPM) replaces the original CLIP visual guidance with the coarse-grained mask features and the original text guidance with the knowledge-graph-enriched text features, then processes them through three stacked Swin Transformer blocks (local window → shifted window → local window) for precise part-level feature aggregation. Training uses a weighted sum of object-level and part-level cross-entropy losses with AdamW and a cosine schedule over 50K iterations on 4× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On PartImageNet, KPS achieves 72.69% mIoU and 82.46% mACC, surpassing the closest baseline CAT-Seg (67.73% mIoU) by 4.96 points. On Pascal-Part, KPS reaches 62.42% mIoU and 72.13% mACC, a 3.73-point improvement over CAT-Seg. In the open-vocabulary setting on Pascal-Part-116, KPS attains 33.92% hIoU, improving over the previous best PartCLIPSeg (30.67%) by 3.25 points, with particularly strong gains on seen categories (52.23% vs. 43.91% mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KPS outperforms all 11 compared baselines across all three datasets. On PartImageNet, the most direct comparison is with CAT-Seg [12] (same ViT-B/16 backbone, same CLIP-based cost aggregation architecture), where KPS gains 4.96% mIoU (72.69 vs. 67.73), confirming the improvement comes from knowledge guidance rather than a stronger backbone. Against part-specific methods like Compositor [20] (64.64% with Swin-T) and UniLSeg-100 [38] (63.87%), KPS leads by 8.05 and 8.82 points respectively. Classical segmentation models (MaskFormer, DeepLab v3+, SegFormer) trail by 7–12 points, underscoring the difficulty of part-level tasks. In the open-vocabulary Pascal-Part-116 setting, KPS (33.92% hIoU) surpasses PartCLIPSeg [13] (30.67%) by 3.25 points, and dramatically outperforms general open-vocabulary methods like CLIPSeg [41] (17.96%), VLPart [52] (14.39%), and ZSSeg+ [62] (6.20%), with the largest gap on seen categories (52.23% vs. 43.91% mIoU over PartCLIPSeg).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAT-Seg's cost aggregation architecture for open-vocabulary segmentation, extending it from object-level to part-level segmentation by adding two knowledge-guidance modules. It departs from prior part segmentation methods (Compositor, VLPart, PartCLIPSeg) that treat each part as an independent category, instead encoding inter-part structural relationships extracted via GPT-4 into a knowledge graph and propagating them through a TAGConv-based GCN into CLIP text features. The coarse-to-fine cognitive framing draws on brain-inspired perception literature, positioning the method as a hybrid data-driven and knowledge-guided approach rather than a purely learned one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that explicitly encoding structural priors (which parts are adjacent to which) via a lightweight knowledge graph can yield substantial gains (~5% mIoU) over a strong CLIP-based baseline without changing the backbone, suggesting that part segmentation has been underperforming partly due to missing relational context. The open-vocabulary results, however, reveal a persistent seen/unseen gap (52.23 vs. 25.11 mIoU), indicating that the knowledge graph's benefit is concentrated on categories it was constructed for and that generalizing structural priors to truly novel objects remains an open challenge. Future work could explore whether the GPT-4-extracted adjacency structure is optimal, whether richer relational types (containment, symmetry, functional) beyond binary adjacency would help, and whether the approach scales to the long tail of part categories in open-world settings.
