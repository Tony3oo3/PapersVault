## Problem

Portrait shadow removal remains challenging because large-scale paired real-world data is scarce, and existing approaches either target natural images (failing on facial fidelity), rely on synthetic data with domain gaps, or use GAN inversion that tampers with facial identity. The paper addresses how to robustly remove facial cast shadows while preserving identity, fine-scale details (eyelashes, moles), and overall naturalness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250146/cropped_page0_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB portrait image with cast shadows and a binary shadow mask (a dilated version of a trained shadow detector's output) as input, and produces a shadow-free portrait image. Notably, the method is highly robust to inaccurate masks—it only requires the mask to cover the shadow regions, even if it includes large non-shadow areas—and a shadow mask refinement step (Otsu thresholding on the inpainting residual) further corrects mask errors before final compositing.

## Method

The approach casts portrait shadow removal as diffusion-based inpainting with three stages. First, a shadow-independent structure extraction network (SE-Net, an encoder-decoder CNN) is trained on synthetic relit portraits (physics-based relighting applied to CelebA images) to produce structure maps that capture facial details while excluding shadow boundaries, supervised by a pre-trained PDG model's output on the original well-lit image. Second, a structure-guided inpainting diffusion model (U-Net, trained on FFHQ with random masking) reconstructs shadow-free portraits by conditioning the DDIM reverse denoising on the structure map, masked input, and mask. Third, a gradient-guided detail restoration diffusion model refines the result using gradients within shadow regions to recover fine-scale details (eyelashes, moles, spots) that the structure map cannot capture. The key insight is that conditioning inpainting on a shadow-independent structure map—rather than the raw image or a shadow-sensitive edge map—prevents the diffusion model from hallucinating or altering facial geometry behind shadows.

## Results

On the PSM real-world portrait shadow removal dataset, the full method achieves SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.056, and RMSE 17.162 (in LAB color space) across the entire image, outperforming all 8 baselines on all three metrics. In the shadow region specifically, the method reaches SSIM 0.973 and LPIPS 0.011, indicating near-perfect reconstruction of shadowed areas. The closest competitor, HomoFormer, achieves SSIM 0.786 / LPIPS 0.100 / RMSE 21.724, yielding a gap of ~0.044 SSIM and ~0.044 LPIPS in favor of the proposed method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines on every metric (SSIM, LPIPS, RMSE) in both the full-image and shadow/non-shadow region breakdowns. Among portrait-specific methods, PSM [56] (SSIM 0.782, LPIPS 0.074) is the closest on LPIPS but still trails by 0.044 SSIM and 0.018 LPIPS; UPSR [14] and BSR [28] lag further behind (SSIM 0.731 and 0.605 respectively), while IC-light [52] performs worst overall (SSIM 0.514, RMSE 62.539) due to its reliance on single-image relighting. Among natural-image shadow removal methods, HomoFormer [45] (SSIM 0.786) and ShadowFormer [10] (SSIM 0.772) are the strongest, yet both fall short by 0.044 and 0.058 SSIM respectively. The gap is most pronounced in the shadow region, where the proposed method's SSIM of 0.973 and LPIPS of 0.011 substantially exceed the next-best ShadowFormer (SSIM 0.926, LPIPS 0.027), confirming that structure-guided inpainting with detail restoration is critical for faithful shadow-region reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes portrait shadow removal as diffusion-based inpainting, building on the DDPM/DDIM framework for the generative backbone and the PDG portrait drawing model as the source of structural supervision. It departs from prior portrait shadow removal methods (PSM, UPSR, BSR, IC-light) that rely on synthetic paired data with simple photometric adjustments, GAN inversion, or single-image relighting, all of which struggle with identity preservation or shadow residuals. The key architectural departure is the two-stage design: a shadow-independent structure extraction network (trained via physics-based relighting synthesis) guides a structure-conditioned inpainting diffusion model, followed by a gradient-guided detail restoration diffusion model — a pipeline that has no direct precedent in the shadow removal literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Portrait shadow removal is practically critical for face recognition, detection, and editing pipelines, yet has been hampered by the absence of large-scale paired real-world data; this paper's relighting-based synthesis strategy for training the structure extractor (rather than the shadow removal model itself) is a useful workaround. The demonstrated robustness to inaccurate shadow masks (requiring only that the mask covers the shadow, not precisely delineates it) lowers the practical barrier to deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-shadow or dynamic lighting scenarios, whether the 9.5-second inference time and 185M parameter count can be reduced, and whether the structure-guided inpainting paradigm transfers to other portrait editing tasks beyond shadow removal.
