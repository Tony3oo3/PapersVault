## Problem

Dehazing models trained on real-world hazy–clean pairs suffer significant performance drops when applied to unseen real-world hazy images from different domains, and existing adaptation strategies (GAN-based haze synthesis, cycle-consistency test-time training) are limited by mode collapse, training instability, and inability to capture region-specific non-uniform haze patterns. The paper exploits the observation that haze patterns are smoother and more regular than scene content, making haze extraction fundamentally easier than clean-content recovery, to enable a transfer-based domain adaptation approach.

## Input / Output

PHATNet takes a hazy RGB image from an unseen target domain and a haze-free RGB image from the source domain (both at 1600×1200 resolution) and produces a single haze-transferred image that overlays target-domain haze patterns onto source-domain scene content. By cross-combining M target-domain hazy images with N source-domain haze-free images, it generates an M×N domain-adaptive fine-tuning set used for one-epoch offline fine-tuning of a pre-trained dehazing model, so no additional inference latency is incurred at test time.

## Method

PHATNet is a multi-scale CNN built around the Parametric Haze Disentanglement and Transfer (PHDT) module, a dual-branch network where an Atmospheric Light Encoder (ALE) and Transmission Map Encoder (TME) extract haze features from the hazy image, while a Content Encoder (CE) extracts scene features from the haze-free image. The key architectural choice is to extend the Atmospheric Scattering Model into a 128-channel latent space at H/8×W/8 resolution, producing haze-aware attention maps that are invariant to scene depth and thus avoid the ghosting artifacts of image-domain ASM. Features are fused via the ASM formula F^I = F^J × F^TM + F^AL × (1 − F^TM) and decoded by a Rehazing Encoder, with a 3-stage coarse-to-fine multi-scale structure for capturing haze at multiple spatial scales. Training is supervised by two losses: a Haze-Transfer-Consistency loss enforcing cyclic consistency between the transferred image and the original hazy input, and a Content-Leakage loss that feeds unpaired haze-free images through both branches to penalize any scene-content leakage into the haze encoders.

## Results

Under Setting 1 (source: NH-Haze20), PHATNet consistently improves average PSNR across four SOTA dehazing models: FocalNet +0.82 dB (16.25→17.07), Dehamer +0.96 dB (16.33→17.29), MITNet +1.48 dB (14.83→16.31), and SGDN +1.93 dB (14.84→16.77). Under Setting 2 (source: HD-NH-Haze), gains are even larger for weaker baselines: MITNet +3.35 dB and SGDN +3.18 dB. On the large no-reference RTTS dataset, PHATNet reduces BRISQUE from 34.27 to 22.21 (SGDN) and NIQE from 4.30 to 3.85, confirming gains in the absence of ground-truth pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the direct head-to-head comparison (Table 8, Setting 1, FocalNet as dehazing model, average PSNR across five target domains), PHATNet achieves 17.07 dB, outperforming all four competing methods: TMD [Liu, CVPR 2022] at 15.71 dB (+1.36 dB), PTTD [Chen, ECCV 2024] at 16.02 dB (+1.05 dB), HTFANet [Li, KBS 2022] at 16.21 dB (+0.86 dB), and D4+ [Yang, IJCV 2024] at 16.69 dB (+0.38 dB). PHATNet also offers a practical advantage: it operates offline (0.153 s per image on an A5000), adding no inference latency, whereas TMD and PTTD require online test-time optimization (1.328 s and 0.237 s respectively). Among the GAN-based haze-synthesis baselines, D4+ is the closest competitor but still trails by 0.38 dB; HTFANet, which uses vanilla ASM in the image domain, leaks content features into the transmission map, producing ghosting artifacts that PHATNet avoids by operating in latent space. The largest absolute gains from PHATNet appear on weaker baselines (MITNet, SGDN), suggesting the method is most beneficial when the underlying dehazer has limited domain robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHATNet carves new ground by extending the Atmospheric Scattering Model from the image domain into a latent feature space, enabling disentanglement of haze and content features that standard ASM cannot achieve. It departs from GAN-based haze synthesis methods (HTFANet, D4+) that suffer from mode collapse and instability, replacing them with a deterministic, physics-guided dual-branch network. The domain adaptation strategy also differs from prior test-time training approaches (TMD, PTTD) by generating an offline fine-tuning dataset rather than directly updating the dehazing model at inference, and the Content-Leakage Loss introduces a GAN-free unpaired consistency mechanism to enforce haze-content separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PHATNet offers a practical, model-agnostic plug-and-play framework for domain-adaptive dehazing that requires only one epoch of fine-tuning and adds no inference latency, making it attractive for deployment across diverse real-world haze conditions. A key limitation is its reliance on ASM as an inductive bias, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world haze distributions, and the authors acknowledge that comprehensive parametric-domain augmentation remains challenging. Open questions include whether the latent-space ASM extension generalizes to other atmospheric degradations (fog, rain) and whether the approach can be extended to video or high-resolution settings without the current 1600×1200 resolution constraint.
