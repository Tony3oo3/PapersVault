## Problem

Large-scale vision-language models achieve strong benchmark scores but fail at simple zero-shot compositional tasks that require recombining learned primitive concepts into novel compositions. This failure is especially acute for video, where spatiotemporal entanglement makes it difficult to separate spatial (object) and temporal (verb) semantics. The paper asks whether an information-theoretic representation learning objective can explicitly enforce the disentanglement needed for compositional generalization without relying on massive VLM-scale training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T frames at H×W resolution) or an RGB image, encoded by a frozen pre-trained backbone (VideoSwin-T or CLIP) into a feature map that is flattened and augmented with learnable spatiotemporal position embeddings. It produces 128 latent query vectors (M=128, D-dimensional) that are average-pooled into two separate D-dimensional vectors—one for spatial and one for temporal semantics—whose cosine similarities against learnable label embeddings yield independent probability distributions. The final composite action score is the product of the spatial and temporal probabilities, enabling zero-shot prediction of unseen object-verb or object-attribute combinations.

## Method

The core contribution is a resampler-based transformer decoder (6 layers) where each layer performs a cross-attention update from the encoder feature map to latent queries, followed by a single gradient-ascent step on a coding rate reduction objective. The rate reduction is decomposed into a global coding rate (GE operator) that pushes representations apart across K=24 subspaces and a conditional coding rate (GC operator) that compresses representations within each subspace, using sparse partitioning matrices that split the D-dimensional feature space. This unrolled optimization is interpreted as iteratively disentangling the spatiotemporally entangled encoder output into hierarchical spatial and temporal subspaces. Training is supervised with cross-entropy on composite action, spatial, and temporal labels, using cosine-similarity scoring against learnable category embeddings. The approach requires no label encodings in the model output, no prompt learning, and no dense spatial supervision, yet outperforms both compositional baselines and 8B-parameter VLMs on zero-shot compositional benchmarks.

## Results

On the Sth-Com dataset with CLIP backbone, the proposed method achieves 42.6 HM and 23.5 AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline C2C (41.0 HM, 21.7 AUC) by 1.6 and 1.8 points respectively. On CATER, the method reaches 74.0 mAP on unseen classes with only a 0.16 seen-unseen gap, surpassing C2C (56.4 mAP, 0.31 gap) and the 8B-parameter VLMs InternVL2-8B (37.0 mAP) and Qwen2-VL-8B (36.7 mAP) by a large margin. On C-GQA, the method achieves 23.6 HM and 8.2 AUC, setting a new state-of-the-art over C2C (23.0 HM, 7.7 AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across all three benchmarks. On Sth-Com (CLIP setting), it beats C2C [30] by 1.6 HM and 1.8 AUC, and surpasses prompt-based methods Coop [61], CSP [44], and SPM [37] by 6.0–7.1 HM points without any learnable prompts. On Sth-Com (VideoSwin-T setting), it improves over C2C (Enhanced) by 0.6 HM and 0.9 AUC, and over OADis [48] by 7.1 HM. On CATER, the method achieves 74.0 unseen mAP versus C2C's 56.4 and R3D's [5] 25.1, while ST-GT [58] (71.5 unseen) relies on ground-truth graph inputs unavailable at test time. Against 8B-parameter VLMs, the method outperforms InternVL2-8B [7] by 37.0 unseen mAP and Qwen2-VL-8B [50] by 37.3 unseen mAP. On C-GQA, it leads C2C [30] by 0.6 HM and 0.5 AUC, and CoT [25] by 1.5 HM and 0.8 AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coding rate reduction framework (Chan et al., JMLR 2022; Ma et al., TPAMI 2007) and the white-box transformer architecture (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2023) from image/point-cloud representation learning to spatiotemporal video disentanglement. It departs from prior compositional zero-shot learning methods—which rely on prompt engineering, graph propagation, or modular networks—by formulating disentanglement as an explicit information-theoretic optimization objective within a resampler decoder. The key architectural departure is the insertion of a gradient-ascent rate reduction step at each resampler layer, combined with learnable spatiotemporal position embeddings, to explicitly separate spatial and temporal semantic subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most striking result is that a lightweight resampler (128 queries, 6 layers on a frozen encoder) substantially outperforms 8B-parameter VLMs (InternVL2-8B, Qwen2-VL-8B) on CATER's unseen split (74.0 vs. ~37 mAP), suggesting that compositional generalization may not require massive scale but rather explicit structural disentanglement. However, this comparison is limited to a synthetic dataset and a single LoRA fine-tuning protocol for the VLMs, so the generality of the claim remains open. The approach's reliance on a fixed number of subspaces (K=24) and its evaluation on relatively small benchmarks (CATER: 5.5k videos; C-GQA: single-image) leave open whether the method scales to open-world, multi-object, or long-horizon compositional reasoning. The interpretability analysis (PCA alignment, attention maps) is a genuine strength that could guide future work on structured video understanding.
