## Problem

Deep learning models deployed in real-world settings (e.g., medicine) fail to generalize to domain-shifted target data, yet most domain adaptation methods require full access to source data or abundant target labels—both unrealistic under privacy constraints, storage limits, and label scarcity. Existing source-free DA approaches are designed for classification tasks and do not address the common neuroscience and medical imaging scenario of continuous-valued (regression) outputs with only a handful of labeled target samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained source model (not source data) and adapts it to a target domain given a small set of labeled target samples plus a larger pool of unlabeled target samples. Inputs are modality-specific: 128-channel EEG time series sampled at 500 Hz, 3D T1-weighted structural MRI volumes (160×192×160), or RGB images; outputs are continuous scalars (saccade amplitude in pixels, brain age in years, crowd count, or tumor size). The model is initialized from the source model's parameters and fine-tuned only on target data, with the unsupervised CUDA regularizer applied to all target samples regardless of label availability.

## Method

CRAFT extends the Contradistinguisher (CUDA) framework to source-free semi-supervised regression by combining a supervised MSE loss on labeled target data with an unsupervised joint-distribution regularizer over all target data, weighted by a tunable hyperparameter α. The unsupervised term normalizes each sample's predicted label density by the total probability mass across the dataset and aligns predictions with a target label marginal p(y), thereby removing source-induced predictive bias without any explicit representation alignment between domains. For continuous outputs, pseudo-labels are selected by maximizing the joint distribution over a discrete set of bin midpoints (a practical optimization strategy that does not constrain the model to discrete outputs), followed by a gradient-descent update of model parameters with those pseudo-labels fixed. Theoretically, the unsupervised objective is derived as a maximum-entropy prior on model parameters, making the full CRAFT loss a MAP estimation objective. The approach is architecture-agnostic and was instantiated with EEGNet-LSTM for EEG decoding and SFCN for MRI-based brain age prediction.

## Results

On the EEGEyeNet saccade amplitude prediction task (1% labeled target data), CRAFT achieves RMSE of 84.17±3.95 pixels and R=0.81, outperforming transfer learning (TL) by ~9% in RMSE (92.26) and all four competing SF-SSDA methods by >4%. On the TLSA brain age prediction task (20% labeled target data), CRAFT achieves RMSE of 7.14±0.11 years and R=0.51, outperforming TL by ~4% and other SOTA methods by >3%. CRAFT also outperforms all baselines by >5% on people counting and >2% on tumor size prediction benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT outperforms all four baselines on both primary neuroscience tasks. TASFAR [26] (pseudo-labeling via uncertainty-based recalibration) is the closest competitor, with CRAFT achieving 2.24 pixels lower RMSE on saccade prediction (84.17 vs 86.41) and 0.33 years lower on brain age (7.14 vs 7.47). DataFree [15] (feature-distribution alignment via KL divergence) is second-closest, with CRAFT improving by 3.47 pixels on saccade and 0.22 years on brain age. Progressive Mixup [37] (hybrid/self-mixup on confident/uncertain samples) performs worst overall, with RMSE of 135.70 on saccade prediction—far worse even than plain TL—suggesting its classification-oriented mixup strategy does not transfer well to regression. BBCN [35] (prototype-based bilateral consistency) also underperforms CRAFT by 5.63 pixels on saccade and 0.86 years on brain age, consistent with the authors' note that its class-level prototype generation is ill-suited to continuous outputs. The advantage of CRAFT grows with the fraction of unlabeled data, confirming its suitability for label-sparse settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT extends the Contradistinguisher (CUDA) framework, originally designed for unsupervised domain adaptation with full source data access and classification, to a source-free semi-supervised regression setting. It departs from the dominant representation-alignment paradigm (DANN, MMD, CMD) by instead using CUDA's joint-distribution normalization as a parameter-space regularizer that requires no source data. Among source-free methods, CRAFT occupies a distinct niche: unlike pseudo-labeling approaches (TASFAR) or feature-alignment methods (DataFree), it directly optimizes a combined supervised-unsupervised objective where the unsupervised term is derived as a maximum-entropy prior on model parameters, and unlike prototype-based SF-SSDA methods (BBCN, Progressive Mixup), it avoids class-level discretization that is ill-suited to continuous outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in medical and neuroscience applications where source data are inaccessible (privacy, storage) and target labels are scarce, yet predictions are continuous-valued. The theoretical grounding of the unsupervised loss as a MAP prior provides a principled justification for the hyperparameter α, and the sampling-bias experiment (Table 3) demonstrates that the method can correct for skewed training distributions when an informative marginal is supplied. Limitations include the reliance on a binning heuristic for pseudo-label selection (whose sensitivity is studied but not fully resolved), evaluation on relatively small datasets (N≈188 for MRI), and modest absolute improvements (3–9% RMSE) over strong baselines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large target datasets, whether the binning strategy can be replaced by differentiable alternatives, and how CRAFT performs when the source and target domains are more severely shifted.
