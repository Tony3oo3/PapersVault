## Problem

Existing unsupervised RGB-D point cloud registration methods struggle in two common real-world scenarios: low overlap between frames produces a high proportion of correspondence outliers that corrupt geometric fitting, and photometric inconsistency (reflections, viewpoint-dependent shading, rendering artifacts) prevents the model from learning discriminative features. Prior approaches rely on conventional differentiable renderers that project 3D points directly into image space, making them particularly vulnerable to both challenges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired point clouds P, Q ∈ R^{N×3} along with their corresponding RGB-D frames (RGB images and depth maps). Feature descriptors are extracted via KPConv on the point clouds and ResNet on the images, then fused through a multi-scale bidirectional fusion network. The output is a 6-DoF rigid transformation T = {R, t} (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) that aligns P to Q, produced in two stages: a coarse estimate from geometric fitting on filtered correspondences, followed by a refined estimate from Gaussian rendering optimization.

## Method

OG-UPCR introduces two key modules. The OCID module detects inliers via an overlapping constraint: it iteratively samples random subsets of initial correspondences, computes a Weighted SVD transformation for each, predicts the overlapping region, and applies reward/penalty factors to correspondence weights based on whether the predicted overlap ratio exceeds a threshold; a subsequent global-to-local stage uses second-order spatial compatibility (SC²) to select uniformly distributed seed points for geometric fitting. The GRPA module converts each 3D point into a 3D Gaussian (position, RGB color, radius, opacity), rasterizes both views via Gaussian splatting, and applies a per-pixel nonlinear photometric adaptation (exp(α)·R + β) to absorb viewpoint-dependent color shifts before computing photometric and depth consistency losses. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner using photometric, depth, and correspondence consistency losses without ground-truth poses.

## Results

On ScanNet (trained on 3DMatch, generalization setting), OG-UPCR achieves 97.9% rotation accuracy (<5°), 87.7% translation accuracy (<5 cm), and 95.5% chamfer accuracy (<1 mm), outperforming the best unsupervised method PointMBF (94.6%/81.0%/91.3%) and even surpassing the best supervised method PARENet (97.5%/85.9%/94.2%). On the challenging ScanNet1500 dataset (trained on ScanNet), OG-UPCR reaches 81.3% rotation accuracy (<5°) and 72.4% chamfer accuracy (<1 mm), exceeding the supervised BUFFER method (74.9%/66.5%) and the best unsupervised PointMBF (55.9%/48.5%) by large margins. Ablation shows the OCID module contributes +22.2% rotation accuracy on ScanNet1500, while GRPA adds a further +2.8% over OCID alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OG-UPCR outperforms all unsupervised baselines across every metric on both ScanNet and ScanNet1500. On ScanNet (3DMatch-trained), it beats the strongest unsupervised competitor PointMBF by +3.3% rotation accuracy (<5°), +6.7% translation accuracy (<5 cm), and +4.2% chamfer accuracy (<1 mm), while also surpassing the best supervised method PARENet on translation and chamfer metrics. On the challenging ScanNet1500 (ScanNet-trained), the gap widens dramatically: OG-UPCR achieves 81.3% rotation accuracy (<5°) versus 55.9% for PointMBF and 74.9% for the supervised BUFFER, a +25.4% and +6.4% margin respectively. Conventional methods (SIFT, SuperPoint, FCGF) are far behind, with rotation accuracy (<5°) below 71%. The ablation confirms that the OCID module (overlapping constraint + global-to-local inlier detection) is the primary driver of gains on low-overlap scenes (+22.2% rotation accuracy on ScanNet1500), while GRPA (Gaussian splatting rendering) provides an additional +2.8% by refining the transformation and improving photometric consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised RGB-D registration line initiated by UR&R and advanced by PointMBF, retaining their differentiable-rendering training paradigm and multi-scale bidirectional fusion backbone. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the conventional point-splatting renderer with 3D Gaussian Splatting to introduce per-pixel photometric adaptation parameters, and (2) introducing an overlapping-constraint-based inlier detection module (OCID) that departs from purely spatial-consistency or RANSAC-style outlier rejection by explicitly leveraging the geometric fact that inliers must lie within the overlapping region. The spatial compatibility metric from SC2-PCR is retained for local seed-point selection, but the global overlapping prediction step is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical failure modes—low overlap and photometric inconsistency—that limit the real-world deployability of unsupervised RGB-D registration, and the ScanNet1500 results (narrowing the gap to supervised methods from ~25% to ~2% on rotation accuracy) demonstrate tangible progress on a genuinely challenging benchmark. The repurposing of 3D Gaussian Splatting from novel-view synthesis to registration refinement is a conceptually interesting direction that could inform future work on learned scene representations for geometric tasks. Limitations include reliance on RGB-D input (not pure geometry), evaluation restricted to indoor scenes, and a modest runtime increase (~0.136s vs. 0.079s for PointMBF) that may matter in real-time SLAM pipelines. Open questions include whether the Gaussian parameters can be fixed rather than learned, and whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes.
