## Problem

Existing BEV-based perception methods are limited to 50–100 m ranges, but safe highway driving and heavy-duty trucking at speeds above 100 km/h demand perception beyond 250 m. Dense BEV representations grow quadratically in memory and compute with range, and the sparsity of objects at long distances makes supervised training prohibitively data-hungry. Prior self-supervised pretraining approaches are restricted to a single modality (camera-only or LiDAR-only), limiting their applicability to the multi-sensor systems deployed in autonomous vehicles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5 synchronized surround cameras (3848×2168 px, 5 Hz) and Aeva Aeries 4D FMCW LiDAR (up to 400 m range, 10 Hz) that provides both 3D point positions and per-point radial velocity, along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and vehicle odometry. LiDAR points are projected onto each camera frame to form 4-channel RGBD inputs before feature extraction. Outputs include a dense per-pixel depth map, a 4D occupancy-and-velocity field queryable at arbitrary (x, y, z, t), a predicted future LiDAR point cloud up to 3 s ahead, and 3D bounding boxes for 7 object classes out to 250 m, all derived from a single sparse voxel latent representation.

## Method

LRS4Fusion replaces the dense BEV grid with a fully sparse voxel representation that remains sparse across all pyramid scales, avoiding the quadratic memory blow-up of dense BEV at long range. Camera features are extracted with a Vision Mamba backbone and FPN, then lifted into 3D sparse voxels via a lightweight multi-scale recurrent depth-completion network that uses a Minimal Gated Unit (MGU) and a custom depth-integration module to fuse sparse LiDAR depth with image features; LiDAR features are encoded with voxel-wise PointNet and a sparse U-Net. The two modalities are concatenated in a unified sparse voxel space and processed through Completion and Contextual Aggregation blocks (following SparseOcc) to build a multi-scale sparse latent. A novel sparse windowed attention layer fuses the current frame with a velocity- and odometry-warped past frame, where each occupied voxel queries only a local 3D window of past occupied voxels, preserving sparsity while capturing motion. Self-supervised pretraining uses two auxiliary decoders—occupancy and velocity—that are supervised entirely by raw LiDAR returns (occupied vs. free-space labels and measured radial velocities), eliminating the need for manual annotations during the large-scale pretraining stage; a three-stage training schedule first learns the image encoder and depth model, then the full multi-modal sparse network with occupancy/velocity self-supervision, and finally adds a CenterPoint detection head.

## Results

On the proposed Long Range Dataset (up to 250 m), LRS4Fusion achieves 52.61 mAP and 58.06 NDS for 3D object detection, improving mAP by 26.6% (+11.06) over the best prior method SAMFusion (41.55 mAP). For LiDAR forecasting on NuScenes, the method sets new state-of-the-art Chamfer Distance across all history/future horizon combinations, achieving 0.48 CD (1s-in/1s-out, +61.6% over second best) and 1.23 CD (3s-in/3s-out, +28.9%). The depth completion module also outperforms CompletionFormer and OGNI-DC in both accuracy (MAE 3.46 vs. 4.98/4.76) and efficiency (64 ms, 1.3 GB vs. 188 ms/2.1 GB and 364 ms/2.4 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRS4Fusion outperforms all baselines on the Long Range Dataset object detection task. Against the strongest prior fusion method SAMFusion [39] (41.55 mAP), it gains +11.06 mAP (26.6%); against BEVFusion [36] (40.10 mAP) it gains +12.51 mAP; and against the LiDAR-only PointPillars [26] (39.31 mAP) it gains +13.30 mAP. The camera-only BEVFormer [30] (23.67 mAP) and its ViDAR-pretrained variant (24.51 mAP) are far behind, confirming that camera-only approaches struggle at long range. For LiDAR forecasting on NuScenes, LRS4Fusion beats ViDAR [70] by large margins (e.g., 1.25 vs. 1.97 CD on 1s-in/3s-out) and 4DOcc [24] (1.25 vs. 2.11 CD on 3s-in/3s-out), and also surpasses the VLM-based HERMES [79] on sub-2s horizons. Under the UnO [1] evaluation protocol, LRS4Fusion achieves 0.88 CD vs. UnO's 1.80 CD (51% reduction) and 0.72 NFCD vs. 0.89, while also outperforming SPFNet [60] and S2Net [61]. On the long-range LiDAR forecasting task, LRS4Fusion (15.82 CD, 1s-in/1s-out) substantially outperforms both 4DOcc [24] (18.93) and ViDAR [70] (58.23), with the gap widening at 3s horizons (42.93 vs. 47.81 and 56.20 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse voxel representation line (SparseOcc [48]) by maintaining sparsity across all pyramid scales and introducing a sparse windowed attention mechanism for temporal fusion that avoids the quadratic memory blowup of dense BEV grids at long ranges. It departs from existing self-supervised pretraining for driving (UnO [1], ViDAR [70]) by jointly pretraining a multi-modal camera-LiDAR encoder rather than a single-modality backbone, and leverages FMCW LiDAR velocity measurements for ego-motion-corrected temporal alignment. The depth completion module builds on iterative refinement architectures (OGNI-DC [83]) but replaces GRU update blocks with a lighter MGU and a custom depth-integration module, while the overall fusion strategy contrasts with LSS-based BEV methods (BEVFusion [36]) and depth-lifting approaches (SAMFusion [39]) by operating entirely in sparse 3D space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete gap: existing BEV perception methods are limited to ~50–100 m ranges due to quadratic memory/compute scaling, making them unsuitable for highway trucking where braking distances demand 250 m+ perception. The sparse voxel formulation and self-supervised pretraining (occupancy + velocity reconstruction from raw sensors) offer a practical path to scaling perception range without proportional increases in labeled data or compute. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even longer ranges or denser urban scenes, whether real-time inference on production hardware is achievable at 250 m, and how the method performs under adverse weather or sensor degradation beyond the Texas/New Mexico/Virginia captures.
