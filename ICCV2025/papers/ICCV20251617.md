## Problem

2D diffusion models generate diverse and realistic multi-view images but lack explicit geometric constraints, leading to view inconsistencies that corrupt 3D reconstruction. Conversely, native 3D diffusion models suffer from dataset scarcity, producing limited diversity and low-fidelity textures. The paper addresses this gap by enforcing 3D geometric consistency on a 2D diffusion backbone without sacrificing generative diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20251617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces both a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (renderable from arbitrary viewpoints) and N=16 multi-view images uniformly distributed around the object. The Gaussian Splatting output encodes per-Gaussian position, covariance, opacity, and color attributes, enabling real-time rendering. The framework also supports text-to-3D by chaining a text-to-image model (e.g., Stable Diffusion or FLUX) before the single-image-to-3D pipeline.

## Method

GSV3D builds on SV3D (a Stable Video Diffusion-based multi-view model) and introduces a Gaussian Splatting Decoder that converts multi-view latents into an explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation via a 16-layer ViT with DINO cross-attention and a pixel-shuffle upsampler. The key innovation is geometric distillation: during training, the frozen Gaussian Splatting Decoder renders RGB and depth from the diffusion model's denoised latents, and the resulting 3D loss (RGB MSE + depth MSE) is backpropagated through a LoRA module on the UNet, providing explicit geometric supervision that amplifies inter-view inconsistencies. Training is two-stage: first the decoder is trained from scratch on 100K Objaverse objects (80K steps), then the diffusion model is distilled with the frozen decoder for 40K steps. This explicit 3D constraint corrects view inconsistencies that implicit attention-based methods cannot resolve, while preserving the generative diversity of the 2D diffusion prior.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset, GSV3D achieves the best performance across all appearance and geometry metrics. For appearance, it reaches PSNR 20.39, SSIM 0.884, FID 50.79, and KID 0.21%, substantially outperforming the strongest 2D baseline SV3D (PSNR 17.77, FID 74.86, KID 0.80%). For geometry, GSV3D attains Chamfer Distance 0.100, IoU 0.721, and F-Score 0.661, compared to the best 2D baseline Zero123Plus (CD 0.165, IoU 0.525, F-Score 0.409). In the user study, GSV3D receives the highest scores for both appearance (6.31/7) and geometry (6.69/7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSV3D outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest 2D baseline SV3D (its direct predecessor), GSV3D improves PSNR by 2.6 dB (20.39 vs 17.77), reduces FID by 32% (50.79 vs 74.86), and nearly doubles IoU (0.721 vs 0.418) and F-Score (0.661 vs 0.311). Against the best 3D-native baseline TGS, GSV3D achieves higher PSNR (20.39 vs 18.87) and dramatically better geometry (IoU 0.721 vs 0.415, F-Score 0.661 vs 0.232), while also surpassing it on FID (50.79 vs 85.25). The 2D baselines LGM, Zero123Plus, and Era3D all suffer from multi-view inconsistency that degrades both appearance (ghosting, blurriness) and geometry (holes, distortions); GSV3D's geometric distillation via the frozen Gaussian Splatting Decoder addresses this, yielding the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.100) and highest user-study scores (6.31 appearance, 6.69 geometry) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSV3D extends SV3D (itself built on Stable Video Diffusion) by introducing a Gaussian Splatting Decoder—architecturally inspired by GRM—that converts multi-view latents into an explicit 3D representation. The key departure from prior 2D-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Zero123++, MVDream, Era3D) is the use of 'geometric distillation': a frozen 3D decoder provides explicit geometric supervision (RGB + depth losses) during LoRA-based fine-tuning of the diffusion model, rather than relying solely on implicit attention mechanisms for multi-view consistency. This positions the work between native 3D diffusion (limited by dataset scarcity) and 2D diffusion-based approaches (limited by geometric inconsistency), leveraging the diversity of 2D priors while enforcing 3D structural coherence through an explicit representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric distillation framework offers a practical recipe for injecting 3D awareness into pretrained 2D diffusion models without retraining from scratch, which could be adapted to other video/multi-view diffusion backbones. However, the approach is constrained to object-level generation, relies on a frozen decoder (limiting how much 3D knowledge can be transferred), and is evaluated on a single held-out dataset (GSO, 100 objects). Open questions include whether the method scales to scene-level or articulated-object generation, whether unfreezing the decoder during distillation would yield further gains, and how the approach behaves under domain shift beyond the Objaverse training distribution.
