## Problem

Deploying image enhancement on mobile devices remains challenging because Transformer- and Diffusion-based methods are too computationally expensive, while existing lightweight CNNs sacrifice enhancement quality across diverse degradation scenarios. The paper targets the gap of achieving real-time inference (100+ FPS) on resource-constrained hardware with competitive image quality using an extremely compact model of only ~4K parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single degraded image as input—low-light RGB, underwater RGB, or a 256×256 RAW Bayer pattern for the ISP task—and produces a single enhanced RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. Evaluation resolutions range from 448×448 (ISP) to 640×480 (UIE) and 600×400 (LLE), with the same 4K-parameter architecture applied universally across all three tasks without task-specific modifications.

## Method

MobileIE is a single-block CNN built around MBRConv, a multi-branch reparameterized convolution that captures multi-scale features via parallel 3×3 and 5×5 branches with Batch Normalization, then fuses them through a 1×1 conv that reparameterizes into a single standard convolution at inference. An Incremental Weight Optimization (IWO) strategy freezes well-trained weights from the first 1000 epochs and adds learnable residual weights to the fusion kernel, breaking training plateaus without adding inference cost. A Feature Self-Transform (FST) module introduces quadratic (squaring) feature interaction with learnable scale and bias to boost nonlinear expressiveness, and a Hierarchical Dual-Path Attention (HDPA) mechanism fuses global (average-pooling) and local (max-pooling) attention paths via element-wise multiplication. Training uses a Local Variance Weighted (LVW) loss that dynamically reweights pixel errors by their local deviation from the mean, scaled by local variance, to better optimize outlier regions without increasing parameter count.

## Results

MobileIE achieves 23.62 dB PSNR and 0.812 SSIM on LOLv1, and 25.08 dB PSNR and 0.845 SSIM on LOLv2-Real, with only 4.047K parameters and 0.02 MB model size. Inference runs at 0.895 ms on GPU (1,120 FPS at 600×400) and 6.72 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 SoC, making it the fastest method in the comparison. On UIEB, MobileIE reaches 22.81 dB PSNR and 0.906 SSIM, surpassing FiveA+ by 0.3 dB while using only 45% of its parameters. On the ZRR ISP benchmark, it achieves 21.43 dB PSNR at 980 FPS, delivering 3× faster inference than the next-lightest SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileIE outperforms all 12 LLE baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across LOLv1 and LOLv2-Real, while also achieving the lowest GPU latency (0.895 ms) and highest FPS (1,120). Against the closest lightweight competitor IAT [19] (86.9K params), MobileIE delivers comparable PSNR (23.62 vs 23.38 on LOLv1) using only 4.7% of the parameters and running 6.9× faster. Compared to SYELLE [27], the prior best lightweight CNN, MobileIE gains +2.59 dB PSNR on LOLv1 and +3.82 dB on LOLv2-Real while being slightly faster (0.895 vs 0.944 ms). On the UIE task, MobileIE surpasses FiveA+ [40] by 0.3 dB PSNR (22.81 vs 22.51) with only 45% of its parameters, and outperforms Boths [47] by 0.58 dB. On the ISP task, MobileIE achieves 21.43 dB PSNR on ZRR—beating SYEISP [27] (20.84 dB) and NAFNet [9] (21.12 dB)—while running 3× faster than the next-lightest method. The only metric where MobileIE does not lead is LPIPS on LOLv1 (0.198 vs IAT's 0.216), where IAT actually scores slightly better, though the gap is marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileIE extends the reparameterization paradigm from RepVGG (train multi-branch, infer single convolution) to image enhancement, where prior direct applications yielded unsatisfactory results. The Feature Self-Transform module adapts StarNet's quadratic interaction mechanism for lightweight feature nonlinearity, while the Hierarchical Dual-Path Attention draws on SE-Net-style channel attention but restructures it into a dual global-local pathway. The paper also builds on the SYE-Net line of work from the same group, which established that simple CNNs can achieve real-time IE on mobile, but pushes the parameter budget down from ~5K to ~4K and introduces the IWO training strategy and LVW loss to overcome the quality ceiling of ultra-compact models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobileIE demonstrates that a 4K-parameter CNN can match or slightly exceed methods with 10–86K parameters across three distinct IE tasks while sustaining ~1,100 FPS on mobile SoCs, making real-time UHD enhancement on consumer devices practically feasible. The IWO strategy (freezing prior weights and adding learnable increments) and LVW loss offer general-purpose training techniques for compact models that could transfer beyond IE. Open questions include whether this reparameterization-plus-IWO recipe scales to more complex degradations (e.g., multi-frame, video) or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the modest quality gap over the best lightweight baselines widens or narrows on more challenging real-world data.
