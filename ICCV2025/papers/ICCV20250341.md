## Problem

Video-LLMs require selecting a small number of key frames from long videos, but existing strategies—uniform sampling and query-relevance matching—fail to simultaneously capture three essential principles: query relevance, list-wise diversity among selected frames, and temporal sequentiality. This paper identifies that no prior method addresses all three, and that the resulting list-wise subset selection problem is NP-hard with no available supervised training data for a scoring function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 128 uniformly sampled candidate frames from a video and a natural-language query as input, producing a set of k=8 frame indices to feed into a downstream Video-LLM. Frame and query embeddings are extracted using the vision and text encoders of a pretrained VLM (e.g., SigLIP), so no additional parameters are introduced. The output is model-agnostic: the selected indices can be plugged into any Video-LLM at inference time without retraining, and the entire pipeline runs in O(nk²) time with k≪n.

## Method

MDP³ constructs a conditional similarity matrix via a Conditional Multiple Gaussian Kernel (CMGK) in RKHS, where frame-frame similarity is weighted by frame-query relevance, then applies a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) to this matrix so that the log-determinant score simultaneously encodes query relevance and list-wise diversity. To enforce sequentiality, the video is partitioned into T segments of length m, and DPP is applied within each segment conditioned on the preceding segment's selection, forming a Markov Decision Process whose state is (segment index, previous selection size, cumulative selections). A dynamic programming algorithm then optimally allocates the total budget k across segments in pseudo-polynomial time, yielding a (1−1/e)-approximate solution to the NP-hard problem. Lazy and parallel update strategies reduce practical complexity to O(nk²), matching standard DPP, and the entire procedure is training-free and model-agnostic.

## Results

On Video-MME (without subtitles), Ovis2+MDP³ achieves the highest overall accuracy of 63.9%, outperforming the previous best (LLaVA-OneVision* with 32 well-tuned frames at 58.2%) by 5.7 points while using only 8 frames. On MLVU, Ovis2+MDP³ reaches 73.9% (vs. 60.9% baseline), and on LongVideoBench it scores 62.7% (vs. 56.9% baseline). Across all four Video-LLMs tested, MDP³ consistently improves Video-MME accuracy by 4.8–5.4 points and MLVU by 11.2–13.0 points over uniform 8-frame sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDP³ consistently outperforms uniform 8-frame sampling across all four primary Video-LLMs: VILA-V1.5 (47.5→53.3), MiniCPM-V2.6 (52.6→58.0), LLaVA-OneVision (53.6→59.6), and Ovis2 (58.9→63.9) on Video-MME. Against FRAME-VOYAGER, a trained end-to-end selector tailored to VILA, MDP³ achieves higher accuracy on both VILA-V1.5 (53.3 vs. 50.5) and LLaVA-OneVision (59.6 vs. 57.5) despite being training-free and model-agnostic. Notably, LLaVA-OneVision+MDP³ with only 8 frames (59.6) surpasses the official LLaVA-OneVision* result using 32 well-tuned frames (58.2), demonstrating that intelligent frame selection is more effective than simply increasing frame count. On MLVU, MDP³ yields the largest absolute gains (e.g., +13.0 for Ovis2: 60.9→73.9), suggesting its list-wise diversity and sequentiality modeling is particularly beneficial for multi-task long-video understanding. On LongVideoBench, improvements are most pronounced for medium-length videos (15s–1m: +4.0 to +6.4 points), while very short videos (8s–15s) show minimal or slightly negative gains, consistent with the observation that frame selection matters less when uniform sampling already yields ~1 fps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential DPP framework for video summarization (Gong et al. 2014; Zheng & Lu 2021) to the VidQA setting by introducing a conditional multi-Gaussian kernel in RKHS to incorporate query relevance, and by formulating the allocation of selection size k across temporal segments as a Markov Decision Process solvable via dynamic programming. It departs from prior DPP-based video summarization work, which either allocates k uniformly across segments or relaxes integer-programming constraints, by providing an exact pseudo-polynomial DP solution without constraint relaxation. Unlike recent learned frame selectors such as FRAME-VOYAGER that require supervised training on a specific model, MDP³ is training-free and model-agnostic, reusing pretrained VLM embeddings at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of MDP³ makes it immediately deployable across any Video-LLM without retraining, which is practically significant given the rapid proliferation of new Video-LLM architectures. The (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for an NP-hard problem, combined with the finding that 8 well-selected frames outperform 32 uniformly sampled ones, challenges the common assumption that more frames always help. Open questions include whether the lazy DP approximations (using only the most recent frame as conditioning) lose substantial quality on very long videos, whether the approach transfers to tasks beyond VidQA (e.g., video grounding, summarization), and whether the method can be leveraged for test-time compute scaling as the authors suggest.
