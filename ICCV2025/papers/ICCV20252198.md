## Problem

CLIP-ViT, the standard vision encoder in MLLMs, flattens 2D images into 1D patch sequences, disrupting intrinsic spatial relationships between adjacent patches and limiting fine-grained visual understanding. Existing remedies—higher resolution, multiple encoders, or more visual tokens—inflate training and inference costs. The paper asks whether visual representation can be meaningfully enhanced without substantially increasing the number of visual tokens fed to the LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20252198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 336×336 RGB image and a natural-language instruction. The image is encoded by CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 into 576 patch features (24×24 grid, 1024-dim), to which only 6 additional visual spatial tokens are appended, yielding 582 total visual tokens—nearly identical to LLaVA-1.5's 576. The LLM (Vicuna-1.5-7B) autoregressively generates a text answer, with inference speed of ~20.3–20.5 tokens/s on a single A40 GPU, comparable to the baseline.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel Projector with two modules. The Spatial Feature Extractor (SFE) reshapes ViT patch features back to their 24×24×C spatial layout, then produces six multi-scale feature maps either by progressive inward cropping (Cropping variant, ordered 'central-to-global') or adaptive average pooling (Pooling variant, ordered 'abstract-to-specific'); convolutional kernels of matching sizes (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24) are applied to each map to yield six 1×1×C visual spatial tokens. The Detail Feature Integrator (DFI) then uses cross-attention—treating the six spatial tokens as queries and a larger feature map (from a 16×16 kernel) as keys/values—to inject fine-grained local details into the spatial tokens without adding extra tokens. Two model variants (Cropping for fine-grained tasks, Pooling for global semantics) are trained with LoRA under LLaVA-1.5's two-stage protocol (558K pre-training + 665K instruction data), outperforming fully-trained LLaVA-1.5 on 10 of 11 benchmarks.

## Results

LLaVA-SP-Cropping and LLaVA-SP-Pooling, fine-tuned with LoRA, outperform the fully trained LLaVA-1.5 on 10 out of 11 multimodal benchmarks. The most notable gains are on VizWiz (+5.9% over LLaVA-1.5*), TextVQA (+2.5%), SQA-Image (+2.1%), and MMBench (+1.3%), while the only benchmark where LLaVA-1.5 (full training) retains an edge is MME-Perception (1510.7 vs. 1473.8/1475.9). Inference speed is nearly identical to LLaVA-1.5 (20.51 and 20.28 tokens/s vs. 20.76 tokens/s on a single A40 GPU), with only 6 additional visual tokens (582 vs. 576 total).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-1.5 (both full-training and LoRA-reproduced), LLaVA-SP wins on 10 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest margin on VizWiz (+5.9% over LoRA LLaVA-1.5) and TextVQA (+2.5%); the sole loss is MME-Perception where full-training LLaVA-1.5 scores 1510.7 vs. 1473.8–1475.9. Compared to DeCo [60], LLaVA-SP-Pooling leads on VQAv2 (+5.1), GQA (+8.4), TextVQA (+2.1), POPE (+0.6), MME-P (+102.5), MMB (+5.1), and SEED-I (+4.7). Against Honeybee [3] on visual spatial understanding sub-tasks (Table 8), LLaVA-SP-Cropping achieves the best Avg^N of 46.5 vs. 44.7 for Honeybee and 46.3 for DeCo. LLaVA-SP also outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat [2] on VQAv2 (+0.9), GQA (+5.0), VizWiz (+12.7), and POPE (reported vs. not reported), while Qwen-VL-Chat retains an edge on TextVQA (61.5 vs. 58.7). In the generalization experiment (Table 7), applying the SP module to InternVL-2.0 [9] (InternViT-300M + Qwen2-0.5B) yields consistent gains of +1.6 to +2.3 in Avg^N over the base model, confirming the method transfers across encoder architectures. On inference speed (Table 2), LLaVA-SP matches LLaVA-1.5 (20.51/20.28 vs. 20.76 tokens/s) and is substantially faster than Qwen-VL (13.01) and Qwen2-VL (12.23) despite using a smaller encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLaVA-1.5 by replacing its simple MLP projector with a two-component projector (Spatial Feature Extractor + Detail Feature Integrator) that adds only six visual spatial tokens derived from the existing ViT patch features via convolutional kernels. It departs from the dominant trend of increasing visual token counts (higher resolution in LLaVA-1.5/InternVL, multiple encoders in SPHINX, dynamic resolution in LLaVA-NeXT) by instead reorganizing and re-extracting spatial information already present in the 576 patch features. Conceptually, it is closest to Honeybee (locality-enhanced projector) and DeCo (adaptive pooling for token compression), but differs in using progressive cropping or adaptive pooling to create structured multi-scale features before applying size-matched convolutions, and in adding a cross-attention fusion step (DFI) that injects fine-grained details without increasing token count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: six extra tokens (576→582) yield consistent gains across 10/11 benchmarks with negligible latency cost, making this a low-risk architectural add-on for existing LLaVA-based pipelines. The ablation showing DFI helps Cropping but actively hurts Pooling is a useful diagnostic about when attention-based feature fusion is appropriate versus when it disrupts already-abstracted representations. However, the absolute improvements are modest (1–2% on most benchmarks), the method is validated primarily on the 336-resolution CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 setup, and generalization to higher-resolution or dynamic-token MLLMs remains an open question. Whether the six-token budget is a fundamental limit or merely a practical trade-off for inference speed is not fully explored.
