## Problem

Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods for Vision Transformers are largely task-agnostic, applying uniform parameter updates and ignoring the varying importance of model components across different downstream tasks. Moreover, most approaches optimize parameter selection and token processing in isolation, despite the fact that token informativeness is tightly coupled to which parameters are active. This leads to suboptimal efficiency and accuracy, as redundant parameters are updated and uninformative tokens continue to consume computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images at 224×224 resolution, which are split into 196 patch tokens plus a [CLS] token by a ViT-B/16 backbone. During both training and inference, the token sequence is progressively shortened through selection and merging at designated layers, so the model processes fewer tokens in deeper layers without any additional inference-time modules. The output is a single class label, and only 0.34%–0.60% of the pre-trained parameters are updated, with no extra parameters introduced at inference.

## Method

TR-PTS jointly selects task-relevant parameters and tokens in a coordinated manner. For parameters, it computes the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) to score each parameter's task sensitivity, then applies a layer-wise allocation strategy: layers with higher aggregate FIM scores receive more trainable connections per neuron, while every layer retains at least one active connection. For tokens, it uses [CLS]-token attention scores to retain the top-ρ fraction of tokens and merges the remainder into a single weighted-average token, progressively shortening the sequence across layers. The key design insight is an observed correlation between parameter sparsity and token redundancy: token reduction is applied preferentially to layers with fewer task-relevant parameters (sparse layers), minimizing interference with critical computation. Training uses a binary mask to update only the selected parameters, and the co-adaptive loop between refined tokens and optimized parameters reinforces task-specific feature extraction.

## Results

On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained datasets), TR-PTS achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 91.94% while tuning only 0.60% of parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (88.54%) by 3.40% and the closest competitor GPS (91.78%) by 0.16%. On VTAB-1k (19 diverse tasks), TR-PTS reaches 75.92% mean accuracy with just 0.34% trainable parameters, exceeding full fine-tuning (65.57%) by 10.35% and GPS (75.18%) by 0.74%. TR-PTS also achieves the lowest FLOPs and memory consumption among all compared methods, with the shortest inference time due to progressive token reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR-PTS outperforms all seven compared baselines on both FGVC and VTAB-1k. Against the closest competitor GPS (gradient-based parameter selection), TR-PTS gains 0.16% on FGVC (91.94% vs. 91.78%) and 0.74% on VTAB-1k (75.92% vs. 75.18%), while using fewer trainable parameters (0.60% vs. 0.77% on FGVC; 0.34% vs. 0.25% on VTAB). Compared to full fine-tuning, TR-PTS achieves substantially higher accuracy (by 3.40% on FGVC and 10.35% on VTAB) while updating less than 1% of parameters. Against reparameterization-based methods like LoRA (84.78% FGVC, 72.63% VTAB) and SSF (90.72% FGVC, 73.10% VTAB), TR-PTS leads by 7.16% and 1.22% on FGVC respectively, and by 3.29% and 2.82% on VTAB. Addition-based methods (Adapter, AdaptFormer, VPT) trail by 2.8–6.3% on FGVC and 6.5–20.1% on VTAB, with TR-PTS also avoiding their inference-time overhead since no extra modules are introduced.

## Relation to Prior Work

TR-PTS extends the gradient-based parameter selection paradigm introduced by GPS (CVPR 2024) by replacing raw gradient magnitudes with Fisher Information Matrix scores, arguing that FIM better captures task-specific parameter sensitivity. It also draws on the token reduction literature (pruning and merging) but departs from prior work by unifying parameter selection and token refinement into a single task-driven framework, motivated by an observed correlation between parameter sparsity and token redundancy across layers. Unlike addition-based PEFT methods (VPT, Adapter, LoRA) that introduce extra modules, TR-PTS operates purely on the existing backbone parameters and token sequence without adding any new learnable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint parameter-token selection strategy offers a practical efficiency gain: TR-PTS achieves the lowest FLOPs and memory usage among compared methods while matching or slightly exceeding GPS accuracy, making it attractive for deployment-constrained settings. However, the empirical improvements over the closest baseline (GPS) are modest (0.74% on VTAB, 0.16% on FGVC), and the method has only been validated on ViT-B/16 for image classification. Open questions include whether the FIM-based selection generalizes to larger backbones, non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the parameter-token correlation holds under different pre-training regimes or architectural choices.
