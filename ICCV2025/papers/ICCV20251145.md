## Problem

Conventional navigation algorithms define success by proximity to a target object, ignoring whether the robot can actually reach and manipulate it from that position. In cluttered household environments, the robot may stop beyond its arm's workspace or be blocked by furniture, making the final 'last-mile' positioning between navigation and manipulation a critical but largely unaddressed gap. Existing datasets and benchmarks provide no supervision for learning manipulation-feasible final positions, and training-free LLM approaches cannot adapt to different arm morphologies or base configurations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB-D images and floor point clouds captured from a first-person camera mounted on the robotic arm (10 viewpoints per target, alternating left/right at increasing distances), along with robot-specific parameters encoding base platform height and arm operational radius. The output is a dense per-point navigation affordance map over the floor with continuous values in [0,1], where each value indicates the likelihood of successful manipulation from that position. Affordance labels are generated by physically attempting grasps at discrete floor positions within a semicircular region (radius = arm reach) around the target, then densified via k-nearest-neighbor Gaussian interpolation.

## Method

NavAff consists of two sequential modules. The Visual Alignment Module (VAM) extracts target and obstacle masks from the RGB image using BestMan, projects them onto the global point cloud via inverse depth projection to create binary target (+1) and obstacle (−1) channels, and processes the enriched point cloud with PointNet++. The Navigation Affordance Grounding Module (NAG) then applies PointNet++ separately to the feature-enhanced global point cloud and the floor point cloud, tokenizes both with an MLP, and fuses them via multi-head cross-attention where floor-point tokens serve as queries and the concatenation of global-point tokens with robot-specific tokens (base height, arm reach) serves as keys and values. A decoder maps the fused features to the final affordance map. Training uses a Weighted MSE loss that down-weights zero-valued ground-truth elements (weight λ applied with probability 0.5) to counteract the heavy class imbalance inherent in sparse affordance labels.

## Results

On the MoMa-Kitchen benchmark, NavAff achieves an RMSE of 0.147, logMSE of 0.0115, PCC of 0.680, and SIM of 0.696, outperforming all three adapted point-cloud baselines across every metric. In terms of practical manipulation success, NavAff reaches a Top-1 Manipulation Success Rate (MSR) of 72% and Top-5 MSR of 66%, compared to 60%/58% for PointNet++, 56%/53% for VoteNet, and 54%/47% for H3DNet. The Random baseline (no learned affordance) achieves only 8% Top-1 MSR, confirming the value of affordance-grounded positioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavAff outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against PointNet++ (the strongest baseline), NavAff reduces RMSE by 10.4% (0.147 vs. 0.164) and improves PCC by 20.4% (0.680 vs. 0.565) and SIM by 18.2% (0.696 vs. 0.589). Against VoteNet, the gains are larger: 12% lower RMSE and 25.2% higher PCC. H3DNet, the weakest baseline, trails NavAff by 15.5% in RMSE and 35.2% in PCC, which the authors attribute to its larger parameter count causing slower convergence under the fixed training budget. In the downstream Manipulation Success Rate evaluation, NavAff's 72% Top-1 MSR exceeds PointNet++ (60%), VoteNet (56%), and H3DNet (54%) by 12, 16, and 18 percentage points respectively, demonstrating that the affordance prediction gains translate directly into practical manipulation success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'navigation affordance grounding' as a distinct task: predicting floor-level affordance maps that specify where a mobile manipulator's base should stop to enable successful manipulation, rather than merely reaching a target location. It departs from conventional object-goal navigation (which defines success by proximity) and from LLM-based position-selection approaches that cannot account for arm morphology or manipulation feasibility. Methodologically, it repurposes PointNet++ as a point-cloud backbone and multi-head cross-attention for fusing global scene, floor, and robot-specific features, while leveraging BestMan and PartNet-Mobility for automated scene generation and affordance labeling across six robot platforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoMa-Kitchen provides the first large-scale (127K+ episodes) supervised signal for the 'last mile' positioning problem that has been a persistent gap between navigation and manipulation in mobile robotics. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer (using Grounded-SAM and Depth Anything v2 for real-world inference) suggests the benchmark can serve as a training substrate for heterogeneous hardware. However, the scope is limited to kitchen scenes and a small set of robot arms, and the Top-1 manipulation success rate of 72% indicates substantial room for improvement; open questions include generalization to non-kitchen environments, more complex multi-step manipulation, and whether the affordance formulation scales to articulated whole-body control.
