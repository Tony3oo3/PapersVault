## Problem

Standard 3D vision methods rely on dense depth measurements, yet low-cost SPAD-based ToF sensors (under $3, <5 mm) offer only a single pixel with a wide field-of-view per unit. The paper asks whether a small distributed set of such sensors (as few as 15) can recover parametric scene geometry—specifically 6D object pose—by exploiting the rich temporal information in raw transient histograms rather than reducing them to single depth values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICCV20250644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 15 raw 128-bin transient histograms (each a 1D photon-arrival waveform integrated over a ~30° FoV) from spatially distributed TMF8820 SPAD sensors, along with a known untextured 3D mesh and fixed sensor poses. It outputs a 6D pose (rotation via 6D representation + translation) for the object; for secondary tasks it outputs sphere center/diameter or MANO hand pose and articulation parameters. Unlike point-cloud pipelines, the input retains full temporal resolution (~1.4 cm bin width) rather than a single depth estimate per pixel.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage analysis-by-synthesis pipeline. First, a 4-block Transformer with MLP embeddings predicts initial scene parameters from the set of transient histograms, trained with full supervision on 200K synthetic samples per object generated by a custom differentiable renderer (with domain randomization on sensor position and albedo). Second, an optimization-based refiner iteratively minimizes the L2 difference between measured and rendered histograms using gradient descent (Adam, 200 steps), where the renderer models one-bounce Lambertian reflection, non-uniform laser intensity (fitted Gaussian-quartic profile), sigmoid-approximated time binning, and a jitter kernel derived from the sensor's reference histogram. Differentiable mesh rasterization is handled via Nvdiffrast, and albedo is jointly optimized during refinement. The key insight is that the full transient waveform—rather than a peak-detected depth—carries sufficient geometric information to disambiguate pose when combined with a strong parametric prior.

## Results

On the YCB benchmark (symmetric objects), the full method (feedforward + refiner) achieves a mean AUC-ADD-S of 92.20 using only 15 ToF pixels, matching the 407K-pixel RGB-D baseline (FoundationPose, 92.01) and substantially outperforming the single-pixel point cloud baseline (82.96, which also uses oracle ground-truth initialization). On non-symmetric 3D-printed objects, the method reaches 80.71 AUC-ADD with 15 pixels, a 21-point improvement over the single-pixel point cloud (59.73) but still trailing the RGB-D baseline (86.14). The refiner contributes a consistent 1–10 point gain over the feedforward network alone, with the largest improvement (10 points) on the 3D-printed objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-pixel point cloud baseline (which uses oracle GT pose for ICP initialization), the proposed method wins by 9.2 points on YCB (92.20 vs 82.96) and 21.0 points on 3D-printed objects (80.71 vs 59.73), demonstrating that the full transient histogram encodes far more information than a single depth estimate. Against the 16×16 point cloud baseline (256× more pixels, still with oracle GT), the method trails by 4.9 points on YCB and 15.7 points on 3D-printed objects, confirming that dense point clouds still hold an advantage when available. Against FoundationPose (RGB-D, 407K pixels), the method essentially matches on YCB (92.20 vs 92.01) but lags by 5.4 points on 3D-printed objects (80.71 vs 86.14). Against FoundPose (single-view RGB, 407K pixels), the method wins decisively by 26.0 points on YCB and 15.8 points on 3D-printed objects, as the RGB baseline struggles without metric depth or texture. The hand-pose comparisons to DiscoBand (11.96 mm PA-MPJPE) and HaMeR (6.0 mm) are explicitly noted as non-comparable context due to different datasets and protocols; the authors' best hand result (8.18 mm) is competitive but not directly benchmarked against these.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analysis-by-synthesis framework for low-cost SPAD ToF sensors established by Mu et al. [32] and Sifferman et al. [39], modifying their sensor model to support differentiable mesh rendering for parametric scene recovery. Where prior AbS work required >100 single-pixel measurements [32] or combined ToF with RGB [3, 25], this paper pushes the sensing budget to just 15 diffuse pixels by exploiting strong geometric priors (known object meshes, parametric shape models). The feedforward component draws on Transformer architectures and sim-to-real transfer strategies from the broader 6D pose estimation literature [19, 20, 46], while the differentiable renderer builds on Nvdiffrast-based pipelines adapted for transient histogram synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 15 diffuse ToF pixels—roughly 27,000× fewer than a standard depth camera—can achieve 6D pose estimation competitive with RGB-D baselines, opening a path toward ultra-low-cost, distributed 3D sensing for wearables and robotics. However, the current system is constrained to tabletop scenes with known sensor poses, Lambertian surfaces, and a limited 1.5 m range, and the sim-to-real gap remains significant for close-range tasks like hand pose (where unmodeled gating/pile-up effects degrade synthetic data). Key open questions include robustness to uncontrolled environments, scaling to larger and more complex scenes, and whether large-scale synthetic training can close the remaining sim-to-real gap without real-data fine-tuning.
