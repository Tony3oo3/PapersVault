## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a fixed accuracy-latency footprint during inference, making them unable to adapt to varying compute budgets or input content complexity in real-world deployments where resource availability fluctuates. Prior efficiency methods (lightweight architectures, token pruning, mixture-of-experts) produce static tradeoffs and cannot dynamically reconfigure a single model to meet different latency requirements at runtime.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a text query, and a scalar latency budget (expressed as a percentage of the base model's prefill FLOPs, ranging from 50% to 100%). The latency budget is encoded via sinusoidal positional encoding into a 256-D vector, then converted by a 2-layer MLP into a latency token appended to the input sequence. The output is a text answer generated autoregressively, conditioned on a binary execution plan that specifies which Transformer blocks, attention heads, or MLP neuron groups in the latter half of the LLM are activated.

## Method

AdaLLaVA equips the LLM component of an MLLM with K binary switches (on entire Transformer blocks for AdaLLaVA-L, or on individual attention heads and grouped MLP neurons for AdaLLaVA-H), effectively turning the model into a collection of shallower sub-models with shared parameters. A lightweight learned scheduler, implemented as a linear layer reading the processed latency token from the first few Transformer blocks, outputs a categorical distribution over the K switches; exactly k switches are then sampled without replacement, where k is the maximum number that satisfies the latency budget. The key technical choice is probabilistic rather than deterministic modeling: the scheduler learns a distribution over valid execution plans, and training jointly optimizes the LLM and scheduler by minimizing negative log-likelihood with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to backpropagate through the discrete sampling. At inference, the most probable execution plan (argmax over the scheduler's distribution) is selected, bypassing intractable marginalization while maintaining 0% latency violation.

## Results

On six MLLM benchmarks (VQAv2, ScienceQA, TextVQA, POPE, MME, MMBench), AdaLLaVA-H with LLaVA-1.5-7B achieves 99.0% of baseline average performance at 85% latency budget (7.2T FLOPs, 69ms prefill) and 98.2% at 60% budget (5.1T FLOPs, 49ms prefill), with only a 1.0% and 1.9% average accuracy drop respectively. At 100% budget, AdaLLaVA-H matches or slightly exceeds the base LLaVA-1.5 on most benchmarks (e.g., 68.5 vs. 66.8 on ScienceQA, 86.9 vs. 85.9 on POPE). When integrated with PruMerge+ token selection at 85% budget, AdaLLaVA-H reduces FLOPs by 70% (2.6T vs. 8.6T) while maintaining within 1.7% accuracy of the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LLaVA-1.5, AdaLLaVA-H at 100% budget matches or slightly exceeds the baseline on ScienceQA (68.5 vs. 66.8) and POPE (86.9 vs. 85.9), while being marginally lower on VQAv2 (77.9 vs. 78.5) and TextVQA (57.1 vs. 58.2). At 85% budget, AdaLLaVA-H retains 99.0% of baseline average accuracy with only a 1.0% drop, and at 60% budget it retains 98.2% with a 1.9% drop—significantly better than AdaLLaVA-L which drops 3.4% at 60%. Compared to PruMerge+ (a token-selection baseline at 3.0T FLOPs), AdaLLaVA-H integrated with PruMerge+ at 85% budget (2.6T FLOPs) achieves a 2.45% average performance boost over PruMerge+ alone, and at 60% budget (1.8T FLOPs) only drops 1.01% from PruMerge+. Against FastV (4.9T FLOPs), AdaLLaVA-H integrated with FastV at 60% budget (3.0T FLOPs) matches or exceeds FastV on ScienceQA (68.3 vs. 68.7) and MMBench (65.3 vs. 64.3) while using 39% fewer FLOPs. The paper also demonstrates generalization to Mipha-3B (Phi-2.7B-based), where AdaLLaVA-L at 85% budget retains 99.3% of Mipha-3B's average benchmark performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive inference / dynamic neural network literature (early exit, layer skipping, conditional computation) from CNNs and LLMs to the multimodal setting, specifically targeting latency-budget-aware scheduling for MLLMs. It builds directly on LLaVA-1.5 as the base model and is positioned as orthogonal to prior efficiency efforts like token pruning (PruMerge+, FastV) and lightweight architectures (Phi-2, TinyLLaVA), demonstrating compatibility with both. The key conceptual departure from prior adaptive inference work is the introduction of a learned probabilistic scheduler that jointly optimizes the LLM and scheduler under a hard latency constraint, rather than relying on hand-crafted routing rules or relaxed penalty-based objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in MLLM deployment: the inability of current models to adapt to fluctuating compute resources (edge device contention, server load variation) while maintaining quality. The probabilistic modeling approach that enforces zero latency violations during training is a clean technical contribution that outperforms the deterministic alternative in both accuracy and resource utilization. Limitations include focusing only on the LLM component (not the vision encoder), using FLOPs as a proxy for actual latency, and leaving system-level serving optimization for future work. Open questions include cross-device portability of the learned scheduler and whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities.
